# CADEngBench: It Looks Like CAD, but Does It Work? Evaluating Parametric Design, Assembly Reasoning, and Physics Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CADEngBench is a two-track benchmark treating CAD as an executable engineering model rather than a shape. CADEngBench-P scores 300 parametric parts over 600 zero-to-CAD and functional-editing tasks via B-Rep validity, DFM checks, parameter perturbation and matched CalculiX FEA; CADEngBench-A grounds 150 body pairs in exact mating entities, joints and kinematics. Across eight models, editing far outpaces generation while matched FEA and joint grounding stay weak.

## Problem

- Existing CAD benchmarks score shape similarity or one-shot executability, so a program can render correctly while its named parameters drive the wrong feature.
- Prior datasets evaluate single parts at one final state, never testing whether supplied CAD survives controlled functional edits without damaging dependent non-target geometry.
- Assembly and physics work is evaluated in isolation from executable parametric generation, and joint prediction rarely requires naming the exact mating faces or edges.

## Method

- **CADEngBench-P** layers ==L0 execution== and ==STEP round-trip==, ==L1 engineering plus DFM screens==, ==L2-Z parameter-family perturbation==, and ==L2-E hidden-measurement functional editing== over 300 parts.
- ==L3 physics verification== rebuilds generated and reference solids, meshes them in ==Gmsh==, solves linear-static cases in ==CalculiX==, comparing p95 von Mises stress, displacement, compliance.
- **CADEngBench-A** supplies indexed ==B-Rep== candidate atlases for 150 body pairs; ==A1== scores ranked joint and exact entity retrieval, ==A2== verifies joint frames through ==PyBullet== URDF motion.

## Results

- Only **1,030** of **2,400** generated programs execute, and **58.1%** of those violate a stated engineering or DFM check; Gemini leads L1 at **30.0%**, Claude L0 at **58.0%**.
- Editing wins over generation by **4.64x** in exclusive passes, yet single independent-body edits pass **99.6%** versus **40.2-45.8%** once joins, cuts, or multiple bodies couple the history.
- CalculiX fails only **5** of **1,809** attempts; correct rank-1 entities appear in **542/960** predictions but just **26.4%** also type the joint, and **11.5%** pass end-to-end.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Executability is an entry condition, not correctness: a solver that completes says nothing about whether the structural response matches the reference design.
> - Test parameter families rather than single design states; 22 of 435 matched cases flip verdict away from the default parameter value.
> - Localizing where two bodies meet is far easier than naming the relation, and capability ranks across generation, editing, and assembly are nearly uncorrelated.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence in computer-aided design (CAD) has seen advancements in generating geometric models from various inputs, including text, images, and existing designs. Much of this progress has focused on generating visually plausible or geometrically similar CAD outputs. Existing benchmarks, such as ABC, DeepCAD, and Fusion 360 Gallery, have contributed large-scale B-Rep datasets and construction histories, establishing CAD as an executable sequence. Subsequent work, including Text2CAD, CAD-Coder, and CADEvolve, has focused on text-to-CAD generation, while benchmarks like HistCAD, BenchCAD, and CADTests have introduced elements of executable code editing and prompt requirement checks. Efforts such as MUSE and BenDFM have expanded evaluation to include functional, manufacturable, and assemblable specifications. Concurrently, specialized research avenues have explored assembly prediction (e.g., AutoMate, JoinABLe, ArtiCAD, AssemblyBench) and physics-aware CAD agents leveraging finite element analysis (FEA) for design feedback.

However, the authors identify a critical evaluation gap: most existing benchmarks address discrete stages of the CAD workflow. They often stop at generating a single-value shape, performing local edits, retrieving a joint, or providing FEA feedback. These benchmarks do not comprehensively test whether generated executable CAD models maintain their integrity and desired behavior through parametric changes and functional edits, whether assembly predictions correctly identify exact B-Rep entities and support valid kinematic motion, or if physics simulations remain consistent across parametric design families. The CADEngBench framework positions itself to bridge this gap by integrating these diverse engineering-grade evaluations into a unified two-track benchmark, thus providing a more holistic assessment of AI-generated CAD models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing CADEngBench stems from the observation that an AI-generated CAD model, despite appearing geometrically correct, may not possess engineering-grade quality. This discrepancy can manifest in several critical ways that existing shape-similarity or basic execution metrics fail to capture. Specific problems include:
- A generated program might fail to execute correctly or consistently, leading to an invalid model.
- Named dimensions or parameters might not control the intended geometric features, or their manipulation could inadvertently affect unrelated parts of the design.
- Functional edits, such as increasing a flange radius, might damage critical design elements like mounting holes or alter non-target properties.
- The simulated physical response (e.g., stress, deformation) of a generated model might differ significantly from a reference, even under identical analysis conditions.
- In assembly contexts, a model might identify a plausible region for a joint but fail to select the exact mating entities required for a correct and functional assembly relationship.

The authors argue that simply comparing images or geometric shapes is insufficient to evaluate CAD models, as CAD is fundamentally "both geometry and an executable engineering model." Therefore, the key objectives of CADEngBench are:
1. **To develop a comprehensive two-track benchmark**: This includes CADEngBench-P for evaluating single parts (covering generation, editing, and physics simulation) and CADEngBench-A for evaluating assembly relations and motion between body pairs.
2. **To establish a layered evaluation hierarchy**: Moving beyond basic B-Rep validity and nominal shape similarity, the benchmark aims to test engineering requirements, design-for-manufacturability (DFM) checks, operational parameterization, functional editing capabilities (including preservation of non-target properties), and physics verification.
3. **To introduce rigorous assembly evaluation**: This involves requiring models to accurately retrieve exact mating faces or edges, predict the correct joint type, and demonstrate valid motion through kinematic simulation.
4. **To integrate FEA-based physics verification**: Using CalculiX, the benchmark compares stress, deformation, compliance, and stress concentration across parametric design families, ensuring physical consistency of the generated models.
By addressing these objectives, CADEngBench seeks to provide a more robust and industrially relevant assessment of AI capabilities in CAD, identifying shortcomings that superficial evaluations overlook.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CADEngBench is structured as a two-track benchmark, CADEngBench-P for parametric parts and CADEngBench-A for assembly reasoning, each featuring a layered evaluation design.

**Dataset Construction:**
- **CADEngBench-P**: Comprises 300 parts (159 from BenchCAD CadQuery programs and 141 from Fusion 360 Reconstruction histories). Each part is used for two tasks: one zero-to-CAD generation task and one functional-editing task, totaling 600 tasks. The dataset balances difficulty (100 easy, 100 medium, 100 hard parts) and covers 102 BenchCAD families, 55 CAD operations, and 12 edit types. For 164 parts, L3 FEA tests are also available. Source CAD artifacts are replayed to extract B-Reps, parameters, operations, and interfaces. Annotations from an engineering LLM and a visual VLM provide functional and visual context, but only claims grounded in source metadata, B-Rep measurements, or labeled benchmark scenarios are used for scoring.
- **CADEngBench-A**: Contains 150 body pairs derived from Fusion 360 assembly-joint data, covering rigid, revolute, slider, cylindrical, pin-slot, planar, and ball relations. Bodies are decomposed into B-Reps, faces and edges are indexed, and recorded joints are bound to specific entities, frames, and motion semantics. 30 pairs were reserved for evaluator development, leaving 120 for model evaluation.

**CADEngBench-P: Parametric Generation, Editing, and FEA**
Models are prompted to convert an engineering brief, reference views, parameters, and requirements into an executable CadQuery program. For editing tasks, the model receives source CAD and a functional-edit request, with non-target features specified for preservation.
- **L0: Program and Solid Validity**: Tests if the generated CadQuery program executes, creates at least one valid B-Rep solid, exports to STEP, and can be successfully re-imported.
- **L1: Engineering Requirements and DFM**: Checks if the valid solid satisfies engineering requirements (e.g., dimensions, feature counts, interfaces) and DFM criteria (e.g., minimum wall thickness 1.0mm, minimum hole diameter 2.0mm, hole depth/diameter ratio ≤ 8).
- **L2-Z (Zero-to-CAD): Parametric Perturbations**: Evaluates parametric integrity by rebuilding the generated part at default, intermediate, and valid boundary parameter values. It verifies if the declared parameter controls the intended geometry and preserves protected properties.
- **L2-E (Edit): Functional-Design Edits**: Assesses the model's ability to perform controlled modifications on existing CAD. The edited part must execute, achieve the requested change, and preserve specified non-target properties.
- **L3: CalculiX FEA Across Parameter Changes**: Compares generated and reference CAD under identical linear-static FEA conditions (material, supports, loads). For each parameter state, both solids are meshed (Gmsh) and solved (CalculiX). It measures multiplicative differences in 95th-percentile von Mises stress, maximum displacement, and normalized compliance or stress concentration. A family passes if all states yield valid simulations, satisfy engineering limits, and remain within FEA comparison tolerances.

**CADEngBench-A: Joint Grounding and Motion**
Models receive two bodies, multiview renders, and indexed B-Rep face/edge candidates.
- **A0: Dataset Integrity**: Verifies item identity, candidate IDs, source provenance, split separation, and absence of hidden data in model inputs.
- **A1: Joint and B-Rep Entity Retrieval**: Models return up to three hypotheses, each with a joint family, one entity from Body A, one from Body B, and a confidence score. Evaluation involves strict JSON parsing and exact role-aware matching against source entity pairs and joint types.
- **A2: Joint Frame and Motion**: Conditions on the rank-1 A1 hypothesis. Models predict the joint origin and family-specific axes/directions in Body A's coordinate frame, as well as the relative transform from Body B to Body A. Predicted frames are executed as URDF joints in PyBullet to verify allowed motion and detect forbidden translations/rotations within defined tolerances.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **CADEngBench-P**: L0 (program/solid validity), L1 (engineering/DFM compliance), L2-Z (parametric integrity across perturbations), L2-E (functional edit success and preservation), L3 reach (FEA simulation possible), L3 pair pass (FEA agreement with reference).
- **CADEngBench-A**: Valid (output schema), Entity@k (correct B-Rep entity pair in top k), Typed@k (correct joint family AND entity pair in top k), MRR (Mean Reciprocal Rank for full correctness), A2 E2E (end-to-end pass: correct rank-1 joint, frame, and verified motion).

**Experimental Protocol**: Eight multimodal, code-capable models (GPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3 Flash, GLM-4.6V, Kimi K2.5, Mistral Medium 3.5, Llama 4 Maverick, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B) were evaluated. Each model provided one response per task without retries. Confidence intervals were computed using 10,000 item-clustered bootstrap samples, and pairwise comparisons for L0-L2 used McNemar tests with Holm correction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of eight multimodal, code-capable models across CADEngBench revealed several patterns and limitations in current AI capabilities for engineering-grade CAD:

**Overall Performance Profile:**
- There was substantial variation across models and evaluation stages, with no single model demonstrating superior performance across all metrics. For instance, Gemini led in engineering-requirement satisfaction, functional editing, matched FEA, and assembly retrieval, while Claude excelled in executability, parametric behavior, and L3 simulation reach.
- Performance ranks showed weak correlation between different capabilities; generation and editing ranks had a Spearman correlation (ρs) of 0.214, and parametric construction and assembly motion ranks were nearly unrelated (ρs = 0.048). This suggests that different underlying model strengths are required for distinct CAD tasks.

**CADEngBench-P (Parametric Generation, Editing, and FEA):**
- **Execution vs. Engineering Requirements**: Only 1,030 out of 2,400 generated programs passed L0 (executability), and of these, only 432 also passed L1 (engineering and DFM requirements). This indicates that 58.1% of executable outputs violated at least one engineering or DFM requirement, demonstrating that executability alone is an insufficient indicator of engineering quality.
- **Editing vs. Generation Difficulty**: Editing supplied CAD was considerably easier than generating it from scratch. Among scoreable item-model pairs, 1,071 passed only editing, compared to 231 that passed only generation, representing a 4.64 times asymmetry.
- **Impact of Edit Complexity**: Editability significantly decreased when edits were structurally coupled to existing CAD model histories (e.g., involving joins, cuts, or multiple bodies), with pass rates ranging from 40.2% to 45.8%. In contrast, edits involving single independent-body additions achieved a 99.6% pass rate. This highlights the difficulty in maintaining dependent geometry during complex operational history modifications.
- **Physics Verification Challenges**: While models capable of producing more valid CAD (passing preceding layers) reached more L3 FEA cases (ρs = 0.976), this "reach" was unrelated to the agreement with the reference FEA response (ρs = 0.024). Many failures occurred before the solver (573 attempts failed due to meshing or boundary condition assignment), and a significant number (554 attempts) solved but either violated engineering limits or disagreed with the reference structural response. Furthermore, testing only a single parameter value could conceal physics failures, as 22 out of 435 matched cases showed a change in verdict when evaluated across a parameter family.

**CADEngBench-A (Joint Grounding and Motion):**
- **Entity Localization vs. Joint Specification**: Models frequently localized the correct B-Rep entity pair (542 out of 960 requests at Entity@1), but a smaller fraction (143, or 26.4%) also correctly identified the joint family and body ordering (Typed@1). This indicates a common failure where models identify relevant regions but cannot fully specify the precise joint.
- **Joint Family Dependency**: Performance varied significantly by joint family. Revolute (59.9% Typed@3) and cylindrical (49.4% Typed@3) joints were retrieved more successfully than slider (14.7%), planar (10.0%), and pin-slot (0.0%) joints. The pin-slot joint, a compound constraint, often led to misclassification (e.g., as revolute or slider), resulting in a 0% score across all models.
- **Joint Frame and Kinematic Motion**: Only 110 out of 142 correctly identified rank-1 joints had their frame placed within tolerance. Overall, only 11.5% of all requests passed A2 E2E (correct rank-1 joint, accurate joint frame, and verified allowed/blocked motion). Errors predominantly occurred in entity selection, joint classification, and frame placement, rather than in the kinematic execution itself; every correctly specified ideal joint successfully completed its requested motion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CADEngBench represents an advancement in the rigorous evaluation of AI models for CAD, moving beyond superficial geometric validation to assess engineering-grade capabilities. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Elevating CAD AI Standards**: By establishing a comprehensive framework that incorporates engineering requirements, parametric integrity, functional editing, physics simulation, and assembly reasoning, CADEngBench sets a higher standard for what constitutes a "correct" or "functional" AI-generated CAD model. This encourages the development of AI systems that are not just aesthetically pleasing but also technically sound and usable in engineering workflows.
- **Identifying Critical Gaps in Current AI Models**: The benchmark systematically exposes fundamental limitations of existing multimodal AI models, revealing that while they may generate plausible shapes, they often struggle with the nuanced demands of engineering design. Specific findings, such as the high failure rate of executable code to meet engineering requirements, the difficulty in complex structural edits, the disconnect between FEA "reach" and accurate physical response, and the challenges in precisely grounding assembly relations, pinpoint areas where future AI research efforts need to be concentrated.
- **Guiding Future Research Directions**: The layered evaluation design and detailed failure diagnostics provide a roadmap for AI researchers. Instead of simply aiming for higher generation scores, developers can now focus on specific bottlenecks, such as improving parameter binding, enhancing constraint-aware editing, ensuring physical consistency across design variations, and refining exact B-Rep entity grounding for assembly. The observation that different capabilities require different model strengths also suggests a need for more specialized or modular AI architectures.
- **Facilitating Industrial Adoption**: For AI-powered design tools to be adopted in industry, they must demonstrate reliability, predictability, and adherence to engineering principles. CADEngBench provides a metric-driven approach to quantify these qualities, building confidence in AI-generated designs. It supports the transition from AI as a conceptual ideation tool to a reliable partner in critical engineering tasks.
- **Resource for Dataset and Benchmarking Community**: The curated datasets, encompassing 600 part tasks and 150 assembly pairs, complete with detailed annotations and executable validation checks, serve as a valuable public resource. This dataset can be used to train, fine-tune, and benchmark new AI models, accelerating progress in the field of intelligent CAD.
- **Challenging the Definition of "Correctness"**: The core premise that "CAD looks correct, but does it work?" fundamentally challenges the community to redefine "correctness" in AI-generated CAD, shifting it from visual fidelity to functional and engineering veracity. This perspective is crucial for developing AI systems that can truly act as "co-engineers."
