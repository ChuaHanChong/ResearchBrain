# MME: A Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MME introduces a new comprehensive benchmark to quantitatively evaluate Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), featuring manually constructed, leakage-free instruction-answer pairs across 14 perception and cognition subtasks. The benchmark assesses 30 MLLMs, revealing significant performance gaps and identifying prevalent issues such as instruction non-compliance, perceptual failures, reasoning breakdowns, and object hallucination.

## Problem

- A lack of comprehensive and quantitative evaluation frameworks for emergent MLLM capabilities, often relying on qualitative demonstrations.
- Risk of data leakage when evaluating MLLMs on traditional multimodal datasets due to their vast and opaque training sets.
- Challenges in objective scoring and fair comparisons due to open-ended evaluations, small sample sizes, and MLLM sensitivity to prompt engineering.

## Method

- Developed **MME**, a novel benchmark featuring ==14 subtasks== divided into 10 perception and 4 cognition abilities.
- Manually constructed all instruction-answer pairs into concise ==yes/no questions== to prevent ==data leakage== and minimize prompt engineering impact.
- Employed strict evaluation metrics like ==Accuracy (ACC)== and ==Accuracy+ (ACC+)== to ensure objective and robust quantitative analysis of MLLM performance.

## Results

- Evaluated **30** advanced MLLMs, producing detailed leaderboards for each of the 14 subtasks and overall perception/cognition scores.
- GPT-4V achieved a score of **185** in OCR and **170** in Code Reasoning, significantly outperforming other models in these specific tasks.
- Object hallucination was a major issue, leading to approximately **50%** accuracy and near **0%** Accuracy+ for questions designed to test this failure mode.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing MLLMs still have substantial room for improvement across both perception and cognition abilities, particularly in areas like object position and complex reasoning.
> - Identified four prominent problems: inability to follow basic 'yes/no' instructions, fundamental perceptual failures, breakdowns in logical reasoning chains, and object hallucination.
> - GPT-4V demonstrated superior performance in OCR and code reasoning, but also exhibited policy-based refusals for certain image-based queries involving individuals or private artwork.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have inevitably led to the emergence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which integrate powerful LLMs with capabilities to process and reason over multimodal information, such as images. Recent studies have showcased astonishing emergent abilities in MLLMs, including generating poems from images, performing OCR-free mathematical reasoning (e.g., PaLM-E), and exhibiting sophisticated instruction following (e.g., MiniGPT-4, GPT-4V), in-context learning (e.g., Flamingo), and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. These qualitative demonstrations have generated significant excitement, heralding a new era in artificial intelligence.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in the research landscape: the lack of a comprehensive and quantitative evaluation framework for these emergent MLLMs. While anecdotal examples and case studies highlight impressive capabilities, they fail to provide a systematic and objective measure of model performance across diverse tasks and abilities. The paper points out several limitations of existing evaluation approaches:
- **Reliance on Traditional Multimodal Datasets:** Evaluating MLLMs on older datasets designed for tasks like image captioning or Visual Question Answering (VQA) may not adequately capture their emergent reasoning capabilities. Furthermore, the lack of unified training sets for large models raises the significant risk of data leakage, where models might have inadvertently seen portions of the test data during their extensive pre-training, leading to inflated and misleading performance metrics.
- **Limited Open-Ended Evaluations:** Existing attempts at open-ended evaluations often suffer from unavailable data, small sample sizes (e.g., only 50 images), or challenges in objective scoring, making comprehensive and fair comparisons difficult.
- **Narrow Focus of Specialized Benchmarks:** Other benchmarks focus on specific, isolated aspects of MLLMs, such as object hallucination or adversarial robustness. While valuable, these do not offer a holistic view of an MLLM's overall capabilities.

MME steps into this void by presenting itself as the "first comprehensive MLLM Evaluation benchmark." It aims to bridge the gap between qualitative demonstrations and robust quantitative assessment. By addressing the issues of comprehensiveness, data leakage, prompt engineering sensitivity, and objective scoring, MME establishes a much-needed standardized tool for the systematic analysis and comparison of MLLMs. This work is crucial for moving beyond anecdotal evidence and towards a more scientific understanding of MLLM strengths and weaknesses, thereby guiding future research and development in a more focused and efficient manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to introduce MME, a novel and comprehensive evaluation benchmark specifically designed for Multimodal Large Language Models. This benchmark aims to provide a systematic and quantitative framework for assessing the multifaceted capabilities of MLLMs, addressing the shortcomings of current evaluation paradigms.

The core motivations behind the development of MME stem directly from the limitations observed in the existing evaluation landscape:
- **Need for Comprehensive Assessment:** While MLLMs demonstrate impressive emergent abilities, their performance is largely understood through qualitative examples. There is an urgent need for a benchmark that can quantitatively measure a wide range of abilities, covering both fundamental perception and higher-level cognition, to truly understand how well these models perform across various aspects.
- **Mitigating Data Leakage Risks:** The vast and often opaque training datasets of MLLMs make it impossible to guarantee that models have not been exposed to conventional test sets. This renders evaluations on existing public datasets unreliable. MME aims to circumvent this by creating entirely new, manually designed instruction-answer pairs and leveraging existing image data without its original annotations, or by generating new images, thereby ensuring a fair and leakage-free evaluation.
- **Ensuring Fair Model Comparison:** The sensitivity of MLLMs to prompt engineering means that subtle changes in instructions can significantly alter performance. This makes fair comparisons between different models challenging. MME's design of concise and standardized "yes/no" instructions seeks to minimize this variability, allowing for a more equitable assessment of intrinsic model capabilities rather than prompt engineering prowess.
- **Enabling Objective Quantitative Analysis:** Evaluating the open-ended responses of MLLMs poses significant challenges for objective quantitative scoring, often relying on subjective manual grading or imperfect AI-based metrics. MME's forced-choice "yes/no" answer format simplifies this, enabling accurate and objective statistical analysis.
- **Identifying Strengths and Weaknesses to Guide Development:** By comprehensively evaluating a large number of advanced MLLMs, the authors aim to identify common limitations and "prominent problems" (such as instruction non-compliance, perceptual failures, reasoning breakdowns, and object hallucination). This diagnostic capability is critical for revealing "potential directions for subsequent model optimization" and driving the evolution of MLLM research.

In essence, MME is motivated by the desire to establish a rigorous scientific foundation for MLLM evaluation, ensuring that future advancements are built upon a clear and measurable understanding of model capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MME benchmark is meticulously designed around four core characteristics to achieve its objectives: comprehensiveness, data leakage avoidance, concise instruction design, and objective quantitative statistics.

#### 3.1. Instruction Design

The hallmark of MME's instruction design is its simplicity and binary response format. Each instruction consists of a concise question followed by the mandatory phrase "Please answer yes or no." For every test image, two distinct questions are manually designed: one with a ground truth "yes" answer and another with a "no" answer. This dual-question approach for each image serves as a stricter test, ensuring that a model comprehends the image and underlying knowledge rather than merely guessing. It also standardizes the output format, facilitating automated evaluation. The concise nature of the instructions aims to minimize the impact of prompt engineering, asserting that robust MLLMs should generalize well to such straightforward directives.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Metrics

Given the "yes" or "no" output constraint, MME employs two key metrics:
- **Accuracy (ACC):** This is a standard measure calculated for each individual question, representing the proportion of correctly answered questions. Random accuracy for a binary choice is 50%.
- **Accuracy+ (ACC+):** This is a stricter metric, calculated per image. For a model to score correctly on ACC+, it must answer *both* the "yes" and "no" questions associated with that image correctly. This ensures a deeper understanding and prevents models from consistently guessing "yes" or "no" to achieve a moderate ACC score. Random accuracy for ACC+ is 25%.
- **Subtask Score:** The final score for each subtask is the sum of its ACC and ACC+ values. This combined score provides a holistic view of performance within each specific capability.
- **Overall Scores:** Perception and cognition overall scores are derived by summing the scores of their respective subtasks. The full perception score is 2000, and the full cognition score is 800.

#### 3.3. Data Collection

A significant effort was made to ensure data integrity and avoid leakage:
- **Manual Construction:** All instruction-answer pairs across all 14 subtasks are manually constructed by human annotators.
- **Image Sourcing:**
 - For **Perception Tasks**:
 - **Coarse-Grained Recognition (Existence, Count, Position, Color):** Images are sampled from the COCO dataset. Crucially, *only the images* are used, and the original COCO annotations are *not* utilized. Instead, new instruction-answer pairs are manually created, forcing MLLMs to interpret instructions and infer answers independently. Each subtask includes 30 images with 60 instruction-answer pairs.
 - **Fine-Grained Recognition (Poster, Celebrity, Scene, Landmark, Artwork):** Images are drawn from publicly available datasets (e.g., MovieNet, Google Landmarks, Places), but again, all instruction-answer pairs are manually designed. For celebrity recognition, a red bounding box is added around the person for clarity. Image counts vary from 147 to 200 per subtask.
 - **OCR:** Images are sampled from a curved text detection dataset, with manually designed instruction-answer pairs. This subtask includes 20 images and 40 pairs, focusing on relatively simple examples given the nascent stage of MLLMs.
 - For **Cognition Tasks**:
 - **Commonsense Reasoning, Numerical Calculation, Text Translation, Code Reasoning:** Images for these tasks are predominantly manually photographed or generated using diffusion models. All instruction-answer pairs are manually designed. These tasks are crafted to require MLLMs to integrate visual perception with internal knowledge and logical reasoning. Each cognition subtask typically contains 20-70 images and 40-140 instruction-answer pairs, starting with relatively easy problems.

#### 3.4. Experimentation

The authors conducted extensive experiments, evaluating the zero-shot performance of 30 "advanced MLLMs" on the MME benchmark. This comprehensive evaluation includes well-known models such as BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, GPT-4V, LLaVA, and others, representing a broad spectrum of current MLLM architectures and capabilities. The zero-shot setting ensures that models are tested on their generalized understanding without specific in-context examples, reflecting their inherent abilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation of 30 advanced MLLMs on the MME benchmark revealed significant insights into their current capabilities and prevalent limitations. The results were categorized into perception and cognition abilities, with detailed leaderboards for each of the 14 subtasks.

#### 4.1. Perception Abilities

Across the 10 perception subtasks, MLLMs exhibited varying degrees of proficiency:
- **Coarse-Grained Recognition (Existence, Count, Position, Color):**
 - **Existence:** Models generally performed well, with Otter, Lynx, WeMM, Muffin, and SPHINX achieving the highest score of 195 (out of 200), demonstrating strong fundamental object recognition.
 - **Count, Position, Color:** Performance varied, indicating different models have unique strengths. Muffin led in object count, Lion (parallel with SPHINX) in position, and InfMLLM in color. Notably, MLLMs performed worst on object position, suggesting a common struggle with spatial reasoning.
- **Fine-Grained Recognition (Poster, Celebrity, Scene, Landmark, Artwork):**
 - **Poster Recognition:** GPT-4V, Lion, and Qwen-VL-Chat emerged as top performers. Interestingly, Qwen-VL-Chat's strong performance here contrasted with its relatively lower scores in coarse-grained tasks, validating the distinct nature of these subcategories.
 - **Celebrity Recognition:** WeMM, SPHINX, and Otter achieved top scores. GPT-4V notably received a zero score due to its policy of refusing to answer questions involving individuals, highlighting ethical or policy-based limitations.
 - **Scene, Landmark, Artwork Recognition:** WeMM, InfMLLM, and Lynx showed strength in scene recognition; Lion, WeMM, and LLaVA in landmark recognition; and WeMM, GPT-4V, and GIT2 in artwork recognition. GPT-4V also declined certain private art collection questions.
- **OCR:** GPT-4V exhibited a "huge advantage" with a score of 185, significantly outperforming the second-place Skywork-MM by over 22 points, indicating its superior optical character recognition capabilities.
- **Overall Perception:** WeMM, InfMLLM, and SPHINX secured the top three positions in the overall perception leaderboard.

#### 4.2. Cognition Abilities

The four cognition subtasks proved more challenging for many models:
- **Commonsense Reasoning:** GPT-4V, WeMM, and XComposer-VL led, with GPT-4V again showing strong performance, achieving a score of 142.14.
- **Numerical Calculation:** GPT-4V maintained its lead, but overall scores remained relatively low.
- **Text Translation:** Performance across all models was generally modest, with no model exceeding a score of 150, suggesting significant room for improvement in this area.
- **Code Reasoning:** GPT-4V demonstrated exceptional capability, achieving a high score of 170 and significantly outperforming all other MLLMs.
- **Overall Cognition:** GPT-4V, Lion, and WeMM were the top three models in overall cognition.

#### 4.3. General Observations and Identified Problems

The evaluation underscored that "existing MLLMs still have a large room for improvement" across many capabilities, particularly in cognition. Beyond quantitative scores, the authors identified four "prominent problems" common among MLLMs:
1. **Inability to Follow Basic Instructions:** Despite concise "Please answer yes or no" instructions, many models provided free-form declarative statements without the required binary response. This indicates a fundamental weakness in instruction following, even after instruction tuning.
2. **Lack of Basic Perception:** Models frequently misidentified basic visual elements, such as object counts or text within an image. This perceptual vulnerability was also noted to be sensitive to subtle instruction nuances, leading to inconsistent or contradictory responses for the same image.
3. **Lack of Reasoning (Broken Logic Chain):** In several instances, models appeared to correctly process intermediate information (e.g., identifying that an object was absent or calculating an arithmetic sum correctly) but then failed to translate this into the correct "yes" or "no" final answer, suggesting a breakdown in their logical reasoning chain.
4. **Object Hallucination:** A significant problem observed was that when an instruction mentioned an object not present in the image, models would frequently "imagine" its existence and respond with "yes." This led to roughly 50% accuracy and near 0% accuracy+ for hallucination-prone questions, severely impacting reliability.

These findings not only provide a quantitative ranking but also offer crucial diagnostic insights into the current state and specific challenges facing MLLM development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MME marks a significant contribution to the field of Multimodal Large Language Models, with profound implications for research, development, and the overall trajectory of AI.

**Addresses a Critical Research Gap:** MME directly confronts the previously identified lack of a comprehensive, quantitative, and objective evaluation benchmark for MLLMs. By moving beyond anecdotal demonstrations and narrowly focused tests, it provides a holistic framework that measures a wide spectrum of perception and cognition abilities. This is essential for a mature research field to systematically understand and compare different models.

**Establishes a Fair and Robust Evaluation Standard:** The meticulous design choices—such as manually constructing all instruction-answer pairs (avoiding data leakage), using standardized "yes/no" instructions (minimizing prompt engineering bias), and employing robust metrics like Accuracy and Accuracy+—ensure a fair and objective playing field for comparing MLLMs. This standardization is crucial for tracking progress and fostering healthy competition within the research community.

**Provides Invaluable Diagnostic Insights:** Beyond simply ranking models, MME's detailed subtask evaluations and the identification of four "prominent problems" (instruction non-compliance, perceptual failures, reasoning breakdowns, and object hallucination) offer critical diagnostic feedback. These insights are not merely observations but actionable guidance for researchers and developers. They pinpoint specific areas where MLLMs are deficient, thereby directing future efforts towards targeted model optimization, architectural improvements, and training strategies. For instance, the consistent struggle with object position or the prevalence of hallucination suggests areas requiring fundamental algorithmic solutions.

**Catalyzes Future MLLM Development:** By clearly articulating current limitations, MME serves as a powerful catalyst for the next generation of MLLMs. Researchers now have a clearer roadmap to focus on developing models that are not only more capable but also more reliable, robust, and truly understand and follow instructions. This will likely drive innovation in areas such as fine-grained visual grounding, robust reasoning architectures, and hallucination mitigation techniques.

**Promotes Transparency and Reproducibility:** The public release of the MME dataset ensures transparency and allows other researchers to reproduce the evaluation, build upon it, and extend its scope. This fosters a collaborative environment and accelerates scientific progress.

**Shapes the Definition of "Intelligence" for MLLMs:** By categorizing and evaluating distinct perception and cognition abilities, MME contributes to a more nuanced understanding of what constitutes "intelligence" in multimodal AI. It moves beyond generic conversational prowess to examine specific facets of understanding, knowledge integration, and logical deduction.

While the authors acknowledge that MME itself has room for future iteration (e.g., covering more complex reasoning scenarios), its initial release is a foundational step. The potential impact of MME lies in its ability to bring scientific rigor to MLLM evaluation, transforming a field driven by impressive demonstrations into one guided by systematic measurement and targeted improvement, ultimately pushing towards more capable and trustworthy general-purpose multimodal AI.
