# Chain of Event-Centric Causal Thought for Physically Plausible Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Chain of Event-Centric Causal Thought (CoECT) is introduced for physically plausible video generation, which explicitly models complex physical phenomena as a sequence of causally ordered and dynamically evolving events. This approach demonstrated an 8.19% improvement in Physical Commonsense Alignment on PhyGenBench and a 3.4% gain in combined Semantic Adherence and Physical Commonsense on VideoPhy compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Current video diffusion models struggle to incorporate detailed physical laws from brief prompts, often failing to represent the temporal progression of physical events.
- Existing Physically Plausible Video Generation (PPVG) methods tend to simplify physical phenomena into static moments, neglecting the dynamic and causally ordered progression of events.
- Language alone often proves inadequate for conveying continuous causal relationships between events, and obtaining tightly aligned visual priors for specific physical phenomena is challenging, leading to issues with physical consistency and smooth transitions.

## Method

- The ==Physics-driven Event Chain Reasoning== (PECR) module of **CoECT** decomposes complex physical phenomena into causally ordered event units by grounding reasoning in physical formulas and computational analysis.
- The ==Transition-aware Cross-modal Prompting== (TCP) module bridges the inferred event chain to video generation by progressively synthesizing semantic and visual prompts for each event, preserving temporal progression.
- The framework uses an ==LLM== for progressive narrative revision and Qwen-Image-Edit for interactive keyframe synthesis, guided by evolving physical conditions and scene graphs to maintain coherence and consistency.

## Results

- The framework achieved an average Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA) score of **0.66** on the PhyGenBench benchmark, which is an **8.19%** improvement over the previous state-of-the-art, PhysHPO.
- On the VideoPhy benchmark, the framework attained an overall score of **49.3%** for scenarios with both Semantic Adherence (SA=1) and Physical Commonsense (PC=1), surpassing PhysHPO by approximately **3.4%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Physics Formula Grounding (PFG), Physical Phenomena Decomposition (PPD), and Interactive Keyframe Synthesis (IKS) components are critical for generating realistic physical progressions and maintaining structural fidelity in the produced videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling complex physical phenomena as a sequence of causally ordered event units, grounded in physical formulas, is crucial for generating videos that accurately reflect real-world physics.
> - Decomposing physical processes into fine-grained events with dynamically evolving physical conditions and scene graphs allows for the generation of realistic and causally coherent temporal progressions.
> - Leveraging a dual-condition strategy through progressively revised semantic narratives and interactively synthesized, physically constrained keyframes effectively guides video diffusion models, ensuring both temporal consistency and visual plausibility across event transitions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the domain of Physically Plausible Video Generation (PPVG). This field aims to synthesize videos that not only appear photorealistic but also adhere to real-world physical laws and phenomena, such as fluid dynamics, light refraction, and thermodynamic effects. Recent advancements in video diffusion models, including Kling and OpenAI-Sora, have demonstrated capabilities in generating photorealistic scenes from textual prompts. However, these models often struggle with complex physical phenomena because brief prompts typically lack the detailed physical laws required for accurate simulation.

Existing PPVG approaches, as discussed in the "Related Works" section, often augment user prompts with physical concepts using Large Language Model (LLM)-assisted reasoning (e.g., VLIPP, DiffPhy, PAG-SAD, PhyT2V). Some methods characterize physical phenomena through simulations based on graphics engines (e.g., DiffTaichi, AutoVFX), or leverage knowledge from foundation models (e.g., VideoREPA, WISA, PhysHPO). While these methods introduce physical awareness, the paper identifies a key limitation: they tend to simplify physical phenomena into static moments defined by prompts, failing to capture the dynamic and causally ordered progression of events.

The paper notes that physical events unfold as causally ordered processes. Current methods, lacking explicit causal modeling, may represent complex phenomena as a single, undifferentiated scene. This work positions itself by proposing an event-centric framework that explicitly models physical phenomena as a sequence of causally connected and dynamically evolving events, a departure from methods that treat them as singular or poorly structured occurrences. It draws inspiration from Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which has been applied in visual generation for semantic and spatial reasoning, but extends it to address deterministic causal relationships for physical phenomena. It also leverages dual-prompting strategies (semantic and visual cues) which are used in other video generation works to guide individual scenes, but adapts them to ensure smooth, physically consistent transitions between multiple events.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for Physically Plausible Video Generation (PPVG) that can model and generate videos depicting the causally connected and dynamically evolving events inherent in complex physical phenomena. The authors aim to overcome limitations of current video diffusion models, which struggle to incorporate detailed physical laws from brief prompts and often fail to represent the temporal progression of physical events.

The motivation for this work stems from two identified challenges in existing PPVG approaches:

1. **Causal Ambiguity:** Real-world physical phenomena are not static; they unfold as a sequence of causally ordered event units. Current methods that rely on semantic tags to describe complex phenomena often fail to capture this dynamic, temporal nature. This limitation results in generated videos that may depict the individual elements of a physical event but miss the logical, sequential progression and cause-and-effect relationships. The authors argue that a structured decomposition of physical phenomena through deterministic causal reasoning is necessary to address this.

2. **Insufficient Physics-consistent Constraints:** Language alone is often inadequate for conveying the continuous causal relationships between events. While visual cues, such as reference videos, can provide observable evidence of event transitions, obtaining tightly aligned visual priors for specific physical phenomena is often difficult. Consequently, existing frameworks struggle to ensure that the generated video maintains continuity and adheres to physical laws across different stages of a phenomenon. The motivation is to provide mechanisms that explicitly constrain the generation process to maintain physical consistency and smooth transitions across event boundaries.

By addressing these motivations, the paper seeks to enable the generation of videos that are not only photorealistic but also accurately reflect the temporal, causal, and physically governed progression of events in various real-world scenarios, which could be beneficial for applications like movie production, autonomous driving, and embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for physically plausible video generation, named Chain of Event-Centric Causal Thought (CoECT), processes a user-provided linguistic description of a physical phenomenon into a corresponding physically plausible video. The framework is composed of two primary modules: Physics-driven Event Chain Reasoning (PECR) and Transition-aware Cross-modal Prompting (TCP).

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The framework takes a linguistic description `w` as input and aims to generate a video `V` that characterizes the progression of the described phenomenon. This process is defined as `Γ : w → V`. The PECR module first interprets the complex phenomenon into an ordered collection of physical events. Subsequently, the TCP module bridges these inferred event chains to the video generation process by dynamically synthesizing dual-conditions (semantic and visual prompts) that evolve with the physical processes.

#### 3.2. Physics-driven Event Chain Reasoning (PECR)

The PECR module is designed to decompose complex physical phenomena into a sequence of fine-grained, causally ordered event units. Each event is conceptualized as a composite unit containing descriptive information of objects' semantics and interactions, alongside measurable physical conditions governed by physical formulas.

- **Physics Formula Grounding:**
 - This sub-module numerically describes physical processes by reasoning over physical formulas. Physical laws (`L`) relevant to the linguistic description (`w`) are first determined, often through question-answering techniques with predefined options.
 - Formula names (`N_L`) associated with these laws are then inferred from the description and used to query knowledge bases to retrieve relevant physical formulas (`F*`). A scoring function is used to rank candidate formulas. If no direct match is found, formula names are regenerated.
 - Once formulas are retrieved, the physical parameters required for analysis are set using commonsense reasoning.

- **Physical Phenomena Decomposition:**
 - This process decomposes the phenomenon into an ordered sequence of key events `({E_t}, {C_t}, {G_t})`, where `E_t` is event metadata, `C_t` specifies physical conditions, and `G_t` denotes the dynamic scene graph. `T` is the total number of events.
 - Physical conditions (`C_t`) are calculated based on the retrieved formulas. Intermediate quantities from these calculations provide physically meaningful signals. Event boundaries are determined by analyzing significant variations in physical parameters. An event `t` is identified if the change in physical parameters `P_t` from the previous state `P_{t-1}` exceeds a threshold `τ_p`.
 - To ensure physical consistency, parameters for the current event are validated against neighboring events to detect abrupt changes that violate physical continuity. If inconsistencies are found, parameters and contexts are fed back for re-inference.
 - Following this, the scene graph (`G_t = {V_t, R_t}`) is updated based on `C_t`. Nodes (`V_t`) representing objects can have their appearance (e.g., color) or semantic labels (e.g., "burn to ashes") updated based on physical parameter variations. Edges (`R_t`) representing interactions between objects are updated due to changes like distance variations, which require coordinated changes in physical parameters across multiple objects.

#### 3.3. Transition-aware Cross-modal Prompting (TCP)

The TCP module bridges the inferred event chain from PECR to the video generation process by progressively synthesizing semantic and visual prompts for each event, while preserving temporal progression. These dual conditions guide the video diffusion model.

- **Progressive Narrative Revision:**
 - To maintain holistic coherence across event descriptions, this sub-module performs minimal progressive revisions conditioned on preceding context. Physical conditions (`C_t`) constrain permissible transitions (e.g., rising temperature allows "melting" but not "freezing"). Scene graphs (`G_t`) preserve object identities and specify attributes or relations that may change.
 - An LLM is used to revise the description of the `t`-th event (`w_t`) by incorporating the previous description (`w_{t-1}`) and incremental semantic revisions guided by `C_t` and `G_t`.
 - Individual event descriptions are merged into a concise positive semantic prompt (`w*+`) using semantic condensation and causal connectives. A negative description (`w*-`) is also constructed. Both are embedded using a text encoder and concatenated to form the final linguistic embedding `W`.

- **Interactive Keyframe Synthesis:**
 - Acknowledging that language alone may underspecify physically realistic details, this sub-module synthesizes keyframes (`v_t`) for each physical event to serve as visual priors, embedding physics-aware details into the random Gaussian noise.
 - An appropriate editing operator (e.g., drag, mask, add, remove) is selected from a predefined set. The change in physical parameters across consecutive conditions regularizes the action space for editing (e.g., bounding dragging magnitude or area of visual change).
 - Operator cues are drawn on a source image, and Qwen-Image-Edit is used to perform the modifications. Each keyframe `v_t` is obtained by editing the previous keyframe `v_{t-1}` using operator `O_t`, which is determined by the transition of physical conditions and scene graphs. The first keyframe `v_1` is directly synthesized from its event description.
 - To ensure smooth progression, linear interpolation is applied between keyframes. A physically plausible time span (`d_t`) for the variation between `v_{t-1}` and `v_t` is predicted, determining the number of in-between frames.
 - Keyframes are encoded into a compressed feature space using a VAE. Interpolated features (`z_{0,t}`) are then combined and noise is added (`Z_τz`), serving as visual priors for the video diffusion model's denoising process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework was evaluated on two benchmarks, PhyGenBench and VideoPhy, using various metrics.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on PhyGenBench

The framework was compared against several video foundation models (e.g., Lavie, VideoCrafter v2.0, Open-Sora v1.2, Kling, Pika, Gen-3) and physics-aware video generation models (e.g., WISA, DiffPhy, PhyT2V, SGD, PhysHPO). The Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA) metric was used, which assesses key phenomena detection, physics order verification, and overall naturalness.

- **Quantitative Results:** The proposed framework achieved an average PCA score of 0.66, which is stated to be a 8.19% improvement over PhysHPO, the previously reported state-of-the-art on this benchmark. It consistently demonstrated higher performance across the four physical domains: Mechanics (0.67), Optics (0.72), Thermal (0.65), and Material (0.60).
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations in Figure 4 indicated that the framework generated visually more realistic progressions for phenomena such as gradual sinking, light refraction, realistic melting, and natural combustion, when compared to the CogVideo-5B baseline. These results suggest an improved ability to understand physical laws and their corresponding visual dynamics by explicitly modeling causally ordered events.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on VideoPhy

Performance was also assessed on the VideoPhy benchmark against several video generation models (e.g., LaVIE, SVD-T2I2V, OpenSora, Pika, Dream Machine, Lumiere) and physics-aware models (e.g., CogVideoX-5B, PhyT2V, Vanilla DPO). The evaluation used Semantic Adherence (SA) and Physical Commonsense (PC) metrics, which assess semantic grounding and conformity to physical laws, respectively.

- **Quantitative Results:** The framework achieved an overall score of 49.3% for scenarios where both SA=1 and PC=1, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art PhysHPO by approximately 3.4%. This performance indicates effectiveness in adhering to semantic cues and physical laws through vision-language prompts derived from CoT reasoning.
- **Qualitative Results:** Figure 5 illustrated scenarios like butter spreading, honey pouring, and spring compression. The framework generated clearer causal progression in these interactions (e.g., butter spreading along the knife path, continuous honey inflow with rising liquid level, monotonic spring compression), indicating its capability to capture object interactions across diverse physical phases.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on PhyGenBench analyzed the contribution of individual blocks within the PECR and TCP modules:

- **PECR Module (Physics Formula Grounding (PFG) and Physical Phenomena Decomposition (PPD)):**
 - Removing the PFG block resulted in an average performance drop of approximately 6%, suggesting the importance of physical formulas for quantitative understanding.
 - Removing the PPD block led to an average decline of around 11%, indicating its effectiveness in generating realistic physical progressions by decomposing complex processes into logically ordered event chains.
 - Qualitatively (Figure 6), disabling CoT reasoning in PECR (direct scene graph inference from descriptions) resulted in a persistently red litmus solution instead of a gradual purple-to-red transition, confirming PECR's role in modeling evolving physical conditions and causal progression.

- **TCP Module (Progressive Narrative Revision (PNR) and Interactive Keyframe Synthesis (IKS)):**
 - Excluding the PNR block caused a moderate average drop of about 3%, supporting its role in improving scene continuity and smooth evolution.
 - Removing the IKS block resulted in a significant average decrease of approximately 17%, emphasizing the critical role of dedicated keyframes for each physical phase in anchoring cross-frame dynamics and maintaining physically grounded visual progression.
 - Qualitatively (Figure 7), removing visual prompts from TCP led to an implausible result (a rugby ball sinking into the ground), whereas keyframes provided reasonable visual cues preserved the ball's rounded shape and realistic interaction, highlighting the direct impact of visual prompt realism on structural fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an event-centric framework for physically plausible video generation that addresses the limitations of existing models in capturing the dynamic and causally ordered progression of physical phenomena. By explicitly decomposing complex phenomena into a sequence of causally linked physical events, the framework offers a structured approach to generating videos that adhere more closely to real-world physical laws.

The key contributions that underscore its significance include:
- **Causal Reasoning with Deterministic Physical Constraints:** The introduction of the Physics-driven Event Chain Reasoning (PECR) module, which grounds event decomposition in physical formulas and computational analysis, mitigates causal ambiguity. This allows for the inference of physically realistic event sequences with clear cause-and-effect relationships, a noted advancement over methods relying solely on semantic tags.
- **Temporal Alignment and Continuity:** The Transition-aware Cross-modal Prompting (TCP) module, which synthesizes temporally aligned vision-language prompts, enables continuous generation between physical events. By progressively revising narratives and interactively synthesizing keyframes, the framework ensures both semantic coherence and visual continuity, addressing the challenge of maintaining smooth transitions across event boundaries.

The quantitative and qualitative results on PhyGenBench and VideoPhy benchmarks demonstrate that the framework achieves higher scores in physical commonsense alignment, semantic adherence, and overall physical plausibility compared to various baseline video generation models and previous physics-aware approaches. This indicates an improved capability to model and visualize complex, evolving physical interactions.

The potential impact of this work extends to several domains:
- **Enhanced Realism in Content Creation:** The ability to generate videos with accurate physical progressions could significantly improve the realism in movie production, special effects, and virtual reality environments.
- **Advanced AI for Simulation and Training:** More physically plausible video generation could benefit autonomous driving simulations, allowing for more accurate representations of real-world physics in diverse scenarios. In embodied AI, it could contribute to creating more realistic training environments where agents learn to interact with objects and environments in a physically consistent manner.
- **Foundational Research in AI Understanding:** By enabling AI models to explicitly reason about and simulate physical causality, this work contributes to a deeper understanding of commonsense physics within AI systems, moving beyond superficial pattern recognition towards a more grounded comprehension of the physical world.

Overall, this research represents a step towards developing AI systems that can generate dynamic visual content not just photorealistically, but also with a deeper, physically informed understanding of how the world operates.
