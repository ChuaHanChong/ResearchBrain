# EA-Nav: Learning Safe Visual Navigation Policies with Embodiment Awareness

> [!summary] Summary
> Zhejiang University researchers developed EA-Nav, an imitation-learning framework that enhances visual navigation by explicitly incorporating robot embodiment geometry and risk-aware trajectory correction. The system achieved improved safety and generalization across diverse robotic platforms in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Visual-only navigation policies lead to action ambiguity, as optimal actions vary significantly for robots with different physical dimensions in the same scene.
- Existing imitation learning methods for cross-embodiment navigation struggle with data scarcity for diverse embodiment-environment interactions and have limitations in representing continuous physical action spaces.
- The lack of explicit modeling for embodiment geometry and collision risk in continuous space can lead to unsafe or short-sighted navigation decisions, especially in cluttered environments.

## Method

- **EA-Nav** is an imitation-learning-based framework that explicitly injects ==embodiment geometry== (length, width, height, traversable height) as a conditional input to the navigation policy.
- It employs a modular, multi-stage design with an ==Input Encoder==, ==Action Prediction==, ==Spatial Perception==, and ==Risk-Aware Correction== module, allowing for pretraining on diverse internet videos and fine-tuning with real-world data.
- A ==risk trajectory augmentation== strategy generates synthetic high-risk samples and corresponding multi-modal correction targets to train the spatial perception and risk-aware correction modules effectively.

## Results

- The Spatial Perception module achieved up to **84.9%** True Positive Rate for high-risk samples and **95.1%** True Positive Rate for collision samples on the i2Nav dataset, with risk augmentation providing approximately a **5x** improvement.
- The Risk-Aware Correction module demonstrated higher Correction Success Rate (e.g., **65.97%** for Body-L) and lower Mean Deflection Angle compared to baselines, showing robust embodiment-aware trajectory refinement.
- In real-world experiments, the full EA-Nav model maintained high Success Rates and low Collision Rates (e.g., **0.80 SR**, **0.20 CR** for Unitree Go2), outperforming competing methods which showed noticeable degradation for diverse embodiment sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating embodiment geometry during pre-training improves policy learning, reduces action ambiguity, and enhances cross-embodiment generalization, even when visual cues might implicitly contain some embodiment information.
> - Risk trajectory augmentation is crucial for training safety-critical modules, significantly improving the recognition of high-risk and collision samples while maintaining low false alarm rates.
> - A decoupled architecture enables effective training of spatial perception and risk-aware correction modules independently, enhancing their ability to learn geometry-aware features and refine unsafe paths in continuous space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has seen notable advancements in vision-based navigation, where models convert high-level goals into low-level executable trajectories for robotic agents. Most existing research in this area primarily relies on visual observations, often neglecting the significance of embodiment information, such as geometry and physical morphology. This oversight can limit a model's ability to predict reliable trajectories, as different embodiments vary in size and traversability, leading to action ambiguity under identical visual observations.

Traditional approaches to incorporating embodiment information frequently involve reinforcement learning (RL). While RL allows agents to learn through interaction and trial-and-error, it is often characterized by large exploration spaces and convergence instability. This necessitates pre-trained models, typically provided by imitation learning (IL), to offer a suitable initialization. However, existing IL-based methods for cross-embodiment navigation face their own challenges, including the difficulty of acquiring sufficient training data that captures diverse embodiment-environment interactions and limitations in representing continuous physical action spaces through discretized waypoint sequences.

Prior work in vision-based navigation has explored deterministic models for trajectory prediction, diffusion models for multimodal action distributions, and the integration of vision foundation models for enhanced spatial perception and long-horizon planning. Despite these developments, the explicit modeling of embodiment geometry in the context of cross-embodiment scenarios remains an area with limited dedicated research. Some existing IL frameworks, such as ExAug, have attempted to incorporate embodiment awareness through signed distance fields and discrete waypoints, but these approaches have been noted to struggle with accurately reflecting trajectory safety in continuous space and may lead to short-sighted navigation decisions.

In terms of datasets, existing resources for visual navigation broadly fall into real-world, synthetic, and pseudo-labeled categories. Real-world datasets often have limited scene diversity or embodiment coverage due to the constraints of specific robotic platforms. Synthetic datasets offer scalability but may suffer from a sim-to-real gap. Pseudo-labeled datasets, leveraging large-scale internet videos, have improved supervision but often lack comprehensive coverage across multiple embodiment types.

This work addresses these gaps by proposing an imitation-learning-based framework that explicitly injects embodiment geometry into navigation policies. It distinguishes itself by:
1. Focusing on an IL framework to overcome the scalability and stability issues of RL for pretraining and real-world adaptation.
2. Developing a modular, multi-stage design to systematically incorporate embodiment awareness.
3. Constructing a novel cross-embodiment navigation dataset from internet videos, featuring diverse embodiments and pseudo-labels, to support large-scale pretraining.
4. Introducing a risk trajectory augmentation strategy to address the scarcity of high-risk samples, a common limitation in real-world data collection.
5. Moving beyond discrete waypoint constraints by operating on continuous trajectory adjustments, particularly for risk-aware correction.

By integrating embodiment geometry as a conditional input and developing a structured training approach, this research aims to mitigate action ambiguity, enhance spatial perception, and enable safer, more adaptive navigation policies across a range of robot physical characteristics, thereby advancing the capabilities of embodied agents in diverse environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an imitation-learning-based framework that enables navigation models to predict safe and reliable trajectories by explicitly incorporating embodiment geometry. This framework aims to facilitate cross-embodiment navigation, where a single policy can adapt to agents with diverse physical characteristics.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several challenges observed in current visual navigation systems:

1. **Action Ambiguity from Visual-Only Observation:** Many existing navigation models rely solely on visual observations. However, the same visual scene can imply different optimal actions for agents of varying sizes or morphologies. For instance, a narrow gap might be traversable for a small robot but an obstacle for a large one. Relying exclusively on vision can lead to ambiguous action predictions and suboptimal, potentially unsafe, navigation behaviors when the agent's physical attributes are not considered.

2. **Limitations of Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While RL offers a pathway to learn complex interactions between an embodiment and its environment, it typically requires extensive trial-and-error exploration. This often results in a large exploration space, unstable convergence, and substantial computational resources, making scalable pretraining and seamless adaptation to real-world scenarios challenging. The demand for carefully designed reward functions further complicates its application.

3. **Deficiencies in Existing Imitation Learning (IL) Approaches:** Current IL methods for cross-embodiment navigation have not fully addressed critical limitations. Firstly, obtaining sufficient, high-quality training data that adequately represents how various embodiments interact with diverse environments is difficult. This data scarcity hinders the learning of robust, generalizable policies. Secondly, many IL approaches discretize expert demonstrations into waypoint sequences. This discretization can disrupt the continuous mapping between physical actions and the visual environment, making it difficult to assess the safety of modified or alternative trajectories in continuous space. The lack of explicit modeling for collision risk in continuous space contributes to this limitation.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Integrate Embodiment Geometry:** Explicitly introduce embodiment geometry (length, width, height, maximum traversable height) as a conditional input to the navigation policy, allowing the model to make decisions tailored to the robot's physical dimensions.
- **Develop a Scalable, IL-based Framework:** Propose a modular, multi-stage imitation learning framework that supports both large-scale pretraining on diverse data and fine-tuning on high-quality navigation data.
- **Reduce Action Ambiguity:** Enable the navigation model to resolve action ambiguity by conditioning predictions on embodiment geometry, leading to more stable and embodiment-specific action outputs.
- **Enhance Spatial Perception and Risk Awareness:** Introduce mechanisms for embodiment-aware spatial perception to identify high-risk trajectories and implement a risk-aware correction module to refine unsafe paths.
- **Overcome Data Scarcity:** Design a trajectory augmentation strategy to generate synthetic high-risk samples and corresponding correction targets, thereby mitigating the limitations of scarce real-world high-risk data.
- **Improve Trajectory Safety:** Develop a method that adjusts entire trajectories in continuous space rather than operating on discrete waypoints, which is intended to better capture and address collision risks.
- **Create a Cross-Embodiment Dataset:** Construct a heterogeneous dataset from internet videos to provide diverse embodiment categories and support the pretraining of embodiment-aware navigation policies.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to contribute to the development of more adaptable, safe, and robust visual navigation systems for a variety of robotic platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed EA-Nav framework is an imitation-learning-based, embodiment-aware navigation system characterized by a modular, multi-stage design. It explicitly incorporates embodiment geometry to predict safe trajectories.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The embodiment parameter `m` is defined as a 4-dimensional vector `[L_b, W_b, H_b, P_max]`, representing the agent's length, width, height, and maximum traversable height, respectively. The navigation task is formulated as learning a policy `π_θ` that predicts an `H`-step action sequence `a_{t:t+H}` given the current state `s_t`. The state `s_t` comprises image sequences `I_{t-k:t}`, current depth `D_t`, ego-centric target location `g`, and the embodiment parameter `m`. Training minimizes the L2 error between predicted and expert action sequences.

#### 3.2. Embodiment-Aware Navigation Framework

The framework utilizes a decoupled architecture with an Input Encoder, Action Prediction, Spatial Perception, and Risk-Aware Correction modules.

- **Input Encoder:** This module encodes multimodal inputs (RGB image sequences, depth map, goal vector, and embodiment geometry `m`) into a shared context feature `z_context`. It employs a unidirectional cross-attention design to prevent downstream prediction queries from altering the core context features, ensuring `z_context` remains focused on observations, goal, and embodiment information for subsequent modules. Pretrained encoders (DINOv3-S for RGB, ConvNeXt-T for depth) extract visual features, which are then fused with goal and geometry tokens. The `z_context` retains a relatively large number of tokens (130x384) to preserve fine-grained spatial information.

- **Action Prediction:** This module takes a dedicated query token, interacts with `z_context` via cross-attention, and decodes the action representation into an `H`-step action sequence (`v_t`, `w_t` for linear and angular velocities). This predicted action sequence is then converted into a local waypoint trajectory `z_traj` for use by subsequent modules.

- **Spatial Perception:** This module is responsible for estimating the minimum distance to obstacles along the predicted trajectory. It requires capturing the interplay between embodiment geometry and the environment under predicted actions. The module takes the `z_context` and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) encoding of `z_traj` as input to a Transformer, which outputs `z_dist`. An MLP then predicts the nearest-obstacle distance `d̂` for each waypoint. Critically, this module's loss function is allowed to update the shared `z_context`, promoting the learning of geometry-aware features beyond those optimized solely for action regression. It is decoupled from Action Prediction, allowing its input trajectory to be substituted with augmented high-risk trajectories during training.

- **Risk-Aware Correction:** Triggered when the Spatial Perception module identifies a trajectory as high-risk (distance below a safety threshold `δ_s = 0.5m`). Instead of predicting an incremental action sequence, this module predicts a low-dimensional global yaw offset (Δ`θ̂`) to refine the trajectory. The correction space `[-45°, 45°]` is discretized into 5° bins. The module processes `z_context`, `z_dist`, and `MLP(z_traj)` through a Transformer to output `z_corr`, which an MLP converts into feasibility probabilities `p_corr` for each bin. During inference, the feasible bin with the smallest deviation from the original trajectory is selected. This module is also decoupled to facilitate training with augmented high-risk and corresponding correction samples.

#### 3.3. Risk Trajectory Augmentation

To address the scarcity of high-risk trajectories in real datasets, a three-step augmentation pipeline is designed:

1. **Point Cloud Map and Inflated Occupancy Grid:** For a given observation and predicted `H`-step trajectory, a robot-centric local point cloud map is constructed by fusing short-horizon future depth observations. A RANSAC-based algorithm identifies the ground plane. Obstacle regions are then defined and inflated based on the robot's dimensions (`L_b, W_b, H_b, P_max`) to create an embodiment-aware occupancy grid for collision checking.

2. **Augmented Trajectory Generation:** A safety coefficient `s_d` is calculated based on the minimum obstacle clearance. Original spline-smoothed trajectories are scaled uniformly by a factor `α` (sampled around `s_d`) and rotated (from -45° to 45° in 5° intervals) to generate a pool of candidate trajectories.

3. **Labeling:** Candidates that collide with the inflated occupancy grid are labeled as risk trajectories. Collision-free candidates define feasible correction bins, which are used as multi-hot supervision targets for the Risk-Aware Correction module. This method generates diverse high-risk samples and corresponding multi-modal corrections.

#### 3.4. Embodiment-Aware Training Framework

- **Training Data Construction:**
 - **Pretraining Data:** A heterogeneous cross-embodiment navigation dataset is collected from Internet first-person videos (approximately 1,000 hours across eight embodiment categories). Depth-Anything-3 is used to estimate depth, trajectories, and camera intrinsics, with manual annotation for embodiment geometry. Samples are filtered for trajectory smoothness. A mini-version of HM3D is also incorporated.
 - **Fine-tuning Data:** Real-world datasets (GND, SCAND-Spot, SiT) are used. Depth-Anything-3 generates physically consistent depth observations for metric-scale trajectories and camera intrinsics. Local point clouds are used to extract ground plane and obstacle regions for geometric supervision.

- **Pretraining Stage:** The base policy is trained on the constructed pretraining dataset. Only the Action Prediction branch is optimized using behavior cloning, as internet videos may contain noise inconsistent with reliable geometric supervision.

- **Fine-tuning Stage:** The model is initialized from the pretrained checkpoint. Behavior cloning supervises the Action Prediction branch with real actions. Spatial Perception is supervised by occupancy-grid-derived targets, and Risk-Aware Correction is supervised by the feasible correction bins generated via augmentation. All three objectives are jointly optimized using a weighted sum.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation provides insight into the effectiveness of incorporating embodiment awareness into navigation policies.

#### 4.1. Embodiment Modality in Pre-training

- **Action Ambiguity Analysis:** A qualitative analysis using t-SNE showed that while visual observations from different embodiments (e.g., wheelchair and person) might overlap in feature space, their corresponding motion attribute distributions (straightness, speed deviation, yaw change) differ significantly. This indicates that relying solely on visual input leads to action ambiguity, where the model might average behaviors instead of learning embodiment-specific actions.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study demonstrated that explicitly introducing embodiment geometry during pre-training consistently improved training loss, validation loss, and test-set Success Rate (SR). While removing this modality did not cause a drastic performance drop (suggesting implicit embodiment encoding in visual sequences), explicit modeling was found to be beneficial for policy learning and cross-embodiment generalization.

#### 4.2. Distance Prediction Performance (Spatial Perception)

- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations showed that the Spatial Perception module could differentiate minimum obstacle distances for trajectories based on varying embodiment sizes, confirming its ability to capture embodiment-conditioned spatial risk. For the same trajectory, a larger embodiment would have a shorter minimum obstacle distance.
- **Quantitative Results (i2Nav Dataset):** Experiments on the i2Nav dataset across five agent sizes (0.5m to 2.5m radius, including an out-of-range 2.5m 'Body-L' setting) demonstrated robust performance. Risk augmentation ("w/ Aug") significantly improved the recognition of high-risk (distance < 0.5m) and collision (distance < 0m) samples, with TPR (True Positive Rate) for high-risk samples reaching up to 84.9% and TPR* (for collision samples) up to 95.1% for Body-M. This improvement was approximately 5 times higher compared to training without augmentation ("w/o Aug"), while maintaining a low False Alarm Rate (FAR). Mean Absolute Error (MAE) was also consistently lower with augmentation. This highlighted the efficacy and robustness of the augmentation strategy across different embodiment sizes.

#### 4.3. Trajectory Correction Performance (Risk-Aware Correction)

- **Quantitative Results (i2Nav Dataset):** The model achieved strong embodiment-aware trajectory correction performance. Compared to a random correction baseline, the proposed method showed higher Correction Success Rate (CSR) and lower Mean Deflection Angle (MDA), particularly for larger embodiments. For example, for Body-L, the proposed method achieved a CSR of 65.97% with an MDA of 18.29°, significantly outperforming the random baseline's CSR of 39.83% and MDA of 22.59°.
- **IoU_bin Metric:** The Intersection over Union (IoU) of feasible correction bins (IoU_bin) was used to evaluate the model's understanding of safe correction directions. High IoU_bin scores (e.g., 70.97% for Body-S) indicated that the model could identify appropriate correction regions in the angle space. The paper noted that for smaller embodiments, higher CSR for random strategies might occur due to larger feasible correction regions, but this comes with reduced controllability. The proposed method maintained stability and controllability, especially as embodiment size increased and feasible regions shrank.

#### 4.4. Embodiment-Aware Navigation Evaluation

- **Simulation Environments (InternUtopia/NavDP assets):**
 - The base model (Ours w/o corr) showed lower performance compared to NavDP, attributed to a sim-to-real distribution gap as our training data was primarily real-world and less diverse than NavDP's large-scale synthetic data.
 - The full model (Ours w/ corr), incorporating Spatial Perception and Risk-Aware Correction, improved average performance by approximately 31% over Ours (w/o corr). The gains were more pronounced in cluttered environments (Scene2 and Scene3), indicating that embodiment conditions and task-specific inductive biases effectively compensated for training data limitations.
- **Real-World Experimental Evaluation (TurtleBot and Unitree Go2):**
 - **Qualitative Behavior:** The model demonstrated adaptive navigation strategies based on embodiment size. Smaller robots were observed navigating through narrow passages, while larger robots opted for wider paths or detours, showcasing the model's ability to adjust behavior to physical dimensions.
 - **Quantitative Performance (Table 5):** In real-world trials, the proposed method maintained a high SR and low Collision Rate (CR) across different robot sizes (Body, Body+, Body++ for TurtleBot and Unitree Go2). Competing methods (iPlanner, NavDP, NoMaD*, ExAug*) showed noticeable degradation, with higher collision rates, especially for larger or non-trained embodiment sizes. This indicated the practical importance of explicit embodiment geometry for robust point-goal navigation in real-world deployments. For example, for Unitree Go2 (Body), the proposed method achieved 0.80 SR and 0.20 CR, compared to NavDP's 0.60 SR and 0.60 CR.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for learning safe visual navigation policies with embodiment awareness, which holds several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addressing Cross-Embodiment Navigation:** The work addresses a core challenge in embodied intelligence: enabling a single navigation policy to operate effectively across diverse robotic platforms with varying physical dimensions. This is crucial for developing general-purpose robots capable of adapting to different tasks and environments without requiring a new policy for each robot type.
- **Mitigating Action Ambiguity:** By explicitly incorporating embodiment geometry, the framework directly tackles the problem of action ambiguity inherent in vision-only navigation systems. This leads to more reliable and context-appropriate action decisions, as the model understands how its physical form interacts with the environment.
- **Advancing Imitation Learning for Safety:** The research demonstrates an effective imitation learning (IL) approach for instilling safety awareness into navigation policies. Unlike reinforcement learning (RL) which often struggles with scalability and reward design for safety, this IL-based method offers a more stable and data-efficient pathway to acquire safe navigation behaviors.
- **Novel Two-Stage Training Strategy:** The proposed two-stage training (pretraining with conditional tokens from internet videos, fine-tuning with explicit geometric injection and augmentation) is a significant methodological contribution. It leverages abundant, noisy internet data for initial learning while refining capabilities with smaller, high-quality real-world datasets, offering a practical pathway for deployment.
- **Data Augmentation for High-Risk Scenarios:** The introduction of a risk trajectory augmentation strategy is impactful. Real-world datasets often lack sufficient examples of high-risk situations and corresponding safe corrections. This method systematically generates such samples, enabling robust training of critical safety modules like spatial perception and risk-aware correction.
- **Contribution of a Heterogeneous Dataset:** The construction of a large-scale, heterogeneous cross-embodiment navigation dataset from internet videos is a valuable resource for future research, fostering broader exploration of embodiment-aware learning.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Safety:** Robots equipped with EA-Nav-like policies could navigate complex, dynamic environments more safely and effectively. This can reduce collision rates and improve mission success in applications such as logistics, exploration, service robotics, and autonomous driving where diverse vehicle types might operate.
- **Improved Generalization Across Robot Platforms:** The ability to train a more generalizable navigation policy means less effort and cost associated with adapting robots to new tasks or changing their physical configurations. A single underlying intelligence could potentially power a fleet of varied robots.
- **Broader Accessibility of Embodied AI:** By providing a more robust and adaptable framework, this work could make advanced embodied AI capabilities more accessible to a wider range of developers and organizations, lowering the barrier to entry for developing and deploying intelligent robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays a foundation for future work to incorporate even more comprehensive embodiment-related factors, such as dynamic constraints, turning radii, and material properties. This could lead to increasingly sophisticated and physically aware navigation systems.
- **Advancements in Multi-Modal Learning:** The effective integration of visual, depth, goal, and geometric modalities into a unified framework contributes to the broader field of multi-modal learning, demonstrating how diverse data types can be combined for enhanced performance in embodied tasks.

In conclusion, the EA-Nav framework represents a step towards creating more intelligent, safer, and versatile autonomous agents by systematically integrating embodiment awareness into visual navigation policies. The methodology and empirical results demonstrate its potential to significantly impact the development and deployment of robotic systems in the real world.
