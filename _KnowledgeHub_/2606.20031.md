# A Neuromorphic Reinforcement Learning Framework for Efficient Pathfinding in Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> The SDQN-RMFS framework for efficient pathfinding in Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems leverages a Deep Q-Network converted to a Spiking Neural Network on a neuromorphic chip (SPECK2E), achieving up to 11,281 times lower energy consumption and halving inference latency compared to GPU-based ANNs for pathfinding decisions.

## Problem

- Traditional pathfinding algorithms struggle with high computational complexity and latency in dynamic, multi-agent Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems (RMFS).
- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methods, while adaptive, incur substantial computational and energy costs, limiting their deployment on power-constrained mobile Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs).
- Integrating RL with energy-efficient Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) on neuromorphic hardware faces challenges in practical deployment and maintaining policy fidelity, particularly due to ANN-to-SNN conversion issues at low time-steps.

## Method

- A Deep Q-Network (DQN) is trained using an SNN-friendly binary state representation, shaped rewards, and a collision-allowing exploration strategy for robust multi-agent pathfinding in warehouse environments.
- A two-stage ANN-to-SNN conversion process is introduced, featuring parameter scaling for faster spike emission and a hard-label knowledge distillation scheme to resolve the continuous-to-discrete output mismatch inherent in RL policies.
- The converted SNN is deployed and evaluated on the SPECK2E neuromorphic chip, augmented with an external action detection safety layer to prevent highly probable collisions.

## Results

- The framework achieved an energy reduction of up to 11,281 times, consuming **0.89**-**1.95** × 10⁻³ mJ per decision on the Speck chip compared to **10.04**-**10.73** mJ per decision for GPU-based ANN inference.
- Inference latency was halved to **0.061**-**0.079** ms per decision on the Speck neuromorphic chip, significantly faster than the **0.13**-**0.14** ms per decision on a high-performance RTX 4090 GPU.
- The proposed error alleviation techniques (fine-tuning via distillation and parameter scaling) enabled the SNN to achieve a lossless conversion rate (CR=**1.00**) at an ultra-low 4 time-steps, crucial for real-time and energy-efficient operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A collision-allowing training strategy significantly enhances DQN stability and efficiency in confined RMFS environments by preventing premature learning stagnation from frequent failures.
> - Parameter scaling of initial layer weights in SNNs effectively accelerates information flow and spike emission, enabling ultra-low time-step inference without severe accuracy degradation.
> - Hard-label knowledge distillation for ANN-to-SNN conversion successfully mitigates output distribution mismatch for RL policies, preserving decision capability at minimal time-steps and overcoming quantization errors.
