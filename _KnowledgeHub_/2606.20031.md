# A Neuromorphic Reinforcement Learning Framework for Efficient Pathfinding in Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> The SDQN-RMFS framework for efficient pathfinding in Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems leverages a Deep Q-Network converted to a Spiking Neural Network on a neuromorphic chip (SPECK2E), achieving up to 11,281 times lower energy consumption and halving inference latency compared to GPU-based ANNs for pathfinding decisions.

## Problem

- Traditional pathfinding algorithms struggle with high computational complexity and latency in dynamic, multi-agent Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems (RMFS).
- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methods, while adaptive, incur substantial computational and energy costs, limiting their deployment on power-constrained mobile Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs).
- Integrating RL with energy-efficient Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) on neuromorphic hardware faces challenges in practical deployment and maintaining policy fidelity, particularly due to ANN-to-SNN conversion issues at low time-steps.

## Method

- A Deep Q-Network (DQN) is trained using an SNN-friendly binary state representation, shaped rewards, and a collision-allowing exploration strategy for robust multi-agent pathfinding in warehouse environments.
- A two-stage ANN-to-SNN conversion process is introduced, featuring parameter scaling for faster spike emission and a hard-label knowledge distillation scheme to resolve the continuous-to-discrete output mismatch inherent in RL policies.
- The converted SNN is deployed and evaluated on the SPECK2E neuromorphic chip, augmented with an external action detection safety layer to prevent highly probable collisions.

## Results

- The framework achieved an energy reduction of up to 11,281 times, consuming **0.89**-**1.95** × 10⁻³ mJ per decision on the Speck chip compared to **10.04**-**10.73** mJ per decision for GPU-based ANN inference.
- Inference latency was halved to **0.061**-**0.079** ms per decision on the Speck neuromorphic chip, significantly faster than the **0.13**-**0.14** ms per decision on a high-performance RTX 4090 GPU.
- The proposed error alleviation techniques (fine-tuning via distillation and parameter scaling) enabled the SNN to achieve a lossless conversion rate (CR=**1.00**) at an ultra-low 4 time-steps, crucial for real-time and energy-efficient operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A collision-allowing training strategy significantly enhances DQN stability and efficiency in confined RMFS environments by preventing premature learning stagnation from frequent failures.
> - Parameter scaling of initial layer weights in SNNs effectively accelerates information flow and spike emission, enabling ultra-low time-step inference without severe accuracy degradation.
> - Hard-label knowledge distillation for ANN-to-SNN conversion successfully mitigates output distribution mismatch for RL policies, preserving decision capability at minimal time-steps and overcoming quantization errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The present work addresses critical challenges in autonomous navigation within Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems (RMFS), which are increasingly integral to modern warehouse logistics. Historically, RMFS pathfinding has relied on classical graph search algorithms, such as Dijkstra and A*. While these methods are effective in static, single-agent environments, their computational overhead becomes prohibitive in the dynamic, multi-agent, and resource-constrained settings characteristic of large-scale RMFS operations. Multi-agent extensions of these algorithms still face exponential complexity with increasing agent counts.

Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), has emerged as a promising alternative, demonstrating adaptability to dynamic, rule-free navigation scenarios. However, DRL inference typically involves synchronous, dense floating-point operations, leading to substantial computational and energy demands. This characteristic fundamentally limits its deployment on mobile Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) that operate under strict size, weight, and power constraints at the edge.

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) are positioned as an energy-efficient solution to this bottleneck. SNNs emulate biological neuron behavior, processing information through sparse, event-driven spikes, which can translate into orders-of-magnitude energy reductions when deployed on specialized neuromorphic hardware. Despite this potential, a notable gap exists in the integration of RL with SNNs: most existing studies operate solely in simulation, lacking empirical validation of energy savings on physical neuromorphic chips. Furthermore, standard ANN-to-SNN conversion techniques, primarily developed for supervised classification tasks with sparse one-hot label distributions, are not directly applicable to RL policies, which typically involve dense, continuous action-value distributions. This mismatch introduces accuracy degradation during conversion, especially at the low time-steps desired for efficient neuromorphic inference.

This research contributes to the field by presenting an end-to-end framework, SDQN-RMFS, which systematically addresses the aforementioned challenges. It bridges the gap between theoretical RL-SNN integration and practical neuromorphic chip deployment for RMFS pathfinding. The work aims to realize the energy efficiency and real-time performance benefits of SNNs on hardware while maintaining policy fidelity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an energy-efficient and low-latency pathfinding framework for Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems (RMFS) that can be practically deployed on resource-constrained hardware. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and addresses identified limitations:

- **Addressing Computational Inefficiency in RMFS:** Conventional search- and rule-based methods for RMFS pathfinding struggle with high computational complexity and long decision latency due to dynamic environmental changes, confined workspaces, and stringent real-time constraints. This necessitates a more efficient approach.
- **Mitigating Energy Consumption of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL):** While DRL offers a powerful alternative to classical methods, its reliance on dense floating-point operations makes it computationally and energy-intensive. This limits its viability for deployment on mobile AGVs with strict power, size, and weight constraints at the edge. The motivation is to find a less energy-demanding inference mechanism.
- **Bridging the Gap to Neuromorphic Hardware Deployment:** Existing RL-SNN research largely remains confined to simulation, lacking empirical evidence of practical energy savings on physical neuromorphic chips. A key motivation is to substantiate the promised efficiency gains by achieving actual hardware deployment.
- **Overcoming Challenges in ANN-to-SNN Conversion for RL:**
 - **Exploration Stagnation in Constrained Environments:** In RMFS environments with narrow passages, standard DQN's random exploration often leads to immediate collisions and task failures, preventing positive rewards and causing learning stagnation. The framework needs a robust training strategy to overcome this.
 - **Insufficient Information Transmission at Low SNN Time-steps:** For ultra-low inference latency and energy efficiency, SNNs must operate at minimal time-steps. However, the zero-initialized membrane potential in deep SNNs can cause delayed spike emission, hindering effective information flow within limited time windows. The conversion process must address this to ensure functionality.
 - **RL-SNN Output Distribution Mismatch:** Traditional ANN-to-SNN conversion methods are optimized for sparse, one-hot output distributions typical of supervised classification. RL policies, however, produce continuous action-value (Q-value) distributions. Directly applying these methods introduces severe discrete quantization errors at low time-steps, leading to policy degradation. A motivation is to develop a conversion method tailored to RL's output characteristics.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the SDQN-RMFS framework are:

1. To develop a robust Deep Q-Network (DQN) architecture, enhanced with a collision-allowing training strategy, to efficiently train high-performance agents for multi-agent pathfinding in warehouse environments.
2. To propose a hard-label knowledge distillation scheme for Artificial Neural Network (ANN)-to-Spiking Neural Network (SNN) conversion that addresses the output distribution mismatch between continuous RL Q-values and discrete SNNs, thereby preserving policy capability and reducing inference latency.
3. To perform physical deployment of the converted SNN onto a neuromorphic chip (SPECK2E) and conduct comprehensive hardware evaluations to quantify the energy savings and latency reduction, thereby validating the framework's practical applicability at the edge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SDQN-RMFS framework is structured as a three-stage pipeline: ANN agent training, ANN-to-SNN conversion, and neuromorphic hardware deployment.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The RMFS pathfinding task is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The state `s` observed by an AGV includes its current position (`pos_c`), previous position (`pos_l`), target position (`pos_t`), loading status (`loaded`), and the positions of other AGVs (`pos_o`). The action space consists of five discrete actions: four cardinal movements and a stationary action.

**B. SNN-friendly DQN with Collision-Allowing Strategy (ANN Training)**

1. **SNN-Friendly State Representation:** The inputs to the DQN are designed to be natively binary, with sparse activation (elements equal to 1). This representation directly translates into spatial spikes for the SNN, eliminating the need for intermediate rate- or latency-coding conversions and contributing to ultra-low inference latency and energy consumption during neuromorphic deployment.
2. **Reward Shaping:** Beyond standard positive reward for reaching the goal (`R_goal`) and negative reward for collisions or boundary violations (`R_fail`), a shaped reward `R_s` is implemented. This reward is based on the Manhattan distance to the target, discouraging repetitive exploration by penalizing movements that do not reduce the distance and rewarding those that do.
3. **Collision-Allowing Exploration:** To address exploration stagnation in narrow RMFS passages, a collision-allowing strategy is introduced. Instead of immediately terminating an episode upon collision, the agent is penalized (`R_fail`) and reverted to its previous position, provided the collision count remains below a predefined limit (`CL`). This strategy allows the agent to continue exploration within certain bounds, preventing premature learning cessation from frequent failures. As training progresses, the reliance on this allowance diminishes.
4. **DQN Architecture and Enhancements:** The DQN employs a convolutional neural network architecture with nine layers (3x3 and 1x1 convolutions, interspersed with average pooling for hardware compatibility). It incorporates a target network, prioritized experience replay, and the Double DQN (DDQN) formulation to enhance training stability and performance by decoupling action selection from action evaluation.

**C. Robust ANN-to-SNN Conversion and Error Alleviation**

The conversion from the trained ANN to an SNN is a critical phase, focusing on minimizing performance degradation at ultra-low time-steps. The core conversion principle maps the ReLU activation of the ANN to the equivalent firing rate of an SNN's integrate-and-fire (IF) neurons. The authors acknowledge that residual membrane potential (v_l(t)/t) introduces conversion errors, especially magnified at low time-steps. To mitigate these, a two-stage error alleviation pipeline is proposed:

1. **Parameter Scaling for Information Flow:** To counteract delayed spike emission and accelerate information propagation within the SNN, especially critical for the asynchronous processing of neuromorphic chips, the weights of the first layer are amplified by a scaling factor `k` (W^1_snn = k * W^1_ann). This forces initial neurons to emit spikes earlier and more frequently, ensuring rapid forward transmission of information through deeper layers and meeting latency requirements. Additionally, bias components (`b_l = 0`) are explicitly removed from the network to align with the neuromorphic chip's native architecture, avoiding synchronization overhead and maintaining low-latency performance.
2. **ANN-to-SNN Fine-tuning via Distillation:** This method reframes the pre-conversion fine-tuning as a knowledge distillation process. A fully trained ANN (teacher) generates hard, one-hot pseudo-labels by applying an argmax function to its Q-value outputs. A new, trainable ANN (student) is then fine-tuned using a cross-entropy loss against these pseudo-labels. This approach directly incentivizes the student ANN to widen the decision margin for the optimal action, producing a sharpened, one-hot-like output distribution. This makes the network's decisions more resilient to the quantization noise inherent in IF neuron conversion, effectively bridging the continuous-discrete gap without complex layer-wise optimization.

**D. Agent Deployment**
After conversion, the SNN agent is deployed for pathfinding. An action detection mechanism is implemented as a safety layer: before executing an SNN-predicted action, the system checks for potential collisions. If a collision is highly probable, the action is overridden to 'stop'. This external rule-based layer provides a safety fallback, while the SNN's primary decisions are ensured by its high Conversion Rate (CR) from the fine-tuned conversion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the training strategy, architectural choices, conversion methods, and hardware performance.

**A. Effect of Collision Limit (CL)**
Experiments demonstrated that increasing the collision limit (CL) during training positively impacts the DQN's performance. As CL increased from 1 to 40, the training curves for completion degree exhibited enhanced stability and efficiency, particularly in scenarios with a higher number of AGVs. This outcome supported the adoption of CL=40 for subsequent experiments.

**B. DQN Alternatives Comparison**
The authors benchmarked their DQN against Dueling DQN, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and a standard Actor-Critic algorithm in a single-AGV scenario. Value-based methods (DQN and Dueling DQN) successfully converged, achieving high episode rewards. In contrast, policy-based methods (PPO and Actor-Critic) failed to perform pathfinding effectively during the entire training process. While Dueling DQN showed slightly faster initial convergence, both DQN and Dueling DQN achieved comparable final pathfinding performance. The standard DQN was chosen for its robust performance and simpler architecture, which is considered more advantageous for the subsequent ANN-to-SNN conversion pipeline.

**C. Effect of ANN-to-SNN Error Alleviation**
The effectiveness of the proposed error alleviation methods (parameter scaling and fine-tuning via distillation) was evaluated.
- An SNN converted directly from an ANN without fine-tuning or scaling exhibited limited accuracy, achieving a Conversion Rate (CR) of 0.84 even at 128 time-steps, which implies higher latency and energy consumption.
- The fine-tuned ANN-to-SNN model dramatically improved conversion accuracy, achieving a CR of 1.00 by 32 time-steps.
- The combination of fine-tuning and parameter scaling further minimized the required time-steps. For example, a fine-tuned model with a scaling factor of k=7 maintained a lossless CR of 1.00 at an ultra-low 4 time-steps.
- These results indicated that the proposed methods significantly enhance the pathfinding performance of SNN agents, ensuring high efficiency and decision capability under strict latency constraints. The study also noted that even a slightly lower CR does not necessarily lead to severe performance degradation due to the implemented action detection (safety) mechanism.

**D. Hardware Deployment and Performance**
The energy consumption and inference latency were profiled across different hardware platforms: the original ANN on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, the SNN simulated on the same GPU, and the SNN deployed on the SPECK2E neuromorphic chip. The time-step for the converted SNN on Speck was set to 4 with a scaling factor of 7.

1. **Energy Efficiency:**
 - The ANN on the RTX 4090 GPU consumed approximately 10.04 to 10.73 mJ per decision.
 - Simulating the SNN on the same GPU resulted in significantly higher energy consumption, between 182.80 and 188.47 mJ, attributed to the Von Neumann architecture's inefficiencies for sparse SNN processing.
 - The SNN deployed on the Speck neuromorphic chip achieved an energy consumption of 0.89 × 10⁻³ to 1.95 × 10⁻³ mJ per decision.
 - This represents an energy reduction of up to 11,281 times compared to the GPU-ANN baseline.

2. **Real-Time Inference Latency:**
 - The ANN on the RTX 4090 GPU achieved an inference time of 0.13 to 0.14 ms per decision.
 - Processing the SNN on the GPU led to a latency increase to over 2.41 ms due to the iterative simulation of multiple time-steps.
 - The Speck chip, capable of native and asynchronous processing of binary spike events, achieved an inference latency of 0.061 to 0.079 ms per decision.
 - This indicates that the SDQN-RMFS framework, when deployed on neuromorphic hardware, halved the inference latency compared to a high-performance GPU, while also achieving substantial energy savings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SDQN-RMFS framework represents a notable advancement in pathfinding solutions for Robotic Mobile Fulfillment Systems, particularly for large-scale and resource-constrained operations. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Enabling Energy-Sustainable RMFS Operations:** The most prominent impact is the demonstrated achievement of ultra-low power pathfinding. The framework’s ability to reduce energy consumption by up to 11,281 times compared to GPU-based ANN inference addresses a critical bottleneck for continuous, large-scale multi-AGV operations. This allows RMFS to operate more sustainably and cost-effectively, reducing reliance on frequent battery recharges or large onboard power sources.
- **Facilitating Edge Deployment on Resource-Constrained AGVs:** The substantial energy savings and reduced inference latency (nearly half that of a high-performance GPU) on a specialized, power-constrained edge chip like Speck directly enables the practical deployment of sophisticated pathfinding intelligence on individual AGVs. This capability is crucial for mobile robots where size, weight, and power (SWaP) constraints are stringent.
- **Advancing Neuromorphic Reinforcement Learning:** This work moves beyond simulated RL-SNN studies by providing empirical validation of energy efficiency and real-time performance on physical neuromorphic hardware. This constitutes a critical step in substantiating the practical utility of neuromorphic computing for complex, real-world robotic control tasks.
- **Addressing Key ANN-to-SNN Conversion Challenges for RL:** The proposed hard-label knowledge distillation scheme and parameter scaling method specifically tackle the persistent problem of output distribution mismatch and information flow at low time-steps during ANN-to-SNN conversion for RL policies. This methodology can contribute to improved fidelity and efficiency in other applications involving SNNs and RL.
- **Improving Robustness and Efficiency of RMFS Pathfinding:** The collision-allowing training strategy enhances the stability and efficiency of DQN training in complex, narrow RMFS environments, leading to more robust initial policy learning. The end-to-end framework ensures that this robust policy is preserved and efficiently executed on target hardware.
- **Potential for Broader Applicability:** The methodologies developed within SDQN-RMFS, such as the SNN-friendly state representation, hardware-aware parameter scaling, and specialized ANN-to-SNN conversion for decision-making tasks, may be transferable to other domains requiring energy-efficient AI at the edge, including other mobile robotic applications, autonomous vehicles, or industrial automation.
- **Unlocking Reactive Multi-AGV Coordination:** The ultra-low latency inference capability allows for highly reactive decision-making, which is essential for coordinating large fleets of AGVs in dynamically changing warehouse environments, minimizing collisions, and maximizing throughput.
