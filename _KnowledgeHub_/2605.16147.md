# Registers Matter for Pixel-Space Diffusion Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> This research investigates register tokens in pixel-space Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), finding that while DiTs do not exhibit the patch-token outliers characteristic of Vision Transformers, registers still improve generation quality by regularizing feature maps and absorbing high norms. It introduces Register Guidance, a novel technique leveraging these tokens to enhance image structure and detail, achieving improved FID scores, especially when combined with Classifier-Free Guidance.

## Problem

- It was unclear if Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), particularly pixel-space DiTs, experienced high-norm patch-token outliers similar to those observed in Vision Transformers (ViTs).
- The functional role and impact of register tokens in DiTs were underexplored, including whether they provided comparable benefits to their use in ViTs for outlier mitigation or other representational improvements.
- The empirical success of existing pixel-space DiT architectures using conditioning tokens, such as in-context class conditioning, lacked a detailed explanation regarding any potential implicit register-like functionality.

## Method

- The study conducted a systematic analysis of token behavior across various ==DiT== architectures (pixel-space and latent-space), utilizing techniques like ==feature norm measurement==, ==attention map visualization==, and ==Total Variation analysis==.
- ==Register tokens== were explicitly introduced into DiTs as additional learnable parameters, and their impact on internal representations and image generation quality (==FID==) was thoroughly evaluated across different configurations.
- A novel '**Register Guidance**' (RG) technique was developed, enabling a single DiT model, trained with occasional ==register dropping==, to use its 'without registers' prediction as a negative guidance direction to improve generation quality.

## Results

- Register tokens consistently improved the **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)** of pixel-space DiTs, with more pronounced gains in pixel-space models compared to latent-space variants.
- Register Guidance (RG) enhanced generation quality by improving object structure and visual details, and it proved complementary to Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), yielding the **best FID scores** when combined.
- Implicit register-like behavior, including high-norm outliers and global information encoding, was observed in in-context conditioning tokens of existing DiTs and text tokens of large text-to-image models, contributing to their performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pixel-space DiTs do not exhibit high-norm patch-token outliers like ViTs; instead, explicitly introduced register tokens develop high norms and act as 'sinks', regularizing patch token feature norms to be more uniform.
> - Register tokens lead to cleaner, smoother intermediate feature maps in DiTs, particularly at high noise levels, indicating a role in representation regularization beyond simple outlier mitigation.
> - Registers serve a dual function: some encode global semantic information with high classification accuracy, while others (typically higher norm) primarily act as magnitude sinks, suggesting a specialized division of labor.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and Diffusion Models (DMs), specifically focusing on Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). ViTs have demonstrated robust performance in visual representation learning but are known to exhibit high-norm patch-token outliers. These outliers can degrade the quality of feature maps and attention interpretations, impacting performance on dense prediction tasks. The concept of "register tokens" was introduced in ViTs as a mechanism to mitigate these outliers by providing auxiliary tokens that act as "sinks" for global information, thereby preventing patch tokens from being repurposed for such roles.

In parallel, diffusion models have increasingly adopted transformer-based architectures, replacing traditional convolutional backbones. Recent advancements have also explored training these DiTs directly in pixel space, as an alternative to the more common latent diffusion models that rely on pre-trained autoencoders. The architectural shift towards pixel-space DiTs brings them structurally closer to ViTs. This convergence motivates an inquiry into whether DiTs, particularly those operating in pixel space, face similar challenges related to token outliers and whether register tokens could offer comparable benefits.

The paper also connects to a broader research trend investigating attention sinks and the behavior of special tokens in various transformer architectures, including those used in large language models and other generative models. While attention sinks have been studied in these contexts, their specific presence, role, and utility in image diffusion transformers, especially in pixel-space, remained an underexplored area prior to this work. This research aims to address this gap by systematically analyzing token behavior in DiTs and proposing a practical method to leverage register-like mechanisms for improved generation quality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were twofold:

1. **To ascertain the presence of high-norm patch-token outliers in Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), particularly in pixel-space DiTs (pDiTs), similar to those observed in Vision Transformers (ViTs).** This was motivated by the architectural similarities emerging between pDiTs and ViTs. Understanding whether DiTs inherit these outlier characteristics is fundamental to determining if ViT-inspired solutions, such as register tokens, might be applicable.
2. **To investigate whether register tokens can be effectively utilized in DiTs, and to characterize their functional role and impact on generation quality.** If DiTs do not exhibit the same outlier behavior as ViTs, then the mechanism by which registers might be beneficial, or indeed if they are beneficial at all, requires detailed examination. This includes analyzing how registers influence internal representations and if their effectiveness varies across different diffusion model spaces (pixel vs. latent).

Beyond these core questions, the research was further motivated by several underlying considerations:

- **Understanding Internal Mechanisms**: To gain a deeper understanding of how information is processed and represented within DiTs, especially concerning the flow of global versus local information and the potential for token-specific specializations.
- **Addressing Performance Limitations**: If token outliers or inefficient information flow contribute to suboptimal performance in DiTs, identifying and mitigating these issues could lead to significant improvements in image generation quality.
- **Providing Rationale for Existing Designs**: To analyze whether recent pixel-space DiT architectures, which often incorporate additional conditioning tokens (e.g., in-context class conditioning), implicitly leverage register-like mechanisms, thereby accounting for some of their empirical success.
- **Developing Practical Enhancements**: To develop new techniques that leverage the observed properties of register tokens to further enhance the generation capabilities of DiTs. This led to the proposal of Register Guidance.

By addressing these motivations, the study aimed to contribute both to the theoretical understanding of transformer mechanisms in generative models and to the practical advancement of high-quality image synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach combining experimental model training, detailed internal representation analysis, and the development of a novel guidance technique.

**A. Model Selection and Training Regimes:**
- **Vision Transformer (ViT) Baseline**: DINOv2 was used as a representative ViT-based model to demonstrate the typical patch-token outlier behavior.
- **Diffusion Transformers (DiTs)**:
 - **Pixel-space DiTs (pDiTs)**: Models based on the standard DiT architecture with improvements from (referred to as JiT-like models) were trained. These models used flow matching on ImageNet datasets at 256x256 and 512x512 resolutions with a patch size of 16. PixelDiT, another advanced pixel-space architecture, was also analyzed.
 - **Latent-space DiTs**: SiT and RAE (which uses a DINOv2-based backbone) were employed, following their original backbone designs and training pipelines.
- **Register Token Implementation**: Register tokens were implemented as additional learnable parameters appended to the patch-token sequence, consistent with the approach in. These tokens did not directly participate in the training objective. Ablation studies were conducted on the number of register tokens and the transformer layers at which they were introduced.

**B. Internal Representation Analysis Techniques:**
- **Token-wise Feature Norms**: The L2 norms of token features were measured across different transformer layers and diffusion timesteps to identify instances of high-norm outliers. This was performed for both patch tokens and register/conditioning tokens.
- **Attention Map Visualization**: Attention maps were visualized to detect artifacts or unusual focus patterns in low-information regions, a known characteristic of ViT outliers.
- **Total Variation (TV) Analysis**: The spatial smoothness of intermediate transformer features was quantified using Total Variation. TV ratios (with registers / without registers) were calculated across transformer blocks and diffusion timesteps, averaged over 1,000 images, to assess the impact of registers on feature smoothness.
- **Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Visualizations**: PCA was applied to intermediate feature maps for qualitative assessment of their coherence and structure.
- **Linear Probing**: Linear classifiers were trained on register-token features extracted from an intermediate transformer block (5th out of 12) to evaluate their capacity to encode global semantic information (e.g., image class). This helped differentiate between registers acting as norm sinks and those carrying meaningful data.
- **Correlation Decay Slope**: (Detailed in Appendix D.1) This metric, indicating spatial organization, was also used to assess the impact of registers on feature quality.
- **Outlier Localization**: The location of outlier formation within transformer blocks (e.g., after attention vs. MLP layers) was investigated.
- **Cross-Domain Analysis**: Token behavior was examined in large-scale text-to-image models (e.g., FLUX, SD3.5, Qwen-Image, Z-Image, PixArt-α) to observe potential implicit register-like roles of text tokens.

**C. Register Guidance (RG) Development:**
- **Motivation**: Based on the finding that registers improve image structure and coherence while preserving content, the researchers hypothesized that a model *without* registers could serve as a "weaker" version for guidance, similar to AutoGuidance.
- **Inference Procedure**: Register Guidance (RG) was formulated to use the prediction of a model without registers as a negative direction to guide the prediction of a model with registers. A hyperparameter, `w_rg`, controlled the guidance strength. RG was also combined with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG).
- **Training Procedure**: To enable a single model to operate both with and without registers, the training regimen involved occasionally dropping all register tokens from the input sequence with a probability `p`. This allowed the model to learn both prediction modes within a unified architecture.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning**: Extensive ablation studies were conducted on `w_rg` (RG scale) and `p` (register-drop probability) to find optimal configurations.
- **Training and Fine-tuning**: RG was applied in training models from scratch and also in fine-tuning pre-trained JiT checkpoints, demonstrating its applicability to existing models.

**D. Dual-Stream Architecture Exploration (Appendix D.2):**
- To address the distinct roles of register and patch tokens, investigations were conducted into dual-stream architectures that could process these token types more effectively.
- Initial studies involved naive duplication of transformer components (RMSNorm, adaLN, Attention, MLP) for each token stream.
- A "compact" dual-stream design was developed to balance performance gains with parameter efficiency, selectively applying separate parameters (e.g., using LoRA for attention/adaLN or partial MLP splitting) to register tokens. Register tokens were introduced in later layers based on earlier findings regarding their effectiveness.

**E. Evaluation Metrics for Generation Quality:**
- Generation quality was primarily evaluated using the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). Inception Score (IS) was also reported for some comparisons.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the behavior of register tokens in Diffusion Transformers and their impact on image generation.

**A. Absence of ViT-like Outliers in DiTs, Yet Register Benefits:**
- **No Patch-Token Outliers**: Unlike Vision Transformers (ViTs) such as DINOv2, Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) (including both pixel-space and latent-space variants) do not exhibit prominent high-norm outliers among patch tokens. Their attention maps also remain focused on objects rather than showing artifacts in low-information regions (Figure 1, Figure 2). Patch-token norms were observed to be largely uniform.
- **Emergence of Register Outliers**: Despite the absence of inherent patch-token outliers, introducing register tokens into DiTs leads to the emergence of high-norm tokens specifically within these registers (Figure 2, Figures 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17).
- **Improved Generation Quality**: Register tokens consistently improved the generation quality (FID) of pixel-space DiTs across various model sizes and training epochs (Table 1, Table 9). This effect was significantly more pronounced in pixel-space models compared to latent-space counterparts.
- **Outlier Formation**: Analysis showed that these high-norm register tokens primarily form after the MLP layer within transformer blocks, rather than after the attention layer (Figure 13).

**B. Registers Lead to Cleaner Internal Feature Maps:**
- **Reduced Patch-Token Norms**: Register tokens consistently reduced the feature norms of *all* patch tokens in DiTs (Figure 3, Figure 18). This phenomenon was not observed in DINOv2, where registers primarily absorbed pre-existing outliers.
- **Smoother Representations**: Registers contributed to smoother intermediate feature maps, particularly at high noise levels (t ∈ [0, 0.2]). This was quantified by a reduction in Total Variation (TV) ratio (Figure 4a, Figure 19) and qualitatively supported by PCA visualizations (Figure 4b). Improved spatial organization was also indicated by the correlation decay slope (Figure 20). This effect at high noise levels is noted as significant for flow-matching models.

**C. Dual Role of Registers: Global Information and Norm Sinks:**
- **Diverse Functionality**: Linear probing of register tokens revealed that they perform a dual function. Some registers, typically those with lower norms, encode meaningful global semantic information about the image, achieving high classification accuracy. Other registers, characterized by higher norms and low classification accuracy, appear to function primarily as norm sinks, absorbing magnitude from patch tokens (Figure 5).
- **Semantic Attention**: Visualizations of attention maps confirmed that high-accuracy register tokens attend to distinct semantic regions (e.g., foreground objects, background elements), while norm sinks did not exhibit meaningful semantic structure (Figure 6).

**D. Registers More Effective in Pixel Space:**
- **Differential Impact**: Register tokens provided the largest improvements in pixel-space DiTs, showed moderate gains in VAE-space models, and could even degrade performance in RAE-based models (Table 2, Table 10).
- **Noisier Pixel Representations**: Pixel-space models were found to generate noisier intermediate features and exhibit higher patch-token norms compared to latent-space models (Figures 22, 23). This suggested that pixel-space training requires stronger regularization, making register tokens more beneficial in this context.

**E. Optimal Register Configuration for DiTs:**
- **Deeper Layer Introduction**: Unlike ViTs, where registers are typically introduced from the first layer, pDiTs benefited most when register tokens were introduced in deeper layers (e.g., blocks 4–11). Introducing them in early layers (0–11 or 0–4) did not provide significant performance gains, and could even degrade performance (Table 3, Table 18). This was hypothesized to be due to a lack of semantic structure in early layers, leading to uninformative register signals (Figure 21).
- **Increased Register Count**: pDiTs generally required more register tokens (e.g., 32 tokens for pDiT-B) for optimal performance compared to typical ViT setups (e.g., 4 tokens).

**F. Implicit Registers in Existing DiT Architectures:**
- **In-Context Conditioning**: In-context class conditioning tokens, as used in architectures like JiT, exhibited similar behavior to explicit register tokens. Some of these tokens developed high-norm outliers and acted as norm sinks, while others encoded global semantic information beyond just class-specific features (Figure 7).
- **Source of Performance Gains**: A significant portion of the performance improvement attributed to in-context conditioning was found to stem from this implicit register-like behavior rather than solely from the explicit class information (Table 1).
- **Text-to-Image Models**: Similar high-norm outlier behavior was observed in text tokens of large text-to-image models (e.g., SD3.5, Qwen-Image, Z-Image, FLUX, PixArt-α), suggesting a general need for "outlier slots" in DiTs, which patch tokens cannot reliably fulfill due to loss constraints (Figure 25).

**G. Register Guidance (RG) for Enhanced Generation:**
- **Improved Quality**: Register Guidance (RG), which uses a model without registers as a "weak" negative guidance direction, consistently improved generation quality by enhancing object structure and visual details while preserving overall content (Figure 8).
- **Unified Model for Guidance**: Training a single model capable of operating both with and without registers (by randomly dropping registers during training) eliminated artifacts that arose when using two separately trained models for guidance (Figure 9, Figures 26, 27).
- **Complementary to CFG**: RG was found to be complementary to Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG). Combining both (RG+CFG) yielded the best FID scores across various model sizes and image resolutions (Figure 10, Table 4, Table 5, Figure 28, Figures 29, 30).
- **Fine-tuning Effectiveness**: RG could be effectively applied by fine-tuning pre-trained JiT checkpoints with register dropping, providing strong performance with minimal additional effort (Table 11, Table 12).

**H. Dual-Stream Architectures (Appendix D.2):**
- **Benefits of Decoupling**: Naively duplicating transformer components for registers and patches improved FID but significantly increased parameters (Table 13).
- **Parameter-Efficient Design**: A compact dual-stream design, which selectively dualized MLP, adaLN, and RMSNorm components while keeping Attention shared, offered a better trade-off, consistently improving generation quality with only a modest increase in parameters (Tables 14, 15, 16).
- **Compatibility with REPA**: Register-like tokens and the compact dual-stream design also showed benefits when combined with representation alignment methods like REPA (Table 17).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of generative AI by providing both theoretical insights into transformer mechanisms and practical methods for improving image generation quality.

**A. Enhanced Understanding of Diffusion Transformers:**
The work fundamentally advances the understanding of token behavior in DiTs. It clarifies that while DiTs do not exhibit the patch-token outliers characteristic of ViTs, they still benefit significantly from register tokens, but through distinct mechanisms. This shifts the perspective on registers in DiTs from outlier mitigation to a broader role in representation regularization, feature smoothing, and global information encoding. This distinction is particularly important for pixel-space models, where the inherent "noisiness" of intermediate representations is higher.

**B. Improved Architectural Design Principles:**
The findings provide a rationale for successful pixel-space DiT architectures that utilize in-context conditioning, revealing their implicit register-like functionality. This insight can guide future architectural designs, promoting more explicit and optimized integration of register-like mechanisms. The exploration of dual-stream architectures, which process different token types separately, offers a promising direction for creating more specialized and efficient DiT models.

**C. Practical Advancement in Image Generation:**
The introduction of Register Guidance (RG) offers a new and effective technique for boosting the quality of generated images from pixel-space DiTs. Its compatibility with existing methods like Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) makes it a valuable addition to the diffusion model toolkit. The ability to implement RG within a single model, trained to operate with and without registers, streamlines its application and avoids the introduction of visual artifacts, which is crucial for practical deployment.

**D. Broader Implications for Generative Models:**
The observation of implicit register-like behavior in conditioning tokens across various transformer-based generative models, including large-scale text-to-image models, suggests that the concept of specialized "sink" tokens may be a more general phenomenon. This could stimulate further research into the role of auxiliary tokens in other transformer applications, potentially leading to more robust and higher-quality generative outputs across different modalities.

**E. Future Research Directions:**
The research opens several avenues for future work, including the development of more sophisticated dual-stream DiT architectures that can leverage the distinct roles of register and patch tokens more effectively. Extending Register Guidance to other generative tasks, such as text-to-image synthesis, also represents a significant potential impact area. Further theoretical investigation into the exact mechanisms by which registers improve intermediate representations and spatial coherence could lead to more targeted and profound advancements.
