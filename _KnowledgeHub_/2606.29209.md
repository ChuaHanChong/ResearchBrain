# AnyBody: Free-Form Whole-Body Humanoid Control from Arbitrary Keypoint Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UNC Chapel Hill and Georgia Tech developed AnyBody, a humanoid control framework that generates coordinated whole-body motions from arbitrary, sparse keypoint inputs. The system achieved high tracking success rates of 93.7%-97.6% across various sparse keypoint configurations and demonstrated efficient adaptation to new tasks, improving success rates for obstacle-reach tasks from a range of 3.04%-54.68% to 95.56%-99.47% via latent-space reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots possess high degrees of freedom, making their direct control inherently complex.
- Existing physics-based motion tracking relies on expensive and cumbersome full-body motion capture, which limits scalable data collection and flexible teleoperation.
- Hierarchical control frameworks often decouple body parts, sacrificing coordinated whole-body movements essential for complex loco-manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **AnyBody** uses a three-stage training process that first learns a ==dynamics-aware latent space== via ==privileged teacher-student distillation==, followed by a partial keypoint encoder, and then task-specific RL finetuning.
- Introduces a ==Transformer-based keypoint encoder== with ==masked self-attention== that interprets arbitrary, masked subsets of body keypoints with temporal context to infer and generate whole-body actions.
- Utilizes a ==spherical latent motion space== and a frozen dynamics-aware decoder as a ==motor prior==, enabling efficient adaptation to new tasks via a lightweight ==residual reinforcement learning corrector== (PPO).

## Results

- Achieved tracking success rates from **93.7%** for single-wrist to **97.6%** for full-body (5-point) keypoint control, with average Point-of-Interest position errors consistently around **10 cm**.
- Demonstrated robust open-ended generation of diverse, physically plausible whole-body behaviors (e.g. locomotion, arm swings, squats) from sparse keypoint commands, and enabled real-world teleoperation of a Unitree G1 via Apple Vision Pro wrist-keypoint capture, showing stable sim-to-real transfer.
- Latent-space RL finetuning dramatically improved success rates for obstacle-reach tasks (from **3.04%-54.68%** to **95.56%-99.47%**) and wrist-writing (from **0.00%** to **97.87%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A compact, dynamics-aware spherical latent space can effectively represent diverse whole-body motor skills for humanoid robots.
> - Sparse, arbitrary subsets of keypoints, when combined with temporal context, are sufficient inputs for inferring and generating coordinated full-body humanoid motions.
> - Reinforcement learning within a learned latent space, building on a robust motion prior, allows for efficient adaptation and acquisition of new, complex skills without learning whole-body behaviors from scratch.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The control of humanoid robots, particularly for complex loco-manipulation tasks in human-centric environments, presents a significant challenge due to their high degrees of freedom and the inherent difficulty in maintaining stable whole-body coordination. Current approaches in humanoid control predominantly fall into two categories, each with distinct limitations that AnyBody aims to address.

The first dominant paradigm involves **physics-based motion tracking**, where robotic policies are trained to imitate human motions captured using motion capture (mocap) systems. While effective in enabling humanoids to replicate complex movements, this approach is constrained by the practicalities of mocap systems. These systems are typically expensive, cumbersome, and require a controlled environment, which significantly bottlenecks the scalable collection of whole-body control data and limits flexible humanoid teleoperation in diverse real-world settings. Methods such as DeepMimic, Perpetual Humanoid Control, and SONIC exemplify advancements in this area, demonstrating high fidelity in motion tracking but inherently relying on dense, full-body motion references.

The second category explores **hierarchical humanoid control frameworks** that attempt to simplify control by decoupling upper-body manipulation from lower-body locomotion. These methods often delegate locomotion to a pre-trained lower-body controller, allowing users to teleoperate only the upper body through higher-level commands. While offering some flexibility by reducing the control interface complexity, these approaches typically assume a fixed control interface and explicitly separate the control of different body parts. This separation can compromise the coordinated whole-body motions that are often crucial for complex loco-manipulation tasks where upper and lower body movements must be integrated for stability and efficacy. Examples include works that focus on mapping sparse human signals like monocular pose or VR tracking to whole-body motion, or those that admit partially specified targets through masked state variables. However, these often maintain predefined command structures or separate control modules.

AnyBody positions itself to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It seeks to overcome the reliance on expensive full-body mocap and the limitations of fixed, decoupled control interfaces. By enabling whole-body control from *arbitrary subsets* of body keypoints, the proposed framework aims to offer a more flexible, intuitive, and scalable control mechanism that preserves coordinated whole-body behavior, thereby advancing the practical deployment and data collection capabilities for humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing AnyBody stems from the aforementioned limitations in current humanoid control strategies. Specifically, the authors identify several key challenges:
- **High Degrees of Freedom:** Humanoid robots possess numerous joints, making their control inherently complex and difficult to manage directly.
- **Cost and Scalability of Data Collection:** Existing physics-based motion trackers largely depend on full-body motion capture, which is resource-intensive and not conducive to scalable data acquisition or widespread humanoid teleoperation. This creates a bottleneck for expanding the range of behaviors humanoids can learn and perform.
- **Lack of Whole-Body Coordination:** Hierarchical control methods, while simplifying control by separating upper and lower body functions, often sacrifice the synchronized, integrated movements essential for sophisticated loco-manipulation tasks. This limits the robot's ability to perform tasks requiring dynamic balance and coordinated interaction with its environment.
- **Fixed Control Interfaces:** Many existing sparse or partial-input control systems assume a predefined and fixed set of commands or body parts for interaction, reducing flexibility for diverse tasks and operator preferences.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the AnyBody research are to:
- **Develop a unified whole-body humanoid controller:** The aim is to create a single, cohesive control framework capable of generating complex, coordinated whole-body motions.
- **Enable control from arbitrary keypoint subsets:** A core objective is to allow operators to command the humanoid using any chosen subset of body keypoints at deployment time, moving beyond fixed control interfaces. This flexibility is intended to cater to different task requirements and operator preferences.
- **Synthesize physically plausible and robust motions:** Despite receiving sparse keypoint guidance, the controller should automatically generate stable, physically realistic, and human-like whole-body movements that are consistent with the provided constraints.
- **Eliminate reliance on full-body motion capture and retargeting:** The project aims to provide an alternative to cumbersome mocap systems and the often error-prone process of retargeting human motion data to robot kinematics.
- **Preserve coordinated whole-body behaviors:** Unlike decoupled hierarchical approaches, AnyBody seeks to maintain the integrated movement of the entire robot, which is critical for complex tasks.
- **Provide an intuitive and efficient control interface:** By allowing users to specify motion intent through task-relevant keypoints, the system intends to simplify interaction and control.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create a more adaptable, scalable, and user-friendly humanoid control system capable of operating effectively in dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AnyBody framework is developed through a three-stage training process designed to create a unified whole-body humanoid controller capable of responding to arbitrary keypoint guidance.

**Stage 1: Learning the Latent Space and Dynamics-Aware Decoder**
The initial stage focuses on learning a compact, dynamics-aware latent motion representation. This is achieved by:
- **Privileged Teacher Training:** A privileged teacher policy (π_T) is trained using reinforcement learning (PPO) to track a large-scale human motion dataset. The dataset, Bones-Seed, comprises approximately 140 hours of motion data, filtered to focus on loco-manipulation behaviors by removing physically implausible or extreme motions (e.g., in-air climbing, crawling, dynamic jumps). This teacher observes a comprehensive proprioceptive state and reference motion information, allowing it to generate high-fidelity, whole-body actions.
- **Online Distillation to Student:** The teacher's behavior is then distilled online into a deterministic encoder-decoder student model. The student comprises an encoder (E_θ) that maps the goal command and proprioceptive state to a latent representation, and a decoder (D_ϕ) that transforms this latent and the current proprioceptive state into an action (PD targets for actuated joints).
- **Spherical Latent Space:** The latent vector `z` produced by the encoder is projected onto a unit sphere (S_d_z-1). This spherical constraint ensures a compact and geometrically well-defined latent space, preventing norm-drift failures and promoting smoothness in latent trajectories.
- **Loss Function (L_1):** The student is trained to minimize a combination of a behavioral cloning (BC) term, comparing its decoded action to the teacher's action, and a cosine smoothness regularizer on consecutive latents, encouraging smooth transitions in the learned motion manifold. The resulting encoder-decoder pair effectively compresses motor skills into a reusable latent space, with the decoder serving as a dynamics-aware motor prior.

**Stage 2: Learning the Partial Keypoint Encoder by Online Distillation**
With the latent space and dynamics-aware decoder established and frozen from Stage 1, the second stage introduces the core innovation of partial keypoint control:
- **Keypoint Encoder (E_kp_ψ):** A transformer-based keypoint encoder is developed. This encoder receives a set of masked keypoints (`k ⊙ m`) and the proprioceptive state. For deployment, a sparse subset of five keypoints (KP5: torso, left/right wrist, left/right ankle) is used.
- **Temporal Context:** Each keypoint token provided to the encoder includes a 0.5-second window of historical and future positions (15 frames). This temporal context enables the encoder to infer motion velocities and predict future states, crucial for robust control from sparse input.
- **Masked Self-Attention:** The transformer architecture utilizes masked self-attention, allowing it to process any arbitrary subset of keypoints. Absent keypoints are marked as NaNs and excluded from attention calculations, ensuring adaptability to varying input configurations.
- **Latent Distillation:** The keypoint encoder is trained through online distillation to align its latent output (`ˆz_kp`) with the *frozen* privileged latent (`sg[ˆz]`) generated by the Stage 1 encoder. An additional L2 loss term aligns the decoded action from `ˆz_kp` with the *frozen* decoded action from the privileged latent (`sg[D(ˆz, s_p)]`), providing further anchoring in the action space.
- **Loss Function (L_2):** This stage minimizes a cosine distance between `ˆz_kp` and `sg[ˆz]`, combined with an L2 action alignment term.
- **Masking Curriculum:** Training progresses through a three-phase curriculum: initially all keypoints are visible, then Bernoulli masking is introduced with annealing probabilities (1.0 to 0.4), and finally, a mix of semantic deployment modes (e.g., torso-only, wrists-only) is sampled. This curriculum prepares the encoder for robust operation under diverse, sparse keypoint observations.

**Stage 3: Task-Specific RL Finetuning in Latent Space**
The final stage expands the controller's capabilities beyond direct motion tracking to specific downstream tasks:
- **Residual Latent-Space Reinforcement Learning:** The Stage 1 decoder and Stage 2 keypoint encoder are kept frozen. Instead of directly modifying these pretrained components, a lightweight residual corrector (g_ξ) is learned using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Action Space:** The RL agent's action is the latent vector `z`. The frozen decoder `D(z, s_p)` acts as a motor prior, translating this latent action into physically plausible joint commands for the robot.
- **Corrector Architecture:** The corrector g_ξ takes the same tokenized inputs as the Stage 2 encoder (keypoint tokens, proprioception frames, and the frozen encoder's latent `ˆz_enc`). For tasks like obstacle reaching, additional task-specific observations (e.g., obstacle bounding boxes) are appended.
- **Small-Gain Initialization:** The corrector is initialized with a small gain (output projection gain of 0.01), ensuring that at initialization, the residual correction `∆z` is approximately zero. This means the policy initially reproduces the distilled Stage 2 behavior, providing a safe and stable starting point for RL and preserving the pretrained motion prior.
- **Downstream Tasks:** The framework is evaluated on tasks such as command-conditioned locomotion, obstacle reaching (requiring adaptive whole-body strategies like bending or squatting), and in-air writing (demanding fine-grained end-effector control and coordination with locomotion).
- **Reward Composition:** Task-specific rewards (e.g., position/velocity tracking, obstacle avoidance) are combined with penalties for undesired contacts, high action rates, joint limit violations, acceleration, and torque.

**Simulation and Hardware:**
All training is conducted in the Isaac Lab simulation environment, utilizing parallel environments with mixed flat and rough terrain. A simulation timestep of 0.005 seconds (200 Hz) and a control frequency of 50 Hz are used. Domain randomization, including friction, restitution, joint jitter, and random push impulses, is applied to enhance sim-to-real transfer. Real-world experiments are performed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, with an Apple Vision Pro headset used for sparse keypoint capture and teleoperation input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AnyBody addressed four key questions, demonstrating its capabilities across quantitative tracking, open-ended generation, real-world teleoperation, and downstream task adaptation.

**Q1: Quantitative Tracking Performance with Sparse Keypoint Subsets**
AnyBody demonstrated consistent and robust tracking performance across various sparse keypoint configurations. As detailed in Table 1:
- **High Success Rates:** The policy maintained high tracking success rates, ranging from 93.7% for single-wrist tracking to 97.6% for full-body (5-point) control. This indicates stability even under highly under-constrained input.
- **Coordinated Whole-Body Motion:** The average Point-of-Interest (POI) position error remained consistently around 10 cm across most masking modes (e.g., 10.90 cm for full body, 10.79 cm for single wrist, 8.34 cm for torso only). This suggests that AnyBody can effectively infer and synthesize coordinated whole-body motions from sparse user guidance without significant degradation in overall tracking accuracy.
- **Comparable to Joint-Space Tracking:** While not a direct apples-to-apples comparison due to differences in input information, AnyBody's performance was in a range comparable to joint-space tracking policies, particularly when considering its ability to operate with significantly less input data.

**Q2: Open-Ended Generative Analysis and Generalization**
Beyond quantitative metrics, AnyBody exhibited strong generative capabilities when commanded by manually specified sparse keypoint trajectories (Figure 3):
- **Diverse Behavior Synthesis:** The system robustly followed a wide range of simple, sparse commands, synthesizing physically plausible and coordinated whole-body behaviors. This included directional locomotion, arm swings, arm raises, bending, and squatting, even with single-keypoint control. These results suggest that sparse, task-relevant keypoint trajectories are sufficient to induce a variety of complex whole-body motions through the learned latent representation.
- **Lightweight Interface:** A simple trajectory-creation interface was developed, facilitating rapid qualitative evaluation of the controller's capabilities.
- **Identified Limitations:** The system encountered limitations when commanded trajectories corresponded to poses rarely present in the training corpus (e.g., extremely low reaching motions) or were significantly out-of-distribution (e.g., large-scale in-air handwriting). These specific failure modes informed the need for downstream adaptation.

**Q3: Real-World Humanoid Teleoperation**
AnyBody demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer, enabling practical real-world teleoperation on a Unitree G1 humanoid:
- **Flexible VR Teleoperation:** Using an Apple Vision Pro headset for sparse keypoint capture, operators could teleoperate the G1 humanoid using one or both wrist keypoints under various control configurations (Figure 5, 7-9).
- **Stable Real-World Execution:** Despite receiving only sparse upper-body guidance, the policy synthesized coordinated full-body motions that maintained stability during real-world execution. This included tasks like raising/lowering arms, navigating around a table, performing deep squats to avoid obstacles, and torso-guided locomotion.
- **Simplified Data Collection:** The flexible and intuitive control interface for real robots substantially simplified the process of collecting loco-manipulation demonstrations.

**Q4: Latent Space for Downstream RL Finetuning**
The research confirmed that the learned latent space captures a rich distribution of physically plausible whole-body motions and serves as an effective motion prior for efficient downstream adaptation via reinforcement learning:
- **Expanded Capability Coverage:** Latent-space RL significantly expanded AnyBody's capabilities beyond direct motion following (Figure 6). For tasks like obstacle-reach, the controller adapted its motion prior to discover task-aware whole-body strategies (e.g., leaning, bending, squatting, reaching into containers) to avoid collisions and achieve targets.
- **Significant Improvement in Success Rates (Table 2):**
 - For obstacle-reach tasks (Open, Barrier, Low-clearance, Container), success rates dramatically improved from a range of 3.04%-54.68% without RL finetuning to 95.56%-99.47% with RL finetuning.
 - For the wrist-writing task, the success rate increased from 0.00% without RL to 97.87% with RL, demonstrating the ability to synthesize fine-grained end-effector trajectories and coordinate locomotion for large writing motions.
- **Efficient Adaptation:** The rapid and stable convergence of latent-space RL across these diverse tasks indicates that the process primarily involves composing and adapting existing motion modes within the learned latent manifold, rather than learning new whole-body behaviors from scratch. This efficient adaptation successfully mitigated the out-of-distribution failure cases observed in Q2.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AnyBody framework represents a notable advancement in humanoid control, offering several significant implications for robotics research and application:

- **Enhanced Flexibility and Intuition in Control:** By enabling whole-body humanoid control from arbitrary subsets of body keypoints, AnyBody provides a more flexible and intuitive interface for human operators. This removes the constraint of fixed control modes, allowing users to interact with the robot through task-relevant keypoints, which can simplify complex teleoperation scenarios and reduce cognitive load.
- **Scalable Data Collection for Loco-Manipulation:** The reduction in reliance on expensive and cumbersome full-body motion capture systems is a critical contribution. This allows for more scalable and accessible data collection of humanoid loco-manipulation demonstrations, fostering the creation of larger and more diverse datasets for policy learning.
- **Maintenance of Coordinated Whole-Body Behavior:** Unlike hierarchical control frameworks that decouple body parts, AnyBody ensures the synthesis of coordinated, physically plausible, and human-like whole-body motions. This capability is essential for humanoids performing tasks in unstructured environments where dynamic balance and integrated body movements are paramount.
- **Retargeting-Free Motion Following:** The ability to directly follow human motion from generated human keypoints, entirely bypassing conventional motion-retargeting pipelines, streamlines the process of transferring human intent to robotic action. This can reduce errors and simplify the development workflow for humanoid behaviors.
- **Reusable Motion Prior and Efficient Skill Acquisition:** The learned shared spherical latent motion representation serves as a robust and reusable motor prior. This prior allows for efficient specialization to downstream tasks through latent-space reinforcement learning, where new skills can be acquired by adapting existing motion modes rather than learning from scratch. This significantly improves the efficiency of learning new, complex behaviors.
- **Bridging Research Domains:** AnyBody contributes a step towards more general-purpose humanoid control systems that integrate aspects of motion teleoperation, imitation learning, and autonomous skill learning within a unified framework. This integration has the potential to accelerate advancements across these interconnected fields.

**Potential Impact:**
The capabilities demonstrated by AnyBody could have a substantial impact on the development and deployment of humanoid robots in various domains, including:
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Enabling more intuitive and flexible control could facilitate closer and more effective collaboration between humans and humanoids in manufacturing, healthcare, and service industries.
- **Automated Skill Generation:** The framework's ability to synthesize diverse whole-body behaviors from sparse commands, coupled with efficient downstream adaptation, could lead to more efficient and scalable methods for teaching robots new skills.
- **Robotics in Unstructured Environments:** Humanoids could operate more effectively in complex, dynamic, and human-centered environments by adapting to partial and varied commands while maintaining stability and coordination.

**Limitations (as acknowledged by the authors):**
Despite its advancements, the research identifies areas for future work:
- **Limited Coverage for Edge-Case Motions:** The controller may still struggle with certain highly uncommon or out-of-distribution behaviors due to limitations in the training corpus coverage.
- **Absence of Dexterous Hand Control:** The current platform does not include dexterous hand control, limiting its application to fine-grained manipulation tasks requiring intricate hand movements. Extending the framework to humanoids with dexterous hands is an important future direction.

These limitations present clear pathways for continued research, further enhancing the capabilities and generality of whole-body humanoid control.
