# Cross-Modal Redundancy and the Geometry of Vision-Language Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced the Iso-Energy Assumption and an Aligned Sparse Autoencoder (SAE-A) to dissect the geometric structure of Vision-Language Model embeddings. This framework successfully disentangles cross-modal representations into distinct bimodal and unimodal components, enabling the elimination of the modality gap and precise semantic edits without impacting model performance.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack a principled understanding of how they achieve cross-modal alignment within their embedding spaces.
- Concept-based interpretability methods on VLMs often show a "modality gap," where extracted concepts activate exclusively for either image or text.
- Interventions to reduce the modality gap typically degrade VLM cross-modal performance, highlighting a knowledge gap regarding shared vs. modality-specific features.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Iso-Energy Assumption==, positing that genuinely shared concepts in VLMs should have invariant average activation energy across modalities.
- Develops an ==Aligned Sparse Autoencoder (SAE-A)== that incorporates the Iso-Energy Assumption as a soft regularization term during training.
- Validates the method on synthetic data and applies it to ==six foundational dual-encoder VLMs==, employing a suite of multimodality-sensitive metrics to decompose embeddings into bimodal and unimodal subspaces.

## Results

- SAE-A achieves **comparable** reconstruction fidelity to standard SAEs while showing **superior** performance on multimodality-sensitive metrics, including an **order-of-magnitude increase** in Functional Alignment ("rho").
- Filtering unimodal atoms effectively **collapses the modality gap**, merging image and text distributions **without degrading** VLM retrieval or zero-shot performance, surpassing prior "embedding shift" methods.
- Semantic vector arithmetic restricted to the bimodal subspace ensures in-distribution edits and **improves** retrieval performance on benchmarks such as FashionIQ compared to conventional methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Iso-Energy Assumption effectively guides concept learning, allowing the SAE-A to correctly identify bimodal concepts exhibiting consistent activation energy across modalities.
> - VLM embedding spaces feature a geometric decomposition into sparse bimodal atoms, responsible for cross-modal alignment, and unimodal atoms, which hold modality-specific information and explain the modality gap.
> - High-energy unimodal features are largely responsible for the observed modality gap, acting as modality-specific biases rather than contributing to cross-modal semantics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **interpretable AI (XAI)**, specifically focusing on **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**. VLMs like CLIP and SigLIP have achieved remarkable success in aligning images and text within a shared embedding space, powering diverse applications from visual question answering to embodied AI. However, a significant gap remains: despite their empirical prowess, a principled understanding of their internal organization and how they achieve cross-modal alignment is largely absent.

The paper traces the evolution of interpretability tools:
- **Early Efforts (Attribution Methods):** Focused on "where" a model attends (e.g., saliency maps). These methods, while useful, often fall short of explaining "what" abstract concepts a model learns.
- **Recent Shift (Concept-Based Methods):** Aim to extract meaningful, human-understandable features or "concepts" that models implicitly compute. This typically involves **dictionary learning** problems, often operationalized through **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**, building on the **Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH)**, which posits that activations are sparse combinations of latent conceptual directions. SAEs have shown promise in uncovering semantic structure in both vision and language models.

However, applying SAEs to VLMs has revealed a puzzling phenomenon: the **modality gap**. Despite being trained for cross-modal alignment, extracted concepts often segregate by modality, activating exclusively for either image or text inputs. Prior work has described this geometrically (conical structure) or through training dynamics (contrastive loss, mismatched pairs), but these accounts have not adequately explained the phenomenon at the level of shared concepts. Crucially, previous attempts to "fix" or reduce the modality gap (e.g., shifting means, projecting out specific canonical directions) have often led to *decreased cross-modal performance*, indicating a poor understanding of which components genuinely contribute to alignment versus modality-specific information.

This paper addresses this fundamental challenge by proposing a **generative model-grounded framework** and introducing the **Iso-Energy Assumption**. It posits that genuinely shared, cross-modal concepts should exhibit invariant average activation energy across modalities. This assumption serves as a novel inductive bias to guide the learning of SAEs, aiming to *reveal* an underlying, interpretable geometric decomposition of VLM embedding spaces where previous methods have either failed to disentangle or degraded model fidelity. In essence, it aims to provide both a deeper understanding of the modality gap at the concept level and a practical method to manipulate representations without compromising performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **investigate the geometric structure of cross-modal embeddings** within Vision-Language Models (VLMs). More specifically, the authors seek to answer: "How do VLMs organize the alignment between visual and textual semantics, and what principles govern this shared representational space?"

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:
- **Bridging the Interpretability Gap:** Despite the empirical success of VLMs across numerous applications, there is a significant lack of a principled understanding of their internal workings, particularly how they achieve cross-modal alignment. Enhancing this understanding is crucial for building more robust, reliable, and trustworthy AI systems.
- **Addressing the Modality Gap Puzzle:** Existing concept-based interpretability methods, like Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), applied to VLMs reveal a "modality gap" where learned concepts often activate exclusively for one modality. This contradicts the VLM's goal of cross-modal alignment and creates a puzzling lack of truly bimodal (shared) concepts. The authors are motivated to explain this phenomenon at the conceptual level.
- **Developing Actionable Interpretability Tools:** The ultimate goal is not just to understand but to enable "actionable interventions." Current approaches to mitigating the modality gap often come at the cost of reduced VLM performance. The authors aim to develop a framework that not only reveals interpretable structures but also allows for direct manipulation of representations (e.g., closing the modality gap, performing semantic arithmetic) without degrading the model's core capabilities.
- **Formalizing Cross-Modal Redundancy:** The research is motivated by the intuition that if a concept is truly shared across modalities, it should manifest with redundant statistical traces in each domain. Formalizing this intuition through the "Iso-Energy Assumption" provides a concrete, testable criterion for identifying such bimodal concepts, which can then be used as an inductive bias for concept recovery.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to contribute to both the fundamental understanding of VLMs and the development of practical tools for their interpretability and control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around introducing and operationalizing the **Iso-Energy Assumption** within a Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) framework to analyze Vision-Language Model (VLM) embeddings.

#### 3.1. The Iso-Energy Assumption

The foundational premise is that if a concept is truly shared across modalities, it should exhibit the same average activation "energy" (defined as the average squared activation) regardless of the input modality (image or text). This provides a domain-agnostic inductive bias to guide concept learning. The authors formalize this by defining a "Multimodal Concept Generative Process" where latent concept vectors are rendered through domain-specific generators, and VLM encoders act as partial inverses. The Iso-Energy Assumption is then defined for a learned encoder-SAE composition, stating that the second moment of each concept coordinate should be domain-invariant for shared concepts.

#### 3.2. Operationalization with Aligned Sparse Autoencoder (SAE-A)

To operationalize this assumption, the authors propose an **Aligned Sparse Autoencoder (SAE-A)**.
- **Base Architecture:** They adopt a **Matching Pursuit (MP) Sparse Autoencoder** as their base. MP SAEs enforce ℓ0 sparsity through sequential residual updates, aligning well with the sparse generative model they hypothesize and showing strong empirical reconstruction performance. This choice is discussed in Appendix A, distinguishing it from ReLU, JumpReLU, and TopK SAEs which introduce different inductive biases.
- **Alignment Regularizer:** The Iso-Energy Assumption is incorporated as a soft regularizer into the standard SAE training loss. Given ℓ2-normalized codes Z(d) and Z(d') from two different modalities, the alignment loss (L_align) is defined as the negative average trace of their outer product: `L_align = - (1/b) * Tr(Z(d) * Z(d')^T)`. This loss encourages the activations of the same atom to maintain similar strength (and direction, due to normalization) across modalities. The overall loss becomes `L_SAE-A = L_SAE + β * L_align`, where β is a small weighting coefficient (tuned via log-sweep, typically around 10^-4, as detailed in Appendix B). This ensures the inductive bias gently nudges the dictionary without compromising reconstruction.

#### 3.3. Validation with Controlled Toy Data

Before applying to large-scale VLMs, the approach is validated on synthetic data with known ground truth.
- **Synthetic DGP:** Two data-generating processes are constructed to mimic CLIP-like cosine similarity statistics while providing exact control over unimodal and bimodal atoms. Parameters (τ1 for cross-modal alignment of bimodal atoms, τ2 for average paired image-text similarity) allow for scenarios where Iso-Energy holds (τ1=1) or is violated (τ1!=1).
- **Metrics for Validation:** Recovery is assessed using Wasserstein distance (W) between learned and true atoms and mean matching accuracy (mma) of usage patterns.
- **Results:** When Iso-Energy is violated, both SAE and SAE-A perform equally. Crucially, when Iso-Energy holds, the standard SAE fails, while SAE-A succeeds, demonstrating that the inductive bias is neutral when unnecessary and decisive when appropriate.

#### 3.4. Empirical Evaluation on Foundational VLMs

The framework is then applied to activations from six foundational dual-encoder VLMs: CLIP (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14), OpenCLIP, OpenCLIP-L, SigLIP, and SigLIP2.
- **Training:** Both standard SAEs and SAE-A are trained with identical hyperparameters on a subset of 1 million LAION embeddings.
- **Metrics:** A comprehensive suite of metrics is used (detailed in Appendix E):
 - **Reconstruction Fidelity:** MSE and R^2 (standard).
 - **Multimodality-Sensitive Metrics:** Designed to probe the quality of the learned multimodal structure:
 - **Probing Accuracy (p_acc):** Measures how well the geometry of dictionary atoms reflects modality structure (unimodal atoms as classifiers for domain membership, bimodal as domain-agnostic).
 - **Functional Alignment (ρ):** Instance-level ratio of alignment explained by bimodal vs. unimodal features via co-activation patterns.
 - **Functional and Distributional Agreement (FDA):** Distribution-level analogue of ρ, checking consistency across large batches using optimal transport concepts.
 - **Interventional Robustness (δ_r):** Measures the change in retrieval recall after ablating unimodal atoms, testing their necessity for cross-modal information.

#### 3.5. Actionable Interventions

Finally, the paper demonstrates the practical utility of the discovered structure through targeted interventions:
- **Closing the Modality Gap:** Removing unimodal atoms using a binary mask to generate new embeddings (ẽA) and evaluating its effect on distribution overlap (using KNN distance histograms, as an OOD metric) and retrieval performance.
- **Semantic Vector Arithmetic:** Restricting vector arithmetic to the bimodal subspace for tasks like relative caption-based image retrieval (e.g., in FashionIQ dataset) and evaluating if queries remain "in-distribution."

This systematic approach, from theoretical assumption and algorithmic implementation to rigorous validation and practical intervention, forms the backbone of the research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence for a novel, interpretable geometric decomposition within VLM embedding spaces, unlocked by the Iso-Energy Assumption and the Aligned Sparse Autoencoder (SAE-A).

#### 4.1. Validation of the Iso-Energy Inductive Bias

- **Sanity Checks:** On controlled synthetic datasets with known ground truth, the SAE-A successfully recovers the underlying bimodal concept structure when the Iso-Energy Assumption holds (i.e., when true bimodal atoms exhibit consistent energy across modalities). In contrast, a standard SAE fails in this scenario. When the Iso-Energy Assumption is violated, both SAE and SAE-A perform equally, confirming that the regularizer does not hallucinate non-existent bimodal atoms.

#### 4.2. Superiority of SAE-A on Foundational VLMs

- **Reconstruction Fidelity:** SAE-A maintains comparable reconstruction quality (MSE, R^2) to a standard SAE across all six foundational VLMs (CLIP, OpenCLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2). This indicates that the alignment penalty does not compromise the model's ability to reconstruct the original VLM activations.
- **Enhanced Multimodality Metrics:** Despite similar reconstruction, SAE-A consistently and significantly outperforms standard SAEs on all proposed multimodality-sensitive metrics:
 - **Functional Alignment (ρ):** Increases by more than an order of magnitude, strongly indicating that cross-modal alignment is almost entirely driven by bimodal atoms in SAE-A's learned dictionary.
 - **Functional and Distributional Agreement (FDA):** Improves substantially, confirming that the functional role of bimodal features (supporting alignment) is consistent with their geometric organization at a distributional level.
 - **Probing Accuracy (p_acc):** Modestly improves, reflecting a more distinct and accurate geometric separation of unimodal and bimodal features.
 - **Interventional Robustness (δ_r):** Remains small, demonstrating that unimodal atoms can be safely ablated without harming retrieval performance. This causally confirms they do not carry essential cross-modal information.

#### 4.3. Revealed Geometric Decomposition

The application of SAE-A reveals a clear and actionable structure in VLM embedding spaces:
- **(i) Sparse Bimodal Atoms:** These concepts carry the *entire cross-modal alignment signal*. They consistently activate for the same concept across both modalities (e.g., "colors," "objects," "actions").
- **(ii) Unimodal Atoms:** These concepts carry *modality-specific information* and *fully explain the modality gap*. They often reflect idiosyncratic signals like poor image cropping artifacts or specific textual patterns (e.g., "name patterns" in text), contributing little to cross-modal semantics.
- **Energy Distribution:** The majority of features are bimodal with moderate energy. A small subset of unimodal features, particularly high-energy ones, concentrate disproportionately high energy and act as modality-specific biases, largely responsible for the observed modality gap.
- **Geometric Organization:** Low-dimensional projections show clear separation into three clusters: image-only, text-only, and bimodal. Unimodal atoms align with the modality cones of the embedding space, while bimodal atoms occupy a modality-agnostic subspace *orthogonal* to these unimodal directions.
- **Information Carrier:** Crucially, the work shows that cross-modal information is carried by these *shared atoms*, directly contradicting the observations in some prior work that found concept dictionaries often segregated by modality (Papadimitriou et al., 2025).

#### 4.4. Actionable Interventions

The discovered structure enables practical and effective interventions:
- **(iii) Collapsing the Modality Gap:** By simply filtering out unimodal atoms (applying a binary mask), the modality gap is nearly eliminated, merging image and text distributions significantly more effectively than previous "embedding shift" baselines, and critically, *without harming retrieval or zero-shot performance*.
- **(iv) In-Distribution Semantic Edits and Improved Retrieval:** Restricting vector arithmetic operations (e.g., `Image_source + (Text_diff)`) to the bimodal subspace prevents the query from drifting "out-of-distribution." This yields in-distribution edits, meaning the modified embeddings remain semantically coherent and interpretable, and *improves retrieval performance* on benchmarks like FashionIQ compared to classical vector arithmetic that incorporates unimodal noise.

In summary, the SAE-A successfully disentangles multimodal representations into distinct bimodal (shared) and unimodal (modality-specific) components, offering an interpretable and actionable decomposition that directly addresses the long-standing challenge of the modality gap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the understanding, design, and application of Vision-Language Models, and for the broader field of interpretable AI.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Interpretability and Model Understanding

The most immediate impact is a **principled and actionable understanding** of the VLM embedding space geometry. By introducing the Iso-Energy Assumption, the authors provide a clear criterion to distinguish between truly shared concepts and modality-specific biases. This resolves the puzzling "modality gap" observed in previous SAE applications, clarifying *what* information contributes to cross-modal alignment versus modality-specific signals. This deeper mechanistic understanding is invaluable for researchers and practitioners aiming to demystify complex neural network representations.

#### 5.2. Improved VLM Architecture Design

The findings suggest that explicit incorporation of inductive biases like Iso-Energy during VLM training could lead to more robust, interpretable, and potentially more efficient architectures from the outset. If models are encouraged to learn disentangled bimodal and unimodal features during training, future VLMs could naturally exhibit a clearer, more structured latent space, reducing the need for post-hoc interpretability methods.

#### 5.3. Practical Applications and Interventions

The demonstrated "actionable interventions" highlight the practical utility of this work:
- **Modality Gap Mitigation:** The ability to collapse the modality gap without harming performance is a significant advancement. This could lead to more unified multimodal systems where cross-modal comparisons are more direct and reliable, improving tasks like cross-modal retrieval, multimodal classification, and robust zero-shot learning.
- **Precise Semantic Control:** Restricting vector arithmetic to the bimodal subspace enables more precise and "in-distribution" semantic edits. This has direct implications for creative applications (e.g., text-guided image manipulation), controllable generation, and interactive AI systems where users want to apply conceptual changes without introducing irrelevant modality-specific artifacts or drifting out of the meaningful data manifold. This improves the robustness and interpretability of common techniques like "concept arithmetic."

#### 5.4. Contribution to Interpretability Methodology

The paper champions a "hypothesis-driven approach to interpretability," arguing that inductive biases, when properly aligned with task-relevant objectives, can illuminate hidden mechanisms. This perspective encourages the development of theoretically motivated interpretability methods rather than purely empirical ones, pushing the field towards more rigorous and insightful analysis of AI systems.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Directions

Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges several limitations, which also suggest fertile ground for future research:
- **Sensitivity of β:** The weighting coefficient for the alignment penalty (β) is critical, and a more principled calibration method (beyond simple sweeps) is needed.
- **Reconstruction Dependency:** The analysis is conducted on SAE reconstructions, not the original VLM embeddings, which might constrain the generalizability of performance claims to the autoencoder's reconstruction regime.
- **Scope to Dual-Encoders:** The experiments are limited to dual-encoder VLMs. Investigating whether similar structural invariants hold in models with cross-attention mechanisms (e.g., transformers with decoder-encoder attention) or generative training objectives (e.g., diffusion models) is an important open question.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the understanding and control of Vision-Language Models. By introducing a novel theoretical premise and demonstrating its practical utility, it paves the way for more transparent, robust, and controllable multimodal AI systems.
