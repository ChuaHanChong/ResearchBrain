# MoWorld: A Flash World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> MoWorld introduces a 'Flash World Model' designed for real-time interaction and cinematic visual quality, achieving up to 50 frames per second on Neural Processing Units. The model demonstrates significantly reduced inference costs, operating at 30-50% of existing World Models, by integrating a 3D-native data engine, efficient pre-training, and NPU-optimized inference.

## Problem

- World Models face challenges in practical real-world deployment due to high computational costs and slow inference speeds, often falling below the 30 FPS threshold needed for fluid interactive experiences.
- Many existing World Models rely on high-end GPUs, limiting their scalability and accessibility for widespread adoption on more common hardware like NPUs.
- Current training data for World Models often lacks explicit geometric consistency and controllable signals, hindering robust understanding and simulation of the physical world.

## Method

- **MoWorld** is an end-to-end co-design framework that optimizes data generation, model training, distillation, and efficient inference specifically for Neural Processing Units (NPUs).
- A scalable ==3D-native data engine== curates high-quality, geometrically consistent training data by linking visual appearance, camera motion, and controllable signals.
- ==Autoregressive distillation== converts the pretrained bidirectional model into a few-step student for real-time generation, employing ==ODE-initialization-free== and ==self-forcing== techniques.

## Results

- Achieved up to **50 FPS** real-time interaction on Neural Processing Units (NPUs), outperforming existing World Models in inference speed.
- Demonstrated a **30%-50%** reduction in average inference cost compared to current World Models.
- Attained leading scores on VBench-I2V and in-house camera/world-model datasets, showing strong performance in subject consistency, background consistency, and image-to-video consistency while maintaining visual quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving real-time, high-frame-rate (30+ FPS) world simulation requires a holistic co-design approach spanning data, algorithms, systems, and hardware, particularly when targeting NPUs.
> - Geometric-aware data generation and explicit camera control injection are critical for robust long-horizon consistency and controllable world model outputs.
> - Efficient autoregressive distillation, combined with memory management strategies like history context selection, enables large models to perform continuous, few-step generation for interactive applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of MoWorld is situated within the broader evolution of foundation models in artificial intelligence. Following the advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), the research landscape is shifting towards models capable of understanding and simulating the physical world—known as World Models. These models aim to learn the underlying dynamics of how environments evolve under changes and agent actions, enabling tasks such as predicting future states, simulating interactions, reasoning over outcomes, and planning long-horizon behaviors. World Models are considered foundational for embodied intelligence.

Existing efforts in World Models have largely focused on scaling model capabilities. However, MoWorld identifies a gap in this landscape: the practicality and inference efficiency required for real-world deployment. The paper introduces the concept of "Flash World Models" as a category of real-time world models capable of sustaining generation at 30 frames per second (FPS) or higher, a threshold considered essential for fluid interactive experiences in computer graphics and autonomous systems. The authors observe that many current World Models struggle to meet this real-time performance criterion, particularly without reliance on high-end Graphics Processing Units (GPUs).

MoWorld distinguishes itself by adopting an end-to-end co-design approach that optimizes across data generation, model training, distillation, and efficient inference, specifically targeting Neural Processing Units (NPUs). This emphasis on joint optimization of capability and cost, alongside real-time performance and NPU compatibility, positions MoWorld as an effort to bridge the gap between advanced World Model research and scalable, practical real-world applications in latency-sensitive domains like embodied intelligence and autonomous driving. Furthermore, its reliance on a 3D-native data engine, accumulated from large-scale 3D vision and generative modeling pipelines, represents a departure from approaches that primarily use large-scale video corpora, aiming for geometrically consistent training data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop MoWorld, a cost-effective yet high-performance "Flash World Model" that offers real-time interaction with cinematic visual quality, without requiring high-end GPUs. This model is supported by an end-to-end framework encompassing data generation, pre-training, distillation, and efficient inference, enabling up to 50 FPS performance.

The motivation for MoWorld stems from several considerations:

- **Bridging Capability and Practicality:** While significant progress has been made in scaling World Model capabilities, the authors argue that the next phase of development requires joint optimization of model capability, computational efficiency, deployment cost, and real-time inference performance. This is crucial for enabling widespread adoption beyond research laboratories into real-world intelligent systems.
- **Enabling Responsive Autonomous Systems:** High-frame-rate inference (defined as 30 FPS or higher) is identified as an essential requirement for latency-sensitive closed-loop applications, such as embodied intelligence and autonomous driving. Slow generation in existing World Models fundamentally limits interaction, planning, and control in such systems.
- **Reducing Deployment Barriers:** The prohibitive computational and hardware costs associated with deploying large World Models hinder their practical application. MoWorld aims to reduce both training and inference costs, making these advanced models more accessible and viable for large-scale real-world deployment, particularly by targeting Neural Processing Units (NPUs) which are increasingly integrated into modern edge devices.
- **Addressing Data Limitations:** Existing World Models often rely on large-scale video corpora that may lack the explicit geometric consistency and controllable signals necessary for robust world modeling. MoWorld is motivated to develop a scalable 3D-native data engine that provides geometrically consistent training data across diverse environments, improving the foundation for World Model learning.
- **Holistic Optimization:** Instead of optimizing individual components in isolation, the research is motivated by a vision of holistic data–algorithm–system–hardware co-design. This involves jointly optimizing every stage of the World Model lifecycle to maximize throughput while preserving generation quality and minimizing costs.

In summary, the core motivation is to transform World Models from computationally intensive research prototypes into practical, deployable systems capable of real-time, cost-effective interaction with the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MoWorld's methodology is built upon an end-to-end framework that integrates scalable data generation, efficient model training, accelerated inference, and deployment-oriented system optimization. This approach is structured around four tightly coupled pillars:

#### 3.1. Geometric Aware Data Engine (Section 2)

MoWorld employs a scalable 3D-native data engine to curate high-quality training data. This engine is designed to explicitly link visual appearance, camera motion, and controllable signals, distinguishing it from conventional video generation data that often uses loosely paired video-text clips.

The data generation pipeline comprises four stages:
1. **Data Collection:** Combines open-domain videos for visual coverage and game-scene data for explicit control and geometry.
2. **Geometry Completion and Quality Control:** Recovers missing camera conditions (e.g., poses) and filters out samples with unreliable geometry or visual content.
3. **Vision-Language Model (VLM) Annotation:** Assigns scene-level descriptions to data, ensuring that camera motion is kept separate from text conditions.
4. **Offline Feature Precomputation and Caching:** Stores processed modalities (visual features, geometry features, text features) in a format directly consumable during large-scale training, reducing runtime overhead.

The protocol extends prior geometry-centric data construction by annotating dense 3D point clouds for each scene, supporting downstream spatial reasoning and reconstruction tasks. The data is acquired and processed in-house, ensuring quality, geometric consistency, and distribution control.

#### 3.2. Model Pretraining (Section 3)

The pretraining stage adapts a general video generation model into a camera-controlled, long-horizon generative framework. The model is conditioned on an initial frame, text prompts, and camera trajectory signals, learning to predict future frames that align with these controls while maintaining visual fidelity and temporal consistency.

- **Backbone and Objective:** The model builds upon the Wan2.2-A14B Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) video foundation model, trained on an NPU cluster. The objective is a standard diffusion training loss, where the model learns to denoise a latent representation of future frames, conditioned on the initial frame, text, camera signals, and diffusion timestep. A camera adapter is added to the Video DiT backbone to model camera control.
- **Camera Control Injection:** Per-frame camera intrinsics and extrinsics are converted into dense geometric representations using Plücker coordinates. These 6D representations (ray direction and moment vector) are then projected into the visual token space by a Camera Adapter and injected at the patch-token level, coupling geometric consistency with video generation.
- **Curriculum Cross-Frame Training:** A progressive learning strategy is used, starting with low-cost short clips (125F/250F) to establish appearance generation and basic camera control. Training then moves to medium clips (500F/1000F) for temporal consistency, and finally to long clips (2000F) for spatial memory and global world-state preservation. This approach improves scalability and reduces training cost.
- **Efficient Training System on NPUs:**
 - **Resource Organization and Input Caching:** Training resources align with Wan2.2's high-/low-noise MoE partitioning on two NPU pools. Multimodal signals are offline encoded and cached to stabilize data throughput and improve loading efficiency.
 - **Distributed Parallelism:** Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP) is used for sharding model parameters, gradients, and optimizer states.
 - **Sequence Parallelism (SP) for Long-Clip Video Training:** For long sequences (e.g., 2000F), Ulysses SP is adopted, partitioning attention heads across NPUs. For even longer contexts, Unified Sequence Parallelism (USP) generalizes this to token-level partitioning, distributing sequence length across ranks for finer-grained workload balancing and efficiency.

#### 3.3. Autoregressive Distillation (Section 4)

This stage converts the pretrained bidirectional teacher model into a few-step autoregressive (AR) student model for real-time interaction. It aims for few-step generation and continuous autoregressive generation.

- **History Context Selection:** To manage memory growth during AR generation, MoWorld selects recent, initial, and camera-related latent frames from a History Bank (which stores latent frames, not KV cache). This retrieval strategy reintroduces relevant scene states, reducing geometric and identity drift.
- **Autoregressive Flow Matching Pretraining:** An intermediate adaptation stage constructs prefix history and boundary anchors from ground-truth latents, enabling the student to learn history reading and cross-chunk boundary alignment. Flow Matching supervision is applied only to the current chunk, adapting bidirectional weights to a causal chunk-wise interface and avoiding costly ODE-initialization or multi-step teacher sampling.
- **ODE-Initialization-Free Distillation (Self-Forcing):** MoWorld employs a distribution-matching approach similar to DMD-style methods. It alternates between a "Fake Phase" where a Fake Model learns the velocity field of the current student-generated distribution, and a "Student Phase" where the student is guided by a proxy gradient derived from the difference between the teacher's (real distribution) and Fake Model's (student distribution) velocity fields. This trains the student on its own AR rollouts while leveraging full-video distributional feedback from the teacher, enabling efficient few-step denoising.

#### 3.4. Real-Time Inference (Section 5)

Inference is optimized for low-memory consumption and low-latency on NPUs, addressing pipeline, parallelism, and kernel levels.

- **Pipeline Level Optimization:**
 - **On-demand Module Loading:** Encoders (visual, text, camera) are executed once and then released from memory, allowing most memory to be allocated to the DiT blocks and VAE decoder.
 - **Autoregressive Scheme:** Previously generated frame latents are recycled as historical context, and global temporal positions are encoded using Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE). Multiple frames are generated per step and buffered for streaming.
- **Parallelism Level Optimization:**
 - **Hierarchical Sequence Parallelism:** For multi-NPU deployment, attention heads are partitioned (head-level parallelism), and within each group, input tokens are further split (token-level sequence parallelism). The visual decoder is deployed on a dedicated NPU, while remaining NPUs handle DiT computation.
 - **Inter-Device Communication:** Ring-based communication is used within head partition groups, and AllToAll topology for cross-group communication, implemented via HCCL collective operations.
- **Kernel Level Optimization:**
 - **Dynamic Mixed-Precision Quantization:** Model weights and activations are loaded in BF16 and then quantized to INT8 precision via a one-step kernel warm-up, reducing memory by approximately 2x for DiT blocks. Computation uses INT8xINT8 accumulation to produce INT32 results, dequantized to BF16 outputs.
 - **HBM-efficient End-to-end Attention:** RMSNorm is applied to activations before QKV projection. An NPU-fused attention kernel performs QKV projection, tiling, and online softmax computation as a single HBM transaction, reducing memory movement and improving throughput.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MoWorld demonstrates leading performance across quantitative and qualitative evaluations, showcasing its effectiveness in real-time, camera-controllable world simulation and diverse applications.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results (Section 6.1)

- **VBench-I2V Benchmark:** MoWorld achieved the highest aggregate "Quality" and "Average" scores compared to existing methods like CameraCtrl, SEVA, and Lingbot.
 - It demonstrated strong performance in Subject Consistency (SC), Background Consistency (BC), Aesthetic Quality (AQ), Imaging Quality (IQ), Image-to-Video Subject Consistency (I2V-S), and Image-to-Video Background Consistency (I2V-B).
 - Specifically, MoWorld's high I2V-S and I2V-B scores indicate reliable preservation of subject and background information from input first frames, which is crucial for world models initialized from images.
- **In-house Camera/World-Model Dataset:** On a diverse dataset collected by the authors, MoWorld again achieved the best "Quality" and "Average" scores.
 - It maintained high visual quality and consistency even when incorporating explicit camera-trajectory conditions, indicating its ability to generate viewpoint changes without sacrificing subject consistency, background stability, or image-conditioned preservation.
- **Inference Cost and Speed:** MoWorld's average inference cost was reported to be 30%-50% of existing World Models. It achieved up to 50 FPS real-time interaction on Neural Processing Units (NPUs).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results (Section 6.2)

- MoWorld generated continuous and interactive future visual states across a variety of real and virtual scenes, including indoor libraries, Gothic halls, classical streets, pyramid ruins, shopping malls, and residential areas.
- The model consistently maintained high visual fidelity, scene structure, material appearance, and lighting style during controlled camera motions (e.g., moving forward, backward, translating, rotating).
- Results indicated that MoWorld performs interactive world simulation with spatial continuity and action responsiveness, effectively updating the visual state based on user actions. This was particularly evident in structured environments where it preserved spatial coherence across frames.

#### 4.3. Applications (Section 7)

MoWorld serves as a general-purpose world model, enabling a broad range of downstream tasks:

- **Video Transfer:** The model can transfer motion dynamics and camera trajectories from a source video to novel scenes or styles, preserving temporal coherence and geometric consistency.
- **Video Editing:** It supports joint control over visual content and narrative-consistent camera trajectories, allowing object insertion, removal, or modification while maintaining plausible dynamics.
- **Point Cloud Reconstruction:** Generated videos, being geometrically consistent, can be converted into 3D point clouds through depth estimation, providing explicit 3D scene structures.
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** Coherent multi-view observations from MoWorld can be lifted into high-fidelity 3DGS representations for efficient reconstruction and real-time novel-view rendering.
- **Navigation:** MoWorld can function as an interactive environment for vision-language-action (VLA) agents, simulating egocentric observations under predicted action plans, facilitating agent training and evaluation.
- **Camera-Style Joint Controlling:** By integrating a video style transfer model, MoWorld generates stylized video variants (e.g., rainy, cyberpunk) while preserving original camera motion and spatial layout.
- **Camera-Story Joint Controlling:** The model supports decomposing "scene generation" from "motion generation." It can reconstruct a stable 3DGS scene from its generated video, which then serves as a background reference for a downstream video generation model to synthesize dynamic events and foreground motion, reducing background drift and viewpoint instability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MoWorld represents a notable contribution to the field of generative AI and embodied intelligence, primarily by shifting the focus of World Model research towards practicality, efficiency, and real-time deployment.

**Key Significance Points:**

- **Introduction of "Flash World Models":** The paper formalizes the need for World Models capable of high-frame-rate, real-time interaction (30+ FPS), setting a new benchmark for performance in interactive systems.
- **NPU-Native Design and Optimization:** MoWorld is presented as the first real-time interactive World Model built on the NPU ecosystem. This NPU-centric co-design, spanning operator, parallelism, and pipeline levels, is significant for enabling low-cost and low-power deployment on widely available edge devices and intelligent systems, eliminating the reliance on high-end GPUs.
- **Holistic Approach to Efficiency:** The research demonstrates that substantial improvements in efficiency and cost-effectiveness can be achieved through an integrated framework. This includes a 3D-native data engine, curriculum pretraining, efficient distillation (ODE-initialization-free and self-forcing), and sophisticated inference optimizations (on-demand loading, hierarchical sequence parallelism, dynamic mixed-precision quantization, HBM-efficient attention).
- **Enhanced Controllability and Consistency:** Through camera control injection and curriculum cross-frame training, MoWorld achieves robust controllability and long-horizon consistency in generated scenes, addressing critical limitations in many existing video generation and world modeling approaches. Its ability to preserve subject identity, background structure, and spatial coherence during complex camera movements is a notable technical achievement.
- **Benchmarking Performance and Cost Reduction:** Quantitative evaluations show MoWorld achieving leading generative performance across various metrics while significantly reducing inference costs (30%-50% of existing models) and enabling high frame rates (up to 50 FPS). These metrics indicate a practical advantage for real-world scenarios.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Democratization of World Models:** By lowering hardware barriers and reducing operational costs, MoWorld has the potential to make advanced interactive world simulation more accessible for a wider range of applications and users, moving these technologies from research labs to mainstream adoption.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI and Autonomous Systems:** Real-time, geometrically consistent world simulation is fundamental for training and deploying embodied agents, robots, and autonomous vehicles. MoWorld’s capabilities could lead to more efficient and realistic simulation environments for perception, planning, and control.
- **Revolutionizing Content Creation:** The demonstrated applications in video transfer, editing, and 3D reconstruction (point clouds, Gaussian Splatting) suggest MoWorld can significantly impact content creation workflows, enabling artists and creators to generate diverse scene variants, edit content with narrative consistency, and produce reusable 3D assets more efficiently.
- **Enabling Immersive Experiences:** Applications in cloud gaming, digital twins, and virtual real estate touring could benefit from MoWorld’s ability to generate render-ready 3D content and interactive, explorable environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The end-to-end co-design methodology and the focus on NPU-native optimization provide a valuable blueprint for future research aiming to develop efficient and practical AI foundation models.

In conclusion, MoWorld represents a step towards making powerful World Models a practical reality, addressing the critical balance between advanced capabilities and real-world deployment constraints.
