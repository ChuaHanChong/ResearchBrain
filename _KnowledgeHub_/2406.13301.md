# ARDuP: Active Region Video Diffusion for Universal Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ARDuP enhances video-based robot planning by conditioning a latent video diffusion model on automatically discovered active regions, focusing generative planning on task-critical interactions. The framework achieves up to a 21.3% increase in success rates on unseen manipulation tasks in simulation and demonstrates robust object interaction in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Traditional Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) for universal agents face scalability issues due to environment-specific state/action definitions and reward structures.
- Existing video-based policy learning methods often treat all pixels uniformly during generation, leading to visually plausible but functionally sub-optimal robot actions, such as interacting with the wrong object.
- Acquiring ground truth annotations for 'active regions' (task-relevant interaction areas) in large-scale robotic datasets is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

## Method

- Developed ARDuP, a Latent Unified Predictive Decision Process conditioned on Active Region (LUPDP-AR) framework with three main components.
- Introduced an automatic, unsupervised 'Active Region Generator' that uses dense point tracking (Co-Tracker) and segmentation (SAM) to create pseudo active region masks from demonstration videos.
- Designed a 'Universal Video Planner' (latent video diffusion model) conditioned on the initial frame, text goal, and the automatically generated active region latent, guiding future video frame synthesis toward task-relevant interactions.
- Utilized a 'Task-Specific Latent Inverse Dynamics Model' that directly decodes robot actions from the generated latent video sequence, bypassing the need for computationally expensive RGB frame decoding.

## Results

- Achieved significant success rate improvements on unseen tasks in the CLIPort simulator, with a 21.3% increase for 'Place Bowl' (86.7% vs. 65.4% for UniPi*) and a 17.2% increase for 'Pack Object' (69.0% vs. 51.8%).
- Demonstrated robust real-world transfer capabilities on the BridgeData v2 dataset, consistently generating effective video plans and executing correct object interactions in cluttered environments, outperforming UniPi* qualitatively.
- Ablation studies confirmed that active region conditioning lowers the 'task loss' (L1 error between decoded and ground truth actions) and that higher quality active region guidance directly correlates with enhanced task performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding video generation with 'active regions' significantly improves the relevance and precision of robot plans, leading to higher task success rates by focusing on critical interactive elements.
> - Operating entirely within a compressed latent space for both video planning and action decoding offers substantial computational efficiency benefits, making the approach practical for robotic control.
> - Powerful pre-trained computer vision models can be leveraged in an unsupervised manner to automatically discover active regions from demonstration videos, eliminating the need for manual annotation and improving scalability.
