# ARDuP: Active Region Video Diffusion for Universal Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ARDuP enhances video-based robot planning by conditioning a latent video diffusion model on automatically discovered active regions, focusing generative planning on task-critical interactions. The framework achieves up to a 21.3% increase in success rates on unseen manipulation tasks in simulation and demonstrates robust object interaction in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Traditional Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) for universal agents face scalability issues due to environment-specific state/action definitions and reward structures.
- Existing video-based policy learning methods often treat all pixels uniformly during generation, leading to visually plausible but functionally sub-optimal robot actions, such as interacting with the wrong object.
- Acquiring ground truth annotations for 'active regions' (task-relevant interaction areas) in large-scale robotic datasets is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

## Method

- Developed ARDuP, a Latent Unified Predictive Decision Process conditioned on Active Region (LUPDP-AR) framework with three main components.
- Introduced an automatic, unsupervised 'Active Region Generator' that uses dense point tracking (Co-Tracker) and segmentation (SAM) to create pseudo active region masks from demonstration videos.
- Designed a 'Universal Video Planner' (latent video diffusion model) conditioned on the initial frame, text goal, and the automatically generated active region latent, guiding future video frame synthesis toward task-relevant interactions.
- Utilized a 'Task-Specific Latent Inverse Dynamics Model' that directly decodes robot actions from the generated latent video sequence, bypassing the need for computationally expensive RGB frame decoding.

## Results

- Achieved significant success rate improvements on unseen tasks in the CLIPort simulator, with a 21.3% increase for 'Place Bowl' (86.7% vs. 65.4% for UniPi*) and a 17.2% increase for 'Pack Object' (69.0% vs. 51.8%).
- Demonstrated robust real-world transfer capabilities on the BridgeData v2 dataset, consistently generating effective video plans and executing correct object interactions in cluttered environments, outperforming UniPi* qualitatively.
- Ablation studies confirmed that active region conditioning lowers the 'task loss' (L1 error between decoded and ground truth actions) and that higher quality active region guidance directly correlates with enhanced task performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding video generation with 'active regions' significantly improves the relevance and precision of robot plans, leading to higher task success rates by focusing on critical interactive elements.
> - Operating entirely within a compressed latent space for both video planning and action decoding offers substantial computational efficiency benefits, making the approach practical for robotic control.
> - Powerful pre-trained computer vision models can be leveraged in an unsupervised manner to automatically discover active regions from demonstration videos, eliminating the need for manual annotation and improving scalability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "universal agents" capable of performing a wide array of tasks across diverse environments is a grand challenge in robotic learning. Traditional approaches, often relying on Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), struggle with scalability due to the need for environment-specific state/action definitions, reward structures, and dynamics models. This paper positions itself squarely within the emerging paradigm of addressing this challenge through **video-based policy learning guided by natural language**.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly **diffusion models**, have significantly impacted various decision-making problems. Pioneering works like Diffuser and UniPi have demonstrated the potential of these models. UniPi, in particular, casts sequential decision-making as a text-conditioned video generation problem, where a video diffusion model acts as a "universal policy" by generating future visual trajectories based on a text goal and an initial observation. Subsequent works have built upon this, integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or dense correspondence to enhance action inference and long-horizon planning.

However, a critical limitation identified by ARDuP in these existing video-based policy learning methods is their tendency to treat all pixels uniformly during video generation. While they can produce visually plausible videos, they often fail to **prioritize task-relevant information**, leading to generated trajectories that might appear correct but ultimately result in sub-optimal or incorrect actions by the robot. For instance, a robot might pick up a similarly colored but incorrect object, because the video generation model didn't explicitly focus on the *interactive* elements critical for the task.

ARDuP addresses this gap by introducing the concept of **"active regions"** into video-based policy learning. The idea that certain regions of an image or video are more important for understanding or performing an action is not entirely new; it has been explored in fields like First Person Vision and egocentric activity recognition, to identify "active objects" manipulated by a user. However, ARDuP highlights that this concept is "under-explored" in the context of video-based policy learning for robot control. By explicitly conditioning the video generation process on these active regions—areas where objects are being interacted with—ARDuP aims to align the policy's attention to the specific objects and interactions specified by the task description. This represents a significant refinement of existing video-based planning approaches, moving beyond merely generating plausible visual sequences to generating *action-centric* and *task-aligned* sequences.

Furthermore, ARDuP leverages the computational efficiency benefits of **latent diffusion models**, a trend popularized by models like Stable Diffusion, by operating primarily in a compressed latent space. This allows for more efficient video planning and direct action decoding from latent representations, bypassing the computational overhead of generating full RGB frames for action inference. The method also tackles the practical challenge of acquiring active region annotations by developing an **automatic, unsupervised discovery mechanism** using pre-trained computer vision models (Co-Tracker and SAM), thereby reducing dependence on costly manual labeling and enhancing scalability.

In essence, ARDuP advances the state of the art in universal robotic policies by making video-based planning more intelligent and efficient. It moves beyond generic video generation to *purposeful* video generation, directly focusing on the elements crucial for successful task completion, thus addressing a key bottleneck in the broader research landscape of embodied AI and robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the ARDuP framework is to significantly enhance the effectiveness and reliability of video-based policy learning for robotic manipulation tasks. This is driven by several key motivations and aims:

1. **Improve Task Success Rates through Focused Planning:** The primary motivation stems from the observation that existing text-conditioned video generation models for robot planning often produce visually plausible but functionally deficient plans. By treating all pixels equally, these models can generate videos where the robot interacts with the wrong objects or executes incorrect movements (as illustrated in Figure 1). ARDuP aims to overcome this by introducing "active region" conditioning, which directs the video planner's attention to the most critical interactive areas, thereby leading to more precise, task-aligned video plans and, consequently, higher task success rates.

2. **Develop an Interaction-Aware Policy:** The goal is to create a policy that inherently understands and emphasizes the regions where interactions (e.g., grasping, placing) are most likely to occur. This "interaction-awareness" is believed to be crucial for grounding robot actions more effectively in the task description and environmental context.

3. **Enable Universal and Generalizable Robot Agents:** Building upon the concept of Universal Predictive Decision Processes (UPDPs), ARDuP seeks to further contribute to the development of robot agents capable of performing a vast number of tasks across diverse environments. By improving the quality of the video plans, ARDuP enhances the generalization capabilities of the underlying policy.

4. **Automate Active Region Annotation:** A practical objective is to eliminate the need for manual annotation of active regions, which would be prohibitively expensive and time-consuming for large-scale datasets. The paper aims to develop an unsupervised method for automatically discovering active regions directly from video demonstrations, making the approach scalable and practical for real-world applications.

5. **Enhance Computational Efficiency:** While video generation is powerful, it can be computationally intensive. ARDuP aims to improve efficiency by performing video planning and action decoding primarily within a compressed latent space, rather than directly generating and processing high-resolution RGB frames. This makes the framework more compact and faster, suitable for robotic applications.

In summary, ARDuP's core motivation is to make video-based robot planning smarter and more robust by explicitly guiding the generative process towards critical interactive elements, thereby solving the "wrong object" or "misaligned action" problem prevalent in prior work, all while maintaining computational efficiency and eliminating manual annotation burdens.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ARDuP (Active Region Video Diffusion for Universal Policies) instantiates a novel Latent Unified Predictive Decision Process conditioned on Active Region (LUPDP-AR) framework. Its methodology is structured around three interconnected components: an Active Region Generator, a Universal Video Planner conditioned on active regions, and a Task-Specific Latent Inverse Dynamics Model.

#### 3.1. Active Region Generator

The challenge of incorporating active regions is the lack of ground truth annotations in real-world data. ARDuP addresses this by proposing an automatic, unsupervised method for generating pseudo active regions from existing video demonstrations:

1. **Dense Point Tracking:** Given a video demonstration $V = \{x_h\}_{h=0}^H$, the method first uses a pre-trained dense point tracking model, Co-Tracker, to obtain dense point trajectories $P$. Co-Tracker divides the initial frame into a grid and tracks each point across all frames.
2. **Moving Point Identification:** For each point trajectory $p \in P$, the average movement $\Delta \bar{p}$ over the H timesteps is calculated. Points exceeding a predefined threshold $\tau$ (e.g., $\tau=2$) are identified as "moving point trajectories" ($P_m$), indicating significant displacement and likely interaction.
3. **Pseudo Mask Generation with SAM:** The coordinates of these moving points at the initial frame ($P_0$) are used as prompts for the Segment Anything Model (SAM). SAM, a powerful general-purpose segmentation model, then generates pseudo masks $M$ for the active regions in the initial frame $x_0$.
4. **Pseudo Active Region Frame Construction:** The active region frame $o$ is constructed by masking $x_0$ with $M$ and filling the background with white pixels ($o = x_0 \circ M + x_b \circ (1 - M)$).
5. **Latent Active Region Diffusion Model:** This entire process provides pseudo-active region supervision for training. At inference time, a conditioned latent diffusion model $\psi(\hat{o}|\hat{x_0}, c; \theta_\psi)$ is used. This model takes the latent representation of the initial frame $\hat{x_0}$ and the text description $c$ as input, and outputs the latent representation of the active region $\hat{o}$. This diffusion model's flexibility allows for generating various valid active regions, beneficial for tasks with multiple potential interaction points.

#### 3.2. Universal Video Planner Conditioned on Active Region

This module is responsible for generating future latent video frames, acting as the trajectory planner:

1. **Latent Video Diffusion Model:** ARDuP employs a latent video diffusion model, $\phi(\cdot|\hat{x_0}, c, \hat{o}; \theta_\phi)$, motivated by recent text-to-video models.
2. **Active Region Conditioning:** Crucially, this model is conditioned not only on the initial frame's latent $\hat{x_0}$ and the task text $c$ but also on the *generated active region latent* $\hat{o}$.
3. **Integration Mechanism:** The active region latent $\hat{o}$ is integrated by concatenating it with the latent of each frame during the denoising process. This dual concatenation (with $\hat{x_0}$ and with each subsequent frame's latent) ensures that the model's generation efforts are consistently guided toward the active regions, enhancing relevance and precision.
4. **Latent Space Operation:** The model operates entirely in the latent space, using a pre-trained image encoder $E$ (from Stable Diffusion) to map RGB frames to a compressed latent representation (e.g., $512 \times 512$ RGB to $128 \times 128 \times 4$ latent).

#### 3.3. Task Specific Action Decoding from Latent Sequence

The final component translates the planned latent video sequence into executable robot actions:

1. **Latent Inverse Dynamics Model:** A compact, task-specific latent inverse dynamics model $\pi(\cdot|\{\hat{x_h}\}_{h=0}^H, c)$ is trained. Given two adjacent generated latent frames $\hat{x_h}$ and $\hat{x_{h+1}}$, this model predicts the corresponding robot action $a_h$. The model consists of convolutional layers with skip connections followed by a linear layer to output 7-dimensional action vectors.
2. **Efficiency:** This approach is a significant advancement as it decodes actions directly from latent frames, eliminating the need to decode latents back to RGB frames. This streamlines the action inference process and makes ARDuP more computationally efficient than prior RGB-based inverse dynamics models.
3. **Training Decoupling:** The training of the inverse dynamics model is independent of the video planner, allowing for flexibility in dataset usage.
4. **Action Execution:** During inference, ARDuP utilizes an open-loop control strategy. After generating the H-step latent sequence, the corresponding H actions are predicted upfront by the inverse dynamics model and then executed sequentially. An optional decoder $R$ can be used to visualize the generated latent frames back into RGB for interpretability.

**Overall Flow:** The process begins with an initial frame and a text goal. The Active Region Generator uses these to produce an active region latent. This active region latent, along with the initial frame latent and text, conditions the Universal Video Planner to synthesize a sequence of future latent frames. Finally, the Latent Inverse Dynamics Model decodes this latent sequence directly into a series of robot actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ARDuP demonstrates compelling results across both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, validating its core hypothesis and design principles.

#### 4.1. Multi-Environment Transfer on CLIPort (Simulator)

Experiments on the CLIPort simulator, which involves language-grounded manipulation tasks, show significant quantitative improvements, especially for unseen tasks:
- **Success Rate Improvements:** ARDuP substantially outperforms the reproduced UniPi* baseline and other imitation learning baselines (State + Transformer BC, Image + Transformer BC, Image + TT, Diffuser) on three unseen tasks:
 - **Place Bowl:** 86.7% success rate, a **21.3% improvement** over UniPi* (65.4%).
 - **Pack Object:** 69.0% success rate, a **17.2% improvement** over UniPi* (51.8%).
 - **Pack Pair:** 46.6% success rate, a **15.7% improvement** over UniPi* (30.9%).
- These results highlight ARDuP's strong generalization capabilities to novel task instances and environments.
- **Qualitative Validation (Figure 3):** Visual comparisons on CLIPort demonstrate that ARDuP generates significantly more accurate and task-aligned video plans. For instance, ARDuP successfully plans to pack a white block, while a counterpart model without active region conditioning either targets the incorrect object (a red block) or generates robot arm movements that are not conducive to grasping. This confirms that active region guidance leads to higher visual quality and task relevance, especially around objects of interest.

#### 4.2. Real-world Transfer on BridgeData v2 (Real-World Dataset)

ARDuP's performance extends to the complex and diverse real-world BridgeData v2 dataset:
- **Robustness in Chaotic Environments:** Qualitative results (Figure 4) showcase ARDuP's ability to generate realistic and effective video plans even in challenging real-world scenarios with cluttered environments, varied language instructions, and diverse camera poses.
- **Correct Object Interaction:** The method consistently demonstrates correct object identification and manipulation, such as picking up sushi and placing it in a cup or colander. In contrast, the UniPi* baseline frequently makes errors, like picking up a cucumber instead of sushi or incorrectly placing an object on the desk rather than the target container. This underscores ARDuP's superior understanding of task requirements and object-level interactions in real-world settings.
- **Automatic Pseudo Mask Quality (Figure 6):** Visualizations confirm the high quality of the pseudo active region masks generated by the unsupervised method (Co-Tracker + SAM) in real-world videos, demonstrating the efficacy of eliminating manual annotation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Detailed ablation studies provide insights into the contributions of ARDuP's components:
- **Quantitative Generation Quality (Task Loss - Figure 5):** A "task loss" metric, calculated as the L1 error between actions decoded from generated video latents and ground truth actions, was introduced. ARDuP (with active region conditioning) shows a significantly lower task loss on the CLIPort test set compared to its counterpart without active region input. This directly quantifies the improvement in generation quality specific to control tasks and highlights the active region's crucial role.
- **Impact of Active Region Quality (Table II):** The success rate correlates positively with the quality of the active regions:
 - Using unsupervised predicted active regions (ARDuP's default) yielded notable improvements (e.g., +21.3% on Place Bowl).
 - When the active region prediction model was trained with ground truth active regions (Supervised Predicted), success rates further improved (e.g., +9.9% on Place Bowl compared to the unsupervised version).
 - Using *ground truth active regions during inference* resulted in the highest success rates (e.g., +16.6% on Place Bowl compared to the unsupervised version).
 - These findings strongly validate the core hypothesis: higher quality active region guidance directly translates to enhanced task performance in video-based policy learning.

In conclusion, ARDuP delivers substantial quantitative and qualitative improvements over existing methods, especially concerning generalization to unseen tasks and robustness in real-world environments. Its ability to generate focused, action-centric video plans, supported by an efficient latent-space implementation and automatic active region discovery, marks a significant step forward in universal robotic policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ARDuP framework presents several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact on the field of robotic learning and embodied AI:

1. **Enhanced Task Success and Reliability:** The most direct and impactful contribution is the significant improvement in task success rates demonstrated across both simulated (CLIPort) and real-world (BridgeData v2) benchmarks. By explicitly conditioning video generation on "active regions," ARDuP enables the robot's policy to focus on task-critical interactions, mitigating common failure modes like incorrect object selection or misaligned actions. This translates directly to more reliable and robust robotic agents.

2. **A Step Towards Truly Universal Policies:** The ability of ARDuP to generalize effectively to unseen tasks and adapt to diverse real-world scenarios brings the field closer to the long-sought goal of "universal policies." Such policies are essential for developing intelligent robots that can operate flexibly and autonomously in human environments, rather than being confined to narrow, pre-programmed tasks.

3. **Scalable and Annotation-Free Active Region Discovery:** A major practical bottleneck in robot learning is the need for extensive, often manually labeled, datasets. ARDuP's innovation in automatically discovering pseudo active regions from video demonstrations, leveraging powerful pre-trained models like Co-Tracker and SAM, is a crucial step towards scalable data utilization. This significantly reduces the overhead of data preparation, making it more feasible to train policies on large and diverse datasets.

4. **Improved Computational Efficiency via Latent Space Operations:** By conducting video planning and action decoding directly in a compressed latent space, ARDuP sidesteps the computational expense associated with generating and processing high-resolution RGB frames. This efficiency is critical for deploying complex generative models on resource-constrained robotic platforms and enables faster training and inference times, making the approach more practical for real-time robotic control.

5. **Refinement of Video-Based Planning Paradigms:** ARDuP refines the emerging paradigm of text-conditioned video generation for decision-making. It demonstrates that simply generating visually plausible future states is insufficient; the *focus* and *relevance* of the generated visual information to the task at hand are paramount. This insight can guide future research in generative models for control, emphasizing task-grounded generation rather than mere visual fidelity.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies avenues for future work, such as improving pseudo-active region quality with advanced trackers or text-guided segmentation, and applying noisy label learning techniques. This provides a clear roadmap for further enhancements, underscoring ARDuP's role as a strong foundation for continued innovation in the field.

In summary, ARDuP represents a significant advancement in robot learning by introducing a novel, efficient, and scalable approach to video-based policy learning. Its focus on active regions makes robotic policies more intelligent, reliable, and adaptable, paving the way for more capable and autonomous robots in a wide range of applications, from industrial automation to service robotics.
