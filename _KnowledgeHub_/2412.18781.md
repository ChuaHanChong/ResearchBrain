# Robustness Evaluation of Offline Reinforcement Learning for Robot Control Against Action Perturbations

> [!summary] Summary
> Research from Chiba University systematically evaluated the robustness of offline reinforcement learning methods for robot control against action-space perturbations. The study revealed that offline RL policies are generally more susceptible to these perturbations than online RL, benefiting from broader state-action coverage in datasets, but not from naive data augmentation with perturbed actions.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning (RL) policies for robot control lack empirical evaluation of their robustness against action-space perturbations, such as actuator failures or friction variations.
- Unlike state-space perturbations, action-space disturbances directly alter robot-environment interactions, posing a critical, underexplored challenge for real-world deployment.
- The conservative design of offline RL algorithms, aimed at preventing overestimation of out-of-distribution actions, may inadvertently increase their susceptibility to unencountered adversarial action perturbations.

## Method

- The study empirically assessed three offline RL algorithms (BCQ, TD3+BC, IQL) and three online RL algorithms (PPO, SAC, TD3) using simulated legged robot control tasks in MuJoCo.
- Action-space perturbations were introduced as both random noise and adversarially optimized disturbances via Differential Evolution to stress-test trained policies.
- The impact of training dataset characteristics (coverage from expert, medium, and medium-expert D4RL datasets) and dataset augmentation with perturbed actions was investigated.

## Results

- Offline RL algorithms (BCQ, TD3+BC, IQL) exhibited greater performance degradation under action perturbations, particularly adversarial ones, compared to online RL methods, sometimes resulting in negative average rewards.
- Policies trained on D4RL medium and medium-expert datasets demonstrated enhanced robustness against adversarial perturbations, achieving higher average rewards than those trained on expert datasets.
- Augmenting training datasets with randomly-perturbed actions resulted in negligible robustness improvements, and using adversarially-perturbed datasets consistently led to low performance across all test conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Offline RL methods are generally more susceptible to action-space perturbations, especially adversarial ones, compared to online RL approaches, attributed to their inability to interactively explore and adapt.
> - Training on datasets with broader state-action coverage, like D4RL medium or medium-expert, correlates with improved robustness against adversarial perturbations, indicating the value of diverse data beyond peak performance.
> - Simply augmenting training datasets with perturbed actions does not enhance offline RL robustness; the conservative nature of offline learning prevents it from effectively learning from perturbed actions without corresponding adaptive outcome feedback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline reinforcement learning (offline RL) has gained traction as a methodology for training policies from pre-collected datasets without direct environmental interaction. This approach is particularly appealing for domains such as robot control, healthcare, and energy management, where online data collection can be costly, risky, or time-intensive.

Robustness is a fundamental requirement for the practical application of any reinforcement learning system, especially in safety-critical areas like robotics. The existing research on RL robustness generally bifurcates into training-time robustness and testing-time robustness. Training-time robustness pertains to the ability to learn effectively from datasets that may contain noise, errors, or intentional augmentations. Testing-time robustness, on the other hand, refers to a policy's capacity to maintain performance when deployed in an environment that differs from its training conditions.

In the context of online RL, addressing the "reality gap" – the discrepancy between simulated training and real-world deployment – has been a significant research focus. Techniques such as domain randomization and adversarial training have been developed to enhance generalization and resilience to environmental variations and adversarial manipulations. However, the direct transferability of these solutions to offline RL, which fundamentally operates without interactive feedback, is not definitively established.

Previous work on testing-time robustness in offline RL has primarily concentrated on state-space perturbations, such as sensor noise or inaccurate state observations. These studies often aim to reduce the policy's sensitivity to minor input variations through methods like output smoothing or regularization techniques that constrain policy outputs in perturbed scenarios.

This paper identifies a specific gap within this landscape: the robustness of offline RL against *action-space perturbations*. In robotic systems, action-space perturbations, exemplified by joint actuator failures, discrepancies in friction coefficients, or actuator lag, are common hardware-related issues. These perturbations directly modify the robot's intended actions, consequently altering the environment's dynamics and the resulting rewards. Such deviations can lead to significant performance degradation or catastrophic failures. Given offline RL's reliance on fixed datasets and conservative training to prevent overestimation of out-of-distribution actions, its policies might be inherently susceptible to unencountered action perturbations, particularly those crafted adversarially. Conventional adversarial attack strategies designed for online RL typically require joint optimization with the policy, leveraging interactive environmental feedback, which is not available in offline settings.

Therefore, this research contributes by systematically evaluating the resilience of current offline RL methods to action-space perturbations, an aspect critical for reliable robot control that has received limited empirical investigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this study is to thoroughly evaluate the testing-time robustness of contemporary offline reinforcement learning methods when exposed to various forms of action-space perturbations. This overarching goal is underpinned by several detailed motivations and specific experimental objectives:

Firstly, a significant motivation arises from the growing interest in deploying offline RL for robot control. Offline RL's advantage lies in its potential to mitigate the high costs, safety concerns, and extensive time typically associated with continuous data collection and environmental interaction in online RL. For offline RL to achieve practical viability in real-world robotic applications, policies must demonstrate robustness against common hardware imperfections and operational challenges.

Secondly, real-world robotic systems are inherently subject to mechanical inconsistencies and failures, such as actuator wear, unexpected friction variations, or time delays in torque delivery (actuator lag). These constitute direct action-space perturbations, causing the executed actions to deviate from the policy's intended commands. Unlike state-space perturbations, action perturbations directly influence the physical interaction of the robot with its environment, potentially leading to critical deviations from desired task execution and significantly impacting overall performance. Current research in offline RL robustness has predominantly focused on state-space perturbations, leaving the critical area of action-space resilience largely unexplored.

Thirdly, offline RL algorithms are typically designed with conservative constraints to avoid overestimating Q-values for out-of-distribution actions, i.e., actions not well-represented in the static training dataset. While this conservatism promotes stable learning, it also renders policies potentially vulnerable during deployment to novel or unencountered action perturbations. The researchers hypothesize that this inherent limitation makes offline RL particularly susceptible to adversarial perturbations, which are specifically crafted to exploit system weaknesses and represent worst-case failure scenarios. Understanding these vulnerabilities is crucial for developing robust and reliable autonomous control systems.

In alignment with these motivations, the study defines the following specific objectives:
- To empirically assess the degree of performance degradation experienced by established offline RL methods (BCQ, TD3+BC, IQL) under both random and adversarial action perturbations within simulated legged robot control tasks.
- To conduct a comparative analysis of the robustness levels of these offline RL methods against widely used online RL methods (PPO, SAC, TD3) under identical perturbation conditions, positing that offline methods will exhibit greater vulnerability due to their lack of interactive exploration.
- To investigate how variations in the state-action coverage of training datasets influence the testing-time robustness of offline RL policies. This aims to determine if a broader diversity of data can inherently improve a policy's resilience to perturbations.
- To evaluate the efficacy of a common defensive strategy—training policies on datasets explicitly augmented with action perturbations—within the offline RL context, to ascertain whether this form of data augmentation can enhance future robustness.

Ultimately, the core motivation is to expose and characterize the intrinsic challenges and limitations of current offline RL approaches concerning robustness to action-space failures, thereby providing foundational insights for the design of more robust and deployable robotic control systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed an empirical and comparative methodology to assess the testing-time robustness of offline RL methods. This involved training various reinforcement learning policies and then evaluating their performance in simulated robotic environments under different action perturbation conditions.

**Reinforcement Learning Algorithms:**
The study evaluated three representative offline RL algorithms:
- **Batch-constrained Q-learning (BCQ):** Utilizes policy constraints to ensure the learned policy adheres closely to the behavior policy of the training data.
- **Twin-Delayed DDPG with behavior cloning (TD3+BC):** An offline variant of TD3 that regularizes the learned policy by adding a behavior cloning term to the objective function.
- **Implicit Q-learning (IQL):** Employs regularization of the value function update process, relying on state value distributions from the dataset to mitigate Q-value overestimation.
For comparison, three widely-used online RL algorithms were also included:
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**
- **Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)**
- **Twin-Delayed DDPG (TD3)**
The offline RL algorithms and SAC/TD3 were implemented using the d3rlpy library, while PPO's hyperparameters were adapted from existing literature. Standard hyperparameters, including a discount factor of 0.99, target update rate of 5e-3, and Adam optimizer, were used across algorithms, with a default MLP architecture of two hidden layers (256, 256 neurons). Detailed hyperparameter configurations are provided in Appendix B.

**Simulation Environments and Tasks:**
Experiments were conducted using three standard legged robot environments from OpenAI Gym (Hopper-v2, HalfCheetah-v2, Ant-v2) simulated within the MuJoCo physics engine. The primary task for all robots was forward locomotion. Reward functions incentivized forward velocity while penalizing control effort; Ant-v2 also penalized contact force.

**Action Perturbation Generation:**
Perturbations (δ) were applied to the policy-generated actions (a_t) using the formula: a'_t = a_t + δ ⊙ a_t, where ⊙ denotes the element-wise product. Each perturbation was kept constant for the duration of an episode. Three conditions were tested:
1. **Normal Condition:** No perturbations were applied (δ = 0).
2. **Random Perturbation Condition:** Each element of δ was independently sampled from a uniform distribution U([-ϵ, ϵ]^Na), where Na is the dimension of the action space.
3. **Adversarial Perturbation Condition:** δ was generated using the Differential Evolution (DE) method (Algorithm 1). DE is an optimization algorithm that iteratively searches for perturbations that minimize the average episodic reward under the current policy, effectively identifying worst-case adversarial actions. The DE process involved 30 generations, with population sizes varying by environment (45 for Hopper-v2, 90 for HalfCheetah-v2, 120 for Ant-v2).

**Perturbation Strength (ϵ):**
The perturbation strength ϵ was set to 0.3 for Hopper-v2 and HalfCheetah-v2, and 0.5 for Ant-v2 in most experiments. For experiments involving training with action-perturbed datasets, ϵ for Ant-v2 was adjusted to 0.3 due to observed training difficulties at higher strengths. The selection of these ϵ values was empirically justified in Appendix A.

**Training Datasets:**
The study utilized publicly available datasets from Datasets for Deep Data-Driven Reinforcement Learning (D4RL), offering varying levels of quality and coverage:
- **Expert:** Comprising 1 million trajectories collected by well-trained policies.
- **Medium:** Consisting of 1 million trajectories from less-trained policies.
- **Medium-Expert:** A combination of expert and medium datasets, totaling 2 million trajectories.
To investigate the impact of data augmentation, two additional datasets were synthesized from the expert dataset:
- **Randomly-perturbed datasets:** Actions in the expert dataset were augmented with random perturbations (ϵ=0.3).
- **Adversarially-perturbed datasets:** Actions in the expert dataset were augmented with adversarial perturbations (ϵ=0.3), specifically those generated to minimize reward. Histograms detailing the action distributions in these augmented datasets are presented in Appendix C.

**Experimental Phases:**
The evaluation was structured into three primary experimental inquiries:
1. **Comparative Robustness (Offline vs. Online RL):** Offline RL models, trained on the D4RL expert dataset, were evaluated under normal, random, and adversarial conditions and compared against online RL models.
2. **Impact of State-Action Coverage:** Offline RL models were trained on the D4RL medium-expert and medium datasets, with subsequent robustness evaluations to assess how dataset diversity influences resilience to perturbations.
3. **Effectiveness of Action-Perturbed Datasets:** Offline RL policies were trained on the synthesized randomly-perturbed and adversarially-perturbed datasets. Their robustness was then evaluated to determine if direct data augmentation with perturbations enhances testing-time robustness.

**Evaluation Metric and Procedure:**
Testing-time robustness was quantified by the average episodic reward, calculated over 1,000 trials for each experimental setting (Algorithm 2). Each episode terminated after a maximum of 1,000 time steps or if the robot fell over.

**Dataset Coverage Analysis (Appendix D):**
To characterize dataset diversity, the distribution of state-action pairs was visualized. State and action vectors were concatenated and scaled, then reduced in dimensionality using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). Kernel density estimation was applied to visualize density maps. Additionally, K-means clustering (K=100) was performed to quantitatively analyze the distribution of state-action pairs across expert and medium datasets through cumulative ratio plots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation produced several findings regarding the testing-time robustness of offline reinforcement learning methods when subjected to action perturbations.

**Comparison of Offline RL vs. Online RL Robustness:**
As detailed in Table 1, both offline and online RL methods exhibited a decrease in average episodic rewards under random perturbation conditions, with further, more significant deterioration observed under adversarial perturbation conditions. A primary finding was the greater vulnerability of offline RL methods (BCQ, TD3+BC, IQL) compared to online RL methods (PPO, SAC, TD3) when exposed to action perturbations. In the Hopper-v2 environment, offline RL performance was comparable to online RL under normal and random conditions but showed slightly lower performance under adversarial conditions. Conversely, in HalfCheetah-v2 and Ant-v2, offline RL performed comparably to or better than online RL under normal and random conditions, but its performance substantially declined under adversarial conditions, notably leading to negative average rewards in Ant-v2. This indicated that offline RL's lack of interactive experience during training contributes to its heightened susceptibility to unencountered perturbations. The consistent and pronounced reduction in average episodic rewards across diverse robot models and offline RL algorithms suggested that this vulnerability is intrinsic to the offline learning paradigm.

**Impact of State-Action Coverage on Offline RL Robustness:**
Table 2 summarized the results of training offline RL models on the D4RL medium-expert and medium datasets. For the medium-expert dataset, average episodic rewards under normal and random conditions were largely consistent with those from the expert dataset. However, under adversarial conditions, the medium-expert dataset generally yielded higher average rewards and smaller standard deviations, indicating a more favorable reward distribution and improved resilience.
For the medium dataset, average episodic rewards in HalfCheetah-v2 under normal conditions were approximately half of those observed with the expert dataset, aligning with prior research. In contrast, Hopper-v2 and Ant-v2 showed only slight decreases compared to the expert dataset. Crucially, under adversarial conditions, both the medium and medium-expert datasets led to substantial increases in average rewards compared to the expert dataset. In several instances, the medium dataset even surpassed the medium-expert dataset in average rewards under adversarial attack.
The analysis, supported by state-action coverage visualizations and clustering presented in Appendix D, indicated that the medium-expert and medium datasets provided broader state-action coverage than the expert dataset. This increased diversity in the training data appeared to correlate with enhanced robustness against perturbations. However, the results also suggested a potential trade-off, where simply increasing coverage might occasionally lead to reduced overall policy performance under normal conditions (e.g., HalfCheetah-v2 medium dataset).

**Impact of Action-Perturbed Datasets on Offline RL Robustness:**
The study investigated whether augmenting training datasets with action perturbations could improve robustness (Table 3). Training policies on *randomly-perturbed datasets* resulted in only modest and inconsistent increases in average episodic rewards under both random and adversarial conditions compared to the expert dataset. These small gains were not conclusively interpreted as significant robustness improvements. A potential explanation suggested that weaker random perturbations might introduce diversity similar to that found in medium-level datasets, providing minor, indirect benefits.
Conversely, training on *adversarially-perturbed datasets* led to consistently low average episodic rewards, even when policies were deployed under normal, unperturbed conditions. This finding suggests that merely incorporating perturbed actions into the training data is an ineffective strategy for enhancing testing-time robustness in offline RL. The researchers attributed this ineffectiveness to the fundamental limitation of offline RL: its conservative learning approach. For a policy to learn robustness to action perturbations, the dataset would need to include not only the perturbed actions but also the corresponding *outcomes* (rewards and next states) that represent successful adaptation to those perturbations. Since the datasets in this experiment contained only the perturbed actions without adaptive outcome information, the policy could not effectively learn to evaluate or generate corrective actions. This contrasts with online RL, where interactive feedback from the environment enables agents to learn directly from the consequences of perturbed actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study provides a comprehensive empirical evaluation of the robustness of offline reinforcement learning methods against action-space perturbations, an area of critical importance for real-world robotic applications. The findings have several significant implications and highlight key directions for future research and development.

**Identification of a Critical Vulnerability:** The most substantial contribution of this work is the clear demonstration that existing offline RL methods exhibit considerable vulnerability to both random and, more critically, adversarial action perturbations. This vulnerability is shown to be generally more pronounced than in comparable online RL methods. This insight is vital because action perturbations, such as actuator failures, wear-and-tear, or control delays, are inherent challenges in physical robotic systems and can lead to severe operational failures if not adequately addressed. The research effectively foregrounds a previously underexplored dimension of offline RL robustness.

**Implications for Real-World Robotics Deployment:** The identified vulnerabilities pose a significant hurdle for deploying offline RL policies in safety-critical robotics domains. In environments where robots interact with humans, navigate complex terrains, or perform precise manipulation, policies that catastrophically fail due to minor, hardware-induced action discrepancies are unacceptable. The study underscores that, without mechanisms to anticipate or adapt to post-action distortions—a capability inherently limited by offline RL's static data constraint—these systems cannot be considered reliable for real-world deployment.

**Insights into Dataset Characteristics:** The findings regarding the impact of state-action coverage (where medium and medium-expert datasets often conferred greater robustness under adversarial conditions than expert datasets) reinforce the understanding that dataset diversity is not solely about maximizing peak performance but also about fostering resilience. Policies trained on broader state-action distributions appear to generalize better to unforeseen perturbations, even if their optimal performance might be marginally lower than those trained on highly specialized "expert" data. This suggests a need for more sophisticated strategies in offline dataset design, balancing optimality with robust coverage.

**Limitations of Naive Data Augmentation:** The experiments conclusively demonstrate that simple data augmentation, such as merely injecting perturbed actions into existing datasets, is ineffective for improving robustness in offline RL. This result is a crucial insight for both researchers and practitioners, indicating that techniques which work in online learning (where immediate environmental feedback validates perturbed actions) do not directly translate to the offline setting. This fundamental limitation arises from offline RL's inability to observe and learn from the consequences of actions that deviate significantly from the observed behavior policy's trajectories.

**Guidance for Future Research Directions:**
- **Hybrid Offline-Online Frameworks:** The study strongly advocates for hybrid offline-online reinforcement learning approaches as a promising avenue. By initializing policies with models pretrained offline and subsequently fine-tuning them through limited, controlled online interactions, agents can gain essential exposure to real-world perturbations and learn adaptive, corrective behaviors that are otherwise impossible to acquire from static data. This approach offers a potentially sample-efficient path toward robust deployment.
- **Adaptive Training Strategies for Offline RL:** Future work should explore novel offline RL algorithms or training paradigms that implicitly or explicitly account for potential action perturbations during policy learning. This could involve techniques that infer or model the consequences of perturbed actions within the offline data, or develop policies that are inherently more conservative and resilient to such deviations without relying on direct online feedback.
- **Addressing the "Reality Gap" in Offline Settings:** The research re-emphasizes the ongoing challenge of the reality gap, now extending its focus to action-space robustness within offline contexts. Developing robust offline RL for real-world deployment will necessitate innovative solutions that bridge this gap without the extensive online exploration characteristic of traditional methods.

In summary, this research significantly advances the understanding of robustness challenges in offline reinforcement learning for robot control. By rigorously evaluating the impact of action perturbations, it provides critical insights into the limitations of current methods and establishes foundational knowledge for the development of more robust, reliable, and deployable offline RL systems for real-world robotic applications. The emphasis on hybrid offline-online strategies and a deeper consideration of dataset characteristics will likely guide future endeavors in this vital field.
