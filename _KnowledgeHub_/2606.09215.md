# MotionWAM: Towards Foundation World Action Models for Real-Time Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A real-time World Action Model, MotionWAM, is introduced for humanoid robots, unifying whole-body control into a single motion latent space for complex loco-manipulation tasks. This approach achieved an overall success rate of 76.1% across nine real-world tasks, outperforming existing baselines by over 32% absolute gain and maintaining a closed-loop operating frequency of 4.9 Hz.

## Problem

- Current humanoid loco-manipulation systems use a hierarchical control paradigm, leading to inconsistent action spaces and restricted lower-body functionality for task execution.
- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) are computationally intensive, making them too slow for real-time closed-loop control on dynamically balancing humanoids.
- The decoupling of upper and lower body control prevents task-driven leg involvement, limiting the robot's versatility in human-scale environments.

## Method

- **MotionWAM** is developed as a ==dual-DiT== (Diffusion in Transformers) World Action Model that translates egocentric video dynamics into unified whole-body humanoid actions.
- The architecture integrates a Video DiT that leverages intermediate denoising features in a "one-shot imagination" regime for real-time inference, and a Motion DiT to predict whole-body motion tokens.
- A three-stage progressive learning framework is utilized, beginning with egocentric video pretraining, followed by cross-embodiment action post-training, and concluding with whole-body fine-tuning for specific tasks.

## Results

- MotionWAM achieved an overall success rate of **76.1%** across nine real-world humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, demonstrating an absolute gain of over **32%** compared to the strongest baseline, GR00T-N1.7 (**43.9%**).
- The model consistently outperformed all baselines on individual tasks, with particularly strong relative gains (30-**45%**) on tasks requiring significant whole-body coordination and task-driven leg involvement.
- MotionWAM maintained real-time closed-loop deployment at **4.9 Hz** on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, proving seven times faster than comparable world-model-based policies and meeting the control requirements for dynamically balancing humanoids.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying whole-body control into a single motion latent space enables coordinated upper and lower body actions, allowing for task-driven foot interactions previously limited by decoupled control schemes.
> - Leveraging intermediate denoising features from a video diffusion model allows World Action Models to achieve real-time inference speeds suitable for dynamic humanoid robot control.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training approach starting with egocentric video pretraining is essential for adapting video world models to real-robot humanoid loco-manipulation, since strong dynamics priors (not static image-text priors) are crucial for closed-loop control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is progressing towards enabling robots to operate in human-scale environments, requiring complex coordination of balance, locomotion, and manipulation. Prior research has typically advanced along three distinct axes: robust whole-body imitation, command-following whole-body controllers, and manipulation policies acting as high-level planners. Most autonomous humanoid loco-manipulation systems combine these by employing a hierarchical control paradigm: a high-level manipulation policy controls the upper body, while a low-level controller handles locomotion by tracking coarse base commands (e.g., velocity, torso height, orientation).

This hierarchical approach, however, results in inconsistent action spaces for the upper and lower body. The upper body receives fine-grained joint targets, whereas the lower body is restricted to balance-preserving locomotion. This decoupling prevents task-driven leg involvement, such as stepping on a pedal or kicking an object, limiting the robot's versatility.

Concurrently, World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising method for visuomotor control. WAMs leverage video dynamics priors, often through large-scale video generators, to inform policy decisions. This approach injects a strong dynamics understanding into the policy, leading to temporally coherent and physically grounded behaviors that are challenging for purely image-text-pretrained policies to learn from demonstrations alone. However, existing WAMs generally involve iterative denoising over high-dimensional video-action latents, making them computationally intensive and too slow for real-time closed-loop control, especially on dynamically balancing humanoids. Their applications have largely been confined to short-horizon, fixed-base, arm-only tabletop manipulation tasks.

MotionWAM addresses these limitations by aiming to extend WAM capabilities to real-time, whole-body humanoid loco-manipulation in a unified latent space. It seeks to bridge the gap between powerful video-pretrained dynamics models and the real-time control requirements of complex humanoid tasks, specifically by enabling coordinated upper- and lower-body actions within a single action space, thereby unlocking task-driven foot interactions. This work positions itself at the intersection of generalist robot policies, video world models, and advanced humanoid control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research is motivated by two primary challenges in humanoid robot control: the limitations of the prevalent hierarchical control paradigm and the real-time constraints of existing World Action Models (WAMs) when applied to humanoids.

**Motivation:**

1. **Limitations of Hierarchical Humanoid Control:** Current autonomous humanoid loco-manipulation systems typically decouple upper-body manipulation from lower-body locomotion. This architectural choice leads to:
 - **Inconsistent Action Spaces:** The upper body often receives fine-grained joint targets, while the lower body is controlled by coarse commands like base velocity or torso height. This divergence creates a disjointed control scheme.
 - **Restricted Lower Body Functionality:** The legs are primarily delegated to balance preservation, preventing their active participation in task execution. This design inherently rules out complex, task-driven foot interactions, such as stepping on a pedal, kicking an object, or performing specific squats, which are crucial for human-like manipulation in diverse environments.
 - **Reduced Coordination:** The separation of control limits the robot's ability to execute truly coordinated whole-body movements that integrate locomotion, balance, and manipulation seamlessly.

2. **Real-Time Constraints of Existing World Action Models (WAMs):** While WAMs have demonstrated promise by injecting strong dynamics priors into policies, their current implementations face significant practical hurdles for real-time humanoid control:
 - **Computational Cost:** The iterative denoising process over high-dimensional video-action latents, a common component of WAMs, is computationally expensive. This makes them too slow for the rapid feedback loops required by dynamically balancing humanoids.
 - **Limited Scope:** Existing WAM deployments are largely restricted to simpler scenarios, such as tabletop manipulation with fixed-base robot arms, failing to address the complexities of whole-body control, dynamic balance, and locomotion in real-world environments.

**Objectives:**

To overcome these challenges, MotionWAM sets forth the following key objectives:

1. **Develop a Real-Time World Action Model:** Create a WAM capable of operating in real-time for autonomous humanoid loco-manipulation, utilizing visual input from a single egocentric camera.
2. **Unify Whole-Body Control in a Single Action Space:** Replace the traditional upper–lower body split with a unified motion latent. This latent should predict whole-body motion tokens that jointly cover locomotion, torso motion, height regulation, foot interaction, and hand manipulation, allowing the humanoid to be driven as one integrated system.
3. **Enable Task-Driven Foot Interaction:** Specifically, this unified action space is intended to unlock behaviors where the lower body actively contributes to the task, rather than passively maintaining balance, facilitating actions like pedal stepping or object kicking.
4. **Establish a Progressive Learning Framework:** Introduce a three-stage learning framework designed to progressively adapt a video world model. This framework aims to first specialize the model to egocentric visual dynamics and then to the specific target humanoid embodiment.

These objectives collectively aim to advance the capabilities of humanoid robots towards more human-like, coordinated, and versatile whole-body control in dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionWAM is designed as a dual-DiT (Diffusion in Transformers) World Action Model that translates egocentric video dynamics into whole-body humanoid actions. Its methodology is structured around a specific problem formulation, a dual-DiT architecture, and a three-stage progressive training recipe.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

MotionWAM operates under a predict-video-dynamics-then-invert paradigm, contrasting with direct mapping policies. Given an egocentric observation `o_t`, a language goal `l`, and proprioceptive state `p_t`, the model first predicts future video dynamics and then infers the whole-body motion. Specifically, it predicts a unified whole-body motion latent `m_t` that governs the entire robot in a single action space. This `m_t` is composed of a continuous part `m_cont_t` for dexterous hand commands (e.g., gripper states) and a discretized token `k_t`. The token `k_t` is a 64-dimensional vector, bottlenecked by Finite Scalar Quantization, summarizing locomotion, torso motion, height regulation, and foot-interaction intent, leveraging the SONIC whole-body controller for decoding into joint commands.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The core of MotionWAM is its dual-DiT architecture, comprising a Video DiT and a Motion DiT.
- **Video DiT:** This component is initialized from Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B, which includes a causal spatio-temporal VAE and a flow-matching diffusion transformer. It compresses current egocentric conditioning frames (`o_t`) and future frames (`o_t+1`) into latents `z^0_t` and `z^0_t+1`. A critical design choice for real-time performance is to intercept intermediate denoising features `h^τf_t` from a single transformer block at a fixed, late flow timestep `τ_f` (approximating pure noise). This allows the Video DiT to operate in a "one-shot imagination" regime, providing activations that encode where the scene is likely to go without completing the full denoising process, thereby enabling real-time operation on a closed-loop humanoid. The VAE and text encoder remain frozen throughout training.
- **Motion DiT:** This component consumes the hidden state `h^τf_t` from the Video DiT (via interleaved self/cross-attention), the embedded proprioceptive state `p_t`, and noisy whole-body motion-latent tokens. It outputs a velocity field, the integration of which yields the final motion latent `m_t`. During multi-embodiment pretraining (Stage 2), per-embodiment input/output projectors wrap a shared Motion DiT trunk, which is then reused with a specific Unitree G1 projector during deployment.

#### 3.3. Training Recipe

MotionWAM is trained using a three-stage learning framework, designed to progressively adapt the video world model to egocentric visual dynamics and the target humanoid embodiment. Both Video DiT and Motion DiT are trained using flow-matching objectives, `L_video` and `L_motion` respectively, which minimize the difference between the predicted velocity field and the target velocity.

1. **Stage 1: Egocentric Video Pretraining:**
 - **Objective:** To specialize the Video DiT towards egocentric visual dynamics.
 - **Process:** The Video DiT is trained independently using only the `L_video` objective. Only its parameters are updated.
 - **Data:** A large dataset of approximately 2,136 hours of egocentric human videos and humanoid-robot videos (mixture detailed in Appendix E). Action labels are ignored during this stage; only the video streams are utilized. The insight is that egocentric visual dynamics, rather than action diversity, is the bottleneck, and leveraging inexpensive, action-free video helps the model absorb scale efficiently.

2. **Stage 2: Cross-Embodiment Action Post-Training:**
 - **Objective:** To ground the pretrained world model into an action space across various embodiments.
 - **Process:** The Motion DiT is attached to the Video DiT. Both networks are jointly trained.
 - **Data:** A heterogeneous mixture of Unitree G1 humanoid datasets, encompassing different end-effectors and action-annotation formats (Appendix E). This data is routed through per-embodiment input/output projectors.
 - **Loss:** A joint loss `L_Stage 2 = L_motion + L_video` is used. The video objective (`L_video`) is retained to act as a representation regularizer, preventing the dynamics prior learned in Stage 1 from being overwritten.

3. **Stage 3: Whole-Body Fine-tuning:**
 - **Objective:** To adapt the model to specific humanoid loco-manipulation tasks on the target Unitree G1 embodiment.
 - **Process:** The entire network (Video DiT and Motion DiT) is fine-tuned end-to-end.
 - **Data:** A small amount of teleoperated whole-body demonstrations collected specifically on the Unitree G1 (200 episodes per task across nine real-world tasks, as detailed in Appendix B).
 - **Action Output:** The whole-body motion latent `m_t` = (`m_cont_t`, `~k_t`) is regressed as a continuous scalar `~k_t` alongside continuous channels. At inference, `~k_t` is rounded to the nearest discrete SONIC motion-token index `k_t`, which, combined with `m_cont_t`, is decoded by SONIC into joint commands.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup (Hardware and Tasks)

- **Hardware:** Experiments were conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot equipped with dual ALOHA2 grippers and an Intel RealSense D435i RGB camera for egocentric observations. A PICO VR three-point tracking system was used for teleoperation data collection, retargeting human poses via SMPL to drive the robot. Policies run on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 workstation.
- **Tasks:** A suite of nine real-world loco-manipulation tasks was designed (Fig. 4), requiring active leg and torso involvement beyond mere balance. These tasks stress waist control, height regulation, squatting locomotion, task-driven foot interaction, and body–hand coordination. Each method was evaluated over 20 trials per task, reporting success rates.
- **Baselines:** MotionWAM was compared against Diffusion Policy, ACT, π0.5, GR00T-N1.7, and a custom Qwen3DiT baseline (Qwen3-VL backbone with Motion DiT). All baselines were fine-tuned on the same Stage 3 data and operated within the same action space and observation format.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MotionWAM focused on its performance against baselines, the necessity of its multi-stage training, and its real-time inference capabilities.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Baselines

MotionWAM demonstrated a substantial performance advantage over contemporary Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and visuomotor baselines.
- **Overall Success Rate:** MotionWAM achieved an overall success rate of 76.1% across the nine real-world humanoid loco-manipulation tasks. This represents an absolute gain of over 32% compared to the strongest baseline, GR00T-N1.7, which achieved 43.9%.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** MotionWAM outperformed all five baselines (Diffusion Policy, ACT, π0.5, GR00T-N1.7, Qwen3DiT) on every individual task.
- **Impact of Unified Action Space:** The performance gap was particularly pronounced on tasks demanding whole-body coordination and task-driven leg involvement, such as "Kick Soccer" (+40% relative gain), "Load Cart" (+40%), "Retrieve Item" (+40%), "Wipe Board" (+45%), and "Do Laundry" (+30%). This indicates that MotionWAM's unified motion latent effectively enables the legs and torso to actively contribute to tasks, a capability not achieved by decoupled upper–lower body policies.
- **Limitations of Static VLM Priors:** The VLM-only baseline, Qwen3DiT, exhibited near-zero success rates on locomotion-heavy tasks. Even π0.5 remained below 20% overall. This suggests that strong semantic priors from static image-text models alone are insufficient for transferring to the complex, closed-loop physics required for humanoid loco-manipulation. The integration of a video world model was crucial for closing this performance gap.

#### 4.2. Ablation on the Three-Stage Training Framework

Ablation studies confirmed the necessity and complementary roles of each stage in the MotionWAM training framework.
- **Impact of Stage 1 (Egocentric Video Pretraining):** Removing Stage 1 resulted in an 11% absolute drop in average success rate (from 70% to 59% on a representative subset of five tasks). This indicates that without the initial pretraining on egocentric video, the Video DiT lacks a specialized egocentric dynamics prior, leading to less accurate motion latent predictions in subsequent stages.
- **Impact of Stage 2 (Cross-Embodiment Action Post-Training):** Removing Stage 2 led to a more significant 28% absolute drop in average success rate (from 70% to 42% on the same subset of tasks). When the Motion DiT was directly attached to the Stage 1 trunk and trained only on the small target-task dataset, performance collapsed across tasks. This highlights the critical role of Stage 2 in providing cross-embodiment grounding for the action signal.
- **Complementary Roles:** The results demonstrate that Stage 1 provides an essential egocentric visual-dynamics prior, while Stage 2 grounds this prior into the action space across different embodiments. Both stages are necessary and their progressive specialization strategy is effective for achieving robust real-robot performance.

#### 4.3. Real-Time Inference Frequency

MotionWAM met the real-time control requirements for closed-loop humanoid balance.
- **Inference Speed:** MotionWAM achieved a closed-loop deployment frequency of 4.9 Hz on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** This performance is competitive with VLA baselines of comparable scale (GR00T-N1.7 at 6.5 Hz, Qwen3DiT at 9.0 Hz).
- **Comparison with Other WAMs:** Crucially, MotionWAM was seven times faster than Cosmos Policy (0.7 Hz), another world-model-based policy with a similar parameter count. This speed advantage is attributed to MotionWAM's design choice of conditioning on intermediate denoising features in a single forward pass, rather than requiring iterative denoising of future video frames.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MotionWAM research demonstrates the viability of leveraging video-pretrained World Action Models for complex, real-time humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Real-Time Whole-Body Control:** The work introduces a method for real-time WAM-driven policies for whole-body humanoid loco-manipulation, addressing a critical limitation of previous WAMs that were too slow for dynamic humanoid applications.
- **Unified Action Space:** By replacing the conventional upper-lower body decoupled control with a unified motion latent, MotionWAM enables integrated and coordinated whole-body movements. This is a departure from hierarchical control schemes that restrict lower-body functionality.
- **Task-Driven Foot Interaction:** The unified action space unlocks novel capabilities such as task-driven foot interactions (e.g., ball kicking, pedal stepping), which were previously unattainable by policies relying on balance-preserving lower-body control.
- **Effective Progressive Learning:** The proposed three-stage learning framework provides a validated recipe for progressively adapting large-scale video world models. This includes specializing to egocentric visual dynamics and grounding actions across different embodiments, a strategy that proved essential for real-robot performance.
- **Performance Advancement:** MotionWAM achieved substantial performance gains (over 30% absolute increase in success rate) compared to strong VLA baselines on a suite of challenging real-world tasks, demonstrating the tangible benefits of incorporating video-based dynamics priors.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Humanoid Capabilities:** This research has the potential to significantly advance the capabilities of humanoid robots, enabling them to perform more versatile, coordinated, and human-like actions in complex, unstructured environments. This could broaden the range of applications for humanoids in areas such as logistics, assistance, and manufacturing.
- **New Directions for Robot Learning:** It suggests a promising path for scaling generalist robot policies by effectively integrating large-scale video pretraining with physically grounded robotic control. This could influence the design of future foundation models for embodied AI.
- **Bridging Vision and Action:** The method's ability to translate complex visual dynamics into robust, real-time robot actions offers insights into how to more effectively bridge high-dimensional perceptual understanding with low-level robot control.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:** By embedding a strong dynamics prior, MotionWAM could lead to more robust and adaptable humanoid systems that are less prone to failures in dynamic or unexpected situations.

#### 5.3. Limitations

The study acknowledges certain limitations. The Stage 3 fine-tuning was validated only on the Unitree G1 humanoid, and its transferability to other humanoid platforms remains unverified. The evaluation did not include a controlled study on novel-object generalization, as training and test object sets shared visual similarities. A key failure mode identified is the reliance on a single egocentric camera, which can lead to a loss of visual grounding and policy stalling if the manipulated object leaves the field of view or the head-camera viewpoint deviates significantly from the training distribution.
