# MotionWAM: Towards Foundation World Action Models for Real-Time Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A real-time World Action Model, MotionWAM, is introduced for humanoid robots, unifying whole-body control into a single motion latent space for complex loco-manipulation tasks. This approach achieved an overall success rate of 76.1% across nine real-world tasks, outperforming existing baselines by over 32% absolute gain and maintaining a closed-loop operating frequency of 4.9 Hz.

## Problem

- Current humanoid loco-manipulation systems use a hierarchical control paradigm, leading to inconsistent action spaces and restricted lower-body functionality for task execution.
- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) are computationally intensive, making them too slow for real-time closed-loop control on dynamically balancing humanoids.
- The decoupling of upper and lower body control prevents task-driven leg involvement, limiting the robot's versatility in human-scale environments.

## Method

- **MotionWAM** is developed as a ==dual-DiT== (Diffusion in Transformers) World Action Model that translates egocentric video dynamics into unified whole-body humanoid actions.
- The architecture integrates a Video DiT that leverages intermediate denoising features in a "one-shot imagination" regime for real-time inference, and a Motion DiT to predict whole-body motion tokens.
- A three-stage progressive learning framework is utilized, beginning with egocentric video pretraining, followed by cross-embodiment action post-training, and concluding with whole-body fine-tuning for specific tasks.

## Results

- MotionWAM achieved an overall success rate of **76.1%** across nine real-world humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, demonstrating an absolute gain of over **32%** compared to the strongest baseline, GR00T-N1.7 (**43.9%**).
- The model consistently outperformed all baselines on individual tasks, with particularly strong relative gains (30-**45%**) on tasks requiring significant whole-body coordination and task-driven leg involvement.
- MotionWAM maintained real-time closed-loop deployment at **4.9 Hz** on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, proving seven times faster than comparable world-model-based policies and meeting the control requirements for dynamically balancing humanoids.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying whole-body control into a single motion latent space enables coordinated upper and lower body actions, allowing for task-driven foot interactions previously limited by decoupled control schemes.
> - Leveraging intermediate denoising features from a video diffusion model allows World Action Models to achieve real-time inference speeds suitable for dynamic humanoid robot control.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training approach, starting with large-scale egocentric video pretraining, is essential for effectively adapting video world models to robust real-robot humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.
> - Strong dynamics priors from video world models are crucial for complex, closed-loop humanoid control, as static image-text semantic priors alone are insufficient.
