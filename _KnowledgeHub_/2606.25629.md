# Event-Adaptive Motion Planning with Distilled Vision-Language Model in Safety-Critical Situations

> [!summary] Summary
> The Event-Adaptive Motion Planning (EAMP) framework enhances robot navigation in safety-critical environments by integrating a lightweight, distilled Vision-Language Model to proactively detect behavioral anomalies and adapt Model Predictive Control (MPC) objectives, achieving a 32% improvement in dynamic safety margins and better efficiency.

## Problem

- Robot navigation struggles with unforeseen semantic events (e.g., sudden pedestrian behaviors) in dynamic, safety-critical environments, leading to conservative or unsafe reactions.
- Integrating computationally expensive Vision-Language Models (VLMs) directly into continuous robot control loops causes significant latency, hindering real-time performance.
- Existing VLM integration methods either lack semantic understanding, suffer from computational overhead, or rely on insufficient triggering mechanisms that miss critical behavioral anomalies of known dynamic agents.

## Method

- The **Event-Adaptive** Motion Planning (EAMP) framework proposes a three-level hierarchical semantic control architecture: a prompt-configurable semantic event trigger (PC-SET), a semantic ==model predictive control== (SMPC) module, and an underlying control layer.
- PC-SET, using a lightweight VLM, asynchronously monitors temporal clips for behavioral anomalies in safety-critical situations, activating adaptation only when semantically necessary and ensuring temporal consistency.
- A physically verified semantic distillation pipeline trains a lightweight VLM (SemNav-VLM) by validating teacher-generated strategies through closed-loop simulation, ensuring learned strategies are dynamically feasible and safe for structured MPC reconfiguration.

## Results

- SemNav-VLM achieved **96%** strategy accuracy, outperforming the cloud-based teacher (**86%**) and base models (**42%**), with lower inference latency, and showed robust reasoning for safety-critical maneuvers.
- EAMP demonstrated superior safety margins in hard scenarios, achieving a minimum Time-to-Collision (TTC) of **1.77** s, a **32%** improvement over baselines, while also reducing completion time and speed variability.
- EAMP's PC-SET effectively detected behavioral anomalies of recognized agents (e.g., abrupt braking), leading to timely and proactive control adaptations that improved success rates from **60%** (OCP) to **95%** and prevented collisions, unlike methods relying on object-level perception confidence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Event-driven semantic adaptation, triggered by behavior-level anomaly detection, is more effective and efficient than periodic or object-level confidence-based VLM invocations for managing dynamic safety.
> - Distilling large VLM capabilities into a lightweight student model via physically verified closed-loop simulation can achieve high semantic reasoning accuracy (96%) with acceptable inference latency, suitable for real-time robotic applications.
> - Structured, template-based reconfiguration of MPC objectives and geometric paths, guided by high-level semantic strategies, provides a robust and verifiable interface for integrating VLM reasoning into low-level control.
