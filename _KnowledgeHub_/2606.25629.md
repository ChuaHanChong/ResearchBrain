# Event-Adaptive Motion Planning with Distilled Vision-Language Model in Safety-Critical Situations

> [!summary] Summary
> The Event-Adaptive Motion Planning (EAMP) framework enhances robot navigation in safety-critical environments by integrating a lightweight, distilled Vision-Language Model to proactively detect behavioral anomalies and adapt Model Predictive Control (MPC) objectives, achieving a 32% improvement in dynamic safety margins and better efficiency.

## Problem

- Robot navigation struggles with unforeseen semantic events (e.g., sudden pedestrian behaviors) in dynamic, safety-critical environments, leading to conservative or unsafe reactions.
- Integrating computationally expensive Vision-Language Models (VLMs) directly into continuous robot control loops causes significant latency, hindering real-time performance.
- Existing VLM integration methods either lack semantic understanding, suffer from computational overhead, or rely on insufficient triggering mechanisms that miss critical behavioral anomalies of known dynamic agents.

## Method

- The **Event-Adaptive** Motion Planning (EAMP) framework proposes a three-level hierarchical semantic control architecture: a prompt-configurable semantic event trigger (PC-SET), a semantic ==model predictive control== (SMPC) module, and an underlying control layer.
- PC-SET, using a lightweight VLM, asynchronously monitors temporal clips for behavioral anomalies in safety-critical situations, activating adaptation only when semantically necessary and ensuring temporal consistency.
- A physically verified semantic distillation pipeline trains a lightweight VLM (SemNav-VLM) by validating teacher-generated strategies through closed-loop simulation, ensuring learned strategies are dynamically feasible and safe for structured MPC reconfiguration.

## Results

- SemNav-VLM achieved **96%** strategy accuracy, outperforming the cloud-based teacher (**86%**) and base models (**42%**), with lower inference latency, and showed robust reasoning for safety-critical maneuvers.
- EAMP demonstrated superior safety margins in hard scenarios, achieving a minimum Time-to-Collision (TTC) of **1.77** s, a **32%** improvement over baselines, while also reducing completion time and speed variability.
- EAMP's PC-SET effectively detected behavioral anomalies of recognized agents (e.g., abrupt braking), leading to timely and proactive control adaptations that improved success rates from **60%** (OCP) to **95%** and prevented collisions, unlike methods relying on object-level perception confidence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Event-driven semantic adaptation, triggered by behavior-level anomaly detection, is more effective and efficient than periodic or object-level confidence-based VLM invocations for managing dynamic safety.
> - Distilling large VLM capabilities into a lightweight student model via physically verified closed-loop simulation can achieve high semantic reasoning accuracy (96%) with acceptable inference latency, suitable for real-time robotic applications.
> - Structured, template-based reconfiguration of MPC objectives and geometric paths, guided by high-level semantic strategies, provides a robust and verifiable interface for integrating VLM reasoning into low-level control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot navigation, particularly in dynamic and safety-critical environments, presents challenges related to efficiently maintaining safety while achieving operational efficiency. Traditional approaches often struggle with unforeseen semantic events, where unpredictable behaviors of dynamic agents, rather than static obstacles, pose the primary collision risk. Such "corner cases" are infrequently encountered in real-world scenarios, leading to data scarcity for training robust autonomous systems. Consequently, conventional systems often exhibit conservative behaviors like unnecessary detours or emergency braking when faced with unfamiliar situations, hindering widespread robot deployment in human-centric applications.

Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as a promising solution due to their commonsense reasoning capabilities, which can potentially recognize and interpret complex, unfamiliar scenarios. However, directly integrating the high computational demands of VLMs into continuous robot control loops introduces significant latency, which can destabilize physical execution.

Existing navigation schemes can be broadly categorized:
- **Classical optimization-based methods** (e.g.,) prioritize kinematic feasibility and efficiency through structured optimization but generally lack the semantic understanding necessary for complex behavioral adaptation.
- **End-to-end vision-language-action models** (e.g.,) attempt to directly map multimodal inputs to control actions. While they incorporate semantic reasoning, they often incur substantial computational overhead at each control step, making them computationally prohibitive for high-frequency real-time applications. They also typically lack an explicit structured control interface for behavior-aware and constraint-aware adaptations.
- **Decoupled architectures** (e.g.,) aim to mitigate computational issues by separating high-level reasoning from low-level execution. These include dual-rate systems that perform intermittent large-model adjustments.
- **LLM-guided Model Predictive Control (MPC) approaches** (e.g.,) adjust planner parameters within an optimization framework, retaining kinematic safety. However, their invocation mechanisms may not be optimized for dynamic, event-specific triggers.
- **Opportunistic Collaborative Planning (OCP)** (e.g.,) represents an advancement in decoupled architectures by triggering a large vision model only upon detecting *unknown objects* or drops in perception confidence. A limitation identified in the current work is that this strategy may treat different behaviors of a single entity as equally significant, failing to respond to critical behavioral changes of *known* agents (e.g., a known pedestrian suddenly jaywalking) if perception confidence remains high.

The current work identifies a research gap where existing methods either lack semantic understanding, suffer from computational latency, or rely on rigid periodic invocations or insufficient triggering mechanisms that fail to explicitly model behavior-level anomalies. The proposed Event-Adaptive Motion Planning (EAMP) framework aims to address these limitations by focusing on efficient, event-driven semantic adaptation of control objectives in safety-critical situations, thereby improving dynamic safety margins without compromising real-time efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an efficient and robust robot navigation framework, termed Event-Adaptive Motion Planning (EAMP), for safety-critical scenarios characterized by unforeseen semantic events. This framework aims to effectively integrate the high-level commonsense reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with low-level motion control, while overcoming the computational latency typically associated with frequent VLM invocation.

The motivation for this research stems from several challenges in current autonomous navigation systems:
- **Computational Latency of VLMs:** Directly integrating raw VLM outputs into high-frequency control loops introduces significant computational delays, which can destabilize physical execution and render real-time robot operation impractical. The goal is to leverage VLM reasoning only when semantically necessary, without continuous invocation.
- **Limitations in Handling Safety-Critical Corner Cases:** Current systems often struggle with rare but critical interaction events, such as sudden cut-ins or unexpected pedestrian behaviors. These scenarios are sparsely distributed in real-world data, leading to a scarcity of training examples for conventional learning-based methods. Autonomous systems may respond suboptimally, exhibiting overly conservative or, conversely, unsafe behaviors.
- **Lack of Behavior-Level Anomaly Detection:** Existing decoupled VLM integration strategies often rely on rigid periodic invocations or object-level perception confidence drops (e.g., for unknown objects). This approach overlooks critical behavioral anomalies of *known* or *tracked* dynamic agents, making the system vulnerable to rapidly evolving, dangerous interactions where perception confidence remains high.
- **Need for Structured Control Adaptation:** When semantic reasoning identifies a critical situation, the system needs a principled and stable method to translate high-level strategic decisions into specific adjustments of the low-level motion controller. Direct generation of control actions from VLMs can lead to low-resolution or kinematically infeasible trajectories, while unstructured parameter tuning lacks verifiability.

To address these motivations, the EAMP framework specifically aims to:
- Enable self-adaptive navigation that maintains control stability and interpretability.
- Develop a mechanism for behavior-level anomaly detection, activating semantic adaptation only when interaction semantics change.
- Design a module that translates high-level strategy-level decisions from a VLM into structured reconfigurations of Model Predictive Control (MPC) optimization objectives and geometric references.
- Ensure that the proposed system operates efficiently in real-time, balancing improved safety margins with navigation efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Event-Adaptive Motion Planning (EAMP) framework is a hierarchical semantic control architecture designed to decouple high-level semantic reasoning from continuous motion execution. It operates through a three-level design: a prompt-configurable semantic event trigger (PC-SET), a semantic model predictive control (SMPC) module, and an underlying control layer. Real-time reasoning is supported by an offline physically verified semantic distillation pipeline that trains a lightweight VLM.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The robot's local motion execution is governed by a parametric Model Predictive Control (MPC) system. The system state includes planar position and heading angle, and control inputs are longitudinal velocity and steering angle. The MPC problem minimizes a stage cost function `J(x_k, u_k, epsilon_k; theta)` over a prediction horizon `T`. The core idea is to dynamically adjust a strategy-conditioned parameter vector `theta = [w_s, w_u, lambda_epsilon, d_min, alpha_u]`, which includes weighting factors for path tracking (`w_s`), control regulation (`w_u`), slack penalty for obstacle avoidance (`lambda_epsilon`), nominal safety margin (`d_min`), and a scaling factor for reference control input (`alpha_u`). The challenge lies in determining *when* to adapt `theta` and *how* to translate semantic events into structured modifications of the MPC formulation.

#### 3.2. Prompt-Configurable Semantic Event Trigger (PC-SET)

PC-SET functions as a semantic monitor to determine *when* adaptation is required. It runs asynchronously at a lower frequency (up to 3 Hz) compared to the MPC loop, using a lightweight VLM (Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct).
- **Prompt-conditioned semantic assessment:** Instead of continuous object classification, PC-SET evaluates short temporal clips (three recent frames) for dynamic behavioral anomalies. Given a visual clip `I_c` and a semantic prompt `P_trigger`, the VLM `F_gate` outputs a binary anomaly indicator (`1` if anomaly, `0` otherwise) along with a descriptive text only when an anomaly is present.
- **Decision triggering via temporal consistency:** To ensure stability and mitigate effects of transient noise or occlusions, the raw binary indicator (`tau_raw_c`) from the VLM is processed through a temporal consistency mechanism. The final event-adaptive trigger signal (`tau*_c`) is activated only if a minimum number (`m`) of raw triggers occur within a sliding window of length `K`. This discrete signal is then held constant and resampled at the high-frequency MPC time step (`t`), creating a hybrid structure where semantic decisions are event-driven, and continuous motion optimization remains with MPC.

#### 3.3. Semantic Model Predictive Control (SMPC)

The SMPC module addresses *how* high-level semantic intent is embedded into the MPC formulation upon a trigger from PC-SET. It operates at a strategy level, selecting from predefined MPC configuration templates rather than generating direct control inputs or trajectories.
- **Strategy-level semantic decision:** When `tau*_t = 1`, a distilled lightweight VLM, termed SemNav-VLM, infers the most plausible interaction strategy `s_t` from a finite set of predefined strategies `S` (e.g., yielding, cautious following, overtaking), based on current visual context and ego state. The system maintains its prior control configuration during inference latency and locks the result as the active strategy. This design ensures a verifiable interface to MPC by restricting semantic outputs to discrete strategies.
- **Template-based MPC reconfiguration:** Each selected strategy `s_t` is mapped to a predefined, scenario-agnostic MPC configuration template. This involves two mappings:
 - `Gamma(s_t)`: Selects objective and constraint parameters (`theta_t`) based on the strategy.
 - `Psi(s_t, P_nom)`: Adapts the global path (`P_t`) to reflect the strategy's geometric intent.
 These mappings provide a structured update to the MPC problem without altering its core optimization architecture.
- **Event-adaptive switching and stability:** A sustained activation flag (`sigma_t`) is introduced to suppress chattering and ensure consistent execution of maneuvers. `sigma_t` remains active if `tau*_t = 1` or if the system is within a minimum hold duration since the last trigger. This governs whether the nominal MPC configuration (`theta_nom, P_nom`) or the event-adapted configuration (`tilde_theta_t, tilde_P_t`) is used.

#### 3.4. Physically Verified Semantic Distillation

The strategy-level semantic policy `pi_sem` (instantiated by SemNav-VLM) requires mapping visual observations and ego states to discrete interaction strategies. Since ground-truth annotations for interaction strategies are unavailable, a teacher-student distillation paradigm is employed.
- **Teacher model:** A large cloud-based VLM (Gemini-3.0-Flash) acts as the teacher, producing strategy hypotheses under a structured prompt (Fig. 3(b)).
- **Physical verification stage:** Candidate strategies generated by the teacher are not directly used as supervision. Instead, they are validated through closed-loop simulation in CARLA using the SMPC controller. Only strategies that result in physically feasible (collision-free) behavior are retained to form the supervision dataset `D_pv = {(I, x, s) | s in S_feasible(I, x)}`. This ensures that the learned strategies are not only semantically plausible but also dynamically executable.
- **Student model (SemNav-VLM):** The student semantic policy is fine-tuned from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct using LoRA. The distillation objective minimizes the negative log-likelihood of predicting the physically verified strategies over the `D_pv` dataset. This process enables a lightweight VLM to learn robust and safety-preserving strategy reasoning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in CARLA using ROS, simulating highway (Town06) and dense urban (Town10) environments. The ego vehicle was a delivery robot equipped with an RGB camera and LiDAR. A local MPC controller operated at 20 Hz. Baselines included RDA (geometric MPC), PCS (periodic VLM collaboration), and OCP (opportunistic VLM collaboration based on unknown object perception confidence). Performance was evaluated using success rate (SRate), minimum time-to-collision (TTC), finishing time (FTime), trajectory length (TLen), and speed variability (SVar).

#### 4.1. Experiment 1: Evaluation of SemNav-VLM

This experiment assessed the semantic strategy reasoning capability of the distilled SemNav-VLM. A dataset of 2000 training and 200 evaluation samples, annotated via physical verification, was used.
- **Quantitative Results:** SemNav-VLM achieved a strategy accuracy (SAcc) of 96%, outperforming the local base model Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (42%) and the cloud-based teacher Gemini-3.0-Flash (86%). It also exhibited a lower inference latency (1701 ms) compared to the base model (2117 ms) and the cloud API (11569 ms). The model maintained robust accuracy across various scenario difficulties and significantly enhanced reasoning for safety-critical maneuvers (yield, nudge, stop).
- **Qualitative Results:** In scenarios like lead vehicle braking (cut-in), pedestrian crossing, and slow traffic, SemNav-VLM consistently inferred appropriate strategies (overtake, stop, nudge, respectively) that led to safe and efficient actions for the downstream controller. In contrast, the base Qwen3-VL model often proposed overly cautious or misjudged strategies (e.g., nudge for a cut-in, yield for a pedestrian requiring an immediate stop), potentially compromising safety or efficiency.

#### 4.2. Experiment 2: Evaluation of EAMP

This experiment evaluated the overall EAMP framework in custom-built smart logistics scenarios in CARLA Town10, with increasing difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard) involving pedestrian crossings and two-wheeler cut-ins.
- **Quantitative Results:** EAMP demonstrated superior safety margins. In the hard scenario, it achieved a TTC of 1.77 s, representing a 32% improvement over OCP (1.34 s) and 30% over PCS (1.36 s). This safety advantage was attributed to PC-SET's ability to identify behavioral anomalies proactively. EAMP also achieved the fastest completion time (34.2 s) in hard conditions and maintained lower speed variability (1.40 m/s), indicating smoother control and efficiency without conservative detours. Similar advantages in safety and efficiency were observed across easy and medium scenarios.
- **Qualitative Comparison:** In a hard scenario involving sequential pedestrian jaywalking and two-wheeler cut-ins (Fig. 6), RDA reacted abruptly due to reliance on instantaneous spatial constraints. OCP, while reacting to static obstacles, failed to trigger timely semantic intervention for known entities exhibiting sudden behavioral changes because its perception confidence remained high. EAMP, by explicitly targeting behavioral anomalies via PC-SET, generated precisely timed adaptations for early yielding and proactive defensive following, resulting in smoother deceleration and safer motion.

#### 4.3. Experiment 3: Semantic Triggering Analysis

This experiment specifically investigated the temporal performance of semantic triggering, focusing on dynamic behavioral anomalies where perception confidence remains stable. A CARLA Town06 scenario (Fig. 7) included a static obstacle and a recognized leading vehicle that abruptly braked.
- **Quantitative Results:** EAMP achieved a 95% SRate compared to OCP’s 60%. This indicated OCP's vulnerability when thoroughly tracked entities exhibit sudden behavioral shifts. EAMP also reduced average FTime to 37.85 s, TLen to 113.29 m, and SVar to 1.32 m/s, demonstrating efficiency and smoothness.
- **Qualitative Comparison:** Both OCP and EAMP successfully triggered reasoning for an unknown static obstacle. However, when a *recognized* leading vehicle abruptly braked, OCP's object-level perception confidence remained high, preventing a secondary semantic response. This led to reliance on myopic geometric braking and subsequent stagnation. EAMP's PC-SET, by evaluating interaction dynamics, identified the hazardous deceleration, generating a timely secondary trigger. This allowed the controller to proactively reconfigure for a lane change, preserving momentum and averting collision, highlighting the effectiveness of behavior-level anomaly detection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Event-Adaptive Motion Planning (EAMP) framework contributes to the field of autonomous navigation by addressing critical limitations in integrating high-level semantic reasoning with low-level control, particularly in safety-critical and dynamic environments.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Dynamic Safety:** By proactively detecting behavioral anomalies through the Prompt-Configurable Semantic Event Trigger (PC-SET), EAMP enables timely adaptation of control objectives. This leads to significantly improved dynamic safety margins (as evidenced by higher minimum TTC values) compared to methods relying on periodic invocations or object-level perception confidence, which can react too late to unexpected behaviors of known agents.
- **Computational Efficiency and Real-time Operation:** The framework mitigates the computational latency associated with large VLMs by employing an event-driven triggering mechanism and utilizing a lightweight, distilled SemNav-VLM. This allows the system to leverage VLM's commonsense reasoning only when semantically necessary, preserving real-time efficiency without constant, resource-intensive VLM queries.
- **Interpretable and Stable Control Adaptation:** The Semantic Model Predictive Control (SMPC) module translates high-level strategy-level decisions into structured reconfigurations of MPC objectives and geometric references. This template-based approach ensures that semantic adaptations are systematic, verifiable, and maintain the underlying kinematic feasibility and stability of the MPC controller, avoiding direct, unstructured perturbations to the optimizer.
- **Physically Verified Semantic Learning:** The proposed physically verified semantic distillation paradigm provides a robust method for training strategy-level policies in the absence of explicit ground-truth annotations for interaction strategies. By validating teacher-generated strategies through closed-loop physical simulation, the student VLM (SemNav-VLM) learns dynamically feasible and safe behaviors.

The potential impact of EAMP is substantial for the deployment of autonomous robots in human-centric applications, such as logistics and inspection tasks, where interaction with dynamic agents is frequent and safety is paramount. The framework's ability to respond adaptively and efficiently to unforeseen semantic events could:
- Reduce collision rates in complex urban scenarios.
- Improve the efficiency of robotic operations by avoiding overly conservative or unnecessary detours.
- Increase public trust and acceptance of autonomous systems by ensuring safer and smoother interactions with humans and other road users.
- Provide a blueprint for future robot navigation systems that require a principled way to integrate advanced AI reasoning with robust, safety-critical control.

Future work includes deploying the EAMP framework on physical mobile robots to validate its performance in real-world conditions and extending event-adaptive reasoning to cover a richer set of real-world interaction scenarios beyond those simulated in CARLA.
