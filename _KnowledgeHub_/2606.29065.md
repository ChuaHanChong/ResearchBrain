# A Unified Framework for Multi-Contact Path Planning in the Rolling Robot Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified framework is developed for multi-contact path planning in spherical rolling robotic systems, enabling collision-free and kinematically consistent motions. The framework successfully generates C2-continuous trajectories for systems with multiple rolling spheres while maintaining no-slip constraints, achieving a practical balance between path quality and computation time with approximately 200 Voronoi seeds for sub-2-second planning.

## Problem

- Multi-contact rolling systems face significant challenges due to strongly coupled nonholonomic velocity constraints across multiple bodies, making admissible motion computation difficult.
- Integrating these complex coupled kinematics directly with obstacle avoidance in path planning introduces substantial computational challenges.
- A comprehensive and widely adopted framework that effectively addresses both coupled kinematics and obstacle avoidance for multi-contact rolling path planning was missing.

## Method

- A ==decoupled planning strategy== is introduced, separating clearance-aware geometric path generation from the complex kinematic reconstruction of multi-contact rolling motions.
- A ==Voronoi-based roadmap== is constructed directly on the spherical contact manifold to generate collision-free paths that inherently promote maximum clearance from obstacles.
- Discrete paths are refined using intrinsic ==Riemannian log–exp smoothing== to produce ==C2-continuous== trajectories, and multi-contact kinematics are reconstructed using a specialized ==Montana-based model==.

## Results

- The framework successfully generated feasible, collision-free, and C2-continuous paths for systems with a primary sphere and **three** secondary rolling spheres navigating around **three** spherical obstacles.
- An optimal density of approximately **200** Voronoi seeds provided a practical trade-off, minimizing sharp turns in paths to near **zero or one** while keeping computation time around **1-1.5 seconds**.
- The reconstructed rolling states for all contacts demonstrated continuity and boundedness, validating the numerical stability and consistency of the inverse kinematic process with the smoothed geometric paths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling geometric planning from kinematic reconstruction significantly simplifies the otherwise complex and computationally demanding multi-contact path planning problem.
> - Using Voronoi diagrams on the spherical manifold intrinsically generates paths with optimal clearance from obstacles, improving robustness for rolling motions.
> - Achieving C2-continuous trajectories through manifold-consistent smoothing is crucial for stable and physically realizable no-slip multi-contact rolling, as it avoids abrupt changes in direction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Rolling contact constitutes a fundamental mechanism in robotics, facilitating constrained and repeatable motion across various applications. This includes spherical reconfigurable robots and dexterous manipulation, where rolling contact enables precise control through nonholonomic velocity constraints that couple rotation and translation via contact geometry. While single-contact rolling, particularly in canonical ball-plate scenarios, is a well-studied area, the presence of multiple simultaneous rolling contacts introduces significant complexities.

Historically, kinematic models for rolling contact have evolved from curvature-based formulations, such as those by Woronetz, to the more widely adopted Montana contact equations. The Montana framework expresses local contact-state rates using geometric operators and relative body motion. In multi-contact settings, the nonholonomic velocity constraints become interdependent across multiple contacts and bodies, leading to reduced maneuverability and increased difficulty in computing and coordinating admissible motions. Previous work on multi-contact rolling has investigated trapped-object manipulation with multiple contacts and systematically assembled multi-contact models from Montana-type building blocks.

Despite these advancements, a comprehensive and widely adopted framework for multi-contact rolling path planning, especially when incorporating coupled kinematics and obstacle avoidance, has been largely absent. Direct integration of coupled multi-contact kinematics into path planning introduces substantial computational challenges. These challenges are amplified in scenarios involving obstacle avoidance in three-dimensional environments, aligning with broader observations in path planning for ground robots and Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) where collision avoidance in cluttered or dynamic environments remains a primary bottleneck. Furthermore, for multi-body rolling on a host surface, admissible motions are confined to a curved 2D manifold embedded in R³, and other rolling bodies can occlude parts of the usable contact workspace, further limiting feasible clearance.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a decoupled planning strategy. It prioritizes geometric separation and collision avoidance before enforcing rolling-consistent kinematics. By separating "where to go" (clearance-aware geometric planning) from "how to roll" (nonholonomic multi-contact reconstruction), the work aims to make the coupled multi-contact path planning problem more tractable. This approach positions the work as a foundational contribution to enabling complex, coordinated behaviors in multi-contact spherical rolling systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework for multi-contact path planning in spherical rolling robotic systems operating under no-slip constraints, explicitly incorporating obstacle avoidance.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in multi-contact rolling:

- **Nonholonomic Constraints and Configuration Manifolds:** Rolling contact introduces nonholonomic velocity constraints, meaning that the system's velocity depends on its current configuration, not just on the control inputs. Moreover, the configuration of rolling bodies evolves on curved manifolds, which complicates direct path planning in the full state space.
- **Coupled Multi-Contact Dynamics:** In systems with multiple simultaneous rolling contacts, the nonholonomic constraints become interdependent across different contacts and bodies. This strong coupling significantly reduces the system's maneuverability and makes the computation and coordination of admissible motions substantially more difficult compared to single-contact scenarios.
- **Computational Complexity of Integrated Planning:** Directly embedding these coupled multi-contact kinematics into a path planning algorithm, particularly when simultaneous obstacle avoidance is required, results in considerable computational demands. This complexity presents a significant barrier to practical implementation.
- **Constrained Workspace and Clearance:** For rolling bodies moving on a host surface, admissible motions are restricted to a curved 2D manifold. The presence of obstacles or other rolling bodies can occlude portions of this manifold, further reducing the available free space and requiring careful clearance management.
- **Lack of a Unified Framework:** Prior to this work, a widely adopted and systematic framework for multi-contact rolling path planning that effectively addresses both coupled kinematics and obstacle avoidance was identified as missing in the literature.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the proposed framework are:

1. **Kinematic Model Derivation:** To derive a compact kinematic model specifically for multi-sphere rolling systems, utilizing Montana’s contact-coordinate formulation. This model represents each contact using a stacked five-state vector, providing a structured basis for understanding contact evolution.
2. **Voronoi-Based Roadmap Construction:** To construct a Voronoi-based roadmap directly on the spherical contact manifold. This approach leverages Voronoi diagrams to inherently promote clearance from obstacles and provide a structured representation of spatial separation.
3. **On-Manifold Collision Checking:** To integrate spherical-cap obstacles and mutual-exclusion regions into the planning process through efficient on-manifold collision checking, ensuring that generated paths are collision-free.
4. **Manifold-Consistent Path Smoothing:** To refine discrete graph paths, obtained from the Voronoi roadmap, using manifold-consistent log–exp smoothing techniques. This aims to produce differentiable and C2-continuous trajectories that are physically more realizable for rolling systems.
5. **Kinematic Reconstruction and Validation:** To lift the smoothed surface paths to admissible multi-contact rolling motions by employing the derived Montana kinematics. These motions are then validated through forward simulation to confirm adherence to no-slip constraints.
6. **Performance Evaluation:** To evaluate the feasibility and quality of the generated paths, considering trade-offs between trajectory smoothness, Voronoi seed density, and computation time.
7. **Decoupling Planning Stages:** To establish a decoupled pipeline where clearance-aware geometric path generation is performed separately from the reconstruction of kinematically consistent multi-contact rolling motions. This separation is intended to make the overall planning problem more tractable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The presented framework introduces a unified pipeline for multi-contact path planning, structured into a kinematic modeling phase, a Voronoi-based path planning stage, and an inverse kinematic reconstruction phase.

#### 3.1. Kinematic Model (Section II)

The foundation of the framework is a specialized multi-contact rolling model based on Montana’s contact-coordinate formulation. This model is adapted for a primary sphere (radius R_o) interacting with `n` secondary spheres (radii R_f,i). Each individual contact `i` is described by a five-state vector `χ_i = [η^T_o,i η^T_f,i ψ_i]^T`, where `η_o,i` and `η_f,i` represent local surface coordinates on the primary and secondary spheres, respectively, and `ψ_i` is the relative spin angle. These individual contact states are stacked into a global contact state vector `χ ∈ R^(5n)`.

The generalized multi-contact kinematics are compactly expressed as `˙χ = D_o ν_o + D_f ν_f`. Here, `ν_o ∈ R^6` is the twist (angular and linear velocities) of the primary sphere, and `ν_f ∈ R^(6n)` is the stacked twist of all secondary spheres. The matrices `D_o ∈ R^(5n×6)` and `D_f ∈ R^(5n×6n)` capture how these twists drive the evolution of the contact coordinates, with `D_f` being block-diagonal, indicating per-contact contributions. The paper details the derivation of differential-geometric terms, such as curvatures `K` and shape operators `M`, specific to spherical surfaces, which are essential for constructing the full kinematic matrices. The consistency of this derived model is verified through a simulation involving four simultaneous contacts, where the coupled no-slip constraints are observed to be maintained during motion.

#### 3.2. Voronoi-Based Path Planning (Section III)

The planning process focuses on computing collision-free, piecewise smooth surface trajectories for the primary-side contact points, represented as unit directions `s_o,i(σ) ∈ S^2`. The primary sphere is considered fixed at the workspace center and undergoes pure rotation.

1. **Problem Statement:** The objective is to compute trajectories `s_d,o,i(σ)` on the spherical manifold that connect initial and goal contact configurations, while respecting obstacle avoidance. Secondary-side variables (`u_f,i`, `v_f,i`, `ψ_i`) are treated as induced and are reconstructed later. The generated trajectories must be at least C1 (and C2 after smoothing) for stable kinematic reconstruction.

2. **Seed Sampling and Obstacle Modeling:**
 - **Seed Generation:** To initialize the spherical Voronoi roadmap, a set of approximately uniform seed directions on the unit sphere `S^2` is generated using a best-candidate (max-min) strategy. This method maximizes angular separation between samples, leading to a "blue-noise-like" distribution, superior to simple random projection.
 - **Obstacle Representation:** Stationary obstacles are modeled as spherical caps `O = {(c_obs,j, ρ_j)}`. These forbidden regions are inflated by a safety margin `ϵ ≥ 0` and an angular footprint `α_sec ≥ 0` that accounts for the secondary body's clearance. The effective angular radius for collision checking is `ρ_eff,k = ρ_k + α_sec + ϵ`. Seeds are filtered to ensure they lie outside these inflated forbidden regions.

3. **Roadmap Construction:**
 - A spherical Voronoi tessellation is constructed from the filtered seeds, yielding Voronoi vertices `V_vor` (approximating medial-axis locations) and an initial edge set `E_vor`.
 - **Boundary Reinforcement:** To address potential degeneracies and sparsity of Voronoi vertices near inflated obstacle caps, a structured boundary embedding mechanism is introduced. For each inflated spherical cap, a regular spherical hexagon is constructed as a circumscribed boundary structure. The vertices of these hexagons (`H_k`) are connected by collision-free geodesic arcs to form `E_hex`.
 - **Bridging Edges:** To enhance connectivity and prevent graph fragmentation, especially in narrow passages, additional "bridging" edges (`E_bridge`) are introduced between mutually-visible hexagonal boundary vertices within the same cap.
 - The complete edge set `E = E_vor ∪ E_hex ∪ E_bridge` defines the final undirected weighted graph `G = (V, E)`.

4. **Path Search and Smoothing:**
 - Start and goal configurations are connected to nearby graph vertices. A weighted shortest path is then computed using a modified Dijkstra’s algorithm on the spherical manifold, resulting in a discrete sequence of vertices `P_d`.
 - **Manifold Smoothing:** The discrete path `P_d` is then subjected to a Riemannian log–exp smoothing procedure. This involves mapping the spherical points to a tangent plane via the logarithmic map, performing cubic interpolation on these tangent coordinates to achieve C1-continuity (and C2 after smoothing), and finally mapping the smoothed points back to the sphere using the exponential map. This ensures that the smoothing process is intrinsic to the spherical manifold, preserving the constraint and producing differentiable trajectories suitable for rolling.

#### 3.3. Inverse Kinematics Based on the Montana Framework (Section III.C)

This stage reconstructs the full multi-contact rolling motion from the smoothed primary-side surface trajectories.
- For each contact `i`, the planner-provided primary-side surface trajectory `η_o,i(t) = [u_o,i(t), v_o,i(t)]^T` is used to determine the required relative angular velocity components `ω_xy,i` in the local contact frame. This is achieved via `ω_xy,i = g_o,i^-1(χ_i) ˙η_o,i`, where `g_o,i(χ_i)` is the primary-side angular Jacobian.
- The remaining contact-state rates (`˙η_f,i`, `˙ψ_i`) are then reconstructed linearly from `ω_xy,i` and the normal spin `ω_z,i` (typically set to zero for pure in-surface rolling).
- For simultaneous handling of all contacts, the process is generalized by stacking `˙η_o` and `ω_xy` to obtain `ω_xy = G_o(χ)^-1 ˙η_o`.
- Integrating these reconstructed rates yields the full stacked contact trajectory `χ(t)`, ensuring that all coupled no-slip constraints derived from the Montana kinematics are satisfied. The inverse map utilizes the current integrated state `χ_i(t)` to evaluate `g_o,i(χ_i)`, starting from the initial contact configuration `χ_i(0)`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed framework was conducted using MATLAB simulations on a system comprising a primary sphere and three secondary spheres, with three spherical obstacles.

- **Feasibility and Connectivity of Roadmap:** The Voronoi-based graph search successfully generated feasible, collision-free paths connecting defined start and goal configurations for all three secondary spheres (Fig. 5a, 5b). The roadmap demonstrated robust connectivity across the admissible spherical surface, even when confronted with relatively large inflated forbidden regions. The planned routes consistently maintained significant clearance from obstacle boundaries, which is crucial for robust rolling motion realization, as near-boundary motions are more sensitive to local curvature and discretization.

- **Effectiveness of Path Smoothing:** The raw discrete paths, which inherently contained piecewise geodesic segments and sharp corners, were effectively transformed into smooth, C2-continuous trajectories through the intrinsic log–exp interpolation (Fig. 5c). This smoothing process replaced abrupt directional changes with regular arcs, which is essential for physically realizing no-slip rolling, as sudden changes in direction are challenging to execute under such constraints.

- **Trade-off between Path Quality and Computational Cost:** A quantitative analysis (Fig. 6) investigated the relationship between the number of Voronoi seeds (`N_seeds`), computation time, and the number of sharp turns in the generated path.
 - **Low Seed Counts (e.g., <100):** Resulted in faster computation times (below approximately 1 second) but produced paths with a higher number of sharp turns (up to 4). Such paths were deemed too coarse for reliable rolling realization due to trajectory being dominated by graph corners.
 - **Optimal Seed Count (N_seeds ≈ 200):** This regime was identified as a practical trade-off, where the number of sharp turns significantly decreased to near zero or one, while the computation time remained moderate (approximately 1-1.5 seconds).
 - **High Seed Counts (e.g., >400):** Further increasing seed density (up to 1000) led to only marginal improvements in path regularity (i.e., further reduction in sharp turns) but resulted in a notable increase in computation time (ranging from 2 to 4 seconds). This indicated diminishing returns beyond `N_seeds ≈ 200`.

- **Kinematic Reconstruction Validation:** The reconstructed rolling states (u_f, v_f, ψ) for all three contacts demonstrated continuity and boundedness over the entire 10-second simulation horizon (Fig. 5d). No discontinuities or oscillatory artifacts were observed. Even for paths exhibiting significant geometric correction during the smoothing phase (e.g., Path 2), the recovered kinematic states remained smooth and numerically stable. This finding supports the conclusion that the quality of the geometric path directly influences the conditioning and numerical stability of the inverse rolling reconstruction stage. Smoother and better-cleared surface trajectories reduce the burden on kinematic reconstruction, increasing the likelihood of physically realizable motions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses critical challenges in multi-contact path planning for rolling robotic systems, establishing a foundation with several significant implications:

- **Foundation for Multi-Contact Rolling Robotics:** The framework provides a principled and unified approach for planning admissible motions for multiple rolling bodies. This is particularly relevant for systems where previous methodologies faced limitations in handling coupled nonholonomic constraints alongside obstacle avoidance.
- **Enhanced Path Quality and Realizability:** By incorporating intrinsic log–exp smoothing on the spherical manifold, the framework generates C2-continuous trajectories that are geometrically consistent and physically more realizable for rolling robots than traditional piecewise-geodesic graph paths. This improves the likelihood of stable and robust execution on physical platforms.
- **Tractability Through Decoupling:** The key contribution of decoupling geometric obstacle avoidance (using a Voronoi roadmap) from the complex multi-contact kinematic reconstruction process makes the overall planning problem significantly more tractable. This separation reduces the computational complexity associated with simultaneously solving nonholonomic constraints and collision avoidance.
- **Robust Clearance-Aware Planning:** The Voronoi-based approach inherently promotes paths with maximum clearance from obstacles. The additional boundary reinforcement via hexagonal rings and bridging edges enhances roadmap connectivity and robustness, especially in cluttered environments or narrow passages, leading to safer and more reliable trajectories.
- **Applicability to Reconfigurable Robots:** The framework directly supports applications such as spherical reconfigurable robots, where multiple modules must traverse a host sphere while maintaining continuous, no-slip contact. It suggests practical grouped planning strategies, where subsets of contacts are planned iteratively, consistent with coordinated multi-module systems.
- **Relevance to Dexterous Manipulation:** The method has direct implications for rolling-contact dexterous manipulation, where robotic fingertips must reorient an object by rolling on its surface. The core planning structure can be extended to incorporate secondary-side constraints relevant to grasping, such as fingertip workspace limits or preferred spin evolutions.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper outlines several avenues for future work, which highlight the extensibility and long-term impact of the framework:
 - **Generalization to Non-Spherical Geometries:** Extending the method to plan on general convex and non-convex surfaces represented by meshes or point clouds, moving beyond the current spherical host surface.
 - **Dynamic Environments and Uncertainty:** Incorporating online replanning capabilities to handle time-varying obstacle environments and uncertainty in contact estimation, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
 - **Experimental Validation:** Conducting experimental validation on physical rolling robotic platforms, such as spherical reconfigurable robots or rolling-contact grasping devices, to demonstrate practical utility and refine the approach based on real-world performance.

In conclusion, this work provides a robust and computationally efficient framework that addresses a significant gap in multi-contact rolling path planning, paving the way for more advanced and autonomous robotic systems.
