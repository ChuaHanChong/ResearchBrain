# Scaffolding Foundation Models into Physical-World Agents Pushes the Frontier of Long-Horizon Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> NavMCP is an agentic scaffolding framework that couples a VLM reasoning agent with a navigation foundation model executor through three channels: intent, observation, and memory. The VLM decides what evidence to seek and when to stop, while the navigator grounds semantic sub-goals into closed-loop rollouts. Without retraining either model, it sets state of the art on three embodied-question-answering benchmarks and reaches 78.3% real-robot success.

## Problem

- Vision-language models can plan and infer missing information, but repeatedly grounding their high-level decisions into low-level navigation actions stays brittle and inefficient over long horizons.
- Navigation foundation models execute semantic goals robustly yet run as bounded episodes, never deciding what to seek next or retaining task state across calls.
- Standard episodic tool interfaces return only terminal status and a final view, discarding en-route evidence and accumulating no searched regions, negative findings, or unresolved goals.

## Method

- ==Intent channel== converts an evidence need into a structured navigation call with mode (object-goal or instruction-following), natural-language sub-goal, step budget, and constraints, avoiding low-level control.
- ==Observation channel== samples keyframes along the whole rollout and runs a ==VLM trajectory summarizer== producing a source-grounded journey artifact with observed objects, uncertainty labels, and unexplored-exit cues.
- ==Memory channel== maintains an ==EQA context state== of compact history, an ==evidence ledger==, and unresolved goals, externalizing salient findings before conservative raw-trace compression.

## Results

- Sets state of the art with **76.7%** on HM-EQA, **54.4%** on MT-HM3D, and **79.27** LLM Score on EXPRESS-Bench, at the lowest normalized agent-step fractions (**0.15**, **0.19**).
- Under matched agent, episodes and toolset, reaches **74.0%** versus **63.5%** for FAST-EQA; swapping the three-channel protocol for an episodic interface costs **14.9** points.
- On a Unitree Go2 it attains **78.3%** overall success versus **48.3%** frontier and **38.3%** reactive, with margins growing from **10** to **45** points across horizon tiers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complementary foundation models can be composed by interface design alone; the protocol between reasoner and executor is worth more than retraining either component.
> - Discarding en-route observations is the costliest interface flaw, so tool returns for embodied agents should stream trajectory evidence rather than terminal outcomes.
> - Scaffolding gains scale with task horizon and with either layer's strength, but cross-view duplication still degrades counting and large VLMs slow inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of embodied artificial intelligence, large vision-language models (VLMs), and navigation foundation models (NFMs). The broader landscape of embodied agents aims to develop intelligent systems capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting within physical environments over extended periods. This endeavor requires agents to manage complex, long-horizon tasks, which typically involve sequential decision-making, information gathering, and adaptive planning.

Prior work has seen advancements in both VLMs and NFMs. VLMs demonstrate capabilities in high-level reasoning, goal decomposition, and adapting plans based on inferred information. However, directly translating VLM reasoning into precise, low-level navigation actions has proven to be inefficient and susceptible to errors over long horizons. Conversely, NFMs have become proficient at executing specific semantic goals, such as instruction following, object navigation, or target following, by mapping egocentric observations to closed-loop actions. Yet, these models typically operate within bounded episodes, lacking the capacity for persistent task-level reasoning or autonomous decision-making regarding subsequent exploration goals.

The paper identifies a "complementarity" between these two types of foundation models. It positions its work as an effort to bridge the "episodic interface gap" that arises when attempting to integrate these specialized models. Traditional tool-augmented agent frameworks often treat navigation models as episodic tools, where a single instruction yields a terminal outcome, losing intermediate information and preventing cross-call accumulation of evidence. This work moves beyond such episodic interfaces by proposing a structured scaffolding framework that enables persistent, evidence-centric interaction, thereby extending both the VLM's action horizon and the NFM's reasoning horizon. The research contributes to the development of more capable and efficient long-horizon physical-world agents by synergizing existing foundation models rather than pursuing a single, monolithic solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of current foundation models in enabling long-horizon physical-world agency. Building such agents necessitates two distinct, yet interconnected, capabilities: high-level reasoning over distant task goals and continuous grounding of decisions into reliable closed-loop actions.

The paper highlights specific deficiencies in existing approaches:
1. **VLM Limitations:** While vision-language models excel at inferring missing information, decomposing complex goals, and adapting high-level plans, their direct and repeated grounding of these decisions into fine-grained navigation actions is characterized as brittle and inefficient, especially over extended task durations.
2. **NFM Limitations:** Navigation foundation models are robust at executing specific semantic goals, mapping observations to actions effectively. However, their operation is typically bounded within single episodes, meaning they lack the capacity for persistent task-level reasoning, such as deciding what information to seek next or retaining task state across multiple navigation attempts.

The authors contend that these limitations are not inherent flaws but rather complementary aspects, indicating a natural division of labor. The core challenge is not to develop a single, more powerful model that performs both reasoning and navigation independently, but to effectively organize and integrate their specialized capabilities into a coherent, persistent agent.

This led to the formulation of the following key objectives:
- **Develop an Agentic Scaffolding Framework:** The primary objective is to introduce NavMCP, a framework designed to couple a VLM reasoning agent with an NFM executor. This framework aims to enable long-horizon physical-world exploration by allowing the VLM to handle strategic decisions (what evidence to seek, where to search, when to stop) while the NFM is responsible for grounding these semantic sub-goals into reliable, closed-loop navigation.
- **Address the "Episodic Interface Gap":** The paper specifically targets three critical mismatches inherent in conventional tool-augmented agent interfaces, which hinder persistent physical-world agency:
 - **Mismatch between Instruction and Intent:** Conventional route-level instructions from an agent to a navigator often fail to convey the underlying evidence-seeking intent, search mode, budget, or constraints required for effective exploration. The objective is to design a channel that translates the agent's complex evidence needs into structured semantic navigation calls.
 - **Intermediate Observation Loss:** In typical episodic interactions, only terminal status and a final observation are returned. This results in the loss of valuable intermediate evidence observed along the navigation path (e.g., objects glimpsed in a hallway, rooms partially explored), which could be crucial for subsequent reasoning. The objective is to create a mechanism to convert complete navigation rollouts into source-grounded trajectory evidence.
 - **Lack of Cross-Call Accumulation:** Without a persistent state, independent navigation calls do not retain information about regions already searched, hypotheses ruled out, uncertain findings, or negative outcomes. This forces the agent to repeatedly infer context. The objective is to establish a memory mechanism that accumulates findings, negative evidence, and unresolved goals across multiple calls, providing a persistent EQA context state.
- **Validate on Embodied Question Answering (EQA):** To demonstrate the framework's effectiveness, a specific and demanding testbed, Embodied Question Answering, was chosen. EQA requires an agent to actively explore a physical environment to acquire visual evidence before answering situated questions, providing a robust evaluation of long-horizon agency, search hypothesis formation, observation accumulation, and decision-making regarding evidence sufficiency.

Through these objectives, the research aims to establish an effective paradigm for building long-horizon physical-world agents by fostering a structured collaboration between complementary foundation models without requiring retraining of either core model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NavMCP framework is designed to integrate a VLM as the reasoning agent with an NFM as the embodied executor through a structured, evidence-centric scaffolding protocol. This methodology addresses the limitations of episodic tool interfaces by introducing three specialized channels: Intent, Observation, and Memory.

#### 3.1. System Formulation

The system operates as an agent-executor interface. Given a question and an initial observation, the VLM agent's role is to gather sufficient visual evidence to answer the question, deciding where to search and when to stop. The agent maintains task hypotheses, evidence needs, and context. The NFM executor, instantiated with Qwen-RobotNav, translates semantic sub-goals into short-horizon waypoint trajectories using egocentric observations and navigation state. The VLM agent emits an evidence need, the NFM executes a rollout, and the resulting trajectory-level observations are then converted back into evidence for the VLM agent. This separation allows the executor to provide grounded experience and the agent to perform evidence reasoning.

#### 3.2. Intent Channel: From Evidence Needs to Navigation Calls

This channel translates the VLM agent's abstract evidence needs into concrete navigation episodes for the NFM.
- **Agent's Role:** The VLM agent, reasoning about missing information, emits a semantic navigation call that specifies a `mode`, a natural-language `sub-goal`, a `step budget`, and optional `constraints`. This allows the agent to articulate *what* to search for (e.g., "search the kitchen counters for a coffee maker") without micromanaging low-level movement.
- **Executor Adapter:** This component converts the semantic call into an instruction for the NFM. Two primary modes are exposed:
 - `navigate_to_object`: For object-centric search, aligning with Object Navigation tasks.
 - `navigate_by_instruction`: For broader language-guided movement, aligning with Vision-and-Language Navigation tasks.
The NFM executor receives only egocentric RGB views, pose, and an online map. This design ensures that the NFM can be replaced if it supports the same intent modes and return format, enabling comparative evaluations.

#### 3.3. Observation Channel: From Trajectories to Evidence

This channel converts the NFM's embodied rollouts into agent-facing evidence, preventing the loss of intermediate observations.
- **Raw Trajectory Record:** After a navigation call, the executor returns a raw record comprising rollout status, path information, final pose, and *sampled keyframes*. Keyframes are sampled periodically (e.g., every four simulator steps, capped at 16 frames per call) to capture observations along the entire route, not just at termination.
- **VLM-based Trajectory Summarizer:** A VLM processes the raw trajectory record and sampled keyframes to produce a "grounded journey artifact." This artifact summarizes sub-goal status, route context, salient observed objects, planning cues (e.g., unexplored exits), and any uncertainties.
- **Source-Grounded Evidence:** Each object or room mention in the artifact is linked to its source keyframe and a confidence label. The summarizer is instructed to report only visually observed evidence, explicitly mark uncertainty, and avoid inferring definitive negative claims from limited views. This ensures that the evidence is verifiable and reliable.

#### 3.4. Memory Channel: From Tool Returns to EQA Context State

This channel accumulates trajectory evidence into a compact EQA context state, addressing the lack of cross-call accumulation.
- **EQA Context State (`C_t`):** This state is represented as a tuple `(H_t, E_t, U_t)`.
 - `H_t`: A compact interaction history.
 - `E_t`: A source-grounded evidence ledger. This ledger stores positive observations, records of searched areas, uncertain findings, negative evidence, and support for candidate decisions.
 - `U_t`: Stores unresolved goals for future planning.
- **Update Mechanism:** After each journey artifact or auxiliary tool return, `E_t` and `U_t` are updated with source references.
- **Conservative Context Compression:** Raw tool returns are compressed (e.g., replaced with lightweight placeholders) only after their salient information has been externalized and referenced within `E_t` or `U_t`. This policy ensures that critical, source-grounded evidence persists even when older raw traces are shortened under context limits.
- **EQA Instantiation Details:** For EQA, the agent is permitted to answer only when a response is explicitly supported by current observations, a journey artifact, a reviewed keyframe, or a notebook entry. Positive answers must cite observed evidence and its location, while negative answers require coverage records of likely target locations. Auxiliary perception tools (e.g., panoramic inspection, object detection, close-up inspection) are integrated to refine or verify ledger entries.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

The framework was evaluated on three EQA benchmarks: HM-EQA (single-question search), MT-HM3D (multi-target questions), and EXPRESS-Bench (free-form questions, path quality). Baselines included various EQA systems like Explore-EQA, Memory-EQA, Graph-EQA, ToolEQA, and FAST-EQA, along with internal ablations (e.g., Random Walk, Frontier Exploration for navigation; No Agent, Reactive Agent for VLM control). Performance was measured using accuracy, LLM Score, E_path (exploration path quality), and a "Normalized Equivalent Agent Steps" metric that accounts for both VLM invocations and NFM rollout distances. The main results utilized Qwen3.6-Plus as the VLM agent and Qwen-RobotNav as the NFM executor, with controlled ablations using Qwen3.5. Real-robot transfer was tested on a Unitree Go2 across tasks of increasing horizon.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate NavMCP focused on four key questions, demonstrating the framework's effectiveness and the contributions of its individual components.

#### 4.1. Overall EQA Performance (Q1)

NavMCP demonstrated improved performance across multiple EQA benchmarks:
- **State-of-the-Art Results:** On HM-EQA, NavMCP achieved 76.7% accuracy, surpassing FAST-EQA by 7.5 percentage points. For MT-HM3D, it reached 54.4% accuracy, a 3.9-point improvement. On EXPRESS-Bench, NavMCP obtained an LLM Score of 79.27 and an E_path of 33.96, leading FAST-EQA by 10.57 and 4.71 points, respectively.
- **Efficiency:** These performance gains were accompanied by improved efficiency. NavMCP exhibited the lowest normalized agent-step fractions (0.15 on HM-EQA, 0.19 on MT-HM3D) among reporting methods, indicating a more effective use of high-level VLM invocations, and achieved the highest path efficiency on EXPRESS-Bench.
- **Matched System Comparison:** Under controlled conditions (using the same Qwen3.5 agent, episodes, initial states, and budget for all methods, and providing ToolEQA with the same perception toolset), NavMCP with Qwen-RobotNav-8B achieved 74.0% accuracy on HM-EQA, outperforming FAST-EQA by 10.5 points. This sustained advantage under matched conditions suggests the framework's inherent architectural benefits.

#### 4.2. Complementarity of VLM Agent and NFM Executor (Q2)

Ablation studies confirmed the complementary roles of the VLM agent and NFM executor:
- **Impact of Stronger Agent:** With the NFM (Qwen-RobotNav-8B) fixed, EQA accuracy increased from 38.2% (no agent) through 58.4% (single-round agent) and 62.0% (reactive agent) to 74.0% (full Qwen3.5 agent), reaching 76.7% with Qwen3.6-Plus. This indicated that a capable NFM is insufficient without a sophisticated upper-level agent for adaptive planning and evidence accumulation.
- **Impact of Stronger Executor:** With the VLM agent and NavMCP harness fixed, accuracy improved from 60.9% (Random Walk) and 65.3% (Frontier Exploration) to 69.3% (StreamVLN), 73.3% (Qwen-RobotNav-4B), and 74.0% (Qwen-RobotNav-8B). This demonstrated that a capable upper layer cannot fully compensate for a weak navigation executor, as stronger language-conditioned navigation leads to more useful trajectories and observations.
The results collectively suggest that both layers contribute distinctly, and improvements in either layer enhance overall performance, confirming their complementary nature.

#### 4.3. Efficacy of Closing the Episodic Interface Gap (Q3)

The ablation of NavMCP's three-channel protocol revealed their individual and combined contributions:
- **Total Interface Gap Cost:** Replacing the full three-channel protocol with a basic episodic interface resulted in a significant accuracy drop from 74.0% to 59.1% (a 14.9 percentage point reduction). This quantified the combined cost of the intent, observation, and memory gaps.
- **Intent Channel:** Restricting the intent channel to a single navigation mode reduced accuracy by 2.0 points, indicating the value of distinguishing between object-targeted and instruction-following search strategies.
- **Observation Channel:**
 - Returning only terminal status and the final view, rather than complete trajectory observations, cost 5.9 points in accuracy.
 - Removing journey analysis, which structures rollout evidence, cost 4.4 points.
 - Sparsifying keyframe sampling during rollouts cost 2.8 points. These results emphasize the importance of capturing and structuring intermediate observations.
- **Memory Channel:** Removing the EQA context state, which maintains persistent evidence and unresolved goals, resulted in a 4.6-point accuracy decrease, highlighting the necessity of cross-call accumulation.
- **Auxiliary Capabilities:** Context compaction and the `zoom_in_object` verification tool provided incremental benefits of 0.9 and 0.6 points, respectively.
Overall, the observation and memory channels were identified as having the largest individual effects, but all three channels contributed to closing the episodic interface gap.

#### 4.4. Real Robot Transfer and Task Horizon (Q4)

NavMCP's capabilities were tested on a Unitree Go2 robot in real-world scenarios:
- **Robust Real-World Performance:** NavMCP achieved an overall success rate of 78.3% on the physical robot, significantly outperforming frontier exploration (48.3%) and a reactive learned-navigator baseline (38.3%). This demonstrated the framework's ability to transfer effectively despite real-world sensor noise and odometry drift.
- **Performance Scaling with Horizon:** The margin of NavMCP's success over the strongest baseline increased with the task horizon. For low-difficulty single-room tasks, the margin was 10 points. This increased to 25 points for medium-difficulty cross-room tasks and further to 45 points for high-difficulty tasks involving exploration over routes exceeding 20 meters. This indicated that the benefits of persistent reasoning and structured execution management become more pronounced as task complexity and duration increase.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NavMCP framework represents an architectural advancement for developing long-horizon physical-world agents by strategically integrating vision-language models (VLMs) and navigation foundation models (NFMs).

**Architectural Contribution:** The core significance lies in its structured agentic scaffolding framework. By proposing and validating a three-channel design (Intent, Observation, and Memory), NavMCP effectively addresses the "episodic interface gap" prevalent in conventional VLM-tool integration. This design allows the VLM to perform high-level evidence planning and decision-making, while the NFM handles grounded, closed-loop execution. This separation of concerns, coupled with mechanisms for persistent evidence accumulation and structured observation, provides a robust blueprint for synergizing specialized foundation models.

**Performance and Efficiency Advancement:** The consistent achievement of state-of-the-art results across three distinct Embodied Question Answering (EQA) benchmarks (HM-EQA, MT-HM3D, and EXPRESS-Bench) demonstrates NavMCP's empirical efficacy. Furthermore, the framework exhibits improved efficiency, requiring fewer normalized agent steps, which indicates a more effective utilization of the VLM's reasoning capacity for high-level decision-making. The successful transfer and superior performance on a real robot, particularly with increasing task horizons, underscore its practical viability and robustness in complex physical environments.

**Implications for Embodied AI:** This work suggests that the frontier of long-horizon embodied agents may be advanced more effectively by intelligent orchestration of complementary foundation models rather than solely by developing larger, more generalist models. The ability to inspect, reuse, and control NFM rollouts from an external VLM agent enhances the overall system's adaptability, reasoning depth, and long-term coherence. This paradigm could pave the way for more sophisticated embodied behaviors that require multi-step reasoning, adaptive exploration, and continuous learning from environmental interactions.

**Potential Future Impact:**
- **Beyond EQA:** The principles of NavMCP, particularly its evidence-centric approach and structured communication channels, could be generalized to other complex embodied tasks requiring sustained interaction with the physical world, such as intricate manipulation, scientific discovery, or complex service robotics.
- **Data Collection and Learning:** The framework's capacity for agent-directed exploration could be leveraged to efficiently collect scalable, task-driven embodied data. By coupling high-level reasoning with grounded observations and navigation trajectories, agents could autonomously gather rich datasets tailored for training and improving spatial intelligence and other embodied skills.
- **Resource Optimization:** While the current reliance on a large VLM introduces inference latency, the framework's modularity opens avenues for optimization, such as developing smaller VLM agents or distillation techniques to reduce computational costs while retaining performance benefits. Future work could also explore enhanced cross-view object association and uncertainty tracking to address current limitations, particularly in tasks requiring precise counting.

In summary, NavMCP demonstrates a methodical approach to construct capable physical-world agents by scaffolding the strengths of contemporary foundation models, offering a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI.
