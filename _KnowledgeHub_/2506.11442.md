# ReVeal: Self-Evolving Code Agents via Reliable Self-Verification

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research Asia, Tongji University, and the University of Science and Technology of China developed ReVeal, a framework that enhances large language models' code generation and self-verification through a multi-turn reinforcement learning approach. It achieves an average Pass@1 accuracy of 38.7% on LiveCodeBench V6 at turn 25 and demonstrates sustained refinement for over 20 inference turns beyond its training horizon.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs often lead to unreliable self-verification, producing uninformative reflections or random guesses.
- Current LLM reasoning performance often plateaus when test-time computation exceeds the training horizon, limiting sustained improvement on complex tasks.
- Multi-turn reasoning approaches either require complex separate critic models or depend on pre-existing public test cases, restricting their real-world applicability and autonomy.

## Method

- **ReVeal** employs a multi-turn, iterative ==generation-verification loop== where a single LLM acts as both code generator and test case synthesiser, leveraging external tool (Python interpreter) feedback.
- A novel Turn-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO) algorithm is introduced, utilizing joint verifiable rewards composed of outcome, generation, and verification components to provide granular, turn-level supervision.
- Structured prompting guides the LLM through distinct reasoning stages (think, answer, verify) and incorporates short-term memory to manage context in long rollouts.

## Results

- ReVeal achieved **38.7%** Pass@1 on LiveCodeBench V6 at turn 25, outperforming single-turn RL baselines (**32.8%**) and critic-based methods (**33.4%**).
- The framework demonstrated robust correction rates (∆↑ of **7.50%** on LCB V6 and **15.69%** on CodeContests) with no degradation (∆↓ of **0.0%**), confirming reliable self-correction.
- Despite training with only three turns, ReVeal extrapolated to sustained performance improvements over 20+ inference turns, continuously raising Pass@1 accuracy from **34.8%** at turn 1 to **38.7%** at turn 25.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly optimizing self-verification and test-case generation alongside code generation fosters a co-evolution of these capabilities, making verification reliable and robust.
> - A fine-grained reward structure and turn-aware credit assignment can enable deep test-time scaling, allowing models to continuously improve solutions for many turns beyond their training experience.
> - The inherent verification-generation asymmetry in LLMs can be widened by making verification a first-class optimization target, providing more reliable signals to guide the harder generation process.
