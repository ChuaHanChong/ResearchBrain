# ReVeal: Self-Evolving Code Agents via Reliable Self-Verification

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research Asia, Tongji University, and the University of Science and Technology of China developed ReVeal, a framework that enhances large language models' code generation and self-verification through a multi-turn reinforcement learning approach. It achieves an average Pass@1 accuracy of 38.7% on LiveCodeBench V6 at turn 25 and demonstrates sustained refinement for over 20 inference turns beyond its training horizon.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs often lead to unreliable self-verification, producing uninformative reflections or random guesses.
- Current LLM reasoning performance often plateaus when test-time computation exceeds the training horizon, limiting sustained improvement on complex tasks.
- Multi-turn reasoning approaches either require complex separate critic models or depend on pre-existing public test cases, restricting their real-world applicability and autonomy.

## Method

- **ReVeal** employs a multi-turn, iterative ==generation-verification loop== where a single LLM acts as both code generator and test case synthesiser, leveraging external tool (Python interpreter) feedback.
- A novel Turn-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO) algorithm is introduced, utilizing joint verifiable rewards composed of outcome, generation, and verification components to provide granular, turn-level supervision.
- Structured prompting guides the LLM through distinct reasoning stages (think, answer, verify) and incorporates short-term memory to manage context in long rollouts.

## Results

- ReVeal achieved **38.7%** Pass@1 on LiveCodeBench V6 at turn 25, outperforming single-turn RL baselines (**32.8%**) and critic-based methods (**33.4%**).
- The framework demonstrated robust correction rates (∆↑ of **7.50%** on LCB V6 and **15.69%** on CodeContests) with no degradation (∆↓ of **0.0%**), confirming reliable self-correction.
- Despite training with only three turns, ReVeal extrapolated to sustained performance improvements over 20+ inference turns, continuously raising Pass@1 accuracy from **34.8%** at turn 1 to **38.7%** at turn 25.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly optimizing self-verification and test-case generation alongside code generation fosters a co-evolution of these capabilities, making verification reliable and robust.
> - A fine-grained reward structure and turn-aware credit assignment can enable deep test-time scaling, allowing models to continuously improve solutions for many turns beyond their training experience.
> - The inherent verification-generation asymmetry in LLMs can be widened by making verification a first-class optimization target, providing more reliable signals to guide the harder generation process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

ReVeal operates at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), reinforcement learning (RL), and tool-augmented reasoning, specifically targeting complex problem-solving in code generation.

- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLMs:** The paper acknowledges that RLVR has significantly enhanced the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. This area has seen rapid growth, with models learning to refine their outputs based on feedback, often through iterative processes of reflection and self-verification. Seminal works like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI's efforts in reasoning with LLMs exemplify this trend.
- **The Verification-Generation Asymmetry:** A core concept in current LLM research is the "verification-generation asymmetry," where it is generally easier for an LLM to verify the correctness of a solution than to generate the correct solution from scratch. This asymmetry is a key driver for "test-time scaling," where models can improve their performance by spending more computational effort during inference to self-critique and refine.
- **Limitations of Existing RLVR Methods:** The authors identify critical gaps in current RLVR approaches:
 - **Sole Reliance on Outcome Rewards:** Most methods optimize based on the final outcome, failing to explicitly optimize the intermediate verification process. This leads to unreliable self-verification, characterized by "verbose, uninformative reflections or random guessing on hard problems."
 - **Limited Test-Time Scaling:** Performance often plateaus once test-time compute exceeds the training horizon, hindering sustained improvement.
 - **Challenges in Multi-Turn Reasoning:** Previous attempts at multi-turn reasoning either involve training a separate critic model (adding complexity and inference cost) or rely on pre-existing public tests (limiting applicability in real-world scenarios where custom tests are needed).
- **Tool-Augmented Reasoning:** The paper builds upon the established paradigm of tool-augmented reasoning, where LLMs interact with external tools (e.g., code interpreters, search engines) to overcome their inherent limitations in domain knowledge and computation. Previous works like ReAct, Reflexion, Search-R1, ReTool, and ToRL have demonstrated the power of integrating tools, but often with an outcome-driven focus, lacking explicit optimization for verification itself.
- **Self-Verification of LLMs:** While previous work has explored self-correction via "self-reward" or "self-correction" signals, and some approaches use execution feedback, many still struggle with reliable self-judgment or depend on external test cases. The challenge lies in enabling a single LLM to generate its own high-quality, reliable test cases for self-verification.

ReVeal distinguishes itself by directly tackling the unreliability of self-verification and the limitations of test-time scaling. By explicitly optimizing verification with reliable signals from self-generated tests and tool feedback, it aims to "widen the V-G asymmetry" and foster a co-evolution of generation and verification capabilities, enabling robust, long-horizon reasoning and autonomous agent behavior. This moves beyond merely using tools or reflection heuristically, embedding verification as a first-class objective within the RL training loop.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ReVeal stems from the observed limitations in current large language models' (LLMs) abilities to reliably self-verify and sustainably improve their solutions over multiple reasoning turns, especially in complex tasks like code generation.

The key objectives of the ReVeal framework are:

1. **Enhance the Reliability of Self-Verification:** Current RLVR methods for LLMs often produce unreliable self-verification signals, leading to uninformative reflections or random guesses, particularly on challenging problems. ReVeal aims to make self-verification a robust and reliable driver of improvement by explicitly optimizing it during training.
2. **Enable Deeper and More Sustained Test-Time Scaling:** Existing approaches often see reasoning performance plateau once inference-time computation exceeds the training horizon. ReVeal seeks to overcome this by enabling models to continuously refine their solutions for an extended number of turns beyond their training experience, unlocking the potential for solving previously intractable problems.
3. **Widen the Verification-Generation (V-G) Asymmetry:** By making verification itself an optimization target, ReVeal intends to expand the inherent asymmetry where verification is easier than generation. This widened gap allows more reliable verification signals to effectively guide the harder generation process.
4. **Foster Co-evolution of Code and Test Generation:** ReVeal aims to jointly train the LLM to improve both its ability to generate correct code and its ability to generate high-quality, diverse test cases for self-evaluation. This co-evolution is critical for autonomous refinement without reliance on external critics or pre-defined test suites.
5. **Develop a Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Framework:** To achieve these goals, ReVeal proposes an iterative, multi-turn framework that integrates code generation, self-verification, and tool-based feedback in a closed loop, allowing for progressive refinement and strategic adjustments.
6. **Introduce a Novel Reward and Credit Assignment Mechanism (TAPO):** To effectively optimize both generation and verification in a multi-turn setting, ReVeal seeks to develop a sophisticated reward system that provides dense, turn-level supervision and prevents reward gaming, addressing the imprecision of outcome-only rewards.

In essence, ReVeal is motivated by the need for LLMs to become more autonomous and robust problem-solvers, capable of not just generating initial solutions but also critically evaluating and refining them through self-generated, tool-verified feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReVeal introduces a multi-turn Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework centered on an iterative generation-verification loop, augmented by tool feedback and a specialized Turn-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO) algorithm.

#### 3.1. ReVeal Framework: Iterative Generation-Verification Loop with Tool Feedback

ReVeal structures long-horizon reasoning into an interleaved sequence of generation and verification turns, powered by a single large language model (LLM) acting as both generator and verifier.

- **Single Policy Model:** A single LLM is used for both generating candidate code and synthesizing test cases for verification. This design reduces complexity and cost, promoting a co-evolution of both capabilities under a unified training scheme.
- **Iterative Loop:** Each turn involves:
 1. **Generation:** The policy generates candidate code (e.g., Python code).
 2. **Verification:** The policy then self-verifies this code by constructing test cases. It formulates hypotheses about potential failure modes and edge conditions to generate diverse tests.
 3. **Tool-Based Execution:** External tools (specifically a Python interpreter via "Code Judge") execute the generated code against the generated test cases.
 4. **Feedback Integration:** The fine-grained execution results, including runtime errors, invalid test cases, actual/expected outputs, and pass/fail judgments, are appended to the rollout.
 5. **Refinement:** Based on this feedback, the model interprets the traces, diagnoses errors, and revises both its candidate code and verification strategy in the subsequent turn. The loop continues until a correct solution is found or a turn budget is reached.
- **Structured Prompting:** ReVeal uses a specific structured prompting template that decouples different reasoning stages into distinct segments: `<generation-think>`, `<generation-answer>`, `<verification-think>`, `<verification-answer>`, and `<tool-feedback>`. This guides the LLM to organize its thoughts and outputs for clarity and enables systematic feedback integration.
- **Tool-Augmented Verification:** Interaction with the Python interpreter provides objective, reliable, and fine-grained supervisory signals crucial for systematic refinement and broadening exploration. It reveals concrete failure modes, steering the policy away from local optima.
 - **Training-time Filtering:** To ensure feedback quality during RL training, model-generated test cases are only executed on candidate code if they are first verified against a *golden solution*. This guarantees that training feedback is legitimate and guides learning towards correct solutions.
 - **Test-time Autonomy:** At inference time, no golden reference is available, and all generated test cases are executed. This places a strong emphasis on the model's ability to generate high-quality, reliable tests autonomously, a capability ReVeal explicitly optimizes.
- **Short-Term Memory:** To manage context growth in long multi-turn rollouts efficiently, a short-term memory mechanism retains only the last three turns as context, without sacrificing accuracy.

#### 3.2. Turn-Aware RL for the Generation-Verification Paradigm (TAPO)

ReVeal moves beyond outcome-only RL by introducing a novel reward structure and a customized credit assignment algorithm called Turn-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO).

- **Joint Verifiable Rewards:** The reward signal is decomposed into three complementary components to jointly optimize generation and verification:
 1. **Outcome Reward ($r_{outcome}$):** Supervises the quality of the final solution. It consists of a format reward ($r_{format}$) to ensure correct output structure and a pass rate reward ($r_{passrate}$) measuring the final code accuracy.
 2. **Generation Reward ($r_{k}^{gen}$):** For each generation turn, this reward encourages iterative improvement in code accuracy. It is defined based on the absolute pass rate and, crucially, the *improvement* in pass rate compared to the previous generation turn. The paper sets absolute weighting to 0 and improvement weighting to 1 to strongly incentivize progress.
 3. **Verification Reward ($r_{k}^{ver}$):** For each verification turn, this reward evaluates the quality of generated tests. It is computed as the proportion of generated test cases that succeed when executed on a *golden code*. This explicitly incentivizes the model to construct diverse and reliable tests that can correctly identify flaws.
- **Turn-Aware Policy Optimization (TAPO):** Building on the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework, TAPO replaces standard Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) with a turn-aware return, enabling stable learning across both token and turn granularities.
 1. **Token-level Return:** Uses a pure Monte Carlo return for token steps.
 2. **Turn-level Return:** Designed to prevent adversarial reward gaming. Each *generation reward* is assigned to its own generation turn *and* to the immediately preceding verification turn (linking verification to subsequent generation success). Each *verification reward* is strictly confined to its own verification turn. This ensures generation is rewarded for actual code quality, and verification for the quality of tests, mitigating strategies like generating trivial code to game verification rewards.
 3. **Turn-Aware Return:** The final return combines the token-level and turn-level returns, and these advantages replace the standard GAE estimates in the PPO objective.

This structured reward system and credit assignment mechanism create a symbiotic feedback loop: strong tests expose errors, driving code improvements (reinforced by generation rewards), while improved code raises the bar for verification, pushing the model to generate even richer and more challenging tests (reinforced by verification rewards).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReVeal's experimental evaluation on challenging code generation benchmarks demonstrates significant improvements over existing methods, particularly in reliable self-verification and deep test-time scaling.

- **Superior Performance in Code Generation (Pass@1):**
 - On LiveCodeBench V6, ReVeal achieved a Pass@1 accuracy of 38.7% at turn 25, significantly outperforming the single-turn RL baseline (32.8%) and various critic-based methods (e.g., CTRL at 33.4%). Similar gains were observed on CodeContests (33.6% vs. 21.0%).
 - Even at turn 1, ReVeal achieved 34.8% Pass@1, surpassing the single-turn RL baseline. This indicates that the multi-turn training (even with only 3 turns) strengthens the underlying policy, leading to better initial solutions.
- **Robust Self-Verification and Correction:**
 - ReVeal showed high correction rates (∆↑ of 7.50% on LCB V6 and 15.69% on CodeContests) with negligible degradation (∆↓ of 0.0% on both benchmarks). This highlights its highly robust and reliable self-verification, critique, and revision capabilities, effectively fixing incorrect solutions without introducing new errors.
- **Deeper Test-Time Scaling Beyond Training Horizon:**
 - Despite being trained with a maximum of only three reasoning turns, ReVeal demonstrated remarkable extrapolation by sustaining continuous refinement for over 20+ inference turns on LiveCodeBench. Pass@1 accuracy consistently improved from 34.8% at turn 1 to 38.7% at turn 25. This is a critical finding, showing ReVeal's ability to generalize to longer reasoning horizons and solve previously intractable problems.
- **Expansion of Reasoning Boundaries (Pass@k):**
 - ReVeal consistently outperformed both the base model (DAPO-Qwen-32B) and the single-turn RL baseline across all Pass@k values (from 1 to 128). While the RL baseline showed diminishing returns for higher k, ReVeal maintained its advantage, demonstrating its ability to expand the underlying model's reasoning boundaries through effective, verification-driven exploration.
- **Co-evolution of Generation and Verification Capabilities:**
 - Training curves illustrated a concurrent and steady improvement in both final code accuracy and test-case accuracy. Post-format learning, test-case accuracy rose from ~50% to nearly 88%. Furthermore, for correctly generated test cases, the model achieved over 85% accuracy in judging code correctness, signifying high error-detection coverage. This strong evidence confirms ReVeal's ability to jointly optimize and co-evolve both code generation and self-verification skills.
- **Effectiveness of TAPO with Joint Verifiable Rewards:**
 - Ablation studies confirmed that TAPO with joint verifiable rewards yielded higher Pass@1, increased ∆↑, and suppressed ∆↓ compared to training with only outcome rewards. Outcome-only rewards led to higher ∆↓, indicating that imprecise feedback can drive incorrect revisions. This validates the importance of explicit verification optimization and granular reward assignment.
- **Scalability Across Different Base Models:**
 - ReVeal proved effective across different base models, including Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and the stronger DAPO-Qwen-32B backbone. On stronger backbones, ReVeal unlocked greater headroom, demonstrating its general applicability and potential for even higher performance with more capable foundation models.
- **Necessity of Explicit Optimization:**
 - A "prompt-only ReVeal" variant, which used the same structured prompt and tools but no RL training, failed to sustain effective deep multi-turn refinement. This conclusively demonstrates that performance gains are not merely from sophisticated prompting or tool use, but fundamentally from the explicit RL-based optimization of self-verification.
- **Statistical Significance:** Repeated experiments confirmed the statistical significance of the performance improvements, providing robustness to the findings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReVeal represents a significant advancement in empowering large language models to become more autonomous, robust, and capable problem-solvers, with wide-ranging implications for AI research and practical applications.

- **Novel Paradigm for Reliable Self-Improvement:** By making verification a "first-class optimization target," ReVeal introduces a novel RL paradigm. This shifts LLM training beyond merely optimizing for final outputs to actively developing the meta-skill of self-assessment and refinement. This is crucial for building truly intelligent agents that can operate reliably in complex, open-ended environments.
- **Enhanced Autonomy of AI Agents:** ReVeal equips LLMs with strong self-verification capabilities, enabling them to generate their own high-quality test cases and iteratively refine solutions without human intervention or reliance on pre-existing test suites. This moves towards more autonomous AI agents that can "self-evolve," reducing the need for constant supervision and external critics, which is a major step towards general-purpose AI.
- **Scalable and Generalizable RL Framework:** The framework demonstrates effective test-time scaling, allowing models to continuously improve their solutions even beyond their training horizon. This "compute scaling into deeper inference regimes" holds promise for tackling problems that are too complex to solve within a fixed number of training or inference steps. The authors emphasize that the core concepts of generation-verification, TAPO, and turn-level reward design are generalizable to any domain with verifiable rewards and verification asymmetry, extending its potential impact beyond code generation to various reasoning tasks.
- **Practical Implications for Software Development:** For code generation, ReVeal offers a pathway to more reliable and robust code. By reducing dependency on public test cases, it increases applicability to real-world software systems where novel, context-specific tests are often required. This could significantly impact automated code synthesis, debugging, and verification processes.
- **Foundation for Continuous Learning and Distillation:** The ability for ReVeal to discover "newly discovered solutions" through deeper inference opens avenues for future work. These refined solutions can be distilled back into the code LLM through continued training, creating a powerful loop of self-improvement where the model continuously learns from its own advanced reasoning.
- **Addressing Fundamental LLM Limitations:** ReVeal directly addresses known limitations of LLMs, such as unreliable self-judgment and the inability to sustain improvement over long reasoning chains. Its success in these areas positions it as a foundational method for developing more intelligent and trustworthy LLMs.

In conclusion, ReVeal lays a blueprint for future advances in self-improving, more robust, and autonomous AI agents by fundamentally enhancing their self-verification capabilities and demonstrating unprecedented deep test-time scaling. Its principled approach to jointly optimizing generation and verification, coupled with a sophisticated credit assignment mechanism, makes it a highly significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for large language models.
