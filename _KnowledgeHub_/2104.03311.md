# PlasticineLab: A Soft-Body Manipulation Benchmark with Differentiable Physics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC San Diego, MIT, and the MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab introduce PlasticineLab, a novel benchmark providing 50 distinct soft-body manipulation tasks within a differentiable elastoplastic physics environment. The work demonstrates that gradient-based optimization significantly outperforms model-free reinforcement learning for precision and efficiency in these complex deformable object interactions.

## Problem

- Existing virtual environments primarily focus on rigid-body physics, lacking standardized benchmarks for soft-body manipulation.
- Simulating soft-body dynamics is challenging due to infinite degrees of freedom and complex governing equations, making traditional rigid-body algorithms inefficient.
- Limited application of differentiable physics engines and trajectory optimization methods to the unique complexities of soft-body control.

## Method

- Developed PlasticineLab, a benchmark suite featuring 10 diverse soft-body manipulation tasks with 5 configurations each, totaling 50 scenarios.
- Implemented a differentiable elastoplastic physics engine using the Moving Least Squares Material Point Method (MLS-MPM) and a von Mises yield criterion with Taichi's CUDA backend.
- Introduced a 'softened' contact model to ensure smooth gradient flow during backpropagation, facilitating optimization for contact-rich scenarios.

## Results

- Gradient-based optimization methods (e.g., Adam) consistently found solutions rapidly (within tens of iterations) and with greater precision for many challenging tasks compared to RL.
- Reinforcement learning algorithms (SAC, TD3, PPO) struggled with most tasks, exhibiting limited success, training instability, and poor scalability to multi-stage or long-horizon problems.
- The softened contact model significantly improved gradient quality, leading to better optimization performance for gradient-based methods compared to hard contact models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Differentiable physics-based optimization provides high efficiency and precision for solving single-stage soft-body manipulation tasks, often outperforming reinforcement learning.
> - Current model-free reinforcement learning algorithms face significant challenges with exploration, training stability, and long-term planning in complex soft-body environments.
> - The material properties of soft bodies, specifically yield stress, directly influence task difficulty and learning performance for manipulation agents.
