# PlasticineLab: A Soft-Body Manipulation Benchmark with Differentiable Physics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC San Diego, MIT, and the MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab introduce PlasticineLab, a novel benchmark providing 50 distinct soft-body manipulation tasks within a differentiable elastoplastic physics environment. The work demonstrates that gradient-based optimization significantly outperforms model-free reinforcement learning for precision and efficiency in these complex deformable object interactions.

## Problem

- Existing virtual environments primarily focus on rigid-body physics, lacking standardized benchmarks for soft-body manipulation.
- Simulating soft-body dynamics is challenging due to infinite degrees of freedom and complex governing equations, making traditional rigid-body algorithms inefficient.
- Limited application of differentiable physics engines and trajectory optimization methods to the unique complexities of soft-body control.

## Method

- Developed PlasticineLab, a benchmark suite featuring 10 diverse soft-body manipulation tasks with 5 configurations each, totaling 50 scenarios.
- Implemented a differentiable elastoplastic physics engine using the Moving Least Squares Material Point Method (MLS-MPM) and a von Mises yield criterion with Taichi's CUDA backend.
- Introduced a 'softened' contact model to ensure smooth gradient flow during backpropagation, facilitating optimization for contact-rich scenarios.

## Results

- Gradient-based optimization methods (e.g., Adam) consistently found solutions rapidly (within tens of iterations) and with greater precision for many challenging tasks compared to RL.
- Reinforcement learning algorithms (SAC, TD3, PPO) struggled with most tasks, exhibiting limited success, training instability, and poor scalability to multi-stage or long-horizon problems.
- The softened contact model significantly improved gradient quality, leading to better optimization performance for gradient-based methods compared to hard contact models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Differentiable physics-based optimization provides high efficiency and precision for solving single-stage soft-body manipulation tasks, often outperforming reinforcement learning.
> - Current model-free reinforcement learning algorithms face significant challenges with exploration, training stability, and long-term planning in complex soft-body environments.
> - The material properties of soft bodies, specifically yield stress, directly influence task difficulty and learning performance for manipulation agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of skill learning algorithms for intelligent agents has significantly benefited from simulated virtual environments such as Arcade Learning Environment (ALE), MuJoCo, and OpenAI Gym. These platforms provide standardized settings for evaluating control and planning algorithms. However, a notable limitation of these existing environments is their primary focus on rigid-body physics. While rigid-body simulations are well-established, they do not encompass the complexities of soft-body dynamics.

Soft bodies are prevalent in various applications, including virtual surgery, computer graphics (e.g., humanoid character modeling), biomimetic robotics, and material science (e.g., fracture analysis). Despite this broad relevance, the research landscape for standard soft-body manipulation environments and benchmarks for skill learning remains underdeveloped. Simulating soft-body dynamics presents challenges due to their infinite degrees of freedom (DoFs) and high-dimensional governing equations, which can render many algorithms designed for rigid bodies ineffective or computationally prohibitive.

A related trend in modern machine learning and robotics is the increasing interest in differentiable physics engines. These engines can provide analytical gradient information during the simulation process, which is valuable for planning and control optimizations, particularly when combined with gradient-based learning methods. Existing approaches to differentiable physics include approximating simulators with naturally differentiable neural networks or implementing physics simulators directly using differentiable programming systems. Some systems also leverage adjoint methods for implicit time integrators or techniques for differentiating specific physical models like Linear Complementarity Problems (LCPs) and QR decompositions. This work builds upon and extends previous differentiable simulators for elastic bodies, such as ChainQueen and DiffTaichi, by incorporating plasticity and tailored contact models for differentiability.

The application of differentiable simulation to planning soft-body manipulation is closely linked to trajectory optimization. While trajectory optimization has been extensively studied in robotics for various types of robots, its application to soft-body manipulation has been limited by the high DoFs of soft bodies and the complexity of formulating contacts.

Finally, while there has been work on learning-based techniques for manipulating high-DoF physics systems like cloth, fluids, and ropes, these efforts often focus on providing solutions for specific robotic instances. PlasticineLab distinguishes itself by offering a comprehensive benchmark environment for evaluating and developing novel algorithms across a range of soft-body tasks, rather than addressing a singular problem instance. This work aims to fill the identified gap by introducing a standardized, differentiable soft-body manipulation benchmark.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the scarcity of standardized environments and benchmarks for soft-body manipulation in skill learning. To achieve this, the authors introduce **PlasticineLab**, a novel benchmark designed for evaluating algorithms on a diverse collection of soft-body manipulation tasks.

The core motivations and specific objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Establish a Soft-Body Benchmark:** The authors observed that existing virtual environments primarily focus on rigid-body physics, leaving a gap for soft-body manipulation despite its wide range of applications. PlasticineLab aims to fill this gap by proposing a collection of 10 distinct soft-body manipulation tasks, each with 5 variants, totaling 50 configurations. These tasks require complex operations such as pinching, rolling, chopping, molding, and carving of plasticine.
- **Integrate Differentiable Physics:** A central motivation is to incorporate differentiable physics into the simulation environment. This is presented as a significant advancement because it provides, for the first time in a soft-body benchmark, analytical gradient information. This gradient support is crucial for enabling supervised learning and optimization using gradient-based methods, which can potentially accelerate learning and planning.
- **Model Realistic Material Behavior:** The choice of "plasticine" as the soft-body material is motivated by its elastoplastic properties. Plasticine deforms elastically under small deformations and plastically under large deformations. This material behavior is more diverse and realistic than purely elastic soft bodies and introduces unexplored challenges for manipulation algorithms, making it a representative medium for testing advanced soft-body control.
- **Leverage High-Performance Simulation:** The development of PlasticineLab is motivated by the need for an efficient and accurate simulation. The authors aimed to implement the simulator, including its gradient support and elastoplastic material model, using Taichi. Taichi's CUDA backend is leveraged to provide massive parallelism on GPUs, enabling real-time simulation of a diverse collection of 3D soft bodies. The Moving Least Squares Material Point Method (MLS-MPM) and the von Mises yield criterion are utilized for the material model.
- **Systematically Evaluate Existing Algorithms:** A key objective is to conduct a systematic evaluation of both existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods and gradient-based approaches within the PlasticineLab benchmark. This evaluation aims to assess the strengths and weaknesses of these different algorithmic families when confronted with the complexities of differentiable elastoplasticity.
- **Identify Future Research Directions:** Through the evaluation, the authors intend to deepen the understanding of RL and gradient-based planning algorithms in the context of soft-body manipulation. This understanding is expected to inform and inspire future research directions, particularly focusing on how the benefits of differentiable physics and RL might be combined to address more complex physics-based skill learning tasks that involve long-term planning or intricate contact interactions.

In summary, the work aims to introduce a foundational benchmark with differentiable elastoplastic physics to catalyze advancements in soft-body manipulation skill learning, providing both a challenging environment and a tool for efficient optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the design and implementation of PlasticineLab, a differentiable physics benchmark, and its subsequent use for evaluating diverse control algorithms.

#### 3.1. The PlasticineLab Learning Environment Design

- **Task Representation (Markov Decision Process - MDP):**
 - **State:** The environment state includes the soft body's particle-based representation (positions, velocities, strain, stress for *N<sub>p</sub>* particles, each with *d<sub>p</sub>* dimensions) and the manipulator's end-effector state (3D position and 4D quaternion for orientation, or 7D total, for *N<sub>m</sub>* manipulators). One-way coupling between rigid manipulators and soft bodies is implemented.
 - **Observation:** To handle the high dimensionality of particle states, observations are downsampled. *N<sub>k</sub>* particles serve as landmarks, providing 6D information (position and velocity) each, stacked with the manipulator state.
 - **Action:** Agents control the linear (and sometimes angular) velocities of the manipulators kinematically. The action space has *N<sub>m</sub>* × *d<sub>a</sub>* dimensions, with *d<sub>a</sub>* being 3 or 6 depending on rotational control. Action bounds (*A<sub>min</sub>*, *A<sub>max</sub>*) are provided for simulation stability.
 - **Goal and Reward:** The target shape is represented by its discretized 3D mass tensor (density field on a grid of size *N<sub>grid</sub><sup>3</sup>*). The reward function is defined as: *R = -c<sub>1</sub>R<sub>mass</sub> - c<sub>2</sub>R<sub>dist</sub> - c<sub>3</sub>R<sub>grasp</sub> + c<sub>4</sub>*.
 - *R<sub>mass</sub>*: L1 distance between current and target mass tensors (shapes).
 - *R<sub>dist</sub>*: Dot product of the target shape's Signed Distance Field (SDF) and the current shape's mass tensor.
 - *R<sub>grasp</sub>*: Encourages manipulators to be close to soft bodies.
 - *c<sub>1</sub>, c<sub>2</sub>, c<sub>3</sub>, c<sub>4</sub>*: Constant positive weights and bias.
- **Evaluation Suite:** PlasticineLab comprises 10 distinct tasks (e.g., Rope, Writer, Chopsticks, RollingPin, Move, Pinch, Assembly, Table, TripleMove, Torus) with 5 configurations each, totaling 50 unique reinforcement learning scenarios. These configurations involve perturbations in initial and target shapes, and manipulator locations.

#### 3.2. Differentiable Elastoplasticity Simulation

- **Underlying Engine:** The simulator is implemented in Taichi, executed on NVIDIA CUDA-enabled GPUs.
- **Continuum Mechanics Model:** The Moving Least Squares Material Point Method (MLS-MPM) is utilized. This method combines Lagrangian particles (storing material properties like position, velocity, mass, density, deformation gradients) with an Eulerian background grid (handling particle interactions and collisions with rigid bodies).
- **Material Model (Plasticity):** A von Mises yield criterion is employed for modeling elastoplastic behavior. When the deviatoric stress of a plasticine particle exceeds a threshold, it yields, leading to plastic deformation. This involves a return mapping process, which is a 3D projection on the singular values of the deformation gradients.
- **Differentiability:**
 - Taichi’s two-scale reverse-mode automatic differentiation system (DiffTaichi) is used to compute gradients.
 - Special treatment is required for differentiating the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) within the return mapping process, as Taichi's iterative SVD can be numerically unstable. The approach from Townsend (2016) is adopted, with numerical stabilization for zero denominators.
- **Contact Model:**
 - Standard grid-based contact treatment with Coulomb friction is used for soft-body collisions with rigid obstacles/manipulators and the floor. Rigid bodies are represented as time-varying Signed Distance Fields (SDFs).
 - For backpropagation, a "softened" contact model is introduced to improve gradient quality and reward smoothness. This involves computing a smooth collision strength factor (*s = min{exp(−αd), 1}*), which blends grid point velocities before and after collision projection. This creates a smooth transition zone around boundaries, improving contact gradients.

#### 3.3. Experimental Evaluation

- **Evaluation Metrics:** The performance is measured using a normalized incremental IoU (Intersection over Union) score. This score quantifies the improvement in soft IoU between the final state and the goal state, relative to the initial state and the goal state. Soft IoU is calculated by normalizing and then comparing discretized 3D mass tensors.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Evaluation:**
 - Three state-of-the-art model-free RL algorithms were evaluated: Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3), and Policy Proximal Optimization (PPO).
 - Each algorithm was trained for 10,000 episodes, with 50 environment steps per episode, across the 50 configurations.
- **Differentiable Physics (Gradient-based Optimization) Evaluation:**
 - Gradient-based optimization methods (Adam and Gradient Descent with Momentum (GD)) were applied to plan open-loop action sequences.
 - The objective was to maximize the sum of rewards by backpropagating gradients through the entire simulated trajectory.
 - A comparison was made between optimizing with the soft contact model (Adam) and the hard contact model (Adam-H).
 - These methods were run for 200 episodes (iterations), assuming full environment knowledge.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study was conducted on a simple "Move" task to analyze the impact of varying the yield stress parameter on the performance of SAC agents, examining how material plasticity affects learning difficulty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PlasticineLab yielded several findings regarding the performance of reinforcement learning (RL) and gradient-based methods in soft-body manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Reinforcement Learning

- **Limited Task Solving Capability:** RL-based approaches generally demonstrated difficulty in efficiently solving most of the PlasticineLab tasks. While they could learn reasonable policies for simpler tasks like "Move" and showed partial success in "RollingPin," they struggled with achieving precise target shapes.
- **Training Instability:** A common observation was that RL agents frequently struggled with exploration, sometimes releasing objects which then free-fell under gravity. Regrasping these objects proved challenging, leading to training instability and unsatisfactory results.
- **Scalability Challenges:** Increasing the number of manipulators or plasticine objects, as in "TripleMove," presented significant difficulties for RL algorithms, indicating a deficiency in scaling to higher-dimensional control tasks.
- **Long-term Planning Deficiencies:** For tasks requiring multi-stage or long-term planning, such as "Rope" (winding around a pillar), "Chopsticks" (picking up and rotating a rope), or "Writer" (carving complex trajectories), RL agents seldom found reasonable solutions within 10,000 episodes. In "Assembly," agents often got stuck in local minima, failing to complete multi-stage goals.
- **Algorithmic Performance:** PPO generally outperformed SAC and TD3 across the benchmark, suggesting that its on-policy sampling and specific architecture might be more suitable for the environment's dynamics compared to off-policy methods dependent on MLP critic networks.
- **Sensitivity to Initial Policy:** For some tasks like "Torus," RL performance was dependent on the initial policy, with agents sometimes failing if manipulators did not initially make contact with the plasticine.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Differentiable Physics for Trajectory Optimization

- **Efficiency and Precision:** Gradient-based optimization methods, leveraging the built-in differentiable physics engine, demonstrated high efficiency. They could rapidly find solutions within tens of iterations for many challenging tasks, often achieving more precise results than RL methods.
- **Superior Performance in Complex Tasks:** These methods successfully solved tasks that proved difficult for RL, such as moving the rope around the pillar in "Rope," lifting a sphere onto a box in "Assembly," and using chopsticks to pick up a rope in "Chopsticks." For tasks like "Move," gradient-based methods often achieved better alignment with the target shape and showed more stable optimization.
- **Optimizer Comparison:** The Adam optimizer generally outperformed Gradient Descent with Momentum (GD) across most tasks. This is attributed to Adam's adaptive learning rate scaling, which is better suited for the complex loss surface characteristic of high-dimensional physical processes.
- **Effectiveness of Softened Contact Model:** Optimizing with the softened contact model (Adam) significantly outperformed optimization with a hard contact model (Adam-H) in most tasks. This validates the design choice of using a softened contact model during backpropagation to improve gradient quality and facilitate optimization.
- **Limitations for Multi-stage Tasks:** Despite their efficiency, gradient-based methods exhibited limitations in tasks requiring multi-stage policies or long-term planning, similar to RL. In "Pinch," for example, the optimizer would get stuck after the initial contact because local perturbations did not immediately increase the reward for subsequent stages (detachment and re-pressing).
- **Local Minima and Initialization Sensitivity:** In "TripleMove," the optimizer often converged to local minima where plasticine pieces crowded together rather than reaching distinct target locations, as it lacks exploration capabilities. Gradient-based methods were also observed to be sensitive to the initial action sequence initialization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Yield Stress

- **Correlation with Task Difficulty:** An ablation study on the "Move" task revealed a correlation between yield stress and task difficulty for RL agents. Environments with higher yield stress generally led to faster learning and better overall performance for SAC agents.
- **Plastic Deformation Impact:** In environments with lower yield stress (e.g., below 100), the plasticine tended to deform plastically more easily, making it challenging for agents to grasp and move objects without inadvertently destroying their intended structure. This indicates that the material properties significantly influence the tractability of manipulation tasks.

Overall, the findings suggest a trade-off: differentiable physics enables rapid and precise optimization for single-stage tasks, but both RL and gradient-based methods face challenges with multi-stage planning and exploration in complex soft-body environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of PlasticineLab represents a significant contribution to the fields of robotics, machine learning, and computer graphics, particularly in the domain of skill learning for physical interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Benchmark for Soft-Body Manipulation:** PlasticineLab addresses a critical gap in existing skill-learning environments by providing the first benchmark that simulates elastoplastic materials with differentiable physics. This advancement moves beyond the pervasive rigid-body focus, enabling research into the more complex and realistic interactions with deformable objects crucial for many real-world applications (e.g., surgical robotics, food handling, material manufacturing).
- **Enabling Differentiable Physics for Skill Learning:** By integrating a fully-featured differentiable physical engine, PlasticineLab provides analytical gradient information for soft-body dynamics, including challenging aspects like plasticity and contact. This is a foundational step, as it unlocks the potential for gradient-based optimization techniques, which have proven highly efficient in certain contexts, to be applied to soft-body control and planning.
- **Catalyst for Hybrid Algorithms:** The systematic evaluation, revealing the strengths of gradient-based methods (efficiency, precision for local optimization) and the limitations of both RL and gradient-based approaches (especially for long-term planning and exploration), identifies a crucial research direction. PlasticineLab is expected to encourage the development of novel hybrid algorithms that combine the benefits of differentiable physics (e.g., for local optimization and precise control) with the strengths of reinforcement learning or imitation learning (e.g., for exploration, long-term planning, and handling non-smooth events like detachment/reattachment).
- **Advancing Controller Design and Optimization:** The benchmark provides a platform for investigating improved controller designs, more effective reward functions for RL, and suitable 3D deep neural network architectures capable of capturing the intricate dynamics of soft bodies. The observed struggles of current RL algorithms highlight ample room for innovation in these areas.
- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The high fidelity of MPM-based simulation, known for its accuracy in matching real-world phenomena (e.g., snow dynamics), suggests PlasticineLab can be a powerful tool for sim-to-real research. The differentiable nature of the simulator allows for gradient-guided optimization of physical parameters, potentially minimizing the sim-to-real gap. Furthermore, the environment supports customization for domain randomization, a key technique for bridging the simulation-to-reality divide.
- **Promoting Generalization Studies:** With its capacity for procedural generation of various task configurations and objects, PlasticineLab offers a robust platform for evaluating the generalizability of algorithms across different soft-body manipulation scenarios, a fundamental aspect of building robust intelligent agents.
- **Lowering Research Barriers:** By making PlasticineLab publicly available, the authors significantly lower the entry barrier for researchers to engage in soft-body manipulation skill learning. This accessibility is crucial for fostering a collaborative research environment and accelerating progress in this complex domain.

In conclusion, PlasticineLab is poised to be a foundational resource that not only pushes the boundaries of soft-body manipulation research but also fosters a new generation of algorithms capable of leveraging the power of differentiable physics in conjunction with learning paradigms.
