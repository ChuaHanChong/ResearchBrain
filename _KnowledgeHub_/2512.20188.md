# Asynchronous Fast-Slow Vision-Language-Action Policies for Whole-Body Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DuoCore-FS, developed by the Astribot Team, introduces an asynchronous fast-slow Vision-Language-Action framework that enables high-frequency whole-body robotic manipulation by decoupling large VLM-based semantic reasoning from real-time diffusion-policy action generation. It achieved a 90% success rate on complex tasks at 32.3 Hz inference frequency, significantly outperforming synchronous baselines.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have slow inference speeds, bottlenecking real-time control in robotic manipulation.
- Existing fast-slow VLA architectures often use synchronous operations or fixed frequency ratios, limiting the fast system's true real-time potential.
- Whole-body robotic manipulation requires high-frequency and precise control across many degrees of freedom, which current VLA systems struggle to deliver.

## Method

- Designed DuoCore-FS, an asynchronous fast-slow VLA framework where a large VLM handles slow (1-3 Hz) semantic reasoning and a diffusion-policy network performs fast (25-30 Hz) whole-body action generation.
- Implemented a differentiable bridge buffer to enable truly asynchronous information flow between the slow and fast systems, allowing them to operate at independent optimal frequencies.
- Developed a geometry-aware action tokenizer based on Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ-VAE) to compactly represent the high-dimensional (25-DoF) whole-body actions.
- Introduced a cross-timescale co-training strategy that simulates asynchronous deployment conditions during joint optimization, ensuring effective end-to-end learning.

## Results

- DuoCore-FS achieved a 90% overall success rate on a popcorn-scooping task, operating at 32.3 Hz, which is approximately three times faster than synchronous baselines (e.g., ".0" at 12.5 Hz).
- The framework demonstrated improved generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios (50% overall success vs. 10% for ".0") and enhanced robustness in anomalous conditions (95.8% vs. 91.7% detection/recovery).
- The custom RVQ-VAE action tokenizer was shown to be critical, enabling the slow system to achieve 83.3% success (at 3.27 Hz) while a naive tokenizer resulted in 0% success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action generation through an asynchronous fast-slow architecture effectively overcomes VLM inference bottlenecks while maintaining VLM-driven intelligence.
> - A specialized geometry-aware action tokenizer is essential for efficiently learning and representing high-dimensional, structured whole-body actions, enabling stable policy performance.
> - End-to-end co-training with cross-timescale sampling effectively aligns the slow and fast systems, ensuring robust performance under asynchronous deployment conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the forefront of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, specifically addressing the critical challenge of real-time control for complex, whole-body robotic platforms. The broader landscape of VLA policies has evolved significantly, moving from traditional hard-coded control to end-to-end learning frameworks that interpret visual observations and linguistic instructions to generate actions.

Initially, VLA models, often autoregressive and inspired by large language models (LLMs), demonstrated promise by leveraging internet-scale vision-language pretraining. However, these methods typically suffer from low inference speeds due to their sequential, token-by-token generation, which is a major bottleneck for real-time control. The increasing size and complexity of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) further exacerbate this issue, as their inference latency becomes incompatible with the high-frequency control requirements of physical robots.

To address this, the research community has increasingly adopted "dual-system" or "fast-slow" VLA architectures, drawing inspiration from human cognitive processing (System 1 for fast, reactive control and System 2 for slow, deliberative reasoning). In these architectures, a large VLM typically serves as the "slow" system for high-level semantic understanding, while a lighter-weight model (often diffusion-based) acts as the "fast" system for generating low-level actions.

However, many early dual-system VLA implementations, such as DP-VLA, HiRT, Robodual, G0-VLA, and Pi05, still operate synchronously. This means the fast system remains constrained by the slow VLM's inference speed, preventing true real-time performance. This paper specifically targets this limitation, building upon recent efforts to introduce *asynchronous* dual-system VLA, as seen in works like FiS-VLA, OpenHelix, Helix, and Hume.

DuoCore-FS distinguishes itself from these existing asynchronous frameworks in several key ways:
- **True Parallelism and Asynchrony:** Unlike FiS-VLA and OpenHelix, which enforce fixed frequency ratios between slow and fast modules, DuoCore-FS achieves truly parallel and asynchronous execution. This ensures the fast pathway's execution frequency is independent of the slow VLM's update rate, driven instead by real-time control needs.
- **End-to-End Training:** While Hume offers asynchronous parallel inference, its cascaded architecture is not trained end-to-end. DuoCore-FS, conversely, maintains end-to-end joint training of both pathways, ensuring tight semantic-control alignment without relying on separate or hand-engineered modules. This integrated optimization is critical for robust policy learning.
- **Whole-Body Manipulation Focus:** The paper explicitly targets whole-body robotic manipulation, a more complex domain than typical dual-arm tasks. This involves coordinating a larger number of joints across an expanded motion space, adapting to dynamic viewpoints, and addressing challenges like torso rotation or reaching across obstacles. This increases the demand for precise, high-frequency actions across the entire robot body, which DuoCore-FS is specifically designed to handle.
- **Specialized Action Tokenization:** To manage the high-dimensional action space of whole-body robots, DuoCore-FS introduces a geometry-aware action tokenizer based on Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ-VAE). This is crucial for providing a compact, unified representation of complex whole-body actions, a necessity for effective learning and inference in this challenging domain.

In summary, DuoCore-FS pushes the boundaries of VLA research by offering a comprehensively engineered, truly asynchronous, and end-to-end trainable fast-slow framework. It specifically addresses the heightened demands of whole-body manipulation, aiming to overcome the latency bottlenecks and architectural limitations of prior VLA systems to deliver high-frequency, responsive, and semantically grounded robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of the DuoCore-FS framework is to enable **high-frequency whole-body action generation while effectively leveraging the rich semantic reasoning capabilities of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**. This overarching goal is driven by several critical motivations stemming from the limitations of current VLA systems and the increasing complexity of robotic tasks:

1. **Overcoming the VLM Inference Bottleneck:** Most VLA systems, whether single-stream or early dual-system architectures, are fundamentally bottlenecked by the slow inference speed of large VLMs. As VLMs continue to grow in parameter size (e.g., 3B, 7B parameters), their latency becomes prohibitive for real-time control, especially for dynamic and responsive robotic manipulation. The primary motivation is to decouple the slow, deliberative reasoning from fast, reactive action generation, ensuring that the VLM's computational demands do not impede the robot's control frequency.

2. **Addressing the Demands of Whole-Body Manipulation:** Current robotic tasks are moving beyond simple pick-and-place to more complex, human-like whole-body interactions. These tasks, such as the popcorn-scooping scenario demonstrated, involve coordinating many degrees of freedom (DoF) across the torso, arms, and grippers in expanded motion spaces. This significantly increases the dimensionality of the control problem and intensifies the need for VLA policies that can produce precise, high-frequency action signals across the entire body, adapting to dynamic changes in viewpoints and environmental contexts.

3. **Achieving True Asynchrony and Parallelism:** Existing asynchronous dual-system VLAs often fall short of true parallelism. Some rely on fixed scheduling ratios, meaning the fast controller's updates are still indirectly tied to the slow module's cycle. Others, while allowing parallel inference, may lack end-to-end training, which restricts the joint optimization between high-level reasoning and real-time control. A key objective for DuoCore-FS is to establish a **truly parallel and asynchronous** interaction between the slow and fast systems, where each operates at its optimal frequency, without artificial constraints.

4. **Ensuring Semantic-Control Alignment through End-to-End Training:** While decoupling systems is important for speed, maintaining coherence between high-level semantic understanding and low-level action execution is equally vital. The motivation is to achieve this alignment through **end-to-end training** of both the VLM (slow pathway) and the diffusion policy (fast pathway). This ensures that the latent representations passed from the slow to the fast system are optimized to best support accurate and responsive whole-body action generation, rather than relying on separately trained or hand-engineered modules.

5. **Developing Compact and Unified Whole-Body Action Representations:** The high dimensionality of whole-body actions poses a significant challenge for efficient policy learning and inference. A specific objective is to develop a **compact, unified, and geometry-aware action tokenizer** that can effectively represent the structured patterns of high-dimensional whole-body joint configurations, thereby enabling coordinated control.

In summary, DuoCore-FS is motivated by the need for more capable, responsive, and versatile VLA systems that can handle the complexities of whole-body robotic manipulation in real-world scenarios, unconstrained by the inherent latency of large language models. Its objectives are to achieve this through innovative architectural design, novel action representation, and a robust co-training strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DuoCore-FS introduces a novel fast-slow Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy framework comprising two asynchronously operating subsystems, meticulously designed to couple high-level semantic reasoning with real-time whole-body control.

#### 3.1. Architecture

The core of DuoCore-FS is its dual-system architecture:

- **Slow System (Semantic Reasoning and Intent Extraction):**
 - **Frequency:** Operates at a low frequency of 1–3 Hz.
 - **Components:** Instantiated with a large Vision-Language Model (VLM) such as PaliGemma-3B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, or Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
 - **Functionality:** Processes multimodal observations (images from multiple cameras, proprioceptive states) and task prompts. Generates high-level semantic outputs `r_t`, which may include chain-of-thought reasoning, bounding-box predictions, subtask indicators, and coarse discrete action tokens. These action tokens encode high-level manipulation intent, serving as strong priors for the fast system.
 - **Objective:** Optimized using a negative log-likelihood objective `L_slow` to ensure semantic and reasoning representations are aligned with the multimodal context.

- **Fast System (Diffusion-Policy Whole-Body Action Generator):**
 - **Frequency:** Operates at a high control rate of 25–30 Hz.
 - **Components:** Functions as a Pi0-small style diffusion-policy network, specifically a Transformer-based diffusion-policy decoder.
 - **Functionality:** Continuously generates smooth, continuous, and coordinated whole-body actions. It conditions its generation on current visual observations, proprioceptive states, and crucial latent representations fetched from the bridge buffer. All conditioning modalities are projected into a shared embedding space and processed by a multi-layer Transformer encoder.
 - **Objective:** Learns a conditional vector field over action chunks, optimized with an `L_fast` objective to predict a denoising vector field.

#### 3.2. Bridge Buffer

A critical component enabling asynchronous information flow is the **bridge buffer** `B_t`.
- **Decoupling:** It decouples the generation and consumption of representations, allowing the slow and fast systems to operate at independent frequencies.
- **Contents:** Stores task-relevant semantic and reasoning representations produced by the VLM, specifically instruction embeddings and learnable fusion-query embeddings `q_ψ`.
- **Mechanism:** The slow system periodically refreshes `B_t` (1-3 Hz). The fast system fetches the latest representations from `B_t` at its high control rate (25-30 Hz).
- **Differentiable Interface:** `B_t` serves as a differentiable interface, enabling end-to-end training where the parameters `ψ` of the fusion queries are optimized through the fast system's action-generation objective.
- **Stability:** The original task instruction representation is also fed directly to the fast system, in addition to the buffer's contents, to maintain stable linguistic constraints and prevent loss of crucial task details, especially when relying on dynamically extracted reasoning.

#### 3.3. Action Tokenizer for Whole-Body Manipulation

To handle the Astribot S1 platform's 25-DoF action space (torso, left/right arms, grippers), a specialized **geometry-aware action tokenizer** is employed, inspired by residual VQ-VAE designs:
- **Action Space:** Utilizes Cartesian delta pose control for torso (9D), left arm (9D), right arm (9D), and absolute opening commands for grippers (2D), resulting in a 29-dimensional action vector `a_t`.
- **Factorization:** Actions `a_t` are factorized into semantically grounded components: `a_pos` (position deltas), `a_rot` (orientation deltas using 6D continuous SO(3) representation), and `a_grip` (gripper commands).
- **Chunk Processing:** An action chunk `A_t:t+T-1` is decomposed into structured position (`P`), orientation (`R`), and gripper (`G`) streams.
- **Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ):** Each stream is processed by an independent lightweight 1D convolutional encoder-decoder, producing latent features. These features are then discretized using stream-specific RVQ modules, each maintaining its own codebook (size 1024).
- **Training Objective:** The RVQ-VAE is optimized to reconstruct its corresponding action stream, using `ℓ2` loss for position and gripper (`L_pos_rec`, `L_grip_rec`) and geodesic distance on SO(3) for rotation (`L_rot_rec`). A standard VQ objective (`L_vq`) is also included. The overall objective is `L_token`.

#### 3.4. Cross-Timescale Co-Training

To ensure effective end-to-end optimization and accurately simulate asynchronous deployment, a two-stage training procedure is implemented:
1. **Stage 1 (Slow System Pre-training):** The slow system is independently trained using standard VLM objectives to learn high-level reasoning and align multimodal cues.
2. **Stage 2 (Joint Fast-Slow Training with Cross-Timescale Sampling):**
 - **Asynchronous Simulation:** To address the train-inference mismatch from different operating frequencies, a cross-timescale sampling strategy is introduced. If the slow system processes `o_t0`, the fast system receives a temporally shifted observation `o_t0+Δ`, where `Δ` is uniformly sampled within `[0, Δ_max]`. `Δ_max` approximates the maximum observation delay based on `T_slow`, `T_fast`, and camera frame rate `f_cam`. This strategy ensures the fast system learns under conditions mirroring real deployment.
 - **Unified Optimization:** The entire Fast-Slow VLA framework is optimized jointly using a combined objective `L_joint = λ_slow L_slow + λ_fast L_fast`, with `λ_slow = 1` and `λ_fast = 10`. The higher weight for `L_fast` reflects the need for stronger supervision for the fast controller to master precise actions under asynchronous semantic guidance, given the slow system's pre-training.

#### 3.5. Asynchronous Inference Pipeline

During deployment, the system operates fully asynchronously:
- **Slow System:** Runs at 1-3 Hz, processes current observations/instructions, generates reasoning and bridge embeddings. It uses a Jacobi-style parallel decoding strategy (inspired by CLLMs) to further reduce VLM inference latency. These representations are periodically written to the bridge buffer.
- **Fast System:** Executes at 25-30 Hz. Continuously fetches the most recent latent representations from the buffer, integrates them with current perception, and generates continuous actions via its diffusion policy. It operates smoothly even if the slow system has not yet produced new outputs, using the previously refreshed embeddings. This ensures stable, real-time control aligned with high-level intent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DuoCore-FS on the Astribot S1 platform demonstrates significant improvements in real-time performance and robustness for whole-body manipulation tasks.

1. **In-Distribution Task Performance and Inference Speed:**
 - **Baseline (π0):** Achieved an 85% overall success rate on the popcorn-scooping task with an inference frequency of 12.5 Hz.
 - **DuoCore-FS-slow (slow system only):** Exhibited a lower overall success rate of 55% and a slower inference frequency of 3.27 Hz. This is attributed to the inherent lossiness of abstract reasoning tokens and the autoregressive nature of their generation, which makes fine-grained control challenging.
 - **DuoCore-FS (full system):** Achieved a 90% overall success rate, **surpassing π0** while delivering a remarkable **32.3 Hz inference frequency**, approximately **three times faster** than π0. This is a core finding, validating the effectiveness of the asynchronous dual-system design in achieving high performance without compromising speed. The slow system's reasoning effectively guides the fast system, while asynchrony removes the VLM bottleneck.

2. **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:**
 - When evaluating on unseen cup locations, DuoCore-FS-slow showed improved generalization in initial subtasks (e.g., 70% vs. 20% for π0 on "pick up the cup"), suggesting the VLM's autoregressive reasoning aids in adapting to novel placements.
 - The full DuoCore-FS system achieved a 50% overall success rate, significantly outperforming π0 (10%). This indicates that DuoCore-FS successfully combines the generalization capabilities of the slow VLM with the precision of the fast diffusion policy, leading to better performance in novel scenarios.

3. **Robustness under Anomalous Scenarios:**
 - Under challenging conditions like "Cup fell over," "Cup is upside down," or "Cup was taken away," DuoCore-FS demonstrated slightly higher detection and recovery success (95.8%) compared to π0 (91.7%). This suggests improved robustness in handling unexpected environmental changes, crucial for real-world deployment.

4. **Language Following Ability:**
 - On a low-data task (closing a beverage cabinet door), DuoCore-FS showed substantially stronger language-following ability with a 42.9% success rate, compared to π0's 14.3%. This improvement is attributed to the slow system's autoregressive training, which encourages more consistent token-by-token reasoning aligned with instructions.
 - Despite the improvement, the success rate was not perfect, largely due to extreme data imbalance (the beverage-cabinet task was only 1% of the training set). This highlights a current limitation but also the potential of the slow system for enhanced semantic understanding.

5. **Ablation Study of Whole-Body Action Tokenizer:**
 - The specialized RVQ-VAE tokenizer for whole-body actions proved critical.
 - Using a naive tokenizer like FAST resulted in significantly longer action token sequences (average 81.12 vs. 36 for RVQ-VAE, max 205 vs. 36), leading to an inference speed of only 0.95 Hz for the slow system and 0% task success due to compounding errors and erratic motions.
 - The custom RVQ-VAE tokenizer, in contrast, enabled the slow system to achieve 83.3% success at 3.27 Hz, validating its necessity for compact and effective representation of complex whole-body actions.

In summary, DuoCore-FS successfully delivers on its promise of high-frequency, responsive, and robust whole-body robotic manipulation. It achieves this by effectively decoupling reasoning from control, enhancing generalization, and ensuring strong semantic alignment through its novel architecture and co-training strategy, while a specialized action tokenizer efficiently handles the high-dimensional control space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DuoCore-FS represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics, with profound implications for both academic research and practical applications, particularly within commercial robotics.

1. **Breaking the VLM Inference Barrier:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is DuoCore-FS's ability to achieve a 30 Hz whole-body action-chunk generation frequency while integrating a 3B-parameter VLM. This is a critical breakthrough, as it directly addresses the long-standing bottleneck of slow VLM inference speeds that has hampered the real-time performance and control stability of VLA systems. By demonstrating a threefold speedup compared to synchronous baselines with comparable model sizes, DuoCore-FS makes large VLMs genuinely viable for responsive, real-world robotic control.

2. **Enabling Advanced Whole-Body Manipulation:** The framework is specifically designed for whole-body robotic platforms, a domain that introduces significantly higher complexity due to increased degrees of freedom, expanded motion spaces, and dynamic environmental interactions. DuoCore-FS provides a robust solution for coordinating these complex systems, as evidenced by its successful application to the challenging popcorn-scooping task. This work paves the way for robots to perform more dexterous, human-like, and versatile tasks that require full-body coordination, moving beyond traditional dual-arm manipulation.

3. **Architectural Innovations for Asynchronous Control:** The truly parallel and asynchronous design, coupled with a robust latent representation buffer, is a methodological leap. It offers a principled way to manage the different temporal scales of high-level deliberation and low-level control without compromising either. The end-to-end joint training, enforced by the cross-timescale sampling strategy, ensures that the slow and fast systems are optimally aligned, fostering a deeper integration of semantic understanding and physical action.

4. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The experimental results demonstrate that DuoCore-FS not only achieves high performance in-distribution but also improves generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios and exhibits increased robustness in anomalous conditions. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic environments where unforeseen circumstances are common. The improved language-following ability also points to a stronger semantic grounding, allowing robots to better interpret and execute diverse instructions.

5. **Practical Commercial Impact:** Given that Astribot Team developed DuoCore-FS and plans to provide its implementation as part of the Astribot robotic platform for commercial users, the research has immediate and tangible commercial relevance. It indicates a clear pathway for integrating advanced AI capabilities into real-world robotic products, potentially accelerating the deployment of intelligent, general-purpose robots in various industries, from service robotics to logistics and manufacturing. The focus on deployability, including optimizing for specific hardware (NVIDIA RTX 4090, TensorRT compilation), underscores its practical orientation.

6. **Extensible Framework for Future Research:** The modular nature of DuoCore-FS, allowing different VLM backbones to be plugged into the slow pathway, ensures its extensibility. This flexibility will facilitate future research into integrating more advanced multi-task foundation models, richer real-world data, chain-of-thought reasoning, and even incorporating high-frequency force and tactile signals, further pushing the boundaries of embodied AI.

In conclusion, DuoCore-FS is a landmark achievement that effectively resolves a fundamental challenge in VLA robotics: achieving high-frequency, responsive control with large, semantically powerful models. Its impact will be felt across academic research by setting new benchmarks for asynchronous architectures and in commercial applications by enabling the deployment of more capable, versatile, and robust whole-body manipulation robots.
