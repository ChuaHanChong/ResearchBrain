# Asynchronous Fast-Slow Vision-Language-Action Policies for Whole-Body Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DuoCore-FS, developed by the Astribot Team, introduces an asynchronous fast-slow Vision-Language-Action framework that enables high-frequency whole-body robotic manipulation by decoupling large VLM-based semantic reasoning from real-time diffusion-policy action generation. It achieved a 90% success rate on complex tasks at 32.3 Hz inference frequency, significantly outperforming synchronous baselines.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have slow inference speeds, bottlenecking real-time control in robotic manipulation.
- Existing fast-slow VLA architectures often use synchronous operations or fixed frequency ratios, limiting the fast system's true real-time potential.
- Whole-body robotic manipulation requires high-frequency and precise control across many degrees of freedom, which current VLA systems struggle to deliver.

## Method

- Designed DuoCore-FS, an asynchronous fast-slow VLA framework where a large VLM handles slow (1-3 Hz) semantic reasoning and a diffusion-policy network performs fast (25-30 Hz) whole-body action generation.
- Implemented a differentiable bridge buffer to enable truly asynchronous information flow between the slow and fast systems, allowing them to operate at independent optimal frequencies.
- Developed a geometry-aware action tokenizer based on Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ-VAE) to compactly represent the high-dimensional (25-DoF) whole-body actions.
- Introduced a cross-timescale co-training strategy that simulates asynchronous deployment conditions during joint optimization, ensuring effective end-to-end learning.

## Results

- DuoCore-FS achieved a 90% overall success rate on a popcorn-scooping task, operating at 32.3 Hz, which is approximately three times faster than synchronous baselines (e.g., ".0" at 12.5 Hz).
- The framework demonstrated improved generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios (50% overall success vs. 10% for ".0") and enhanced robustness in anomalous conditions (95.8% vs. 91.7% detection/recovery).
- The custom RVQ-VAE action tokenizer was shown to be critical, enabling the slow system to achieve 83.3% success (at 3.27 Hz) while a naive tokenizer resulted in 0% success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action generation through an asynchronous fast-slow architecture effectively overcomes VLM inference bottlenecks while maintaining VLM-driven intelligence.
> - A specialized geometry-aware action tokenizer is essential for efficiently learning and representing high-dimensional, structured whole-body actions, enabling stable policy performance.
> - End-to-end co-training with cross-timescale sampling effectively aligns the slow and fast systems, ensuring robust performance under asynchronous deployment conditions.
