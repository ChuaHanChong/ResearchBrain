# Dynamic Search for Inference-Time Alignment in Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Dynamic Search for Diffusion (DSearch) introduces a gradient-free method for inference-time alignment in diffusion models, effectively maximizing non-differentiable reward functions while preserving sample naturalness and diversity. The approach consistently outperforms existing gradient-free techniques, improving DNA enhancer activity scores to 7.245 and drug-likeness (QED) to 0.946, among other quantitative gains across various domains.

## Problem

- Many real-world scientific applications require optimizing non-differentiable or black-box reward functions in diffusion models, a challenge for gradient-based alignment methods.
- Existing gradient-free guidance methods (e.g., best-of-N, SMC, SVDD) often yield suboptimal reward alignment or are computationally inefficient in complex search spaces.
- Achieving high reward while simultaneously maintaining the naturalness and diversity of generated samples poses a significant balance challenge.

## Method

- The research frames ==inference-time alignment== in diffusion models as a ==dynamic search problem== over the inherent tree structure of the denoising process.
- It introduces **DSearch** (Dynamic Search for Diffusion), a ==gradient-free algorithm== employing a ==dynamic beam search== strategy that adjusts beam and tree widths across diffusion steps.
- **DSearch** incorporates a ==multi-step Monte Carlo look-ahead heuristic== to accurately estimate future rewards from intermediate states, guiding the search efficiently.

## Results

- DSearch consistently achieved superior reward performance across image generation, biological sequence design (e.g., **7.245** for DNA enhancer activity), and molecular optimization (e.g., **0.946 QED** and **13.7 Parp1 docking affinity**).
- The method maintained competitive naturalness and diversity in generated samples, with lower NLL values for molecular generation compared to SVDD (e.g., **911 vs. 1049** for QED).
- DSearch demonstrated more significant gains in reward scores with increasing computational budgets, efficiently utilizing resources and showing **~35% faster reward-per-second scaling** than baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing inference-time alignment as a search problem enables robust, gradient-free optimization of non-differentiable reward functions in diffusion models.
> - Dynamic scheduling of search expansion, which concentrates search efforts in later diffusion steps, significantly improves reward optimization while reducing computational costs by approximately 35%.
> - A look-ahead heuristic function, which estimates future rewards from intermediate states, enhances the accuracy of beam selection and leads to superior final rewards.
