# Vintix II: Decision Pre-Trained Transformer is a Scalable In-Context Reinforcement Learner

> [!summary] Summary
> VINTIX II scales the Decision Pre-Trained Transformer framework for in-context reinforcement learning to diverse, continuous-control environments by integrating a flow-based policy head. The system demonstrates enhanced generalization and adaptive capabilities on both seen and unseen tasks, outperforming prior large action models.

## Problem

- Developing scalable "Large Action Models" in reinforcement learning to handle extensive task diversity, similar to advancements in NLP and CV.
- Existing Transformer-based RL systems often lack the ability to leverage real-time reward signals for policy correction and adaptation during inference.
- Prior in-context learning (ICL) methods, like Decision Pre-Trained Transformers (DPT), had not established scalability to complex, multi-modal continuous action spaces in diverse environments.

## Method

- Scaled the ==Decision Pre-Trained Transformer== (DPT) framework across 10 diverse domains and 209 distinct training tasks, encompassing robotic control, energy management, and autonomous driving.
- Integrated a flow-based generative policy head with the DPT architecture to explicitly model complex, multi-modal continuous action distributions, utilizing a rectified-flow objective.
- Collected and open-sourced an expanded cross-domain dataset exceeding 700 million transitions, generated using Continuous Noise Distillation (CND) to improve state-action-reward distribution coverage.

## Results

- Achieved over **75%** of demonstrator performance on 46 held-out test tasks, including **102%** on MetaDrive and **100%** on ControlGym, indicating strong generalization to novel environments.
- Demonstrated substantial improvements in scaled return over prior methods, with gains of +**17%** on Bi-DexHands, +**4%** on MuJoCo, and +**63%** on Meta-World ML45 compared to prompted Vintix.
- Exhibited robust adaptive behavior in both online (zero-shot adaptation) and offline (few-shot prompting) inference settings, with cold-start performance on unseen tasks often on par with prompted runs for 8 out of 10 domains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of flow-based policy heads enables DPT to effectively capture and generate complex, multi-modal continuous action distributions, improving performance in challenging control tasks.
> - The multi-domain training paradigm, leveraging a shared transformer backbone, facilitates significant generalization to unseen tasks and parametric variations across different environments.
> - Analysis of action distributions demonstrates a progressive concentration of action beliefs with increasing context length, empirically supporting DPT's interpretation as in-context Bayesian posterior sampling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses a central goal in artificial intelligence: the development of general-purpose agents capable of operating across diverse task domains. While significant advancements have been observed in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV) with models demonstrating broad task-solving capabilities, reinforcement learning (RL) has lagged in developing "Large Action Models" that can handle extensive task diversity.

The current prevailing approach in RL often involves training Transformer-based architectures on offline datasets, similar to large language model (LLM) pretraining, and enhancing inference with retrieval-augmented generation. However, a limitation of these systems is their typical inability to leverage real-time reward signals for policy correction and adaptation.

In-context learning (ICL), which allows models to adapt to new tasks based on a few demonstrations without parameter updates, has gained prominence in LLMs. The transfer of ICL capabilities to meta-reinforcement learning (Meta-RL) seeks to enable agents to optimize reward-driven behavior in novel environments without task-specific fine-tuning. Key paradigms in this area include Algorithm Distillation (AD) and the Decision Pre-Trained Transformer (DPT). While these methods have shown in-context behavior in single-domain or grid-like settings, their scalability to diverse, continuous-control environments has remained less explored.

Prior work, such as Polubarov et al. (2025), scaled AD to multi-domain settings but observed limitations in generalization to unseen tasks. DPT, with its interpretation as Bayesian posterior sampling over actions, offers a promising alternative, but its scalability to complex, multi-modal continuous action spaces had not been established. This work builds upon the DPT framework and aims to extend its capabilities to diverse, multi-domain continuous control scenarios, addressing the challenge of modeling complex action distributions through a flow-based policy head. It contributes to the development of fully parametric generalist agents that can self-correct and adapt in context, bridging a gap between existing offline training paradigms and the desired real-time adaptability of generalist RL agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish the scalability of the Decision Pre-Trained Transformer (DPT) framework for in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) in diverse, multi-domain environments, particularly those with complex continuous and multi-modal action distributions.

The motivation for this work arises from several key observations and existing limitations in the field of generalist agents and ICRL:

1. **Limited Scalability of Prior ICRL Approaches:** While Algorithm Distillation (AD) demonstrated the potential of ICRL and was scaled to multi-domain settings, its generalization performance to entirely unseen tasks remained constrained. The Decision Pre-Trained Transformer (DPT) showed stronger in-context learning abilities in simplified domains but its scalability to a wider array of complex, continuous-control tasks had not been systematically investigated.
2. **Challenges with Continuous, Multi-modal Action Spaces:** Existing DPT variants primarily targeted discrete-action environments, where inference-time sampling is relatively straightforward. Applying DPT to continuous control tasks, especially those requiring expressive policy classes to capture multi-modal behaviors (e.g., in robotic manipulation or autonomous driving), poses a significant challenge. Traditional Gaussian policy heads used in some continuous-action DPT extensions have been noted to yield only moderate results, suggesting an inability to effectively model complex action posteriors.
3. **Need for Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** A core aspiration for generalist agents is the ability to adapt to novel tasks and parametric variations within environments that were not explicitly part of the training data. Prior methods often exhibit strong performance on training tasks but struggle to generalize effectively. This limitation underscores the need for architectures that can infer and leverage task-specific information more robustly for adaptation.
4. **Desire for Real-time Adaptation and Self-Correction:** Current Large Action Models often rely on offline policy execution without leveraging rewards for real-time adjustments. ICRL aims to enable agents to achieve reward-optimizing behavior and adapt in real-time without requiring expensive task-specific fine-tuning. This work seeks to reinforce ICRL as a viable alternative to expert distillation for training such generalist agents.
5. **Expansion of Cross-Domain Datasets:** Effective training of generalist agents requires large and diverse datasets. The authors identify a need to expand existing multi-domain datasets to support broader knowledge transfer and more rigorous evaluation of generalization capabilities.

To address these motivations, the authors aim to:
- Scale DPT to a cross-domain setting encompassing a wide variety of tasks, including robotic locomotion and manipulation, HVAC control, PDE optimization, and autonomous driving.
- Integrate a flow-based policy head with DPT to explicitly model complex, multi-modal continuous action distributions, leveraging Flow Matching as a training objective that preserves DPT's interpretation as Bayesian posterior sampling.
- Demonstrate substantial improvements in test-time performance on unseen tasks compared to prior Large Action Models, both in online (zero-shot adaptation) and offline (few-shot prompting) inference settings.
- Collect and open-source a significantly expanded cross-domain dataset to facilitate future research in ICRL and generalist agent development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology extends the Decision Pre-Trained Transformer (DPT) to accommodate multi-domain continuous control settings characterized by complex, multi-modal action distributions. This extension is primarily achieved by integrating a flow-based policy head.

#### 3.1. Dataset Collection and Preparation

- **Method:** The training dataset is generated using the Continuous Noise Distillation (CND) method, an approach described in Polubarov et al. (2025). CND involves progressively altering the quality of a demonstrator policy through action noising. This technique aims to increase the coverage of the visited state-action-reward tuple distribution, which has been shown to improve test-time performance.
- **Relabeling:** The collected state-action transitions are subsequently relabeled with optimal actions from the demonstrator, as required by the DPT pipeline.
- **Diversity and Scale:** The dataset comprises over 700 million transitions, representing a 3.2x increase over a previous dataset by Polubarov et al. (2025). It spans 10 diverse domains and includes 209 distinct training tasks.
 - **Domains:** Industrial-Benchmark, Bi-DexHands, Meta-World, Kinetix, CityLearn, ControlGym, HumEnv, MuJoCo, SinerGym, and Meta-Drive. These domains cover a variety of control problems, from robotic manipulation and locomotion to energy management and autonomous driving.
- **Train-Test Split:** To evaluate generalization to unseen environments, 46 tasks across all domains are reserved for validation and excluded from the training set. This split ensures a challenging assessment of the model's adaptive capabilities. Specific modifications were made to the Meta-World evaluation setup, fixing goal positions across environment resets to align with traditional Meta-RL formulations.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The model is composed of three main blocks:
1. **Encoder:** This block maps input actions, observations, and rewards into fixed-size embedding vectors. To handle variability in observation and action spaces across diverse tasks, tasks are partitioned into non-overlapping groups that share the same action-observation structure. Each group is processed by its own Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) encoder and decoder, promoting task-agnosticism within groups and encouraging reliance on contextual information for adaptation.
2. **Transformer Backbone:** A causal Transformer (specifically, a TinyLLaMA implementation) serves as the main representational capacity. Positional encodings are removed, consistent with the DPT framework.
 - **Configuration:** The backbone consists of 16 layers, 24 attention heads, an embedding size of 1536, and a post-attention feed-forward hidden size of 6144.
 - **Parameters:** The total model size is 928 million parameters.
3. **Flow-based Decoder Head:** Conditioned on the transformer’s hidden states, this head outputs actions in the original action space. It uses a rectified-flow objective to represent richer, potentially multi-modal action distributions, addressing limitations of Gaussian heads in continuous action spaces.
 - **Vector Field Parametrization:** The context-dependent vector field $u(t, h, x_t)$ is parameterized using a time encoder $\gamma$ and an MLP $v_\eta$, where $h$ is the transformer output and $x_t$ is an intermediate state in the flow.
 - **Policy Definition:** The policy $\pi(\cdot | h)$ is defined as the flow at terminal time $t=1$, $\psi(1, h, \cdot)$, meaning action sampling involves drawing an initial state from a base distribution (e.g., $N(0,I_a)$) and integrating the ODE to $t=1$.

#### 3.3. Training Procedure

- **Data Loading:** The training follows the vanilla DPT setup, sampling tuples $(o_q, C, a^\star)$ from the multi-task cross-domain dataset. $o_q$ is the query observation, $a^\star$ is the demonstrator's optimal action for $o_q$, and $C$ is a task-specific context of length $L$ comprising $\{(o_i, a_i, r_i)\}$ interactions. Unlike some DPT implementations, the next observation $o'$ is omitted.
- **Input Sequence:** Each input sequence to the Transformer consists of a Beginning-Of-Sequence (BOS) token, one query token, and $L$ context tokens, which are randomly permuted.
- **Objective Function:** A causal Transformer encodes the sequence, producing hidden states. A context-conditioned vector-field head supervises all relevant positions to predict $a^\star$ by minimizing the rectified-flow matching objective (Liu et al., 2022).
 - The loss function $L_{RF}$ is the expected squared difference between the predicted vector field and the target vector $(a^\star - x_{0,j})$, where $x_{t,j}$ is a linear interpolation between $x_{0,j} \sim N(0,I_a)$ and $a^\star$.
- **Time Encoding:** The time component $t_j \in$ is encoded using a sinusoidal time embedding, with learnable frequencies initialized on a logarithmic scale.
- **Computational Setup:** Training was performed on 8 H100 GPUs with a batch size of 64 and input sequences of length 4096.

#### 3.4. Inference Procedure

Policy evaluation is conducted in two regimes:
1. **Online Setting:** The model starts with an empty context $C$. As interactions $(o_q, a, r)$ occur during deployment, they are incrementally appended to $C$. If $C$ exceeds its maximum length $L$, the oldest transition is removed (FIFO-style memory). This mode assesses the agent's ability for iterative self-correction.
2. **Offline Setting:** The context $C = \{(o_i, a_i, r_i)\}_{i=1}^{L_C}$ is fixed and contains a set of demonstrator episodes (e.g., 2500 transitions), which remains unchanged throughout inference. This mode evaluates adaptation based on prior demonstrations.
- **Action Decoding:** Given a selected task group and a base distribution $x_0 \sim N(0, I_{g_a})$, the action $a$ is obtained by numerically integrating the learned vector field $v_\eta$ from $t=0$ to $t=1$ using Heun’s method (second-order Runge–Kutta) with $M$ uniform steps. The vector field is conditioned on the hidden state of the last Transformer token.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation assessed the model's performance on both training and unseen tasks, in online and offline settings, comparing it against established baselines.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics and Baselines

- **Metric:** The primary metric is the total episode return, normalized with respect to random and expert policy scores: $score_{normalized} = (score_{raw} - score_{random}) / (score_{demonstrator} - score_{random})$. Inter-quartile mean (IQM) was used for aggregated scores.
- **Baselines:** Vintix (Polubarov et al., 2025) and REGENT (Sridhar et al., 2025). JAT (Gallou´edec et al., 2024) was omitted due to prior under-performance. Vintix was compared in both online and offline modes, while REGENT was primarily compared in prompted (offline) evaluation.
- **Meta-World Protocol:** The evaluation setup for Meta-World was modified to fix goal positions across environment resets, aligning with the ML1 protocol for goal adaptation, which presented a more challenging scenario.

#### 4.2. Performance on Training Tasks

- **Online Deployment (Figure 2, Section 4.2):**
 - The model exhibited consistent improvement in normalized returns over the course of deployment across most domains, demonstrating test-time adaptive behavior.
 - On Kinetix, ControlGym, MuJoCo, and MetaDrive, the model achieved near-demonstrator performance from the first episode, suggesting a strong prior for training tasks and requiring minimal episodes for correction.
- **Offline Deployment (Figure 3, Right):**
 - Providing additional task-specific prompts (2500 transitions) improved performance across all 10 domains, yielding an average gain of +4.1% compared to online performance on training tasks. This indicates that contextual information further enhances performance even on tasks already familiar to the model.

#### 4.3. Performance on Unseen Tasks

- **Offline Deployment (Figure 3, Left):**
 - On the 46 held-out tasks, the model achieved over 75% of demonstrator performance in MetaDrive (102%), CityLearn (78%), SinerGym (92%), and ControlGym (100%).
 - **Comparison with Vintix:** The model showed improvements in scaled return over the prompted version of Vintix: +17% on Bi-DexHands, +4% on MuJoCo (with parametric variations), and +63% on the Meta-World ML45 split.
 - **Comparison with REGENT:** The approach achieved an 8.2% higher normalized return on 5 unseen tasks in the Meta-World ML45 benchmark.
 - These results suggest that the flow-based DPT architecture provides an inductive bias supporting effective in-context imitation, leading to enhanced performance on unseen tasks.
- **Online Deployment (Figure 3, Left):**
 - Cold-start evaluation (empty initial context) on unseen tasks performed on par with the prompted version for 8 out of 10 domains.
 - Performance lagged on Meta-World ML45 and Bi-DexHands ML20 benchmarks, potentially due to the inherent difficulty of these domains for task adaptation without additional information or their high control dimensionality and varying observation/action spaces.
 - On the Meta-World ML1 split (held-out goal variations), the demonstration-less model achieved an 85% normalized score, outperforming REGENT (which used 100 expert episodes) by 3%. This advantage is attributed to the deployment-time self-corrective behavior, potentially stemming from DPT's ability to implement context-based Bayesian posterior sampling.

#### 4.4. Analysis and Ablations

- **Progressive Concentration of Action Beliefs (Figure 4):**
 - Analysis of action distributions as a function of context length revealed a posterior-like contraction. With short contexts, distributions were wide, indicating uncertainty. Longer contexts led to sharper, narrower peaks, accompanied by a monotonic decrease in entropy. This behavior is consistent with DPT's theoretical interpretation as in-context posterior sampling.
- **Effect of Number of Demonstrations (Figure 5):**
 - Offline performance generally improved with increasing prompt size (from 500 to 4000 transitions) for Meta-World, Industrial-Benchmark, and SinerGym. Performance remained stable in other domains, suggesting that additional data either boosts or at least does not degrade performance.
 - The model scaled slightly faster than REGENT on Meta-World but was constrained by its context length limit.
- **Comparison with Gaussian Heads (Appendix K):**
 - Ablation studies comparing Flow Matching (FM) heads with standard Gaussian policy heads (GH) on single-domain models showed that FM heads performed at least as well as GH on unseen test tasks in three out of four domains (SinerGym, HumEnv, Meta-World), with substantial improvements in several cases. GH sometimes showed slightly higher performance on Industrial Benchmark and could fit training tasks more tightly, but FM generally offered better or more robust generalization to held-out test tasks.
- **Multi-Domain vs. Single-Domain Training (Appendix J):**
 - Comparing models trained on a single domain versus the full 10-domain dataset revealed that the multi-domain model generally matched or outperformed single-domain models, particularly on Meta-World and Industrial Benchmark test sets. This suggests that the shared transformer backbone effectively leverages cross-domain data for generalization benefits.
- **Comparison with Vintix on all Domains (Appendix L):**
 - A full comparison across all 10 test domains, with Vintix's model size matched, showed that Vintix II substantially outperformed Vintix in both online and offline settings across most domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work demonstrates that combining the Decision Pre-Trained Transformer (DPT) with a flow-based generative policy head provides a robust framework for scaling In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) across a diverse range of domains. This advancement represents a step towards creating more capable generalist agents.

The key contributions and their potential impact include:

1. **Enhanced Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** The model exhibits improved performance on unseen tasks compared to prior Large Action Models like Vintix and REGENT. This enhanced generalization ability, especially in domains like Meta-World, Bi-DexHands, and ControlGym, suggests a more robust capacity to adapt to new environments and variations without direct training. This is significant for developing agents that can function in dynamic and novel real-world settings.
2. **Dual Online and Offline Adaptability:** The agent demonstrates substantial capabilities in both zero-shot online evaluation (adapting from an empty context) and few-shot offline evaluation (adapting with limited demonstrations). This dual capability positions it as a more versatile generalist agent, capable of quick adaptation during deployment and effective learning from provided examples.
3. **Validation of Flow-Based Policy Heads for Continuous Control:** The successful integration of a rectified-flow objective with DPT's architecture addresses the challenge of modeling complex, multi-modal continuous action distributions. This approach not only enables native sampling during online inference but also preserves DPT's interpretation as Bayesian posterior sampling, a theoretically desirable property. The ablation studies confirm the benefits of flow-matching heads over traditional Gaussian heads for generalization.
4. **Creation of an Expanded Cross-Domain Dataset:** The collection and open-sourcing of a large cross-domain dataset (over 700M transitions across 209 training and 46 test tasks) is a valuable contribution to the research community. This dataset supports large-scale cross-domain training and provides a benchmark compatible with major ICRL approaches, fostering further research in generalist agent development and cross-domain knowledge transfer.
5. **Reinforcing ICRL as a Viable Paradigm:** By demonstrating strong performance and generalization, the work reinforces ICRL as a compelling alternative to traditional expert distillation methods for training generalist agents. The fully parametric nature of the agent, without reliance on additional retrieval modules, simplifies the inference stage.
6. **Insights into In-Context Learning Mechanisms:** The analysis of progressive concentration of action beliefs provides empirical evidence consistent with DPT's theoretical interpretation as in-context posterior sampling, contributing to a deeper understanding of how these models adapt.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations and areas for future work:
- **Token-to-Parameter Ratio:** The current model's token-to-parameter ratio is less than one, significantly below optimal ratios observed in large foundation models (e.g., 20 tokens per parameter). This highlights the need for further scaling of training datasets to fully realize the potential of Large Action Models.
- **Limited Exploration Capabilities:** While strong in exploitation, the models' test-time exploration capabilities in demonstration-less evaluation still lag behind prompted runs. This suggests an area for improvement to enable more effective discovery in new environments.
- **Input-Output Dimension Agnosticism:** The model is not agnostic to input-output dimensions across tasks, which restricts its transferability to entirely unseen domains and limits its applicability in diverse practical scenarios. Addressing this remains an open challenge for generalist action models.

Overall, this research contributes significantly to the development of scalable, adaptive generalist agents in reinforcement learning, paving the way for more capable AI systems that can learn and operate effectively across a broad spectrum of tasks and environments.
