# Qwen-VLA: Unifying Vision-Language-Action Modeling across Tasks, Environments, and Robot Embodiments

> [!summary] Summary
> Qwen-VLA integrates vision-language-action modeling into a single framework applicable across diverse robotic tasks, environments, and robot embodiments. The model exhibits strong performance on manipulation benchmarks like RoboCasa-GR1 (56.7%) and Simpler-WidowX (73.7%), and navigation tasks on VLN-CE (R2R SR 57.5%, RxR SR 59.6%), while demonstrating robust real-world transferability and out-of-distribution generalization on the ALOHA platform.

## Problem

- Embodied AI suffered from fragmentation, with specialized models for specific tasks or robot platforms, limiting generalization.
- Integrating advancements in vision-language understanding and continuous action generation into a single, unified framework for heterogeneous embodied decision-making remained a challenge.
- Existing robotic systems lacked the transferability and scalability seen in general-purpose vision-language pretraining.

## Method

- Extended a Qwen3.5-4B vision-language model with a ==Diffusion== ==Transformer==-based flow-matching policy for fine-grained continuous action generation.
- Implemented embodiment-aware prompt conditioning, leveraging textual descriptions to specify robot platform and control semantics.
- Unified diverse control signals (manipulation, navigation, human egocentric motions) into a consistent, masked action-and-trajectory prediction space.
- Developed a four-stage training process (T2A, CPT, SFT, RL) leveraging a large, heterogeneous pretraining dataset spanning robot, human, and synthetic data.

## Results

- Achieved competitive or superior performance in simulation, including **97.9%** success on LIBERO and **56.7%** on RoboCasa-GR1, surpassing specialist models.
- Demonstrated robust real-world transferability on the ALOHA platform, achieving an **83.6%** average success rate on in-domain tasks and **76.9%** on OOD tasks.
- Attained leading results in vision-and-language navigation on VLN-CE benchmarks, with a **57.5%** success rate on R2R Val-Unseen and **59.6%** on RxR Val-Unseen.
- Exhibited strong zero-shot generalization on dynamic manipulation (DOMINO SR **26.6%**) and out-of-distribution static manipulation (SimplerEnv-OOD **32.0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A common computational structure underlies various embodied tasks, enabling their unification within a single vision-language-action model.
> - A staged training approach, starting with Text-to-Action DiT pretraining, effectively bridges the gap between a pretrained VLM and a newly initialized action decoder.
> - Large-scale, heterogeneous pretraining data, combined with embodiment-aware conditioning, is effective for developing generalist embodied agents capable of real-world transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied intelligence research frequently employs specialized models, each designed for a specific scenario or task such as manipulation or navigation. This specialization has historically resulted in fragmented capabilities, limiting the generalization of robotic agents across diverse tasks, environments, and robot embodiments. Recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs) have improved open-world visual understanding and language grounding, while diffusion- or flow-based policies have demonstrated potential for modeling continuous, high-dimensional robot actions. However, integrating these advances into a unified system that can handle heterogeneous embodied decision-making problems remains a significant challenge. Existing systems are often confined to narrow task families or specific robot morphologies, hindering transferability and scalability comparable to general-purpose vision-language pretraining. This work addresses this fragmentation by proposing a unified vision-language-action (VLA) model capable of addressing manipulation, navigation, and other trajectory-centric tasks within a single framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether various heterogeneous embodied decision-making problems can be unified within a single vision-language-action model. The authors identify a common computational structure underlying diverse embodied tasks: agents must condition on visual observations, language instructions, and embodiment-specific constraints to predict future actions or trajectories. This insight motivates the design of a joint pretraining framework that integrates manipulation, navigation, egocentric human demonstrations, and vision-language reasoning into a single model.

The key motivations are:
- **Overcoming Fragmentation:** To move beyond specialized models that lack generalization across different tasks, environments, and robot embodiments.
- **Unified Capabilities:** To develop a single model that can support embodied control across various task families and robot platforms, facilitating transferable visual grounding, spatial reasoning, and continuous action generation.
- **Scalability:** To enable embodied learning to scale similarly to general-purpose vision-language pretraining by leveraging diverse data sources and a unified modeling approach.
- **Leveraging Foundational Models:** To extend existing vision-language modeling capabilities (perception, understanding, reasoning) to continuous action and trajectory generation for physical interaction.
- **Addressing Heterogeneity:** To manage the varied observation formats, control frequencies, prediction horizons, and action dimensionalities inherent in different embodied tasks through a unified representation and conditioning mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

Qwen-VLA is built upon the Qwen3.5-4B vision-language model, which serves as the multimodal backbone. Qwen3.5 is characterized by early vision-language fusion, interleaving visual tokens from a Vision Transformer (ViT) with spatial merging into the text token stream. This allows unified processing of images, videos, and language. A hybrid attention design within the VLM backbone combines gated linear attention with grouped-query softmax attention for efficient processing of long multimodal sequences.

On top of this backbone, a single-stream Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based flow-matching policy is attached as an action expert. This action expert is designed for fine-grained continuous action generation. It processes VLM hidden states concatenated with a noisy action chunk through joint self-attention, incorporating AdaLN timestep conditioning and multi-section RoPE. This decoupled design allows the action expert to specialize in high-frequency dynamics while preserving the backbone's pretrained capabilities. The action expert comprises approximately 1.15 billion parameters, primarily from 16 DiT blocks.

#### 3.2. Embodiment-aware Prompt Conditioning

To support multiple robot platforms within a shared model, the system employs embodiment-aware prompt conditioning. Each training example is prepended with a textual description specifying the robot platform, arm configuration, control convention, control frequency, and prediction horizon (e.g., "The robot is {robot_tag} with {single arm / dual arms}[, waist][, and mobile base]. The control frequency is {FPS} Hz. Please predict the next {chunk_size} control actions to execute the following task: {ori_instruction}"). This textual prompt informs the model of embodiment-specific control semantics without requiring architectural changes.

#### 3.3. Unified Action and Trajectory Representation

The model represents manipulation actions, navigation waypoints, and human egocentric motions within a shared action-and-trajectory prediction space. All tasks are formulated as a conditional prediction task `pθ(yt:t+H−1 | ot, x, e, z)`, where `o_t` is visual context, `x` is language instruction, `e` is embodiment description, and `z` is an optional task identifier. The target sequence `Y ∈ R^(H×K)` (where `H` is prediction horizon and `K` is channel dimension) unifies control signals from different modalities (e.g., delta end-effector positions, joint positions, gripper states, dexterous-hand configurations for manipulation; `(∆x, ∆y, ∆θ)` for navigation waypoints; structured pose spaces like MANO for human data). A per-channel binary mask `M ∈ {0, 1} ^(H×K)` is used to account for varying action dimensionalities by zero-padding inactive channels, ensuring a consistent tensor interface. Action dimensions are normalized per dataset using quantile statistics.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

The model is trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of two objectives:
- **Flow-matching action loss (`L_act`):** Applied to samples with continuous control targets (manipulation, VLN trajectories, human egocentric data). This objective trains the action expert to predict a conditional velocity field via a flow-matching objective, using a two-level averaging scheme to prevent padding from dominating gradients.
- **Vision-language loss (`L_vl`):** A standard next-token prediction loss applied to auxiliary vision-language data, fine-grained embodied action captions, autonomous driving VQA, and general VLM pretraining corpora. This objective preserves and strengthens the multimodal capabilities of the backbone.
The total loss is `L = λ_act L_act + λ_vl L_vl`, with weights `λ_act` and `λ_vl` tuned to balance gradient magnitudes.

#### 3.5. Staged Training Recipe

The training progresses through four stages to bridge the asymmetry between the pretrained VLM backbone and the randomly initialized DiT action decoder:
1. **Text-to-Action (T2A) DiT Pretraining:** The VLM is frozen, and only the DiT is trained. It conditions on text and embodiment prompts, explicitly withholding images. This stage forces the decoder to learn a structured action prior from language alone.
2. **Continued Pretraining (CPT):** Both the VLM and DiT are unfrozen and trained on a heterogeneous data mixture. This stage grounds the action prior in visual observations and adapts the backbone to embodied perception, producing **Qwen-VLA-Base**.
3. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** From the CPT checkpoint, training branches into two parallel tracks: multi-task SFT (VQA, spatial grounding, manipulation, navigation) and fine-tuning on in-house teleoperation data for real-world robot deployment. This stage aligns the model with curated, high-quality demonstrations.
4. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Starting from the multi-task SFT checkpoint, RL fine-tunes the policy using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). Sparse binary success rewards are collected exclusively in a single simulation environment (SimplerEnv). This stage optimizes closed-loop task success, producing the final model, **Qwen-VLA-Instruct**. Log-probability estimation for flow-matching models is addressed by converting the deterministic probability-flow ODE into a corresponding SDE.

#### 3.6. Pretraining Data

A large-scale, heterogeneous pretraining mixture is employed, covering five main data families:
- **Robot Manipulation Trajectories (74.2%):** Includes publicly available real-robot datasets (e.g., RobotSet, Galaxea, DROID, BridgeData V2, RT-1) and simulation-based manipulation trajectories (e.g., InternData-A1, GR00T-X-Embodiment-Sim), augmented with proprietary datasets. Covers tabletop, mobile, bimanual, and dexterous control across diverse robot embodiments.
- **Human Egocentric Demonstrations (6.0%):** Draws from Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS (processed by VITRA), EgoDex, EgoVerse, and Xperience. Provides broad manipulation priors and fine-grained 3D hand/camera motion trajectories.
- **Synthetic Simulation Data (3.7%):** Generated via an internal ROBOINF pipeline (vision-language-action data) for manipulation scenes and a text-only action dataset for language-action patterns. Used to improve coverage, controllability, and robustness.
- **Navigation and Trajectory-Centric Data (7.5%):** Includes instruction-following navigation, object searching, and target tracking data to equip the model with mobile capabilities.
- **Auxiliary Vision-Language Data (8.5%):** Strengthens semantic grounding and visual reasoning. Includes fine-grained embodied action captions (48,000 video-caption pairs), autonomous driving VQA (temporal scene understanding, surround-view spatial reasoning, language-grounded localization, planning-aware reasoning), spatial grounding (2D bounding box), and general vision-language data (captioning, knowledge-oriented VQA, OCR, interleaved instruction-following).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Manipulation Results in Simulation

Qwen-VLA-Instruct demonstrated strong performance across four simulation benchmarks: LIBERO, Simpler-WidowX, RoboCasa-GR1, and RoboTwin 2.0.
- **LIBERO:** Achieved 97.9% success, comparable to leading specialist models.
- **RoboCasa-GR1:** Scored 56.7%, outperforming specialist baselines like π0.5 (37.0%), GR00T N1.6 (49.9%), and Being-H0.5 (53.3%).
- **Simpler-WidowX:** Attained 73.7%, surpassing specialist baselines including StarVLA-OFT (64.6%).
- **RoboTwin-Easy/Hard:** Achieved 86.1%/87.2%, exceeding the previous best specialist ABot-M0 (86.0%/85.0%).
The results indicate that Qwen-VLA-Instruct, as a single generalist model, consistently outperformed or matched most specialist policies, showing that joint multi-embodiment training can yield strong task-specific performance. Pretraining (Qwen-VLA-Base) established a foundation (e.g., 90.8% on LIBERO), with instruction tuning yielding substantial gains across all benchmarks (e.g., +7.1% on LIBERO).

#### 4.2. Manipulation Results in the Real World (ALOHA)

Evaluations on the ALOHA bimanual robot platform, with a variant fine-tuned from Qwen-VLA-Base (Qwen-VLA-aloha w/ pretrain), demonstrated the transferability of pretraining to real-world tasks.
- **In-domain tasks:** Qwen-VLA-aloha w/ pretrain achieved an average success rate of 83.6% across six categories (e.g., Pick and Place, Table Cleaning, Towel Folding), significantly outperforming the model trained from scratch (Qwen-VLA-aloha w/o pretrain, 48.5%) and other baselines like GR00T N1.6 (28.6%) and π0.5 (71.6%).
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) tasks:** The model achieved an average OOD success rate of 76.9% across five generalization settings (color, instance, position, background, instruction), substantially surpassing π0.5 (41.5%) and Qwen-VLA-aloha w/o pretrain (36.2%). This indicates robust generalization to unseen visual conditions and instruction variations.
Qualitative OOD generalization on ALOHA also showed capabilities such as color-conditioned grasping, grasping novel objects, sequential compositional tasks ("clean up the table"), interaction with entirely unseen object categories ("approach"), and robustness to novel backgrounds.

#### 4.3. Navigation Results (VLN-CE)

Qwen-VLA-Instruct achieved competitive performance on the Val-Unseen splits of the R2R and RxR benchmarks for vision-and-language navigation in continuous environments (VLN-CE).
- **R2R Val-Unseen:** Attained the highest Oracle Success rate (69.0%) and Success Rate (57.5%), surpassing StreamVLN by 4.8 and 0.6 percentage points respectively, while maintaining competitive SPL.
- **RxR Val-Unseen:** Led in both SR (59.6%) and SPL (47.8%), outperforming all baselines.

#### 4.4. Out-of-Distribution Evaluation on Static Manipulation (SimplerEnv-OOD)

On SimplerEnv-OOD, a benchmark designed to test generalization to unseen task types from simple pick-and-place training, Qwen-VLA-Instruct achieved an average success rate of 32.0%, outperforming π0.5 (12.6%). This included stronger performance on positional generalization tasks (e.g., MoveRight, PlaceNear, PlaceRight) and color-based reasoning (StackYellow).

#### 4.5. Out-of-Distribution Evaluation on Dynamic Manipulation (DOMINO)

On the DOMINO benchmark for dynamic manipulation, Qwen-VLA-Instruct demonstrated the best overall performance in a zero-shot setting, achieving the highest Success Rate (SR) of 26.6% and Manipulation Score (MS) of 39.5. This surpassed both other zero-shot VLA models and models explicitly fine-tuned for dynamic manipulation, highlighting the efficacy of the unified action-and-trajectory pretraining strategy for transferable spatial-to-kinematic priors.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Text-to-Action (T2A) Pretraining:** Found that mixing approximately 20% synthetic with 80% real data for T2A yielded the best results (+10.2 pp over no T2A). Full-sequence prediction consistently outperformed chunk prediction (+4.9 pp). Withholding images during T2A was beneficial (−2.9 pp penalty for including vision). A Sigmoid-Normal timestep distribution for T2A combined with a Beta distribution for SFT achieved the highest success rate. 2,000 T2A steps were found to be optimal for efficiency and performance.
- **Multi-Embodiment Co-training:** Including vision-language (VL) data during action learning improved performance on complex benchmarks like RoboCasa-GR1 (+4.9%) and RoboTwin-2.0 (+4.6%). A pretrained DiT decoder converged faster and reached higher peak success. Zero-Padding was identified as an efficient projection design for heterogeneous action spaces, performing comparably to other methods with fewer parameters.
- **Effect of RL Post-Training:** Each stage (CPT, SFT, RL) provided complementary gains. RL provided a further uplift on SimplerEnv (+2.9 pp) and preserved or mildly improved performance on other OOD benchmarks without catastrophic forgetting, including DOMINO dynamic manipulation (SR increased from 25.7% to 26.6%, MS from 39.1 to 39.5).
- **State Conditioning:** Incorporating proprioceptive state (joint angles) yielded only marginal gains (+0.7 to +1.3 pp) regardless of injection strategy (VLM prompt or DiT). The model's reliance on multi-view visual observations and relative action prediction minimized the need for explicit state.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Qwen-VLA demonstrates that unifying vision-language-action modeling across diverse tasks, environments, and robot embodiments is a viable and effective approach to embodied intelligence. By extending a robust VLM backbone with a specialized action decoder and leveraging large-scale, heterogeneous pretraining data with embodiment-aware conditioning, the model achieves strong generalist performance. This work addresses the fragmentation in embodied AI research by providing a single model that can generalize across different robot platforms and task families, reducing the need for specialized models for each scenario.

The potential impacts include:
- **Accelerated Robotic Development:** Providing a foundation model that can be adapted to a wide range of robotic tasks and hardware, potentially lowering the barrier to entry for developing new robotic applications.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** Improving the robustness of robotic systems to variations in scene layout, object configuration, lighting, and robot morphology, which is critical for deployment in real-world, unstructured environments.
- **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control:** Establishing a practical interface between multimodal foundation models and embodied agents, allowing high-level language instructions to be translated into precise physical actions.
- **New Research Avenues:** The identified limitations (e.g., data scale, optimization trade-offs, short-horizon evaluations) highlight future research directions, including autonomous data collection, incorporation of human video data, long-horizon planning, world modeling, and integration of richer physical feedback for more capable embodied agents.

The findings suggest that VLA models can serve as a foundational component for building more intelligent and versatile embodied agents capable of understanding and interacting with the physical world through natural language instructions.
