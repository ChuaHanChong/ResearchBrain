# Reward-Aware Proto-Representations in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research formalizes the Default Representation (DR) as a reward-aware proto-representation in reinforcement learning, developing efficient learning algorithms and theoretically characterizing its relationship to the Successor Representation. The work demonstrates that DR can lead to superior performance in reward shaping, option discovery, and transfer learning, particularly in environments requiring navigation of complex reward landscapes.

## Problem

- Existing proto-representations, such as the Successor Representation (SR), are reward-agnostic, primarily encoding environmental transition dynamics without considering the desirability of future states.
- Reinforcement learning agents require representations that can differentiate pathways based on associated rewards to effectively navigate complex reward landscapes and maximize cumulative returns.
- The Default Representation (DR), despite its initial proposal in neuroscience, lacked comprehensive theoretical development and practical algorithms for its application within computational reinforcement learning.

## Method

- The authors developed and proved convergence for Dynamic Programming (**DP**) ==and Temporal-Difference== (TD) learning algorithms for the tabular Default Representation (DR), making it practical for computational RL.
- They extended the DR formulation to handle state-action dependent rewards and introduced 'Default Features' (DFs) to enable function approximation for use in larger state spaces.
- Theoretical analysis characterized the DR's eigenspectrum and established a formal relationship with the SR's eigenvectors under specific reward conditions, clarifying when reward-awareness differentiates the representations.

## Results

- DR-based reward shaping significantly outperformed SR-based methods and no shaping in grid-world environments with low-reward regions, enabling agents to learn optimal, reward-aware paths.
- Reward-Aware Covering Eigenoptions (RACE) demonstrated a risk-averse exploration strategy, avoiding low-reward regions, which led to faster learning and superior final returns when combined with Q-learning compared to SR-based methods.
- Default Features (DFs) proved highly effective for transfer learning, directly computing optimal policies for new reward configurations when only terminal state rewards changed, outperforming Successor Features (SFs) in this specific scenario.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The DR uniquely encodes the expected exponentiated sum of rewards along all trajectories between states, directly integrating reward information into its representation, unlike SR's focus on expected discounted visits.
> - DR and SR share the same eigenvectors when non-terminal rewards are constant and negative, indicating a fundamental link but also precisely when DR's reward-awareness starts to provide distinct features.
> - Reward-aware proto-representations can intrinsically guide agents towards optimal, risk-averse behavior by encoding information about undesirable regions, leading to more effective exploration and policy learning.
