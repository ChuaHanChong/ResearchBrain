# Accelerating Model-Based Reinforcement Learning with State-Space World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Robotics and Perception Group at the University of Zurich developed S5WM, a method that integrates State-Space Models into model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) world models. This approach reduces world model training time by up to 10 times and overall MBRL training by up to 4 times, delivering comparable performance and sample efficiency on complex agile quadrotor flight tasks.

## Problem

- Model-free reinforcement learning methods require extensive real-world interaction data, which is often impractical and time-consuming for complex robotic systems.
- Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) introduces a computational bottleneck due to the slow, sequential training of recurrent neural network-based world models.
- Extracting informative latent representations from high-dimensional observations for control in partially observable, vision-based robotic tasks presents a challenge.

## Method

- Integrates the parallelizable S5 State-Space Model architecture as the dynamics backbone within a Dreamer-style ==world model== (S5WM) to accelerate sequence processing.
- Implements a 'privileged world model' that reconstructs low-dimensional ground-truth states during training for vision-based, partially observable tasks, rather than high-dimensional observations.
- Employs a curriculum-based reward smoothing strategy, decoupling nominal task rewards from a smoothing reward component to encourage stable and smooth control actions.

## Results

- Achieved up to a 10-fold speedup in world model training and up to a 4-fold overall MBRL training speedup compared to DreamerV3, with comparable task rewards and sample efficiency.
- Outperformed the model-free PPO baseline in terms of sample efficiency and final task reward on both state-based and vision-based agile quadrotor tasks.
- Successfully deployed policies on a real-world racing drone, demonstrating robust, high-speed, and smooth autonomous flight for complex maneuvers like the Split-S and Figure-8.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-Space Models (SSMs) can effectively replace recurrent neural networks in world models, providing substantial computational speedups for model-based reinforcement learning.
> - Incorporating privileged information during world model training in partially observable environments enhances the quality of learned latent representations, improving sample efficiency and policy performance.
> - A curriculum-based reward smoothing strategy facilitates the learning of robust and smooth control policies, which is critical for successful real-world robotic deployment.
