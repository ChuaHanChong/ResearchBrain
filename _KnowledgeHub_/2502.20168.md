# Accelerating Model-Based Reinforcement Learning with State-Space World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Robotics and Perception Group at the University of Zurich developed S5WM, a method that integrates State-Space Models into model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) world models. This approach reduces world model training time by up to 10 times and overall MBRL training by up to 4 times, delivering comparable performance and sample efficiency on complex agile quadrotor flight tasks.

## Problem

- Model-free reinforcement learning methods require extensive real-world interaction data, which is often impractical and time-consuming for complex robotic systems.
- Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) introduces a computational bottleneck due to the slow, sequential training of recurrent neural network-based world models.
- Extracting informative latent representations from high-dimensional observations for control in partially observable, vision-based robotic tasks presents a challenge.

## Method

- Integrates the parallelizable S5 State-Space Model architecture as the dynamics backbone within a Dreamer-style ==world model== (S5WM) to accelerate sequence processing.
- Implements a 'privileged world model' that reconstructs low-dimensional ground-truth states during training for vision-based, partially observable tasks, rather than high-dimensional observations.
- Employs a curriculum-based reward smoothing strategy, decoupling nominal task rewards from a smoothing reward component to encourage stable and smooth control actions.

## Results

- Achieved up to a 10-fold speedup in world model training and up to a 4-fold overall MBRL training speedup compared to DreamerV3, with comparable task rewards and sample efficiency.
- Outperformed the model-free PPO baseline in terms of sample efficiency and final task reward on both state-based and vision-based agile quadrotor tasks.
- Successfully deployed policies on a real-world racing drone, demonstrating robust, high-speed, and smooth autonomous flight for complex maneuvers like the Split-S and Figure-8.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-Space Models (SSMs) can effectively replace recurrent neural networks in world models, providing substantial computational speedups for model-based reinforcement learning.
> - Incorporating privileged information during world model training in partially observable environments enhances the quality of learned latent representations, improving sample efficiency and policy performance.
> - A curriculum-based reward smoothing strategy facilitates the learning of robust and smooth control policies, which is critical for successful real-world robotic deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Reinforcement Learning (RL), Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL), and sequential modeling architectures.

**Reinforcement Learning in Robotics:** RL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for teaching robots complex skills, ranging from manipulation and locomotion to agile aerial robotics. It offers significant advantages over classical control by requiring less human intervention in task encoding and allowing for greater flexibility in system design, often leading to performance that surpasses traditional methods.

**Model-Free vs. Model-Based RL:**
- **Model-Free RL (MFRL):** While successful, MFRL methods (like PPO, a baseline in this paper) typically require vast amounts of interaction data, which is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to obtain in real-world robotic systems. This data inefficiency is exacerbated by the noisy nature of robotic systems, non-linear dynamics, and high-dimensional observations (e.g., images).
- **Model-Based RL (MBRL):** To address MFRL's sample inefficiency, MBRL learns a "world model" of the environment's dynamics and rewards in addition to the control policy. This world model allows the agent to "imagine" future trajectories and optimize its policy in a simulated environment, significantly reducing the need for real-world interactions. Popular MBRL frameworks, such as Dreamer (DreamerV3 being a direct predecessor and baseline for this work), leverage recurrent neural networks (RNNs), specifically Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSMs), to model these dynamics.

**The Challenge of MBRL and the Role of this Paper:** Despite its sample efficiency, MBRL introduces a new computational bottleneck: training the world model itself. Traditional RNN-based world models (like RSSMs) process sequences sequentially, making their training computationally intensive and slow, especially for long trajectories or complex dynamics. This limitation often makes MBRL intractable for rapid iteration and deployment in real-world scenarios where fast training is desirable.

This paper directly addresses this critical bottleneck. It proposes leveraging **State-Space Models (SSMs)**, a class of models that have recently shown great success in capturing long-term dependencies in sequences while offering superior computational efficiency through parallelization. While some prior work has explored Transformers or other SSMs for world modeling (e.g.,), this work specifically integrates an SSM (S5) into the Dreamer-style MBRL framework and demonstrates its practical benefits in terms of training speed for complex robotic tasks, particularly agile drone flight. It also introduces a novel aspect of using "privileged information" for world model training in partially observable vision-based tasks, a technique often used in actor-critic setups but less explored in world models.

In essence, this research pushes MBRL closer to practical viability by significantly reducing its computational cost without sacrificing its key advantage of sample efficiency, thus bridging the gap between theoretical advances in RL and the demands of real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are:

1. **Accelerate Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) Training:** The core objective is to significantly reduce the computational time required to train MBRL agents. While MBRL offers superior sample efficiency over model-free methods, its training process is often slower due to the additional task of learning a world model, which captures environment dynamics. This slowness limits its applicability in time-sensitive or computationally constrained real-world robotic scenarios.
2. **Overcome the Computational Bottleneck of Recurrent World Models:** Existing state-of-the-art MBRL methods, such as DreamerV3, typically employ Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSMs) as their world model backbone. RSSMs rely on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) that process sequences step-by-step, making training computationally expensive and difficult to parallelize, especially for long sequences. The authors aim to replace this sequential dependency with a more efficient, parallelizable alternative.
3. **Leverage State-Space Models (SSMs) for World Modeling:** The paper proposes using modern State-Space Models (SSMs), specifically the S5 architecture, as the backbone for the dynamics model within the world model. The motivation here is to harness SSMs' inherent ability to parallelize sequence processing, thereby directly attacking the computational bottleneck identified in objective 2.
4. **Maintain or Improve Performance and Sample Efficiency:** Crucially, the acceleration achieved must not come at the cost of diminished performance or increased sample inefficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art MBRL methods. The goal is a "speedup without compromising performance."
5. **Enhance Learning in Partially Observable Environments with Privileged Information:** For vision-based and partially observable tasks, learning informative latent representations from high-dimensional observations is challenging. The authors motivate the use of "privileged information" (e.g., low-dimensional ground truth state) during world model training to provide stronger supervisory signals. This aims to improve the quality of learned latent representations and ultimately the policy performance, especially in simulation-to-real transfer scenarios.
6. **Demonstrate Real-World Applicability:** The ultimate motivation is to validate the proposed method on challenging, real-world robotic tasks. Agile quadrotor flight, known for its complex and highly non-linear dynamics, serves as a demanding testbed for both state-based and vision-based control, demonstrating the practical utility and robustness of the approach for real-world deployment.

In summary, the authors are driven by the need to make powerful MBRL techniques more practical and deployable for real-world robotics by tackling their major computational limitations, while also enhancing their ability to handle complex sensory inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **S5WM (State-Space World Model)**, builds upon the successful DreamerV3 framework but introduces significant modifications to accelerate world model training and enhance learning in partially observable environments.

#### 3.1. State-Space World Model (S5WM) Architecture

The core innovation is replacing the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) backbone of DreamerV3 with a modern, parallelizable State-Space Model (SSM), specifically the **S5 architecture**.

- **Encoder:** Maps high-dimensional observations ($o_t$) to a posterior stochastic latent representation ($z_t \sim q_{\phi}(\cdot | o_t)$). Unlike RSSMs where the posterior also incorporates the deterministic state, S5WM infers $z_t$ solely from $o_t$. To compensate for the lack of historical context from the deterministic state, past actions are appended to $o_t$ in vision-based tasks.
- **Sequence Model (SSM):** This is the core dynamics model. It takes the previous stochastic latent ($z_{t-1}$), action ($a_{t-1}$), and continuity ($c_{t-1}$) to predict the next deterministic representation ($y_t$) and hidden state ($x_t$).
 - **Parallelization:** The key advantage of S5 is its ability to process sequences in parallel using the **parallel scan operation**. This contrasts with RNNs which process sequences step-by-step, making them slow. The S5 architecture leverages properties of linear state-space systems and diagonalization to achieve $O(PL)$ complexity (where P is matrix size, L is sequence length), and further optimize parallel scan computation to $O(\log(PL))$.
 - **Resetability:** A crucial feature for RL tasks, S5 allows resetting the hidden state, which is vital for handling episode boundaries and preventing the model from carrying over irrelevant information from previous episodes. The authors adapt the associative binary operator in S5 to incorporate continuity predictions, enabling efficient hidden state resets.
- **Dynamics Model:** Infers the prior stochastic representation ($\hat{z}_t \sim p_{\phi}(\cdot | y_t)$) from the deterministic state.
- **Decoder:** Reconstructs observations ($\hat{o}_t \sim p_{\phi}(\cdot | y_t, z_t)$), or in the case of privileged world models, reconstructs the privileged state ($\hat{s}_t$).
- **Reward and Continue Predictors:** Predict the reward ($\hat{r}_t \sim p_{\phi}(\cdot | y_t, z_t)$) and episode continuation ($\hat{c}_t \sim p_{\phi}(\cdot | y_t, z_t)$).
- **Input Fusion:** The stochastic latent $z_t$ and action $a_t$ are fused using an MLP to form a single vector $u_t$, which is then fed into the SSM block.

#### 3.2. World Model Training

The world model is optimized using a loss function derived from the evidence lower bound, similar to DreamerV3. This loss comprises three main components:
- $L_{pred}$: A prediction loss ensuring accurate reconstruction of observations/privileged states, rewards, and continuity.
- $L_{dyn}$: A dynamics loss that encourages the prior stochastic latent to align with the posterior, helping the world model learn transition dynamics.
- $L_{rep}$: A representation loss that ensures the encoder learns an informative posterior stochastic latent from observations.

#### 3.3. Actor-Critic Training

The actor-critic network is trained using imagined trajectories generated by the learned S5WM, which acts as a differentiable simulator.
- **Imagination Process:** Starting from an encoded initial state, the world model rolls out trajectories over a prediction horizon H. Actions are sampled from the current policy, and the world model predicts subsequent states, rewards, and continuations. This process is sequential due to the policy's dependence on the imagined state for action generation, thus it cannot be parallelized like the world model's dynamics computation.
- **Actor Policy:** Conditioned on the deterministic representations ($y_t$) and prior stochastic latents ($\hat{z}_t$), the actor ($\pi_{\theta}$) outputs actions, typically following a Gaussian distribution.
- **Critic Value Function:** Also conditioned on $y_t$ and $\hat{z}_t$, the critic ($v_{\psi}$) is trained to predict bootstrapped $\lambda$-returns, estimating long-term value.
- **Loss Functions:** The critic is trained to minimize the difference between its predictions and the computed $\lambda$-returns. The actor is trained to maximize expected returns from imagined trajectories, incorporating an entropy regularization term to encourage exploration.

#### 3.4. Privileged World Models

To address challenges in vision-based and partially observable environments, the method introduces a "privileged world model."
- **Concept:** During training in simulation, the world model is given access to low-dimensional ground-truth state information ($s_t$), which is not available during real-world deployment.
- **Implementation:** Instead of decoding high-dimensional image observations ($o_t$), the privileged decoder reconstructs the privileged state ($s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$). This provides a stronger and more direct signal for the encoder to learn representations relevant for control, as reconstructing a low-dimensional state is computationally easier and more informative for policy optimization than reconstructing a complex image. This allows the encoder to capture information that is more useful for downstream tasks, rather than just pixel accuracy.

#### 3.5. Reward Smoothing Strategy

The authors introduce a novel reward smoothing strategy to improve training stability and promote smoother actions, which is critical for real-world deployment.
- **Decoupled Rewards:** The total reward is split into a "nominal reward" ($r_{nom}$), active from the start of training, and a "smoothing reward" ($r_{aug}$), introduced later.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Initially, the agent learns the task with $r_{nom}$. Once proficient, $r_{aug}$ (penalizing abrupt action changes) is added. This curriculum prevents the world model from unlearning and relearning reward signals, fostering exploration first and then refinement for smoother control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings and results demonstrating the effectiveness and advantages of S5WM:

#### 4.1. Significant Training Speedup

- **World Model Training:** S5WM achieved a **speedup of up to 10 times** in world model training time compared to DreamerV3 in the state-based Split-S task.
- **Overall MBRL Training:** This translates to an **overall MBRL training speedup of up to 4 times** for the state-based Split-S task and up to 2 times for the vision-based Figure-8 task.
- **Bottleneck Dependence:** The speedup is most pronounced when the world model's dynamics modeling is the primary computational bottleneck (e.g., in state-based tasks). In vision-based tasks, where learning latent representations from high-dimensional images is also a significant cost, the relative speedup in total training time is still substantial but less dramatic.

#### 4.2. Comparable Performance and Sample Efficiency

- **Task Reward and Sample Efficiency:** Despite the significant speedup, S5WM achieved **similar sample efficiency and task rewards** to the state-of-the-art DreamerV3 across both state-based Split-S and vision-based Figure-8 tasks. This indicates that the parallelizable SSM backbone does not compromise the quality of the learned world model or policy.
- **Outperforming Model-Free RL:** Both S5WM and DreamerV3 significantly **outperformed the model-free PPO baseline** in terms of sample efficiency and final task reward, especially in the state-based Split-S task where PPO struggled to yield competitive policies within the limited data budget. For the vision-based Figure-8 task, PPO exhibited much lower success rates and did not learn to consistently fly the track.

#### 4.3. Benefits of Privileged World Models in Partially Observable Environments

- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** Leveraging privileged information (reconstructing low-dimensional state instead of high-dimensional images) resulted in **higher sample efficiency** for both S5WM and DreamerV3 variants in the vision-based Figure-8 task.
- **Stronger Signal for Encoder:** This approach provides a stronger signal for training the encoder, guiding the latent representation to capture information more relevant for policy optimization, rather than merely accurate image reconstruction. The benefits were particularly pronounced for S5WM due to its posterior stochastic latent being inferred directly from the observation.

#### 4.4. Robust Real-World Deployment

- **Agile Quadrotor Flight:** S5WM policies were successfully deployed on a real-world high-performance racing drone for both the state-based Split-S and vision-based Figure-8 tasks.
- **High-Speed, Smooth Trajectories:** The trained policies enabled **high-speed flight on very smooth trajectories**, successfully navigating complex maneuvers like the Split-S. This demonstrates the practical robustness and effectiveness of the learned policies in challenging real-world scenarios. Lap times and success rates in real-world experiments were comparable to simulation results for both S5WM and DreamerV3.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study Insights

- **Horizon Length (H):** An imagination horizon of H=10 was found to offer a good balance between sample efficiency and computational complexity. Longer horizons increase computational cost without proportional gains due to compounding prediction errors.
- **Reward Smoothing:** The proposed curriculum-based reward smoothing strategy (introducing an augmented reward component halfway through training) significantly **improved the smoothness of control commands** without hindering initial task learning. This is crucial for real-world deployment, preventing motor damage and reducing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Recurrent vs. Parallel S5WM:** A recurrent variant of S5WM (where the posterior depends on both observation and deterministic state, similar to RSSM) showed slightly better performance than the parallel S5WM in the vision-based Figure-8 task, but at the cost of reintroducing sequential dependencies and losing the training speedup. This highlights the importance of historical context in partially observable environments and the trade-offs between speed and information flow in world model design.

#### 4.6. Prediction Accuracy

- The world model demonstrated good prediction accuracy for imagined trajectories over a horizon of H=50, initialized with a context length of C=16, aligning well with ground truth observations for key state variables (position, velocity, orientation).

Overall, the findings strongly support the paper's central claims: S5WM successfully accelerates MBRL training without compromising performance, makes effective use of privileged information, and generalizes well to demanding real-world robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Accelerating Model-Based Reinforcement Learning with State-Space World Models" holds significant implications and potential impact across several domains:

1. **Increased Practicality of Model-Based RL for Robotics:** The most immediate and profound impact is making MBRL a more practical and feasible option for real-world robotic applications. By tackling the critical computational bottleneck of slow world model training, S5WM addresses a major hurdle preventing MBRL from wider adoption. The demonstrated speedup (up to 4x overall training, 10x for world model training) makes MBRL training times comparable to or even better than some tuned model-free methods while retaining its superior sample efficiency. This facilitates faster iteration cycles, reduced development time, and more rapid deployment of learned policies on physical robots.

2. **Enabling Complex Real-World Robotics Tasks:** The successful application of S5WM to agile quadrotor flight, a domain characterized by highly complex, non-linear dynamics and real-time constraints, showcases its robustness and capability. This opens doors for applying MBRL to other challenging robotic tasks in manipulation, locomotion, and autonomous navigation that demand both precision and agility, where rapid learning and robust models are paramount.

3. **Advancement in World Model Architectures:** The successful integration of State-Space Models (SSMs) like S5 into the Dreamer-based MBRL framework represents a significant architectural advancement. It demonstrates that newer, more efficient sequence modeling paradigms can effectively replace traditional RNNs in complex latent dynamics models, while preserving or improving their predictive power. This could inspire further research into other parallelizable sequence models for RL.

4. **Improved Learning in Partially Observable and Vision-Based Settings:** The concept of the "privileged world model," where low-dimensional ground truth states are reconstructed instead of high-dimensional images during training, offers a potent method for improving vision-based robot learning. This provides a stronger, more task-relevant signal for the perception components of the world model, leading to better latent representations and enhanced policy performance, particularly for sim-to-real transfer. This technique could be widely adopted in other vision-based RL tasks.

5. **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap:** Techniques like the proposed reward smoothing curriculum, which encourages smoother actions in simulation, directly contribute to reducing the sim-to-real gap. By learning control policies that are more compatible with physical robot hardware constraints (e.g., avoiding jerky movements that cause wear or instability), the work makes learned policies more directly transferable and safer for real-world deployment.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also clearly lays out avenues for future research.
 - **Accelerating Actor-Critic Training:** While world model training is accelerated, the imagination step for actor-critic training remains sequential. Future work could explore ways to parallelize or speed up this component.
 - **Hyperparameter Robustness:** SSMs are noted to be more sensitive to hyperparameters than mature RSSMs. Enhancing the robustness of S5WM to hyperparameter choices would further improve its usability.
 - **Broader Applicability:** Applying and evaluating S5WM on other robotic domains like manipulation and locomotion would solidify its generalizability.

In conclusion, S5WM offers a promising framework that significantly enhances the feasibility and applicability of model-based reinforcement learning for real-world robotics. By combining high sample efficiency with substantially faster training times, it represents a crucial step towards robust, adaptable, and deployable intelligent robotic systems.
