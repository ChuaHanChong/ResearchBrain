# GraspQP: Differentiable Optimization of Force Closure for Diverse and Robust Dexterous Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspQP introduces a method for synthesizing large-scale, diverse, and physically feasible dexterous grasps by combining a rigorous differentiable force closure metric, formulated as an implicit Quadratic Program, with an enhanced MALA* optimization strategy. The framework synthesizes a comprehensive dataset covering 5,700 objects, five grippers, and three grasp taxonomies, showing improved diversity and stability compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing grasp synthesis methods are often computationally expensive, exhibit poor sample efficiency, or oversimplify crucial physical properties, leading to less realistic and diverse grasp datasets.
- Optimization-based grasp generation frequently suffers from mode collapse, trapping proposals in local minima and limiting the diversity of generated grasps to simpler configurations like power grasps.
- There is a persistent demand for extensive, high-quality, and varied grasp datasets, particularly for refined manipulation strategies (e.g., pinches, precision grasps), to fully exploit dexterous hand capabilities.

## Method

- A rigorous, differentiable ==force closure metric== is introduced, formulated as an implicit ==Quadratic Program (QP)== that accurately models contact and friction through its ==Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions==.
- An enhanced optimization method, **MALA\*** (Metropolis-Adjusted Langevin Algorithm-inspired), is developed with ==dynamic resetting== and ==adaptive temperature scaling== to address mode collapse and improve exploration of diverse grasp configurations.
- The **GraspQP** framework systematically generates a large-scale dataset by processing 5,700 objects across five different robotic grippers and three distinct grasp taxonomies (power, pinch, precision).

## Results

- GraspQP consistently outperformed baseline methods in grasp diversity and stability, achieving an overall Unique Grasp Rate (UGR) of **52%** and Entropy of **3.19** for power grasps (at 5N disturbance), compared to the next best at **45%** UGR and **~3.0** entropy.
- The MALA* optimizer alone improved UGR by **+5.0% to +7.0%**, Entropy by **+0.2 to +0.3**, and reduced average max penetration depth by **-0.3 to -0.4 mm**, confirming its effectiveness in enhancing diversity.
- The method successfully synthesized a large-scale dataset comprising over **1.6 million** grasps for **5,700** objects, five grippers, and three grasp taxonomies, demonstrating its capacity for broad and diverse grasp generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An implicitly defined Quadratic Program, differentiated via KKT conditions, provides a robust and physically accurate force closure metric for gradient-based grasp optimization that includes friction.
> - Mitigating mode collapse through distribution-aware dynamic resetting and adaptive temperature scaling in the MALA* optimizer is essential for discovering a wide range of unique and stable grasp configurations.
> - Maintaining hard constraints within the force closure QP formulation is critical for physical realism and performance, as simplified or unconstrained formulations lead to significant degradation in grasp quality and diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous grasping is a foundational capability in robotics, essential for enabling versatile interactions and manipulation in complex, unstructured environments. Advances in this field are often contingent on the availability of large-scale, high-quality datasets that facilitate the training of grasp prediction models, manipulation policies, and high-level task planning. However, generating such datasets presents challenges.

Existing methods for grasp synthesis typically fall into two categories: sampling-based algorithms and analytical techniques involving force closure evaluation. Sampling-based methods, while capable, are often computationally expensive, particularly for high-degree-of-freedom dexterous hands, and exhibit poor sample efficiency. Both sampling-based and simpler analytical approaches frequently converge to power grasps, which are relatively easy to achieve but limit the diversity of possible interactions, neglecting refined manipulation strategies such as pinches or precision grasps.

More recent gradient-based methods have sought to improve efficiency by optimizing differentiable approximations of grasp quality metrics. While these approaches offer computational advantages and faster convergence, they frequently oversimplify critical physical properties, potentially compromising grasp realism and diversity. For instance, some formulations omit frictional forces or rely on simplified contact assumptions, which are significant for robust grasping. Approaches utilizing bilevel optimization or sampling-based wrench space analysis also exist, but can involve constraints or simplifications to maintain differentiability or prevent issues like vanishing gradients, thereby potentially limiting physical accuracy.

Against this background, this work addresses the limitations of existing dataset generation methods by introducing a framework that aims to synthesize diverse, physically feasible, and large-scale dexterous grasps. It seeks to provide a more analytically rigorous and differentiable approach to force closure, combined with an optimization strategy designed to overcome issues of mode collapse and promote broader grasp diversity, thereby contributing to the development of more comprehensive and realistic grasp datasets for dexterous manipulation. The resulting dataset also extends the diversity of grippers by including the Psyonic Ability Hand, in addition to common research hands like the Shadow and Allegro hands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for synthesizing large-scale, diverse, and physically feasible grasps that extend beyond simple power grasps to encompass refined manipulations such as pinches and tri-finger precision grasps. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in the current state of dexterous grasping research and dataset generation:

1. **Demand for Diverse and High-Quality Grasp Data**: Fully exploiting the capabilities of multi-fingered dexterous hands necessitates access to extensive and varied grasp data. Such data is critical for training robust grasp prediction models from point clouds, developing effective manipulation policies, and supporting high-level task planning with a broader spectrum of action options. Existing datasets are often limited in their diversity of gripper morphologies, grasp types, and overall physical realism, hindering progress in these areas.

2. **Limitations of Existing Grasp Synthesis Approaches**:
 - **Sampling-based algorithms**: These methods are typically computationally intensive and inefficient, especially when requiring accurate physical modeling. They also tend to favor simpler, stronger power grasps, resulting in datasets with limited diversity.
 - **Simplified force closure analysis**: Gradient-based methods, while more efficient, often rely on approximations or relaxed metrics that oversimplify fundamental physical properties, such as neglecting frictional forces. This oversimplification can lead to less realistic grasp predictions and further restrict the diversity of generated grasp configurations.
 - **Mode collapse**: A common issue in optimization-based grasp generation is that grasp proposals can converge to similar configurations, becoming trapped in local minima and significantly reducing the diversity of the final dataset.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a new framework with the following specific objectives:

- **Develop a rigorous, differentiable energy formulation of force closure**: This aims to accurately model grasp stability, including the crucial role of frictional forces, within an implicit Quadratic Program (QP) formulation. Unlike prior approaches that use simplified or relaxed metrics, this formulation is designed to preserve the core physical properties of grasp stability while remaining fully differentiable, enabling robust gradient-based optimization for generating refined grasp types.
- **Introduce an adjusted optimization method (MALA\*)**: This method, inspired by the Metropolis-Adjusted Langevin Algorithm (MALA), is designed to enhance convergence and diversity. It dynamically rejects gradient steps that would lead to poor sample diversity, utilizing information about the global distribution of existing samples. This mechanism is intended to mitigate mode collapse and encourage exploration of a broader range of physically distinct and feasible grasps.
- **Generate a new, large-scale grasp dataset**: The ultimate goal is to validate the proposed approach by synthesizing and providing a comprehensive dataset. This dataset is intended to cover 5,700 objects from DexGraspNet, five different grippers (including dexterous hands like the Shadow, Allegro, and Psyonic Ability Hand, as well as Robotiq 2F140 and 3F), and three distinct grasp taxonomies (power, pinch, and precision grasps), substantially expanding the available data for dexterous manipulation research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named GraspQP, focuses on synthesizing diverse and physically feasible grasp configurations for dexterous hands given a 3D object model. It builds upon existing gradient-based grasp generation pipelines but introduces significant advancements in its force closure metric and optimization strategy.

**A. Grasp Representation and Energy Function:**
Each grasp is parameterized by its wrist pose ($\chi \in SE(3)$), joint positions ($q \in R^{n_q}$), and desired joint torques ($\tau_q \in R^{n_q}$). The grasp interacts with the object at a set of active contact points ($C'$) and associated object normals ($N'$). Friction is incorporated using a four-sided pyramid under-approximation of the friction cone.
The optimization process minimizes a composite energy function $E$, defined as:
$E = E_{FC} + w_{dis} E_{dis} + w_{reg} E_{reg}$
- $E_{FC}$: The novel force closure metric, central to this work.
- $E_{dis}$: Measures the distance of active contact points to the object surface, encouraging firm contact.
- $E_{reg}$: A regularization term comprising penalties for object penetration ($E_{pen}$), self-penetration of the hand, and exceeding joint limits ($E_{joints}$).

**B. Differentiable Force Closure Metric ($E_{FC}$):**
The paper identifies limitations in prior force closure metrics used in gradient-based methods, which often simplify physical properties (e.g., assuming frictionless contacts or equal force magnitudes) or face vanishing gradient issues. GraspQP introduces a more rigorous formulation based on Theorem 3.1 - (iii) regarding positive spanning sets, which characterizes force closure.
The proposed $E_{FC}$ is formulated as:
$E_{FC} = || \sum_{i \le |C'|} \hat{\gamma}_i w_i ||^2 \cdot e^{- \prod_i \sigma_i(W_{FC})} \quad \text{s.t. } \hat{\gamma}_i \ge 1$
- **Wrench Summation**: The term $|| \sum_{i \le |C'|} \hat{\gamma}_i w_i ||^2$ ensures that a convex combination of interaction wrenches $w_i$ (derived from contact forces and locations) can sum to zero, effectively counteracting any external wrench. The coefficients $\hat{\gamma}_i$ directly reflect the magnitude of force applied at each contact point.
- **Bounded Coefficients**: The constraint $\hat{\gamma}_i \ge 1$ (with an additional upper bound $u$) is introduced to prevent vanishing gradients and ensure that the required interaction forces are physically bounded within the robot's capabilities. This is a key distinction from formulations that allow $\hat{\gamma}_i$ to be arbitrarily small.
- **Full Rank Wrench Matrix**: The scaling factor $e^{- \prod_i \sigma_i(W_{FC})}$ incorporates the product of the singular values ($\sigma_i$) of the wrench matrix $W_{FC}$. Maximizing this product ensures that the wrench matrix has full rank, guaranteeing that the contact wrenches linearly span the entire 6D wrench space, a necessary condition for force closure as per Theorem 3.1.
- **Differentiable Quadratic Program (QP)**: The core optimization problem for finding optimal $\hat{\gamma}_i$ coefficients is formulated as a Quadratic Program (QP):
 $E_{FC} = \min_z \frac{1}{2} z^T H z + g^T z \quad \text{s.t. } Az \ge b$
 where $H = W_{FC}^T W_{FC}$, $g = 0$, $b = [1_{n_c}; u_{n_c}]$, $z = [\hat{\gamma}_1..., \hat{\gamma}_{N_c}]$, and $A = diag(1_{n_c \times n_c}, -1_{n_c \times n_c})$.
 The gradients of this implicitly defined optimization problem with respect to the input parameters (grasp pose) are computed by differentiating its Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions, following methods by Amos et al., thereby enabling end-to-end differentiability.

**C. Distribution-based MALA Optimizer (MALA\*):**
To address the common problem of mode collapse during optimization, where grasp proposals get stuck in local minima, the paper introduces MALA*, an enhanced optimization and sampling strategy inspired by the Metropolis-Adjusted Langevin Algorithm. MALA* operates on the full grasp distribution at each iteration, incorporating global knowledge to improve exploration and diversity:
- **Dynamic Resetting**: After a fixed number of steps ($n_{reset}$), grasps whose energies are significantly worse than the overall distribution (i.e., falling within the lowest quantile, determined by a fitted normal distribution $N_E(\mu, \sigma)$ and threshold $p_{th}$) are re-initialized. This prevents unproductive optimization on poor grasp configurations.
- **Adaptive Temperature Scaling**: The Metropolis-Hastings acceptance criterion, which determines whether a gradient step is accepted, is modified. Instead of a fixed temperature $T$, each grasp $i$ has an adaptive temperature $T_i = T \cdot (1 + \Phi_E(E_i))$. Grasps with higher energy (performing worse) receive a higher temperature, making them more likely to accept gradient steps that might temporarily increase energy, thereby enabling them to escape local minima and explore different regions of the energy landscape.

**D. Implementation Details and Dataset Generation:**
- **Simulation Environment**: Isaac Lab (with Isaac Sim) is used for high-fidelity physics simulations. Gravity is disabled, and actuator commands are applied at 200 Hz with PD control mimicking position control.
- **Contact Point Sampling**: Handcrafted contact meshes define potential contact regions. Farthest point sampling is used to select a discrete set of contact points for optimization, ensuring good coverage. For specific grasp types (pinch, precision), subsets of contact meshes (e.g., fingertips) are used.
- **Dataset Processing**: The method is used to generate a new dataset from 5,700 objects in DexGraspNet. Objects are converted to USD, collision meshes are recomputed using Quad Remesher for improved collision handling, and assets are rescaled to ensure consistent and physically meaningful sizes.
- **Baselines**: The method is evaluated against DexGraspNet, GenDexGrasp, TDG, and MultiGripperDataset (which utilizes GraspIt!), with hyperparameters adjusted for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GraspQP involved comprehensive comparisons against existing methods using a test set of 50 objects, across five different robotic grippers (Allegro Hand, Shadow Hand, Robotiq3f, Robotiq2f, Psyonic Ability Hand), and under varying disturbance forces (1N, 2N, 5N, 10N).

**A. Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Unique Grasp Rate (UGR)**: Quantifies the percentage of distinct, stable grasps generated. Grasp poses are discretized across position, orientation (Euler angles), and joint states.
- **Entropy (H)**: Measures the diversity of grasp configurations, encompassing both joint states and the object’s pose (position and orientation). Orientation entropy is calculated using an axis-angle representation converted to spherical coordinates.
- **Average Max Penetration Depth (D)**: Indicates the physical realism by measuring the average worst-case penetration between the gripper and the object.
- **Grasp Success Rate (Succ1, Succ3)**: Assessed by simulating force application in Isaac Lab. `Succ1` denotes success if the object's center of mass (CoM) remains within a 3cm sphere for at least one principal axis force (±x, ±y, ±z). `Succ3` requires resistance to forces along all three principal axes. `Succ1` is used as the default for UGR and H calculations.

**B. Main Results:**
- **Superior Grasp Diversity and Stability**: GraspQP consistently outperformed baseline methods (DexGraspNet, GenDexGrasp, TDG, MultiGripper) across various settings in both Unique Grasp Rate (UGR) and Entropy (H). For power grasps, at a 5N disturbance force, GraspQP achieved an overall UGR of 52% and an entropy of 3.19, compared to the next best at 45% UGR and ~3.0 entropy.
- **Improved Scaling with Contact Points**: The method demonstrated better scalability with an increased number of contact points (12 contacts vs. 4 contacts), promoting greater contact area and force closure satisfaction, albeit with slightly higher penetration depths.
- **Grasp Taxonomy Performance**:
 - **Power Grasps**: Maintained high UGR and entropy across various grippers and disturbance forces.
 - **Pinch and Precision Grasps**: Showed a substantial decline in unique successful grasps, particularly for grippers like Robotiq 3F. However, performance improved significantly under lower external disturbance forces (e.g., at 1N, pinch grasps achieved 40% UGR and 2.81 H, while precision grasps reached 46% UGR and 3.02 H), indicating their inherent sensitivity to external forces due to limited contact points.
- **Computational Cost**: GraspQP is computationally more intensive than baselines (e.g., 3.4 seconds per grasp for a 24-DoF Shadow Hand vs. 1.15 seconds for DexGraspNet). However, this is considered acceptable for offline dataset generation.
- **Diversity Scaling**: Figure 4 illustrates that GraspQP scales more effectively with an increased number of initialized grasps (seeds). It achieved approximately 80 unique grasps with 128 seeds, while DexGraspNet saturated at around 60 unique grasps even with 512 seeds, highlighting GraspQP's ability to overcome diversity bottlenecks.

**C. Ablation Studies (Table 2):**
Ablation studies were conducted to assess the impact of the proposed MALA\* optimizer and the specific energy formulation.
- **Impact of MALA\***: Replacing the standard MALA optimizer with MALA\* consistently improved all reported metrics for power grasps averaged across dexterous hands:
 - Increased UGR by +5.0% to +7.0%.
 - Increased Entropy (H) by +0.2 to +0.3.
 - Reduced average max penetration depth (D) by -0.3 to -0.4 mm.
 - Both dynamic resetting (contributing -4.8% UGR, -0.1 H) and adaptive temperature scaling (contributing -4.5% UGR, -0.1 H) within MALA\* were shown to contribute to these improvements.
- **Energy Formulation Choices**:
 - The QP-based formulation with hard constraints based on Theorem 3.1 (iii) proved crucial. Reformulating the problem as an unconstrained optimization (using barrier functions or softmax) led to significant performance degradation (approx. -10% UGR and -0.2 H), underscoring the necessity of hard constraints.
 - Using the simplified Theorem 3.1 (ii) formulation (even with a constraint on sum of coefficients) also resulted in a decline in performance (-4.8% UGR, -0.1 H) compared to the proposed robust formulation.
 - The exponential scaling term for singular values (e^(-Π σi)) showed a minor but positive impact (-0.3% UGR when removed).

**D. Contact Maps (Figures 5-10):**
Qualitative visualizations of contact regions (heatmaps) for various objects and grippers indicate concentrated contact areas near the objects' centers of gravity, consistent with stable grasping principles. These maps illustrate how different grasp types (e.g., pinch vs. power) result in distinct contact patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several contributions to the field of robotic grasping and has the potential for significant impact:

- **Advancement in Grasp Synthesis Algorithms**: The introduction of a rigorous, differentiable force closure energy formulation implicitly defined through a Quadratic Program (QP) marks an advancement in analytical grasp synthesis. This approach enables accurate modeling of physical contact and friction, overcoming the limitations of simplified metrics used in many gradient-based methods. This improved physical grounding is crucial for generating more realistic and robust grasp configurations.
- **Enhanced Grasp Diversity**: The proposed MALA\* optimization strategy directly addresses the issue of mode collapse, a common challenge in dataset generation. By dynamically adapting gradient steps and re-initializing poor grasp candidates based on the global energy distribution, MALA\* promotes the exploration of a wider range of stable and unique grasp poses. This is particularly impactful for generating diverse grasp taxonomies, including challenging pinch and precision grasps, which are often underrepresented in existing datasets.
- **Creation of a High-Quality, Large-Scale Dataset**: The successful application of GraspQP to synthesize a new, large-scale grasp dataset for 5,700 objects, covering five different grippers and three distinct grasp types, provides a valuable resource for the robotics research community. Such a comprehensive and diverse dataset is essential for training advanced deep learning models for various dexterous manipulation tasks, including grasp prediction, manipulation planning, and reinforcement learning.
- **Improved Foundation for Learning-Based Robotics**: The availability of more diverse and physically grounded grasp data generated by this method can lead to the development of more generalizable and robust robotic manipulation policies. This can accelerate progress in areas such as object manipulation in unstructured environments, human-robot collaboration, and complex assembly tasks where versatile dexterous handling is required.
- **Insights into Grasp Mechanics**: The detailed ablation studies not only validate the effectiveness of each component of the proposed method but also offer valuable insights into the importance of specific design choices. For instance, the demonstrated necessity of hard constraints within the QP formulation and the benefits of dynamic resetting in the optimizer contribute to a deeper understanding of the factors that govern grasp quality and diversity.
- **Future Research Directions**: By acknowledging its limitations, such as increased computational complexity for real-time applications and remaining challenges like mode collapse, the work also signposts clear avenues for future research, including exploring GPU-accelerated solvers and density-based repulsion forces. The provision of modular grasping environments built on IsaacLab further facilitates external research and extension of the framework to dynamic manipulation scenarios.
