# Mitigating Visual Degradation in MLLMs via Spatial-Spectral Visual Anchor Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> An MLLM framework from a multi-institutional team addresses visual representation degradation in deep model layers using learnable visual anchor prompts and spatial-spectral alignment. This method significantly improved fine-grained visual perception on benchmarks while maintaining higher visual representation stability within the LLM.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit persistent deficiencies in visual perception, struggling with fine-grained visual tasks despite advancements in vision encoders and projector designs.
- Internal visual representations within LLM layers progressively deviate from their original semantic states during inference, leading to quantifiable visual information degradation in deeper layers (visual representation deviation).
- Existing solutions like input enhancements or direct VFM alignment improve representation quality but fail to mitigate the *rate* of this deviation across successive LLM layers, lacking a persistent mechanism to stabilize representations.

## Method

- The **Spatial-Spectral Visual Anchor Learning (SSVAL)** framework introduces ==Visual Anchor Prompt Injection (VAPI)== to create stable visual references, using learnable prompts that absorb Vision Foundation Model (VFM) knowledge during training and function as persistent anchors during inference.
- SSVAL incorporates ==Spatial-Spectral Representation Alignment (SSRA)== during training, which includes Spatial Representation Alignment (SpaRA) and Spectral Representation Alignment (SpeRA), providing complementary vision-specific supervision to intermediate LLM layers.
- SpaRA aligns intermediate LLM features with VFM teacher features at multiple spatial scales (1x1, 2x2, 4x4) using feature- and relation-level losses, while SpeRA aligns low- and high-frequency components of features from the ==frequency domain (FFT)== to capture global structures and fine details.

## Results

- SSVAL consistently outperformed baseline and VIRAL models across **six** multimodal benchmarks (e.g., CV-Bench2D_{2D}2D, MMVP, MME), showing up to **+7.86%** improvement on MMVP with Qwen2.5-7B and CLIP.
- The framework effectively mitigated visual representation deviation, maintaining higher cosine similarity between input and intermediate-layer visual representations across increasing LLM depths.
- SSVAL achieves these performance gains with negligible inference overhead, adding only **+5.25M** parameters (**+1.55%**) and **+3.65 GFLOPs** (**+0.04%**) compared to the baseline, as auxiliary losses and external VFMs are training-only.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual representation deviation is a primary cause of MLLM visual degradation, characterized by a sharp decrease in cosine similarity between input and intermediate-layer visual representations as LLM depth increases.
> - Direct alignment of intermediate MLLM layers with external Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) improves the *quality* of visual semantics but does not effectively *mitigate the deviation rate* across layers.
> - Learnable visual anchor prompts, specifically designed to absorb heterogeneous VFM knowledge and operate persistently during inference, can effectively arrest the progressive deviation of visual representations within the LLM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) is characterized by efforts to effectively integrate and align visual and linguistic modalities. Initial advancements, particularly through visual instruction tuning, have leveraged lightweight projectors to bridge vision encoders with pre-trained large language models (LLMs). This approach enables LLMs to process visual tokens, thereby facilitating cross-modal reasoning and improving performance across various downstream tasks.

Despite these developments, MLLMs continue to exhibit limitations in visual perception. Prior research has often attributed these shortcomings to either inadequate vision encoders or suboptimal projector designs. However, this paper identifies and focuses on an under-explored phenomenon occurring *within* the LLM itself: visual representation deviation. This phenomenon involves the internal visual representations in LLM layers progressively drifting from their original semantic states during inference, leading to information degradation in deeper layers. The paper quantifies this deviation by observing a sharp decrease in cosine similarity between input and intermediate-layer visual representations as layer depth increases.

Existing approaches to mitigate visual information degradation include input-stage enhancements (e.g., stronger vision encoders or token compression) and endpoint supervision, which are noted as insufficient for preventing intermediate information loss. More recent methods, such as Visual Representation Alignment (VIRAL), attempt to align intermediate LLM layers with external Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) to provide rich and stable visual features as targets. However, this work presents a counter-intuitive finding: while direct VFM alignment enhances the *quality* of visual representations, it does not effectively *mitigate the deviation rate* across layers. This suggests that simply injecting stronger visual features improves what representations encode but not their stability or how effectively they are maintained through successive layers.

This research positions itself by proposing a mechanism that addresses this specific limitation. Instead of direct feature distribution modification or endpoint supervision, it advocates for a persistent "anchor mechanism" that can absorb VFM knowledge during training and continuously provide stable visual references to arrest the progressive deviation of representations. This work therefore contributes to the landscape by identifying a core problem within MLLM processing and offering a novel architectural and training strategy to address it, distinct from previous input- or output-focused solutions or direct alignment methods. It also extends the concept of visual prompt learning, moving beyond its traditional role in efficient adaptation or knowledge distillation between homogeneous models, to serving as an anchoring mechanism for heterogeneous VFM knowledge within MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the visual perception capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by addressing the issue of visual information degradation that occurs within their internal layers.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:

1. **Persistent Visual Perception Deficiencies:** Despite advancements in MLLMs through visual instruction tuning and the integration of powerful vision encoders, these models still exhibit significant deficiencies in visual perception. This suggests that existing methods do not fully resolve the underlying issues.
2. **Identification of Visual Representation Deviation:** The authors identify and quantify a specific problem termed "visual representation deviation." This phenomenon describes how, after visual instruction tuning, the internal visual representations within LLM layers progressively deviate from their original semantic states as information propagates deeper into the model during inference. This deviation leads to a severe loss of visual information, directly impacting the model's ability to perform fine-grained visual tasks. The paper quantifies this using layer-wise cosine similarity analysis, showing a sharp drop as layer depth increases.
3. **Limitations of Existing Alignment Approaches:** An intuitive approach to counteract visual degradation is to align intermediate LLM representations with external Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), which offer rich and stable visual features. However, the authors present an empirical finding that direct VFM alignment, while enhancing the *quality* of visual semantics, does not effectively *mitigate the deviation rate* itself. The representations still diverge at a similar pace across layers, indicating that merely injecting stronger visual features is insufficient to *anchor* representations against progressive drift. This reveals a fundamental limitation: such approaches improve the content encoded by representations but not their stability across layers.
4. **Need for a Persistent Anchor Mechanism:** The observation that direct alignment fails to stabilize representations motivates the core insight of this research: there is a need for a persistent anchor mechanism. This mechanism should absorb knowledge from VFMs during training and continuously provide stable visual references during inference, thereby preventing the progressive deviation of visual information.
5. **Complementary Supervision for Visual Representations:** Standard MLLM training paradigms primarily rely on text-based supervision. This leaves visual representations within intermediate LLM layers without explicit vision-specific guidance, potentially exacerbating degradation. Therefore, a secondary objective is to provide complementary visual supervision at these intermediate layers.

In summary, the key objective is to propose and develop a framework, Spatial-Spectral Visual Anchor Learning (SSVAL), that effectively mitigates visual representation deviation in MLLMs by introducing a stable anchoring mechanism and complementary vision-specific supervision, thereby improving their visual perception and reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Spatial-Spectral Visual Anchor Learning (SSVAL), is designed to mitigate visual information degradation within MLLMs. It consists of a core inference-time module called Visual Anchor Prompt Injection (VAPI) and auxiliary training-time losses for Spatial-Spectral Representation Alignment (SSRA), which includes Spatial Representation Alignment (SpaRA) and Spectral Representation Alignment (SpeRA).

#### 3.1. Pipeline Overview

SSVAL integrates VAPI and SSRA into the MLLM architecture. During training, VAPI enables learnable prompts to absorb knowledge from external VFMs. These prompts then act as visual anchors during inference. SSRA provides additional vision-specific supervision to intermediate LLM layers during training. The overall process involves a vision encoder extracting features, VAPI processing these features with visual anchors, a projector mapping them to the LLM's embedding space, and the LLM generating responses.

#### 3.2. Visual Anchor Prompt Injection (VAPI)

VAPI is a central component aimed at creating stable visual references. It comprises three elements:

1. **Visual Prompts:** A set of learnable prompts, initialized randomly, with dimension $[N_p, D]$, where $N_p$ is the number of prompts and $D$ is the feature dimension. These prompts are optimized via backpropagation during training to encapsulate visual knowledge.
2. **VFM Feature Sampling and Projection:** During training, features are extracted from a pre-trained external VFM, yielding features of dimension $[N_v, D_v]$. A subset of these features, $[N_p, D_v]$, is uniformly sampled. A two-layer MLP projects these sampled VFM features to the same dimension $D$ as the visual prompts. The projected VFM features are then added to the learnable prompts, combining VFM residual information with the prompts.
3. **Prompt Fusion Module:** This module integrates the knowledge from the combined prompts into the vision features $Z_v$ extracted by the vision encoder. It uses a multi-head cross-attention mechanism, where $Z_v$ serves as queries, and the combined prompts $p$ serve as keys and values. The output is augmented with a gated residual connection: $Z'_v = Z_v + \text{Gate}(Z_v) \odot \text{CrossAttn}(Z_v, p)$. The gating mechanism adaptively controls the fusion of prompt information. Crucially, during inference, only the learned prompts are used, without requiring the external VFM, allowing them to function as stable visual anchors throughout the LLM layers.

#### 3.3. Spatial-Spectral Representation Alignment (SSRA)

SSRA provides complementary vision-specific supervision during training to intermediate LLM layers, addressing the lack of explicit visual guidance in standard text-based training.

1. **Spatial Representation Alignment (SpaRA):** This component aligns intermediate LLM layer representations (student features $s_i$) with corresponding VFM teacher features ($t_i$) from a multiscale spatial perspective.
 - **Multiscale Downsampling:** Visual features are subjected to multiscale downsampling using pooling factors of 1x1, 2x2, and 4x4 to capture semantics at different granularities. Each scale uses an independent projection network to map student features to the teacher's feature space.
 - **Alignment Losses:**
 - **Feature-level alignment ($L_{feat}$):** Maximizes cosine similarity between normalized student and teacher features for each token.
 - **Relation-level alignment ($L_{rel}$):** Uses Mean Squared Error (MSE) to match pairwise similarities of spatially neighboring token pairs in the student to those in the teacher.
 - The total SpaRA loss is a weighted sum of these alignment losses across multiple scales.

2. **Spectral Representation Alignment (SpeRA):** This component provides complementary supervision from the frequency domain.
 - **Feature Transformation:** Student (after projection) and teacher features are reshaped into 2D feature maps $S, T \in \mathbb{R}^{\sqrt{N_v} \times \sqrt{N_v} \times D_v}$ and normalized with LayerNorm. A 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) is applied to obtain frequency spectra $F_S, F_T$.
 - **Frequency Decomposition:** The spectra are decomposed into low-frequency (LF) and high-frequency (HF) components using frequency masks $M_{LF}, M_{HF}$ based on the normalized radial distance from the center. LF components capture global structure, while HF components correspond to edges and local details.
 - **Inverse Transformation and Loss:** Filtered spectra are transformed back to the spatial domain via inverse FFT ($S_f, T_f$).
 - **LF Loss ($L_{LF}$):** MSE between $S_{LF}$ and $T_{LF}$.
 - **HF Loss ($L_{HF}$):** MSE between the logarithmic magnitudes of $S_{HF}$ and $T_{HF}$ (logarithmic compression is used to prevent HF noise from dominating).
 - The total SpeRA loss is a weighted sum of LF and HF losses.

#### 3.4. Training Loss

The overall training loss combines the standard next token prediction (NTP) loss with the auxiliary alignment losses:
$L_{total} = L_{NTP} + \lambda_{SpaRA} \cdot L_{SpaRA} + \lambda_{SpeRA} \cdot L_{SpeRA}$.
$L_{NTP}$ is the cross-entropy loss for language modeling, and $\lambda_{SpaRA}, \lambda_{SpeRA}$ are weight coefficients for the respective alignment losses.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **MLLM Architecture:** LLaVA paradigm with CLIP or SigLIP as vision encoder and Qwen2.5 (3B or 7B) as the LLM backbone. A three-layer MLP projector maps vision features.
- **Training Process:** Initial pre-training of the projector on LLaVA Visual Instruct Pretrain LCS-558K, followed by visual instruction tuning on LLaVA-665K. LoRA fine-tuning (rank=128, $\alpha=256$) is applied to the LLM. AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 2e-4.
- **VFM for Alignment:** DINOv2-B is used by default for VFM knowledge injection and supervision. Other VFMs like CLIP-L, DINOv3-L, DINOv2-L were explored in ablations. Input resolutions are adapted (336x336 or 384x384) to maintain consistent visual token counts.
- **Loss Configuration:** $\lambda_{SpaRA} = 0.3$, $\lambda_{SpeRA} = 0.15$. SpaRA uses 1:1:1 weights for 1x1, 2x2, 4x4 scales. SpeRA uses 1:1 weights for LF and HF losses.
- **Prompt Configuration:** Four visual anchor prompts are used by default.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of SSVAL across various benchmarks, vision encoders, and LLM scales.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Consistent Improvements:** SSVAL consistently outperforms the Baseline (standard visual instruction tuning) and VIRAL (Visual Representation Alignment) across six multimodal benchmarks: CV-Bench$_{2D}$, MMVP, MMStar, MME, POPE, and MM-Bench.
- **Scalability:** Improvements are observed with both Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-7B LLM scales. For the Qwen2.5-7B scale, SSVAL shows more pronounced advantages, with +6.47% on CV-Bench$_{2D}$ and +7.86% on MMVP when using CLIP as the vision encoder. These benchmarks specifically assess fine-grained visual perception and spatial reasoning.
- **Generalization to Stronger Encoders:** When evaluated with SigLIP2, a stronger vision encoder, SSVAL continues to achieve consistent gains across all benchmarks. In contrast, VIRAL often exhibits performance drops (e.g., MMVP -5.34%, MME -68.43%), suggesting that direct alignment can disrupt high-quality initial representations.
- **Benchmark-Specific Gains:** The most significant improvements are observed on CV-Bench$_{2D}$ and MMVP. This supports the hypothesis that visual anchor prompts effectively suppress representation deviation, preserving task-relevant spatial and semantic details crucial for fine-grained visual matching and spatial reasoning.

#### 4.2. Layer-wise Cosine Similarity to Input

- As illustrated in Figure 1(b), SSVAL maintains higher cosine similarity between input visual representations and intermediate-layer representations across increasing LLM depths compared to both the Baseline and VIRAL. This demonstrates that SSVAL effectively mitigates visual representation deviation, preventing the progressive loss of visual information in deeper layers.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Prompt Quantity:** Performance remains stable across different numbers of prompts (4 to 24), demonstrating VAPI's robustness. Four prompts are chosen as a balance between accuracy and efficiency.
- **Vision Foundation Model (VFM) for Injection:** External VFMs like DINOv2 and DINOv3 yield stronger results compared to homogeneous CLIP, suggesting that visual anchor prompts benefit from diverse and complementary VFM knowledge. DINOv2-B provides a balanced performance.
- **Component Analysis:**
 - Visual Anchor Prompt Injection (VAPI) is identified as the dominant contributor to performance gains, significantly improving CV-Bench$_{2D}$ by 3.13 points and MMVP by 11.86 points over the Baseline.
 - Spatial Representation Alignment (SpaRA) and Spectral Representation Alignment (SpeRA) provide complementary benefits, with the full VAPI+SpaRA+SpeRA configuration achieving the best overall scores on several benchmarks.
 - Removing SpaRA or SpeRA can lead to performance degradation on specific benchmarks, indicating the importance of their interaction and context-dependent benefits.
- **SpaRA Multi-scale Configuration:** The multiscale configuration achieves the best overall performance, indicating that supervision across different granularities (fine-grained details and global structures) is most beneficial.
- **SpeRA Frequency Component Analysis:** Low-frequency alignment contributes primary improvements by capturing global semantic structures. High-frequency alignment alone can degrade performance due to noise sensitivity, but when combined with low-frequency components (full SpeRA), it offers complementary fine-grained detail supervision.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis

- SSVAL introduces negligible inference overhead: only +5.25M parameters (+1.55%) and +3.65 GFLOPs (+0.04%) compared to the Baseline.
- SpaRA and SpeRA are training-only losses and do not add to inference computation. The external VFM is also discarded after training. This demonstrates a favorable efficiency-performance trade-off.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

- **PCA Visualizations:** PCA visualizations of final-layer visual tokens show that SSVAL produces embeddings with clearer clustering boundaries, stronger structural organization, and greater semantic coherence, indicating more effective preservation of critical visual information compared to Baseline and VIRAL.
- **Attention Maps:** SSVAL's attention maps show a more focused attention on relevant regions, improving object localization and visual reasoning reliability, while Baseline exhibits more scattered attention.
- **Prompt Attention Analysis:** Visualizations of prompt attention reveal that prompts learn complementary roles. For instance, one prompt may act as a background suppression mask, while others focus on foreground objects, inheriting objectness awareness from VFMs like DINOv2. This demonstrates that prompts not only mitigate deviation but also enhance spatial perception by integrating diverse VFM capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental challenge in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): the degradation of visual information as it propagates through the internal layers of the language model. By identifying and quantifying this "visual representation deviation," the paper highlights a limitation not fully addressed by previous approaches that focused on input enhancements or direct feature alignment.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Improved MLLM Visual Perception and Reasoning:** The core contribution of SSVAL, particularly through Visual Anchor Prompt Injection (VAPI), directly tackles the problem of visual information loss. By stabilizing visual representations, the framework enables MLLMs to perform better on tasks requiring fine-grained visual understanding, precise spatial reasoning, and object localization. This can lead to more accurate and reliable responses from MLLMs in visually intensive applications.
- **Novel Architectural Design for MLLMs:** SSVAL introduces a novel approach of using learnable prompts as persistent visual anchors. Unlike previous prompt-learning methods, these prompts are specifically designed to absorb heterogeneous VFM knowledge during training and provide stable references during inference, rather than merely adapting models or distilling knowledge. This concept could inspire new architectural designs for integrating and stabilizing multimodal information in other complex models.
- **Enhanced Model Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated effectiveness of SSVAL across various benchmarks, different vision encoders (CLIP, SigLIP2), and LLM scales (Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B) indicates its robustness and generalization capabilities. This suggests that the proposed mechanisms are broadly applicable to diverse MLLM configurations.
- **Efficiency in Inference:** A key impact is the minimal inference overhead introduced by SSVAL. The VAPI module's prompts, once trained, function independently of the external VFM. The auxiliary Spatial-Spectral Representation Alignment (SSRA) losses are training-only. This design ensures that the substantial performance gains do not come at the cost of increased inference latency or computational requirements, making the method practical for real-world deployment.
- **Deeper Understanding of MLLM Internal Dynamics:** The work provides insights into the internal workings of MLLMs, specifically how visual information degrades and how prompt-based mechanisms can influence attention and representation stability. The qualitative analyses, particularly PCA visualizations and prompt attention maps, offer valuable insights into how learned prompts acquire specialized roles and enhance feature coherence.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This research opens several avenues for future work, such as exploring the combination of multiple complementary VFMs for even more robust anchors, developing strategies for offline pre-extraction of VFM features to reduce training overhead, and investigating the applicability of SSVAL to mature MLLMs that have undergone multi-stage training. These extensions could further solidify the method's utility and impact on the MLLM landscape.

In conclusion, SSVAL provides an effective, efficient, and generalizable solution to a critical internal limitation of MLLMs, thereby advancing the field's ability to build more visually intelligent and reliable multimodal AI systems.
