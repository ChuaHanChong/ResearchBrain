# VLA-4D: Embedding 4D Awareness into Vision-Language-Action Models for SpatioTemporally Coherent Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-4D introduces a general Vision-Language-Action model that embeds 4D (spatiotemporal) awareness into both visual perception and action planning for robotic manipulation. This system integrates 3D positional and 1D temporal information into visual features and extends the action space with an explicit step-level duration variable, leading to spatiotemporally coherent and efficient robotic actions with a 97.4% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing 2D and 3D Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often produce spatially discontinuous or temporally jittery actions, leading to inefficient and unnatural robotic behaviors.
- Prior 4D VLA approaches primarily focused on enhancing *visual perception* with temporal cues but lacked explicit mechanisms to define or plan *temporal aspects of actions*.
- Current robotic manipulation systems struggle with fine-grained control over action execution dynamics and overall coherence due to coarse-grained spatiotemporal representations.

## Method

- Develops a 4D-aware visual representation by integrating 3D world coordinates and 1D time into visual features using Fourier-based spatiotemporal embeddings and cross-attention fusion.
- Introduces a spatiotemporal action representation that extends the action space with a novel temporal control variable (Δt) for step-level action duration, alongside conventional spatial controls.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy: initial pretraining on large-scale 3D/4D Vision-Language Model (VLM) datasets for general spatiotemporal understanding, followed by fine-tuning on a temporally augmented robotic manipulation dataset.

## Results

- VLA-4D achieved an average success rate of 97.4% and an average completion time of 5.8 seconds on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming existing 2D, 3D, and prior 4D VLA models.
- The model demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization capabilities on unseen robotic manipulation tasks, showing substantially higher success rates and shorter completion times compared to other methods.
- Visualizations of action trajectories confirmed that VLA-4D generates both smooth global trajectories and highly stable local motion speeds, demonstrating spatiotemporally coherent robotic actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving spatiotemporal coherence in robotic manipulation requires *jointly* enhancing 4D awareness in both the visual stream (for perception) and the action stream (for planning).
> - Explicitly defining and predicting temporal control variables (Δt) within the action space is crucial for reducing task completion time and improving action efficiency, beyond just spatial precision.
> - A Fourier-based encoding strategy effectively maps distinct spatial and temporal scales into a unified feature space, preventing gradient conflicts and ensuring stable convergence for 4D-aware visual representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on VLA-4D positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics, building upon the successes of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in general scene reasoning. Historically, VLM advancements have inspired their application in embodied intelligence tasks such as robotic manipulation and navigation.

The broader landscape can be categorized by how these models handle spatial and temporal information:
- **2D VLA Models:** Early VLA approaches (e.g., OpenVLA, Octo, DiffusionPolicy, CogACT) typically rely on single-image inputs. While effective for general tasks, they suffer from coarse visual reasoning, a mismatch between 2D image coordinates and 3D robot workspace coordinates, and a lack of explicit temporal awareness. This leads to actions that are often discontinuous, imprecise, and temporally incoherent, manifested as "idle pauses or jitter."
- **3D VLA Models:** To address spatial limitations, subsequent works (e.g., TraceVLA, SpatialVLA) introduced 3D positional embeddings into visual representations. These "3D VLAs" significantly improved spatial precision and smoothness of actions by enhancing the model's understanding of scene geometry. However, they largely overlooked the temporal dimension, resulting in spatially optimized trajectories that still lacked temporal coherence and fine-grained control over action execution dynamics.
- **Prior 4D VLA Models:** More recent efforts (e.g., Niu et al., Zhang et al.) began integrating temporal signals, such as frame indices, alongside 3D positions into the visual stream. These approaches aimed to improve temporal reasoning (e.g., understanding state precedence) but primarily focused on enhancing *visual perception* rather than explicitly defining or planning *temporal aspects of actions*. Consequently, while they mitigated some temporal ambiguities, they often "still struggle to explicitly improve the precision of temporal action planning and the overall coherence of operation execution."

VLA-4D addresses this critical gap by arguing that true spatiotemporal coherence requires a joint enhancement of 4D awareness in *both* the visual representation (for reasoning) *and* the action representation (for planning). It pioneers the explicit definition of temporal control variables within the action space, moving beyond simply using temporal cues for visual reasoning to actively controlling the *duration* and *speed* of robotic actions. This work represents a significant step towards achieving truly fluid, efficient, and human-like robotic manipulation, pushing the boundaries from spatially aware robots to fully spatiotemporally aware agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve **spatiotemporally coherent robotic manipulation** through the development of VLA-4D, a general Vision-Language-Action model with comprehensive 4D awareness.

This overarching goal is driven by several specific objectives and motivations stemming from the limitations of existing VLA paradigms:
1. **Overcome Spatiotemporal Discontinuity:** The fundamental motivation is to address the inherent challenges of spatiotemporally coherent manipulation that current VLA models, including 2D and 3D variants, struggle with. Existing methods often result in actions that are spatially discontinuous or temporally jittery, leading to inefficient and unnatural robotic behaviors.
2. **Enhance Fine-Grained Representations:** The authors hypothesize that the lack of coherence is due to coarse-grained representations. Therefore, a key objective is to strengthen these representations for both visual reasoning and action planning.
3. **Develop a 4D-Aware Visual Representation:** Specifically, the paper aims to design a visual representation that not only embeds 3D positional information for understanding scene geometry and spatial localization but also integrates 1D temporal information to perceive dynamic patterns and temporal states of actions. This 4D perception is crucial for fine-grained visual reasoning.
4. **Construct a Spatiotemporal Action Representation:** A critical objective is to extend conventional spatial action representations by incorporating explicit temporal control variables. This is motivated by the observation that while spatial control is foundational, temporal control is indispensable for achieving a continuous, smooth, and coherent action process. The model seeks to enable planning not just of *where* to move, but also *how fast* and *for how long*.
5. **Achieve Joint Enhancement:** The core motivation is the belief that coherent manipulation requires *jointly* enhancing spatiotemporal awareness in both the visual stream (for perception) and the action stream (for planning). This distinguishes VLA-4D from prior work that primarily focused on 4D visual reasoning without explicitly modeling temporal action parameters.
6. **Augment Robotic Datasets:** To facilitate the training of such a model, an objective is to extend existing robotic datasets, such as LIBERO, with necessary temporal action annotations, enabling fine-tuning for spatiotemporally coherent tasks.

By addressing these objectives, VLA-4D aims to unlock the full potential of VLA models for complex robotic tasks, enabling them to execute actions with unprecedented precision, fluidity, and efficiency across both space and time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-4D's methodology is structured around two key design principles: a 4D-aware visual representation and a spatiotemporal action representation, integrated within a Large Language Model (LLM)-based framework.

**Overall Framework (Figure 2):**
The model takes video sequences and instruction texts as input. A vision encoder extracts visual features, and a text tokenizer processes linguistic tokens. Proprioceptive states are also incorporated. These multimodal inputs are aligned and fed into a pretrained LLM with an action head to predict spatiotemporal actions.

**4D-Aware Visual Representation:**
1. **Geometric and Temporal Encoding:** To address the spatial (2D image vs. 3D world) and temporal (static image vs. dynamic action) discrepancies, the model first extracts 3D positional information and 1D time.
 - A geometry encoder (VGGT) processes multi-view video sequences (wrist-view and third-view) to obtain camera poses (P) and depths (D) at each timestamp (t).
 - 2D pixel coordinates are transformed into 3D world coordinates (p_3D) using standard geometric projection: `p_3D = P^-1 (D K^-1 p_2D)`.
2. **4D SpatioTemporal Embedding (STE):** The extracted 3D positions (p_3D) and 1D time (t) are converted into a unified 4D representation. A Fourier-based encoding strategy is employed to map these distinct scales into learnable patterns, which are then passed through a linear layer to form 4D spatiotemporal embeddings (f_4D).
3. **Cross-Attention Fusion:** High-level visual features (f_v) obtained from a ViT-variant vision encoder are fused with the MLP-projected 4D embeddings (f_4D) using a cross-attention mechanism. This fusion (`f_4D_v = f_v + softmax(qk^T/√d)v`) ensures that the visual features are enriched with 4D semantic and geometric information, enabling fine-grained spatiotemporal visual reasoning.

**SpatioTemporal Action Representation:**
1. **Augmented Action Definition:** Unlike conventional spatial actions `[Δx, Δθ, Grip]`, VLA-4D extends the action space to `A = [Δx, Δθ, Grip, Δt]`.
 - `Δx`, `Δθ`, `Grip` represent translational displacement, rotational change, and gripper control, respectively.
 - `Δt` is a novel temporal variable that specifies the *step-level action duration*. This variable is designed to be jointly determined by visual cues, linguistic instructions, and proprioceptive states, ensuring adaptive and coherent action execution speed.
2. **Multimodal Alignment:** To integrate these representations into an LLM, the 4D-aware visual features (f_4D_v) and proprioceptive states (f_p) are projected into the LLM's language embedding space via Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), yielding visual (τ_4D_v) and proprioceptive (τ_p) tokens. These are concatenated with linguistic tokens (τ_l) derived from the task instruction.
3. **Task Optimization:** The concatenated multimodal tokens `[τ_4D_v, τ_p, τ_l]` are fed into a pretrained LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) appended with an MLP-based action head. The LLM then predicts the spatiotemporal action parameters `[Δx, Δθ, Grip, Δt]`. The model is trained using an L1-norm loss (`L_action`) comparing predicted actions with ground-truth annotations, promoting precise spatiotemporal action prediction.

**Dataset and Training Pipeline:**
1. **Dataset Extension:** The LIBERO benchmark dataset is extended. Multi-view videos, camera parameters, depths, and proprioceptive states are rendered. Crucially, human-designed trajectories are analyzed to derive *variable temporal action annotations (Δt)* by converting step counts to durations based on sampling frequency, while maintaining original spatial parameters. This results in a dataset of 150k VLA samples across 40 subtasks.
2. **Two-Stage Training:**
 - **Stage 1: 4D Vision-Language Alignment:** The VLM component (vision encoder + LLM) is pretrained on large-scale 3D and 4D vision-language datasets (e.g., Scan2Cap, Chat4D). This stage focuses on enhancing the visual representations with strong 4D spatiotemporal perception and language interaction, optimizing cross-attention, STE, projector, and LLM (via LoRA adapters).
 - **Stage 2: Robotic Task Fine-Tuning:** The entire VLA-4D architecture is fine-tuned on the modified LIBERO dataset using the `L_action` loss. This stage specializes the model for robotic manipulation, enabling it to produce fine-grained spatiotemporal actions conditioned on inputs. Weights of the action head, projector, cross-attention, and LLM (LoRA) are updated, while vision and geometry encoders are frozen.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the LIBERO benchmark, utilizing task success rate (SR) and completion time (CT) as primary metrics, demonstrate the superior performance and effectiveness of VLA-4D.

**Comparison with State-of-the-Art Models:**
- **Fine-Tuned Tasks (Table 1):** VLA-4D consistently outperforms existing 2D, 3D, and prior 4D VLA models across all LIBERO benchmark settings (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). It achieves the highest average success rate (97.4%) and the shortest average completion time (5.8 seconds), significantly surpassing the next best 4D model (88.6% SR) and all 2D/3D baselines. This confirms the benefits of its integrated 4D awareness.
- **Zero-Shot Tasks (Figure 5):** VLA-4D exhibits strong generalization capabilities on unseen robotic manipulation tasks, achieving substantially higher success rates and shorter completion times compared to other methods. This indicates that its spatiotemporal representations enable robust understanding and planning in novel scenarios.
- **Spatiotemporal Planning Visualization (Figure 6):** Visualizing action trajectories reveals that:
 - 2D models produce redundant global motion and noticeable oscillations in local motion speed, lacking coherence.
 - 3D models achieve smoother global trajectories but still show fluctuations in local motion speed.
 - **VLA-4D** generates both smooth global trajectories and highly stable local motion speeds, effectively demonstrating spatiotemporal coherence.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Visual Representation Modules (Table 2):** Each component of the 4D-aware visual representation (spatial embedding, temporal embedding, and cross-attention fusion) contributes positively. Incorporating spatial embedding yields a significant performance boost, and adding temporal embedding further enhances it. The cross-attention fusion mechanism provides an additional synergistic improvement, validating the design choices.
- **Effect of Action Representation Components (Table 3):** The inclusion of temporal parameters (Δt) alongside spatial parameters (Δx, Δθ, Grip) significantly improves efficiency. While spatial parameters ensure a high success rate, temporal parameters dramatically reduce the task completion time, confirming their role in enhancing the coherence and efficiency of robotic operations.
- **Influence of Input Modality (Table 4):**
 - Video-based vision data provides a stronger baseline than image-based input.
 - The introduction of **4D cues (3D positions + 1D time)** leads to the most substantial performance gains in both success rate and completion time.
 - Proprioceptive states offer a minor but consistent complementary improvement.

**Discussion:**
- **How 4D Vision Affects Spatiotemporal Action (Figure 7):** Analysis shows that 3D visual features lead to temporally scattered action features, resulting in longer completion times despite good spatial clustering. In contrast, 4D visual features form a continuous spatiotemporal manifold, yielding spatiotemporally clustered and continuous action features, which translates to the highest success rate and shortest completion time.
- **Role of Spatiotemporal Encoding (Table 5):** Using a dedicated Fourier-based spatiotemporal encoding strategy (Equation 3) is crucial. It maps spatial and temporal information into a unified feature space, preventing gradient conflicts and ensuring stable convergence, leading to better performance than a simple concatenation.
- **Choice of Visual Feature Fusion Strategy (Table 6):** Attention-based fusion proves superior to concatenation and weighting. Its dynamic weight adjustment allows the model to selectively focus on the most relevant 4D-aware visual features for action prediction, highlighting the importance of adaptive fusion.
- **Impact of Training Strategy (Table 7):** The two-stage training strategy (Stage 1 for VLM pretraining, Stage 2 for robotic fine-tuning) is critical. Pretraining on large-scale 3D/4D VLM datasets in Stage 1 significantly enhances spatiotemporal visual reasoning, leading to substantially better robotic manipulation performance compared to training only in Stage 2.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA-4D research introduces a transformative paradigm for robotic manipulation by explicitly integrating 4D (spatiotemporal) awareness into both the perceptual and action planning components of Vision-Language-Action models. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

**Core Contribution:** The primary contribution is the development of a general 4D VLA model that achieves unprecedented spatiotemporal coherence in robotic manipulation. This is accomplished through two novel designs: a 4D-aware visual representation for fine-grained reasoning and a spatiotemporal action representation for coherent planning.

**Technical Innovations:**
- **Comprehensive 4D Integration:** VLA-4D goes beyond previous 4D approaches by not only embedding 3D positions and 1D time into visual features but also extending the action space with an explicit temporal control variable (Δt). This joint treatment of 4D in both perception and action is a crucial innovation.
- **Sophisticated Feature Fusion:** The use of Fourier-based spatiotemporal encoding and cross-attention mechanisms for visual feature fusion allows for a robust and dynamic integration of 4D cues, leading to more meaningful representations.
- **Enhanced Training Methodology:** The two-stage training pipeline, leveraging large-scale 3D/4D VLM pre-training before task-specific robotic fine-tuning, establishes an effective strategy for transferring broad spatiotemporal understanding to specific manipulation tasks.
- **Dataset Augmentation:** The extension of the LIBERO dataset with temporal action annotations provides a valuable new resource for the research community, enabling further advancements in spatiotemporally coherent robot learning.

**Broader Impact on Robotics and AI:**
- **More Natural and Efficient Robotic Actions:** By enabling robots to plan actions that are spatially smooth and temporally coherent, VLA-4D can lead to manipulation behaviors that are more fluid, efficient, and human-like. This is critical for deploying robots in complex, unstructured environments where precision and adaptability are paramount (e.g., healthcare, logistics, assistive robotics).
- **Improved Task Success and Reduced Completion Time:** The demonstrated superior performance in terms of both success rate and completion time indicates practical benefits, making robots more reliable and productive.
- **Strong Generalization Capabilities:** The robust performance on zero-shot tasks suggests that VLA-4D can adapt to novel manipulation scenarios with minimal retraining, which is a major step towards truly general-purpose robot policies.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens new avenues for exploring more advanced spatiotemporal reasoning and planning. It provides a strong baseline for integrating other modalities (e.g., force feedback), exploring different LLM architectures, and applying reinforcement learning for online adaptation and error correction, as suggested in the paper's limitations.
- **Paradigm Shift in Embodied AI:** VLA-4D reinforces the importance of incorporating the temporal dimension explicitly into all levels of embodied AI systems, challenging the limitations of static or purely spatial representations and paving the way for more dynamic and intelligent robotic agents.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that deploying VLA-4D in unseen real-world environments still presents challenges, primarily due to uncontrollable factors like mechanical wear and calibration drift that can introduce action errors. Future work plans to address this by incorporating reinforcement learning for online error correction, aiming to achieve more robust and adaptive robotic planning in complex physical settings. This forward-looking perspective highlights a clear roadmap for translating this significant research into practical, real-world applications.
