# FastUMI: A Scalable and Hardware-Independent Universal Manipulation Interface with Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> FastUMI, developed by Shanghai AI Lab, redesigns the Universal Manipulation Interface to enable scalable and hardware-independent collection of high-quality, real-world robotic manipulation data. It achieves this through decoupled hardware, a streamlined software framework, and algorithmic enhancements tailored for first-person data, providing over 10,000 demonstration trajectories across 22 tasks.

## Problem

- The scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, real-world interaction data impedes the development of generalist action policies for robots.
- Existing data collection methods like teleoperation are labor-intensive and costly, while vision-driven approaches often lack fine-grained interaction dynamics.
- The original Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) suffered from tight hardware dependency on specific robotic components and software instability due to complex, occlusion-prone Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) pipelines.

## Method

- Introduces a decoupled hardware design for both handheld and robot-mounted devices, utilizing a RealSense T265 for robust pose tracking and modular, ISO-standard compatible components for broad platform compatibility.
- Develops a software framework with ROS nodes for synchronized multimodal data acquisition, quality assessment, and flexible data preparation supporting both TCP and joint trajectory generation.
- Implements algorithmic adaptations, including Smooth-ACT and PoseACT for first-person perspectives, Depth-Enhanced Diffusion Policy using Depth Anything V2 for improved depth perception, and a dynamic error-compensation algorithm for non-parallel-jaw grippers.

## Results

- RealSense T265 achieved 10.5 mm average positioning error in low-occlusion tasks, with alternative tracking modules showing more stable performance across varying occlusions.
- Depth-Enhanced Diffusion Policy significantly improved success rates on depth-sensitive tasks, with "Open Ricecooker" increasing from 20% to 93.33%.
- Enhanced ACT (Smooth-ACT and PoseACT) boosted success rates on kinematically challenging tasks (e.g., "Pick Bear" improved from 20% to 80% with PoseACT).
- Over 10,000 real-world demonstration trajectories spanning 22 everyday tasks, 19 object categories, and 12 manipulation skills were open-sourced.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling hardware dependencies and simplifying software integration significantly lowers the barrier to entry for collecting high-quality robotic manipulation data.
> - Robust pose tracking and consistent visual perspective between human demonstrations and robot execution are critical for effective policy transfer and reducing domain shift.
> - Augmenting first-person visual policies with depth information and kinematically aware action representations (e.g., end-effector pose, local smoothing) can substantially improve performance and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical bottleneck in the field of robotic manipulation: the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, real-world interaction data. This scarcity significantly impedes the development of generalist action policies for robots, which are crucial for autonomous operation in complex, unstructured environments.

Current methods for robotic data collection generally fall into three categories:
- **Teleoperation-based techniques:** While offering precision, these methods are labor-intensive, costly, and often require non-intuitive human-to-robot mapping, limiting their scalability.
- **Vision-driven demonstrations:** These approaches can yield large, low-cost datasets but often lack the fine-grained interaction dynamics necessary for robust policy learning.
- **Sensor-enhanced interfaces:** Exemplified by systems like the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI), these interfaces directly capture multimodal signals that align with a robot’s sensory modalities, facilitating high-fidelity data transfer from human demonstrations to robotic frameworks. They offer a promising path for cost-effective, high-quality data collection.

FastUMI emerges as a significant advancement building upon the foundation of UMI. The original UMI system, while innovative, suffered from two main limitations:
1. **Hardware Dependency:** Its tight coupling with specific robotic components (e.g., a particular gripper) restricted adaptability, increased costs, and demanded extensive mechanical redesign and recalibration for integration with different platforms. This hindered widespread adoption.
2. **Software Instability:** Reliance on a GoPro-based Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) pipeline and open-source SLAM algorithms led to difficulties in tasks involving prolonged occlusions and required complex calibration procedures, diminishing data quality and reproducibility.

FastUMI directly tackles these limitations by introducing a "substantial redesign" of UMI. It aims to achieve hardware independence, simplify software integration, and provide robust performance. By doing so, it directly contributes to the goal of "data-driven robotic learning" by making it easier and more efficient to acquire the vast amounts of real-world interaction data needed to train generalist robot policies. The open-sourcing of a large, diverse dataset (over 10,000 trajectories across 22 tasks) further positions this work as a foundational contribution, providing a valuable resource for the wider research community working on imitation learning and generalizable robot skills. This effort aligns with recent trends in robotics to move towards large-scale dataset collection and foundation models, such as Open X-Embodiment and RT-1, by providing a more accessible and scalable method for data generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind FastUMI is to overcome the limitations of existing robotic data collection methods, particularly the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI), to facilitate the scalable acquisition of high-quality, real-world manipulation data. Such data is crucial for training robust and generalizable action policies for robotic arms.

The paper identifies two key limitations of the original UMI system:
1. **Hardware Coupling:** UMI's design was tightly linked to specific robotic components (e.g., Weiss WSG-50 gripper), making it costly, difficult to adapt to different robot platforms, and requiring extensive mechanical and software modifications for each new integration. This significantly increased logistical and financial burdens, hindering its widespread adoption.
2. **Software Complexity and Robustness Issues:** UMI's reliance on a GoPro-based VIO pipeline struggled with prolonged occlusions (common in real-world manipulation tasks), leading to diminished data quality. Furthermore, the VIO process itself was complex, sensitive to parameterization, and required intricate calibration, making it user-unfriendly and difficult to reproduce.

To address these challenges, the authors set out with three primary objectives for FastUMI:

1. **Enhancing Adaptability through Hardware Decoupling:** To enable seamless integration with a wide range of robotic arms and grippers, the hardware design needed to remove strict dependencies on specialized components. This would allow for rapid deployment across diverse platforms, reducing financial and logistical barriers.
2. **Improving Efficiency with Software-Driven Plug-and-Play Functionality:** The software framework aimed for immediate usability with minimal configuration and user training. This objective sought to streamline data collection, reduce operational complexity, and ensure long-term compatibility with evolving hardware, supporting both current and future robotic platforms.
3. **Establishing a Robust Ecosystem to Ensure Data Quality and Algorithmic Compatibility:** Beyond data collection, the project aimed to provide tools for data verification and integration with various imitation learning algorithms (e.g., ACT, Diffusion Policy). This ecosystem would ensure that collected datasets consistently meet the quality standards required to advance manipulation capabilities.

By achieving these objectives, FastUMI seeks to bridge the gap between human demonstrations and autonomous robotic execution, making high-quality, real-world data acquisition more accessible, efficient, and scalable for robotic learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FastUMI's methodology involves a comprehensive redesign of both the hardware and software components of the original UMI system, guided by a decoupled design philosophy.

**A. Hardware-Centric Prototypes Design:**
The core principle is **decoupling**, achieved along three dimensions: Physical Decoupling, Visual Consistency, and Operational Independence.

- **Handheld Device Design:** This device is used by human operators for data collection.
 - **Fisheye Camera Module (GoPro):** A GoPro with a fisheye extension captures wide-angle (155-degree FOV) images, crucial for environmental context and reducing occlusions, proving superior to narrower FOV cameras for First-Person View (FPV) data.
 - **Pose Tracking Module (RealSense T265):** This is a key change. The T265, with its integrated IMU, replaces UMI's complex VIO solution. It provides stable pose estimation even under partial occlusions, eliminating extensive calibration and enhancing usability. The system is also designed to be robust to alternative tracking modules (e.g., RoboBaton MINI).
 - **Top Cover, Fingertip, and Marker Redesign:** The GoPro is repositioned closer to the fingertips, ensuring the top cover remains outside the camera's FOV. Markers on the fingertips are optimized in size and placement to minimize distortion and improve detection accuracy. The camera is factory-calibrated and aligned with fingertips, offering a "plug-and-play" experience.

- **Robot-Mounted Device Design:** This device executes learned policies on robotic arms.
 - **Flange Plate:** Designed to be ISO-standard compatible, allowing integration with a wide range of robotic arms.
 - **Plug-in Fingertip:** These are identical externally to the handheld version, but internally contoured to fit various gripper shapes (e.g., xArm, Robotiq 2f-85). This modularity ensures consistent physical interaction points and facilitates policy transfer.
 - **Adjustable Camera Mounting Structure:** Modular extension arms enable precise alignment of the GoPro with the robot gripper's fingertips. This structure allows users to replicate the handheld device's camera perspective across different robot setups, maintaining **visual consistency** (GoPro's fisheye lens image bottom aligns with gripper fingertips bottom).
- **Other Design Optimizations:** Structural enhancements like reinforced key mounts, carbon fiber components, and standardized male-female interfaces improve stability, durability, and reduce vibration for extended data collection.

**B. Software-Focused Framework:**

- **Raw Data Acquisition:**
 - **ROS Nodes:** Three main ROS nodes manage multimodal data: a camera node (GoPro), a tracking node (T265), and a storage node that aggregates and synchronizes streams into HDF5 files.
 - **Data Sub-Sampling and Synchronization:** To handle differing sensor rates (e.g., T265 at 200 Hz, GoPro at 60 Hz), a unified ROS clock, multi-threaded buffering, and synchronized sub-sampling at the greatest common frequency (20 Hz) are employed. This ensures sub-millisecond offsets and data integrity.
 - **Accumulated Drift Correction for T265:** Strategies include reinitialization in a predefined pose and loop closure using a visually distinct reference region (a blue 3D-printed groove on the table). If loop closure is insufficient, reinitialization is required.

- **Raw Data Quality Assessment:**
 - **Sensor Confidence:** Utilizes T265's four confidence levels (Failed, Low, Medium, High), requiring 95% of samples to be High confidence. Low-confidence poses are excluded and interpolated.
 - **Trajectory Smoothness:** User-defined thresholds on velocity, acceleration, and relative orientation identify and flag abrupt transitions, ensuring data fidelity.

- **Data Preparation for Training:**
 - **Data Types:** Supports two main trajectory types: TCP trajectories (absolute and relative) and joint trajectories, generated from T265 pose estimates, robot URDF, and known offsets.
 - **Absolute TCP Trajectory:** Computes the end-effector (TCP) pose in the robot base frame by aligning the T265 coordinate system and incorporating the camera-to-gripper offset.
 - **Relative TCP Trajectory:** Derived from consecutive absolute TCP frames, representing relative motion. This reduces global reference dependency and improves generalization.
 - **Absolute Joint Trajectory:** Obtained by solving inverse kinematics (IK) for each absolute TCP pose using the robot's URDF, with the previous frame's solution as an initial guess for continuity.
 - **Continuous Gripper Width Computation:** A marker-based method (ArUco markers on gripper jaws) measures pixel distance, linearly mapping it to physical gripper opening width. This decouples software from mechanical structure, allowing compatibility with diverse gripper designs.

**C. Algorithmic Adaptations for FastUMI:**
Recognizing that hardware decoupling alone doesn't solve data distribution challenges inherent in FastUMI's first-person perspective, specific algorithmic enhancements are introduced.

- **Enhanced ACT for First-Person Perspectives:**
 - **Smooth-ACT (Local Temporal Smoothing):** Adds a GRU layer on top of the Transformer decoder to smooth abrupt joint transitions and reduce kinematically invalid actions by enforcing local continuity.
 - **PoseACT (End-Effector Pose Prediction):** Replaces absolute joint predictions with an end-effector (TCP) pose representation (absolute and relative). This improves platform independence, numerical stability, and robustness under limited first-person observations.

- **Depth-Enhanced Diffusion Policy (DP):**
 - Addresses DP's limitation in precise depth estimation by incorporating depth information.
 - Uses a **post-processing strategy** with **Depth Anything V2** to generate depth maps from GoPro frames, without adding hardware. Images are pre-processed (cropped and resized) to enhance depth estimation accuracy.
 - During training, single-channel depth data is expanded into three-channel pseudo-color maps and encoded alongside RGB images using a ViT-based CLIP visual encoder. Real-time inference achieves 20 Hz on an RTX 4090 GPU.

- **Dynamic Error-Compensation Algorithm:**
 - Mitigates TCP shifts in non-parallel-jaw grippers.
 - **Stage 1 (Compensation Distance):** Computes a dynamic compensation distance based on the measured gripper width and known max/min compensation distances.
 - **Stage 2 (Pose Correction):** Adjusts the commanded TCP position along the gripper's local Z-axis (determined by the TCP's orientation) before solving IK, effectively offsetting forward motion caused by jaw closure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate FastUMI's effectiveness in data collection and its impact on robotic policy learning.

- **Data Quality:**
 - Comparison between RealSense T265 and RoboBaton MINI:
 - **T265:** Achieved lower average positioning error (10.5 mm) in low-occlusion tasks ("Pick Cup") but degraded significantly with partial ("Open Container," 17.7 mm) and substantial ("Rearrange Coke," 20-36 mm) occlusions. This highlights its susceptibility to close-range visual obstruction.
 - **MINI:** Showed more stable performance across tasks (15.2 mm for "Pick Cup," 10-12 mm for "Open Container") but with slightly reduced accuracy in low-occlusion scenarios.
 - The VIO error for T265 typically shows low error at trajectory start/end (due to loop closure mechanisms recovering accuracy) but noticeable peaks during intermediate movement, especially when the gripper occludes visual features. This validates the need for drift correction and robust tracking modules.

- **Baseline Performance (ACT vs. DP):**
 - Both ACT (Absolute Joint) and Diffusion Policy (DP, Relative TCP) achieved relatively high success rates across 12 diverse manipulation tasks using 200 training demonstrations, suggesting the FastUMI dataset's generalizability and diversity.
 - **DP's Limitation:** Struggled with tasks requiring high precision in depth estimation, such as "Open Drawer," "Pick Lid," and especially "Open Ricecooker" (20% success rate), indicating inadequacy without explicit depth information.
 - **ACT's Limitation:** Showed susceptibility to generating illicit joint configurations unseen in training, particularly in tasks like "Pick Bear" (20% success) and "Pick Pen" (20% success), where it produced kinematically invalid poses. It also faced workspace mismatch issues in tasks like "Sweep Trash" (6.67% success) due to absolute joint predictions exceeding the robot's operational envelope.

- **Algorithmic Enhancements:**
 - **Depth-Enhanced DP:** Significantly improved success rates in depth-sensitive tasks: "Pick Lid" increased from 53.33% to 80.00% (+26.67%), and "Open Ricecooker" surged from 20.00% to 93.33% (+73.33%). This strongly validates the importance of depth cues for precision.
 - **Enhanced ACT (Smooth-ACT & PoseACT):**
 - **Smooth-ACT:** Improved "Pick Bear" from 20.00% to 60.00% and "Sweep Trash" from 6.67% to 26.67% by enforcing temporal continuity.
 - **PoseACT (Absolute TCP):** Further boosted "Pick Bear" to 80.00% and "Sweep Trash" to 53.33%, demonstrating the benefits of end-effector pose prediction.
 - **PoseACT (Relative TCP):** Performed well on repetitive tasks like "Sweep Trash" (60.00%) but showed a slight drop in "Pick Bear" (73.33%) compared to absolute PoseACT, indicating a trade-off for tasks requiring precise height.

- **Additional Factors:**
 - **Camera Configurations:** A fisheye lens at the end-effector (FastUMI's GoPro setup) achieved performance comparable to multi-view setups in "Pick Bear" (80%) and "Open Container" (100%), highlighting its ability to capture rich contextual information despite being a single-view system. In contrast, a D435i with a narrow FOV or a GoPro with a flat lens (both first-person) performed poorly (0-6.67% success rates).
 - **Training Dataset Size:** Increasing the dataset size from 200 to 400 and then to 800 demonstrations significantly improved success rates in the "Pick Cup" task (20% to 26.67% to 53.33%). This confirms that data abundance enhances the model's generalization capabilities across varied object placements and orientations.

- **Open-Sourced Dataset:** The paper announces the open-sourcing of over **10,000 real-world demonstration trajectories** spanning **22 everyday tasks**, 19 object categories, and 12 manipulation skills. This constitutes one of the most comprehensive UMI-like datasets in terms of task variety, including scenarios with visual occlusions, which are crucial for training robust policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FastUMI represents a significant step forward in making real-world robotic manipulation data collection more accessible, efficient, and scalable. Its impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Democratization of Robotic Data Collection:** By fundamentally decoupling hardware dependencies and simplifying the software pipeline (replacing complex VIO with the plug-and-play T265), FastUMI drastically reduces the cost, complexity, and specialized expertise required to collect high-quality robotic demonstration data. This makes advanced robotic research more accessible to a broader range of academic institutions and industry players, fostering innovation beyond well-resourced labs.
- **Acceleration of Data-Driven Robotic Learning:** The simplified deployment and robust performance mean that researchers can collect large volumes of diverse, high-fidelity data much faster. This directly addresses the "scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, real-world interaction data," which is identified as a major bottleneck for developing generalist robot action policies. More data, collected more efficiently, translates to faster progress in training more capable and generalizable robot behaviors.
- **Enhanced Policy Robustness and Generalization:**
 - The hardware design ensures consistent observation perspectives between human demonstrations and robot execution, minimizing domain shift and facilitating seamless policy transfer.
 - The robust pose tracking (T265) and continuous gripper width computation provide reliable state information, even in challenging real-world conditions with occlusions.
 - The algorithmic adaptations (Smooth-ACT, PoseACT, Depth-Enhanced DP, dynamic error compensation) specifically address the unique challenges of first-person view data, such as kinematic feasibility and depth perception, leading to more stable and accurate robot execution.
- **Valuable Open-Source Resource:** The release of the FastUMI Dataset, comprising over 10,000 diverse real-world trajectories across 22 everyday tasks, is a substantial contribution to the research community. This dataset, especially with its inclusion of occlusion scenarios, will serve as a crucial benchmark and training resource for developing and evaluating next-generation imitation learning algorithms and foundation models for robotics. It enables standardized comparisons and accelerates research that previously might have been hampered by the need to collect custom datasets.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** FastUMI's modular and extensible design sets the stage for future advancements. Its identified limitations, such as restricted sensing modalities (e.g., lack of tactile feedback) and compatibility with complex robot morphologies (e.g., mobile manipulators), clearly define promising avenues for follow-up research. The system's robustness to different camera models (T265 vs. MINI) also suggests a path towards hardware agnosticism and continued improvement with sensor technology.

In conclusion, FastUMI significantly advances the practical aspects of data collection for robot manipulation. By lowering the barrier to entry and providing a high-quality, open-source dataset, it is poised to have a lasting impact on the development of more intelligent, versatile, and autonomous robotic systems.
