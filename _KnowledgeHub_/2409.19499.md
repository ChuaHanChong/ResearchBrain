# FastUMI: A Scalable and Hardware-Independent Universal Manipulation Interface with Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> FastUMI, developed by Shanghai AI Lab, redesigns the Universal Manipulation Interface to enable scalable and hardware-independent collection of high-quality, real-world robotic manipulation data. It achieves this through decoupled hardware, a streamlined software framework, and algorithmic enhancements tailored for first-person data, providing over 10,000 demonstration trajectories across 22 tasks.

## Problem

- The scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, real-world interaction data impedes the development of generalist action policies for robots.
- Existing data collection methods like teleoperation are labor-intensive and costly, while vision-driven approaches often lack fine-grained interaction dynamics.
- The original Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) suffered from tight hardware dependency on specific robotic components and software instability due to complex, occlusion-prone Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) pipelines.

## Method

- Introduces a decoupled hardware design for both handheld and robot-mounted devices, utilizing a RealSense T265 for robust pose tracking and modular, ISO-standard compatible components for broad platform compatibility.
- Develops a software framework with ROS nodes for synchronized multimodal data acquisition, quality assessment, and flexible data preparation supporting both TCP and joint trajectory generation.
- Implements algorithmic adaptations, including Smooth-ACT and PoseACT for first-person perspectives, Depth-Enhanced Diffusion Policy using Depth Anything V2 for improved depth perception, and a dynamic error-compensation algorithm for non-parallel-jaw grippers.

## Results

- RealSense T265 achieved 10.5 mm average positioning error in low-occlusion tasks, with alternative tracking modules showing more stable performance across varying occlusions.
- Depth-Enhanced Diffusion Policy significantly improved success rates on depth-sensitive tasks, with "Open Ricecooker" increasing from 20% to 93.33%.
- Enhanced ACT (Smooth-ACT and PoseACT) boosted success rates on kinematically challenging tasks (e.g., "Pick Bear" improved from 20% to 80% with PoseACT).
- Over 10,000 real-world demonstration trajectories spanning 22 everyday tasks, 19 object categories, and 12 manipulation skills were open-sourced.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling hardware dependencies and simplifying software integration significantly lowers the barrier to entry for collecting high-quality robotic manipulation data.
> - Robust pose tracking and consistent visual perspective between human demonstrations and robot execution are critical for effective policy transfer and reducing domain shift.
> - Augmenting first-person visual policies with depth information and kinematically aware action representations (e.g., end-effector pose, local smoothing) can substantially improve performance and robustness.
