# Chameleon: Mixed-Modal Early-Fusion Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FAIR at Meta introduces Chameleon, an early-fusion, token-based mixed-modal foundation model designed to unify understanding and generation across images, text, and code. The 34 billion parameter model outperforms Llama-2 on text-only tasks, achieves state-of-the-art performance on image captioning, and demonstrates superior human preference for long-form, interleaved mixed-modal generation compared to augmented GPT-4V and Gemini Pro.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal models often employ 'late-fusion' approaches, limiting deep integration and seamless generation of complex, interleaved text and image content.
- Scaling early-fusion token-based multimodal models historically presented significant technical challenges related to optimization stability and training at large scales.
- A lack of truly unified models capable of understanding and generating arbitrary, interleaved sequences of images and text within a single architecture.

## Method

- Develops a family of ==early-fusion, token-based== mixed-modal Transformer models (up to 34 billion parameters) that project all modalities (images, text, code) into a single, ==shared token space==.
- Introduces architectural modifications such as ==Query-Key Normalization (QK-Norm)== and revised Layer Norm placement, along with ==Z-loss regularization==, to ensure training stability at scale.
- Pre-trains the model on 9.2 trillion tokens across two stages, leveraging diverse text-only, text-image, and interleaved text/image datasets, followed by a supervised fine-tuning stage with carefully curated high-quality and safety data.

## Results

- Chameleon-34B outperforms Llama-2-70B on **5** out of **8** text-only reasoning and comprehension benchmarks and is competitive with models like Mixtral 8x7B and Gemini Pro.
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on MS-COCO image captioning and is a leading performer on Flickr30k, outperforming larger late-fusion models like Flamingo and IDEFICS.
- In human evaluations for long-form mixed-modal generation, Chameleon-34B was preferred over augmented GPT-4V+ in **51.6%** of comparisons and over augmented Gemini+ in **60.4%** of comparisons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Early-fusion, token-based architectures can be successfully scaled to large foundation models by introducing specific architectural innovations and training stability techniques.
> - A single, unified model can achieve competitive or state-of-the-art performance across diverse unimodal (text) and multimodal (image-to-text, mixed-modal generation) tasks simultaneously.
> - Human evaluations are critical for assessing novel, open-ended mixed-modal reasoning and generation capabilities, as current benchmarks do not fully capture these complex interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Chameleon emerges at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of multimodal foundation models. Traditionally, AI models have excelled in single modalities (e.g., text-only Large Language Models or vision models). Recent advancements have aimed to bridge these modalities, leading to "multimodal" models capable of processing and generating information across different data types.

This work specifically addresses a critical architectural choice in multimodal learning: **fusion strategy**.
- **Late-Fusion Models:** Many existing prominent multimodal models, such as Flamingo, LLaVA, IDEFICS, and VisualGPT, employ a "late-fusion" approach. This means they typically use separate encoders for each modality (e.g., a vision encoder for images and a text encoder for text) and combine the representations at a later stage for joint reasoning or generation. While effective, this can limit their ability to deeply integrate information across modalities and generate truly interleaved content.
- **Early-Fusion Models (Chameleon's Approach):** Chameleon champions an "early-fusion" paradigm, where all modalities (images, text, and code) are projected into a single, shared, token-based representational space from the very beginning. By quantizing images into discrete tokens, analogous to words, the same transformer architecture can process sequences of both image and text tokens uniformly. This approach enables "seamless reasoning and generation across modalities" and allows for the modeling of "arbitrary sequences of images and text," a key differentiator.

Chameleon builds upon the lineage of **token-based multimodal learning**. This concept was explored in works like BEiT (for self-supervised vision learning) and later extended to mixed-modal documents with CM3 (Aghajanyan et al., 2022) and scaled up for autoregressive text-to-image generation with CM3Leon (Yu et al., 2023). Chameleon takes this token-based, early-fusion concept further by applying it to an end-to-end, dense transformer architecture designed for both understanding and generation of *full multimodal documents*.

The closest contemporary model mentioned in terms of fusion strategy is Google's Gemini (Gemini et al., 2023), which also uses an early-fusion token-based approach. However, Chameleon distinguishes itself by employing a single, dense model without any separate image decoders or routing components, aiming for a more general-purpose and unified model for both multimodal understanding and generation.

Furthermore, Chameleon's development reflects the broader trend in AI of scaling foundation models (akin to the Llama series) and enhancing their capabilities through **supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or alignment techniques**. The paper discusses adapting SFT approaches from text-only LLMs to the mixed-modal setting, demonstrating how alignment recipes are crucial for robust, high-quality, and safe model behavior at scale.

In summary, Chameleon pushes the boundaries of unified multimodal AI, demonstrating that a meticulously engineered early-fusion, token-based autoregressive transformer can achieve broad capabilities, setting a new benchmark for flexible and integrated multimodal content generation and understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Chameleon project is to develop a family of **early-fusion, token-based mixed-modal foundation models** capable of understanding and generating images and text in *any arbitrary sequence*. This ambition is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging Modalities for Full Document Modeling:** Existing multimodal models often treat different modalities somewhat separately, using modality-specific encoders or decoders. This limits their ability to deeply integrate information and fluidly generate complex, interleaved multimodal documents. Chameleon aims to overcome this by being "mixed-modal from inception," allowing for a direct generalization of current unimodal and bimodal (e.g., image-to-text, text-to-image) tasks towards the comprehensive modeling of full documents that can contain arbitrary sequences of text and images.
- **Unified Architecture and Training:** The goal is to achieve this unified capability using a uniform transformer architecture trained end-to-end from scratch on an interleaved mixture of all modalities (images, text, and code). This contrasts with approaches that combine pre-trained unimodal components.
- **Addressing Early-Fusion Challenges at Scale:** While the early-fusion token-based approach offers theoretical advantages, it historically presents significant technical hurdles, particularly concerning optimization stability and scaling to large model sizes and vast datasets. A core motivation was to introduce novel architectural innovations and training techniques to overcome these challenges, enabling stable training for models up to 34 billion parameters on trillions of tokens.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art and Broad Capabilities:** The researchers sought to demonstrate that a single Chameleon model could achieve competitive or state-of-the-art performance across a diverse set of tasks, including visual question answering, image captioning, text generation, and image generation, without sacrificing performance in any single domain.
- **Unlocking New Mixed-Modal Reasoning and Generation:** Beyond standard benchmarks, a crucial motivation was to enable and evaluate entirely new capabilities in long-form, open-ended mixed-modal reasoning and generation, where prompts and outputs can dynamically interleave text and images. This required developing new human evaluation methodologies to assess model quality in these novel interaction scenarios.
- **Safety and Alignment:** As with any large-scale AI model, ensuring the generation of safe and aligned content was a significant motivation, leading to the development of a dedicated alignment recipe and extensive safety testing, including with mixed-modal prompts to address potential new attack vectors.

In essence, Chameleon's objectives reflect a pursuit of truly general-purpose multimodal AI that can interact with and create content across modalities as seamlessly as humans do with complex documents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Chameleon's methodology is characterized by a "from inception" mixed-modal design, emphasizing a uniform architecture and stable training at scale.

#### 3.1. Architecture

The core architecture largely follows the Transformer design, specifically drawing inspiration from LLaMa-2, utilizing RMSNorm, SwiGLU activation, and Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE). However, significant modifications were introduced to address stability issues inherent in mixed-modal training:
- **Query-Key Normalization (QK-Norm):** Layer normalization is applied to query and key vectors within the attention mechanism to directly control norm growth, which was identified as crucial for stability, especially with varying entropy across modalities. This prevents logit drift.
- **Revised Layer Norm Placement:** For the larger Chameleon-34B, a norm reordering strategy similar to Swin Transformer V2 (Liu et al., 2022) was adopted. This bounds norm growth within the feed-forward block, which is critical given the multiplicative nature of SwiGLU.
- **Dropout:** Dropout (0.1) was used for Chameleon-7B for stability, but Chameleon-34B was trained without dropout when norm-reordering was applied.

#### 3.2. Tokenization

- **Image Tokenization:** A new image tokenizer, based on Gafni et al. (2022), converts 512x512 images into 1024 discrete tokens from an 8192-token codebook. Licensed images were used for training, with faces up-sampled. A noted weakness is text-heavy images (OCR).
- **Text Tokenization:** A new BPE tokenizer (Sennrich et al., 2016) with a vocabulary size of 65,536 was trained, explicitly including the 8192 image codebook tokens to create a unified token space.

#### 3.3. Pre-Training Data

Chameleon's training spans 9.2 trillion tokens over 2.1 epochs, segmented into two stages:
- **First Stage (80%):** Primarily unsupervised, large-scale datasets.
 - **Text-Only:** 2.9 trillion tokens, combining pre-training data from LLaMa-2 and CodeLLaMa.
 - **Text-Image:** 1.5 trillion tokens (from 1.4 billion pairs) of public and licensed text-image data.
 - **Text/Image Interleaved:** 400 billion tokens from publicly available web sources (similar to Obelisc), explicitly designed to contain arbitrary sequences of text and images. During training, 50% of Text-To-Image pairs had the image precede the text (captioning).
- **Second Stage (20%):** Lower weight on first-stage data, mixed with higher-quality datasets and instruction tuning sets.

#### 3.4. Optimization and Stability

- **Optimizer:** AdamW with standard parameters.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** Linear warm-up followed by exponential decay.
- **Regularization:** Weight decay (0.1), global gradient clipping (1.0).
- **Z-loss Regularization:** Applied to the final softmax to prevent logit shift, crucial for stability in both model sizes, particularly for Chameleon-34B which did not use dropout.
- **Batch Sizes:** Global batch sizes of approximately 8M tokens for Chameleon-7B and 12M tokens for Chameleon-34B.

#### 3.5. Alignment (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT)

A lightweight SFT stage was crucial for alignment and user-facing capabilities.
- **Data Curation:** Carefully curated high-quality datasets spanning multiple categories:
 - **Text & Code SFT:** Inherited from LLaMa-2 and CodeLLaMa.
 - **Image Generation SFT:** 64K aesthetically filtered (aesthetic classifier score ≥ 6) licensed images.
 - **Visual Chat & Interleaved Text/Image Generation SFT:** High-quality data collected via third-party vendors, without Meta user data, focusing on diverse, open-ended mixed-modal interactions.
 - **Safety Data:** A broad collection of prompts (text-only and mixed-modal) designed to provoke unsafe content, paired with refusal responses, covering sensitive topics and multimodal attack vectors.
- **Fine-Tuning Strategy:** Cosine learning rate, weight decay, batch size 128 (for 4096 tokens sequences). Autoregressive objective with masked loss for prompt tokens. Image handling: border padding for prompt images (retaining info), center-cropping for answer images (visual quality). Data balancing across modalities was found to be critical to prevent biased generation.

#### 3.6. Inference

A standalone PyTorch-based inference pipeline leveraging xformers kernels was built to handle the unique challenges of mixed-modal autoregressive generation:
- **Data-dependencies per-step:** Tokens must be inspected at each step for modality-dependent decoding.
- **Masking:** Modality-constrained generation (e.g., image-only) requires masking tokens outside the desired modality space.
- **Fixed-sized image units:** Image generation produces fixed blocks of tokens, unlike variable-length text. The pipeline supports streaming and fuzed generation of image token blocks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Chameleon's evaluation demonstrates its broad and general capabilities across a diverse range of tasks, particularly its strength in unified mixed-modal generation and understanding.

#### 4.1. Text-Only Capabilities

- **Performance:** Chameleon-34B *outperforms* Llama-2 (including the 70B variant on 5/8 tasks) across various commonsense reasoning and reading comprehension benchmarks (PIQA, SIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-E/C, OBQA, BoolQ). It also shows competitive performance with models like Mixtral 8x7B and Gemini-Pro in these areas.
- **Math and World Knowledge:** On math benchmarks (GSM8k, MATH), Chameleon models show strong capabilities, often outperforming Llama-2 and being competitive with or exceeding Mistral/Mixtral. For MMLU (world knowledge), Chameleon-34B outperforms Llama-2 counterparts and approaches Mixtral 8x7B/Gemini-Pro performance.
- **Factors for Gains:** The paper attributes these text-only gains to factors like more training epochs over LLaMa-2 data, the inclusion of code data in pre-training (improving reasoning), and the use of higher-quality data in the latter stages of pre-training.

#### 4.2. Image-to-Text Capabilities

- **Image Captioning:** Chameleon-34B achieves **state-of-the-art** performance on MS-COCO and Flickr30k image captioning benchmarks.
 - The 2-shot pre-trained Chameleon-34B *outperforms* larger 80B late-fusion models like Flamingo and IDEFICS on COCO and matches them on Flickr30k.
 - Fine-tuned Chameleon-34B (SFT and MultiTask variants) *outperforms all other models* on COCO and is a leading performer on Flickr30k, including closed-source models like GPT-4V and Gemini Pro (with significantly fewer in-context examples).
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** On VQA-v2, the pre-trained Chameleon-34B (2-shot) matches the 32-shot performance of larger Flamingo and IDEFICS models. The fine-tuned Chameleon-34B-Multitask approaches IDEFICS-80B-Instruct and Gemini Pro, though it trails some larger models like Flamingo-80B-FT, GPT-4V, and Gemini Ultra.

#### 4.3. Long-Form Mixed-Modal Generation (Human Evaluations - A Key Differentiator)

This is a novel and highly emphasized aspect, as existing benchmarks do not fully capture Chameleon's unique mixed-modal capabilities.
- **Evaluation Setup:** A large-scale human evaluation was conducted using a new dataset of 1,048 diverse, natural prompts (42.1% mixed-modal, 57.9% text-only) expecting mixed-modal outputs. Chameleon-34B was compared against OpenAI GPT-4V and Google Gemini Pro (both original text-only responses and "augmented" versions, GPT-4V+ and Gemini+, where image captions were generated and then converted to images via DALL-E 3).
- **Absolute Evaluation (Task Fulfillment):** Chameleon's responses were rated as "completely fulfilling" the task in 55.2% of cases, *substantially outperforming* Gemini+ (37.6%) and GPT-4V+ (44.7%). Its performance was even more dominant against the original text-only Gemini (17.6%) and GPT-4V (23.1%).
- **Relative Evaluation (Pairwise Preference):** In direct human preference comparisons:
 - Chameleon achieved a **60.4% preference rate (win + 0.5 tie)** against Gemini+ (41.5% win, 34.5% tie, 24.0% loss).
 - Chameleon achieved a **51.6% preference rate** against GPT-4V+ (35.8% win, 31.6% tie, 32.6% loss).
 - Its dominance was even greater against the original text-only models (69.1% vs. Gemini, 61.7% vs. GPT-4V).
- **Inter-annotator Agreement:** Agreement levels were high for objective questions (e.g., safety) and reasonable for subjective ones (e.g., task fulfillment), validating the human evaluation methodology.

#### 4.4. Safety Testing

- Chameleon demonstrated a high degree of safety. In testing with 20,000 crowdsourced prompts, only 0.095% of Chameleon-34B's responses were deemed unsafe. In 445 adversarial "red team" interactions, only 1.6% were unsafe. This indicates that the safety alignment efforts were largely successful in preventing the generation of harmful content.

In summary, Chameleon-34B establishes itself as a highly capable, general-purpose foundation model that not only performs competitively or achieves SOTA on traditional unimodal and image-to-text benchmarks but also excels in novel, complex mixed-modal generation tasks, where it surpasses strong commercial baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Chameleon represents a pivotal advancement in multimodal AI, with significant implications for research and potential real-world applications:

- **Realization of Unified Multimodal Modeling:** The most significant contribution is demonstrating the viability and high performance of a truly unified, early-fusion, token-based foundation model. By treating all modalities (text, images, code) as discrete tokens within a single, end-to-end transformer architecture, Chameleon moves beyond the limitations of separate encoders or late-fusion approaches. This marks a "significant step forward in a unified modeling of full multimodal documents," pushing towards the vision of AI systems that can reason and generate content as flexibly as humans across interwoven modalities.

- **Validation of Early-Fusion at Scale:** Historically, early-fusion models have struggled with stability and scalability. Chameleon's detailed architectural innovations (QK-Norm, revised layer norm placement) and training techniques (z-loss regularization, data balancing) demonstrate how these challenges can be overcome at large scales (34 billion parameters, 9.2 trillion tokens). This provides a strong blueprint and encourages further research into the early-fusion paradigm, potentially making it the preferred approach for next-generation multimodal models.

- **Unlocking Novel Multimodal Interactions:** Chameleon's prowess in generating long-form, interleaved text and image content, as evidenced by its strong performance in human evaluations against GPT-4V and Gemini Pro, opens up entirely new possibilities for human-AI interaction. This goes beyond simple image captioning or visual question answering, enabling rich, dynamic "conversations" where the AI can seamlessly generate explanations, stories, or instructions accompanied by relevant images, or analyze image-rich prompts. This capability could transform how users interact with AI assistants, content creation tools, and educational platforms.

- **Benchmarking and Evaluation Innovation:** The introduction of a large-scale human evaluation for open-ended mixed-modal reasoning and generation is itself a significant contribution. It highlights the inadequacy of existing static benchmarks for assessing advanced multimodal capabilities and provides a crucial methodology for future research in this area.

- **Performance Leadership Across Modalities:** The fact that a single Chameleon model achieves state-of-the-art in image captioning, outperforms Llama-2 in text-only tasks while being competitive with larger LLMs like Mixtral 8x7B and Gemini-Pro, and performs non-trivial image generation, underscores its exceptional generality. This multi-competence in a single model is a testament to the power of the unified token-based approach and efficient training strategies.

- **Advancing Responsible AI:** The detailed safety testing, including the focus on mixed-modal prompts and red teaming, demonstrates a commitment to responsible AI development. Ensuring the model generates safe and aligned content is critical for real-world deployment and builds trust in these powerful new capabilities.

Overall, Chameleon sets a "new bar for open multimodal foundation models," not only through its impressive empirical results but also by providing a robust architectural and training blueprint for future research. Its potential impact lies in paving the way for more intuitive, comprehensive, and powerful AI systems that can seamlessly understand and generate the complex, multimodal information that characterizes human communication and documentation.
