# Surface-Based Manipulation with Modular Foldable Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A modular robotic system from EPFL employs surface-based manipulation to address the challenges of handling fragile, deformable, or irregularly shaped objects. Composed of two foldable units each with three degrees of freedom, the system executes fundamental tasks like translation at 5.6 cm/s, rotation at 1.16 °/s, and 180° flipping, and can even manipulate the shape of deformable materials such as putty strips.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic grasping struggles with fragile, deformable, irregularly shaped, or slippery objects due to difficulties in establishing stable forces or geometric constraints.
- Existing surface-based manipulation methods often face limitations in workspace, speed, force output, or scalability, requiring a high number of actuators or degrees of freedom per unit.
- Many current nonprehensile systems lack robust closed-loop control, focus on a limited range of rigid objects, or restrict control over contact forces with single-surface approaches.

## Method

- Introduced surface-based manipulation strategies using two modular, foldable robots, each providing three degrees of freedom (roll, pitch, height) via an origami-based design.
- Implemented a hierarchical closed-loop control architecture, integrating real-time object tracking (motion capture or webcam vision) with a state machine, inverse kinematics, and PID motor controllers.
- Developed specific high-level manipulation strategies for translation (using inclination and height oscillations), rotation (via repeated contact breaking and torque application), and flipping (through rapid impulse delivery).

## Results

- Successfully translated soft Play-Doh objects at average velocities of **5.62** cm/s (X) and **5.55** cm/s (Y), with steady-state errors around 1 cm.
- Demonstrated 180° flipping of a rigid cuboid and rotation of a square prism at an average rotational velocity of **1.16** °/s.
- Achieved sequential shape manipulation of a deformable putty strip, transforming its length and form by combining flipping and translation strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimalist flat surfaces can effectively perform dexterous manipulation, diverging from traditional grasping and reducing stress on fragile or deformable objects.
> - Complex manipulation tasks like translation, rotation, and flipping are achievable with a low minimum mobility requirement (MR_min = 6 DoF for two 3-DoF units) through coordinated bimanual surface interaction.
> - The modular and reconfigurable design allows for adaptability to diverse object properties and offers scalability, enabling the potential for shape manipulation of deformable objects.
