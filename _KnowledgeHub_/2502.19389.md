# Surface-Based Manipulation with Modular Foldable Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A modular robotic system from EPFL employs surface-based manipulation to address the challenges of handling fragile, deformable, or irregularly shaped objects. Composed of two foldable units each with three degrees of freedom, the system executes fundamental tasks like translation at 5.6 cm/s, rotation at 1.16 °/s, and 180° flipping, and can even manipulate the shape of deformable materials such as putty strips.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic grasping struggles with fragile, deformable, irregularly shaped, or slippery objects due to difficulties in establishing stable forces or geometric constraints.
- Existing surface-based manipulation methods often face limitations in workspace, speed, force output, or scalability, requiring a high number of actuators or degrees of freedom per unit.
- Many current nonprehensile systems lack robust closed-loop control, focus on a limited range of rigid objects, or restrict control over contact forces with single-surface approaches.

## Method

- Introduced surface-based manipulation strategies using two modular, foldable robots, each providing three degrees of freedom (roll, pitch, height) via an origami-based design.
- Implemented a hierarchical closed-loop control architecture, integrating real-time object tracking (motion capture or webcam vision) with a state machine, inverse kinematics, and PID motor controllers.
- Developed specific high-level manipulation strategies for translation (using inclination and height oscillations), rotation (via repeated contact breaking and torque application), and flipping (through rapid impulse delivery).

## Results

- Successfully translated soft Play-Doh objects at average velocities of **5.62** cm/s (X) and **5.55** cm/s (Y), with steady-state errors around 1 cm.
- Demonstrated 180° flipping of a rigid cuboid and rotation of a square prism at an average rotational velocity of **1.16** °/s.
- Achieved sequential shape manipulation of a deformable putty strip, transforming its length and form by combining flipping and translation strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimalist flat surfaces can effectively perform dexterous manipulation, diverging from traditional grasping and reducing stress on fragile or deformable objects.
> - Complex manipulation tasks like translation, rotation, and flipping are achievable with a low minimum mobility requirement (MR_min = 6 DoF for two 3-DoF units) through coordinated bimanual surface interaction.
> - The modular and reconfigurable design allows for adaptability to diverse object properties and offers scalability, enabling the potential for shape manipulation of deformable objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation traditionally relies on finger-shaped end effectors to establish stable grasps through mechanical interlocking, friction, or adhesion. While these methods are effective for many tasks, they encounter significant challenges when dealing with objects that are fragile, deformable, irregularly shaped, or slippery. These challenges stem from the difficulty in applying stable forces without causing damage or in establishing reliable geometric constraints.

Previous research has attempted to address these limitations through various advancements:
- **Soft robotic grippers:** Designed with mechanical compliance to handle fragile objects.
- **Granular jamming grippers:** Conform to arbitrary shapes for pick-and-place operations.
- **Tactile sensing:** Enhances real-time adjustments for delicate items.
- **Reconfigurable surfaces:** Devices that dynamically alter their surface geometries through actuation. These include grids of individually actuated parallel robots, origami-inspired structures, and modular designs with soft or origami-inspired actuators.
- **Nonprehensile manipulation:** Strategies that impose unilateral constraints on an object without grasping it, such as using paddle-like end-effectors, dual "palms," or vibrating surfaces.

Despite these developments, existing solutions often present their own limitations. Many still depend on stable grasps that match object size, struggle with highly variable objects, or are confined to specific hardware platforms. Reconfigurable surfaces often suffer from limited workspace, slow deformation speeds, reduced force output, and challenges in scaling due to the large number of modules required for a single object. Furthermore, many current nonprehensile systems lack closed-loop control, focus on a limited range of rigid objects, or rely on single-surface methods which restrict control over contact forces and scalability.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a modular, surface-based manipulation approach that explicitly moves away from traditional grasping. It identifies a need for novel design and actuation methodologies to overcome the trade-off between the number of required modules and the degrees of freedom (DoF) per unit in surface-based manipulation. By focusing on fundamental motion primitives (translation, rotation, flipping) using minimalist flat surfaces and a low-DoF modular setup, the authors aim to provide a more dexterous, adaptable, and scalable solution for challenging manipulation tasks, especially involving deformable and irregularly shaped objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from observations of human manipulation and limitations in conventional robotic grasping. Humans frequently resort to nonprehensile, surface-based manipulation when traditional grasping methods are ineffective, such as using a spoon for mashed potatoes or supporting a slippery fish from underneath. This suggests that adapting robot morphology to planar forms could offer a robust solution for handling difficult objects.

Specifically, the paper identifies several key motivations:
- **Addressing limitations of conventional grasping:** Traditional grippers struggle with fragile, deformable, irregularly shaped, or slippery objects due to difficulties in establishing stable forces or geometric constraints.
- **Overcoming limitations of existing surface-based methods:** Current reconfigurable surfaces often face issues like limited workspace, slow deformation speeds, insufficient force output, and complex scalability due to a high number of required actuators (mobility requirement, MR_min) or degrees of freedom per unit (DoF_SU).
- **Developing a comprehensive framework:** The authors aim to analyze and compare existing surface-based manipulation platforms to identify optimal configurations regarding DoF_SU and module count. They introduce a scaling model (Equation 1) to standardize analysis and highlight the trade-off between module complexity and quantity.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
- To present **surface-based manipulation strategies** that utilize flat surfaces as minimalist end-effectors, diverging from classical grasping approaches.
- To enable fundamental manipulation tasks: **translation, rotation, and flipping** of objects.
- To demonstrate the adaptability of these strategies to objects of **various shapes, sizes, and stiffness levels**, including non-rigid and irregularly shaped items, and even enabling the manipulation of the shape of deformable objects.
- To implement these strategies using **modular and foldable robots** with a decoupled actuation procedure and a hierarchical closed-loop control architecture, ensuring hardware adaptability.
- To achieve **dexterous manipulation tasks with a lower minimum mobility requirement (MR_min)** compared to other surface-based methods by using two units, each with three DoF, leveraging their coordinated interaction.
- To provide a platform that supports **closed-loop control and quantitative evaluation** for diverse object forms.
- To explore the potential of combining different manipulation strategies to **alter the shape of flexible and deformable objects**, expanding robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a system for surface-based manipulation using modular, foldable robots, supported by integrated perception and a hierarchical control system.

**Robot Design and Actuation:**
The reconfigurable surface consists of two identical robotic modules placed side-by-side. Each module is designed using an origami-based approach, specifically drawing from the waterbomb origami fold and the Canfield joint principle, to provide three degrees of freedom (DoF) in a compact form. The five main components of each module are:
- **Surface platform:** An acrylic sheet serving as the object-contact interface.
- **Origami legs:** Constructed from a polyimide layer sandwiched between two FR4 layers, providing reconfigurability.
- **Actuators:** Direct-driven BLDC motors (maxon EC-i 40), each generating up to 207mN.m nominal torque, linked via 3D-printed PLA connectors.
- **Base platform:** An acrylic sheet for securing the module.
- **Control hardware:** A STSPIN32F0A module for low-level motor control and a Teensy MicroMod as the main controller for high-level operations and communication via MODBUS RS-485.

**Integrated Perception System:**
To track object pose and module surface positions in real-time, two perception methods are employed:
- **For nearly-rigid objects:** A motion capture setup with six Vicon Vero cameras tracks markers placed on the objects. Data is transmitted via I2C at 100Hz.
- **For deformable/non-rigid objects:** A custom image processing setup using a Logitech C270 webcam and OpenCV detects the object's geometrical center and boundaries.

**Control System Overview:**
A hierarchical closed-loop control architecture is implemented, designed for adaptability across different robotic platforms with roll, pitch, and height DoF. It comprises five main components:
1. **Task Objectives:** Predefined goals that manage manipulation strategy transitions.
2. **State Machine (Task Planner):** Dynamically transitions between three modes: translation, rotation, and flipping. It generates surface roll, pitch, and height references based on real-time vision data.
3. **Inverse Kinematics Algorithm:** Converts the desired surface pose (orientation and height) into corresponding joint angles for each module.
4. **PID-based Servo Motor Controller:** A low-level controller that tracks the computed joint angle commands, implemented with a cascade control structure (feedforward component, current loop within the position loop) for BLDC motors.
5. **Vision System:** Provides real-time feedback on the object's position and orientation.

**High-Level Manipulation Strategies:**

- **Translation:**
 - **Principle:** Moves an object from an initial to a target position, suitable for objects fitting on a single surface.
 - **Mechanism:** A PID controller computes reference pitch and roll angles based on X and Y positional errors.
 - **Friction management:** For friction coefficients exceeding 0.33, the strategy modulates the surface's height using vertical oscillations (H = A sin(ωt) + H_init) to reduce frictional resistance and apply periodic impulses.
 - **Control flow:** Continuously adjusts surface inclination and height until the object's centroid aligns with the target.

- **Rotation:**
 - **Principle:** Reorients an object around its Z-axis by repeatedly establishing and breaking contact between the object and two surfaces.
 - **Mechanism:** A PID controller modulates the roll angle of one surface (Surface 2) based on the object's yaw error to induce torque. The pitch angle of the other surface (Surface 1) is adjusted to incline towards the center, and its roll by a fixed angle to facilitate rotation.
 - **Control flow:** Cycles involve adjusting surface angles to exert torque, then returning to a neutral state to establish new contact points for subsequent rotations, repeating until the target angle is achieved.

- **Flipping:**
 - **Principle:** Flips an object 180° around a horizontal axis by delivering a substantial impulse.
 - **Mechanism:** The object is positioned with its center of gravity on one surface (Surface 1) but in contact with both. The second surface (Surface 2) rapidly elevates with an initial angular tilt relative to the Y-axis, exerting continuous force on one edge of the object.
 - **Success criteria:** For a successful flip, the object's angular velocity at the point of separation from Surface 2 must be sufficient to overcome its gravitational potential energy (Equation 8). The strategy focuses on optimizing the linear velocity and maximizing the acceleration of Surface 2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validated the proposed surface-based manipulation strategies through both open-loop teleoperated demonstrations and quantitative, vision-based closed-loop experiments.

**Generalizability (Open-Loop Teleoperation):**
Initial open-loop experiments demonstrated the broad applicability of the surface-based approach. Diverse objects and substances, including packaged cookies, cakes, a cotton-stuffed toy fish, a roll of tape, and a bag of popcorn, were successfully manipulated. This confirmed that the method inherently avoids grasping complexities and minimizes stress on objects, accommodating various shapes and properties.

**Quantitative Closed-Loop Experiments:**

- **Translation of Soft and Randomly Shaped Objects (Play-Doh putty):**
 - **Task:** Repositioning a soft, deformable Play-Doh object to a consistent reference position from varying initial locations.
 - **Performance:** The control strategy reliably guided the object to its target, even after manual displacements. Surface pitch and roll adjustments correlated with X-axis and Y-axis positional errors, respectively, while height oscillations contributed to object movement.
 - **Metrics:** Average velocities of 5.62 cm/s (X-axis) and 5.55 cm/s (Y-axis) were achieved. Steady-state errors were measured at 1.14 cm (X) and 0.87 cm (Y).
 - **Conclusion:** The strategy effectively translates soft objects while minimizing deformation, demonstrating robustness across different starting points.

- **Translation and Flipping of Rigid Object (Rectangular Cuboid):**
 - **Task:** Translating a rigid cuboid (8 × 8 × 3 cm³, 110 g) from an initial position to the intersection of the two surfaces, then flipping it 180° around its Y-axis, and finally repositioning it.
 - **Performance:** The object was successfully translated and then flipped by rapid height and orientation adjustments of Surface 2. The object's trajectory confirmed successful realignment, and a distinct 180° yaw change was observed during the flip.
 - **Conclusion:** This validates the system's capability to execute complex flipping tasks efficiently without requiring intricate grasping mechanisms or high DoF.

- **Rotation of Rigid Object (Square Prism):**
 - **Task:** Reorienting a rigid square prism around its Z-axis to various target angles (20°, 30°, 60°, 80°).
 - **Performance:** The system successfully rotated the object by dynamically adjusting Surface 2's roll angle and Surface 1's pitch and roll. The object's actual yaw angles aligned with the set target angles.
 - **Metrics:** An average rotational velocity of 1.16 °/s was achieved.
 - **Conclusion:** This experiment demonstrated a novel nonprehensile approach to object reorientation.

- **Shape Manipulation of Deformable Object (Putty Strip):**
 - **Task:** Folding a strip of Play-Doh putty multiple times to alter its length and shape.
 - **Performance:** By combining flipping and translation strategies, the system achieved sequential folds. The geometric center and length of the object were continuously tracked, showing distinct changes at each folding event (e.g., at second 1 and second 15). The object was successfully transformed into a "rolled-up" configuration.
 - **Conclusion:** This highlights the versatility of surface-based manipulation for deforming low-stiffness objects (Young’s modulus 100-200 MPa), relying only on length and geometric center measurements.

Overall, the results quantitatively validate the performance of the system and demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves dexterous tasks with fewer DoF compared to other surface-based methods, as indicated by a minimum mobility requirement (MR_min) of 6 for their two-module, three-DoF-per-unit setup (Table 1).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation by introducing surface-based strategies using modular foldable robots, offering an alternative to conventional grasping methods. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

**Novelty and Capabilities:**
- **Departure from traditional grasping:** The work fundamentally diverges from finger-shaped end effectors, utilizing flat surfaces as minimalist end-effectors. This provides a new perspective for solving complex manipulation problems, particularly for objects that are difficult for traditional grippers to handle.
- **Adaptability to diverse objects:** The surface-based approach inherently extends to objects of varying shapes, sizes, and stiffness levels, including fragile, deformable, irregularly shaped, and slippery items. This capability minimizes stress on objects and simplifies perception and control by reducing the complexity associated with modeling contact dynamics and grasp stability.
- **Dexterous manipulation with fewer DoF:** The system achieves fundamental manipulation tasks—translation, rotation, and flipping—using only two modular units, each with three degrees of freedom. The bimanual design, distributing the required mobility across two smaller units (MR_min of 6), enables dexterous tasks through simultaneous force application from multiple directions, a capability often requiring more complex or higher DoF systems.
- **Modular and scalable design:** The modular nature of the robots enhances dexterity and offers scalability. Functionality can be expanded incrementally by adding more modules, rather than increasing the complexity within a single unit. This design also allows for adaptation to different module sizes and numbers.
- **Hierarchical closed-loop control:** The control architecture overcomes kinematic constraints and is adaptable to various robotic platforms, requiring only roll, pitch, and height DoF at the end-effector level. This makes the strategies broadly applicable, even to systems like conventional dual-arm robots equipped with plane end-effectors.

**Potential Applications:**
The demonstrated capabilities have significant implications for automation in various industries:
- **Food processing and packaging:** Handling items that are typically slippery, delicate, or vary in shape (e.g., chicken, fish, grape bunches) without causing damage, which is a major challenge for traditional grasping methods. Tasks like rotating or positioning items for inspection and packaging could be automated.
- **Manufacturing and assembly lines:** Aligning objects to specific angles or repositioning them for subsequent operations.
- **Domestic robotics and healthcare:** Potential for tasks involving deformable objects like folding clothes or kneading dough, and applications in elderly care, where sensitive handling of objects is crucial.

**Future Directions and Limitations:**
While the current work demonstrates robust control using vision-based feedback for objects with known shapes and sizes, the authors acknowledge limitations and outline future work:
- **Dynamic environments and unknown object properties:** The current approach may be limited in dynamic environments, especially when object shape, friction, and stiffness are unknown.
- **Friction management:** The reliance on posture-based control and low-frequency vibrations may be insufficient for high-friction or extremely delicate objects. Future work will focus on integrating multi-modal perception for better object property estimation, adopting learning-based control strategies, and exploring active friction modulation methods or low-friction materials.
- **Scalability expansion:** The authors plan to investigate both upscaling (e.g., larger modules up to 500x500 mm² for heavy loads requiring stronger actuators) and miniaturization (e.g., 5x5–10x10 mm² modules for integration into robotic palms).
- **Increased modularity:** Expanding to four or six modules could enlarge the workspace, enable more complex deformation modes, and support simultaneous manipulation of multiple objects, enhancing efficiency.
- **Integration of dynamic principles:** Incorporating dynamic manipulation principles from prior works, such as tossing or flipping by momentum, could further extend the system's capabilities.

In summary, this research offers a foundational framework for surface-based manipulation, demonstrating a pathway to more versatile and robust robotic handling of challenging objects, with clear potential for industrial and domestic applications.
