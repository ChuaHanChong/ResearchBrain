# Revisiting Chain-of-Thought Prompting: Zero-shot Can Be Stronger than Few-shot

> [!summary] Summary
> This study demonstrates that for recent, powerful large language models, Zero-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting often outperforms Few-shot variants in mathematical reasoning tasks after correcting evaluation biases, suggesting exemplars primarily serve to standardize output format rather than enhance core reasoning abilities.

## Problem

- The prevailing assumption that Few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) universally improves Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, largely derived from experiments on older, weaker models.
- A lack of clarity regarding the actual role and effectiveness of CoT exemplars for newer, intrinsically stronger LLMs.
- Potential evaluation biases in standard benchmarks that could obscure the true reasoning capabilities of Zero-shot CoT.

## Method

- Systematically compared ==Few-shot and Zero-shot CoT== across a range of strong, ==instruction-tuned LLMs== (e.g., Qwen2.5, LLaMA3) on mathematical reasoning benchmarks like GSM8K and MATH.
- Identified and corrected a critical ==evaluation bias in GSM8K==, ensuring accurate extraction of boxed answers from CoT outputs to enable fair comparisons.
- Conducted extensive ==ablation studies==, including varying exemplar content, retrieval methods, ==noise injection==, and ==attention visualizations== to understand exemplar utility.

## Results

- After correcting a prevalent evaluation bias, Zero-shot CoT consistently achieved substantial performance gains and frequently surpassed Few-shot CoT for strong models on GSM8K.
- Few-shot CoT for strong models generally yielded comparable or worse performance than Zero-shot CoT on both GSM8K and MATH, failing to demonstrate reasoning enhancement.
- Attention visualizations and noisy exemplar ablation studies showed that strong models allocated consistently low attention to exemplar content, suggesting reliance on internal capabilities rather than explicit examples for reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary function of Few-shot CoT exemplars for strong LLMs shifts from enhancing reasoning to mainly aligning output formats for reliable evaluation.
> - Zero-shot CoT, when properly evaluated, can be as strong as or stronger than Few-shot CoT for modern, capable LLMs in mathematical reasoning tasks.
> - The utility of CoT exemplars is model-dependent, still providing reasoning benefits for weaker models but being largely ignored semantically by stronger ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen rapid advancements, with emergent capabilities like In-Context Learning (ICL) becoming a cornerstone for their versatility. ICL allows LLMs to perform new tasks by merely providing a few examples (exemplars) in the input prompt, without requiring any model parameter updates. A significant enhancement to ICL has been the introduction of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, where exemplars not only show input-output pairs but also explicit step-by-step reasoning processes. This approach, known as Few-shot CoT, was initially shown to dramatically improve LLMs' reasoning abilities, particularly in complex tasks like mathematical problem-solving. Furthermore, the discovery of Zero-shot CoT, which involves simply appending an instruction like "Let's think step by step" without any exemplars, demonstrated that models could be prompted to self-generate reasoning paths.

Existing research in CoT prompting has predominantly focused on optimizing various aspects of few-shot exemplars: their quality, quantity, diversity, and ordering. Numerous strategies for exemplar construction and selection (e.g., complexity-based, diverse selection methods like DPP and MMR) have been proposed to maximize performance across different task settings. Alongside these methodological advancements, a growing body of work has sought to understand the underlying mechanisms of ICL and CoT, exploring their theoretical underpinnings and empirical effectiveness.

However, a crucial gap identified by this paper is that most of these prior studies and their conclusions were drawn from experiments with earlier, weaker LLMs. As LLMs continue to scale and become inherently more powerful, their internal reasoning capabilities have likely evolved significantly. This paper directly addresses whether the conventional wisdom surrounding Few-shot CoT, particularly its ability to *enhance reasoning*, still holds true for these newer, stronger models.

By systematically revisiting the role of CoT exemplars, this work challenges a prevailing assumption in the field—that providing explicit step-by-step demonstrations universally benefits LLM reasoning. It complements existing research by offering a contemporary perspective, demonstrating that for highly capable models, the utility of CoT exemplars might shift from improving reasoning ability to primarily serving other functions, such as output format alignment. This research thus pushes the boundaries of our understanding of LLM capabilities, prompting a re-evaluation of established ICL paradigms in light of advanced model architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Investigate the actual role of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) exemplars in mathematical reasoning tasks** when applied to recent, strong Large Language Models (LLMs).
2. **Determine if Few-shot CoT still improves the reasoning performance of these advanced models** compared to Zero-shot CoT, challenging the long-held assumption that exemplars are universally beneficial for reasoning.
3. **Identify and correct potential evaluation biases** that might obscure the true performance of different prompting strategies, especially Zero-shot CoT.
4. **Explore the effectiveness of enhanced CoT exemplars**, generated by even more advanced reasoning models, to see if higher-quality demonstrations can still yield reasoning improvements in strong LLMs.
5. **Understand the underlying mechanisms** behind the observed effectiveness (or ineffectiveness) of CoT exemplars, particularly by analyzing model behavior such as attention patterns.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the continuous and rapid advancement of LLM capabilities. As models become intrinsically stronger, the empirical findings and prompting strategies derived from experiments on weaker, older models may no longer be fully applicable. The authors question whether the benefits attributed to Few-shot CoT in the past truly enhanced reasoning, or if other factors were at play, especially given the increasingly sophisticated internal reasoning abilities of modern LLMs. Specifically, they aim to revisit a central question: *In mathematical reasoning tasks, can CoT exemplars still improve the reasoning performance of recent strong models?* This re-examination is crucial for optimizing future LLM applications and guiding research into more effective prompting strategies for highly capable AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a comprehensive empirical methodology to systematically investigate the role of CoT exemplars in mathematical reasoning.

**Models:**
The study evaluates a wide range of open-source instruction-tuned LLMs, spanning various scales and architectures to ensure robustness of findings:
- **Recent Strong Models:** Qwen2.5 series (0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B, 32B, 72B parameters), LLaMA3 series (1B, 3B, 8B, 70B), Gemma2 series (2B, 9B), and Ministral-8B. All these are instruction-tuned variants, as base models were found to exhibit output instability.
- **Older/Weaker Models (for comparative analysis):** LLaMA2-7B and Qwen-7B. These are included to see if the findings from earlier studies still hold for less powerful models.

**Datasets:**
Two standard mathematical reasoning benchmarks with varying difficulty levels are used:
- **GSM8K (Grade School Math 8K):** Consists of 1,319 grade-school word problems, requiring 3-4 simple reasoning steps.
- **MATH:** Comprises 5,000 high school competition problems across five difficulty levels, presenting a more significant challenge.
Experiments are conducted on the full test sets of both datasets.

**Evaluation Environment and Hyperparameters:**
- The open-source inference framework OpenCompass and vLLM are used for efficient, parallelized inference.
- All prompts consistently include the CoT instruction: "Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}."
- A fixed random seed of 42 is used for reproducibility; greedy decoding ensures deterministic results.

**Key Methodological Steps and Experimental Designs:**

1. **Correction of Evaluation Bias in GSM8K:** A critical initial step involves identifying and rectifying a common evaluation bias in existing frameworks (e.g., OpenCompass) for GSM8K. These frameworks typically extract the *last number* from model outputs as the answer. However, in CoT settings, answers are often enclosed in `\boxed{}`. This mismatch leads to artificially low accuracy for Zero-shot CoT. The authors modify the evaluation script to correctly extract numbers within `\boxed{}`, ensuring fair comparison.

2. **Comparison of Few-shot CoT vs. Zero-shot CoT:** After correcting the evaluation bias, the core comparison is made.
 - **Few-shot CoT:** Models are provided with 2, 4, 6, or 8 exemplars. These exemplars are taken from Wei et al. (2022) or retrieved using various strategies.
 - **Zero-shot CoT:** Models are given only the instruction "Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}" without any examples.

3. **Ablation Studies on Exemplar Content (for Format Alignment):** To understand the function of exemplars beyond reasoning, ablation experiments are conducted by masking parts of the exemplars:
 - `Replace_Q`: Replaces the question in each exemplar with "xxx."
 - `Replace_QA`: Replaces both question and answer with "xxx" but retains the final phrase "So the answer is..."
 - `Replace_ALL`: Replaces the question, answer, and final phrase with "xxx."
 This helps determine if exemplars primarily guide output formatting.

4. **Impact of Exemplar Retrieval Methods:** Various established exemplar selection strategies are tested with a fixed number of 8 exemplars to see if high-quality or diverse exemplars improve performance for strong models:
 - Complexity-based selection (Fu et al., 2023)
 - Fast-Votek (Hongjin et al., 2022)
 - DPP (Ye et al., 2023a)
 - MMR (Ye et al., 2023b)
 - EXPLORA (Purohit et al., 2024)
 - Simple baselines: TopK and Random.

5. **Impact of Model Intrinsic Capability:** Experiments are conducted on both strong and weaker/older models to determine if the effectiveness of CoT exemplars is model-dependent. This helps reconcile their findings with prior work that observed benefits from Few-shot CoT.

6. **Effectiveness of Enhanced CoT Exemplars:** To address whether traditional CoT exemplars might be "too easy" for strong models, the authors create "enhanced" exemplars using responses from state-of-the-art Reasoning Large Language Models (RLLMs):
 - DeepSeek-R1-generated responses.
 - Qwen2.5-Max-generated responses.
 These are used as Few-shot exemplars with varying numbers (up to 4 shots due to length).

7. **Ablation Study on Noisy Exemplars:** To further investigate if models attend to exemplar content, noise is injected into exemplars (Traditional CoT, R1-enhanced, Qwen2.5-Max-enhanced):
 - `Noise50`: Randomly replaces 50% of tokens with "XXX."
 - `Shuffle`: Completely shuffles the words within exemplars.
 - `Replace-XXX`: Replaces all words with "XXX."
 The goal is to see if performance degrades significantly with corrupted exemplar content.

8. **Attention Visualization:** To empirically verify whether models actually "look at" the exemplars, attention distributions of the Qwen2.5-7B model on GSM8K under Few-shot settings are visualized, focusing on the final layer's attention maps. This helps understand if models are utilizing the semantic content of the exemplars or primarily focusing on the prompt.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several counter-intuitive and significant findings, particularly concerning the utility of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) exemplars for recent, strong Large Language Models (LLMs) in mathematical reasoning tasks.

1. **Evaluation Bias Correction Reveals Zero-shot CoT's Strength:**
 - The paper identifies a crucial evaluation bias in common benchmarks like GSM8K, where standard evaluators incorrectly extract answers due to output format mismatches (e.g., missing answers enclosed in `\boxed{}`).
 - After correcting this bias, the **Zero-shot CoT** setting consistently achieved substantial performance gains, often **outperforming all Few-shot CoT configurations** across various strong models (e.g., Qwen2.5-72B). This indicates that Zero-shot CoT's reasoning capabilities were significantly underestimated previously.
 - **Primary Function of Exemplars: Output Format Alignment:** Few-shot exemplars were found to primarily help standardize the output format, making answer extraction more reliable, rather than enhancing raw reasoning ability. Ablation studies (Replace_Q, Replace_QA, Replace_ALL) confirmed that preserving the full answer structure in exemplars was key for format alignment, with performance dropping as more content was masked, reverting to Zero-shot baseline when all informative content was removed.

2. **Traditional CoT Exemplars Do Not Enhance Reasoning for Strong Models:**
 - For recent strong models (e.g., Qwen2.5 series, LLaMA3.1-8B), Few-shot CoT, even with diverse exemplar selection strategies (Complexity-based, Fast-Votek, DPP, MMR, EXPLORA, TopK, Random), generally showed **comparable or worse performance than the Zero-shot baseline** on both GSM8K and MATH datasets.
 - Varying the number of exemplars (2, 4, 6, 8 shots) also failed to consistently surpass Zero-shot performance. Any minor improvements were minimal (e.g., ~0.2% on GSM8K) and attributed to experimental variance rather than genuine reasoning gains. On the more challenging MATH dataset, Few-shot CoT often underperformed Zero-shot.

3. **Effectiveness of Exemplars is Model-Dependent:**
 - While strong models did not benefit from exemplars for reasoning, **weaker or older models** (e.g., LLaMA3.2-1B, LLaMA2-7B, Qwen-7B) **showed significant accuracy improvements** when provided with CoT exemplars.
 - This suggests that for models with less intrinsic reasoning capability, exemplars do play a supportive role by providing intermediate steps. This finding reconciles the paper's results with previous research showing Few-shot CoT benefits, indicating that the utility of exemplars is contingent on the model's inherent strength.

4. **Enhanced CoT Exemplars Offer Limited Benefits:**
 - Even when using highly sophisticated exemplars generated by advanced Reasoning Large Language Models (RLLMs) like DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen2.5-Max, the performance gains for strong models (Qwen2.5-7B/14B/72B) were moderate or negligible compared to Zero-shot CoT.
 - While enhanced exemplars generally outperformed standard Few-shot CoT, Zero-shot CoT consistently achieved strong accuracy without the additional context overhead. This implies that improving exemplar quality, while helpful, yields only limited or no improvement over the strong Zero-shot reasoning capabilities of modern LLMs.

5. **Models Tend to Ignore Exemplar Content:**
 - **Ablation studies with noisy exemplars** (replacing tokens with "XXX," shuffling words) showed that injecting noise did not lead to significant performance degradation for strong models, especially larger ones like Qwen2.5-72B. This suggests that models may selectively ignore exemplar content and rely on their intrinsic reasoning.
 - **Attention visualizations** confirmed this observation: the Qwen2.5-7B model allocated consistently low attention scores to the exemplar section of the input, while focusing more on the prompt and the test question. This empirical evidence supports the hypothesis that strong models primarily rely on their internal knowledge and instructions, largely disregarding the semantic content of provided CoT exemplars for reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper presents findings with significant implications for the development, evaluation, and application of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in the context of reasoning tasks.

1. **Challenges a Core Assumption in LLM Prompting:** The most impactful finding is the demonstration that for recent, strong LLMs, Few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting often fails to improve reasoning performance beyond Zero-shot CoT. This directly challenges the widely held belief that providing step-by-step exemplars is a universal and indispensable method for enhancing LLM reasoning. This re-evaluation necessitates a shift in how researchers and practitioners approach prompting for advanced models.

2. **Highlights the Critical Role of Evaluation Bias:** The discovery and correction of evaluation bias in benchmarks like GSM8K is a crucial contribution. It reveals that previous assessments might have significantly underestimated the true reasoning capabilities of Zero-shot CoT. This underscores the importance of carefully designed and robust evaluation metrics, warning against simplistic extraction methods that can obscure genuine model performance and lead to misinformed conclusions. Future research and benchmark development must explicitly consider and mitigate such biases.

3. **Redefines the Role of Few-shot Exemplars:** The paper suggests that for strong LLMs, the primary function of Few-shot CoT exemplars is not to enhance reasoning but to align the model's output format with human expectations or specific evaluation criteria. This provides a more nuanced understanding of ICL, distinguishing between "reasoning enhancement" and "output formatting guidance." This distinction is vital for designing effective prompts: if reasoning is not the goal, simpler exemplars focused on formatting might suffice.

4. **Model-Dependent Utility of CoT:** The finding that Few-shot CoT still benefits weaker or older models reconciles this work with prior research. It establishes a "strength threshold" for LLMs, below which external exemplars aid reasoning, and above which they become redundant for reasoning (though still useful for formatting). This has practical implications for deploying LLMs of different scales; smaller or older models might still benefit from comprehensive Few-shot CoT, potentially for edge-device applications.

5. **Implications for Future LLM Architectures and Training:** The observed tendency of strong models to ignore exemplar content and rely on internal knowledge suggests that these models have internalized complex reasoning patterns during pre-training and instruction-tuning. This could guide future research towards even more self-sufficient reasoning architectures, perhaps reducing the reliance on extensive explicit demonstrations in prompts. It raises questions about how much more 'knowledge' or 'reasoning ability' can be baked into the model parameters themselves.

6. **Calls for a Re-evaluation of ICL Paradigm:** The paper explicitly calls for a re-examination of the ICL paradigm and the definition of exemplars. If models are largely ignoring provided examples for reasoning, then new strategies are needed to genuinely leverage external information for even more advanced or specialized reasoning tasks. This might involve exploring more complex exemplar structures, dynamic interaction, or alternative forms of in-context guidance that current models might not be "ignoring."

7. **Practical Guidance for Practitioners:** For users and developers of modern, powerful LLMs, the research offers practical advice: Zero-shot CoT is often sufficient, more stable, and less computationally expensive than Few-shot CoT for mathematical reasoning. This simplifies prompting strategies and reduces the overhead associated with exemplar selection and management for these tasks.

In conclusion, this paper provides a timely and critical re-assessment of a foundational technique in LLM prompting. By demonstrating the diminishing returns of Few-shot CoT for strong models and illuminating the true nature of exemplar utility, it offers crucial insights that will shape future research directions, prompt engineering practices, and evaluation methodologies in the rapidly evolving landscape of large language models.
