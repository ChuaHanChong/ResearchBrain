# Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Columbia University, Google DeepMind, and MIT present Diffusion Policy, a framework that leverages denoising diffusion probabilistic models for learning visuomotor robot policies. This approach achieves an average 46.9% success rate improvement over baselines in simulation and robust real-world performance on complex manipulation tasks by effectively modeling multimodal action distributions.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning methods struggle to model multimodal action distributions from human demonstrations, often averaging modes or failing to generalize.
- Learning policies for high-dimensional action spaces and long action sequences often leads to issues with temporal consistency and scalability.
- Implicit policies, while capable of modeling multimodality, typically suffer from training instability due to computationally intensive negative sampling processes.

## Method

- The framework formulates visuomotor policies as conditional ==denoising diffusion probabilistic models== (==DDPMs==) that learn to generate future action sequences conditioned on visual observations.
- A ==receding horizon control== strategy is employed, where the **Diffusion Policy** predicts a sequence of actions, but only the first few are executed before re-planning based on updated observations.
- The ==noise prediction network==, central to the diffusion process, is implemented with either a ==1D temporal CNN== or a ==Time-series Diffusion Transformer==, both capable of efficient, end-to-end training with visual encoders.

## Results

- Diffusion Policy achieved an average **46.9%** success rate improvement over state-of-the-art baselines across **15** diverse simulation tasks, including multi-stage and long-horizon challenges.
- On real-world robot platforms, the policy demonstrated high success rates on complex manipulation tasks such as Push-T (**95%** success) and 6DoF Mug Flipping (**90%** success), exhibiting robustness to perturbations.
- The research found that position control action spaces consistently outperformed velocity control when used with Diffusion Policy, a finding that contrasts with common practices in behavior cloning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Denoising diffusion models effectively capture complex, multimodal action distributions, outperforming prior methods that struggle with mode collapse or training instability.
> - Predicting entire action sequences, rather than single actions, inherently promotes temporal consistency and allows for robust control in high-dimensional action spaces.
> - The inherent training stability of diffusion models, which avoids the need for intractable negative sampling, simplifies policy training and reduces hyperparameter sensitivity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research fits squarely within the field of **robot learning from demonstration (LfD)**, also known as **imitation learning (IL)**, with a particular focus on **visuomotor policies**. The goal of LfD is to enable robots to learn complex behaviors by observing human demonstrations, rather than through explicit programming or extensive trial-and-error reinforcement learning.

The paper identifies key challenges in current LfD approaches:
- **Multimodal Action Distributions:** Human demonstrations often contain multiple valid ways to achieve a goal (e.g., pushing an object from different sides). Traditional explicit policies (e.g., direct regression models) struggle to capture this, often averaging modes or collapsing to a single mode. Methods like Mixture Density Networks (MDNs) or categorical representations attempt to address this but can be sensitive to hyperparameter tuning and limited in expressivity.
- **High-Dimensional Output Spaces:** Many complex robot tasks require predicting sequences of actions or operating in high-dimensional joint/end-effector spaces, which is challenging for most policies due to the curse of dimensionality.
- **Training Stability:** Implicit policies, which learn an energy function over actions and observations, can represent multimodal distributions well. However, they often suffer from training instability due to the need for intractable negative sampling (e.g., Info-NCE loss in Implicit Behavioral Cloning - IBC).
- **Real-time Control:** Learned policies must be computationally efficient to enable closed-loop control on physical robots.

**Diffusion Policy** proposes a novel solution to these challenges by leveraging the power of **denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs)**. DDPMs have revolutionized generative modeling in fields like image and text generation, demonstrating an impressive ability to model complex, high-dimensional data distributions. This paper is among the first to systematically apply these powerful generative capabilities directly to visuomotor robot policy learning.

Prior work has explored diffusion models in control, particularly for **planning** (e.g., Janner et al. 2022a, Huang et al. 2023), where they infer trajectories of actions and states. Other concurrent works (Pearce et al. 2023, Reuss et al. 2023, Hansen-Estruch et al. 2023) also investigate diffusion-based policies, focusing on sampling strategies or RL applications. However, "Diffusion Policy" distinguishes itself by:
1. **Direct Visuomotor Policy Learning:** Focusing on learning a direct mapping from observations to actions, rather than as part of a broader planning framework.
2. **Emphasis on Real-World Practicality:** Addressing critical design factors for physical robots, such as receding-horizon control, efficient visual conditioning, and the choice between position and velocity control.
3. **Comprehensive Real-World Evaluation:** Conducting extensive experiments on diverse physical robot tasks, beyond just simulation.

By integrating diffusion models into the behavioral cloning paradigm, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of both explicit (lack of multimodality, high-D challenges) and implicit (training instability) policy representations, thereby pushing the state-of-the-art in robot learning from demonstration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to introduce and rigorously evaluate **Diffusion Policy**, a novel framework for learning robot visuomotor policies by modeling them as a conditional denoising diffusion process. The core motivation stems from the inherent difficulties faced by existing imitation learning techniques when applied to complex robotic manipulation tasks:

1. **To Robustly Handle Multimodal Action Distributions:** Robot tasks often allow for multiple correct ways to execute an action or reach a goal. Human demonstrations naturally capture this multimodality. Traditional supervised learning policies (e.g., direct regression) tend to average these modes, leading to suboptimal or jittery behavior. Implicit policies can model multimodality but typically suffer from training instability. The authors aim to leverage diffusion models' ability to express arbitrary normalizable distributions to gracefully capture and generate diverse, yet coherent, action sequences corresponding to different modes.

2. **To Effectively Operate in High-Dimensional Action Spaces:** Many realistic robot tasks involve controlling multiple degrees of freedom over a sequence of time steps, leading to high-dimensional action spaces. Explicit policies often struggle to scale effectively, while implicit policies face challenges in sampling from complex, high-dimensional energy landscapes. Diffusion models have demonstrated impressive scalability in high-dimensional generative tasks (like image generation), which the authors hypothesize can be directly transferred to predicting high-dimensional action sequences (e.g., future trajectories). This is crucial for temporal action consistency and avoiding myopic planning.

3. **To Achieve Stable and Efficient Policy Training:** Energy-Based Models (EBMs), which underpin many implicit policies, are notoriously difficult to train due to the need for estimating intractable normalization constants via negative sampling, leading to training instability and sensitivity to hyperparameters. Diffusion models, by learning the gradient of the action-distribution score function, can bypass this computationally intensive and unstable negative sampling process, thereby offering inherently more stable training. This stability aims to reduce the practical challenges of deploying learned policies.

4. **To Enable Real-time, Closed-Loop Control on Physical Robots:** For practical deployment, learned policies must operate efficiently and robustly in real-time. The paper aims to develop specific technical contributions (like vision-conditioned diffusion, receding horizon control, and efficient network architectures) to ensure that the Diffusion Policy can achieve sufficiently low inference latency and maintain responsiveness for closed-loop control on physical hardware.

In essence, the motivation is to harness the proven generative modeling power and training stability of diffusion models to create a new class of robot policies that are more expressive, robust, and easier to train and deploy than current state-of-the-art imitation learning methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Diffusion Policy formulates visuomotor robot policies as **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)**. Instead of directly predicting an action, the policy learns to infer the "action-score gradient" which refines an initial random noise into a desired action sequence over several denoising iterations.

The core methodological adaptations for robotics are:

1. **DDPM for Action Generation:**
 - Unlike typical DDPMs that generate images, Diffusion Policy generates a sequence of robot actions, `A_t`, representing future movements.
 - The denoising process is made **conditional** on visual observations, `O_t`. This means the policy learns `p(A_t | O_t)`, treating observations as conditioning inputs rather than part of the data being denoised. This significantly speeds up inference as observations don't need to be diffused.
 - The training objective is a simple Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, minimizing the difference between the predicted noise and the actual noise added to the action. This bypasses the complex negative sampling issues faced by energy-based models, contributing to training stability.

2. **Closed-Loop Action-Sequence Prediction with Receding Horizon Control:**
 - The policy predicts `T_p` steps of future actions (action prediction horizon) based on `T_o` steps of past observations (observation horizon).
 - Only the first `T_a` actions (action execution horizon) from this predicted sequence are executed before the policy re-plans. This **receding horizon control** strategy ensures temporal consistency by predicting a short trajectory and maintaining responsiveness to new observations, striking a balance between long-term planning and reactive control.
 - The next inference step can be "warm-started" with the previous action sequence prediction, further improving smoothness.

3. **Network Architectures for `ε_θ` (Noise Prediction Network):**
 - **CNN-based Diffusion Policy:** Utilizes a 1D temporal Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) backbone. Visual observation features and the denoising iteration step (`k`) are conditioned using **Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)** layers within the CNN. This architecture is generally stable and effective for many tasks, but can suffer from "over-smoothing" for high-frequency action changes.
 - **Time-series Diffusion Transformer:** To counter over-smoothing, a novel Transformer-based architecture is proposed. It treats noisy action sequences as input tokens and processes them with transformer decoder blocks. Visual observation embeddings are passed as input features to cross-attention layers. This architecture excels in tasks requiring high-frequency action changes (e.g., velocity control) but can be more sensitive to hyperparameter tuning.

4. **Visual Encoder:**
 - A standard **ResNet-18** (modified with spatial softmax pooling and GroupNorm for stability) is used to map raw image sequences into a latent embedding `O_t`.
 - The visual encoder is trained **end-to-end** with the diffusion policy, allowing the vision features to be optimized directly for the control task. Ablations show that fine-tuning pre-trained vision encoders (e.g., CLIP-trained ViT) can yield excellent results, but end-to-end training is generally effective.

5. **Noise Schedule and Inference Acceleration:**
 - The **Square Cosine Schedule** (from iDDPM) is empirically found to be effective for controlling the noise levels during the diffusion process.
 - To enable real-time control, **Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM)** is employed during inference. DDIM allows decoupling the number of training diffusion iterations (e.g., 100) from inference iterations (e.g., 10-16), drastically reducing latency without significant performance degradation.

6. **Action Space and Data Handling:**
 - The paper surprisingly finds that **position control** action spaces consistently outperform velocity control for Diffusion Policy, contrasting with much of the prior behavior cloning literature. This is attributed to Diffusion Policy's better handling of multimodality and position control's reduced compounding error.
 - The formulation is robust to "idle actions" (pauses in demonstrations), which often pose a challenge for single-step policies.

This comprehensive methodological framework combines the theoretical strengths of diffusion models with practical engineering solutions tailored for robotic control, addressing key limitations of existing imitation learning paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Diffusion Policy is extensive, covering 15 tasks across 4 different benchmarks in both simulated and real-world environments. The results consistently demonstrate the superior performance and robustness of Diffusion Policy compared to existing state-of-the-art imitation learning methods.

**Quantitative Performance (Simulation):**
- **Overall Improvement:** Diffusion Policy consistently outperforms baselines (LSTM-GMM, IBC, BET) across all tested benchmarks and variants (state and image observations), achieving an average success rate improvement of **46.9%**.
- **State-based Policies:** In Tab. 1, Diffusion Policy (both CNN-based 'C' and Transformer-based 'T' variants) achieves near-perfect success rates on many RoboMimic tasks (Lift, Can, Square, Transport, ToolHang) and significantly higher performance on Push-T compared to baselines.
- **Visual Policies:** In Tab. 2, Diffusion Policy maintains its strong performance with visual observations, again outperforming baselines, especially on complex tasks like Transport and ToolHang.
- **Multi-Stage Tasks (Block Push, Kitchen):** In Tab. 4, Diffusion Policy shows substantial improvements in handling long-horizon multimodality. For BlockPush, it performs much better on `p2` (pushing both blocks). For Kitchen, it shows a remarkable 213% improvement on the `p4` metric (interacting with 4 or more objects), demonstrating its ability to cope with arbitrary sub-goal ordering.

**Key Qualitative and Ablation Findings:**

- **Multimodal Action Distributions:**
 - **Short-Horizon:** On the Push-T task, Diffusion Policy successfully models and commits to distinct modes (e.g., pushing from left or right), unlike baselines that either bias towards one mode or fail to commit.
 - **Long-Horizon:** Its ability to handle long-horizon multimodality (e.g., arbitrary order of sub-tasks in Block Push or Kitchen) is a significant advantage, leading to higher task completion rates.
- **Synergy with Position Control:** Diffusion Policy performs significantly better with a position-control action space compared to velocity control (Fig. 4), a counter-intuitive finding that distinguishes it from most prior behavior cloning work. This is attributed to its ability to better handle multimodality and reduce compounding errors inherent in velocity control.
- **Benefits of Action-Sequence Prediction:** The ability to predict high-dimensional action sequences naturally addresses temporal action consistency and robustness to "idle actions" (pauses in demonstrations), which often cause single-step policies to get stuck. The ablation on action horizon (Fig. 5 left) shows an optimal balance around 8 steps.
- **Training Stability:** Unlike IBC, which exhibits unstable training error and oscillating evaluation performance (Fig. 6), Diffusion Policy demonstrates stable training, making hyperparameter tuning and checkpoint selection less critical. This is attributed to its formulation bypassing the need for intractable normalization constant estimation.
- **Robustness to Latency:** Diffusion Policy (especially with position control) is robust to simulated latency, maintaining peak performance up to 4 steps of delay (Fig. 5 right), a crucial feature for real-world deployment.
- **Visual Encoder Choices:** While end-to-end training of ResNet-18 performed well, fine-tuning pre-trained vision encoders (especially CLIP-trained ViT-B/16) showed promising results and faster learning, achieving 98% success on `square (ph)` with only 50 epochs (Tab. 5).

**Real-world Evaluation:**
- **Real-world Push-T:** Achieves near-human performance (95% success rate, 0.8 IoU) on a multi-stage, high-precision task, vastly outperforming IBC (0% success) and LSTM-GMM (20% success). It demonstrates impressive robustness to visual occlusion and physical perturbations, even synthesizing novel re-planning behaviors not explicitly demonstrated (Fig. 8).
- **6DoF Mug Flipping:** Achieves 90% success rate on a complex 6DoF rotation task involving highly multimodal demonstrations, showing its ability to sequence multiple pushes or regrasps.
- **Sauce Pouring and Spreading:** Achieves close-to-human performance (e.g., 0.74 IoU vs. 0.79 human for pouring) on tasks involving non-rigid objects and periodic actions, demonstrating graceful reaction to perturbations and robust handling of idle actions and self-termination.
- **Bimanual Tasks (Egg Beater, Mat Unrolling, Shirt Folding):** Diffusion Policy works "out of the box" for these challenging coordinated tasks (55-75% success rates), validating its scalability to multi-arm setups and highlighting the importance of appropriate teleoperation methods (haptic feedback, VR) for data collection.

In summary, the results provide compelling evidence that Diffusion Policy significantly advances the state of imitation learning for robotics, particularly for complex, high-dimensional, and multimodal tasks, with strong transferability to real-world systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Diffusion Policy represents a significant step forward in the field of robot learning from demonstration and has several far-reaching implications:

1. **A New Paradigm for Policy Learning:** By successfully adapting denoising diffusion models – a powerful class of generative models – to visuomotor policy learning, this work proposes a "new generation of policy learning techniques." This fundamentally shifts the approach from direct regression or energy-based optimization to an iterative denoising process, opening up a new avenue for research in robot control.

2. **Addressing Core Challenges in Imitation Learning:** Diffusion Policy directly tackles long-standing, critical problems in imitation learning that have plagued traditional methods:
 - **Graceful Handling of Multimodality:** Its ability to naturally express and sample from multimodal action distributions is a game-changer for learning from diverse human demonstrations, leading to more robust and flexible robot behaviors.
 - **Scalability to High-Dimensional Action Spaces:** By predicting entire action sequences, it inherently promotes temporal consistency and handles complex, multi-DoF tasks, overcoming the limitations of single-step predictions.
 - **Improved Training Stability:** The inherent stability of diffusion models, by avoiding the need for intractable normalization constant estimation, simplifies the training process, reduces hyperparameter sensitivity, and makes deployment more practical.

3. **Enhanced Real-World Robot Capabilities:** The extensive evaluation on 15 tasks, including challenging real-world manipulation and bimanual tasks (Push-T, Mug Flipping, Sauce Pouring/Spreading, Egg Beater, Mat Unrolling, Shirt Folding), strongly validates Diffusion Policy's practical applicability. Its demonstrated robustness to perturbations and ability to synthesize novel behaviors (even when not explicitly demonstrated) are crucial for reliable robot deployment in unstructured environments. The focus on real-time inference via DDIM further ensures practical utility.

4. **Enabling Better Data Utilization:** By effectively handling multimodal data, the policy can leverage richer, less filtered human demonstration datasets, which often contain variations and alternative strategies. Its robustness to "idle actions" also means less stringent data pre-processing is required.

5. **Guiding Future Research:**
 - The surprising finding that **position control** outperforms velocity control for Diffusion Policy (unlike many baselines) challenges conventional wisdom in robot learning and warrants further investigation into optimal action representations.
 - The detailed ablation studies on action horizons, visual encoder designs, and training strategies provide valuable insights for future research in designing and implementing visuomotor policies.
 - The open-sourcing of code, data, and training details (diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu) is a significant contribution, fostering reproducibility and accelerating future research in this promising direction.

6. **Bridging Generative Models and Control:** This work serves as a compelling example of how advancements in generative AI can be directly translated to impactful applications in robotics, fostering cross-pollination between these fields. It suggests that many properties making diffusion models powerful for image generation could be equally beneficial for generating complex, coherent robot movements.

In conclusion, Diffusion Policy offers a robust, stable, and highly performant approach to visuomotor policy learning. Its ability to effectively model complex action distributions and scale to high-dimensional problems, coupled with its proven success on real-world robots, positions it as a foundational work that could motivate a new era of robot learning algorithms based on powerful generative models.
