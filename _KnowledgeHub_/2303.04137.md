# Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Columbia University, Google DeepMind, and MIT present Diffusion Policy, a framework that leverages denoising diffusion probabilistic models for learning visuomotor robot policies. This approach achieves an average 46.9% success rate improvement over baselines in simulation and robust real-world performance on complex manipulation tasks by effectively modeling multimodal action distributions.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning methods struggle to model multimodal action distributions from human demonstrations, often averaging modes or failing to generalize.
- Learning policies for high-dimensional action spaces and long action sequences often leads to issues with temporal consistency and scalability.
- Implicit policies, while capable of modeling multimodality, typically suffer from training instability due to computationally intensive negative sampling processes.

## Method

- The framework formulates visuomotor policies as conditional ==denoising diffusion probabilistic models== (==DDPMs==) that learn to generate future action sequences conditioned on visual observations.
- A ==receding horizon control== strategy is employed, where the **Diffusion Policy** predicts a sequence of actions, but only the first few are executed before re-planning based on updated observations.
- The ==noise prediction network==, central to the diffusion process, is implemented with either a ==1D temporal CNN== or a ==Time-series Diffusion Transformer==, both capable of efficient, end-to-end training with visual encoders.

## Results

- Diffusion Policy achieved an average **46.9%** success rate improvement over state-of-the-art baselines across **15** diverse simulation tasks, including multi-stage and long-horizon challenges.
- On real-world robot platforms, the policy demonstrated high success rates on complex manipulation tasks such as Push-T (**95%** success) and 6DoF Mug Flipping (**90%** success), exhibiting robustness to perturbations.
- The research found that position control action spaces consistently outperformed velocity control when used with Diffusion Policy, a finding that contrasts with common practices in behavior cloning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Denoising diffusion models effectively capture complex, multimodal action distributions, outperforming prior methods that struggle with mode collapse or training instability.
> - Predicting entire action sequences, rather than single actions, inherently promotes temporal consistency and allows for robust control in high-dimensional action spaces.
> - The inherent training stability of diffusion models, which avoids the need for intractable negative sampling, simplifies policy training and reduces hyperparameter sensitivity.
