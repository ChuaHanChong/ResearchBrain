# Seeing Realism from Simulation: Efficient Video Transfer for Vision-Language-Action Data Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> The research from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and The University of Sydney introduces an efficient video augmentation framework that converts simulated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) videos into visually realistic training data, improving VLA model generalization from simulation to real-world tasks. This method boosts average success rates by 10.0% in challenging simulated scenarios and 13-15% on real robots, while reducing video generation time by 61.2%.

## Problem

- Large-scale real-world robotic trajectory datasets for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are costly, time-consuming, and difficult to acquire at scale.
- VLA models trained on simulated data often exhibit a substantial visual domain gap and lack environmental diversity, leading to poor generalization in real-world settings.
- Existing VLA models show brittleness, failing with minor perturbations in object layout, lighting, camera viewpoint, or instruction phrasing, suggesting an inability to learn robust semantic understanding.

## Method

- An end-to-end ==video augmentation framework== is proposed, using temporal video captioning and an LLM to rewrite captions and depth maps for structural control, then synthesizing visually diverse videos with a conditional video diffusion model.
- Velocity caching is introduced to accelerate video generation, segmenting the denoising process into three phases to reuse predicted velocity fields during stable periods, achieving a 61.2% reduction in generation time.
- A ==coreset sampling== strategy is employed to selectively augment the most impactful trajectories by estimating difficulty via policy loss and diversity via embedding similarity, prioritizing challenging and visually distinct samples.

## Results

- The framework improved average success rates by **10.0%** (from **29.0%** to **39.0%**) for RDT-1B in "Hard" scenarios on Robotwin 2.0, and by **5.1%** on LIBERO-Plus (e.g., +**16.6%** for object layout).
- Real-world experiments on an AgileX Piper manipulator showed average success rate increases of 13-**15%** (e.g., from **60%** to **73%** for π0.5) under out-of-distribution conditions like position and background shifts.
- Velocity caching reduced video generation time by an average of **61.2%** across tasks without compromising policy learning, while coreset sampling demonstrated significant improvements with only **10%** data augmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The denoising process in flow-based diffusion models exhibits distinct phases (rapid change, stable, adjustment), allowing for velocity caching to significantly reduce computational costs without compromising output quality.
> - Enriching simulated data with diverse visual contexts, generated through conditional video transfer, effectively improves the generalization and robustness of VLA policies to real-world variations.
> - Selectively augmenting a small subset of training trajectories that are both challenging and visually diverse, identified through coreset sampling, can yield substantial performance gains efficiently.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics, which aim to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and execute complex manipulation tasks. A significant challenge in this domain is the reliance on large-scale, real-world robotic trajectory datasets, which are costly, time-consuming, and difficult to acquire at scale. Simulated data offers an inexpensive and highly parallelizable alternative. However, models trained primarily on simulated data frequently exhibit a substantial visual domain gap and limited environmental diversity, resulting in poor generalization to real-world scenarios.

Previous studies have highlighted the brittleness of VLA models, demonstrating that while they may achieve high performance within a specific simulation, they often fail catastrophically when faced with minor perturbations in object layout, lighting, camera viewpoint, or instruction phrasing. This suggests that models may be memorizing training sequences rather than acquiring robust, semantic task understanding.

The paper addresses this limitation by proposing a data augmentation framework. This framework leverages recent advancements in diffusion-based video generation, style transfer, and data-centric artificial intelligence. It builds upon foundational works in video generation such as Stable Diffusion, Stable Video Diffusion, and more contemporary models like Sora and the Cosmos series, which provide capabilities for world model-based video transfer and generative techniques applied to embodied data. Furthermore, the work draws on principles from data-centric AI, including intelligent data selection and pruning strategies like D$^2$ Pruning, which emphasize balancing data diversity and difficulty to enhance training efficiency and model performance. By bridging the sim-to-real gap through efficient video transfer and targeted data augmentation, this research aims to improve the generalization and robustness of VLA models, thereby reducing the dependency on extensive real-world data collection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in generalizing from simulated environments to real-world robotic tasks. While large-scale VLA models have advanced robotic control, their effectiveness is often bottlenecked by the availability of diverse and extensive real-world training data. Collecting such data is resource-intensive, slow, and challenging to scale.

Simulated data presents an attractive alternative due to its low cost and high scalability. However, models trained on simulated environments frequently exhibit brittleness, achieving high performance within their training distribution but failing under minor real-world variations or perturbations. This "sim-to-real gap" is characterized by a lack of visual and environmental diversity in simulated data. Simulated scenes are often too clean and static, causing models to overfit to spurious regularities rather than learning robust instruction-to-action mappings and semantic task understanding. Benchmarks like LIBERO-Plus and LIBERO-PRO have quantitatively demonstrated this brittleness, showing significant drops in success rates when environments are perturbed with changes in object layout, lighting, or camera angles. This suggests that existing models may be memorizing fixed action sequences rather than developing generalizable reasoning capabilities.

To address these issues, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop an efficient video augmentation framework:** The central aim is to create a system that can convert simulated VLA videos into visually realistic training videos.
2. **Preserve task semantics and action trajectories:** The augmentation process must ensure that the core meaning of the task and the robot's intended actions remain consistent, even as visual elements are diversified.
3. **Introduce visual and environmental diversity:** The framework seeks to enrich training data by generating variations in backgrounds, object textures, lighting conditions, and other environmental contexts that are typically lacking in sterile simulated datasets.
4. **Mitigate computational costs:** A major challenge with video generation is its high computational expense. The research aims to introduce mechanisms that accelerate the video synthesis process, making large-scale data augmentation practical and scalable.
5. **Enable selective data augmentation:** Given that augmenting an entire large dataset can still be prohibitive, a further objective is to develop a strategy to identify and prioritize the most impactful trajectories for augmentation—those that are both challenging for existing policies and visually diverse—to maximize the utility of limited computational resources.
6. **Enhance real-world generalization:** Ultimately, the motivation is to improve the robustness and generalization capabilities of VLA models when deployed in physical robots and real-world scenarios, thereby bridging the sim-to-real gap.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology presents an efficient video augmentation framework designed to enhance the real-world generalization of VLA models trained on simulation data. The framework operates as an end-to-end pipeline, which involves structured condition extraction, environmental diversification through caption rewriting, conditional video synthesis, and strategies for improving efficiency and selectivity.

**Overall Framework:**
The pipeline takes a large-scale simulation training set as input. It incorporates a coreset sampling algorithm to select a subset of important and diverse samples. These selected samples then undergo augmentation to produce realistic video strips, which are subsequently used for VLA model training.

**Diversifying Data via Conditional Video Transfer:**
This core component involves generating visually diverse videos while maintaining the semantic integrity of the task and the fidelity of the action trajectories.
- **Semantic and Structural Condition Extraction:**
 - **Caption Generation:** A temporal video captioning model, VideoChat2, is employed to generate descriptive captions for source videos. These captions summarize robot-object interactions, identified objects, and their spatial relationships.
 - **Caption Rewriting:** To introduce environmental variations, these initial captions are then rewritten using a large language model, Qwen3-8B. The rewriting process focuses on diversifying scene contexts, such as background materials or object colors, while explicitly preserving the original task intent.
 - **Structured Control:** To ensure geometric consistency and maintain the structural integrity of the scene during video synthesis, depth maps are extracted from the source videos. These depth maps serve as robust structural control signals for the subsequent video generation step.
- **Conditional Video Synthesis:**
 - A conditional video diffusion model, Cosmos-Transfer 2.5, synthesizes realistic videos. This model receives the rewritten captions and extracted depth maps as conditioning inputs. It iteratively denoises video tokens to generate temporally coherent scenes. These synthesized videos retain the original action trajectory and geometric layout but present diversified visual contexts, thereby enriching the training distribution.

**Efficient Video Generation via Velocity Caching:**
To address the substantial computational cost associated with large-scale video diffusion, a velocity caching mechanism is introduced to accelerate the generation process.
- **Observation:** An analysis of flow-based diffusion models reveals that during the denoising process, the predicted velocity field (v_θ(x_t, t)) undergoes distinct phases: an initial phase of rapid change, followed by a stable phase with minimal variation, and a final adjustment phase. Since velocity prediction constitutes over 70% of the per-step runtime, reusing cached velocities during the stable phase can yield efficiency gains.
- **Three-Stage Caching Strategy:** The N-step denoising process is segmented into three stages:
 1. **Initial Phase (t < t_s):** Velocity predictions are computed at every step due to high variability.
 2. **Stable Phase (t_s ≤ t < t_f):** Velocity values are computed only every α steps, and the cached values are reused for the intermediate steps. The onset of the stable phase is determined by a relative smoothness threshold (||v_t - v_t+1|| / ||v_0 - v_1|| < k).
 3. **Adjustment Phase (t ≥ t_f):** Full computation of velocity predictions resumes for the final *m* steps to ensure fine-grained refinement of the synthesized video.
- **Parameters:** Empirical tuning established parameters as k = 0.4, α = 8, and m = 3 adjustment steps. This strategy is reported to achieve an average 61.2% reduction in generation time with minimal impact on quality.

**Selective Augmentation via Coreset Sampling:**
To maximize the utility of augmentation under limited computational resources, a coreset sampling strategy, extended from D$^2$ Pruning, is employed to identify and select trajectories that are both challenging for policy learning and visually diverse.
- **Difficulty Estimation:** The difficulty of each trajectory (s_i) is quantified using the average action prediction loss (L_policy) from a pre-trained VLA policy, such as RDT-1B, computed over a subset of time steps. Higher loss values indicate more challenging trajectories.
- **Diversity Estimation:** Visual and semantic diversity are captured by extracting trajectory embeddings using Cosmos-Embed1 (ϕ(s_i) ∈ R^768). A sparse k-NN graph (k=5) is constructed over these embeddings, with edges weighted by an RBF kernel (e_i,j = exp(−γ_f · ||v_i − v_j||^2), with γ_f = 0.1), to represent the topological structure of the visual space.
- **Scoring via Forward Message Passing:** A refined difficulty score (x_i') is calculated by aggregating the difficulty scores of neighboring trajectories (x_i' = x_i + Σ_j∈N(i) e_i,j · x_j). This emphasizes samples that are challenging and reside within clusters of similarly difficult cases.
- **Coreset Construction with Redundancy Suppression:** A greedy selection algorithm is used:
 1. The trajectory with the highest current score (s* = arg max_i∈S_remaining x_i') is selected and added to the coreset.
 2. The scores of visually similar neighbors of s* are suppressed using a reverse message passing mechanism (x_j' ← x_j' − exp(−γ_r · ||v_s* − v_j||^2) · x_s*', with γ_r = 0.1). This iterative process balances the selection of hard samples with the avoidance of visual redundancy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results across several benchmarks, including Robotwin 2.0, LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and a real robotic platform, to evaluate the efficacy of the proposed data augmentation framework.

**Robotwin 2.0 Experiments:**
- **Single-task learning:** Using RDT-1B, the augmented data led to an average success rate improvement of 10.0% (from 29.0% to 39.0%) in "Hard" scenarios, which include domain-randomized factors. In "Easy" (clean environment) scenarios, a 6.0% increase (from 49.0% to 55.0%) was observed. Similar trends were noted for ACT (average 3.0% improvement in "Hard") and π0 (average 2.0% improvement in "Hard"), demonstrating robustness across different VLA policies.
- **Multi-task learning:** When training RDT-1B on 32 tasks (300 trajectories each), augmenting only 10% of the data using coreset sampling resulted in an average 8.0% improvement in "Hard" scenarios (from 23.0% to 31.0%) across nine representative tasks. This suggests that even a small, strategically augmented subset can significantly enhance generalization.

**LIBERO-Plus and LIBERO Experiments:**
- **LIBERO-Plus (Spatial Suite):** Evaluation on π0 (mixture strategy) and π0.5 (replacement strategy) across 7 perturbation types showed performance enhancements. For π0, an average 5.1% improvement (from 42.7% to 47.8%) was achieved, with notable gains in "objects layout" (+16.6%) and "language instructions" (+22.0%). Some minor performance decreases were observed for "sensor noise," "robot initial states," and "camera viewpoints," which the authors attribute to the current method's primary focus on appearance augmentation rather than geometry or viewpoint changes. For π0.5, an average 1.0% improvement (from 89.8% to 90.8%) was noted, with gains in "light conditions" (+3.2%) and "sensor noise" (+2.2%).
- **LIBERO:** A slight performance drop (average 0.2% for π0 and 0.5% for π0.5) was observed. The authors explain this by noting that LIBERO’s evaluation settings are nearly identical to its original training distribution, meaning aggressive augmentation can introduce distributional disturbances rather than helpful diversity for an already well-performing model.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- Experiments on an AgileX Piper manipulator across two tasks (Stack Tape, Slot Pen) under "In-Distribution," "Position Shift" (OOD), and "Background Shift" (OOD) conditions demonstrated consistent improvements.
- For π0.5, the average success rate increased from 60% to 73% (+13%).
- For π0, the average success rate increased from 60% to 75% (+15%).
- The augmented models exhibited enhanced robustness to both spatial perturbations (Position Shift) and visual domain shifts (Background Shift) while maintaining high in-distribution performance.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Velocity Caching:** Comparison of RDT-1B training with and without velocity caching on Robotwin 2.0 showed comparable accuracies. The caching mechanism reduced video generation time by an average of 61.2% across tasks, indicating its effectiveness in improving efficiency without compromising the policy learning benefits of augmentation.
- **Coreset Sampling:** A t-SNE visualization of video embeddings revealed that selected coreset samples were well-distributed across diverse and challenging clusters of the dataset, avoiding redundancy. Performance analysis on LIBERO-Plus indicated that even a small augmentation ratio (10%) yielded substantial improvements, with diminishing returns beyond 30-50%, demonstrating the efficiency of the sampling strategy.

**Video Transferring Performance Assessment:**
- Compared against RoboTransfer, the proposed method achieved superior geometric consistency (e.g., RMSE for "adjust bottle" improved from 0.46 to 0.28, with 2–6x reductions in Abs.Rel and Sq.Rel metrics across tasks).
- Semantic alignment, measured by prompt-video similarity, consistently increased (from 18.6–24.2 to 25.6–27.7).
- These improvements were achieved with a marginal increase in runtime compared to the baseline.

In summary, the experimental results indicate that the proposed video augmentation framework consistently improves the generalization and robustness of VLA models across various simulation benchmarks and in real-world settings. The efficiency enhancements, particularly velocity caching and coreset sampling, contribute to making this augmentation practical for large-scale application.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for efficient video transfer and data augmentation for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts.

**Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The core significance lies in providing an effective and scalable method to bridge the visual domain gap between simulated and real-world data. By converting inexpensive simulated videos into realistic, visually diverse training data, the framework directly addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: enabling models trained in controlled simulations to generalize robustly to the complexities of physical environments.

**Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across multiple benchmarks (Robotwin 2.0, LIBERO-Plus) and real-world robotic platforms highlight the framework's ability to enhance model generalization. The increased success rates, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios involving object position shifts, lighting changes, and background variations, suggest that VLA models trained with augmented data learn more robust semantic understanding rather than merely memorizing training sequences. This improved robustness is crucial for the reliable deployment of robotic systems in uncontrolled, dynamic real-world settings.

**Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The introduction of the velocity caching mechanism for video generation significantly reduces the inference cost of conditional video diffusion models. This acceleration, reported at over 60% time reduction, makes large-scale video augmentation computationally feasible. Furthermore, the coreset sampling strategy optimizes the utilization of computational resources by intelligently identifying and prioritizing trajectories that are both challenging and diverse. These efficiency improvements are critical for practical application, enabling researchers and practitioners to scale their data augmentation efforts.

**Reduced Data Collection Dependency:** By making simulated data more effective for training, this work potentially reduces the extensive and costly reliance on real-world data collection. This could lower the barriers to entry for robotic research and development, accelerating progress in various application domains without requiring immense resources for data acquisition.

**Model Agnostic Augmentation:** The demonstrated benefits across different VLA model architectures (RDT-1B, ACT, π0, π0.5) indicate that the proposed augmentation framework is model-agnostic. This broad applicability means that the method can be integrated into diverse VLA training pipelines, offering widespread utility across the field.

**Future Research Directions:** This work opens avenues for future research in several areas: further optimizing video generation efficiency, exploring advanced strategies for identifying and synthesizing critical data points, and investigating how to better address geometric and viewpoint-dependent perturbations in addition to appearance-based variations.

In summary, the research offers a practical and effective solution to a critical bottleneck in robotic learning, contributing to the development of more generalizable, robust, and efficiently trained VLA models, with implications for a wide range of robotic applications.
