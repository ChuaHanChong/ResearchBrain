# PhysHanDI: Physics-Based Reconstruction of Hand-Deformable Object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST developed PhysHanDI, a framework that reconstructs dense 3D interactions between hands and non-rigid objects from sparse-view RGB-D videos. It achieved superior accuracy and future prediction over baselines like PhysTwin, particularly on dense contact interactions, by leveraging physics-based simulation and demonstrating reciprocal refinement between hand and object models.

## Problem

- Existing methods for hand-object interaction primarily focused on rigid or part-wise rigid objects, limiting their applicability to highly deformable materials like cloth.
- Approaches for deformable objects often used sparse hand representations (e.g., points from depth maps), leading to imprecise interaction force modeling and suboptimal reconstructed object topology.
- Prior work had not demonstrated that a physics-based deformable object model could, in turn, improve the accuracy of the interacting hand's reconstruction, especially under sparse observational data.

## Method

- **PhysHanDI** employs a ==physics-based interaction model== that uses the parametric ==MANO model== for hands and a custom ==spring-mass model== for deformable objects, with ==virtual springs== dynamically connecting them upon contact.
- The framework processes sparse-view RGB-D videos through a ==three-stage learning pipeline==: initial hand reconstruction, object reconstruction (where physics-based simulation is driven by dense 3D MANO hand geometry), and a final hand refinement stage.
- Interaction forces are derived from the ==dense 3D MANO hand mesh==, allowing for more precise control and deformation modeling compared to methods relying on sparse points from depth maps.

## Results

- PhysHanDI consistently outperformed PhysTwin and other baselines in reconstruction and future prediction tasks, showing superior accuracy (e.g., lower Chamfer Distance) on both the PhysTwin-dense and newly collected DENSEHDI datasets.
- The inverse physics-based hand refinement stage improved hand fitting accuracy, reducing the Hand Chamfer Distance from **7.57 mm** to **7.17 mm** in single-view future prediction scenarios.
- The optimized spring-mass model topology achieved approximately **2x lower** Radius-to-Resolution Deviation (RRD) for object springs and **over 7x lower** RRD for virtual springs compared to PhysTwin, indicating more optimal contact modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using dense 3D hand geometry (from MANO) to drive physics-based object simulation results in more precise interaction force modeling, leading to more accurate object deformation and an optimized spring-mass model topology.
> - The physics-based deformable object model can reciprocally refine the initial hand reconstruction through an inverse physics approach, improving hand accuracy in challenging sparse input scenarios.
> - A hierarchical optimization scheme for object reconstruction, adapted from PhysTwin but with dense hand inputs, successfully models large and complex non-rigid deformations with physical plausibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The modeling and reconstruction of 3D hand-object interactions are recognized as critical for advancing machine perception and reasoning capabilities, with applications in areas such as immersive AR/VR experiences, robot learning from human demonstrations, and teleoperation. Prior research in this domain has demonstrated progress, but largely with limitations.

A significant portion of existing methods for hand-object interaction modeling has focused on interactions with rigid or part-wise rigid objects. While this simplifies the representation of object dynamics to a limited set of rigid transformations, it restricts applicability to many real-world objects that exhibit highly non-rigid deformations, such as cloth or stuffed animals. Some works have addressed part-wise rigid objects, allowing for more expressive deformation than global rigidity, but still do not readily extend to general non-rigid deformations.

For hand-deformable object interactions, fewer studies exist. Methods like HMDO (Xie et al., 2023a) and Qi et al. (2025) primarily model localized deformations resulting from finger pressure and are not designed for large-scale, global non-rigid deformations like twisting or bending. Another approach, PhysTwin (Jiang et al., 2025), is capable of modeling large non-rigid deformations through physical simulation, primarily focusing on the object itself. However, PhysTwin represents the interactee (the hand) using only a sparse set of points sampled from depth maps, which can limit the precision of interaction force modeling and lead to suboptimal model topology reconstruction for simulation due to unobservable contact points from occlusions.

In the broader context of deformable object modeling, dynamic reconstruction-based methods aim to recover 3D representations (e.g., neural radiance fields) from various sensor inputs. These methods typically focus on reconstructing 3D representations that match observed inputs but do not explicitly model physical properties, which limits their utility for future prediction or generalization to unseen interactions. Simulation-based methods, in contrast, facilitate physically plausible dynamics and generalization. Early works in this category often required dense viewpoints or synthetic data. More recent simulation-based approaches utilize sparse-view real RGB-D images, but similar to PhysTwin, they often do not explicitly model the full 3D geometry of the interactee, instead relying on sparse control signals.

PhysHanDI positions itself by directly addressing these identified gaps. It aims to overcome the limitations of rigid object assumptions and the sparse hand representations in deformable object interaction modeling. By enabling dense 3D reconstruction of *both* hands and non-rigid objects and incorporating physics-based simulation driven by these dense hand motions, the work seeks to provide a more comprehensive and accurate framework for complex hand-deformable object interactions. It also introduces a novel aspect where the physics-based object model can, in turn, refine the hand reconstruction, establishing a mutual benefit.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named PhysHanDI, that enables the full 3D reconstruction of both interacting hands and non-rigid objects from sparse-view RGB-D videos. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the gap in interaction modeling**: Current methods for reconstructing hand-object interactions predominantly focus on rigid or part-wise rigid objects. This limits their applicability to a wide range of real-world objects that exhibit complex, non-rigid deformations (e.g., cloth, stuffed animals). While some methods address deformable objects, they often reconstruct the object without a complete 3D representation of the interacting hand, or are limited to small, localized deformations. PhysHanDI aims to bridge this gap by offering a solution that handles highly non-rigid object deformations in conjunction with full 3D hand reconstruction.

2. **Ensuring physical plausibility and coherence**: The proposed framework is motivated by the need for object dynamics to be both physically plausible and coherent with the interacting hand movements. To achieve this, PhysHanDI's central idea is to physically simulate object deformations. This simulation is explicitly driven by forces induced from densely reconstructed 3D hand motions, rather than sparse approximations. This direct coupling ensures that the reconstructed interaction reflects realistic physical behavior and maintains consistency between the hand's actions and the object's deformation.

3. **Enhancing reconstruction accuracy through inverse physics**: A specific motivation for PhysHanDI is to demonstrate that the simulation of object deformations can reciprocally refine and improve the reconstruction of the interacting hand. This is achieved through an inverse physics approach, where the physics-based object model is leveraged to optimize the initial hand reconstruction. This is a novel aspect of the work, as previous methods have not shown that a deformable object model can enhance hand reconstruction accuracy, particularly in challenging scenarios with sparse input data.

4. **Advancing state-of-the-art performance**: The authors are motivated to develop a system that outperforms existing methods, particularly the most relevant state-of-the-art approaches like PhysTwin, in terms of reconstruction accuracy and future prediction capabilities for hand-deformable object interactions. This includes addressing limitations in how interaction forces are modeled (e.g., PhysTwin's reliance on sparse depth map points for the interactee).

In summary, the key objectives are to provide dense, physically plausible, and coherent 3D reconstructions of both hands and deformable objects, to introduce a mechanism for inverse physics-based hand refinement, and to achieve improved performance over current methods, thereby enabling more robust modeling of complex human-object interactions for various applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysHanDI is presented as a physics-based framework designed for the dense 3D reconstruction of interacting hands and non-rigid objects from sparse-view RGB-D videos. The methodology comprises two main components: a physics-based interaction model and a three-stage learning pipeline for reconstruction.

#### 3.1. Physics-Based Interaction Modeling

The framework models hands and objects using established representations and defines their interaction through a custom spring-mass simulation driven by hand motions.

- **Hand Representation**: Each hand is represented by the **MANO model** (Romero et al., 2017), a parametric PCA-based model. It takes pose (θ ∈ R<sup>45</sup>), shape (β ∈ R<sup>10</sup>), global rotation (R ∈ SO(3)), and translation (t ∈ R<sup>3</sup>) parameters to generate a dense 3D hand mesh. This low-dimensional parameter space helps constrain the solution space for hand reconstruction.
- **Object Representation**: Each deformable object is modeled using a **Spring-Mass model** (Liu et al., 2013; Jiang et al., 2025). This model represents an object as a graph of *N* mass nodes (nᵢ) connected by *E* springs. Each node has a position (xᵢ), velocity (vᵢ), and mass (mᵢ). The dynamics are governed by forces acting on each node: spring forces (Fˢᵖʳⁱⁿᵍ), dashpot damping forces (Fᵈᵃᵐᵖⁱⁿᵍ), and external forces (Fᵉˣᵗᵉʳⁿᵃˡ) like gravity. Node positions are updated by numerically integrating Newton's second law. Unit mass is assigned to all nodes.
- **Hand-Deformable Object Interaction Modeling**: Interaction is modeled by simulating object deformation via the spring-mass system. Virtual springs are established between object nodes and MANO hand vertices when contact is detected within a specified connection radius (δ). The positions and velocities of these virtual hand nodes, derived from the reconstructed MANO mesh, are treated as fixed boundary conditions. During simulation, these virtual springs ensure that object regions in contact with the hand deform smoothly and coherently with the hand's motion, as the spring and damping forces work to maintain the contact topology. This mechanism supports the modeling of large and complex non-rigid deformations while ensuring physical plausibility.

#### 3.2. Learning from Sparse-View RGB-D Videos

The reconstruction process from sparse-view RGB-D video inputs is structured into three sequential stages:

1. **Hand Reconstruction**:
 - **Objective**: To fit the MANO hand model parameters (Θʰ = {θ, β, R, t}) to the multi-view RGB-D video data for each frame.
 - **Loss Function**: A combination of three terms:
 - **L₂D**: Measures the reprojection error between projected MANO keypoints and 2D keypoint supervision (U). For robust supervision, this term incorporates data from both an off-the-shelf estimator (MediaPipe) and a monocular MANO parameter estimator (Dong et al., 2024), with a weighted contribution.
 - **L_d**: Quantifies the discrepancy between the rendered MANO depth and the observed depth maps (D).
 - **L_t**: A regularization term to enforce temporal smoothness of MANO parameters relative to the previous frame.
 - **Optimization**: AdamW optimizer is used for 1500 steps, with an initial learning rate of 2 × 10⁻³ and a decay factor of 0.98 every 40 steps. Parameters for each frame are initialized from the previous frame's results, with the first frame randomly initialized.

2. **Object Reconstruction**:
 - **Objective**: To fit the spring-mass model parameters for the deformable object, conditioned on the previously reconstructed 3D hands. This stage largely follows the hierarchical optimization scheme from PhysTwin.
 - **Stages**:
 - **Sparse (zero-order) stage**: Optimizes coarse, non-differentiable parameters (Θ₀), including the spring-mass topology (connection radius δ and maximum connected nodes dₘₐₓ), a global spring stiffness (sᵍˡᵒᵇᵃˡ), and collision parameters (η). Zero-order optimization is performed for 100 iterations.
 - **Dense (first-order) stage**: Refines differentiable per-spring stiffness (sᵢⱼ) and damping (γᵢⱼ) parameters (Θ₁). The Adam optimizer is used for 200 iterations with an initial learning rate of 1 × 10⁻³.
 - **Loss Function**: Minimizes the sum of two terms over all timesteps:
 - **L_ch**: Chamfer distance between simulated object node positions and observed 3D point clouds lifted from input depth maps.
 - **L_tr**: L₂ loss between simulated object node positions and pseudo-ground-truth 3D points tracked by CoTracker3 (Karaev et al., 2024).
 - **Distinction from PhysTwin**: Crucially, PhysHanDI's spring-mass simulations derive interaction forces from the *dense 3D hand geometry* provided by the MANO model, unlike PhysTwin which approximates these forces using sparse points sampled from input depth maps. This difference is posited to lead to more precise interaction force modeling and a more optimal reconstructed model topology.

3. **Hand Refinement**:
 - **Objective**: To further refine the initially fitted MANO hand parameters by leveraging the physics-based object model reconstructed in the previous stage. This stage uses inverse physics.
 - **Loss Function**: The same L_ch and L_tr losses from the object reconstruction stage are used to measure the discrepancy between simulated object nodes (driven by the current hand parameters) and ground-truth observations.
 - **Optimization**: Adam optimizer performs 40 optimization steps. MANO parameters are initialized from the hand reconstruction stage, with a learning rate of 2 × 10⁻⁵, decaying by 0.99 per iteration.
 - **Significance**: This inverse physics-based refinement enforces that the reconstructed hands produce object simulations that are more consistent with the input observations. It is particularly effective for sparse input settings (e.g., single-view future prediction) and is presented as the first work demonstrating that a physics-based deformable object prior can improve hand reconstruction accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PhysHanDI was conducted across several tasks and datasets, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art physics-based object reconstruction methods.

#### 4.1. Experiment Settings

- **Datasets**:
 - **PhysTwin dataset (Jiang et al., 2025)**: Comprising three-view RGB-D videos of hand-deformable object interactions.
 - **PhysTwin-dense dataset**: A subset of the PhysTwin dataset curated to exclude sequences with only sparse, point-based hand-object contacts, focusing on scenarios with more meaningful deformation.
 - **DENSEHDI dataset (Newly collected)**: 19 sequences with 10 new objects, specifically designed to capture denser hand-object contacts. This dataset will be released.
- **Tasks**: (1) Reconstruction & Resimulation, (2) Future Prediction, and (3) Generalization to unseen interactions (reported in supplementary material).
- **Baselines**: PhysTwin (Jiang et al., 2025), Spring-Gaus (Zhong et al., 2024), and GS-Dynamics (Zhang et al., 2024a).
- **Metrics**:
 - **3D metrics**: Chamfer Distance (CD), including CD_dyn (for dynamically deforming regions) and CD_full (for all object points), and Tracking Error (Track Err.).
 - **2D metrics**: Intersection over Union (IoU) and Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), which leverage surrogate Gaussian splats for rendering.
 - **Hand CD**: Chamfer Distance between fitted hand meshes and ground-truth 3D hand point clouds (for hand accuracy evaluation).

#### 4.2. Experimental Comparisons

- **Reconstruction & Resimulation (Multi-View RGB-D Inputs)**:
 - On the **PhysTwin-dense dataset** (Table 1), PhysHanDI demonstrated superior performance across all metrics (CD_dyn, CD_full, Track Err. lower; IoU, PSNR higher) compared to all baselines, including PhysTwin.
 - On the newly collected **DENSEHDI dataset** (Table 2), PhysHanDI also outperformed PhysTwin on most metrics, validating its effectiveness in scenarios involving dense hand-object interactions.
 - Qualitative results (Figure 3) indicated that PhysHanDI produced more accurate object simulations. Spring-Gaus exhibited unstable simulations with broken object geometry, and GS-Dynamics failed to capture meaningful deformation in shorter sequences. PhysTwin's approximations of interaction forces from sparse points led to less accurate simulations.

- **Future Prediction (Multi-View RGB-D Inputs)**:
 - PhysHanDI consistently outperformed Spring-Gaus and GS-Dynamics by a substantial margin, and PhysTwin on most metrics, for future prediction on both the **PhysTwin-dense** and **DENSEHDI** datasets (Tables 1 and 2, right sections). Qualitative comparisons further supported this, showing more accurate future predictions.

- **Future Prediction (Single-View RGB-D Inputs)**:
 - In this challenging scenario (Table 3), PhysTwin was observed to frequently fail in object simulation due to errors in identifying hand-object contact points from highly partial single-view depth data, and thus was not included in direct comparisons.
 - PhysHanDI robustly addressed single-view future prediction.
 - The **hand refinement stage** of PhysHanDI proved significantly effective: it improved all object reconstruction metrics (CD, Track Err. lower; IoU, PSNR higher) and noticeably enhanced hand fitting accuracy (Hand CD decreased from 7.57 to 7.17 mm). This result is presented as the first demonstration of a physics-based deformable object prior improving hand reconstruction.

- **Robustness Analysis**:
 - Under perturbed input signals (1 mm depth noise, 1 px CoTracker perturbation, 10 mm MPJPE hand pose perturbation) (Table 4), PhysHanDI exhibited *noticeably smaller performance degradations* across all settings compared to PhysTwin. This robustness is attributed to PhysHanDI's use of denser hand reconstruction, providing more reliable contact cues and mitigating the impact of upstream noise.

- **Contact Topology Analysis**:
 - A numerical analysis comparing the optimized spring-mass model topology (Sec. 4.4, Table 5, Figure 4) revealed that PhysHanDI yielded approximately 2x lower Radius-to-Resolution Deviation (RRD) for object springs and over 7x lower RRD for virtual springs compared to PhysTwin. This suggests PhysHanDI's model topology is more optimal, consistent with principles from peridynamics.
 - PhysHanDI's dense hand reconstruction facilitated precise contact localization without excessive virtual spring elongation, unlike PhysTwin's sparse control points. This resulted in control forces concentrated at actual contacts, favoring local and detailed manipulation.

- **Additional Supplementary Results**:
 - **PhysTwin-full dataset** (Table 6): PhysHanDI generally outperformed baselines even on this dataset, which includes sparse-contact sequences, with larger gains observed on dense-contact sequences (Table 7).
 - **Generalization to Unseen Interactions** (Table 8): PhysHanDI demonstrated superior generalizability compared to PhysTwin on all metrics.
 - **Contact Consistency Analysis** (Table 9): PhysHanDI achieved higher Contact Accuracy at both 5mm and 10mm distance thresholds, indicating more consistent and precisely localized contact estimation.
 - **Sensitivity to Initial Hyperparameters** (Table 10): PhysHanDI showed robustness within a reasonable range of initial connection radius and maximum connected nodes.
 - **Computational Cost** (Table 11): While PhysHanDI incurred additional runtime for hand reconstruction and refinement, its object reconstruction stages and inference-time simulation were slightly faster than PhysTwin, potentially due to a more optimal spring-mass topology.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents PhysHanDI as a notable advancement in the field of 3D reconstruction of hand-object interactions, particularly for scenarios involving highly non-rigid objects. The work addresses a recognized limitation in prior research, which often focused on rigid objects or modeled deformable objects without fully reconstructing the interacting hand.

The primary significance of PhysHanDI lies in its capability to perform *dense 3D reconstruction of both interacting hands and non-rigid objects simultaneously* from sparse-view RGB-D videos. This comprehensive modeling provides a more complete and accurate representation of complex human-object interactions. By physically simulating object deformations driven by forces derived from densely reconstructed 3D hand motions, the framework ensures that the reconstructed object dynamics are both physically plausible and inherently coherent with the interacting hand movements. This direct integration of detailed hand motion into the physical simulation process represents an improvement over methods that rely on sparse or approximated hand representations.

A distinct contribution and significant impact of this work is the demonstration that the physics-based object model, once reconstructed, can be utilized through inverse physics to *refine and improve the accuracy of the hand reconstruction*. This establishes a mutual benefit between hand and object modeling, which is a novel finding in this research area. The effectiveness of this inverse physics-based hand refinement is particularly evident in challenging conditions such as single-view input, suggesting a path towards more robust hand pose estimation in partially observed scenarios.

The experimental results, including comparisons against state-of-the-art baselines, indicate PhysHanDI's superior performance in reconstruction accuracy, future prediction, and generalization to unseen interactions. The robustness analysis further highlights the method's ability to maintain performance under noisy input signals, which is important for real-world applications where data quality can vary. The detailed contact topology analysis provides a theoretical basis for PhysHanDI's improved simulation accuracy by demonstrating that its dense hand representation leads to a more optimal spring-mass model topology.

The potential impact of this work extends to several application domains. In **embodied AI** and **robotics**, a more accurate understanding of hand-deformable object interactions can facilitate learning from human demonstrations and improve robot manipulation capabilities for complex tasks involving non-rigid items. For **digital human modeling** and **computer graphics**, the ability to reconstruct detailed, physically consistent interactions between hands and deformable objects can lead to more realistic animations and virtual character interactions. In **immersive AR/VR experiences**, accurate real-time reconstruction of such interactions is fundamental for creating compelling and believable virtual environments where users can naturally interact with virtual deformable content.

The authors note that the approach does not introduce new data modalities or privacy risks beyond those already inherent in existing vision-based reconstruction systems. However, recognized limitations include the current assumption of a static hand-object contact topology within a sequence and the interaction force modeling acting as a boundary condition rather than accounting for actual finger pressure, indicating avenues for future research.
