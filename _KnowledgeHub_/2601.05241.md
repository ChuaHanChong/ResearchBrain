# RoboVIP: Multi-View Video Generation with Visual Identity Prompting Augments Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboVIP augments robot manipulation data by inpainting multi-view videos with a LoRA-tuned Wan2.1 video diffusion model. It masks robot and interacted objects via an action-guided segmentation pipeline, then regenerates backgrounds and tabletops conditioned on text plus visual identity prompting, exemplar images drawn from an automatically curated million-scale pool. Training VLA and visuomotor policies on the augmented data raises success rates in SimplerEnv and on a real Franka arm.

## Problem

- Prior generative augmentation inpaints single frames from a single view, mismatching modern policies that consume temporal history and multi-view observations for spatial cues.
- Text prompts in robotics datasets are terse and VLM captions hallucinate, so language alone cannot specify tabletop layout or low-level appearance detail of a scene.
- Pixel-aligned conditioning like edges or depth restricts edits to appearance level, while real-to-sim-to-real and green-screen pipelines need heavy manual setup per environment.

## Method

- ==Action-guided segmentation== uses gripper-state change to bound the interaction window, names the object with a video-reasoning ==VLM==, then propagates open-vocabulary masks through the video with ==SAM2==.
- A multi-view inpainting diffusion model stitches views vertically per timestamp, channel-concatenates the masked video, and ==LoRA==-tunes ==Wan2.1-I2V== 720p plus a trainable patchification layer.
- **Visual identity prompting** packs several panoptic-segmented exemplar crops into one frame, VAE-encodes and frame-wise concatenates them as dropped-at-loss context; an agentic quality, sharpness, ==CLIP== and resolution filter builds the pool.

## Results

- On Droid held-out **300** cases RoboVIP reaches FID **39.97**, FVD **138.4** and **2242.1** cross-view matches, versus Cosmos-Transfer2.5 at **47.43**/**325.4**/**1583.4** and RoboEngine at **62.77**/**1788.8**/**1301.9**.
- In SimplerEnv, Octo with identity-conditioned augmentation averages **18.5%** success against **12.8%** for Bridge SFT, while pi-0 reaches **29.0%** versus **17.25%** SFT and **18.5%** for RoboEngine.
- Real Franka cube stacking with Diffusion Policy goes **10/10** open-space and **9/10** cluttered, versus **7/10** and **0/10** unaugmented; raters preferred identity conditioning in **97.3%** of pairs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video-level augmentation scales with temporal context where image-level augmentation degrades: the baseline collapses to near-zero success at six history frames while video-generated data holds up.
> - Exemplar images convey low-level appearance and layout that text prompts cannot, and the injected clutter acts as free distractor curriculum improving robustness to unseen background objects.
> - Cheap action signals such as a one-dimensional gripper state can rescue off-the-shelf perception models that fail on narrow-field, fast-moving wrist-camera views.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is heavily reliant on diverse, high-quality visual data to train robust and effective policies. However, collecting such data in the real world is a formidable challenge, constrained by hardware costs, complex physical setups, precise calibration requirements, and the need for synchronized multi-modal sensing. These factors severely limit the scalability and environmental diversity of real-world robotics datasets.

To address this data scarcity, researchers have increasingly turned to generative models. Early approaches included traditional data augmentations like cropping or rotation, but these offer limited improvements in diversity. More sophisticated methods have involved manual interventions, such as using green screens for background replacement or employing complex real-to-sim-to-real pipelines that require significant manual effort and struggle with generalization.

A burgeoning area of research utilizes image diffusion models to augment visual observations. These methods typically involve segmenting the robot arm and interacted objects, then using text-prompt-guided generative models to "inpaint" (fill in) the masked regions, thereby diversifying backgrounds and tabletop contents. Other approaches, like Cosmos-Transfer and RoboTransfer, leverage pixel-aligned conditions (e.g., edges, depth, segmentation masks) to guide generative models for appearance-level editing.

RoboVIP critically identifies and addresses several key limitations of these existing generative data augmentation techniques:

1. **Single-View and Single-Frame Constraint:** Many prior generative augmentation methods operate in a single-frame, single-view setting. However, state-of-the-art robot policy models increasingly rely on *multi-view* observations and *temporal coherence* across frames for effective 3D reasoning and understanding long-horizon tasks. A policy executing a task might need to distinguish between pre-interaction and post-interaction states of an object that appear visually identical in a single frame, requiring historical context.
2. **Limitations of Text-Only Prompting:** Text prompts, while flexible, are often simplistic, lack fine-grained detail, or suffer from "hallucinations" and misalignment when generated by Vision-Language Models (VLMs). They struggle to reliably specify intricate scene setups or capture low-level visual attributes, thus limiting the fidelity and diversity of generated content.

RoboVIP positions itself as a significant advancement by moving beyond these limitations. It introduces a multi-view *video* generation framework that maintains temporal consistency, directly supporting the needs of modern robot policy learning architectures. Furthermore, it pioneers "visual identity prompting," which provides explicit visual guidance through exemplar images, overcoming the vagueness and limitations of text-only conditioning. This approach allows for richer, more semantically consistent, and diverse scene generation, marking a crucial step towards truly plug-and-play and scalable visual data augmentation for robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the RoboVIP project is to fundamentally enhance robot manipulation capabilities by addressing the persistent challenge of data scarcity and lack of diversity in visual observations. The core aim is to **develop a scalable, plug-and-play multi-view video generation augmentation framework that can diversify backgrounds and tabletop scenes in robotic manipulation datasets.**

This primary objective is driven by several specific motivations and goals:

1. **Overcome Data Collection Bottlenecks:** The authors acknowledge that collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality real-world manipulation data is inherently difficult, time-consuming, and expensive due to hardware, calibration, and synchronization complexities. RoboVIP seeks to offer a complementary, automated solution through generative AI.
2. **Meet the Demands of Modern Policy Models:** Contemporary robot policy models (e.g., Octo, π0, Diffusion Policy) require not just individual diverse images, but *multi-view* and *temporally coherent* sequences of observations to perform effectively. Prior generative augmentation methods often fall short in providing this critical video-level consistency, leading to indecisive or poor robot behaviors. RoboVIP aims to fill this gap by generating complete video sequences.
3. **Enhance Generative Model Control and Fidelity:** The authors identify that text prompts alone are insufficient for reliably specifying complex scene setups and low-level visual details, often resulting in generic or inconsistent generations. A key motivation is to introduce a more explicit and reliable conditioning mechanism. This leads to the proposal of **visual identity prompting**, where exemplar images serve as direct visual guidance to ensure the generated content is both semantically rich and visually consistent with desired attributes.
4. **Automate and Scale Data Curation:** To ensure the practical utility and scalability of visual identity prompting, RoboVIP is motivated to develop an automated pipeline for curating a large-scale pool of visual identities from existing robotics datasets, eliminating the need for manual human intervention.
5. **Demonstrate Practical Effectiveness:** Ultimately, the motivation is to prove that this augmented data can lead to tangible and consistent performance gains for downstream robot policy learning, both in simulated environments and on real-world robot deployments, thereby validating RoboVIP's utility for large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and visuomotor policy training.

In essence, RoboVIP is motivated by the vision of enabling robots to learn more robustly and generalize better by leveraging generative AI to autonomously create a wealth of realistic, diverse, and functionally relevant visual experiences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboVIP presents a sophisticated, multi-stage pipeline designed to augment robotic manipulation data by generating multi-view, temporally coherent videos with diverse visual content. The workflow encompasses data curation, diverse visual augmentation, and downstream VLA model training (Figure 1).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The core problem is formulated as learning a conditional joint distribution for generating multi-view latent video frames $I = \{I_0..., I_N\}$ given masked multi-view videos $M = \{M_0..., M_N\}$, a text prompt $y$, and visual identity prompts $f = \{f_1..., f_k\}$. The original action sequences are directly reused with the augmented visual observations.

#### 3.2. Action-Guided Segmentation of Robots and Interacted Objects

A critical first step is to accurately segment the robot arm and the objects it interacts with, allowing the generative model to inpaint the surrounding scene. Traditional off-the-shelf segmentation models often fail in dynamic, narrow field-of-view robot settings. RoboVIP proposes an **action-oriented segmentation pipeline** (Figure 2) to mitigate these issues:

1. **Gripper-Action-Triggered Keyframe Detection:** The 1D gripper state (open/close) provides crucial temporal cues for interaction. The pipeline identifies the frame range corresponding to gripper-closure intervals in the wrist camera view, effectively localizing the preparation and execution of object interaction. A temporal buffer is applied to these intervals to encompass the full interaction window.
2. **Object Naming and Open-Vocabulary Segmentation:** The extracted video clip from the wrist view is fed into a video-reasoning VLM (Cosmos-Reason1) to infer the object's semantic label. This label is then reused for other third-person camera views. An open-vocabulary segmentation model (EVF-SAM from RoboEngine) uses this object name to generate reliable masks.
3. **Robot Arm Segmentation:** For the robot arm itself, a fixed query "robot" is used with the open-vocabulary segmentation model. Five frames are uniformly sampled, and the one with the largest robot arm masking region is selected to ensure the robot is visible.
4. **Mask Refinement and Temporal Consistency:** Separately extracted robot and object masks undergo median blurring to filter outliers. To achieve temporal consistency across video frames, k-means sampling is performed on the masks, generating keypoints that serve as prompts for a video segmentation model (SAM2) to track the complete video segmentation. The refined robot and object masks are then merged.

#### 3.3. Multi-View Inpainting Video Diffusion Model

RoboVIP leverages a **multi-view inpainting video diffusion model** (Figure 3) capable of generating temporally consistent and cross-view aligned video sequences:

1. **Base Model and Fine-Tuning:** The base architecture is a Wan2.1 image-to-video variant, a large 14-billion parameter model. To enable computationally feasible fine-tuning and prevent catastrophic forgetting, the authors employ a **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** strategy. LoRA injects trainable low-rank adapters into the linear projections of attention blocks. Additionally, the patchification layer of the model, which transforms latent images into patches, is also fine-tuned, an empirical finding that yields slight performance improvements.
2. **Multi-View Input Handling:** To support multi-view inputs, a structured vertical stitching strategy is adopted, concatenating masked frames from different views at the same timestamp. This ensures view-aligned learning and encourages the model to capture cross-view spatial consistency. The base model's input structure is modified by replacing single-image padding with channel-wise concatenation of the full video sequence, providing a minimally invasive but effective video-conditioned objective.
3. **Conditioning:** The model is conditioned on the segmented multi-view video sequence, a structured text prompt (generated by Qwen2.5-VL for both scene setup and action description), and the proposed visual identity prompting.

#### 3.4. Visual Identity Prompting

This is a novel component for providing explicit visual guidance, addressing the limitations of text-only prompts:

1. **Agentic Curation Pipeline (Figure 4):** To create a large-scale, diverse, and automatically curated visual identity pool, the pipeline uses a panoptic segmentation model (OneFormer) on large robotics datasets (BridgeV1/V2, Droid). It automatically identifies and extracts common tabletop objects and background elements by filtering panoptic segmentation class labels (e.g., cups, bottles, bowls, chairs, etc., excluding large background elements). This process generates a million-scale visual identity pool.
2. **Quality Filtering:** Extracted object images are often suboptimal. To improve quality, cropped identity images undergo several filtering steps:
 - **Background Mask Coverage:** Identities where the masked background covers more than 60% of the bounding box are discarded.
 - **Image Quality Assessment:** CLIP-IQA is used to remove the lowest 50% based on overall image quality.
 - **Resolution Size:** Extremes (smallest and largest 10%) are discarded to exclude tiny or disproportionately large objects.
 - **Sharpness Clarity:** The variance of the Laplacian on grayscale images filters out the lowest 30% for sharpness.
 - **CLIP-based Semantic Completeness:** CLIP text-image scoring (using the panoptic segmentation class label) filters out 60% of samples with the weakest similarity, ensuring semantic completeness.
3. **Integration into Diffusion Model:** Multiple visual identity references are efficiently packed into a single image frame (randomly resized to prevent scale overfitting). During training, identity images are sampled from a single view to avoid ambiguity. The packed identity images are encoded by a shared causal VAE encoder and concatenated with the latent video segmentation inputs along the frame dimension. The identity tokens are dropped from loss computation, ensuring they serve as contextual guidance without being optimization targets. During inference, new encoded identity images are injected at each diffusion timestep.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboVIP's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated across quantitative generative metrics, simulation-based robot policy learning, and real-world robot experiments.

#### 4.1. Video Generation Results

- **Quantitative Performance (Table 2):** RoboVIP consistently outperforms existing methods, Cosmos-Transfer2.5 and RoboEngine, on standard generative video metrics using the Droid dataset. It achieves the best FID (39.97 vs 47.43 and 62.77), FVD (138.4 vs 325.4 and 1788.8), and a strong LPIPS (0.409). Crucially, its **MV-Mat. score (2242.1)** for cross-view correspondence is significantly higher than competitors (1583.4 and 1301.9), indicating superior spatial consistency across multiple generated views.
- **Qualitative Performance (Figure 5, 12):** Qualitatively, RoboVIP produces temporally consistent and visually diverse results. Compared to RoboEngine (an image-based method), RoboVIP exhibits superior temporal coherence. Against Cosmos-Transfer2.5 (pixel-aligned conditions), RoboVIP unleashes diverse scene generation beyond appearance-level editing, thanks to its inpainting design and visual identity prompting. The ability to generate long-horizon, zero-shot real-world robot augmentations with continuously changing tabletop textures and diverse backgrounds is also demonstrated (Figure 12).

#### 4.2. Simulation Results

The augmented data generated by RoboVIP is used to train two prominent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, Octo-base and π0, evaluated on the SimplerEnv simulation benchmark (Table 1).

- **Performance Gains for Octo:**
 - RoboVIP (Text+ID) achieves an average success rate of **18.5%**, significantly outperforming Octo zero-shot (12.2%), Octo SFT on BridgeV2 (12.8%), and even its own text-only variant (13.0%).
 - The "Put" success rate (conditional success given a successful grasp) is particularly improved, with RoboVIP (Text+ID) achieving **41.1%** compared to Octo SFT's 23.0%, indicating enhanced closed-loop control and task completion reliability.
- **Performance Gains for π0:**
 - RoboVIP (Text-only) yields the highest overall success rate at **29.0%**, surpassing the SFT baseline (17.25%) and RoboEngine (18.5%). The Text+ID variant performs similarly at 27.75%.
 - Again, the "Put" success rate is notably higher, with RoboVIP (Text-only) achieving **55.0%** compared to the SFT baseline's 43.1%.
- **Impact of History Length (Figure 7):** RoboVIP consistently maintains higher success rates across varying conditioned history lengths for Octo, outperforming RoboEngine, whose performance collapses at longer history lengths (e.g., six frames). This highlights RoboVIP's robustness to longer temporal contexts, emphasizing the benefit of video-level augmentation.

These results indicate that RoboVIP's generated data, with its temporal consistency, multi-view alignment, and scene diversity from visual identity prompts, effectively enriches the training set, allowing policies to learn stronger generalizations and more reliable task execution.

#### 4.3. Real-World Robot Results

Experiments on a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 robotic arm for a cube stacking task, using Diffusion Policy (DP), demonstrate RoboVIP's real-world efficacy (Figure 8, Table 3, Figure 11).

- **Robustness to Distractors:** The baseline DP model's success rate dropped from 7/10 in an "Open space" (clean background) to **0/10** in a "Cluttered" scene (with unseen distractors). In stark contrast, DP + RoboVIP achieved a perfect **10/10** success in the open space and maintained a robust **9/10** success rate in the cluttered setting.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** When compared to other augmentation methods, DP + RoboVIP significantly outperformed DP + RoboEngine (8/10 open, 2/10 cluttered) and DP + Cosmos-Transfer2.5 (3/10 open, 3/10 cluttered) in real-world settings (Table 3).
- **Qualitative Real-World Validation (Figure 11):** Visualizations of real-world rollouts show that baseline methods fail to grasp or stably place the cube in cluttered environments, while the policy augmented by RoboVIP successfully grasps, transports, and precisely stacks the blue cube amidst distractors.

#### 4.4. User Study on Visual Identity Prompting

A user study comparing visual-identity-conditioned generation against a text-only variant confirmed the benefits of visual identity prompting (Section 8.2). Raters preferred the visual-identity–conditioned generation in **97.3%** of comparisons for "faithfully incorporating the provided visual identity" and in **80.0%** of comparisons for presenting a "richer tabletop in visual content."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboVIP represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation and generative AI, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck in Robotics:** The most immediate and critical impact is its ability to **alleviate the pervasive data scarcity problem** in robotic manipulation. By providing a scalable, automated framework for generating diverse, multi-view, and temporally consistent video data, RoboVIP bypasses the high costs and logistical challenges of real-world data collection, democratizing access to large-scale training data.
2. **Elevating Robot Policy Learning:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across state-of-the-art VLA (Octo, π0) and visuomotor (Diffusion Policy) models, in both simulation and real-world environments, underscore RoboVIP's capacity to produce **more robust, generalizable, and reliable robot policies**. The significant improvements in "Put" success rates and robustness to cluttered scenes signify better closed-loop control and enhanced task completion reliability.
3. **Advancing Generative AI for Embodied AI:** RoboVIP pushes the boundaries of video generation by introducing **visual identity prompting**. This novel conditioning mechanism offers finer-grained control and richer semantic detail than text-only prompts, enabling generative models to synthesize more realistic and diverse scenes specifically tailored for embodied AI tasks. The multi-view, temporally coherent video generation capability is crucial for models that rely on spatial and temporal reasoning.
4. **Enabling Scalable and Plug-and-Play Augmentation:** The automated segmentation pipeline and the agentic curation of a million-scale visual identity pool make RoboVIP a truly **plug-and-play and scalable solution**. This ease of integration and automation reduces the human effort required for data augmentation, making it accessible for broader research and practical deployment.
5. **Accelerating Sim-to-Real Transfer and Generalization:** By generating augmented data that closely approximates real-world distributions and introduces controlled clutter, RoboVIP can potentially **bridge the sim-to-real gap** and improve policy generalization to unseen real-world conditions. This is evident in the remarkable performance improvement in cluttered real-world scenes.
6. **Paving the Way for Long-Horizon Robotics:** The demonstrated robustness of RoboVIP's augmentation even with longer historical frame conditioning suggests that video-level generative augmentation is a **forward-looking direction** for tackling complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks that require extensive temporal reasoning.
7. **Future Research Directions:** While powerful, the paper candidly acknowledges limitations, such as challenges with existing segmentation tools (gripper localization, flickering), VLM reasoning failures for interactive objects, and multi-view consistency in open-vocabulary segmentation. These identified limitations highlight critical areas for future research and development, providing a clear roadmap for the community.

In conclusion, RoboVIP is a timely and impactful contribution that leverages advanced generative AI to address a core challenge in robotics. Its innovations in multi-view video generation, visual identity prompting, and automated data curation offer a powerful toolkit for accelerating the development of more capable, robust, and autonomous robotic systems.
