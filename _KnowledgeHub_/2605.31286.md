# DeMaVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Foundation Model for Generalizable Deformable Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at AIRC, Midea Group, along with Tongji University, developed DeMaVLA, a Vision-Language-Action foundation model for generalizable deformable manipulation. This single-checkpoint policy achieved a 92.5% average success rate in real-world multi-category clothing folding, outperforming prior methods, and demonstrated robust generalization across diverse tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with generalizing manipulation skills for deformable objects (e.g., clothing) across diverse categories, physical states, and task conditions in real-world household robotics.
- Developing category-specific policies for each deformable object (e.g., different clothing items) is not scalable or practical for household environments due to the vast number of items and initial states.
- Naively training a single policy on mixed multi-task data often leads to performance degradation due to task interference, as different categories may require incompatible intermediate states or motion patterns.

## Method

- **DeMaVLA** employs a VLM-conditioned action-generation architecture with Qwen3-VL as its backbone and a layer-aligned, pruned action expert for efficient, continuous bimanual action prediction using conditional ==flow matching==.
- The model undergoes extensive pre-training on approximately 5,000 hours of real-world dual-arm demonstration data to acquire broad manipulation priors, followed by post-training with Training-time Real-Time Chunking (RTC) to mitigate inference latency.
- A human-in-the-loop Data Aggregation (DAgger) pipeline is utilized for post-training, where human interventions correct failure modes encountered by the mixed folding policy on real robots, providing targeted supervision for robustness.

## Results

- DeMaVLA achieved the highest average success rates in simulation (RoboTwin **2.0**) with **88.42%** in clean settings and **86.78%** in randomized settings, outperforming other VLA foundation models.
- In a real-world household folding benchmark, DeMaVLA achieved an average success rate of **92.5%** across four diverse clothing items (shirt, skirt, pants, towel) when trained on mixed data, surpassing the π₀ baseline (**76.3%**).
- The pre-training scaling analysis showed that increasing real-world pre-training data to 5,000 hours resulted in **100%** success rate for real-world shirt folding after post-training, compared to **55%** with 500 hours.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single-checkpoint VLA policy can effectively learn and share folding priors across multiple deformable object categories (e.g., shirts, pants, towels) while executing diverse folding skills without significant task interference.
> - Large-scale real-world dual-arm pre-training data is crucial for acquiring general manipulation priors, with a clear scaling trend showing that increasing data from 500 to 5,000 hours significantly improves downstream task success rate and efficiency (e.g., from 55% SR to 100% SR for shirt folding).
> - Integrating a human-in-the-loop DAgger pipeline with continuous post-training effectively targets and corrects policy failure modes, substantially enhancing robustness for complex, long-horizon deformable object manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the emerging field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models, which aim to develop general-purpose robot agents capable of interpreting visual observations and language instructions to perform physical actions. Previous VLA systems have demonstrated the potential to transfer semantic knowledge from large vision-language models (VLMs) to physical control. However, a significant challenge in real-world household robotics, particularly for tasks involving deformable objects, has been the generalization of manipulation skills across diverse object categories, physical states, task conditions, and environments.

Specifically, deformable-object folding, such as handling clothing items, presents a representative challenge. Garments can appear in various initial states (crumpled, self-occluded) and belong to different categories (shirts, pants, towels, skirts), each potentially requiring distinct action sequences. Existing VLA-based folding systems typically address this diversity by training separate, task-specific policies for each clothing category. This approach encounters scalability issues in household environments, where managing numerous specialized models for a wide array of foldable items and random initial conditions becomes impractical. A straightforward alternative, training a single policy on mixed multi-task data, has often led to performance degradation due to task interference, especially when different categories demand incompatible intermediate states or motion patterns.

DeMaVLA directly addresses these limitations by proposing a unified VLA foundation model designed for generalizable deformable manipulation. It seeks to move beyond category-specific policies by learning shared folding priors from large-scale dual-arm data and applying them through a single model checkpoint to handle diverse folding tasks. This approach represents an advancement towards creating more adaptable and autonomous household robots capable of performing complex manipulation tasks with deformable objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model, named DeMaVLA, that can enable real-world household robots to acquire reusable manipulation skills, specifically for deformable-object folding, across a wide range of objects, task conditions, and household environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and limitations identified in the current state of robotic manipulation, particularly concerning deformable objects:

- **Generalization in Real-World Scenarios:** Real-world household robots need to perform manipulation tasks with high reusability of skills, adapting to variations in object categories, their physical states (e.g., crumpled clothing), task conditions, and scene layouts. Current VLA models, while promising, often struggle with this level of generalization, especially for complex and varied tasks like deformable object manipulation.
- **The Challenge of Deformable Object Folding:** Deformable objects, such as clothing items, represent a significant hurdle for robot manipulation. They exhibit high degrees of freedom, their states are difficult to perceive and model, and their manipulation often requires long-horizon, bimanual actions. Handling items like shirts, pants, towels, or skirts from arbitrary initial states necessitates diverse action sequences but also relies on shared underlying physical priors (e.g., grasping corners, aligning fabric, smoothing wrinkles).
- **Limitations of Existing VLA Approaches for Deformable Manipulation:**
 - **Category-Specific Policies:** Current VLA systems frequently resort to training separate policies for different object categories. This approach is not scalable for household environments, where robots encounter a vast and unpredictable variety of items and initial states. Manual selection and maintenance of numerous specialized models are impractical.
 - **Task Interference in Naive Multi-task Training:** A simple alternative of training a single policy on mixed multi-task data often leads to task interference. Different object categories may require incompatible intermediate states or motion patterns, causing the model to struggle and performance to degrade when attempting to generalize across them.

To overcome these limitations, the researchers aim to develop DeMaVLA with the following specific objectives:

- **Unified Folding Policy:** Create a single-checkpoint policy capable of multi-category bimanual folding, moving beyond the need for separate policies for each clothing type. This policy should be able to leverage shared physical priors while also executing task-specific behaviors.
- **Efficient Continuous Action Generation:** Formulate continuous action generation using flow matching, enabling smooth action prediction for long-horizon dual-arm manipulation.
- **Optimized Model Architecture:** Design an action expert that is layer-aligned with a VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL) but also computationally efficient. This includes pruning every other transformer layer in the action expert to reduce training and inference costs without compromising architectural alignment.
- **Leverage Scalable Real-World Data:** Pre-train the model on a large dataset of real-world dual-arm demonstrations (approximately 5,000 hours) to acquire broad, general manipulation priors relevant to household tasks and deformable object handling.
- **Robustness through Corrective Learning:** Post-train the model using a human-in-the-loop Data Aggregation (DAgger) pipeline. This method aims to target and correct failure modes encountered by the mixed folding policy on real robots, thereby improving its robustness and multi-category folding capability.
- **Demonstrate Performance:** Evaluate DeMaVLA's effectiveness in both simulation (RoboTwin 2.0) and a real-world household folding benchmark to validate its generalization abilities and practical utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeMaVLA is designed as a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model intended for generalizable deformable manipulation, specifically for multi-category bimanual folding tasks. The methodology integrates a VLM-conditioned architecture, flow-based action generation, multi-stage training with large-scale data, and a human-in-the-loop corrective learning pipeline.

**Model Architecture:**
DeMaVLA uses a VLM-conditioned action-generation architecture for bimanual manipulation.
- **Observation Context:** At each timestep *t*, the model receives an observation context \(O_t\), which includes multi-view image observations (three operational views \(I_{1,2,3_t}\)), a natural language instruction (\(\ell_t\)), and the robot's proprioceptive state (\(q_t\)).
- **Action Generation:** Continuous actions are predicted as action chunks \(A_t = [a_t, a_{t+1}..., a_{t+H-1}]\), where H is the action horizon and each \(a_t\) is a continuous dual-arm control command. This continuous action generation is formulated using conditional flow matching, which is suited for smooth dual-arm control.
- **VLM Backbone:** The core VLM backbone is Qwen3-VL. Multi-view images and language instructions are tokenized into visual-language tokens, providing semantic and spatial context.
- **Action Expert:** An action expert component handles robot-specific inputs and generates continuous bimanual action predictions. The proprioceptive state \(q_t\) is projected into the transformer embedding space. Noisy action chunks \(A_{t,s}\) are represented as H action tokens, processed by the action expert conditioned on the VLM context.
 - **Layer-Aligned Pruned Action Expert:** To align the action expert with the VLM backbone and improve efficiency, the LLM transformer structure of Qwen3-VL is reused. However, instead of mirroring the full L transformer layers of the VLM backbone, DeMaVLA prunes every other layer in the action expert. This design reduces computational cost during training and inference (especially for flow-based generation requiring repeated forward passes) while maintaining a hierarchical correspondence and enabling conditioning on VLM representations.

**Model Training:**
DeMaVLA's training proceeds in two main stages: pre-training and post-training.

- **Pre-training:**
 - **Dataset:** Approximately 5,000 hours of real-world dual-arm demonstrations. This dataset covers diverse bimanual manipulation skills (household organization, folding, object transfer, industrial handling) and specifically includes a substantial portion of folding-oriented tasks (e.g., folding t-shirts, shorts, dresses, pants, rolling towels, stacking).
 - **Objective:** To acquire general manipulation priors, such as reaching, grasping, placement, object rearrangement, and two-arm coordination, as well as dense supervision for garment handling.
 - **Method:** The action expert is trained to denoise noisy action chunks conditioned on the VLM context and robot state using conditional flow matching. This objective learns the conditional action distribution \(p(A_t | O_t)\) while preserving action space continuity.

- **Post-training with Training-time Real-Time Chunking (RTC):**
 - **Objective:** To adapt the pre-trained policy for real-world deployment and mitigate inference latency.
 - **Method:** RTC simulates inference latency during training. Each action chunk is conceptually divided into a "committed prefix" (actions already being executed) and a "predicted postfix" (future actions to be generated). During post-training, the prefix serves as a clean conditioning context, while flow noise is applied only to the postfix. The training loss is calculated only on the postfix actions. This encourages the model to generate temporally consistent future trajectories that respect already committed actions, enabling asynchronous action-chunk execution without further inference-time inpainting or guidance.

- **Human-in-the-Loop DAgger (Data Aggregation):**
 - **Motivation:** To enhance robustness by addressing failure states that are underrepresented in passive demonstration datasets and arise from compounding errors in long-horizon deformable object manipulation.
 - **Procedure:**
 1. The policy is initialized using behavior cloning on pre-training and task-specific demonstration data.
 2. The mixed folding policy is deployed on a real robot for folding rollouts across different clothing categories.
 3. A human operator monitors the robot's execution. If the policy enters an unsafe, unstable, or suboptimal state (signaled by a binary gate \(g_t = 1\)), the human intervenes.
 4. During intervention, control is fully transferred to the human expert (\(\pi_H\)), who corrects the robot's behavior and guides it to a valid task state.
 5. Once the correction is complete, control is released back to the autonomous policy.
 6. Expert-labeled correction data, consisting of observations and human actions during intervention intervals (\(D_{int} = \{(o_t, a_H_t) | g_t = 1\}\)), is collected.
 7. This intervention data is aggregated into the existing training dataset (\(D \leftarrow D \cup D_{int}\)).
 8. DeMaVLA is then continually post-trained on this updated, aggregated dataset using the same flow-matching objective.
 - **Benefit:** This approach focuses supervision on actual failure modes, teaching the policy recovery behaviors for common issues like misaligned cloth or incomplete grasps, thereby improving multi-category folding robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DeMaVLA encompassed both simulated and real-world benchmarks, focusing on its ability to generalize across diverse manipulation tasks and deformable object categories.

**Simulation Benchmark (RoboTwin 2.0):**
DeMaVLA was compared against several existing VLA foundation models (π₀, π₀.₅, X-VLA, ABot-M0, LingBot-VLA) on 50 bimanual manipulation tasks in both clean (fixed initial configurations) and randomized (varying object poses and scene layouts) settings.
- DeMaVLA achieved the highest average success rate in both settings: 88.42% in the clean setting and 86.78% in the randomized setting.
- These results indicated that DeMaVLA exhibited strong generalization capabilities and maintained robustness even under variations in object poses and scene layouts, surpassing the performance of other tested VLA models.

**Real-world Household Folding Benchmark:**
This benchmark utilized an ALOHA-style dual-arm robot to perform a full household folding procedure: picking an item from a basket, placing it on a table, unfolding/spreading it, and completing a specific folding routine. Tasks included folding a shirt, skirt, pants, and a towel, each evaluated over 20 trials with two sets of items. Metrics were Success Rate (SR) and Completion Time.

- **Single-task Comparison (Shirt Folding):**
 - When trained and evaluated only on shirt folding data, DeMaVLA achieved a 100.0% SR, outperforming the single-task π₀ baseline which achieved 80.0% SR.
 - DeMaVLA also slightly reduced the average completion time from 2:13 for π₀ to 2:04. This demonstrated its strong task-specific folding capability even when trained in isolation.

- **Multi-task Comparison (Mixed Folding):**
 - Both DeMaVLA and π₀ were trained on a mixed dataset of 37 hours of real-robot data (combining demonstrations for shirts, skirts, pants, and towels).
 - DeMaVLA achieved a higher average SR of 92.5% across the four tasks, compared to 76.3% for π₀.
 - Significant improvements were observed across all tasks:
 - Shirt: DeMaVLA 95.0% SR (vs. π₀ 90.0%)
 - Skirt: DeMaVLA 100.0% SR (vs. π₀ 95.0%)
 - Pant: DeMaVLA 75.0% SR (vs. π₀ 65.0%)
 - Towel: DeMaVLA 100.0% SR (vs. π₀ 55.0%). The substantial gain on towel folding indicated improved robustness for highly deformable and visually ambiguous objects.
 - DeMaVLA also maintained a lower average completion time across all tasks (2:18 vs. 2:26 for π₀), suggesting improved efficiency in addition to higher success rates.
 - These results supported the conclusion that DeMaVLA's single-checkpoint policy could effectively share folding priors across different garment categories and execute diverse folding skills robustly in real-world settings.

**Pre-training Scaling Analysis:**
To investigate the impact of pre-training data scale, three DeMaVLA checkpoints were trained with 500, 2,500, and 5,000 hours of real-world pre-training data, respectively. All were subsequently post-trained with the same shirt-folding data.
- A clear scaling trend was observed:
 - 500 hours: 55.0% SR, 3:34 completion time.
 - 2,500 hours: 70.0% SR, 3:21 completion time.
 - 5,000 hours: 100.0% SR, 2:04 completion time.
- This analysis indicated that increasing the scale of real-world pre-training data substantially enhanced both the downstream task success rate and execution efficiency after post-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DeMaVLA presents several areas of significance and potential impact within robotic manipulation and broader AI research:

- **Advancement in Generalizable Deformable Manipulation:** DeMaVLA directly addresses the challenge of generalizing manipulation skills for deformable objects, moving beyond task- or category-specific policies. By enabling a single model checkpoint to handle diverse garment types and initial states, it represents a step towards robots that can interact with a wider range of unstructured, real-world items without extensive re-training.
- **Contribution to VLA Foundation Models:** The work highlights specific architectural and training strategies that contribute to effective VLA policies for real-world scenarios. The combination of a VLM backbone, a layer-aligned pruned action expert for efficiency, flow-matching for continuous action generation, and training-time RTC for asynchronous execution demonstrates an integrated approach to building robust robotic agents.
- **Emphasis on Data Scale and Quality:** The pre-training scaling analysis empirically validates the critical role of large-scale, real-world dual-arm demonstration data in acquiring general manipulation priors. This reinforces the paradigm that extensive and diverse data is fundamental for improving both the success rate and efficiency of downstream robotic tasks.
- **Practicality of Human-in-the-Loop Corrective Learning:** The human-gated DAgger pipeline offers a practical method for iteratively refining policies by targeting actual failure modes encountered during robot deployment. For complex, long-horizon tasks like deformable object folding, where small errors can compound, this approach provides valuable corrective data, potentially enhancing policy robustness more efficiently than passive data collection alone.
- **Potential for Household Robotics:** The strong performance demonstrated on a real-world household folding benchmark suggests direct applicability in domestic environments. Robots capable of reliably performing chores such as laundry folding could significantly contribute to home automation and assist in various daily tasks, potentially improving quality of life for individuals.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work could motivate further research into efficient VLA model architectures, advanced data aggregation strategies, and the application of foundation models to other challenging deformable object manipulation tasks (e.g., bed-making, cooking with flexible ingredients, packaging). The findings also encourage exploration of how shared manipulation priors can be more effectively transferred and adapted across vastly different physical contexts.
