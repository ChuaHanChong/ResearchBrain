# Synergizing Understanding and Generation with Interleaved Analyzing-Drafting Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced the interleaved Analyzing–Drafting (AD-Loop) paradigm for Unified Vision–Language Models (UVLMs), enabling dynamic alternation between analytical and drafting operations to foster explicit interaction between understanding and generation. The approach achieved an average of +2.3% improvement on multimodal understanding benchmarks and an 86% score on the GenEval benchmark for multimodal generation.

## Problem

- Unified Vision-Language Models (UVLMs) typically integrate understanding and generation capabilities architecturally but lack explicit, dynamic interaction between these modules during task execution.
- Existing UVLMs primarily treat understanding and generation as parallel or independently callable skills, failing to leverage their inherent complementarity for mutual reinforcement.
- Architectural unification efforts often overlook the potential for one capability to inform and refine the other, leading to missed opportunities for enhanced problem-solving.

## Method

- A novel thinking paradigm, the "interleaved Analyzing–Drafting problem-solving loop" (**AD-Loop**), is introduced to enable UVLMs to dynamically alternate between analytical and drafting operations.
- The approach models synergistic understanding and generation by producing interleaved textual thoughts (T-T) and compact latent visual thoughts (V-T).
- A two-stage training strategy, comprising supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning (GRPO) for adaptive invocation, teaches the model to use the AD-Loop effectively.

## Results

- The AD-Loop achieved an average of +**2.3%** improvement across standard multimodal understanding benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art unified models.
- On the GenEval benchmark, the method reached an overall score of **86%**, demonstrating superior performance in multimodal generation, particularly for compositional aspects like positional accuracy (**0.80**) and attribute correctness (**0.74**).
- The framework proved architecture-agnostic, consistently improving both understanding and generation tasks when applied to different UVLM backbones, such as Janus-Pro and BAGEL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly interleaving textual (analytical) and latent visual (drafting) thoughts creates a mutually reinforcing loop, iteratively refining task comprehension and output quality.
> - Using compact latent visual thoughts instead of full pixel-level rendering during the thought phase is computationally efficient and effective for internal visual reasoning.
> - An adaptive policy, learned through reinforcement learning, allows the UVLM to intelligently decide whether and when to invoke the AD-Loop, maximizing its benefits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal learning has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Unified Vision–Language Models (UVLMs). These models aim to integrate both understanding (comprehension of visual and textual input) and generation (creation of visual or textual content) capabilities within a single framework, moving towards more general multimodal intelligence. Recent progress in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has enhanced comprehension, while diffusion-based generative transformers (DiTs) have improved content creation.

Early efforts in UVLMs largely focused on integrating existing specialist models, effectively co-locating understanding and generation modules without enabling significant interaction or mutual reinforcement between them. More recent approaches have explored coherent architectural designs, such as casting both tasks as autoregressive next-token prediction, employing decoupled encoders with multi-head outputs, or using hybrid autoregressive-diffusion models. While these efforts attempt to unify the capabilities, they primarily focus on architectural integration, often overlooking the need for explicit and dynamic interaction between understanding and generation during problem-solving. Consequently, these models tend to treat understanding and generation as parallel or independently callable skills rather than inherently synergistic processes.

Complementary research has also focused on endowing MLLMs with stronger reasoning capabilities, often inspired by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigms. These involve decomposing problems into substeps with supervised rationales, scaling test-time computation via self-consistency or search-based inference, and utilizing reinforcement learning (RL) for improved credit assignment. Some studies have extended textual-only reasoning to incorporate visual evidence, either through tool-augmented pipelines or by directly generating visual traces.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the identified gap: the lack of explicit, interleaved interaction between understanding and generation modules during task execution. It proposes a novel thinking paradigm that aims to move beyond mere co-existence or architectural unification towards genuine, dynamic synergy between these two fundamental capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while UVLMs aim to unify understanding and generation, existing models predominantly focus on architectural integration without facilitating explicit interaction between these two capabilities during task solving. The paper argues that understanding and generation are inherently complementary processes: robust understanding provides the semantic foundation for faithful generation, and successful generation can, in turn, serve as tangible evidence of visual comprehension. However, current UVLMs largely treat these as parallel skills, missing the opportunity for a mutually reinforcing loop.

The limitations of existing approaches are highlighted through several points:
- **Architectural unification over interaction:** Many models prioritize a unified architecture, which can lead to issues such as parameter competition and task interference.
- **Lack of explicit interaction:** Even in more coherent designs, the understanding and generation modules often operate in isolation during inference, failing to engage in close, explicit interaction that could enhance problem-solving.
- **Untapped synergy:** The potential for one capability to inform and refine the other (e.g., understanding proposing solutions for generation to verify, or generation producing initial results for understanding to guide refinement) remains largely unrealized.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **To introduce a novel thinking paradigm:** This paradigm, termed the "interleaved Analyzing–Drafting problem-solving loop" (AD-Loop), enables a UVLM to dynamically alternate between analytical (understanding) and drafting (generation) operations.
2. **To foster genuine synergy:** By interleaving textual and visual thoughts, the AD-Loop aims to allow models to iteratively refine both their comprehension of the task and the quality of their outputs, thereby achieving true synergy.
3. **To develop an effective training strategy:** A two-stage training strategy is designed to equip UVLMs with this interleaved thinking mechanism, progressing from guided imitation to adaptive, autonomous control.
4. **To demonstrate broad applicability:** The proposed framework is intended to be architecture-agnostic, capable of enhancing a wide range of existing UVLMs.
5. **To validate the effectiveness:** Through extensive experimentation, the research aims to demonstrate that AD-Loop improves performance across both understanding and generation benchmarks and to provide visual evidence of its operational rationality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces the interleaved Analyzing–Drafting problem-solving loop (AD-Loop) within a UVLM framework. This approach models synergistic understanding and generation by alternating between analytical operations (producing textual thoughts) and drafting operations (producing visual thoughts) before generating a final output.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The model takes an input `x = (q, I)`, where `q` is text and `I` is an optional set of images. A vision encoder processes input images into visual embeddings, which are fused with the textual stream and processed by an LLM backbone. The model's output includes a thinking trace, structured within `<think>` and `</think>` tags, containing interleaved textual thoughts (`[T-T]`) and visual thoughts (`[V-T]`), followed by a final `[Answer]` (text or image).

A key design choice for `[V-T]` is to replace full pixel-level visual rendering with a compact set of latent visual thoughts `({v_j})`. These latent thoughts summarize only the factors useful for reasoning, addressing issues of latency and pixel-level entanglement that arise from full image generation during the thought phase. This approach is informed by cognitive theories suggesting internal imagery is schematic rather than pixel-accurate.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Strategy

The AD-Loop mechanism is trained using a two-stage pipeline:

**Stage 1: Supervised Training of Interleaved Thinking**
This stage aims to initialize the UVLM with a foundational ability to perform interleaved thinking through supervised fine-tuning.
- **Dataset Construction:** A comprehensive interleaved corpus is constructed.
 - **Understanding Tasks:** Existing multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) resources (CMoT, Visual-CoT, CoF) are leveraged. Rationales are reorganized, and referenced regions are cropped to form interleaved schemas. Additionally, AD-Loop thinking traces are synthesized for schematic grid tasks (Visual Spatial Planning - VSP) by visualizing movement steps. This portion includes approximately 20,000 interleaved examples.
 - **Generation Tasks:** The GoT dataset and a newly constructed Inter-T2I set (derived from X-to-image corpora, augmented with interleaved thinking traces using QVQ-72B-Preview and Flux1-dev for visual hypotheses) are utilized. This yields approximately 22,000 interleaved generation traces.
- **Latent Visual Thoughts Construction:** A practical challenge is converting explicit visual thoughts (pixel images) from the dataset into the model's required latent visual thoughts. Each explicit image `I` is converted into a compact set of latent tokens `({v_j})` using a frozen generator-side encoder and a deterministic density peaks clustering mechanism. This process reduces a grid of latent tokens `({z_i})` to a smaller, semantically coherent set, where each `v_j` represents the average token within a cluster.
- **Training Objective:** The model is fine-tuned on this corpus. The objective `L_S1` combines:
 - Cross-entropy loss (`L_CE`) for textual thoughts (`T_hat`, `T_star`).
 - Mean-squared error (`L_vis`) for latent visual thoughts (`V_hat`, `V_star`).
 - Original task loss (`L_out`) for the final output (`o_hat`, `o_star`).

**Stage 2: RL-Driven Adaptive Interleaved Thinking**
After Stage 1, the model possesses initial interleaved thinking capability. Stage 2 enhances this by enabling the model to adaptively decide whether to invoke the AD-Loop for a given query or to use a simpler, isolated thinking mechanism. This is achieved through reinforcement learning (RL) using group-relative preference optimization (GRPO).
- **Adaptive Interleaved Thinking:** For each query `q`, the policy samples two groups of trajectories from the old policy (`π_old`): one with the AD-Loop enabled (`{o+_i}`) and one with it disabled (`{o-_i}`).
- **Reward Assignment:** Each trajectory receives a scalar reward `r(o)` comprising a format component (`r_format`, ensuring structural validity) and a content component (`r_content`).
 - For generation tasks, `r_content` aggregates alignment and quality scores (e.g., human preference feedback, unified scoring, text-image alignment).
 - For understanding tasks, `r_content` uses rule-based checks for deterministic answers or a learned reward model (e.g., InternLM-XComposer2.5-Reward) for open-ended questions.
 - An explicit margin-based bonus is added to AD-Loop enabled trajectories (`r(o+_i)`) if they are correct and meaningfully outperform the strongest AD-Loop-disabled candidate by a margin `δ`.
 - Inter-group rewards `r_inter` are introduced to indicate which thinking mode (with or without AD-Loop) performs best for a given query.
- **Optimization:** The policy is updated using a group-relative advantage `A_i`, which combines intra-group and inter-group terms. This encourages the model to prioritize responses with higher relative quality. KL-divergence regularization is also applied to prevent the policy from diverging too much from a frozen reference model.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Backbone:** BAGEL-7B (Deng et al., 2025) is used, with SigLIP2-so400m/14 (Tschannen et al., 2025) as the image encoder for understanding and FLUX pre-trained VAE (Yang et al., 2024) for image latent encoding/decoding in generation. The framework is also tested on Janus-Pro (Chen et al., 2025c) to demonstrate architecture agnosticism.
- **Hyperparameters:** Stage 1 involves a global batch size of 256, a cosine learning rate schedule starting at 1e-5, and a maximum of 16 latent visual thought tokens (`K=16`). Stage 2 uses the VERL framework (Sheng et al., 2025) with AdamW, a constant learning rate of 2e-6, a global batch size of 64, 8 rollouts per prompt, a clipped policy-gradient loss (ϵ=0.5), and a KL regularization weight of 0.01. Reward parameters are set to λ=1.0 and δ=0.2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of AD-Loop encompassed a range of multimodal understanding and generation benchmarks, ablation studies, and qualitative analyses.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Multimodal Understanding (Table 1):** AD-Loop demonstrated consistent performance improvements across various standard benchmarks including POPE, MME-P, MMB, SEED, GQA, MMMU, and MM-Vet. It achieved an average of +2.3% improvement on understanding tasks, outperforming both understanding-only models (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5, Qwen-VL-Chat, InstructBLIP) and other state-of-the-art unified models (e.g., Emu3, Janus-Pro, BAGEL, MMaDA). Specifically, AD-Loop achieved 90.1% on POPE, 1696.0 on MME-P, 87.6% on MMB, 74.4% on SEED, 63.8% on GQA, 57.3% on MMMU, and 69.7% on MM-Vet.
- **Multimodal Generation (Table 2):** On the GenEval benchmark, AD-Loop achieved the highest overall score of 86%. This performance surpassed both generation-only models (e.g., SDXL, FLUX.1-dev, SD3-Medium) and other unified understanding-and-generation models (e.g., Show-o, Janus-Pro, BAGEL, MindOmni). The method showed particular improvements in compositional generation, including fine-grained attributes, positional accuracy, and attribute correctness. For instance, it achieved 0.80 on Position and 0.74 on Attri.

#### 4.2. Ablation on Thinking Types (Table 3, Fig. 3)

- **Impact of Reasoning:** Equipping models with any reasoning capability ("T-think," referring to text-only analytical thinking) yielded substantial improvements in both understanding (e.g., MathVista +6.8%, LogicVista +3.9%) and generation (e.g., WISE cultural +0.23), confirming the benefit of structured reasoning.
- **Visual Thoughts:** Augmenting textual reasoning with visual thoughts (both explicit "T + I (explicit)" and implicit "T + eI (implicit)") further boosted performance. Implicit visual thoughts generally showed a slight advantage over explicit ones.
- **Adaptive Interleaving:** The adaptive strategy, which intelligently selects between thinking with or without AD-Loop ("T / T + eI"), achieved the best results across all tasks, demonstrating its ability to leverage complementarity. For example, it achieved 75.8% on MathVista, 49.5% on LogicVista, and 0.89 on WISE SAT.
- **Qualitative Evidence (Fig. 3):** Qualitative comparisons illustrated that raw prompts yielded superficial outputs. Text-only self-thinking provided more detail but could be abstract or misaligned. Interleaved thoughts, however, guided the model to produce faithful and detail-oriented outputs. Filtering interleaved traces to text-only often reintroduced errors, underscoring the contribution of visual thoughts to high-fidelity control.

#### 4.3. Analyses and Discussion

- **Architecture Agnosticism (Fig. 4):** AD-Loop consistently improved both understanding and generation tasks when applied to two different UVLM backbones: Janus-Pro (which uses discrete tokenization for visual content) and BAGEL (which uses continuous embeddings). This demonstrated the method's transferability and broad applicability.
- **Source of Visual Thoughts (Table 4):** Visual thoughts derived from the generation encoder consistently produced better results for both understanding and generation tasks compared to those derived from the understanding encoder. This was attributed to the generation encoder's pre-training and its ability to capture both semantic and pixel-level information.
- **Implicit Visual Thoughts (Fig. 5):** Visualizations showed that latent visual thoughts effectively encode semantically coherent information while preserving coarse pixel-level structures, enabling the model to recover overall contours and unify conceptually similar regions (e.g., watermelons and lemons represented by the same latent token).
- **Adaptive Usage of Visual Thoughts (Fig. 6):** An analysis of visual-thought usage on the LogicVista dataset indicated that AD-Loop thoughts improved performance across a wide range of questions, with particularly pronounced gains in spatial and mechanistic reasoning. Visual thoughts were preferentially activated for tasks like rotation, complex OCR, and 3D perception, while usage decreased for tasks like tables, sequences, and symbolic reasoning where text-only chains were sufficient. This demonstrated the adaptive policy's ability to selectively invoke visual thoughts when most beneficial.
- **Case Studies (Figs. 7, 10-15):**
 - **Text-to-Image Generation:** AD-Loop generated outputs that aligned more faithfully with reasoning-intensive prompts compared to baselines. Examples included correctly depicting a "traffic light signaling drivers to prepare to stop" as yellow (not red/green), a chameleon blending in, and a kangaroo using its tail for support.
 - **Understanding Tasks:** The model exhibited stronger spatial reasoning, mathematical reasoning (with intermediate visual sketches), and abstract content interpretation (e.g., tangram analysis).
 - **Editing Tasks:** AD-Loop produced more faithful and semantically aligned image edits, such as inferring missing elements in patterns, generating realistic popcorn from corn kernels, and correcting unreasonable parts of a bicycle.

#### 4.4. Challenging Cases (Figs. 16, 17)

The study also identified areas where the model encountered limitations. In understanding tasks, the model occasionally exhibited "over-drafting" on simple queries, leading to unnecessary elaboration or incorrect predictions. It also struggled with problems requiring strict symbolic mathematical reasoning, where the AD-Loop could sometimes introduce deviations from the optimal logical path. For generation tasks, difficulties persisted in rendering fine-grained visual details, such as scene text, subtle body parts (e.g., fingers), and numerically precise elements. The compact nature of latent visual representations was suggested as a potential factor for these fine-grained detail limitations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces the interleaved Analyzing–Drafting problem-solving loop (AD-Loop) as a novel strategy for synergizing understanding and generation within Unified Vision–Language Models (UVLMs). Its significance lies in shifting the focus from mere architectural unification to fostering explicit, dynamic interaction between these two capabilities during the task-solving process.

The AD-Loop framework, combined with its two-stage learning paradigm (supervised initialization followed by RL-driven adaptive control), represents a principled approach to teaching UVLMs when and how to leverage their understanding and generation abilities in a complementary manner. This adaptive mechanism allows the model to intelligently decide the optimal interaction strategy, promoting a genuine synergy that transcends the limitations of models treating these capabilities as parallel.

The consistent performance improvements observed across a diverse set of multimodal understanding and generation benchmarks, particularly on reasoning-driven tasks, indicate that AD-Loop is an effective strategy. Furthermore, its demonstrated architecture-agnosticism suggests broad applicability to a wide range of existing and future UVLM designs.

The introduction of compact latent visual thoughts, which abstract salient visual cues without the computational cost or noise of pixel-level rendering during the thought process, offers a computationally efficient and cognitively plausible mechanism for internal visual reasoning.

Overall, the AD-Loop framework offers a pathway towards advancing unified multimodal intelligence by establishing a more interactive and mutually reinforcing relationship between comprehension and content creation. It has the potential to serve as a general mechanism for improving the robustness, faithfulness, and reasoning capabilities of next-generation UVLMs across complex multimodal tasks.
