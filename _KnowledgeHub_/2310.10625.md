# Video Language Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIDEO LANGUAGE PLANNING (VLP) algorithm enables long-horizon visual planning for robotic tasks by combining vision-language models (VLMs) with text-to-video models in a tree-search framework. This approach substantially improves task completion rates on complex multi-step tasks across simulated and physical robot platforms.

## Problem

- Existing robotic planning methods struggle with complex, long-horizon tasks in variable real-world environments.
- Large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) provide abstract planning but lack an intrinsic understanding of physical dynamics and how objects interact over time.
- Text-to-video models can learn dynamics for short sequences but cannot generate long-horizon plans or perform high-level abstract reasoning.

## Method

- A **VLP** (Video Language Planning) algorithm orchestrates ==vision-language models (VLMs)== and ==text-to-video models== within a ==parallel hill climbing tree-search== framework.
- VLMs serve as both ==policy generators== for abstract text actions and ==heuristic functions== to evaluate the favorability of future visual states.
- Text-to-video models function as ==dynamic simulators==, predicting detailed short video sequences showing the outcome of proposed actions. Synthesized video plans are translated into robot actions using a ==goal-conditioned policy== that targets intermediate frames, coupled with a ==receding horizon control== strategy for execution.

## Results

- VLP achieved up to **98%** success rate in synthesizing long-horizon video plans for tasks like "Group Color" in simulation, significantly outperforming baselines (**2-64%**).
- On robot execution, VLP completed tasks such as "Group Color" in simulation at **92%**, substantially exceeding baseline performance (**0-4%** for challenging long-horizon tasks).
- The framework generalized to new objects, lighting conditions, and novel instructions, and was demonstrated on 7DoF mobile and 14DoF bi-manual robot manipulators.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining large generative foundation models—specifically VLMs for abstract reasoning and text-to-video models for dynamics—via a planning algorithm enables emergent capabilities for long-horizon visual tasks.
> - A VLM can effectively serve as a heuristic function within a tree search, guiding the generation of physically plausible and goal-directed video plans.
> - Synthesizing detailed, multimodal video plans that link high-level language instructions to specific visual states provides a robust representation for robot execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "VIDEO LANGUAGE PLANNING" (VLP) sits at the critical intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: classical robotics planning, large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and generative AI, particularly text-to-video models. It addresses a fundamental challenge in intelligent interaction with the physical world: enabling robots to plan for complex, long-horizon tasks that require both high-level semantic understanding and detailed, low-level dynamic reasoning.

Historically, robotics planning has tackled this problem by factorizing it into abstract task planning (what to do) and concrete motion planning (how to execute). Approaches like Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) exemplify this, often relying on symbolic representations and explicit world models. While effective in structured environments, these methods struggle with the complexity and variability of the real world and the need for adaptive, context-aware reasoning.

The advent of **large language models (LLMs)** like GPT-3 and PaLM has revolutionized high-level abstract planning. LLMs can generate step-by-step plans for long-horizon tasks based on linguistic abstractions. However, as the paper notes, LLMs are inherently limited by their textual representation; they "struggle with grounding" – reasoning over shapes, physics, and real-world constraints. They lack an intrinsic understanding of physical dynamics.

**Vision-language models (VLMs)**, such as PaLM-E and RT-2, integrate visual inputs with LLMs, allowing them to condition plans on perceived states. While an improvement, current VLMs are often trained on static image-captioning and Q&A datasets, meaning they still "struggle to reason over dynamics" – how objects move or interact over time. They can generate more feasible plans, but lack predictive power regarding future states.

Concurrently, **text-to-video models** have seen remarkable progress, driven by internet-scale video data. These models can synthesize detailed video predictions, demonstrating an ability to learn underlying dynamics and motions. However, existing video models typically generate only short-horizon clips, and their application to long-horizon planning has been unclear.

VLP directly addresses the gap between the abstract planning capabilities of LLMs/VLMs and the dynamic understanding of text-to-video models. It proposes a novel "composition through forward search" that leverages these complementary strengths. Instead of relying solely on text for planning or short-term video prediction, VLP creates a rich multimodal planning framework where:
- VLMs provide high-level policy guidance and state evaluation (heuristic function).
- Text-to-video models act as dynamic simulators, predicting detailed visual outcomes of abstract actions.

This work represents a significant step towards "modern re-examination of visual planning by integrating large generative models pretrained on Internet-scale data." It moves beyond purely symbolic or purely visual representations to combine the best of both worlds, offering a scalable solution for generating long-horizon, physically grounded plans. The paper also connects to the broader theme of "test-time composition" of foundation models, where combining pre-trained models can yield emergent capabilities far beyond what each component can achieve alone.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of "VIDEO LANGUAGE PLANNING" (VLP) is to **enable visual planning for complex, long-horizon tasks in the space of generated videos and language**. The authors are motivated by the inherent limitations of existing approaches to robotic planning, particularly in the context of emerging large generative models.

The specific motivations and objectives can be broken down as follows:

- **Bridging the gap between abstract and low-level planning:** Intelligent interaction with the physical world requires planning at both high-level semantic abstractions (e.g., "what to do next") and low-level dynamics (e.g., "how the world works"). Traditional methods often factorize these, but current large models struggle to integrate them effectively. LLMs provide abstract plans but lack physical grounding and dynamic reasoning. VLMs improve grounding but still struggle with dynamic predictions. Text-to-video models understand dynamics but are limited to short horizons and lack high-level reasoning. The primary objective is to build a planning algorithm that synergistically leverages both.
- **Leveraging recent advances in large generative models:** The paper aims to harness the power of large generative models (VLMs and text-to-video models) pretrained on internet-scale data. These models encapsulate vast amounts of knowledge about the world, but their application to sequential, physically-grounded decision-making for long-horizon tasks remains an open challenge. The motivation is to unlock this potential for robust robotic planning.
- **Synthesizing detailed multimodal plans:** The goal is to produce not just textual plans, but "long video plans that provide detailed multimodal (video and language) specifications" for task completion. This ensures that the plans are rich in visual detail, allowing for more precise execution by a robot.
- **Scalability with computation budget:** A key objective is to design an algorithm where "more computation time results in improved video plans." This highlights a desire for an inherently scalable planning framework that can refine its output given more resources, a characteristic often seen in search-based algorithms.
- **Improved long-horizon task success rates:** Ultimately, the motivation is to enable robots to perform multi-step, complex tasks more reliably than existing methods. This is evaluated by measuring the success rates of executing the generated plans on both simulated and real robot platforms.
- **Generalization to novel scenarios:** The authors aim for a system that can generalize to new objects, configurations, and tasks, drawing on the broad knowledge encoded in internet-scale pre-trained models. This is crucial for real-world deployment where perfect prior knowledge is rarely available.
- **Robustness in execution:** The motivation extends to ensuring that the generated video plans can be effectively translated into real robot actions, even with accumulating errors or the need for replanning in dynamic environments.

In essence, VLP seeks to create a robust, scalable, and generalizable visual planning system by orchestrating state-of-the-art generative models within a tree-search framework, thereby overcoming the individual limitations of these models when applied to complex robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Video Language Planning (VLP) is an algorithm designed to synthesize long-horizon multimodal (video and language) plans for robotic tasks by integrating vision-language models (VLMs) and text-to-video models within a tree search procedure.

**Core Components (Planning Submodules):**
VLP utilizes three specialized submodules, each playing a distinct role in the planning process:

1. **Vision-Language Models as Policies (π_VLM):**
 - **Function:** Given a current image observation (`x`) and a natural language goal (`g`), the VLM policy generates multiple possible next-step abstract text actions (`a`).
 - **Implementation:** This is built upon models like PaLM-E. It can be implemented by (i) few-shot prompting the VLM with examples of text actions or (ii) finetuning PaLM-E on short trajectory snippets labeled with abstract actions within longer trajectories.
 - **Role in search:** Proposes a branching set of high-level actions to explore.

2. **Video Models as Dynamics Models (f_VM):**
 - **Function:** Given a current image (`x`) and a short-horizon text instruction (abstract action `a`), this model predicts a concrete resulting state or, more specifically, synthesizes a short video sequence (`x_1:S`) showing the visual outcome of executing that action.
 - **Implementation:** Constructed by training text-to-video models on short image trajectory snippets and associated language labels.
 - **Role in search:** Simulates the low-level visual dynamics of proposed actions, generating future visual states for evaluation.

3. **Vision-Language Models as Heuristic Functions (H_VLM):**
 - **Function:** Takes an image observation (`x`) and a natural language goal (`g`), and outputs a scalar "heuristic" value predicting how many steps are remaining to reach a state satisfying the goal. A higher value indicates closer proximity to the goal (by using the negated number of predicted steps).
 - **Implementation:** Finetuned PaLM-E model on long trajectory snippets to predict the number of steps left until the end of the trajectory.
 - **Role in search:** Evaluates the favorability of future states (generated videos) to prune search branches and guide the selection of the most promising plan.

**Planning with VLP (Tree Search Procedure - Algorithm 1):**
VLP employs a parallel hill climbing tree-search strategy to construct long-horizon video plans (`x_1:H`) that optimize the VLM heuristic function.

1. **Initialization:** A set of `B` parallel video plan "beams" is initialized, each starting with the current visual observation (`x_0`).
2. **Iterative Expansion (for each planning step `h`):**
 - For each beam:
 - Retrieve the latest image state (`x`) from the beam.
 - **Action Generation:** The VLM policy (π_VLM) generates `A` different possible next-step text actions (`a_1:A`) based on `x` and the overall goal `g`.
 - **Video Rollout:** For each of the `A` actions, the video model (f_VM) synthesizes `D` different video rollouts, simulating the low-level visual outcomes. This results in `A × D` potential future video sequences.
 - **Heuristic Evaluation & Selection:** The VLM heuristic function (H_VLM) evaluates the final image state of each of these `A × D` generated videos. The video with the highest heuristic value is selected, and its frames are appended to the current plan beam.
3. **Beam Management:** Periodically (e.g., every 5 steps), the beam with the lowest heuristic value is discarded and replaced by a copy of the beam with the highest value, promoting exploration of promising paths.
4. **Final Plan:** After a fixed planning horizon (`H`), the beam with the highest heuristic value represents the optimal long-horizon video plan (`x*_1:H`).

**Preventing Exploitative Model Dynamics:**
To prevent the planning procedure from exploiting inaccuracies in the video dynamics model (e.g., objects teleporting), a threshold is applied. If a generated video from `f_VM` increases the heuristic estimate (`H_VLM`) above a fixed threshold, it is discarded, encouraging more physically plausible outcomes.

**Action Regression from Video (Execution):**
Once a video plan (`x_1:H`) is synthesized, it needs to be translated into actual robot actions (`u`).
- **Goal-Conditioned Policies (π_control):** Instead of simple inverse-dynamics models for each frame, VLP uses a short-horizon goal-conditioned policy (`π_control(x, x_g)`) which takes the current observation (`x`) and a target future frame from the video plan (`x_g`) and outputs a low-level control action (`u`) to make progress towards `x_g`. This policy is trained on paired image and low-level control snippets.
- **Execution Strategy:** For each frame in the video plan, the goal-conditioned policy is executed for a fixed number of timesteps. The approach found most effective is to condition the policy on *each intermediate frame* of the synthesized video.

**Replanning (Receding Horizon Control):**
For very long-horizon tasks, VLP uses a receding horizon control strategy. It generates video plans for a fixed horizon (which may not complete the task), executes a fixed number of actions, and then regenerates/replans a new video plan from the current observed state. This helps mitigate accumulated errors and extends the effective planning horizon.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results presented in the paper demonstrate that Video Language Planning (VLP) substantially improves performance on complex long-horizon robotic tasks, both in terms of video plan synthesis and actual robot execution.

#### 4.1. Long-Horizon Video Synthesis (Sec. 3.1)

- **Superior Plan Quality:** VLP significantly outperforms baselines in synthesizing complete and coherent long-horizon video plans.
 - **Baselines:** Two main baselines were used: (i) UniPi (a text-to-video model directly trained on long-horizon goals, without VLP's planning process) and (ii) VLP without the heuristic function (chaining VLM policy with video model).
 - **Quantitative Results (Table 1):** On simulated Language Table tasks ("Move Area," "Group Color," "Make Line") and real Language Table tasks ("Move Area," "Group Color," "Make Line"), VLP achieved substantially higher success rates (e.g., 98% for "Group Color" in sim, 100% for "Move Area" in real) compared to UniPi (2-12%) and VLP (No Value Function) (4-64%). This highlights the importance of the hierarchical planning structure and the VLM heuristic function.
- **Qualitative Illustration (Figure 2):** VLP successfully generates detailed video plans with associated language sub-goals for complex tasks like "group blocks by color" and "make a horizontal line" on both simulated and real images, starting only from an initial image and a language goal.
- **Effect of Search (Figure 3 & Table 3):** VLP's performance scales positively with increased computation budget, specifically with higher branching factors in the tree search (language branching, video branching) and more parallel plan beams. Increased search budget leads to significantly higher success rates in synthesizing long-horizon plans.
- **Generalization to Complex Manipulators (Figures 4, 5):**
 - **7DoF Mobile Manipulators:** VLP can generate plans for higher-DoF manipulators, demonstrating the ability to reason about actions like opening and closing drawers.
 - **14DoF Bi-Manual Manipulators (ALOHA):** VLP successfully synthesizes multi-view (4-camera) consistent video plans for dexterous manipulation tasks like stacking bowls, cups, and utensils, using top and side views as input for VLM policy and heuristic.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Execution (Sec. 3.2)

- **Substantial Improvement over Baselines:** When plans are executed on physical or simulated robots, VLP-based systems exhibit dramatically higher task completion rates.
 - **Baselines:** Compared against (i) PaLM-E (VLM for text plans), (ii) UniPi (direct video generation), (iii) LAVA (language-conditioned behavioral cloning), and (iv) RT-2 (vision-language-action model).
 - **Quantitative Results (Table 2):** VLP achieved 64% completion for "Move Area," 92% for "Group Color," and 16% for "Make Line" in simulation. These rates significantly surpass all baselines, many of which had 0-4% completion for challenging long-horizon tasks, indicating that baselines often became "stuck."
- **Effective Execution Strategy:**
 - **Goal-Conditioned Policy:** Using a goal-conditioned policy that acts towards *each intermediate frame* of the synthesized video (vs. only the last frame) yielded the best execution performance (Table 4). This fine-grained control allows for better progress and error correction.
 - **Replanning:** The receding horizon control strategy (replanning after a fixed number of executions) is implicitly shown to be effective, as tasks of ~1500 steps were completed, far exceeding a single planning horizon.
- **Real-World Robot Execution (Figures 7, 8):** VLP plans were successfully executed on three hardware platforms: the Language Table robot (for object rearrangement), a 7DoF mobile manipulator (for drawer tasks), and the 14DoF bi-manual ALOHA platform (for dexterous stacking), confirming its applicability beyond simulation.

#### 4.3. Generalization (Sec. 3.3)

- **Generalization to New Objects and Lighting (Figure 9):** VLP demonstrates strong generalization capabilities due to its modular design. The video model focuses on visual dynamics, while the goal-conditioned policy focuses on relevant visual details for nearby goals. This allows VLP to generalize execution to scenes with novel objects (e.g., rubber donut, wooden hexagon) and different lighting conditions (new office environment).
- **Generalization to New Tasks (Figure 10):** By co-training VLM and text-to-video models on internet-scale data, VLP can generalize to novel instructions and unseen objects, such as "Pick snicker energy bar" or "Move moose toy near green pear," showing signs of transferring internet knowledge.

**Limitations (Sec. 5):**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including:
- **Limited World State Representation:** Images alone may not capture full 3D state, physics, or latent factors like mass. Multi-view videos or richer heuristic inputs could mitigate this.
- **Inaccurate Dynamics Simulation:** The video dynamics model can sometimes produce physically inconsistent results (objects appearing/disappearing, teleporting). Larger models, more training data, or explicit reinforcement learning for physics might improve this.

Overall, the findings strongly support VLP as a robust and scalable framework for long-horizon visual planning in robotics, effectively combining the strengths of various large generative models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "VIDEO LANGUAGE PLANNING" (VLP) paper marks a significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics, offering a novel paradigm for tackling complex, long-horizon tasks. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Synergistic Integration of Foundation Models:** VLP provides a compelling example of how different large, pre-trained generative "foundation models"—specifically vision-language models (VLMs) and text-to-video models—can be compositionally combined to achieve capabilities far beyond what any single model could accomplish. This "test-time composition" paradigm, where models are orchestrated through a planning procedure rather than end-to-end fine-tuning, is a powerful new direction for AI. It enables leveraging the abstract reasoning of VLMs and the dynamic prediction of video models in a coherent framework.

2. **Bridging the High-Level/Low-Level Planning Gap:** The paper effectively addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics: connecting high-level symbolic task planning with low-level continuous motion control. By generating multimodal video plans, VLP creates a rich, visually grounded "plan" that seamlessly bridges the abstract language instruction with concrete visual sub-goals, which can then be directly executed by a goal-conditioned policy. This offers a more robust and interpretable planning pathway than purely textual or purely end-to-end approaches.

3. **Enhanced Long-Horizon Task Performance:** The demonstrated improvements in long-horizon task success rates on both simulated and real robots are substantial. Outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like PaLM-E and RT-2 by a significant margin for tasks involving multiple steps and object interactions highlights VLP's effectiveness. This is critical for moving robotics beyond simple, short-duration tasks towards more human-like capabilities in complex environments.

4. **Scalability and Adaptability:** The tree-search mechanism allows VLP to scale with increased computational resources, yielding higher-quality plans with more compute. This inherent scalability is valuable for tackling increasingly complex problems. Furthermore, the modular nature (separate policy, dynamics, and heuristic models) allows for individual components to be improved or updated, potentially leading to more adaptable systems. The use of receding horizon control also ensures robustness against execution errors and allows for indefinite task completion.

5. **Improved Generalization:** VLP demonstrates strong generalization to new objects, lighting conditions, and even novel tasks, attributed to the broad knowledge encoded in internet-scale pre-trained models. This is a crucial step towards deploying robots in diverse, unstructured real-world environments where encountering unseen scenarios is common.

6. **Broader Implications for AI and Media Generation:** While primarily focused on robotics, the authors acknowledge that VLP's principles could extend to other domains, such as "steerable media generation." The ability to plan and synthesize long, coherent video sequences guided by high-level language opens avenues for more sophisticated content creation tools, allowing users to direct generative models with complex, multi-step narratives.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** By enabling robots to reliably execute long-horizon, complex tasks, VLP could significantly accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in logistics, manufacturing, domestic assistance, and hazardous environments.
- **More Capable Embodied AI:** This work contributes to the vision of more generally capable embodied AI agents that can understand, plan, and act in the physical world with a richer, more intuitive understanding of dynamics and task semantics.
- **New Research Directions:** VLP paves the way for further research into improving the fidelity of video dynamics models, incorporating more sophisticated 3D world representations, and integrating explicit physics-based reasoning or reinforcement learning feedback within the planning loop. The concept of compositional foundation models is likely to inspire further exploration across AI.

In conclusion, VLP represents a powerful convergence of recent advancements in generative AI and classical planning principles. It offers a robust, scalable, and generalizable framework for visual planning, pushing the boundaries of what robots can achieve and laying groundwork for future intelligent systems that seamlessly integrate high-level cognition with low-level physical interaction.
