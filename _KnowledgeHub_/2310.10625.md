# Video Language Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIDEO LANGUAGE PLANNING (VLP) algorithm enables long-horizon visual planning for robotic tasks by combining vision-language models (VLMs) with text-to-video models in a tree-search framework. This approach substantially improves task completion rates on complex multi-step tasks across simulated and physical robot platforms.

## Problem

- Existing robotic planning methods struggle with complex, long-horizon tasks in variable real-world environments.
- Large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) provide abstract planning but lack an intrinsic understanding of physical dynamics and how objects interact over time.
- Text-to-video models can learn dynamics for short sequences but cannot generate long-horizon plans or perform high-level abstract reasoning.

## Method

- A **VLP** (Video Language Planning) algorithm orchestrates ==vision-language models (VLMs)== and ==text-to-video models== within a ==parallel hill climbing tree-search== framework.
- VLMs serve as both ==policy generators== for abstract text actions and ==heuristic functions== to evaluate the favorability of future visual states.
- Text-to-video models function as ==dynamic simulators==, predicting detailed short video sequences showing the outcome of proposed actions. Synthesized video plans are translated into robot actions using a ==goal-conditioned policy== that targets intermediate frames, coupled with a ==receding horizon control== strategy for execution.

## Results

- VLP achieved up to **98%** success rate in synthesizing long-horizon video plans for tasks like "Group Color" in simulation, significantly outperforming baselines (**2-64%**).
- On robot execution, VLP completed tasks such as "Group Color" in simulation at **92%**, substantially exceeding baseline performance (**0-4%** for challenging long-horizon tasks).
- The framework generalized to new objects, lighting conditions, and novel instructions, and was demonstrated on 7DoF mobile and 14DoF bi-manual robot manipulators.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining large generative foundation models—specifically VLMs for abstract reasoning and text-to-video models for dynamics—via a planning algorithm enables emergent capabilities for long-horizon visual tasks.
> - A VLM can effectively serve as a heuristic function within a tree search, guiding the generation of physically plausible and goal-directed video plans.
> - Synthesizing detailed, multimodal video plans that link high-level language instructions to specific visual states provides a robust representation for robot execution.
