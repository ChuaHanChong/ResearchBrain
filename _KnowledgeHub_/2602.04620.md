# QUATRO: Query-Adaptive Trust Region Policy Optimization for LLM Fine-tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> QUATRO presents a query-adaptive trust-region policy optimization framework for fine-tuning large language models, explicitly enforcing per-query Kullback-Leibler divergence constraints to improve training stability and preserve solution diversity. The method consistently achieves higher performance and maintains stable policy entropy across mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming existing heuristic reinforcement learning approaches.

## Problem

- Existing GRPO-style reinforcement learning fine-tuning methods rely on heuristic importance-ratio clipping, which can lead to "gradient masking" and unstable policy updates.
- These methods exhibit high sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., learning rates) and policy staleness, causing performance degradation and instability during training.
- Uniform trust-region constraints across all queries in current methods often result in "entropy collapse" and reduced diversity of reasoning, limiting exploration for complex tasks.

## Method

- **QUATRO** formulates a prompt-wise trust-region optimization problem, applying a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence constraint independently for each input query to limit policy deviation.
- It derives an exact query-adaptive objective function using Lagrangian dual analysis, which includes an intrinsic log-ratio stabilizer term for adaptive regulation of policy updates.
- The method dynamically determines query-specific dual variables that control the advantage term, providing instance-level regularization based on the reward distribution for each query.

## Results

- QUATRO consistently outperformed GRPO-style baselines in pass rates across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating enhanced problem-solving capabilities, especially at higher sampling budgets.
- It maintained stable token-level policy entropy throughout training, avoiding the rapid entropy collapse observed in baseline methods, which indicates preserved output diversity.
- The framework achieved significantly higher Unique Correct Count (UCC@k) and greater output diversity (measured by various similarity metrics), generating a broader range of distinct correct solutions.
- Demonstrated robustness to increased policy staleness and larger learning rates, maintaining stable performance where baseline methods exhibited degradation or divergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent log-ratio term within the objective provides a symmetric stabilization mechanism, steering the policy towards the trust-region optimum without relying on heuristic clipping.
> - Query-specific dual variables act as dynamic regularization parameters, adapting policy update magnitudes based on the variance of rewards for individual prompts.
> - Directly enforcing per-query KL constraints effectively mitigates entropy collapse and fosters greater exploration, allowing the model to generate a wider variety of correct reasoning paths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) fine-tuning, specifically using reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Recent advancements in LLMs, particularly for complex tasks like mathematical reasoning and code generation, have been significantly driven by reward-based fine-tuning. This approach allows for iterative refinement of LLM policies beyond static data imitation. A central challenge in this domain is to ensure policy updates improve performance without compromising the model's existing capabilities.

Trust-region policy optimization (TRPO), a concept originating from classical reinforcement learning, offers a framework for this by limiting the magnitude of policy updates. While various practical RL fine-tuning algorithms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Grouped Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), have incorporated simplified or heuristic trust-region principles (e.g., importance-ratio clipping), they often lack the explicit enforcement of trust-region constraints. This leads to issues such as sensitivity to hyperparameters, instability under policy staleness or aggressive learning rates, and a tendency towards "entropy collapse" or "mode concentration," where the model focuses on a narrow subset of responses, limiting exploration and diversity.

QUATRO positions itself as an advancement over these heuristic GRPO-style methods by proposing a principled, query-adaptive approach to enforce trust-region constraints directly. It aims to provide a more stable, interpretable, and robust optimization framework for LLM fine-tuning, particularly for long-horizon reasoning tasks where maintaining solution diversity and stable exploration is beneficial.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome the structural instabilities and limitations observed in existing GRPO-style RL fine-tuning methods for LLMs. The motivation stems from several identified issues:

- **Heuristic Importance-Ratio Clipping:** Current methods, exemplified by GRPO and its variants, rely on heuristic importance-ratio clipping to regulate policy updates. This mechanism truncates gradients when importance ratios fall outside a predefined range, which can lead to "gradient masking." Consequently, these methods do not effectively control policy updates once the clipping threshold is exceeded, failing to capture the true deviation from the sampling policy and contributing to training instability.

- **Sensitivity to Hyperparameters and Policy Staleness:** The reliance on heuristic clipping makes existing methods highly sensitive to optimization hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate) and policy staleness (the mismatch between the current policy and the policy used to sample data). Large learning rates or reusing rollout samples excessively can increase policy staleness, causing importance ratios to frequently fall outside the clipping range and leading to unstable performance degradation, as demonstrated empirically by the authors.

- **"One-Size-Fits-All" Trust-Region Constraints:** Existing methods apply uniform trust-region constraints and deviation tolerances across all queries, regardless of their intrinsic difficulty or uncertainty. This undifferentiated approach can lead to imbalanced learning: easy prompts with confident high-reward responses might be over-amplified to become deterministic, while complex reasoning tasks may lack the necessary exploration budget to find correct paths. This imbalance contributes to probability mass concentrating on a small subset of responses, resulting in "entropy collapse" and reduced diversity of reasoning. This phenomenon forces models into repetitive patterns, hindering their ability to "search" for varied solutions.

To address these limitations, QUATRO aims to:
- **Formulate a Query-Conditioned Trust-Region Problem:** Introduce a trust-region problem that explicitly accounts for the heteroscedasticity of reasoning tasks, applying constraints independently for each prompt.
- **Derive an Exact Query-Adaptive Objective:** Utilize a Lagrangian dual analysis to derive an objective that directly enforces the trust-region (TR) constraint, rather than relying on heuristic approximations like clipping.
- **Achieve Stable and Entropy-Controlled Optimization:** By explicitly keeping each policy update within a controlled deviation, eliminate the need for importance-ratio clipping, resolve gradient masking, and ensure training robustness to off-policy staleness and learning-rate variations. This approach is intended to preserve exploration and mitigate entropy collapse.
- **Provide a Clear and Interpretable Objective:** Develop an objective that offers explicit control over policy updates and includes stabilizer terms arising intrinsically from the exact trust-region formulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QUATRO reformulates the LLM fine-tuning problem as a query-adaptive trust-region policy optimization. The methodology involves:

1. **Prompt-wise Trust-Region Problem Formulation:**
 - For each input query `q`, the objective is to maximize the expected trajectory-level reward `R(o | q)` under an updated policy `π_θ`.
 - This maximization is subject to a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence constraint: `KL(π_θ(· | q) || π_old(· | q)) ≤ δ`. This constraint limits the deviation of the updated policy `π_θ` from the reference policy `π_old` for each specific query `q`, where `δ > 0` defines the maximum allowed deviation. This constraint is enforced independently for each prompt.

2. **Lagrangian Dual Analysis and Optimal Policy Derivation:**
 - The constrained optimization problem is transformed into a Lagrangian dual formulation by introducing dual variables `λ_q ≥ 0` for the KL constraint and `μ_q ∈ ℝ` for the probability normalization constraint of `π_θ(· | q)`.
 - By finding the first variation of the Lagrangian with respect to `π`, the optimal policy `π_*(o | q)` is derived as:
 `π_*(o | q) = π_old(o | q) * exp((R(o | q) - μ*_q) / λ*_q - 1)`.
 - This expression indicates that the ratio between the optimal and old policies for a trajectory is an exponential function of the reward, scaled by `λ*_q` and shifted by `μ*_q`.
 - The optimal dual variables `λ*_q` and `μ*_q` are then derived by minimizing the dual objective. Notably, `λ*_q` is found to be query-specific, acting as a regularization parameter that adapts based on the variance of the reward distribution for query `q`.

3. **Objective Function Derivation (QUATRO Objective):**
 - The optimal policy `π_*` is shown to be the solution to minimizing a loss function `L_q` (Eq. 14). This objective function is given by:
 `L_q = - Σ [ (π_θ(o^(i)|q) / π_old(o^(i)|q)) * (A_i_q - log(π_θ(o^(i)|q) / π_old(o^(i)|q))) ]`
 - Here, `π_bar_θ` denotes a gradient-detached version of `π_θ`, and `A_i_q = (R(o^(i)|q) - μ*_q) / λ*_q - 1` is the query-adaptive advantage term.
 - The objective function retains a log-ratio term `log(π_bar_θ / π_old)`, which acts as a stabilizer by providing symmetric corrections that steer the current policy `π_θ` towards the trust-region optimal policy `π_*` without relying on heuristic clipping.
 - The paper also shows that incorporating an auxiliary KL-divergence penalty `βKL(π_θ | π_pre)` (where `π_pre` is a pretrained model) into the objective (Eq. 17) results in an optimal policy that is a geometric interpolation between the TR optimal policy `π_*` and the pretrained model `π_pre`.

4. **Approximation of Adjoint Variables and Algorithm:**
 - The dual objective `f_q(λ)` is approximated using Monte Carlo samples `{o^(i)}` from `π_old`. A scalar optimization is then performed to find `λ*_q`.
 - **Algorithm 1 (Adaptive Trust-Region Policy Optimization):**
 1. Initialize `π_θ` with a pretrained policy `π_pre`.
 2. Iteratively:
 a. Sample a batch of prompts `D_b`.
 b. Update `π_old` to `π_θ`.
 c. Sample rollouts `{o^(i)}` from `π_old` for each `q ∈ D_b`.
 d. Compute rewards `{R_i}`.
 e. Optimize `(λ*_q, μ*_q)` for each `q ∈ D_b` using the empirical approximation.
 f. Compute query-adaptive advantages `A_i_q`.
 g. Update `π_θ` by maximizing the objective (Eq. 17) for `K` inner-loop updates.

5. **Interpretation:**
 - The **log-ratio term** `log(π_θ / π_old)` acts as a stabilizer, providing adaptive regulation of policy updates to counteract over- or under-estimation relative to the trust-region optimum.
 - The **dual variables (λ*_q, μ*_q)** explicitly control the advantage term. `λ*_q` functions similarly to the standard deviation in GRPO/GSPO's heuristic normalization but is dynamically determined by the trust-region radius `δ` and the reward distribution, leading to query-specific regularization. A larger `δ` allows for smaller `λ*_q`, amplifying the advantage and permitting larger updates, aligning with the intuition of a looser trust-region constraint.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of QUATRO was conducted on mathematical reasoning tasks using Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B models.

- **Overall Performance (Pass@k):**
 - QUATRO consistently outperformed GRPO-style baselines (GRPO, GPG, GMPO, GSPO) across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, AMC23, AIME24, AIME25, Minerva-Math, and OlympiadBench) for both model scales.
 - The performance gains were more pronounced at higher `k` values (number of samples), suggesting better problem-solving capabilities when more attempts are allowed.
 - The trust-region radius `δ` (a hyperparameter for QUATRO) allowed for explicit control: a larger `δ` (e.g., 0.1) emphasized exploitation for higher accuracy, while a smaller `δ` (e.g., 0.001) fostered exploration for increased solution diversity.

- **Entropy Stability and Optimization Behavior:**
 - Baseline methods like GSPO exhibited rapid entropy collapse early in training, indicating a reduction in output diversity.
 - QUATRO maintained stable token-level policy entropy throughout training, avoiding premature distribution sharpening. This behavior is linked to improved exploration and reasoning robustness.

- **Diversity in Unique Correct Count (UCC@k):**
 - The paper introduced a new metric, UCC@k, to quantify the number of unique correct solutions found among `k` samples, addressing the limitation of Pass@k which only measures if at least one correct answer is present.
 - QUATRO achieved significantly higher UCC@k values across all datasets and sampling budgets compared to baselines. This indicates that QUATRO generates a broader range of distinct correct reasoning paths rather than merely repeated variations of a single solution.
 - Output similarity analysis using TF-IDF Cosine, ROUGE-L, Jaccard, and Normalized Edit Similarity metrics further corroborated that QUATRO produces more diverse responses, suggesting a wider coverage of correct solution patterns.

- **Stability Analysis:**
 - **Under Policy Staleness:** When the number of offline inner-loop updates (rollout reuse) was increased, GRPO-style baselines showed reward degradation or collapse. QUATRO maintained stable performance, demonstrating robustness to increased policy staleness.
 - **Under Large Learning Rates:** Baselines quickly diverged under larger learning rates. QUATRO tolerated significantly higher learning rates without instability, supporting faster convergence and efficient optimization in aggressive settings.
 - **Role of the Log-Ratio Penalty (Ablation Study):** Empirical ablation confirmed that removing the log-ratio term from the QUATRO objective led to reward collapse and abrupt spikes in KL divergence. Its inclusion yielded stable reward improvement with controlled KL dynamics, validating its role as a stabilizer.

- **Behavioral Case Studies (Appendix):**
 - **Flip Rate Analysis:** QUATRO consistently achieved higher "flip rates" (percentage of problems transitioning from unsolved to solved after fine-tuning) in harder difficulty buckets, indicating its ability to solve previously challenging prompts.
 - **Temperature Variation:** While increasing sampling temperature improved diversity for baselines, QUATRO maintained higher unique correct ratios without relying on inference-time temperature adjustments. This suggests that QUATRO's diversity stems from learning multiple solution modes rather than simply adding stochastic noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

QUATRO offers a significant methodological contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for LLM fine-tuning by moving beyond heuristic approximations to a principled, query-adaptive trust-region policy optimization. The core significance lies in:

- **Enhanced Training Stability and Robustness:** By directly enforcing per-query KL constraints and intrinsically including stabilizer terms, QUATRO mitigates common failure modes of GRPO-style methods, such as gradient masking, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and instability under policy staleness or aggressive learning rates. This leads to a more reliable and robust fine-tuning process.
- **Improved Policy Interpretability and Control:** The derived objective provides a clear optimization interpretation with an analyzable control knob (`δ`) for regulating policy updates. The query-adaptive dual variables (`λ*_q`, `μ*_q`) allow for fine-grained, instance-level control over policy updates, which is a notable advancement over global or heuristic penalties.
- **Mitigation of Entropy Collapse and Promotion of Diversity:** QUATRO's ability to maintain stable policy entropy throughout training and achieve higher Unique Correct Count (UCC@k) signifies its capacity to preserve and foster exploration. For complex reasoning tasks, this means LLMs can generate a wider variety of valid solutions, making them more robust and less prone to falling into repetitive or limited reasoning ruts.
- **Improved Performance on Complex Reasoning Tasks:** The consistent outperformance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, especially at higher sampling budgets (k), indicates that QUATRO contributes to more effective problem-solving capabilities in LLMs. The method's ability to tackle previously unsolved or very hard problems (as shown by flip-rate analysis) further underscores its utility.

The potential impact of QUATRO lies in its ability to enable more stable, efficient, and effective fine-tuning of large language models for complex, long-horizon reasoning tasks. This could lead to:
- **More Reliable LLM Deployment:** Models fine-tuned with QUATRO may exhibit fewer brittle optimization behaviors, making them more predictable and trustworthy in real-world applications where robustness is critical.
- **Advanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By fostering diverse solution generation and preventing mode collapse, QUATRO could push the boundaries of LLMs' reasoning abilities, allowing them to explore a wider range of problem-solving strategies.
- **Reduced Computational Costs:** The robustness to aggressive learning rates and policy staleness might allow for more efficient use of computational resources during fine-tuning, potentially accelerating the development cycle for advanced LLMs.

The work does not anticipate specific negative ethical or societal impacts beyond those already associated with large language models generally.
