# Rake-Compress Riccati Recursions for Parallel Scenario-Tree Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> João Sousa-Pinto developed rake–compress Riccati recursions for parallel scenario-tree Model Predictive Control, providing an algebraically exact solver for tree-structured LQR problems. This method achieves an optimal O(log N) parallel span and O(N) work, confirmed by GPU benchmarks showing up to 14.74x speedup for an autonomous driving application.

## Problem

- Scenario-tree Model Predictive Control (MPC) for uncertain nonlinear systems relies on solving tree-structured Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR) subproblems.
- Traditional tree Riccati recursions, while efficient in work (O(N)), suffer from a computational span (longest dependency chain) proportional to the tree's height, hindering parallelization, especially for irregular or tall trees.
- A direct, algebraically exact, and work-efficient parallel factorization for arbitrary rooted trees was needed to overcome these height-dependent limitations.

## Method

- Developed **rake–compress Riccati recursions** by adapting the ==rake–compress tree contraction algorithm== to solve dual-regularized branched LQR problems.
- Implemented a two-phase process: a factorization phase (forward contraction, reverse expansion) for computing quadratic factors, and a solve phase for efficiently computing affine solution components given a right-hand side.
- Designed a ==deterministic static scheduler== for rake–compress operations, guaranteeing ==O(log N)== structural rounds and O(log N) primitive dependency depth for arbitrary rooted trees, enabling optimal parallel complexity.

## Results

- The solver demonstrated high algebraic accuracy, with maximum absolute KKT residuals of **3.89e-15** and differences from independent dense solutions of **1.25e-14**.
- Empirical measurements of primitive operation levels across various tree topologies confirmed the theoretical O(log N) growth in dependency depth.
- Achieved significant GPU speedups, including up to **15.14x** faster factorization and **24.33x** faster solves for synthetic LQR problems, and a **14.74x** reduction in end-to-end nonlinear solve time for a constrained autonomous driving example.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating rake–compress tree contraction with Riccati recursions yields an algebraically exact parallel solver for tree-structured LQR problems with an optimal O(log N) span and O(N) work.
> - The standard nonanticipative node-control formulation of scenario MPC can be exactly transformed into an edge-control normal form, simplifying the internal algebra without increasing problem complexity.
> - A deterministic static scheduler, combined with a readiness-weighted reduction strategy for sibling contributions, ensures logarithmic dependency depth for any rooted tree topology.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a widely used control strategy, and its application to problems involving uncertainty often employs scenario trees to represent possible future information histories. This approach, known as scenario-tree MPC, is relevant across various domains, including stochastic power dispatch, dual control, and multimodal motion planning. When applied to nonlinear systems, scenario-tree MPC typically involves solving a sequence of linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) subproblems derived from linearization and quadratic approximation, often within a primal-dual interior-point method.

Existing numerical methods for solving tree-structured LQR problems include sparse solvers and tree Riccati recursions. While these methods achieve computational work linear in the number of nodes (`O(N)`), their backward and forward passes exhibit a dependency chain proportional to the tree's height. This "height-dependent span" becomes a significant limitation for parallel computation, particularly for tall or unbalanced trees common in real-world applications where uncertainty resolution times may vary across branches or pruning leads to irregular structures.

Other approaches to parallelizing Riccati methods, such as temporal parallel methods for chains, focus on one-dimensional systems or specific decomposition strategies. Scenario decomposition methods address large trees by dualizing nonanticipativity or dynamics, but this work focuses on direct optimal control factorization. The concept of rake–compress tree contraction, introduced by Miller and Reif, is a known technique for parallel graph algorithms, offering `O(log N)` span for tree operations. Previous work has used similar contraction for probabilistic graphical models.

This research integrates the established concept of rake–compress tree contraction with Riccati recursions to develop a parallel solver for tree-structured LQR problems. It aims to overcome the height-dependent span limitation of traditional tree Riccati methods, extending temporal parallelism from chains to arbitrary rooted trees, and providing an algebraically exact, work-efficient solution. The work differentiates itself from existing methods by directly factoring the original sparse problem on arbitrary rooted trees, without requiring specific topology patterns or forming a dense global system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to enable efficient, high-performance solution of scenario-tree MPC problems, particularly those involving large and irregular scenario trees. The existing sequential nature of standard tree Riccati recursions presents a computational bottleneck, especially in contexts requiring real-time performance or handling extensive future uncertainties.

The key objectives are as follows:

1. **Develop an Algebraically Exact Parallel Solver:** The overarching objective is to create a numerical method that is algebraically equivalent to solving the underlying Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) system for dual-regularized branched LQR problems. "Algebraically exact" implies correctness in exact arithmetic, distinguishing it from approximate or heuristic parallelization strategies.
2. **Achieve Optimal Parallel Complexity:** A central goal is to attain `O(N)` arithmetic work and `O(log N)` span (the longest dependency chain under unlimited parallelism) for factorization and each right-hand-side solve. This efficiency must be independent of the tree's height, balance, or maximum out-degree, which is a significant improvement over traditional methods.
3. **Support Dual Regularization:** The solver should accommodate positive-semidefinite dual regularization blocks, which are common in primal–dual interior-point methods for nonlinear programming. This allows the method to be directly integrated into state-of-the-art nonlinear optimization frameworks.
4. **Handle Standard Scenario MPC Formulations:** The work aims to provide an exact linear-size transformation from the common nonanticipative node-control formulation of scenario MPC to the internal edge-control form used by the solver, ensuring broad applicability without duplicating decisions or increasing problem size disproportionately.
5. **Design a Reusable Deterministic Contraction Schedule:** To achieve consistent parallel performance, the research seeks to develop a static topology plan for arbitrary rooted trees. This plan should guarantee geometric progress (removing a constant fraction of nodes in each structural round) and minimize primitive dependency depth.
6. **Provide Open-Source Implementation and Validation:** A practical objective is to implement the proposed algorithms in open-source JAX packages. This includes the bidirectional rake–compress contraction, the dual-regularized LQR solver, and a user-facing primal–dual interior-point solver for tree-structured optimal control. The implementation is to be validated through KKT accuracy checks, accelerator benchmarks, and a demonstration on a constrained nonlinear autonomous-driving example.

These objectives collectively aim to provide a robust, scalable, and practically usable solution for a class of optimal control problems critical in many engineering and scientific domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on adapting the rake–compress tree contraction algorithm to solve dual-regularized branched LQR problems. The approach is structured into problem formulation, local condensation, the rake–compress factorization, the rake–compress solve, and parallel complexity analysis.

**Problem Formulation:**
The core problem is formulated as a dual-regularized branched LQR in an "edge-control normal form" (Equation 1). This formulation minimizes/maximizes a quadratic Lagrangian subject to linear dynamics and includes quadratic costs and dual regularization terms ($\Delta_i$). The paper proves well-posedness under convexity assumptions (positive definite control costs $R_e$, positive semidefinite dual regularizers $\Delta_i$, and a convexity condition on state costs $Q_i$ and control matrices $M_e$). A key aspect is the exact linear-size lifting from the standard scenario-MPC node-control formulation to this edge-control form (Proposition 2), which simplifies the internal algebra without increasing complexity.

**Local Control Condensation:**
Before contraction, each control variable $u_e$ associated with an edge $e=(i,j)$ is eliminated locally. This initial step transforms the original node and edge data ($Q_i, M_e, R_e, A_e, B_e, \Delta_j$) into a reduced set of node data ($U_i = Q_i$) and edge data triples ($\alpha_e = (\bar{A}_e, \bar{C}_e, \bar{P}_e)$). These triples represent conditional quadratic functions that are closed under the subsequent rake and compress operations.

**Rake–Compress Factorization:**
This phase computes the homogeneous quadratic factors (Hessians of subtree value functions).
1. **Forward Contraction:** The algorithm applies two fundamental primitives:
 - **Rake:** For a non-root leaf node $j$ with parent $i$ and edge $e=(i,j)$, node $j$ and edge $e$ are removed. Their combined quadratic contribution $\tau(\alpha_e, U_j)$ is added to the parent node $U_i$.
 - **Compress:** For a non-root unary node $j$ with incoming edge $e_\ell=(i,j)$ and outgoing edge $e_r=(j,k)$, node $j$ and edges $e_\ell, e_r$ are removed. They are replaced by a single edge $e_{\ell r}=(i,k)$ whose data $\alpha_{e_{\ell r}}$ result from composing $\alpha_{e_\ell}$, $U_j$, and $\alpha_{e_r}$ using an associative conditional-value composition (Lemma 1).
 These operations are performed repeatedly, contracting the tree to its root. Intermediate factors (e.g., $U_j$ for rake, $\bar{A}_e, \bar{C}_e, \bar{P}_e$ for compress) are stored on a "tape" for reverse expansion. Proposition 3 guarantees that all intermediate operations are well-defined and preserve convexity invariants.
2. **Reverse Expansion:** After the tree is contracted to its root, the stored factors are used in reverse order to reconstruct the matrices $P_i$ for all original nodes (Theorem 1). These $P_i$ matrices represent the Hessians of the homogeneous subtree value functions.
3. **Local Factor Construction:** Once all $P_i$ are available, node- and edge-local factors ($F_i, W_i, G_e, H_e, K_e, A_{cl_e}, T_e$) are computed in parallel. These factors are reused for subsequent solves.

**Rake–Compress Solve:**
This phase computes the affine (vector-valued) components of the solution for a given right-hand side $(q, r, c)$, using the factorization from the previous phase.
1. **Affine Subtree Recursion:** The linear coefficients $p_i$ of the non-homogeneous subtree value functions are recursively defined (Lemma 2).
2. **Forward Contraction (Affine):** The same rake–compress topology plan is used, but applied to affine data ($h_i = q_i$ and affine edge maps $\zeta_e(v) = Z_e v + z_e$). This process computes $p_\rho$ at the root (Proposition 5).
3. **Reverse Expansion (Affine):** Using the stored affine data and the computed $p_\rho$, all $p_i$ are recovered by reverse induction.
4. **Root State Solution:** The root state $x_\rho$ is determined using $p_\rho$ and the local factors.
5. **State Recovery:** A second pass, leveraging the same rake–compress plan, contracts state transition maps $\psi_e(x) = T_e x + b_e$. In reverse expansion, starting from $x_\rho$, all $x_i$ are recovered concurrently based on the contraction plan.
6. **Control and Multiplier Recovery:** Finally, all control variables $u_e$ and dual variables $y_i$ are computed independently using the recovered states and local factors (Theorem 2).

**Parallel Complexity:**
1. **Deterministic Structural Schedule:** A static schedule for rake–compress operations is constructed once per topology. This schedule first rakes all non-root leaves, then compresses a maximal set of pairwise non-adjacent unary nodes. Lemma 3 proves that each non-trivial round removes at least one-third of the nodes, guaranteeing `O(log N)` structural rounds.
2. **Static Dependency Levels:** The structural rounds are further decomposed into primitive numerical operations (rakes, binary combinations of sibling contributions, absorptions, compressions). Lemma 4 introduces a readiness-weighted reduction strategy for sibling contributions, ensuring efficient parallel combination. Lemma 5 proves that the maximum dependency depth (span) of this entire process is `O(log N)`, regardless of the tree's original height or branching factor.
3. **Work and Storage:** Theorem 4 establishes `O(N)` work and `O(N)` storage for fixed state and control dimensions, matching the sequential complexity while achieving logarithmic span.

**Implementation:**
The methodology is implemented using three open-source JAX packages: `jax-bidirectional-tree-rake-compress` for the core contraction, `regularized_lqr_jax` for the dual-regularized LQR solver, and `primal-dual-lipa` for the complete primal–dual interior-point method. JAX's features like `jax.vmap` for vectorization, JIT compilation, and automatic differentiation are leveraged for performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several theoretical and empirical findings.

**Theoretical Findings:**
1. **Algebraic Exactness and Equivalence:** The rake–compress Riccati solver is algebraically exact, meaning it is mathematically equivalent in exact arithmetic to solving the full Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) system of the dual-regularized branched LQR problem (Theorem 3). The well-posedness of all elimination and reconstruction steps is proven under standard convexity assumptions (Proposition 1, 3, Theorem 1, 2).
2. **Optimal Parallel Complexity:** For a tree with $N$ nodes and fixed state/control dimensions, the numerical stages (factorization and solve) achieve `O(N)` arithmetic work and `O(N)` storage. Crucially, they attain an `O(log N)` span (longest dependency chain under unlimited parallelism), independent of the tree's height, balance, or maximum out-degree (Theorem 4). This logarithmic span represents a significant improvement in parallel efficiency compared to height-dependent traditional Riccati methods.
3. **General Applicability via Lifting:** An exact linear-size transformation is provided to convert the standard nonanticipative node-control formulation of scenario MPC into the edge-control normal form used by the solver. This ensures that the complexity results apply directly to common scenario-MPC models without introducing extraneous operations or increasing problem size beyond linearity (Proposition 2, Corollary 1).
4. **Deterministic Contraction Schedule:** A deterministic static scheduler ensures geometric progress of node removal, with at least one-third of the nodes eliminated in each structural round, leading to at most $\lceil \log_{3/2} |V| \rceil$ rounds (Lemma 3). The method for constructing readiness-weighted reductions for sibling contributions guarantees the `O(log N)` primitive dependency depth (Lemma 4, 5).

**Numerical Validation and Performance:**
1. **KKT Accuracy:** Comparative tests against independently assembled dense KKT systems show high accuracy. For 26 test instances in IEEE 754 double precision, the maximum absolute KKT residual was $3.89 \times 10^{-15}$, and the maximum absolute difference from the independent dense solution was $1.25 \times 10^{-14}$.
2. **Contraction Depth:** Empirical measurements of primitive operation levels for various topology families (chain, balanced, comb, star) confirm the logarithmic growth predicted by the theory, demonstrating `O(log N)` depth in practice.
3. **Accelerator Benchmarks (GPU Speedups):**
 - **Synthetic Tree-LQR Scaling:** For synthetic problems with $N=8192$, GPU factorization was up to $15.14 \times$ faster than sequential CPU factorization (for a star topology), and the reused-factor solve was up to $24.33 \times$ faster. When comparing the parallel workload on GPU vs. CPU, factorization speedups were up to $16.80 \times$. These speedups generally increased with tree size, consistent with the logarithmic dependency depth exposing greater accelerator parallelism.
 - **Autonomous-Driving Refinement:** On a constrained nonlinear autonomous-driving example, refining the temporal discretization to $N=8185$ nodes, the GPU-accelerated Newton factorization was $10.72 \times$ faster than the parallel CPU execution, and the reused-factor solve was $7.81 \times$ faster. This translated to a $14.74 \times$ reduction in end-to-end nonlinear solve time when using the complete primal–dual interior-point algorithm on the GPU. For smaller tree sizes ($N=129$), GPU overhead made factorization and solve slightly slower than on the CPU, indicating a crossover point for efficiency.
4. **Demonstration of Integration:** The constrained autonomous-driving example successfully exercised the complete solver stack on an irregular scenario tree with nonlinear dynamics and inequality constraints. The primal–dual interior-point method converged with high accuracy (largest absolute residual $9.44 \times 10^{-8}$), demonstrating the practical utility of the parallel LQR kernel within a larger nonlinear solver. The resulting policy geometry visually confirmed nonanticipative decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a significant advancement in the computational methods for scenario-tree model predictive control, with broad implications for various scientific and engineering disciplines.

1. **Enabling Large-Scale Uncertainty Management:** The primary significance lies in addressing the computational bottleneck of height-dependent span in traditional tree Riccati recursions. By achieving `O(log N)` span, this work unlocks the ability to efficiently solve LQR subproblems on very large and complex scenario trees, regardless of their height or branching structure. This is crucial for applications that demand detailed and extensive modeling of future uncertainties, such as advanced autonomous systems, energy grid management, and financial modeling.
2. **Advancing Real-Time Scenario MPC:** The improved parallel performance, particularly on accelerators like GPUs, makes real-time implementation of scenario-tree MPC more feasible for safety-critical and high-speed applications. In domains like autonomous driving, where fast decision-making under uncertainty is paramount, this solver can enable more sophisticated and robust planning capabilities.
3. **Robustness and Generality:** The algebraically exact nature of the solver, combined with its support for dual regularization and a rigorous lifting for standard scenario-MPC formulations, ensures its robustness and wide applicability. It can be integrated into existing and future primal–dual interior-point methods for nonlinear optimal control, providing a high-performance linear algebra kernel.
4. **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of JAX-based open-source packages facilitates adoption by the research community and industry practitioners. This lowers the barrier to entry for developing and experimenting with advanced tree-structured optimal control problems, potentially accelerating further research and development in related areas.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework established for rake–compress Riccati recursions provides a theoretical and practical foundation for exploring other tree-structured optimization problems, dynamic programming algorithms, or even generalizations to more complex graph structures. The insights into parallel scheduling for irregular topologies are also valuable beyond optimal control.
6. **Economic and Environmental Impact:** In applications such as stochastic power dispatch, more efficient scenario-tree MPC can lead to better management of renewable energy sources, improved grid stability, and potentially reduced operational costs and environmental impact by optimizing resource allocation under uncertain demand and supply.

In summary, this research provides a high-performance, theoretically sound, and practically implemented solution that significantly enhances the capabilities of scenario-tree MPC, offering a pathway to tackling more complex problems in a scalable manner.
