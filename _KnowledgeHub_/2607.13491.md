# DeepLoop: Depth Scaling for Looped Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepLoop is a residual-scaling rule for looped Transformers, which reuse a small stack of physical blocks across rounds. A first-order perturbation bound with a visit-alignment coefficient shows tied depth requires the DeepNorm exponent to rise from 1/4 to 1/2, giving alpha=(2N)^(1/2) and beta=(8N)^(-1/2). It lowers validation loss and lifts downstream accuracy at every loop count above one.

## Problem

- DeepNorm and related residual-scaling analyses assume every unrolled sublayer owns a distinct parameter tensor contributing one independent update, an assumption weight sharing directly violates.
- Under depth-wise tying a shared update aggregates gradients from all repeated visits and is then read back by those same visits, an aggregation path prior bounds never model.
- Width-and-depth parameterizations such as muP and Depth-muP, and recurrent-depth architectures, prescribe no scaling that accounts for correlation across loop visits.

## Method

- A ==visit-alignment coefficient== bounded between zero and the round count converts the untied stability condition into a tied one, multiplying the sublayer-visit count by that coefficient.
- An ==exponent threshold== proposition shows the admissible exponent is (1+gamma)/4, recovering 1/4 for decorrelated visits and forcing 1/2 for fully aligned visits at fixed physical depth.
- **DeepLoop** keeps the ==Post-LN DeepNorm== block with an inner ==RMSNorm== branch input, proven inert by a ==Jacobian lemma==, and sets alpha=(2N)^(1/2), beta=(8N)^(-1/2) with no gates or learned scalars.

## Results

- On FineWeb-Edu **50B** tokens, DeepLoop ties the pre-LN baseline at one round (**+0.0004 nats**) but cuts validation loss by **0.023 nats** at five rounds (small) and **0.028 nats** at seven rounds (medium).
- On the eight-task lm-evaluation-harness suite at **350M** scale, DeepLoop reaches **55.20%** one-shot average at seven rounds versus **54.62%** baseline, winning **seven of eight** individual tasks.
- Applying the exponent to the Hierarchical Reasoning Model lifts ARC-AGI-1 two-vote accuracy from **36.50%** to **39.75%**, roughly a **six-sigma** effect given **0.5pp** per-seed deviation; a p-sweep shows **0/5** seeds escaping the unigram floor at p=0.30 versus **5/5** at p=0.55.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Residual scaling should depend on how depth is realized, counting parameter visits rather than nominal unrolled layers, whenever blocks are shared across depth.
> - Gradient truncation changes the binding regime: a module updated once per step sits in the untied regime, while the inner module grown by aligned revisits sets the exponent.
> - The conservative exponent trades learning signal for stability; smaller exponents reach lower loss when they converge but destabilize a majority of seeds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Transformer models have demonstrated that increasing model depth is a consistent method for improving their expressivity. In standard Transformer architectures, each additional layer necessitates a new set of attention and feed-forward weights, leading to a direct correlation between model depth and parameter count. This parameter growth can become a limiting factor for deploying very deep models.

Looped Transformers, including concepts from Universal Transformers, ALBERT, and Subformer, address this limitation by decoupling effective depth from parameter count. These architectures reuse a compact stack of *K* physical blocks for *R* rounds, achieving an unrolled depth of *N = KR* while storing only *K* blocks. This approach aligns with recent trends in recurrent-depth models and test-time-compute mechanisms, which aim to leverage additional sequential computation without a proportional increase in stored parameters.

However, existing theoretical analyses for stabilizing deep Transformers, such as DeepNorm, are primarily formulated for *untied* depth. These analyses assume that each unrolled residual sublayer possesses a distinct set of parameters, and thus contributes an independent update term during optimization. This assumption is violated in looped Transformers, where the same physical parameters are repeatedly revisited across multiple rounds. The repeated use of shared weights introduces a coupled aggregation path: a single physical parameter's optimizer update aggregates gradients from all *R* visits, and this single updated parameter is then reused by all *R* visits in the subsequent forward pass. This work identifies and addresses this specific gap in the theoretical understanding of residual scaling for weight-shared, recurrent-depth architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust residual scaling methodology for Looped Transformers that ensures optimization stability as effective depth is increased through parameter reuse. This involves extending current theoretical frameworks, specifically those building on DeepNorm, to account for the unique challenges posed by weight sharing.

The motivation for this work stems from several points:
- **Decoupling Depth and Parameters:** Looped Transformers offer a mechanism to increase the effective depth (*N*) of a model by revisiting a fixed number of physical blocks (*K*) for *R* rounds. This allows for improved expressivity or increased test-time computation without a proportional increase in the total number of stored parameters.
- **Stability Challenges with Weight Sharing:** While beneficial for efficiency, weight sharing fundamentally alters the dynamics of parameter updates compared to untied architectures. In an untied Transformer, each residual branch receives a unique parameter update. In a looped Transformer, a single, shared parameter update aggregates gradients from *R* repeated visits. This shared update is then read back by those same *R* visits in the next linearized forward pass. This creates a potential for magnified instability if not properly addressed, as the update's influence is compounded.
- **Limitations of Existing Scaling Theories:** Current residual-scaling analyses, such as DeepNorm, which provide critical guidelines for training very deep Post-LN Transformers, assume distinct parameters for each unrolled layer. They do not account for the correlated effects introduced when parameters are tied across multiple applications. Consequently, applying standard DeepNorm rules to looped architectures might lead to instability as the loop count (*R*) increases.
- **Need for a Loop-Aware Correction:** The research is motivated by the hypothesis that stable recurrent depth requires residual scaling rules that explicitly consider the dynamics of parameter visits, rather than solely the nominal unrolled layer count. The aim is to formalize this "tied-depth effect" and derive a corrective scaling rule.
- **Practical Applicability:** Developing a stable parameterization is crucial for making looped depth a practical and widely adoptable scaling axis for various models, including large language models and recurrent reasoning architectures. The goal is to provide a deterministic, architecture-agnostic solution that avoids complex gates, learned coefficients, or auxiliary losses.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves both theoretical derivation and empirical validation.

**Theoretical Framework:**
1. **Formalizing the Tied-Depth Effect:** The authors formalize the impact of weight sharing by analyzing a first-order perturbation bound for a looped Transformer. This bound accounts for the aggregation of gradients and sensitivities across repeated visits to the same physical parameters.
2. **Visit-Alignment Coefficient (κ_R):** A key theoretical contribution is the introduction of a visit-alignment coefficient, κ_R. This coefficient quantifies the degree of correlation between visit-wise gradients and sensitivities across different rounds for a shared physical sublayer.
 - κ_R is bounded by 0 ≤ κ_R ≤ R.
 - If visit-wise gradients and sensitivities are nearly orthogonal (decorrelated), κ_R approaches O(1).
 - If they are coherent (fully aligned), κ_R approaches Θ(R).
3. **Modified Stability Condition:** The derived first-order stability condition for a looped Transformer becomes M κ_R (β/α)^2 = O(1), where M = 2N is the total number of unrolled residual sublayer visits, α is the skip connection scale, and β is the residual branch initialization gain. This contrasts with the untied DeepNorm condition M (β/α)^2 = O(1).
4. **Exponent Threshold Derivation:** The analysis considers the scaling family α = (cN)^p and β = (dN)^-p.
 - In the decorrelated regime (κ_R = O(1)), the DeepNorm exponent p = 1/4 is recovered.
 - In the conservative, worst-case aligned regime (κ_R = Θ(R), with fixed physical depth *K* and increasing loop count *R*), the analysis predicts that the exponent *p* must increase to 1/2 to maintain stability.
5. **DeepLoop Parameterization:** Based on the theoretical derivation, DeepLoop is proposed as a specific residual scaling rule. It maintains the Post-LN DeepNorm architecture, augmented with a normalized branch input (a "sandwich" placement common in recurrent-depth models). This inner normalization is shown to be inert to the perturbation analysis. The core of DeepLoop is setting α = (2N)^(1/2) and β = (8N)^(-1/2) for unrolled depth *N*. This represents a "one-line conservative correction" by changing only the exponent from p=1/4 to p=1/2, without introducing gates, learned coefficients, or auxiliary losses.

**Empirical Validation:**
1. **GPT-Style Looped Language Models:**
 - **Architecture:** GPT-2 small (12 layers, hidden size 768) and GPT-2 medium (24 layers, hidden size 1024) backbones are used. Both models incorporate looped residual-block sharing, tied input-output embeddings, and an input-embedding RMSNorm.
 - **Baseline:** The baseline uses a standard pre-normalized looped Transformer block (x ← x + g(Norm(x))) with standard initialization and no residual scaling.
 - **DeepLoop Variant:** Applies the post-normalized sandwich block of Eq. (3.1) with the derived DeepLoop scaling rule.
 - **Training:** Models are trained on FineWeb-Edu for 50 billion tokens (100K optimizer steps), with a context length of 1024.
 - **Loop Counts:** Experiments are conducted across various loop counts R ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}.
 - **Metrics:** Final validation cross-entropy loss and downstream accuracy on an eight-task lm-evaluation-harness suite (zero- and one-shot accuracy).
2. **Hierarchical Recurrent Reasoners (ARC-AGI):**
 - **Architecture:** The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) is used, which combines looping with hierarchical modules and a one-step gradient truncation.
 - **Application of DeepLoop:** The DeepLoop scaling rule with p = 1/2 is applied to HRM, particularly addressing the *L* module which performs aligned revisits. The gradient-visible visit count *M_g* is used for scaling.
 - **Metrics:** Voted accuracy on ARC-AGI-1 puzzles, comparing against the vanilla HRM.
3. **Empirical P-Sweep (Appendix C):** An ablation study is performed by sweeping the exponent *p* across values from 0.30 to 0.60 at a fixed loop count R=3 (GPT-2 small backbone). This directly tests the theoretical prediction of p=1/2 as a stability threshold. Training stability and validation loss are observed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Theoretical Findings:**
- The first-order perturbation bound for looped Transformers is found to be proportional to M κ_R (β/α)^2, directly incorporating the visit-alignment coefficient κ_R.
- The exponent threshold for the scaling family α = (cN)^p, β = (dN)^-p is derived as p ≥ (1 + γ)/4, where κ_R = Θ(R^γ).
- In the specific case where visits decorrelate (γ = 0, κ_R = O(1)), the DeepNorm threshold p = 1/4 is recovered.
- In the conservative aligned regime (γ = 1, κ_R = Θ(R), corresponding to fixed physical depth K and increasing R), the required exponent increases to p = 1/2.
- DeepLoop's proposed scaling rule, α = (2N)^(1/2) and β = (8N)^(-1/2), effectively sets p = 1/2, ensuring the tied-depth bound remains O(1) at fixed physical depth K.
- For hierarchical recurrent reasoners with one-step gradient approximation, the framework predicts p = 1/2 as the binding threshold due to the aligned revisits of the inner module.

**Empirical Results - GPT-Style Looped Language Models:**
- **Validation Loss (Table 1, Figure 2):**
 - At R=1 (no physical block revisited), DeepLoop exhibits comparable validation loss to the baseline (differences of +0.0004 nats at small scale, +0.0011 nats at medium scale).
 - For R ≥ 3, DeepLoop consistently improves final validation loss compared to the baseline. For GPT-2 small, the improvement is -0.0160 nats at R=3, peaking at -0.0231 nats at R=5, and -0.0186 nats at R=7. For GPT-2 medium, the gap widens monotonically, reaching -0.0153 nats at R=3, -0.0196 nats at R=5, and -0.0278 nats at R=7.
- **Downstream Evaluation (Table 2, Figure 3):**
 - DeepLoop and the baseline show comparable average downstream accuracies at R=1 (e.g., -0.01 percentage points for 0-shot Avg, -0.16 for 1-shot Avg at medium scale).
 - As recurrent depth increases (R > 1), DeepLoop generally improves average downstream accuracy. At R=7 for the medium backbone, DeepLoop achieves 53.88% 0-shot Avg (+0.93 pp vs. baseline R=7) and 55.20% 1-shot Avg (+0.58 pp vs. baseline R=7), representing the highest average accuracies in the evaluation.
 - Specifically, at R=7, DeepLoop outperforms the baseline on seven out of eight individual tasks across both 0-shot and 1-shot settings (PIQA being the exception). Noteworthy is a +1.74 pp increase on WinoGrande 0-shot (59.04% vs. 57.30%).

**Empirical Results - Reasoning Evaluation on ARC-AGI (Table 3):**
- Applying the DeepLoop scaling rule (p = 1/2) to the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) results in improved voted accuracy on ARC-AGI-1.
- The paper-protocol two-vote accuracy increases from 36.50% (Vanilla HRM) to 39.75% (DeepLoop), a gain of +3.25 percentage points.
- DeepLoop demonstrates improved accuracy across all evaluated voting budgets (K=1 to K=1000).

**Empirical P-Sweep (Appendix C, Figure 6):**
- The p-sweep at R=3 on the GPT-2 small backbone provides empirical support for the p=1/2 stability threshold.
- Training failures (not escaping the unigram frequency floor) are frequent below p=0.45 (e.g., 0/5 successful seeds at p=0.30, 1/5 at p=0.35, 2/5 at p=0.40 and p=0.45).
- Training becomes universally stable at p ≥ 0.55 (5/5 successful seeds at p=0.55 and p=0.60).
- The transition from unstable to stable training empirically brackets the theoretically predicted p=1/2 threshold.
- Conditional on successful training, larger *p* values (more conservative scaling) resulted in slightly higher validation loss, suggesting a trade-off between aggressive learning and training stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DeepLoop research contributes to the field of deep learning by providing a theoretical framework and a practical solution for stabilizing recurrent-depth Transformers that employ weight sharing. This work has several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Advancement in Transformer Scaling Theory:** The paper extends the existing theory of residual scaling (e.g., DeepNorm) to explicitly account for parameter tying in looped architectures. The introduction of the visit-alignment coefficient (κ_R) provides a formal mechanism to quantify the impact of gradient and sensitivity correlations across repeated visits, which was previously unaddressed. This theoretical underpinning establishes a deeper understanding of stability conditions in weight-shared computational graphs.

2. **Enabling Efficient Deep Models:** By demonstrating how to robustly scale Transformer depth through recurrent computation, DeepLoop makes it more feasible to build very deep models without a proportional increase in parameter count. This is particularly relevant for scenarios where memory footprint is a constraint, or where increased inference-time computation is acceptable for improved performance, such as in large language models (LLMs) and specialized reasoning architectures.

3. **Practical and Minimalist Solution:** DeepLoop offers a simple, deterministic, and effective residual parameterization. It is a "one-line correction" that modifies only the exponent in the DeepNorm scaling rule (from p=1/4 to p=1/2) and does not require complex architectural additions like gates, learned scalars, or auxiliary losses. This simplicity makes it readily applicable and integrate-able into existing Transformer implementations that utilize weight sharing.

4. **Improved Performance and Stability:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that DeepLoop improves validation loss and downstream task performance in looped GPT-style language models as the loop count increases, while performing comparably at R=1 (untied case). Its successful application to the Hierarchical Reasoning Model on ARC-AGI-1 further indicates its broad utility in complex recurrent reasoning architectures. This suggests that the proposed scaling rule effectively mitigates the instability issues associated with increased recurrent depth.

5. **Guidance for Future Architecture Design:** The research provides actionable insights for designing and training future recurrent-depth models. It highlights that the choice of residual scaling parameters should be contingent on how effective depth is achieved—whether through untied layers or repeated block visits—rather than merely the nominal unrolled depth. The empirical p-sweep further offers practical guidance on the safe operating regime for the scaling exponent.

6. **Foundation for Further Research:** The work opens several avenues for future investigation, including directly measuring the visit-alignment coefficient κ_R to better understand the empirical dynamics of gradient alignment, exploring the generalizability of the p=1/2 boundary to larger model scales or different parameterizations, and investigating methods to encourage decorrelated visits that might allow for less conservative scaling exponents.
