# Unleashing More Actions via Action Compositional Training for VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The ACT-VLA framework, developed by researchers at Shenzhen Technology University, significantly improves the compositional generalization of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by automatically synthesizing novel training demonstrations. This approach yielded absolute gains of up to 52.7 and 49.0 percentage points on out-of-distribution compositional tasks within the LIBERO benchmark, enabling VLA models to learn complex skill recombinations without additional human teleoperation.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models frequently overfit to their training data, exhibiting poor generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) compositional scenarios.
- Expanding dataset diversity to cover the combinatorially growing space of possible task compositions is prohibitively costly and labor-intensive through human teleoperation.
- Existing compositional methods often rely on external planners, auxiliary perception components, or inference-time interventions, adding complexity to deployment.

## Method

- **ACT-VLA** (Action Compositional Training for VLA Models) is introduced as an ==offline data augmentation== framework to synthesize novel compositional demonstrations.
- The ==Text Latent Interpolation (TLI)== mechanism is repurposed from an inference-time control to a systematic ==data generation engine==, steering VLA model ==latent representations== to create continuous behavioral transitions.
- Synthesized demonstrations undergo a ==dual quality filtering== process (task completion and step-budget) and are associated with precise, automatically derived natural language instructions before being added to the training set.

## Results

- ACT-VLA achieved an absolute gain of **52.7** percentage points on LIBERO-Spatial-OOD (from **35.5%** to **88.2%**) and **49.0** percentage points on LIBERO-Goal-OOD (from **46.6%** to **95.6%**) with a π0.5 backbone.
- The framework surpassed inference-time TLI, showing higher OOD success rates (e.g., **88.2%** vs. **81.0%** on Spatial-OOD; **95.6%** vs. **85.0%** on Goal-OOD).
- ACT-VLA attained an unweighted mean success rate of **95.6%** across six evaluation suites, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like UniVLA (**70.7%**) and OpenVLA-OFT (**64.9%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models implicitly encode compositional structures in their latent representations that can be effectively exploited for automated data generation, not just inference-time steering.
> - Internalizing compositional transitions into the model's weights through training-time data synthesis provides a more robust and effective strategy for OOD generalization than relying on per-step latent manipulation during inference.
> - Expanding the effective training distribution with high-quality, synthetically generated compositional data without additional human effort significantly enhances VLA generalization and mitigates overfitting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, integrating visual perception, language understanding, and action generation to enable robots to follow natural-language instructions. Despite their capabilities, a fundamental limitation persists: VLA models often overfit to their training data, exhibiting poor generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. This is particularly evident in compositional generalization, where models struggle with novel combinations of previously learned sub-skills. The high cost and labor-intensive nature of collecting diverse robotic demonstration data further exacerbate this challenge, making it impractical to achieve comprehensive generalization solely through manual dataset expansion.

Within the broader landscape of VLA research, numerous efforts have focused on scaling models and data (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, GR00T N1, π0) and enhancing capabilities through methods like instruction tuning (InstructVLA), reconstructive objectives (ReconVLA), or spatial awareness integration (Spatial Forcing, 3D-VLA). This paper builds upon these foundational VLA models.

Regarding compositional generalization, existing approaches can be broadly categorized. Decomposition-based methods (e.g., DeCo, LiLo-VLA) segment tasks into reusable primitives but frequently rely on external planners or auxiliary perception components during inference. Planning-centric approaches (e.g., Generative Skill Chaining, Long-VLA, Plan-Seq-Learn, Inner Monologue) also address long-horizon manipulation, often depending on predefined skill libraries or external reasoning. A particularly relevant line of research, representation-based approaches, has demonstrated that transformer-based VLAs implicitly encode compositional structures within their latent representations. Specifically, prior work by Li introduced Text Latent Interpolation (TLI) to synthesize behavioral transitions by steering these internal representations, albeit primarily as an *inference-time* mechanism.

This research positions itself at the intersection of representation-based and data-centric robot learning paradigms. It leverages the insight that compositional behaviors can emerge from VLA's latent representations, but critically *repurposes* the TLI mechanism from an inference-time intervention to an *offline data augmentation framework*. This distinguishes it from prior compositional methods that rely on external components or only dynamic inference-time adjustments. By generating new training data, this work aligns with data-centric strategies aimed at enriching existing robot datasets without additional manual teleoperation, a direction explored by methods like Interleave-VLA, PixelVLA, and GraspVLA. The paper's novelty lies in transforming compositional trajectory synthesis into a systematic method for expanding the training distribution to directly improve VLA generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to significantly enhance the compositional generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The aim is to enable these models to "unleash more actions"—that is, to compose known sub-skills into a much broader and more diverse set of executable behaviors—beyond those explicitly present in their original demonstrations. This objective is driven by a combination of observed limitations in current VLA models and the practical challenges associated with their development.

Firstly, a primary motivation stems from the inherent problem of overfitting in VLA models. Standard imitation learning paradigms cause models to specialize narrowly on the specific behavioral patterns encountered during training. Consequently, VLA policies often exhibit a severe lack of generalization when faced with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, even those that merely require novel combinations of individual sub-skills that the model has ostensibly learned. For example, a model trained to place a cube on a rectangular tray and a mug on a circular plate may fail to execute the task of placing the mug on the rectangular tray, as this specific pairing was never directly observed. This inability to recombine familiar primitives into unseen sequences fundamentally limits the practical applicability of VLA models.

Secondly, addressing this generalization gap through conventional data acquisition methods is increasingly unsustainable. Expanding dataset diversity and scale to cover the combinatorially growing space of possible task compositions requires extensive and costly human teleoperation. The process involves significant manual supervision, environmental setup, hardware maintenance, and repeated trials for each new task, making it economically and practically unfeasible for large-scale generalization.

To circumvent this impasse without relying on expensive manual data collection, the authors are motivated to explore alternative, scalable approaches to data generation. The core idea is to capitalize on recent findings that VLA models implicitly encode latent compositional structures which can be exploited to synthesize valid trajectories for previously unseen task combinations. Rather than using such compositional synthesis solely as an inference-time control mechanism, the key motivation is to repurpose this latent capability as an automated source of *new training data*.

Therefore, the main objective is to propose and implement ACT-VLA (Action Compositional Training for VLA Models), an offline data augmentation framework. This framework aims to leverage the VLA model’s own latent task representations to automatically synthesize novel, physically valid demonstrations directly from existing tasks. By doing so, the method seeks to automatically expand the effective training distribution, mitigate overfitting, and enable VLA models to learn compositional transitions intrinsically, thereby broadening their generalization capabilities without additional human teleoperation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Action Compositional Training for VLA Models (ACT-VLA), is an offline data augmentation framework designed to synthesize novel demonstrations from existing tasks. The approach is structured into three main stages: representation-guided trajectory synthesis, data recording and processing, and augmented policy training.

**Representation-Guided Trajectory Synthesis:**
This initial stage focuses on generating demonstrations for novel compositional tasks without requiring human teleoperation. It builds upon the Text Latent Interpolation (TLI) mechanism, previously identified in research by Li. TLI operates on the principle that transformer-based VLA models encode task-specific semantics within the hidden states of their text tokens. By averaging these states across demonstrated episodes, a task-specific text latent (T) is extracted, which encapsulates the behavioral context required for that task.

To synthesize a continuous behavioral transition between two base tasks (Task 1 and Task 2), their respective text latents, T1 and T2, are interpolated. At each timestep *i*, the model's text hidden states are modified using the following formula:
h_T(i) ← h_T(i) + (1−α)T1 + αT2 − [(1−α)T2 + αT1]
Here, α = *i*/λ, where λ is a scaling factor, ensures that the interpolation coefficient α varies linearly from over the episode. This process gradually shifts the model's internal representation, suppressing Task 2's context while reinforcing Task 1's at the beginning (α ≈ 0), and then gradually injecting Task 2's context while fading out Task 1's as the episode progresses (α → 1). This steering of latent representations is intended to produce a physically continuous compositional trajectory.

The core contribution of ACT-VLA lies in repurposing this TLI mechanism from an inference-time intervention to a systematic data generation engine for training. This transformation necessitated addressing specific design considerations:
- **Instruction Design:** For training-time synthesis, each generated compositional demonstration must be associated with a precise natural language instruction (ℓ). Unlike inference-time TLI, which can guide behavior solely through latent steering, training requires explicit linguistic supervision. The instruction ℓ is systematically derived by semantically splitting and recombining textual components from the original base task instructions (e.g., pairing an object description from one task with a target location from another) to accurately reflect the newly composed task structure.
- **Trajectory Quality Filtering:** Not all synthesized rollouts are suitable for training, as TLI guides latent representations rather than guaranteeing physically correct execution. To ensure that only high-quality, physically valid data is incorporated into the training set, a dual filtering strategy is applied. First, rollouts are rigorously checked for task completion against the strict criteria defined by the LIBERO benchmark; only trajectories that successfully complete the entire compositional task are retained. Second, a step-budget constraint is imposed, filtering out rollouts that exceed a predefined maximum episode length, thereby excluding unnecessarily long or inefficient behavioral segments. This filtering step is crucial to prevent the propagation of incorrect state-action associations.
- **Synthesis Scale:** To manage the potential distribution shift introduced by synthetic data, the volume of synthesized demonstrations is scaled to match that of the original LIBERO task suites. Specifically, for each compositional task within an out-of-distribution (OOD) suite, approximately 30-40 successful trajectories are collected, resulting in a total of roughly 300-400 synthesized demonstrations per OOD suite. This parity between data volumes is intended to prevent the synthetic distribution from overwhelming the original and causing catastrophic forgetting of base skills.

**Data Recording and Processing:**
In the second stage, a dedicated data recording module processes the successful, quality-filtered synthesized rollouts. At each timestep *i*, the module captures multimodal observations, including RGB frames from all available camera views, the robot's proprioceptive state (comprising joint positions and end-effector pose), the executed action (a_i), and the corresponding natural language instruction (ℓ) for the compositional task. The recorded data is strictly formatted to match the demonstration structure of the original dataset, ensuring seamless integration into the existing VLA training pipeline without requiring modifications.

**Augmented Policy Training:**
Finally, in the third stage, the VLA model is trained on an augmented dataset (D_syn) that combines both the original demonstrations and the newly synthesized compositional demonstrations. Standard behavioral cloning is employed, utilizing the same loss function and hyperparameters as the baseline VLA model, and crucially, without any modifications to the model's architectural design. This design principle ensures that the acquisition of compositional capability is achieved entirely through exposure to the expanded data distribution, rather than through architectural interventions. By internalizing these compositional transitions into the model's weights during training, the method eliminates the need for any inference-time latent manipulation or external planners during deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ACT-VLA was evaluated using the LIBERO simulation benchmark, with a specific focus on its ability to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) compositional tasks, namely LIBERO-Spatial-OOD and LIBERO-Goal-OOD. These OOD suites were designed with novel task compositions—formed by recombining objects and target locations from original tasks—that were not present in the initial training demonstrations.

The method was benchmarked against several state-of-the-art VLA models, including UniVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, GR00T-N1, and π0.5, all trained solely on the original LIBERO demonstrations. A direct comparison was also made against π0 with Text Latent Interpolation (TLI) applied at inference time, which represents a prior approach to compositional synthesis. The evaluation protocol involved executing each task over 150 episodes (across 3 random seeds, 50 episodes per task), reporting the mean success rate (%) across all tasks within each suite. All models underwent training for 30,000 steps under identical settings.

**Main Results:**
The experimental findings demonstrate substantial improvements in compositional generalization (Table I). When integrated with a π0.5 backbone, ACT-VLA yielded significant gains on the OOD suites:
- On LIBERO-Spatial-OOD, the success rate increased from 35.5% for the π0.5 model trained on original data only, to 88.2% with ACT-VLA, representing an absolute gain of 52.7 percentage points.
- Similarly, on LIBERO-Goal-OOD, the success rate improved from 46.6% (π0.5, original data) to 95.6% with ACT-VLA, an absolute gain of 49.0 percentage points.
These results indicate that the synthesized compositional demonstrations are effective in broadening VLA generalization to novel task compositions. Furthermore, ACT-VLA maintained competitive performance on the standard in-distribution benchmarks (LIBERO-Spatial, -Goal, -Object, -Long).

In a comparative analysis, ACT-VLA also achieved higher OOD success rates than π0 with inference-time TLI (88.2% vs. 81.0% on Spatial-OOD; 95.6% vs. 85.0% on Goal-OOD). This outcome suggests that internalizing compositional transitions into the model's weights through training-time data synthesis is a more effective strategy than relying on per-step latent steering during inference, while also eliminating deployment-time computational overhead. Overall, ACT-VLA achieved an unweighted mean (AVG) success rate of 95.6% across all six evaluation suites, outperforming all other baselines, including UniVLA (70.7%), OpenVLA-OFT (64.9%), GR00T-N1 (71.1%), π0.5 (original data, 78.6%), and π0 with inference-time TLI (90.4%).

**Ablation Study:**
An ablation study conducted on the π0 backbone (Table II) provided further insights into the specific contributions of ACT-VLA.
- A π0 model trained solely on original data achieved minimal success on OOD tasks (2.0% on Spatial-OOD and 16.0% on Goal-OOD). This confirmed that standard imitation learning, without exposure to compositional data, fails to generalize effectively to unseen task compositions, even if the constituent sub-skills are individually mastered.
- The integration of ACT-VLA's synthesized data with the π0 backbone substantially improved these scores to 85.9% on Spatial-OOD (an increase of 83.9 percentage points) and 90.5% on Goal-OOD (an increase of 74.5 percentage points). This result demonstrates that the synthesized compositional demonstrations provide an effective training signal, irrespective of the specific VLA backbone architecture.
- A direct comparison on the π0 backbone between inference-time TLI (81.0% Spatial-OOD, 85.0% Goal-OOD) and training-time synthesis with ACT-VLA (85.9% Spatial-OOD, 90.5% Goal-OOD) reinforced that internalizing compositional behaviors into the model's weights during training is a more robust and practical approach, yielding superior OOD performance while eliminating per-step inference overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by directly addressing a critical limitation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their constrained compositional generalization capabilities. Current VLA models tend to overfit to their training data, struggling with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios that require novel combinations of familiar sub-skills. The proposed Action Compositional Training (ACT-VLA) framework offers a data-efficient and scalable solution to this problem.

The primary significance of ACT-VLA lies in its ability to automatically and systematically expand the training distribution for VLA models. By repurposing the concept of latent interpolation for offline demonstration synthesis, the method enables models to learn compositional skill transitions directly from data. This approach effectively mitigates the problem of overfitting and substantially enhances VLA models' capacity to generalize to novel task compositions, as evidenced by the considerable performance improvements observed on OOD benchmarks.

A key practical impact of this work is the reduction in reliance on labor-intensive and costly human teleoperation for expanding robotic capabilities. ACT-VLA leverages existing datasets to synthesize novel, physically valid demonstrations, thereby broadening the model's generalization without requiring additional manual data collection efforts, offering a more economically and logistically sustainable pathway for developing advanced robotic systems.

Furthermore, by internalizing compositional transitions into the model's weights during training, ACT-VLA streamlines the deployment process. It eliminates the need for inference-time interventions, architectural modifications, or auxiliary external planners, leading to more robust and self-contained VLA policies. The substantial performance gains on OOD compositional tasks, while maintaining competitive performance on in-distribution tasks, validate the effectiveness of this approach.

The potential impact extends to the development of more adaptable and versatile robotic systems. Robots trained with ACT-VLA could theoretically perform a much wider array of tasks by intelligently recombining their learned atomic skills, even in scenarios not explicitly present in their initial training. This could accelerate the practical adoption of VLA models in complex and dynamic real-world environments where unanticipated task variations are common.

While the research demonstrates promising results, the authors acknowledge certain limitations that delineate avenues for future impact. The current validation primarily focuses on sequential two-skill compositions within tabletop environments. Extending this framework to handle longer, recursively structured multi-skill chains or more complex, open-world scenarios would require further investigation. Additionally, the quality of synthesized demonstrations is dependent on the semantic disentanglement capabilities of the pretrained VLA backbone, suggesting potential benefits from integrating advanced representation learning techniques. Finally, all experiments were conducted in simulation, and validating the method's performance and generalizability across the sim-to-real gap on physical hardware represents a crucial next step for achieving real-world impact.
