# Towards Large-Scale Small Object Detection: Survey and Benchmarks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northwestern Polytechnical University introduced two large-scale benchmarks, SODA-D for driving scenarios and SODA-A for aerial scenarios, explicitly tailored for multi-category small object detection. This work also provides a comprehensive survey of deep learning-based small object detection methods and benchmarks state-of-the-art detectors on the new datasets, revealing that performance on extremely small objects (e.g., those below 144 pixels in area) remains low (e.g., 14.1% AP_eS on SODA-D) despite overall advancements and highlighting the benefits of modern backbones like Swin-T and ConvNext-T.

## Problem

- Deep learning models struggle with small object detection due to limited visual information, noisy feature representations, and high sensitivity to bounding box errors.
- A lack of large-scale, multi-category datasets specifically designed for small object detection (SOD) hinders the development of robust deep learning models.
- Existing general object detection datasets contain an insufficient number of small instances or concentrate them within a few categories, making them inadequate for comprehensive SOD training and evaluation.

## Method

- The authors conducted a comprehensive survey, categorizing deep learning-based ==small object detection (SOD)== methods into six distinct groups: ==sample-oriented==, ==scale-aware==, ==attention-based==, ==feature-imitation==, ==context-modeling==, and ==focus-and-detect==.
- Two new large-scale benchmarks, **SODA-D** (for driving scenarios) and **SODA-A** (for aerial scenarios), were constructed with exhaustive annotations of millions of small and extremely small instances.
- A wide range of mainstream object detection methods and specialized SOD approaches were rigorously benchmarked on the new **SODA** datasets, using standard evaluation protocols like ==COCO AP==, to provide an impartial platform for comparison.

## Results

- Northwestern Polytechnical University (NWPU) introduced SODA-D (**24,828 images**, **278,433 instances**) and SODA-A (**2,513 images**, **872,069 instances**), providing large-scale multi-category benchmarks with an unprecedented number of small and extremely small objects, averaging **14.75 pixels** in SODA-A.
- On the SODA-D benchmark, the best performing model, Cascade RCNN, achieved an overall Average Precision (AP) of **31.2%**, but its AP for extremely Small objects (AP_eS) was only **14.1%**.
- For the SODA-A benchmark, RoI Transformer achieved the top performance with **36.0% AP**, though its AP for extremely Small objects (AP_eS) remained low at **13.5%**, confirming the difficulty in detecting the smallest instances in aerial scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Two-stage detectors generally outperform one-stage and anchor-free methods for small object detection, especially for extremely small objects and in dense scenes.
> - Modern backbone networks like Swin-T and ConvNext-T consistently improve small object detection performance, showcasing their strong representation capabilities.
> - Performance on "extremely Small" objects (area <= 144 pixels) remains very low across all evaluated models, indicating a persistent challenge for the tiniest instances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical and persistent challenge within the broader field of computer vision, specifically object detection. While deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have propelled generic object detection to remarkable successes in recent years, the detection of small objects remains a significant bottleneck. This "unsatisfactory situation" is a well-recognized problem, stemming from the inherent difficulties of small objects having limited visual information, noisy feature representations, low tolerance for bounding box perturbations, and a scarcity of adequate training data.

The paper posits that the performance degradation in Small Object Detection (SOD) primarily arises from two factors: the intrinsic difficulty of learning robust representations from limited and distorted information of small objects, and the scarcity of large-scale datasets specifically tailored for SOD. Existing general datasets like COCO, while containing some small objects, are not optimized for this task, as small instances often represent a minority or are distributed unevenly across categories. Furthermore, while some smaller or single-category SOD datasets exist (e.g., TinyPerson for pedestrians, WiderFace for faces), they often lack the scale and multi-category diversity required to train robust, generalizable deep learning models.

In this context, the paper makes a two-fold contribution to the broader landscape:

- **Comprehensive Survey:** It provides a timely and in-depth review of existing deep learning-based methods for SOD. Unlike previous surveys that either cover generic object detection or specific tasks (e.g., pedestrian, text detection), or offer limited analysis of SOD, this paper meticulously categorizes SOD approaches into six distinct groups: sample-oriented, scale-aware, attention-based, feature-imitation, context-modeling, and focus-and-detect methods. This systematic taxonomy and analysis helps researchers navigate the vast literature, understand the strengths and weaknesses of different approaches, and identify promising research directions.
- **Novel Large-Scale Benchmarks:** Recognizing the critical need for data to drive advancements in SOD, the authors introduce two large-scale datasets: SODA-D (for Driving scenarios) and SODA-A (for Aerial scenarios). These benchmarks are a direct response to the "data scarcity" problem. By providing exhaustively annotated, multi-category datasets with a vast collection of small and extremely small instances, they aim to serve as "impartial platforms" for rigorous evaluation and comparison of SOD algorithms. This echoes the historical impact of foundational datasets like PASCAL VOC, ImageNet, and COCO, which significantly accelerated progress in their respective fields. The introduction of SODA datasets is poised to catalyze the development of data-driven SOD models, similar to how previous large-scale benchmarks spurred generic object detection.

By combining a comprehensive survey with the release of large-scale, purpose-built benchmarks, this work directly addresses the foundational challenges hindering progress in SOD, thereby positioning itself as a pivotal contribution to the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged "unsatisfactory situation of Small Object Detection (SOD)" despite the significant advancements in generic object detection using deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The authors identify two core reasons for this predicament:
1. **Intrinsic Difficulty:** Small objects possess poor visual appearance and noisy feature representations due to their limited size. Standard CNN architectures, which often involve down-sampling operations, further extinguish the already weak signals of tiny objects, making it challenging to learn discriminative information. Their features are also highly susceptible to contamination from background noise and other instances. Furthermore, small objects exhibit low tolerance for bounding box perturbations, meaning even slight inaccuracies in prediction lead to significant drops in evaluation metrics like Intersection over Union (IoU).
2. **Data Scarcity:** There is a significant lack of large-scale datasets specifically designed for multi-category small object detection. Existing datasets either have a limited number of small instances, focus on single categories (e.g., faces, pedestrians), or contain small objects concentrated in only a few classes, rendering them insufficient for training robust, data-hungry deep learning models.

Given these challenges, the paper outlines several key objectives:
- **Conduct a Thorough Review:** To systematically survey the existing landscape of small object detection methods, focusing on deep learning-based approaches. This involves categorizing them, analyzing their pros and cons, and reviewing relevant datasets.
- **Construct Large-Scale Benchmarks:** To alleviate the data scarcity bottleneck, the core objective is to build two new, large-scale datasets, SODA-D (Driving scenarios) and SODA-A (Aerial scenarios), explicitly tailored for multi-category small object detection. These datasets are designed to feature a vast collection of exhaustively annotated small instances.
- **Evaluate Mainstream Methods:** To provide an impartial benchmarking platform by evaluating the performance of representative general object detection methods and newly published SOD approaches on the newly created SODA datasets.
- **Catalyze Development:** Ultimately, the overarching goal is to facilitate the development of SOD and inspire more breakthroughs in this challenging sub-field of computer vision by providing both a structured understanding of the state-of-the-art and essential data resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research is two-pronged: a comprehensive survey of existing literature and the construction of novel, large-scale benchmarks with subsequent performance evaluation.

#### 3.1. Survey and Problem Formulation

The authors begin by defining the problem of small object detection, clarifying that "small" typically refers to objects with an area less than or equal to 1024 pixels, following the COCO dataset's definition. They articulate the main challenges specific to SOD, including information loss during feature extraction, noisy feature representation, low tolerance for bounding box perturbation, and inadequate training samples.

For the survey of algorithms, the authors categorize deep learning-based SOD methods into six distinct groups:
- **Sample-oriented methods:** Focus on addressing the data scarcity and insufficient positive sample assignment issues, primarily through data augmentation strategies (e.g., copying and pasting small objects, adaptive resampling) and optimized label assignment schemes (e.g., scale compensation anchor matching, using alternative similarity metrics like DotD or Gaussian receptive fields).
- **Scale-aware methods:** Aim to handle the significant scale variations, either by constructing scale-specific detectors (e.g., multi-branch architectures, multi-scale prediction, tailored training schemes like SNIP/SNIPER) or by employing hierarchical feature fusion (e.g., FPN, PANet, StairNet, IPG-Net) to enrich features with both low-level spatial detail and high-level semantic information.
- **Attention-based methods:** Leverage visual attention mechanisms to highlight small object regions and suppress background noise by allocating different weights to feature map parts (e.g., pixel and channel attention, cross-layer attention).
- **Feature-imitation methods:** Address the low-quality representation of small objects by making them mimic the features of larger, more discriminative instances. This is achieved through similarity learning (imposing constraints to bridge the representation gap) or super-resolution-based frameworks (using deconvolution, sub-pixel convolution, or GANs to restore distorted structures).
- **Context-modeling methods:** Exploit contextual cues—semantic or spatial associations between objects and their environment or other objects—to aid in the recognition of poorly visible small objects (e.g., context regions, global and local context modeling, context-aware RoI pooling).
- **Focus-and-detect methods:** Tackle the inefficiency of processing high-resolution images by first identifying regions likely to contain objects, then performing detection only on these "cluster chips" (e.g., density map estimation, adaptive searching regions, tiling).

The survey also meticulously reviews existing datasets relevant to small object detection, such as COCO, WiderFace, TinyPerson, TT100K, VisDrone, and DOTA, highlighting their characteristics, limitations, and how they fall short for comprehensive SOD benchmarking.

#### 3.2. Dataset Construction (SODA-D and SODA-A)

The paper's core methodological contribution is the creation of two new large-scale datasets:
- **Definition of "Small":** A key aspect is the precise definition of valuable objects. Objects with an area less than or equal to 1024 pixels are defined as "Small," further subdivided into "extremely Small" (eS, 0-144 pixels), "relatively Small" (rS, 144-400 pixels), and "generally Small" (gS, 400-1024 pixels). Objects with areas between 1024 and 2000 pixels are "Normal." Instances larger than 2000 pixels or excessively small/heavily occluded objects that cannot be confidently distinguished even at high zoom are annotated as "ignore regions."
- **SODA-D (Driving Scenario):**
 - **Data Source:** Built from the Mapillary Vistas Dataset (MVD), self-shooting with on-board cameras/mobile phones in various Chinese cities, and images crawled from search engines.
 - **Scale:** Comprises 24,828 high-quality traffic images with 278,433 instances across nine categories (people, rider, bicycle, motor, vehicle, traffic-sign, traffic-light, traffic-camera, warning-cone).
 - **Annotation:** Uses horizontal bounding boxes (HBB). Emphasizes high spatial resolution (average 3407 × 2470 pixels) and rich diversity in locations, weather, time, and views.
- **SODA-A (Aerial Scenario):**
 - **Data Source:** Extracted from Google Earth, focusing on challenging scenes with cluttered backgrounds and high density.
 - **Scale:** Contains 2,513 high-resolution aerial images with 872,069 instances across nine categories (airplane, helicopter, small-vehicle, large-vehicle, ship, container, storage-tank, swimming-pool, windmill).
 - **Annotation:** Employs oriented bounding box (OBB) annotations. To handle diverse orientations and dense packing, different annotation methods (crisscross polygons for cruciform objects, HBB for simple shapes, enclosed polygons for complex contours) are used and then converted to unified OBBs. Noted for high resolution (average 4761 × 2777 pixels), large density variation, and arbitrary orientations.
- **Dataset Split:** Both datasets are split into train, validation, and test sets with approximate ratios (50%:20%:30% for SODA-D, 40%:25%:35% for SODA-A).
- **Annotation Process:** Professional annotators are employed, utilizing Labelimg and Labelme tools for fine-grained, high-zoom annotations, ensuring quality control and precise labeling, including 'ignore' regions.

#### 3.3. Benchmarking and Evaluation

- **Evaluation Protocol:** Follows the COCO evaluation metrics, primarily using Average Precision (AP) averaged over 10 IoU thresholds (0.5 to 0.95). Specific metrics for different size categories (AP_eS, AP_rS, AP_gS, AP_N) are also used to highlight performance on small objects.
- **Implementation:** All experiments are conducted on the open-source object detection toolboxes MMDetection and MMRotate for fair comparison. Due to high image resolution, images are cropped into 800x800 patches with a 650-pixel stride, then resized to 1200x1200 for model input. Patch-wise NMS and image-wise NMS are applied.
- **Models Evaluated:** A wide range of mainstream object detectors are tested, including two-stage (Faster RCNN, Cascade RCNN, RoI Transformer, Oriented RCNN), one-stage (RetinaNet, FCOS, YOLOX), and anchor-free/keypoint-based methods (CornerNet, CenterNet, RepPoints, ATSS). Various backbone networks (ResNet-50, ResNet-101, Swin-T, ConvNext-T) are also explored to assess their impact on SOD performance. The effects of different label assignment strategies (e.g., RFLA, FreeAnchor, PAA, ATSS, AutoAssign) and loss functions (GHM, GFL) are also investigated for SODA-D.

This comprehensive methodology ensures a rigorous and well-supported analysis of the small object detection problem, providing both a theoretical framework and practical resources for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive statistical analysis of the new SODA datasets and benchmark results of various state-of-the-art detectors.

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Characteristics

- **Scale and Smallness:** SODA-D features 24,828 high-quality traffic images with 278,433 instances across 9 categories. SODA-A contains 2,513 high-resolution aerial images with an impressive 872,069 instances across 9 categories. Both datasets *far exceed existing benchmarks* in the number of small objects, especially "extremely Small" ones (area <= 144 pixels). The average absolute size of small objects in SODA-D is 20.31 pixels, and in SODA-A, it is strikingly lower at 14.75 pixels.
- **Diversity and Resolution:** SODA-D boasts rich diversity in locations, weather, period, and shooting views, with an average image resolution of 3407 × 2470 pixels. SODA-A features high-resolution images (average 4761 × 2777 pixels) from hundreds of cities worldwide, leading to high intra-class variation and complicated backgrounds.
- **Challenging Attributes:** SODA-A exhibits significant density variation (1 to 11134 instances per image, average 347.02), posing a challenge for handling clustered situations. Both datasets include substantial "ignore regions" (especially SODA-D with 153,976 regions) to maintain training stability for truly valuable small instances. SODA-A uses oriented bounding boxes (OBB), accounting for arbitrary rotations common in aerial imagery.
- **Comparison to existing datasets:** The paper highlights that unlike AI-TOD, SODA-A provides balanced multi-category distribution rather than being dominated by a single class like `vehicle`. DOTA, while large, also centralizes its small objects primarily in `small-vehicle`.

#### 4.2. Benchmarking Results on SODA-D (Horizontal Bounding Boxes)

- **Overall Performance:** Cascade RCNN achieves the best overall AP (31.2%), followed by Faster RCNN (28.9%). Two-stage detectors generally outperform one-stage and anchor-free methods, confirming the benefit of high-quality proposals for SOD.
- **Small Object Performance (AP_eS):** Performance on "extremely Small" objects (AP_eS) is consistently very low across all models (e.g., Cascade RCNN at 14.1%, RetinaNet at 11.9%, FCOS at 6.9%), significantly lagging behind performance on larger objects. This underscores the immense difficulty of detecting the smallest instances.
- **Methodology Insights:**
 - One-stage detectors (RetinaNet, FCOS) perform worse on extremely small objects compared to two-stage methods, suggesting misalignments issues are more severe for tiny objects.
 - Anchor-free methods (CenterNet, CornerNet, FCOS) generally struggle with small objects. YOLOX shows competitive performance among anchor-free approaches.
 - Query-based detectors (Deformable DETR, Sparse RCNN) deliver notably lower AP, possibly because their sparse query paradigm cannot adequately cover small, numerous objects.
- **Label Assignment:** While some advanced label assignment strategies like RFLA improve overall AP for Faster RCNN, they can surprisingly degrade AP_eS, implying that they might not universally suit *all* extremely tiny scales. ATSS significantly boosts FCOS, especially for AP_eS, showing dynamic assignment's robustness for objects of all scales.
- **Loss Functions:** GHM and GFL show marginal improvements over baseline RetinaNet and ATSS respectively, indicating that specialized loss functions can offer some benefits, particularly GFL in capturing uncertain boundaries of small instances.
- **Backbone Networks:** Deeper backbones like ResNet-101 offer only slight improvements or even degradation compared to ResNet-50 for some models, suggesting that simply increasing depth might not be optimal for small object features due to information loss. In contrast, modern backbones like **Swin-T and ConvNext-T consistently yield substantial performance gains**, highlighting their powerful representation ability and potential for SOD.

#### 4.3. Benchmarking Results on SODA-A (Oriented Bounding Boxes)

- **Overall Performance:** RoI Transformer achieves the top performance with 36.0% AP, followed by Oriented RCNN (34.4%). Similar to SODA-D, methods with robust proposal generators (two-stage) excel, especially for the densely packed and oriented small objects in aerial scenes.
- **Comparison to SODA-D:** The AP values are generally higher on SODA-A than SODA-D, possibly due to clearer object definitions or less varied backgrounds for certain categories. However, AP_eS remains low (e.g., RoI Transformer at 13.5%).
- **Methodology Insights:**
 - Oriented two-stage detectors leveraging rotated proposals demonstrate superior performance.
 - One-stage oriented detectors (Rotated RetinaNet, S2A-Net, Oriented RepPoints) generally lag behind their two-stage counterparts, confirming the challenge for dense and tiny objects in a single-shot paradigm.
 - Gliding Vertex and DODet, which use novel oriented representations, show comparable results, but their performance varies across categories, reflecting the nuances of different representation schemes.
- **Backbone Networks:** ResNet-101 again offers minimal improvement. Intriguingly, Swin-T improves RPN-based detectors but *causes significant performance decline* for RPN-free detectors (e.g., Rotated RetinaNet, S2A-Net). This suggests a specific interaction issue between Swin-T's window-based attention and the misalignment problem in RPN-free methods when dealing with dense distributions. ConvNext-T shows similar trends.
- **Number of Proposals:** For dense scenes, increasing proposal numbers from 2000 to 8000 yields only marginal AP gains while significantly decreasing inference speed, indicating an optimal balance is crucial.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Visualizations confirm the ongoing challenges: current detectors struggle with small objects in complicated backgrounds, heavy occlusion, low illumination, and low contrast. Instances resembling background or heavily occluded ones are often missed or misclassified. The difficulty of retaining detailed information for identification often leads to false positives and incorrect predictions.

In summary, the extensive experiments on SODA datasets vividly demonstrate the persistent challenges in small object detection, particularly for extremely tiny instances. They highlight the strengths of two-stage detectors, the critical role of advanced backbones like Swin-T and ConvNext-T, and the need for further innovations in label assignment, loss design, and feature extraction specifically tailored for the unique properties of small objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper holds significant implications and potential impact across several facets of the computer vision community and real-world applications:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in Data:** The most immediate and profound impact comes from the introduction of **SODA-D and SODA-A, the first-ever large-scale, multi-category benchmarks explicitly tailored for Small Object Detection (SOD)**. This directly addresses the severe data scarcity that has long bottlenecked progress in the field. Prior datasets were either too small, focused on single categories, or contained small objects as a minor, often imbalanced, subset. SODA's sheer volume of exhaustively annotated small instances provides a much-needed robust foundation for training and validating deep learning models, akin to how ImageNet or COCO revolutionized generic object recognition.
2. **Catalyst for Algorithm Development:** By providing dedicated and challenging benchmarks, the SODA datasets are expected to **catalyze innovation in SOD algorithms**. Researchers now have a standardized, fair, and representative platform to:
 - Develop and test novel feature extractors designed to preserve fine-grained details of small objects.
 - Invent more effective hierarchical feature fusion strategies that can bridge the semantic-spatial gap for tiny instances.
 - Design sophisticated label assignment strategies that can reliably provide sufficient positive samples for training.
 - Explore new loss functions and training paradigms specifically optimized for the unique challenges of small object localization and classification.
 - Develop more robust methods for handling dense object distributions, heavy occlusions, and varied orientations, as present in SODA-A.
3. **Comprehensive Research Guide:** The paper's **thorough and timely survey of existing SOD algorithms** serves as an invaluable resource for the research community. By meticulously categorizing methods and discussing their pros and cons, it provides a structured understanding of the state-of-the-art, helps identify under-explored areas, and guides future research directions. This structured overview prevents redundant efforts and accelerates targeted solutions.
4. **In-depth Benchmarking Insights:** The extensive evaluation of mainstream object detection methods on SODA provides **critical insights into their strengths and weaknesses when applied to SOD**. The results clearly demonstrate that even state-of-the-art general detectors struggle significantly with "extremely Small" objects. The analysis highlights that:
 - Two-stage detectors generally outperform one-stage and anchor-free methods for SOD, particularly in dense scenarios.
 - Simply increasing backbone depth (e.g., ResNet-101) is not necessarily beneficial for small objects, suggesting a need for specialized feature learning.
 - Modern backbones like Swin-T and ConvNext-T show great promise, but their interaction with different detection paradigms (e.g., RPN-based vs. RPN-free) needs further investigation for dense aerial scenes.
 - Current label assignment strategies and loss functions still face limitations for the tiniest objects.
5. **Practical Applications:** Improved SOD capabilities directly translate to significant advancements in numerous real-world applications:
 - **Autonomous Driving:** More reliable detection of distant vehicles, pedestrians, traffic signs, and traffic lights, enhancing safety and decision-making.
 - **Drone Scene Analysis:** Enhanced surveillance, disaster monitoring, urban planning, and infrastructure inspection by accurately detecting small objects (vehicles, ships, structures) from aerial perspectives.
 - **Security and Surveillance:** More effective monitoring of crowded areas and detection of minute details.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Potential for detecting small abnormalities or structures.
 - **Remote Sensing:** Improved environmental monitoring, target recognition, and resource management.
6. **Paving the Way for New Metrics:** The discussion on the need for "Proper evaluation metric for SOD" is also significant. By suggesting that current multi-IoU thresholds might be too stringent for extremely small objects and that a focus on recall/rough location might be more practical in certain scenarios (akin to crowd counting), the authors open a dialogue for developing more suitable evaluation criteria that genuinely reflect the practical needs and inherent difficulties of SOD.

In conclusion, this paper's combined contribution of a comprehensive survey, the release of two pioneering large-scale benchmarks, and extensive performance analysis lays a robust foundation for the next wave of advancements in small object detection. It is poised to be a cornerstone reference for researchers and a critical enabler for numerous real-world applications where detecting the tiniest details matters most.
