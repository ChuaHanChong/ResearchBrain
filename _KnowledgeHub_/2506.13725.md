# CEED-VLA: Consistency Vision-Language-Action Model with Early-Exit Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> CEED-VLA introduces a framework to accelerate Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model inference by optimizing Jacobi decoding. It achieves up to 4.1x speedup in simulation and enables robust, high-frequency robotic control in real-world dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face a severe inference speed bottleneck, limiting their practical deployment in high-frequency, real-time robotic control and dexterous manipulation tasks.
- Existing Jacobi decoding methods provide only marginal speedups (e.g., 1.28x) for autoregressively trained VLAs because these models struggle to generate accurate predictions when conditioned on the 'incorrect' intermediate tokens common in Jacobi iterations.
- Certain 'inefficient iterations' in Jacobi decoding consume disproportionate time for marginal token updates, further reducing average decoding speed.

## Method

- Develops **CEED-VLA**, which fine-tunes pre-trained VLA 'teacher models' into 'student models' via consistency distillation: collecting Jacobi trajectories (intermediate states, final fixed points) and training the student to map any intermediate state to the fixed point in one iteration.
- Introduces 'mixed-label autoregressive supervision' to preserve native manipulation skills by selectively using either teacher-generated fixed points or ground-truth actions as supervisory labels, mitigating error accumulation during distillation.
- Implements 'early-exit decoding' during inference, which prematurely halts the Jacobi decoding process at a predefined iteration, emitting the intermediate output to circumvent inefficient convergence steps.

## Results

- Achieves up to **4.1x** inference acceleration (**225.0** Tokens/s) and **4.3x** frequency increase (**25.60** Hz) on OpenVLA in simulation compared to baselines, while maintaining comparable success rates.
- Demonstrates superior performance in real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, boosting control frequency to **13.0** Hz (a **3.9x** increase) and significantly improving success rates (e.g., **80%** on pour water, **75%** on fold towel) compared to LLaVA-VLA.
- Ablation studies confirm that consistency training and early-exit decoding are the primary drivers of acceleration, and the mixed-label supervision strategy is crucial for maintaining action accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Consistency training can enable autoregressively trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to robustly predict multiple correct action tokens in parallel during Jacobi decoding, even from imperfect intermediate states.
> - Relaxing strict convergence criteria through early-exit decoding is viable for robotic tasks, as perfect action sequence convergence is not always necessary for success, and can significantly boost inference speed.
> - A mixed-label supervision strategy is essential during consistency distillation to maintain a VLA model's native manipulation skills and prevent performance degradation from teacher-generated errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a significant frontier in robotics, aiming to equip robots with impressive multimodal understanding and generalization capabilities for complex tasks. These end-to-end architectures, trained on large-scale robotic datasets, allow robots to interpret visual inputs and language instructions to directly generate executable actions. However, a critical bottleneck hindering their practical deployment, particularly in high-frequency and dexterous manipulation tasks, is their **inference speed**. This paper directly addresses this pervasive challenge.

The research landscape for accelerating large models, including Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has seen various strategies such as quantization and token pruning. While effective in some contexts, these methods often fall short of the stringent real-time requirements for robotic action generation. More specialized efforts have explored architectural modifications in VLA models, like dynamic inference depth (DeeR-VLA), quantization-aware training (QAIL), or designing lightweight models from scratch (RoboMamba, TinyVLA). These often necessitate significant model re-training or additional data collection, limiting their general applicability. Other approaches include selective token caching (VLA-Cache) and efficient action tokenization (FAST).

A particularly relevant line of research, and the direct antecedent to this work, involves reframing autoregressive (AR) decoding as a system of nonlinear equations solvable through **Jacobi fixed-point iteration methods**. Pioneering works like PD-VLA and OpenVLA-OFT have shown that Jacobi decoding can theoretically offer parallel processing of action tokens, reducing the number of sequential forward passes. However, a significant practical limitation arises: standard VLA models are trained in a strictly autoregressive manner, where predictions are conditioned on *correct* preceding tokens. When faced with the *incorrect* intermediate tokens common in Jacobi iterations, these models struggle, leading to slow convergence and only marginal real-world speedups (e.g., 1.28x).

This paper introduces **consistency distillation training** to tackle this specific limitation of Jacobi decoding in VLAs. The concept of "consistency models" has recently gained prominence in other domains, notably in accelerating diffusion policies for image generation and robotics (e.g., Consistency Policy, ManiCM, FlowPolicy, SDM policy). These models accelerate inference by directly mapping intermediate states of an iterative process to its final solution in a single step. CEED-VLA innovatively adapts this principle: instead of applying it to ODEs as in diffusion, it applies consistency training to enable VLAs to map arbitrary intermediate points within a Jacobi decoding trajectory directly to the fixed point. This is the **first work to explore consistency training techniques specifically for efficient VLAs**, addressing the challenge of robust prediction even with noisy or incorrect preceding tokens.

Furthermore, the paper identifies and tackles a *secondary* bottleneck in Jacobi decoding: the persistence of "inefficient iterations" that require a disproportionately large number of steps for marginal convergence. To address this, they propose an **early-exit decoding strategy**, a novel application for VLA acceleration that leverages insights into the nature of robotic task success (i.e., often determined by key states rather than perfect action sequences).

In summary, CEED-VLA contributes to the broader landscape by:
1. **Bridging the gap** between theoretical benefits of Jacobi decoding and its practical limitations in VLA models.
2. **Introducing consistency distillation** as a powerful paradigm for accelerating iterative inference processes in VLAs, an approach previously unexplored in this context.
3. **Proposing early-exit decoding** as a practical and effective strategy to further optimize Jacobi inference by relaxing strict convergence criteria tailored to robotic task characteristics.
This work pushes VLA models closer to real-time performance, broadening their applicability in complex, dynamic robotic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **significantly improve the inference efficiency of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models while retaining, or even enhancing, their manipulation performance** for practical robotic deployment.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several critical challenges currently facing VLA models:

1. **Inference Speed Bottleneck:** Despite their impressive multimodal understanding and generalization capabilities, VLA models are severely constrained by their inference speed. This bottleneck fundamentally limits their deployment, especially in applications requiring high-frequency, real-time control, such as dexterous manipulation tasks where rapid, continuous action generation is crucial.
2. **Limitations of Existing Acceleration Methods:** While various acceleration techniques (e.g., quantization, architectural modifications) exist, they often fail to meet the stringent real-time requirements of robotics or necessitate extensive model redesign and retraining, thus lacking generality and ease of adoption.
3. **Inefficiency of Vanilla Jacobi Decoding:** Recent works have explored Jacobi decoding as a more efficient alternative to traditional autoregressive (AR) decoding, theoretically offering parallelism. However, in practice, its benefits have been marginal (e.g., 1.28x speedup). This is because existing VLA models are trained autoregressively, meaning they expect correct preceding tokens. During Jacobi decoding, where intermediate tokens can be incorrect, the models struggle to generate accurate subsequent predictions, leading to slow convergence of the full action sequence. This lack of robustness to "wrong prefixes" is a major practical hurdle.
4. **Inefficient Iterations in Jacobi Decoding:** Even with some improvements, empirical observations reveal that certain "inefficient iterations" in Jacobi decoding remain a critical bottleneck. These iterations, often occurring at the tail end of the convergence process, require a large number of steps to achieve exact convergence, yielding only marginal updates to action tokens. This significantly reduces the average decoding speed and can introduce delays in high-frequency tasks.

To address these motivations, the authors set out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Objective 1: Enable Robust Parallel Prediction via Consistency Distillation.** The primary goal is to train VLA models to predict multiple correct action tokens in each Jacobi iteration, even when conditioned on arbitrary or partially incorrect intermediate states. This aims to unlock the full potential of parallel decoding.
- **Objective 2: Mitigate Error Accumulation and Preserve Manipulation Skills.** During the consistency distillation process, there's a risk of the student model deviating from the teacher's performance or accumulating errors from imprecise teacher signals. A key objective is to design a training framework that ensures robust learning while maintaining the model's core manipulation capabilities.
- **Objective 3: Further Accelerate Inference by Addressing Inefficient Iterations.** The final objective is to introduce a mechanism that circumvents the delays caused by "inefficient iterations" in Jacobi decoding, thereby improving average inference efficiency without significant performance degradation.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to provide a universally applicable, efficient, and general paradigm for accelerating multimodal decision-making in robotics, making VLA models truly viable for real-world high-frequency and dexterous manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CEED-VLA framework is designed to significantly accelerate VLA inference by optimizing the Jacobi decoding process through a multi-pronged approach involving consistency distillation and an early-exit strategy.

#### 3.1. Teacher Model

The proposed method uses pre-trained, vanilla VLA models as "teacher models." Specifically, the paper employs:
- **LLaVA-VLA**: A VLA model based on LLaVA, augmented with action chunking at the output stage for temporally coherent action generation.
- **OpenVLA**: A popular open-source VLA built upon LLaMA 2 with a visual encoder integrating DINOv2 and SigLIP features.
These teacher models operate autoregressively and, as previously discussed, exhibit limited acceleration when directly applying Jacobi decoding due to their training paradigm. Each action `a_t` is 7-dimensional (X, Y, Z, φ, θ, ψ, G for position, rotation, and gripper state), and action chunking extends predicted actions into a sequence `A_t = [a_{t+1}..., a_{t+m}]`.

#### 3.2. Student Model Training (CEED-VLA)

The core of CEED-VLA lies in fine-tuning the teacher model into a "student model" (`Q_θ`) using a novel consistency training procedure.

- **Jacobi Trajectory Collection:**
 - **Purpose:** To generate a dataset (`D`) suitable for consistency distillation.
 - **Process:** The pre-trained teacher model (`P`) is run with Jacobi decoding on a standard robot dataset (`C`). During this process, the entire Jacobi trajectory (`J`), which includes intermediate states (`Y`) and the final converged fixed point (`Y*`), is recorded for each input (`x` comprising image, instruction, proprioception). This dataset captures how the teacher model iteratively refines its predictions.

- **Consistency Training:**
 The training procedure for the student model `Q_θ` optimizes two objectives:
 - **Consistency Loss (L_C):**
 - **Goal:** To enable the student model to directly map *any* intermediate state `Y` along a Jacobi trajectory `J` to the final fixed point `Y*` in a single iteration. This addresses the challenge of the model struggling with "incorrect prefixes."
 - **Mechanism:** `L_C = E_{(x,J)∼D, Y∼J} [ Σ_{i=1}^n KL(Q_{θ-}(\cdot|Y^*_i, x) || Q_θ(\cdot|Y_i, x)) ]`. This minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the student model's prediction from an intermediate state `Y` and the teacher's fixed point `Y*`. `θ-` indicates that the teacher's parameters are kept fixed (stop-gradient), preventing the student from simply mimicking the teacher's iterative behavior.
 - **Mixed-label AR Supervision (L_AR):**
 - **Goal:** To preserve the student model's native manipulation skills (autoregressive generation capability) and mitigate error accumulation that can arise from relying solely on potentially inaccurate teacher-generated `Y*` labels during distillation.
 - **Mechanism:** `L_AR = E_{(x,Y*)∼D} [ -Σ_{i=1}^N log Q_θ(Y^*_i | Y^*_{<i}, x) ]`. This is a standard autoregressive loss. The innovation is the "mixed-label" strategy:
 - For each sample, the L1 distance between the teacher's generated fixed point `Y*` and the *ground-truth (GT)* action from the original dataset `C` is computed.
 - If `L1(Y*, GT) < δ_max` (a correctness threshold), the teacher's `Y*` is used as the supervisory label for `L_AR`.
 - If `L1(Y*, GT) ≥ δ_max` (indicating the teacher's output is an outlier or inaccurate), the ground-truth `GT` value is used as the supervisory label instead.
 This adaptive strategy ensures robust learning by selectively leveraging reliable teacher signals while preventing the propagation of teacher-generated errors.
 - **Total Loss:** The overall training objective is a weighted sum: `L(θ) = L_C + ωL_AR`, where `ω` is a weighting coefficient.

#### 3.3. Inference (Early-Exit Decoding)

After consistency training, the student model (`CEED-VLA`) uses Jacobi decoding. However, to address the "inefficient iterations" bottleneck, the authors introduce:

- **Early-exit Decoding:**
 - **Rationale:** Empirical observation shows that the final steps of Jacobi iterations often yield only marginal token updates, yet consume significant time due to strict convergence conditions. Moreover, for many robotic tasks, perfect convergence at every action token is not strictly necessary; success is often determined by key actions at specific states, and "good-enough" actions suffice for intermediate steps.
 - **Mechanism:** A predefined "exit point" (`σ`) is introduced. The Jacobi decoding process is prematurely halted at the `σ`-th iteration, and the intermediate output at that step is directly emitted as the final action sequence. This relaxes the strict convergence condition `Y^(k) = Y^(k-1)`.
 - **Variants:** `CEED-VLA` uses a moderately chosen `σ`. `CEED-VLA-Turbo` employs an even smaller `σ` for aggressive acceleration, accepting a minor performance trade-off.

This combined approach allows CEED-VLA to significantly reduce the number of iterations required for action generation, leading to substantial speedups while maintaining high task success rates by robustly predicting multiple tokens per iteration and intelligently exiting the decoding process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CEED-VLA consistently demonstrates significant inference acceleration while maintaining or even improving manipulation performance across various benchmarks and deployment environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Benchmarks:** Experiments were conducted on two widely used robot learning benchmarks: CALVIN (34 tasks, 4 environments, ABC→D setup) and LIBERO-Long (10 long-horizon, multi-step tasks).
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against naive autoregressive (AR) decoding, advanced VLM acceleration methods (FastV, SparseVLM), and parallel decoding VLAs (PD-VLA). LLaVA-VLA and OpenVLA served as base teacher models.
- **Key Acceleration & Performance (Table 2):**
 - **Significant Speedup:** CEED-VLA achieves substantial acceleration compared to baselines. On LLaVA-VLA, it offers a **2.0x speedup** (79.2 Tokens/s vs. 39.6) and a **3.3x frequency increase** (7.27 Hz vs. 2.23 Hz) on CALVIN, with negligible reduction in average sequential completions (3.67 vs. 3.70 for PD-VLA).
 - On OpenVLA, the speedup is even more pronounced: **4.1x acceleration** (225.0 Tokens/s vs. 54.4) and **4.3x frequency increase** (25.60 Hz vs. 5.95 Hz) on LIBERO-Long, while maintaining comparable success rates (62.2% vs. 62.4% for PD-VLA).
 - **Why OpenVLA achieves higher acceleration:** The authors hypothesize this is due to LIBERO tasks being relatively easier and shorter-horizon than CALVIN's complex, multi-subtask scenarios, requiring fewer iterations in Jacobi decoding.
- **Underlying Acceleration Phenomena (Table 4, Figure 4):**
 - **Fixed Tokens:** CEED-VLA significantly increases the number of "fixed tokens" (correctly predicted tokens even when preceding ones are incorrect) in each iteration. For LLaVA-VLA, it goes from 0 (vanilla) to 13.5 fixed tokens, directly correlating with the observed 2.0x speedup. This confirms that consistency training enables the model to predict multiple correct tokens in parallel.
 - **Early-Exit Visualization:** Figure 4 visually demonstrates how early-exit decoding works, showing the model terminating before exact convergence (exit point value not matching the fixed point), which further reduces iterations and contributes to speedup.

#### 4.2. Ablation Experiments

- **Effectiveness of Key Designs (Table 3):**
 - Consistency Training and Early-Exit Decoding are the primary drivers of acceleration.
 - Mixed-label Supervision is crucial for maintaining action accuracy and success rates, preventing performance degradation.
- **Early-exit Decoding (Figure 5 left, Table 8):**
 - Setting the exit point (`σ`) to 16 (CEED-VLA) provides a noticeable speedup with negligible performance drop. This validates that relaxed convergence conditions are feasible for robotic tasks.
 - Reducing `σ` further to 8 (CEED-VLA-Turbo) yields even greater acceleration (e.g., 126.7 Tokens/s for LLaVA-VLA vs. 79.2 for CEED-VLA) with only a slight performance degradation (3.04 avg. len. vs. 3.47).
 - Crucially, consistency training *preserves* the model's performance even with aggressive early-exit points, unlike vanilla Jacobi decoding which suffers significant degradation.
- **Mixed-label Supervision (Table 5):**
 - The proposed mixed-label strategy (combining teacher output and ground truth) achieves the best balance between speedup and performance.
 - Using only teacher output leads to error accumulation and shorter episode lengths.
 - Using only ground truth creates conflicting supervision and yields the worst performance.
- **Loss Design (Table 6):**
 - The chosen weighting (`ω=1` for `L_C + L_AR`) provides a good balance.
 - Increasing the emphasis on `L_AR` (e.g., `ω=10`) slightly improves accuracy but reduces speedup, highlighting the trade-off.
- **Data Size (Figure 5 right):**
 - CEED-VLA demonstrates strong data efficiency, with 60k Jacobi trajectories being sufficient for significant acceleration.
 - Performance gains plateau beyond 120k trajectories, suggesting that large datasets are not strictly necessary for the core acceleration benefits.

#### 4.3. Real-world Experiments

- **Setup:** Dual-arm AgileX PiPer robotic system with multiple depth cameras for observation.
- **Tasks:** Evaluated on basic tasks (push button, lift block) and more demanding dexterous tasks (pour water, fold towel) with different data collection frequencies (10 Hz vs. 30 Hz).
- **Results (Table 7):**
 - **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation:** LLaVA-VLA struggles with high-frequency dexterous tasks (e.g., 15% on pour water, 5% on fold towel), often leading to discontinuous actions and failures. PD-VLA improves this slightly.
 - **CEED-VLA's Superiority:** CEED-VLA significantly boosts inference speed and control frequency (e.g., **13.0 Hz** vs. 3.3 Hz for LLaVA-VLA, a **3.9x frequency increase**). This enables the model to learn and execute high-frequency actions more effectively, resulting in a substantial improvement in success rates on dexterous tasks (e.g., **80% on pour water, 75% on fold towel**).
 - This real-world validation confirms CEED-VLA's practical efficacy in enabling robust, high-frequency robotic control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "CEED-VLA: Consistency Vision-Language-Action Model with Early-Exit Decoding" holds significant implications for the field of robotics and embodied AI, offering a robust solution to a critical practical bottleneck.

#### 5.1. Key Significance

1. **Addressing a Core Bottleneck:** The primary significance of CEED-VLA lies in its effective solution to the inference speed bottleneck of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Current VLA models, despite their powerful capabilities, are often too slow for real-time robotic deployment, especially in tasks requiring high-frequency, continuous control and dexterous manipulation. CEED-VLA directly tackles this limitation.
2. **Unlocking Jacobi Decoding's Potential:** The paper successfully addresses the inherent limitation of vanilla Jacobi decoding when applied to autoregressively trained VLAs. By introducing consistency distillation, it enables the model to robustly predict multiple correct action tokens in parallel, even when conditioned on imperfect intermediate states, thus unlocking the true acceleration potential of parallel decoding.
3. **General and Universal Approach:** CEED-VLA is demonstrated to be a "universal acceleration method." It seamlessly integrates with existing VLA architectures (LLaVA-VLA, OpenVLA) without requiring fundamental model redesigns or additional GPU memory overhead. This adaptability makes it a highly practical and accessible solution for a broad range of VLA models and robotic systems.
4. **Balancing Speed and Performance:** Crucially, CEED-VLA achieves significant speedup (e.g., 4x inference acceleration and frequency boost) while *maintaining or even improving* task success rates. The introduction of mixed-label supervision and early-exit decoding ensures that acceleration does not come at the cost of manipulation accuracy, particularly highlighting its benefits for challenging high-frequency dexterous tasks in the real world.
5. **Novel Application of Consistency Models:** This work marks the first application of consistency training techniques to accelerate iterative inference in VLA models, drawing inspiration from their successful use in diffusion policies. This opens a new avenue for research into optimizing iterative processes in complex multimodal systems.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

1. **Enabling Practical VLA Deployment:** By making VLA inference fast enough for real-time operation, CEED-VLA dramatically lowers the barrier for deploying these advanced models in practical robotic applications, from industrial automation to domestic assistance.
2. **Advancing Dexterous Manipulation:** The ability to achieve high execution frequencies and maintain high success rates on dexterous tasks (like pouring water or folding towels) is transformative. This can lead to robots performing more complex and human-like manipulation skills that were previously infeasible due to control latency.
3. **Facilitating Research in Complex Robotic Systems:** Faster inference allows for more rapid iteration in research and development cycles. It also opens possibilities for exploring more complex VLA architectures or task formulations that were previously computationally prohibitive.
4. **Broader Impact on Multimodal AI:** The principles demonstrated here, particularly the application of consistency distillation to iterative decoding processes, could extend beyond robotics to other multimodal decision-making systems or generative models where fast, high-quality output is critical.
5. **Foundation for Future Work:** The paper itself points to promising future directions, such as combining CEED-VLA with more efficient action tokenization schemes for even greater real-time performance. It also suggests applications in embodied chain-of-thought reasoning and knowledge insulating models, where the generation of extensive intermediate tokens or simultaneous linguistic/action outputs could benefit from significant acceleration.

In conclusion, CEED-VLA represents a significant step forward in making powerful Vision-Language-Action models truly practical and deployable, fundamentally enhancing the capabilities of autonomous robotic systems in dynamic, real-world environments.
