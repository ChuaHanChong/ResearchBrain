# BarlowWalk: Self-supervised Representation Learning for Legged Robot Terrain-adaptive Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> BarlowWalk integrates self-supervised learning with PPO to enable bipedal robots to learn terrain-adaptive locomotion strategies solely from proprioceptive data. The TRON1 robot demonstrated robust navigation across diverse obstacles, achieving stable performance up to difficulty level 6 with an efficient 4-hour training time.

## Problem

- Bipedal robots face challenges in achieving robust and adaptive locomotion across diverse and complex terrains due to their inherent stability limitations.
- Mainstream reinforcement learning methods for bipedal terrain traversal often suffer from long training times and rely on privileged terrain information or multi-stage teacher-student knowledge distillation.
- Policies typically lack direct access to comprehensive terrain information in real-world scenarios, necessitating efficient learning from partially observable proprioceptive inputs.

## Method

- **BarlowWalk** proposes a single-stage, end-to-end training framework that combines self-supervised representation learning (using Barlow Twins) with an improved Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm.
- The policy network learns robust latent representations from historical proprioceptive observations, without direct external terrain information, while the critic uses privileged terrain data during training.
- An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is employed, with specific MLP and latent encoders for processing observations, alongside domain randomization and curriculum learning to enhance sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The TRON1 bipedal robot, controlled by BarlowWalk, achieved robust locomotion across six diverse terrain types (e.g., rough surfaces, slopes, stairs), consistently passing challenges up to difficulty level 6.
- BarlowWalk demonstrated slight improvements in linear velocity tracking errors compared to an adapted SLR baseline and significantly better velocity tracking performance than a degraded baseline lacking self-supervised learning.
- The entire training process was completed in 4 hours on an RTX 4090 GPU using 4,096 parallel simulation agents, highlighting the efficiency of the single-stage framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Barlow Twins loss effectively encourages the policy network to learn compact, discriminative, and generalizable latent features from proprioceptive data by promoting invariance and reducing redundancy.
> - Learned latent representations exhibit distinct distribution patterns for different terrain behaviors, with functional similarities (e.g., uphill slopes and ascending stairs) being inherently captured.
> - While initial reward growth might be slower, the Barlow Twins mechanism contributes to accelerated reward improvement and more stable convergence in the later stages of training compared to a baseline without self-supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robot locomotion control, particularly for bipedal robots, presents challenges in achieving robust and adaptive movement across diverse and complex terrains. Historically, model-based approaches have been employed, leveraging kinematic and dynamic principles. While these methods have demonstrated results, they frequently encounter difficulties in balancing model accuracy with computational efficiency required for real-time control. Furthermore, they necessitate detailed knowledge of robot dynamics, which can elevate the technical barrier for researchers.

In recent years, reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as an alternative, allowing robots to acquire complex motor skills through interaction with simulation environments and reward functions, bypassing the need for explicit dynamic modeling. However, mainstream RL approaches for bipedal robot terrain traversal face their own set of challenges, particularly concerning partial observability in real-world scenarios. Robots typically lack direct access to comprehensive terrain information and must rely on proprioceptive inputs. To address this, existing RL research commonly employs two strategies: (1) feeding privileged terrain information to the critic network to guide policy learning, or (2) utilizing teacher-student frameworks where a teacher policy with full terrain access distills knowledge to a student policy that only uses proprioception. These methods often require full observability during simulation for effective training and can lead to extended training times.

Research in quadruped robots has shown some success in complex terrains using only current and recent proprioceptive information due to their inherent stability. Bipedal robots, however, possess limited support stability, making reliance solely on proprioceptive inputs more difficult for stable locomotion on varied terrains. This paper contributes to the landscape by proposing a method that aims to reduce the reliance on external terrain perception and knowledge distillation in bipedal locomotion, while also addressing the efficiency concerns of long training times inherent in some established RL paradigms. It positions itself as a development within the RL framework that seeks to enhance representation learning from proprioceptive data to improve adaptability and reduce training complexity for bipedal robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a bipedal robot to autonomously navigate diverse and complex terrain obstacles without direct access to external terrain information and without requiring a multi-stage teacher-student training process. The authors identify two key motivations:

First, current mainstream reinforcement learning methods for bipedal robot terrain traversal, particularly those employing teacher-student policy knowledge distillation, are noted to suffer from long training times. This extended training duration limits development efficiency, impeding the rapid iteration and refinement of locomotion control policies. The authors aim to overcome this limitation by proposing a more efficient training paradigm that can be completed in a single, end-to-end stage.

Second, in real-world deployment, bipedal robots typically operate with limited sensory input, primarily relying on proprioceptive information (e.g., joint positions, velocities, body orientation). Existing solutions often mitigate this partial observability by either providing privileged terrain information to the critic network during training or by using a teacher policy that has access to such information to guide a student policy. This reliance on external terrain perception or explicit knowledge transfer mechanisms is a constraint. The authors are motivated to develop a control framework where the robot's policy network can learn robust locomotion strategies solely from historical proprioceptive data, thereby significantly reducing dependence on external terrain perception or the complexity of knowledge distillation techniques. They posit that autonomously learned latent representations from proprioceptive data can be more adaptive and expressive than handcrafted features, leading to better generalization across complex and dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed BarlowWalk, integrates self-supervised representation learning with an improved Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm within an asymmetric actor-critic framework. The core idea is to learn robust latent representations of historical proprioceptive observations to enhance the policy's ability to navigate complex terrains without direct terrain information.

**A. Hardware Platform:** The research utilizes the TRON1 bipedal robot from Limx Dynamics. This robot, standing at 845 mm and weighing under 20 kg, has eight degrees of freedom (four per leg) and operates in a footboard mode.

**B. Overall Framework (BarlowWalk Overview):** The training process is designed to be end-to-end and completed in a single stage. The policy and critic networks receive different inputs:
- The **policy network** receives current observations (excluding linear velocity, which is not directly measurable in real-world scenarios) along with latent representations of historical proprioceptive observations derived from two sets of encoders.
- The **critic network** receives current observations and privileged terrain information, which is available during simulation.
The Barlow Twins loss function is applied to the latent representations across consecutive time steps to optimize these features.

**C. Training Pipeline (POMDP and PPO Integration):** The robot's interaction with the environment is modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The PPO algorithm is employed for policy optimization, with its objective function modified to include the self-supervised learning component.

- **Observation Processing:** For the policy network, historical observations spanning 10 time steps are maintained. A segment of these historical observations (the last 5 time steps) is fed into a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) encoder to produce an intermediate latent feature, $l_t$. This feature is then passed through a latent encoder to generate a lower-dimensional representation, $z_t$. This $z_t$, along with the current observation, forms the input to the policy network. Crucially, the historical observations fed to the MLP encoder exclude linear velocity.
- **Barlow Twins for Self-Supervision:** To achieve self-supervised learning, two sets of historical observations are used: $obs_{old\_hist}$ (from a previous time step) and $obs_{new\_hist}$ (from the current time step). Both are processed by the same MLP encoder and latent encoder to produce $z'_t$ and $z''_t$ respectively. These outputs are then fed into an additional Barlow encoder, resulting in $u'_t$ and $u''_t$. The Barlow Twins loss, $L_{BT}$, is computed on the cross-correlation matrix ($C$) of these outputs. The loss function encourages the diagonal elements of $C$ to be close to 1 (invariance) and the off-diagonal elements to be close to 0 (redundancy reduction), thereby learning discriminative and generalizable features without negative samples.
- **Total Loss Function:** The final optimization objective combines the standard PPO loss for policy updates ($L_{\pi}$) with the Barlow Twins loss ($L_{BT}$): $L_{total} = L_{\pi} + L_{BT}$.

**D. State and Action:** The robot's action space is 8-dimensional, corresponding to the joint commands for its two legs. The observation space is detailed in Table I, including body velocity, angular velocity, gravity vector, commanded velocities, joint positions, joint velocities, previous actions, and phase information. The MLP encoder for the policy receives a 175-dimensional input (historical proprioceptive data), while the critic receives a 225-dimensional input that includes additional terrain information.

**E. Policy Architecture and Parameters:** Both the actor (policy) and critic networks utilize Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), each with a single layer and a hidden size of 64, followed by an MLP layer of dimension 32. The architecture includes three encoders:
- MLP Encoder: Input dimension 175, output dimension 64.
- Latent Encoder: Input dimension 64, output dimension 16.
- Barlow Encoder: Input dimension 16, output dimension 64.
PPO hyperparameters such as batch size, mini-batch size, clipping range, entropy coefficient, and discount factors are specified. The Barlow Twins parameter $\lambda$ is set to $5e^{-3}$.

**F. Reward Function:** A multi-objective reward function guides the training, comprising three components:
1. **Tracking Performance Reward ($r_{tracking}$):** Encourages accurate tracking of desired base velocity, height, and posture commands.
2. **Action Smoothness Penalty ($r_{action}$):** Minimizes joint torque fluctuations and energy expenditure.
3. **Constraint Satisfaction Reward ($r_{constraint}$):** Enforces safety conditions like contact force limits and joint motion ranges.
The total reward is a weighted sum of these components.

**G. Domain Randomization:** To improve policy transferability from simulation to reality, domain randomization is applied to robot dynamics (link mass, payload mass, CoM of base, joint motor parameters) and environmental interactions (friction, restitution, external forces). Parameters are uniformly sampled within defined ranges.

**H. Curriculum Learning:** A progressive training strategy is implemented, involving both terrain and instruction curricula. The robot is trained across six characteristic terrain types: rough flat surfaces, uphill slopes, downhill slopes, ascending stairs, descending stairs, and randomly distributed obstacles. A 10-level terrain difficulty assessment system is used, starting with standardized straight paths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research utilized IsaacGym for simulation, training 4,096 parallel TRON1 agents on an RTX 4090 GPU. Each episode lasted 20 seconds, with policy updates every 24 steps, and total training completed in 4 hours.

**A. Experimental Results:** The TRON1 robot demonstrated robust locomotion across all six designed terrain types (rough flat surfaces, uphill slopes, downhill slopes, ascending stairs, descending stairs, and obstacle terrains). The robot consistently passed challenges up to difficulty level 6 within a predefined 10-level terrain difficulty assessment system.

**B. Latent Space Analysis:** To evaluate the learned latent representations, t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) was used to visualize the latent features generated by the latent encoder while the robot traversed various terrains. The analysis revealed distinct distribution patterns for embedded latent representations corresponding to different terrain behaviors. A notable observation was the significant similarity between the latent representations for uphill slopes and ascending stairs, suggesting that the robot's policy perceives these climbing behaviors as functionally similar.

**C. Comparative Experiment:** BarlowWalk was compared against two baseline approaches focusing on tracking errors for linear and angular velocities:
- **Ours (BarlowWalk):** The proposed full framework.
- **Baseline 1 (SLR adaptation):** An adaptation of the SLR framework (originally for quadruped robots) to the TRON1 bipedal robot, maintaining analogous reward functions.
- **Baseline 2:** A degraded version that eliminated all terrain perception, feeding only 5-step historical proprioceptive observations into a single MLP encoder for both policy and critic networks.

Results showed that BarlowWalk exhibited slight improvements in linear velocity tracking errors compared to Baseline 1, indicating the effectiveness of its self-supervised approach in extracting useful latent features. Baseline 2, relying solely on a historical proprioceptive encoder without the Barlow Twins mechanism, demonstrated significantly poorer velocity tracking performance for both linear and angular velocities. For angular velocity tracking errors, BarlowWalk achieved comparable performance to Baseline 1.

**D. Ablation Study for the Learning Framework:** An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of the Barlow Twins loss term to the PPO algorithm.
- **Algorithm Convergence:** The model without the Barlow Twins loss term showed faster reward growth during the early training phase. However, in the mid-to-late stages of training, the model incorporating the Barlow Twins loss exhibited accelerated reward improvement and more stable convergence, suggesting superior optimization efficiency over the long run.
- **Generalization Capability:** The model without Barlow Twins initially progressed slowly in adapting to terrain difficulty levels. However, after the early phase, it advanced rapidly, reaching near Level 6 around 4,000 steps and achieving stable convergence. The exact comparative progression with the Barlow Twins model for this metric isn't explicitly detailed as "better" or "worse" in the provided text beyond the convergence stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study presents a locomotion control framework for bipedal robots that offers several significant contributions and potential impacts.

**Reduced Reliance on External Perception and Knowledge Distillation:** A primary significance of BarlowWalk is its demonstrated ability to achieve terrain-adaptive locomotion without requiring direct external terrain perception for the policy network or relying on traditional teacher-student knowledge distillation techniques. This approach simplifies the control pipeline and enhances the autonomy of bipedal robots in real-world environments where explicit terrain maps or privileged information may not be readily available.

**Efficient and Stable Learning:** The integration of the Barlow Twins self-supervised learning algorithm allows the policy to learn high-quality, compact, and informative latent representations from historical proprioceptive data. The ablation study suggests that while initial reward growth might be slower, the Barlow Twins mechanism contributes to accelerated reward improvement and more stable convergence in the later stages of training, potentially leading to more robust and generalized policies. The end-to-end, single-stage training process also contributes to development efficiency by reducing the complexity and duration typically associated with multi-stage training methods.

**Improved Adaptability and Robustness:** The simulation experiments and hardware tests demonstrate that the TRON1 robot, controlled by BarlowWalk, can achieve robust and adaptive locomotion across a variety of complex terrains, including rough surfaces, slopes, and stairs, up to a considerable difficulty level. The ability to extract meaningful features from proprioceptive data enables the robot to infer necessary gait adjustments without explicit environmental inputs, thus improving its resilience to varying conditions. The latent space analysis further supports that the learned representations effectively capture behavioral similarities across different climbing terrains.

**Advancement in Legged Robot Control:** This work contributes to the broader field of legged robot control by proposing a viable alternative to existing RL paradigms that often struggle with partial observability and training efficiency for bipedal systems. By focusing on self-supervised representation learning from internal robot states, it paves the way for more intrinsically intelligent and adaptable locomotion controllers.

**Future Impact:** The authors plan to optimize the framework for more complex motion control tasks and ultimately deploy it onto physical robot platforms for practical verification. Successful physical deployment would validate the simulation-to-reality transfer capabilities of the learned policies, opening avenues for real-world applications of bipedal robots in unstructured environments, such as search and rescue, logistics, or human assistance.
