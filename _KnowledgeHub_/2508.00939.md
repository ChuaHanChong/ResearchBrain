# BarlowWalk: Self-supervised Representation Learning for Legged Robot Terrain-adaptive Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> BarlowWalk integrates self-supervised learning with PPO to enable bipedal robots to learn terrain-adaptive locomotion strategies solely from proprioceptive data. The TRON1 robot demonstrated robust navigation across diverse obstacles, achieving stable performance up to difficulty level 6 with an efficient 4-hour training time.

## Problem

- Bipedal robots face challenges in achieving robust and adaptive locomotion across diverse and complex terrains due to their inherent stability limitations.
- Mainstream reinforcement learning methods for bipedal terrain traversal often suffer from long training times and rely on privileged terrain information or multi-stage teacher-student knowledge distillation.
- Policies typically lack direct access to comprehensive terrain information in real-world scenarios, necessitating efficient learning from partially observable proprioceptive inputs.

## Method

- **BarlowWalk** proposes a single-stage, end-to-end training framework that combines self-supervised representation learning (using Barlow Twins) with an improved Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm.
- The policy network learns robust latent representations from historical proprioceptive observations, without direct external terrain information, while the critic uses privileged terrain data during training.
- An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is employed, with specific MLP and latent encoders for processing observations, alongside domain randomization and curriculum learning to enhance sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The TRON1 bipedal robot, controlled by BarlowWalk, achieved robust locomotion across six diverse terrain types (e.g., rough surfaces, slopes, stairs), consistently passing challenges up to difficulty level 6.
- BarlowWalk demonstrated slight improvements in linear velocity tracking errors compared to an adapted SLR baseline and significantly better velocity tracking performance than a degraded baseline lacking self-supervised learning.
- The entire training process was completed in 4 hours on an RTX 4090 GPU using 4,096 parallel simulation agents, highlighting the efficiency of the single-stage framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Barlow Twins loss effectively encourages the policy network to learn compact, discriminative, and generalizable latent features from proprioceptive data by promoting invariance and reducing redundancy.
> - Learned latent representations exhibit distinct distribution patterns for different terrain behaviors, with functional similarities (e.g., uphill slopes and ascending stairs) being inherently captured.
> - While initial reward growth might be slower, the Barlow Twins mechanism contributes to accelerated reward improvement and more stable convergence in the later stages of training compared to a baseline without self-supervision.
