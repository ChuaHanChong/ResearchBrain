# Dual-Process Atomic Skill Learning: Decoupling Semantic Reasoning and Real-Time Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named Dual-Process Atomic Skill Learning (DASL) addresses training instability and skill codebook collapse in language-conditioned hierarchical imitation learning by asynchronously decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action execution. The approach achieves robust skill acquisition and compositional generalization, demonstrating an 88% success rate in real-world robot manipulation and outperforming previous methods on benchmarks such as CALVIN.

## Problem

- Existing language-conditioned hierarchical imitation learning systems suffer from training instability, mutual interference, and "codebook collapse" due to the tight coupling of high-level semantic reasoning and low-level action generation.
- Current hierarchical skill learning methods often require multi-stage training or additional architectural constraints to mitigate issues arising from this tight coupling.
- Developing robots that can perform complex, multi-step compositional tasks based on natural language instructions remains challenging, especially regarding generalization to unseen instructions.

## Method

- Proposed **Dual-Process Atomic Skill Learning (DASL)**, an asynchronous ==hierarchical imitation learning== framework inspired by cognitive dual-process theory, to explicitly decouple semantic reasoning and real-time control.
- Designed a Slow-Frequency Policy (==Option Transformer== with ==Vector Quantization==) for deliberative, high-level skill planning and a High-Frequency Policy (==Decision Transformer==) for low-level, precise action execution.
- Integrated a **Latent Trajectory Diffusion Module** exclusively during training to regularize the latent skill space and a dedicated ==asynchronous coordination mechanism== to manage decoupled execution schedules.

## Results

- DASL achieved a **48.05%** success rate on the CALVIN benchmark, outperforming baselines like HULC (**32.50%**) and LCSD (**35.60%**) by a substantial margin.
- In real-world experiments, DASL successfully executed both atomic and complex sequential instructions on a Realman manipulator with an **88%** average success rate, compared to the LISA baseline's **56%**.
- The framework maintained high inference frequencies (**13.61-13.97 Hz**) and efficient resource consumption, comparable to or faster than leading baselines, due to its deterministic Decision Transformer inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asynchronously decoupling slow semantic reasoning from fast action execution, rather than co-training them synchronously, significantly enhances training stability and mitigates mutual interference between policies in hierarchical learning.
> - Employing a training-only latent trajectory diffusion model effectively regularizes the skill representation space, preventing codebook collapse and ensuring semantically distinct skill usage without impacting inference efficiency.
> - Leveraging discrete Vector Quantization for skill representation provides an information bottleneck that promotes interpretable atomic skill discovery and improves performance in complex manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a significant challenge within language-conditioned imitation learning (IL) for robotics: enabling robots to perform complex, multi-step compositional tasks based on natural language instructions. Language-conditioned IL is recognized as a powerful paradigm for task specification, offering greater generality than goal images or trajectories.

Existing approaches to this problem typically fall into two categories: methods that directly map language embeddings and observations to actions, and those that leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) for sub-task decomposition. Hierarchical methods, which decompose complex tasks into atomic skills, are a key strategy for improving generalization, especially when comprehensive task data is scarce.

However, a persistent limitation in current hierarchical skill learning methods is the tight coupling between high-level skill reasoning and low-level action generation. This coupling, particularly in end-to-end co-training paradigms, often leads to training instability, mutual interference between policies, and a phenomenon known as "codebook collapse." Codebook collapse refers to the underutilization or degradation of the learned skill representations, where only a small subset of the potential skills is effectively learned or used. To counteract this, existing methods often resort to multi-stage training or additional architectural constraints, which can be cumbersome. While dual-system architectures have begun to emerge in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, their application to hierarchical skill acquisition, specifically in addressing stability and skill representation issues, has been limited. This paper proposes a framework to specifically mitigate these identified problems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more robust and efficient framework for language-conditioned hierarchical imitation learning, specifically designed to handle complex, multi-step compositional tasks. The authors identify the central problem as the training instability and skill codebook collapse that result from the tight coupling of high-level semantic reasoning and low-level action execution in existing hierarchical imitation learning systems.

The motivation for their proposed solution is drawn from Kahneman’s Dual-Process Theory of cognition, which distinguishes between "System 1" (fast, intuitive) and "System 2" (slow, deliberative) processes. The authors hypothesize that applying a similar decoupling principle—asynchronously separating slow, deliberative semantic reasoning from fast, real-time motion control—can significantly improve both training stability and execution efficiency in robotic skill learning. This approach aims to address the mutual interference between skill abstraction and motion generation that commonly occurs in synchronously trained frameworks.

Specifically, the key objectives are:
- To propose an asynchronous hierarchical imitation learning framework that explicitly decouples high-level skill reasoning from low-level action execution.
- To design an architecture that integrates asynchronous execution and latent skill diffusion to unify skill representation and mitigate codebook interference.
- To learn reusable and interpretable atomic skills directly from language-conditioned demonstrations.
- To maintain high-frequency action execution despite the introduction of a slower reasoning component.
- To achieve state-of-the-art performance in skill acquisition and compositional generalization to unseen instructions across various simulation benchmarks and real-world experiments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose Dual-Process Atomic Skill Learning (DASL), an asynchronous hierarchical imitation learning framework that decouples high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action execution. The framework is designed around three main components: a Slow-Frequency Policy, a High-Frequency Policy, and an Asynchronous Coordination mechanism.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The problem is modeled as a Task-Augmented Markov Decision Process (MDP). Given an offline dataset of language-trajectory pairs, the goal is to learn a language-conditioned sequential policy that generalizes to unseen tasks, while also performing unsupervised discovery of reusable skill primitives.

#### 3.2. Slow-Frequency Policy (High-Level Semantic Skill Planning)

This policy operates at a coarse temporal resolution, making deliberative decisions about semantic skills.
- **Timescale:** It processes a sliding context window of length `L` at a fixed interval `I` (e.g., every `I` environment steps). At each slow timestep `t_s`, it predicts a continuous latent skill.
- **Skill Predictor:** An Option Transformer is used for cross-modal reasoning. Visual observations at `t_s` are encoded and temporally concatenated with embedded language instructions. Causal self-attention processes this joint sequence, and hidden states from observation positions are projected to continuous latent skills (`¯z_ts`).
- **Discrete Skill Vector Quantization (VQ):** The continuous skills (`¯z_ts`) are mapped to a learnable, discrete codebook (`C`) using a nearest-neighbor lookup, resulting in discrete skills (`z_ts`). The codebook is optimized with an auxiliary commitment loss, and gradients are approximated via the Straight-Through Estimator (STE).

#### 3.3. High-Frequency Policy (Low-Level Skill-Conditioned Decision Transformer)

This policy operates at the original control frequency to generate precise actions. It employs a hybrid architecture.
- **Skill Fusion:** The discrete skill sequence is projected into embeddings and upsampled by repeating each vector `I` times to match the high-frequency observation length. These upsampled skill embeddings are then added element-wise to the high-frequency observation embeddings, providing semantic guidance without increasing feature dimensionality.
- **Latent Trajectory Diffusion Module:** This module is utilized *exclusively during training* to robustly regularize the latent dynamics. A forward diffusion process adds noise to a trajectory prefix, and a 1D U-Net is trained to predict this injected noise, conditioned on the skill sequence and diffusion step. This mechanism aims to structure the latent manifold and ensure semantic alignment between skills and trajectories. Crucially, iterative denoising sampling is *not* performed during inference, preserving efficiency.
- **Decision Transformer (DT):** A GPT-2 backbone forms the core of the low-level policy. It interleaves timestep-encoded action and fused observation embeddings to predict actions (`ˆa_t`) at each timestep. During deployment, the action predicted at the final position of the sliding context window is executed.

#### 3.4. Asynchronous Coordination Mechanism

This mechanism explicitly decouples the execution schedules of the slow-frequency skill planner and the high-frequency action executor. This asynchronous design is intended to balance the need for deliberative compositional reasoning with the requirement for precise, real-time control, thereby mitigating interference and enhancing overall training stability.

#### 3.5. Training Objectives

DASL employs a joint optimization strategy to learn stable behavioral semantics:
- **Action Prediction Loss (`L_action`):** A mean squared error (MSE) loss is applied between predicted actions and ground-truth expert actions (behavioral cloning). To account for the timescale discrepancy, gradients are accumulated across `I` execution steps before semantic updates.
- **VQ Commitment Loss (`L_vq`):** This loss stabilizes the discrete skill representations by encouraging continuous skill outputs to cluster around learnable codebook centroids, promoting structured and compositional high-level policies.
- **Latent Diffusion Loss (`L_diff`):** An auxiliary loss is introduced to model the trajectory manifold by training the diffusion model to predict noise in corrupted trajectory prefixes, conditioned on the skill sequence. This objective helps link discrete skills to future trajectory distributions, encouraging distinct codebook usage and preventing index collapse.
- **Total Training Objective (`L_total`):** The final loss is a weighted sum of these three components: `L_total = L_action + λ_vq L_vq + λ_diff L_diff`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates DASL across several simulation benchmarks and a real-world experiment, demonstrating its performance against various baselines.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Skill Acquisition

- **LOReL Sawyer:** DASL consistently achieved higher success rates across all rephrasal types (seen, unseen noun, unseen verb, unseen noun & verb, human-provided) and both state and image observation modalities. It showed resilience to codebook collapse, a limitation observed in baselines like LISA. The results indicated that modeling decision-making within a latent vector space and using skill-conditioned diffusion enhances alignment and stability.
- **Franka Kitchen:** DASL demonstrated robustness in tasks with image-based observations and unseen instructions, achieving higher N-rates (average number of completed sub-tasks) than baselines. This suggests its asynchronous architecture and latent diffusion effectively maintain semantic consistency and model skill trajectory distributions, even with visual noise.
- **CALVIN:** DASL achieved a 48.05% success rate, outperforming official baselines like HULC (32.50%) and LCSD (35.60%) even with simplified state inputs. This indicates the benefits of decoupling slow semantic planning from fast action execution in preventing codebook collapse and structural instability.
- **BabyAI GoToSeq:** DASL consistently outperformed baselines across varying data regimes (1k, 10k, 100k demonstrations), demonstrating effective skill learning capabilities even from limited data.

#### 4.2. Generalization Capabilities to Compositional Tasks

On the LOReL Sawyer dataset’s compositional tasks, DASL outperformed competing methods, indicating its ability to learn atomic skills and recompose them for novel, multi-step instructions. This generalization was particularly pronounced in image-based settings. Visual analysis (skill heat maps and word clouds) supported that DASL learned distinct, semantically explicit atomic skills, with codebook usage for composite tasks reflecting a composition of atomic skill distributions.

#### 4.3. Contributions of Architectural Components (Ablation Studies)

- **Latent Skill Embedding:** Introducing latent skill representation yielded a substantial performance gain over a baseline without it, suggesting raw observation spaces are insufficient for directly capturing high-level semantics.
- **Asynchronous Decoupling:** Decoupling high-level and low-level control frequencies led to a significant performance improvement (e.g., from 28.34% to 38.26% success rate in LOReL state), confirming that breaking temporal coupling mitigates interference.
- **Latent Trajectory Diffusion:** Integrating the latent diffusion module into the asynchronous framework resulted in a notable performance leap, demonstrating a synergistic relationship where diffusion effectively regularizes the latent space and mitigates covariate shift in behavioral cloning.
- **Frequency Ratios:** A sensitivity analysis revealed that an asynchronous ratio (e.g., 1:2) significantly outperforms a synchronous 1:1 ratio, highlighting the importance of the decoupling.
- **Action Generation Architectures:** Using a Decision Transformer for action generation within DASL significantly outperformed a pure generative dual-diffusion architecture (54.51% vs. 27.53% on LOReL state), indicating the DT’s deterministic mapping provides crucial stability.
- **Latent Space Regularization:** The diffusion module proved more effective than VAE or MLP Autoencoders with matched parameters, suggesting its specific capability to model complex, multimodal trajectory distributions.
- **Skill Discretization:** Discrete VQ representations yielded better performance in complex manipulation tasks (LOReL state: 54.51% vs. 31.95% for continuous skills), reinforcing the benefit of the information bottleneck.
- **Skill Codebook Size:** An optimal codebook size (K=20 for LOReL) was found to balance representational capacity and optimization stability.
- **Temporal Embeddings and OOD Horizons:** The "Clamp" strategy for temporal embeddings showed robustness and even improved performance on out-of-distribution (OOD) episode lengths, suggesting temporal embeddings infer macroscopic task phases rather than rigid step counts.

#### 4.4. Inference Frequency and Resource Consumption

DASL achieved an inference frequency of 13.61 Hz (state) and 13.97 Hz (image), comparable to LISA (15.68 Hz) and considerably faster than SkillDiffuser (2.02 Hz). This efficiency stems from using the diffusion model solely during training, with the low-level policy operating as a fast, deterministic Decision Transformer during inference. Resource utilization was also efficient, with 67.9M parameters and 768 MB GPU memory (state), approximately three times less memory than SkillDiffuser.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiment

Deployed on a Realman manipulator, DASL demonstrated practical viability. It successfully executed both atomic and complex sequential instructions, significantly outperforming the LISA baseline with an average success rate of 88% compared to LISA’s 56%. Qualitative results showed DASL achieving smoother transitions and more robust skill execution (e.g., complete drawer closure, firm object grasping) compared to LISA’s observed failures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a novel framework, DASL, that addresses critical limitations in language-conditioned hierarchical imitation learning. By introducing an asynchronous dual-process architecture and integrating a latent diffusion model for training-time regularization, the work significantly enhances training stability and mitigates skill codebook collapse, which are common issues in tightly coupled hierarchical systems.

The significance of DASL lies in its ability to:
- **Improve Skill Acquisition and Generalization:** It achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating robust skill acquisition and strong compositional generalization to unseen language instructions. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in varied and unstructured environments.
- **Enhance Training Stability:** The asynchronous decoupling mechanism, inspired by cognitive dual-process theory, effectively separates slow semantic reasoning from fast action execution. This design choice demonstrably stabilizes the learning process for hierarchical policies, preventing interference between high-level and low-level control.
- **Optimize Efficiency:** By using a latent diffusion model solely during training for representation regularization and relying on a deterministic Decision Transformer for inference, DASL achieves high inference speeds and low computational resource consumption, making it suitable for real-time robotic control applications.
- **Provide Interpretable Skills:** The use of Vector Quantization helps in discovering discrete, interpretable atomic skills, facilitating a more transparent understanding of the robot's learned behaviors.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for the field of robotics and artificial intelligence. It contributes to the development of more capable and reliable embodied agents that can interpret and execute complex human instructions. The framework's ability to generalize to novel compositional tasks, even with limited demonstrations, is particularly valuable in data-constrained scenarios. This could lead to advancements in areas such as industrial automation, assistive robotics, and human-robot interaction, where intuitive natural language interfaces and adaptable robotic behaviors are increasingly desired.

**Limitations and Future Work (as noted by the authors):**
The current framework relies on offline demonstrations, which limits its adaptability through online interaction. Future work is proposed to explore online fine-tuning via reinforcement learning. Additionally, integrating large-scale vision-language foundation models is suggested to support more open-ended instructions and broader manipulation scenarios.
