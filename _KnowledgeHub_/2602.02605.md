# Fine-Tuning Language Models to Know What They Know

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the Evolution Strategy for Metacognitive Alignment (ESMA), a method to enhance Large Language Models' (LLMs) capacity for self-knowledge by optimizing their ability to report what they know. The approach significantly increased LLMs' metacognitive ability, as measured by the d'type2 metric, to approximately 1.0, and robustly improved discrimination between correct and incorrect responses across various contexts.

## Problem

- Human intelligence features metacognition, specifically 'knowing what one knows,' a capability that is not well-understood or reliably replicated in LLMs.
- Existing LLM metacognition research primarily focuses on mitigating undesirable behaviors like hallucination or refusal, rather than investigating the fundamental alignment between an LLM's internal knowledge and its explicit reporting of that knowledge.
- It remains unclear whether LLMs genuinely reference their internal states when answering meta-level questions or merely produce memorized text patterns, with previous studies offering conflicting insights.

## Method

- A systematic measurement framework for LLM metacognition was developed, adapting the ==d'type2 metric from Signal Detection Theory== using a dual-prompt method comprising "Direct Questions" and "Meta Questions" to isolate and quantify knowledge self-assessment.
- The ==Evolution Strategy for Metacognitive Alignment (ESMA)== was introduced as a gradient-free optimization method to fine-tune LLMs, using a joint reward function to simultaneously promote factual correctness and metacognitive alignment.
- Open-source LLMs (Qwen2.5, Llama3.2, Gemma 3 Instruct) were trained using ESMA on the TriviaQA dataset, and their metacognitive performance was evaluated and compared against proprietary models across various metrics.

## Results

- ESMA consistently elevated d'type2 scores across all models to approximately **1.0** (moderate sensitivity), with Qwen2.5 3B ESMA achieving **1.02**, demonstrating superior metacognitive discrimination compared to baselines and even some closed-source models.
- Type 2 ROC curve analysis showed that ESMA-trained models significantly improved AUC to around **0.75**, indicating a robust enhancement in their ability to use continuous confidence scores to discriminate between correct and incorrect judgments.
- Metacognitive capabilities acquired through ESMA generalized effectively across unseen prompt formats (e.g., Qwen2.5 3B's IDK Alignment increased from **59.88%** to **78.07%**), novel information, other English QA datasets, and cross-lingual evaluations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs possess a foundational, measurable metacognitive capacity that can be substantially improved through targeted training with ESMA, moving them toward a moderate sensitivity level.
> - ESMA fundamentally shifts LLMs' internal representations, leading to clear separation and polarization of confidence distributions for correct and incorrect answers, indicating a deeper integration of self-monitoring signals rather than superficial pattern recognition.
> - Metacognitive improvements from ES are driven by a sparse set of high-impact weight adjustments rather than diffuse changes across the entire parameter space, suggesting potential for optimizing the efficiency of future evolutionary training methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM) research is actively exploring the capabilities of these models, including aspects that draw parallels with human cognitive functions. One such area is metacognition, defined as the ability to monitor and regulate one's own cognitive processes, with a central component being self-knowledge—the capacity to assess what one knows. While interest in LLM metacognition is increasing, particularly in practical applications like calibrating confidence, reducing hallucinations, and improving refusal behavior, the rigorous measurement of *genuine* metacognitive abilities in LLMs remains a challenge.

Prior work on LLM metacognition often conflates functional reliability with true introspective access. Improvements in areas like calibration or hallucination mitigation, while beneficial for practical applications, can arise from various heuristics (e.g., judging confidence based on task difficulty or prompt familiarity) rather than actual access to internal knowledge states. This can lead to an "illusion of knowing," where apparent metacognitive performance is inflated by biases rather than reflecting a model's understanding of its own knowledge. Some studies report a lack of metacognition in LLMs, while others suggest functional access to internal states, indicating an ongoing debate in the literature.

This paper addresses this gap by introducing a framework designed to rigorously evaluate and enhance LLM metacognition while systematically controlling for confounding factors prevalent in both human psychological studies and LLM-specific evaluations. By drawing directly from methodologies established in human metacognitive research, such as bias-controlled metrics and multi-stage questioning, this work aims to move beyond functional reliability to investigate and improve the LLM's intrinsic capacity for self-knowledge. It seeks to establish a more robust understanding of whether LLMs can genuinely assess what they know, rather than merely exhibiting behaviors that appear metacognitive due to heuristic exploitation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a framework for the rigorous evaluation and enhancement of metacognition, specifically self-knowledge, in Large Language Models (LLMs). This overarching goal is motivated by the inherent challenges in discerning genuine metacognitive ability from confounding factors that can artificially inflate apparent performance in both human and artificial intelligence contexts.

The detailed motivations and specific objectives are as follows:

- **Disentangling True Metacognition from Heuristics and Biases:** A core motivation stems from the observation that observed behaviors in both humans and LLMs, such as expressing confidence or refusing to answer, can reflect not only access to internal knowledge states but also the influence of response biases and heuristics. For instance, an LLM might report low confidence for seemingly difficult questions regardless of its actual knowledge, or its apparent metacognitive alignment could stem from recognizing familiar terms in a prompt (cue-familiarity heuristic). The paper aims to isolate true metacognitive sensitivity from these confounds, which are often overlooked in functional-reliability focused LLM studies.
- **Addressing the "Illusion of Knowing" (IoK):** In psychology, the IoK describes situations where subjective knowledge reports appear valid even when they do not reliably track actual knowledge. The researchers are motivated to design a framework that explicitly mitigates this by requiring consistent verification of knowledge across independent inference passes and different elicitation formats, mirroring psychological methodologies.
- **Controlling for LLM-Specific Evaluation Confounds:** Beyond traditional psychological biases, LLMs are susceptible to model-specific confounds. These include prompt-template bias (where performance is tied to specific training prompt structures), benchmark-specific shortcut heuristics (where models learn coarse regularities of a dataset rather than item-level discrimination), and language-surface bias (where learned alignment might rely on language-specific fluency or lexical priors). A key objective is to systematically identify and mitigate these factors through robust experimental design.
- **Developing a Robust Metacognitive Measurement Method:** To achieve unbiased evaluation, the study aims to establish a measurement method that isolates metacognitive functions from innate response tendencies. This involves adopting the d′type2 metric from signal detection theory, coupled with a dual-questioning protocol, to quantify the discrimination capacity of LLMs between correct and incorrect answers, independent of overall response bias.
- **Proposing an Effective Strategy for Metacognitive Enhancement:** The researchers recognize that optimizing for holistic behavioral patterns, such as the consistent alignment between direct answers and self-knowledge, poses a challenge for standard gradient-based learning methods which operate on individual examples. Therefore, an objective is to propose and evaluate an optimization technique, the Evolution Strategy for Metacognitive Alignment (ESMA), capable of improving this complex, non-differentiable objective.
- **Investigating the Neuromodulation of Metacognition:** A further objective is to explore the underlying mechanisms of metacognitive improvement by analyzing how model parameters are altered. The paper aims to conduct parameter analysis to determine whether behavioral gains are driven by a broad shift or by a sparse, influential subset of parameters, thereby offering insights into potential pathways for targeted metacognitive optimization and the existence of specialized subnetworks.

By addressing these motivations and objectives, the research seeks to advance the scientific understanding of metacognition in LLMs, moving beyond purely functional improvements to a more fundamental inquiry into their capacity for self-knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on a bias-controlled framework for both measuring and enhancing metacognition in Large Language Models (LLMs). It draws heavily from psychological research paradigms for human metacognition.

#### 3.1. Metacognitive Measurement Framework

The study establishes a framework to measure d′type2 in LLMs, adapted from human metacognitive assessment.

- **Task Setup: Dual-Questioning Protocol:** For each data point, the LLM addresses two distinct tasks in independent contexts:
 1. **Direct Questions:** To elicit factual answers (e.g., "Where's the capital of US?").
 2. **Meta Questions:** To self-evaluate knowledge (e.g., "Do you know the capital of US?").
 This isolation aims to prevent self-confirmation biases where the meta answer might be influenced by the direct answer, or vice-versa.
- **Primary Metric: d′type2:** This is a standardized metric from signal detection theory that quantifies metacognitive ability. It measures the capacity to discriminate between accurate and inaccurate judgments by calculating the statistical separation between the internal confidence distributions for correct and incorrect decisions. The formula is d′type2 = Φ−1(Hit Rate) − Φ−1(False Alarm Rate), where Hit Rate is P(Meta-Yes|Correct) and False Alarm Rate is P(Meta-Yes|Incorrect). A higher d′type2 value indicates that the LLM's self-reported "Yes" or "No" reliably predicts the correctness of its factual answer, controlling for overall response tendencies.
- **Complementary Metrics:** Several intuitive metrics are also reported:
 - **Raw Alignment:** The average agreement between the meta answer and the correctness of the direct outcome (susceptible to bias).
 - **Accuracy:** Ratio of correct answers for direct questions.
 - **Yes Ratio:** Frequency of positive responses to meta questions.
 - **Yes Failure Ratio (YFR):** Proportion of times the model claims to know but is incorrect.
 - **No Failure Ratio (NFR):** Proportion of times the model claims ignorance but is correct.
- **Continuous Confidence-based Metacognition:** To provide a continuous check on metacognitive discrimination, Type 2 AUROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve) is utilized. Confidence (D) is calculated as the normalized probability of a positive self-assessment from the logits of "Yes" and "No" tokens. The ROC curve plots True Positive Rate (TPR) against False Positive Rate (FPR) as a confidence threshold is varied, with AUC summarizing the model's ranking ability to assign higher confidence to correct answers.

#### 3.2. Evolution Strategy for Metacognitive Alignment (ESMA)

To enhance metacognition, the Evolution Strategy (ES) is employed due to its ability to optimize non-differentiable objectives and holistic behavioral patterns across independent inference passes, a limitation for standard gradient-based methods.

- **Process (Algorithm 1):**
 1. **Initialization:** Start with an initial LLM (parent model).
 2. **Perturbation:** Generate a population of `N` model variants by adding Gaussian noise (σϵᵢ) to the parent model's parameters (θₜ).
 3. **Evaluation:** Each perturbed variant (θᵢ) is evaluated using the dual-questioning protocol.
 4. **Reward Calculation:** A "Joint Reward" (Fᵢ) is calculated for each variant based on the alignment between its direct answer correctness and its meta answer.
 5. **Parameter Update:** The next-generation LLM (θₜ₊₁) is produced by a weighted average of the perturbations, where weights are proportional to the z-standardized fitness (rewards) of the variants, controlled by a learning rate (α). This effectively shifts parameters towards configurations that yield higher rewards.
 6. **Iteration:** This process iterates for multiple generations.
- **Reward Design:** The reward function `R(C, A)` combines direct correctness (`C`) and metacognitive alignment (`A`) to prevent reward hacking.
 - `R(C=1, A=1)` (Correct & Yes) = 2
 - `R(C=1, A=0)` (Correct & No) = 1
 - `R(C=0, A=1)` (Incorrect & No) = 1
 - `R(C=0, A=0)` (Incorrect & Yes) = 0
 This design rewards both correctness and aligned self-assessment, while penalizing incorrect self-assessment. (Correct & No) and (Incorrect & No) receive the same reward to avoid incentivizing incorrectness.

#### 3.3. Bias Control Mechanisms and Experimental Design

The study implements several controls to ensure the observed improvements are robust and reflect genuine metacognitive ability.

- **IDK Unified Prompt Experiment:** To rule out prompt-template bias and the illusion of knowing, models are tested with a single prompt instructing them to respond "I don't know" if uncertain. Performance is assessed via IDK Accuracy, IDK Alignment, and "All Alignment" (consistency across dual-question and IDK formats).
- **Metacognition on New Information (FictionalQA):** To control for the cue-familiarity heuristic (reliance on pre-existing semantic familiarity), LLMs are fine-tuned on half of a novel, entirely fictional QA dataset (FictionalQA) via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), followed by ESMA on the TriviaQA training set. Metacognitive performance is then evaluated on the remaining FictionalQA data.
- **External Datasets:** To control for benchmark-specific shortcut heuristics, ESMA-trained models are evaluated on FreebaseQA, NQ Open, and WebQuestions without further training.
- **Cross-lingual Evaluation:** To control for language-surface bias, evaluations are conducted on Chinese, Korean, and Spanish questions from the MKQA benchmark, using models trained only on English TriviaQA.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** ESMA is compared against Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to demonstrate its advantage over other training paradigms.

#### 3.4. Parameter Optimization Analysis

- **Procedure:** The difference in weights between the final ESMA-tuned model and the original model (ΔW) is calculated. Scalar values within ΔW are ranked by L1 magnitude. Partially patched models are constructed by adding only the top `p%` of these influential weight changes to the original model. Metacognitive metrics (d′type2 and raw alignment) are then measured for varying `p`.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Open-source models (Qwen2.5, Llama3.2, Gemma3 Instruct across various sizes) are utilized, with proprietary models (OpenAI GPT 5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3 Flash) included for comparison. TriviaQA is chosen as the primary training and benchmark dataset due to its fact-based questions, isolating knowledge retrieval from complex reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding LLM metacognition and the efficacy of the proposed ESMA framework and its associated bias controls.

#### 4.1. Metacognitive Ability (Table 1)

- **Proprietary Models:** Proprietary models exhibited high raw alignment (e.g., Gemini 3 Flash at 90.71%), often correlated with very high direct-question accuracy and strong positive response tendencies (e.g., Gemini 3 Flash Yes Ratio of 96.34%). However, their d′type2 scores varied, with Gemini 3 Flash having the lowest d′type2 (0.68) despite high raw alignment, indicating that its apparent alignment was partly driven by response bias rather than genuine item-level discrimination. This validated d′type2 as a bias-controlled metric.
- **Open-Source Baselines:** Untuned open-source LLMs showed comparatively weak baseline metacognitive performance, characterized by low d′type2 scores, suggesting their meta-answers provided limited information about the correctness of their direct answers.
- **ESMA's Impact:** Application of ESMA consistently and substantially increased d′type2 across all open-source models, elevating scores towards 1.0 (moderate sensitivity). For instance, Qwen2.5 3B ESMA achieved the highest d′type2 of 1.02 among all evaluated models, including proprietary ones, despite having lower direct-question accuracy. This indicates ESMA enhances metacognitive discrimination beyond a simple shift in response strategy. A correlation between model size and metacognitive ability was observed within the Qwen series, with ESMA consistently boosting performance beyond these natural baselines across all sizes.

#### 4.2. Confidence-based Metacognition (Figure 2, Figure 3)

- **Type 2 AUROC:** Original models exhibited AUC values above the random baseline (0.5), indicating some weak, continuous confidence-based information about answer correctness. After ESMA, ROC curves consistently shifted toward the upper-left, and AUC values increased significantly across all model scales (e.g., Qwen2.5 1.5B from 0.672 to 0.751; 3B from 0.601 to 0.755; 7B from 0.676 to 0.747), all converging to approximately 0.75. This demonstrated stronger ranking of correct answers by confidence.
- **Confidence Distribution:** Visualization of confidence distributions showed substantial overlap between correct and incorrect instances in original models, with scores concentrated in an ambiguous middle region. ESMA polarized these distributions, shifting confidence for correct answers towards 1.0 and for incorrect answers towards 0.0, significantly reducing overlap. This suggested a fundamental reshaping of the continuous confidence signal, not just binary decision boundary changes.

#### 4.3. Metacognitive Alignment with Unified Prompt (Table 2)

- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** In the IDK unified prompt setting (never seen during training), ESMA-trained models showed significant improvements in IDK Alignment across all scales (e.g., Qwen2.5 3B increased from 59.88% to 78.07%). This zero-shot improvement indicated that ESMA's gains were not due to prompt-template bias or overfitting to the dual-question training format.
- **Mitigating Illusion of Knowing:** The "All Alignment" metric, requiring consistency across dual-question and IDK formats, also improved substantially and consistently (e.g., Qwen2.5 3B from 32.75% to 59.71%), particularly in larger variants. This demonstrated enhanced cross-format consistency, mitigating concerns about an "illusion of knowing."

#### 4.4. Metacognition on New Information (Table 3)

- **FictionalQA:** After SFT on FictionalQA direct QA pairs and ESMA training on TriviaQA, the Qwen2.5 1.5B model achieved a d′type2 of 0.65 on FictionalQA. This significant increase, despite no prior exposure to meta-questions about the fictional content, suggested that ESMA establishes a functional link between the model’s self-reports and newly acquired knowledge, independent of the cue-familiarity heuristic.

#### 4.5. Sparse Contribution of Evolutionary Weight Updates (Figure 4, Figure 5, Figure 6)

- **Non-Linear Improvement:** A parameter patching analysis revealed a highly non-linear relationship between the volume of weight changes and metacognitive improvement.
- **Concentrated Gains:** Applying only the top 10% of weight changes (by L1 magnitude) yielded a dramatic increase in d′type2 (from 0.20 to 0.63 for Qwen2.5 1.5B), capturing approximately 80% of the total improvement. Performance plateaued thereafter, with the remaining 50% of updates contributing almost no additional metacognitive ability. Within this top tier, most gains occurred with the top 5% of updates.
- **Spatially Distributed:** Exploratory analysis indicated that these highly influential weights were not localized to specific layers or component types but were distributed relatively evenly across the model architecture.

#### 4.6. Comparison with Other Training Methods (Table 5, Table 6, Appendix C.2)

- SFT, PPO, and GRPO demonstrated some metacognitive improvements over the original model, but ESMA achieved statistically significantly higher d′type2 scores across all comparisons.
- In the zero-shot IDK unified prompt setting, ESMA substantially outperformed all baselines in IDK Alignment and All Alignment, suggesting that standard RL methods primarily induce superficial behavioral changes specific to the training format, while ESMA cultivates a more inherent, format-independent metacognitive capability.

#### 4.7. External and Cross-Lingual Evaluation (Table 7, Table 8)

- **External Datasets:** ESMA-trained models (on TriviaQA) showed consistent improvements in d′type2 across unseen external datasets (FreebaseQA, NQ Open, WebQuestions), indicating robustness against benchmark-specific shortcut heuristics.
- **Cross-Lingual Transfer:** ESMA models also demonstrated improved d′type2 across non-English languages (Chinese, Korean, Spanish) in the MKQA benchmark, despite being trained solely on English TriviaQA. This suggested that the learned metacognitive alignment was not tied to English-specific prompt priors or surface-form patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the understanding and development of Large Language Models (LLMs) by addressing the fundamental question of whether these models can genuinely know what they know, rather than merely exhibiting behaviors that mimic metacognition.

**Scientific Significance:**
- **Rigorous Metacognitive Measurement:** The work establishes a bias-controlled framework for measuring LLM metacognition, directly translating methodologies from human psychological research (e.g., d′type2, dual-questioning, controls for illusion of knowing and cue-familiarity heuristics). This provides a more robust and scientifically grounded approach to assessing LLM self-knowledge, moving beyond superficial metrics.
- **Advancement in LLM Capabilities:** It demonstrates that true metacognitive ability, specifically the capacity for self-knowledge, can be robustly enhanced in LLMs through the proposed Evolution Strategy for Metacognitive Alignment (ESMA). This shows that LLMs are capable of developing internal mechanisms to track their own knowledge states, rather than relying solely on external cues or shortcut heuristics.
- **Distinctive Role of Evolution Strategies:** The study highlights the unique advantage of Evolution Strategies (ES) for optimizing high-level, holistic cognitive traits that are non-differentiable or require consistent behavioral patterns across diverse contexts. This underscores ES as a powerful alternative to traditional gradient-based methods for complex AI training objectives.
- **Insights into Model Architecture:** The discovery that metacognitive improvements are driven by a sparse subset of weight updates (e.g., top 10%) suggests the potential existence of a specialized subnetwork fundamentally linked to metacognitive functions within LLMs. This finding opens new avenues for research into the interpretability and architectural underpinnings of complex cognitive abilities in AI.

**Potential Practical Impact:**
- **Enhanced LLM Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By enabling LLMs to better assess their own knowledge and express appropriate confidence or uncertainty, the work contributes to reducing issues like hallucinations and inappropriate refusals. This improved self-awareness can lead to more reliable and trustworthy AI systems, particularly in high-stakes applications where accuracy and honesty about knowledge limitations are critical (e.g., medical, legal, financial domains).
- **Improved User Interaction:** Models that can accurately indicate when they "don't know" can provide more helpful and transparent interactions with users, preventing misdirection and fostering more productive engagement.
- **Efficient Model Optimization:** The identification of sparse, influential parameters for metacognition suggests possibilities for more targeted and efficient fine-tuning strategies. Instead of updating all parameters, future work could focus on this critical subset, potentially reducing computational costs and training time for specific cognitive enhancements.
- **Foundation for Advanced AI Cognition:** Establishing a clearer understanding of self-knowledge in LLMs is a foundational step toward developing more sophisticated AI systems with higher-order cognitive capabilities, including more robust reasoning, planning, and self-correction.

**Broader Impacts and Ethical Considerations:**
The paper acknowledges that while enhancing metacognitive abilities can lead to a reduction in misinformation and increased consistency for safe AI deployment, a potential risk exists. In malicious contexts, an LLM with a high awareness of its own knowledge state could be exploited to orchestrate more sophisticated forms of deception or information control. Responsible development of metacognitive AI would require careful consideration of these dual-use risks.

**Future Work:**
The study also outlines several critical directions for future research. These include investigating metacognition for more complex reasoning tasks beyond factual retrieval, developing methods to measure higher-order and multi-dimensional metacognition (e.g., distinguishing specific attributes from broader associations), further isolating and understanding the hypothesized "specialized subnetwork" for metacognitive functions, and exploring the broader application of Evolution Strategies to optimize other complex, holistic cognitive traits or strategic reasoning in AI.
