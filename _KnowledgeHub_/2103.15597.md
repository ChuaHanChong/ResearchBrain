# RobustNet: Improving Domain Generalization in Urban-Scene Segmentation via Instance Selective Whitening

> [!summary] Summary
> RobustNet introduces an Instance Selective Whitening (ISW) loss that enhances domain generalization for urban-scene semantic segmentation. This method improves model robustness to unseen real-world conditions by selectively suppressing style-sensitive components within feature covariance matrices, while preserving content-related information, all without significant additional computational cost.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks experience performance degradation (domain shift) when applied to data from environments different from their training data, posing a critical challenge for safety-sensitive applications like autonomous driving.
- Traditional Domain Adaptation (DA) methods require access to target domain samples during training, limiting their applicability when target domains are truly unknown, while multi-source Domain Generalization (DG) methods can be costly due to data collection and labeling.
- Non-selective application of whitening transformations can inadvertently remove domain-invariant content alongside domain-specific style, potentially diminishing the network's discriminative power and distorting object boundaries.

## Method

- **RobustNet** proposes an ==Instance Selective Whitening (ISW)== loss that identifies and selectively suppresses only the 'style-sensitive' components of ==feature covariance matrices== within a neural network.
- The method uses ==photometric augmentations== to simulate domain shifts and compute a variance matrix, allowing ==K-means clustering== to distinguish between style-sensitive (high variance) and content-related (low variance) covariance elements.
- A binary mask generated from this clustering is applied to the covariance matrix, forcing only the style-sensitive correlations towards zero while preserving content information, integrated into ==instance normalization== layers of a segmentation network.

## Results

- RobustNet with ISW loss consistently achieved substantial mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) gains (e.g., up to **12.15%** on Mapillary from GTAV training) on unseen real-world urban-scene datasets compared to baseline models and other advanced domain generalization methods.
- The method demonstrated broad applicability across various backbone architectures (ResNet, ShuffleNetV2, MobileNetV2) and maintained its performance advantages even when trained on multiple source domains, indicating robust generalizability.
- ISW introduces negligible additional computational cost, maintaining comparable numbers of parameters, GFLOPS, and inference time to baseline models, making it a practical solution for real-time deployment in applications like autonomous driving.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature covariance matrices encode both domain-specific style information (e.g., illumination, texture) and domain-invariant content (e.g., scene structure), and these components exhibit differential sensitivity to photometric transformations.
> - Selectively targeting and removing only the style-sensitive feature correlations is crucial for robust domain generalization, as it allows the network to disregard superficial domain variations without losing essential discriminative features for the task.
> - The proposed ISW mechanism offers an efficient and practical way to disentangle and suppress domain-specific style from feature representations using higher-order statistics, improving generalization from a single source domain without substantial computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep neural networks (DNNs) frequently experience performance degradation when applied to data from real-world environments that differ from their training data, a phenomenon known as domain shift. This issue is particularly critical for safety-sensitive applications such as autonomous driving, where models trained on controlled datasets must perform reliably under diverse and unpredictable conditions (e.g., varying illumination, adverse weather, new geographic locations).

The broader research landscape addresses domain shift primarily through two paradigms: Domain Adaptation (DA) and Domain Generalization (DG). Domain Adaptation methods aim to mitigate performance drops by aligning the data distributions between a source domain (where labeled training data is abundant) and a specific target domain (where labeled data is scarce). A significant limitation of DA is its requirement for access to samples from the target domain during training, which restricts its applicability when the target domain is unknown or comprises the entire spectrum of real-world variability.

Domain Generalization (DG) seeks to overcome this limitation by developing models that are robust to arbitrary, *unseen* target domains. Traditional DG approaches often rely on learning shared representations across multiple source domains, employing techniques such as meta-learning, adversarial training, autoencoders, metric learning, or data augmentation. However, these multi-domain methods can be costly due to the labor-intensive process of collecting and labeling diverse datasets, and their performance may depend on the quantity and diversity of the source datasets available.

A recent development in DG, exemplified by IBN-Net, has shown that incorporating instance normalization layers can enhance generalization without requiring multiple source domains. While instance normalization standardizes features, it primarily addresses first-order statistics (mean and variance) and does not inherently consider correlations between feature channels. Previous research in areas like style transfer and image-to-image translation has indicated that higher-order statistics, specifically feature covariance, encode domain-specific style information such as texture and color. Whitening transformation, which aims to remove feature correlation, has been explored to eliminate such style information. However, directly applying a full whitening transformation can inadvertently remove domain-invariant content alongside domain-specific style, potentially diminishing the discriminative power of features and distorting object boundaries.

This paper addresses the gap by focusing on urban-scene segmentation, a task less explored by DG methods compared to image classification. It builds upon the idea of leveraging normalization layers but aims to refine the whitening process. The proposed method specifically targets the selective removal of only the *style-sensitive* components of feature covariance, rather than a blanket whitening, thus aiming to improve domain generalization while preserving crucial content information. This approach is positioned as a novel application of feature whitening principles to the domain generalization problem, particularly for complex real-world segmentation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capability of deep neural networks for urban-scene semantic segmentation when deployed in unseen target domains. This objective is driven by the requirements of safety-critical applications, such as autonomous driving, where models trained on a limited set of conditions must perform robustly across a wide array of unpredictable real-world scenarios, including variations in illumination, adverse weather, and novel scene structures.

The motivation for the proposed approach stems from several observations and limitations of existing methods:
1. **Challenge of Domain Shift:** Real-world data inherently presents significant domain shifts (e.g., images taken at night, during rain, or in unfamiliar locations) that are impractical to fully model or collect within a single training dataset. DNNs typically struggle when encountering such unseen variations.
2. **Limitations of Domain Adaptation (DA):** While DA mitigates domain shift, it necessitates access to samples from the target domain during training. This requirement is often not met in practical DG settings where the target domain is arbitrary and unknown at training time.
3. **Limitations of Existing Domain Generalization (DG) Methods:** Many DG methods rely on multiple source domains, incurring high costs for data collection and labeling, and their performance may scale with the number of available source datasets. Simpler, single-source DG approaches like those based on instance normalization have shown promise but may not fully exploit higher-order feature statistics.
4. **Role of Feature Covariance in Style:** Prior work has established that feature covariance matrices encode domain-specific style information, such as texture and color. This suggests that manipulating these statistics could be a mechanism to address domain shift.
5. **Drawbacks of Non-Selective Whitening:** A naive application of whitening transformation to remove all feature correlations risks diminishing the discriminative power of the network by removing domain-invariant content alongside domain-specific style. This implies that a more selective approach is necessary.
6. **Empirical Observation of Covariance Sensitivity:** The authors observe that certain feature covariance elements are highly sensitive to photometric transformations (e.g., color changes, blurring), indicating they primarily capture style. Other covariance elements are observed to be robust to such changes but sensitive to content variations (e.g., scene structure). This differential sensitivity forms the core motivation for developing a *selective* whitening mechanism.

Therefore, the key objective is to develop a novel instance selective whitening loss that can:
- Disentangle domain-specific style and domain-invariant content within the higher-order statistics (feature covariance) of deep feature representations.
- Selectively suppress only the style information that contributes to domain shift, while preserving content-related information essential for the task.
- Achieve improved domain generalization for urban-scene semantic segmentation without incurring significant additional computational cost or requiring multiple source domains during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, RobustNet, introduces an Instance Selective Whitening (ISW) loss designed to improve domain generalization in urban-scene segmentation by selectively removing style-related information from feature representations. The approach builds upon the concept of feature whitening but refines it to address the limitations of non-selective application.

The methodology can be broken down into the following stages:

#### 3.1. Instance Whitening (IW) Loss

- **Addressing Whitening Limitations:** Traditional whitening transformations (WT) require eigenvalue decomposition, which is computationally expensive and hinders back-propagation. Furthermore, simultaneously forcing diagonal elements of the covariance matrix to one and off-diagonal elements to zero can lead to optimization conflicts and potential loss of discriminative power.
- **Standardization:** To resolve the optimization conflict, the intermediate feature map $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times HW}$ is first standardized using instance normalization. This ensures that the scale of each channel is unit, meaning diagonal elements of the subsequent covariance matrix are already approximately one.
- **Covariance Calculation:** After instance standardization, a covariance matrix $\Sigma_s \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times C}$ is calculated from the standardized feature map $X_s$.
- **IW Loss Formulation:** The Instance Whitening (IW) loss, $L_{IW}$, is defined as the mean absolute error of the strict upper triangular part of $\Sigma_s$. This focuses on decorrelating features by driving off-diagonal elements towards zero, as diagonal elements are already normalized.

#### 3.2. Margin-based Relaxation of Whitening Loss (IRW)

- **Preserving Discriminative Power:** The IW loss, by forcing all off-diagonal covariance elements to zero, might eliminate feature correlations that are beneficial for discriminative power within the network.
- **IRW Loss Formulation:** To address this, an Instance-Relaxed Whitening (IRW) loss, $L_{IRW}$, is introduced. This loss allows the total covariance (strict upper triangular part) to exist within a specified margin $\delta$ rather than being strictly zero. It is formulated as $max(E[||\Sigma_s M||_1] - \delta, 0)$, where $M$ is the strict upper triangular mask. This relaxation aims to retain essential feature correlations.

#### 3.3. Separating Covariance Elements (Instance Selective Whitening - ISW)

The core innovation is to selectively suppress only the style-encoded covariances. This is achieved through a multi-step process:

1. **Initial Training for Covariance Statistics:** The network is initially trained for a few epochs (empirically set to 5) with only instance standardization layers and the task loss, *without* any whitening loss. This phase allows the network to learn feature representations and their covariances under normal training conditions.
2. **Photometric Augmentation for Domain Shift Simulation:** To identify style-sensitive covariances, photometric augmentations (color jittering and Gaussian blur) are applied to training images. These transformations simulate domain shifts related to appearance (style) without altering content.
3. **Variance Matrix Computation:** For each image $x_i$, its original covariance matrix $\Sigma_s(x_i)$ and its photometrically transformed counterpart $\Sigma_s(\tau(x_i))$ are extracted from intermediate feature maps. A variance matrix $V \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times C}$ is then computed. Each element $V_{i,j}$ represents the variance of the corresponding covariance element (across the dataset) when subjected to photometric transformations. High values in $V$ indicate that the corresponding covariance element is sensitive to style changes.
4. **Clustering for Style-Content Separation:**
 - K-means clustering is applied to the strict upper triangular elements of the variance matrix $V$. The elements are grouped into $k$ clusters based on their variance magnitude.
 - These clusters are then divided into two groups: $G_{low}$ (low variance values, assumed to encode domain-invariant content) and $G_{high}$ (high variance values, assumed to encode domain-specific style). Empirically, $k=3$ and $m=1$ (meaning the cluster with the lowest variance is considered content-related) are chosen.
5. **Selective Mask Generation:** A binary mask matrix $\tilde{M} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times C}$ is created. Elements in $\tilde{M}$ are set to 1 if their corresponding variance $V_{i,j}$ belongs to $G_{high}$ (style-sensitive), and 0 otherwise.
6. **ISW Loss Formulation:** The Instance Selective Whitening (ISW) loss, $L_{ISW}$, is then defined as the mean absolute error of the covariance matrix $\Sigma_s$ multiplied element-wise by the mask $\tilde{M}$: $L_{ISW} = E[||\Sigma_s \tilde{M}||_1]$. This ensures that only the style-sensitive covariance elements are pushed towards zero.

#### 3.4. Network Architecture Integration

- **Backbone:** The method is applied to DeepLabV3+ with various backbones (ResNet, ShuffleNetV2, MobileNetV2).
- **Layer Placement:** Following observations from IBN-Net that early layers primarily encode style, the proposed ISW loss is applied to instance normalization layers within the input convolutional layer and the first two ResNet stages (conv1, conv2_x, conv3_x). An affine transformation is not used, as equivalent functionality can be achieved by subsequent convolution layers.
- **Total Loss:** The overall training loss, $L_{total}$, combines the primary task loss (e.g., per-pixel cross-entropy for semantic segmentation) and the ISW loss, weighted by a hyper-parameter $\lambda$: $L_{total} = L_{task} + \lambda (\frac{1}{L} \sum_{i=1}^{L} L_{i}^{ISW})$, where $L$ is the number of layers with ISW.

This methodology aims to achieve domain generalization by implicitly encouraging the network to learn a selective whitening transformation that disentangles and removes only the domain-specific style components from its feature representations, thereby increasing robustness to unseen domains without significant overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments primarily evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed Instance Selective Whitening (ISW) loss in improving domain generalization for urban-scene semantic segmentation. The evaluation uses mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on various real-world and synthetic datasets.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

1. **Effectiveness of Instance Selective Whitening Loss:**
 - **Ablation Studies (Tables 1 & 2):** When models are trained on GTAV or Cityscapes, ISW consistently outperforms a baseline model, as well as models using Instance Whitening (IW), Instance-Relaxed Whitening (IRW), and other normalization techniques like Switchable Whitening (SW), IBN-Net, and IterNorm on unseen real-world datasets (Cityscapes, BDD-100K, Mapillary).
 - **Performance on Unseen Domains:** For example, when trained on GTAV, ISW achieved 36.58% mIoU on Cityscapes, 35.20% on BDD-100K, and 40.33% on Mapillary, significantly higher than the baseline (28.95%, 25.14%, 28.18% respectively) and also surpassing other advanced normalization methods. Similar trends are observed when training on Cityscapes.
 - **Source Domain Performance:** The method may exhibit a slight decrease in performance on the source domain compared to some baselines (e.g., 72.10% on GTAV for ISW vs. 73.45% for baseline, and 76.41% on Cityscapes for ISW vs. 77.51% for baseline). This trade-off is interpreted as prioritizing generalizability to unseen domains over maximal performance on the training distribution.

2. **Wide Applicability Across Backbone Networks (Table 3):**
 - The benefits of ISW are not limited to a specific backbone. When applied to ShuffleNetV2 and MobileNetV2 architectures trained on GTAV, ISW consistently yielded higher mIoU scores on unseen real-world datasets (Cityscapes, BDD-100K, Mapillary) compared to both baseline and IBN-Net models, demonstrating its broad applicability.

3. **Performance with Multiple Source Domains (Table 4):**
 - Even when models are trained on aggregated multiple synthetic domains (GTAV + SYNTHIA), the ISW method maintains its advantage. It achieved 37.69% mIoU on Cityscapes, 34.09% on BDD-100K, and 38.49% on Mapillary, surpassing IBN-Net and baseline models. This indicates that ISW effectively learns domain-invariant features even in multi-source settings.

4. **Comparison with Other DG and DA Methods (Table 5 & Fig. 9):**
 - Comparing against other published DG methods like IBN-Net and DRPC (Domain Randomization and Pyramid Consistency), ISW showed the largest performance increase on average, particularly on diverse datasets like BDD-100K and Mapillary. For instance, trained on GTAV, ISW showed a 7.63% mIoU gain on Cityscapes, 10.06% on BDD-100K, and 12.15% on Mapillary over its respective baseline.
 - While Domain Adaptation (DA) methods generally achieve higher absolute performance (due to accessing target domain data), ISW demonstrates comparable mIoU gains relative to their baselines, highlighting its efficacy within the stricter DG setting.

5. **Computational Cost Analysis (Table 6):**
 - The proposed ISW method introduces negligible additional computational cost. It shows comparable numbers of parameters (45.081M), GFLOPS (554.31), and inference time (10.43 ms) to the baseline and IBN-Net models. In contrast, IterNorm, which uses iterative whitening, incurs significantly higher inference time (40.31 ms), making ISW more practical for real-world deployment.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

1. **Segmentation Results (Figures 1, 8, 13, 14, 15):**
 - Visual examples demonstrate that models equipped with ISW produce more robust and accurate semantic segmentation maps on unseen domains characterized by low-illumination, adverse weather (rain, snow, fog), and unexpected scene structures.
 - The baseline model frequently exhibits significant failures or incorrect predictions under these challenging conditions (e.g., misclassifying snowy sidewalks as buildings, failing to detect roads at night, or struggling with foggy scenes). In contrast, the ISW-enhanced models maintain reasonable predictions for key urban-scene categories. On seen domains (Cityscapes), ISW models perform comparably to the baseline.

2. **Comparison of Covariance Matrices (Figure 6):**
 - Visualizations of covariance matrices extracted from intermediate feature maps of IBN-Net and ISW models show that ISW selectively whitens most covariance elements. While IBN-Net's covariance matrices exhibit diverse patterns, ISW's matrices appear sparser, indicating that many correlations have been suppressed. A small number of covariance elements remain large, supporting the claim of *selective* suppression rather than uniform whitening.

3. **Reconstructed Images with Whitened Features (Figure 7):**
 - Images reconstructed from ISW-whitened feature maps, using a newly trained decoder, reveal that the essential content information (scene structure, objects) is largely preserved. However, domain-specific style attributes like illumination, color, and overall appearance are significantly suppressed or altered. This empirically supports the hypothesis that ISW effectively disentangles and removes style information while retaining content relevant for segmentation.

4. **Covariance Effects in Images (Figure 16):**
 - By sorting images based on the magnitude of the most sensitive and insensitive covariance elements to photometric transformations, the authors observed distinct trends. Images sorted by the *most sensitive* covariance show a progression from high to low illumination, suggesting these covariances encode illumination (a style attribute). Images sorted by the *most insensitive* covariance show a progression from complex to simple scene structures, suggesting these covariances capture scene complexity (a content attribute). This reinforces the premise that feature covariances contain distinct style and content information, which can be selectively targeted.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the Instance Selective Whitening loss effectively improves domain generalization performance in urban-scene semantic segmentation. It achieves this by selectively addressing style-related feature covariances, demonstrates wide applicability, incurs negligible computational cost, and produces qualitatively more robust segmentation results in diverse and challenging unseen environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "RobustNet: Improving Domain Generalization in Urban-Scene Segmentation via Instance Selective Whitening" holds significance for both theoretical understanding and practical applications of deep learning.

1. **Addressing a Critical Real-World Problem:** The primary impact lies in tackling the domain generalization problem, which is fundamental for deploying deep neural networks in safety-critical real-world applications such as autonomous driving. Autonomous vehicles must operate reliably under myriad unforeseen conditions, where models trained on limited data often fail due to domain shift. This research contributes directly to improving the robustness of such systems, making them safer and more dependable.

2. **Novel Approach to Disentanglement and Whitening:** The paper introduces a novel method for disentangling domain-specific style and domain-invariant content from feature representations by leveraging higher-order statistics (feature covariance). Critically, it proposes a *selective* whitening strategy based on the sensitivity of covariance elements to photometric transformations. This moves beyond generic whitening or simple instance normalization by precisely targeting and suppressing only the style components that cause domain shift, a distinct contribution to the field of domain generalization.

3. **Efficiency and Practicality:** A significant advantage of RobustNet is its negligible computational overhead during inference. Unlike some iterative whitening methods, the proposed loss function is applied during training and does not add substantial parameters or computational load at inference time. This makes the method highly practical for real-time systems, which are common in applications like autonomous driving where computational resources might be constrained.

4. **Reduced Data Dependence:** The method demonstrates strong domain generalization performance without explicitly requiring multiple source domains for training. This addresses a major bottleneck in many DG approaches, which often rely on costly and labor-intensive multi-domain datasets. By achieving robustness from a single source domain, the approach offers a more cost-effective and scalable solution.

5. **Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across various backbone architectures (ResNet, ShuffleNetV2, MobileNetV2) suggests that the proposed instance selective whitening loss is a generalizable technique that can be readily integrated into diverse existing segmentation models, enhancing their generalization capabilities.

6. **Insights into Feature Representation:** The qualitative analyses, particularly the visualization of covariance matrices and reconstructed images, provide insights into how deep networks encode style and content. The ability to reconstruct content-preserving but style-altered images from whitened features supports the hypothesis of disentanglement. Furthermore, the correlation between covariance sensitivity and image characteristics (illumination vs. scene complexity) deepens the understanding of feature statistics.

7. **Stimulating Future Research:** The work highlights the importance of domain generalization in practical urban-scene segmentation, an area less explored compared to image classification. It also opens avenues for future research, such as exploring a wider range of photometric augmentation techniques, further investigating the role of affine parameters in normalization layers after selective whitening, and potentially extending the selective covariance manipulation idea to enhance domain adaptation methods.

In conclusion, RobustNet offers a principled, efficient, and effective solution for improving domain generalization in semantic segmentation, particularly relevant for critical real-world applications. Its technical novelty in selective feature whitening and its practical advantages position it as a significant contribution to making deep learning models more robust and reliable in diverse, unseen environments.
