# WorldScore: A Unified Evaluation Benchmark for World Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University introduced WorldScore, the first unified benchmark for evaluating diverse world generation models, including those for 3D, 4D, text-to-video, and image-to-video. This benchmark standardizes evaluation through next-scene generation tasks and a multi-faceted metric system that assesses controllability, quality, and dynamics, revealing 3D models excel in static consistency while video models struggle with camera control and long-sequence coherence.

## Problem

- The absence of a unified and comprehensive evaluation framework for "world generation" that goes beyond single-scene assessments to evaluate complex, multi-scene environments.
- Existing benchmarks are fragmented, focusing on isolated visual generation aspects like single-scene video quality, without explicit spatial layout control or multi-scene coherence evaluation.
- Incompatibility among current evaluation methodologies, preventing fair comparison across diverse generative models (e.g., 3D, 4D, T2V, I2V) due to varied input and output requirements.

## Method

- Introduces **WorldScore**, the first unified benchmark for world generation, which decomposes the complex task into standardized "next-scene generation tasks" guided by image, text, and camera trajectory inputs.
- Curates a comprehensive dataset of 3,000 test examples covering static and dynamic worlds, indoor and outdoor environments, and both photorealistic and stylized visual domains.
- Develops a multi-faceted evaluation metric system, "**WorldScore**," aggregating 10 individual metrics across three critical aspects: controllability, quality, and dynamics.

## Results

- 3D scene generation models like WonderWorld (72.69) and LucidDreamer (70.40) achieved the highest WorldScore-Static, significantly outperforming video models in generating geometrically consistent static worlds.
- Video generation models exhibited low camera controllability scores (e.g., CogVideoX-T2V at 40.22), substantially lower than any 3D/4D model, indicating difficulty in following explicit camera trajectories.
- Some open-source video models, such as CogVideoX-I2V, achieved overall WorldScore-Static (62.15) and WorldScore-Dynamic (59.12) performances comparable to leading commercial closed-source models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D scene generation models demonstrate superior performance in static world consistency, camera controllability, and geometric fidelity compared to video models, but generally lack dynamic generation capabilities.
> - Video generation models face substantial challenges in camera controllability and maintaining coherence over long sequences, especially in outdoor scenes, limiting their spatial guidance adherence.
> - Dynamic world generation presents a trade-off between motion smoothness and magnitude, where models capable of larger motions often exhibit lower temporal stability, and struggle with accurate motion placement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual generation has experienced explosive growth, with rapid advancements in models capable of creating realistic images, videos, and 3D/4D scenes. Breakthroughs like OpenAI's Sora have demonstrated the potential for "world simulation," sparking immense interest in generating complex, dynamic environments. However, as the capabilities of these models expand, a critical bottleneck has emerged: the lack of a unified and comprehensive evaluation framework for "world generation"—the creation of large-scale, diverse worlds with seamlessly integrated, multiple varied scenes and detailed spatial layout controls.

Existing benchmarks primarily focus on isolated aspects of visual generation. For instance, many video generation benchmarks, such as VBench and WorldModelBench, concentrate on evaluating single-scene video quality based on text prompts, often without explicit spatial layout control or the ability to assess multi-scene coherence. These benchmarks are also often incompatible with 3D and 4D scene generation methods, which typically require camera specifications or reference images as input. This fragmentation prevents a holistic comparison of diverse generative approaches (e.g., 3D, 4D, text-to-video (T2V), image-to-video (I2V) models) and hinders the systematic identification of their strengths and weaknesses in creating coherent, navigable worlds.

WorldScore directly addresses this critical gap by introducing the first unified benchmark for world generation. It moves beyond single-scene evaluation to assess the generation of *sequences of interconnected scenes* with explicit *spatial layout specifications*. By decomposing world generation into "next-scene generation tasks" with standardized inputs (image, text, camera trajectory), WorldScore enables a fair and direct comparison across a wide spectrum of generative models. This innovation provides a common ground for evaluating performance on key attributes like controllability, quality, and dynamics, which are essential for creating functional and immersive virtual worlds for applications in entertainment, education, simulation, and embodied AI. In essence, WorldScore provides the much-needed infrastructure to objectively measure progress towards sophisticated, comprehensive world generation, thereby shaping the future direction of research in this rapidly evolving domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind WorldScore stems from the burgeoning interest in "world generation"—the creation of expansive, diverse virtual environments comprising multiple interconnected scenes. While recent advances in generative AI have demonstrated impressive capabilities in producing high-quality individual visual elements (e.g., single images or short videos), the demand from users and researchers alike is shifting towards generating more comprehensive worlds that seamlessly integrate varied scenes with precise spatial and temporal control. Applications across entertainment, education, realistic simulation, and embodied AI increasingly rely on the ability to generate coherent, navigable, and interactive environments.

The primary challenge identified by the authors is the conspicuous absence of a unified evaluation benchmark capable of systematically assessing these diverse world generation models. Existing benchmarks fall short by:
1. **Fragmented Focus:** Largely concentrating on single-scene video generation quality (e.g., VBench) without considering the multi-scene coherence or spatial progression vital for world building.
2. **Lack of Spatial Control:** Failing to provide explicit camera trajectory or layout specifications, which are crucial for defining inter-scene relationships and guiding complex world navigation.
3. **Incompatibility:** Not being designed to accommodate the distinct input requirements of diverse generative methodologies, particularly 3D and 4D scene generation methods that often rely on camera poses or image-based conditioning.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the WorldScore benchmark are precisely articulated:
- To **introduce the first unified benchmark for world generation**, providing a standardized framework for evaluating various generative approaches.
- To **decompose the complex task of world generation into a sequence of next-scene generation tasks**, each guided by explicit camera trajectory-based layout specifications, allowing for systematic assessment.
- To **enable unified evaluation of diverse models**, including those for 3D, 4D, text-to-video (T2V), and image-to-video (I2V) generation, on a common output format (rendered videos).
- To **curate a comprehensive and diverse dataset** of 3,000 test examples that span static and dynamic worlds, indoor and outdoor environments, and photorealistic and stylized visual domains.
- To **develop a multi-faceted evaluation metric, "WorldScore,"** that aggregates performance across three critical aspects: controllability (adherence to inputs), quality (fidelity and consistency), and dynamics (accurate and stable motion).
- To **conduct extensive evaluations of representative models** (20 in total) using WorldScore, thereby revealing key insights, current limitations, and future challenges for each category of models.

By achieving these objectives, WorldScore aims to serve as a foundational tool to guide future research, foster innovation, and accelerate progress towards truly capable and versatile world generation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WorldScore benchmark is meticulously designed to provide a unified and comprehensive evaluation framework. Its methodology revolves around three core components: a standardized world specification, a carefully curated dataset, and a multi-faceted metric system.

#### 3.1. World Specification

The central innovation in WorldScore's design is to decompose world generation into a sequence of "next-scene generation tasks." Each task is defined by a structured triplet: (Current Scene (C), Next Scene (N), Layout (L)).
- **Current Scene (C):** Provided as both an image (I) and a text prompt (P), serving as a visual and semantic anchor for the subsequent scene. This dual input accommodates both Image-to-Video (I2V) and Text-to-Video (T2V) models, as well as 3D/4D generation methods that can leverage image conditioning.
- **Next Scene (N):** A text prompt describing the desired semantic content of the new scene.
- **Layout (L):** Specified by a camera trajectory (T), given as a sequence of camera matrices, and a textual description of camera movement (Y). This explicit layout control is crucial for evaluating a model's ability to transition between scenes with precise spatial guidance.

All evaluated models, regardless of their native input format, are pre-processed to align with this specification and produce a standardized output: a video. This ensures fair comparison across diverse methodological paradigms, from 3D/4D scene generation which typically accepts camera poses, to video generation models that may primarily use text or images.

The benchmark further differentiates between two types of generation tasks:
- **Static World Generation:** Focuses on generating varying-length sequences of distinct scenes, primarily assessing controllability and visual quality through new scene content (N) and large camera movements (L).
- **Dynamic World Generation:** Focuses on animating in-scene motion within a fixed camera view, evaluating a model's ability to create accurate and stable dynamics based on new scene content (N) that describes changes within the existing environment.

#### 3.2. Dataset Curation

The WorldScore dataset comprises 3,000 high-quality test examples (world specifications), split into 2,000 for static world generation and 1,000 for dynamic world generation, ensuring diversity across various domains:
- **Current Scene (C) Curation:**
 - **Photorealistic Worlds:** For static worlds, 10 categories (5 indoor: Dining, Living, Passageways, Public, Workspaces; 5 outdoor: City, Suburb, Aquatic, Terrestrial, Verdant landscapes) were defined. Images were sourced from existing scene datasets (e.g., Matterport3D, Hypersim) and Unsplash. A rigorous filtering process was applied based on:
 - **Quality:** Using CLIP-IQA and CLIP Aesthetic predictors.
 - **Perspective:** Filtering out extreme roll/pitch angles and narrow fields of view using Perspective Fields.
 - **Similarity:** Removing redundant images with CLIPSIM.
 - **Brightness:** Excluding overly dark images.
 - **Human Judgment:** A final manual review to ensure alignment with human perception.
 - **Stylized Counterparts:** For each photorealistic example, a stylized version was created. A style (from 7 candidates like anime, ukiyo-e, impressionism) was randomly selected, added to the text prompt, and a commercial text-to-image model was used to generate the stylized image.
 - **Dynamic Worlds:** 5 motion types (articulated, deformable, fluid, rigid, multi-motion) were defined. 100 images per category were manually curated from Unsplash, with similar processes for prompts and stylized counterparts, including motion mask annotations.
- **Next-Scene Text Prompts (N) Curation:** GPT-4o was employed in an auto-regressive manner to generate coherent and contextually relevant scene sequences. Prompts were designed for both "small worlds" (one new scene) and "large worlds" (three new scenes), ensuring variety in sequence length. For dynamic worlds, prompts described in-scene motion.
- **Layouts (L) Curation:** A set of 8 widely used camera movements (e.g., push in, pull out, orbit, pan, move) from the movie industry was curated. Each static scene generation task was randomly assigned one of these layouts, specified by both camera matrices (T) and textual descriptions (Y).

#### 3.3. The WorldScore Metrics

WorldScore comprises two aggregate scores: WorldScore-Static and WorldScore-Dynamic, derived from 10 individual metrics grouped into three key aspects:

1. **Controllability (3 metrics):** Measures adherence to input controls.
 - **Camera Controllability:** Evaluates how well models follow the instructed camera trajectory (L). Uses DROID-SLAM to estimate frame-wise camera poses and computes scale-invariant rotation and translation errors against ground truth.
 - **Object Controllability:** Assesses if specified objects in the next-scene prompt (N) appear in the generated scene. Employs a state-of-the-art open-set object detector (Grounding DINO) to measure detection success rate.
 - **Content Alignment:** Quantifies the semantic alignment between the generated scene and the entire next-scene text prompt (N) using CLIPScore.

2. **Quality (4 metrics):** Measures visual fidelity and consistency.
 - **3D Consistency:** Evaluates geometric stability across frames. Uses DROID-SLAM for dense pixel-wise depth estimation and computes reprojection errors between co-visible pixels in consecutive frames.
 - **Photometric Consistency:** Assesses appearance stability (e.g., texture consistency, absence of flickering). Estimates optical flow (SEA-RAFT) between consecutive frames and computes Average End-Point Error (AEPE).
 - **Style Consistency:** For stylized worlds, measures how well the generated video maintains the specified style, by computing differences in Gram matrices between the first and last frames.
 - **Subjective Quality:** Automatically assesses human perceptual quality. An ensemble of CLIP-IQA+ and CLIP Aesthetic metrics was chosen after a human preference study to best match subjective evaluations.

3. **Dynamics (3 metrics – for WorldScore-Dynamic only):** Measures accuracy and stability of motion.
 - **Motion Accuracy:** Determines if motion specified in N occurs in the designated regions. Compares optical flow within intended motion masks (tracked by SAM2) versus outside these regions.
 - **Motion Magnitude:** Quantifies the extent of motion generated, measured by the median optical flow magnitude between consecutive frames.
 - **Motion Smoothness:** Evaluates temporal stability and absence of jitter. Compares reconstructed frames from a video frame interpolation model (as ground truth) with original frames using MSE, SSIM, and LPIPS.

**Normalization and Aggregation:** Individual metric scores are linearly normalized to a 0-100 range based on empirically determined bounds, where higher values indicate better performance. WorldScore-Static is the arithmetic mean of controllability and quality metrics. WorldScore-Dynamic includes all 10 metrics (controllability, quality, and dynamics). For 3D models not supporting dynamic tasks, dynamics metrics are assigned a score of 0.

#### 3.4. Validation

The WorldScore metrics were validated through extensive human studies, confirming their alignment with human preferences. These studies also demonstrated the robustness of the metrics to variations in video resolution and aspect ratio, ensuring their applicability across diverse model outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of 20 diverse world generation models—including closed-source I2V (Gen-3, Hailuo), open-source T2V/I2V (e.g., CogVideoX, DynamiCrafter, VideoCrafter), 3D scene generation (e.g., WonderWorld, LucidDreamer), and 4D generation (4D-fy) models—yielded several critical insights and highlighted key challenges:

1. **3D Models Excel in Static World Generation but Lack Dynamics:**
 - **Strength:** 3D scene generation models (e.g., WonderWorld (72.69) and LucidDreamer (70.40)) achieved the highest WorldScore-Static, significantly outperforming video models. This is attributed to their inherent ability to generate 3D consistent geometry, leading to superior camera controllability, 3D consistency, and photometric consistency.
 - **Weakness:** These models, by design, often do not support dynamic content. The sole 4D generation model evaluated, 4D-fy, performed poorly overall (WorldScore-Dynamic 32.10), indicating the intrinsic difficulty and nascent stage of 4D scene generation for complex world building.

2. **Video Models Suffer from Poor Camera Controllability:**
 - A major challenge for video generation models (both T2V and I2V) is their low camera controllability. Even the best video model in this aspect (CogVideoX-T2V with 40.22) scored considerably lower than any 3D/4D model. This limitation severely hampers their ability to generate spatially guided multi-scene worlds, as they struggle to adhere to explicit camera trajectories.

3. **Competitive Open-Source Video Models:**
 - Surprisingly, some open-source video generation models, such as CogVideoX-I2V, demonstrated overall WorldScore-Static (62.15) and WorldScore-Dynamic (59.12) performances comparable to, and in some cases even exceeding, leading commercial closed-source models like Gen-3 and Hailuo. However, this often came with trade-offs; for instance, CogVideoX-I2V showed better camera controllability but lagged in object controllability and content alignment compared to its closed-source counterparts.

4. **Trade-offs in Motion Characteristics:**
 - A notable challenge in dynamic world generation is the trade-off between motion smoothness and motion magnitude. Models capable of generating larger motions often exhibit lower smoothness, highlighting the difficulty in maintaining both significant movement and temporal stability simultaneously.
 - Furthermore, high motion magnitude does not guarantee accurate motion placement. Models frequently hallucinate unintended camera motions or irrelevant object movements, indicating a need for more robust motion modeling that balances magnitude, accuracy, and adherence to instructions.

5. **Video Models Struggle with Long Sequences and Outdoor Scenes:**
 - Subdomain analysis revealed that video generation models perform significantly worse on tasks requiring long-sequence generation (i.e., large worlds) compared to short sequences.
 - They also exhibit a notable performance gap against 3D models in outdoor scenes, although this gap is narrower in indoor environments. This suggests difficulties in maintaining coherence over extended temporal durations and generating the complexity and scale typical of outdoor environments.

6. **T2V vs. I2V Models: Different Strengths:**
 - T2V models generally achieved higher scores in controllability aspects and showed greater motion magnitude, suggesting they are more amenable to steering and producing explicit changes.
 - I2V models, conversely, tended to score higher in the quality aspect, indicating better fidelity to the input image. However, this often made them less willing to generate new scene content or significant camera movements, as they tended to "stick" to the initial viewpoint. This highlights a challenge in controlling I2V models to diverge significantly from the input image while maintaining coherence.

These findings collectively underscore the current limitations across different world generation paradigms and provide a clear roadmap for future research directions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WorldScore benchmark represents a significant contribution to the rapidly evolving field of generative AI, particularly in the domain of world generation. Its potential impact is multi-faceted and far-reaching:

1. **Establishes a Unified Standard:** WorldScore is the first benchmark to offer a unified evaluation framework for diverse generative approaches, including 3D, 4D, T2V, and I2V models. This standardization is crucial for fostering fair comparisons, transparent progress tracking, and constructive competition among researchers and developers. It provides a common language and objective metrics for assessing advancements that were previously difficult to compare due to fragmented evaluation methods.

2. **Drives Comprehensive Model Development:** By decomposing world generation into multi-scene tasks and evaluating models across critical dimensions—controllability, quality, and dynamics—WorldScore pushes researchers to develop models that are not only capable of generating visually appealing content but also exhibit precise control, temporal coherence, and accurate physics. This holistic approach encourages the development of more robust and versatile generative systems.

3. **Identifies Key Research Challenges:** The extensive evaluation of 20 representative models vividly highlights the current limitations of existing technologies. For instance, the findings clearly show that 3D models excel in static world consistency but struggle with dynamics, while video models severely lack camera controllability and face difficulties in long-sequence generation and outdoor scenes. These specific insights pinpoint critical areas where future research efforts are most needed, providing a clear roadmap for the community.

4. **Guides Future Research Directions:** The identified challenges naturally translate into concrete directions for future work:
 - **Bridging 3D and 4D:** Encouraging research into more effective methods for integrating dynamic elements into 3D scene generation, moving towards full 4D representations.
 - **Enhanced Controllability:** Motivating the development of more robust and intuitive control mechanisms, especially for camera movements in video generation models.
 - **Long-Sequence Coherence:** Pushing for architectures and training methodologies capable of maintaining consistency and narrative flow over extended scene sequences.
 - **Balancing Dynamics:** Inspiring new approaches to balance motion magnitude, accuracy, and smoothness, addressing the observed trade-offs.
 - **Steering I2V Models:** Promoting research into techniques that allow I2V models to generate new content and perspectives while maintaining high quality.

5. **Accelerates Application Development:** High-quality, controllable world generation is fundamental to numerous applications in entertainment (e.g., game development, virtual reality), education (e.g., interactive learning environments), realistic simulation (e.g., autonomous vehicle training, robotics), and embodied AI (e.g., training agents in complex virtual worlds). By providing a benchmark that measures the capabilities essential for these applications, WorldScore will accelerate the development of more effective and deployable generative systems.

6. **Promotes Open Science:** The public availability of the dataset, evaluation code, and a leaderboard ensures transparency, reproducibility, and community engagement. This open-source approach allows researchers worldwide to contribute, validate, and build upon the benchmark, further solidifying its role as a central tool for advancing the field.

In conclusion, WorldScore is more than just a new set of metrics; it is a foundational infrastructure that defines the ambitions and measures the progress of generative AI in creating complex, immersive, and controllable virtual worlds. By providing a clear and comprehensive framework, it promises to be a powerful catalyst for innovation in an area with profound implications for numerous scientific and industrial applications.
