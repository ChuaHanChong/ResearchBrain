# Embodied Human-Robot Interaction via Acoustics: A MARL Approach with AcoustoBots for Spatial Data Physicalization

> [!summary] Summary
> AcoustoBots introduces a mobile robotic platform that utilizes multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) and acoustophoretic levitation to physically render spatial data. This system successfully co-locates dynamic data representations with their physical referents, demonstrating stable, in-motion particle levitation and coordinated multi-robot navigation for urban data physicalization tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional static data visualizations impose a cognitive burden on users by detaching information from its real-world context, hindering embodied understanding.
- Existing data physicalization systems are often static or restricted to fixed locations, lacking the mobility and real-time responsiveness needed for dynamic spatial data representation.
- Integrating non-contact acoustophoretic levitation with mobile platforms faces challenges in maintaining acoustic trap stability during robot motion and coordinating multiple agents effectively.

## Method

- Developed **AcoustoBots**, a mobile ==acoustophoretic levitation== physicalization platform that couples autonomous navigation with in-situ acoustic levitation for dynamic data display.
- Implemented a high-rate acoustic control module using a ==Gerchberg-Saxton-Phased Array of Transducers (GS-PAT)== solver to maintain stable particle levitation and regulate height during continuous robot motion.
- Designed a ==Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)== framework, specifically ==MADDPG== with physics-informed reward design, to enable cooperative multi-robot navigation, collision avoidance, and continuous data physicalization.

## Results

- MARL training consistently achieved stable learning, with success rates for multi-robot navigation reaching **80-90%** and collision counts approaching zero over **20,000** episodes.
- Single-robot city-to-city traversal experiments demonstrated a **90%** success rate, showing stable in-motion particle levitation and accurate physicalization of location-dependent scalar values.
- Two-robot cooperative coverage tasks achieved an **80%** success rate, where robots successfully coordinated to cover target cities while avoiding collisions and continuously rendering population data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mobile acoustophoretic levitation is feasible, extending non-contact data physicalization from static setups to dynamic, exploratory robotic platforms for embodied human-robot interaction.
> - A multi-agent reinforcement learning approach can effectively coordinate multiple mobile robots for tasks that require both efficient navigation and simultaneous, in-motion data physicalization.
> - Real-time acoustic control can maintain stable particle levitation and accurate height encoding during robot movement, providing a continuous and glanceable physical data cue.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional data visualization and physicalization methods often present information in a static manner, detached from the real-world environments they represent. This can impose a cognitive burden on users who must mentally map abstract data back to physical space, potentially hindering embodied understanding and rapid sense-making in situ.

The field of data physicalization aims to address this by encoding information into tangible physical forms. Prior research indicates that physical representations can offer advantages over screen-based displays for information retrieval and spatial tasks due to their tangibility, depth cues, and body-centric spatial reasoning. Within this domain, a distinction is made between "situated representations" (data placed near its referent) and "embedded representations" (data spatially coinciding with its referent), with embedded representations posited to support more natural perception. However, many existing physicalization systems remain static or are restricted to fixed locations, particularly those employing acoustophoretic levitation. Mobile robotic physicalizations exist, such as those involving swarms of wheeled micro-robots, but these typically rely on surface contact and do not integrate non-contact acoustic manipulation.

This work integrates several distinct research areas:
- **Data Physicalization and Embodied Interaction:** Moving beyond static displays to create dynamic, mobile, and embedded data representations.
- **Acoustophoretic Levitation:** Utilizing ultrasound phased arrays for non-contact manipulation, which traditionally has been confined to static platforms or limited workspaces. This research extends this technology to mobile platforms.
- **Multi-Robot Coordination:** Employing multiple robots to cooperatively explore and physicalize data, a field where challenges include balancing efficiency, coverage, and collision avoidance.
- **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL):** Applying MARL algorithms, specifically Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (MADDPG), to enable decentralized control and coordinated learning for multi-robot navigation and data physicalization tasks.

The research positions AcoustoBots at the intersection of these fields, aiming to develop a platform that simultaneously achieves mobility, non-contact physicalization, and data-responsiveness, enabling robots to travel to a referent location and render data as an embodied signal in situ. This addresses the gap where mobile, non-contact, and data-responsive physicalization systems are not commonly found together.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the limitations of current data visualization and physicalization paradigms. Urban environments, in particular, generate vast amounts of spatially resolved information (e.g., noise exposure, traffic flow, air quality, population dynamics) critical for planning and operations. However, the common practice of presenting this data on static, screen-based displays detaches it from the physical environments it describes, placing a cognitive load on users to interpret and re-contextualize the information. The goal is to facilitate more intuitive, embodied understanding and rapid sense-making by bringing data representations directly into the physical world and co-locating them with their referents.

The work identifies several technical challenges in achieving this vision:
1. **Simultaneous Integration:** Developing a system that concurrently achieves mobility, data physicalization, and data-responsiveness in a manner that follows the user's or robot's focus of attention.
2. **Acoustic Trap Stability under Motion:** Acoustic levitation traps are susceptible to disturbances from robot movement (acceleration, turning, vibration). Maintaining stable levitation and accurate particle height control during continuous locomotion is a significant technical hurdle.
3. **Multi-Robot Coordination Complexity:** For multiple robots, coordination policies must optimize several conflicting objectives: efficient exploration or coverage, accurate physicalization fidelity (correct height rendering at the correct location), collision avoidance between agents and obstacles, and managing potential acoustic interference.
4. **Coupling Control Layers:** The system requires bridging two tightly coupled control layers—mobile navigation and acoustic manipulation—each with distinct update rates, control loops, and associated uncertainties.
5. **Human Interpretability:** Ensuring that the physicalized data channel remains legible and spatially co-located with its referent as robots navigate through an environment, maintaining its utility for human interpretation.

To address these motivations and challenges, the research established the following key objectives, articulated as contributions:
- **Development of AcoustoBots:** To create a mobile acoustophoretic physicalization platform that couples autonomous navigation with in-situ acoustic levitation, thereby extending acoustophoretic physicalization from static installations to mobile, exploratory settings.
- **Real-time Acoustic Control Module:** To develop a high-rate acoustic control module capable of mapping scalar field values to discrete levitation heights and maintaining acoustic trap stability via Gerchberg-Saxton-Phased Array of Transducers (GS-PAT) phase updates throughout robot motion.
- **MARL Coordination Framework:** To design a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning framework, specifically using the MADDPG algorithm with physics-informed reward design, to enable multiple AcoustoBots to cooperatively cover geographic waypoints while continuously rendering population data, ensuring both navigation safety and physicalization fidelity.
- **Experimental Evaluation:** To perform a comprehensive experimental evaluation, integrated with ROS 2 and utilizing PhaseSpace-based localization on a scaled UK map, to demonstrate robust navigation and embodied, location-dependent particle-height rendering, including initial multi-robot deployment and coordination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AcoustoBots system is designed to establish a closed-loop control system: robot pose on a grid leads to data sensing, which informs levitation-height physicalization, guiding policy decisions, and ultimately resulting in motion with continuous height regulation.

#### 3.1. Hardware Design

Each AcoustoBot consists of a TurtleBot3 differential-drive mobile base. This base is equipped with:
- An upward-facing 8×8 ultrasonic phased-array transducer (PAT) board, operating at 40 kHz, for acoustic levitation.
- Wheel encoders for local state estimation.
- Onboard computing via a Raspberry Pi 4B, running ROS 2 Humble on Ubuntu 22.04.
- Active LED markers for external localization by a PhaseSpace motion capture system, providing millimeter-level accuracy at 240 Hz.

#### 3.2. Software Architecture (ROS 2 Framework)

The system employs a ROS 2-based architecture to manage perception, control, and communication:
- **`mapper` node:** Maintains an occupancy grid for collision avoidance, a scalar field layer ($\phi$) representing urban data (e.g., population density) sampled and interpolated over the map, and a coverage map ($\Psi$) for multi-robot tasks.
- **`acoustic_controller` node:** Receives local scalar values from the `mapper`, maps them to a commanded particle height ($h^*$) between 1–10 cm, and computes holographic phases for a single-focus acoustic trap using a GS-PAT solver. This controller continuously updates array phases at a high rate to stabilize the trap and regulate particle height during motion. It also exposes acoustic pressure signals for safety monitoring.
- **`policy_node`:** Executes the learned Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) actor ($\pi_\theta(a|o)$) in a decentralized manner (operating at 10–20 Hz). It consumes robot state (pose, velocity), map context, and physicalized signals (e.g., height differentials) to generate motion commands.
- **`base_ctrl` node:** Tracks the received motion commands (linear velocity $v$, angular velocity $\omega$) and applies jerk limits to ensure smooth robot movement.
- **`safety_supervisor` node:** Acts as a runtime guardian, enforcing acoustic pressure caps and publishing health flags. It can gate execution if limits are approached and logs safety-related events. Violations are fed back to the MARL policy as penalties.
- **`telemetry_logger` node:** Records critical data such as robot pose, levitation height, acoustic pressure, rewards, and events for post-experiment analysis and evaluation.
- **Localization:** A dedicated Ubuntu client receives 6-DoF rigid-body pose data from the PhaseSpace server, which is then published by a ROS 2 bridge node as synchronized pose data on topics `/tb3_1/pose` and `/tb3_2/pose`.

#### 3.3. Policy Training (Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning)

The MADDPG algorithm, implemented in PyTorch, was utilized for policy training, employing a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm.
- **MADDPG Formulation:** Each agent maintains an actor (mapping local observations to actions) and a centralized critic (conditioning on joint state/action during training). During execution, only the local actor is used. Target networks are updated via soft updates for stability.
- **Environment Setup:** A 2D grid-world environment (40×40 cells) with static obstacles was designed using the PettingZoo parallel API. Two agents are trained to navigate between predefined start positions and a shared goal location.
- **Observation Space:** Each agent's observation ($o_i$) includes its normalized position, relative teammate position, relative goal position, and a local obstacle patch (radius $r=3$).
- **Action Space:** A discrete action space of 9 actions: four cardinal moves, four diagonal moves, and a no-op (staying in place).
- **Reward Design:** The reward function encourages cooperative goal-reaching and penalizes unsafe or inefficient behaviors:
 - $R_{goal_i}$: Terminal bonus for reaching the goal.
 - $R_{dist_i}$: Shapes progress towards the goal (distance reduction).
 - $R_{step_i}$: Penalty for unnecessary motion.
 - $R_{col_i}$: Penalty for agent-agent collisions.
 - $R_{obs_i}$: Penalty for proximity to obstacles.
 - $R_{team_i}$: Bonus for successful coordinated completion when all agents reach the goal.
- **Trajectory Smoothing and Resampling:** To convert discrete grid-based trajectories into physically feasible and interpretable paths for real robots:
 - **Line-of-sight path smoothing:** Removes redundant waypoints by checking direct line-of-sight between points, using Bresenham's algorithm to avoid obstacles.
 - **Uniform resampling:** Interpolates waypoints along the smoothed path to ensure consistent spacing and temporal alignment.

#### 3.4. Use Case and Data Physicalization

The platform operates on a 4m x 3m scaled UK map, where urban scalar data (e.g., population density) is bilinearly interpolated across a metric grid. Each robot's levitated particle height (1–10 cm) encodes the local scalar value at its current position, providing a continuous, glanceable, and co-located physical cue.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation encompassed both the learning performance of the MARL policy and the real-world performance of the AcoustoBots platform on a scaled UK map.

#### 4.1. Model Evaluation (MARL Training)

- **Total Reward:** The MADDPG training showed consistent learning trends across five random seeds. The total reward increased sharply within the initial 2,000 episodes, indicating rapid acquisition of basic navigation and penalty avoidance. It then converged more gradually, stabilizing between approximately 100–120 by 20,000 episodes.
- **Consistency:** A relatively narrow variance across the five seeds (average total reward of 112.62 ± 8.30) indicated reproducible learning behavior.
- **Success Rate and Efficiency:** The success rate increased and stabilized around 80% (with some seeds achieving nearly 100%). Episode lengths decreased from approximately 100 steps to 40–50 steps, and collision counts converged to near zero. The mean distance-to-goal dropped rapidly within the first 5,000 episodes.
- **Trajectory Post-processing:** The line-of-sight smoothing and uniform resampling successfully transformed raw, zig-zagging grid-based trajectories into smoother, shorter, and more readable routes suitable for real-robot execution, as demonstrated in comparative visualizations.

#### 4.2. Experimental Setup

- **Environment:** A 4 m × 3 m physical map of the UK was deployed on the floor, discretized into 10 cm metric grid cells. Geodetically consistent city centroids were marked as waypoints. A scalar field of population density was sampled at these centroids and bilinearly interpolated across the grid.
- **Platform:** TurtleBot3 bases with 8×8 PAT boards were used. PhaseSpace motion capture provided high-precision localization.

#### 4.3. Real-world Experiments and Quantitative Summary

Experiments were conducted for two distinct tasks, each over 10 trials:

- **Task 1: Single-Robot City-to-City Traversal**
 - **Description:** One AcoustoBot navigated a fixed route (London → Liverpool → Edinburgh) with continuous height rendering of the local population density along the path.
 - **Success Rate:** 90% of trials were successfully completed.
 - **Collisions:** 1 collision was recorded across the 10 trials.
 - **Physicalization Fidelity:** The robot demonstrated stable in-motion levitation of a particle, with its height consistently and accurately encoding the location-dependent scalar value. The physical cue remained legible during robot motion.

- **Task 2: Two-Robot Cooperative Coverage**
 - **Description:** Two AcoustoBots coordinated to cover a set of target cities efficiently while emphasizing high-gradient regions of the scalar field (areas of rapid population change), within a specified path-length budget.
 - **Success Rate:** 80% of trials were successfully completed.
 - **Collisions:** 2 collisions were recorded across the 10 trials.
 - **Coordination:** The robots successfully demonstrated team-level cooperative coverage behavior, maintaining safe separation and avoiding collisions while simultaneously performing in-motion physicalization.

#### 4.4. Discussion of Robustness and Limitations

The MARL policy consistently generated stable, collision-aware navigation. However, the plateauing of success rates at 80%–90% (rather than 100%) and occasional fluctuations during training suggest sensitivity to exploration noise and environmental stochasticity. The experiments were conducted with a small number of robots in a simplified 2D grid-world abstraction, which may not fully capture the complexities of larger, more heterogeneous teams or unstructured real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AcoustoBots, a mobile acoustophoretic data physicalization platform that integrates multi-agent reinforcement learning with acoustic levitation to communicate spatial data through an embodied, glanceable physical cue. This system effectively bridges the gap between traditional static data representations and the dynamic, context-rich physical environment.

#### 5.1. Core Contribution to Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)

The key outcome from an HRI perspective is the successful demonstration that robot behavior and the physicalized data signal can remain co-located and interpretable. By mapping urban scalar fields (like population density) to the height of a levitated particle while robots navigate a scaled map, the system creates a direct, in-situ, and embodied form of robot-mediated communication. This approach enhances human comprehension of geographically contextualized information by providing a tangible, real-time representation that moves with the robot.

#### 5.2. Advancements in Data Physicalization and Robotics

- **Mobile Acoustophoretic Physicalization:** The work extends acoustophoretic levitation from static laboratory setups to mobile, autonomous robotic platforms, enabling dynamic data display in exploratory settings.
- **Integrated Control System:** The successful coupling of autonomous navigation with real-time, in-motion acoustic manipulation represents a technical achievement, addressing challenges related to trap stability during robot movement and coordinating two distinct control layers.
- **MARL for Embodied Data:** The application of MADDPG with physics-informed reward design for multi-robot coordination in data physicalization tasks demonstrates a novel use of reinforcement learning to optimize for both navigation efficiency, safety, and physicalization fidelity.

#### 5.3. Potential Practical Applications

Beyond a proof-of-concept, the AcoustoBots approach suggests practical applications for robot-assisted decision support, particularly in urban analytics. Robots could traverse mapped environments and physically render local conditions such as traffic flow, noise levels, or air quality as simple, intuitive physical signals. This could provide on-site, glanceable information to urban planners, emergency responders, or citizens, enhancing their understanding of complex spatial data.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Broader Impact

The research identifies several avenues for future work, which also outline potential broader impacts:
- **Scalability and Robustness:** Improving scalability to larger teams with enhanced robustness and safety guarantees, including tighter collision avoidance and interference-aware coordination in more complex environments.
- **Levitation Stability:** Further improving the stability of acoustic levitation under more varied motion profiles and environmental disturbances.
- **Richer Physicalization:** Extending data physicalization beyond a single height channel by integrating additional sensing modalities (e.g., vision, audio, environmental sensors) and developing richer encoding schemes to convey more complex information.

These advancements could position AcoustoBots as adaptive, embodied agents capable of human-centered monitoring and communication in complex real-world environments, fostering more intuitive human-robot symbiosis and robot-mediated contextual awareness.
