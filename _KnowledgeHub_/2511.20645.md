# PixelDiT: Pixel Diffusion Transformers for Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PixelDiT, a diffusion transformer, generates high-resolution images directly in pixel space, bypassing the need for a separate autoencoder. The model achieved an FID of 1.61 on ImageNet 256x256, surpassing previous pixel-space models, and demonstrated competitive text-to-image generation performance at 1024x1024 while preserving fine details in image editing tasks.

## Problem

- Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) suffer from lossy autoencoder reconstruction, which can remove high-frequency details and limit image fidelity.
- The two-stage nature of LDMs introduces objective misalignment between the autoencoder and the diffusion model, leading to error accumulation and increased pipeline complexity.
- Direct pixel-space diffusion models are computationally prohibitive for high-resolution images due to the quadratic scaling of global attention with the number of pixels.

## Method

- A single-stage, end-to-end ==Pixel Diffusion Transformer== (**PixelDiT**) operates directly on raw pixels, eliminating the need for a separate autoencoder.
- A dual-level transformer architecture separates global semantic modeling (patch-level pathway) from local detail refinement (pixel-level pathway).
- The pixel-level pathway employs pixel-wise Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) for fine-grained conditioning and pixel token compaction for efficient global attention over dense pixel tokens.

## Results

- PixelDiT-XL achieved an FID of 1.61 on ImageNet 256x256, outperforming prior pixel-space models and exhibiting rapid convergence.
- The model demonstrated competitive text-to-image generation performance with a GenEval score of **0.74** and DPG of 83.5 at 1024x1024, competitive with state-of-the-art latent models.
- In image editing tasks, PixelDiT preserved fine details, showing **6.0x** lower MSE and higher SSIM in unedited regions compared to latent diffusion models like FLUX.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity image generation comparable to or exceeding latent diffusion models is achievable directly in pixel space without the autoencoder bottleneck.
> - Efficient pixel modeling can be realized by disentangling global semantics and local details, combined with techniques like pixel-wise conditioning and temporary token compaction.
> - The proposed architecture significantly improves detail preservation in image editing workflows, avoiding reconstruction artifacts common in latent-space approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "PixelDiT" addresses the prevailing paradigm in high-resolution image generation, which largely relies on Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) coupled with Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). LDMs operate by performing denoising in a compressed latent space, typically generated by a separately pretrained autoencoder, such as a Variational Autoencoder (VAE). This two-stage approach offers computational and memory efficiencies, enabling the training of large models at higher resolutions.

However, the authors identify several structural limitations inherent to this latent-space modeling paradigm. Firstly, the autoencoder's reconstruction objective is often only partially aligned with the downstream generative objective, potentially leading to error accumulation. Secondly, the VAE introduces lossy reconstruction, which can remove high-frequency details and thus impose a ceiling on sample fidelity, affecting both image generation and editing tasks. Additionally, the need for a separate autoencoder adds complexity, pretraining overhead, and decoding latency to the overall pipeline.

Pixel-space diffusion models, which perform denoising directly on raw pixels, predated latent-space methods and remain an active area of research. These models aim to circumvent the limitations of autoencoders by operating in the original image space. Historically, the primary challenge for pixel-space diffusion has been the immense computational cost associated with modeling dense, per-pixel interactions, especially at high resolutions, where global attention mechanisms incur quadratic complexity with respect to the number of pixels. Previous attempts to address this, such as aggressive patchification or designs pushing for near-pixel granularity, have faced a trade-off between computational affordability and the preservation of fine-grained details. Cascaded pipelines have also been explored, but these can introduce additional stages and accumulated errors.

PixelDiT positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome the limitations of latent diffusion models while simultaneously addressing the computational challenges of pixel-space diffusion. It proposes a single-stage, end-to-end transformer-based model that operates directly in pixel space without an autoencoder. The core innovation lies in its efficient pixel modeling mechanism, designed to capture both global semantic structures and fine-grained texture details effectively, thus aiming to close the quality gap with latent diffusion models while simplifying the generative pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the PixelDiT research is to develop a single-stage, end-to-end generative model capable of directly learning and performing the diffusion process in pixel space, thereby eliminating the reliance on a separate autoencoder. This objective is driven by a detailed motivation stemming from the identified limitations of existing latent diffusion models (LDMs) and the inherent challenges in pixel-space diffusion.

**Motivation 1: Addressing the Limitations of Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs)**

- **Lossy Reconstruction and Fidelity Limits:** A primary motivation is the recognition that the autoencoders (typically VAEs) used in LDMs introduce a "reconstruction bottleneck." These VAEs perform lossy compression, which can discard high-frequency details and fine textures present in the original image. This loss of information inherently limits the maximum achievable fidelity of generated images, even if the diffusion model operating in the latent space is otherwise powerful. The paper highlights how this limitation negatively impacts critical applications such as image editing, where minor details like scene text can be severely distorted by the VAE's encoding and decoding process (Figure 1b).
- **Objective Misalignment and Error Accumulation:** The objectives of the autoencoder (reconstruction) and the diffusion model (generation) are not perfectly aligned. This partial misalignment can introduce distribution shifts in the latent space, such as texture smoothing or color shifts, which the diffusion model then needs to compensate for. This coupling can lead to error accumulation throughout the two-stage pipeline and complicates joint optimization.
- **Pipeline Complexity and Overhead:** The two-stage nature of LDMs requires separate pretraining or co-training of a large autoencoder, which adds computational and data overhead. Additionally, the decoding step during sampling incurs extra latency, making the overall process more complex and potentially slower compared to a truly end-to-end single-stage approach.

**Motivation 2: Overcoming Challenges in Pixel-Space Diffusion**

While pixel-space diffusion avoids the VAE bottleneck, it presents its own set of computational and architectural challenges, which PixelDiT aims to resolve:

- **Computational Prohibitive Expense:** Directly processing high-resolution images in pixel space means dealing with a very large number of tokens (pixels). Applying global self-attention mechanisms, which scale quadratically with sequence length (O((H*W)^2)), becomes computationally prohibitive for high-resolution images (e.g., megapixel scales), demanding vast compute and memory resources.
- **Balancing Global Semantics and Local Details:** Prior attempts in pixel-space modeling have struggled with a fundamental trade-off:
 - **Aggressive Patchification:** Using large patch sizes to reduce token sequence length for efficiency (e.g., in some DiT variants) leads to weakened per-pixel token modeling, hindering the generation of fine visual content and textures.
 - **Fine-Grained Modeling:** Adopting small patch sizes or near-pixel granularity to preserve details necessitates processing very long token sequences, leading to the aforementioned quadratic complexity and high training/sampling costs.
 - **Cascaded Approaches:** While mitigating some costs, multi-stage cascaded pipelines can introduce their own complexities and accumulate errors.

In summary, the core motivation for PixelDiT is to develop an efficient and practical single-stage pixel-space diffusion transformer that can effectively model both global image semantics and intricate per-pixel details, thereby addressing the limitations of latent diffusion models and the inherent computational difficulties of pixel-space modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PixelDiT is designed as a single-stage, fully transformer-based diffusion model that operates directly in pixel space, bypassing the need for a separate autoencoder. Its methodology focuses on making pixel token modeling computationally efficient while preserving the quality and convergence properties typically associated with latent-space approaches.

The core of PixelDiT's architecture is a **dual-level transformer organization** (Figure 2), which partitions the image generation process into semantic learning and detail refinement.

#### 3.1. Dual-level DiT Architecture

- **Patch-level Pathway (DiT Blocks):**
 - **Input:** The input image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C \times H \times W}$ is first divided into non-overlapping $p \times p$ patches. These patches are then flattened and linearly projected to form patch tokens $s_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L \times D}$, where $L = (H/p)(W/p)$ is the number of tokens and $D$ is the hidden dimension.
 - **Processing:** This pathway consists of $N$ augmented DiT blocks. These blocks perform long-range attention over the relatively short sequence of patch tokens to capture global layout and content.
 - **Conditioning:** Timestep $t$ and class $y$ (or text embeddings for text-to-image) are combined and projected into a global conditioning vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1 \times D}$. This vector modulates the DiT blocks using AdaLN (Adaptive Layer Normalization) parameters ($\alpha, \gamma, \beta$). Importantly, at the patch level, this AdaLN modulation is token-independent, applying identical parameters to all patch tokens.
 - **Architectural Enhancements:** Following prior work, LayerNorm is replaced with RMSNorm, and 2D RoPE (Rotary Positional Embeddings) is applied in all attention layers for improved performance.
 - **Output:** After $N$ blocks, the pathway outputs semantic tokens $s_N \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L \times D}$. These, combined with the timestep embedding $t$, form the conditioning signal $s_{cond}:= s_N + t$ for the subsequent pixel-level pathway.

- **Pixel-level Pathway (PiT Blocks):**
 - **Role:** This pathway is dedicated to dense per-pixel token modeling, focusing on refining local texture details based on the semantic context provided by the patch-level pathway.
 - **Input:** Raw pixel tokens are derived by reshaping the input image (or an intermediate representation) into individual pixel tokens, which are then linearly embedded into a lower hidden dimension $D_{pix} \ll D$ (e.g., $D_{pix}=16$). These pixel tokens, along with $s_{cond}$, serve as inputs.
 - **Processing:** Composed of $M$ layers of PiT (Pixel Transformer) blocks. The design uses a reduced hidden dimension $D_{pix}$ to keep dense per-pixel computations efficient.

#### 3.2. Key Techniques within PiT Blocks for Efficient Pixel Modeling

Each PiT block incorporates two core components to enable efficient and detailed pixel-level modeling:

- **Pixel-wise AdaLN Modulation:**
 - **Function:** This mechanism enables fine-grained conditioning at the level of individual pixels, aligning per-pixel updates with the global semantic context.
 - **Implementation:** Unlike the patch-wise AdaLN, which broadcasts parameters to all pixels within a patch, pixel-wise AdaLN generates distinct modulation parameters for each pixel. The semantic conditioning token $s_{cond} \in \mathbb{R}^{(B \cdot L) \times D}$ is passed through a linear projection $\Phi: \mathbb{R}^{D} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{p^2 \cdot 6D_{pix}}$. This projection expands $s_{cond}$ into $p^2$ sets of AdaLN parameters, each comprising six elements ($\beta_1, \gamma_1, \alpha_1, \beta_2, \gamma_2, \alpha_2$), for every pixel within its corresponding $p \times p$ patch. These learned, pixel-specific parameters are then applied to the pixel tokens, allowing for highly localized and context-aware updates.

- **Pixel Token Compaction:**
 - **Function:** To manage the computational cost of global attention over a large number of pixel tokens, this mechanism temporarily compresses pixel tokens within each patch before applying global attention and expands them afterward.
 - **Implementation:** Within each $p \times p$ patch, the $p^2$ pixel tokens (each of dimension $D_{pix}$) are compressed into a single, compact patch token of dimension $D$ using a learned linear map $\mathcal{C}: \mathbb{R}^{p^2 \times D_{pix}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{D}$. Global attention then operates on this much shorter sequence of $L = (H/p)(W/p)$ compacted tokens, reducing the attention sequence length by a factor of $p^2$ (e.g., 256x for $p=16$). After attention, a learned expansion $\mathcal{E}: \mathbb{R}^{D} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{p^2 \times D_{pix}}$ reconstructs the pixel-level representation.
 - **Detail Preservation:** The authors emphasize that this compaction is purely for computational efficiency during attention and is not a lossy bottleneck. High-frequency information is preserved through residual connections and the learned expansion layers, which effectively bypass potential information loss.

#### 3.3. PixelDiT for Text-to-Image Generation (PixelDiT-T2I)

- **Extension:** For text-to-image generation, the patch-level pathway is augmented with Multi-Modal DiT (MM-DiT) blocks. These blocks integrate text embeddings $y$ (obtained from a frozen Gemma-2 encoder) with image semantics.
- **Pixel-level Pathway:** The pixel-level pathway remains architecturally identical to the class-conditioned model. It operates only on pixel tokens, conditioned solely by the semantic tokens from the patch-level pathway and the timestep embedding, without direct routing of text tokens to the pixel stream. This design suggests that semantic tokens from the patch level are sufficient to convey textual intent for pixel-level refinement.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

- **Rectified Flow (RF) Formulation:** The model is trained using the Rectified Flow formulation in pixel space, employing a velocity-matching loss: $\mathcal{L}_{diff} = \mathbb{E}_{t,x,\epsilon} \left[ \|f_{\theta}(x_t, t, y) - v_t\|^2_2 \right]$.
- **Representation Alignment Objective (REPA):** An additional alignment objective $\mathcal{L}_{repa}$ is included, which encourages mid-level patch-pathway tokens to align with features extracted from a frozen DINOv2 encoder. The overall training objective is $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{diff} + \lambda_{repa} \mathcal{L}_{repa}$. This objective is applied consistently for both class-conditional and text-conditional models.

#### 3.5. Implementation and Scaling

PixelDiT is instantiated in various model sizes (Base, Large, Extra Large) and trained on ImageNet-1K. For text-to-image, it is trained at 1024x1024 resolution on a dataset of approximately 26 million image-text pairs. Standard training practices such as logit-normal sampling, EMA, AdamW optimizer, and bfloat16 mixed precision are utilized.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the performance and capabilities of PixelDiT across various image generation tasks.

#### 4.1. Class-conditioned Image Generation on ImageNet

- **ImageNet 256x256:** PixelDiT-XL achieves a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of 1.61 after 320 epochs, which is reported to significantly outperform existing pixel-space models. For instance, PixelFlow-XL, PixNerd-XL, and EPG-XXL/16 reported FIDs of 1.98, 1.93, and 1.81, respectively. Beyond fidelity, PixelDiT-XL exhibits a balanced trade-off between diversity and faithfulness, with a recall of 0.64 (compared to 0.60 for PixelFlow-XL) and a precision of 0.78.
 - The model also demonstrates rapid convergence. After only 80 epochs, PixelDiT-XL reached an FID of 2.36 and an Inception Score (IS) of 282.3, surpassing older pixel diffusion models like ADM-U (FID 4.59 at 400 epochs) and autoregressive baselines like JetFormer (FID 6.64). This rapid convergence suggests that the structured pixel modeling approach is effective in accelerating learning.
- **ImageNet 512x512:** At this higher resolution, PixelDiT achieves an FID of 1.81, an sFID of 5.61, an IS of 278.6, a precision of 0.78, and a recall of 0.67. These metrics indicate performance superior to prior pixel-space models such as EPG-L/32 and PixNerd-XL, and also surpass some latent-space models like REPA (FID 2.08), notably without using a pretrained autoencoder. Qualitative results (Figure 4, Figure 6) show generated images with sharp textures, coherent object boundaries, and plausible global structures, supporting the effectiveness of the dual-level architecture.

#### 4.2. Text-to-Image Generation

- **Quantitative Performance:** PixelDiT-T2I demonstrates competitive performance in text-to-image generation:
 - At 512x512 resolution, it achieves a GenEval score of 0.78 and a DPG score of 83.7, outperforming recent pixel-space models like PixNerd and PixelFlow.
 - At 1024x1024 resolution, PixelDiT-T2I achieves a GenEval score of 0.74 and a DPG score of 83.5. These scores are competitive with state-of-the-art latent diffusion models, surpassing many on GenEval and remaining comparable on DPG benchmarks, despite typically having fewer parameters.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Figure 1a and additional visualizations in the appendix illustrate the model's ability to synthesize high-fidelity, text-aligned images at megapixel scales with consistent multi-resolution generation.
- **Efficiency:** PixelDiT-T2I exhibits practical sampling throughput. On a single NVIDIA A100 GPU (fp16 precision), it achieves 1.07 samples per second at 512x512 resolution, surpassing other pixel-space baselines. At 1024x1024 resolution, it reaches 0.33 samples per second, which is comparable to latent diffusion models, despite directly operating in pixel space.

#### 4.3. Detail Preservation in Image Editing

- The paper demonstrates that PixelDiT's operation directly in pixel space avoids the VAE reconstruction artifacts inherent to latent diffusion models. This is shown in image editing tasks using FlowEdit, where PixelDiT preserves fine details (e.g., small scene text on a wall) significantly better than latent models like Stable Diffusion 3 and FLUX (Figure 1b). Quantitative analysis on the FlowEdit dataset (Table 14) shows PixelDiT achieving substantially better background consistency, with 6.0x lower MSE and higher SSIM compared to FLUX in unedited regions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Contribution of Core Components:**
 - A baseline "Vanilla DiT/16" (patch-only, no pixel pathway) yielded a high FID of 9.84.
 - Introducing a dual-level architecture without pixel token compaction resulted in out-of-memory (OOM) errors, highlighting the necessity of compaction.
 - Adding Pixel Token Compaction significantly improved FID to 3.50.
 - Further incorporating Pixel-wise AdaLN led to the final FID of 2.36 (at 80 epochs) and 1.61 (at 320 epochs). These results underscore the critical role of both mechanisms for efficiency and detail preservation.
- **Analysis on Pixel Token Compaction:** The study confirms that pixel token compaction is essential for computational feasibility. Removing self-attention from the pixel-level pathway ("No Pixel-Pathway Attention") consistently degraded performance (e.g., FID 2.56 vs. 2.36), indicating that compact global attention is vital for aligning local updates with global context.
- **Impact of Model Size and Patch Size:**
 - Smaller patch sizes generally lead to faster convergence and better image quality for smaller models.
 - However, the benefits of very small patches diminish as model capacity increases, with `p=16` often providing a near-optimal balance of quality and computational cost for larger models like PixelDiT-XL.
 - Scaling up model capacity (from Base to Extra Large) consistently improved both image quality and convergence speed.
- **Representation Alignment (REPA):** Ablation showed that removing the REPA loss led to significantly worse FID and IS scores and training instability, indicating its importance for stable and high-quality training.
- **Pixel Token Compaction Rate:** Exploring different compaction rates (e.g., compressing $p^2$ pixels to 1, 2, or 4 tokens) indicated that the most aggressive compression (1 token per patch) yielded slightly better or comparable results, suggesting that a compact representation is sufficient and can aid optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of PixelDiT represents a notable advancement in the field of generative artificial intelligence, particularly for image synthesis. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to demonstrate high-fidelity image generation directly in pixel space, challenging conventional approaches.

**Advances Pixel-Space Generative Modeling:** PixelDiT provides empirical evidence that the performance gap between pixel-space and latent-space diffusion models can be substantially narrowed or even closed. This finding suggests that the historical reliance on latent spaces, primarily for efficiency, may not be a fundamental necessity for high-quality generation. It positions pixel-space models as a more competitive and viable paradigm for future research and applications.

**Eliminates the Autoencoder Bottleneck:** By operating end-to-end in pixel space, PixelDiT bypasses the reconstruction bottleneck inherent to VAEs in latent diffusion models. This elimination addresses issues such as lossy compression, which can degrade fine details, and the potential misalignment between reconstruction and generative objectives. The resulting single-stage pipeline simplifies the overall model architecture and training process, potentially reducing development complexity and resource overhead associated with managing a separate autoencoder.

**Introduces an Efficient Pixel Modeling Paradigm:** The proposed dual-level architecture, coupled with pixel-wise AdaLN modulation and pixel token compaction, offers a novel and effective strategy for managing the high computational demands of pixel-space modeling. This methodology disentangles the learning of global semantics from the refinement of pixel-level details, enabling efficient processing without sacrificing fine-grained fidelity. This architectural design could serve as a foundational blueprint for future pixel-space generative models, inspiring new approaches to high-dimensional data processing.

**Scalability and Broad Applicability:** The demonstration of PixelDiT's scalability to high-resolution (1024x1024) text-to-image generation, achieving competitive performance against sophisticated latent diffusion models, broadens the potential applications of pixel-space models. This suggests that end-to-end pixel-space diffusion can be a robust solution for complex generative tasks requiring strong text-image alignment and compositional reasoning.

**Enhanced Image Editing and Detail Preservation:** A direct and practical impact of PixelDiT's pixel-space operation is its superior preservation of fine details in unedited regions during image manipulation tasks. This capability addresses a critical limitation of latent diffusion models, where VAE-induced distortions can compromise the integrity of background elements. This makes PixelDiT particularly valuable for professional creative workflows where high-fidelity editing and background consistency are paramount.

**Foundation for Future Research:** The insights derived from PixelDiT, particularly regarding the importance of efficient pixel modeling and the role of components like pixel-wise AdaLN and token compaction, can guide future research efforts. It opens avenues for exploring alternative architectural designs, optimization strategies, and noise scheduling techniques specifically tailored for pixel-space diffusion, potentially leading to further breakthroughs in generative AI.
