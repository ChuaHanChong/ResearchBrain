# Frontis-MA1: Training an AI4AI Model towards Recursive Self-Improvement in Machine Learning Engineering

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Horizon Research, Frontis.AI, Tsinghua University, and others developed OpenMLE, a full-stack system enabling AI agents to recursively improve in machine learning engineering. The system, including the Frontis-MA1-35B model, achieved a 71.21% Medal Average and 0.8126 Human Rank on MLE-Bench Lite, improving by over 30 percentage points in Medal Average compared to its base language model, and demonstrated transferability to scientific AutoResearch tasks.

## Problem

- The emerging field of AI for AI (AI4AI) and Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) lacks concrete, measurable testbeds for development and evaluation.
- Existing AI agents for Machine Learning Engineering (MLE) often lack a unified, reproducible, full-stack solution that spans task construction, execution-grounded agent training, and long-horizon evolutionary search.
- Developing AI systems that can learn to improve other AI systems (meta-evolution) faces challenges with delayed, noisy, and heterogeneous feedback from complex, time-consuming experiments.

## Method

- Introduction of **OpenMLE**, a full-stack system comprising ==OpenMLE-Gym== (scalable, verifiable MLE environments), ==OpenMLE-ERL== (execution-grounded reinforcement learning for agents), and ==OpenMLE-Evo== (experience-driven long-horizon search).
- Training a meta-evolution agent, **Frontis-MA1**, using a shared interface of four atomic ==program-evolution operators== (Draft, Improve, Debug, Crossover) that are targeted for both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.
- Implementation of advanced mechanisms within OpenMLE-Evo, such as ==multi-factor parent selection== (balancing quality, progress, and novelty) and operation-triggered, bounded memory synthesis, to enable efficient and sustained long-horizon search.

## Results

- Frontis-MA1-35B, when integrated with OpenMLE-Evo-Max, achieved a **71.21%** Medal Average and **0.8126** Human Rank on MLE-Bench Lite, outperforming its base language model by over **30** percentage points in Medal Average.
- The system demonstrated sustained performance improvement over a 12-hour search budget, with later Debug, Crossover, and Improve operations accounting for **85.0%** to **91.9%** of total validation gains by accumulating and recombining evidence.
- OpenMLE reduced search cost by decreasing total model token consumption by **41.7%** and prompt tokens by **50.3%**, while also showing strong generalization by improving Match-SOTA by **20** percentage points on NatureBench Lite.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining execution-grounded post-training with experience-driven evolutionary search yields complementary performance gains, enabling AI systems to effectively learn from and adapt to real-world ML experiment feedback.
> - Structuring agent capabilities into atomic, reusable program-transformation operators (Draft, Improve, Debug, Crossover) facilitates both efficient training and flexible composition for complex, long-horizon problem-solving.
> - Multi-factor parent selection and context-aware memory construction within an evolutionary harness significantly improve search efficiency and the ability to escape local optima by promoting diverse exploration and targeted recombination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research situates itself within the emerging field of AI for AI (AI4AI), where artificial intelligence systems are employed to build and improve other AI systems. The ultimate aspiration within this domain is Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI), wherein each iteration of an improved AI system contributes to enhancing the process that generates its successors. Current AI capabilities extend beyond human engineering, encompassing code generation, experimentation, design search, and assistance in developing subsequent AI generations.

Machine learning engineering (MLE) is presented as a concrete and measurable testbed for exploring AI4AI and RSI. In this context, an AI agent is tasked with constructing and iteratively refining machine learning solutions based on execution feedback, often involving complex experiments that can span minutes or hours. This environment offers a demanding setting to study AI system improvement under delayed, noisy, and heterogeneous feedback.

Prior research in MLE agents has progressed along three primary lines: the development of inference-time harnesses (e.g., structured or evolutionary search), the creation of executable tasks and environments, and the post-training of MLE agents using execution feedback. While some existing systems integrate subsets of these functionalities, the paper identifies a gap in jointly spanning scalable task and environment construction, execution-grounded agent post-training, and an evolutionary harness that deploys trained agents in long-horizon search, alongside releasing the necessary artifacts for full reproduction. This work aims to bridge this gap by offering a full-stack system for reproducible research on executable AI4AI towards RSI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a full-stack system, named OpenMLE, that enables AI systems to recursively self-improve in the domain of machine learning engineering. The specific aim is to train a meta-evolution agent, Frontis-MA1, capable of enhancing the process of building AI solutions through iterative experimentation and learning from execution feedback.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Advancing AI4AI and RSI:** The broader goal is to contribute to the realization of recursive self-improvement. The authors propose that directly studying AI systems that improve other AI systems (AI4AI) is a practical step towards this ambitious long-term objective. MLE is chosen as a concrete, executable testbed because it requires agents to inspect data, propose algorithms, execute experiments, diagnose failures, and manage computational resources, mirroring the multifaceted demands of AI research and development.
- **Addressing limitations of existing systems:** Current research, while advancing specific aspects of MLE automation (e.g., inference harnesses, environments, or post-training), often lacks a unified, open, and reproducible full-stack solution. Many systems do not jointly cover the entire loop from verifiable task creation to execution-grounded agent training and deployment in long-horizon search. This fragmented landscape hinders systematic research into how learning and evolution can be coupled.
- **Enabling meta-evolution:** The work specifically aims to implement a "meta-evolutionary loop." This concept involves reusing the experience generated during evolutionary search trajectories (i.e., attempts to improve ML solutions) to train the underlying language model that proposes future modifications. This means the "improver" (the AI agent) itself is trained, leading to a system that can learn to evolve more effectively over time.
- **Developing reusable evolutionary operators:** Instead of training agents on full, complex trajectories, a key motivation is to train a compact set of reusable program-transformation operators (Draft, Improve, Debug, Crossover). This modular approach aims to make the learned skills transferable and composable by different evolutionary search procedures, enhancing flexibility and efficiency.
- **Providing a reproducible research platform:** A significant motivation is to release the complete OpenMLE stack, including datasets, training/evaluation code, sandbox infrastructure, harness code, and model weights. This open release aims to foster reproducible research and accelerate progress in meta-evolution within executable AI4AI settings.
- **Improving efficiency and quality in MLE:** Practically, the work seeks to demonstrate that execution-grounded post-training and experience-driven long-horizon search can lead to more efficient exploration, higher-quality solutions, and better generalization across diverse machine learning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces OpenMLE, a full-stack system for training and deploying AI agents in Machine Learning Engineering (MLE), structured around three interconnected components: OpenMLE-Gym, OpenMLE-ERL, and OpenMLE-Evo. A core design principle is the shared interface of four atomic program-evolution operators: Draft, Improve, Debug, and Crossover, which are targets for post-training and building blocks for inference-time search.

#### 3.1. OpenMLE-Gym: Scalable Verifiable Environments

OpenMLE-Gym provides a scalable and verifiable environment for MLE tasks.
- **Environment Contract:** Each task is defined by a natural-language specification, public data assets, a hidden evaluator, resource budget, and workspace state. Agents submit MLE programs (actions) which are executed in a sandboxed environment. Observations include structured records of execution status, task scores, logs, error types, and runtime metadata, providing informative feedback.
- **Scalable Task Construction:** Tasks are sourced from three complementary paths:
 - **Curated Anchors:** Manually selected high-quality tasks from existing literature and benchmarks.
 - **Kaggle Datasets:** Automatically processed datasets, extended from the MLE-Smith pipeline, with induced objectives and package-level quality control.
 - **Kaggle Competitions:** Utilizes a custom pipeline to convert Kaggle competition slugs into standardized executable packages, including problem specifications, evaluation metrics, and submission protocols. This process involves automated file perception, task description generation, and `prepare.py`/`metric.py` script generation and validation. MLE-Bench overlapping competitions are excluded.
- **Task Quality Filtering:** An LLM-based filter inspects task descriptions, raw files, scripts, and data samples to ensure quality across five dimensions: task validity, data sufficiency, raw-data usage, task complexity, and data quality. Only tasks passing strict semantic quality gates are retained.
- **Sandbox Execution:** A centralized scheduler dispatches agent-submitted programs to isolated CPU/GPU Docker workers. Each worker executes the program, invokes the task-specific evaluator, and returns structured feedback, including execution status, score, logs, error type, and runtime metadata. This supports reproducible scoring and scalable parallel execution.
- **Composition and Statistics:** OpenMLE-Gym comprises 5,758 tasks (156 Curated Anchors, 3,362 Kaggle Datasets, 2,240 Kaggle Competitions) covering various data modalities and ML task types.

#### 3.2. OpenMLE-ERL: Reinforcing Reusable Evolutionary Operators

This component trains the AI agent (Frontis-MA1) to perform the four atomic evolutionary operations.
- **Trainable Atomic Operators:** Draft (create new code), Improve (refine code), Debug (repair code), and Crossover (merge parents) are defined as explicit targets for both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). This separation allows different search algorithms to compose these learned skills.
- **Execution-Grounded Supervised Warm Start:**
 - **Budget-Adaptive Collection:** Programs are sampled and executed in the sandbox. Collection for a task stops when an accepted-example quota is reached or its execution budget is exhausted, directing compute to tasks where further exploration is likely to yield useful supervision.
 - **Parallel Path:** Independently samples and executes complete Draft solutions, retaining high-scoring, valid candidates (e.g., Top-4 within a task, with deduplication).
 - **Evolutionary Path:** Applies Improve, Debug, and Crossover operators on executed programs within search trees. A separate LLM-based annotator identifies and retains useful steps from high-quality local trajectory segments based on a "causal inheritance" criterion.
 - **SFT Corpus:** The collected examples (26,259 in total) form the SFT corpus for warm-starting the policy model.
- **Execution-Grounded Reinforcement Learning:**
 - **Making Heterogeneous Outcomes Comparable:** Raw task scores (e.g., accuracy, log loss) are converted into a unified, direction-aligned signed score. An adaptive reward function then rescales these scores to a range using dynamic bounds derived from the task's historical on-policy score frontier, preserving discriminative feedback.
 - **Concentrating Learning Signal on the Upper Tail:** An entropic advantage function amplifies reward gaps among the best candidates within a rollout group, directing more learning signal to top-performing programs, similar to Best-of-N objectives.
 - **Asynchronous Rollouts:** To mitigate latency from variable sandbox execution times, generation-and-execution groups are launched independently. Completed groups are immediately consumed by the trainer, decoupling policy updates from the slowest jobs in a batch.
 - **Selecting Informative States for Operator Learning:** Parents for evolutionary operations are sampled based on a fitness-proportional utility combining three terms: normalized reward of the parent, variance of child rewards (to identify regions with unresolved learning signal), and a visit-cooling coefficient (to prevent single incumbents from monopolizing rollout budget). This promotes diverse and informative learning contexts.

#### 3.3. OpenMLE-Evo: Scaling Experience-driven Long-Horizon Search

This component orchestrates the trained operators for long-horizon search at inference time.
- **Structured Experience Accumulation:**
 - **Experience Card:** After each sandbox evaluation, a node-level experience card is created, capturing provenance, performance, execution outcome, resource usage, and a coarse method family.
 - **Experience Board:** Aggregates all experience cards into a task-global board, providing population-level statistics, method-family coverage, progress trends, and failure patterns.
- **Experience-Guided Parent Selection:** Unlike prior work relying mainly on scalar fitness, OpenMLE-Evo selects parents using a multi-factor utility combining:
 - **Normalized Validation Score:** Current solution quality.
 - **Normalized Positive Improvement:** Progress relative to the strongest parent.
 - **Method-Family Novelty:** Algorithmic distinctness of the solution direction.
 This creates a more comprehensive expansion policy, balancing exploitation of high-quality solutions with exploration of promising, underexplored approaches.
- **Operation-Triggered Memory Synthesis:** Language model-based memory summarization is deferred. Instead of eager synthesis, rich natural-language memory is generated on-demand only when an Improve, Crossover, or Debug operation selects relevant nodes. This reduces inference budget and ensures the summary is tailored to the specific operator and context.
- **Operator-Conditioned Context Construction:** For each operation, a bounded, operator-conditioned context is constructed. For example, Improve operations receive the selected node's deterministic experience record, recent ancestors, direct siblings (ranked by utility), and relevant global board statistics. Debug operations retrieve prior attempts with the same error signature. This approach avoids ever-growing histories and provides directly actionable evidence for refinement, recombination, or repair.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, efficiency, and generalization of the OpenMLE system and Frontis-MA1-35B. Evaluation is conducted on MLE-Bench Lite (22 tasks) with a 12 GPU-hour per-task budget and on NatureBench Lite (10 tasks) for transfer. Metrics include Valid Rate, Medal Average, and Human Rank for MLE-Bench Lite, and Match-SOTA and Surpass-SOTA for NatureBench Lite.

- **Training and Search Gains Composition:**
 - **Post-training effect:** Under the identical OpenMLE-Evo harness, Frontis-MA1-35B improves its Medal Average from 39.39% (base Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) to 60.61%. The Human Rank increases from 0.5828 to 0.7647. A companion model, Frontis-MA1-30B, shows similar gains, improving from 34.85% to 53.03% Medal Average over its Qwen3-30B-A3B base.
 - **System-level performance:** Combining Frontis-MA1-35B with OpenMLE-Evo-Max (enhanced configuration with cross-task priors and asynchronous search) achieves 71.21% Medal Average and 0.8126 Human Rank, surpassing GPT-5.5 + Codex (68.18%) and approaching GPT-5.6 Sol and Kimi K3 (both 72.73%). This indicates that training and search mechanisms provide complementary improvements.
 - **Harness gains:** OpenMLE-Evo consistently converts various models (e.g., GLM-5.2, MiniMax M3, Kimi K2.6) into stronger MLE systems compared to general-purpose coding-agent harnesses like Claude Code and Codex. For Frontis-MA1-35B, OpenMLE-Evo increases Medal Average from 53.03% (original AIRA-Evo) to 60.61%.

- **Long-Horizon Self-Improvement:**
 - OpenMLE-Evo demonstrates sustained improvement throughout the 12-hour search budget, with later Improve and Crossover operations yielding substantial performance gains. The final solutions generalize effectively from validation to test sets.
 - Case studies (e.g., `leaf-classification`, `mlsp-2013-birds`) show that Frontis-MA1-35B uses Debug to establish viable branches, then leverages Crossover to integrate complementary evidence and Improve to refine the fused models. These later operations account for a significant portion (85.0% and 91.9%) of the total validation gain, indicating that the long horizon is used for accumulating and recombining useful branch evidence rather than merely repairing single programs.

- **Solution Ceiling:**
 - The improvements are not limited to crossing the Bronze medal threshold. Stronger systems, particularly Frontis-MA1-35B with OpenMLE-Evo-Max, produce a larger share of Gold solutions, indicating improved solution quality rather than just increased coverage of lower medal tiers.

- **Search Efficiency and Mechanism:**
 - **Lower search cost:** Compared to original AIRA-Evo, OpenMLE-Evo reduces total model token consumption by 41.7% and prompt tokens by 50.3% while only slightly decreasing the number of evaluated nodes (12.4%). This suggests efficiency gains primarily from cheaper expansions.
 - **Higher search yield:** OpenMLE-Evo records more new-best validation updates per million total model tokens (+84.3%) and a higher fraction of Improve operations resulting in new best solutions (from 4.73% to 9.36%).
 - **Bounded operation context:** Mean serialized user-prompt length for Improve and Crossover operations is reduced by 65.3% and 60.6% respectively, with even larger reductions in the 99th percentile (86.1% and 81.3%). This indicates that structured, operation-conditioned memory effectively prevents long histories from being repeatedly serialized.
 - **Targeted recombination and multi-factor selection:** Case studies (e.g., `nomad2018-predict-transparent-conductors`, `the-icml-2013-whale-challenge-right-whale-redux`) illustrate how OpenMLE-Evo's targeted Crossover escapes single-branch Debug loops by fusing complementary evidence, and how three-factor parent selection preserves high-gain, structurally distinct branches that might be discarded by score-only selection.

- **Meta-Ability and Transfer:**
 - **Cross-modality gains:** On MLE-Bench Lite, Frontis-MA1-35B demonstrates broad improvements across all five evaluated modalities (image, text, tabular/structured, audio, multimodal), increasing mean Human Rank and never lowering Medal Rate in any group.
 - **Generalization to NatureBench Lite:** On a 10-task NatureBench Lite subset (spanning diverse scientific domains and ML task types), Frontis-MA1-35B improves Match-SOTA by 20 percentage points (from 5/10 to 7/10) and Surpass-SOTA by 10 points (from 2/10 to 3/10) over its base model under the NatureBench adapter. The OpenMLE-Evo NatureBench adapter itself improves Match-SOTA by 30 points (from 2/10 to 5/10) over original AIRA-Evo. The combined system matches the performance of GPT-5.4, GLM-5.1, and MiniMax-M3 on this subset, demonstrating transfer beyond competition-style MLE.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents OpenMLE as an open, full-stack technical solution for the study of meta-evolution in executable AI for AI (AI4AI), providing a concrete testbed for research into recursive self-improvement (RSI). The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Reproducible Research for AI4AI and RSI:** By releasing the OpenMLE stack—including datasets, training/evaluation code, sandbox infrastructure, evolutionary harness, and trained model weights—the authors aim to enable reproducible research. This facilitates broader study of meta-evolutionary loops, where the AI system itself learns to improve the process of building other AI, which is a key step towards achieving RSI.
- **Unified Framework for MLE Agent Development:** OpenMLE integrates three critical components: scalable verifiable task environments (OpenMLE-Gym), execution-grounded agent post-training (OpenMLE-ERL), and experience-driven long-horizon search (OpenMLE-Evo). This holistic approach addresses the fragmentation often observed in prior work, where individual components are developed in isolation. The shared operator interface (Draft, Improve, Debug, Crossover) between training and inference promotes a cohesive learning and search paradigm.
- **Advancements in Execution-Grounded Learning:** The methodology demonstrates how execution feedback, even when delayed and heterogeneous, can be effectively leveraged for both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. Techniques like budget-adaptive SFT data collection, adaptive reward normalization, entropic advantage for upper-tail signal amplification, and asynchronous rollouts contribute to more efficient and effective learning from real-world execution outcomes.
- **Improved Long-Horizon Search Capabilities:** OpenMLE-Evo introduces innovations in evolutionary search, moving beyond greedy score maximization. The use of structured experience accumulation, multi-factor parent selection (incorporating quality, progress, and novelty), and operation-triggered, bounded memory synthesis allows for more intelligent exploration, efficient resource allocation, and targeted recombination of solutions over extended search horizons. This leads to sustained improvement and higher-quality solutions.
- **Demonstrated Performance and Generalization:** The Frontis-MA1-35B model, trained and deployed within OpenMLE, achieves notable performance improvements on MLE-Bench Lite, surpassing its base model and competing favorably with other frontier AI systems, including some that rely on larger model sizes or more computational resources. The demonstrated transferability to NatureBench Lite provides initial evidence that the learned meta-ability is not confined to competition-style MLE but extends to scientific AutoResearch problems, indicating potential for broader application in AI-driven discovery.
- **Shift Towards Higher-Quality Solutions:** The analysis of medal distributions suggests that the system not only increases the number of valid solutions but also drives solutions towards higher quality tiers (e.g., Gold medals), indicating a qualitative improvement in problem-solving capability.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The authors acknowledge that OpenMLE does not yet fully realize the vision of RSI and identify several areas for future development:
- **Richer Objectives:** Moving beyond simple execution outcomes to include objectives for judging research directions, generalizability, robustness, and transferable strategies.
- **Integrated Search and Agents:** Combining the evolutionary search harness with general coding agents to expand the range of autonomous actions.
- **Broader AI Development Participation:** Enabling agents to engage in more aspects of AI development, especially self-improvement of language models themselves.
- **Evolving the Evolutionary System:** Making the evolutionary system (not just candidate solutions) an object of evolution.
- **Richer Use of Experience:** Incorporating more factors into parent selection and learning task-dependent factor weights, moving towards an evolutionary system that discovers its own search behavior.

In conclusion, OpenMLE provides a significant contribution by integrating previously disparate elements into a coherent, open, and reproducible full-stack system for studying AI4AI. Its demonstrated performance and generalizability, coupled with a clear roadmap for future enhancements, position it as a foundational platform for advancing research towards recursive self-improvement in AI.
