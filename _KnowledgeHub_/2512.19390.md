# TwinAligner: Visual-Dynamic Alignment Empowers Physics-aware Real2Sim2Real for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed TwinAligner, a unified Real2Sim2Real system that precisely aligns both visual and dynamic properties between real and simulated environments. The system enables policies trained purely in simulation to achieve strong zero-shot generalization to the real world and provides reliable cross-environment policy evaluation for robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- High cost and logistical challenges of collecting sufficient, diverse, and high-quality real-world data for robot learning.
- The persistent "Sim2Real gap," characterized by visual and dynamic discrepancies, prevents policies learned in simulation from reliably transferring to the real world.
- Lack of a robust, unified framework that simultaneously addresses both visual and dynamic alignment for physics-aware robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A "Mesh-GS Digital Twin" module reconstructs objects, scenes, and robots using a combination of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for photorealistic rendering and SDF/mesh for accurate collision geometry.
- A "Visual-Dynamic Real2Sim Alignment" module aligns simulation viewpoint and rigid physics parameters (friction, mass, center of mass, robot controller) using gradient-free optimization, robust to rendering errors.
- The aligned environment supports "Sim2Real Policy Learning" for zero-shot generalization to the real world and allows for trustworthy cross-environment evaluation of robotic policies.

## Results

- Achieved superior object digital twin quality with a PSNR of 38.03 for rendering and consistent, watertight meshes across diverse object types, outperforming baselines.
- Demonstrated significantly improved dynamic alignment, reducing average ADD to 1.39 cm and ADD-S to 0.78 cm for object trajectories, showing better consistency with real-world physics than previous methods.
- Enabled strong zero-shot Sim2Real generalization with policies trained in TwinAligner achieving higher success rates (e.g., 10/15 for Pushing Milk Box) across four manipulation tasks, including articulated objects, compared to other Real2Sim2Real baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining 3DGS with SDF reconstruction effectively balances high-fidelity rendering with watertight, simulator-friendly collision geometry for diverse objects and scenes.
> - Gradient-free optimization for dynamic parameter alignment provides robustness to visual errors (like relighting) and handles physically disturbed objects more effectively than differentiable simulation methods.
> - A unified approach that meticulously aligns both visual appearance and physical dynamics across objects, scenes, and robots is crucial for enabling effective physics-aware manipulation in a Real2Sim2Real pipeline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is undergoing a significant transformation, moving towards data-driven, end-to-end learning paradigms, mirroring the success of large multimodal models in other AI domains. This shift is driven by the potential for robots to learn complex behaviors directly from data, rather than through tedious manual programming or modular skill design. However, a major bottleneck in this transition is the **cost and logistical challenges of collecting sufficient, diverse, and high-quality real-world data** from embodied agents.

Physics-based simulators offer a compelling alternative, providing cost-effective and controllable environments for data generation and rapid experimentation. This has led to the emergence of **Real2Sim2Real systems**, which aim to bridge the "simulation-to-reality" (Sim2Real) gap, allowing policies learned in simulation to be effectively transferred to the real world. However, this gap is multifaceted, primarily manifesting in two critical dimensions:
1. **Visual Gap:** Mismatches in rendering fidelity, texture realism, and geometric consistency between simulated and real environments.
2. **Dynamic Gap:** Discrepancies arising from approximate physical models and inaccurate physical parameters within simulators.

Previous research efforts have attempted to address these challenges:
- **Domain Randomization:** While a common technique (e.g.,), it requires extensive data collection and high parameter variability, often compromising sample efficiency and failing to provide a strictly aligned environment for accurate policy evaluation.
- **Visual Real2Sim2Real Systems:** Many works, inspired by advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have focused on improving the visual realism of simulated environments (e.g., RialTo, SplatSim, Re3Sim). However, these often neglect the crucial aspect of dynamic alignment, limiting their applicability to physics-aware manipulation tasks.
- **Physics-aware Real2Sim Systems:** Some approaches focus on identifying real-world physical properties (e.g., Scalable Real2Sim), but often ignore the inherent differences between *simulated* and *real* physics parameters. Others use MPM-based methods for precise dynamic alignment (e.g., PhysTwin, Real-is-Sim) but suffer from generalization limitations.
- **Unified but Limited Approaches:** PIN-WM attempts to optimize physical parameters by combining differentiable rigid physics and rendering. However, its reliance on differentiability makes it sensitive to reconstruction, viewpoint, and relighting errors, thus restricting its robustness and generalizability.

**TwinAligner positions itself as a novel and significant advancement** in this landscape by introducing a unified Real2Sim2Real system that comprehensively addresses *both* visual and dynamic gaps in a robust and scalable manner. Unlike many predecessors, it explicitly aims for pixel-level visual alignment and dynamic consistency for objects, scenes, and robots simultaneously, empowering physics-aware manipulation. This work is a direct response to the need for a truly reliable and efficient iterative cycle for robot learning, moving beyond partial solutions to enable more rapid and trustworthy algorithm development and deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive and unified Real2Sim2Real system, named TwinAligner, that can precisely bridge the visual and dynamic gaps between real and simulated environments. This system aims to create a highly aligned "digital twin" of the real world, enabling more effective and scalable robot learning.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the limitations of current data-driven robotics approaches:
- **High Cost and Uncontrollability of Real-World Data:** The current reliance on expensive real-world data collection through teleoperation (as seen in initiatives like Open X-Embodiment and AgiBot World Colosseo) significantly hinders the pace and scale of data-driven imitation learning. A cost-effective alternative is desperately needed.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Simulators:** While physics-based simulators are appealing for their cost-efficiency and controllability, the persistent "Sim2Real gap" prevents policies trained in simulation from reliably transferring to the real world. This gap is multi-faceted, encompassing both visual and dynamic discrepancies.
- **Lack of Unified Solutions:** Prior works have often addressed either the visual or dynamic aspects of the Sim2Real gap in isolation, or have proposed unified systems that are too sensitive, limited in scope, or not robust enough for complex manipulation tasks. A truly comprehensive and robust alignment framework is missing.
- **Need for Reliable Policy Evaluation:** Without a strictly aligned simulation environment, it is challenging to accurately evaluate robotic policies and iterate on algorithms effectively. This slows down the development cycle for robot learning.

To address these motivations, TwinAligner sets forth several specific objectives:
- **Achieve Pixel-level Visual Alignment:** Reconstruct visual assets (objects, scenes, robots) with high fidelity to real-world appearance, ensuring consistent rendering and geometric representation.
- **Ensure Dynamic Consistency:** Accurately identify and align rigid physics parameters (friction, mass, center of mass, robot controller) to match real-world physical interactions.
- **Enable Efficient Data Collection:** Provide a method for generating high-quality, physics-aware synthetic data that closely mimics real-world data, thereby enhancing sample efficiency.
- **Establish a Trustworthy Iterative Cycle:** Create a closed-loop system for data collection, model training, and model evaluation that accurately reflects real-world performance, accelerating algorithm development and iteration.
- **Support Zero-Shot Sim2Real Generalization:** Enable policies trained purely in the aligned simulation to directly transfer to the real world without further real-world fine-tuning.
- **Empower Physics-Aware Manipulation:** Specifically cater to challenging high-dynamics and non-prehensile manipulation scenarios, which are highly sensitive to physical accuracy.

By achieving these objectives, TwinAligner aims to significantly advance scalable robot learning by making simulation a much more reliable and efficient testbed for real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TwinAligner introduces a novel, unified Real2Sim2Real framework designed to tackle both visual and dynamic alignment challenges. The methodology is structured into three main steps: Mesh-GS Digital Twin, Visual-Dynamic Real2Sim Alignment, and Sim2Real Policy Learning.

#### 3.1. Mesh-GS Digital Twin for Physics Simulation

This module focuses on creating a precise digital replica (digital twin) of the real-world scenario, ensuring both high-quality visual rendering and accurate geometry for physical collision in the simulator.
- **Input:** Multiview RGB images of objects and scenes, captured using a smartphone.
- **Output:** Detailed 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations (G_obj, G_scene) for photorealistic rendering and continuous collision meshes (ϕ_obj, ϕ_scene) for physics simulation. For robots, 3DGS (G_robot) and articulated models (G'_robot) are derived.
- **Rigid Object Reconstruction:** Combines SDF (Signed Distance Function) representation for watertight meshes (ϕ_obj) with 3DGS for high-quality rendering (G_obj). The 3DGS is initialized from mesh vertices and optimized with various losses including L1 RGB, SSIM, mask loss, and monocular depth loss to suppress artifacts and ensure visual and geometric fidelity. This approach balances rendering quality with simulator-friendly collision geometry.
- **Articulated Object Adaptation:** For objects with movable parts, the method integrates with monocular articulation estimation techniques like 3DOI. An automated pipeline uses 3DOI's annotations to segment the 3DGS and meshes into parts, and estimates 3D articulation axes, making articulated objects compatible with the digital twin framework.
- **Scene and Robot Reconstruction:** The scene's 3DGS (G_scene) is optimized using planar-based PGSR for geometry continuity and rendering quality, with its mesh (ϕ_scene) extracted via TSDF fusion. For the robot, its URDF (U_robot) is aligned with the reconstructed scene's 3DGS using ICP and k-NN algorithms to obtain the transformation (T_scene2robot) and extract the robot's 3DGS (G_robot), to which forward kinematics are applied. All assets are then converted into a unified robot axis system for simulation.

#### 3.2. Visual-Dynamic Real2Sim Alignment

After building the digital twin, this module aligns the simulation's viewpoint and dynamics with the real world. The core idea is to treat the 3DGS (G) and meshes (ϕ) as fixed from the previous step, and optimize for viewpoint (p) and dynamics parameters (θ).
- **Viewpoint Alignment:**
 - Estimates the robot-to-camera transformation (T_robot2cam).
 - Uses an off-the-shelf object pose estimator, FoundationPose, to get a coarse initial estimate (T_coarse) from an RGB-D image of the robot.
 - Refines this pose using a gradient-free optimizer by minimizing a binary cross-entropy (BCE) mask matching loss (L_BCE). This loss compares silhouettes (M' and M'f) of obvious objects (robot, workspace) in real and rendered images, obtained using Segment Anything 2. This gradient-free approach enhances compatibility with non-differentiable simulators.
- **Dynamic Alignment:** Addresses the rigid physics parameters: friction (θ_friction), mass (θ_mass), center of mass (θ_com), and robot controller parameters (θ_robot).
 - **Data Collection Strategy:** A "Control-Hit-Slide" interaction sequence is proposed. The robot controls, hits the object, and the object slides, exposing various physical attributes.
 - **Robot Dynamics Alignment:** Identifies robot controller parameters (e.g., stiffness, damping) by minimizing the L2 loss (L_robot, Eq. 3) between the joint positions resulting from real and simulated PD control functions given the same control signals. This ensures that the robot's simulated motion matches its real counterpart.
 - **Object Dynamics Alignment:** Focuses on object translation and rotation during the "Slide" stage.
 - The system uses FoundationPose to estimate the 6-DoF poses ({T_i}) of the real-world object throughout its trajectory.
 - In simulation, the same robot control signals are replayed to obtain simulated poses ({^bT_i}).
 - A gradient-free optimizer is used to minimize a point cloud-based loss (L_obj, Eq. 6), combining ADD and ADD-S metrics, between the real and simulated object trajectories. This allows for optimization of rigid physics parameters (friction, mass, center of mass) while being robust to rendering errors. The gradient-free approach ensures compatibility with non-differentiable physics engines like Genesis.

#### 3.3. Sim2Real Policy Learning

With the environment meticulously aligned, TwinAligner enables two key applications for robot learning:
- **Zero-shot Policy Generalization:**
 - Expert demonstration trajectories are collected via teleoperation within the TwinAligner simulation environment, recording joint angles, end-effector states, and object states.
 - Camera observations (RGB-D images) are rendered from the simulation.
 - Imitation learning policies, such as Diffusion Policy (DP) (using RGB images and end-effector state) and RISE (using partial point clouds), are trained on this simulated data.
 - The goal is to demonstrate that these policies can achieve zero-shot generalization to the real world without further real-world data collection.
- **Cross-environment Policy Evaluation:**
 - The aligned environment allows pre-trained policies to be reliably evaluated in simulation.
 - By closing the "Sim-Render-Policy" loop, starting from randomized initial states, the policy interacts with TwinAligner, and its success rates can be measured. This provides a fast and reliable proxy for real-world performance, accelerating the iteration of models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate TwinAligner's robust capabilities in achieving both visual and dynamic Real2Sim alignment, leading to significant improvements in zero-shot Sim2Real policy generalization and reliable cross-environment policy evaluation.

#### 4.1. Real2Sim Consistency

- **Object Digital Twin Quality:**
 - **Rendering:** TwinAligner achieves the highest average PSNR (38.03) for object rendering quality compared to baselines (2DGS, 2DGS-Mono, PGSR, PGSR-Mono), indicating superior photorealism.
 - **Geometry:** The method consistently reconstructs detailed and watertight meshes for diverse objects (concave, reflective, textureless), surpassing baselines that often suffer from inaccurate depths, glitches, and holes, even with depth regularization. This highlights the effectiveness of combining SDF reconstruction with 3DGS.
- **Dynamic Real2Sim Alignment:**
 - Evaluated on four objects with varying physical characteristics, including those with disturbed physics (empty milk box, biased Oreo box).
 - TwinAligner significantly outperforms PIN-WM in aligning object trajectories, achieving lower ADD and ADD-S metrics (average ADD 1.39 cm vs. 2.37 cm; average ADD-S 0.78 cm vs. 1.28 cm).
 - This superiority is attributed to TwinAligner's gradient-free optimization, which is more robust to relighting and rendering errors, and its stability when dealing with physically disturbed objects, a challenge for differentiable simulation methods like PIN-WM. Qualitative comparisons visually confirm the pixel-level alignment of robot-object interaction trajectories.

#### 4.2. Zero-shot Sim2Real Generalization

- Policies trained in TwinAligner's simulation environment demonstrate strong zero-shot generalization to the real world across four diverse manipulation tasks: Pushing Milk Box, Stacking Biscuit Boxes, Pick-and-place, and Closing Laptop (articulated object).
- **Superior Performance:** TwinAligner consistently achieves higher success rates compared to baselines (SplatSim + Gemini, PIN-WM) for all supported tasks, regardless of whether Diffusion Policy (DP) or RISE (using RGB or point cloud observations, respectively) is used.
 - For example, in "Pushing Milk Box," TwinAligner achieved 10/15 (DP) and 7/15 (RISE) successes, compared to SplatSim + Gemini's 5/15 and 3/15, and PIN-WM's 6/15 and 5/15.
 - For "Stacking Biscuit Boxes," TwinAligner achieved 14/15 (DP) and 7/15 (RISE), while SplatSim + Gemini managed only 0/15 for both.
- **Broader Task Coverage:** Unlike baselines, TwinAligner supports all four evaluated task types, including articulated object manipulation (Closing Laptop), which SplatSim does not. PIN-WM is limited to the pushing task.
- The results indicate that TwinAligner effectively mitigates both visual and dynamic Sim2Real gaps, allowing policies trained purely on synthetic data to perform comparably well to those trained on real-world trajectories (Real2Real baseline).

#### 4.3. Cross-environment Policy Evaluation

- Experiments on the "Pushing Milk Box" task demonstrate that TwinAligner can serve as a reliable evaluator for model iteration.
- The trend of model performance with respect to the number of training trajectories (data scale) shows high consistency between the real-world environment and the TwinAligner simulation environment.
- This finding validates TwinAligner's potential to establish a robust closed-loop system for data collection, model training, and evaluation, thereby accelerating the iterative development and optimization of robot learning algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TwinAligner represents a significant step forward in addressing one of the most persistent and challenging problems in robotics: the Sim2Real gap. By providing a unified and robust solution for both visual and dynamic alignment, this work has several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Accelerated Robot Learning and Development:** The most direct impact is the acceleration of robot learning algorithm development. By offering an economical and efficient method for generating high-quality, physics-aware synthetic data, TwinAligner drastically reduces the reliance on expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection. This enables researchers to rapidly iterate on policies and explore new algorithms in a controlled, scalable environment.
- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Generalization:** The demonstrated ability of policies trained in TwinAligner's simulation to achieve strong zero-shot generalization to the real world is a critical advancement. This means that robots can learn complex manipulation skills in simulation and immediately apply them in real-world scenarios without extensive real-world fine-tuning, dramatically improving the practical deployability of learned policies.
- **Trustworthy Evaluation and Benchmarking:** TwinAligner establishes a reliable closed-loop system for data collection, model training, and, crucially, model evaluation. The high consistency between simulated and real-world policy performance metrics means that researchers can use the TwinAligner environment as a trustworthy proxy for real-world evaluation, speeding up the identification of effective policies and facilitating robust benchmarking.
- **Empowerment of Physics-Aware Manipulation:** Many challenging robotic tasks, especially those involving non-prehensile manipulation, contact-rich interactions, or objects with complex dynamics, are highly sensitive to accurate physical modeling. TwinAligner's dedicated dynamic alignment module, particularly its robustness to disturbed physical properties and its gradient-free optimization, makes it exceptionally well-suited for these physics-aware scenarios, which traditional simulators often struggle with.
- **Scalability for Embodied AI:** By making simulation a more faithful and reliable testbed, TwinAligner contributes directly to the vision of scalable robot learning and embodied AI. It paves the way for generating massive, diverse datasets in simulation that are genuinely useful for training foundation models for robotics, mimicking the data abundance seen in other AI domains.
- **Reduced Development Costs and Accessibility:** The cost-effectiveness of simulation-based data collection, coupled with improved policy transfer, lowers the barrier to entry for robotics research and development. This could enable smaller labs, startups, and individual researchers to contribute to advanced robotics without requiring extensive real-world robot hardware and infrastructure.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The limitations acknowledged by the authors (human effort for data collection, reliance on simulation engine accuracy, lack of deformable object support) highlight exciting avenues for future research. TwinAligner provides a solid foundation upon which to build more autonomous data collection systems, learn more precise NN-based world models, and expand capabilities to handle complex materials.

In conclusion, TwinAligner addresses a fundamental challenge in data-driven robotics by creating a highly aligned digital twin. Its holistic approach to bridging both visual and dynamic gaps unlocks significant potential for accelerating robot learning, ensuring reliable policy transfer, and ultimately contributing to the widespread adoption of intelligent robotic systems.
