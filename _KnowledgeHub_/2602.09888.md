# TriPilot-FF: Coordinated Whole-Body Teleoperation with Force Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> TriPilot-FF introduces a whole-body teleoperation system for mobile manipulators that integrates force-feedback on a custom foot pedal for base control and arm-side force reflection. This system improved task success rates (e.g., 100% in "BlindCarry" vs. 55% without pedal feedback), reduced collision incidents, and lowered completion times, while also providing valuable torque-augmented data for training more robust autonomous robot policies.

## Problem

- Whole-body teleoperation of mobile manipulators demands simultaneous coordination of locomotion, bimanual manipulation, collision avoidance, and task goals, leading to high operator cognitive burden.
- Existing teleoperation interfaces often neglect human lower limbs, underutilizing a robust control channel for base motion, which can increase operator workload and collision incidents.
- A lack of multi-modal feedback in current systems hinders operator awareness of constraints like obstacle proximity, reachability limits, and contact forces, resulting in less efficient operations and suboptimal data for robot learning.

## Method

- **TriPilot-FF** developed an open-source ==whole-body teleoperation system== featuring a custom 3-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) ==force-feedback foot pedal== for continuous mobile base control.
- The system provides ==multi-modal guidance==, including lidar-driven resistive force feedback on the pedal for collision avoidance and haptic/visual cues for bimanual manipulability, alongside ==arm-side force reflection== for contact awareness.
- It is designed to collect ==torque-augmented demonstration data==, integrating joint-torque signals into the ==Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)== policy to capture crucial contact and interaction dynamics for autonomous robot learning.

## Results

- TriPilot-FF achieved a **100%** success rate and zero average collisions in tasks requiring precise coordination in cluttered spaces ("BlindCarry", "NarrowTransport"), compared to a **55%** success rate and **1.0** average collisions without pedal feedback in "BlindCarry".
- Manipulability guidance reduced task completion time by over **50%** (**7.39s** vs. **15.17s**) and decreased time spent in low manipulability regions by **25%** (**30.59%** vs. **41.04%**) in reach-limited retrieval tasks.
- Augmenting ACT policies with joint-torque signals significantly improved autonomous task success rates, for example, from **22% to 50%** in simulated "CubeTransfer" and from **12% to 60%** in real-world "BasketPack" (with co-training).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging human lower limbs with a force-feedback pedal provides a robust, low-latency control channel for mobile base motion, significantly reducing operator cognitive load and improving whole-body coordination.
> - Task-relevant haptic and visual feedback, such as collision avoidance resistance and manipulability guidance, can proactively bias operator commands towards safer and kinematically more efficient behaviors.
> - Integrating teleoperation-derived force feedback, specifically joint torques, into robot learning policies like ACT, enriches the observation space and leads to substantially more robust, force-reactive, and adaptable autonomous behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mobile manipulators represent an advancement in robotic capabilities, extending the operational envelope of fixed-base robotic arms by integrating locomotion. This allows robots to interact with their environment over larger areas and recover from failed interactions through repositioning rather than re-grasping. However, this increased capability introduces a challenge in whole-body teleoperation, as operators must simultaneously coordinate a mobile base, typically with two arms, while managing obstacles, contact forces, reachability, and task intent.

Existing teleoperation interfaces predominantly employ hand-centric devices such as VR controllers and joysticks. These interfaces often allocate limited bandwidth to lower-limb control, despite human motor capabilities suggesting that feet can provide a robust, low-latency control channel suitable for base motion. This design gap often leads to operators lacking an intuitive and informative modality for continuous base control, potentially increasing collision incidents and overall coordination burden.

Furthermore, teleoperation is increasingly recognized as a critical method for data acquisition and supervision in the development of autonomous robot policies, especially with the rise of large-scale real-world datasets for robot learning. Interfaces that can reduce operator workload, prevent common failures like base collisions, and expose informative feedback channels can contribute to cleaner demonstrations and higher-quality training signals for policy learning. This work aligns with efforts to develop teleoperation systems that not only enable human control but also serve as effective data collection tools for imitation learning, particularly for visuomotor policies and transformer-based architectures like Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT).

This research distinguishes itself from prior mobile manipulation approaches, such as motion planning and control pipelines and recent learning-based systems like MobileALOHA, by introducing a dedicated foot-operated channel with haptic feedback and multi-modal guidance across both upper and lower limbs. While MobileALOHA focuses on low-cost, co-located whole-body teleoperation, TriPilot-FF emphasizes remote operation and comprehensive haptic and visual feedback to enhance operator awareness and mitigate common teleoperation challenges, providing a richer data stream for downstream policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding the development of the TriPilot-FF system are detailed as follows:

- **Address Challenges in Whole-Body Teleoperation:** The integration of a mobile base with bimanual manipulators necessitates the simultaneous coordination of locomotion, collision avoidance, and manipulation. Operators face a significant cognitive burden in continuously managing these coupled systems, including reasoning about obstacles, contact forces, reachability limits, and task goals. The work aims to alleviate this burden by providing intuitive and informative feedback.
- **Leverage Underutilized Control Modalities:** Existing teleoperation interfaces largely neglect the human lower limbs, despite their capacity for continuous, robust, and low-latency control, particularly for base motion. A key motivation is to reclaim the feet as a dedicated and effective channel for continuous mobile base control.
- **Enhance Operator Awareness and Reduce Cognitive Load through Force Feedback and Multi-modal Guidance:** Providing operators with only visual input for complex tasks often proves insufficient, requiring mental simulation of constraints. The system seeks to render task-relevant constraints through haptic and visual guidance.
 - **Collision Avoidance:** A specific motivation is to prevent common teleoperation failures such as near-collisions or small clearance errors, especially when the operator's attention is focused on manipulation tasks. This is addressed by providing resistive force feedback on the foot pedal based on obstacle proximity, thereby physically biasing base commands towards collision-averse behavior without requiring an explicit collision-avoidance controller.
 - **Reachability Management:** In bimanual mobile manipulation, tasks often require the robot to operate at or near the limits of its workspace, necessitating base repositioning. The objective is to provide real-time guidance (both haptic and visual) on bimanual manipulability to prompt operators to adjust the mobile base proactively, thereby improving reach and preventing kinematically fragile postures.
 - **Contact Awareness:** For tasks involving constrained contact, the motivation is to improve the operator's understanding of interaction forces during manipulation. This is achieved through arm-side force reflection, aiming to reduce oscillatory corrections and improve contact stability.
- **Improve Data Quality for Robot Learning:** Recognizing teleoperation's role in collecting data for autonomous robot policies, a core motivation is to design an interface that reduces operator workload and prevents failures, thereby generating "cleaner" and more informative demonstration data. The system aims to collect not just state-action pairs, but also structured feedback signals (e.g., resistive torques) that reflect task constraints, which can be incorporated into robot learning models.
- **Develop an Open-Source, Low-Cost System:** The project is motivated by a desire to contribute an accessible and replicable system to the robotics community, including the pedal device design and the full software stack, facilitating further research and application.

In summary, the work is motivated by the need for more intuitive, safer, and less cognitively demanding whole-body teleoperation for mobile manipulators, with the dual benefit of generating higher-quality data for autonomous robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TriPilot-FF is an open-source whole-body teleoperation system designed for a custom bimanual mobile manipulator. Its methodology focuses on distributing whole-body control across complementary human modalities and augmenting these modalities with real-time, task-relevant feedback.

#### 3.1. A. System Hardware

The teleoperation station comprises three synchronized modalities:
- **Visual Input:** Two Intel RealSense D405 wrist cameras, one RealSense D435 head camera, and a SLAMTEC C1 Lidar for depth sensing and 360-degree environment perception.
- **Arm and Gripper Input:** Bimanual leader-follower arms (AgileX Piper arms) providing an isomorphic mapping from the operator's leader pair to the remote follower pair.
- **Foot Input:** A custom 3-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) pedal device for commanding the mobile base.

The remote robot is an AgileX Ranger Mini V3 mobile base equipped with two AgileX Piper arms. It executes unified whole-body commands and streams proprioceptive and force-related signals back to the operator.

**Pedal Design:** The pedal is a low-cost, kinematically decoupled device designed to leverage human lower-limb capabilities for continuous base control. It measures and renders resistive forces along three independent control dimensions: x-translation, y-translation, and yaw rotation. The device integrates three dmBots DM-J4310 motors for sensing and actuation. A turntable-linkage mechanism couples the moving pedal surface to a stationary base, transmitting foot motion to the motors and reflecting motor-generated resistance back to the operator. Most structural components are 3D printed.

**Perception Calibration:** To ensure consistent feedback and overlay of information, accurate rigid transforms between cameras, grippers, and the base are crucial. The system estimates unknown extrinsics (gripper-to-wrist-camera and base-to-head-camera transforms) using a pose-graph style formulation over SE(3). This involves collecting multi-view checkerboard observations across multiple calibration configurations and minimizing an aggregated SE(3) residual using Lie-algebra parameterization and Levenberg-Marquardt optimization.

#### 3.2. B. System Software

The system operates on a control cycle where the operator provides leader-arm joint states and pedal inputs. The robot executes a combined command consisting of base twist (`u_b`) and dual-arm joint targets (`q_L`, `q_R`).

**Feedback on Bimanual Leader Arms:**
- Both leader and follower arms utilize a joint-space impedance controller. The commanded torque for each joint is a function of proportional and derivative gains, and an additive feedforward torque.
- **Leader-Follower Mapping:** The follower arms act as high-gain position trackers, receiving target joint states directly from the leader arms, with their feedforward torque disabled to maintain compliance.
- **Gravity-Compensated Force Reflection:** To provide contact awareness without excessive operator fatigue, the leader arms operate in a gravity-compensated compliant mode. Follower arm interaction torques are estimated from motor currents, gravity is subtracted, and the resulting interaction torque is scaled and reflected back to the leader arms as feedforward torque, with an additional leader gravity compensation.

**Collision-Avoidance Pedal Assistance:**
- A resistive force is rendered on the pedal to discourage unsafe base motions. Using lidar data, the system estimates the free-space distance (`r(d)`) to the closest obstacle in the operator's intended translational base direction (`d`).
- A smooth repulsive potential function (`ϕ`) is defined, where potentials rapidly increase as obstacle distance `r` approaches a robot surface radius `r_0`. The pedal resistance magnitude (`f_Rep`) is proportional to the derivative of this potential function, scaled by `k_ϕ`. This resistance is applied in the direction opposite to `d`, encouraging collision-averse driving.

**Reachability Pedal Assistance:**
- To address reachability and coordination failures, the system provides real-time cues for base repositioning.
- **Manipulability Metric and Field:** The system uses Yoshikawa's manipulability (`w(q)`) as a dexterity proxy. To provide Cartesian guidance, a manipulability field (`m(x)`) is defined as the maximum achievable manipulability at a given end-effector position `x`. Since online computation is impractical, a differentiable neural network (`ˆm(x)`) is trained to approximate this field from large-scale forward-kinematics sampling.
- **Force Guidance:** The system computes a guidance direction (`v_guide`) as a weighted combination of the end-effector's current orientation and the gradient (`∇_x ˆm(x)`) that ascends manipulability. This direction, projected onto the horizontal plane, generates a pedal feedback force (`f_guide`). Assistance is activated only when the arm is stretched and predicted manipulability falls below a threshold.
- **Visual Guidance:** Manipulability information is also presented visually by overlaying a per-pixel manipulability value onto the wrist camera video streams, allowing operators to identify reachable regions.

#### 3.3. C. Policy Learning

TriPilot-FF is also designed to collect data for training autonomous robot policies.
- **Torque-Augmented Observation Space:** The standard Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policy, which typically uses visual observations and joint positions, is augmented with measured joint torques (`τ_t`). These torques provide proprioceptive signals about contact, interaction forces, and unmodeled dynamics.
- **Extended Action Space:** The action space for ACT is extended to include both base velocity commands (`v_base`) and dual-arm joint positions (`q_arms`), enabling whole-body control.
- **Integration into ACT:** Torque information is integrated into both the encoder and decoder of the ACT model via learned linear projections. In the encoder, a torque embedding is concatenated with proprioceptive and action-sequence embeddings. In the decoder, the torque embedding provides an additional conditioning signal alongside latent samples, proprioception, and visual features, ensuring force information influences both latent representation and action generation.
- **Training and Inference:** The model is trained with the standard ACT objective (L1 reconstruction loss over action chunks and KL regularization). During inference, actions are generated using temporal aggregation across overlapping predicted chunks with exponential weighting for smoothness. Real-time joint-torque measurements from the robot are directly fed into the policy for closed-loop, force-reactive behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations of TriPilot-FF addressed five research questions across various real-world and simulated tasks.

**Q1: Do arm-side force reflection and lidar-driven resistive pedal cues jointly improve coordination in tight or cluttered space?**
- **BlindCarry (occluded bimanual transport):** This task involved the robot carrying a bulky object that obscured its forward view while navigating a cluttered environment.
 - TriPilot-FF achieved a 100% success rate, 0.0 average collision count, and a completion time of 131.34 seconds.
 - Without pedal feedback, success dropped to 55%, average collision count rose to 1.0, and completion time increased to 150.08 seconds.
 - **Finding:** Lidar-driven resistive pedal cues significantly improved task reliability and collision avoidance in situations with poor visibility, as the feedback effectively translated obstacle proximity into a physical "risk gradient."
- **NarrowTransport (constrained passage):** The robot transported an elongated bar through a narrow door.
 - TriPilot-FF achieved a 100% success rate, 0.0 average collision count, and a completion time of 16.32 seconds.
 - Without pedal feedback, success was 75%, collision count 0.3, and time 48.36 seconds.
 - Without any feedback, success was 60%, collision count 0.4, and time 49.16 seconds.
 - **Finding:** Both pedal feedback and arm-side force reflection (enabling stable bimanual grasping for rigid object transport) contributed to higher success rates, fewer collisions, and faster completion in tasks requiring precise whole-body coordination in constrained spaces.

**Q2: Does differentiable manipulability guidance reduce coordination burden and time-to-completion in reach-limited retrieval?**
- **GuidedReach (reach-limited retrieval):** The robot retrieved an object beyond its initial reach, requiring base adjustments and arm handover.
 - TriPilot-FF (with guidance) had a mean completion time of 7.39 seconds and spent 30.59% of the time in low manipulability regions.
 - Without guidance, mean completion time was 15.17 seconds, and time in low manipulability regions was 41.04%.
 - **Finding:** Manipulability guidance on the pedal reduced task completion time and the proportion of time spent in kinematically fragile (low-dexterity) postures, indicating improved efficiency and kinematic feasibility. This suggests guidance accelerates base repositioning decisions.

**Q3: Does arm-side force reflection improve efficiency and contact regulation in constrained contact tasks that require simultaneous base motion and manipulation?**
- **MobileSwipe (surface contact):** The robot maintained tight contact with a vertical glass surface while swiping with a roller, requiring coordinated base and arm motion.
 - TriPilot-FF (with force reflection) had a completion time of 30.67 seconds, a standard deviation of torque (σ_tor) of 2.532, and energy consumption (P) of 0.196.
 - Without force reflection, completion time was 32.06 seconds, σ_tor was 3.239, and P was 0.313.
 - **Finding:** Arm-side force reflection improved contact stability and energetic efficiency, as evidenced by lower torque standard deviation and reduced energy consumption, despite a modest impact on completion time. This indicates better contact regulation and reduced need for high-frequency visual corrective actions by the operator.

**Q4: Can TriPilot-FF sustain reliable performance and usability over long-horizon, real-world task sequences?**
- **Long-Horizon Teleoperation:** The system was evaluated in extended episodes in a kitchen and a tool room, involving multiple chained sub-tasks (e.g., opening fridge/microwave, pouring milk, pushing trolley, packing boxes).
 - **Finding:** Qualitative observations and supplementary videos indicated that TriPilot-FF enabled operators to sustain performance over long time horizons, effectively switching between navigation, bimanual manipulation, and contact-rich interactions without resetting robot or environment states. The "co-pilot" feedback mechanisms supported continuous operation.

**Q5: Do teleoperation haptic signals provide useful supervision for imitation learning, improving downstream policy performance when injected into ACT?**
- **Validation in Simulation (MuJoCo):**
 - **CubeTransfer:**
 - ACT: 22.0% success, 826 average cumulative reward.
 - ACT + Torque: 50.0% success, 1222 average cumulative reward.
 - **CubePickPlace:**
 - ACT: 28.0% success, 1685 average cumulative reward.
 - ACT + Torque: 36.0% success, 1714 average cumulative reward.
 - **Finding:** Augmenting ACT policies with joint-torque signals significantly improved performance in simulation tasks, suggesting that torque provides valuable task-relevant information for bimanual load sharing, transient contacts, and handover dynamics.
- **Real-World Validation:**
 - **BasketPack:**
 - ACT: 12% success.
 - ACT + Torque: 24% success.
 - ACT + Co-training: 36% success.
 - ACT + Torque + Co-training: 60% success.
 - **HangerHandOff:**
 - ACT: 68% success.
 - ACT + Torque: 92% success.
 - **OOD HangerHandOff (out-of-distribution):**
 - ACT: 56% success.
 - ACT + Torque: 72% success.
 - **Finding:** In real-world tasks, injecting torque information into the ACT policy's state consistently yielded significant performance increases. Co-training with additional upper-body motion data also improved performance, especially with limited whole-body data. The torque-augmented policies demonstrated improved robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios and produced smoother, more coordinated actions.
- **Long-Horizon Autonomous Execution (LaundryTransport):**
 - **Finding:** A torque-augmented ACT model trained with TriPilot-FF data successfully executed a complex long-horizon task involving folding, packing, and transporting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TriPilot-FF system demonstrates several advancements in whole-body teleoperation and its application to robot learning, with implications for both direct human control and autonomous robot development.

**Advancements in Teleoperation:**
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interface:** By integrating a dedicated, force-feedback-enabled foot pedal for mobile base control, TriPilot-FF reintroduces a robust and intuitive control channel for lower-limb movements. This complements traditional hand-centric interfaces, potentially reducing cognitive load on operators and enabling more nuanced whole-body coordination.
- **Improved Safety and Efficiency:** The lidar-driven resistive pedal cues for collision avoidance represent a novel method of haptic guidance that actively shapes operator commands towards safer behaviors without explicit collision-avoidance controllers. This "co-pilot" approach has been shown to reduce collisions and improve task reliability, particularly in occluded or confined environments.
- **Proactive Task Assistance:** The real-time manipulability guidance, delivered through both haptic feedback on the pedal and visual overlays, provides operators with proactive information about kinematic limits and optimal base repositioning. This capability can decrease time spent in kinematically precarious postures and accelerate task completion in reach-limited scenarios.
- **Refined Contact Awareness:** Arm-side force reflection improves the operator's perception of contact forces during manipulation, leading to more stable interactions, reduced corrective movements, and increased energetic efficiency in contact-rich tasks. This is crucial for delicate operations and prolonged contact.

**Impact on Robot Learning:**
- **Higher-Quality Demonstration Data:** The improved teleoperation experience, characterized by reduced operator burden and fewer errors, directly translates into cleaner and more consistent demonstration data. This addresses a critical need in robot learning, where data quality heavily influences policy performance.
- **Enriched Observation Space for Policies:** The research demonstrates that teleoperation feedback signals, specifically joint torques, are not merely interface elements but also valuable supervisory signals for imitation learning. Augmenting the observation space of transformer-based policies (ACT) with torque information consistently improved performance in both simulated and real-world tasks, suggesting that contact and interaction dynamics are important features for robust robot behaviors.
- **Foundation for More Robust Autonomous Systems:** By leveraging the enriched data from TriPilot-FF, policies can be trained to exhibit more coordinated, force-reactive, and adaptable whole-body behaviors, even in out-of-distribution scenarios or complex long-horizon tasks. This moves towards autonomous systems that are more adept at handling real-world variability and interactions.

**Open-Source Contribution:** The release of the pedal device design and the full software stack facilitates further research and development in mobile manipulation and teleoperation, lowering the barrier to entry for other researchers and practitioners.

Future work could focus on adapting the cue gains dynamically to individual operator styles for personalized assistance and exploring methods to provide guidance based on predicted user intent, further enhancing the system's adaptiveness and effectiveness.
