# What about gravity in video generation? Post-Training Newton's Laws with Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stony Brook University and LIX, École Polytechnique, developed NewtonRewards, a post-training framework that integrates verifiable, rule-based rewards to instill Newtonian physics into video generation models. This method significantly improved physical plausibility, motion smoothness, and temporal coherence, reducing velocity RMSE by +5.87% and acceleration RMSE by +8.46% on a custom NewtonBench-60K dataset.

## Problem

- Modern video generation models often produce visually compelling content that lacks physical realism, violating fundamental laws of motion like gravity.
- This 'physics gap' limits the utility of generated videos as reliable 'world models' for applications such as robotics or autonomous driving, leading to inconsistent learning signals.
- Existing approaches rely on indirect feedback (e.g., from LLMs/VLMs), complex external simulators, or costly human annotations, which are often insufficient for precise physical enforcement or unscalable.

## Method

- **NewtonRewards** introduces a post-training framework that applies ==verifiable, rule-based rewards== to fine-tune video generators, explicitly enforcing ==Newtonian kinematic== and ==mass conservation laws==.
- It computes ==measurable proxies== from generated video frames using frozen utility models: ==optical flow== for image-plane velocity and ==visual feature embeddings== for object 'mass'.
- Two differentiable rewards are defined: a ==kinematic reward== penalizing deviations from constant acceleration and a ==mass conservation reward== ensuring visual feature consistency, preventing reward hacking.

## Results

- NewtonRewards achieved an average improvement of **+9.75%** across key physical and visual metrics on the in-distribution NewtonBench-60K benchmark, with significant reductions in velocity RMSE (**+5.87%**) and acceleration RMSE (**+8.46%**).
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization, showing consistent improvements across all **5** Newtonian Motion Primitives and achieving an average improvement of **+8.60%** in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the kinematic residual constraint and the mass conservation reward are critical components, with the latter directly preventing degenerate solutions like objects freezing or disappearing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct, verifiable rewards derived from automatically extracted proxies (like optical flow) are effective for enforcing precise physical laws, unlike indirect visual feature alignment, which fails to ensure physical consistency.
> - The Newtonian kinematic residual constraint, derived from constant acceleration, is crucial for aligning generated object motion with fundamental physical dynamics.
> - A mass conservation reward is essential to prevent reward hacking, where models might otherwise minimize kinematic residuals by causing objects to vanish or freeze.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has witnessed rapid advancements in recent years, largely driven by the success of diffusion models. Models like OpenSora, Stable Video Diffusion, and Lumiere can now synthesize visually stunning and perceptually realistic video clips from various inputs, such as text prompts or initial images. These models excel at rendering high-fidelity visuals, intricate textures, and diverse scenes, pushing the boundaries of what is aesthetically possible in synthetic media.

However, a critical and persistent challenge in this landscape is the lack of physical realism. While generative models can create beautiful imagery, they frequently produce videos that violate fundamental physical laws. Objects might float unrealistically, collisions might behave inconsistently, accelerations can drift, and motions often unfold without discernible cause. This "physics gap" means that despite visual plausibility, the generated worlds are often inconsistent and unpredictable from a scientific standpoint.

This limitation is not merely an aesthetic concern; it has significant implications for applications where generated videos are used as "world models." In fields like robotics, autonomous driving, game development, and more generally, in training AI agents for perception, reasoning, and action, physically inconsistent dynamics can lead to erroneous learning signals, unrealistic affordances, and a failure to generalize to the real world. For instance, a robot trained in a physically unrealistic simulated environment may perform poorly when deployed in the real world. Therefore, embedding physical plausibility is increasingly recognized as a necessity for the next generation of intelligent systems.

Prior work has attempted to address this physics gap through several strategies:
- **Instruction and Feedback-Based Fine-tuning:** Some methods fine-tune diffusion models using textual instructions or feedback from Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs). While these approaches can improve perceived physical consistency to some extent, the feedback is often indirect, relying on high-level linguistic or visual cues rather than explicit physical laws. As the paper points out, "neither humans nor 'VLMs-as-judge' can precisely evaluate how well physical constraints are being followed."
- **Physics-Guided Simulation and Representation:** Another category involves leveraging external physics simulators or 3D representations to guide video generation. These methods might pre-compute physically plausible conditions as auxiliary inputs or integrate simulation and rendering pipelines. While effective, they enforce physics indirectly through the generator's interpretation of conditioned data, requiring complex multi-modal setups.
- **Physics-Rich Datasets and Post-Training:** Approaches like PISA have explored fine-tuning models on datasets containing real or synthetic videos with clear physical dynamics (e.g., objects falling). While PISA introduced post-training strategies using optical flow, depth, and segmentation for physical consistency, its scope was limited, focusing primarily on free-fall scenarios. Other methods in this category often rely on costly human annotations for preference data (e.g., PhysMaster).

NewtonRewards distinguishes itself by proposing a novel, *verifiable reward* framework. Unlike previous methods, it does not rely on indirect feedback, complex external simulators, or human labels for explicit physics enforcement. Instead, it directly extracts *measurable proxies* (optical flow for velocity, appearance features for mass) from generated videos and constructs rule-based rewards that explicitly quantify adherence to Newtonian kinematic and mass conservation laws. This approach offers a scalable and precise mechanism to bridge the gap between visual realism and physical realism, moving beyond superficial improvements to genuinely grounded physical dynamics. It positions itself as a foundational step towards building truly physics-aware generative models, addressing a critical bottleneck in the pursuit of more intelligent and general-purpose AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enhance the physical plausibility of videos generated by modern diffusion models, specifically by ensuring adherence to fundamental Newtonian laws of motion. The authors aim to achieve this by introducing a novel post-training framework that explicitly enforces physics constraints through verifiable, rule-based rewards.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations about the current state of video generation:
1. **Gap between Visual and Physical Realism:** Despite significant progress in synthesizing visually compelling and photorealistic video clips, contemporary generative models frequently produce content that violates basic physical laws. Objects often float, accelerate inconsistently, or interact unrealistically, leading to "worlds unbound by physics." This creates a persistent disparity between what looks real and what behaves real.
2. **Functional Imperative for Physical Realism:** The lack of physical realism is not merely an aesthetic shortcoming. In numerous crucial applications—such as creating immersive game environments, training world models for robotics and autonomous driving, or simulating complex scenarios for scientific research—generated videos serve as data for perception, reasoning, and action. In these contexts, physically inconsistent dynamics can lead to unreliable learning signals, incorrect predictions of object affordances, and a failure to generalize robustly to real-world conditions. A model that truly "understands" that objects fall due to gravity or that collisions alter momentum is far more valuable for these applications.
3. **Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Current methods for embedding physical plausibility into video generation face notable challenges:
 - **Indirectness of Feedback:** Approaches relying on feedback from Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as "judges" provide indirect supervision. These models often assess perceived realism rather than precise physical consistency, struggling to evaluate subtle violations of physical laws.
 - **External Simulator Reliance:** Methods that integrate physics simulators or 3D representations enforce physics indirectly by conditioning the generator. This can be complex, may not fully translate into the generated pixel space, and still relies on the pre-trained model's interpretation.
 - **Data Scarcity and Cost:** Fine-tuning on physics-rich datasets, while beneficial, can be limited by the availability of diverse, well-annotated data. Methods requiring human-annotated preference data are often costly and unscalable.
 - **Lack of Explicit Enforcement:** Many existing techniques embed physics as a *condition* rather than an *explicit constraint*, leading to models that might mimic physical behavior without truly understanding or enforcing the underlying laws.

Given these limitations, the key objective is to propose the "first verifiable rewards for physical laws in video generation." This entails:
- Developing a framework that can *automatically verify* the correctness of physical outputs.
- Estimating unobservable physical quantities (e.g., velocity, mass) from raw video frames using *measurable proxies* derived from frozen utility models (e.g., optical flow networks, video encoders).
- Formulating explicit physics constraints and rewards based on these proxies to enable post-training of video generators.
- Demonstrating the effectiveness of this framework on canonical Newtonian motion primitives, thereby paving a scalable path toward physics-aware video generation.

The motivation is thus to move beyond merely visually plausible videos to physically *verifiable* and *consistent* generated content, addressing a fundamental challenge that underpins the development of more intelligent and reliable AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of NewtonRewards revolves around a physics-grounded post-training framework for video generators that explicitly enforces Newtonian laws through verifiable, rule-based rewards. This approach sidesteps the limitations of indirect feedback or human supervision by extracting measurable proxies directly from the generated videos.

The method comprises two main conceptual components:
1. **Computing Measurable Proxies:** Physical quantities like velocity, acceleration, or mass are not directly observable from raw video pixels. NewtonRewards addresses this by using pre-trained, frozen "utility models" to extract observable surrogates, termed "measurable proxies," from the generated video frames.
 - **Optical Flow as Velocity Proxy:** For estimating object motion, the framework employs a pre-trained optical flow model, specifically RAFT. Given two consecutive frames ($V_t, V_{t+1}$), RAFT computes a dense per-pixel displacement field ($\phi_t$), which serves as a differentiable proxy for the image-plane velocity ($v_t \approx \phi_t/\Delta t$, where $\Delta t$ is the frame interval). This allows the system to infer how objects are moving within the video.
 - **Visual Features as Mass Proxy:** To account for mass-related properties, a pre-trained video encoder (V-JEPA 2) is used to extract high-level visual feature embeddings ($z_t$) for each frame. These embeddings capture consistent object appearance, which implicitly correlates with object identity, material, and thus, an "effective mass." This mass proxy is crucial for ensuring that the visual representation of an object remains consistent over time and across different scenarios, preventing degenerate solutions.

2. **Defining Physics Constraints and Rewards:** Based on these measurable proxies, NewtonRewards formulates two complementary physics constraints, which are then used as differentiable reward signals for fine-tuning the video generator.
 - **Newtonian Kinematic Constraint (Constant-Acceleration Reward):** The fundamental insight is that for many common Newtonian motion primitives (e.g., free fall, throws, ramp sliding), objects exhibit constant acceleration in the image plane (under certain camera assumptions, like a fixed side-view). Constant acceleration implies that the second discrete derivative of position (or first discrete derivative of velocity) is zero. In terms of optical flow, this translates to the constraint: $\phi_{t+1} - 2\phi_t + \phi_{t-1} \approx 0$. The kinematic reward, $R_{kinematic}$, is defined as the squared L2 norm of this residual, penalizing deviations from constant-acceleration dynamics. This constraint unifies the enforcement of Newton's Second Law across all considered motion primitives.
 - **Mass Conservation Reward:** This reward ensures that the "mass" (as proxied by visual features) of an object remains consistent throughout its motion, and also aligns with the expected appearance from a physically simulated reference. The mass conservation reward, $R_{mass}$, is defined as the average squared L2 distance between the visual feature embeddings of the generated video ($z_{gen}$) and those from a corresponding physically simulated reference video ($z_{sim}$). This prevents degenerate solutions where the model might minimize the kinematic residual by causing objects to vanish or freeze (a form of "reward hacking").

**Post-Training Objective:** The final physics-grounded objective for post-training the video generator ($G_\theta$) is a weighted sum of these two rewards:
$L_{phys} = \lambda_{kinematic} R_{kinematic} + \lambda_{mass} R_{mass}$
where $\lambda_{kinematic}$ and $\lambda_{mass}$ are weighting coefficients. The gradient of this loss is used to update the generator's parameters ($\theta$).

**Newtonian Motion Primitives (NMPs):** The framework is evaluated on five canonical NMPs, each characterized by constant acceleration and specific force configurations:
1. **Free Fall:** Object under uniform gravity, zero initial velocity.
2. **Horizontal Throw:** Object under uniform gravity, non-zero horizontal initial velocity.
3. **Parabolic Throw:** Object under uniform gravity, arbitrary initial velocity (combining horizontal and vertical components).
4. **Ramp Sliding Down:** Object sliding down an inclined plane with kinetic friction.
5. **Ramp Sliding Up:** Object sliding up an inclined plane with kinetic friction and an uphill initial velocity.

**NewtonBench-60K Dataset:** To enable robust evaluation, the authors constructed a large-scale, controlled simulated dataset. This dataset includes 50,000 training videos (10,000 per NMP) and a 10,000-video benchmark (2,000 per NMP). The simulations use Kubric for scene orchestration, PyBullet for rigid-body dynamics, and Blender for rendering. It features diverse objects, backgrounds, and systematically varied physical parameters for each NMP, including both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. This dataset is designed to provide ground truth for object masks and centroids, essential for evaluating physical and visual metrics.

**Experimental Setup:** The base video generation model used is OpenSora v1.2, which is first supervisedly fine-tuned (SFT) on NewtonBench-60K to generate 32-frame clips conditioned on text and initial frames. NewtonRewards then applies its post-training objective to this SFT baseline. RAFT is used for optical flow, and V-JEPA 2 for visual features.

This comprehensive approach, combining a novel verifiable reward system with a custom-designed benchmark, provides a rigorous and scalable method for imbuing generative video models with explicit physical understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate that NewtonRewards significantly improves the physical plausibility, motion smoothness, and temporal coherence of generated videos, outperforming existing post-training methods.

The key findings and results are summarized as follows:

1. **Superior Performance Across Metrics:**
 - On the in-distribution (ID) NewtonBench-60K benchmark, NewtonRewards achieves an average improvement of +9.75% across all five evaluated metrics (L2, CD, IoU, RMSE\_v, RMSE\_a) compared to the OpenSora (SFT) baseline.
 - Crucially, it shows substantial reductions in both velocity RMSE ($RMSE_v$) and acceleration RMSE ($RMSE_a$), indicating a direct improvement in adherence to Newtonian kinematics. For instance, $RMSE_v$ improves by +5.87% and $RMSE_a$ by +8.46%.
 - Visual metrics (L2, CD, IoU) also show strong improvements (+12.39%, +7.25%, +14.78% respectively), suggesting that physics-grounded training also enhances perceptual and spatial fidelity.

2. **Ineffectiveness of Visual Feature Alignment for Physics:**
 - Comparative analysis with PISA-based object reward optimization (ORO) strategies (Optical Flow, Depth Map, Segmentation) reveals that while these methods might offer minor improvements in specific visual metrics, they generally fail to enforce physically consistent motion.
 - PISA ORO-Depth, for example, showed small spatial gains but degraded temporal consistency (worsened $RMSE_v$ and $RMSE_a$). PISA ORO-Optical Flow and Segmentation also showed inconsistent or even regressive behavior on physics metrics.
 - This finding (Finding 1) clearly states: "Visual feature alignment improves perceptual and spatial fidelity but fails to enforce adherence to physical laws of motion." This underscores the necessity of explicit physics constraints over mere visual similarity.

3. **Consistent Generalization Across Newtonian Motion Primitives (NMPs):**
 - NewtonRewards demonstrates uniformly positive improvements across all five NMPs (free fall, horizontal throw, parabolic throw, ramp sliding down, ramp sliding up).
 - It shows its largest gains on more challenging motions like parabolic throws (+12.7% average change) and ramp-up scenarios (+11.7% average change), where other methods often struggle or regress. This indicates robust generalization across diverse Newtonian dynamics.

4. **Strong Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Robustness:**
 - When evaluated on OOD scenarios (unseen heights, speeds, angles, friction coefficients), the OpenSora (SFT) baseline degrades significantly.
 - In contrast, NewtonRewards consistently improves across all five metrics in the OOD setting, achieving an average improvement of +8.60% over the SFT baseline. This demonstrates that enforcing Newtonian kinematic and mass constraints yields models that extrapolate more reliably to unseen physical regimes, showcasing enhanced generalization capabilities.

5. **Direct Evidence of Kinematic Adherence (Constant-Acceleration Residual Analysis):**
 - By computing the mean discrete second-order residual of the optical flow field ($\phi_{t+1} - 2\phi_t + \phi_{t-1}$), the authors directly assess adherence to constant-acceleration dynamics.
 - NewtonRewards produces markedly smoother residual maps with minimal magnitude across both horizontal and vertical axes (Figure 6). This provides direct quantitative evidence that the generated trajectories more closely align with true constant-acceleration behavior, a fundamental prediction of Newtonian mechanics. Baseline methods, even those using ground-truth visual signals, show substantial structured residuals.

6. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Importance:**
 - **Kinematic Residual Constraint is Essential:** Ablating the Newtonian kinematic residual term (NewtonRewards w/o residual) largely nullifies the benefits, resulting in only marginal overall improvement (+0.25% vs. +9.75% for the full model). This confirms that the constant-acceleration constraint is critical for stabilizing motion and aligning generated dynamics with physics.
 - **Mass Conservation Prevents Reward Hacking:** Ablating the mass conservation reward leads to "reward hacking" (Finding 2). Without this constraint, the model minimizes the kinematic residual by driving all object velocities to zero, effectively causing objects to disappear or freeze (Figure 7, Table 4). The mass conservation reward is crucial for anchoring visual and feature-level mass consistency, thereby preventing this degenerate solution and ensuring stable, physically meaningful motion.

7. **Real-World Transfer (Supplementary Material):**
 - Further validating its robustness, NewtonRewards, despite being trained purely on simulated data, achieves superior and consistent gains on a real-world free-fall dataset from the PISA benchmark. This indicates strong transferability of the physics-grounded post-training to natural scenes and real gravitational motion.

In conclusion, the main findings unequivocally demonstrate that NewtonRewards successfully bridges the gap between visual and physical realism in video generation. Its verifiable, physics-grounded reward system enables models to produce temporally coherent and physically plausible videos, generalize robustly, and avoid common failure modes, marking a significant advancement in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of NewtonRewards marks a significant stride in the field of video generation and artificial intelligence, offering a novel and scalable approach to imbue generative models with physical understanding. Its significance and potential impact can be articulated across several dimensions:

1. **Pioneering Verifiable Physics Rewards:** NewtonRewards introduces a paradigm shift by proposing the first framework for physics-grounded post-training based on *verifiable rewards*. Unlike prior methods that rely on indirect human or VLM feedback, or complex external simulators, this approach leverages automatically extracted measurable proxies (optical flow, visual features) to define rule-based rewards. This verifiability provides a precise and objective mechanism to quantify and enforce physical laws, moving beyond subjective perceptual realism.

2. **Enhanced Physical Plausibility and Robustness:** The consistent improvements across various visual and physics metrics, both in-distribution and out-of-distribution, demonstrate NewtonRewards' capability to generate videos that are not only visually compelling but also adhere strictly to Newtonian dynamics. This significantly reduces instances of floating objects, inconsistent accelerations, and unnatural motion, leading to a more coherent and believable synthetic world.

3. **Scalability and Efficiency:** The methodology of extracting rewards from frozen, pre-trained utility models (optical flow, video encoders) makes the entire post-training process highly scalable and efficient. It avoids the need for costly human annotations for physical plausibility or the computationally intensive integration of complex physics simulators during the generation or training loop. This scalability is crucial for deploying physics-aware generative models in real-world applications.

4. **Foundation for Robust World Models:** The ability to generate physically consistent videos is a crucial step toward building more robust and reliable "world models" for AI agents. In domains like robotics, autonomous driving, and interactive gaming environments, agents need to understand and predict physical interactions to operate effectively. NewtonRewards provides a foundational capability for creating training environments and synthetic data that accurately reflect real-world physics, thereby improving the generalization and safety of AI systems.

5. **Generalizability of the Framework:** A key aspect of NewtonRewards' impact lies in its inherent generality. The authors explicitly state that "Given a measurable proxy of a variable in a physical law, the same steps can be followed to come up with verifiable rewards appropriate for that law." This principle opens up a vast avenue for future research. It suggests that the framework can be extended to enforce other fundamental physical laws, such as momentum conservation, energy conservation, elasticity, or fluid dynamics, by simply identifying appropriate measurable proxies and formulating corresponding differentiable constraints. This could lead to a new generation of generative models capable of simulating highly complex and multi-faceted physical phenomena.

6. **Contribution of a Standardized Benchmark:** The release of NewtonBench-60K is a valuable contribution to the research community. This large-scale, controlled dataset, designed specifically for evaluating dynamic motion realism and physical consistency across diverse Newtonian primitives, provides a standardized benchmark. It will enable researchers to rigorously test and compare new methods for physics-aware video generation, accelerating progress in the field.

7. **Mitigation of Reward Hacking:** The identification and mitigation of "reward hacking" through the mass conservation reward highlight the sophistication required in designing robust physics-grounded objectives. This insight is valuable for future work in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and other reward-based optimization schemes in generative AI, where models might find trivial ways to satisfy a reward function without achieving the intended behavior.

In conclusion, NewtonRewards not only solves an immediate problem in video generation but also introduces a powerful and extensible framework for embedding explicit physical understanding into generative AI. Its impact is likely to be felt across multiple domains, from the development of more intelligent and reliable AI systems to the creation of richer, more realistic digital content, paving the way for truly physics-aware artificial intelligence.
