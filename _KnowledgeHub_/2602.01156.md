# PolicyFlow: Policy Optimization with Continuous Normalizing Flow in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> PolicyFlow is an on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm that effectively integrates Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) as policy models by introducing an efficient importance ratio approximation and a novel Brownian entropy regularizer. This approach enables agents to learn complex, multimodal action distributions, outperforming existing flow-based methods and standard PPO in various control tasks while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Traditional on-policy RL methods with Gaussian policies struggle to represent complex, multimodal action distributions required for sophisticated control tasks.
- Directly incorporating expressive generative models like CNFs into PPO-style RL is computationally expensive and numerically unstable due to the need for iterative ODE simulations and path-wise backpropagation for likelihood evaluations.
- Developing a principled and computationally lightweight entropy regularization mechanism for flow-based policies is challenging, often leading to mode collapse or inefficient exploration.

## Method

- **PolicyFlow** parameterizes policies using ==Continuous Normalizing Flows== (CNFs) to model intricate action distributions, where actions are generated by integrating a flow and adding Gaussian noise.
- It introduces an efficient approximation for the importance ratio in the PPO objective by leveraging velocity field variations along a linear interpolation path, avoiding costly full ODE simulations during policy optimization.
- A novel "Brownian Regularizer" encourages exploration and prevents mode collapse by guiding the learned velocity field to align with a reference flow's negative score, complemented by the entropy of injected Gaussian noise.

## Results

- PolicyFlow demonstrated superior performance in the MultiGoal test, achieving near-complete and balanced coverage of goal locations, effectively preventing mode collapse observed in baselines.
- The algorithm achieved competitive or superior performance on MuJoCo Playground benchmarks compared to FPO, DPPO, and PPO, often exhibiting faster convergence and higher sample efficiency.
- On challenging IsaacLab robotics benchmarks, PolicyFlow consistently matched or surpassed PPO in asymptotic performance, with training time increasing by less than **50%** for comparable model sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Approximating the importance ratio based on velocity field variations significantly reduces computational overhead and improves stability when integrating CNFs into on-policy PPO.
> - A "Brownian Regularizer" can effectively promote entropy growth and diverse action exploration in flow-based policies without requiring explicit, costly log-likelihood computations.
> - Combining the expressiveness of CNFs with the stability and efficiency of PPO is feasible through these specific algorithmic advancements, enabling the learning of richer, multimodal behaviors in RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly policy gradient (PG) methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), has achieved remarkable successes across diverse domains, from complex robotic control to aligning large language models with human preferences. PPO, in particular, has become a cornerstone algorithm due to its balance of simplicity, numerical stability, and robust empirical performance. Its core mechanism involves optimizing a surrogate objective using importance ratios, which traditionally relies on evaluating policy likelihoods.

However, PPO's reliance on straightforward likelihood evaluation typically mandates the use of relatively simple policy parameterizations, such as Gaussian distributions. While convenient, Gaussian policies struggle to represent complex action distributions that might be multimodal, highly skewed, or exhibit intricate dependencies, which are often observed in sophisticated control tasks requiring diverse behaviors.

This limitation has spurred interest in integrating more expressive generative models, like diffusion models and continuous normalizing flows (CNFs), into RL policies. These generative models excel at capturing intricate, high-dimensional, and multimodal distributions, as demonstrated in areas like imitation learning where they effectively model diverse expert demonstrations. The challenge, however, lies in porting these powerful models to online RL settings, especially with PPO-style updates. Computing importance ratios or policy likelihoods for CNFs or diffusion models typically involves computationally intensive iterative Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) or Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) simulations and path-wise backpropagation. This process is slow, memory-intensive, and prone to numerical instabilities such as exploding or vanishing gradients, severely limiting their practicality for efficient on-policy RL.

Existing approaches to bridge this gap include:
- **Direct backpropagation:** Some works attempt to directly backpropagate policy gradients through the full generative chain (e.g., Wang et al., 2024; Chao et al., 2024; Ding et al., 2025), but this often leads to unstable optimization.
- **DPPO (Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization):** Ren et al. (2024) proposed DPPO, effective for fine-tuning diffusion policies, but it struggles when training from scratch due to limitations in off-manifold exploration. DPPO also treats diffusion as an internal Markov Decision Process, which has its own set of complexities.
- **FPO (Flow Matching Policy Gradients):** McAllister et al. (2025) introduced FPO, which estimates importance ratios using an ELBO (Evidence Lower Bound) objective. While scalable, this introduces an asymmetric estimation bias that can amplify variance and affect stability, often necessitating larger batch sizes.
- **Entropy Regularization:** A crucial component for exploration and preventing mode collapse in RL. However, extending it to flow-based policies is challenging due to the intractability of action log-likelihoods and the high computational cost of explicit entropy estimation (e.g., via divergence evaluation and path integration). Heuristic solutions like Gaussian mixture models or injecting uniform noise have been explored, but they often come with computational overheads or specific limitations.

PolicyFlow emerges in this landscape as a novel on-policy algorithm designed to specifically address these limitations. It seeks to combine the expressiveness of CNFs with the stability of PPO-style optimization by proposing efficient approximations for importance ratios and a lightweight entropy regularization mechanism, without succumbing to the computational or stability drawbacks of prior art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of PolicyFlow are multi-faceted:

1. **Overcome the expressiveness limitations of traditional Gaussian policies in PPO:** The authors recognize that while PPO is robust, its reliance on simple Gaussian policies restricts its ability to model complex, multimodal, or highly skewed action distributions. The goal is to leverage the high capacity of Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) to represent richer policy distributions, enabling agents to learn more sophisticated and diverse behaviors.

2. **Enable efficient and stable integration of CNFs into on-policy RL:** The direct application of CNFs in PPO-style algorithms faces significant computational and numerical stability hurdles. Evaluating policy likelihoods or importance ratios along the full flow trajectory requires costly ODE/SDE simulations and path-wise backpropagation, which can lead to slow training and unstable gradients. A key objective is to bypass these computational bottlenecks while preserving the benefits of CNF expressiveness.

3. **Develop a principled and computationally lightweight entropy regularization mechanism for flow-based policies:** Standard entropy regularization, vital for encouraging exploration and preventing mode collapse, is difficult to apply to CNF policies due to the intractability of their log-likelihoods. Existing methods are either too expensive or heuristic. The authors aim to introduce an elegant and efficient approach to promote diverse actions without explicit and costly entropy calculations.

In essence, PolicyFlow is motivated by the desire to create a practical and powerful on-policy RL algorithm that can effectively learn complex, multimodal behaviors by combining the strengths of PPO with the generative capabilities of CNFs, all while addressing the inherent computational and stability challenges associated with such integration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PolicyFlow proposes a novel on-policy RL algorithm that integrates Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) as policy models within a PPO-style optimization framework. Its core contributions lie in its efficient importance ratio approximation and a unique entropy regularization technique.

**Policy Parameterization with Continuous Normalizing Flows:**
The policy is parameterized using a conditional flow $\phi: \times \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^d$, defined by an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE):
$$ \frac{d}{dt} \phi_t(z; s) = v_t(\phi_t(z; s); s), \quad \phi_0(z; s) = z $$
Here, $z \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is a latent variable (sampled from a standard Gaussian $p_z(z) = \mathcal{N}(z; 0, 1)$), $s \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the state, and $v: \times \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is a time-dependent velocity field modeled by a neural network. Actions are generated by integrating the flow to its terminal time ($t=1$) and adding Gaussian noise: $a = \phi_1(z; s) + n$, where $n \sim \mathcal{N}(n; 0, \sigma^2)$. This construction allows for modeling complex, multimodal action distributions beyond simple Gaussians.

**Importance Ratio Approximation for CNF Policies:**
A central challenge in PPO with CNFs is computing the importance ratio $\pi(a|s) / \hat{\pi}(a|s)$. Directly evaluating this requires simulating the full ODE trajectories for both the current policy $\phi$ and the reference policy $\hat{\phi}$, which is computationally expensive and unstable. PolicyFlow addresses this by:
1. **Exploiting Gaussian Likelihood Properties:** The policy $\pi(a|z,s)$ is defined by a Gaussian around $\phi_1(z;s)$ with variance $\sigma^2$. The likelihood ratio between two Gaussians is shift-invariant. Thus, $\frac{\pi(a|z,s)}{\hat{\pi}(a|z,s)} = \frac{p_n(a; \phi_1(z;s), \sigma^2)}{p_n(a; \hat{\phi}_1(z;s), \hat{\sigma}^2)}$. This can be rewritten using the terminal shift $\delta_{\phi_1}(z;s) = \phi_1(z;s) - \hat{\phi}_1(z;s)$.
2. **Velocity Field Variation Approximation:** Instead of computing $\delta_{\phi_1}(z;s)$ by simulating ODEs, PolicyFlow approximates it using the velocity field variations along a simple linear interpolation path $x_t = (1-t)z + t\hat{\phi}_1(z;s)$. The approximate importance ratio $\rho$ is then:
 $$ \rho = \frac{p_n(a - \hat{\phi}_1(z;s); v_t(x_t;s,\theta) - \hat{v}_t(x_t;s), \sigma^2)}{p_n(a - \hat{\phi}_1(z;s); 0, \hat{\sigma}^2)} $$
 This approximation means that ODE simulation (to get $\hat{\phi}_1(z;s)$) is only needed during data collection (sampling actions), but *not* during the policy optimization step, where gradients are computed efficiently along the interpolation path without backpropagating through the full ODE. The authors provide a theoretical analysis showing this approximation introduces only a first-order error in the log of the importance ratio, which is robust within the PPO framework's clipping mechanism.
3. **Clipped Surrogate Objective:** The optimization objective remains PPO-style:
 $$ J_{\text{Flow}}(\theta, \sigma) = E_{p_{\hat{\pi}}(s),p_z(z)} E_{\hat{\pi}(a|z,s), p(t)} \left[ \min(\rho \hat{A}_{\hat{\pi}}(s, a), \text{clip}(\rho, 1 - \epsilon, 1 + \epsilon) \hat{A}_{\hat{\pi}}(s, a)) \right] $$

**Brownian Entropy Regularization:**
To encourage exploration and prevent mode collapse without expensive entropy estimation, PolicyFlow introduces a novel "Brownian Regularizer":
1. **Brownian Motion Inspiration:** Inspired by Brownian dynamics, where particles naturally spread and entropy monotonically increases. The heat equation $\partial p_t(x)/\partial t = \nabla^2_x p_t(x)$ connects to the continuity equation if $v_t(x) = -\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$.
2. **Velocity Field Regulation:** The regularizer guides the learned velocity field $v_t$ to align with the negative score of a reference flow, promoting expansion of trajectories. Leveraging a known relationship for rectified flows (Liu et al., 2025): $\nabla_x \log \hat{p}_t(x_t; s) = \frac{1}{1-t} (t \hat{v}_t(x_t; s) - x_t)$.
3. **Regularization Term:** The full regularization objective is:
 $$ J_{\text{Reg}}(\theta, \sigma) = E_{p_{\hat{\pi}}(s), p_z(z), p(t)} \left[ -w_b \|(1-t)v_t(x_t; s, \theta) - (x_t - t \hat{v}_t(x_t; s))\|^2_2 + \frac{w_g}{2} \sum_{i=1}^d \log(2\pi e \sigma^2_i) \right] $$
 The first term (Brownian regularizer) encourages the learned velocity field to promote entropy growth, designed to avoid singularity as $t \to 1$. The second term is the entropy of the injected Gaussian noise. This approach is computationally lightweight as it avoids explicit log-likelihood calculation or divergence integration.

**Algorithm 1 (PolicyFlow) Summary:**
The algorithm iteratively collects trajectories using the current policy (which involves ODE simulation for action sampling), computes advantage estimates, and then updates the policy and value function parameters. The policy update maximizes the PPO-clipped objective augmented with the Brownian regularizer, crucially utilizing the approximate importance ratios based on velocity field variations along interpolation paths, thus avoiding costly backpropagation through full ODEs during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PolicyFlow was rigorously evaluated against state-of-the-art flow-based and diffusion-based baselines (FPO, DPPO) as well as the standard PPO algorithm across a variety of continuous control tasks in diverse environments.

**MultiGoal Test (Multimodality and Mode Collapse):**
This custom 2D environment with six equidistant goals was designed to assess the policy's ability to capture multimodal action distributions and prevent mode collapse.
- **PPO with Gaussian policies** (even with standard entropy regularization) exhibited limited coverage, concentrating on only a subset of goals.
- **DPPO and FPO**, despite utilizing generative models, also showed mode collapse, failing to cover all goals adequately, attributed to their lack of effective entropy regularization.
- **PolicyFlow without the Brownian regularizer** improved coverage over PPO but still left some regions under-explored.
- **PolicyFlow with uniform noise injection** (a heuristic entropy method) still suffered from mode collapse.
- **PolicyFlow with the proposed Brownian regularizer** (combined with Gaussian noise entropy) demonstrated superior performance, achieving near-complete and balanced coverage of all feasible goal locations. This strongly validated the regularizer's effectiveness in leveraging the CNF's expressiveness for diverse, multimodal behaviors.

**MuJoCo Playground Benchmarks:**
PolicyFlow was compared to FPO, DPPO, and PPO on a suite of MuJoCo Playground tasks.
- **Competitive/Superior Performance:** PolicyFlow consistently achieved performance comparable to or exceeding FPO in most environments, and generally outperformed DPPO. It also matched or surpassed PPO, which uses simpler Gaussian policies.
- **Faster Convergence/Sample Efficiency:** Training curves indicated that PolicyFlow often converged faster than baselines, suggesting higher sample efficiency and more effective exploration, which aligns with the qualitative observations from the MultiGoal test.

**IsaacLab Benchmarks:**
PolicyFlow was further evaluated on the challenging IsaacLab benchmarks, a suite of robotics environments for locomotion, manipulation, and navigation. Due to implementation framework differences, direct comparisons with FPO/DPPO were not feasible, so PolicyFlow was primarily benchmarked against PPO (using RSL-RL's implementation).
- **Asymptotic Performance:** PolicyFlow consistently matched or surpassed PPO in asymptotic performance across all IsaacLab tasks (e.g., Lift-Cube, Navigation, Anymal-D).
- **Early-Stage Learning:** PolicyFlow sometimes exhibited slower early-stage learning compared to PPO, likely due to the inherently more complex optimization problem of learning a time-dependent velocity field.
- **Training Time Efficiency:** Per-iteration training time analysis on IsaacLab environments showed that PolicyFlow increased computational cost by less than 50% compared to PPO when model parameters were comparable. Even with an eightfold increase in embedding dimensions, the cost remained below twice that of PPO, demonstrating its practical efficiency.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Sensitivity to Clipping Range (ϵ):** Confirmed the theoretical finding that a smaller $\epsilon$ (reducing approximation error) can hinder learning progress by making updates overly conservative. An $\epsilon=0.2$ was found to be a good trade-off.
- **Network Initialization:** Glorot initialization with the output layer additionally set to zero (GI+ZOL) yielded the best empirical performance.
- **Time Sampling Strategy:** Uniform sampling from a discrete ODE simulation time grid (USD) proved efficient and effective, showing only minor performance differences compared to uniform continuous sampling or multi-sampling.
- **Different Interpolation Paths:** Evaluation of Rectified-Flow, Stochastic-Interpolant, and TrigFlow paths showed similar performance on locomotion tasks (ANYmal-D), but Rectified-Flow and TrigFlow paths performed better on the MultiGoal test, suggesting nuances in their approximation quality, particularly concerning the relationship between score function and velocity field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PolicyFlow represents a significant advancement in the field of reinforcement learning by effectively bridging the gap between powerful generative models and stable on-policy optimization. Its key contributions address long-standing challenges and open up new avenues for research and application.

1. **Enabling Expressive Policies in On-Policy RL:** The most direct impact of PolicyFlow is that it makes highly expressive Continuous Normalizing Flows a practical policy class for on-policy RL algorithms like PPO. This capability moves beyond the limitations of simple Gaussian policies, allowing agents to learn richer, multimodal action distributions, which are essential for tasks requiring diverse behaviors, nuanced control, and robust decision-making in complex environments.

2. **Computational Efficiency and Stability:** By proposing a clever approximation for importance ratios that avoids costly full ODE simulations and path-wise backpropagation during training, PolicyFlow overcomes a major computational bottleneck. This efficiency, combined with PPO's inherent stability, makes it a viable solution for online learning where previous flow-based or diffusion-based methods struggled with speed or numerical issues. The theoretical justification for the first-order error approximation provides confidence in its practical effectiveness within the PPO framework.

3. **Principled Entropy Regularization:** The introduction of the Brownian regularizer is a novel and impactful contribution. It offers a computationally lightweight and principled approach to encourage exploration and prevent mode collapse in flow-based policies, without resorting to expensive log-likelihood computations or ad-hoc heuristics. This mechanism is crucial for learning diverse and robust policies, as qualitatively demonstrated in the MultiGoal environment.

4. **Advancing Robotics and Control:** The strong performance of PolicyFlow on benchmarks like MuJoCo Playground and IsaacLab (a suite for large-scale robot learning) highlights its potential for significant impact in robotics. The ability to capture multimodal action distributions could lead to more agile, adaptable, and generalized robotic control policies, especially in tasks requiring complex manipulation, locomotion over varied terrains, or robust interaction with dynamic environments.

5. **Versatile Framework for Generative RL:** PolicyFlow provides a versatile foundation that could be extended to other areas. The authors suggest future work in fine-tuning flow-matching policies, applying it to offline RL, and exploring connections to diffusion models through score-based objectives. This indicates a broader potential for PolicyFlow to serve as a cornerstone for integrating generative modeling principles more deeply and practically within the RL paradigm.

In conclusion, PolicyFlow significantly pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with on-policy reinforcement learning. By resolving critical computational and stability challenges, and introducing an effective exploration mechanism, it makes expressive generative policies practical, robust, and efficient. This work promises to unlock new levels of performance and behavioral complexity in AI agents, particularly in domains like robotics and complex decision-making systems.
