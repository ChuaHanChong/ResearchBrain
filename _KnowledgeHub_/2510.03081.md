# Embracing Evolution: A Call for Body-Control Co-Design in Embodied Humanoid Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> This position paper proposes a framework for body-control co-design in humanoid robots, advocating for the joint optimization of robot morphology and control policies. It formalizes this approach as a bi-level optimization problem and outlines methodologies to address design complexity, physical evaluation, and scalability challenges to achieve enhanced embodied intelligence and adaptability.

## Problem

- Current humanoid robot development primarily optimizes control policies for fixed physical structures, which are often manually engineered.
- The robot's physical morphology is treated as a static component, limiting the full realization of embodied intelligence and adaptation to diverse environments.
- Co-design (joint hardware-software optimization) is largely underexplored for complex humanoid robots, despite its potential for creating more capable physical agents.

## Method

- Formalize humanoid co-design as a bi-level optimization problem, where the outer loop optimizes morphology and the inner loop learns control policies for a given body.
- Address design complexity through strategic exploration within constrained design spaces and mitigate physical evaluation challenges via a Sim2Real paradigm utilizing high-fidelity simulations.
- Enable scalability across tasks by developing meta-policies capable of adapting to various goal specifications and leveraging advanced generalizable decision models.

## Results

- The paper successfully formalizes humanoid co-design as a bi-level optimization framework, integrating morphology optimization with reinforcement learning for control.
- It demonstrates the conceptual feasibility of co-design by proposing practical strategies to overcome challenges in design complexity, physical evaluation, and task scalability.
- Arguments are presented for co-design's necessity in advancing embodied intelligence, fostering interdisciplinary research, and enabling adaptive, high-performance humanoid robots for diverse applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embodied intelligence in humanoids fundamentally benefits from co-evolving both physical structure and control policies, mirroring principles of biological adaptation.
> - The complexity of humanoid co-design can be made tractable by adopting structured optimization techniques, constrained design parameterization, and leveraging advanced simulation environments.
> - Co-design provides a systematic, principled approach to robot development that moves beyond heuristic design, leading to more efficient and application-specific performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) focuses on creating agents that learn through active interaction with their environment. Within this paradigm, humanoid robots are recognized as general-purpose physical agents with the potential to operate in diverse real-world settings due to their human-like form and capabilities. Recent advancements in this area have largely concentrated on optimizing control policies for these robots, often leveraging deep neural networks to enable scalability across various tasks. These control policies are typically developed for predefined physical structures, which are frequently the product of manual engineering and heuristic design principles.

The current research landscape, as described by the authors, primarily treats the robot's physical morphology as a fixed component of the environment. While co-design—the joint optimization of robot control policies and morphological design—has been explored in simpler robotic systems such as quadruped robots, soft robots, bipedal robots, and modular robots, its application to advanced humanoid robots remains largely underexplored. This gap represents a significant limitation, as embodied intelligence is posited to be fundamentally grounded in an agent's physical structure, analogous to biological evolution where organisms adapt their bodies to environmental conditions.

This position paper aims to address this gap by advocating for a co-design mechanism in humanoid robotics. It seeks to reframe the approach to developing embodied humanoid intelligence by arguing for the simultaneous evolution of both control strategies and the robot's physical structure. By providing a principled formulation and analyzing the feasibility and necessity of this approach, the paper positions co-design as a fundamental requirement for the development of future intelligent and adaptable humanoid agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to issue a call for the integration of "body-control co-design" into the development of embodied humanoid robots. This entails jointly optimizing both the control strategies and the physical structure (morphology) of the robots.

The motivation for this advocacy stems from several considerations:

- **Biological Inspiration:** The authors draw inspiration from natural systems, where organisms iteratively evolve their body morphology and behaviors to adapt to dynamic environmental conditions and optimize performance. This biological precedent suggests that for humanoid robots to achieve true embodied intelligence and operate effectively in complex, diverse real-world environments, they should similarly be capable of adapting both their form and behavior.
- **Limitations of Current Paradigms:** Existing research in humanoid robotics predominantly focuses on optimizing control policies for robots with fixed, pre-defined physical structures. These structures are often the result of manual engineering and heuristic approaches, which are not systematically optimized as part of the embodied system. This approach is viewed as insufficient because embodied intelligence is not solely determined by control performance but is also fundamentally linked to the agent’s physical characteristics. Without morphological adaptation, robots may not fully realize their potential for general-purpose operation and task-specific optimization.
- **Underexplored Domain for Humanoids:** While the concept of co-design has been applied to less complex robots (e.g., quadrupedal, soft, bipedal, modular robots), its extension to humanoid robots, which feature intricate upper and lower body structures and a wider range of capabilities (dexterity, mobility, perception, intelligence), is largely undeveloped. This leads to fundamental questions regarding the feasibility and necessity of co-design in achieving advanced embodied intelligence in humanoids.
- **Addressing Inherent Challenges:** The implementation of co-design for humanoid robots presents significant challenges, including algorithmic complexity due to high-dimensional design spaces, difficulties in physical evaluation and iterative hardware modification, and limited scalability of task-specific optimizations to general-purpose humanoid platforms. The paper is motivated to propose practical methodologies to address these challenges, thereby demonstrating the tractability of the co-design approach.
- **Advancing Embodied Intelligence:** Ultimately, the authors are motivated to position co-design as a cornerstone for developing a new generation of intelligent and adaptable humanoid agents. They argue that by embracing an evolutionary co-design mechanism, humanoid robots can achieve greater dexterity, mobility, perception, and intelligence, enabling more robust and generalizable performance across a wide range of tasks and environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper presents a conceptual and argumentative methodology rather than experimental results, as it is a position paper advocating for a research direction. The core approach involves:

#### 3.1. Formalizing Humanoid Co-Design as a Bi-Level Optimization Problem

The authors propose framing the humanoid co-design problem as a bi-level optimization.
- **Outer Loop (Level II): Morphology Optimization:** This level involves optimizing the robot's physical structure, denoted by $\psi \in \Psi$, to maximize overall performance, subject to system-level constraints such as cost ($f_c(\psi) \leq \epsilon$).
- **Inner Loop (Level I): Control Policy Learning:** For a given robot morphology $\psi$, this level focuses on learning an optimal control policy $\pi$ using reinforcement learning (RL). The objective is to maximize the expected cumulative discounted rewards $J(\pi, M_\psi)$ within the Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by the specific physical robot structure $M_\psi$. The reasoning model, often a large Vision-Language Model (VLM), is generally considered fixed during this process due to its computational cost and lesser dependence on detailed physical structure.

#### 3.2. Addressing Challenges and Demonstrating Feasibility

The paper acknowledges several challenges associated with humanoid co-design and proposes strategies to address them:

- **Complexity of the Co-design Problem:**
 - **Strategic Exploration:** Instead of random exploration methods common in genetic algorithms, the paper proposes framing the physical robot structure process as an MDP to apply more efficient and provably effective strategic exploration techniques from RL, such as Bayesian updates, Upper Confidence Bound (UCB), and uncertainty-driven heuristics.
 - **Constrained Design Space:** To manage computational burden and ensure design identifiability, the authors suggest limiting structural modifications to a few key modules (e.g., thigh/shank lengths, motor configurations, inertial parameters) and bounding the range of design parameters. This approach, leveraging the Extreme Value Theorem, aims to guarantee the existence of an optimal solution and aid convergence.

- **Difficulties in Physical Evaluation:**
 - **Sim2Real Paradigm:** To overcome the complexity and cost of iterative physical modifications and real-world evaluation, the paper advocates for conducting both robot design and evaluation within simulated environments (e.g., MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, RoboCasa).
 - **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** Strategies proposed to mitigate the simulation-to-reality gap include:
 - Extending domain randomization and domain adaptation techniques to humanoid control environments.
 - Utilizing high-fidelity and photorealistic simulation platforms (e.g., RoboCasa, Isaac Lab, Genesis).
 - Exploring World Function Models, which learn environmental dynamics directly from real-world data, treating simulation as a regression task rather than relying on manually specified physics.

- **Limited Scalability Across Tasks:**
 - **Meta-Policy Learning:** To enable the co-designed robot to perform a wide range of general-purpose tasks, the paper emphasizes learning a meta-policy $\pi$. This goal-conditioned policy ($\pi: S \times G \rightarrow A$) can adapt to diverse task specifications (commands, target poses, natural language descriptions) across varied environments.
 - **Advanced Backbones:** Leveraging recent advances in generalizable decision models like Decision Transformers and Diffusion Policies is suggested for implementing such scalable meta-policies. During training, goals can be sampled from a pool, and the robot's policy is conditioned on each goal, adapting to the proposed design configuration.

#### 3.3. Establishing the Necessity of Co-Design

The paper presents arguments for the necessity of co-design from three perspectives:

- **Methodological:** Co-design offers a principled, systematic optimization framework for robot morphology, moving beyond intuitive or experience-based manual design. This approach aims to enhance both the efficiency and efficacy of robot development by exploring a larger, jointly optimized search space of morphology and control.
- **Application-driven:** Co-design facilitates adaptive body shaping tailored for specific real-world tasks and environments. It enables dynamic adjustments to the robot's structure to balance inherent trade-offs (e.g., dexterity vs. mobility), thereby optimizing performance for target applications, analogous to biological specialization.
- **Community-oriented:** Co-design inherently promotes cross-disciplinary collaboration by integrating machine learning, robotics design principles, and mechanical engineering knowledge within a unified optimization framework. This aligns with successful "AI + X" paradigms that have led to breakthroughs in other scientific and industrial domains.

#### 3.4. Identifying Open Research Questions

To guide future research, the paper outlines specific short-term and long-term open questions, covering areas such as efficient representation for robot design, benchmarking for humanoid co-design, design-aware policy optimization, and long-term integration with world models and reasoning systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a position paper, this work does not present empirical data or experimental results in the traditional sense. Instead, its "findings" are the substantiated arguments and conclusions drawn from its analytical discourse regarding the viability and imperative of co-design in humanoid robotics.

The main findings and arguments presented are:

- **Formalization of Co-Design:** The paper establishes that humanoid robot co-design can be formally structured as a bi-level optimization problem. This framework aims to jointly optimize both the physical morphology ($\psi$) and the control policy ($\pi$), with the inner loop focusing on policy learning for a given morphology and the outer loop refining the morphology based on policy performance and constraints.
- **Demonstrated Feasibility of Co-Design:** The authors contend that despite its complexity, humanoid co-design is feasible through specific methodological advancements. These include:
 - **Strategic Exploration:** Moving beyond random search, the paper proposes applying strategic exploration algorithms within a constrained design space to efficiently identify optimal morphological parameters.
 - **Sim2Real Paradigm:** It is argued that the challenges of physical evaluation can be largely mitigated by conducting design and evaluation in high-fidelity simulated environments, with ongoing research focused on bridging the Sim2Real gap through domain randomization, advanced simulators, and World Function Models.
 - **Meta-Policy Learning:** The development of generalizable meta-policies capable of performing across a wide range of tasks and environments is presented as a solution to the scalability limitations of traditional task-specific co-design.
- **Established Necessity of Co-Design:** The paper asserts that co-design is not merely an optional enhancement but an essential component for achieving advanced embodied intelligence in humanoid robots, based on multi-faceted arguments:
 - **Methodological Enhancement:** Co-design provides a principled and systematic approach to optimizing robot morphology, departing from heuristic or intuition-based designs. This systematic optimization is argued to be more efficient and effective, leading to performance gains unattainable through control optimization alone on fixed structures.
 - **Application-Specific Adaptation:** The capability for adaptive body shaping is critical for humanoid robots to effectively operate across diverse real-world applications. Co-design enables the dynamic tailoring of morphology to address task-specific requirements and design trade-offs (e.g., dexterity versus mobility), allowing for specialized performance analogous to biological evolution.
 - **Catalyst for Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration:** The inherently multidisciplinary nature of co-design—spanning machine learning, robotics design, and mechanical engineering—is identified as a crucial driver for fostering collaborative research and developing integrated systems. This collaboration is presented as vital for groundbreaking advancements, drawing parallels with successful "AI + X" initiatives in other domains.
- **Identification of Key Research Gaps:** The paper highlights specific areas that require further research to fully realize the potential of humanoid co-design. These include the need for efficient representations of robot design, standardized benchmarking environments, and the development of design-aware policies that can generalize across varied morphologies.
- **Formulation of Future Research Directions:** Both short-term and long-term open questions are presented, providing a roadmap for future investigations in the field. These include advanced topics such as integrating co-design with learned World Function Models and extending optimization to include high-level planning and reasoning models.

In summary, the paper's main "findings" are its comprehensive arguments and proposed framework, which collectively establish the conceptual foundation for embracing body-control co-design as a fundamental paradigm for future humanoid robot development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Paradigm Shift in Humanoid Robotics:** The paper advocates for a fundamental shift from the prevailing paradigm of optimizing control policies on fixed robot structures to a co-design approach where both morphology and control evolve simultaneously. This could redefine how humanoid robots are conceived, designed, and developed, moving closer to principles observed in biological evolution.
- **Enhanced Embodied Intelligence and Adaptability:** By enabling robots to iteratively adapt their physical form and behavior, co-design has the potential to produce humanoid robots with significantly greater embodied intelligence. These robots could exhibit superior adaptability, robustness, and generalizability, allowing them to perform more effectively and seamlessly in diverse, dynamic, and unstructured real-world environments.
- **Improved Task-Specific Performance:** The ability to dynamically tailor a robot's body structure for specific tasks and resource constraints could lead to optimized performance that surpasses what is achievable with fixed designs. This includes addressing fundamental trade-offs, such as those between dexterity and mobility, to create specialized agents for particular applications.
- **More Principled and Efficient Robot Development:** The proposed bi-level optimization framework offers a principled methodology for robot morphology design, replacing reliance on manual engineering and heuristics. This systematic approach has the potential to make the design and development process more efficient, reducing the time and resources required for design iterations and uncovering novel, high-performing configurations.
- **Wider Range of Applications:** Humanoid robots capable of adapting their physical attributes to specific operational contexts (e.g., industrial manipulation, patrol, service tasks) could unlock a broader array of real-world applications. This flexibility would increase their utility in sectors ranging from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and domestic assistance.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Research:** The paper's emphasis on co-design intrinsically promotes deeper collaboration between traditionally distinct fields: machine learning, robotics design, and mechanical engineering. This interdisciplinary synergy is crucial for tackling the complex challenges of embodied AI and could lead to integrated breakthroughs that leverage expertise from across these domains.
- **Guidance for Future Research Directions:** By clearly articulating open research questions, both short-term and long-term, the paper provides a roadmap for future investigations. This includes critical areas such as developing efficient representations for robot morphology, establishing standardized benchmarks for co-design, designing morphology-aware control policies, and integrating advanced concepts like World Function Models and reasoning systems into the co-design loop. This guidance can focus research efforts and accelerate progress in the field.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation Robotics:** Ultimately, the work positions co-design as a foundational approach for creating the next generation of intelligent, adaptable, and general-purpose humanoid robots. These robots would not only be capable of advanced control but also possess the inherent ability to evolve their physical being, offering a pathway toward truly autonomous and versatile physical agents.
