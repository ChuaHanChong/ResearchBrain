# In-N-On: Scaling Egocentric Manipulation with in-the-wild and on-task Data

> [!summary] Summary
> UC San Diego researchers developed a scalable method called "In-N-On" that leverages large-scale egocentric human data, categorized into "in-the-wild" and "on-task" types, to train foundational robot manipulation policies. This approach enables emergent capabilities such as zero-shot language following for instructions unseen by the robot and 1-shot learning for complex bimanual tasks, showing improved performance and robustness on humanoid robots.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation policies lack real-world generalizability due to limited, often task-specific, robot demonstration data.
- Harnessing human egocentric data is challenging because its vast heterogeneity (diverse "in-the-wild" vs. task-aligned "on-task") can lead to poor transfer or catastrophic forgetting.
- Bridging the embodiment gap between human demonstrations and robot execution requires a unified representation and effective domain adaptation for skill transfer.

## Method

- A two-stage "**In-N-On**" training recipe: initial pre-training on large-scale "in-the-wild" human data, followed by post-training on task-aligned "on-task" data.
- Curation of PHSD, a comprehensive human-humanoid dataset, coupled with a unified human-centric state-action space and retargeting tools for cross-embodiment compatibility.
- Training a language-conditioned Human0 model using flow matching and a ==Gradient Reversal Layer==-based domain adaptation to create embodiment-invariant features.

## Results

- Human0 demonstrates zero-shot language following for instructions learned exclusively from human data, achieving 30/30 success in O.O.D. multi-object grasping compared to 16/30 for baselines.
- The model enables 1-shot learning, achieving a 5/20 success rate on the complex bimanual pouring task with only a single robot demonstration, outperforming all comparative methods.
- Significant performance gains were observed on challenging, long-horizon tasks like burger assembly, with over **100%** relative improvement in O.O.D. settings against baselines.
- Domain adaptation with a Gradient Reversal Layer was crucial, improving few-shot pouring task success from **15%** to **25%** by fostering embodiment-invariant representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic, two-stage approach leveraging both diverse "in-the-wild" and task-specific "on-task" human data is essential for emergent robot capabilities like zero-shot language following.
> - Explicit domain adaptation through techniques like Gradient Reversal Layer prevents models from "cheating" by merely distinguishing embodiments, ensuring the learning of truly transferable skills.
> - Pre-training with large human datasets provides strong priors that significantly enhance few-shot learning, allowing robots to quickly adapt to new tasks with minimal robot-specific demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen significant progress, particularly with learning from real robot demonstrations. However, a major challenge remains the lack of robust real-world generalizability in existing foundational manipulation policies, often attributed to the comparatively smaller scale of available robot data compared to fields like large language models (LLMs) or self-driving cars.

This paper positions itself within the emerging trend of "cross-embodiment learning" and "learning from human data" to address this data scarcity. Historically, researchers have explored using human data for modular approaches (e.g., learning affordances, planning) or for pre-training visual representations. More recently, advancements in computer vision, such as precise finger keypoint tracking, have enabled direct end-to-end training using high-quality human data.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: the vast heterogeneity of human data (ranging from daily activities to specific manipulation tasks) often leads to sub-optimal outcomes when simply used for pre-training followed by fine-tuning with robot data. This common approach can suffer from "catastrophic forgetting" due to the misalignment between diverse "in-the-wild" human data and specific "on-task" robot data. While some methods have used either in-the-wild data for pre-training or on-task data for co-training, this paper argues that the *full potential* of human data is unlocked only by systematically combining both.

The novelty of "In-N-On" lies in providing a **scalable recipe** that leverages both categories of human data – large-scale, diverse "in-the-wild" data for initial pre-training to bootstrap a base model, and smaller, task-aligned "on-task" data for subsequent post-training to refine the policy for specific tasks. This systematic dual-stage approach, coupled with a unified human-centric action space and domain adaptation techniques, directly addresses the challenges of data heterogeneity and embodiment gap in learning generalizable manipulation skills. It pushes the boundaries of how human data can inform robot learning, particularly for achieving emergent properties like zero-shot language following and few-shot learning, which are critical for real-world robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are multi-faceted, aiming to overcome the limitations of current robot manipulation policies by harnessing the power of human data:

- **Develop a Scalable Recipe for Egocentric Data Utilization:** The core motivation is to provide a principled methodology for collecting and effectively utilizing egocentric human data. This involves categorizing human data into "in-the-wild" (diverse, easy-to-collect) and "on-task" (task-aligned, smaller scale) and systematically analyzing how each type contributes to the learning process.
- **Curate a Large-Scale Human-Humanoid Dataset (PHSD):** To support the proposed recipe, the authors aim to build a comprehensive dataset, PHSD, comprising over 1,000 hours of diverse in-the-wild egocentric data and over 20 hours of specific on-task data. This dataset is designed to model egocentric human-humanoid behavior in a unified format.
- **Learn a Foundational Egocentric Language-Conditioned Policy (Human0):** The paper seeks to train a base model, Human0, capable of understanding language instructions and generating manipulation actions. This model is explicitly designed to leverage the scaled human data and incorporate domain adaptation techniques to bridge the gap between human and humanoid embodiments.
- **Achieve Novel Properties in Robot Manipulation:** A key motivation is to demonstrate emergent capabilities in Human0 resulting from the scaled and structured use of human data. These include:
 - **Language following of instructions unseen in robot data:** Enabling robots to understand and execute commands learned primarily from human demonstrations.
 - **Few-shot learning:** Allowing robots to quickly adapt to new tasks or objects with very minimal robot demonstrations.
 - **Improved on-task performance and robustness:** Enhancing the robot's ability to perform complex, long-horizon tasks reliably, even in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Systematic Analysis of Data Usage and Domain Adaptation:** The authors are motivated to conduct a rigorous study on how in-the-wild and on-task data interact and how domain adaptation techniques can prevent models from "cheating" by merely distinguishing between human and robot inputs, instead forcing them to learn embodiment-invariant representations.

Ultimately, the motivation stems from the desire to enable more generalizable, robust, and adaptable robot manipulation policies by leveraging the rich and scalable source of human expertise available through egocentric videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "In-N-On" methodology centers on a two-stage training recipe for a base egocentric manipulation model, Human0, supported by a novel data curation strategy and domain adaptation techniques.

#### 3.1. PH

S D: Physical Humans-Humanoids Dataset

The paper advocates for a unified human-centric state-action space to overcome hardware heterogeneity and facilitate large-scale cross-embodiment learning.
- **Unified Human-centric State-Action Space:** This space includes:
 - `T_head ∈ SE(3)`: Head pose (rotation and translation relative to the world frame, often from wearable devices).
 - `T_Lwrist, T_Rwrist ∈ SE(3)`: Wrist poses relative to the head pose.
 - `P_Lfinger, P_Rfinger ∈ R^(3×5)`: Fingertip keypoints, crucial for dexterous manipulation and optimization-based retargeting.
 - `D_Lgripper, D_Rgripper ∈ R`: Optional gripper distance, mapped from thumb-index fingertip distance for parallel grippers.
- **Retargeting Software Suite:** An IK/FK (Inverse Kinematics/Forward Kinematics) and retargeting software suite, based on Pinocchio, is implemented to convert robot joint positions to/from this human-centric space. This crucial tool standardizes data from diverse humanoid hardware, making different robot datasets compatible for training.
- **Aggregating In-the-wild Datasets for Pre-training:**
 - **EgoDex:** Over 800 hours of skill-rich human demonstrations collected using Apple Vision Pros, providing head, wrist, and finger pose data.
 - **ActionNet:** Over 100 hours of humanoid demonstrations, primarily from Fourier GR1T1 robots with dexterous hands.
 - **PH2D:** Contains both human (via Apple Vision Pro) and humanoid (Unitree H1 with Inspire hands) demonstrations.
 This aggregation provides over 1,000 hours of diverse, large-scale data for initial model pre-training.
- **Data for Post-training:** High-quality on-task human data is collected using commercial-grade devices like Apple Vision Pro and Meta Aria Glass for precise head, wrist, and fingertip tracking. Robot data is collected using Apple Vision Pro with OpenTV for task-aligned demonstrations. This data is significantly smaller (e.g., 20+ hours) but directly relevant to target tasks.

#### 3.2. Human

0: Foundational Egocentric Base Model

The Human0 model employs a language-conditioned flow matching architecture and is trained in two stages with a crucial domain adaptation component.

- **Architecture:**
 - **Vision Module:** A SigLIP-based encoder extracts visual tokens, ensuring strong alignment between visual inputs and text.
 - **Language Embeddings:** Text embeddings are combined with visual tokens to form a joint multimodal representation, processed by transformer blocks.
 - **State/Action Encoding:** Lightweight MLPs encode the human-centric state into a pose latent `x` and decode output actions.
 The model is initialized with a pre-trained VLM checkpoint, with projection modules adapted for the human-centric representation.
- **Pre-training on Human and Robot Data:**
 - **Objective:** To learn a unified vision-language-action prior from over 1,000 hours of mixed in-the-wild data (EgoDex, ActionNet, PH2D). This stage aims for broad visuomotor priors and alignment of the VLM to human behavior.
 - **Loss Function:** A flow-matching objective (Eq. 2) trains the model to predict the flow vector towards the target action given noisy samples.
 - **Data Mixing:** Due to the dominance of human data, a manually adjusted sampling ratio is used to balance human and robot data during training (Fig. 4a).
- **Post-training on Human and Robot Data:**
 - **Objective:** To refine language grounding and visuomotor control using task-specific on-task human and robot data (e.g., ~20 hours). This stage aligns the policy with real-world task distributions.
 - **Loss Function:** The same flow-matching objective (Eq. 2) is used.
 - **Data Mixing:** Post-training datasets have more robot data, but the authors found sampling slightly more often from human data (e.g., 70%) helps preserve human data semantics (Fig. 4b).
- **Domain Adaptation: Blurring the Line between Embodiments:**
 - **Problem:** Without intervention, the model can implicitly learn to distinguish between human and robot inputs (shown by a linear probe achieving 100% success in Fig. 5), leading to overfitting and poor transfer.
 - **Solution:** A discriminator network `D_ϕ` with a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) is introduced.
 - The discriminator takes concatenated visual and proprioceptive features as input and attempts to classify the embodiment type (human or robot).
 - The GRL ensures that the policy's backbone encoders are optimized adversarially: the discriminator tries to minimize `L_D` (Eq. 4), while the policy's encoders try to maximize it (Eq. 5), effectively making the features embodiment-invariant.
 - **Final Loss:** The overall training objective combines the flow matching loss with the domain adaptation loss (Eq. 6), with `λ = 0.1` balancing the two.

This comprehensive approach, from standardized data representation and collection to a two-stage training paradigm with explicit domain adaptation, forms the robust foundation of Human0.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate Human0 's capabilities across four humanoid manipulation tasks: single object grasping, multi-object grasping, burger assembly, and pouring. These tasks are tested in both in-distribution (I.D.) and out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) settings on Unitree H1 and G1 humanoid robots, against baselines including π0, GR00T N1, HAT (with human data), and Human0 w/o human.

- **Zero-shot Language Following Capability from Human Data:**
 - Human0 demonstrates an emergent ability to follow language instructions that were *only present in the human training data* and unseen by the robot during its specific training.
 - In the multi-object grasping task, Human0 robustly grasps unseen objects based on language cues, outperforming baselines like π0 and GR00T N1 which often guess randomly (Table 1, O.O.D. for Multi Object Grasping: 30/30 success for Human0 vs. 16/30 for π0).
 - For the burger assembly task, Human0 successfully uses tools (tongs) to pick up ingredients (e.g., Mozzarella cheese) that were only in human data, showing tool use and reasoning beyond robot-specific training (Table 1, O.O.D. for Burger assembly: 7/12 for Human0 vs. 3/12 for GR00T N1). This highlights a significant advancement in language understanding for robot manipulation.

- **Human0 Enables 1-Shot Robot Data Learning:**
 - Despite the limitations in zero-shot *behavior* learning from human data alone, Human0 significantly enhances few-shot learning.
 - In the challenging bimanual pouring task, with only a *single* robot demonstration, Human0 achieves a 5/20 success rate, outperforming all baselines (Table 1). This indicates that the broad priors learned from human data allow the model to generalize rapidly from minimal robot-specific examples, suggesting a path towards truly data-efficient robot learning.

- **Human Data Improves Overall Performance on Challenging Tasks:**
 - Human0 shows substantial performance improvements on complex, long-horizon tasks like burger assembly. It achieves over 100% relative improvement compared to baselines in O.O.D. scenarios (Table 1).
 - The model exhibits greater robustness to external disturbances like lighting and background changes (as further detailed in the supplementary material, Table 4, showing consistent performance across different tablecloths). This indicates that the diverse human data contributes to more generalized and resilient policies.

- **Ablation Study: Domain Adaptation Prevents Model from 'Cheating' and Helps Few-shot Learning:**
 - Initial linear probing revealed that a vanilla model could perfectly discriminate between human and robot input features (Fig. 5), indicating it was learning embodiment-specific cues rather than general skills.
 - The introduction of the Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) successfully addressed this. Post-GRL, linear probing shows that the intermediate features become domain-invariant, with predicted probabilities centered around 50% (Fig. 7), meaning the model can no longer reliably distinguish between human and robot data at the feature level.
 - This algorithmic improvement directly translates to better robot execution, particularly in few-shot learning scenarios. For the pouring task, domain adaptation improved the success rate from 15% to 25% (Table 2), demonstrating that forcing embodiment-invariant representations allows the robot to leverage human priors more effectively.

- **Performance Dynamics with Varying Robot Data:**
 - An ablation on the single-object grasping task showed that human data effectively regularizes the learning process, especially in low robot data regimes (Fig. 8). This reinforces the value of pre-training with large human datasets to provide a strong foundation for subsequent robot-specific learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "In-N-On" research makes significant contributions to the field of robot manipulation, primarily by offering a principled and scalable approach to leverage human expertise for robot learning.

- **Advancing Generalizability and Robustness:** By systematically combining large-scale in-the-wild human data for pre-training and task-aligned on-task human data for post-training, the paper demonstrates a clear path toward more generalizable and robust robot manipulation policies. The emergent properties—zero-shot language following of unseen instructions and few-shot learning—are crucial steps towards truly adaptable robots capable of operating in diverse, unstructured environments.
- **Bridging the Embodiment Gap:** The proposed unified human-centric state-action space and the accompanying retargeting software suite provide a robust framework for integrating heterogeneous human and robot data. The domain adaptation technique with the GRL is a critical algorithmic contribution, ensuring that the model learns embodiment-invariant representations, thus facilitating effective knowledge transfer from humans to robots. This addresses a long-standing challenge in cross-embodiment learning.
- **Democratizing Robot Learning:** The demonstration of few-shot learning, particularly with just a single robot demonstration for a complex bimanual task, suggests that this methodology can significantly reduce the data burden for deploying new robot skills. This has the potential to democratize access to advanced robot capabilities, making it easier for researchers and practitioners with limited resources to train capable manipulation policies without needing vast amounts of expensive robot data.
- **Enhancing Language Understanding in Robotics:** The ability of Human0 to follow language instructions unseen in robot training data, but present in human data, represents a substantial leap in language grounding for robotic tasks. This implies that robots can inherit a richer understanding of task semantics and object properties directly from human demonstrations, opening up new avenues for natural language interaction with robots.
- **Practical Applications and Future Directions:** The focus on real-world tasks like "fast food worker" assembly showcases the direct applicability of this research to industrial and service robotics. The commitment to open-sourcing the PHS D dataset and Human0 model weights is a vital contribution to the research community, enabling further development and benchmarking. Future work could involve scaling human data even further, exploring different robot embodiments beyond humanoids, and investigating how these techniques can be extended to full whole-body loco-manipulation tasks.

In conclusion, "In-N-On" presents a compelling blueprint for how to unlock the full potential of human egocentric data, laying the groundwork for a new generation of more intelligent, versatile, and human-like robotic systems.
