# World Action Models are Zero-shot Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamZero, a 14B World Action Model (WAM) developed by NVIDIA, enables zero-shot robot policies by jointly predicting future video states and actions from diverse data. It achieves an average task progress of 39.5% on unseen tasks and demonstrates 42% relative improvement in cross-embodiment transfer using video-only demonstrations, all while maintaining real-time control at 7Hz through significant optimization.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models struggle to generalize to unseen physical motions in novel environments — static image-text pretraining provides limited spatiotemporal priors.
- Learning generalist robot policies requires extensive, repetitive, task-specific demonstrations — inefficient and doesn't scale.
- Cross-embodiment transfer is inefficient, typically requiring new action-labeled data for each robot platform.

## Method

- Introduces **DreamZero**, a 14B WAM using an ==autoregressive diffusion transformer== that jointly predicts future video frames and robot actions.
- Leverages a pretrained video diffusion backbone with a ==flow-matching objective== to learn world physics and spatiotemporal dynamics from diverse robot data.
- **DreamZero-Flash**: asynchronous execution with decoupled noise schedules to achieve real-time closed-loop control at ==7Hz==.

## Results

- **62.2%** task progress on seen tasks in unseen environments and **39.5%** on 10 entirely unseen tasks, both far exceeding SotA VLA baselines (**27.4%** and **16.3%** respectively).
- **42%** relative improvement in cross-embodiment transfer using only 10–20 minutes of video-only demonstrations.
- DreamZero-Flash: **7Hz** inference (**150ms**), achieving **74%** task progress vs. **83%** for full 4-step DreamZero.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly predicting future world states (video) + actions provides powerful spatiotemporal priors for zero-shot generalization.
> - Generalist policies can be learned from diverse, non-repetitive data — reducing reliance on laborious demonstrations.
> - Cross-embodiment transfer with WAMs is highly efficient — video-only demos from other robots or humans suffice.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has seen significant advancements with the advent of large-scale models, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, often built upon pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), excel at semantic generalization, leveraging vast internet knowledge to understand and respond to diverse language instructions. For instance, a VLA might successfully execute "move coke can to Taylor Swift" by recognizing the object and target location through its linguistic priors.

However, existing VLAs face a critical limitation: their generalization to *unseen physical motions* in *novel environments* remains constrained. They struggle with tasks requiring precise spatial awareness, geometric understanding, and dynamic physical interactions (ee.g., "untie a shoelace" if that specific skill was not in the training data), primarily because their pretraining on static image-text datasets does not imbue them with rich spatiotemporal priors necessary for physical dynamics and motor control. This deficiency often necessitates collecting extensive, task- and environment-specific action data, which is highly impractical for open-world deployment.

"World Action Models" (WAMs) represent an emerging paradigm that addresses these VLA limitations. WAMs, including DreamZero, are designed to learn physical dynamics explicitly by *jointly predicting future world states (represented by video) and actions*. By leveraging pretrained video diffusion backbones, WAMs inherit powerful spatiotemporal priors from web-scale video data, shifting the action learning problem from direct observation-to-action imitation to an inverse dynamics problem – essentially, aligning motor commands with predicted visual futures.

DreamZero distinguishes itself from other contemporary WAMs by:
- **Systematically exploring data diversity and scale:** Demonstrating that generalist policies can be learned effectively from heterogeneous, non-repetitive data.
- **Adopting an autoregressive architecture:** Which proves superior for long-horizon world-action modeling and real-time inference.
- **Achieving state-of-the-art generalization:** Across novel tasks, environments, and embodiments.
- **Enabling efficient cross-embodiment transfer:** Including learning from video-only demonstrations and few-shot adaptation to new robots.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts WAMs with other world model architectures, such as latent-space world models (e.g., V-JEPA, Dreamer series) and 3D point cloud world models (e.g., PointWorld). A key distinction is that these alternative approaches typically model forward dynamics and require separate inverse dynamics models or explicit planning/search procedures at test time. In contrast, WAMs like DreamZero directly produce action trajectories aligned with predicted visual futures without test-time optimization, which is crucial for achieving real-time, closed-loop control. This direct action generation, combined with rich visual dynamics priors, positions WAMs as a promising direction for developing truly generalist robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DreamZero is to overcome the inherent limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly their struggles with physical generalization to unseen motions and novel environments. The research identifies three core challenges that VLAs face:
1. **Limited physical generalization:** VLAs struggle to adapt to new environments or generalize to novel tasks beyond the distribution of expert demonstrations, especially when new physical skills or motions are required.
2. **Reliance on repetitive demonstrations:** Learning generalist robot policies traditionally requires carefully repeated, task-specific demonstrations, which is inefficient and scales poorly to diverse, real-world scenarios.
3. **Inefficient cross-embodiment transfer:** Transferring learned skills to different robot platforms or leveraging human demonstrations remains challenging, often requiring extensive action-labeled data for each new embodiment.

DreamZero aims to address these challenges by introducing a "World Action Model" (WAM) paradigm, built on the hypothesis that jointly predicting future video states and actions, particularly using pretrained video diffusion models, can imbue robots with a deeper understanding of world physics and dynamics. This approach is motivated by the idea that:
- Video as a dense representation of world evolution can provide strong priors for learning physical dynamics and how actions should be executed with precise spatial awareness.
- Shifting action learning from direct imitation to inverse dynamics (aligning motor commands with predicted visual futures) can significantly enhance generalization capabilities.

Specifically, the key objectives for DreamZero are:
- To enable **zero-shot generalization** to novel tasks and environments, moving beyond semantic generalization to encompass novel physical motions and skills.
- To facilitate **effective learning from diverse, heterogeneous robot data**, reducing the need for repetitive demonstrations.
- To achieve **efficient cross-embodiment transfer**, allowing robots to learn from video-only demonstrations (e.g., from other robots or humans) and adapt to new embodiments with minimal data.
- To develop **real-time closed-loop control** for WAMs, addressing the computational overhead inherent in video diffusion models through architectural and system-level optimizations.

By achieving these objectives, DreamZero seeks to establish a new benchmark for generalist robot policies that are more adaptive, data-efficient, and capable of operating in open-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamZero, a 14B World Action Model (WAM), is built upon a pretrained video diffusion backbone (Wan2.1-I2V-14B-480P) to jointly predict future video frames and actions. This design enables it to learn world physics priors and spatiotemporal dynamics, which are crucial for open-world generalization.

**Model Architecture:**
DreamZero employs an **autoregressive diffusion transformer** (DiT backbone). It takes three inputs:
1. **Visual Context:** Encoded from current and past observations via a VAE (Variational AutoEncoder).
2. **Language Instructions:** Processed by a text encoder.
3. **Proprioceptive State:** Encoded via a state encoder.
The DiT backbone jointly processes these inputs to predict future video frames and corresponding actions through separate decoders. The autoregressive nature of the model is critical, leveraging a KV-cache for faster inference and allowing the policy to utilize visual observation history. This design preserves the native frame rate, ensuring precise alignment between video frames and robot actions, which is vital for closed-loop control. Training is performed chunk-wise, with each chunk predicting a fixed number of latent frames (K=2) and actions, spanning 1.6 seconds per chunk (5 FPS video, 30 Hz actions for AgiBot).

**Training Objective:**
DreamZero utilizes **flow-matching** as its training objective, similar to recent video diffusion models. It trains a single end-to-end model to jointly denoise video and action modalities with a shared objective, which fosters deep integration and alignment between the two. The model is trained with **teacher forcing**, meaning it denoises a noisy current chunk conditioned on clean previous chunks. The loss function is an L2 distance between the predicted joint velocity (for both video latents and normalized actions) and the target velocity, which represents the difference between the clean and noise-added versions of the respective modalities.

**Real-time Execution and Optimizations (DreamZero-Flash):**
A key challenge for video diffusion models in robotics is their computational overhead, making real-time closed-loop control difficult. DreamZero addresses this with a suite of optimizations to achieve 7Hz control (from a naive 5.7 seconds per action chunk latency):

1. **Asynchronous Closed-Loop Execution:** Decouples model inference from action execution. The robot controller executes the most recent action chunk while inference concurrently runs on the latest observation. This transforms the latency constraint to "inference must complete before the current action chunk expires" (targeting <200ms for 1.6s action chunks). Crucially, after each action execution, ground-truth observations replace predicted frames in the KV cache, preventing error accumulation.

2. **System-level Optimizations:**
 - **CFG Parallelism:** Distributes the conditional and unconditional forward passes (required for classifier-free guidance) across two GPUs, nearly halving per-step latency.
 - **DiT Caching:** Exploits temporal redundancies by reusing cached velocity predictions when cosine similarity between successive velocities exceeds a threshold, reducing effective DiT steps (e.g., from 16 to 4).

3. **Implementation-level Optimizations:**
 - **Torch Compile and CUDA Graphs:** Reduces CPU overhead and fuses operators for static shapes, accelerating inference.
 - **Post-Training Quantization (NVFP4):** Optimizes for NVIDIA Blackwell architecture by quantizing weights and activations to NVFP4, while keeping sensitive operations in FP8 and non-linear ops in FP16 for numerical stability.
 - **Kernel and Scheduler Enhancements:** Uses cuDNN backend for attention and migrates scheduler operations to GPU to minimize CPU-GPU synchronization stalls.

4. **Model-level Optimizations (DreamZero-Flash):**
 - Addresses the train-test mismatch that arises when reducing denoising steps. Standard DreamZero samples a shared timestep for video and action. DreamZero-Flash **decouples noise schedules**, biasing video timesteps towards high-noise states (e.g., using a Beta(7,1) distribution) while keeping action timesteps uniform. This trains the model to predict clean actions from noisy visual context, directly matching the few-step inference regime. As a result, it enables a reduction from 4 to 1 denoising steps, achieving 2.33x speedup with minimal performance degradation.
 - **Action Chunk Smoothing:** Upsamples, applies a Savitzky-Golay filter, then downsamples action chunks to suppress high-frequency noise and ensure smoother robot motions.

Collectively, these optimizations achieve a **38x inference speedup** on GB200, reducing latency from 5.7s to 150ms, making DreamZero viable for real-time robotic control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DreamZero's experimental evaluation on AgiBot G1 mobile bimanual manipulator and Franka single-arm robot demonstrates significant advancements across several key areas:

#### 4.1. Learning from Diverse, Non-repetitive Data (Seen Tasks in Unseen Environments)

- **Performance:** DreamZero (AgiBot G1) achieved **62.2% average task progress** on seen tasks in unseen environments, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art pretrained VLAs (27.4%). From-scratch VLAs showed near-zero success. Similar outperformance was observed on the DROID-Franka dataset.
- **Implication:** This confirms that WAMs, by leveraging video generation as a strong prior, can effectively learn from diverse, heterogeneous robot data without relying on repetitive demonstrations, which is a departure from conventional wisdom in robot learning.

#### 4.2. Zero-shot Generalization to Unseen Tasks

- **Performance:** On 10 tasks entirely absent from pretraining, DreamZero (AgiBot G1) achieved **39.5% average task progress**, markedly higher than pretrained VLA baselines (16.3%) and scratch VLAs (<1%). Specific tasks like "Remove Hat from Mannequin" (85.7%) and "Shake Hands" (59.2%) showed strong performance. Similar trends were observed on DROID-Franka.
- **Qualitative Insight:** VLAs often overfit to dominant training behaviors (e.g., pick-and-place), attempting grasping regardless of instruction. DreamZero, in contrast, performs visual planning for unseen tasks and executes them with strong alignment between generated videos and real-world actions, indicating a deeper understanding of novel task semantics.

#### 4.3. Improved Post-training Performance

- **Performance:** After fine-tuning on task-specific data, DreamZero matched or outperformed VLA baselines across tasks like shirt folding, fruit packing, and table bussing. Importantly, its environment generalization was **retained even after post-training**.
- **Implication:** This indicates that the strong priors learned by WAMs provide a superior foundation for downstream fine-tuning, leading to more robust and generalizable task-specific policies.

#### 4.4. Strong Cross-Embodiment Transfer to Unseen Tasks (Video-only Data)

- **Performance:** Utilizing only 10–20 minutes of *video-only* demonstrations from other robots (YAM) or humans yielded significant relative improvements in unseen task performance for the target AgiBot G1:
 - **Robot-to-robot transfer (YAM → AgiBot):** 38.3% to 55.4% average task progress (**42% relative improvement**).
 - **Human-to-robot transfer (Human → AgiBot):** 38.3% to 54.3% average task progress (**42% relative improvement**).
- **Implication:** This demonstrates WAMs' promising ability to leverage visual information without action labels for cross-embodiment skill transfer, opening pathways to scale learning using abundant human video data.

#### 4.5. Few-shot New Embodiment Adaptation

- **Performance:** DreamZero, pretrained on AgiBot G1, adapted to an entirely new YAM robot with only **30 minutes of play data**. The adapted policy retained strong language-following abilities and generalized to novel objects unseen during this adaptation phase.
- **Implication:** This sets a new benchmark for data-efficient embodiment adaptation, suggesting that learning an implicit inverse dynamics model from predicted videos is inherently more sample-efficient than direct policy learning, especially when leveraging pretrained understanding of physical dynamics.

#### 4.6. DreamZero-Flash for Real-time Performance

- **Performance:** DreamZero-Flash, using a decoupled noise schedule, achieved **74% task progress** on table bussing with a **single denoising step** (150ms inference speed), recovering most of the performance of the 4-step DreamZero baseline (83%) which was 2.33x slower (350ms). Standard DreamZero dropped to 52% at 1 step.
- **Implication:** DreamZero-Flash provides a crucial speed-accuracy trade-off for real-time deployment, enabling high-frequency control without significant performance degradation.

#### 4.7. Model and Data Ablations

- **Data Diversity:** Diverse data (500 hours) yielded **50% task progress** on PnP Easy, significantly outperforming repetitive data (33%), highlighting the importance of varied state-action correspondences for robust inverse dynamics learning.
- **Model Scale:** The 14B DreamZero model (50%) substantially **outperformed the 5B model (21%)**, indicating that policy performance is fundamentally tied to video generation quality and scales with model size. In contrast, larger VLAs still showed 0% task progress on diverse data.
- **Architecture (Autoregressive vs. Bidirectional):** While task progress was similar (50%), the **autoregressive architecture produced smoother motions and was 3-4x faster** due to KV caching, demonstrating its practical advantages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamZero represents a significant advancement in the quest for generalist robotic policies, moving beyond the limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its introduction of World Action Models (WAMs) and the demonstrated capabilities have profound implications for the future of robotics:

**1. A New Paradigm for Generalization:** DreamZero establishes WAMs as a powerful new paradigm capable of achieving unprecedented zero-shot generalization to *unseen physical motions, tasks, and environments*. This addresses a fundamental bottleneck in robotics, where policies typically struggle outside their training distribution. By explicitly modeling world physics through joint video and action prediction, DreamZero empowers robots to truly understand "how" to execute actions, not just "what" to do.

**2. Enhanced Data Efficiency and Scalability:** The ability to learn effectively from diverse, non-repetitive robot data is a game-changer. It means robot learning is less constrained by the laborious and expensive process of collecting highly structured, repetitive demonstrations. This, combined with strong cross-embodiment transfer capabilities from *video-only* data (both robot and human), opens up a massive scaling pathway. The potential to leverage vast, unannotated human video datasets—orders of magnitude larger than any robot dataset—could accelerate robot skill acquisition dramatically.

**3. Practical Real-time Deployment:** The suite of model, system, and implementation optimizations, particularly DreamZero-Flash, is crucial for translating theoretical advancements into practical applications. Achieving real-time closed-loop control at 7Hz, despite the computational intensity of video diffusion models, brings WAMs significantly closer to deployment in dynamic, real-world scenarios requiring reactive behavior.

**4. Foundation for Future Robotic Systems:** DreamZero provides a robust foundation for next-generation robotic systems. Its strong generalization and adaptation capabilities can enable robots to be quickly deployed in novel settings with minimal fine-tuning, performing a wide array of tasks. This work contributes to the vision of truly general-purpose robots that can operate autonomously and adapt fluidly to changing environments and user needs.

**Future Impact and Research Directions:**
The paper also outlines several promising avenues for future work, indicating the trajectory of WAM research:
- **Scaling Laws for WAMs:** Investigating how WAM performance scales with model size, dataset size, and computational resources will be critical for guiding future development, potentially revealing scaling tendencies different from VLAs.
- **Learning from In-the-wild Human Data:** Expanding on the cross-embodiment transfer to leverage vast, uncurated internet human video data could unlock an unprecedented diversity of skills.
- **Further Inference Speedups:** Continued optimization is needed to reach the reactivity levels of simpler VLA models and enable deployment on edge devices.
- **Long-horizon Reasoning:** Integrating WAMs with "System 2" planning capabilities or extending context windows will be essential for tackling complex, multi-step tasks that require memory and strategic planning.
- **High-Precision Tasks:** While DreamZero excels at broad generalization, further research is needed to refine its capabilities for tasks requiring sub-centimeter precision, potentially by re-evaluating the balance between breadth and dense demonstrations.
- **Embodiment Design for WAMs:** Exploring how robot morphology, particularly human-like embodiments with dexterous manipulation, can synergize with WAMs to leverage human motion priors from video pretraining, is a fascinating direction.

By addressing critical challenges in generalization, data efficiency, and real-time control, DreamZero not only pushes the state-of-the-art in robot learning but also lays a robust groundwork for the development of adaptable, intelligent, and widely deployable robotic systems. The open-sourcing of the model and code further ensures that this impactful research can be built upon by the wider scientific community.
