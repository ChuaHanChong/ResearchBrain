# Joint Embeddings Go Temporal

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KTH, NYU, and the Flatiron Institute developed Time Series JEPA (TS-JEPA), a self-supervised learning framework that adapts the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for time series data. This approach learns robust representations by performing masked reconstruction in a latent space, demonstrating balanced performance across classification and forecasting tasks and superior sample efficiency with limited labeled data.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods for time series are vulnerable to noise and confounding variables, as input-space reconstruction can learn superficial patterns instead of meaningful features.
- Many current time series SSL techniques tend to specialize, excelling in either classification or forecasting but rarely achieving strong, balanced performance across diverse tasks.
- Developing general-purpose time series foundation models is challenging due to the unique characteristics of temporal data and the limitations of prior SSL approaches.

## Method

- The paper introduces ==Time Series JEPA== (**TS-JEPA**), which applies the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm to time series by predicting masked segments in a latent space.
- TS-JEPA employs a Transformer-based architecture with an encoder, a predictor, and an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) encoder to ensure stable learning targets and prevent representation collapse.
- A one-dimensional CNN tokenizer converts time series into patches, which are then masked, encoded, and predicted in the latent space using an L1 loss function.

## Results

- TS-JEPA consistently outperformed contrastive (TS2Vec) and autoregressive baselines in most classification tasks, achieving performance competitive with, or occasionally surpassing, MAE, and approaching fully-supervised transformer models.
- For long-term forecasting, TS-JEPA showed superior performance to autoregressive strategies on two out of three datasets (ETT-Small and Electricity), suggesting enhanced stability over extended prediction horizons.
- In scenarios with limited labeled data, TS-JEPA achieved higher classification accuracy than a fully-supervised transformer model when only 5-**20%** of labels were available, highlighting its sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent-space prediction, as implemented in TS-JEPA, offers a robust alternative to input-space reconstruction for time series, effectively mitigating the impact of noise and irrelevant details.
> - A single TS-JEPA pre-trained model can achieve a strong balance of performance across both classification and forecasting tasks, addressing a limitation of specialized time series SSL methods.
> - TS-JEPA demonstrates superior sample efficiency, achieving higher classification accuracy than fully supervised models with significantly less labeled data (e.g., 5-20% of labels).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Joint Embeddings Go Temporal" is positioned at the intersection of self-supervised learning (SSL), foundation models, and time series analysis—a rapidly evolving area of artificial intelligence.

Self-supervised learning has revolutionized representation learning in domains like natural language processing and computer vision, enabling the development of powerful "Foundation Models" that are first pre-trained on vast unlabeled datasets and then fine-tuned for various downstream tasks. While SSL has seen immense success, its application to time series data is a more recent and active area of research.

Existing SSL methodologies for time series generally fall into two categories:
1. **Contrastive Approaches**: Methods like SimCLR, adapted for time series (e.g., TS2Vec), learn representations by distinguishing between positive (similar) and negative (dissimilar) pairs of samples. These methods aim to pull embeddings of augmented views of the same data point closer while pushing apart embeddings of different data points.
2. **Predictive or Generative Methods**: Techniques such as Masked Auto-Encoders (MAE) and autoregressive models predict missing segments or future values of the input. MAE reconstructs masked parts of the input sequence, while autoregressive encoders aim to predict subsequent time steps. These approaches leverage the inherent temporal ordering and structure of time series.

The authors identify a critical limitation of existing input-space reconstruction methods (like MAE) for time series: their susceptibility to noise and confounding variables. When models are tasked with reconstructing missing parts directly in the input space, they can be forced to learn superficial patterns related to noise rather than extracting meaningful, predictive features. This can prevent the model from capturing the underlying dynamics of the data.

To address this challenge, the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) was introduced by Yann LeCun and collaborators. JEPA performs masked reconstruction not in the raw input space, but in a more abstract, latent space. This design makes it inherently more robust to input-level noise and irrelevant details, as the model focuses on learning robust, high-level features. JEPA has already demonstrated competitive results in image (e.g., I-JEPA) and video (e.g., V-JEPA) domains.

This paper fills a crucial gap by systematically adapting and evaluating the JEPA paradigm for general time series representation learning. While there have been prior mentions or specific applications of JEPA-like concepts to time series (e.g., for encoded frames or in-context prediction), "Joint Embeddings Go Temporal" claims to be "the first systematic study of the JEPA architecture to time series." By thoroughly investigating whether latent-space prediction can compete with (and potentially surpass) more common autoregressive or contrastive approaches, this work aims to establish JEPA as a robust foundation for building future time series foundation models. It seeks to provide a versatile SSL framework that can extract general-purpose representations suitable for a wide array of downstream tasks, overcoming the limitations of noise sensitivity in previous methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "Joint Embeddings Go Temporal" stems from the limitations of current self-supervised learning (SSL) techniques when applied to time series data, particularly their vulnerability to noise and confounding variables. Time series are often inherently noisy, and input-space reconstruction methods can struggle to extract meaningful, predictive features from such data, instead focusing on the noise itself.

Against this backdrop, the authors outline several key objectives for their work:

1. **Adapt Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) for Time Series (TS-JEPA):** The core objective is to translate the successful principles of JEPA, which performs masked reconstruction in a latent space for robustness against input noise, to the unique characteristics of temporal sequences. This involves designing an architecture specifically tailored for time series data.

2. **Validate TS-JEPA on Diverse Downstream Tasks:** The authors aim to rigorously evaluate the learned representations of TS-JEPA on standard time series applications, specifically classification and forecasting. This involves testing the model across various real-world datasets to demonstrate its generalizability and effectiveness.

3. **Achieve or Surpass State-of-the-Art Performance:** A crucial goal is to show that TS-JEPA can match or exceed the performance of existing state-of-the-art baselines, including contrastive learning methods (TS2Vec) and input-space masked autoencoders (MAE), as well as autoregressive models. The paper particularly emphasizes demonstrating a "strong performance balance across diverse tasks," implying that TS-JEPA should not excel in one task at the expense of another.

4. **Demonstrate Robustness and Generalization:** By leveraging JEPA's latent-space prediction, a key objective is to show that TS-JEPA can learn more robust and generalizable representations than methods susceptible to input noise. This includes evaluating its performance under conditions of limited labeled data and its transferability between similar datasets.

5. **Lay Groundwork for Time Series Foundation Models:** Ultimately, the work aims to position TS-JEPA as a promising "building block for developing future time series foundation models based on Joint Embedding." This long-term objective is to establish a new paradigm for learning general, adaptable representations for time series data that can serve as a foundation for a wide range of applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Time Series JEPA (TS-JEPA)**, an adaptation of the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) designed for self-supervised representation learning in time series. The fundamental idea is to predict masked parts of a time series from its non-masked parts, but crucially, this prediction occurs within an abstract latent space rather than the raw input space. This approach is intended to enhance robustness against noise and confounding factors prevalent in time series data.

The TS-JEPA architecture comprises four primary components:

1. **Tokenizer**:
 - **Function**: Transforms an input time series `x` into a sequence of non-overlapping patches `p_i`.
 - **Implementation**: Uses a one-dimensional Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN) to embed these patches, capturing local patterns along the temporal dimension.
 - **Positional Encoding**: Incorporates absolute sin-cos positional embeddings to preserve temporal order information.
 - **Masking Strategy**: Patches are split into a set of masked patches `P_M` and non-masked patches `P_N` using a uniform masking strategy (70% masking ratio for TS-JEPA).

2. **Encoder (E_θ)**:
 - **Function**: The core component that transforms the observed (non-masked) patches into a meaningful latent representation.
 - **Implementation**: A standard Transformer architecture with self-attention mechanisms.
 - **Input/Output**: Takes the non-masked patches `P_N` and outputs their latent representations `z_N = E_θ(P_N)`.

3. **Predictor (P_β)**:
 - **Function**: Maps the encoded observed tokens to predictions of the encoded unobserved (masked) tokens.
 - **Implementation**: Another Transformer-based architecture, similar to the Encoder, also incorporating self-attention mechanisms.
 - **Input/Output**: Takes the encoder's output `z_N` and generates predictions `z'_M = P_β(E_θ(P_N))` for the masked patches.

4. **EMA-Encoder (E_θ̄)**:
 - **Function**: Provides a stable target for the predictor, crucial for preventing "representation collapse" during training (where the encoder learns trivial constant outputs).
 - **Implementation**: An instance of the Encoder, but its weights are not updated directly via backpropagation. Instead, its weights are updated as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the main Encoder's weights (with an update parameter `m = 0.998`).
 - **Input/Output**: Takes the masked patches `P_M` and produces their target representations `t_M = E_θ̄(P_M)`.

**Learning Task and Loss Function**:
The model learns by minimizing the difference between the predictor's output for masked tokens and the EMA-encoder's output for the same masked tokens in the latent space. The objective is to minimize the L1 norm:
`L = (1/|M|) * Σ_{i∈M} || P_β(E_θ(P_N))_i - E_θ̄(p_i) ||_1`

**Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol**:
- **Pre-training**: TS-JEPA is pre-trained using all available training samples for each dataset.
- **Frozen Evaluation**: After pre-training, the encoder is frozen, and small, task-specific classification or regression heads are trained on top of its learned representations. This protocol assesses the quality and transferability of the learned features.
- **Architecture Details**: Both the encoder and predictor use a Transformer with 2 attention heads and an embedding dimension of 128.
- **Optimization**: AdamW optimizer is used, with learning rates (1e-03, 1e-04, 1e-05, 1e-06) tuned for optimal performance.
- **Baselines**: TS-JEPA is compared against:
 - **TS2Vec**: A contrastive learning approach.
 - **MAE**: A mask-and-reconstruct method operating in the input space.
 - **Autoregressive approach**: Predicts future values based on past observations.
 - **Randomly Initialized Encoder**: To ascertain the benefit of pre-training.
 - **Fully Supervised Transformer and CNN**: Serve as upper bounds for performance.
 - All baseline architectures are kept consistent with TS-JEPA's encoder for fair comparison.
- **Datasets**: A mix of classification datasets (FordA, FordB, FaultDetectionA/B, ECG500) and forecasting datasets (Weather, ETT-Small, Electricity) are used.
- **Evaluation Tasks**:
 - **Classification**: Includes same-dataset evaluation, cross-dataset transferability tests (e.g., FordA to FordB), and fine-tuning with limited labeled data (5-20% of labels).
 - **Forecasting**: Assessed for both short-term (predicting the next patch) and long-term (autoregressively rolling out a horizon window).
- **Metrics**: Classification accuracy for classification tasks, and Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for forecasting tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of TS-JEPA demonstrates its promising capabilities across both classification and forecasting tasks, highlighting a strong balance in performance.

#### 4.1. Classification Tasks

- **General Performance**: On standard real-world classification datasets (Table 1), TS-JEPA consistently outperforms both contrastive learning methods (TS2Vec) and autoregressive approaches in the majority of tasks. Its accuracy is competitive with, or occasionally surpasses, the Masked Auto-Encoder (MAE) approach. Importantly, TS-JEPA's performance closely approximates that of a transformer model trained in a fully supervised manner, indicating its effectiveness in learning high-quality representations without explicit labels.
- **Sample Efficiency with Limited Labeled Data**: In scenarios mimicking real-world constraints where supervised labels are scarce (Figure 2), TS-JEPA exhibits superior sample efficiency. It achieves higher classification accuracy than a fully-supervised transformer model when only a small fraction (e.g., 5-20%) of labeled data is available. As the amount of labeled data increases, the performance gap between TS-JEPA and the fully supervised method naturally narrows, confirming the value of its self-supervised pre-training.

#### 4.2. Forecasting Tasks

- **Short-Term Forecasting (Table 2)**: For short-term forecasting, predicting the immediate next patch in a sequence, the autoregressive approach generally outperforms TS-JEPA. This is an anticipated outcome, as autoregressive models are explicitly designed and optimized for sequential prediction based on preceding values. TS-JEPA, while competitive, does not consistently match the specialized performance of autoregressive methods in this specific setup.
- **Long-Term Forecasting (Figure 3)**: In the more challenging long-term forecasting task, where predictions are made over an extended horizon by autoregressively rolling out the model, both TS-JEPA and autoregressive baselines exhibit the inherent "uncertainty amplification effect." However, TS-JEPA demonstrates superior performance to autoregressive strategies on two out of three datasets (ETT-Small and Electricity datasets), suggesting enhanced stability and robustness for longer prediction horizons. This indicates that while autoregressive models might be strong locally, TS-JEPA's latent-space representation could capture more stable, global patterns beneficial for extended predictions.

#### 4.3. Overall Performance Balance

The authors emphasize that while TS-JEPA does not uniformly outperform all baselines on every single dataset and task, its strength lies in its ability to achieve a "strong performance balance" across diverse tasks. It delivers competitive forecasting capabilities while significantly outperforming other baselines in classification. This balance is a critical characteristic, as many existing methods tend to excel in one task (e.g., autoregressive for forecasting) at the expense of others. TS-JEPA's versatility from a single architecture is a key takeaway.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TS-JEPA represents a significant step forward in self-supervised learning for time series analysis, laying crucial groundwork for the development of future time series foundation models.

**Primary Significance:**
1. **Robust Representation Learning:** By adapting the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture to time series, TS-JEPA offers a novel approach to learning robust representations. Its latent-space reconstruction mechanism inherently addresses the vulnerability of input-space methods to noise and confounding variables—a critical issue in often-noisy time series data. This robustness is key to extracting meaningful, generalizable features that capture underlying data patterns rather than superficial artifacts.
2. **Balanced Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** A standout contribution of TS-JEPA is its ability to achieve a compelling trade-off and strong performance balance across both classification and forecasting downstream tasks. Unlike many existing methods that often specialize and excel in one domain (e.g., autoregressive models for forecasting), TS-JEPA provides a versatile framework capable of competitive results in multiple, distinct applications using a single pre-trained architecture. This versatility is highly desirable for general-purpose foundation models.
3. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The demonstrated superior sample efficiency with limited labeled data is a practical and impactful finding. In many real-world scenarios, obtaining large quantities of labeled time series data is expensive or infeasible. TS-JEPA's ability to achieve high performance with fewer labels, leveraging its self-supervised pre-training, can significantly reduce data annotation costs and accelerate model deployment.
4. **Foundation for Time Series Foundation Models:** The paper explicitly positions TS-JEPA as a "promising building block" for time series foundation models. This is perhaps its most ambitious and far-reaching potential impact. Just as large models pre-trained on vast text or image datasets have revolutionized NLP and computer vision, a robust self-supervised paradigm like TS-JEPA could enable the creation of powerful, generalized time series models capable of adapting to a myriad of specific tasks with minimal fine-tuning. This could unlock new capabilities in areas ranging from industrial anomaly detection and predictive maintenance to financial forecasting and healthcare monitoring.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Democratization of Time Series AI:** By reducing the reliance on extensive labeled datasets and providing versatile representations, TS-JEPA could make advanced time series analytics more accessible to domains lacking vast labeled resources.
- **Accelerated Research and Development:** A robust foundation model for time series could streamline the development cycle for new applications, allowing researchers and practitioners to build upon pre-trained, high-quality representations rather than starting from scratch for each task.
- **Improved Generalization and Transferability:** The learned representations are expected to be more general and transferable across different time series datasets and domains, fostering greater innovation in time series machine learning.
- **New Research Avenues:** The work explicitly points to "exploration of scaling strategies for TS-JEPA" as a next step. This suggests future research will focus on developing even larger and more powerful variants, potentially leveraging larger datasets and computational resources, to further solidify the "new paradigm for time series foundation models."

In conclusion, "Joint Embeddings Go Temporal" presents a significant methodological advancement in self-supervised learning for time series. By addressing a critical limitation of previous approaches and demonstrating a balanced, robust performance across diverse tasks, it lays a credible and promising foundation for the next generation of time series AI models.
