# Use the Online Network If You Can: Towards Fast and Stable Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Technical University of Darmstadt, Hessian.AI, DFKI, University of Würzburg, and RIG introduced MINTO, an update rule for deep reinforcement learning that intelligently combines estimates from online and target networks. It achieves faster and more stable learning by selectively leveraging the latest online estimates while mitigating overestimation, leading to performance improvements of up to 125% in offline settings.

## Problem

- Target networks, while crucial for stabilizing Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) by using lagged estimates, inherently slow down the learning process.
- Using only the online network for bootstrapping leads to unstable training dynamics and significant overestimation bias, hindering effective learning.
- Existing DRL methods struggle to find a principled balance between leveraging fresh information for faster learning and maintaining stability during value function approximation.

## Method

- **MINTO** (MINimum estimate between Target and Online network) introduces a novel update rule where the bootstrapped target value is computed as the minimum of the Q-estimates from the target network and the online network for the next state-action pair.
- The `min` operator intelligently selects `Q_target` when `Q_online` suggests overestimation or fluctuation, and `Q_online` when it offers a potentially faster-converging, lower estimate.
- The method integrates seamlessly into a wide range of existing DRL algorithms across discrete/continuous action spaces and online/offline settings, adding negligible computational overhead and no new hyperparameters.

## Results

- MINTO improved standard DQN performance on 15 Atari games by approximately 18-**24%** in Area Under the Curve (AUC) and consistently outperformed other overestimation-mitigation methods like Double DQN and ScDQN.
- When integrated into Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), MINTO dramatically boosted offline RL performance on 14 Atari games, showing up to a **125%** improvement in AUC.
- The approach also generally yielded improvements in sample efficiency and asymptotic performance for Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) on 26 continuous control tasks across MuJoCo, HBench, and DMC-Hard benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The judicious application of a minimum operator on combined online and target network estimates effectively mitigates overestimation bias and stabilizes learning without sacrificing the freshness of online information.
> - Empirical studies demonstrated that the `min` operator significantly outperforms other naive combinations of online and target network estimates for DRL tasks.
> - MINTO maintains theoretical convergence guarantees in the tabular setting by conforming to the Generalized Q-learning framework, providing a strong foundation for its practical efficacy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes across various domains, including complex games, continuous control, and robotic manipulation. A foundational element in the success of Deep RL, initiated by the Deep Q-Network (DQN), is the use of target networks. Target networks were introduced to address the "deadly triad" problem—the combination of function approximation, off-policy data, and bootstrapping—which can lead to unstable learning in RL algorithms. By providing a lagged copy of the online network to compute regression targets, target networks enhance training stability and convergence by mitigating the "moving target problem."

However, this stability comes with a trade-off: the reliance on delayed targets inherently slows down the learning process. This has prompted research into methods that can accelerate learning by incorporating more recent estimates while preserving stability. Some studies have explored approaches that exclusively use the online network for target bootstrapping, reporting performance improvements in specific contexts. Conversely, subsequent findings have indicated that reintroducing a target network can further enhance the results in these same approaches.

Other related lines of research include methods that utilize additional resources or pre-training to enable target-free learning, and those that modify the network architecture or apply specialized operators (e.g., MellowMax) to stabilize single-estimator training. Hybrid methods have also emerged, combining online network updates with regularization from an older network copy. Additionally, a significant challenge in off-policy RL is overestimation bias, arising from the interaction between the maximum operator and the stochasticity of bootstrap estimates. Techniques such as Double Q-learning, Clipped Double Q-learning (CDQ), and Maxmin Q-learning have been developed to address this bias, often by employing multiple value estimators and taking the minimum of their estimates.

This work contributes to this landscape by exploring a complementary perspective: jointly leveraging both online and target networks to compute regression targets. The objective is to combine the benefits of stability from target networks with the accelerated learning potential of fresh online estimates, specifically by addressing the overestimation bias that often accompanies the direct use of online estimates.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central problem addressed by this research is the inherent trade-off in deep RL between learning speed and stability when using target networks. While target networks are effective at stabilizing training by preventing the online network from rapidly chasing its own estimates, they do so by relying on outdated information, which consequently delays the learning process. Conversely, using the online network for bootstrapping offers the potential for faster learning due to more current estimates, but is widely recognized to lead to unstable training dynamics and increased overestimation bias.

The key objectives and motivations for this work are:
1. **Accelerate Learning**: To find a method that allows for faster value function learning by incorporating more recent information from the online network.
2. **Preserve Stability**: To ensure that the accelerated learning does not compromise the stability of the training process, a characteristic typically provided by target networks.
3. **Mitigate Overestimation Bias**: To specifically address the potential overestimation bias that can arise when the online network is used for bootstrapping, which can destabilize learning and degrade performance, particularly in value-based methods.
4. **Develop a Principled Combination**: To introduce a novel update rule that effectively combines the benefits of both online and target networks in a principled manner, aiming to achieve both fast and stable learning.
5. **Broad Applicability**: To design a technique that is simple, lightweight, and seamlessly integrable into a wide range of existing off-policy RL algorithms, spanning different paradigms (value-based, actor-critic, distributional) and settings (online, offline, discrete, continuous).

The paper proposes the MINTO method as a solution, motivated by the observation that an appropriate selection criterion is needed to regulate the impact of overestimation bias when incorporating online estimates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, MINTO (MINimum estimate between Target and Online network), is a modified Bellman update rule designed to combine the benefits of both online and target networks for computing regression targets.

#### 3.1. MINTO Update Rule

The standard temporal-difference target for a Q-value update in deep Q-learning is typically `y = r + γ * max_a' Q_bar_theta(s', a')`, where `Q_bar_theta` is the target network. MINTO modifies this by incorporating the online network `Q_theta`:
`y = r + γ * max_a' (min(Q_bar_theta(s', a'), Q_theta(s', a')))`

This means the target value is computed using the minimum of the Q-value estimates from the target network and the online network for the next state-action pair, after which the maximum over actions is taken. The `min` operator is applied to mitigate potential overestimation bias from the online network.

#### 3.2. Loss Function and Gradient Control

The regression loss `L(theta)` is then computed using this new target, similar to DQN:
`L(theta) = 0.5 * (gradient_stop(y) - Q_theta(s, a))^2`
A `stop_gradient` operator (⌈.⌉) is explicitly applied to the target `y` to prevent gradients from flowing back to the online network parameters `Q_theta` present within the target computation. This ensures that `Q_theta` is only updated through the primary loss, maintaining the integrity of the target calculation.

#### 3.3. Algorithmic Integration

MINTO is designed for seamless integration into a variety of off-policy RL algorithms. The paper demonstrates its application in:
- **Value-based methods**: Deep Q-Networks (DQN).
- **Distributional RL**: Implicit Quantile Networks (IQN).
- **Offline RL**: Conservative Q-Learning (CQL).
- **Actor-Critic methods**: Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) using SimbaV1 and SimbaV2 architectures.
The integration typically involves modifying only the target computation part of the respective algorithms, requiring a single additional feedforward pass of the online network on the next state. The authors state that this computational overhead is negligible in efficient deep learning frameworks.

#### 3.4. Empirical Study on Operator Selection

To validate the choice of the minimum operator, an empirical study was conducted comparing MINTO against alternative operators for combining online and target network estimates on 15 Atari games using a CNN architecture. The compared operators included:
- **Online Only**: Relies solely on online estimates.
- **Target Only**: Standard DQN, using only target network estimates.
- **Max**: Selects the larger of the two estimates (online vs. target).
- **Mean**: Averages the online and target estimates.
- **Random**: Randomly chooses between online and target networks with equal probability.
- **Min (MINTO)**: Uses the minimum of the online and target estimates.
The Area Under the Curve (AUC) metric was used for comparison.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Convergence

The convergence of MINTO is analyzed in the tabular setting by leveraging the Generalized Q-learning framework (Lan et al., 2020). This framework guarantees convergence for update operators that satisfy specific non-expansion properties. The paper demonstrates that the MINTO operator, `G_MINTO(Q_s) = max_a (min_j Q_sa(j))` (where `Q_sa(j)` represents historical Q-values including online and target estimates), can be cast as a special case of this framework. By satisfying the non-expansion conditions (as detailed in Appendix A), MINTO is shown to converge to the optimal action-values, `Q*`, under standard stochastic approximation assumptions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive empirical evaluations of MINTO across diverse RL benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Operator Ablation Study

The empirical study comparing different operators for target computation on 15 Atari games (Figure 1, Appendix D.7) yielded the following:
- The "Online Only" approach performed poorly, indicating instability from rapidly changing targets.
- The "Max" operator yielded the worst results, attributed to severe overestimation bias and uncontrolled target value increases.
- "Random" and "Mean" operators performed comparably to "Target Only" (DQN), offering no substantial improvement.
- The "Min" operator (MINTO) demonstrated a clear advantage over all alternatives, supporting its role in mitigating overestimation bias while leveraging online estimates stably.

#### 4.2. Online RL and Discrete Control

When integrated into DQN and evaluated on 15 Atari games (Figures 2, 9):
- **CNN architecture**: MINTO improved Area Under the Curve (AUC) performance by approximately 17% compared to DQN.
- **IMPALA with LayerNorm (LN) architecture**: MINTO demonstrated an improvement of about 22% in AUC.
- Learning curves illustrated that MINTO consistently exhibited superior sample efficiency and achieved higher asymptotic performance.

Comparison with related baselines (Double DQN, FR-DQN, ScDQN, Maxmin DQN) on 15 Atari games with CNN architecture (Figures 3, 10, 11) showed:
- MINTO consistently outperformed Double DQN, FR-DQN, and ScDQN, which are also designed to address overestimation or leverage online estimates.
- MINTO also outperformed Maxmin DQN (N=2 estimators), suggesting a benefit from leveraging up-to-date online estimates more effectively without the overhead of multiple Q-functions. MINTO introduces no additional hyperparameters.

#### 4.3. Distributional RL

MINTO was integrated into Implicit Quantile Networks (IQN) and evaluated on 15 Atari games (Figures 4, 12, 13):
- **CNN architecture**: MINTO+IQN achieved approximately 7% improvement in AUC over standard IQN.
- **IMPALA with LN architecture**: Performance improved by about 10% in AUC.
- The improvements in this setting, while smaller than for DQN, were consistent across sample efficiency and asymptotic performance.
- Tracking the online selection ratio (Figure 4, right) on "Breakout" revealed that the online network was selected approximately 45% of the time in later training stages, indicating its active contribution to the target computation.

#### 4.4. Offline RL

MINTO was applied to Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) and tested on 14 Atari games using offline datasets (Figures 5, 14, 15):
- **CNN architecture**: CQL+MINTO boosted performance by roughly 125% in AUC compared to original CQL.
- **IMPALA with LN architecture**: An improvement of approximately 20% in AUC was observed, with a clear sample-efficiency advantage.
- These results suggest that MINTO effectively incorporates recent online estimates and mitigates overestimation bias in the offline setting.

Further comparison with offline RL baselines (Double CQL, FR-CQL, ScCQL, Maxmin CQL) using CNN architecture (Figures 6, 16, 17) showed:
- MINTO consistently outperformed single-estimator baselines.
- Maxmin CQL (N=2) achieved higher performance than MINTO alone in this setting. However, when MINTO was integrated into Maxmin CQL (Maxmin CQL+MINTO), it further enhanced the performance of this strong baseline.

#### 4.5. Online RL and Continuous Control

MINTO was integrated into Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) using SimbaV1 and SimbaV2 architectures and evaluated on 26 continuous control tasks from MuJoCo, Humanoid Bench (HBench), and DeepMind Control Suite Hard (DMC-Hard) (Figures 7, 18-32):
- A clear improvement in sample efficiency was observed on MuJoCo and HBench benchmarks.
- Performance on DMC-Hard was comparable to the base algorithms or slightly lower in some instances.
- Overall, MINTO exhibited a consistent positive impact, suggesting its value in incorporating recent online estimates in continuous control actor-critic settings.

#### 4.6. Analysis of Value Estimation Error and Bias

An analysis on the Car-on-the-Hill environment (Appendix E, Figures 35, 36) provided insights into bias and error:
- DQN showed strong overestimation, while Double DQN offered moderate mitigation. Maxmin DQN exhibited significant underestimation.
- MINTO produced substantially lower overall bias compared to DQN and Double DQN, without the severe underestimation seen in Maxmin DQN, and avoided the bias overshoot common in other methods.
- While MINTO's estimation error was comparable to baselines, it displayed a slight increase in variance due to the online component.
- MINTO solved the task faster and without learning instability.
- A direct comparison to an "Online Only" baseline confirmed that relying solely on online estimates leads to severe overestimation, high error, and pronounced instability, thus validating MINTO's design for bias mitigation and stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MINTO represents a practical advancement in deep reinforcement learning by offering a simple yet effective bootstrapping rule. By taking the minimum estimate between the target and online networks, MINTO addresses a long-standing challenge of simultaneously achieving faster learning and maintaining training stability.

The **significance** of this work stems from several key aspects:
- **Improved Performance and Efficiency**: MINTO consistently improves sample efficiency and asymptotic performance across a wide range of RL settings, including online and offline RL, discrete and continuous action spaces, and value-based, distributional, and actor-critic algorithms. This suggests a broad applicability beyond specific domains or algorithmic types.
- **Stability and Bias Mitigation**: The method effectively mitigates the overestimation bias that can arise from incorporating fresh online estimates, thereby alleviating the "moving target problem" without the severe underestimation associated with some other bias-reducing techniques. The theoretical convergence analysis provides a foundation for its stability.
- **Computational Efficiency**: MINTO introduces minimal computational overhead, requiring only a single additional feedforward pass of the online network. Furthermore, it adds no new hyperparameters, simplifying its integration and tuning compared to more complex methods.
- **Generalizability**: Its seamless integrability into existing algorithms, as demonstrated by its application to DQN, IQN, CQL, and SAC, positions MINTO as a general-purpose enhancement for off-policy deep RL.

The **potential impact** of MINTO is considerable:
- **Standard Practice Evolution**: MINTO could become a default modification for many deep RL algorithms that currently rely on conventional target network designs, offering a straightforward path to improved performance.
- **Accelerated Research and Development**: By providing a more efficient learning mechanism, MINTO may accelerate the development and deployment of RL agents in various applications, from robotics to complex decision-making systems.
- **Foundation for Future Work**: The findings open avenues for further research into adaptive mechanisms that dynamically balance online and target estimates, potentially based on uncertainty or learning dynamics, to further optimize the trade-off between conservatism and learning speed. Its consistent performance also encourages exploration in more challenging multi-task and multi-agent RL scenarios.

While MINTO demonstrates robust improvements, the authors acknowledge potential trade-offs, such as possible over-conservatism in low-noise environments leading to slight underestimation, and its interaction with exploration strategies. These aspects are identified as promising directions for future investigation. Overall, MINTO presents a substantial contribution to the field by offering a simple, yet robust and broadly applicable technique for advancing stable and efficient deep RL.
