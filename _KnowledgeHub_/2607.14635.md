# Action QFormer: Structured Representation Shaping under Action Supervision in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Qizhi Institute, Tsinghua University, and UC Berkeley developed Action QFormer, a query-based interface for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models designed to mediate how action supervision reshapes multimodal representations. This approach notably reduced out-of-distribution instruction generation to 0% and improved zero-shot sim-to-real navigation task success rates to 37.5-62.5% compared to a direct-fusion baseline.

## Problem

- Inherited multimodal representations in VLA models are often suboptimal for action control, leading to a structural mismatch between language understanding and action generation requirements.
- Direct application of action supervision, while necessary for forming action-compatible representations, can destabilize the rich semantic structures vital for language understanding and object grounding.
- Existing VLA frameworks lack a specific mechanism to control how action supervision shapes inherited representations, often resulting in unstable instruction generation and weak grounding in real-world deployment.

## Method

- Introduce **Action QFormer**, a ==query-based action-facing interface== that reorganizes inherited multimodal information into action-compatible representations using ==instruction-conditioned queries==.
- Utilize ==learnable query states== that first absorb instruction context and then attend to visual evidence, explicitly abstracting and selecting control-relevant visual information.
- Provide an intermediate, query-based route for ==action-loss gradients==, thereby controlling their propagation upstream to the inherited multimodal pathway and enabling targeted adaptation.

## Results

- Action QFormer achieved a **0%** out-of-distribution instruction generation rate and improved zero-shot sim-to-real navigation task success rates to **37.5-62.5%** (up to **100%** with provided instructions) compared to **0-25%** for the direct-fusion baseline.
- The approach facilitated clearer action-facing directional distinctions while demonstrating substantially lower token-wise drift energy and a more localized rewriting spread in the inherited multimodal backbone.
- Action QFormer promoted more stable and constructive instruction-to-visual attention, characterized by higher reference-region mass and smaller center shifts, enhancing visual grounding and reducing instruction instability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action supervision acts as a dual-effect force, essential for forming action-compatible distinctions but potentially disruptive to language-side grounding if unmediated.
> - Query-based interfaces can effectively mediate this representation shaping by localizing adaptation to action-specific needs, preserving the integrity of broader inherited multimodal representations.
> - Targeted adaptation within the interface leads to clearer action-compatible distinctions, such as left-right directional awareness, without causing widespread 'rewriting' or instability in the upstream backbone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, robot learning, and representation learning. Recent advancements in VLA models often leverage large, pre-trained vision-language backbones (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, π₀) to enable embodied control by reusing rich multimodal representations. A significant line of work has focused on developing advanced action interfaces (e.g., FAST, VQ-VLA, LAPA, UniVLA, RotVLA) to improve VLA scalability, action decoding, and cross-embodiment generalization. This paper identifies a gap in this landscape: while action interfaces and backbones have been a focus, the specific mechanism by which action supervision reshapes the inherited multimodal representations themselves has remained largely unexplored. This work aims to address this by introducing a framework for "structured representation shaping" under action supervision.

The paper also builds upon research into query-based interfaces and multimodal alignment. Approaches such as Perceiver, Flamingo, BLIP-2, and InstructBLIP have demonstrated the utility of intermediate query or resampling interfaces to bridge pre-trained encoders with downstream language modules, enabling effective multimodal integration and few-shot learning. Similarly, in robotics, work like VEGA, DynaFLIP, HARP-VLA, LARA, and AGRA has explored alignment strategies for action-relevant structure. This paper extends this concept by employing learnable queries to specifically reorganize inherited image and instruction representations for downstream action prediction, aligning with the principle that interfaces should be designed to match their downstream objectives.

Furthermore, the research is contextualized within the application of foundation models to robot navigation. Contemporary navigation methods, including GNM, ViNT, NoMaD, and those incorporating large language models like LM-Nav and Uni-NaVid, highlight the dependence on robust language- or goal-conditioned visual grounding across diverse and changing scenes. The choice of zero-shot sim-to-real navigation as an experimental setting provides a sensitive testbed for studying representation instability, as this environment places high demands on the robustness and stability of learned representations when transferring from simulation to real-world scenarios without additional fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from an identified structural mismatch in current VLA models. These models often build action generation on top of pre-trained vision-language backbones, expecting the same inherited multimodal pathway to support two distinct functions:
1. **Language-side processing:** Requiring rich semantic and grounding structures for instruction understanding and generation.
2. **Action-side control:** Needing to abstract behaviorally consequential factors into stable and robust control signals.

This mismatch implies that representations optimized for vision-language understanding may not be optimally organized for action, lacking the specific form required for effective control.

The paper posits that action supervision is not merely a downstream training signal but a powerful representation-shaping force. During action fine-tuning, action-loss gradients propagate through the inherited multimodal pathway, potentially reshaping its organization. This process exhibits a dual effect:
- **Constructive necessity:** It is essential for forming action-compatible representations, enabling the model to learn distinctions vital for control (e.g., left vs. right).
- **Potential disruption:** When action supervision is applied too directly, it can destabilize representations that are fundamental for language-side processing and object grounding, leading to a degradation in overall multimodal understanding.

The central objective is to address this tension by investigating how VLA systems can permit action supervision to reshape multimodal representations inherited from vision-language pre-training without causing destabilization. The authors propose "structured representation shaping" as a solution, which involves introducing an intermediate module to mediate where action-supervised shaping occurs.

This motivation culminates in the introduction of **Action QFormer**, a query-based action-facing interface. The specific objectives of Action QFormer are to:
- Reorganize inherited multimodal information into action-facing representations before downstream action generation, utilizing instruction-conditioned queries.
- Provide an intermediate, query-based route for action-loss gradients, thereby controlling the propagation of these gradients upstream to the inherited multimodal pathway. This aims to allow targeted adaptation without broad disruption.

The selection of zero-shot sim-to-real navigation as the experimental domain is motivated by its ability to expose representation instability through explicit behavioral failures, such as unstable instruction generation, weak object grounding, or directional control errors, which can compound into task failure in closed-loop settings. The paper’s overarching mechanism-level thesis is that query-based interfaces can mediate the interaction between action supervision and inherited multimodal representations, leading to more controlled and constructive adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology focuses on comparing a direct-fusion baseline with the proposed Action QFormer within a single-frame, instruction-conditioned robot navigation setting.

**Terminology:** The paper defines key terms: "inherited multimodal representations" (outputs from the pretrained backbone), "action-supervised representation shaping" (gradient-induced reshaping), "action-facing representation" (input to the policy head), and "action-facing interface" (module mapping inherited to action-facing representations). Action QFormer is positioned as implementing "structured representation shaping."

**Problem Setup:** At each timestep, the model first generates an intermediate instruction and then predicts an 8-step future action trajectory conditioned on it. Input consists of a current-frame image observation ($I_t$), a task text ($g_t$), a supervised navigation instruction ($s_t$), and an 8-step future action trajectory ($A_t$). The pre-trained multimodal backbone processes this into hidden states ($H_t$), from which image-side ($H_{I_t}$) and instruction-side ($H_{S_t}$) representations are extracted. Each action step is represented by a local relative position and a heading vector.

**Direct-fusion Baseline:** This baseline implements a common VLA interface pattern where inherited image-side and instruction-side representations are concatenated and then directly fused via self-attention. A pooling layer then produces the action-facing representation ($z_{Fuse_t}$) which directly conditions the policy head.

**Action QFormer Architecture:** Action QFormer replaces this direct-fusion interface with a query-based intermediate module. It maintains a set of 16 learnable query states, which are updated through 4 transformer layers. In each layer, queries first absorb instruction context via self-attention with the instruction-side representation ($H_{S_t}$), and then attend to visual evidence via cross-attention with the image-side representation ($H_{I_t}$). The final, pooled query states are then combined with the instruction-side representation to form the action-facing representation ($z_{AQF_t}$). This design explicitly introduces instruction-conditioned selection and abstraction of visual information before it is combined with language features for action prediction.

**Representation-Shaping Mechanism:** The core of the methodology lies in analyzing how action-loss gradients propagate backward through the interfaces. For both models, gradients flow through the action-facing representation to reshape the inherited multimodal pathway. However, Action QFormer introduces additional, query-specific adaptation paths for its trainable query-interface parameters and learnable query initialization. This allows a portion of the action-loss update to be expressed by updating the queries themselves, thereby optimizing the selection and organization of multimodal information within the interface before gradients propagate further upstream to reshape the backbone.

**Diagnostics for the Dual Effect:** To dissect the impact of action-supervised shaping, stop-gradient interventions are applied to specific parts of the backpropagation path:
- `Action update blocked`: Gradients are halted before reaching the extracted image-side and instruction-side representations, serving as a reference for language-only updates.
- `Lang. backbone blocked`: Prevents gradients from updating the language backbone.
- `Vision encoder frozen`: Freezes the vision encoder parameters.
- `Full update`: Allows gradients to fully propagate through the entire inherited multimodal pathway.
These settings enable a controlled analysis of how different levels of action-loss exposure affect representation shaping.

**Supervision and Sequence Construction:** Each training sample includes a GPT-generated navigation instruction ($s_t$) and an 8-step future action trajectory ($A_t$). The GPT instructions are generated from the observation and future trajectory using a constrained prompt, ensuring diversity while maintaining spatial grounding. Samples are formatted as multimodal sequences with explicit boundary markers for visual and instruction spans. Language loss ($L_{lang}$) is applied only to the supervised instruction span.

**Action Prediction Objective:** The policy head, implemented as a conditional diffusion policy, predicts the local-frame action trajectory conditioned on the action-facing representation ($z_t$). It is trained with a denoising objective, minimizing the mean squared error between predicted and ground-truth noise conditioned on noised actions and the action-facing representation.

**Overall Training Objective:** The total training objective combines language-side and action-side supervision using a scalar language-loss weight `r`: $L = (1 - r)L_{action} + rL_{lang}$. The weight `r` is cosine-scheduled from a high language-side emphasis to a lower value, allowing for a gradual shift in training focus.

**Inference:** At inference, the model operates in a closed-loop perception-to-instruction-to-action pipeline. From a real-scene observation, it generates an intermediate instruction, extracts representations, feeds them to the action interface, and then samples an action trajectory for execution. This closed-loop nature tests representation stability under dynamic real-world conditions.

**Optimization Details:** All main experiments use consistent optimization settings, including a cosine learning-rate schedule with a warmup ratio, gradient clipping, a learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-4}$, and 6000 optimization steps. Batch configurations differ (16x4 for direct-fusion, 8x8 for Action QFormer) to accommodate memory footprints, but maintain the same effective batch size.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from zero-shot sim-to-real navigation experiments and mechanistic analyses, demonstrating the impact of Action QFormer on VLA model performance and representation shaping.

**A. Zero-shot Sim-to-Real Closed-Loop Experiment:**
- **Performance Comparison:** In challenging zero-shot sim-to-real ObjectNav scenarios, the direct-fusion baseline exhibited high rates of out-of-distribution (OOD) instruction generations (often 100% OOD rate in certain scenarios) and weaker instruction-direction correctness. This translated into lower action-direction accuracy and overall task success (e.g., 0-25% success across scenarios). Action QFormer significantly improved performance, virtually eliminating instruction OOD outputs (0% in all scenarios), producing more stable directional instructions, and achieving substantially higher task success (37.5-62.5% success, up to 100% with provided instructions).
- **Failure Analysis:** Baseline failures often initiated with unstable intermediate instruction generation, including OOD outputs, direction switches, or target mismatches, which compounded through closed-loop execution.
- **Instruction Instability Control:** When models were provided with correct instructions, the performance gap between the baseline and Action QFormer decreased significantly. This indicated that instruction instability under real-scene visual shifts was a major source of degradation for the baseline. However, Action QFormer still maintained an advantage in scenarios requiring complex visual inference (e.g., "Weak Initial-Cue Directional Inference") even with fixed instructions, suggesting its benefits extend beyond instruction generation quality to stronger visually grounded action execution. The method's limitations include obstacle-aware instruction planning.

**B. Fixed-Instruction Action Generation:**
- **Targeted Performance Gains:** When instruction instability was controlled by providing fixed instructions, Action QFormer demonstrated its strongest advantages in tasks requiring "directional control" and "object grounding." Gains were smaller for "motion templates," where the baseline was more competitive. This suggests that Action QFormer enhances the reliability of action-facing representations primarily when behavior depends on perceptual disambiguation of direction or specific visual targets.
- **Interface Ablations:**
 - An "image-latent" Action QFormer (using image representations processed by the language backbone) showed reduced advantage, particularly for directional control and object grounding, implying that raw visual features from the vision encoder are crucial for action-relevant spatial information.
 - A "query-only" Action QFormer (removing the direct instruction-side representation in the final action-facing representation) often produced plausible motion but lost reliable instruction-following, indicating that explicit instruction context is necessary even after queries extract instruction-conditioned visual information.

**C. Mechanistic Analysis:**
- **Action-facing Directional Distinction:**
 - For the direct-fusion baseline, a clear left-right directional distinction in action-facing representations emerged only when both visual and language pathways were jointly shaped by action-loss gradients, particularly in later stages of training.
 - Action QFormer, under full action-loss updates, sharpened this left-right distinction in both its action-facing representation and final action output more effectively than the baseline, while exhibiting smaller changes in the inherited visual and instruction representations. This indicates Action QFormer actively reorganizes information for clearer action-compatible distinctions.
- **Reduced Upstream Rewriting with Targeted Adaptation:**
 - Action QFormer exhibited substantially lower token-wise drift energy (rewriting magnitude) and a more confined rewriting spread (lower active-dimension fraction) compared to the direct-fusion baseline, especially with full action-loss exposure.
 - Despite reduced overall rewriting, Action QFormer preserved targeted adaptation on key control and spatial tokens (e.g., "left," "right," "turn," "stop"). Subspace analysis showed its adaptation was less dominated by a few principal directions (higher effective rank, lower top-5 variance concentration), suggesting a more distributed and selective shaping process.
 - Action QFormer allocated a larger fraction of its rewriting budget to boundary tokens, reducing broad rewriting of semantically meaningful content tokens.
- **Stable Attention with Constructive Adaptation:**
 - The direct-fusion baseline, under full action-loss updates, frequently showed high-risk attention changes, characterized by low reference-region mass and large center shifts in instruction-to-visual attention.
 - Action QFormer shifted the attention distribution towards higher reference-region mass and smaller center shifts, indicating more stable instruction-to-visual attention.
 - Qualitative analysis suggested that Action QFormer could sometimes sharpen attention toward target object regions (e.g., a staircase), while the baseline's attention might drift or become weakly grounded. This constructive effect potentially contributes to reduced instruction instability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several contributions to the understanding and design of VLA systems, extending beyond simply improving performance to exploring the underlying mechanisms of representation learning.

**Reframing the VLA Problem:** The research establishes "structured representation shaping under action supervision" as a fundamental mechanism-level problem in VLA. It shifts the perspective from viewing action supervision solely as a downstream training objective to recognizing its dual role as a force that actively reshapes the multimodal representation space. This shaping is deemed necessary for developing action-compatible distinctions but can be disruptive if applied without mediation.

**New Design Principles for Action Interfaces:** The findings suggest that effective action-interface design should not only focus on decoding actions from multimodal representations but also critically consider how action supervision interacts with and reshapes these representations during fine-tuning. This encourages the development of interfaces that can manage this dynamic interaction.

**Alleviating Structural Tension:** Action QFormer addresses a core tension in VLA models: the requirement for inherited multimodal representations to simultaneously support the semantic and contextual needs of language-side processing and the stable, control-relevant distinctions necessary for action generation. By introducing instruction-conditioned queries, Action QFormer provides a mechanism to select and abstract control-relevant visual information without directly disrupting the broader inherited representations.

**Mechanism of Action QFormer's Efficacy:** The paper proposes that Action QFormer improves performance by concentrating a significant portion of the action-relevant learning pressure within its query interface. This allows for targeted adaptation to action-specific needs while making the remaining upstream shaping more selective. This preserves the robustness of representations crucial for language-side grounding and attention patterns, facilitating constructive, localized adaptation under action supervision.

**Implications for Visual Robustness:** The study suggests that brittleness in real-scene VLA deployment may stem not only from domain transfer issues (e.g., visual shifts) but also from a lack of action-oriented abstraction of visual information. Even rich visual backbones might carry appearance-level variations that are irrelevant to action, making policies sensitive to real-scene changes. This implies that improving VLA reliability may require better action-oriented abstraction of visual data, complementing the need for more diverse training data or stronger visual backbones.

**Future Research Directions:** While the current work primarily uses navigation as a testbed for representation stability, the authors suggest extending Action QFormer to more complex embodied tasks such as manipulation, loco-manipulation, and humanoid control. These domains, with their demands for contact dynamics, whole-body coordination, and high-dimensional action spaces, would further stress the requirements for action-compatible multimodal representations.

In conclusion, this research highlights that reliable embodied behavior is critically dependent on how multimodal information is selected, organized, and reshaped under action supervision. This perspective offers a valuable lens for understanding and enhancing the robustness of VLA systems in complex real-world environments.
