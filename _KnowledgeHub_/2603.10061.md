# Decision-Aware Uncertainty Evaluation of Vision-Language Model-Based Early Action Anticipation for Human-Robot Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> This work evaluates how reliable confidence is in vision-language-model Top-K short-term egocentric action anticipation for human-robot interaction. Repeated stochastic decoding of a black-box VLM yields varying Top-K sets, aggregated by consistency voting, verbalized-confidence weighting, or Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking, then scored for correctness, calibration, selective gating and confidence geometry. Aggregation reshapes uncertainty structure, trading calibration fidelity against decision separability, without improving ranking.

## Problem

- Prior VLM early-action-anticipation work reports only Top-1 and Top-K ranking accuracy, leaving confidence reliability under partial temporal-prefix observation uncharacterized.
- Existing calibration and selective-prediction evaluations assume single-label correctness, so they ignore the multi-intent set-level decision regime that prefix anticipation creates.
- Uncertainty-aware interaction planners simply assume upstream perception supplies trustworthy confidence, an assumption never verified for language-driven, logit-free vision-language predictors.

## Method

- ==Stochastic multi-run sampling== queries a black-box VLM repeatedly under identical prompts with temperature decoding, using cross-run ==Top-K variability== as an implicit predictive-uncertainty proxy.
- Three aggregation operators: ==consistency-based rank-wise majority voting== with agreement frequency, ==confidence-weighted voting== using model-verbalized scores, and **PairRank** fitting ==Bradley-Terry latent utilities== over pairwise preferences.
- A ==decision-aware evaluation protocol== spanning correctness, Top-1 and ==Set-level expected calibration error== versus K, coverage-accuracy thresholding for ==confidence-gated execution==, and ==confidence geometry== via ==normalized entropy==.

## Results

- On EGTEA Gaze+, PairRank gives the best Recall@10 at **0.487** versus about **0.401-0.425** for other strategies, while Top-1 accuracy stays near **0.15-0.16** for all.
- The single-run baseline attains far lower Top-1 calibration error, roughly **0.24-0.25**, than any aggregation, whose Top-1 error reaches **0.60-0.70**; PairRank is worst at K equals one.
- PairRank yields the lowest normalized Top-10 entropy, **0.184** on EGTEA and **0.419** on EPIC-KITCHENS-100, with steeper coverage decay yet higher retained selective accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Better ranking does not imply better uncertainty: aggregation can raise Recall@K and decision separability while making the top-hypothesis confidence markedly less calibrated.
> - Calibration must be read jointly with the intended decision horizon K, since set-level error rankings among strategies invert as K grows.
> - Confidence geometry, not accuracy, decides interaction behavior: sharp distributions risk overconfident commitment, flat ones inflate clarification burden, so no aggregator is universally best.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses a critical challenge in Human-Robot Interaction (HRI): enabling robots to interpret human actions from incomplete and ambiguous observations, particularly in egocentric views. This capability, known as early action anticipation or short-term action recognition, is fundamental for collaborative robots operating in shared workspaces. Overconfident early predictions by robots can lead to unsafe or disruptive interactions, necessitating reliable uncertainty estimates alongside action hypotheses.

Traditional approaches to action recognition and anticipation often focus on maximizing accuracy (e.g., Top-1 or Top-K performance) but do not always provide trustworthy confidence measures that reflect the underlying uncertainty under partial observation. This gap is particularly pronounced in the context of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). VLMs have recently gained prominence in action understanding due to their open-vocabulary capabilities and context-aware reasoning. However, their reliability in quantifying uncertainty, especially in the temporal-prefix (early observation) regime, has largely remained uncharacterized.

Existing literature on HRI acknowledges the importance of uncertainty-aware decision-making, incorporating it into planning and interaction strategies such as risk-aware decision-making and active uncertainty reduction. However, these downstream HRI modules typically assume that upstream perception components provide reliable uncertainty signals. This paper aims to validate this assumption for VLM-based action anticipation.

The work also connects to the field of uncertainty quantification, particularly calibration and selective prediction, which have been widely studied in supervised classification and, more recently, in VLMs. However, previous evaluations often assume single-label correctness and do not explicitly address the multi-intent, Top-K decision regimes inherent in early action anticipation for HRI. This study extends uncertainty evaluation beyond conventional Top-1 accuracy to encompass set-level validity and the structural distribution of confidence across ranked hypotheses, making it directly relevant to HRI planning and control modules that may consider multiple candidate actions. By systematically evaluating VLM uncertainty in this specific context, the paper contributes to bridging the gap between VLM capabilities and the stringent reliability requirements of HRI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct the first systematic evaluation of uncertainty in Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based short-term action prediction for Human-Robot Interaction (HRI). The authors aim to characterize the reliability of VLM confidence signals under partial observations and in the temporal-prefix regime, where early evidence is often ambiguous.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in human-robot collaboration:
- **Ambiguity in Early Observations:** Robots in shared workspaces must interpret human actions from partial, evolving observations. Early evidence is frequently ambiguous, making it challenging to decipher intent from initial motions.
- **Risks of Overconfident Predictions:** Committing too early to an incorrect action hypothesis can result in disruptive or unsafe robot behavior. Conversely, excessive inaction while waiting for more confirming evidence can paralyze the robot and hinder effective collaboration.
- **Need for Trustworthy Confidence:** Downstream HRI modules require not only an action hypothesis but also a reliable estimate of confidence that accurately reflects uncertainty under partial observation. Without evidence of VLM confidence reliability in the prefix regime, especially under challenging conditions like egocentric viewpoints and occlusions, integrating these predictions into confidence-gated HRI control systems is problematic.
- **Uncharacterized VLM Uncertainty:** While VLMs have shown promise for action understanding due to their open-vocabulary and context-aware reasoning, their uncertainty reliability in early, short-term action prediction has remained largely unexplored. Existing VLM evaluations primarily focus on ranking accuracy rather than the trustworthiness of associated confidence scores.

To address these motivations, the authors outline three main contributions:
1. **Reframing Short-Term Action Anticipation as a Reliability Problem:** The work redefines Top-K short-term action anticipation as a reliability problem, emphasizing the necessity of evaluating uncertainty under partial observation in HRI settings, rather than solely as a ranking problem.
2. **Introducing a Decision-Aware Evaluation Framework:** A novel evaluation framework for Top-K outputs is proposed. This framework assesses correctness (candidate validity), uncertainty reliability (calibration fidelity), selective decision utility (decision gating), and confidence geometry (ambiguity modeling). The framework provides tools to determine if VLM confidence signals are suitable for confidence-gated interaction.
3. **Empirical Analysis of Uncertainty Geometry:** Through systematic empirical analysis, the study aims to demonstrate how different aggregation strategies for VLM predictions fundamentally reshape the geometry of uncertainty. This analysis seeks to reveal trade-offs between calibration fidelity and decision-level separability, illustrating that improved ranking performance does not necessarily equate to improved uncertainty reliability. The ultimate goal is to empower VLM predictions with a confidence score suitable for downstream uncertainty-aware control modules.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a methodology to evaluate the decision-aware uncertainty of VLM-based early action anticipation. This involves generating uncertainty estimates from black-box VLMs, aggregating these estimates, and then evaluating them using a specific protocol.

#### 3.1. Uncertainty Generation: Stochastic Multi-Run Sampling

Given that direct access to internal logits or probabilistic representations is typically unavailable in black-box VLMs, the authors leverage stochastic decoding as an implicit sampling mechanism to approximate predictive uncertainty. For an input egocentric video clip, the VLM is queried multiple times (M independent stochastic forward passes) using identical prompts and decoding configurations (e.g., temperature-based sampling). Each run produces a Top-K action prediction set. The variability observed across these stochastic Top-K outputs serves as a proxy for model uncertainty; higher variability indicates increased uncertainty. The study used M=5 sampling runs and a temperature of T=0.8.

#### 3.2. Aggregation of Stochastic Top-K Predictions

The multiple stochastic Top-K outputs are aggregated into a single ranked prediction set with associated confidence scores. Three aggregation strategies are investigated:
- **Consistency-Based Aggregation:** For each rank position (k), the aggregated action is determined by majority voting across the M runs. The confidence for this action at that rank is defined as its agreement frequency (proportion of runs where it appeared at that rank). This method primarily considers empirical agreement.
- **Confidence-Weighted Aggregation:** This strategy incorporates the VLM's reported verbalized confidence for each predicted action. For each rank (k), the aggregated action is selected by a confidence-weighted majority vote, where each vote is scaled by the model's confidence. The associated confidence is the normalized sum of confidence-weighted votes.
- **Pairwise Ranking Aggregation (PairRank):** This method models the global ranking structure. All unique actions across the stochastic Top-K outputs form a candidate set. Pairwise preference events (action 'a' ranked above 'b' in a run) are aggregated. A Bradley-Terry model is then fitted to estimate latent utility scores for each action, from which a normalized probability distribution is derived. Actions are sorted by these utility scores to form the aggregated Top-K prediction, and their derived probabilities serve as confidence signals.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

The evaluation framework assesses confidence signals across four decision-relevant dimensions for HRI:

- **Correctness (Candidate Validity):** This dimension evaluates the ranking quality of the predicted Top-K actions, independent of uncertainty interpretation. Metrics include Top-1 Accuracy (if the highest-ranked prediction matches the ground truth) and Recall@K (if the ground truth appears within the predicted Top-K set). Recall@K is particularly relevant for prefix-based anticipation as it reflects ambiguity coverage.

- **Uncertainty Reliability (Probabilistic Fidelity):** This measures how well scalar confidence estimates align with empirical correctness.
 - **Top-1 Calibration:** Assessed using Expected Calibration Error (ECE) for the highest-ranked action's confidence, reflecting classical single-class calibration.
 - **Set-Level Calibration:** For multi-intent anticipation, the ground-truth action's presence within the Top-K set is the event of interest. Set-ECE is computed by binning samples based on the mean confidence of the Top-K set and measuring the discrepancy between average set confidence and empirical set correctness. This metric is reported as a function of K to assess robustness under increasing ambiguity.

- **Selective Decision Utility (Decision Gating):** This dimension evaluates the effectiveness of confidence-based abstention, simulating a confidence-gated execution policy for a robot. The system executes the highest-ranked prediction only if its confidence exceeds a threshold (τ). Metrics include:
 - **Coverage:** The fraction of inputs for which the system commits to execution.
 - **Selective Accuracy:** Correctness over the subset of retained (executed) predictions. A useful signal should show a monotonic trade-off: decreasing coverage with increasing accuracy as τ rises.

- **Diagnostic Analyses: Confidence Geometry (Ambiguity Modeling):** This examines the internal structure of Top-K confidence distributions to understand how ambiguity is represented.
 - **Rank-wise Confidence Distribution:** Analyzes how confidence mass is allocated across different ranks, visualized through boxplots. Structured decay or even distribution can reveal insights into ambiguity.
 - **Normalized Entropy:** Quantifies the dispersion of confidence within the Top-K set. Low entropy indicates concentrated belief (clear intent), while high entropy reflects distributed ambiguity (multiple plausible intents).

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on two standard egocentric action anticipation benchmarks: EGTEA Gaze+ (10K action segments in kitchen environments) and EPIC-KITCHENS-100 (90K action segments across diverse subjects and environments).
- **Vision-Language Model:** A state-of-the-art VLM, Gemini 2.5 Flash-lite, was used as a black-box API. A lightweight model variant was chosen for scalable uncertainty analysis.
- **Evaluation Implementation:** Top-1 ECE and Set-ECE were computed using 10 equal-width confidence bins. K=10 was used for Top-K evaluations unless otherwise specified.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that aggregation strategies significantly influence the uncertainty characteristics of VLM-based early action anticipation.

#### 4.1. Correctness

- **Moderate Impact on Ranking:** Aggregation generally had a moderate impact on ranking performance. PairRank consistently achieved the highest Recall@10 on both datasets. Top-1 accuracy remained comparable across all aggregation methods and the single-run baseline. This suggests that aggregation primarily reorganizes candidate rankings rather than drastically altering immediate prediction correctness.

#### 4.2. Uncertainty Reliability

- **Top-1 Calibration Variability:** Top-1 ECE did not show uniform improvement with aggregation. The single-run baseline often exhibited the lowest Top-1 ECE, while PairRank generally showed the highest Top-1 ECE, particularly at K=1. This indicates that methods optimizing for ranking (like PairRank) might not be well-calibrated for single-hypothesis confidence.
- **Set-Level Calibration Nuances:** The Set-ECE vs. K curves revealed K-dependent behavior. While PairRank showed higher calibration error at small K, its Set-ECE decreased more rapidly as K increased. On EGTEA Gaze+, PairRank achieved the lowest Set-ECE around K=3-4, and on EPIC-KITCHENS-100, its calibration improved steadily with larger K. This suggests that PairRank can provide better set-level reliability for moderate to larger K values, even if its Top-1 confidence is less calibrated. Consistency aggregation generally achieved lower Set-ECE at K=10 on EGTEA Gaze+, while PairRank showed lower Set-ECE at K=10 on EPIC-KITCHENS-100.

#### 4.3. Selective Decision Utility

- **Sharp Threshold Separability:** PairRank demonstrated superior selective decision utility. It maintained higher accuracy under increasing confidence thresholds while reducing coverage more decisively compared to averaging-based strategies (Consistency and Confidence-Weighted). This sharper threshold separability indicates that PairRank's confidence effectively distinguishes reliable predictions from uncertain ones, which is beneficial for principled decision gating in safety-critical HRI. This benefit, however, occurred despite weaker Top-1 calibration, highlighting a trade-off.

#### 4.4. Confidence Geometry

- **Impact on Ambiguity Modeling:** The analysis of confidence geometry provided insights into the observed behaviors:
 - **Entropy Distribution:** PairRank consistently produced the lowest normalized Top-10 entropy across both datasets. This indicates a strong concentration of probability mass.
 - **Rank-wise Confidence:** PairRank assigned very high confidence to rank-1 predictions and sharply suppressed confidence for lower ranks, resulting in a steep confidence hierarchy. In contrast, Consistency and Confidence-Weighted aggregations distributed confidence more evenly across ranks, leading to higher entropy and smoother confidence decay.
- **Interaction-Level Implications:** These structural differences in confidence distributions have direct implications for HRI. Sharp, low-entropy structures suggest a clear intent, potentially enabling efficient targeted confirmation. However, they carry a higher risk of severe misjudgment if the top prediction is incorrect (overconfident misprediction). Smoother, higher-entropy distributions reflect ambiguous multi-intent states, increasing robustness by expanding the candidate space but potentially introducing higher clarification overhead in time-sensitive interactions. Low-confidence outputs, especially from single-run baselines, might suppress interaction entirely under threshold-based gating.

#### 4.5. Summary of Findings

Aggregation strategies do not merely affect ranking performance but fundamentally reshape the geometry of uncertainty. Confidence distributions generated by different aggregation methods influence clarification scope, interaction risk, and system responsiveness in HRI. The appropriate trade-off between accuracy, reliability, and interaction complexity depends on the specific operational context and decision-making requirements of the human-robot system.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic framework and empirical evidence for evaluating uncertainty in VLM-based early action anticipation, which has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Robot Decision-Making in HRI:** The work directly addresses the critical need for reliable confidence signals in human-robot collaboration. By characterizing VLM uncertainty, it enables robots to make more informed decisions, such as executing an action, deferring it, seeking clarification, or gathering more information, based on trustworthy confidence estimates. This can lead to safer, more effective, and less disruptive human-robot interactions.
- **Guidance for VLM Integration in HRI:** The findings provide empirical guidance for integrating VLM predictions into confidence-gated HRI systems. It highlights that traditional accuracy metrics are insufficient and that decision-aware uncertainty evaluation is essential. Roboticists and VLM developers can use this framework to select or design aggregation strategies that align with specific HRI requirements, such as a preference for decisive action (even with higher Top-1 ECE) or robust handling of ambiguity (with better set-level calibration).
- **Advancement of Uncertainty Quantification for VLMs:** The study extends existing uncertainty quantification methodologies to the complex domain of temporal-prefix, multi-intent action anticipation. By introducing metrics like Set-ECE and analyzing confidence geometry, it pushes the boundaries of how VLM reliability is assessed, particularly for applications where multiple plausible outcomes exist.
- **Understanding Trade-offs:** The research illuminates the trade-offs between calibration fidelity, decision-level separability, and interaction complexity. This understanding is crucial for system designers, as no single aggregation strategy is universally optimal. The choice must be guided by domain-specific risk tolerance and interaction dynamics.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several avenues for future work, including exploring adaptive or context-aware parameter selection for aggregation and integrating the proposed framework into closed-loop robotic systems for real-time decision-making studies. This suggests a roadmap for further advancing uncertainty-aware HRI.
- **Promoting Robust and Ethical AI:** By advocating for uncertainty-aware evaluation, the work contributes to the broader goal of developing more robust, transparent, and ethically responsible AI systems, especially in safety-critical domains like robotics. Reliable uncertainty estimates are a cornerstone of safe and explainable AI.

In summary, this work provides foundational insights and practical tools for developing more trustworthy and context-aware VLM-based action anticipation systems, thereby directly impacting the safety, efficiency, and naturalness of human-robot interaction.
