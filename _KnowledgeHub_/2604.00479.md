# All Roads Lead to Rome: Incentivizing Divergent Thinking in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Australian National University and GE Research developed Multi-Group Policy Optimization (MUPO) to overcome "diversity collapse" in Vision-Language Models trained with Reinforcement Learning. MUPO fosters divergent thinking by simultaneously refining multiple distinct reasoning strategies, leading to up to a 6.2% increase in multi-attempt accuracy on various reasoning benchmarks and allowing 3B models to match 7B baselines.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically GRPO, causes "diversity collapse" in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), where models converge on a narrow set of reasoning strategies.
- RL-trained VLMs exhibit deeper but narrower reasoning, often failing to capture the broader, more diverse problem-solving strategies inherent in base models.
- The loss of divergent thinking limits model scalability and can lead to local optima, where models master suboptimal strategies instead of discovering globally superior ones.

## Method

- ==Multi-Group Policy== Optimization (**MUPO**) partitions sampled responses into multiple distinct groups based on reasoning embedding similarity, representing different solution strategies.
- MUPO applies a weighted sum of individual GRPO-like objectives, allowing for localized advantage estimation and independent refinement within each reasoning group.
- A conditional, annealed diversity reward is incorporated, which incentivizes inter-group separation and promotes exploration of varied reasoning paths, applied only to accurate responses to prevent reward hacking.

## Results

- MUPO-Thinker-**7B** improved average `acc@1` by **2.5%** on mathematical and **2.3%** on general-purpose reasoning benchmarks, outperforming leading open-source models.
- MUPO-Thinker-**7B** significantly enhanced test-time scaling, achieving gains of **6.0%** in `acc@4` on mathematical and **6.2%** on general-purpose benchmarks, demonstrating superior capacity to find correct solutions through diverse attempts.
- Visualizations confirm MUPO induces a broad, multimodal reasoning structure, successfully mitigating diversity collapse observed in GRPO, and enabling the discovery of more effective and accurate reasoning strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing RL methods like GRPO lead to a rapid "diversity collapse" during VLM training, causing models to discard valuable alternative reasoning paths and converge on narrow strategies.
> - Base VLMs possess an inherent capacity for divergent thinking, generating diverse solutions that correlate strongly with higher success rates when multiple attempts are permitted.
> - Explicitly incentivizing and managing divergent reasoning during RL training can simultaneously enhance both the depth of individual solutions and the breadth of strategies, improving overall VLM performance and scalability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their reasoning capabilities, particularly as influenced by Reinforcement Learning (RL). The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their subsequent extension to VLMs has underscored the importance of robust reasoning for complex tasks. Initially, reasoning in these models was primarily elicited through prompt engineering techniques, such as chain-of-thought, or through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on curated human-like reasoning trajectories.

More recently, Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has emerged as a promising paradigm. RL enables models to engage in self-reflection and verification, leading to notable self-improvement capabilities. This has been leveraged to enhance VLMs' ability to process and reason over visual information, demonstrating performance gains on challenging logical visual questions and traditional VLM tasks.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in the understanding of RL's application to VLM reasoning. While RL models often achieve higher accuracy, questions have arisen regarding the underlying mechanisms driving their effectiveness and their inherent limitations. Prior research on LLMs noted that RL models, despite accuracy improvements, might be constrained by the base model's capabilities and struggle to generate novel reasoning patterns. Furthermore, some VLM studies have observed instances where RL models underperform their base counterparts, particularly when reasoning is explicitly incorporated. These observations suggest an underexplored tension between the refinement offered by RL and the broader generative capacity of base models. This paper directly addresses this gap by investigating a fundamental behavioral distinction between RL-trained VLMs and their base models, proposing a novel approach to leverage the strengths of both.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and address a fundamental limitation in how Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), influences the reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The authors are motivated by observed disparities in problem-solving behaviors between RL-trained models and their vanilla base counterparts.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Identify and Characterize Behavioral Distinctions:** The initial motivation stems from the question: "Do RL models truly outperform their base counterparts?" To answer this, the authors conduct a behavioral comparison. They observe that while RL models generally achieve higher accuracy on a single attempt (indicative of deeper, more refined reasoning along a specific path), base models consistently solve a broader range of problems when allowed multiple attempts. This suggests that base models possess a greater capacity for generating diverse, alternative reasoning pathways, which RL models often fail to capture. For instance, in geometric problems, RL models might exclusively use equation-solving, which can be error-prone, whereas base models might propose simpler verification strategies. Similarly, for object counting, RL models tend to use tedious sequential enumeration, while base models employ efficient elimination strategies. This leads to the central finding that RL models engage in "deeper yet narrow reasoning," while base models exhibit "broader and more diverse thinking patterns." The work aims to empirically demonstrate and analyze this distinction.

2. **Investigate the Cause of Divergence Loss in RL Models:** Building on the observed behavioral distinction, the authors aim to understand why RL models, despite their refinement, appear to forgo the inherent divergent thinking present in their base counterparts. Through an analysis of training dynamics, they discover a phenomenon termed "diversity collapse" in GRPO. During the early stages of training, reasoning diversity sharply declines to a negligible level. This indicates that the model rapidly converges on a narrow set of reasoning strategies, effectively discarding the majority of potential alternative solution paths. Consequently, the model spends most of the training period optimizing within this limited subset.

3. **Address the Limitations of Diversity Collapse:** The diversity collapse identified in GRPO training presents two significant issues that motivate the development of a new approach:
 - **Exploitation over Exploration:** The model prioritizes optimizing a dominant strategy, neglecting to explore alternative reasoning modes. This can lead to convergence to local optima, where the model masters a suboptimal strategy rather than discovering a globally superior one.
 - **Poor Scalability:** Convergent reasoning, being narrow, struggles to generalize across the broad spectrum of problem types encountered at test time, thereby limiting the model's ability to scale effectively and solve more complex or varied problems.

4. **Propose a Method to Incentivize Divergent Thinking:** The core objective is to develop a novel RL algorithm that can preserve and foster divergent thinking from base models. This new approach should enable models to:
 - Reason deeply and refine individual solutions (the strength of RL).
 - Maintain a repertoire of diverse strategies (the strength of base models).
 - Explore multiple reasoning paths to discover globally optimal solutions.

To achieve this, the authors propose Multi-Group Policy Optimization (MUPO), designed as a direct replacement for GRPO, specifically to incentivize divergent reasoning across multiple solutions and thereby overcome the diversity collapse.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Multi-Group Policy Optimization (MUPO), is designed to address the diversity collapse observed in traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) when applied to Vision-Language Models (VLMs). MUPO aims to incentivize divergent thinking by simultaneously refining multiple distinct reasoning strategies.

The methodology can be broken down into the following components:

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

The authors first contextualize MUPO by revisiting GRPO. In a multimodal setting (question `q`, gold answer `y`, visual input `I`), GRPO aims to maximize an objective by sampling a group of responses `{o_i}` from an old policy `π_θ_old` and rewarding superior ones while penalizing less effective alternatives. The core of GRPO involves calculating an estimated token-level advantage `ˆA_i` for each response `o_i`, derived from a normalized sequence-level reward `R_i`. This reward typically includes verifiable metrics like accuracy (`R_acc`) and format consistency (`R_fmt`). The objective function involves a ratio of probabilities from the current and old policies, clipped to maintain stable on-policy training. A critical observation made by the authors is that GRPO performs global advantage estimation over all sampled responses, which they hypothesize contributes to diversity collapse.

#### 3.2. Multi-Group Policy Optimization (MUPO)

MUPO modifies GRPO by introducing mechanisms to promote and maintain reasoning diversity:

- **Multi-Group Objective:**
 - **Response Partitioning:** Instead of processing all `N` sampled responses as a single group, MUPO partitions them into `K` distinct groups, `{G_k}_k=1^K`. This partitioning is based on the similarity of reasoning embeddings of the generated responses, which are obtained using an embedding model (e.g., Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B). Constrained clustering is employed to group responses with similar reasoning trajectories while ensuring a minimum group size (`G_min`) for reliable advantage estimation. Each `G_k` is intended to represent a distinct reasoning mode or strategy.
 - **Composition of GRPO Objectives:** The overall objective function `J_MUPO(θ)` is formulated as a weighted sum of `K` individual GRPO-like objectives, one for each group `G_k`. This structure allows for independent optimization and refinement of each distinct reasoning mode.
 - **Localized Advantage Estimation:** Crucially, the advantage `ˆA_k_i` for a response `o_k_i` is computed *locally* within its assigned group `G_k`, using the mean and standard deviation of rewards within that specific group. This contrasts with GRPO's global advantage estimation and ensures that each mode is refined independently.
 - **Load-Balance Scaler (w_k):** A weight `w_k = (N / (K * |G_k|))^β` is introduced for each group's objective. This scaler, controlled by a sensitivity exponent `β`, modulates the contribution of each group to the overall optimization. Its purpose is to prevent larger or dominant groups from monopolizing the learning process, thereby encouraging a more balanced refinement across all `K` diverse strategies.

- **Diversity Reward:**
 - **Inter-Group Separation:** To actively encourage models to explore and maintain varied reasoning strategies, MUPO incorporates a novel diversity reward (`R_div`). For a given trajectory `o_k_i` from group `G_k`, its diversity reward is calculated as the average cosine distance between its reasoning embedding and the embeddings of all responses from *all other groups* (`G_m` where `m ≠ k`). A higher `R_div` indicates greater separation from other groups, thus incentivizing the generation of unique strategies.
 - **Conditional Application:** The diversity reward is applied conditionally: `λ · 1[R_acc = 1] · R_div`. This means `R_div` is only factored into the total reward if the response is factually accurate (`R_acc = 1`). This condition is vital to prevent "reward hacking," where the model might generate diverse but incorrect outputs purely to maximize diversity.
 - **Annealing Schedule for Diversity Weight (λ):** The weight `λ` of the diversity reward is not static but is annealed over the training steps (`t_cur`) using a cosine schedule, from an initial maximum value (`λ_max`) to a final minimum value (`λ_min`). This annealing schedule is designed to promote broad exploration of diverse reasoning strategies during the early stages of training (when `λ` is high) and gradually shift the focus towards identifying and refining globally optimal solutions in later stages (as `λ` decreases).

In summary, MUPO extends GRPO by explicitly modeling and optimizing for diversity across multiple reasoning groups, leveraging localized advantage estimation and a carefully designed, annealed diversity reward to achieve a balance between exploration and exploitation, fostering both depth and breadth in VLM reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness of Multi-Group Policy Optimization (MUPO) in incentivizing divergent thinking and enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

#### 4.1. Divergent vs. Convergent Reasoning Behaviors

- **Behavioral Distinction:** The initial analysis (Fig. 2) confirms a fundamental difference between RL models (specifically GRPO-trained) and base models. While GRPO models show higher `acc@1` (accuracy on a single, greedy attempt), indicating deeper, more refined reasoning along one path, base models achieve significantly higher `acc@k` for `k > 1` (accuracy when multiple attempts are allowed). This suggests that base models exhibit a greater capacity for generating diverse, effective alternative solutions in challenging cases, a behavior termed "divergent thinking." In contrast, GRPO models tend to adopt "narrowly focused reasoning."
- **Correlation with Success:** Further experiments show a strong positive correlation between reasoning diversity and `acc@4` scores in base models (Fig. 2 C, D). This indicates that exploring diverse reasoning paths substantially increases the probability of finding a correct answer, aligning with human problem-solving strategies.
- **Embedding Space Visualization:** t-SNE projections of reasoning embeddings (Fig. 4) reveal that GRPO reasoning embeddings are densely clustered, reflecting a concentrated distribution, while base model embeddings are more sparsely distributed and cover a wider region. This structural difference explains why base models are more likely to retrieve correct solutions from alternative regions in failure cases of RL models.

#### 4.2. Diversity Collapse in GRPO

- **Training Dynamics:** Analysis of GRPO training (Fig. 3A) shows that reasoning diversity drops sharply within the first 20 training steps. This "diversity collapse" suggests that GRPO models prematurely converge on a narrow set of strategies, discarding most potential solution paths and spending the majority of training refining a limited subset. This leads to issues of exploitation over exploration and limited scalability.

#### 4.3. MUPO Performance and Divergent Thinking

- **State-of-the-Art Performance (Tables 1, 2, 3):**
 - MUPO-Thinker-7B achieved an average `acc@1` improvement of 2.5% on mathematical benchmarks (from 49.1% to 51.6%) and 2.3% on general-purpose benchmarks (from 63.3% to 65.6%) over previous leading open-source models.
 - MUPO-Thinker-3B also showed significant gains, averaging 2.0% on mathematical (from 41.5% to 43.5%) and 2.4% on general benchmarks (from 55.4% to 57.8%).
 - These results indicate that incorporating divergent thinking enables models to discover globally superior solutions and demonstrate more sophisticated reasoning even along individual paths.
- **Enhanced Test-Time Scaling (Tables 1, 2, 3):**
 - MUPO-Thinker-7B significantly outperformed existing strong RL models in `acc@4`, with gains of 6.0% on mathematical benchmarks (from 52.8% to 58.8%) and 6.2% on general-purpose benchmarks (from 66.2% to 72.4%). MUPO-Thinker-3B also showed a substantial `acc@4` gain of 5.9%.
 - The improved scalability allowed MUPO-Thinker-3B to achieve performance comparable to several strong 7B baselines. This demonstrates MUPO's success in integrating the complementary strengths of RL (depth) and base models (breadth).
- **Visual Confirmation of Divergent Thinking (Fig. 6):** t-SNE projections of MUPO's reasoning embeddings show a broad, multimodal structure, with each mode corresponding to a distinct solution strategy. This contrasts sharply with GRPO's narrow, single-mode clusters, visually confirming that MUPO successfully induces divergent thinking.
- **Discovery of Better Solutions (Fig. 7):** A qualitative example illustrates that while GRPO uses a rigid, error-prone layer-by-layer estimation for building height, MUPO leverages surrounding reference objects for a more accurate estimation. This highlights MUPO's ability to learn a broader set of reasoning strategies and generate more effective solutions.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics and Ablation Studies

- **Learning Curves (Fig. 8):** MUPO's learning curves show steady accuracy improvement alongside a "rise-fall-plateau" trend in diversity reward. The initial rise indicates active exploration, the decline (due to annealing `λ`) signals a shift to exploiting promising strategies, and the plateau represents stabilization around effective solutions. This demonstrates a balanced trade-off between exploration and exploitation, leading to a much more gradual decline in diversity compared to GRPO's sharp collapse.
- **Number of Groups (K) (Table 4):** Performance improves as `K` increases, peaking at `K=3`. `K=1` (equivalent to GRPO) performs worse. Mathematical benchmarks benefited from larger `K` (more diversity), while general problems favored smaller `K` (more uniform reasoning), reflecting task-specific needs for diversity.
- **Diversity Reward Weights (λ_max, λ_min) (Fig. 9):** An ablation study confirmed the optimality of the default annealing schedule parameters (`λ_max=0.4`, `λ_min=0.1`). Deviations, such as excessively high weights, led to diversity at the expense of accuracy, while overly low weights reduced exploration and increased the risk of local optima.

Overall, the findings indicate that MUPO effectively mitigates diversity collapse, enabling VLMs to explore and refine multiple reasoning strategies, resulting in significantly improved performance and enhanced test-time scalability across various reasoning benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and artificial intelligence reasoning.

**Conceptual and Empirical Contribution:**
The paper's primary significance lies in identifying and empirically demonstrating a "fundamental behavioral distinction" between RL-trained models and their base counterparts. It highlights that while current RL methods (like GRPO) deepen reasoning along a narrow path, they inadvertently suppress the inherent divergent thinking and broad problem-solving strategies present in base models. This discovery of "diversity collapse" in GRPO training is a critical insight, revealing a previously underexplored limitation that restricts model scalability and can lead to local optima. This challenges the assumption that simply applying RL to refine reasoning uniformly leads to universally superior performance.

**Methodological Innovation:**
In response to the identified diversity collapse, the proposed Multi-Group Policy Optimization (MUPO) offers a novel and effective solution. By partitioning responses into multiple groups, applying localized advantage estimation, and incorporating a conditional, annealed diversity reward, MUPO provides a principled mechanism to explicitly incentivize and manage divergent thinking during RL training. This methodology represents a significant advancement by actively maintaining and refining multiple distinct reasoning modes simultaneously, thereby bridging the gap between the "depth" of RL-refined reasoning and the "breadth" of base model generation.

**Enhanced Performance and Scalability:**
The experimental results demonstrate that MUPO significantly advances the state of the art for open-source VLMs on a range of challenging reasoning benchmarks. Notably, MUPO-Thinker models show substantial improvements in both `acc@1` (indicating more sophisticated individual reasoning) and, more importantly, `acc@4` (highlighting superior test-time scaling capabilities). The ability of a 3B MUPO model to achieve performance comparable to some 7B baselines due to enhanced scalability underscores the efficiency and power of this approach. This indicates that encouraging diverse strategies can lead to more robust and generalized problem-solving.

**Implications for Future Research and Development:**
1. **Rethinking RL for Reasoning:** This work suggests a paradigm shift in how RL should be designed for reasoning tasks. Instead of solely focusing on refining a single optimal path, future RL algorithms could benefit from explicitly encouraging exploration across multiple valid reasoning strategies.
2. **More Robust and Generalizable AI:** By fostering divergent thinking, MUPO contributes to developing AI models that are more robust to varied problem types and less susceptible to the failures of a single dominant strategy. This moves towards more human-like problem-solving, where multiple perspectives are often considered.
3. **Efficiency in Training:** The understanding of diversity collapse and MUPO's balanced exploration-exploitation strategy can inform more efficient training regimes, potentially reducing the computational cost of finding globally optimal solutions compared to brute-force optimization of a single mode.
4. **Beyond VLMs:** The concept of incentivizing divergent thinking and addressing diversity collapse might be applicable to other domains where RL is used for complex reasoning, such as in pure Large Language Models or other multi-modal AI systems.

In conclusion, this research not only provides a critical analysis of the limitations of current RL approaches for VLM reasoning but also offers a practical and effective solution that pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities. Its impact is likely to be felt in the design of future RL algorithms, the development of more generalized and robust AI reasoning systems, and a deeper understanding of the interplay between exploration, exploitation, and diversity in complex machine learning models.
