# Active Surface-Driven Reconfigurable Gripper: Robust Grasping and Sequential Manipulation of Thin Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> A reconfigurable three-finger gripper grasps thin objects by pairing a belt-driven active-surface thumb with underactuated wedge-tipped fingers. The rolling belt repositions a book into the palm without arm repositioning or finger gait control, and symmetric finger rotation switches between grasping and separation modes. Only three motors are needed; it grasped flat books 78/80 and shelved books 69/80.

## Problem

- Pre-grasp, scooping and prying strategies shift contact points continuously, so maintaining force closure demands predicted object trajectories plus tightly coordinated arm and finger motion.
- Suction cups and electro-adhesion require absolutely smooth surfaces, while external-dexterity grippers struggle with high bending stiffness, narrowing which thin materials can be handled.
- Prior active-surface grippers pick only single sheets, deform stiff objects excessively, or couple belt and finger motion so the robot arm must adjust mid-grasp.

## Method

- **Reconfigurable three-finger gripper**: a silicone-coated ==belt active-surface thumb== with six idler pulleys plus two wedge-tipped ==underactuated spring fingers==; three motors drive reconfiguration, opening/closing, and belt rotation.
- ==Kinematic model== of the ==parallel four-bar linkage== and lead-screw transmission solves the initial configuration (finger angle and gripper rotation) from book thickness and fingertip-to-thumb spacing.
- ==Quasi-static contact analysis== with an ==Euler-Bernoulli beam model== of the book yields a no-slip friction condition, setting fingertip wedge angle to 20 degrees and maximizing finger opening near 40 mm.

## Results

- Desktop grasping succeeded **78/80** across four books; only the thickest (**14 mm**, bending stiffness **7.7e-3 N·m²**) failed twice, at the moment of finger contact.
- Bookshelf grasping reached **69/80** (**16 to 18** per **20** trials per book); every failure came from open-loop finger insertion, none after insertion completed.
- Plastic film, fabric, A4 paper and mouse pad each scored **20/20** despite differing friction and softness, down to **0.08 mm** thick; grasp-place sequences ran both directions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A rolling active surface fixes the contact point in the gripper frame, converting a gait-control problem into sequential single-input commands with no coupled motion.
> - Bending stiffness, not thickness or surface friction, sets the failure boundary; slender-beam analysis predicts where the thumb's no-slip condition breaks down.
> - Asymmetric friction pays off: a high-friction driven surface on one digit plus low-friction wedge tips lets the hand both pull objects in and release them into clutter.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has achieved notable advancements in grasping and interacting with diverse objects. However, a persistent challenge remains in the robust and versatile grasping and manipulation of thin, deformable objects, such as books, paper, or fabric. These objects are characterized by their low thickness-to-length ratio (typically h/l < 1/10) and significant deformability under applied forces, which complicates contact prediction and maintenance during grasping.

Existing approaches to grasping thin objects primarily fall into several categories:
- **Precision-based methods:** These often rely on complex finger gait control or coordinated robot arm and finger movements to maintain force closure. Such methods typically demand accurate mathematical models or extensive training data, increasing control complexity and computational overhead. Examples include scooping or prying strategies, which involve inserting fingers into gaps or lifting objects, requiring precise motion control and continuous adaptation of contact points.
- **Specialized grippers:** Some designs employ suction cups, electro-adhesion, or external dexterity. While effective for specific materials or surface properties, these grippers often have stringent requirements, such as smooth surfaces for adhesion or specific bending stiffness, which limits their general applicability across a range of thin objects. For instance, suction cups require smooth surfaces, and electro-adhesion is specific to certain materials, while grippers relying on external dexterity may struggle with objects possessing high bending stiffness.
- **Soft grippers:** These utilize passive compliance to manage uncertainties during grasping. However, they still often require prediction of finger motion and may face challenges with multilayered deformable objects like books, where interlayer separation can occur if contact is made only with the upper surface.
- **Active surface grippers:** These grippers incorporate belts or rollers to simplify control strategies by using continuous rotation to reposition objects. While some designs have shown promise for fabrics or objects with specific bending stiffness, they can still have limitations such as not being suitable for smooth surfaces or requiring coupled active surface and finger movements that necessitate robot arm adjustments. Previous work with belt-based active surfaces on 1-DOF grippers also showed such coupling.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a gripper design and grasping strategy intended to offer a generalizable solution for thin deformable objects, specifically focusing on books in common scenarios (flat on a surface and vertically packed on a shelf). It seeks to reduce control complexity and enhance robustness by integrating an active surface with underactuated compliance and a reconfigurable structure, diverging from methods that require extensive finger gait control or impose strict object property requirements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic gripper capable of robustly grasping and sequentially manipulating thin deformable objects, particularly books, across different common environmental configurations with reduced control complexity.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in manipulating thin objects:
- **Difficulty in grasping thin deformable objects:** Their low thickness-to-length ratio and susceptibility to deformation make stable contact difficult to predict and maintain. Traditional grippers often struggle with these characteristics.
- **Varied object states:** In real-world environments like libraries, books can be found in multiple states, such as lying flat on a desktop or arranged vertically on a bookshelf. Current gripping solutions often specialize in one state, limiting their versatility.
- **Interlayer separation:** For objects like books, which consist of separable layers, grasping methods that apply force only to a single surface can lead to pages separating from the main body, hindering successful manipulation.
- **Control complexity:** Many existing robust grasping strategies for thin objects rely on precise finger gait control or coordinated robot arm movements, which demand complex mathematical models or extensive data for learning. This increases the computational burden and operational complexity.
- **Limitations of specialized grippers:** While specialized grippers (e.g., suction-based) exist, they often impose strict requirements on object properties (e.g., surface smoothness, thickness, friction), thereby limiting their general applicability.

To address these issues, the authors aim to:
1. **Design a novel gripper architecture:** This involves integrating an active surface, reconfigurable mechanisms, and underactuated joints to achieve versatility and simplified control.
2. **Develop an active surface-based grasping strategy:** This strategy seeks to reposition thin objects without complex finger gait control, relying instead on the stable contact provided by a continuously rolling active surface.
3. **Optimize structural parameters and grasping strategy:** Utilize mechanical analysis, specifically beam theory, to understand contact forces during grasping and refine gripper design and operational parameters.
4. **Validate the gripper's performance:** Conduct comprehensive experiments to demonstrate the gripper's ability to stably grasp various thin objects, including those with separable layers, and to perform sequential manipulation tasks.

By focusing on these objectives, the research intends to provide a generalizable and practical solution for automating tasks involving thin deformable objects in diverse scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves a multi-faceted approach, encompassing mechanical design, kinematic analysis, grasping strategy development, and parameter optimization, followed by experimental validation.

**A. Gripper Design**
The proposed gripper is a reconfigurable three-finger mechanism featuring an active surface on one thumb and two underactuated fingers. It is actuated by three motors, aiming for functional versatility with reduced control complexity.

1. **Underactuated Fingers:** The two main fingers are designed for parallel grasping, utilizing a parallel four-bar linkage (links 3, 4, 5, 6). They incorporate a reconfigurable feature, allowing them to rotate symmetrically relative to the base, which is driven by motor 1 via link 6 and a gear. The fingertips are wedge-shaped to facilitate insertion into gaps and smooth repositioning. A spring connects the fingertip to link 3, creating an underactuated joint. This passive compliance allows the fingertip to bend inwards based on external contact, eliminating the need for an additional active degree of freedom for fingertip control. The fingers' opening and closing motion is driven by motor 2, which actuates a lead screw and nut mechanism, transmitting linear motion to the four-bar linkage through links 8, 7, and 4. Link 8 is designed as a sleeve to decouple reconfiguration and opening/closing motions.
2. **Active Surface Thumb:** The thumb is equipped with a belt-based active surface for continuous rotation, driven by motor 3 via a bevel gear transmission and an active pulley. A silicone layer coats the belt surface to enhance friction, and six idler pulleys are strategically placed, with five densely arranged on the contact surface, to ensure a large and stable contact area. The thumb is fixed relative to the gripper's base.
3. **Transmission Mechanism:** To ensure decoupled operation, the transmission is divided into two independent layers. The upper layer controls finger reconfiguration via a planetary gear system, where the rotation of the planet gears is restricted, enabling symmetric rotation of the two fingers. The lower layer controls the opening/closing motion. Specific gear parameters are chosen to satisfy conditions for symmetric rotation and coaxial alignment of components in the planar crank-slider mechanism.

**B. Kinematic Analysis**
A detailed kinematic model of the gripper is established. The gripper's coordinate system, along with proximal, distal, and slider joint coordinate systems, are defined. Transformation matrices are used to derive the coordinates of key points (endpoints of the fingers and thumb) in the gripper's frame. This analysis allows for expressing finger rotation angles in terms of slider displacement, essential for calculating initial grasp configurations. Equations are provided to describe the positions of the finger endpoints P and Q in the gripper coordinate frame, as well as the relationships between finger and slider movements.

**C. Grasping Strategy**

1. **Grasping Flat Books:**
 - **Initial Configuration:** The gripper is adjusted so the thumb presses the book's upper surface, and the underactuated fingers passively conform to the surface. Equations derived from the kinematic model (relating finger opening angle $\theta_2$ and gripper rotation angle $\psi$ to book thickness $h$ and horizontal distance $s$ between fingertip and thumb) determine this initial state.
 - **Repositioning:** The belt on the active thumb rotates to pull the book inwards. The continuous rolling surface provides a stable, gripper-relative contact point, avoiding complex finger gait control.
 - **Grasping:** After repositioning, the fingers close to secure the book.
2. **Grasping Vertically Arranged Books:**
 - **Separation Mode:** The gripper switches to a separation mode. The two fingers, aligned with the gap, insert their wedge-shaped tips between the target book and adjacent books to separate them. The active surface on the thumb contacts the side of the target book (facing upward).
 - **Repositioning:** The active surface belt rotates to pull the target book into the gripper.
 - **Grasping:** The gripper reconfigures into grasping mode, and the high-friction active surface establishes a stable grasp. The initial configuration is determined by the book's thickness $h$, correlating to the distance between the two fingers.

**D. Parameter Optimization**
To ensure successful grasping of flat books without relative slipping, the structural parameters (fingertip angle $\alpha$) and strategy parameters (spacing $s$) are optimized. This involves an analysis of contact forces during the repositioning phase:
- **Force Equilibrium:** Equations for friction forces ($F_{f2}$) and normal forces ($F_1$, $F_2$, $F_3$) are established for the stages before and after the book contacts the fingers.
- **Beam Model:** For thin, deformable objects like books (approximated as Euler-Bernoulli beams), the bending moment $M(x)$ and deflection $\omega(x)$ are analyzed. Boundary conditions (fixed right end, free left end) are applied to derive the bending moment at the contact point ($M_0$).
- **Friction Condition:** By combining the force equilibrium and beam model, the friction force $F_{f2}$ is calculated. The condition $F_{f2} \le \mu_2 F_2$ (no slipping) is used for optimization.
- **Optimization Results:** Analysis shows that larger fingertip angles $\alpha$ and smaller spacing $s$ lead to higher $F_{f2}$, increasing the risk of slipping. Based on these findings and structural constraints, $\alpha = 20^\circ$ and a larger $s$ (approximately 40mm) are chosen, implying the fingers should open as wide as possible within their range. The analysis also accounts for objects with and without layered structures by modifying the moment of inertia $I_z$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of the proposed gripper and grasping strategy was conducted using a prototype mounted on a UR5e robotic arm. The experiments covered grasping books on a desktop, books on a bookshelf, other thin objects, and a sequential "grasp-place" task.

**A. Grasping Books on the Desktop**
- **Procedure:** The gripper followed a four-step sequence: 1) initial configuration with the gripper adjusted; 2) robot arm moves down for thumb contact; 3) active surface rotates to pull the book inward; 4) fingers close for grasping. Control inputs were sequential and uncoupled, with each step (except the first) having a single input.
- **Objects:** Four books of varying sizes, thicknesses (4mm to 14mm), masses (89g to 440g), and surface friction coefficients were tested. Their bending stiffness (EI_z) ranged up to 7.7e-3 N m^2 for the thickest book.
- **Results:** The gripper achieved a 100% success rate for the three thinner books (20/20 trials each). For the thickest book, the success rate was 18/20, with failures occurring when the book first contacted the fingers, attributed to its high bending stiffness.
- **Non-Bound Side Grasping:** An additional 20 trials were conducted for each book, grasping from the non-bound side (where pages are separable). All four books achieved a 100% success rate (20/20), indicating that the strategy is robust against interlayer sliding due to the wedge-shaped fingertip design.

**B. Grasping Books on the Bookshelf**
- **Procedure:** A similar four-step process was used: 1) initial configuration and alignment with the target book; 2) fingers insert into the gap between the target and adjacent books, with the thumb pressing the target book's side; 3) active surface rotates to pull the book inward; 4) gripper reconfigures and grasps the book. Control was also sequential and uncoupled.
- **Results:** Success rates for grasping bookshelf books ranged from 16/20 to 18/20. Failures were primarily attributed to positional deviations under open-loop control, causing physical obstruction during finger insertion. However, once insertion was successful, no failures occurred during the repositioning and grasping phases, indicating the stability of the active surface-based method for this scenario.

**C. Other Applications (Grasping Thin Objects)**
- **Objects:** Four extremely thin objects placed flat on a desktop were tested: plastic film (0.08mm thick), fabric (0.61mm thick), A4 paper (0.10mm thick), and a mouse pad (2.6mm thick). These objects presented diverse characteristics in friction coefficients and softness.
- **Results:** The gripper achieved a 100% success rate (20/20 trials each) for all four thin objects. This demonstrates the gripper's versatility in handling objects with thicknesses as low as 0.08mm and varying material properties.

**D. "Grasp-Place" Task**
- **Procedure:** The gripper performed sequential tasks of grasping a book from a desktop and placing it onto a bookshelf, and vice versa.
- **Results:** The task was completed reliably. A key finding was the gripper's ability to place books onto a bookshelf without needing to open its fingers inside the shelf. Instead, it utilized the active surface's reverse rotation to "push" the book out while maintaining a hold. This capability is attributed to the combination of low-friction fingertips and the high-friction active surface, simplifying the placement process and enhancing stability compared to traditional methods that might struggle with finger obstruction within confined spaces.

Overall, the experiments indicated that the gripper exhibits a high level of robustness and adaptability across different states of books and various thin deformable objects, with success rates generally high, particularly after successful initial contact or insertion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by presenting a reconfigurable gripper and an associated grasping strategy designed for thin deformable objects. The work addresses existing challenges related to control complexity, versatility, and robustness in handling such objects.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

1. **Reduced Control Complexity:** The integration of an active surface, reconfigurable mechanisms, and underactuated joints allows for robust grasping with a simplified control scheme. The active surface provides a continuously stable contact point for object repositioning, circumventing the need for complex finger gait control or coordinated robot arm movements, which are often required in traditional approaches. Control inputs are provided sequentially rather than through coupled motion, simplifying the operational paradigm.
2. **Enhanced Generality and Versatility:** The reconfigurable design enables the gripper to adapt to different object configurations, specifically demonstrated by its capacity to grasp books lying flat on a surface and those arranged vertically on a bookshelf. The successful manipulation of diverse thin objects, from rigid books to flexible plastic film and fabric, indicates the gripper's broad applicability beyond a single object type.
3. **Robustness in Handling Separable Objects:** The design, particularly the small-angle wedge-shaped fingertips, facilitates smooth deformation and separation of thin objects from surfaces or adjacent objects. This design choice contributes to a high success rate even when grasping from the non-bound, separable side of books, addressing a common challenge in manipulating layered materials.
4. **Optimized Design and Strategy:** The systematic optimization of structural parameters and grasping strategy based on beam theory provides a principled approach to ensure stable contact and prevent slipping during the repositioning process. This analytical foundation supports the robustness observed in experiments.
5. **Facilitation of Sequential Manipulation Tasks:** The "grasp-place" task demonstration highlights the gripper's capability for sequential operations, including the innovative method of placing books on shelves using reverse active surface rotation. This suggests potential for automation in logistics, library management, and manufacturing environments involving thin objects.

The research suggests that active surfaces could serve as an alternative or complementary technology to multi-finger dexterous hands for certain robotic grasping and manipulation tasks, offering a balance between functionality and control simplicity. Future work, including the incorporation of perception systems (e.g., vision) and adaptive active surface mechanisms, could further enhance the autonomy and capabilities of such grippers, potentially expanding their application to a wider array of deformable objects and complex manipulation scenarios.
