# Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards for World Model Post-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research developed Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards (RLIR), a post-training framework that significantly enhances the action-following capabilities of pre-trained video world models. The method achieves 5-10% gains in action classification accuracy and up to 10% improvements in visual quality metrics across diverse model architectures, using Inverse Dynamics Models to derive objective reward signals.

## Problem

- Pre-trained video world models often lack precise action-following capabilities, making them less reliable as interactive simulators despite advances in visual fidelity and temporal consistency.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) based post-training to video world models faces significant challenges due to the prohibitive cost and bias of human preference annotations, and the infeasibility of creating general rule-based video verifiers.
- Existing pixel-level or human preference rewards for video generation prove ineffective or impractical for robustly improving action fidelity.

## Method

- Introduces **RLIR** (Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards), a post-training framework that leverages an ==Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM)== to provide an objective and scalable reward signal.
- The IDM is pre-trained to predict actions from consecutive video frames, then used to evaluate generated videos by comparing its inferred actions to the ground-truth input actions, providing a ==frame-level reward==.
- Optimizes the world model using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to maximize these IDM-derived rewards, with adaptations for both autoregressive (e.g., **MineWorld**) and diffusion-based (e.g., **NFD**) world model architectures.

## Results

- **RLIR** consistently improved action classification accuracy by **5-10%** (F1, recall, precision) and enhanced visual quality by up to **10%** (FVD, PSNR, VBench scores) on both autoregressive and diffusion world models.
- Human evaluations showed a clear and significant preference for RLIR-post-trained models regarding both action-following ability and overall visual quality compared to baseline models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the IDM-derived inverse reward function is superior to human preference or pixel-level rewards, which failed to yield consistent improvements in action-following.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, action-conditioned video generations must faithfully encode the input actions, making them recoverable by an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) as a verifiable reward signal.
> - IDMs are inherently sensitive to subtle visual inconsistencies and action deviations, allowing them to naturally penalize lower-quality or unfaithful video generations.
> - Employing an IDM for reward generation overcomes the limitations of human preference annotations (cost, bias) and pixel-level rewards (correlation with pre-training, reward hacking) for post-training video world models.
