# Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards for World Model Post-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research developed Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards (RLIR), a post-training framework that significantly enhances the action-following capabilities of pre-trained video world models. The method achieves 5-10% gains in action classification accuracy and up to 10% improvements in visual quality metrics across diverse model architectures, using Inverse Dynamics Models to derive objective reward signals.

## Problem

- Pre-trained video world models often lack precise action-following capabilities, making them less reliable as interactive simulators despite advances in visual fidelity and temporal consistency.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) based post-training to video world models faces significant challenges due to the prohibitive cost and bias of human preference annotations, and the infeasibility of creating general rule-based video verifiers.
- Existing pixel-level or human preference rewards for video generation prove ineffective or impractical for robustly improving action fidelity.

## Method

- Introduces **RLIR** (Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards), a post-training framework that leverages an ==Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM)== to provide an objective and scalable reward signal.
- The IDM is pre-trained to predict actions from consecutive video frames, then used to evaluate generated videos by comparing its inferred actions to the ground-truth input actions, providing a ==frame-level reward==.
- Optimizes the world model using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to maximize these IDM-derived rewards, with adaptations for both autoregressive (e.g., **MineWorld**) and diffusion-based (e.g., **NFD**) world model architectures.

## Results

- **RLIR** consistently improved action classification accuracy by **5-10%** (F1, recall, precision) and enhanced visual quality by up to **10%** (FVD, PSNR, VBench scores) on both autoregressive and diffusion world models.
- Human evaluations showed a clear and significant preference for RLIR-post-trained models regarding both action-following ability and overall visual quality compared to baseline models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the IDM-derived inverse reward function is superior to human preference or pixel-level rewards, which failed to yield consistent improvements in action-following.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, action-conditioned video generations must faithfully encode the input actions, making them recoverable by an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) as a verifiable reward signal.
> - IDMs are inherently sensitive to subtle visual inconsistencies and action deviations, allowing them to naturally penalize lower-quality or unfaithful video generations.
> - Employing an IDM for reward generation overcomes the limitations of human preference annotations (cost, bias) and pixel-level rewards (correlation with pre-training, reward hacking) for post-training video world models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: **generative AI, world models, reinforcement learning (RL), and controllable content generation**.

**World Models:** The paper acknowledges the foundational role of world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018) in simulating dynamic environments for intelligent agents. Recent progress in generative modeling (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022) and large-scale video datasets has significantly improved the fidelity and diversity of action-conditioned video world models (Hu et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2025b; Bar et al., 2025). The broader community recognizes three critical requirements for reliable simulators: high-fidelity visuals, long-horizon temporal consistency, and accurate action-following. While much research has focused on visual quality and temporal consistency (e.g., extending context windows, incorporating 3D priors), the authors accurately identify **accurate action-following** as a comparatively underexplored, yet central, challenge (Tot et al., 2025). This paper directly tackles this gap.

**Reinforcement Learning for Generative Models:** The success of RL-based post-training in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences and enhancing reasoning (Ouyang et al., 2022; Wen et al., 2025) has inspired similar efforts in other generative domains. Methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024), initially developed for LLMs, have seen rapid advancements in generative models (Liu et al., 2025a; Xue et al., 2025). However, directly transferring these techniques to **video world models** presents significant hurdles. The paper highlights two main obstacles: (1) the prohibitive cost and bias of collecting large-scale human preference annotations for video, and (2) the infeasibility of constructing general, rule-based video verifiers analogous to those used in narrow domains like coding. Existing reward functions for generative models often rely on subjective metrics (aesthetics, text-image alignment) or large multimodal models (MM-LLMs) as judges, which are prone to their own biases and limitations. This paper aims to overcome these specific transfer challenges.

**Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs):** A crucial component of this work, IDMs are models that predict actions given observations (o_t, o_{t+1}) (Du et al., 2023; Black et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025). Their established effectiveness in domains like robotic manipulation, game environments (Baker et al., 2022), and 3D perception provides the foundation for the verifiable reward signal proposed in RLIR. The paper leverages the IDM's ability to map high-dimensional video data to low-dimensional action space with high precision, and its sensitivity to subtle visual inconsistencies that denote incorrect actions.

By pinpointing the underexplored area of action-following in world models and identifying the practical limitations of current RL post-training paradigms for video, the authors carve out a clear niche for their contribution. They propose an elegant solution that combines the proven power of RL with the objective, verifiable signal derived from IDMs, thus pushing the boundaries of controllable and reliable generative world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The **primary objective** of this research is to enhance the action-following capability of pre-trained video world models. The authors aim to improve how accurately these models can translate human-specified actions into generated video sequences, making them more reliable and controllable simulators.

This objective is driven by several key **motivations**:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap:** While world models have made strides in visual fidelity and temporal consistency, the challenge of reliably executing *human-specified actions* remains significantly underexplored. Accurate action-following is fundamental for world models to serve as truly interactive and controllable simulators for intelligent agents (e.g., in robotics, gaming, or planning).
2. **Leveraging RL Success:** The remarkable success of reinforcement learning-based post-training in aligning large language models with human preferences and improving their reasoning capabilities provides a strong precedent. This suggests that RL could similarly benefit video world models by fine-tuning their behavior.
3. **Overcoming Practical Barriers to RL Post-Training for Video:** Directly applying existing RL post-training methods to video encounters two major practical impediments:
- **Prohibitive Cost of Annotation:** Collecting human preference annotations at the scale and granularity required for video data is extremely expensive and logistically challenging, in addition to being prone to subjective bias.
- **Infeasibility of Rule-Based Verifiers:** Unlike some narrow domains where rule-based verifiers can provide objective rewards (e.g., for coding or mathematical proofs), designing such verifiers to reliably assess the quality and action fidelity of complex, high-dimensional video content is generally infeasible.
4. **The "Inverse Reward" Insight:** The core motivation for the proposed solution, RLIR, stems from a crucial insight: "although multiple valid videos may correspond to the same action sequence, all high-quality generations must faithfully encode the input actions." This means that if a generated video accurately represents the intended actions, then those actions should be reliably recoverable from the video itself. This insight forms the basis for deriving an objective, verifiable, and scalable reward signal without human intervention or complex rule engineering.

In essence, the research aims to make world models truly controllable and reliable by developing a novel, practical, and effective method for post-training them to better follow actions, thereby bridging a critical gap in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards (RLIR)**, a post-training framework designed to improve the action-following capabilities of world models. The methodology revolves around using an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) to generate an objective, verifiable reward signal, which is then used to optimize the world model via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

The core methodological steps are as follows:

1. **Problem Formulation:** The world model's task is defined as generating a sequence of frames ($\hat{x}$) conditioned on an initial state ($x_0$) and a sequence of actions ($a_1, \dots, a_n$). The goal is to ensure these generated frames accurately reflect the input actions.

2. **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) as Reward Model:**
- **Function:** A pre-trained IDM estimates the action ($a_t$) that transitions from observation $o_t$ to $o_{t+1}$, effectively modeling $p_{\text{IDM}}(a_t | o_t, o_{t+1})$.
- **Training:** The IDM is trained on a dataset with ground-truth action annotations, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the true action given consecutive frames.
- **Key Property:** The IDM is shown to be highly sensitive to minor visual artifacts and subtle action variations (e.g., distinguishing 'forward' from 'sprint'), naturally penalizing lower-quality or inconsistent generations.
- **Reward Generation:** For a generated video trajectory $T_j = [x_0, \hat{x}_1, \dots, \hat{x}_n]$ corresponding to ground-truth actions $a_1, \dots, a_n$:
 - The IDM takes the generated trajectory as input and predicts a sequence of "inverse actions" $\hat{a}_1, \dots, \hat{a}_n$.
 - A frame-level reward $r(\hat{a}_i, a_i)$ is calculated, which is 1 if the inferred action $\hat{a}_i$ matches the ground-truth action $a_i$, and 0 otherwise.
 - The total reward for the trajectory $R_{T_j}$ is the average of these frame-level rewards: $R_{T_j} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n r(\hat{a}_i, a_i)$.
- **Advantages:** This IDM-derived reward is objective, scalable (no human annotation), low-bias, and semantically aligned, addressing the limitations of human preference or pixel-level rewards.

3. **Reinforcement Learning Optimization with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
- **Algorithm Choice:** GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) is adopted, which is memory-efficient as it dispenses with a value network.
- **Advantage Estimation:** Given a context (e.g., initial state and input actions), GRPO samples a group of $G$ responses (generated videos) from the behavior policy. The advantage of each response is calculated by normalizing its reward within the group relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards in that group.
- **Objective Function:** Similar to PPO, GRPO uses a clipped objective to ensure policy updates are not too drastic, combined with a KL divergence penalty ($D_{\text{KL}}[p_\theta || p_{\text{ref}}]$) to prevent the policy from deviating too far from the reference (pre-trained) model.
- **Training Loop:** The world model's parameters ($\theta$) are optimized to maximize this GRPO objective, thereby improving the likelihood of generating videos that yield high inverse rewards.

4. **Application to Specific World Model Architectures:**
- **Autoregressive World Models (e.g., MineWorld):**
 - Utilizes a visual-action autoregressive Transformer.
 - States are encoded using a VQ-VAE, and actions are tokenized separately.
 - The model predicts the next token in a sequence of interleaved visual and action tokens.
 - A critical adaptation for RLIR: **loss masking for action tokens**. The policy-gradient objective is computed *only* on visual tokens generated by the world model, ignoring action tokens, which are external inputs. This prevents undesirable training dynamics from trying to optimize input actions.
- **Diffusion World Models (e.g., NFD):**
 - Leverages Diffusion Forcing for autoregressive denoising of future frames.
 - Uses an image-level tokenizer for frames and a linear layer with AdaLN-Zero for action conditioning.
 - The denoising dynamics are cast as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
 - Reward application: Reward $r(z_0, c)$ (derived from IDM prediction of the final generated frame $z_0$ and conditioning $c$) is applied *only at the final output timestep* ($t=0$), with zero reward at intermediate denoising steps.

This comprehensive approach leverages existing robust models (pre-trained world models and IDMs) and a proven RL algorithm (GRPO), ingeniously combining them to create a novel and effective post-training paradigm for controllable video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments consistently demonstrate that Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards (RLIR) significantly improves the performance of world models across various metrics and architectures.

1. **Quantitative Improvements in Action-Following and Visual Quality:**
- **Action-Following:** RLIR post-training leads to substantial improvements in action classification accuracy. Across both autoregressive (MineWorld) and diffusion (NFD) paradigms, and varying parameter scales (300M to 1.2B), models show consistent **5%–10% gains in F1, recall, and precision scores**. Notably, the post-trained models nearly achieve the theoretical upper bound of the IDM's accuracy for ground-truth videos.
- **Visual Quality:** Beyond action-following, RLIR also yields improvements in visual quality metrics. Models show **up to 10% improvements in FVD (lower is better), PSNR (higher is better), and VBench image quality scores**. This indicates that improving action fidelity does not come at the cost of visual realism but rather enhances it, likely because better action-following necessitates more consistent and artifact-free video generation.
- **Consistency across Architectures and Scales:** The effectiveness of RLIR is demonstrated across both autoregressive (MineWorld) and diffusion (NFD) generative paradigms, as well as different model parameter scales, suggesting its broad applicability.

2. **Qualitative Improvements:**
- Visual comparisons clearly illustrate that RLIR post-training resolves issues present in baseline models. Examples include:
 - Accurate depiction of digging actions, resolving mismatches in excavation progress.
 - Improved fine-grained distance perception, leading to better alignment of character positions with the ground truth.
 - Mitigation of localized pixel blur under rapid movement, resulting in sharper and more consistent video.
- These qualitative observations align directly with the quantitative improvements in both action-following and visual quality.

3. **Superior Human Preference Scores:**
- Human evaluations, where evaluators scored outputs based on action-following ability and visual quality, consistently showed a clear and significant preference for RLIR-post-trained models over their baseline counterparts. This validates that the automatic metrics align with human perception of quality and controllability.

4. **Effectiveness of Inverse Rewards vs. Alternatives:**
- An ablation study comparing RLIR with alternative reward functions demonstrated its superior performance:
 - **Human Preference Reward (e.g., VideoAlign):** Showed limited effectiveness due to inherent biases and noise from human evaluators, and their difficulty in discerning subtle motion differences in videos.
 - **Pixel-Level Verifiable Reward (e.g., L1+LPIPS):** Also yielded no consistent improvements. The authors attributed this to:
 - High correlation with pre-training objectives, limiting effective RL exploration.
 - Uniform weighting of all pixels, neglecting the importance of action-relevant regions.
 - Conflict with the generative objective, as pixel-level rewards penalize genuinely novel (but valid) content lacking ground truth.
 - Susceptibility to "reward hacking" (e.g., models uniformly darkening frames to reduce L1/LPIPS loss in dark datasets).
- RLIR, by contrast, provides a precise, semantically aligned frame-level reward signal that is robust to such issues and better suited for the task.

5. **Hyperparameter Ablations:**
- For autoregressive models (MineWorld), the KL penalty was found to yield measurable gains primarily for smaller models, suggesting that larger models might be more stable or less prone to catastrophic forgetting.
- For diffusion models (NFD), the optimal number of denoising steps was identified as 10–20, with diminishing returns for more steps. A noise level between 0.5 and 0.75 proved effective, with lower levels diminishing gains.

In summary, the results conclusively establish RLIR as a highly effective post-training framework that significantly improves both the action-following fidelity and the visual quality of state-of-the-art video world models, surpassing other reward definition strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Reinforcement Learning with Inverse Rewards (RLIR) represents a significant advancement in the field of controllable generative AI and world modeling, with several notable aspects of significance and potential impact:

1. **Pioneering Post-Training for Action-Following:** RLIR stands out as the **first post-training method specifically designed to enhance action-following ability** in video world models. This directly addresses a critical and previously underexplored challenge, moving world models closer to becoming truly reliable and interactive simulators. This focused approach distinguishes it from broader efforts in improving visual fidelity or temporal consistency.

2. **Solves the Reward Problem for Video RL:** The framework elegantly bypasses the major practical hurdles of applying RL post-training to high-dimensional video data: the prohibitive cost of human preference annotations and the infeasibility of crafting rule-based video verifiers. By leveraging the inherent structure of world models (mapping actions to observations) and the capabilities of Inverse Dynamics Models, RLIR provides an **objective, scalable, and verifiable reward signal**. This breakthrough unlocks the potential of RL for fine-tuning video generative models in ways previously deemed impractical.

3. **Enhances Controllability and Reliability of World Models:** By significantly improving the accuracy with which world models execute specified actions, RLIR makes these models much more dependable and controllable. This is paramount for their application in critical domains such as:
- **Robotics:** More precise action execution within simulated environments allows for safer and more efficient policy learning and validation before deployment in the physical world.
- **Interactive AI and Gaming:** Enables more responsive and realistic interactions in virtual environments, improving agent training, content generation, and user experience.
- **Planning and Decision-Making:** Agents operating within world models can rely on more accurate predictions of action outcomes, leading to better planning and decision-making capabilities.

4. **Broader Impact on Generative Model Alignment:** The core concept of "inverse rewards"—deriving a reward signal by inverting the generative process back to the control input—is highly generalizable. This methodology could inspire similar approaches for aligning other generative models (beyond video) where verifying the fidelity of generated output to its conditioning input is challenging but crucial. This could include text-to-image, text-to-3D, or other multimodal generative tasks where direct human feedback is too expensive or rule-based verification is elusive.

5. **Synergistic Improvement of Action-Following and Visual Quality:** The finding that improving action-following also leads to better visual quality is significant. It suggests a fundamental connection between a model's understanding of dynamics and its ability to generate realistic and consistent visuals. An action that is accurately depicted will inherently lead to a more temporally consistent and artifact-free video, reinforcing the idea that semantic correctness underpins visual quality in dynamic generation tasks.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The authors acknowledge that the performance of RLIR is bounded by the accuracy of the underlying IDM, which is not perfect. Additionally, the largest model used (1.2 billion parameters) might not fully exploit the theoretical upper bound of RLIR's potential due to computational and data constraints. Future work will naturally focus on evaluating RLIR's scalability with even larger world models and expanding its application to other world models and modalities beyond video, further solidifying its impact and broadening its reach within the field of artificial intelligence.

In conclusion, RLIR offers a robust, scalable, and effective solution to a critical problem in world modeling, paving the way for more controllable, reliable, and ultimately more useful generative AI systems.
