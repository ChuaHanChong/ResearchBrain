# Harnessing Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models for Face Anti-Spoofing

> [!summary] Summary
> A new large-scale multimodal dataset, FaceCoT, featuring Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations, is introduced alongside a CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) strategy, enabling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for robust and interpretable Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS). This approach yielded an average AUC increase of 4.06% and HTER reduction of 5.00% across 11 benchmark datasets, demonstrating enhanced cross-domain generalization and explicit decision rationales.

## Problem

- Most existing Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS) methods rely on single visual modalities, leading to limited generalization across diverse presentation attacks, devices, and environmental conditions.
- Traditional FAS models provide only binary classifications without explicit rationales, restricting their interpretability and trustworthiness in security-sensitive applications.
- A lack of high-quality, domain-specific image-text multimodal datasets with structured reasoning chains hinders the effective training and application of MLLMs for FAS tasks.

## Method

- Constructed **FaceCoT**, the first large-scale ==Visual Question Answering== (VQA) dataset for FAS, featuring 1.08 million samples with hierarchical Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations covering 14 attack types, initially generated by GPT-4o and refined by experts.
- Developed a specialized FAS caption model, expanded with a rule-verifiable Reinforcement Learning (RL) strategy, to generate high-quality CoT annotations, creating FaceCoT-Silver982K from unlabeled images.
- Proposed a CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) strategy for MLLMs, involving initial visual enhancement pre-training with CoT data, followed by multi-task joint training with both CoT annotations and binary labels to mitigate task interference.

## Results

- Achieved an average Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC) increase of **4.06%** and Half Total Error Rate (HTER) reduction of **5.00%** compared to previous methods across 11 benchmark datasets, setting new state-of-the-art performance.
- Demonstrated enhanced cross-domain generalization, improving previous best AUC by approximately **10%** on HKBU-MARs-V1+ and **14%** on HiFiMask for unseen attack modalities, indicating robustness to novel spoofing scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of CoT data, showing an HTER of **7.65%** and AUC of **96.59%** with CoT, outperforming binary-label-only training (HTER **9.07%**, AUC **95.05%**), and the CEPL strategy improved AUC by **0.68%** and HTER by **1.19%** over single-stage multi-task joint training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chain-of-Thought annotations provide richer intermediate information, significantly enhancing task understanding, generalization, and robustness of MLLMs for FAS tasks, enabling systematic reasoning analysis.
> - The two-stage CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) strategy effectively manages task interference between reasoning and classification, fostering synergistic development of fine-grained visual feature extraction and high-level linguistic reasoning.
> - Integrating visual and linguistic co-inference through MLLMs, supported by CoT data, substantially improves cross-domain generalization capabilities against diverse and previously unseen spoofing attack types.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS) is a component of face recognition systems designed to detect presentation attacks, such as printed photos, screen replays, and 3D masks. Historically, most FAS methods have relied on a single visual modality (e.g., RGB, infrared, or depth images) for detection. These single-modality approaches often exhibit limitations in their generalization capabilities across various devices, environmental conditions, and diverse attack types. Additionally, they typically do not provide explicit rationales for their decisions, which can restrict their interpretability in practical applications.

In parallel, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have advanced capabilities in integrating image and text information, performing visual question answering (VQA), and executing semantic reasoning tasks. These models demonstrate an ability to engage in causal reasoning and semantic interpretation, suggesting a potential utility for FAS tasks. A multimodal approach combining visual and linguistic co-inference could offer a novel direction for enhancing FAS robustness and interpretability.

However, a principal challenge to applying MLLMs in the FAS domain has been the absence of high-quality, domain-specific image-text multimodal datasets. Existing public FAS datasets primarily provide visual inputs (images or videos) paired with binary "real" or "fake" labels, lacking the structured linguistic information, such as reasoning chains, that MLLMs require for effective training and to generate interpretable outputs. Training MLLMs directly on these limited datasets may lead to issues like overfitting and fail to leverage the models' reasoning potential for improved interpretability.

This research addresses this gap by introducing FaceCoT, a large-scale Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset specifically designed for FAS, incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations. It also proposes a CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) strategy to effectively utilize this data for MLLM training. This work aims to bridge the gap between MLLM capabilities and the specific requirements of FAS, moving beyond single-modality, black-box systems towards more robust, generalized, and interpretable multimodal FAS solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness, generalization, and interpretability of Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS) systems by integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This overarching goal is driven by several motivations related to the limitations of existing FAS methods and the potential of MLLMs.

One key motivation stems from the generalization limitations of current FAS models. Most existing approaches predominantly rely on a single visual modality, which has been observed to restrict their performance when confronted with diverse presentation attacks (such as print attacks, screen replays, and 3D masks) or variations in devices and environmental conditions. MLLMs, with their capacity for integrating visual and linguistic information and performing semantic reasoning, are hypothesized to offer a pathway to overcome these generalization issues through large-scale pre-training.

Another motivation centers on the need for improved interpretability in FAS. Conventional FAS methods typically provide a binary classification (real or spoof) without offering any explicit rationale or explanation for their decisions. In security-sensitive applications, understanding the decision-making process of an AI system is important for trustworthiness, auditing, and debugging. MLLMs, with their capability for strong visual-to-text alignment and language reasoning, are posited as suitable for generating clear decision rationales, thereby enhancing the interpretability of FAS predictions.

A significant bottleneck identified by the authors is the absence of high-quality multimodal datasets suitable for training MLLMs in the FAS domain. Publicly available FAS datasets generally contain only image or video data with simple binary labels, lacking the structured linguistic information (e.g., descriptions of features, reasoning steps) that MLLMs require to learn complex reasoning patterns. This data scarcity can lead to suboptimal MLLM training, including overfitting, and hinders the development of models that can provide explicit reasoning chains.

To address these motivations and challenges, the research outlines three specific objectives:
1. **Develop a large-scale, high-quality multimodal dataset for FAS:** This involves creating FaceCoT, presented as the first Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset tailored for FAS. FaceCoT is designed to include Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations that guide models through a human-like reasoning process, covering a wide range of 14 spoofing attack types. The dataset aims to provide structured visual CoT question-answering processes to improve detection accuracy and generalization.
2. **Propose an effective learning strategy to leverage CoT data:** The authors aim to introduce a CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) strategy. This strategy is designed to enable MLLMs to fully exploit the fine-grained visual cues embedded in CoT annotations by balancing CoT reasoning with binary classification tasks, thereby strengthening the visual encoder's ability to extract spoof-relevant features.
3. **Demonstrate and evaluate enhanced performance and interpretability:** A final objective is to provide empirical evidence that models trained with the FaceCoT dataset and utilizing the CEPL strategy outperform existing FAS methods in terms of detection accuracy and cross-domain generalization. Furthermore, the work seeks to illustrate the improved interpretability offered by the CoT-trained models through their ability to generate explicit decision rationales.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research comprises three main components: the construction of the FaceCoT dataset, the development of a specialized FAS caption model for dataset expansion, and the implementation of a CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) strategy for model training.

#### 3.1. FaceCoT Dataset Construction

The objective was to create a large-scale Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations specifically for Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS).
- **Data Collection:** Images were sourced from two existing FAS datasets: CelebA-Spoof and Wild-FAS (WFAS). CelebA-Spoof provided 625,000 images with 10 spoofing types, and WFAS contributed 1.38 million images with 14 attack types captured in unconstrained environments. This combination was selected for diversity and realism.
- **Data Selection for FaceCoT-Gold100K:** Samples were selected from the source datasets and divided into four categories: live, replay, print, and mask, with an initial target of 25,000 samples per category to ensure balance. Live-face images were exclusively drawn from WFAS to include real-world scenes. Spoofing categories were exhaustively sampled to include multiple attack styles and subtypes (e.g., seven subtypes for print attacks), aiming for an approximately equal number of samples per subtype.
- **CoT Structure Design:** A hierarchical "global-to-local" reasoning path, mimicking human authenticity judgment, was designed. This path consists of six modules:
 1. **Caption:** A holistic description of the image for global and environmental context.
 2. **Facial Description:** Focuses on describing facial features.
 3. **Facial Attributes:** Describes fine-grained facial features, textures, and expressions.
 4. **Reasoning:** Combines multi-scale information for comprehensive analysis of spoofing behavior.
 5. **Spoofing Description:** Details spoofing features and methods to enhance interpretability.
 6. **Conclusion:** Provides a final binary judgment (Yes/No).
 The annotations were standardized using a `<Tag>...</Tag>` format to ensure clear, structured input.
- **GPT-4o Annotation:** GPT-4o was employed to generate initial CoT annotations. System prompts guided the model to act as an "examiner," adhere to the six-module structure, and ensure the final conclusion matched the ground-truth label. Specific hints were provided for various spoofing types to assist the model in establishing decision boundaries.
- **Hard Case Handling:** Annotations were checked by comparing the extracted `<Conclusion>` with ground-truth labels. Samples that failed after two rounds of GPT-4o annotation were designated as "hard cases" (581 instances). These hard cases were manually reviewed and corrected by expert annotators to ensure logical coherence and accuracy, resulting in FaceCoT-Gold100K, a subset of 100,000 high-quality CoT annotations.
- **Data Expansion (FaceCoT-Silver982K):** To expand the dataset, a specialized FAS caption model was trained on FaceCoT-Gold100K using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). To address issues of semantic and format errors in auto-generated annotations on unseen images, a rule-verifiable Reinforcement Learning (RL) strategy was integrated.
 - **Reward Functions:** A dual-reward scheme was designed: an accuracy reward (1 if `<Conclusion>` matches ground-truth, 0 otherwise) and a format compliance reward (1 if output follows FaceCoT template, 0 otherwise).
 - **Training Strategy:** The policy model was initialized with SFT pre-training. Rewards guided RL updates, with a KL divergence penalty to prevent the policy from drifting from the initial semantic space.
 - **Data:** Unlabeled images from the target annotation corpus were used during the RL stage.
 - The RL-optimized caption model achieved 99.6% accuracy (compared to 88% for SFT-only) and was used to generate FaceCoT-Silver982K, adding 982,000 high-quality annotations. The combined FaceCoT dataset totals 1.08 million samples.

#### 3.2. CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) Strategy

This strategy was designed to effectively leverage the FaceCoT data by mitigating task interference between reasoning and classification.
- **Visual Enhancement Pre-training (Stage 1):** The MLLM's full parameters were fine-tuned using only CoT annotations (Supervised Fine-Tuning). This stage aimed to focus the vision encoder on extracting fine-grained, spoof-relevant facial features by aligning visual features with linguistic descriptions, creating a robust visual foundation. MiniCPMV-2.6-8B was used as the backbone VLM.
- **Multi-task Joint Training (Stage 2):** The vision encoder from Stage 1 was retained. The connector and language decoder modules were reset to their original pre-trained weights. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) modules were applied to the Large Language Model (LLM) component for targeted fine-tuning. Training proceeded jointly on CoT annotations and binary labels using a multi-task loss, aiming to achieve synergistic enhancement of reasoning and classification.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Implementation Details:** Images were resized to 448 × 448 × 3 RGB. The AdamW optimizer was used (learning rate 1e-6, weight decay 0.1). Training ran for up to 10 epochs with a global batch size of 256 across 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Experiments were run with three different random seeds, and averaged results were reported.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Half Total Error Rate (HTER) and Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC) were used. VLM outputs ("Yes" or "No" tokens) were converted to continuous confidence scores by extracting token-level logits and applying a softmax function to estimate the probability of a sample being real.
- **Datasets:** FaceCoT served as the source domain. Cross-domain testing was performed on 11 target benchmark datasets: MSU-MFSD, CASIA-MFSD, Idiap Replay Attack, OULU-NPU, SIW, Rose-Youtu, HKBU-MARs-V1+, WMCA, SIW-M-V2, CASIA-SURF-3DMask, and HiFiMask. An additional comparison was made by training only on an annotated CelebA-Spoof subset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance of models trained with FaceCoT and the CEPL strategy.

- **Overall Performance Improvements:** Models trained with the full FaceCoT dataset ("Ours-All") demonstrated an average Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC) increase of 4.06% and a Half Total Error Rate (HTER) reduction of 5.00% compared to previous methods across 11 benchmark datasets. The method achieved the highest performance across all examined evaluation sets.
- **Cross-Domain Generalization:** The approach exhibited robustness to unseen attack modalities. For instance, on the HKBU-MARs-V1+ and HiFiMask datasets, which contain spoofing types not explicitly in the source data (e.g., transparent, plaster, resin masks), the method improved the previous best AUC by approximately 10% and 14%, respectively. This indicates a capacity for generalizing to novel attack scenarios.
- **Performance with Limited Source Data:** When training was restricted to the CelebA-Spoof dataset annotated by the FAS caption model ("Ours-CelebA"), the model still improved upon previous methods, showing an average HTER decrease of 2.74% and an AUC increase of 2.66%. This suggests the effectiveness of CoT annotations and the CEPL strategy even under conditions of more limited source data.
- **Effectiveness of CEPL Strategy (Ablation Study):** An ablation study demonstrated that the CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) method outperformed a single-stage Multi-task Joint Training (MJT) approach. CEPL showed an increase of 0.68% in AUC and a decrease of 1.19% in HTER. This indicates that the two-stage progressive learning process assists the model in managing task interference between reasoning and classification, contributing to improved overall performance. The study also found that applying additional Reinforcement Learning (RL) after CEPL did not yield notable further performance gains, suggesting that the benefits of RL are more pronounced when supervised fine-tuning data is limited.
- **Impact of CoT Data (Ablation Study):** Comparing training with CoT data against training with only binary-labeled data revealed that CoT data contributes to improved model performance. The model trained with CoT data achieved an HTER of 7.65% and an AUC of 96.59%, which surpassed the performance of the model trained without CoT annotations (HTER 9.07%, AUC 95.05%). This suggests that CoT data provides richer intermediate information, enhancing task understanding, generalization, and robustness. Furthermore, the CoT-trained model provided systematic reasoning analyses, demonstrating its interpretability.
- **Impact of Resolution (Ablation Study):** An investigation into input resolution indicated that a higher resolution (448 × 448) contributed to better performance (HTER 8.84%, AUC 95.91%) compared to a lower resolution (224 × 224, HTER 11.28%, AUC 94.05%). This suggests that increased resolution enables the model to capture more fine-grained detailed features, which are relevant for FAS.
- **Supplementary Findings (from Supplementary Material):**
 - **Cross-Domain Protocol:** Under a widely adopted leave-one-out cross-domain protocol on four standard benchmarks, the method achieved an average HTER of 1.06% and AUC of 99.85%, showing an advantage over previous methods.
 - **Spoof Type Robustness:** A fine-grained analysis on the Rose-Youtu dataset indicated consistent performance improvements across all seven attack categories after fine-tuning with FaceCoT, demonstrating a general enhancement of spoof detection capabilities.
 - **Zero-shot vs. CoT-trained Models:** Zero-shot performance of prominent VLMs (MiniCPMV-2.6-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B) was lower than supervised methods. After CoT-based fine-tuning, both VLMs exhibited substantial performance gains, confirming the stable benefits of FaceCoT and CEPL across different VLM architectures.
 - **RL on Annotation Quality:** The integration of Reinforcement Learning during the caption model's training improved the quality of CoT annotations, leading to better model performance when these annotations were used for training.
 - **FaceCoT under Low-Resolution:** At a resolution of 224x224, training with CoT data still provided substantial gains (6.70% HTER reduction, 4.61% AUC increase) compared to binary-label-only training, suggesting CoT annotations help recover fine-grained cues at lower resolutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several areas of significance and potential impact within the field of Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS) and broader AI applications.

One significant aspect is the direct addressing of limitations prevalent in current FAS systems. By moving beyond single-modality visual analysis, the work endeavors to mitigate issues of limited generalization across diverse attack types, environments, and devices. The demonstrated improvements in cross-domain performance suggest a step towards more robust FAS systems applicable in varied real-world scenarios.

The introduction of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for FAS using Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) represents a new methodological direction. This approach offers a departure from traditional black-box classification models by providing explicit, step-by-step rationales for decisions. This enhanced interpretability is valuable in security-critical applications, as it can foster trust, enable better auditing, and facilitate debugging of FAS systems.

The FaceCoT dataset is a specific contribution. As the first large-scale Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset with detailed CoT annotations for FAS, it provides a new resource for the research community. This dataset, covering 1.08 million samples across 14 attack types, has the potential to accelerate research in multimodal FAS, the application of MLLMs in security contexts, and the development of more interpretable AI models. Its structured annotation format is designed to guide models towards human-like reasoning.

Methodologically, the CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL) strategy offers an optimized approach for training MLLMs on complex multimodal data with reasoning chains. This two-stage learning process provides insights into how to effectively manage multi-task learning objectives, ensuring that both fine-grained visual feature extraction and high-level reasoning capabilities are developed synergistically. The integration of reinforcement learning for scalable, high-quality data expansion also represents a methodological advancement in dataset creation.

The empirical results, including performance improvements across multiple benchmark datasets and enhanced generalization, support the efficacy of the proposed dataset and learning strategy. These findings suggest that the integration of multimodal reasoning can lead to more accurate and reliable FAS.

From a broader perspective, the open-source release of the FaceCoT dataset could serve as a foundation for building more trustworthy FAS systems. Beyond predictive accuracy, the dataset's design, which incorporates explicit reasoning chains, has the potential to act as a benchmark for evaluating model interpretability and the coherence of generated explanations. This aligns with the broader objectives of responsible AI development. Furthermore, the methodologies for creating structured CoT datasets and applying progressive learning could be transferable to other domains where robust, interpretable decision-making from multimodal input is critical, such as medical diagnostics or other security applications.
