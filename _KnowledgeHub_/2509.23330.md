# Structured In-context Environment Scaling for Large Language Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Structured In-context Environment (SIE) framework from Shanghai Jiao Tong University automates the construction of scalable and verifiable reasoning environments from structured data like knowledge graphs. Fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) in SIEs yielded average performance gains of 34.4% on WebQSP and 62.6% on GrailQA, and demonstrated robust generalization to out-of-domain tasks such as mathematical and logical reasoning with up to 20.4% improvement on GSM8K.

## Problem

- Existing LLM reasoning environments, such as those for mathematics or coding, struggle with scalability due to their reliance on expensive expert annotations.
- Game-based environments, while scalable and verifiable, often yield specialized reasoning skills that exhibit limited generalization to broader domains.
- A critical challenge lies in automating the construction of high-quality LLM reasoning environments that simultaneously offer scalability, generalizable reasoning, and verifiability.

## Method

- The Structured In-context Environment (**SIE**) framework models LLM exploration as ==implicit actions== within a structured context embedded in the prompt, using an external verifier to provide reward signals for RL fine-tuning.
- SIE automatically constructs reasoning environments from large-scale structured data (e.g., Freebase Knowledge Graph), leveraging multi-hop knowledge graph question answering (KGQA) tasks.
- The construction pipeline involves seed subgraph retrieval, supporting subgraph extraction, and distractor subgraph filtering to create varied and challenging reasoning contexts.

## Results

- RL fine-tuning with SIEs achieved consistent and substantial performance improvements in structured reasoning across various LLMs, with average gains of **34.4%** on WebQSP, **50.2%** on CWQ, and **62.6%** on GrailQA.
- RL fine-tuning in SIEs was more efficient, providing an additional performance gain exceeding **40%** compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning with Structured Reasoning Data (SFT w/ SRD).
- Reasoning skills learned in SIEs generalized to out-of-domain tasks, showing average improvements of **20.4%** on GSM8K, **18.1%** on MATH500, **12.3%** on KK-easy, and **11.1%** on KK-hard.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing a structured environment as context during RL fine-tuning is crucial for eliciting and enhancing LLM structured reasoning capabilities, outperforming both Chain-of-Thought prompting and supervised fine-tuning on structured reasoning data.
> - The compositional reasoning strategies learned within SIEs are highly generalizable, transferring effectively to diverse out-of-domain tasks like mathematical and logical reasoning.
> - LLMs can achieve robust reasoning and generalization even when fine-tuned in partial or highly incomplete SIEs, suggesting adaptation from shallow context retrieval to deeper compositional reasoning by exploring and inferring information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning has seen considerable advancements, particularly through reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning. This post-training paradigm has been instrumental in enabling LLMs to acquire complex reasoning strategies such as self-reflection, backtracking, and chain-of-thought, leading to progress in areas like mathematical reasoning, code generation, and interactive agent development.

However, existing research has primarily focused on algorithmic optimizations for RL fine-tuning, with comparatively less attention given to the design and properties of the training environment itself. The intrinsic characteristics of an environment directly influence the capabilities that an LLM can learn and internalize. Current LLM training environments typically fall into two categories, each with limitations:
- **Internalized-rule environments:** Examples include mathematics and coding tasks, where underlying structures are implicitly learned during pre-training. Their primary limitation is scalability, as their construction heavily relies on expensive expert annotations.
- **Externalized-rule environments:** These are often game-based simulations with explicit rules. While providing clear feedback, the skills acquired from such environments tend to be highly specialized and exhibit limited generalization to broader reasoning domains.

This work addresses a critical gap by proposing a framework for automatically constructing scalable, generalizable, and verifiable reasoning environments for LLMs. It departs from the conventional focus on algorithmic improvements by emphasizing the foundational role of environment design. Unlike concurrent research exploring tool use, symbolic reasoning, or NP-hard graph problems, this paper provides a formal definition of an ideal LLM reasoning environment and proposes a concrete method for its automated construction from structured data. Furthermore, it differentiates itself from existing approaches to structured reasoning (which often involve task decomposition or supervised fine-tuning on distilled reasoning chains) by advocating for RL training within dynamic, structured environments to cultivate more generalizable reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing LLM reasoning environments. The authors posit that an ideal environment for enhancing LLM reasoning should possess three core characteristics:
1. **Scalability:** The ability to construct large-scale, high-quality training environments automatically and cost-effectively from vast data sources.
2. **Generalizable Reasoning:** The capacity for learned reasoning strategies and cognitive patterns to transfer effectively to diverse, general-purpose reasoning domains.
3. **Verifiability:** The presence of clear, objective rules or mechanisms within the environment to reliably assess the correctness of an LLM's responses.

Existing environments, such as those for mathematics or coding, typically struggle with scalability due to their reliance on expert annotation. Conversely, game-based environments, while scalable and verifiable, often yield specialized skills that do not generalize broadly. This creates a critical challenge: how to automate the construction of high-quality LLM reasoning environments that simultaneously satisfy all three desiderata.

To bridge this gap, the authors explore the potential of leveraging massive structured data for environment construction. They are motivated by the inherent advantages of structured data, which include:
- **Abundance:** Real-world structured resources (e.g., knowledge graphs, tabular data) are widely available, facilitating automated and scalable environment generation through multi-hop retrieval and data composition.
- **Compositional Patterns:** Structured data often represents condensed human experience and domain knowledge, featuring rich compositional patterns that are conducive to learning generalizable reasoning.
- **Explicit Schemas and Constraints:** The explicit nature of structured data's schemas and constraints provides a natural foundation for rigorous, rule-based verification of facts and reasoning outcomes.

Therefore, the key objective of this work is to introduce the Structured In-context Environment (SIE) framework. This framework aims to:
- Automatically construct scalable and high-quality reasoning environments by extracting structured contexts from large-scale structured data sources.
- Enable LLMs to learn generalizable compositional reasoning skills through reinforcement learning fine-tuning within these environments.
- Systematically investigate the effectiveness and efficiency of RL fine-tuning on SIEs, particularly by varying the environmental complexity and the amount of available context information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose the Structured In-context Environment (SIE) framework, which models LLM exploration as implicit actions within a structured context embedded directly in the prompt. This context acts as a soft constraint, and the LLM's output is used to derive reward signals for RL fine-tuning.

The SIE framework is instantiated using multi-hop knowledge graph question answering (KGQA) tasks and their underlying Knowledge Graphs (KGs). In this setup, a correct answer is associated with a specific subgraph containing the complete reasoning path, which serves as the structured context. The LLM's interaction is conceptualized as an implicit Markov Decision Process (MDP), where states correspond to explored subgraphs, actions involve selecting entities for further exploration, and rewards are provided by an external verifier based on the LLM's response.

The automated SIE construction pipeline comprises four steps:

1. **Seed Subgraph Retrieval:** For each KGQA instance (question Q, answer A, question entity set E_Q, answer entity set E_A), the pipeline treats E_Q as seed nodes. It performs a bidirectional multi-hop retrieval from both E_Q and E_A on the original KG (Freebase). By enforcing that the sum of hops from both directions equals the maximum task hop (`n_hop`), this strategy efficiently generates an initial `G_seed` (seed subgraph), significantly reducing its size compared to a naive breadth-first search.

2. **Supporting Subgraph Extraction:** From `G_seed`, the pipeline extracts `G_support`, which consists of all valid reasoning paths connecting E_Q to E_A. It retains all question entities and the top ten correct answers, then applies Dijkstra's algorithm to find all shortest paths within the `n_hop` limit. This process ensures `G_support` contains essential knowledge while remaining manageable. Instances where no supporting subgraph can be found are retained to study the impact of environmental incompleteness.

3. **Distractor Subgraph Filtering:** The remaining triples after removing `G_support` from `G_seed` initially form a large distractor subgraph (`G_distract`). To manage LLM context length limits, a two-stage semantic filtering process is applied using the `ms-marco-MiniLM-L12-v2` cross-encoder for reranking:
 - **Relation Filtering:** Relations from the initial `G_distract` are semantically compared to the question Q, and only top-ranking relations (`rel_retain`) are kept.
 - **Triple Filtering:** Only triples containing `rel_retain` are considered, and their semantic similarity to Q is calculated to select the top-ranking triples for the final `G_distract`. This method aims to generate challenging yet contextually relevant distractors.

4. **Constructing Partial SIEs:** The filtered `G_support` and `G_distract` triples are merged and randomly shuffled to form the Structured In-context Environment (SIE). Each sample includes the question Q, the structured in-context SI, and the answer A. To investigate the effects of varying difficulty and incomplete information, a series of partial SIEs are constructed by controlling the retention ratio of `G_support` (100%, 75%, 50%, 25%, 0%). The size of `G_distract` is adjusted accordingly to maintain a constant total context length, creating environments ranging from complete to progressively more incomplete.

**RL Post-training:**
The authors employ the GRPO algorithm for RL fine-tuning, which is chosen for its efficiency by eliminating the need for a separate critic model and using group relative scoring for advantage calculation. The LLM (policy `π_θ`) takes the question and structured in-context `x = (Q, SI)` as input and generates a response.
A modified DeepSeek-R1 prompt template guides the model to perform step-by-step reasoning within `<think>` tags and place the final answer in `<answer>` tags.
The reward mechanism includes:
- **Answer Reward:** A reward of 1.0 for an exact match between the extracted answer and the ground truth, and 0.0 otherwise.
- **Format Reward:** An additional positive reward is given for adhering to the specified thinking and answer paradigm. This rule-based system aims to guide the LLM towards learning compositional reasoning without reward hacking.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Datasets:** SIE instances are built using the Freebase KG, with question-answering pairs from WebQSP and CWQ for training.
- **Test Sets:** In-domain structured reasoning is evaluated on WebQSP, CWQ, and GrailQA (for in-domain generalization). Out-of-domain generalization is tested on mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH500) and logical reasoning (Knights and Knaves: KK-easy, KK-hard). All evaluations use strict zero-shot settings with pass@1 as the metric.
- **Baselines:** Include Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, RL fine-tuning without context (`RL w/o Context`), and Supervised Fine-Tuning with Structured Reasoning Data (`SFT w/ SRD`).
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct (instruction-tuned), Qwen2.5-7B, and Qwen3-8B (base/instruction-tuned) are used.
- **Infrastructure:** The VeRL framework implements the RL post-training pipeline.
- **Analysis:** Additional experiments assess the framework's compatibility with other RL algorithms (REINFORCE++, PPO), the impact of starting RL from an SFT checkpoint, the importance of verifiable feedback through ablation studies on reward mechanisms, and a decomposition of performance sources.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments address four main research questions, demonstrating the effectiveness, efficiency, and generalizability of the SIE framework.

**RQ1: The SIE Framework Effectively Enhances LLM Structured Reasoning.**
- Comparison between `RL w/o Context` and `RL w/ SIE` (Table 1) shows consistent and substantial performance improvements across all tested LLMs (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-8B) on structured reasoning tasks (WebQSP, CWQ, GrailQA).
- Average performance gains: 34.4% on WebQSP, 50.2% on CWQ, and 62.6% on GrailQA. This indicates that providing a structured environment as context during RL fine-tuning is crucial for eliciting structured reasoning capabilities.

**RQ2: RL Fine-tuning in SIE is More Efficient than SFT on SRD.**
- Comparing `CoT`, `SFT w/ SRD`, and `RL w/ SIE` (Table 2) reveals that both fine-tuning approaches outperform `CoT`.
- `SFT w/ SRD` achieved a modest average improvement of approximately 11.4% in structured reasoning.
- `RL w/ SIE` resulted in a significantly greater average improvement of approximately 53.7%.
- Crucially, `RL w/ SIE` provided an additional performance gain exceeding 40% across all three structured reasoning tasks compared to `SFT w/ SRD`. This suggests that the environmental exploration facilitated by RL in SIEs is more effective than imitation learning via SFT for structured reasoning.

**RQ3: Structured Reasoning Learned in SIEs Generalizes to Out-of-Domain Reasoning Domains.**
- Evaluation on out-of-domain mathematical (GSM8K, MATH500) and logical (KK-easy, KK-hard) reasoning tasks (Table 3) shows that LLMs fine-tuned by `RL w/ SIE` achieve better generalization performance compared to `CoT` prompting.
- Average improvements: 20.4% on GSM8K, 18.1% on MATH500, 12.3% on KK-easy, and 11.1% on KK-hard. These results indicate that the compositional reasoning strategies acquired within the structured environments are transferable to general reasoning domains.

**RQ4: RL in Partial SIEs Achieves Robust Reasoning and Generalization Performance.**
- **Robustness of Structured Reasoning (Table 4):** All LLMs showed positive improvements in structured reasoning across partial SIEs (from SIE-100% to SIE-0%). Performance decreased with increasing environmental difficulty (less supporting information), but even in highly challenging scenarios (SIE-25% and SIE-0%), `RL w/ SIE` still yielded substantial improvements. This suggests that LLMs adapted their reasoning from shallow context retrieval to deeper compositional reasoning by exploring and composing information within the environment.
- **Robustness of Generalization (Table 5):** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct demonstrated positive improvements in general reasoning ability across all tasks, even when fine-tuned in partial SIEs. This confirms the robust generalization of learned compositional structured reasoning to general reasoning domains. A case study illustrated a shift from hallucination to leveraging intrinsic parametric knowledge and inferring missing information in an information-limited (SIE-0%) scenario.

**Additional Analysis Findings:**
- **Applicability to Mainstream RL Algorithms (Table 6):** REINFORCE++ and GRPO exhibited comparable performance, while PPO also led to improvements, confirming the universality of the SIE framework across different RL algorithms.
- **RL Starting Checkpoint (Table 7):** Starting RL training from an SFT-fine-tuned checkpoint (`RL w/ SIE f/ SFT`) improved generalization compared to SFT alone, but it limited the maximal potential gains in structured reasoning compared to `RL w/ SIE` without a prior SFT step. This indicates a trade-off between refining existing reasoning and fostering novel environmental exploration.
- **Verifiable Environmental Feedback is Critical (Table 8):** Ablation studies showed that rewarding only format adherence (`Format Only`) yielded marginal gains, and introducing random rewards (`Random + Format`) caused performance collapse. This confirms that the observed performance improvements are driven by the model learning correct reasoning logic guided by verifiable structured feedback, not mere format adherence or gaming spurious signals.
- **Reasoning Evolution (Table 9):** A decomposition of performance gains revealed a stepwise evolution: `RL w/o Context` activated internal parametric knowledge for simpler questions; `RL w/ SIE-0%` (distractors only) provided negative constraints, guiding models to prune incorrect paths; and `RL w/ SIE-100%` (full context) integrated parametric knowledge with external evidence for complex multi-hop compositional reasoning.
- **Scalability and Tool-Using Agents (Appendix B):** The SIE framework scales effectively to larger models (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct outperformed its 7B counterpart, Table 14). Moreover, `RL w/ SIE` on 7B models significantly outperformed tool-using agents like Think-on-Graph (ToG) when applied to 7B models, and even matched GPT-3.5+ToG performance under information-limited conditions (Table 15). This suggests that SIE fosters the internalization of reasoning heuristics into model parameters, overcoming planning bottlenecks in smaller models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Structured In-context Environment (SIE) framework addresses a fundamental challenge in advancing LLM reasoning by providing an automated, scalable, and verifiable method for constructing training environments. By shifting the focus from solely optimizing RL algorithms to the design of the environment itself, this work highlights the crucial role of structured data in eliciting and shaping advanced reasoning capabilities.

The primary significance of this work lies in its demonstration that:
1. **Automated Environment Construction:** It offers a practical and scalable solution for generating high-quality reasoning environments from readily available structured data, bypassing the limitations of labor-intensive expert annotation.
2. **Generalizable Reasoning:** The framework enables LLMs to learn compositional reasoning patterns that are not only effective within the structured domain but also generalize significantly to diverse out-of-domain tasks such as mathematics, logic puzzles, and tabular data. This suggests that the learned skills represent fundamental cognitive paradigms rather than task-specific memorization.
3. **Robust Learning in Partial Information Settings:** The ability of LLMs to robustly improve and generalize even in information-constrained partial SIEs indicates that the framework encourages active exploration and inference of missing information, leading to deeper compositional reasoning rather than shallow retrieval.
4. **Efficiency over Supervised Methods:** The superior efficiency of RL fine-tuning in SIEs compared to supervised fine-tuning on distilled structured reasoning data underscores the benefits of interactive environmental feedback in skill acquisition.
5. **Empowering Smaller Models:** By internalizing complex reasoning heuristics, SIE can empower smaller LLMs to autonomously perform complex structured reasoning, potentially reducing reliance on larger, resource-intensive models or external tool-using agents.

The potential impact of this research is substantial. It could lead to the development of more capable and versatile LLMs that can handle complex, multi-hop reasoning problems across various domains. By providing a scalable approach to environment creation and demonstrating the generalizability of learned reasoning skills, this work lays a foundation for future research in building truly intelligent and adaptable AI systems. Moreover, the detailed analysis of reasoning evolution within SIEs offers valuable insights into how LLMs integrate parametric knowledge with external evidence and handle negative constraints, contributing to a deeper understanding of LLM cognitive processes.
