# Vision Transformer for Small-Size Datasets

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Inha University developed Shifted Patch Tokenization (SPT) and Locality Self-Attention (LSA) to enable Vision Transformers (ViTs) to learn efficiently from scratch on small-size datasets. These modules collectively enhanced ViT performance, achieving an average 2.96% Top-1 accuracy improvement on Tiny-ImageNet and making ViTs competitive with CNN baselines in data-constrained environments.

## Problem

- Initial Vision Transformers (ViTs) heavily depend on massive pre-training datasets due to their limited locality inductive bias.
- Standard ViT tokenization yields visual tokens with small, non-expanding receptive fields, providing insufficient local spatial information.
- The self-attention mechanism in ViTs often suffers from a 'smoothing phenomenon,' where attention scores are broadly distributed, hindering focus on critical local features.

## Method

- Introduces **Shifted Patch Tokenization (SPT)**, which generates and concatenates spatially shifted versions of the input image to expand the receptive field of visual tokens.
- Develops **Locality Self-Attention (LSA)** to sharpen attention distributions by employing ==diagonal masking== (preventing self-token attention) and ==learnable temperature scaling== for softmax.
- These modules are designed to be generic and additive, enhancing the ==locality inductive bias== of various ViT architectures without extensive redesign.

## Results

- Achieved consistent Top-1 accuracy improvements across small-size datasets, including an average **2.96%** gain on Tiny-ImageNet and a **4.00%** improvement for standard ViT (from **57.07%** to **61.07%**).
- Enhanced ViTs (e.g., SL-CaiT, SL-Swin) demonstrated performance competitive with or superior to CNN baselines (ResNet, EfficientNet) on most small datasets.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both SPT and LSA independently contribute to performance gains, with their combined application showing synergistic benefits with only a modest increase in inference latency (e.g., **1.06%** to **1.15%** overhead).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enhancing the locality inductive bias in both the tokenization and attention stages of ViTs is crucial for their effective performance on limited data.
> - Explicitly excluding self-token attention and dynamically adjusting the softmax temperature significantly improves the focus and discriminative power of the attention mechanism.
> - Expanding the spatial information embedded in visual tokens through shifted patch processing directly addresses the limited receptive field issue in standard ViTs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has historically been dominated by Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which have demonstrated effectiveness in learning visual representations for various tasks, including image classification. More recently, the Transformer architecture, originally developed for Natural Language Processing (NLP), has been adapted for vision tasks, leading to the emergence of Vision Transformers (ViTs). ViTs have shown the capability to outperform CNNs on large-scale image classification benchmarks.

However, a key characteristic of initial ViT models, such as the original Vision Transformer, is their dependence on extensive pre-training with massive datasets like JFT-300M. This requirement is attributed to ViTs possessing a lower locality inductive bias compared to CNNs, which inherently leverage local spatial information through their convolutional filters. The absence of strong inductive biases in early ViTs necessitates large amounts of data to learn robust visual representations from scratch.

Recognizing this limitation, subsequent research has focused on developing data-efficient ViTs capable of learning from scratch on mid-size datasets, such as ImageNet, without relying on gargantuan pre-training datasets. These efforts generally aim to introduce or enhance locality inductive bias within the Transformer architecture. Examples include models that adopt hierarchical structures similar to CNNs to capture multi-scale features (e.g., Swin Transformer, PiT, CvT), or those that modify the self-attention mechanism or tokenization process to incorporate more local information (e.g., T2T, CaiT, LocalViT). While these advancements have reduced the data requirements, learning ViTs from scratch on *small-size* datasets remains a significant challenge due to the inherent trade-off between dataset capacity and model performance.

This paper addresses this specific gap by focusing on enabling ViTs to learn effectively from scratch on small-scale datasets. It proposes modifications to the fundamental tokenization and self-attention mechanisms of ViTs to overcome their limitations in capturing local spatial relationships and focusing attention, thereby increasing their locality inductive bias sufficiently for resource-constrained scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Vision Transformers to learn effectively from scratch, without extensive pre-training, even when presented with small-size datasets. To achieve this, the authors identify two fundamental problems within standard ViT architectures that impede their performance on limited data:

1. **Poor Tokenization:** Standard ViTs divide an input image into non-overlapping patches of equal size, which are then linearly projected into visual tokens. While this tokenization maintains permutation invariance, a property beneficial for capturing relationships between patches, it results in a relatively small receptive field for each visual token. The receptive field of these tokens is determined solely by the patch size, and unlike CNNs, subsequent transformer encoder layers do not inherently expand this receptive field. For example, a ViT with a 16x16 patch size has visual tokens with a receptive field of 16x16 pixels, which is significantly smaller compared to the receptive fields of features in deeper layers of CNNs (e.g., 483x483 for ResNet50 on ImageNet). This limited receptive field means that each visual token embeds insufficient spatial information from its immediate neighborhood, thereby contributing to a low locality inductive bias and hindering the ViT's ability to learn intricate local patterns from small datasets.

2. **Poor Attention Mechanism (Smoothing Phenomenon):** Image data typically has a much higher feature dimension than natural language, leading to a large number of embedded tokens. In such scenarios, the distribution of attention scores within the self-attention mechanism tends to become overly smooth. This smoothing phenomenon prevents ViTs from focusing sharply on important visual tokens, resulting in highly redundant attention. The authors observe that this lack of focused attention often causes ViTs to concentrate on background elements rather than accurately capturing the shape and features of the target object. Two specific causes for this smoothing are identified:
 - **Dominance of Self-Token Relations:** Since Query and Key vectors are derived from the same input tokens, self-token relations (dot products of a token with itself) tend to be larger than inter-token relations. After the softmax function, this often results in relatively high attention scores for self-tokens and lower, less discriminative scores for other tokens.
 - **High Temperature in Softmax:** The standard practice of dividing the similarity matrix by the square root of the Key dimension ($\sqrt{d_k}$) before applying softmax is intended to prevent vanishing gradients. However, this division can inadvertently act as a high temperature in the softmax function, further smoothing the attention score distribution and diminishing the model's capacity to identify and focus on truly relevant local features.

By addressing these two issues, the research aims to significantly enhance the locality inductive bias of ViTs, thereby enabling them to effectively learn from scratch on small-size datasets and improving their overall performance in such data-constrained environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose two novel, generic, and additive modules: Shifted Patch Tokenization (SPT) and Locality Self-Attention (LSA). These modules are designed to enhance the locality inductive bias of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and enable learning from scratch on small datasets.

#### 3.1. Shifted Patch Tokenization (SPT)

SPT aims to enrich the spatial information embedded in visual tokens by expanding their receptive fields during the tokenization process.

- **Formulation:** Given an input image, SPT first generates multiple spatially shifted versions of the image. The paper specifically details a strategy (named 'S') where the input image is shifted by half the patch size in four diagonal directions (left-up, right-up, left-down, and right-down). These shifted images are then cropped to the original input image size and concatenated with the original image along the channel dimension. This concatenated feature map is then subjected to standard patch partitioning, flattening, layer normalization, and a linear projection to obtain visual tokens. This process allows each visual token to implicitly incorporate information from adjacent pixels that would otherwise be excluded by non-overlapping patches, effectively increasing its receptive field.
- **Application as Patch Embedding Layer (S_pe):** When used as the initial patch embedding layer, the output of SPT (visual tokens with enhanced spatial information) is combined with a learnable class token (if used by the specific ViT architecture) and positional embeddings. This directly feeds spatially rich tokens into the transformer encoder.
- **Application as Pooling Layer (S_pool):** For hierarchical ViT architectures that use pooling layers to reduce the number of tokens across stages (e.g., Swin Transformer, PiT), SPT can also be employed. In this setup, the 2D-matrix visual tokens from a preceding transformer stage are reshaped back into a 3D-tensor with spatial dimensions. SPT is then applied to this 3D tensor to generate new visual tokens with a reduced number of tokens, while still benefiting from the increased receptive field and embedded spatial information. A linearly projected class token is then reconnected.
- **Shifting Strategy Exploration (Supplementary):** The supplementary material details an exploration of various shifting strategies, including 4 cardinal directions, 4 diagonal directions, and 8 cardinal directions, as well as different shift ratios (e.g., 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.00 relative to patch size). The authors found that 4 diagonal directions with a shift ratio of 0.5 generally provided a reasonable balance, though optimal settings could be dataset-dependent. For the main experiments, 4 diagonal shifts with a 0.5 ratio were fixed.

#### 3.2. Locality Self-Attention (LSA)

LSA is designed to mitigate the smoothing phenomenon of attention score distributions, making the attention mechanism more locally focused.

- **Diagonal Masking:** This component directly addresses the issue of self-token relations dominating attention scores. Before the softmax operation, diagonal masking forces the diagonal components of the similarity matrix (R, representing self-token relations) to negative infinity. This explicitly prevents a token from attending to itself, thereby forcing the attention mechanism to focus more on inter-token relations and promoting a sharper distribution across other relevant tokens.
- **Learnable Temperature Scaling:** To counteract the smoothing effect of the fixed $\sqrt{d_k}$ division in standard softmax attention, LSA introduces a learnable temperature parameter ($\tau$). This parameter is optimized during training, allowing the model to dynamically control the sharpness of the attention score distribution. Experimental results show that the learned temperature typically converges to a value lower than $\sqrt{d_k}$, which is known to produce sharper distributions and consequently more focused attention.
- **Combined Effect:** By combining diagonal masking and learnable temperature scaling, LSA effectively sharpens the attention score distribution. This forces ViTs to attend more locally and discriminatively to tokens with strong semantic relations, rather than distributing attention uniformly across all tokens or primarily to self-tokens.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Implementations:** The proposed methods were implemented using PyTorch.
- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on multiple small-size datasets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and Tiny-ImageNet. Additionally, evaluation on the mid-size ImageNet dataset was performed to assess broader applicability.
- **Models:** Various contemporary ViT architectures were evaluated as base models, including standard ViT, T2T, CaiT, PiT, and Swin Transformer. For comparison, CNN baselines like ResNet and EfficientNet were also included. Model configurations were adapted for small datasets, typically with adjustments to depth, hidden dimensions, number of heads, and patch sizes (e.g., patch size 8 for patch embedding, 16 for pooling layers in small dataset experiments).
- **Training Regime:** A comprehensive training regimen was employed, incorporating several advanced data augmentation techniques (CutMix, Mixup, Auto Augment, Repeated Augment) and regularization methods (label smoothing, stochastic depth, random erasing). The AdamW optimizer was used, with specific settings for learning rates, batch sizes, warm-up epochs, and cosine learning rate decay across 100 epochs.
- **Metrics:** Performance was primarily measured by Top-1 accuracy. Computational efficiency was assessed through throughput (images/sec), FLOPs (Millions), and number of parameters (Millions).
- **Ablation Studies:** Extensive ablation studies were conducted to isolate and quantify the contributions of individual components of LSA (diagonal masking and learnable temperature scaling) and the independent and combined effects of SPT and LSA on overall performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrated that the proposed Shifted Patch Tokenization (SPT) and Locality Self-Attention (LSA) modules consistently improved the performance of various Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures, particularly on small-size datasets, while also showing effectiveness on mid-size datasets.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results on Small-Size Datasets

- **Consistent Performance Improvement:** When both SPT and LSA (denoted as SL) were applied to various ViTs, a general performance enhancement was observed across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and Tiny-ImageNet.
- **Significant Gains on Challenging Datasets:** The average Top-1 accuracy improvement on Tiny-ImageNet, a representative small-size dataset, was 2.96%. Specific models showed even greater gains:
 - Swin Transformer achieved a notable 4.08% improvement on Tiny-ImageNet.
 - Standard ViT improved by 4.00% on Tiny-ImageNet (from 57.07% to 61.07%).
 - On CIFAR100, CaiT improved by +3.43% (from 76.89% to 80.32%), and PiT improved by +4.01% (from 74.99% to 79.00% when S_pool was used).
- **Reduced Gap with CNNs:** The enhanced ViTs (e.g., SL-CaiT, SL-Swin) demonstrated performance competitive with or superior to CNN baselines like ResNet and EfficientNet on most small-size datasets, indicating that the proposed methods effectively address the locality inductive bias issue. For instance, SL-CaiT achieved the best performance over ResNet and EfficientNet on all datasets except CIFAR10.
- **Acceptable Computational Overhead:** The performance improvements were achieved with a relatively modest increase in inference latency. For models like ViT, T2T, and CaiT, the proposed method resulted in latency overheads ranging from approximately 1.06% to 1.15%, suggesting favorable cost-effectiveness.
- **Effectiveness of S_pool:** Replacing the pooling layers in hierarchical ViTs (like PiT and Swin) with SPT (S_pool) led to additional performance gains, confirming that SPT is effective not only for initial patch embedding but also for intermediate token reduction stages.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results on Mid-Size Datasets (ImageNet)

- The proposed method also proved effective when training on the mid-size ImageNet dataset from scratch.
- ViT saw an improvement of +1.60% (from 69.95% to 71.55%).
- PiT improved by +1.44% (from 75.58% to 77.02%).
- Swin Transformer improved by +1.06% (from 79.95% to 81.01%).
These results suggest that the enhancements to locality inductive bias are beneficial beyond just very small datasets.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study Results

- **Elements of LSA:** Both learnable temperature scaling and diagonal masking independently contributed to performance improvements. On Tiny-ImageNet, learnable temperature scaling improved ViT by +0.88% (from 57.07% to 57.95%), and diagonal masking improved it by +1.22% (from 57.07% to 58.29%). When combined in LSA, the synergy resulted in a +1.43% improvement (from 57.07% to 58.50%), and the Kullback-Leibler Divergence analysis showed that LSA attenuated the attention score smoothing.
- **SPT and LSA Synergy:** Both SPT and LSA individually yielded significant performance gains. On Tiny-ImageNet, LSA alone improved ViT by +1.43%, while SPT alone provided a more substantial +3.60% improvement (from 57.07% to 60.67%). When both modules were applied together (SL-ViT), the performance improvement reached +4.00% (from 57.07% to 61.07%), demonstrating their combined effectiveness and synergy.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

- **Attention Map Visualization:** Visualization of the attention scores of the final class token for various ViTs demonstrated that when SPT and LSA were applied, the attention shifted from being dispersed across the background to being more concentrated on the target object shapes. This was particularly evident in models like CaiT, T2T, ViT, and PiT, indicating that the proposed methods successfully increased the locality inductive bias and improved the attention focus of ViTs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the field of computer vision, particularly concerning the practical application of Vision Transformers (ViTs) in data-constrained environments.

- **Overcoming Data Dependency:** The most significant impact lies in directly addressing a critical limitation of traditional ViTs: their heavy reliance on massive pre-training datasets. By enabling ViTs to learn effectively from scratch on small-size datasets, this work significantly broadens the applicability of this powerful architecture to a wider range of real-world scenarios and research domains where large datasets are not available or are expensive to acquire. This democratizes access to high-performing ViT models for users with limited data resources.
- **Enhanced ViT Performance and Robustness:** The consistent and substantial performance improvements observed across various ViT architectures and small-scale datasets demonstrate that the proposed SPT and LSA modules effectively enhance the locality inductive bias. This makes ViTs more robust and capable of learning meaningful representations even with limited training data, thereby reducing the performance gap between ViTs and CNNs in these contexts.
- **Generic and Modular Approach:** The design of SPT and LSA as generic, easily applicable add-on modules is a key strength. This modularity facilitates their integration into existing and future ViT architectures without requiring extensive architectural redesigns, promoting their widespread adoption and further development.
- **Improved Interpretability and Focus:** The qualitative results, showcasing more focused attention on target objects, suggest that the proposed methods lead to more interpretable and semantically meaningful attention mechanisms within ViTs. This can aid in understanding model decisions and potentially lead to safer and more reliable AI systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This study provides a strong foundation for future research into developing more data-efficient ViTs. It highlights the importance of locality inductive bias in the tokenization and attention mechanisms, opening avenues for further exploration of optimal shifting strategies, more advanced local attention mechanisms, and other methods to embed crucial spatial information.
- **Reduced Computational Cost:** By negating the need for pre-training on enormous datasets, the proposed methods also have the potential to reduce the significant computational and energy costs associated with training state-of-the-art vision models, making high-performance deep learning more sustainable and accessible.

In conclusion, this work offers practical and effective solutions for making Vision Transformers more adaptable and efficient for small-size datasets, pushing the boundaries of ViT applicability and opening new possibilities for their deployment in diverse applications.
