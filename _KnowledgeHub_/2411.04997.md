# LLM2CLIP: Powerful Language Model Unlocks Richer Visual Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> The LLM2CLIP framework integrates large language models (LLMs) with pre-trained CLIP models, employing a two-stage post-training strategy to unlock richer visual representations. This approach achieves superior performance in zero-shot English and cross-lingual text-image retrieval, yielding gains of up to +15.8 points over EVA02 on long-text benchmarks and enhancing multimodal large language models.

## Problem

- CLIP's text encoder is limited in processing long, complex, or dense image captions, hindering its ability to fully leverage advanced language understanding for visual representation.
- Large Language Models (LLMs), despite their superior language comprehension and open-world knowledge, do not naturally produce discriminative text embeddings suitable for CLIP's contrastive learning objectives.
- Existing multimodal frameworks struggle to effectively translate LLMs' advanced linguistic capabilities into corresponding improvements for visual representation learning.

## Method

- A two-stage post-training strategy is introduced: first, ==LLM Caption Contrastive (CC) fine-tuning== to make LLMs discriminative encoders, and second, ==LLM2CLIP== post fine-tuning.
- Stage 1 modifies LLMs with bidirectional attention and LoRA, fine-tuning them via textual contrastive learning (==Supervised SimCSE==) on augmented caption datasets to improve feature separability.
- Stage 2 integrates the discriminative LLM (with a ==linear adaptor== and frozen gradients for efficiency) as a rich supervisor to post-train the CLIP visual encoder, leveraging dense captions.

## Results

- The Caption Contrastive fine-tuning increased LLama 3 8B's caption-to-caption retrieval accuracy from **5.2%** to **29.5%**, confirming its role in enabling discriminative text encoding.
- LLM2CLIP achieved superior zero-shot text-image retrieval performance, demonstrating gains of up to **+15.8 points** over EVA02 on long-text benchmarks and consistent improvements over SigLip2 on both English and cross-lingual datasets.
- Integration into the Llava 1.5 framework enhanced multimodal model performance on over **87.5%** of VQA and other multimodal benchmarks, indicating improved visual features for complex reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) inherently lack discriminative feature separability for contrastive learning, but targeted Caption Contrastive fine-tuning can overcome this limitation, transforming them into effective text encoders for multimodal tasks.
> - Efficiently integrating a discriminative LLM as a supervisor allows CLIP's visual encoder to learn richer, more semantically nuanced visual representations from complex and dense captions.
> - Strategic gradient freezing, adaptor utilization, and offline feature precomputation during post-training enable a highly efficient integration of LLMs, drastically reducing computational demands for enhancing existing vision-language models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

CLIP (Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training) has been a groundbreaking model, establishing a shared representation space for images and text through contrastive learning on massive datasets. It has become a foundational component for numerous multimodal applications, from zero-shot classification and retrieval to powering visual encoders in large language models (VL-LMs) and generative models like Stable Diffusion. However, the original CLIP architecture has known limitations, particularly its fixed-length text encoder (typically 77 tokens), which struggles with long, complex, or dense captions, limiting its ability to capture nuanced linguistic details and broad world knowledge.

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized textual understanding and generation, offering unparalleled linguistic capabilities and vast open-world knowledge. This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two powerful paradigms, aiming to address CLIP's textual limitations by efficiently injecting LLM capabilities.

Existing efforts to integrate LLMs into multimodal contexts or improve CLIP's text handling include:
- **LLM-as-embedding models**: Works like LLM2Vec and NV-Embed-v2 demonstrated that LLMs can be adapted to become competitive text embedders for pure-text tasks, often outperforming traditional text encoders on benchmarks like MTEB.
- **Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs)**: Models like LLaVA and Qwen-VL leverage CLIP's visual features, but often use their own language models, not directly enhancing the CLIP text encoder. VLM2Vec extends LLM-as-embedding to multimodality, but typically involves larger models and higher computational costs, often struggling to match CLIP's lightweight design and efficiency in standard retrieval tasks despite larger parameter counts.
- **CLIP with longer captions**: Several papers (e.g., DCI, LaCLIP, DreamLIP, Recap-DataComp-1B, Long-CLIP) have attempted to extend CLIP's capacity for longer texts, often resorting to workarounds like summarization, segmentation, or positional encoding fine-tuning. These methods typically focus on increasing the *length* of processed text rather than fundamentally enhancing the *linguistic understanding* or *world knowledge* capabilities inherent in LLMs.
- **Direct LLM integration into CLIP**: Jina-CLIP used a BERT-variant as a text encoder, but its linguistic power was limited compared to modern LLMs. MATE designed adaptors to bridge CLIP and LLMs, but did not fully address the critical issue of feature separability of raw LLM embeddings for contrastive training.

LLM2CLIP differentiates itself by proposing a *highly efficient* two-stage fine-tuning framework that specifically addresses two key challenges:
1. **Feature Separability**: Vanilla LLM embeddings are not inherently optimized for the discriminative nature of CLIP's contrastive learning on image-caption pairs. LLM2CLIP devises a "CLIP-specific embeddingization strategy" for LLMs.
2. **Training Cost**: Integrating a large LLM into CLIP's already expensive pre-training or fine-tuning process is computationally prohibitive. LLM2CLIP aims for a "lightweight fine-tuning procedure" that incurs "almost no extra cost."

By focusing on efficiently harnessing the superior linguistic comprehension and broad world knowledge of LLMs to strengthen *existing* pre-trained CLIP models, LLM2CLIP aims to elevate multimodal capabilities without the need for colossal retraining, positioning it as a practical and impactful advancement in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the LLM2CLIP framework are clearly articulated:

1. **Strengthen CLIP's Linguistic Understanding**: The core motivation is to enhance CLIP's ability to process and understand text, especially long, complex, and dense captions. Traditional CLIP's text encoder is known to have limitations in handling detailed linguistic information, which can hinder performance on tasks requiring fine-grained text comprehension.
2. **Leverage LLMs' World Knowledge and Reasoning**: To inject the advanced linguistic understanding and broad world knowledge capabilities of modern Large Language Models (LLMs) into the CLIP framework. LLMs, trained on vast text corpora, possess rich semantic representations and reasoning abilities that can provide deeper textual supervision to multimodal learning.
3. **Efficient Integration**: To develop an efficient fine-tuning framework that embeds an LLM into a pre-trained CLIP model without incurring prohibitive training costs. CLIP pre-training is already resource-intensive, and a naive joint fine-tuning of an LLM would be impractical for most researchers and practitioners. The goal is to achieve significant performance gains with training costs comparable to, or slightly above, regular CLIP fine-tuning.
4. **Improve Cross-Modality Representation**: Ultimately, the aim is to produce richer and more discriminative cross-modality representations, leading to improved performance across a wide spectrum of downstream tasks, including various forms of image-text retrieval, classification, detection, segmentation, and as a backbone for multimodal LLMs.
5. **Address LLM Embedding Separability**: To overcome the challenge that raw LLM embeddings, while powerful for pure-text tasks, may not be sufficiently discriminative for the contrastive learning paradigm used in CLIP. This requires a specific "embeddingization" strategy for LLMs tailored to the cross-modal setting.

In essence, LLM2CLIP seeks to bridge the gap between powerful visual encoders and advanced language models, creating a more capable and versatile multimodal foundation model efficiently.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LLM2CLIP framework employs an efficient two-stage fine-tuning approach to integrate LLMs into pre-trained CLIP models. The core idea is to first "embedding-ize" an LLM for contrastive learning and then couple it with a frozen CLIP vision encoder through a lightweight adaptor.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: LLM Caption Contrastive Fine-tuning

This stage focuses on adapting a generic LLM to produce discriminative text embeddings suitable for CLIP's cross-modal contrastive learning. The authors introduce several design choices across model architecture, training methods, and data:

- **Model Architecture**:
 - **Sentence Token Representation**: Instead of using the `[EOS]` token for sentence representation (common in some LLM embedding models), the authors found that **average pooling** across all output tokens performed better for CLIP's setting and is thus adopted as the default.
 - **Bidirectional Attention**: The causal attention mask typically used in generative LLMs is removed. This allows the LLM to model bidirectional textual relationships, enhancing its comprehension capabilities as an encoder rather than a generator.
 - **Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT)**: To efficiently activate and tune the LLM's textual comprehension, **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** is applied. This significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters and computational overhead.
 - **Adaptor Design**: Experiments with Transformer-based, Linear, or no adaptors after the LLM's output layer showed negligible impact on performance in Stage 1, so no adaptor is used by default in this stage.
- **Training Methods**:
 - **Textual Contrastive Learning (SimCSE)**: This is identified as the most critical component. The authors employ a **supervised SimCSE contrastive loss**, where positive pairs are generated by using two different captions for the same image from high-quality caption datasets. This pushes semantically similar captions closer and dissimilar ones apart, enhancing feature separability.
 - **Masked Next Token Prediction (MNTP)**: Inspired by BERT's masked language modeling, MNTP was explored but found to underperform when used alone or in combination with contrastive learning, compared to direct supervised contrastive fine-tuning for multimodal scenarios. It is not used by default.
- **Training Data**:
 - The LLM (specifically LLaMA 3.1 8B by default) is fine-tuned on **caption corpora extracted from image-text datasets**, such as DreamLIP, which provides multiple captions per image. This dataset structure is ideal for supervised textual contrastive learning.
 - To maintain general language understanding and prevent overfitting to image captions, a mix of **1.5M pure-text pairs from Echo Embeddings** is also incorporated into the mixed contrastive training setup.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: LLM2CLIP Post Fine-tuning

This stage focuses on efficiently integrating the caption-contrastive fine-tuned LLM (from Stage 1) with a pre-trained CLIP vision encoder to establish a stronger cross-modal representation.

- **Model Architecture**:
 - **PEFT for CLIP**: Recognizing the high cost of full CLIP pre-training, PEFT methods are employed for this stage.
 - **Frozen LLM with Adaptor**: A key innovation for efficiency is to **freeze all LLM gradients** from Stage 1. Its sentence embeddings are treated as fixed features. A small, learnable **adaptor** (by default, four linear blocks using an inverted bottleneck MLP architecture similar to FuseMix) is appended after the LLM's output layer. This adaptor is then trained with the CLIP visual encoder.
 - **Offline Inference**: Freezing the LLM gradients allows for precomputing and storing text features via offline inference. This eliminates the need to load the LLM into GPU memory during training, drastically reducing GPU memory usage, accelerating experimentation, and saving training time.
 - **Original CLIP Text Encoder Discarded**: The original, smaller CLIP text encoder is completely discarded during both training and inference in this stage, leveraging the LLM as the sole text encoder.
- **Training Methods**:
 - **Cross-Modal Contrastive Learning**: The core training method involves standard CLIP-style cross-modal contrastive learning between the (now LLM-enhanced) text encoder and the **unfrozen CLIP Vision Encoder**. Opening the visual encoder's gradients allows it to learn to align better with the richer LLM-derived text features.
 - **Integration Strategy Ablation**: The authors explored several integration strategies (e.g., separate CLIP losses for both LLM and original text encoder, adding a contrastive loss between the two text encoders, concatenating features) but found that simply **replacing the original CLIP text encoder with the LLM-adaptor module** (method 'a' in Section 3.2) was the most effective and simple approach for their default.
- **Training Data**:
 - The method inherently excels with dense and detailed image captions. Datasets like DreamLIP and Recaption, which provide multiple rich captions per image (often MLLM-generated), are utilized.
 - A strategy blending **real captions and dense MLLM-generated captions at a 50% ratio** is adopted by default, after ablation studies showed it offered the most balanced improvement for both short and long text tasks. Training data volumes of 3M, 15M, and 60M samples were explored.

By meticulously designing these two stages, LLM2CLIP aims to infuse the linguistic prowess of LLMs into CLIP's visual understanding capabilities in a computationally efficient manner, achieving substantial performance improvements without large-scale retraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LLM2CLIP demonstrates significant and consistent performance improvements across a broad range of multimodal tasks, often setting new state-of-the-art results for CLIP-based architectures while maintaining computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot English Text-Image Retrieval

- **Substantial Gains**: LLM2CLIP consistently boosts retrieval performance for both short and long-text captions across various pre-trained CLIP variants (OpenAI CLIP, EVA02, SigLIP2) and visual backbones (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, So-ViT/14).
- **Long-Text Superiority**: The improvements are particularly pronounced for long, dense captions. For instance, on EVA02 (ViT-L/14, 224 resolution) with 60M training data, LLM2CLIP achieved an average Top-1 retrieval accuracy improvement of +12 (I2T) and +14.8 (T2I) across five diverse datasets (Flickr, COCO, ShareGPT4V, Urban1K, DOCCI).
- **SOTA Baselines Enhanced**: Even on highly optimized models like SigLIP2, which was pre-trained on an enormous 40 billion image-text pairs, LLM2CLIP still provided an average performance improvement of +9.4 (I2T) and +10.6 (T2I). For short-caption datasets like Flickr and COCO, where SigLIP2 is already highly optimized, LLM2CLIP still yielded improvements of +1.0 and +1.9, respectively.
- **Outperforming Larger MLLMs**: LLM2CLIP, despite its lightweight fine-tuning, surpassed larger MLLMs like InternVL (6B parameters) and VLM2Vec (7B parameters) in retrieval tasks, highlighting its efficiency and effectiveness.
- **Data Volume Impact**: Increasing the training data from 3M to 60M consistently improved retrieval performance, with 3M data already showing noticeable gains, especially for long-text retrieval.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Multilingual Text-Image Retrieval

- LLM2CLIP successfully endowed EVA02, which previously lacked multilingual capabilities, with strong cross-lingual performance. It also significantly enhanced the already robust multilingual performance of SigLIP2, despite being trained exclusively on English captions in its fine-tuning stage. This demonstrates the inherent multilingual advantage gained by incorporating an LLM.

#### 4.3. Zero-shot & Linear Probe ImageNet Classification

- **Zero-shot Performance**: A modest drop in zero-shot accuracy on ImageNet was observed compared to the original CLIP baseline.
- **Linear Probe Improvement**: Conversely, the accuracy from linear probing on ImageNet *improved* over the original CLIP, indicating that the learned visual features (from the CLIP visual encoder) became stronger.
- **Hypothesis**: The authors hypothesize that the zero-shot drop, particularly for fine-grained categories, might be due to a trade-off where the model prioritizes broader, retrieval-oriented vocabulary over uniform discriminability across all (potentially obscure) classification nouns, similar to patterns seen in other CLIP fine-tuning studies.

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot / Supervised Segmentation and Object Detection

- **Stronger Visual Features**: Linear probe results were further corroborated by improvements in both zero-shot and fully supervised object detection and segmentation benchmarks (COCO-S, ADE20K, VOC20, Cityscapes, OV-COCO).
- **Enhanced Spatial Understanding**: The gains are attributed to the LLM's ability to parse dense captions more accurately, particularly spatial terms and object-to-object relations, and inject this knowledge into the joint vision-language space.

#### 4.5. Multimodal Large Language Models Performance

- When LLM2CLIP (EVA02-L-336) was used as the visual feature extractor for LLaVA-v1.5-7B (replacing its original CLIP encoder), it enhanced multimodal model performance on over 87.5% of benchmarks (VQA, POPE, MMBench, SEED), with only minor losses on two tasks. This underscores its potential to improve complex image reasoning and understanding in MLLMs.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies and Efficiency Analysis

- **Stage 1 Ablations**:
 - **SimCSE Criticality**: Supervised SimCSE was confirmed as the most critical loss function, significantly outperforming unsupervised SimCSE and MNTP.
 - **LoRA Necessity**: Freezing LLM gradients in Stage 1 yielded poor results, confirming that LoRA fine-tuning for the LLM is essential.
 - **Pooling/Attention**: Average pooling was superior to `[EOS]` token for sentence representation, and bidirectional attention showed negligible difference from causal attention for this encoding task.
- **Stage 2 Ablations**:
 - **Adaptor Effectiveness**: A lightweight linear adaptor (4 layers) proved effective, with performance improving as adaptor size increased. Transformer adaptors showed similar performance.
 - **Text Encoder Integration**: Simply *replacing* the original CLIP text encoder with the LLM-adaptor module was the most effective strategy. Combining both text encoders or explicitly aligning them yielded marginal or negative gains. Concatenating features (original CLIP text encoder + LLM) showed promising uplift but was not adopted as default for simplicity.
- **Data Ratio Ablation**: A 1:1 ratio of real and MLLM-generated dense captions provided the most balanced improvement for both short and long texts. Purely synthesized long texts improved long-text retrieval but could harm short-text performance.
- **Efficiency**: The proposed "LLM Frozen + Linear Adaptor + Offline-loading" strategy significantly improved efficiency. It allowed for a tenfold increase in batch size and fourfold extension of training time with identical computational resources, ultimately leading to higher performance by effectively reducing experimental overhead.

In summary, LLM2CLIP effectively addresses the limitations of CLIP's text encoder by leveraging LLMs, achieving substantial performance boosts across diverse multimodal tasks, particularly with long and complex captions, all while maintaining high computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LLM2CLIP framework represents a significant advancement in multimodal AI, primarily due to its efficient and effective integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) with pre-trained CLIP models. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

1. **Enhanced Multimodal Understanding**: By unlocking the superior linguistic understanding and broad world knowledge of LLMs for CLIP, LLM2CLIP dramatically improves the ability of multimodal models to process and comprehend complex, long, and dense captions. This moves beyond merely handling more tokens to truly grasping richer semantic nuances and contextual information, which is crucial for real-world applications.

2. **Cost-Efficient Upgrade for Foundational Models**: One of the most impactful aspects of LLM2CLIP is its efficiency. It provides a lightweight fine-tuning framework that upgrades state-of-the-art CLIP variants with LLM power at a cost "almost identical to regular CLIP fine-tuning." This low computational overhead makes powerful multimodal representation more accessible to researchers and practitioners who may not have access to the vast resources required for training LLMs or large multimodal models from scratch. It allows for continuous improvement of existing robust vision encoders.

3. **Broader Applicability and Generalization**: The consistent performance gains across a wide spectrum of downstream tasks—including various types of image-text retrieval (short, long, cross-lingual), zero-shot classification, object detection, segmentation, and as a visual encoder for MLLMs—demonstrates the framework's versatility and generalization capabilities. This suggests that LLM2CLIP can serve as a more robust and capable backbone for future multimodal systems, providing stronger visual and linguistic features.

4. **Improved Performance on Challenging Tasks**: The substantial improvements in long-caption retrieval, multilingual tasks, and zero-shot detection/segmentation highlight its ability to tackle previously challenging multimodal scenarios. This makes it particularly valuable for applications requiring detailed textual descriptions or understanding across different languages and open-vocabulary settings.

5. **Refined Text Embedding for Contrastive Learning**: The specific "caption-contrastive fine-tuning" recipe for LLMs is a key technical contribution. It demonstrates how to transform general-purpose LLMs into highly discriminative text embedders optimized for cross-modal contrastive learning, addressing a critical gap identified in prior work. This recipe could inform future designs for multimodal text encoders.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research**: LLM2CLIP opens new avenues for research, encouraging further exploration into how LLM capabilities can be integrated into existing multimodal architectures with minimal computational cost. It suggests that specialized fine-tuning recipes can bridge the gap between powerful unimodal models, fostering more efficient and effective multimodal AI development. The observed trade-off in zero-shot classification vs. linear probe also provides an interesting direction for future work on balancing global and fine-grained semantic understanding.

In conclusion, LLM2CLIP is a highly significant work that efficiently unlocks richer cross-modality representations by intelligently leveraging the strengths of LLMs within the established CLIP framework. Its cost-effectiveness, broad applicability, and demonstrated performance gains position it as an important contribution that will likely influence the development of more powerful and accessible multimodal AI systems.
