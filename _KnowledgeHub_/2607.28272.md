# MemHarness: Memory Is Reconstructed, Not Replayed

> [!summary] Summary
> MemHarness is a memory-augmented LLM agent that, instead of replaying retrieved experiences verbatim, has one unified policy critique each memory against its stored source observation and the current state, then keep, rewrite, or reject it as state-specific guidance before acting. Trained end-to-end with GRPO on task reward only, a 7B Qwen model reaches 85.2% on ALFWorld and 75.6% on WebShop, beating GRPO and memory baselines.

## Problem

- Most memory-augmented agents replay retrieved trajectories or principles verbatim, conflating semantic retrieval relevance with action-level applicability to the agent's current interaction state.
- Memories formed under different environmental conditions inject state-mismatched guidance, causing negative transfer; naively adding external memory to GRPO training sharply degrades interactive-task performance.
- Parametric memory approaches adapt to state but keep experience implicit and hard to inspect or revise, while explicit memory banks stay traceable but lack adaptive, state-conditioned use.

## Method

- ==Contextual Memory Reconstruction==: the **MemHarness** policy compares each retrieved strategy's stored ==source observation== with the current history window, then retains, revises, or rejects it, outputting <EMPTY> to fall back on self-reasoning.
- ==Unified retrieve-reconstruct-act policy==: one shared Qwen2.5-7B model decides when to query a ==self-distilled experience bank== (BGE-M3, top-3), generates guidance as a ==latent step==, then emits the executable action.
- End-to-end ==GRPO== training with a ==trajectory-level reward== (10 for success plus a 0.1-weighted format bonus), after a brief 200-trajectory ==cold start==, so reconstruction emerges without annotated reconstruction traces.

## Results

- Reaches **85.2%** ALFWorld and **75.6%** WebShop success, beating plain GRPO by **8.8** and **9.5** points and Gemini-2.5-Pro by **23.1** and **39.7** points.
- On unseen ALFWorld layouts scores **85.9%** versus **76.3%** for RL plus raw memory; removing reconstruction drops to **82.4%**, below the memory-free variant's **83.0%**.
- Swapping in a generic instruction-tuned rewriter drops ALFWorld from **85.2%** to **77.7%**; random source states raise the rejection rate from **8.7%** to **13.3%**, confirming genuine state comparison.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Retrieval relevance is not applicability: memory should pass through a state-conditioned critique step before reaching the actor, or it can hurt more than having no memory.
> - Storing each memory's source context alongside the abstract lesson is what makes mismatch detection possible; without it, agents silently accept misaligned guidance and lose success.
> - Training with a reconstruction step improves the policy itself: the memory-free variant still beats plain GRPO (83.0% vs 76.4%), suggesting reconstruction acts as latent reasoning scaffolding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has led to their application as autonomous agents in sequential decision-making tasks, often employing techniques such as ReAct for reasoning and acting, or self-reflection for improvement. A common strategy to enhance these agents involves augmenting them with experiential memory, allowing them to reuse prior successes and avoid repeated errors.

Current approaches to memory-augmented LLM agents largely fall into two categories. The first, and most prevalent, is a "verbatim replay" paradigm, where retrieved past experiences (e.g., trajectories or principles) are treated as static records and directly injected into the agent's context. This method can suffer from negative transfer because the semantic relevance of a retrieved memory does not guarantee its applicability to the agent's specific current interaction state, which may differ significantly from the historical context in which the memory was formed.

The second category involves internalizing experience directly into the model's parameters, enabling state-conditioned behavior without explicit replay. While this "parametric memory" offers adaptive generation, the underlying experience and its influence on decisions become implicit, reducing traceability and making inspection or revision challenging.

This work identifies a gap between these two paradigms: the need for a mechanism that retains explicit, inspectable memories while dynamically adapting them to the current state. The paper draws inspiration from cognitive science, noting that human memory is reconstructive rather than purely reproductive. Humans rarely recall experiences verbatim; instead, they interpret, reorganize, and adapt retrieved memories based on present cues and existing knowledge to inform current behavior. This suggests a potential paradigm shift for memory-augmented agents: moving from a "retrieve and replay" model to a "retrieve, evaluate, and reconstruct" one.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the limitations of the verbatim replay paradigm in memory-augmented LLM agents. The motivation stems from the observation that injecting static, unadapted past experiences into the agent's context can lead to an "applicability gap." Even if a retrieved memory is semantically relevant to the task, its specific details might be misaligned with the agent's concrete, dynamic current state, resulting in suboptimal actions or "negative transfer."

Inspired by the reconstructive nature of human memory, the authors propose to equip LLM agents with the ability to actively "harness" and reconstruct past experiences. The key objectives are:

- **Develop a reconstructive view of agent memory:** To conceptualize memory-guided decision-making as a multi-stage process that explicitly includes critique and reconstruction of retrieved experiences.
- **Propose a reconstruction-centric framework (MemHarness):** To design a framework that inserts an explicit memory critique and reconstruction stage between retrieval and action generation. This aims to transform static memory fragments into context-sensitive, state-aligned guidance.
- **Enable end-to-end learning of reconstruction:** To achieve this reconstructive capability through end-to-end reinforcement learning, specifically using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), without requiring additional human annotations or direct supervision for the reconstruction process itself. The goal is for the agent to implicitly learn a discriminative reconstruction strategy by optimizing for task success.
- **Demonstrate empirical effectiveness:** To validate that this framework can significantly improve performance on complex, long-horizon interactive decision-making benchmarks, outperforming both pure RL and static memory-augmented baselines.
- **Evaluate robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios:** To assess if the reconstructive mechanism enhances the agent's ability to generalize to novel environments where direct application of past memories might be misleading.
- **Analyze the underlying mechanism:** To investigate whether the reconstruction process genuinely relies on state comparison and how adaptive memory utilization behaviors evolve during training.

The core motivation is to bridge the gap between the traceability of explicit memory banks and the adaptivity offered by parameterized experience, thereby enabling more robust and intelligent LLM agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MemHarness proposes a framework that shifts LLM agents from a verbatim memory replay paradigm to a state-conditioned reconstruction approach. The method decomposes memory-guided decision-making into five conceptual stages: environment observation, experience retrieval, memory critique, contextual memory reconstruction, and action generation. During inference, this process is condensed into a three-stage pipeline that operates within a unified policy.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The MemHarness framework operates as follows:

1. **Memory Retrieval:** At each decision step `t`, based on the current history window `h_t`, the agent decides whether to query its experiential memory bank `B`. The memory bank consists of distilled natural-language experiences `e_i`, each paired with its source observation `o_src_i`. A retriever, utilizing an embedding model (BGE-M3 in implementation), fetches the top-`k` most relevant memory entries `E_t` in response to a context-derived query `q_t`.
2. **Contextual Memory Reconstruction:** This is the central component of MemHarness. The policy `π_θ` takes the task description `T`, current history `h_t`, and the retrieved memory tuples `(e_t,i, o_src_t,i)` as input. It then generates state-specific guidance `g_t`. This process implicitly involves critiquing the retrieved experiences `E_t` against `h_t` by comparing the current state with the source observations `o_src_t,i`. The policy is trained to preserve transferable knowledge while revising or discarding content that is misaligned or incompatible with the current situation. If the policy determines that no retrieved experience is applicable, it outputs `<EMPTY>`, which triggers a fallback to the agent's own self-reasoning (`p_self`) without memory guidance.
3. **Action Generation:** The same unified policy `π_θ` generates an executable action `a_t`. This action is conditioned on the task description `T`, current history `h_t`, and the reconstructed guidance `˜g_t` (which is either the adapted `g_t` or `p_self`). This design ensures that actions are informed by memory that has been explicitly vetted and tailored to the current context.

#### 3.2. Training with GRPO

A key aspect of MemHarness is that the reconstruction `g_t` is a latent variable without direct ground-truth supervision. Therefore, the entire retrieve-reconstruct-act pipeline is optimized end-to-end using reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

- **Reward Design:** The reward function `R(τ_i)` for a trajectory `τ_i` is primarily driven by sparse task outcomes (e.g., +10 for success, 0 for failure). A minor formatting bonus (0.1 × `R_format`) is added to encourage adherence to structural constraints, such as producing valid `<think>` and `<action>` blocks, appropriate memory retrieval frequency (1 to 5 times per episode), and strictly English output. This bonus guides the agent toward an agentic protocol without overriding the primary task objective.
- **Policy Update:** GRPO operates by sampling groups of trajectories. For each trajectory `τ_i` in a group, a group-normalized advantage `A_i` is computed based on the difference between its reward and the group's mean reward, normalized by its standard deviation. This `A_i` is then assigned to all tokens generated in `τ_i`, providing a shared credit assignment for the thinking process, memory reconstruction, and action generation. The policy `π_θ` is updated using a clipped surrogate objective, similar to PPO, which minimizes the difference between the new and old policies while incorporating a KL divergence penalty to prevent excessive deviation from a reference policy `π_ref`. This joint optimization allows the model to learn context-specific reconstruction and adaptive action generation from task-level rewards.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Backbone Model:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is used as the foundational LLM for the policy `π_θ`.
- **Cold-Start Stage:** A brief supervised fine-tuning stage precedes RL training. This stage utilizes a small dataset (200 multi-turn interaction trajectories with chain-of-thought, 200 question-answer examples for memory summarization) generated by GPT-5.1. Its purpose is to align the model with the required interaction protocol and memory abstraction formats, rather than acquiring task-specific skills.
- **Memory Bank Management:** The memory bank is initialized as empty and populated online during RL training by distilling successful and failed trajectories into natural-language principles. BGE-M3 serves as the embedding model for retrieval. Mechanisms for write-time deduplication (cosine similarity threshold `θ_sim = 0.85`), retrieval-time deduplication, and periodic pruning of low-utility entries are employed to manage memory bank growth and quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MemHarness was conducted on two interactive decision-making benchmarks, ALFWorld (embodied household tasks) and WebShop (online shopping).

#### 4.1. Overall Effectiveness (RQ1)

MemHarness demonstrated performance improvements across both benchmarks:

- **ALFWorld:** Achieved an average success rate of 85.2%, notably outperforming its base model Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (14.5%) and strong closed-source models like Gemini-2.5-Pro (62.1%) and GPT-4o (49.2%). It also exceeded the performance of pure RL baselines like GRPO (76.4%) by 8.8 percentage points.
- **WebShop:** Achieved an average task score of 75.6%, surpassing Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (7.8%), Gemini-2.5-Pro (35.9%), GPT-4o (23.7%), and GRPO (66.1%) by 9.5 percentage points.

These results indicate that MemHarness effectively leverages historical experience through state-conditioned reconstruction, outperforming methods that either lack memory or apply it verbatim.

#### 4.2. Component Analysis (Ablation Studies, RQ2)

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of MemHarness's components:

- **Necessity of RL:** The cold-start model alone showed limited performance (7.6% on ALFWorld), confirming that reinforcement learning is essential for acquiring robust decision-making skills.
- **Impact of Raw Memory:** Injecting unrefined memory ("RL + Raw Memory" or "w/o reconstruction") led to a decrease in ALFWorld performance compared to MemHarness (70.1% vs 85.2% for MemHarness, and 79.6% for "w/o reconstruction"), suggesting that verbatim replay introduces state-mismatch noise.
- **Intrinsic Policy Improvement:** Disabling memory retrieval during inference ("w/o memory") still resulted in higher performance (83.0% on ALFWorld) compared to the "RL Only" baseline (76.4%). This indicates that the reconstruction objective during training enhances the agent's fundamental reasoning capabilities, acting as a form of latent guidance.
- **Policy-Internal Reconstruction:** Replacing MemHarness's learned reconstruction module with a generic instruction-tuned LLM for zero-shot text rewriting significantly degraded performance (e.g., 77.7% on ALFWorld compared to 85.2% for MemHarness). This highlights the importance of end-to-end RL for learning task-grounded adaptation.

#### 4.3. Out-of-Distribution Generalization (RQ3)

MemHarness demonstrated robustness in ALFWorld Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenarios, where environmental layouts and object placements were unseen during training:

- MemHarness achieved the highest average success rate (85.9%) in OOD settings.
- The "RL + Raw Memory" baseline achieved 76.3%, indicating that unadapted memories are detrimental in novel environments.
- The "w/o reconstruction" variant, which uses verbatim memory replay, also showed degraded performance (82.4%), further confirming that mismatched historical information can act as noise.
MemHarness's ability to dynamically filter and rewrite mismatched guidance allowed it to safely leverage past experiences, contributing to superior OOD generalization.

#### 4.4. Mechanism Deep Dive: How Memory is Reconstructed (RQ4)

Analyses were conducted to understand the reconstruction mechanism:

- **Macro-level Filtering:** Removing the historical source state (`o_src`) during reconstruction led to a decrease in task success (80.0% on ALFWorld vs. 85.2% with correct `o_src`). Replacing `o_src` with a mismatched state from a random memory significantly increased rejection rates (from 8.7% to 13.3% on ALFWorld), indicating that the policy actively compares historical and current states to filter incompatible memories.
- **Micro-level Counterfactual Adaptation:** Counterfactual probes, where current states were minimally edited to invalidate retrieved memories, showed that reconstruction outputs adapted or rejected memories in response to fine-grained state changes. In ALFWorld, these edits reduced "unchanged" outputs and increased "adaptation" and "rejection." In WebShop, they primarily increased "rejection." This demonstrates the policy's sensitivity to subtle state variations.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics and Behavioral Evolution (RQ5)

Tracking internal memory decisions and task success rates during RL training revealed:

- MemHarness developed task-specific memory strategies. ALFWorld converged to a sparse retrieval policy (2-3 retrievals per trajectory), while WebShop maintained frequent retrieval with active filtering.
- Trajectories containing *accepted* reconstructed memories consistently achieved higher success rates than those with *rejected* memories, confirming the agent learned to effectively judge memory applicability and utility over time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MemHarness framework introduces a reconstructive paradigm for memory-augmented LLM agents, offering several points of significance and potential impact:

- **Paradigm Shift:** By moving beyond verbatim replay to state-conditioned reconstruction, MemHarness challenges the conventional approach to memory utilization in LLM agents. This shift is grounded in cognitive science, suggesting a more biologically plausible and robust way for agents to leverage past experiences.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The ability to critique and reconstruct memories mitigates the problem of negative transfer, particularly in out-of-distribution environments. This leads to more robust agent performance and improved generalization to unseen scenarios, which is critical for real-world applications.
- **Intrinsic Policy Improvement:** The training objective, which incorporates the reconstruction process, serves as a form of latent guidance. This not only improves the agent's ability to adapt external memories but also fundamentally enhances its intrinsic reasoning capabilities, even when external memory is not explicitly used during inference.
- **Bridging Explicit and Parametric Memory:** MemHarness effectively combines the benefits of explicit memory (traceability, inspectability) with the adaptivity typically associated with parametric memory, offering a promising "middle ground" for agent design.
- **Implications for Agent Architectures:** The framework suggests that future agent architectures might benefit from explicit modules for memory evaluation and adaptation, rather than simply retrieving and injecting information. This could lead to more sophisticated and nuanced ways for agents to learn from and apply past knowledge.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of end-to-end learning for reconstruction without explicit human supervision opens avenues for exploring similar latent reasoning processes in LLM agents and scaling these capabilities to larger models and more complex, open-ended environments.
