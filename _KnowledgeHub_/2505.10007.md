# Sample Complexity of Distributionally Robust Average-Reward Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from HKUST and Stanford provides the first finite-sample convergence guarantees for Distributionally Robust Average-Reward Markov Decision Processes (DR-AMDPs) in a tabular setting. The work proposes two algorithms achieving an optimal sample complexity of Õ(|S||A|t_mix^2 ε^-2) for estimating robust average rewards and policies under divergence-based uncertainty sets.

## Problem

- Prior Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning (DR-RL) research primarily focused on discounted-reward or finite-horizon settings, leaving a gap for average-reward criteria.
- Standard average-reward Bellman equations become ill-defined if uncertainty sets in DR-MDPs contain non-unichain (not uniformly ergodic) MDPs.
- Real-world applications often require policies robust to environmental uncertainty and optimized for long-term average performance, a challenging combination for existing RL methods.

## Method

- Derived structural conditions on divergence-based ==uncertainty sets== (KL and f_k) that guarantee uniform ergodicity for all perturbed MDPs within the set.
- Developed a 'Reduction to DR-DMDP' algorithm that transforms the DR-AMDP problem into an auxiliary DR-Discounted MDP with a calibrated discount factor.
- Proposed an 'Anchored DR-AMDP' algorithm that modifies the uncertainty set by introducing an anchoring state, ensuring uniform ergodicity directly.

## Results

- Achieved the first finite-sample convergence guarantee of Õ(|S||A|t_mix^2 ε^-2) for DR-AMDPs, which is optimal in its dependence on state-action space and accuracy.
- Demonstrated that the minorization time of any perturbed MDP within the constrained uncertainty set is bounded by 2t_minorize of the nominal MDP.
- Empirical validation confirmed a √n-convergence rate (error reduction proportional to n^-1/2) and showed favorable performance against baselines on numerical experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A sufficiently small uncertainty radius (e.g., for KL-divergence, δ ≤ O(p_∧ / m_∨^2)) ensures that all MDPs within the uncertainty set remain uniformly ergodic.
> - The average-reward DR-MDP problem can be effectively solved by reducing it to a DR-Discounted MDP with a specific, sample-dependent discount factor.
> - Both proposed algorithms are 'a priori knowledge-free', meaning they do not require prior knowledge of the nominal MDP's mixing time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a machine learning framework where agents learn to make decisions to maximize long-term rewards through interaction with an environment. While RL has demonstrated successes in various domains, its practical application is often challenged by discrepancies between training and real-world deployment environments. Policies trained in simulated settings can exhibit fragility when faced with environmental mismatches.

The field of Distributionally Robust Markov Decision Processes (DR-MDPs) and, more recently, Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning (DR-RL), emerged to address these issues by formulating problems to be robust against uncertainty in the environment's transition dynamics. Prior work in DR-RL has largely concentrated on discounted-reward or finite-horizon settings. This includes developments in model-free and model-based DR-RL, as well as approaches for offline learning and generative models. These studies have provided sample complexity bounds for various divergence-based uncertainty sets in discounted-reward scenarios. For example, some works established O(|S||A|(1-γ)^-4 ε^-2) bounds for KL-divergence, with tighter bounds for l_p-norm constraints.

In contrast, the average-reward setting within DR-RL has remained underexplored, particularly concerning finite-sample convergence guarantees. Average-reward RL is pertinent for applications requiring stable, long-term performance rather than short-term gains, such as steady-state control systems, inventory management, and energy optimization. The analysis of average-reward RL is inherently more complex than discounted-reward RL, even in non-robust contexts. For instance, while minimax sample complexity for generative models in discounted-reward RL was established relatively early, analogous results for non-robust average-reward settings were developed more recently, with bounds like O(|S||A|t_mix^2 ε^-2).

This paper addresses this gap by conducting a systematic analysis of the statistical properties of Distributionally Robust Average-Reward MDPs (DR-AMDPs) in a tabular setting. It provides the first finite-sample convergence guarantees for DR-AMDPs, thus extending the theoretical foundations of DR-RL to the average-reward criterion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to analyze the statistical properties of Distributionally Robust Average-Reward Markov Decision Processes (DR-AMDPs) and to develop algorithms that achieve near-optimal sample complexity for this problem. The motivation for this work is rooted in several practical considerations and theoretical gaps:

1. **Practical Relevance of Average-Reward Criterion**: Many real-world applications require stable, long-term operational performance, where the maximization of average reward is a more suitable objective than cumulative discounted rewards or finite-horizon rewards. Examples include:
 - **Control Systems**: Robotics and autonomous vehicles often need to optimize steady-state behavior and long-run performance rather than short-term cumulative gains, which are better captured by average reward.
 - **Operations Research**: Problems such as inventory management and queueing systems depend on long-run average metrics to ensure stability and efficiency over extended periods.
 - **Healthcare and Energy Management**: These fields often prioritize sustained optimal performance, making average-reward optimization a critical requirement for policy effectiveness.

2. **Addressing Environmental Uncertainty**: The assumption that training environments perfectly replicate real-world deployment conditions is frequently violated. This mismatch can lead to policies that are effective in training but brittle and underperforming in reality. Distributional robustness, which seeks policies robust to a set of possible environmental dynamics (an uncertainty set), is a framework for mitigating this issue. Integrating distributional robustness with the average-reward criterion aims to develop policies that are both robust to environmental uncertainty and optimized for long-term stability.

3. **Theoretical Gap in DR-RL Literature**: While significant advancements have been made in Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning (DR-RL) for discounted-reward and finite-horizon settings, the average-reward case has been largely neglected. Existing DR-RL literature provides sample complexity guarantees primarily for discounted scenarios, leaving a critical void in understanding the statistical efficiency of learning optimal policies and values in DR-AMDPs. This paper aims to fill this gap by providing the first finite-sample convergence guarantees for DR-AMDPs.

4. **Challenges of Average-Reward RL**: Average-reward RL is inherently more complex than discounted-reward RL, even in non-robust settings, both in terms of algorithm design and theoretical analysis. This complexity is amplified when distributional robustness is introduced. Specifically, traditional uncertainty sets in DR-MDPs can contain MDPs that are not unichain (i.e., do not possess a unique stationary distribution), which invalidates standard Bellman equations and complicates analysis. A key objective is to derive structural conditions on the uncertainty set that ensure the stability of all MDPs within it, thereby enabling robust average-reward analysis.

By addressing these motivations, the paper seeks to provide foundational theoretical insights and practical algorithms for a crucial, yet underexplored, area of robust reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the problem of distributionally robust average-reward reinforcement learning in a tabular setting, assuming access to a generative model (simulator) that allows sampling from the nominal controlled transition kernel. The approach involves developing two distinct algorithms and providing theoretical guarantees for their sample complexity.

**Problem Formulation and Assumptions:**

- **Tabular MDPs**: The state space $|S|$ and action space $|A|$ are finite. Rewards $r(s,a)$ are bounded within $$.
- **Generative Model**: The authors assume access to a simulator that can provide independent samples from the nominal transition probability $p_{s,a}$ for any state-action pair $(s,a)$. These samples are used to construct empirical transition probabilities $\hat{p}_{s,a}$.
- **Uncertainty Sets**: The adversarial perturbations to the transition kernels are defined using divergence-based uncertainty sets. Specifically, KL-divergence $D_{KL}(q||p)$ and $f_k$-divergence $D_{f_k}(q||p)$ (which includes $\chi^2$-divergence as a special case for $k=2$) are considered. The uncertainty set $P_{s,a}(\mathcal{D}, \delta)$ for each $(s,a)$ contains all transition probabilities $p$ such that its divergence from the nominal $\hat{p}_{s,a}$ is within a radius $\delta$. The overall uncertainty set $P(\mathcal{D}, \delta)$ is "SA-rectangular," meaning it's a Cartesian product of these individual state-action uncertainty sets.
- **Uniform Ergodicity**: A core assumption is that the *nominal* MDP is uniformly ergodic (a stronger version of the unichain property), implying that any stationary, Markovian, and deterministic policy induces a Markov chain that converges to a unique steady state. The mixing time $t_{mix}$ (or equivalently, minorization time $t_{minorize}$) is a key complexity parameter.
- **Constrained Adversarial Power**: A crucial assumption (Assumption 2) is placed on the uncertainty radius $\delta$. For KL-divergence, $\delta \le O(p_{\wedge} / m_{\vee}^2)$ and for $f_k$-divergence, $\delta \le O(p_{\wedge} / (k m_{\vee}^2))$, where $p_{\wedge}$ is the minimum non-zero probability of the nominal transitions, and $m_{\vee}$ is an upper bound on the minorization step parameter. This restriction ensures that even under adversarial perturbations within the uncertainty set, all resulting MDPs remain uniformly ergodic, with their minorization times bounded by a constant multiple of the nominal MDP's minorization time. This is vital because standard average-reward Bellman equations are only well-defined for unichain MDPs.

**Algorithms:**

Two distinct algorithms are proposed, both designed to be "a priori knowledge-free" regarding the mixing time $t_{mix}$:

1. **Reduction to DR-DMDP (Algorithm 2)**:
 - This algorithm leverages the relationship between average-reward and discounted-reward MDPs. It reduces the DR-AMDP problem to an auxiliary Distributionally Robust Discounted Markov Decision Process (DR-DMDP).
 - An empirical DR-DMDP model is constructed using the gathered samples $\hat{p}_{s,a}$ and the defined uncertainty sets.
 - A discount factor $\gamma$ is carefully chosen as $\gamma = 1 - 1/\sqrt{n}$, where $n$ is the total sample size. This choice balances the bias introduced by discounting against the statistical error from finite samples.
 - Value iteration is applied to this auxiliary empirical DR-DMDP to compute an optimal value function $V^*_{\hat{P}}$ and an optimal policy $\hat{\pi}^*$.
 - The robust average reward is then estimated as $V^*_{\hat{P}} / \sqrt{n}$.

2. **Anchored DR-AMDP (Algorithm 3)**:
 - This approach modifies the uncertainty set of controlled transition kernels directly. It introduces a fixed "anchoring state" $s_0$ and a calibrated "anchoring strength" parameter $\xi = 1/\sqrt{n}$.
 - The modified uncertainty set $\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{s,a}$ for each $(s,a)$ is defined such that any perturbed transition kernel $q$ within it is a convex combination of an original perturbed kernel $p$ (from the divergence-based uncertainty set centered at $\hat{p}_{s,a}$) and a Dirac mass on the anchoring state $s_0$, i.e., $q = (1-\xi)p + \xi\mathbf{1}_{s_0}$.
 - This anchoring mechanism guarantees that all MDPs within the modified uncertainty set are uniformly ergodic.
 - The algorithm then solves the empirical DR average-reward Bellman equation using this modified uncertainty set to obtain an optimal relative value function $v^*_{\hat{P}}$ and an optimal robust average reward $g^*_{\hat{P}}$.

**Analytical Approach:**

The theoretical analysis relies on several key techniques:

- **Duality for Divergence Constraints**: For KL-divergence and $f_k$-divergence, the problem of minimizing expected value over the uncertainty set (i.e., $\Gamma_{P_{s,a}}(V)$) can be reformulated using strong duality. This transforms the problem into a convex optimization suitable for analysis.
- **Concentration Inequalities**: Bernstein's inequality is used to bound the error between empirical transition probabilities $\hat{p}_{s,a}$ and the true nominal probabilities $p_{s,a}$, establishing a high-probability "good event" region where the empirical model is sufficiently close to the true model.
- **Span Semi-Norm**: The analysis leverages the span semi-norm of value functions in MDPs, relating it to the minorization time. This connection is crucial for bounding errors in average-reward settings.
- **Relationship between Discounted and Average Rewards**: For the reduction method, a fundamental lemma connects discounted value functions $V^{\pi}_P$ with the corresponding average reward $g^{\pi}_P$ using the discount factor $\gamma$ and minorization time $t_{minorize}$.
- **Envelope Theorem**: For analyzing the $f_k$-divergence, the Envelope Theorem is utilized to characterize the variational behavior of the dual optimization problem.

By combining these methods, the authors establish finite-sample convergence rates and error bounds for both proposed algorithms under the specified conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

This research delivers the first finite-sample convergence guarantees for Distributionally Robust Average-Reward Markov Decision Processes (DR-AMDPs) in a tabular setting under a generative model. The main findings and results are detailed below:

1. **First Finite-Sample Convergence Guarantee for DR-AMDPs**: The paper establishes that both proposed algorithms achieve a sample complexity of $\tilde{O}(|S||A|t_{mix}^2 \varepsilon^{-2})$ for estimating the optimal policy and the robust average reward to within an $\varepsilon$ accuracy. This result is presented under KL and $f_k$-divergence-based uncertainty sets, provided the uncertainty radius $\delta$ is sufficiently small. Here, $|S|$ is the number of states, $|A|$ is the number of actions, $t_{mix}$ is the mixing time of the nominal MDP (or $t_{minorize}$, which is equivalent up to constants), and $\varepsilon$ is the target accuracy. This rate is noted to be optimal in its dependence on $|S||A|$ and $\varepsilon$ when compared to standard (non-robust) average-reward RL literature.

2. **Addressing a Fundamental Modeling Challenge**: The work identifies and resolves a critical issue in DR-AMDPs: conventional uncertainty sets can include MDPs that are not unichain, which would invalidate standard Bellman equations used for average-reward problems.
 - The authors derive structural conditions on the uncertainty set (specifically, the constraint on the uncertainty radius $\delta$ related to $p_{\wedge}$ and $m_{\vee}$) that guarantee uniform ergodicity for *all* MDPs within the uncertainty set. This ensures the stability and well-definedness of the problem's theoretical foundations.
 - For KL-divergence, $\delta \le \frac{1}{8m_{\vee}^2 p_{\wedge}}$, and for $f_k$-divergence, $\delta \le \frac{1}{\max\{8,4k\}m_{\vee}^2 p_{\wedge}}$. Under these conditions, the minorization time of any perturbed MDP in the uncertainty set is bounded by $2t_{minorize}$ of the nominal MDP.

3. **Developed and Analyzed Two Algorithms**:
 - **Reduction to DR-DMDP (Algorithm 2)**: This algorithm translates the DR-AMDP problem into a DR-Discounted MDP (DR-DMDP) using a calibrated discount factor $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{n}$. The analysis of this reduction yields a stability-sensitive sample complexity bound for DR-DMDPs of $\tilde{O}(|S||A|t_{mix}^2(1-\gamma)^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2})$. This represents a refinement over existing DR-DMDP bounds by introducing a dependence on mixing time, where the effective horizon dependence improves to $(1-\gamma)^{-2}$ for uniformly ergodic nominal kernels. This refined DR-DMDP bound is instrumental in achieving the $\varepsilon^{-2}$ dependence for DR-AMDPs.
 - **Anchored DR-AMDP (Algorithm 3)**: This novel algorithm modifies the uncertainty set by introducing an anchoring state and a calibration probability $\xi = 1/\sqrt{n}$. The theoretical analysis demonstrates that the output of this anchored algorithm coincides with that of the reduction approach under a suitable choice of the anchoring parameter, and it achieves the same $\tilde{O}(|S||A|t_{mix}^2 \varepsilon^{-2})$ sample complexity.

4. **A Priori Knowledge-Free Design**: Both proposed algorithms are designed to operate without requiring prior knowledge of model-specific parameters, such as the mixing time $t_{mix}$. This enhances their practical applicability.

5. **Empirical Validation**: Numerical experiments, including on a "Hard MDP family" instance, were conducted. These experiments empirically validate the theoretical $\sqrt{n}$-convergence rate of the algorithms, showing an error reduction proportional to $n^{-1/2}$ on a log-log scale. Comparisons with existing baselines like DR RVI Q-learning showed favorable performance for the proposed algorithms, and experiments on larger-scale MDPs demonstrated their practical effectiveness, even when theoretical assumptions on $\delta$ might be mildly violated.

In summary, the paper provides a unified theoretical framework that links robustness, stability, and finite-sample guarantees for average-reward reinforcement learning, marking a foundational contribution to DR-RL theory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper holds significance for both the theoretical understanding and practical application of reinforcement learning in uncertain environments. Its potential impact spans several areas:

1. **Advancement in Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning (DR-RL) Theory**: The work fills a critical theoretical void in the DR-RL literature by providing the first finite-sample complexity guarantees for the average-reward setting. Previously, theoretical developments in DR-RL predominantly focused on discounted or finite-horizon problems. This paper extends the rigorous statistical analysis to the average-reward criterion, which is essential for understanding long-term system performance under uncertainty. This establishes a foundational piece of theory necessary for more complex DR-RL studies.

2. **Enabling Reliable Long-Term Control in Real-World Applications**: The average-reward objective is crucial for applications demanding stable and sustained performance over extended periods. By developing algorithms that offer robust policies and value estimates for DR-AMDPs, the research directly impacts domains such as:
 - **Robotics and Autonomous Systems**: Policies designed for stable, continuous operation rather than temporary gains.
 - **Operations Research**: Improved resilience in systems like inventory management, supply chains, and resource allocation, where long-run average costs or throughputs are key.
 - **Healthcare and Energy Management**: More robust strategies for optimizing patient care pathways or energy grid operations, ensuring sustained effectiveness despite environmental fluctuations.
 The ability to learn robust policies with quantifiable sample efficiency can increase confidence in deploying RL systems in safety-critical and long-running applications.

3. **Unified Treatment of Robustness, Stability, and Sample Efficiency**: The paper offers a cohesive framework that connects robustness to environmental uncertainty with the stability properties of Markov chains (uniform ergodicity) and the finite-sample requirements for learning. By demonstrating how a constrained uncertainty radius ensures the uniform ergodicity of perturbed MDPs, it provides a crucial link between robust problem formulation and the applicability of standard average-reward solution techniques. This integrated perspective can guide future research in designing robust and stable learning algorithms.

4. **Improved Algorithmic Design**: The proposed "Reduction to DR-DMDP" and "Anchored DR-AMDP" algorithms provide practical, a priori knowledge-free methods for tackling DR-AMDPs. The conceptual insights behind these algorithms, particularly the anchored approach, might inspire new design paradigms for robust learning in other complex sequential decision-making problems.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research**: The work explicitly identifies limitations and proposes clear avenues for future research, including:
 - **Generalizing Ergodicity Assumptions**: Extending results to weakly communicating or multichain MDPs would broaden applicability beyond uniformly ergodic settings.
 - **Exploring Broader Uncertainty Sets**: Investigating other uncertainty metrics beyond KL and $f_k$-divergence (e.g., $l_p$-balls, Wasserstein metrics) could lead to more flexible and powerful robust models.
 - **Deepening the Understanding of Robustness-Stability Trade-offs**: Further exploring the relationship between the uncertainty radius $\delta$ and the system's ergodicity properties could lead to more nuanced understanding and potentially relaxation of current assumptions.

In essence, this paper significantly advances the theoretical underpinnings of robust reinforcement learning, moving it closer to practical applicability in real-world scenarios that demand both long-term stability and resilience to environmental uncertainty.
