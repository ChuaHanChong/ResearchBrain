# Frequency-Conditioned Flow Matching for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FreqFM is a frequency-conditioned Flow Matching framework for vision-language-action models. Applying a DCT along the action horizon, it builds a spectrum-matched Gaussian source, PSD-normalizes and adaptively reweights the per-frequency velocity-regression objective, and clips classifier-free guidance residuals to a per-frequency transport budget. It needs no backbone change and lifts LIBERO-Plus success by 9.3 points.

## Problem

- Flow Matching action experts generate in temporal coordinates, never explicitly modeling the highly non-uniform frequency energy of correlated robot action trajectories.
- Isotropic white sources mismatch action spectra, so the linear transport path's relative second-moment spectrum shifts with time across frequency bins.
- Raw temporal MSE lets high-energy low-frequency components dominate gradients, and single-coefficient guidance ignores per-frequency transport scale, injecting jerky motion.

## Method

- ==Spectrum-matched source==: per-frequency, per-DoF power estimated offline from training actions rescales a white Gaussian, preserving relative spectrum along the linear path.
- ==PSD-normalized adaptive objective==: coefficient-wise losses divided by power raised to a tempered exponent, then reweighted by learned log-scales via likelihood-based multi-task weighting.
- ==Spectral transport-budgeted CFG==: guidance residual radially clipped into a ball of radius set by each frequency's reference transport scale, recomputed each solver step.

## Results

- LIBERO-Plus gains **5.5** and **9.3** points over paired temporal baselines; largest gains on camera, robot, and noise perturbations, correlating negatively with baseline success.
- LIBERO averages rise **0.7** and **1.2** points to **97.9** near saturation; VLA-Arena totals rise **2.6** and **4.2** points, though extrapolation regresses.
- Real humanoid suite averages **67.8** versus **57.8** success over six tasks; budgeting guidance cuts mean absolute jerk **56%**, from **0.442** to **0.193**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Matching source second moments to target spectra keeps the relative spectrum constant along transport time, the single most important of the three components.
> - Loss normalization matters where signal energy spans orders of magnitude: unnormalized regression silently discards the low-energy corrections fine alignment needs.
> - Fixed training-set spectral statistics fail when deployment action statistics shift, so extrapolation tasks regress; condition-dependent spectra remain open.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot control has increasingly adopted generative models, particularly Flow Matching-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, to generate multi-step robot actions. These models, exemplified by Diffusion Policy (Chi et al. 2024), π0 (Black et al. 2025), and π0.5 (Physical Intelligence et al. 2025), learn to transport samples from a simple source distribution to desired action chunks through a conditional velocity field. However, robot actions are not merely unstructured vectors; they are temporally correlated trajectories with distinct frequency components. Low frequencies typically represent global motion trends, while higher frequencies encode localized corrections. Existing Flow Matching VLA models predominantly operate in temporal coordinates, learning these temporal correlations implicitly without explicitly modeling or systematically leveraging the heterogeneous energy distribution across different frequency components of actions.

Prior research has acknowledged the importance of frequency structure in robot action generation and other generative tasks. For instance, FAST (Pertsch et al. 2025) employs Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)-based tokens for autoregressive VLA models, and Frequency Autoregressive FreqPolicy (Zhong et al. 2025) progressively generates hierarchical frequency components. Other approaches like Frequency-Consistency FreqPolicy (Su et al. 2025) and FocalPolicy (He et al. 2026) align DCT-domain velocities or regularize frequency structure across action chunks. Methods such as FGO (Wang 2026) and FAFM (Guo et al. 2026) guide diffusion or combine DCT-domain Flow Matching with derivative supervision for smoother actions. In the domain of image diffusion, FreSca (Huang et al. 2025) and frequency-decoupled guidance (Sadat et al. 2025) scale classifier-free guidance (CFG) predictions in frequency bands.

The current work, FreqFM, positions itself within this landscape by elevating action frequency from an implicit property to an explicit conditioning dimension that spans the *entire* Flow Matching generation pipeline. Unlike previous methods that might use frequency for tokenization, consistency, or prescribed sub-frequency traversal, FreqFM integrates frequency-dependent statistics into three fundamental stages of Flow Matching: source distribution construction, objective optimization, and inference guidance. This approach directly addresses the challenge of non-uniform energy distribution across frequencies and the implicit learning of temporal correlations, aiming to provide a more structured and efficient mechanism for generating precise and robust robot actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for FreqFM stems from a fundamental observation regarding robot actions: they are not arbitrary sequences of values but rather temporally correlated trajectories. These trajectories possess distinct frequency components that correspond to different scales of motion. Specifically, low-frequency components typically capture broad, global motion trends, such as reaching a target location, while high-frequency components encode finer, local adjustments and corrections, like precise object manipulation.

A critical characteristic of these frequency components is their highly non-uniform energy distribution. Empirical analysis presented in the paper indicates that, on real-robot data with a horizon of 30 timesteps, energy can vary by more than seven orders of magnitude across frequency bins, with over 99.9% concentrated in the lowest three bins. Similarly, on simulation benchmarks, the range spans 3.8 orders of magnitude on LIBERO and 2.6 orders on VLA-Arena. This substantial disparity suggests that different frequency components contribute disproportionately to the overall action, and models that treat them uniformly might be suboptimal.

Existing Flow Matching-based VLA models, while effective, typically generate actions in raw temporal coordinates. They attempt to learn the inherent temporal correlations and frequency heterogeneity implicitly through neural network architectures. However, a frequency-selective transformation, which would be crucial for effectively handling this heterogeneity, generally corresponds to complex, dense cross-timestep couplings when viewed in temporal coordinates. In contrast, in frequency coordinates, such transformations can often be parameterized more compactly and explicitly.

This observation motivates the central question driving this research: can action frequency be elevated from an implicit trajectory property to an explicit modeling dimension for the full Flow Matching pipeline? The authors hypothesize that by making frequency an explicit condition, it becomes possible to systematically exploit the structural properties of robot actions, leading to more accurate, robust, and controllable action generation.

Therefore, the key objective of FreqFM is to introduce a frequency-conditioned Flow Matching framework that explicitly leverages action frequency across three core stages of the generation process:
1. **Source distribution construction:** To better match the spectral characteristics of target actions.
2. **Training objective optimization:** To adaptively balance the learning signal across different frequency components, preventing high-energy low frequencies from dominating the learning process.
3. **Inference guidance:** To provide frequency-aware control over the classifier-free guidance mechanism, enabling more precise and smoother trajectory generation.

By achieving these objectives, FreqFM aims to improve the performance, robustness, and trajectory quality of VLA models in complex robotic manipulation tasks, both in simulation and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FreqFM introduces a frequency-conditioned Flow Matching framework that integrates frequency-dependent statistics into the core components of a VLA model's action generation pipeline. This is achieved by transforming robot action chunks into frequency coordinates and then leveraging these coordinates for source distribution definition, objective optimization, and inference guidance.

**Preliminaries:**
The framework begins by representing an action chunk `a ∈ R^(H×D)` (where `H` is the horizon and `D` is the action dimension) in Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) frequency coordinates. The orthonormal DCT-II matrix `C ∈ R^(H×H)` is applied along the horizon axis: `˜a = Ca`, where `˜a` represents the action chunk in frequency coordinates. The inverse DCT (`a = C^⊤˜a`) is used to recover the temporal action chunk after processing. The rationale for using DCT-II is its effectiveness as a fixed, data-independent basis for highly correlated signals, which robot actions typically are. FreqFM operates on these DCT coefficients for "smooth" action dimensions, implying that joint positions or velocities, rather than gripper states, are typically targeted.

**Spectrum-Matched Source:**
Traditional Flow Matching typically uses a centered isotropic Gaussian source `q0 = N(0, I)`. When transformed by DCT, this source remains spectrally flat (i.e., `E[˜z_0,k,d^2] = 1` for all frequencies `k` and dimensions `d`). This contrasts sharply with robot actions, which exhibit highly non-uniform power spectra. Consequently, connecting a flat source to a spectrally rich target via a linear path results in a reference path whose second-moment spectrum changes shape over time, introducing a spectral mismatch.

FreqFM addresses this by shaping the source distribution's frequency-wise scale to match the observed action spectrum. It estimates the root-mean-square (RMS) coefficient scale `A_k,d` and corresponding per-frequency power `P_k,d = A_k,d^2` from the training action data for each frequency `k` and smooth action dimension `d`. The source is then sampled in frequency coordinates as `˜z_0,k,d = √(P_k,d) * ε_k,d`, where `ε_k,d ∼ N(0, 1)`. This results in `q0 = N(0, diag(P))`, ensuring that the source shares the same relative spectral structure as the target trajectory. This spectrum-matching preserves the relative second-moment spectrum along the linear reference path, avoiding the frequency-dependent rescaling that occurs with a white source.

**PSD-Normalized Adaptive Objective:**
In frequency coordinates, directly minimizing a raw Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective (`E∥vθ(˜zt, t, c) - ˜ut∥2^2`) would lead to low-frequency, high-energy components dominating the loss and gradients. This is because these components naturally have larger squared errors for the same *relative* prediction error.

FreqFM mitigates this by first normalizing the coefficient-wise regression errors. The squared error `ℓ_k,d` for frequency `k` and dimension `d` is normalized by `(P_k,d + ε)^ρ`, where `P_k,d` is the estimated power spectrum and `ρ` is a tempering exponent (set to 0.25). This `PSD-normalization` reduces the power-induced dynamic range of the individual loss terms. Following this, a likelihood-based multi-task weighting formulation (Kendall et al. 2018) is applied. FreqFM learns a separate log scale `s_k,d = log τ_k,d^2` for each `(k, d)` regression term, effectively allowing the model to adaptively weigh the contributions of different frequency components to the total loss. This preconditioning limits early domination by high-energy components during training and results in a more balanced optimization across the frequency spectrum.

**Spectral Transport-Budgeted Classifier-Free Guidance (STB-CFG):**
Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) typically applies a single extrapolation coefficient (`λ0`) to the difference between conditional (`˜v_cond`) and unconditional (`˜v_uncond`) velocity predictions. However, given the vastly different magnitudes of action power across frequencies, a uniform extrapolation can lead to excessive and undesirable motion in high-frequency components, potentially causing jerky or unstable robot behavior.

FreqFM introduces STB-CFG to constrain this guidance. It defines a frequency-resolved reference scale for transport velocity based on `E[˜u_t,k,d^2] = 2P_k,d`, derived from the spectrum-matched source and target. A frequency-wise metric `M_k = diag_d(P_k,d + ε)` and a reference radius `R_k` are established, where `∥x∥^2_M^-1k = x^⊤M^-1k x`. This metric effectively scales the admissible residual in raw units with `√(P_k,d)`. During inference, the additional CFG extrapolation residual `w_0,k(t) = λ0Δ_k(t)` is measured in these reference-transport units. If its magnitude `b_k(t) = ∥w_0,k(t)∥_M^-1k` exceeds a budget `βR_k` (where `β` is a shared multiplier), the residual is radially clipped to the boundary of the `βR_k` ball. The guided velocity then becomes `˜v_STB,k(t) = ˜v_cond,k(t) + w*_k(t)`. This mechanism prevents excessive extrapolation in low-power, high-frequency bins, which tend to generate fast, jerky motions, while allowing in-budget residuals to pass through unchanged.

**Integration and Experimental Setup:**
FreqFM is designed to be compatible with existing Flow Matching VLA backbones, requiring no modification to the underlying vision-language model architecture. The framework is integrated into the action expert component of these models. Experiments were conducted using π0 and π0.5 backbones from the LeRobot codebase. Training involved 20k optimization steps on two NVIDIA A800 GPUs. The evaluation encompassed three simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, VLA-Arena) and six real-robot tasks on an AgiBot Expedition A2 humanoid, measuring task success rate (SR) and subtask success rate (Sub-SR). Ablation studies were performed to assess the contribution of each proposed component.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FreqFM across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks demonstrated consistent performance improvements over temporal Flow Matching baselines.

**Simulation Results:**

- **LIBERO:** On the four LIBERO suites, FreqFM exhibited improvements of +0.7 and +1.2 percentage points (p.p.) for the π0 and π0.5 backbones, respectively. While these gains appear modest, they were observed in a setting where most methods were already approaching saturation, indicating a positive contribution in a highly competent domain.
- **LIBERO-Plus:** This benchmark, which introduces seven perturbation families to test robustness, showed the most significant gains. FreqFM achieved improvements of +5.5 p.p. on π0 and +9.3 p.p. on π0.5. Notably, the largest gains occurred on perturbations where the temporal baseline was weakest, specifically "Camera," "Robot," and "Noise." The gain negatively correlated with the baseline success rate (e.g., -0.95 correlation on π0.5), suggesting that FreqFM particularly enhances robustness in challenging, low-performing scenarios for the baseline. Conversely, on "Light" and "Background" perturbations where the baseline already performed strongly, minor regressions were observed for π0.
- **VLA-Arena:** On VLA-Arena, FreqFM improved the total success rate by +2.6 p.p. on π0 and +4.2 p.p. on π0.5. However, the "Extrapolation" category showed a regression on both backbones. This was attributed to FreqFM's reliance on fixed, training-set-level spectral statistics for its source spectrum and transport budget, which may not generalize well to tasks in the "Extrapolation" category whose action statistics deviate significantly from the training distribution.
- **General Trend on Backbones:** Across all three simulation benchmarks, FreqFM consistently yielded larger gains when integrated with the π0.5 backbone compared to π0. This suggests that the pre-training of π0.5, which includes exposure to DCT-based FAST action tokens, might predispose its backbone to better leverage frequency-domain action representations.

**Real-World Evaluation:**

- FreqFM was evaluated on an AgiBot Expedition A2 humanoid robot across six tasks covering single-arm, bimanual, and whole-upper-body control.
- The framework demonstrated a notable average overall success rate gain of +10 p.p. (from 57.8% to 67.8%) and an average subtask success rate gain of +8.4 p.p. (from 70.6% to 79.0%) across the six tasks.
- The gains were particularly pronounced in tasks requiring fine positional alignment, such as "Cup Stacking" (+23.3 p.p.), "Folder Filing" (+13.4 p.p.), and "Stamp & Handover" (+13.3 p.p.). These tasks necessitate small terminal corrections, which correspond to low-energy, high-frequency components of the action spectrum. In a raw MSE objective, these components would typically be weighted least due to their small magnitudes.
- Tasks with looser placement tolerances, such as "Kitchen Tidy" and "Table Cleanup," showed more modest gains (+3.3 p.p. and +6.7 p.p., respectively). "Toy Storage," an easier task where both policies were already near the ceiling, showed no difference in success rate.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Necessity of Frequency Coordinates:** A temporal control experiment, which implemented time-domain counterparts of FreqFM's three stages, showed modest improvements over the temporal Flow Matching baseline (+0.3 and +2.5 p.p. on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, respectively). These gains were considerably smaller than those achieved by the full FreqFM, indicating that the explicit frequency representation is a crucial factor for the observed performance enhancements.
- **Component Contributions (on LIBERO-Plus):**
 - **Matched Source:** Removing the spectrum-matched source had the largest negative impact, reducing performance by 4.2 p.p.
 - **Adaptive Objective:** Removing the PSD-normalized adaptive objective resulted in a 3.6 p.p. reduction.
 - **Transport Budget:** Removing the spectral transport-budgeted guidance (reverting to standard CFG) led to a 1.9 p.p. reduction in success rate. While its impact on task completion was smaller, this component significantly improved trajectory quality. Specifically, at a comparable success rate on LIBERO, budgeting the guidance residual reduced the mean absolute jerk of executed arm trajectories by 56% (from 0.442 to 0.193). This confirms that the transport budget effectively controls fast motions in high-frequency bins, which are typically under-weighted in a raw MSE objective and susceptible to excessive unbudgeted extrapolation.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that explicitly incorporating frequency conditioning throughout the Flow Matching pipeline enhances the performance and robustness of VLA models, particularly in tasks requiring fine motor control, and improves the quality of generated robot trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of FreqFM represents a methodological advancement in the design of Flow Matching-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with several significant implications for robotic control and broader generative modeling.

Firstly, FreqFM directly addresses a fundamental limitation of existing Flow Matching VLA models: their implicit handling of the inherent temporal correlations and non-uniform energy distribution across frequency components in robot actions. By elevating action frequency to an explicit conditioning dimension, the framework provides a more principled and structured approach to modeling these complex trajectory characteristics. This is significant because it moves beyond merely learning correlations implicitly in temporal space, offering a transparent and manipulable mechanism for controlling different scales of motion.

Secondly, the consistent performance improvements across diverse benchmarks highlight FreqFM's practical utility. The gains observed in LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, particularly the substantial +9.3 percentage-point gain on LIBERO-Plus, demonstrate enhanced robustness against various perturbations. This improved resilience is critical for deploying robotic systems in real-world, unstructured environments where conditions can vary. The framework's ability to boost performance on challenging tasks where baselines struggle underscores its capacity to push the boundaries of what current VLA models can achieve in terms of reliability.

Thirdly, the real-world validation on the AgiBot Expedition A2 humanoid robot provides empirical evidence of FreqFM's effectiveness in practical applications. The significant gains in tasks requiring fine positional alignment, such as "Cup Stacking" and "Folder Filing," indicate that the explicit frequency conditioning enables more precise and accurate control. This is particularly impactful for tasks demanding high dexterity and manipulation, which are often bottlenecked by the ability of policies to generate subtle, high-frequency corrections.

Fourthly, the ablation studies reveal the individual contributions of FreqFM's components and, crucially, the importance of explicit frequency representation. The finding that temporal counterparts of the proposed components yield only minor gains, whereas the full frequency-conditioned framework achieves substantial improvements, confirms that merely applying similar ideas in the time domain is insufficient. The explicit transformation into frequency coordinates is a key enabler of these benefits. Furthermore, the ability of the spectral transport-budgeted guidance to drastically reduce trajectory jerk, even when its impact on task success rate is smaller, points to a direct improvement in the quality and smoothness of generated actions. Smoother trajectories are paramount for safety, energy efficiency, and wear-and-tear reduction in physical robot systems.

Finally, FreqFM's design allows for integration into existing Flow Matching VLA backbones without requiring architectural modifications. This ease of integration suggests a broad applicability, enabling a wide range of VLA models to benefit from frequency conditioning with minimal implementation overhead. The identified limitations, such as the reliance on fixed, training-set-level spectral statistics, also open clear avenues for future research. Exploring condition-dependent frequency statistics, where the spectrum-matched source and guidance budget adapt to the current observation or instruction, could further enhance the model's adaptability and generalization capabilities, potentially mitigating issues observed in tasks like VLA-Arena's "Extrapolation" category. The potential extension of frequency-conditioned action generation to other generative architectures, such as world-action models, could also broaden its impact across the field of robot learning.

In conclusion, FreqFM offers a structured and effective approach to integrate frequency-domain insights into Flow Matching for VLA models. Its demonstrated improvements in performance, robustness, precision, and trajectory quality, alongside its modular design, positions it as a significant contribution with the potential to influence future developments in generalist robot control and generative action modeling.
