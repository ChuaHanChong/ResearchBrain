# Single-Rollout Asynchronous Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Single-Rollout Asynchronous Optimization (SAO) framework enhances asynchronous reinforcement learning for Large Language Models by stabilizing training and mitigating off-policy effects through single-trajectory processing and improved value model training. This approach leads to sustained performance gains and faster adaptation in complex agentic coding and reasoning tasks compared to synchronous baselines, achieving 97.3% accuracy on AIME2025.

## Problem

- Existing synchronous reinforcement learning (RL) pipelines for Large Language Models (LLMs) are inefficient for agentic tasks with highly variable and often long rollout lengths, leading to significant idle GPU time.
- Asynchronous RL, while improving throughput, introduces policy lag and off-policy effects that destabilize training, an area whose impact on stability and effectiveness has been largely unexplored.
- Popular group-wise sampling methods (e.g., GRPO) are incompatible with asynchronous and online agentic settings, as they reintroduce latency and cannot handle single-trajectory feedback.

## Method

- Introduces ==Direct Double-Sided Importance Sampling (DIS)== which uses the rollout policy as a direct behavior proxy and employs token-level masking to impose a strict trust region, preventing unstable updates.
- Adopts a **single-rollout** approach for immediate trajectory processing, mitigates variance by updating the value model more frequently than the policy, and stabilizes value training by freezing attention layers.
- Develops a ==Skip-Observation Token-level GAE== for agentic tasks, modifying the Bellman target to bypass environment observation tokens and ensure advantage estimation relies purely on model outputs.

## Results

- SAO consistently achieved higher accuracy across agentic coding (SWE-Bench Verified) and math reasoning benchmarks (e.g., **97.3%** on AIME2025 vs. **80.4%** for SFT baseline) and demonstrated stable training for up to **1000 steps**, while baselines often collapsed earlier.
- Ablation studies confirmed that faster value model updates and freezing attention layers significantly contribute to performance and training stability, with a value-based critic outperforming a running-mean baseline (**97.3%** vs. **79.8%** on AIME2025).
- In a simulated online learning task, SAO demonstrated rapid policy realignment and superior adaptation to dynamically changing reward preferences compared to a running-mean baseline, showing faster recovery and higher convergence levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asynchronous RL for LLMs can be stabilized by directly managing policy lag and off-policy effects through token-level importance sampling with aggressive clipping, rather than relying on historical policy probabilities.
> - Effective single-rollout learning in asynchronous settings requires a robust value model, achieved through faster updates and regularization (e.g., freezing attention layers), to provide stable advantage estimates.
> - For multi-turn agentic trajectories with alternating actions and observations, a specialized Skip-Observation GAE is necessary to accurately attribute advantages to model actions by ignoring environmental feedback tokens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) is increasingly relying on post-training reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance capabilities beyond supervised pre-training. This shift has seen methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) become prevalent for optimizing LLMs based on human feedback or derived reward signals. Historically, many LLM RL pipelines have operated synchronously and in a batch-interleaved manner. This approach entails collecting a complete batch of rollouts before initiating any optimization, which can be inefficient for agentic tasks characterized by highly variable rollout lengths. Long trajectories can become "stragglers," leading to significant idle time for GPU clusters while waiting for the slowest computations to complete.

Asynchronous RL has emerged as a strategy to mitigate this inefficiency by processing rollouts continuously as they become available, thereby improving computational resource utilization and wall-clock efficiency. However, existing asynchronous RL systems have predominantly focused on throughput optimization, often leaving aspects of training stability and task effectiveness relatively unexplored. A notable challenge in this context is the increased off-policy effects arising from trajectories being generated by multiple versions of the policy model during asynchronous operations. Furthermore, popular group-wise sampling methods, such as GRPO, are not inherently compatible with asynchronous agentic training. These methods typically require a group of responses per prompt for advantage estimation, introducing latency as the system waits for all members of a group to complete, which exacerbates off-policy issues and is incompatible with online settings that provide single-trajectory feedback.

This research introduces Single-Rollout Asynchronous Optimization (SAO) as a direct response to these stability and off-policy challenges within asynchronous RL for LLMs. It aims to bridge the gap between the efficiency benefits of asynchronous RL and the need for stable and effective training, particularly in agentic and online learning scenarios where existing group-wise methods are suboptimal. By proposing a single-rollout approach combined with specific value-model training designs and a robust clipping strategy, SAO positions itself as an advancement in the practical deployment of RL for complex LLM agentic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an asynchronous reinforcement learning framework, termed Single-Rollout Asynchronous Optimization (SAO), that effectively addresses the challenges of training stability and off-policy effects in the context of agentic LLM tasks. The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current RL pipelines for LLMs:

1. **Inefficiency of Synchronous RL for Agentic Tasks:** Current LLM RL pipelines are largely synchronous and batch-interleaved. While functional, this design is inefficient for agentic tasks that often involve long and highly variable-length trajectories (e.g., coding, complex reasoning). In such scenarios, short rollouts finish quickly, but the system must wait for the longest "straggler" rollouts to complete before optimization can proceed, leading to underutilization of computational resources and increased wall-clock time. This inefficiency directly motivates the exploration of asynchronous approaches.

2. **Unexplored Stability and Effectiveness in Asynchronous RL:** While asynchronous RL offers a solution to the efficiency problem by allowing continuous processing of rollouts, existing implementations have primarily focused on maximizing throughput. The implications of asynchrony on training stability and the overall effectiveness of the learned policy for complex tasks have remained largely underexplored. Asynchrony introduces "policy lag," where rollouts are generated by older policy versions while the training process updates a newer version, leading to more pronounced and unpredictable off-policy effects that can destabilize training.

3. **Mismatched Group-wise Sampling with Asynchronous and Online Settings:** Frameworks like GRPO, which rely on sampling a group of responses for each prompt and averaging group-level advantages, pose a particular challenge.
 - **Latency-driven Off-Policy:** In an asynchronous setting, group-wise sampling requires waiting for all rollouts within a group to complete, which can reintroduce latency and delay feedback to the learner, intensifying off-policy issues.
 - **Incompatibility with Online/Agentic Feedback:** Many online or complex agentic environments naturally provide only a single trajectory feedback per prompt. Group-wise sampling methods are fundamentally incompatible with such feedback structures, limiting their applicability in these crucial settings.

To address these motivations, the key objectives for SAO are:

- **Stabilize Training under Policy Lag:** To ensure asynchronous RL training remains stable despite the varying delays between rollout generation and policy updates.
- **Reduce Off-Policy Effects:** To minimize the divergence between the behavior policy (used for data collection) and the target policy (being optimized), which can lead to inefficient or unstable learning.
- **Enable Effective Single-Rollout Optimization:** To develop a practical framework for RL that can effectively learn from individual trajectories, moving away from the limitations of group-wise sampling.
- **Improve Generalization:** To enable the model to generalize well by reducing off-policy effects and providing stable optimization.
- **Demonstrate Adaptability in Dynamic Environments:** To show that the single-rollout strategy is particularly effective in simulated online learning settings where the environment may evolve.

By achieving these objectives, SAO aims to provide a more robust and efficient reinforcement learning paradigm for post-training LLMs, especially for demanding agentic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Single-rollout Asynchronous Optimization (SAO) framework is designed to enhance the stability and effectiveness of asynchronous reinforcement learning for agentic LLMs. Its methodology centers on three core components: a modified importance sampling strategy, a single-rollout approach with improved value model training, and a specialized GAE estimator for agentic tasks.

#### 3.1. Stabilizing Asynchronous RL via Direct Double-Sided Importance Sampling (DIS)

Asynchronous RL inherently introduces "policy lag" where rollouts might be generated by multiple versions of the policy model (`π_rollout`) while the training process updates the current policy (`π_θ`). Tracking exact behavior probabilities (`π_θ_old`) for importance sampling becomes computationally prohibitive due to the need to maintain an extensive history of model checkpoints.

SAO addresses this by:
- **Direct Behavior Proxy:** It directly uses `π_rollout` as the behavior proxy and `π_θ` for importance sampling, effectively computing the probability ratio `r_t(θ) = π_θ / π_rollout` and eliminating the need for `π_θ_old`. This leverages log-probabilities already generated during rollout, reducing computational overhead.
- **Double-Sided Calibration Token-Level Masking:** Unlike standard PPO clipping which clips only on one side (e.g., `A > 0, r_t(θ) > 1+ϵ_h`), SAO imposes a stricter trust region. Tokens whose probability ratio `r_t(θ)` falls outside the interval `[1 − ϵ_ℓ, 1 + ϵ_h]` are masked from gradient computation. This aggressive clipping aims to prevent instabilities caused by extreme policy divergence, promoting stable updates. The objective function is formulated to incorporate this masking through a calibration function `f(x; ϵ_ℓ, ϵ_h)`.

#### 3.2. Reducing Off-Policy with Single Rollout

Current group-wise sampling methods like GRPO can introduce "imbalanced generation" bias and worsen off-policy effects in asynchronous settings because training must wait for the slowest sample in a group. SAO replaces this with a single-rollout approach, where each generated trajectory is immediately available for training. This design, however, can lead to high variance in gradient estimation, which SAO mitigates through strategies focused on the value model:

- **Faster Value Update than Policy:** SAO decouples the optimization frequencies of the policy (`π_θ`) and the value model (`V_ϕ`). For every single gradient update applied to the policy, the value network undergoes `K` updates (K=2 in experiments). This strategy allows the value estimates to adapt more quickly and accurately to the current policy distribution before being used for advantage computation, thereby reducing variance.
- **Stabilizing Value Model Training via Parameter Freezing:** Pilot experiments revealed that value model training could be unstable, particularly due to large gradient norms in the full attention layers. SAO employs a "Frozen-Attention" training strategy for the value model, freezing the parameters of attention modules in `V_ϕ` and optimizing only the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) projections. This is hypothesized to regularize the value model, as pre-trained attention weights are assumed to possess sufficient semantic capabilities.
- **Skip-Observation Token-level GAE for Agentic Tasks:** Agentic tasks involve trajectories structured as `[a_0, o_0, a_1, o_1, ...]`, where `a_i` are model actions and `o_i` are environment observations. Standard GAE, which calculates value differences between adjacent tokens, introduces noise when transitioning from an action to an observation because the model does not generate `o_i`. SAO derives a "Skip-Observation" GAE that modifies the Bellman target to bypass environment feedback tokens. It directly links the value of the current action's end token (`a_i,end`) to the value of the subsequent action's start token (`a_i+1,0`), ensuring advantage estimation relies purely on model outputs and filters out environmental stochasticity.
- **Scaling Value Pretraining:** To support these mechanisms and overcome the "cold start" problem in value estimation, SAO emphasizes significantly scaling the data used for value model pretraining. This robust initialization is intended to enhance the effectiveness of the single-rollout and faster-value-update mechanisms from the early stages of training.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507, further finetuned on Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) data from GPT-OSS-120B.
- **Benchmarks:** Agentic coding (SWE-Bench Verified) and math reasoning (AIME2025, BeyondAIME, HMMT Nov 2025, IMOAnswerBench).
- **Training Details:** Batch size of 128, group size of 1 (for SAO), max-length of 128k tokens. Policy learning rate 1e-6, token clipping `ϵ_low = 0.3`, `ϵ_high = 5.0` (for reasoning) or `ϵ_low = 0.8`, `ϵ_high = 3.0` (for coding). Length-adaptive GAE with `λ_policy = 1 - 1/(αl)` and `α = 1.5`. Value model learning rate 5e-6, `λ_critic = 1`, with a 10-step warmup. Value model updates `K=2` times per policy batch. GRPO variants used 16 prompts with 8 rollouts per prompt (total 128 batch size).
- **Evaluation:** Pass@1 accuracy, with top-p=1.0, temperature=1.0, max generation length 128k tokens. Math reasoning allows up to 50 turns; SWE-Bench Verified up to 300 OpenHands interaction turns. Mean performance reported across multiple evaluation runs.
- **Baselines:** SFT model, standard GRPO, GRPO with Direct Double-Sided Importance Sampling (GRPO w/ DIS).
- **Ablation Studies:** Variants testing the impact of faster value updates, full vs. frozen-attention value model training, Vanilla VAPO (without DIS), and a running-mean baseline for advantage estimation.
- **Online Learning Simulation:** A simulated online writing task with dynamically changing reward preferences (cute, chuunibyou, classical styles) judged by GLM-4.7, designed to test adaptability in non-stationary environments. Compared SAO against a running-mean advantage estimation approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and stability of the Single-rollout Asynchronous Optimization (SAO) framework across various agentic tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Stability

- **Consistent Outperformance:** SAO consistently achieved higher accuracy compared to all baselines (Qwen3-30B-A3B SFT, standard GRPO, and GRPO with Direct Double-Sided Importance Sampling (DIS)) on both agentic coding (SWE-Bench Verified) and math reasoning benchmarks (AIME2025, BeyondAIME, HMMT Nov 2025, IMOAnswerBench). For instance, on AIME2025, SAO achieved 97.3% accuracy, significantly higher than the SFT baseline (80.4%) and GRPO (84.2%).
- **Enhanced Training Stability:** Standard GRPO exhibited performance collapse at approximately 160 training steps. The scores reported for GRPO reflect its final valid performance before this collapse. In contrast, SAO demonstrated stable training for up to one thousand steps. The inclusion of DIS (Direct Double-Sided Importance Sampling) improved the stability of GRPO, allowing it to train longer without collapsing, but SAO still showed superior performance in the long run. Performance comparisons across training steps (Figure 3) indicate that while SAO and GRPO (w/ DIS) had comparable initial performance, SAO showed a distinct performance divergence and sustained improvement after approximately 400 training steps.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of individual SAO components:

- **Faster Value Update:** When the value model was updated only once per batch (instead of twice as in SAO), a performance decline was observed (e.g., AIME2025 accuracy dropped from 97.3% to 95.0%). This suggests that faster value adaptation is crucial for stable advantage estimation and policy updates.
- **Frozen-Attention Value Model:** Training the value model with full-parameter updates (instead of freezing attention layers) also led to a performance decrease (e.g., AIME2025 accuracy dropped to 90.62%). This indicates that freezing attention regularizes critic optimization and contributes to stability in complex reasoning tasks.
- **Value-Based Critic vs. Running-Mean Baseline:** A single-rollout baseline using a running mean of recent rewards for advantage estimation achieved decent performance (e.g., 79.8% on AIME2025) but significantly lagged SAO's value-based critic (97.3%). This highlights the necessity of a well-trained parametric value model for robust single-rollout RL.
- **Vanilla VAPO (w/o DIS):** Similar to vanilla GRPO, Vanilla VAPO without the proposed DIS strategy also collapsed quickly during training.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics Analysis

Analysis of training dynamics provided insights into SAO's mechanisms:

- **Explained Variance (Figure 4a):** SAO exhibited significantly higher Explained Variance (a measure of how well the value function predicts returns) after about 400 training steps compared to a single-critic-update baseline. This indicates that the faster value update strategy in SAO leads to quicker value convergence and better alignment with the policy distribution.
- **Critic Gradient Norm (Figure 4b):** Full-parameter value training showed larger and more unstable critic gradient norms. In contrast, SAO's frozen-attention strategy maintained lower and smoother gradient norms, confirming improved numerical stability in value optimization.
- **Clipped Tokens (Figure 4c):** SAO, with its Direct Double-Sided Importance Sampling (DIS), maintained a controlled token-level clip ratio, effectively gating divergent off-policy updates. The standard VAPO baseline without DIS, however, failed to control these updates, leading to a rapid training collapse.

#### 4.4. Online Learning Simulation

- **Rapid Adaptation:** In a simulated online learning task with dynamically changing stylistic preferences (cute, chuunibyou, classical writing styles), SAO demonstrated rapid policy realignment following each reward preference shift. It suppressed previously dominant styles and quickly adapted to new target styles, maintaining adherence to evolving environmental feedback (Figure 5a).
- **Superior to Running-Mean Baseline:** Compared to a running-mean advantage estimation baseline, SAO's value-based critic facilitated faster recovery and consistently higher convergence levels of training rewards following stylistic shifts (Figure 5b). The running-mean baseline exhibited a pronounced adaptation lag due to the inertia of its historical window. This indicates that SAO's state-dependent baseline provides the precision necessary for effective alignment in non-stationary environments.

#### 4.5. Additional Experimental Results (Appendix)

- **RL with Agentic Step as Action:** Experiments with step-wise GAE calculations (averaging token values per step or using only the last token) showed worse performance than token-wise value training (Table 5, Figure 6). This suggests that fine-grained token-level supervision is essential for capturing logical transitions in complex reasoning trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Single-rollout Asynchronous Optimization (SAO) framework addresses critical limitations in existing reinforcement learning paradigms for Large Language Models (LLMs), offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Stability and Efficiency for Agentic RL:** SAO provides a solution to the long-standing challenges of training instability and off-policy drift in asynchronous RL for LLMs. By combining direct double-sided token-level importance sampling, single-rollout sampling, and refined value-model training techniques, SAO enables stable training for extended durations (e.g., one thousand steps) where traditional methods like GRPO often collapse. This stability, coupled with the efficiency of asynchronous processing, is particularly impactful for long-horizon agentic tasks that require extensive rollouts, such as complex coding and reasoning.

2. **Broader Applicability to Real-world Scenarios:** The single-rollout strategy is a key innovation, making RL more compatible with real-world online learning environments where feedback is typically restricted to a single trajectory per prompt. This removes the "imbalanced generation" bias and latency associated with group-wise sampling, expanding the practical applicability of RL to dynamic, evolving environments where immediate, individual feedback is the norm. The successful deployment of SAO in the agentic RL pipeline for training the open GLM-5.2 model (750B-A40B) provides concrete evidence of its scalability and real-world utility.

3. **Improved Performance on Complex Tasks:** The consistent outperformance of SAO over GRPO and its variants on challenging agentic coding and reasoning benchmarks (e.g., SWE-Bench Verified, AIME2025) indicates that the proposed methodologies lead to more effective and capable LLM agents. This suggests that the principles of stable asynchronous single-rollout optimization can lead to advancements in the reasoning and problem-solving abilities of LLMs.

4. **Advancements in RL Algorithm Design:** The methodological contributions, such as Direct Double-Sided Importance Sampling (DIS) for managing off-policy effects in asynchronous settings, faster value function updates, frozen-attention for stabilizing value model training, and the skip-observation token-level GAE for multi-turn agentic trajectories, represent significant refinements in RL algorithm design specifically tailored for LLM agents. These techniques provide a blueprint for future research in optimizing deep reinforcement learning for sequential decision-making in natural language contexts.

5. **Reduced Training Costs:** By improving the stability and efficiency of LLM reinforcement learning, this work can contribute to reducing the computational costs associated with training highly capable agentic systems. Efficient resource utilization (via asynchronous processing) and reduced need for extensive hyperparameter tuning (due to improved stability) can make advanced LLM training more accessible.

However, the work also highlights potential impacts that require consideration. The increased capability and efficiency in training powerful agentic systems could also facilitate optimization for harmful objectives if not paired with robust data filtering, access controls, and comprehensive evaluation. Therefore, responsible deployment and continuous monitoring of any systems derived from this work are important ethical considerations.
