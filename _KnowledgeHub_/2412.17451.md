# Diving into Self-Evolving Training for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at HKUST and collaborating institutions present M-STAR, a self-evolving training framework for large multimodal models (LMMs) designed to enhance reasoning capabilities by systematically optimizing training methods and reward functions, and dynamically managing exploration-exploitation. M-STAR achieves up to a 6.7% relative improvement in MathVista accuracy for models like MiniCPM-V-2.5 and demonstrates consistent gains across diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality, human-annotated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data for complex multimodal reasoning tasks limits the development of advanced LMMs.
- Existing self-evolving training efforts for multimodal reasoning are sporadic and lack a systematic framework, hindering their consistent application and optimization.
- Performance saturation is a pervasive challenge in self-evolving training, where models plateau and fail to achieve further significant improvements after initial gains.

## Method

- The work reframes self-evolving training through a Reinforcement Learning (RL) lens, allowing for a systematic investigation of training methods, reward models, and prompt variations.
- It introduces =="Continuous Self-Evolving" training==, which maintains optimizer states and learning rate schedulers across iterations to promote smoother and more stable learning.
- A novel ==multimodal Process Reward Model (PRM)== is developed to evaluate the quality of intermediate reasoning steps, primarily utilized as a reranker for selecting high-quality correct responses.

## Results

- M-STAR achieved a **6.7%** relative improvement in MathVista accuracy for MiniCPM-V-2.5, reaching **59.5%**, and demonstrated consistent performance gains across multiple multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- Continuous Self-Evolving training on MiniCPM-V-2.5 yielded **57.2%** MathVista accuracy, surpassing iterative RFT (**55.7%**) and ReST_EM (**55.1%**).
- The integration of the PRM, specifically by selecting the Top-2 correct responses, boosted MiniCPM-V-2.5's MathVista accuracy to **59.2%**, indicating its effectiveness in identifying higher-quality rationales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous Self-Evolving training significantly outperforms traditional iterative methods like RFT and ReST_EM by fostering more stable and effective learning trajectories.
> - The multimodal PRM is most effective when used as a reranker to select superior reasoning paths among already correct answers, rather than as a direct verifier.
> - Performance saturation is largely driven by a continuous decline in the model's exploration ability (Pass@K), highlighting a crucial exploration-exploitation trade-off.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and rapidly evolving area at the intersection of large multimodal models (LMMs), complex reasoning, and self-improvement techniques. The broader research landscape is characterized by:

**The Rise of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs):** LMMs are becoming increasingly crucial for real-world applications such as intelligent agents, robotics, and autonomous driving, requiring them to process and reason across various modalities (e.g., visual and textual information). Tasks like visual mathematical reasoning, which involve interpreting diagrams, charts, and figures alongside text, exemplify the complexity LMMs must handle.

**The Challenge of Data Scarcity for Reasoning:** A significant bottleneck in training powerful reasoning models, especially LMMs, is the scarcity of high-quality, human-annotated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data. Generating step-by-step reasoning explanations for complex multimodal problems is labor-intensive and expensive. This limitation hinders the ability of models to learn intricate reasoning processes effectively.

**Self-Evolving Training as a Solution:** To overcome data scarcity, "self-evolving training" (also known as self-improvement or self-play) has emerged as a promising paradigm. In this approach, models iteratively learn from their own generated outputs, reducing the reliance on external human-annotated data. While this technique has shown considerable success in text-only reasoning tasks (e.g., STaR, ReST, DeepSeek-R1), its application and effectiveness in the more intricate domain of multimodal reasoning have remained largely underexplored. Existing multimodal self-training efforts are often sporadic and lack a unified framework.

**Performance Saturation:** A pervasive challenge in self-evolving training, both in text and multimodal settings, is performance saturation. Models tend to plateau after a certain number of iterations, failing to achieve further significant improvements. This suggests fundamental limitations in the current understanding and design of self-evolving algorithms.

This paper directly tackles these gaps by systematically investigating self-evolving training within the multimodal context. It uniquely reframes the self-evolving process through the lens of Reinforcement Learning (RL), allowing for a structured analysis of critical factors like training methods, reward models, and prompt variations. By delving into the dynamics of training and proposing mechanisms to mitigate saturation, this work aims to establish robust design principles and a generalizable framework for enhancing multimodal reasoning capabilities. It contributes to advancing LMMs beyond mere perception and generation, pushing them towards more sophisticated, human-like reasoning abilities in multimodal environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's key objectives and motivations stem from the identified limitations in current self-evolving training paradigms, particularly when applied to the complex domain of multimodal reasoning.

The **primary motivation** is to address the **scarcity of high-quality chain-of-thought (CoT) data** for multimodal reasoning tasks. While LMMs are increasingly vital for real-world applications, their ability to perform complex, step-by-step reasoning in multimodal scenarios is hindered by the limited availability of human-annotated rationales. Self-evolving training offers a promising alternative to mitigate this data dependency by allowing models to iteratively improve from their own generated outputs.

Building on this, the authors set out to achieve the following **key objectives**:

1. **Systematic Exploration of Self-Evolving Training for Multimodal Reasoning:** The paper aims to move beyond sporadic applications and conduct a rigorous, systematic analysis of self-evolving training's effectiveness in the multimodal domain. This includes identifying and investigating the critical factors that govern its performance, which are reframed through the lens of Reinforcement Learning (RL) as Training Method, Reward Model, and Prompt Variation.
2. **Establish Optimal Design Principles:** Through controlled studies, a core objective is to identify and propose relatively optimal design principles for each of the three pivotal factors. This involves comparing different strategies within each component to determine the most effective configurations for enhancing multimodal reasoning capabilities.
3. **Uncover and Mitigate Performance Saturation:** A significant challenge in self-evolving training is performance saturation, where models cease to improve after a certain point. The authors aim to delve into the underlying training dynamics to understand the roots of this saturation, particularly focusing on the trade-offs between exploration and exploitation. Subsequently, an objective is to propose a novel mechanism, an automatic balancing strategy, to mitigate this limitation and sustain performance gains.
4. **Develop a Robust and Generalizable Framework (M-STAR):** The ultimate objective is to integrate all derived insights and optimal design choices into a unified framework, named M-STAR (Multimodal Self-evolving Training for Reasoning). This framework is designed to achieve consistent and significant performance improvements across models of varying sizes and diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating its generalizability and practical utility.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to unlock the full reasoning potential of LMMs in multimodal contexts by making self-evolving training more effective, stable, and scalable, moving towards a future where LMMs can learn complex reasoning with minimal human supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic, multi-faceted approach to investigate and improve self-evolving training for multimodal reasoning. Their methodology is structured around identifying and optimizing key design components, analyzing training dynamics, and integrating these insights into a unified framework.

#### 3.1. Reframing Self-Evolving Training as Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The core conceptual approach is to view self-evolving training through the paradigm of RL. This reframing helps identify three pivotal factors influencing the training process:
1. **Training Method:** How the policy model ($\pi_\theta$) is updated iteratively.
2. **Reward Model:** The design and utilization of the reward function (R) to evaluate generated responses.
3. **Prompt Variation:** Whether to incorporate additional unlabeled inputs into training.

#### 3.2. General Setup and Warm-Up Phase

- **Models:** The primary model for exploration is MiniCPM-V-2.5 (8B), with validation on Phi-3.5-Vision (4B) and InternVL2 (2B) to assess generalizability.
- **Datasets:** MathV360K is used as the seed training dataset (180K labeled, 180K unlabeled), with 750 samples for in-domain (ID) testing. MathVista, M3CoT, MMStar-R, MMBench-R, and AI2D serve as out-of-domain (OOD) benchmarks focusing on reasoning.
- **Self-Warmup:** A crucial initial step where models are fine-tuned to generate detailed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. This addresses the common issue of open-source LMMs directly outputting answers without CoT, which is essential for effective self-evolving training. Models are prompted with extra instructions to generate 16 CoT samples per query, filtered for correctness, and used to create a 100K CoT dataset for initial SFT.

#### 3.3. Systematic Investigation of Three Design Components (Static Factors): Training Methods

- **Comparison:** The authors compare traditional iterative approaches like Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT) and ReST_EM (which typically re-initialize from an early checkpoint) with a proposed "Continuous Self-Evolving" scheme.
- **Continuous Self-Evolving:** This novel approach inherits the optimizer states and learning rate schedulers from the last iteration, alongside the model checkpoint, mimicking a smoother online RL process.
- **Iteration Interval:** The study also explores the impact of varying the iteration interval (the percentage of training queries processed per iteration, e.g., 6.25% to 100%) within the continuous self-evolving framework.
- **Reward:** For this section, a simple binary exact-match reward (1 if correct, 0 otherwise) is used.

#### 3.3.2. Reward Models

- **Motivation:** Address the limitations of sparse binary rewards by incorporating the quality of intermediate reasoning steps.
- **Process Reward Model (PRM):** The authors train the *first multimodal, process-based reward model*. This PRM is trained on data collected via Monte Carlo rollouts from a highly-converged policy model (ˆπ_0_θ), with step-level annotations derived from completions.
- **Integration Strategies:** Two main strategies for leveraging PRM scores are explored after initial filtering for correct final answers:
 - **Top-K:** Selecting the top K correct responses with the highest PRM scores.
 - **Filtering by Threshold (α):** Retaining correct responses whose PRM scores exceed a predefined threshold.
- **PRM Analysis:** An in-depth analysis is conducted to understand *why* PRM works, evaluating its verification ability (Best-of-N, weighted voting) and its role as a reranker (analyzing relativity scores and number of reasoning steps of top-ranked responses).

#### 3.3.3. Prompt Variation

- **Labeled vs. Unlabeled Prompts:** The study investigates the impact of incorporating unlabeled prompts (without ground truth answers) into self-evolving training.
- **Skyline Experiments:** Establish a baseline using unlabeled prompts *with oracle reward signals* (i.e., true answers available but not used for SFT) to understand potential benefits without reward model noise.
- **Unlabeled Prompt Integration:** For true unlabeled prompts, a pseudo-label (˜a) is derived using weighted voting from multiple rollouts. These are then combined with PRM-based reranking (Top-2) and mixed with labeled prompts.
- **Timing of Introduction:** The authors examine when to introduce unlabeled prompts into the training process (e.g., from the beginning, or after 25%, 50%, 75% of training).

#### 3.4. Analysis of Training Dynamics and Adaptive Exploration

- **Monitoring Metrics:** The authors track three key metrics across training iterations to understand dynamics:
 - **Greedy Accuracy:** Standard accuracy for reference.
 - **Pass@K Accuracy:** Measures the model's exploration ability (at least one correct response among K samples).
 - **Reward-Pass@2:** A novel metric reflecting exploitation efficacy (existence of correct responses among the top 2 ranked by PRM).
- **Problem Identification:** The dynamics reveal a decline in Pass@K and saturation of Reward-Pass@2, indicating exploration loss and diminishing exploitation potential.
- **Adaptive Exploration (Automatic Balancing Mechanism):** Inspired by the observation that higher temperatures better maintain exploration, the paper proposes dynamically adjusting the sampling temperature (from 0.3 to 1.6) at each iteration based on the validation Reward-Pass@2 scores. This aims to sustain the exploration-exploitation trade-off.

#### 3.5. M-STAR Framework Development

The M-STAR (Multimodal Self-evolving Training for Reasoning) framework is the culmination of all these insights. It combines the optimal static design choices (Continuous Self-Evolving, PRM as a reranker for labeled data) with the adaptive exploration strategy derived from monitoring training dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic investigation yielded several critical findings, leading to the development of the M-STAR framework:

#### 4.1. Optimal Static Design Principles

- **Training Methods:**
 - **Continuous Self-Evolving:** This approach, which inherits the optimizer and learning rate scheduler from the previous iteration, significantly outperforms traditional iterative methods like Iterative RFT and ReST_EM. For MiniCPM-V-2.5, Continuous Self-Evolving achieved 57.2% accuracy on MathVista, compared to 55.7% for Iterative RFT and 55.1% for ReST_EM.
 - **Iteration Interval:** A shorter, appropriate iteration interval (e.g., 25% of the data processed per iteration, approximately 45k queries) was found to be most effective. This balance allows for timely updates without destabilizing the learning process.

- **Reward Models (PRM):**
 - **PRM as a Reranker:** The proposed multimodal Process Reward Model (PRM) is most effective when used as a *reranker* for already correct responses, rather than as a general verifier. It helps select high-quality reasoning steps among correct solutions.
 - **Top-K Selection:** Selecting the *Top-K* correct responses based on PRM scores (specifically **Top-2**) significantly improved performance. For MiniCPM-V-2.5, using Top-2 with PRM boosted MathVista accuracy to 59.2%, a notable gain over 57.2% without PRM.
 - **Ineffective as a Verifier/Thresholding:** The PRM was found to be less effective as a direct verifier (i.e., distinguishing correct from incorrect answers among many rollouts) or when applying a fixed threshold for filtering responses. Thresholding often led to either too many or too few responses, reducing diversity.
 - **Quality of Reranking:** PRM-selected responses generally had *fewer reasoning steps* and were *more relevant* to the query, indicating its ability to identify genuinely high-quality, concise rationales.

- **Prompt Variation (Unlabeled Data):**
 - **Limited Benefit, Potential Harm:** Contrary to intuition, incorporating additional unlabeled prompts, even with oracle reward signals or pseudo-labels combined with PRM, generally *did not improve* or even *harmed* model performance on out-of-domain benchmarks.
 - **Reasoning:** This is attributed to the PRM's inability to reliably verify responses without ground-truth answers and its limited generalization to unseen data, leading to noisy signals that can cause the policy model's distribution to deviate.
 - **Conclusion:** The authors conclude that unlabeled data is currently not beneficial without sufficient and precise surrogate supervision, suggesting a focus on high-quality labeled data is paramount.

#### 4.2. Insights into Training Dynamics

- **Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** As training progresses, greedy accuracy improves, but Pass@K (exploration ability) continuously *declines*. This indicates a loss of exploration potential, leading to performance saturation.
- **Reward-Pass@2 Saturation:** The Reward-Pass@2 metric (exploitation efficacy) initially increases but quickly plateaus, suggesting that the reward model's ability to exploit further diminishes, partly due to reduced exploration.
- **Temperature's Role:** Larger sampling temperatures were observed to resist the decline in Pass@K more effectively, highlighting the importance of dynamic temperature adjustment.

#### 4.3. M-STAR Framework Performance

- **Adaptive Exploration:** By introducing an automatic balancing mechanism that dynamically adjusts the sampling temperature based on validation Reward-Pass@2 scores, M-STAR effectively mitigates exploration loss and sustains higher exploitation.
- **Consistent Gains:** M-STAR, combining optimal static design choices with adaptive exploration, achieved the highest performance across various LMMs and benchmarks:
 - **MiniCPM-V-2.5:** Improved MathVista accuracy to **59.5%**, representing a **6.7% relative improvement** over the pre-evolved (+warmup) model. It also showed consistent gains across M3CoT, MMStar-R, MMBench-R, and AI2D.
 - **Phi-3.5-Vision:** Achieved **54.5%** on MathVista (a **5.2% relative improvement**) and consistent gains on other benchmarks.
 - **InternVL2-2B:** Showed more modest improvements (e.g., 50.3% on MathVista, a 2.7% relative improvement) and even slight decreases on some perception-heavy benchmarks, suggesting that smaller models struggle more with generalization despite the optimized training.
- **Overall:** M-STAR demonstrates its effectiveness in enhancing multimodal reasoning across models of different scales by optimizing self-evolving training through careful design and dynamic adjustments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions to the fields of large multimodal models (LMMs), self-evolving training, and reinforcement learning, with a substantial potential impact on the development of more capable AI systems.

**1. Pioneering Systematic Study for Multimodal Reasoning:** This paper represents the first systematic and comprehensive investigation into self-evolving training specifically for multimodal reasoning. By identifying and analyzing three pivotal factors—Training Method, Reward Model, and Prompt Variation—the authors provide a much-needed structured understanding of this complex domain, moving beyond ad-hoc applications.

**2. Establishment of Optimal Design Principles:** The research provides concrete, empirically-backed guidelines for building effective self-evolving training systems. Key findings, such as the superiority of "Continuous Self-Evolving" and the utility of a Process Reward Model (PRM) as a reranker, offer practical recipes for researchers and practitioners. This reduces the trial-and-error often associated with developing self-improvement algorithms for LMMs.

**3. Introduction of the First Multimodal Process Reward Model (PRM):** The development and successful integration of a multimodal PRM are a notable achievement. While process-based rewards have been explored in text-only settings, extending this to multimodal contexts is crucial for evaluating complex visual-linguistic reasoning steps. The finding that PRM acts as an effective reranker, identifying high-quality reasoning within correct solutions, highlights a powerful new mechanism for refining LMM outputs.

**4. Addressing Performance Saturation with Dynamic Balancing:** One of the most critical contributions is the identification of exploration loss as a root cause of performance saturation in self-evolving training. The proposed automatic balancing mechanism, which dynamically adjusts the sampling temperature based on the novel Reward-Pass@2 metric, offers a practical and effective solution to sustain exploration and extend performance gains. This innovation can significantly improve the scalability and long-term effectiveness of self-improvement pipelines.

**5. Robust and Generalizable Framework (M-STAR):** The culmination of these insights into the M-STAR framework demonstrates a holistic solution. M-STAR's consistent performance improvements across various LMMs (MiniCPM-V-2.5, Phi-3.5-Vision, InternVL2) and diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, M3CoT, MMStar-R, MMBench-R, AI2D) validate its robustness and generalizability. This framework serves as a strong baseline and a powerful tool for future LMM development.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerating LMM Development:** By providing clear design principles and a robust framework, the paper will enable faster and more efficient development of LMMs with advanced reasoning capabilities, reducing the dependency on expensive human annotation.
- **Advancing Real-World AI Applications:** Enhanced multimodal reasoning is fundamental for next-generation intelligent agents, robotics, and autonomous driving systems. M-STAR's improvements will directly contribute to making these applications more reliable and intelligent.
- **Informing Future Research:** The insights into training dynamics, particularly the exploration-exploitation trade-off and the limitations of unlabeled data without strong reward signals, open new avenues for research in self-supervised learning, reward model design (especially for complex, unverified multimodal data), and online reinforcement learning for LLMs. The public release of resources (https://mstar-lmm.github.io) will further foster community engagement and accelerate research in this area.
- **Bridging RL and LMMs:** The successful reframing of self-evolving training through an RL lens strengthens the connection between these two fields, encouraging further cross-pollination of ideas and techniques.

In conclusion, "Diving into Self-Evolving Training for Multimodal Reasoning" provides both foundational insights and practical solutions that are poised to significantly advance the state-of-the-art in multimodal AI, enabling models to reason more effectively and autonomously in complex, real-world environments.
