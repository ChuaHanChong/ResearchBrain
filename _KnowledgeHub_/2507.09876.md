# ViTCoT: Video-Text Interleaved Chain-of-Thought for Boosting Video Understanding in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ViTCoT introduces a Video-Text Interleaved Chain-of-Thought paradigm for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), integrating key visual information directly into the step-by-step reasoning process. The approach consistently improves video understanding accuracy by an average of 3.5% across various MLLMs and mainstream CoT methods, supported by a newly constructed, human-verified benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for video understanding predominantly rely on text-only reasoning, treating visual data as an initial input rather than an iterative reference point.
- This text-only reasoning paradigm overlooks the human cognitive tendency to re-examine visual content during complex comprehension tasks.
- A lack of dedicated benchmarks exists to evaluate models capable of true video-text interleaved reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces ==ViTCoT==, a ==two-stage video-text interleaved reasoning paradigm==: initial text reasoning followed by refined reasoning with key-videos integrated into the textual thought process.
- Constructs the ==Video-Text Interleaved Benchmark (ViTIB)== by leveraging MLLM-assisted key-video selection from VideoEspresso, followed by rigorous human verification to ensure quality.
- Enables MLLMs to refine their understanding by iteratively interacting with both textual and ==key visual information==, mimicking human cognitive patterns during problem-solving.

## Results

- ViTCoT consistently improved video understanding accuracy by an average of **3.5%** across diverse MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, Gemini-2.0-Flash) and mainstream CoT methods compared to text-only approaches.
- The interleaving process itself contributed an average **2.8%** performance gain over vanilla methods, even when baselines were provided with both original and key videos, suggesting its critical role.
- Qualitative analysis revealed ViTCoT activated more neuron values across attention heads and layers within MLLMs, indicating deeper and more intensive multimodal engagement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating visual information iteratively into the reasoning process, rather than just as initial input, significantly enhances MLLM video understanding.
> - The act of interleaving visual and textual cues during reasoning is critical, providing substantial benefits beyond merely providing additional visual input.
> - High-quality, condensed "key-videos" can effectively capture essential information for reasoning, sometimes outperforming reliance on full, longer videos.
