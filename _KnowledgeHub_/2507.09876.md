# ViTCoT: Video-Text Interleaved Chain-of-Thought for Boosting Video Understanding in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ViTCoT introduces a Video-Text Interleaved Chain-of-Thought paradigm for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), integrating key visual information directly into the step-by-step reasoning process. The approach consistently improves video understanding accuracy by an average of 3.5% across various MLLMs and mainstream CoT methods, supported by a newly constructed, human-verified benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for video understanding predominantly rely on text-only reasoning, treating visual data as an initial input rather than an iterative reference point.
- This text-only reasoning paradigm overlooks the human cognitive tendency to re-examine visual content during complex comprehension tasks.
- A lack of dedicated benchmarks exists to evaluate models capable of true video-text interleaved reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces ==ViTCoT==, a ==two-stage video-text interleaved reasoning paradigm==: initial text reasoning followed by refined reasoning with key-videos integrated into the textual thought process.
- Constructs the ==Video-Text Interleaved Benchmark (ViTIB)== by leveraging MLLM-assisted key-video selection from VideoEspresso, followed by rigorous human verification to ensure quality.
- Enables MLLMs to refine their understanding by iteratively interacting with both textual and ==key visual information==, mimicking human cognitive patterns during problem-solving.

## Results

- ViTCoT consistently improved video understanding accuracy by an average of **3.5%** across diverse MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, Gemini-2.0-Flash) and mainstream CoT methods compared to text-only approaches.
- The interleaving process itself contributed an average **2.8%** performance gain over vanilla methods, even when baselines were provided with both original and key videos, suggesting its critical role.
- Qualitative analysis revealed ViTCoT activated more neuron values across attention heads and layers within MLLMs, indicating deeper and more intensive multimodal engagement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating visual information iteratively into the reasoning process, rather than just as initial input, significantly enhances MLLM video understanding.
> - The act of interleaving visual and textual cues during reasoning is critical, providing substantial benefits beyond merely providing additional visual input.
> - High-quality, condensed "key-videos" can effectively capture essential information for reasoning, sometimes outperforming reliance on full, longer videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video understanding is a critical field in artificial intelligence, serving as a cornerstone for applications ranging from autonomous driving and embodied AI to the ultimate pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). It necessitates the sophisticated interpretation and extraction of semantic and contextual information from dynamic visual, auditory, and temporal data.

The recent exponential advancements in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have significantly propelled progress in video understanding tasks. Concurrently, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning techniques, which compel models to break down complex problems into a series of intermediate steps, have proven instrumental in augmenting the reasoning capabilities of LLMs across various domains. Researchers have increasingly explored the synergy between MLLMs and CoT for video understanding, leading to notable contributions such as "Video-of-Thought" and the "VideoCoT" benchmark. These prior works demonstrated the potential of extending CoT by decomposing video tasks into simpler steps or by providing dense video text retrieval for fine-grained information.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation in existing approaches: despite processing both video and text inputs, the reasoning process itself predominantly relies on textual information. This "text-only" reasoning paradigm, as depicted in Figure 1(a) of the paper, overlooks the intuitive human tendency to re-examine visual content during complex comprehension tasks. Humans naturally iterate between textual descriptions and visual cues to refine their understanding and formulate reasoning, a process currently unaddressed by most MLLMs.

"ViTCoT" directly aims to fill this gap. By proposing a "Video-Text Interleaved CoT" paradigm, the research attempts to imbue MLLMs with a more cognitively aligned reasoning process, wherein visual information (specifically, key-videos) is actively integrated and reviewed *during* the step-by-step reasoning process, rather than being merely an initial input. This work represents a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to perform video reasoning in a manner that more closely mirrors human cognitive processes, thereby enhancing their robustness and accuracy in complex, real-world video understanding scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ViTCoT stems from an observation about human cognition that is notably absent in current MLLMs designed for video understanding: the natural inclination to re-examine visual information during complex reasoning. While existing CoT-based video understanding models process video inputs, their internal reasoning largely proceeds in a text-only fashion, treating visual data as an initial input rather than an iterative reference point throughout the thought process. The authors emphasize that "A picture is worth a thousand words," highlighting the rich cognitive value of visual information that is currently underutilized in the reasoning loops of MLLMs.

Based on this insight, the **key objectives** of this research are multi-faceted:

1. **Introduce a Novel Video Reasoning Paradigm:** The overarching objective is to propose and implement "Video-Text Interleaved CoT (ViTCoT)," a paradigm that enables MLLMs to simulate human-like iterative review of key visual information. This aims to foster a more intuitive and cognitively aligned reasoning process for video understanding.
2. **Construct a Dedicated Benchmark:** To facilitate research and evaluation of this novel paradigm, a crucial objective is the development of the "Video-Text Interleaved Benchmark (ViTIB)." This benchmark is specifically designed to integrate video frames with corresponding text, enabling the assessment of models capable of video-text interleaved reasoning. Its construction involves an innovative process of MLLM-assisted key-video selection followed by rigorous human verification.
3. **Extensively Explore and Validate ViTCoT's Potential:** The paper aims to thoroughly investigate the effectiveness and generalizability of the ViTCoT paradigm. This includes applying ViTCoT to various mainstream CoT methods and evaluating its performance across different MLLMs, from open-source to closed-source models. The goal is to demonstrate that ViTCoT significantly enhances reasoning capabilities in complex video understanding tasks and to understand its underlying mechanisms, such as its impact on MLLM neuron activation.

In essence, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between how humans naturally process and reason with multimodal information (visual and textual) and how current AI models perform these tasks, thereby pushing MLLMs closer to achieving more sophisticated and human-like video comprehension.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ViTCoT paradigm is a two-stage process designed to integrate visual information directly into the Chain-of-Thought reasoning of MLLMs, along with a newly constructed benchmark to support this.

#### 3.1. ViTCoT Paradigm

The core of ViTCoT lies in its two sequential reasoning stages:

- **Stage 1: Initial Text Reasoning:**
 - The process begins by providing the MLLM with the `[Original Video V]`, the `[Question Q]`, and the `[Options O]`.
 - The MLLM is then prompted to generate an `[Initial Reasoning R']` without directly providing the final answer. This initial phase encourages the model to analyze the video and question, laying a textual foundation for subsequent reasoning steps. The instruction explicitly guides the MLLM to "generate an Initial Reasoning based on the video and Options that can solve the Question, rather than directly providing the answer." This stage produces a preliminary text-based thought process.

- **Stage 2: Video-Text Interleaved Reasoning:**
 - Once `[Initial Reasoning R']` is obtained, the crucial "interleaving" step occurs. A `[Key-Video V_k]` (a concise sequence of frames deemed most relevant to the reasoning task) is integrated into `[Initial Reasoning R']`. This means that `V_k` is effectively "embedded" within the textual reasoning context.
 - Both the `[Original Video V]` and the `[Key-Video V_k]` (now interleaved within `[Initial Reasoning R']`) are provided as a combined input to the MLLM.
 - The MLLM then generates the `[Final Reasoning R]` process and the ultimate answer. The instruction guides the MLLM to "generate the process of Final Reasoning for the Question by integrating the [Initial Reasoning R' and Key-Video V_k] process." This iterative interaction with both textual and key visual information enables the MLLM to refine its understanding and arrive at a more accurate solution, mimicking how humans re-examine visual cues during problem-solving.

#### 3.2. Video-Text Interleaved Benchmark (ViTIB) Construction

Recognizing the absence of suitable datasets for evaluating video-text interleaved reasoning, the authors meticulously constructed the "Video-Text Interleaved Benchmark (ViTIB)." This benchmark is built upon the existing VideoEspresso dataset. The construction involves a multi-step process:

- **Key-Video Extraction:**
 - **Automatic Sample Removal:** Initial filtering to discard incomplete or irrelevant video-question-answer triplets, ensuring data quality.
 - **Key-Frames Recognition:** The powerful Gemini-2.0-Flash MLLM is leveraged to automatically identify frames most relevant to the reasoning task. Given the video, question, reasoning steps, and answer, Gemini selects frame indices that are most supportive of accurate answer derivation.
 - **Assemble Key-Video:** The identified key frames are extracted from the original video at a frame rate of FPS = 1 to form the `Key-Video V_k`.

- **Human Recheck:** This is a crucial step to ensure the high quality and accuracy of the benchmark's key-videos.
 - Each data entry is independently reviewed by three human annotators.
 - A detailed scoring guideline (on a 0-100 scale) is provided to ensure consistency, with specific criteria for different relevance levels (e.g., 90-100 for independent reasoning from key-video alone, 80-90 for complete reasoning when combined with original video).
 - Only data entries where all three reviewers assign scores above 80 are retained. If scores are below this threshold, reviewers collaboratively discuss, reselect key-frames, and reassemble the key-video, leading to an iterative refinement process. This rigorous quality control resulted in an average score of 83.6 for the benchmark.

- **ViTIB Statistics:** The final ViTIB comprises 1,382 videos categorized into 14 diverse reasoning types (e.g., Causal Analysis, Event Dynamic Analysis, Narrative Analysis). It contains 5,051 key-frames, with an average of 3.7 frames per key-video. Quantitative analysis using CLIP and UMAP demonstrated a strong semantic overlap between key-frame features and textual reasoning content, confirming that key-frames effectively capture the essence of the reasoning process.

#### 3.3. Experimental Settings

- **Baselines:** The authors compare ViTCoT against standard "Direct" querying (without CoT) and mainstream text-only CoT methods: Vanilla Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Vanilla Desp-CoT, and Vanilla Plan-and-Solve.
- **ViTCoT Adaptations:** The ViTCoT paradigm is applied to each of these baseline CoT methods (e.g., ViTCoT, ViT Desp-CoT, ViT Plan-and-Solve) using the Oracle key-videos from ViTIB (and later, non-Oracle key-videos for robustness testing).
- **MLLMs Evaluated:** Experiments were conducted on several open-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct, VideoLLaMA3-7B, Intern2.5-VL-8B) and the closed-source Gemini-2.0-Flash.
- **Uniformity:** All video inputs were standardized to FPS=1, and model parameters (top-p, temperature) were kept at default MLLM values for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on the ViTIB benchmark across various MLLMs yielded several significant findings:

- **Universal Performance Boost of ViTCoT:** The most prominent result is the consistent and significant performance improvement observed when applying the Video-Text Interleaved Reasoning paradigm. On average, methods incorporating ViTCoT demonstrated a 3.5% accuracy improvement compared to their traditional text-only CoT counterparts. The paper highlights that while vanilla reasoning methods sometimes underperformed even direct querying in complex video understanding scenarios, ViTCoT consistently boosted performance.

- **Applicability Across MLLMs:** ViTCoT's effectiveness was validated across a diverse range of MLLMs, including open-source models like Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct, VideoLLaMA3-7B, and Intern2.5-VL-8B, as well as the closed-source Gemini-2.0-Flash. For instance, on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, ViTCoT methods achieved an average improvement of 5.4% over vanilla reasoning. This broad applicability underscores the paradigm's generalizability and robustness.

- **Robustness Beyond Oracle Key-Videos:** To address concerns that performance gains might solely stem from using "perfect" Oracle key-videos, the authors conducted additional analyses:
 - **Interleaving Process is Key:** Even when vanilla reasoning methods were provided with *both* the original video and the Oracle key-video, ViTCoT still outperformed them by an average of 2.8%. This strongly suggests that the *interleaving process* itself, which allows iterative interaction with visual cues during reasoning, is a critical factor, not merely the presence of additional visual input.
 - **Effectiveness with Non-Oracle Key-Videos:** When using "rough" key-videos automatically extracted by CLIP (a general image-text model) based on initial textual reasoning, the ViTCoT paradigm still achieved an average 1.7% improvement over vanilla methods. This demonstrates that the performance benefits are inherent to the ViTCoT paradigm and are not solely reliant on perfectly curated key-videos.

- **High Quality of ViTIB Benchmark:** The newly constructed ViTIB dataset proved its value. Experiments showed that using *only* the key-video as input for reasoning could sometimes outperform using *only* the original, longer video (e.g., 0.8% and 1.2% improvement for CoT and Desp-CoT, respectively). This indicates that the human-verified key-videos in ViTIB effectively capture the essential information required for reasoning while filtering out irrelevant content, validating the benchmark's quality and utility.

- **Deeper Neuron Activation:** A qualitative analysis on Qwen2.5-VL-3B revealed that ViTCoT reasoning significantly activated more neuron values across attention heads and layers within the MLLM compared to vanilla reasoning. This suggests that the interleaved paradigm encourages deeper and more intensive model engagement with the multimodal information, leading to more complex and refined internal representations, which correlates with the observed performance improvements.

- **Qualitative Case Study:** A case study illustrated ViTCoT's interpretability and effectiveness. While Vanilla Desp-CoT, relying solely on text-based reasoning, failed to provide the correct answer, ViT Desp-CoT successfully arrived at the correct solution by integrating the key-video into its multi-stage reasoning process. This practical example vividly demonstrates how re-examining visual cues guides the model towards accurate conclusions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "ViTCoT: Video-Text Interleaved Chain-of-Thought for Boosting Video Understanding in Large Language Models" introduces a paradigm-shifting approach that holds substantial significance for the field of multimodal AI, particularly video understanding.

**Novel Reasoning Paradigm:** ViTCoT is the first to propose and empirically validate a "Video-Text Interleaved" reasoning paradigm. This move away from solely text-based Chain-of-Thought in MLLMs addresses a critical limitation, enriching the reasoning process by allowing models to actively "re-examine" visual information during their thought steps. This more closely mimics human cognitive processes, where visual evidence is continuously referenced and integrated with verbal reasoning.

**Enhanced MLLM Capabilities:** The consistent and significant performance gains across various MLLMs (both open-source and closed-source) and CoT methods demonstrate ViTCoT's power. It indicates that integrating visual context iteratively into reasoning pathways can unlock a deeper understanding within MLLMs, enabling them to tackle more complex video understanding tasks with higher accuracy and robustness. The observed increase in neuron activation further supports that ViTCoT prompts MLLMs to engage more profoundly with the multimodal input.

**New Research Benchmark:** The creation of the Video-Text Interleaved Benchmark (ViTIB) is a crucial contribution. By providing a meticulously curated dataset with human-verified key-videos specifically designed for interleaved reasoning, ViTIB fills a significant gap in existing resources. This benchmark will be instrumental for future research, enabling the development, evaluation, and comparison of new multimodal reasoning architectures and techniques that aim to achieve more human-like video comprehension.

**Advancement towards AGI and Embodied AI:** The ability to seamlessly integrate and iterate between visual and textual information is fundamental for developing more intelligent and autonomous AI systems. ViTCoT's approach could significantly impact fields such as autonomous driving (where real-time visual assessment is critical for decision-making), embodied AI (where agents need to reason about their visual environment to complete tasks), and more generally, in the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), which necessitates comprehensive and adaptable reasoning across modalities.

**Improved Interpretability and Robustness:** By making the reasoning process more transparent through interleaved visual cues, ViTCoT potentially offers a pathway towards more interpretable MLLMs. This interpretability, coupled with the demonstrated performance improvements, suggests a more robust and reliable foundation for future video understanding applications, especially in high-stakes domains.

**Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing the data and code will undoubtedly accelerate research in this burgeoning area. It allows other researchers to build upon this work, replicate findings, and explore new directions, fostering collaborative innovation within the AI community.

In conclusion, ViTCoT represents a pivotal step in multimodal reasoning, shifting the paradigm from parallel processing to a truly interleaved, cognitively inspired approach. Its success in boosting MLLM performance and the provision of a new benchmark lay a strong foundation for the next generation of intelligent systems capable of richer and more human-like video understanding.
