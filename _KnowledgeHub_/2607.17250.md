# EvolvingWorld: An Open-Schema Framework for Co-Evolving Role-Play Agents and World Model in Interactive Literary World

> [!summary] Summary
> The EvolvingWorld framework enables interactive literary world simulations where character profiles and the world model dynamically co-evolve over long narratives. It leverages Large Language Models with an open-schema approach to adaptively update character attributes and world states, demonstrating improved consistency and more coherent long-horizon evolution compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing role-playing agents often use static character profiles, failing to capture the dynamic evolution inherent in literary characters.
- Multi-agent environments and narrative systems typically generate isolated scenes or partial updates, neglecting the persistent, cumulative impact of events on a story's progression.
- Previous systems are constrained by fixed schemas for representing character and world states, limiting their adaptability across diverse literary genres and character archetypes.

## Method

- Introduces **EvolvingWorld**, an ==open-schema framework== that allows the system to infer and adapt relevant character and world dimensions directly from each book, rather than relying on predefined slots.
- Decomposes the complex long-horizon simulation process into ==seven supervised, trainable tasks==, covering scene initialization, interaction generation, and both ==multi-timescale character and world state updates==.
- Constructs a large-scale benchmark dataset from 57 chronologically narrated books, providing over 138,000 supervised training samples and a comprehensive ==trajectory-level evaluation protocol==.

## Results

- EvolvingWorld-trained models consistently demonstrated improved performance in both Character Agent and World Model tasks, significantly enhancing character consistency, evolution quality, and environmental grounding compared to baselines.
- The framework mitigated performance degradation observed in prior systems over longer simulation trajectories, with metrics like **Profile Evolution Smoothness** showing improvement over extended narratives.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both character and world state updates, the hidden tracker, and the open-schema design are all critical components for achieving the framework's overall performance and dynamic co-evolution capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An open-schema representation allows for flexible and adaptive modeling of character and world dimensions, enabling the system to support diverse literary genres and settings without rigid constraints.
> - The multi-timescale character evolution mechanism, which includes a "hidden tracker," facilitates nuanced character development by accumulating weak evidence over time before making persistent profile changes.
> - Coupled updates between the Character Agent and the World Model are crucial for maintaining narrative coherence and realism, ensuring that character actions influence the world and world changes influence character motivations over long narratives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly the application of Large Language Models (LLMs), has seen advancements in simulating human-like conversation and behavior. A significant area of this research involves developing "role-play agents" capable of imitating fictional characters and sustaining persona-grounded dialogue. Early systems in this domain often focused on generating fluent dialogue based on static character profiles or short conversational contexts. Examples such as ChatHaruhi, CharacterLLM, and RoleLLM primarily treated personas as fixed entities, concentrating on immediate conversational coherence.

Subsequently, research expanded to include multi-agent environments and more complex world simulations. Systems like GenerativeAgents explored social interactions within predefined "sandboxes," while others, such as CoSER and AdaMARP, improved multi-agent role-playing without extensive world modeling. BookWorld represented an advance by introducing a world agent to update global events and some character states. However, these systems generally faced limitations: character profiles remained largely static or updated only a few predefined fields, world environments were often rigid or lacked detailed entity-level tracking, and the scope of updates was partial, neglecting full character profile evolution or fine-grained world state changes.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a framework for "long-horizon" literary world simulation. It positions itself as a development that integrates and extends prior work by enabling characters and worlds to "co-evolve" dynamically over extended narratives. The key innovation lies in its "open-schema" approach, which allows for adaptive representation of character and world dimensions, moving beyond fixed templates. By explicitly modeling and continuously updating both character and world states in a coupled manner, this work aims to provide a more comprehensive and coherent simulation of interactive literary worlds, where actions of characters can genuinely reshape their environment and, in turn, influence their motivations and identities. This contrasts with previous systems that tended to treat characters as static entities and worlds as passive backdrops, thereby failing to capture the intricate interplay essential for complex, evolving narratives.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce EvolvingWorld, a framework and benchmark designed for simulating the co-evolution of characters and their literary world over long narrative horizons. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in existing interactive literary simulation systems:

- **Static Persona Imitation:** Many current role-playing agents primarily focus on imitating fictional characters based on static profiles or limited dialogue contexts. This approach fails to capture the dynamic nature of literary characters, who typically undergo significant internal and external changes, revise their beliefs, motivations, and relationships as a story progresses. The paper highlights that a character's state should evolve beyond initial descriptions.
- **Isolated Scene Generation:** Existing multi-agent environments or book-grounded systems often treat interactions as isolated scenes or generate partial long-horizon updates. This neglects the persistent, cumulative impact of events and character actions on the overall narrative. For instance, while some systems update goals or global events, they may lack full profile evolution or detailed location/entity-level world updates.
- **Fixed Schemas:** Previous systems frequently rely on predefined, fixed schemas for representing character and world states. This rigidity limits their applicability across diverse literary genres, settings, and character archetypes, where the relevant dimensions for description can vary substantially (e.g., a detective's investigative habits versus a Victorian orphan's social position).

To address these issues, the key objectives of EvolvingWorld are:

1. **Model Literary Simulation as a Long-Horizon Process:** To conceptualize interactive literary simulation not as a series of disconnected interactions but as a continuous process where characters interact, scenes unfold, and both character and world states are persistently updated over time.
2. **Implement an Open-Schema Framework:** To allow the system to infer and adapt relevant character and world dimensions directly from each book, rather than forcing stories into fixed, predefined slots. This flexibility aims to support simulation across a wide array of literary worlds.
3. **Develop Coupled Character and World Co-evolution:** To establish a framework where character actions can dynamically reshape locations, social orders, or other world dimensions, and conversely, world changes can influence character motivations and profiles. This coupling is central to maintaining narrative coherence and realism.
4. **Introduce Multi-Timescale Character Evolution:** To model that different character profile dimensions evolve at varying speeds (e.g., emotions shift quickly, personality traits require accumulated evidence). This involves using a "hidden tracker" to store weak or emerging evidence separately until it justifies a full profile update.
5. **Establish an LLM-based World Model for Comprehensive State Tracking:** To develop a World Model capable of maintaining global world states (e.g., historical background, social institutions) and detailed location/entity-level physical states (e.g., descriptions of locations and important non-character entities). These states are designed to be dynamically updated based on interactions.
6. **Decompose the Simulation into Trainable Tasks:** To break down the complex long-horizon simulation process into seven specific, supervised tasks covering scene initialization, interaction generation, and state updates, thereby facilitating targeted training.
7. **Construct a Large-Scale, Diverse Benchmark Dataset:** To create a dataset from 57 books, yielding a substantial number of supervised training samples and test snapshots, enabling robust training and evaluation.
8. **Formulate a Trajectory-Level Evaluation Protocol:** To introduce a comprehensive LLM-as-Judge evaluation framework that assesses persistent character and world development across an entire simulated trajectory, going beyond local role-playing quality.

The overarching motivation is to advance the capabilities of LLMs in creating interactive, immersive, and logically consistent literary worlds that can dynamically evolve alongside their inhabitants, reflecting the complexities of real-world narrative development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvolvingWorld is structured around two interconnected modules: a Character Agent and an LLM-based World Model, both designed to facilitate open-schema, long-horizon co-evolution in interactive multi-agent literary worlds.

#### 3.1. Design Principles

- **Open-Schema Representation:** Unlike previous systems with fixed attribute slots, EvolvingWorld allows the system to infer and dynamically construct relevant character and world dimensions from each book. For characters, this means profiles can include dimensions specific to their role (e.g., "detective's investigative habits" or "Victorian orphan's social position"). For the world, global states adapt to reflect unique elements like "school rules and class hierarchies" or "political orders."
- **Multi-Timescale Character Evolution:** Character Agent profiles are designed to evolve. A "hidden tracker" is introduced to store weak or emerging evidence (e.g., fleeting emotions, subtle shifts in opinion) separately from the main profile. This prevents premature updates to stable traits while allowing repeated signals to accumulate and eventually trigger significant, justified profile changes.
- **Hierarchical World Modeling:** The World Model maintains both a `global world state` (capturing overarching settings like social institutions, historical background) and `location-level physical states` (detailing specific locations, their descriptions, and important non-character entities). Both are open-schema, allowing for diverse and detailed world representations.

#### 3.2. Simulation Pipeline and Task Formulation

The framework operates in a scene-by-scene manner, iteratively planning, generating interactions, and updating states. The process is decomposed into seven supervised, trainable tasks:

1. **`scene_cast` (World Model):** Determines the set of participating characters (`z_t`) for the upcoming scene, based on the global world state and individual character descriptions.
2. **`location_scenario` (World Model):** Given the selected cast, the World Model generates a scene plan, specifying the `location (ℓ_t)` and a `scenario (r_t)` description for the scene.
3. **`motivation_update` (Character Agent):** For each character participating in the scene, a scene-specific `motivation (M_i,(t))` is generated, grounded in their current profile, hidden tracker, and the scene plan.
4. **`next_character` (World Model):** During the scene, the World Model dynamically selects the next acting character (`i_t,k`) from the participants or determines if the scene should end. This selection considers interaction history and current states.
5. **`interaction_gen` (Character Agent):** The selected character (or a group/environment agent) generates their interaction (`y_t,k`), which can include thoughts (in `[...]`), speech (plain text), and actions (in `(...)`). The output is based on their state, motivation, and the ongoing scene.
6. **`world_update` (World Model):** After each interaction, the World Model assesses if the global and/or location state requires an update (`S_ℓ_t,(t,k+1)_w`) due to persistent changes caused by the interaction.
7. **`character_update` (Character Agent):** After the entire scene concludes, the Character Agent updates the `profile (P_i,(t+1))`, `hidden tracker (H_i,(t+1))`, and `short description` for all participating characters (`S_i,(t+1)_c`), reflecting the long-term impact of the scene's events.

#### 3.3. Dataset Construction

- **Source Data:** 57 chronologically narrated books from Project Gutenberg, chosen for their public domain status and suitability for sequential analysis.
- **Extraction Process:** Gemini-2.5-Pro LLM is used to extract structured data.
 - **Scene Extraction:** Books are segmented into chunks, from which scenes are extracted with summaries, scenarios, key characters, and multi-turn interactions (thoughts, speech, actions). Truncated scenes are handled across chunks.
 - **Character Construction:** LLM unifies character names, initializes open-schema profiles and hidden trackers from early scenes, and then updates these states scene-by-scene, using later narrative as look-ahead evidence to justify persistent changes.
 - **World Construction:** Global world states and location-level states are initialized and updated interaction-by-interaction, capturing systemic conditions and physical changes.
- **Train/Test Split:** The extracted timelines are converted into 138,596 supervised training samples for the seven tasks. Test data consists of 222 snapshots from specific time points, divided into in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) sets to evaluate generalization.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Framework

A trajectory-level LLM-as-Judge protocol is employed, measuring performance across complete multi-scene simulations rather than isolated turns.

- **Metrics:** 10 dimensions and 20 sub-metrics (0-100 scale), categorized into:
 - **CHARACTER Score:** (6 dimensions, 11 metrics) measures Character Consistency (Profile Fidelity, Speaking Style Fidelity, Motivation-Driven Behavior), Evolution Quality (Profile Update Fidelity, Profile Evolution Smoothness), Environmental Grounding (Environment Awareness, Environmental Utilization), Interaction Quality (Contextual Responsiveness, Narrative Progression), Motivation Generation (Motivation Quality), and Instruction Compliance.
 - **WORLD Score:** (4 dimensions, 9 metrics) measures Scene Planning (Cast Selection Rationality, Location & Scenario Rationality, Scene Continuity & Coherence), Speaker Management (Turn & Scene Orchestration), World State Maintenance (Global Update Sensitivity, Global State Accuracy, Location Update Sensitivity, Location State Accuracy), and Instruction Compliance.
- **Scoring:** Each metric uses an independent LLM judge receiving relevant inputs and a rubric. Scores are aggregated hierarchically.
- **Error Penalties:** Logarithmic-decay penalties are applied to Instruction Compliance for premature terminations, and task-specific metrics receive a penalty equivalent to a failed scene.
- **Human Evaluation:** A pairwise human evaluation study with three trained native English speakers validated the reliability of the LLM-as-Judge by measuring agreement on model preferences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the EvolvingWorld framework yield several findings regarding its capabilities for long-horizon literary simulation:

- **Overall Performance Improvement:** EvolvingWorld-trained models consistently demonstrated improved performance in both Character Agent and World Model tasks compared to their untrained backbone counterparts and existing role-playing baselines (CoSER, Crab). This indicates that the book-to-world supervision effectively enhances simulation quality.
- **Enhanced Character Agent Capabilities:**
 - **Character Consistency and Evolution:** Significant gains were observed in metrics related to character consistency (e.g., Profile Fidelity, Speaking Style Fidelity, Motivation-Driven Behavior) and especially in evolution quality (Profile Update Fidelity, Profile Evolution Smoothness). This suggests that EW training helps models maintain stable character traits while enabling smoother, more coherent profile updates based on narrative events.
 - **Environmental Grounding:** Improvements were noted in Environment Awareness, indicating better integration of characters with their surrounding world context.
 - **Superiority over Role-Play Baselines:** EW-trained Character Agents substantially outperformed role-play-only baselines (CoSER, Crab) on matched backbones, highlighting the necessity of modeling coupled character and world state evolution for robust long-horizon role-playing.
- **Improved World Model Performance:**
 - **Structured State Tracking:** World Model tasks, which involve explicit structured state tracking, are generally more challenging than character-agent tasks for LLMs. However, EW training led to clear improvements, particularly in long-range scene continuity, turn/scene orchestration, and location-level updates.
 - **Competitive Performance:** An EW-trained Qwen-32B model achieved a World Model Average score of 59.87, surpassing several closed-source models like Claude-4.6-Sonnet and Gemini-2.5-Flash, demonstrating the efficacy of targeted supervision.
- **Comparison with BookWorld:**
 - **Higher Overall Scores:** EvolvingWorld consistently achieved higher average scores across shared metrics compared to BookWorld (Ran et al., 2025), especially in character evolution metrics (e.g., PUF, PES, MQ) and world modeling aspects like scene continuity.
 - **Mitigation of Long-Horizon Degradation:** BookWorld exhibited performance degradation in Profile Evolution Smoothness (PES) and Scene Continuity & Coherence (SCC) as simulations lengthened. EvolvingWorld, conversely, mitigated this trend, with PES even showing improvement over longer trajectories, indicating its effectiveness in sustaining coherent long-horizon evolution.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Importance of State Updates:** Ablating either character state or world state updates led to significant performance drops, confirming that both mechanisms are crucial and interact in a coupled manner. Removing world updates particularly impacted world-side continuity and orchestration, while character updates affected character evolution and grounding.
 - **Hidden Tracker Validation:** Removing the hidden tracker reduced Profile Update Fidelity and Profile Evolution Smoothness, confirming its role in accumulating weak evidence for justified profile changes.
 - **Open vs. Fixed Schema:** Replacing open schemas with fixed ones for character profiles and global world states consistently reduced performance, supporting the value of adaptive, book-specific dimension inference.
- **In- and Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** Full-mixture training on EvolvingWorld data led to consistent improvements on both in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) test examples, suggesting that the learned abilities are generalizable beyond the specific training books.
- **Evaluation Robustness:** The LLM-as-Judge evaluation protocol demonstrated high agreement across different LLM judges (Claude-4.6-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5.1-Chat) and strong agreement with human evaluators in pairwise comparisons. This indicates the reliability and stability of the evaluation framework.
- **Downstream Application:** The structured scene representations generated by EvolvingWorld were successfully used as input for automatic video generation, demonstrating potential for downstream creative applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EvolvingWorld introduces a new formulation for interactive literary world simulation, redefining it as a long-horizon process where characters and the world actively co-evolve rather than remaining static or undergoing isolated updates. This represents a methodological shift from merely reproducing aspects of an original book to sustaining a grounded, continuously developing interactive world.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Advancement in LLM-driven Narrative Generation:** By addressing the limitations of static personas and fixed world models, EvolvingWorld provides a framework for more dynamic, coherent, and grounded interactive narratives. This can lead to richer, more engaging experiences in interactive fiction, games, and other story-driven applications.
- **Improved Character Dynamics:** The introduction of open-schema character profiles and a multi-timescale evolution mechanism (including the hidden tracker) allows for more nuanced and believable character development. This provides a foundation for creating literary agents that exhibit genuine growth, learning, and adaptation over time, reflecting the complexities of human-like behavior.
- **Robust World Modeling:** The LLM-based World Model, with its open-schema design for both global and location/entity-level states, enables the creation of diverse and detailed virtual environments. The continuous updating of these states in response to character interactions establishes a more responsive and immersive world, where character actions have persistent consequences.
- **Comprehensive Benchmark and Evaluation Protocol:** The creation of a large-scale dataset from 57 books and a detailed, trajectory-level LLM-as-Judge evaluation framework are significant contributions to the research community. This benchmark, covering 10 dimensions and 20 metrics, provides a standardized tool for assessing the persistent quality of character and world evolution, facilitating future research and development in this domain. The validated robustness of the LLM-as-Judge further enhances its utility.
- **Foundation for Controllable Interactive Worlds:** By decomposing the complex simulation into seven trainable tasks, EvolvingWorld offers a modular and controllable architecture. This modularity could enable finer-grained control over narrative progression, character arcs, and world dynamics, opening avenues for new forms of interactive storytelling.
- **Enabling Downstream Applications:** The structured nature of the generated scenes and evolving states can serve as direct inputs for various downstream applications, such as automated video generation, as demonstrated in the paper. This integration could reduce the manual effort in creating animated narratives from text.

While acknowledging limitations such as the objective nature of the world state, constraints on context length for world representation, and the domain specificity of the current dataset, EvolvingWorld lays foundational groundwork for developing more sophisticated literary agents and creating truly interactive, evolving virtual narrative environments. Its open-schema design, in particular, suggests adaptability to a broader range of narrative domains in future work.
