# TacWAM: Anchor-Guided World Action Model with Mechanics-Aware Tactile Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University and Manifold AI developed TacWAM, a World Action Model that integrates mechanics-aware tactile prediction to enhance robot manipulation in contact-rich tasks. The model achieved an average success rate of 75.0% across four complex manipulation tasks, representing a 37.5 percentage point improvement over prior strong baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional Vision-only World Action Models (WAMs) lack direct information about local mechanics (force, deformation, slip) crucial for contact-rich robot manipulation tasks.
- Visual data offers indirect and insufficient supervision for fine manipulation, as visually similar states can correspond to different underlying physical interactions.
- Integrating predictive tactile information into WAMs without creating a train-test mismatch, where future data used in training is unavailable at deployment, remains a challenge.

## Method

- Developed **TacWAM**, a ==tri-modal World Action Model== generator that integrates mechanics-aware tactile prediction into a visual WAM.
- Introduced a ==Spatially Aligned Fusion (SAF) Tactile Encoder== that maps raw tactile data (image, force field, deformation flow) to a mechanics-aware latent space, regularized by global wrench reconstruction.
- Designed an ==Anchor-Guided Tri-Modal (AGT) Attention== mechanism to strictly control information flow, allowing future tactile states to supervise training without being direct inputs for action generation during deployment.

## Results

- TacWAM achieved an average success rate of **75.0%** across four contact-rich manipulation tasks (potato-chip grasping, cherry grasping, whiteboard wiping, two-pen twirling).
- The model demonstrated a **37.5 percentage point** improvement in average success rate compared to the strongest baseline, VT-WAM.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of tactile history, showing a **27.5 percentage point** performance drop without it, and demonstrated that direct access to future tactile predictions by action tokens during training significantly impairs deployment performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a tactile history context significantly improves the accuracy of future tactile prediction, compensating for the ambiguity of single-frame tactile observations and enhancing manipulation performance.
> - A controlled information flow, like that enabled by Anchor-Guided Tri-Modal Attention, is essential for leveraging future sensory predictions during training without degrading performance due to train-test mismatch during deployment.
> - Representing tactile information with dense force fields, mesh deformation flow, and global wrench signals provides a physically meaningful latent space that offers superior supervision for learning contact-rich manipulation skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing the challenges of contact-rich tasks through the integration of multimodal sensing and predictive learning.

**World Action Models (WAMs):** Recent advancements in robot control have seen the emergence of World Action Models (WAMs), which combine future-state prediction with robot action generation. These models learn predictive representations of how an environment evolves and use this predictive capacity to inform and improve control policies. Current WAMs predominantly rely on visual state transitions for their predictions, capturing scene structure, object appearance, and visible motion.

**Limitations of Vision for Contact-Rich Tasks:** While visual prediction is effective for many scenarios, it provides limited direct information about local mechanics during contact-rich manipulation. Forces, deformation, shear, and slip, which are critical for tasks like fragile grasping or maintaining sustained contact, are only indirectly expressed in visual data. Visually similar states can correspond to substantially different underlying physical interactions (e.g., a stable-looking grasp near slip, or an object appearing intact while experiencing unsafe forces).

**Visuo-Tactile Learning and Predictive Tactile Learning:** To overcome these limitations, prior research has explored the use of tactile sensing. Tactile information offers direct evidence of interaction processes, enabling improved contact perception, multimodal fusion, policy refinement, and control in dexterous manipulation. A significant avenue within this research involves predictive tactile learning, where future tactile signals are predicted to enhance contact-aware action learning. Existing works have demonstrated that both current tactile conditioning and predictive tactile learning can benefit manipulation.

**TacWAM's Contribution:** TacWAM extends the current landscape by focusing on *how* tactile futures are represented and *how* they are utilized within WAM training. It aims to integrate physically informative tactile futures into WAM training as a supervisory signal, without allowing these future targets to become direct inputs for action generation during deployment. This addresses a design challenge: providing rich contact-related supervision during training while maintaining deployment consistency in information access for action generation. The paper differentiates itself by introducing a mechanics-aware tactile representation and an attention mechanism that explicitly manages information flow between observed anchors, future predictions, and action generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop a World Action Model that effectively incorporates tactile information for contact-rich manipulation tasks.** The motivation stems from the observation that traditional vision-centric WAMs lack direct supervision for critical physical interactions like force, deformation, shear, and slip, which are crucial for fine manipulation.
2. **Design a tactile future prediction mechanism that provides physically meaningful supervisory signals during WAM training.** This involves creating a tactile representation that captures the nuances of contact mechanics beyond simple binary contact states, such as dense force fields, deformation flow, and global wrench information.
3. **Ensure that future tactile states enhance WAM training without becoming privileged inputs to the action generation process during deployment.** This addresses a potential train-test mismatch problem, where policies trained with future information might perform poorly when only current observations are available at inference. The goal is for tactile futures to enrich the learned predictive representations rather than serving as direct anticipatory inputs for actions.
4. **Integrate recent tactile history to provide temporal context for future tactile prediction.** Recognizing that single-frame touch can be ambiguous, the model aims to leverage a history of tactile interactions to better disambiguate the current contact phase and predict future tactile evolution more accurately.

The motivation behind these objectives is to enhance robot manipulation capabilities in tasks that involve intricate physical contact, such as grasping fragile objects, maintaining sustained surface contact, or performing dynamic in-hand manipulation. By providing a WAM with a richer understanding of contact mechanics through tactile prediction and a controlled information flow, the authors aim to achieve more robust and skillful robot control in these challenging scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TacWAM enhances a visual World Action Model with predictive tactile state modeling, implemented through a tri-modal WAM generator. Its methodology involves three primary, interconnected design choices: a specialized tactile encoder, a tactile history mechanism, and a unique attention architecture.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

TacWAM operates at time *t* by observing visual data (*o<sub>v</sub>*), tactile data (*o<sub>tac</sub>*), proprioception (*q*), and task/language conditions (*l*). Raw tactile readings are mapped into a learned tactile representation, *z<sub>tac</sub>*, which serves as the prediction space for future tactile modeling. At the beginning of an action chunk *t<sub>0</sub>*, TacWAM predicts future visual states (F<sub>V</sub>), future tactile states (F<sub>T</sub>), and an action sequence (F<sub>A</sub>). The tactile prediction branch also incorporates a compact tactile history context (*c<sub>tac</sub>*). Action tokens are represented as target robot states for the next transition.

#### 3.2. Spatially Aligned Fusion (SAF) Tactile Encoder

The SAF Tactile Encoder (E<sub>SAF</sub>) and a tactile reconstructor (R<sub>tac</sub>) define the tactile latent prediction space. Each tactile observation (*o<sub>tac</sub>*) comprises three synchronized and spatially registered signals from two gripper-side tactile sensors:
- **Rectified tactile image (I<sub>rect</sub>):** Visual appearance of the contact surface.
- **Dense local force field (F):** Distribution of forces across the sensor surface.
- **Mesh deformation flow (M<sub>flow</sub>):** Vectors indicating the deformation of the sensor surface.

These inputs are encoded by corresponding branches for each sensor side, and the features are then fused bilaterally into a single-frame tactile representation *z<sub>tac</sub>*. This encoder is designed to ensure that both observed and predicted tactile states occupy the same latent space. To maintain physical relevance, R<sub>tac</sub> reconstructs the dense force fields, mesh deformation flow, and a bilateral resultant wrench (3D force and 3D torque for each gripper) from *z<sub>tac</sub>*. This reconstruction acts as a global mechanics supervision signal, compelling the latent state to preserve critical local and global contact information.

#### 3.3. Tactile History-Modulated Prediction

To address the ambiguity of single-frame tactile observations, TacWAM incorporates a tactile history encoder (E<sub>hist</sub>). This component summarizes recent tactile changes from a window of past tactile latents (*z<sub>tac</sub>* from *t<sub>0</sub>-T<sub>hist</sub>+1* to *t<sub>0</sub>*) into a compact, chunk-level tactile context (*c<sub>tac</sub>*). This history context does not become an additional memory token but instead modulates the tactile prediction expert through adaptive normalization. This modulation helps the tactile prediction branch to disambiguate the current interaction phase and infer the tactile evolution trend, enabling more accurate predictions of force and deformation changes. The history context is not directly exposed to the action stream.

#### 3.4. Anchor-Guided Tri-Modal (AGT) Attention

AGT Attention manages the information flow between visual, tactile, and action streams within a Mixture-of-Transformers backbone, where modality-specific experts communicate via masked mixed self-attention. The tokens are arranged as: [V<sub>0</sub>, V<sub>1:H</sub> | T<sub>0</sub>, T<sub>1:H</sub> | A], where:
- V<sub>0</sub>: Current visual anchor.
- V<sub>1:H</sub>: Future visual prediction tokens.
- T<sub>0</sub>: Current clean tactile anchor (*z<sub>tac</sub>* at *t<sub>0</sub>*).
- T<sub>1:H</sub>: Future tactile prediction tokens (*z<sub>tac</sub>* from *t<sub>0</sub>+1* to *t<sub>0</sub>+H*).
- A: Action tokens.

The core principle of AGT is to isolate future sensory prediction targets from the action branch. Specifically:
- Action tokens can only read V<sub>0</sub>, T<sub>0</sub>, and other action tokens. They are prohibited from accessing future visual (V<sub>1:H</sub>) or tactile (T<sub>1:H</sub>) prediction tokens.
- Visual tokens V<sub>0</sub> and V<sub>1:H</sub> operate under a video first-frame-causal mask.
- Tactile future tokens T<sub>1:H</sub> can read V<sub>0</sub>, T<sub>0</sub>, and other future tactile tokens under a tactile first-frame-causal mask, but not action tokens.

This design ensures that while future tactile and visual states provide rich supervisory signals during training, the action generation process relies solely on information available at deployment (current observations and task context). Task language and proprioception are provided separately via expert cross-attention.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference

Training proceeds in two stages:
1. **Tactile Representation Pretraining:** E<sub>SAF</sub> and R<sub>tac</sub> are jointly optimized using a tactile reconstruction loss (L<sub>SAF</sub>) combining force-field, bilateral resultant wrench, and mesh-flow reconstruction. After this, the tactile encoder and decoder are frozen.
2. **WAM Generator Training:** The WAM generator is trained to jointly predict visual futures, action chunks, and tactile futures using scheduler-weighted flow-matching objectives. The tactile history encoder is trained concurrently. Future tactile prediction (T<sub>1:H</sub>) is supervised by the frozen tactile representation, and gradients flow through the frozen, but differentiable, R<sub>tac</sub> to further regularize the predicted tactile latents with semantic consistency. An auxiliary contact-event loss (L<sub>contact</sub>) is applied to the tactile history encoder, derived from binary contact sequences.

During deployment, the model operates in a closed-loop, receding-horizon manner. It uses current visual/tactile anchors and task/proprioceptive context to output an action sequence. Predicted future tactile states can be decoded for analysis but are not used for online correction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TacWAM was conducted on a real-world manipulation platform across four contact-rich tasks, comparing its performance against four baseline categories.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Platform:** Agilex Piper manipulator with two Xense G1-WS tactile sensors on gripper sides, overhead and wrist cameras, synchronized at 30 Hz.
- **Data:** 300 real-world demonstration episodes per task, averaging 500 frames, including synchronized visual, proprioceptive, action, and tactile streams (rectified image, dense force fields, mesh deformation flow).
- **Tasks (Figure 2):**
 - **Potato-chip grasping:** Fragile, requiring precise force regulation.
 - **Cherry grasping:** Variable size, requiring stable contact confirmation.
 - **Whiteboard wiping:** Sustained surface contact, stressing pressure, shear, and continuity.
 - **Two-pen twirling:** Dynamic bilateral contact changes, requiring evolving force and rotation dynamics.
- **Success Criteria:** Task completion without violating specific physical constraints (e.g., no breakage, stable grasp, sustained contact).
- **Baselines:**
 - **π<sub>0.5</sub>:** Vision-language-action (VLA) policy.
 - **Fast-WAM:** Vision-only World Action Model.
 - **RDP:** Reactive Diffusion Policy (visual-tactile, reactive).
 - **VT-WAM:** Visual-Tactile World Action Model (reimplemented).
- **Evaluation Protocol:** 20 trials per method per task, varying object poses and initial robot configurations. Success rate reported as percentage of successful executions.

#### 4.2. Main Results (RQ1)

Table 2 summarizes the task success rates:

| Method | Chip | Cherry | Wiping | Twirling | Avg. |
| :---------- | :-------- | :-------- | :-------- | :-------- | :-------- |
| π<sub>0.5</sub> | 10.0% | 60.0% | 35.0% | 20.0% | 31.3% |
| Fast-WAM | 15.0% | 40.0% | 20.0% | 5.0% | 20.0% |
| RDP | 50.0% | 45.0% | 0.0% | 35.0% | 32.5% |
| VT-WAM | 45.0% | 45.0% | 30.0% | 30.0% | 37.5% |
| **TacWAM** | **90.0%** | **70.0%** | **75.0%** | **65.0%** | **75.0%** |

- **Quantitative Analysis:** TacWAM achieved the highest success rates across all tasks, averaging 75.0%. This represents a 37.5 percentage point improvement over the strongest baseline (VT-WAM, 37.5%).
 - Significant gains were observed in potato-chip grasping (+40.0 points over RDP) and whiteboard wiping (+40.0 points over π<sub>0.5</sub>).
 - Substantial improvements were also noted for cherry grasping (+10.0 points over π<sub>0.5</sub>) and two-pen twirling (+30.0 points over RDP).
- **Qualitative Analysis:**
 - Tactile sensing and predictive training aided in precise grasping of irregular/fragile objects (potato-chip, cherry), where vision-only baselines often failed due to missed contact or over-squeezing.
 - For tasks with visual occlusion or intricate contact dynamics (whiteboard wiping, two-pen twirling), TacWAM maintained sustained contact and handled dynamic bilateral interactions more reliably than vision-only or reactive tactile models, which often produced visually plausible but physically ineffective motions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Results (RQ2, RQ3)

Ablation studies analyzed the roles of tactile history and AGT visibility on selected contact-rich tasks (Chip grasping, Whiteboard wiping). Results are in Table 3:

| Variant | Chip | Wiping | Avg. |
| :------------------------ | :-------- | :-------- | :-------- |
| **TacWAM** | **90.0%** | **75.0%** | **82.5%** |
| w/o History | 50.0% | 60.0% | 55.0% |
| w/o History + Attn-AT | 30.0% | 45.0% | 37.5% |
| w/o History + Attn-VT | 10.0% | 5.0% | 7.5% |

- **Without Tactile History:** Removing the tactile history encoder and context resulted in a substantial average performance drop from 82.5% to 55.0% (−27.5 points). This was particularly pronounced in chip grasping (90.0% to 50.0%), suggesting that temporal tactile context is crucial for inferring force buildup and contact phase beyond a single current observation.
- **Without Tactile History + Attn-AT (Action-to-Tactile Access):** Further relaxing AGT to allow action tokens to read the full future tactile prediction sequence (T<sub>0:H</sub>) led to another degradation, averaging 37.5%. This indicated that exposing action tokens to future prediction targets during training created an unreliable shortcut, leading to a train-test mismatch during deployment when such future information is unavailable.
- **Without Tactile History + Attn-VT (Bidirectional Visual-Tactile Access):** The most relaxed attention mask, allowing direct bidirectional interaction between visual and tactile future tokens, resulted in a near collapse of performance, averaging 7.5%. This suggests that unrestricted inter-modal future information exchange can destabilize co-training and severely impair action generation.

#### 4.4. Future Tactile State Prediction (RQ2)

Analysis of predicted force evolution (Figure 4) revealed:
- TacWAM, with tactile history, tracked both the magnitude and temporal trend of ground-truth resultant force more closely than the "w/o History" variant on tasks like whiteboard wiping and two-pen twirling.
- The "w/o History" variant exhibited temporal delays in force prediction, suggesting it struggled to promptly update its estimate of the current contact phase without recent tactile context. TacWAM's history component allowed it to infer contact states more promptly and predict force changes with reduced delay.

These ablations provide consistent evidence that tactile history and controlled information flow through AGT Attention are important components for the robust performance of TacWAM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of TacWAM presents several areas of significance and potential impact within robotic manipulation research:

1. **Enhanced Contact-Rich Manipulation:** TacWAM's demonstrated superior performance in tasks requiring precise force regulation, sustained contact, and dynamic bilateral interaction indicates a significant step forward for robots operating in complex physical environments. The ability to grasp fragile objects, maintain consistent contact during wiping, and manipulate multiple objects dynamically, all with higher reliability, expands the range of practical applications for robotic systems.
2. **Mechanics-Aware Tactile Representation:** The Spatially Aligned Fusion (SAF) Tactile Encoder, which integrates tactile appearance, dense force fields, and deformation flow with global wrench supervision, establishes a more informative and physically grounded tactile representation. This approach moves beyond simple contact detection to capture detailed mechanical interactions, which is crucial for learning nuanced manipulation skills. This encoder could potentially be adopted or inspire similar representations in other tactile-sensing applications.
3. **Principled Integration of Predictive Tactile Learning:** The core principle of using future tactile states as a supervisory signal *without* making them direct inputs for action generation during deployment addresses a fundamental challenge in predictive control. This design (Anchor-Guided Tri-Modal Attention) offers a robust framework for leveraging rich future information for training without introducing a train-test mismatch, thereby promoting more generalizable and reliable policies.
4. **Importance of Temporal Context in Tactile Sensing:** The finding that tactile history significantly improves future force prediction and manipulation success highlights the critical role of temporal context in interpreting ambiguous single-frame tactile observations. This emphasizes that contact state is not merely instantaneous but a dynamic process, and models benefit from understanding its recent evolution.
5. **Foundation for Future Multimodal World Models:** TacWAM provides a concrete example of how vision-centric world models can be effectively augmented with other modalities, specifically touch, to enhance predictive capabilities and downstream task performance. This work contributes to the broader effort of building more comprehensive multimodal world models that can reason about a wider range of physical phenomena.
6. **Potential for Human-Robot Interaction and Dexterous Manipulation:** Improved understanding and control of contact mechanics could facilitate more natural and safe human-robot interactions, particularly in shared workspaces or tasks requiring physical collaboration. Furthermore, the principles demonstrated in TacWAM could be extended to more complex dexterous manipulation scenarios with multi-fingered hands, where intricate contact dynamics are paramount.

Overall, TacWAM provides a robust and principled approach to integrating mechanics-aware tactile prediction into World Action Models, addressing key limitations of vision-only approaches and offering a framework for learning more capable and reliable robot manipulation skills in contact-rich environments.
