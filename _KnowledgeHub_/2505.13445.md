# Trust, But Verify: A Self-Verification Approach to Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tencent and CUHK-Shenzhen introduce RISE, a reinforcement learning framework that trains language models to simultaneously improve reasoning and self-verification capabilities through online verification, demonstrating enhanced performance across mathematical reasoning benchmarks while maintaining strong verification abilities across multiple model sizes (1.5B-7B parameters).

## Problem

- Existing LLMs often exhibit superficial self-reflection and struggle with robust self-assessment
- Current approaches tend to decouple problem-solving from verification, lacking direct feedback for verification skills

## Method

- ==Unified RL framework== combining problem-solving and self-verification training
- ==Online verification system== where models verify their own on-policy generated solutions
- Integration of ==verification-weighted majority voting== for enhanced test-time performance

## Results

- Consistent outperformance of Zero-RL baseline in both reasoning and self-verification accuracy
- Significant improvements over standard SFT and base models across different model sizes (**1.5B-7B** parameters)
- Enhanced test-time scaling through improved consistency with verification-weighted majority voting
- Demonstrated scalability across different model sizes while maintaining verification capabilities

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online verification of self-generated solutions is crucial for developing robust verification capabilities
> - Unified training of both solving and verification skills leads to better overall performance
> - Model performance continues to scale with increased training compute

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research fits into the rapidly evolving field of LLMs and their application to complex reasoning tasks. It builds upon two key areas of existing work:

- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** RLVR is a promising approach to improve LLMs' reasoning abilities, especially in domains where correctness can be programmatically verified (e.g., mathematics, programming). This paper acknowledges existing RLVR methods and aims to address their limitations.
- **Self-Critique and Self-Verification in LLMs:** There's growing interest in equipping LLMs with the ability to assess their own outputs. This work responds to the issue of superficial self-reflection and decoupling of learning to solve and verify.

The paper makes a novel contribution by integrating problem-solving and self-verification into a unified RL framework with verifiable rewards driving both. RISE distinguishes itself from previous approaches by:

- **Online Verification:** Training the model to verify its own *on-policy* generated solutions, providing direct and contemporaneous feedback for the verification skill itself within the RL loop.
- **Simultaneous Training:** Explicitly training the LLM to improve both its problem-solving *and* self-verification abilities within a single, integrated RL process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** The primary objective is to develop a reinforcement learning framework (RISE) that enables LLMs to improve their reasoning abilities while simultaneously fostering robust self-verification skills.
- **Motivation:**
 - LLMs show potential in complex reasoning, but can struggle with robust self-assessment skills, leading to superficial self-reflection.
 - Existing RLVR methods may not sufficiently address this, as they might focus on generating correct answers without developing deeper understanding or self-verification.
 - Existing self-critique approaches often decouple the problem-solving and verification processes, or lack direct feedback for the verification skill within the RL loop.
 - The researchers aim to create a more dynamic and grounded self-improvement loop where the model learns to both solve problems and critically evaluate its own solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces the RISE framework, which operates in two key stages within each training iteration:

- **Problem Solution and Verification Generation:**
 - The model generates solutions for a batch of problems from the training set, using chain-of-thought reasoning.
 - A rule-based outcome verifier provides rewards based on the correctness and formatting of the solutions.
 - The problem and the model-generated solution are then formatted into a verification prompt, prompting the model to critique its own solution.
 - The original reward from the generation phase is reused as the ground-truth score for the verification task.

- **RL Optimization:**
 - The generation data and verification data are combined into a single training batch.
 - A unified RL objective (Proximal Policy Optimization - PPO) is used to update the model's parameters based on both problem-solving and self-verification trajectories and rewards.

The authors use the PPO algorithm for RL, with Qwen2.5 models of varying sizes (1.5B, 3B, and 7B). They evaluate the framework on several mathematical reasoning benchmarks, comparing RISE against:

- **Zero-RL baseline:** Trained only on problem-solving supervision.
- **Instruction-tuned models:** Qwen-3B-Instruct
- **Standard Supervised Fine-Tuned (SFT) models.**
- **Base models.**

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **RISE improves both Reasoning and Self-Verification:** RISE consistently outperforms the Zero-RL baseline in both reasoning accuracy and self-verification accuracy across all model sizes. Notably, the self-verification improvements are significant, demonstrating the framework's success in fostering these skills.
- **Scaling Model Size Helps:** Scaling model size improves reasoning performance while maintaining strong verification capabilities.
- **RISE models outperform SFT and base models:** RISE models demonstrate substantial improvement compared to standard SFT and base models
- **Online Verification is Crucial:** Ablation studies demonstrate that online verification, where the model verifies its own on-policy generated solutions, is crucial to the success of RISE.
- **Verification Compute Benefits:** Model performance keeps scaling as training compute increases, further demonstrating the benefits of the RISE method.
- **Enhanced Test-Time Performance:** RISE enhances test-time scaling by improving consistency. RISE models outperform standard majority voting when enhanced with verification-weighted majority voting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Addresses a Key Limitation of LLMs:** The paper directly addresses the problem of superficial self-reflection in LLMs, which is a critical barrier to building more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.
- **Novel and Effective Framework:** RISE offers a novel and effective approach to integrating self-verification into the RL training process, leading to significant improvements in both reasoning and self-assessment abilities.
- **Scalable and Adaptable:** The framework is scalable (demonstrated across different model sizes) and potentially adaptable to other domains with verifiable rewards.
- **Improved Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By improving self-verification, the research contributes to building more reliable and trustworthy LLMs, which is crucial for their deployment in real-world applications.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper suggests several promising avenues for future research, including exploring different reasoning domains, incorporating other RL algorithms, and integrating RISE with retrieval-augmented generation or external tools.

In conclusion, the research presents a significant advancement in the field of LLMs and RL, with the potential to contribute to the development of more robust, reliable, and self-aware AI systems.
