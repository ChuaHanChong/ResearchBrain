# Control of Legged Robots using Model Predictive Optimized Path Integral

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Calgary and the Technical University of Munich developed Model Predictive Optimized Path Integral (MPOPI), a control strategy for legged robots that integrates Model Predictive Path Integral with Cross-Entropy and Covariance Matrix Adaptation methods. This approach enhances sample efficiency and adaptive exploration, enabling a simulated Go1 quadruped robot to climb stairs approximately 40% faster and successfully overcome taller obstacles than a standard MPPI controller.

## Problem

- Gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) is often too computationally intensive for real-time whole-body dynamics and collision constraints in complex legged robot tasks.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) faces difficulties with exploration in complex environments due to sparse rewards and demands extensive, task-specific effort for reward design and sim-to-real transfer.
- There is no established consensus on which sampling-based MPC technique is most effective for complex legged locomotion control, despite their potential to bridge gaps between traditional MPC and RL.

## Method

- Developed Model Predictive Optimized Path Integral (MPOPI), a novel sampling-based ==MPC== strategy that unifies components from MPPI, Cross-Entropy (CE), and Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA).
- MPOPI iteratively refines the sampling distribution over multiple inner cycles by selecting 'elite' samples (CE) and adaptively updating the mean and covariance (CMA), before applying the final MPPI weighting.
- The controller is integrated into a hierarchical locomotion framework, utilizing parallel trajectory rollouts in the MuJoCo physics engine and a PD controller for real-time torque commands.

## Results

- MPOPI enabled a simulated Go1 robot to climb stairs in approximately 30 seconds, outperforming MPPI which required about 53 seconds, and successfully climbed a 45 cm tall box where MPPI failed.
- In loco-manipulation tasks, MPOPI achieved smoother and more accurate trajectories for both the robot and the pushed box, successfully reaching distant targets that MPPI failed to achieve.
- MPOPI consistently exhibited sharper, narrower cost distributions with lower average costs and reduced variability compared to MPPI across various challenging locomotion and manipulation scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining MPPI with CE and CMA significantly enhances sample efficiency and accelerates the search for optimal control actions in high-dimensional joint spaces.
> - The adaptive update of the covariance matrix in MPOPI is critical for dynamically adjusting exploration magnitude, allowing the robot to effectively find viable policies for complex tasks.
> - Utilizing multiple inner optimization cycles (L > 1) in MPOPI is particularly beneficial for complex tasks requiring greater exploration in joint space, leading to improved cost distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The control of legged robots for navigating rough terrains and cluttered environments remains a significant challenge in robotics. Current algorithms for legged locomotion and manipulation generally fall into two categories: gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) and gradient-free Reinforcement Learning (RL).

Gradient-based MPC approaches leverage system models for real-time trajectory optimization, not requiring offline training. However, these methods are often computationally intensive, making it difficult to incorporate whole-body dynamics and collision constraints in real-time without employing simplified models. While some recent works have enabled whole-body MPC and reduced online computation through learning solutions, these methods still face inherent computational demands.

Alternatively, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated progress in sim-to-real transfer, facilitated by parallel simulation and domain randomization. Despite these advancements, RL faces challenges such as difficult exploration in complex environments due to sparse rewards and the significant effort required to design suitable reward functions and locomotion parameters for each task. The process of sim-to-real randomization for RL policies also tends to be ad hoc and task-dependent.

Sampling-based MPC has emerged as an intermediate approach that combines elements from both traditional MPC and RL. It does not require offline training, can leverage parallel simulation, and is capable of mitigating issues encountered by gradient-based methods when dealing with discontinuous and non-convex dynamics. Prominent sampling-based techniques include Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA), and the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM). Despite the increasing recognition of sampling-based approaches for high-dimensional systems, there is an ongoing discussion regarding which specific technique is most suitable for complex legged locomotion control. This paper contributes to this area by investigating a combined sampling-based MPC strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate a sampling-based model predictive strategy for generating real-time whole-body motions for legged robots across multiple scenarios. This strategy, termed Model Predictive Optimized Path Integral (MPOPI), combines the Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) with Cross-Entropy (CE) and Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA) methods.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations observed in existing legged locomotion control paradigms:
- **Computational Intensity of MPC:** Traditional gradient-based MPC struggles with real-time whole-body dynamics and collision constraints due to high computational demands, often necessitating simplified models that limit the robot's capabilities in complex tasks.
- **Challenges in RL:** Reinforcement learning, while powerful, faces difficulties with exploration in complex environments (sparse rewards) and requires extensive, task-specific effort in designing reward functions and performing sim-to-real randomization.
- **Lack of Consensus in Sampling-Based MPC:** While sampling-based MPC offers a promising middle ground by avoiding offline training and handling non-convex dynamics, there is no established consensus on which specific technique (e.g., MPPI, CMA, CE) is most effective for complex legged locomotion control.

Given these challenges, the authors aim to develop and evaluate a unified approach that leverages the strengths of multiple sampling-based methods. Specifically, the paper proposes that by combining the benefits of MPPI (for weighted averaging of samples), CE (for elite sample selection and parameter updates), and CMA (for adaptive covariance matrix updates), a controller can achieve greater sample efficiency. This is intended to enable robots to attain superior locomotion results using fewer samples compared to typical MPPI algorithms, thereby addressing the computational efficiency and performance limitations of current methods.

The explicit contributions outlined in the paper are:
1. **Comparative Analysis:** To present an analysis of different sampling-based MPCs, highlighting the practical advantages and effectiveness of the combined MPOPI strategy for locomotion and loco-manipulation problems.
2. **Application to Quadrupedal Loco-manipulation:** To apply MPOPI to the problem of quadrupedal locomotion and loco-manipulation, demonstrating its utility in this context.
3. **Extensive Simulation Experiments:** To conduct a wide range of simulation experiments to demonstrate MPOPI's capabilities in solving high-dimensional and complex tasks for real-time whole-body motion generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on MPOPI, a novel sampling-based model predictive controller that integrates components from Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA), and the Cross-Entropy Method (CE).

**Preliminary Sampling-Based MPC Formulations:**
The paper first reviews three foundational sampling-based MPC algorithms:

- **Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI):** This method samples `N` control trajectories from a multivariate Gaussian distribution defined by a mean (μ) and a fixed covariance matrix (Σ). Each sampled trajectory's cost is evaluated via parallel simulation. Optimal control input is derived from an exponentially weighted average of samples, where weights are inversely proportional to trajectory costs, scaled by a temperature parameter (λ). The mean is updated in a receding horizon fashion, while the covariance matrix remains constant (Algorithm 1).
- **Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA):** Unlike MPPI, CMA dynamically updates both the mean and the covariance matrix of the search distribution. It employs a learning rate (α) to control the influence of new samples. The weights for updating the parameters are computed using a logarithmic ranking scheme, prioritizing better-performing samples while smoothly decaying influence for lower-ranked ones (Algorithm 2).
- **Cross Entropy (CE):** CE differs by sorting trajectory costs and selecting a fixed number of "elite" samples (`K_e`). Only these elite samples are used to determine the updated mean and covariance of the distribution for the next iteration. For elite samples, a uniform weight of `1/K_e` is assigned, and the learning rate (α) is set to 1 (Algorithm 3).

**Proposed Locomotion Framework (MPOPI):**
MPOPI combines these methods to enhance sample efficiency and accelerate the search for optimal control actions. The core idea is to iteratively refine the sampling distribution before the final MPPI weighting.

- **MPOPI Algorithm (Algorithm 4):**
 1. An initial set of randomized samples (`N/L` samples per cycle) is generated using a current mean (μ') and covariance (Σ').
 2. For `L` cycles, the framework performs an iterative refinement:
 - Each `N/L` sample sequence is simulated, and its cost is computed.
 - If `l < L` (i.e., not the final cycle), the samples are sorted by cost, and `K_e` elite samples are selected.
 - The mean (μ') and covariance (Σ') of the sampling distribution are updated using a combination of CMA and CE principles. Specifically, a weighted sum of the elite samples is used, with weights determined by the logarithmic ranking scheme from CMA, and the learning rate (α) influences the update step. A term `λ(1-α)μ'Σ^-1(u_t + μ' - μ_t)` is also added to the cost calculation, influencing the exploration.
 3. After `L` cycles, the final set of `N` trajectories (generated cumulatively over the cycles) and their costs are processed using the standard MPPI method. This involves calculating exponential weights (Equation 1a) and updating the main control trajectory mean (μ) (Equation 1b).
 4. The control input for the current timestep is the first element of the updated mean trajectory. The mean trajectory is then shifted for the receding horizon principle.
- **Locomotion Control Integration (Figure 1):** The MPOPI controller is integrated into a hierarchical locomotion framework. A user-specified goal directs the robot. The MPOPI module performs parallel trajectory rollouts using the MuJoCo physics engine. It evaluates costs, selects elite trajectories, and iteratively updates the mean and covariance of the sampling distribution over `L` cycles using CE and CMA principles. The resulting joint targets, refined by the MPPI weighting, are then passed to a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller operating at 500 Hz to compute and send torque commands to the robot. This approach aims to reduce the search space and generate smoother control inputs.

**Simulation Setup:**
- **Robot:** Go1 quadruped robot.
- **Physics Engine:** MuJoCo, running on a multi-core CPU.
- **Comparison:** MPOPI is compared against standard MPPI. For a fair comparison, the total number of samples is kept constant: MPPI uses 30 samples, while MPOPI uses 10 samples repeated over 3 cycles (`L = 3`), resulting in 30 effective samples.
- **Shared Parameters:** Time horizon (`T = 40`), temperature parameter (`λ = 0.1`).
- **Scenarios:**
 - Climbing stairs (various heights and tread lengths).
 - Climbing over large boxes (40 cm and 45 cm high).
 - Pushing a box (loco-manipulation tasks) along different trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The simulation experiments conducted on a Go1 quadruped robot demonstrate the performance of MPOPI compared to MPPI across various challenging locomotion and loco-manipulation tasks.

**A. Stair Climbing**
- **Performance:** MPOPI exhibited superior stability and accuracy, maintaining a smooth Center of Mass (CoM) trajectory with minimal lateral deviation. MPPI, in contrast, showed greater trajectory fluctuations.
- **Efficiency:** MPOPI achieved task completion significantly faster, taking approximately 30 seconds to climb the stairs, whereas MPPI required about 53 seconds for the same task.
- **Cost Distribution:** The cost density for MPOPI displayed a sharp and narrow peak near the lower end of the cost axis, indicating low average cost and low variability across trials. MPPI's cost distribution was broader, flatter, and spread across higher, more inconsistent costs.

**B. Climbing Large Boxes**
- **40 cm Box Height:** Both MPOPI and MPPI successfully climbed the 40 cm obstacle. MPOPI's cost distribution showed a high density near the lower cost range with more spread, while MPPI also maintained a low cost.
- **45 cm Box Height:** When the obstacle height increased to 45 cm (exceeding the robot's nominal body height of 27 cm), MPOPI maintained a prominent, sharp peak in its cost distribution at the lower cost range, indicating efficient and consistent performance despite increased task difficulty. MPPI, however, exhibited a broader and flatter cost distribution, with a larger mass spread toward higher cost values, suggesting reduced efficiency and increased performance variability. Ultimately, MPPI was unable to successfully complete the 45 cm climbing task.

**C. Pushing a Box (Loco-manipulation)**
- **Linear Trajectory:** For pushing a box along a straightforward linear path, both MPPI and MPOPI controllers achieved smooth trajectories and reached the goal with minimal errors.
- **Non-Rectilinear (3-point) Trajectory:** When commanded to push the box along a 3-point trajectory that was not rectilinear, MPOPI demonstrated smoother and more direct trajectories for both the robot and the box. This resulted in the box reaching its goal with higher accuracy. MPPI exhibited a more irregular box trajectory, particularly in the final stage, leading to deviations from the final goal. The robot's path under MPPI also showed greater lateral oscillation, suggesting less coordinated motion.
- **Far Target (2,2):** In a test where the box's goal was set at a more distant location (2, 2), MPOPI successfully guided the robot to push the box to the target, closely following a direct path. MPPI, in contrast, failed to reach the target, with both the robot and the box deviating significantly from the desired trajectory. This indicates MPOPI's superior ability to maintain task accuracy over extended distances.

**D. MPOPI Key Parameters Analysis**
- **Number of Loops (L):** An analysis of the effect of the number of inner optimization cycles (L) revealed that for simpler tasks like walking, increasing L did not significantly affect the cost distribution. However, for more complex tasks requiring greater exploration in joint space, such as stair climbing, setting L greater than one (L > 1) was observed to be beneficial, improving the cost distribution across various sample sizes.
- **Covariance Matrix Adaptation:** The adaptive update of the covariance matrix (Σ) in MPOPI was identified as a critical factor in its performance. For a difficult task like climbing a tall box (where MPPI failed), MPOPI successfully completed the task. The analysis showed that the exploration magnitude (largest eigenvalue of Σ) increased during challenging phases (e.g., actively climbing the box) and subsequently decreased and stabilized once an effective policy was discovered, indicating that the robot had found a viable strategy and required less variability in action sampling. This adaptive exploration mechanism contributed to MPOPI's ability to handle complex motion planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents Model Predictive Optimized Path Integral (MPOPI) as a robust and efficient control strategy for legged robots, particularly in challenging locomotion and loco-manipulation scenarios. The integration of MPPI with CE and CMA methods yields a controller that addresses several limitations observed in existing model predictive control and reinforcement learning approaches.

The primary significance of this work lies in its demonstration of enhanced **sample efficiency**. MPOPI is shown to achieve superior locomotion results using fewer samples compared to traditional MPPI algorithms. This increased efficiency translates to faster task completion times, as observed in the stair-climbing experiments, and the ability to solve more complex problems where MPPI alone might fail, such as climbing taller obstacles or executing intricate loco-manipulation trajectories.

The **adaptive nature** of MPOPI, particularly its ability to dynamically update the mean and covariance of the sampling distribution through CE and CMA, is a key contribution. This adaptive exploration allows the controller to effectively search the high-dimensional joint space, enabling robots to find viable paths and policies for tasks requiring significant dexterity and contact planning. This contrasts with MPPI's fixed covariance, which can limit its exploration capabilities in highly non-linear and constrained environments.

The successful application of MPOPI to **quadrupedal locomotion and loco-manipulation tasks** (stair climbing, obstacle traversal, box pushing) in simulation demonstrates its versatility. The results indicate that MPOPI provides improved performance across several metrics, including tracking error and overall cost, producing smoother and more accurate whole-body motion plans. This suggests MPOPI is a promising framework for robots operating in unstructured environments or performing complex, coupled tasks.

The findings also provide insights into the **hyperparameter tuning** of such combined methods, specifically highlighting the importance of the number of inner optimization cycles (L) for complex tasks. This contributes to the understanding of how to configure sampling-based MPC for optimal performance.

The potential impact of this research is in advancing the capabilities of legged robots for real-world deployment. By offering a more sample-efficient and robust control strategy, MPOPI could facilitate the development of autonomous systems capable of navigating highly complex and dynamic environments with greater agility and reliability. The stated future plan to apply the framework on real hardware suggests a pathway towards practical implementation, which could benefit applications in search and rescue, logistics, inspection, and other domains where legged locomotion is advantageous.
