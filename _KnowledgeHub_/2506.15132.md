# Booster Gym: An End-to-End Reinforcement Learning Framework for Humanoid Robot Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Booster Gym is presented as an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework for humanoid robot locomotion, utilizing an asymmetric actor-critic PPO architecture and comprehensive domain randomization to bridge the sim-to-real gap. It achieves zero-shot policy transfer to a physical humanoid robot, showcasing robust omnidirectional walking, adaptability to various terrains, and resistance to external disturbances.

## Problem

- Bridging the "sim-to-real gap" for reinforcement learning policies to enable robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to physical humanoid robots.
- The absence of an integrated, end-to-end framework for training and deploying RL-based humanoid locomotion, forcing researchers to assemble various disparate components.
- Challenges in simulating and controlling humanoid robots with parallel mechanical structures, as many physics engines are optimized for serial chains.

## Method

- Developed "**Booster Gym**," an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework using an asymmetric actor-critic architecture and PPO for policy optimization.
- Incorporated comprehensive domain randomization during training, covering robot dynamics, actuator properties, environmental factors, external disturbances, and communication latency, to address the sim-to-real gap.
- Designed a Python-based deployment pipeline that includes a series-parallel conversion module within the SDK to support complex mechanical structures like parallel linkages.

## Results

- The Booster T1 humanoid robot demonstrated successful omnidirectional walking capabilities, accurately tracking linear and angular velocity commands.
- Policies trained with the framework enabled stable locomotion on various terrains, including a 10-degree slope, and exhibited strong resistance to external impulsive and continuous disturbances.
- Achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, with joint trajectories from Isaac Gym closely mirroring real-world robot behavior after accounting for quantitatively measured real-world parameters in domain randomization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of an asymmetric actor-critic architecture and PPO allows for robust policy learning leveraging privileged simulation data while maintaining real-world deployability with limited observations.
> - Comprehensive and quantitatively informed domain randomization, including specific latency and parallel structure handling, is key to achieving effective zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer.
> - An integrated, open-source framework can significantly accelerate humanoid robot locomotion research by consolidating diverse components and reducing infrastructure setup burden.
