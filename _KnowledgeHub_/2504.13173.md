# It's All Connected: A Journey Through Test-Time Memorization, Attentional Bias, Retention, and Online Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified framework from Google Research introduces "Miras" for sequence modeling that reinterprets attention mechanisms and forgetting gates as forms of memory optimization, leading to three new architectures (Moneta, Yaad, and Memora) that demonstrate improved performance on language modeling and recall-intensive tasks while providing theoretical insights into existing models.

## Problem

- Existing sequence models like Transformers face limitations with long sequences due to quadratic complexity
- Lack of unified understanding of how different sequence modeling architectures relate to each other theoretically

## Method

- Developed ==Miras framework== that unifies different sequence models through ==associative memory== and ==attentional bias== concepts
- Created new architectures (==Moneta==, ==Yaad==, ==Memora==) with alternative attention mechanisms and retention gates based on framework insights

## Results

- **Three new architectures** outperform state-of-the-art baselines on various sequence modeling tasks
- Models demonstrate better scaling with context length and model size
- Framework successfully unifies understanding of existing architectures like Transformers and linear RNNs
- Moneta variant shows improved robustness to noise through **p-norm** objectives

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Forgetting mechanisms in sequence models can be reinterpreted as retention regularization
> - Different sequence models share underlying principles in how they manage memory and attention
> - Novel attention configurations and retention gates can improve model performance
