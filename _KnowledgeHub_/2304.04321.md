# ARNOLD: A Benchmark for Language-Grounded Task Learning With Continuous States in Realistic 3D Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> ARNOLD is a new benchmark for language-grounded robotic task learning using continuous object states within realistic 3D scenes. It challenges agents to interpret natural language and achieve precise continuous goals in eight manipulation tasks, built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim with PhysX 5.0, and reveals current models struggle with generalization, particularly to novel continuous states.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks simplify object goal states to discrete or binary, hindering learning of fine-grained continuous manipulation.
- Prior simulation environments lack the visual and physical fidelity necessary for realistic robot operation and effective Sim2Real transfer.
- Few benchmarks investigate precise language grounding to continuous object states, making it difficult for agents to interpret nuanced instructions like 'pour 75% of the water'.

## Method

- ARNOLD is built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim with PhysX 5.0, offering photo-realistic rendering and physically accurate simulations across 40 objects and 20 diverse 3D scenes.
- It provides eight language-conditioned manipulation tasks that require understanding and achieving continuous object states, such as 'half open' or '75% full'.
- The benchmark offers 10,000 expert demonstrations and systematic generalization splits to rigorously evaluate models on novel objects, scenes, and continuous goal states.

## Results

- PerAct, utilizing 3D voxelized representations, consistently outperformed 6D-CLIPort, which relied on 2D images, across all tasks.
- All tested models exhibited substantial performance drops on generalization splits, especially the 'Novel State' split, indicating poor generalization to unseen continuous goal values.
- Multi-task training unexpectedly led to performance degradation compared to single-task models, while preliminary Sim2Real experiments showed promising predictions but continued challenges in physical execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art models for language-conditioned manipulation struggle significantly with continuous control and generalization to novel goal states.
> - Representing rich 3D context (e.g., via voxelized grids) is crucial for complex manipulation tasks, outperforming 2D-based approaches.
> - More sophisticated language encoders like T5 may improve fine-grained language grounding compared to global representations from models like CLIP.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of ARNOLD is situated at the crucial intersection of embodied AI, robot learning, and natural language processing. The broader research landscape in these fields has seen significant advancements in creating simulated environments and benchmarks for training intelligent agents. However, the authors identify several critical limitations in existing benchmarks that ARNOLD seeks to overcome.

Firstly, many prior benchmarks, such as Alfred, ManiSkill, and CALVIN, frequently simplify object goal states to be discrete or binary (e.g., an object is either "open" or "closed"). This simplification, while reducing complexity, significantly constrains the learning of fine-grained manipulation skills and the robot's ability to interpret and act upon human instructions that often refer to continuous states (e.g., "half open," "a bit of water"). This disconnect poses a substantial challenge for transferring learned policies from simulation to the nuances of the real world. ARNOLD directly addresses this by focusing on tasks that require understanding and achieving *continuous* object states.

Secondly, existing environments often lack the visual and physical fidelity necessary for realistic robot operation. Many simulations are set in simple, "clean" environments, neglecting the complexities of real-world scenes filled with clutter, diverse textures, and dynamic lighting. Furthermore, physics simulations in some benchmarks tend to be simplified, for instance, by attaching objects to grippers rather than simulating realistic friction-based grasping, or by not accounting for the low-level geometry and dynamics of objects. ARNOLD explicitly tackles these limitations by leveraging NVIDIA's Isaac Sim, which provides photo-realistic rendering via ray tracing and physically accurate simulations powered by PhysX 5.0, including advanced fluid dynamics and friction-based grasping.

Thirdly, while language grounding in images and physical environments has received attention, few benchmarks have thoroughly investigated the grounding of language to *precise* and *continuous* object states for daily tasks. This means that instructions like "pour 75% of the water" are difficult for current systems to interpret accurately and execute. ARNOLD bridges this gap by explicitly providing language-conditioned tasks where goals are sampled from a continuous range of object states, pushing agents to ground human instructions to precise physical properties.

Finally, the problem of generalization remains a critical hurdle for embodied AI. Robots trained in one environment or with a specific set of objects often struggle to perform in novel scenarios. ARNOLD significantly advances this area by offering a systematic evaluation framework that includes dedicated data splits for novel goal states, novel objects, and novel scenes. This rigorous evaluation aims to reveal the true generalizability of learned policies, an aspect often less comprehensively tested in prior work.

In essence, ARNOLD distinguishes itself by creating a more challenging and realistic benchmark that combines language understanding, continuous state manipulation, and high-fidelity physical simulation within diverse, naturalistic 3D scenes. This holistic approach aims to push the boundaries of what robot learning models can achieve in interpreting and executing human-like instructions in complex physical environments, ultimately facilitating better Sim2Real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ARNOLD is to establish a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating language-grounded task learning in robotic systems, specifically focusing on tasks involving continuous object states within realistic 3D scenes. This objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of existing research and the requirements for developing truly intelligent and capable robots.

The overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between human capabilities and robotic performance in understanding and executing daily tasks. Humans effortlessly interpret nuanced instructions like "a cup half full" or "a drawer slightly open" and translate them into precise physical actions and desired object states. Current robotic systems, however, largely struggle with this, often being limited to tasks with discrete, binary goal states. This limitation prevents robots from learning complex, fine-grained manipulations and from effectively following natural human instructions, which are inherently continuous and often implicitly refer to specific ranges of object properties.

Specifically, the authors identify three core abilities an intelligent manipulator agent should possess, which directly motivate ARNOLD's design:
1. **Comprehension and Grounding of Human Instructions to Precise World States:** Robots need the ability to parse natural language commands and map them to quantifiable, precise states of objects in the physical world. This goes beyond simple object recognition to understanding specific attributes like height, angle, or volume percentage.
2. **Acquisition of Policies for Accurate Actions and Plans Towards Precisely Defined Goal States:** Once an instruction is understood, the robot must learn robust policies to execute the necessary sequence of continuous actions (e.g., movements, rotations, grasping forces) to achieve the exact target state. This requires fine motor control and an understanding of object dynamics.
3. **Feasibility of Transferring Such Abilities to the Real World (Sim2Real Transfer):** Learned policies must generalize beyond the training environment to real-world scenarios. This necessitates a simulation environment that is as realistic as possible in terms of visual appearance, physics, and object behavior, minimizing the "reality gap" that often hinders effective deployment.

To address these motivations, ARNOLD was developed to tackle the following critical challenges:
- **Discrete vs. Continuous States:** Moving beyond discrete object states to enable learning with continuous goals, which is crucial for real-world complexity.
- **Lack of Precise Language Grounding:** Ensuring that language instructions are explicitly grounded to precise continuous states, rather than just symbolic actions or coarse properties.
- **Unrealistic Environments:** Providing a physically accurate and visually photo-realistic simulation environment that closely mimics real-world conditions, including clutter and diverse textures, to promote robust policy learning and better Sim2Real transfer.
- **Generalization:** Systematically evaluating the generalization capabilities of agents to novel goal states, novel objects, and novel scenes, thereby identifying the weaknesses of current models and guiding future research.

By addressing these challenges, ARNOLD aims to foster the development of more capable and generalizable robot learning algorithms that can effectively operate in human environments and respond to natural language instructions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ARNOLD benchmark is meticulously constructed to facilitate language-grounded task learning with continuous states in realistic 3D scenes. Its methodology encompasses a robust simulation environment, carefully designed tasks, a comprehensive data collection process, and a rigorous evaluation framework.

**Simulation Environment:**
ARNOLD is built upon **NVIDIA Isaac Sim**, a powerful robotic simulation application that offers both photo-realistic rendering (powered by GPU-enabled ray tracing) and physically accurate simulations (based on **PhysX 5.0**). This choice prioritizes realism over mere speed, which is crucial for Sim2Real transfer. The physical simulation incorporates detailed parameters for rigid-body objects (weight, friction) and fluids (cohesion, surface tension, viscosity), with fluids simulated using GPU-accelerated Position-Based Dynamics (PBD).

The environment features **40 distinct objects and 20 diverse scenes**, curated from datasets like 3D-FRONT and SAPIEN. These assets are professionally designed with high-quality 3D models and realistic textures. To ensure stable physics-based grasping, object meshes underwent convex decomposition to create precise collision proxies.

The robot used is a **7-DoF Franka Emika Panda manipulator with a parallel gripper**. Agents have direct control over the robot's seven joints and gripper, with end-effector actions (translation and quaternion for rotation) being converted to joint space using Isaac Sim's built-in motion planner. The robot's base remains fixed, focusing on manipulation rather than navigation.

For visual input, ARNOLD provides observations from **five RGB-D cameras** (front, left, robot base, and two wrist cameras), each delivering 128x128 resolution data. A notable feature is the **stochastic rendering** due to ray tracing, which introduces subtle non-determinism, making the environment more realistic and challenging. Auxiliary observations like camera parameters, robot base pose, and part-level semantic masks are also available.

**Task Design:**
ARNOLD includes **eight language-conditioned tasks** designed to require continuous state understanding and manipulation. These tasks span a variety of skills:
- **PICKUPOBJECT** and **REORIENTOBJECT**: Basic object manipulation, requiring grounding of heights and angles.
- **OPENDRAWER**, **CLOSEDRAWER**, **OPENCABINET**, **CLOSECABINET**: Manipulation of articulated objects, requiring precise control over joint distances (prismatic) or angles (revolute).
- **POURWATER** and **TRANSFERWATER**: Fluid manipulation, where goals are specified as percentages of water volume.
Each task defines continuous goal states with specific success ranges, requiring the agent to maintain the desired object state for 2 seconds.

**Data Collection:**
The benchmark provides **10,000 valid expert demonstrations**, each containing 4-6 keyframes. These demonstrations are generated using a **template-based motion planner** for each task, which partitions tasks into sub-stages and uses an RMPflow controller to plan motions towards keypoints. This approach balances efficiency with realism.
To enhance diversity and quality, the data is augmented with approximately **2,000 human annotations** of task configurations (e.g., object positions, robot-object relative positions), collected via Xbox controller teleoperation. These annotations inform the motion planner's keypoint generation.
**Language instructions** are generated using a template-based engine, allowing for flexible lexicalization of placeholders (e.g., "pull the top drawer 50% open" or "pull the top drawer half open"). Critically, the instructions do not specify the initial state, forcing the agent to perceive the current state from observations.

**Benchmark and Evaluation Framework:**
ARNOLD provides systematic data splits for robust evaluation:
- **Normal Split**: Standard Train/Val/Test sets with seen objects, scenes, and goal states.
- **Generalization Splits**:
 - **Novel Object**: Evaluates transfer to unseen objects with seen scenes and states.
 - **Novel Scene**: Evaluates transfer to unseen scenes with seen objects and states.
 - **Novel State**: Evaluates transfer to unseen goal states with seen objects and scenes.
 - **Any State**: Goals uniformly sampled from a continuous range, evaluating interpolation within the continuous spectrum.
The primary metric is **Success Rate**, defined by maintaining the object state within a tolerance threshold of the goal for 2 seconds. TRANSFERWATER includes an additional constraint on spillage. Evaluation proceeds through a two-phase procedure: grasping the object, then manipulating it to the goal state.

**Models and Experimental Setup:**
The authors evaluate two state-of-the-art language-conditioned manipulation models: **6D-CLIPort** and **PerAct**.
- **6D-CLIPort** uses an RGB-D top-down view, predicting end-effector poses. It processes multimodal input via Transporter-ResNet and CLIP encoders.
- **PerAct** takes multi-view RGB-D images to construct a 3D voxelized grid, incorporates proprioception, and uses a Perceiver-IO architecture to output Q-functions for action translation and categorical distributions for rotation.
Variants of PerAct are also tested: **PerAct without language (w/o L)**, **PerAct with additional state supervision (PerAct†)**, and **PerAct trained in a multi-task setting (PerAct MT)**. Visual representations for these models are generated from the 5 camera views: 6D-CLIPort uses a 224x224 top-down image, while PerAct uses a 120³ voxel grid. Models are trained on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU, with checkpoints selected based on validation set performance. To isolate manipulation performance from grasping failures, an "intermediate oracle" evaluation is also conducted where the first-phase (grasping) ground truth is provided.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on ARNOLD reveal significant challenges for state-of-the-art language-conditioned manipulation models, particularly concerning generalization and fine-grained continuous control.

**Performance Across Models:**
- **6D-CLIPort** demonstrated poor performance, failing on most tasks. The authors attribute this to information loss when compressing complex 3D scenes into a single 2D image and the difficulty in regressing precise target height values for action translation.
- **PerAct**, leveraging 3D voxelized representations, significantly outperformed 6D-CLIPort across all tasks and splits, underscoring the importance of rich 3D context for complex manipulation.
- **Multi-task training (PerAct MT)**, surprisingly, showed performance drops compared to single-task PerAct. This suggests that despite a larger and more diverse dataset, current multi-task learning approaches struggle to efficiently leverage data for tasks requiring both precise grounding and complex manipulation in a challenging environment like ARNOLD.

**Performance Across Tasks:**
- The most challenging tasks identified were **REORIENTOBJECT** and the **{OPEN,CLOSE}CABINET** tasks. REORIENTOBJECT is difficult due to visual ambiguities in bottle orientation (e.g., 45° vs. 135° can look similar but require different actions). Cabinet manipulation is tough due to the requirement for accurate prediction of interaction positions and rotations, coupled with precise continuous motion control for articulated joints.
- **TRANSFERWATER** was harder than POURWATER, primarily because transferring water necessitates precise positional alignment between two cups to avoid spillage, an additional layer of complexity.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
- All models exhibited **significant performance drops when tested on generalization splits**, especially the **Novel State split**. This is a crucial finding, indicating that current models struggle to generalize the grounding of *seen* continuous goal states to *unseen* ones. This highlights a fundamental weakness in current approaches' ability to learn a robust continuous state representation.
- Performance drops on **Novel Object** and **Novel Scene** splits varied by task. Tasks where objects occupy substantial space, like opening/closing drawers, were more affected by unseen objects, suggesting that object geometry and interaction points are critical.
- The **Any State split**, where goal states are uniformly sampled across a continuous range, showed performance superior to Novel State but inferior to Novel Object/Scene. This implies that interpolation within the learned continuous range is somewhat achievable, but systematic generalization across the entire continuous spectrum, particularly extrapolation to out-of-range states, remains a significant challenge.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Influence of Language:** Comparing PerAct with **PerAct (w/o L)** clearly demonstrated the critical role of language instructions. Removing language led to a substantial performance degradation, except for REORIENTOBJECT where visual ambiguity might bottleneck performance regardless of language. This confirms language's importance in grounding visually identifiable concepts like translation distance.
- **Importance of State Modeling:** **PerAct†**, which included explicit state prediction supervision, showed only marginal improvements on the main test set and limited benefits on generalization splits. This suggests that a simple end-to-end state supervision is insufficient for robust continuous state modeling in ARNOLD. However, when provided with **intermediate oracle** (ground truth for grasping phase), PerAct† showed larger gains, indicating that explicit state modeling *does* help goal state understanding once initial manipulation challenges are bypassed.
- **Choice of Language Encoder:** An ablation study comparing CLIP (default) with **T5-base** as the language encoder for PerAct revealed that T5-base generally outperformed CLIP on the OPENDRAWER task across splits. This intriguing finding suggests that more detailed, general-purpose language representations from models like T5 might be more effective for fine-grained language grounding compared to the global representations learned by CLIP.

**Sim2Real Transfer:**
- Preliminary Sim2Real experiments with a Franka robot and RealSense D435 camera showed that models trained in ARNOLD exhibit **reasonable predictions** for picking objects and manipulating drawers in the real world.
- However, **actual robot manipulation still struggled** significantly. This is primarily attributed to the persistent Sim2Real gap, involving factors like high friction in real-world drawers and sensory noise from imperfect depth cameras, which can lead to prediction errors and inexecutable actions. The authors note that ARNOLD's flexible design allows for further refinement of assets to potentially reduce this gap.

In summary, the key findings from ARNOLD's experiments are that current state-of-the-art models face substantial hurdles in mastering complex, continuous manipulation tasks. Their generalization capabilities, especially concerning novel goal states and across the continuous spectrum, are notably weak. These results strongly advocate for the urgent development of new algorithms that incorporate more fine-grained perceptual representations, advanced continuous object state modeling, and improved language-action alignment for robust embodied AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ARNOLD benchmark represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robot learning, with profound implications for future research and development. Its significance stems from its direct confrontation of several critical limitations in existing benchmarks, pushing the boundaries of what is expected from intelligent robotic agents.

**Key Contributions:**
1. **Realistic 3D Interactive Environment:** ARNOLD provides a high-fidelity simulation environment featuring diverse scenes, varied objects, and, crucially, continuous object states. Leveraging NVIDIA Isaac Sim with GPU-enabled ray tracing and PhysX 5.0, it offers photo-realistic rendering and physically accurate simulations (including rigid body dynamics and particle-level fluid simulation). This level of realism is vital for training policies that can effectively transfer from simulation to the real world.
2. **Systematic Language-Grounded Benchmark:** It introduces eight challenging robot manipulation tasks that require understanding and achieving continuous goal states based on natural language instructions. The benchmark is designed with a systematic evaluation framework, including specific data splits for assessing generalization to novel scenes, novel objects, and, most notably, novel goal states (both discrete and across a continuous spectrum). This structured approach allows researchers to pinpoint specific weaknesses in current models.
3. **Extensive Experimental Analysis:** The paper provides a thorough evaluation of state-of-the-art language-conditioned manipulation models (6D-CLIPort and PerAct) within the ARNOLD environment. The detailed results and ablation studies reveal significant limitations in current models' ability to handle complex manipulations, generalize to unseen scenarios, and effectively model continuous object states or leverage language for precise control.

**Potential Impact:**
ARNOLD's comprehensive design and challenging nature are poised to stimulate several crucial research directions:
- **Driving Advancements in Continuous State Understanding:** The benchmark's emphasis on continuous goal states directly motivates the development of new perception and representation learning techniques that can accurately infer, represent, and predict fine-grained object states beyond binary classifications.
- **Enhancing Language-Action Grounding:** By requiring agents to ground natural language instructions to precise, continuous physical properties, ARNOLD will push research into more nuanced language understanding models that can translate abstract human commands into specific robotic actions. The findings on the T5 language encoder hint at the potential for richer language models to improve grounding.
- **Fostering Generalizable Robot Skills:** The systematic generalization splits highlight the current models' deficiencies in adapting to novel environments, objects, and goal states. This will accelerate research into learning generalizable policies that are robust to variations in the physical world, crucial for practical robot deployment. The "Any State" split, in particular, opens up research into interpolation and extrapolation capabilities within continuous state spaces.
- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** While the initial Sim2Real experiments show challenges, ARNOLD's high fidelity and flexible design provide a strong foundation for future work on reducing this gap. Improvements in assets, physics modeling, and domain adaptation techniques can be rigorously tested within this framework, leading to more deployable robotic solutions.
- **Encouraging Multi-Modal and Multi-Task Learning:** The observation that multi-task learning currently underperforms single-task learning in this complex environment presents a critical challenge for the community, urging the development of more effective multi-task learning architectures that can leverage diverse data efficiently without performance degradation.
- **Facilitating Research in General Purpose AI:** In line with BIGAI's broader mission, ARNOLD contributes to the foundational research for general AI by integrating perception, language, and precise physical interaction in a complex environment. It sets a higher bar for what "intelligent" means in the context of embodied agents.

**Limitations and Future Work (as acknowledged by the authors):**
The authors openly discuss several limitations, which also serve as clear directions for future research:
1. **Remaining Sim2Real Gap:** Despite significant efforts towards realism, a gap still exists. Future work will involve adding even more realistic variations of scenes and objects to the asset library.
2. **Focus on Short-Horizon Control:** Current tasks primarily focus on fine-grained, short-horizon control. Future efforts will extend to long-horizon tasks requiring higher-level planning and reasoning.
3. **Template-Based Language:** The current template-based language generation, while diverse, is restrictive. Future work aims to incorporate richer language instructions, potentially by leveraging large language models (LLMs) to generate more natural and varied commands.
4. **Learning from Scarce Data:** The need for better methods to model continuous object states and learn generalizable policies from potentially scarce expert data is highlighted.
5. **Scaling Demonstrations:** Scaling up the number and diversity of expert demonstrations is seen as crucial for inducing stronger capabilities in robot learning models.

In conclusion, ARNOLD serves as a demanding yet essential benchmark that confronts the limitations of existing robot learning paradigms. By emphasizing continuous states, precise language grounding, and realistic simulations, it provides a crucial platform to accelerate the development of more capable, generalizable, and truly intelligent embodied AI systems that can effectively interact with and understand the nuances of the real world.
