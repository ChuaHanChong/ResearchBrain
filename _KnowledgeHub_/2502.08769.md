# Cluster and Predict Latent Patches for Improved Masked Image Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> A self-supervised learning framework, CAPI, improves Masked Image Modeling (MIM) by employing a novel clustering-based loss and a cross-attention predictor. It establishes new state-of-the-art for pure-MIM approaches, achieving up to 82.9% ImageNet-1k top-1 accuracy and demonstrating strong out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- Masked Image Modeling (MIM) approaches historically produce suboptimal frozen representations compared to other self-supervised learning methods.
- Online latent MIM models often exhibit training instability and high sensitivity to hyperparameters, frequently necessitating additional stabilizing objectives.
- A lack of systematic understanding of optimal design choices within the MIM paradigm hinders its full capabilities.

## Method

- A ==teacher-student framework== called **CAPI**, utilizing an ==Exponential Moving Average (EMA)== teacher for target generation and a masked online student for prediction.
- An ==online clustering== mechanism applied to teacher patch representations generates soft pseudo-category assignments, rebalanced by a modified ==Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm== to prevent positional collapse.
- A ==split cross-attention predictor== efficiently forecasts these clustering assignments for masked patches, querying the student encoder's visible patch features.

## Results

- CAPI outperforms prior reconstruction-based SSL methods on ImageNet-1k classification (**82.9%** top-1 accuracy for ViT-L/14), out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., **>19 point** gain on ObjectNet), and dense prediction.
- The framework narrows the performance gap with DINOv2, even surpassing it on some benchmarks, such as achieving **35.2% mIoU** on ADE20K k-NN segmentation, **1.2 points** higher than DINOv2+reg.
- Training demonstrates high stability, and the method shows promising scaling properties with increased model size, training length, and dataset scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A carefully designed pure-Masked Image Modeling (MIM) framework can achieve state-of-the-art performance among MIM methods without relying on auxiliary stabilizing losses.
> - Online clustering with spatial Sinkhorn-Knopp rebalancing effectively prevents representation collapse and yields semantically rich targets for MIM.
> - Split cross-attention predictors offer an efficient and effective architectural choice for processing masked tokens and improving overall MIM performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of **self-supervised learning (SSL)** for visual representation learning, particularly concerning the development of **foundation models** in computer vision. The authors draw a parallel between the success of large self-supervised models like BERT and GPT in natural language processing (NLP) and the current efforts to achieve similar breakthroughs in vision. SSL is critical for domains with limited annotations (e.g., medical, satellite imagery) and for enabling generalist models that perform well across various downstream tasks.

Within SSL, the paper specifically addresses **Masked Image Modeling (MIM)**. MIM aims to learn representations by predicting missing parts of an image given its context, conceptually similar to masked language modeling. The broader landscape of MIM can be categorized into:

- **Pixel-level reconstruction (e.g., MAE):** These methods predict raw pixel values of masked patches. While effective for fine-tuning, they often yield suboptimal frozen representations, possibly due to the low-level nature of the target, which struggles to capture high-level semantic uncertainty.
- **Latent reconstruction with frozen encoders (e.g., BeiT):** These approaches reconstruct targets in a pretrained encoder's latent space, but they require an existing, pre-trained encoder, limiting their ability to learn representations from scratch.
- **Online latent reconstruction (e.g., iBOT, Data2Vec, I-JEPA, DINOv2):** This is the most promising direction, where the learning target is derived from the online model's own latent representations or an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of it. This allows for bootstrapping an informative latent space without relying on external pretrained models. However, these methods historically suffer from instability, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and often necessitate additional stabilizing objectives, such as contrastive learning or the DINO loss, to achieve competitive performance (e.g., DINOv2 combines MIM with a DINO objective).

**CAPI directly intervenes in this third category.** The authors observe that despite the promise, existing online latent MIM approaches struggle to achieve state-of-the-art performance purely through MIM. Their work is a systematic attempt to isolate and improve the core MIM objective, demonstrating that with proper design choices, a *pure-MIM framework* can be stable and competitive. This directly challenges the prevailing notion that auxiliary losses are essential for high-performing online latent MIM models. By approaching the performance of DINOv2 (which combines MIM with a DINO loss), CAPI pushes the boundaries of what MIM alone can achieve and provides crucial insights into its intrinsic capabilities. It also re-integrates explicit clustering ideas (seen in earlier SSL works like DeepCluster, SwAV, DINO) into the MIM paradigm to enhance stability and representation quality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to introduce **CAPI (Cluster and Predict Latent Patches for Improved Masked Image Modeling)**, a novel self-supervised learning framework that specifically aims to enhance the performance and stability of pure-MIM models.

The primary motivations driving this work are:

- **Bridging the Performance Gap:** Despite the conceptual appeal of MIM, existing MIM models consistently lag behind state-of-the-art self-supervised learning methods (like DINOv2) in terms of representation quality, especially for frozen features. The authors are motivated to narrow or even close this performance gap using a purely MIM-based approach.
- **Addressing Instability and Hyperparameter Sensitivity in Online Latent MIM:** The most promising MIM variants, which use online or EMA latent representations as targets (e.g., iBOT, I-JEPA), are known for their training instability and high sensitivity to hyperparameters. This often forces researchers to combine MIM with other stabilizing objectives (like DINO or contrastive learning), preventing a clear understanding of MIM's standalone potential. A key motivation is to make the online latent MIM objective stable and robust on its own.
- **Systematic Analysis of MIM Design Choices:** The paper is motivated by a desire to systematically investigate the fundamental design choices within the masked image modeling principle. This includes a deep dive into:
 - The nature of the **target representation** to be predicted.
 - The formulation of the **loss function** used for prediction.
 - The **architecture** employed for making predictions.
 By scrutinizing these components, the authors aim to uncover optimal configurations for MIM.
- **Learning Informative Latent Spaces from Scratch:** The goal is to develop a method that can bootstrap a highly informative and semantically rich latent space from unlabeled data, without relying on external pretrained encoders or complex multi-objective optimization strategies.
- **Advancing Foundation Models:** Ultimately, the work contributes to the broader goal of developing more capable and robust visual foundation models that can generalize effectively across diverse tasks and domains, particularly those with scarce annotations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CAPI operates within a **teacher-student framework** designed for **online latent masked image modeling**. The core innovation lies in its novel clustering-based loss formulation and efficient predictor architecture.

**High-Level Overview of Training:**
The method involves a pair of Vision Transformers (ViTs): an "online" (student) model and a "target" (teacher) encoder.
1. **Teacher Processing:** The teacher, an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the online encoder, receives the *full* input image. It processes the image to produce dense patch representations.
2. **Target Generation:** These teacher patch representations are then subjected to an **online clustering** process to generate soft assignments to "pseudo-categories." These assignments serve as the learning targets.
3. **Student Processing:** The student encoder receives a *partially masked* version of the input image (a patch embedding layer processes visible patches, and a high proportion, e.g., 65%, are dropped). It also incorporates learnable "register tokens" to improve feature map quality.
4. **Prediction:** A separate **cross-attention predictor** then takes a small number of special "[MSK]" tokens (corresponding to sampled masked patches) and, by cross-attending to the student encoder's output (visible patches + registers), predicts the clustering assignments of these missing patches.
5. **Loss Calculation:** A cross-entropy loss is computed between the student's predicted assignments for the masked patches and the teacher's clustering assignments for those same patches.

**Key Design Choices and Innovations:**

**a. Target Representation:**
CAPI focuses on the **EMA encoder's latent representations** as targets, following the successful strategy of iBOT, Data2Vec, and I-JEPA. This choice allows the model to learn a semantic latent space directly from the online model's evolving capabilities, avoiding reliance on fixed, potentially suboptimal, pixel-level targets or external pretrained encoders.

**b. Clustering-based Loss Formulation (Central Innovation):**
The paper critically analyzes prior online latent MIM approaches like iBOT, which project both teacher and student representations through an MLP head and use a cross-entropy loss. CAPI argues that in iBOT's MIM context, this interpretation of clustering is flawed due to a "distribution mismatch" (teacher uses regular patch tokens, student uses [MSK] tokens with an asymmetric architecture), leading to instability and reliance on additional DINO losses.

CAPI's solution is to **explicitly use an online clustering mechanism** on the EMA teacher's patch representations to generate targets, thus decoupling the teacher's target generation from the student's prediction head:
- **Online Clustering Process:**
 - Teacher's L2-normalized patch features are projected onto a set of `p` "centroids" (learned parameters `C`) to obtain assignment logits.
 - Soft assignments are derived using a softmax function with a temperature `τ`.
 - To prevent empty clusters and ensure a balanced distribution of tokens across clusters, the **Sinkhorn-Knopp (SK) algorithm** is applied to these logits, producing "rebalanced" assignments `a'`. This ensures a near-uniform distribution of tokens over clusters.
- **Positional Collapse Prevention:** A critical refinement to the SK algorithm is proposed: instead of applying SK globally, it is run **separately at each spatial position**. This modification explicitly forces the joint distribution of tokens over positions and clusters to be near-uniform, effectively **removing positional information from the targets**. This prevents "positional collapse," where the model learns to predict patch positions instead of semantic content.
- **Loss:** The student is trained to predict `a'` using a cross-entropy loss, comparing its predicted assignments for masked patches with the corresponding `a'` from the teacher.
- **Optimization:** The clustering centroids `C` are learned simultaneously with the main model parameters using a separate AdamW optimizer, ensuring no interference.

**c. Predictor Architecture:**
CAPI adopts a **split cross-attention predictor architecture**, drawing inspiration from CrossMAE, for both efficiency and performance:
- **Fused Architecture (e.g., BeiT, iBOT):** A single transformer processes both visible and mask tokens. Shown to be difficult to train and less effective.
- **Split Self-Attention (e.g., MAE, I-JEPA):** An encoder processes only visible tokens, and a separate predictor processes *both* visible and mask tokens. More efficient than fused.
- **CAPI's Split Cross-Attention:** The encoder processes only visible tokens (plus registers). The predictor, a shallower transformer (e.g., 12 blocks for a 24-block encoder), processes *only* the masked tokens, using **cross-attention** layers to access the context provided by the encoder's output.
 - **Advantages:** This design is highly efficient, as it processes a reduced set of tokens in the predictor. It also ensures that predictions for different masked tokens are independent, simplifying the prediction process and avoiding complex multi-forward passes.

**Additional Implementation Details:**
- **Backbone:** Vision Transformer (ViT-L/14) with ~300M parameters.
- **Masking Strategy:** Inverse block masking with a random circular shift ("+roll") at 65% ratio.
- **Positional Encoding:** RoPE (Rotary Positional Embeddings) for better transferability.
- **Registers:** Learnable tokens added to the encoder input to improve feature map quality and stabilize training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CAPI demonstrates remarkable improvements in masked image modeling, establishing new state-of-the-art for pure-MIM approaches and significantly closing the performance gap with general SSL methods like DINOv2.

**Key Findings:**

- **Superior Performance over Previous MIM Methods:**
 - **Image Classification:** CAPI substantially outperforms all previous reconstruction-based SSL models (e.g., MAE, Data2Vec 2.0, I-JEPA, AIM) on ImageNet-1k, iNaturalist 2021, Places205, and SUN397 classification using attentive probes. For instance, CAPI ViT-L/14 trained on ImageNet-1k achieves 82.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k, significantly higher than MAE's 79.4% or I-JEPA's 79.5%.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Robustness:** CAPI shows exceptional OOD generalization, outperforming all baselines by more than 19 points on ObjectNet. It also performs strongly on ImageNet-A.
 - **Dense Prediction (Segmentation):** CAPI trained on ImageNet-1k outperforms all reconstruction-based baselines by a wide margin on ADE20K, Pascal VOC 2012, and Cityscapes using k-NN and linear probes.
- **Approaching and Sometimes Exceeding DINOv2:** CAPI successfully narrows the performance gap with DINOv2 (a leading method that combines MIM with a DINO objective). When trained on larger datasets (e.g., LVD-142M), the gap on ImageNet reduces significantly. Notably, CAPI trained on Places205 achieves a 35.2% mIoU on ADE20K k-NN segmentation, surpassing DINOv2+reg by 1.2 points, marking the first time DINOv2+reg is bested on this specific benchmark with frozen features.
- **Stability and Robustness:** The proposed clustering-based loss with the modified Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm proves to be highly stable during training, effectively preventing representation collapse (particularly positional collapse) without needing additional global objectives. The loss curve trends smoothly downwards without instability.
- **Efficiency of Cross-Attention Predictor:** The split cross-attention predictor architecture contributes to improved efficiency, allowing for faster training (up to 18% speedup compared to split self-attention) and flexible sampling of masked tokens.
- **Promising Scaling Properties:** CAPI demonstrates consistent performance improvements with increasing model size (ViT-S to ViT-L), training length, and dataset size (ImageNet-1k to LVD-142M), suggesting strong potential for future scaling.
- **Qualitative Feature Quality:** Visualizations show that CAPI produces highly discriminative and smooth dense feature maps, exhibiting clearer object boundaries and less noise compared to DINOv2+reg, and better semantic focus than MAE features.
- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - The **cross-attention predictor** is superior in performance and speed.
 - **Inverse block masking with random circular shift (+roll)** is the most effective masking strategy.
 - The **clustering-based loss with a linear head** is crucial for stable training and strong representations; direct Huber loss or iBOT's MLP head fail in this pure-MIM setup.
 - **Registers** significantly boost performance (e.g., +3.2 ADE, +2.1 IN1k with 16 registers).
 - The **modified Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm** (spatial normalization) is essential to prevent positional collapse and yields a small performance gain.
 - Performance generally improves with an **increased number of prototypes** in the clustering.
 - Shallower predictors (e.g., 5 blocks instead of 12) can offer slightly better performance but might be less stable for longer schedules.
- **Global Representations:** An interesting finding is that pooling representations from the predictor (after cross-attention) provides better global representations than simple average pooling of encoder features, although a learned attentive probe remains superior for ImageNet classification.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CAPI framework represents a significant advancement in the field of self-supervised visual representation learning, particularly for Masked Image Modeling. Its potential impacts are multi-faceted:

- **Redefining the Power of Pure MIM:** CAPI decisively demonstrates that a purely masked image modeling objective, when carefully designed and implemented, can achieve state-of-the-art performance among MIM methods and even compete with, or occasionally surpass, complex multi-objective SSL approaches like DINOv2. This fundamentally changes the perception of MIM's inherent capabilities, suggesting that the "lag" was not intrinsic to MIM but rather to its prior formulations.
- **Simplification of SSL Training:** By achieving high performance without requiring auxiliary stabilizing objectives (like DINO or contrastive losses), CAPI offers a simpler, more robust, and potentially easier-to-tune recipe for self-supervised pretraining. This could accelerate research and development of foundation models, making high-quality SSL more accessible.
- **Advancing Visual Foundation Models:** The improved representation quality, particularly in out-of-distribution generalization and dense prediction tasks, directly contributes to building more versatile and robust visual foundation models. Such models are invaluable for downstream tasks in various fields, especially those with limited labeled data, such as medical imaging, remote sensing, and robotics.
- **Deeper Understanding of MIM:** The systematic ablation studies and the detailed analysis of target representations, loss formulations, and predictor architectures provide critical insights into the underlying mechanisms that make MIM effective. The identification and effective mitigation of "positional collapse" through the modified Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm is a key technical contribution that benefits future MIM research.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** CAPI's promising scaling properties (with model size, training length, and dataset size) open up new avenues for exploration. It encourages further research into how MIM can be scaled even further, which datasets are optimal for training, and whether pure reconstruction-based approaches can entirely match or exceed all facets of contrastive learning methods.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The authors' commitment to releasing all code and models is highly significant, fostering reproducibility, accelerating further research, and allowing the broader community to leverage and build upon CAPI's advancements.

In conclusion, CAPI is a landmark paper that not only pushes the state-of-the-art in masked image modeling but also provides a deeper understanding of its core principles, paving the way for simpler, more stable, and more powerful self-supervised visual foundation models.
