# VAG: Dual-Stream Video-Action Generation for Embodied Data Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-stream generative framework, VAG, is introduced for joint video and action sequence generation, leveraging a unified flow-matching approach for strong cross-modal alignment. This method synthesizes high-quality embodied data, demonstrably improving downstream robot policy generalization and enabling the system to function directly as a World-Action policy.

## Problem

- The high cost and labor intensity of collecting large-scale human teleoperation data for training robot foundation models.
- Existing generative video models produce visually rich scenarios but lack corresponding executable action trajectories needed for policy learning.
- Current World-Action models exhibit insufficient alignment between generated videos and actions, and two-stage approaches that first generate video then infer actions suffer inefficiencies and error accumulation over longer horizons.

## Method

- A unified, dual-stream generative framework (**VAG**) employing a ==flow-matching==-based formulation for joint video and action sequence generation.
- The framework synchronously denoises both video and action signals, conditioned on an initial image and a textual instruction, ensuring temporal coherence.
- An adaptive 3D pooling module efficiently transfers compact global video context from the video stream to the action branch, promoting rigorous cross-modal alignment without learning additional parameters.

## Results

- Achieved competitive or superior video generation performance (e.g., FVD 965, FID 130) on the AgiBot dataset, outperforming baselines like SVD and Cosmos-Predict2.
- Improved action prediction accuracy, with an Euclidean Distance of 0.81 and a **45%** success rate on the AgiBot dataset, surpassing two-stage baselines (e.g., VAG-Video + AnyPos at **29%** SR).
- Pretraining VLA models with VAG-generated synthetic data increased real-world success rates by **20%** (**35%**→**55%**), improved OOD generalization, and produced executable trajectories for bimanual manipulation on an Agilex Cobot Magic robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synchronous, parallel denoising of both video and action sequences within a unified flow-matching framework is effective in maintaining strong cross-modal consistency.
> - Adaptive 3D pooling provides a compact and efficient method to transfer global visual context from the video stream to guide action generation, avoiding error accumulation inherent in two-stage pipelines.
> - Pretraining vision-language-action (VLA) models with VAG-synthesized data significantly enhances their generalization capabilities and robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios in real-world settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is situated within the field of robot learning, specifically focusing on the development of robot foundation models and embodied artificial intelligence. These models, often trained on extensive human teleoperation data, have enabled robots to execute increasingly intricate real-world tasks. However, a primary challenge in scaling these systems is the substantial cost and labor associated with collecting task-specific demonstration data.

The broader landscape includes several established approaches:
- **Video and Image Synthesis:** Recent advancements in generative models, particularly for video and image synthesis, offer methods to create diverse embodied scenarios.
- **World Models (WMs):** These models excel at generating realistic and controllable videos, providing rich visual information. However, they typically do not produce paired action trajectories, which limits their direct applicability for policy learning in robotics (as illustrated in Figure 2b).
- **World-Action (WA) Models:** These models attempt to bridge the gap by predicting actions alongside visual outputs. Their primary focus has often been on enhancing action prediction through predictive visual information, sometimes overlooking a rigorous alignment between the generated video and the corresponding actions, which is necessary for policy-training-oriented data synthesis (as shown in Figure 2c).
- **Two-stage Pipelines:** Some methods adopt a two-stage approach, first generating a video and then inferring actions from the synthesized video using techniques like Inverse Dynamics Models (IDM) or vision transformers. While capable of producing longer video-action pairs, these pipelines introduce inefficiencies, can degrade cross-modal consistency, and are susceptible to cumulative errors (as depicted in Figure 2d).

VAG positions itself by addressing these limitations. It aims to provide a unified, dual-stream framework that simultaneously generates both video and action with strong cross-modal alignment, thereby improving efficiency and reducing error accumulation compared to two-stage systems. The work is motivated by the need for scalable and high-quality synthetic data to train robust robot policies, thereby reducing reliance on costly human teleoperation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the inherent challenges in scaling robot foundation models. These models demonstrate capabilities in executing complex tasks, yet their reliance on large-scale human teleoperation data poses a significant bottleneck. Manually collecting task-specific demonstrations is an expensive and labor-intensive process, hindering the deployment of these systems across a wider range of tasks and environments.

To overcome this, synthetic data, particularly generated videos, is recognized as a promising avenue. However, existing generative approaches exhibit limitations:
- **World Models (WMs)** can generate visually rich embodied scenario videos but lack corresponding action trajectories. This absence prevents their direct use for training robot policies, as policy learning fundamentally requires paired visual observations and actions.
- **World-Action (WA) models** partially address the action generation gap by predicting actions alongside visual outputs. Nevertheless, these models often prioritize improving action prediction via visual cues and tend to exhibit a lack of strong video-action alignment. This misalignment reduces the utility of the generated data for robust policy learning.
- **Two-stage pipelines**, which first generate a video and then infer actions from it, introduce inefficiency and can lead to the accumulation of errors. The sequential nature of these processes can result in degraded consistency between the visual and motor signals over longer horizons.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of the VAG project are:
1. **Develop a unified, dual-stream generative framework:** The primary objective is to create a single model capable of synchronously generating both video sequences and corresponding action trajectories. This framework is designed to operate under both visual (initial frame) and language (textual instruction) conditioning.
2. **Enhance cross-modal consistency and alignment:** A core goal is to ensure that the generated video and action data are rigorously aligned at every timestep. This is achieved by synchronizing the denoising processes in both the video and action branches and incorporating a mechanism to transfer compact global video context to the action branch, thereby avoiding cumulative errors inherent in two-stage pipelines.
3. **Produce executable trajectories:** The model aims to generate action trajectories that are not only numerically accurate but also functionally correct, allowing for successful real-world robot replay.
4. **Facilitate embodied data synthesis for policy training:** A significant objective is for VAG to serve as an effective data source for pretraining robot policies. By providing high-quality synthetic data, the goal is to improve the generalization capabilities of downstream policies, thereby mitigating the need for extensive real-world data collection.
5. **Function as a World-Action policy:** Ultimately, the system is designed with the potential to directly function as a World-Action policy, capable of controlling a robot by generating executable actions in response to visual observations and instructions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VAG is a dual-stream generative framework designed for joint video-action generation, leveraging a unified flow-matching-based formulation. Its core methodology involves synchronously denoising both video and action signals, conditioned on an initial image and a textual instruction.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Flow Matching

VAG builds upon the flow matching generative modeling technique. Flow matching is a velocity-based formulation that aims to learn a vector field representing the probability flow from a simple noise distribution to a complex data distribution. This approach is reported to offer a more direct training target, smoother optimization, and improved sample quality compared to alternative generative methods.
Given a data sample `x`, a noise vector `ϵ` sampled from a standard normal distribution, and a timestep `t` uniformly sampled from `` (drawn from a logit-normal distribution), the interpolated latent `x_t` is defined as `x_t = (1 - t)x + tϵ`. The corresponding ground-truth velocity `v_t` is `ϵ - x`. The denoising model `u(x_t, t, c; θ)` is trained to predict `v_t` by minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between its prediction and the ground truth, where `c` represents conditioning information such as text embeddings or reference images.

#### 3.2. Dual-Stream Video-Action Generation

VAG employs two parallel branches: one for video prediction and one for action prediction. Both branches utilize the flow-matching formulation and denoise synchronously, ensuring temporal coherence and semantic consistency between the generated video and action.

- **Video Prediction Branch:**
 This branch is adapted from the Cosmos-Predict2 video foundation model. It conditions on an initial image and a textual prompt to generate a future video sequence of `T` frames (e.g., 93 frames, corresponding to approximately 10 seconds at 10 Hz).
 1. A Gaussian noise vector `ϵ_v` is initialized and scaled by a weight `σ` to match the training noise distribution.
 2. This amplified noise is concatenated with the input image's latent representation, forming a prefix condition.
 3. This combined latent input is fed into a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for denoising.
 4. The textual instruction, encoded by a T5-XXL model, is integrated into the DiT via cross-attention layers to enhance text-context alignment. Classifier-free guidance is also applied.
 5. At each denoising step, the DiT predicts the clean latent `z_0` corresponding to the current noise intensity.
 6. After `N` denoising steps, the final clean video latent is obtained and subsequently decoded to produce the predicted video `V`.

- **Action Prediction Branch:**
 This branch operates in parallel and synchronously with the video branch, receiving contextual information from the video stream to guide action generation. It predicts an action sequence `A` for `T` frames.
 1. A Gaussian noise vector `ϵ_a` is initialized for the action sequence.
 2. **Adaptive 3D Pooling:** A key component for cross-modal conditioning is the adaptive 3D pooling module. It takes the clean latent `z_0` predicted by the video branch at each denoising step and compresses it into a compact global embedding. This is achieved by averaging the entire spatiotemporal features of each channel of `z_0`, mapping it to `R^(C' x 1 x 1 x 1)`, and then reshaping it into `R^(1 x C')`. This vector is then repeated across channels to create an embedding `e` (`R^(1 x C'')`), which serves as a global condition for action generation. This non-learnable pooling mechanism is designed for efficiency and to preserve global visual information, thereby promoting rigorous alignment between the generated video and action.
 3. A modified 1D U-Net, adapted from Diffusion Policy, functions as the denoiser for the action branch.
 4. The global video embedding `e` and the encoded timestep `t` are fed into the U-Net for denoising.
 5. Similar to the video branch, after `N` denoising steps synchronized with the video branch, the U-Net generates the final action sequence `A`.

#### 3.3. Training Dual-Stream Video-Action Model

The training of VAG involves embodied video-action pairs coupled with textual instructions.
1. **Data Preparation:** For each ground-truth video, a textual instruction is extracted using Qwen2.5-VL and encoded with T5-XXL to obtain the conditioning embedding.
2. **Initialization:** VAG is initialized with the pretrained weights of Cosmos-Predict2, leveraging its visual priors.
3. **Video Branch Training:**
 - Raw video `V` is compressed into a latent representation `z` using a VAE tokenizer, reducing computational costs while retaining spatiotemporal structure.
 - `z` is then noise-perturbed to `z'`.
 - The DiT is trained to denoise `z'` back to `z` by minimizing an MSE loss `L(θ_1)`. The training is conditioned on the first frame of `V` and the textual instruction.
4. **Action Branch Training:**
 - The predicted clean video latent `ϕ_1(D(z'; θ_1))` from the video branch (with gradients detached to prevent backpropagation into the video branch) is used as the conditioning signal.
 - Adaptive 3D pooling is applied to this detached latent to derive the global video embedding.
 - The ground-truth action sequence `A` is perturbed with noise `A'` of the same intensity as in the video branch.
 - A 1D U-Net is trained to denoise `A'` back to `A` by minimizing an MSE loss `L(θ_2)`, conditioned on the global video embedding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VAG was conducted across simulated and real-world environments, assessing video generation quality, action prediction accuracy, and its utility in improving downstream robot policy generalization.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Implementation:** VAG was post-trained on Cosmos-Predict2 (2B-Video2World) to generate 480P videos at 10 Hz. The model predicts 93 video-action frames (approximately 10 seconds). Training involved 40,000 iterations on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs with a batch size of 1 per GPU.
- **Datasets:**
 - **AgiBot:** A large-scale real-world dataset of 1 million trajectories across 217 tasks using a dual-arm humanoid robot. 1,794 video-action pairs for training, 200 for testing, with an action dimension of 16.
 - **LIBERO:** A simulated dataset featuring various goals, objects, and layouts. 400 video-action pairs for training, 50 for testing, with an action dimension of 7 (single-arm robot).
 - **Self-collected:** Data from an Agilex Cobot Magic dual-arm robot. 131 samples for VAG training, 20 for VLA training, with an action dimension of 14.
- **Baselines:** Video prediction was compared against SVD, Wan2.2, and Cosmos-Predict2 (CP2). Action prediction was evaluated against two-stage pipelines: VAG-Video + ResNet (IDM-like) and VAG-Video + AnyPos (vision transformer-based action regression). For downstream policy generalization, VAG-synthesized data was used to pretrain the π0.5 VLA model.

#### 4.2. Evaluation in Real-World Environment (AgiBot Dataset)

- **Video Generation:** VAG produced videos that visually aligned with the textual instructions (Figure 5), demonstrating understanding of prompts and coherent synthesis. Quantitatively (Table 1), VAG achieved competitive or superior performance across metrics such as FVD (965), FID (130), LPIPS (0.320), SSIM (0.512), and PSNR (15.1) compared to SVD, Wan2.2, and CP2.
- **Action Prediction:** Compared to two-stage baselines, VAG demonstrated improved action prediction (Table 2). It achieved a Euclidean Distance (ED) of 0.81 and a Success Rate (SR) of 45%, outperforming VAG-Video + ResNet (ED 1.54, SR 8%) and VAG-Video + AnyPos (ED 0.98, SR 29%). Qualitative plots (Figure 6) showed that predicted actions closely followed ground-truth trajectories across all 16 dimensions for a grasping task, capturing both general motion trends and fine-grained variations.

#### 4.3. Evaluation in Simulation Environment (LIBERO Dataset)

- **Video Generation:** VAG generated videos that were aligned with instructions, as shown in Figure 7.
- **Action Prediction:** VAG achieved lower Euclidean Distance (0.38) and higher Success Rate (79%) than the two-stage baselines on the LIBERO dataset (Table 2). Trajectory replay in simulation environments showed successful task completion that closely matched generated videos (Figure 7). Table 3 further supported this, with VAG achieving an average replay success rate of 62% across various LIBERO tasks (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), surpassing VAG-Video + ResNet (25%) and VAG-Video + AnyPos (54%). Qualitative visualizations (Figure 9) indicated close alignment between VAG's predicted trajectories and ground truth in both spatial and temporal aspects.

#### 4.4. Unlocking Generalization of VLA (Self-collected Dataset)

To assess the impact of VAG-synthesized data on policy generalization, a VLA model (π0.5) was trained on a small real dataset (Xb, 20 samples) and compared with a version pretrained on VAG-generated synthetic data (Xsyn) before fine-tuning on Xb (π0.5-w-VAG-pretrain).
- **Real-world Success Rate:** In 20 real-world pick-and-place trials, π0.5 achieved a 35% success rate, whereas π0.5-w-VAG-pretrain achieved a 55% success rate, representing a 20% absolute improvement (Figure 11).
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** π0.5 demonstrated failures when presented with unseen relocation or color changes of objects, while π0.5-w-VAG-pretrain exhibited improved generalization and more consistent success under these conditions (Figure 10). This indicates that VAG-generated data can effectively augment policy learning for better robustness.

#### 4.5. Serving as a World-Action Policy (Self-collected Dataset)

VAG's ability to jointly generate videos and actions indicates its potential to function as a direct World-Action policy. After training on the self-collected dataset, VAG was used to generate actions from an initial image and textual instruction, which were then replayed on an Agilex robot. Figure 12 provides examples for left-arm, right-arm, and bimanual manipulations. The generated videos and action trajectories demonstrated high consistency with the real-world robot's execution, and the tasks were successfully completed, illustrating VAG's practical applicability as a policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VAG represents an advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the generation of synthetic data for robot learning. The work addresses several critical limitations of existing approaches and offers implications for the scalability and robustness of robot policies.

The primary significance of VAG lies in its ability to **jointly and synchronously generate aligned video-action pairs** within a unified flow-matching-based dual-stream framework. This directly tackles the shortcomings of World Models (WMs), which generate videos without paired actions, and World-Action (WA) models, which often lack robust cross-modal alignment. Furthermore, it overcomes the inefficiencies and error accumulation issues inherent in two-stage pipelines that infer actions after video generation. By ensuring tight alignment between visual and motor signals, VAG produces data that is more directly suitable for training visuomotor policies.

The **provision of high-quality, executable synthetic data** offers a scalable solution to the persistent challenge of costly and labor-intensive human teleoperation data collection. This reduces the dependence on manually acquired demonstrations, which is a major bottleneck for the widespread deployment and generalization of robot foundation models. The ability to generate extended, coherent trajectories for approximately 10 seconds enhances the utility of this synthetic data for learning longer-horizon tasks.

A key impact demonstrated by the research is the **improvement in downstream policy generalization**. Experiments showed that pretraining VLA models with VAG-generated synthetic data significantly increased their success rates and enhanced their ability to generalize to out-of-distribution scenarios, such as changes in object position or color, compared to models trained solely on limited real-world data. This suggests that VAG can act as an effective data augmentation engine, fostering the development of more robust and adaptable robot behaviors.

Furthermore, VAG's capability to **function directly as a World-Action policy** represents a significant step towards more autonomous robotic systems. By generating both future visual states and corresponding actions from an initial observation and instruction, VAG demonstrates the potential for real-time robot control and planning, where the model itself can dictate executable trajectories in the physical world.

In summary, VAG contributes to the field by offering a method for creating a scalable source of high-quality, aligned embodied data, improving the training and generalization of robot policies, and demonstrating the potential for direct robot control. This work has implications for accelerating the development of more capable and general-purpose embodied agents by making data acquisition more efficient and effective.

The authors also noted limitations and future work, including:
- Currently, visual information guides action generation, but the action branch does not influence video generation. Future work plans to allow action guidance for video generation to further improve alignment.
- Exploring the replacement of the action U-Net with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for potentially improved model capacity.
- Scaling up training data and conducting experiments across a broader spectrum of tasks to further validate and extend VAG's capabilities.
