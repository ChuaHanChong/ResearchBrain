# SUGAR: A Scalable Human-Video-Driven Generalizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Learning Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> SUGAR is a framework that enables humanoid robots to learn generalizable loco-manipulation skills directly from diverse human videos. It leverages a three-stage pipeline to transform noisy kinematic data into robust, autonomous policies capable of zero-shot real-world transfer, achieving high success rates across six complex tasks and demonstrating autonomous failure recovery.

## Problem

- Existing methods for humanoid loco-manipulation (RL from scratch, reference-motion tracking, teleoperation) face scalability issues, high data collection costs, and limited generalization to novel scenarios.
- Utilizing abundant human video data for robot learning is challenging due to inherent imperfections (occlusion, contact artifacts, retargeting errors) in extracted kinematic priors, making them physically implausible for direct use.
- Prior video-based methods are limited to locomotion or rigid reference replay, lacking object interaction capabilities or robust generalization and sim-to-real transfer for contact-rich tasks.

## Method

- An automated pipeline extracts coarse kinematic interaction priors (human-object motions and VLM-labeled contact labels) from unannotated human videos.
- A privileged ==reinforcement learning== 'refiner' policy transforms these imperfect kinematic priors into physically feasible, high-fidelity skill demonstrations using a unified mimic-style reward and a Progressive State Pool for stable learning.
- A hierarchical autonomous policy, consisting of a Behavior Cloning/RL-trained Command Tracker and a ==Diffusion Policy==-based Command Generator, distills these refined demonstrations for robust real-world execution, trained on a rollout dataset to bridge kinematic planning and dynamic execution.

## Results

- SUGAR demonstrated superior performance over reference-tracking baselines (Resmimic, HDMI) across six diverse loco-manipulation tasks on a Unitree G1 humanoid, showing higher success rates and lower object position errors.
- The framework exhibited positive data scaling, with success rates improving from **32.7%** to **76.0%** for tasks like Kick Box when increasing training data from 20 to 100 trajectories, indicating efficient leverage of larger datasets.
- Zero-shot real-world transfer was achieved on a Unitree G1 robot, showcasing robust closed-loop execution, autonomous failure recovery from disruptions, stability under external disturbances, and generalization to objects with varying properties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A physics-based 'refiner' policy is crucial for transforming imperfect kinematic priors from human videos into physically feasible and dynamically consistent expert demonstrations, essential for stable policy learning.
> - The Progressive State Pool effectively stabilizes training by providing diverse, physically consistent initialization states, overcoming issues caused by initial kinematic reconstruction errors and improving skill acquisition.
> - A hierarchical policy structure, with a low-level command tracker and a high-level diffusion-based command generator trained on closed-loop rollouts, enables robust long-horizon task completion and autonomous failure recovery in real-world settings.
