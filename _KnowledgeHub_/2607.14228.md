# SeeSE3: Emergence of 3D Space in Vision Features

> [!summary] Summary
> This research reveals that self-supervised vision models, trained on passive visual input, develop latent representations whose structure aligns with 3D Euclidean space. A lightweight "Poincaré adapter" is introduced, enabling linear decoding of 3D transformations and efficient latent-space navigation without explicit 3D supervision.

## Problem

- Investigating whether passive vision foundation models intrinsically reflect the properties of 3D Euclidean space (SE(3)) in their latent representations.
- Addressing Henri Poincaré's hypothesis that active agency is necessary for an entity to acquire the concept of space.
- Probing the inherent organization of vision feature spaces for 3D geometry, rather than merely extracting 3D information using complex decoders.

## Method

- A set of four metrics, ranging from ==topological alignment== to ==linear equivariance==, was introduced to probe the spatial awareness embedded in visual features.
- A lightweight **Poincaré adapter** (a two-layer ==MLP==) was developed to transform non-linear feature manifolds into a ==homogeneous coordinate system== where 3D pose changes become linearly decodable.
- ==Latent Space Navigation== techniques, including a training-free **Inverse Poincaré** method, were developed to perform ==visual odometry== and localization directly within the adapted latent space.

## Results

- DINOv2 and DINOv3 exhibited high topological alignment (mutual k-nn scores ≈ **0.38**) with camera pose space, comparable to explicitly geometric models.
- The Poincaré adapter enabled DINOv2 to achieve an R² of approximately **0.65** for the linear recovery of 6D Lie Algebra twist vectors, demonstrating significant linear equivariance.
- The training-free 'Inverse Poincaré' method yielded competitive Pose-Error Hits for latent space navigation, with **60%** success in unseen test rooms, indicating effective navigation in geometrically structured manifolds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised vision models, despite passive training, develop robust latent subspaces whose topology and structure correlate with 3D Euclidean space, potentially challenging Poincaré's hypothesis.
> - A 'Visual Grid Code' emerges in these models, allowing 3D transformations to be linearly decodable after a non-linear projection using a lightweight adapter.
> - Rotation components of SE(3) are more reliably decodable than translational magnitudes, attributed to differences in depth dependence of optical flow and scale ambiguity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work investigates the internal structure of vision foundation models, specifically focusing on whether their latent representations inherently reflect the properties of 3D Euclidean space. This positions the research at the intersection of several fields:

**Probing 3D Awareness of Vision Models:** Prior efforts in this area, such as Probe3D, evaluated vision features by regressing image-centric quantities like depth or surface normals, or by assessing multi-view feature consistency. The current study differentiates itself by not focusing on the *extractability* of 3D information through high-capacity decoders, but rather on the *intrinsic organization* of the feature space itself and its alignment with the Special Euclidean group SE(3) of rigid transformations.

**Explicit 3D Reconstruction and Visual Odometry:** Traditional methods for 3D estimation, like DUSt3R or VGGT, typically rely on extensive Transformer architectures to compute geometry. Similarly, classical Visual Odometry (VO) explicitly solves for camera pose. This paper diverges by questioning whether the visual features are structured to reflect 3D space inherently, rather than solely demonstrating that geometric information can be extracted through complex decoders.

**Connections to Generative Models:** The work relates to RUST, which showed that generative models trained on static scenes can organize their latent spaces into geometric structures isomorphic to physical camera parameters. This research extends that inquiry to discriminative models trained on diverse, arbitrary datasets (e.g., ImageNet, YouTube), examining their capacity to discover similar geometric substructures for static scenes.

**Cognitive and Neuroscientific Foundations:** The paper draws inspiration from cognitive science and philosophy, particularly Henri Poincaré's hypothesis that agency is necessary for the acquisition of the concept of space. It also connects to studies on the primary visual cortex (V1) transforming curved video trajectories into straighter paths. By investigating "motionless observers" (models trained passively), the work empirically tests these hypotheses within modern AI systems and questions whether grid-cell-like representations require active motor agency.

**Visual Navigation Models (VNMs) and Navigation World Models (NWMs):** While not directly focused on action selection, the study’s objective of pose linearization and latent space navigation is relevant to these fields. It aims to establish and improve the fundamental mechanisms for spatial reasoning and navigation within vision feature spaces, moving beyond primarily architectural and scaling advancements. The paper positions itself as exploring the underlying geometry that enables such navigation and prediction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether the internal representations constructed by vision foundation models inherently reflect the intrinsic properties of 3D Euclidean space. Unlike prior work that typically focused on extracting 3D quantities (e.g., depth, normals) or poses using dedicated decoders, this study probes the fundamental relationship between the structure of visual feature spaces and the group of Euclidean transformations SE(3).

The primary motivation stems from a long-standing philosophical and cognitive science debate, notably Henri Poincaré's hypothesis, which posits that a being without agency or voluntary movement could not acquire the concept of space. Modern vision foundation models, even those trained on video, are essentially passive observers without direct control over their environment or viewpoint. This provides an opportunity to test what the authors term "The Poincaré Task": Can a motionless observer, given only passive visual input, "discover" the structure of 3D space? Specifically, do these models organize their latent spaces in a manner that reflects the Special Euclidean group SE(3)?

Key aspects of this motivation include:
- **Intrinsic Geometric Discovery:** The paper aims to determine if a low-dimensional, homogeneous coordinate system, characteristic of Euclidean 3D space (6D for position and orientation), emerges within the high-dimensional, content-dependent visual features of powerful foundation models. This is not assumed to be obvious due to the inherent differences in dimensionality and dependence on visual content.
- **Moving Beyond Explicit Supervision:** The research seeks to understand if such geometric understanding can arise from minimal intervention, purely from the statistical regularities of visual data, rather than through explicit 3D supervision or predefined coordinate systems.
- **Enabling Latent-Space Navigation:** A practical motivation is to leverage any discovered geometric structure to develop new "Latent-Space Navigation" techniques. These techniques would perform visual odometry and localization directly within the latent space, potentially bypassing the need for explicit 3D reconstruction.

To address these objectives, the authors formulated the problem of inferring 3D spatial structure from visual features, introduced a set of probes to evaluate this, and developed a "Poincaré adapter" to reveal the underlying geometric organization. They also aimed to provide theoretical analysis for the emergence of such structure and investigate the relative difficulty of recovering different components of SE(3).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research investigates the emergence of 3D space in vision features by analyzing how the latent space of vision models aligns with the geometry of Euclidean transformations. The methodology focuses on static scenes, where camera pose changes are the sole source of visual variation, simplifying the relationship between pose and features.

**Problem Formulation:**
For a static scene, the process involves mapping a camera pose P$_t$ ∈ SE(3) at time *t* to an image I$_t$, which is then encoded into a feature vector z$_t$ = f$_\theta$(I$_t$). The goal is to compare the properties of the set of features {z$_t$} to the structure of SE(3) as defined by {P$_t$}.

**Metrics and Probes:**
The study introduces four metrics of increasing complexity to probe spatial awareness:

- **Metric 1 (M1): Topological Alignment:** This metric assesses whether the local neighborhood structure in the visual feature space mirrors that in the camera pose space. The mutual k-nearest neighbor (k-nn) alignment metric is used, comparing k-nn graphs (k=10) computed in feature space (using cosine similarity) against those in a 9D camera pose space (3D translation + first two columns of the rotation matrix in world coordinates). This metric is training-free and evaluated across both short and long temporal strides to test global spatial awareness.

- **Metric 2 (M2): Intrinsic Dimensionality (ID):** This metric estimates the Local Intrinsic Dimensionality of the feature space using MLE and Two-NN estimators. An ID close to 6 suggests that the features capture the underlying 6 degrees of freedom of SE(3). This metric is also training-free.

- **Metric 3 (M3): Linear Equivariance:** This metric tests if the feature space is globally Euclidean. A linear regressor W is trained to predict the 6-dimensional Lie Algebra twist vector (ΔP) representing relative pose change from the raw feature difference (z$_{t+s}$ - z$_t$). A high R² score would indicate a natively flat manifold. Regression targets are standardized to account for scale differences.

- **Metric 4 (M4): The Poincaré Adapter:** Recognizing the restrictive nature of M3, this metric introduces a lightweight, trainable adapter (φ$_\theta$, a 2-layer MLP) in a Siamese configuration. The objective is to predict ΔP ≈ W(φ$_\theta$(z$_{t+s}$) - φ$_\theta$(z$_t$)). The adapter "unrolls" the non-linear feature space to construct a homogeneous coordinate system where pose changes become linear. The adapter is applied independently to each latent vector before subtraction. It is optimized with AdamW on MSE loss for standardized ΔP targets.

**Data and Models Evaluated:**
The evaluation was conducted on static scenes from diverse datasets: ScanNet, ARKitScenes, TUM RGB-D, 12 Scenes, and 7 Scenes. A wide array of vision foundation models were tested, including:
- **Baselines:** Raw Pixels.
- **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Models:** DINOv2, DINOv3, CroCo (3D-pretext SSL), V-JEPA 2.1.
- **Text-Aligned Models:** CLIP.
- **Metric-Based Models:** DUSt3R, MoGe, Depth Anything v3, Perception Encoder, Metric3d v2.
- **Generative Models:** Stable Diffusion variants (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, LCM, SD-DINO VAE/UNet), Stable Video Diffusion (SVD).
- **Video-Based Models:** VideoMAEv2, RVM, 4DS.
For all metrics M1-M4, a layer sweep was performed across encoder blocks to identify the optimal layer for feature extraction.

**Application: Latent Space Navigation:**
As a proof-of-concept, the study explores Latent Space Navigation. The idea is that if the visual latent space reflects SE(3) topology, an agent can "imagine" movement consequences without explicit 3D reconstruction. Given a starting adapted feature g$_t$ = φ$_\theta$(z$_t$) and a desired physical displacement ΔP, the goal is to predict the destination feature ˆg$_{t+s}$.
- **Inverse Poincaré:** A training-free approach where ˆg$_{t+s}$ = g$_t$ + W^†ΔP, with W^† being the pseudo-inverse of the linear projection weights from M4.
- **Parametric Correctors:** Linear, MLP, and Attention layers trained to predict a non-linear residual correction on top of the Inverse Poincaré prediction.
- **Evaluation:** Nearest-neighbor retrieval in the adapted feature space, measuring Pose-Error Hits (Hit@ϵ) based on the L2 distance between predicted and ground-truth 6D twist vectors. Cross-room generalization and multi-step navigation were also assessed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the emergence of 3D spatial structure within vision features:

**Topological Alignment (Metric M1):**
- Explicitly geometric models (DUSt3R, MoGe) demonstrated the highest mutual k-nn alignment scores (≈ 0.35 − 0.40) at high strides, indicating strong alignment between their representations and camera pose space.
- Among purely self-supervised models, DINOv2 and DINOv3 exhibited comparable topological alignment (≈ 0.38) to the geometric models. This suggests the emergence of spatial topology from passive image-level self-supervision, without explicit 3D signals.
- Video models (VideoMAE, RVM, 4DS) performed less successfully (≈ 0.15 − 0.25), indicating that while they capture temporal motion, their latent states may be context-dependent and less suited for forming globally consistent spatial maps.

**Geometry of Raw Features (Metrics M2 & M3):**
- **Intrinsic Dimensionality (M2):** Most encoders produced features with an intrinsic dimensionality around 6. DINOv2 and DINOv3 showed higher IDs (≈ 5.8 − 10.0), potentially reflecting richer semantic manifolds. CroCo, with explicit 3D pre-training, exhibited an ID closer to the physical 6 degrees of freedom (≈ 5.9, MLE).
- **Linear Equivariance (M3):** Models generally failed to recover physical displacements (∆P) via a global linear projection from raw feature differences, yielding negative or near-zero R² scores (e.g., DINOv2 R² ≈ −0.28, Pixels R² ≈ −5.29). This outcome indicated that the raw feature manifold is non-linear and standard vector arithmetic on raw features does not directly correspond to physical motion.

**Emergence of Linear Structure (Metric M4 – Poincaré Adapter):**
- The application of a lightweight Siamese MLP adapter (φ) enabled the recovery of substantial linear equivariance. DINOv2 achieved an R² of approximately 0.65 on the test set, indicating that its latent space contains a locally Euclidean sub-manifold that can be linearly mapped to SE(3) transformations. This suggests the existence of a "Visual Grid Code" accessible via a non-linear projection.
- CroCo, despite its explicit 3D training, underperformed DINOv2, suggesting that the scale of passive observation in models like DINOv2 might be more effective for discovering spatial laws.
- Raw pixels still exhibited negative R² even with the adapter, confirming that the decodable structure arises from the properties of learned features.
- **Generalization and Scaling:** Adapters trained on varying numbers of ScanNet rooms demonstrated monotonic improvement in zero-shot generalization to unseen rooms across DINOv2, V-JEPA, and DUSt3R. The recoverable signal consistently increased without plateauing at N=200 rooms, suggesting geometric awareness is a scalable and emergent property.
- **Dataset Difficulty:** The success of pose recovery varied significantly across datasets, influenced by data sufficiency, visual richness, and workspace geometry (e.g., 12-Scenes with dense captures achieving high decodability, while sparse or noisy datasets like 7-Scenes performing lower).

**Latent Space Navigation:**
- The training-free "Inverse Poincaré" approach, which utilizes the pseudo-inverse of the adapter's linear projection (W^†), yielded competitive retrieval performance in terms of Pose-Error Hits (Hit@ϵ), significantly outperforming a static identity baseline.
- While a learned Attention corrector achieved better regression metrics (R²), it lagged behind Inverse Poincaré in physical region retrieval, suggesting that simple vector arithmetic is effective for navigating to correct spatial neighborhoods.
- **Cross-Room Generalization:** Inverse Poincaré achieved successful navigation in approximately 60% of unseen test rooms. When adapted via "Ridge-Refit" (fitting a linear mapping component on the test room), navigation success increased to 90–94% across DINOv2, DUSt3R, and V-JEPA, indicating a robust and transferable geometrically structured manifold.
- **Multi-Step Navigation:** Open-loop multi-step navigation in the latent space was demonstrated. For moderate displacements, retrieved trajectories accurately reached the target. For extreme displacements, rotation recovery remained precise (< 5° error) despite accumulation of translation drift.

**Analysis of Emergence and Obstructions:**
- **Probe Design:** Ablations showed that Lie algebra linearization (predicting 6D twist) and the Siamese structure of the adapter (φ(z$_{t+s}$) - φ(z$_t$)) were crucial for performance. Predicting absolute poses or using SE(3) matrix regression performed poorly.
- **Theoretical Basis:** Theorem 5 indicated that a single linear readout's error is controlled by the curvature of the feature manifold. The nonlinear Poincaré adapter functions by "unrolling" this curved manifold, removing extrinsic curvature. A residual error remains due to the non-commutative nature of SE(3).
- **Rotation-Translation Asymmetry:** Empirical analysis and Theorem 7 revealed that rotation is more reliably decodable than translation magnitude. This is attributed to rotational optical flow being depth-independent, while translational flow scales with inverse depth (1/Z), introducing greater variability in the Jacobian. Translation magnitude is also subject to a scale ambiguity (v, Z 7 → λv, λZ).
- **Training Conditions:** Ablations of DINOv2 trained from scratch showed that topological alignment (M1) was stable across various training recipes, while geometric decodability (M4 R²) was more fragile and sensitive to hyperparameters and stochasticity. DINO self-distillation was identified as crucial for topological alignment and deeper layer abstract representations. Batch size was critical for manifold formation, and data scale was a dominant factor for the emergence of robust geometric understanding, particularly for translation magnitude.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of how visual foundation models construct and represent 3D spatial information, with several implications:

**Challenge to Cognitive Hypotheses:** The finding that self-supervised vision models, trained purely through passive observation, develop robust latent subspaces correlated with 3D Euclidean space directly addresses and potentially challenges Henri Poincaré’s hypothesis that active agency is required for the concept of space. This suggests that the statistical regularities inherent in visual data alone may be sufficient for a "motionless observer" to discover fundamental geometric principles.

**Intrinsic Geometric Understanding in AI:** The demonstration that a "Visual Grid Code" emerges in these models, accessible via a lightweight adapter, indicates that these models learn more than superficial correlations. They appear to internalize core geometric properties of the world without explicit 3D supervision. This offers insights into the implicit spatial reasoning capabilities of large vision models.

**Efficient Latent Space Navigation:** The proposed Latent Space Navigation techniques, particularly the training-free Inverse Poincaré method, offer an alternative paradigm for visual odometry and localization. By operating purely in the latent space and leveraging linear operations after adaptation, these methods could provide a computationally efficient alternative to explicit 3D reconstruction. This has potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and other areas requiring real-time pose estimation and path planning.

**Foundation for World Models:** By establishing the conditions for the emergence and accessibility of 3D spatial structure in vision features, this work lays fundamental groundwork for advanced Navigation World Models (NWMs) and Visual Navigation Models (VNMs). Understanding the geometry underlying spatial reasoning in latent space can inform the design of more robust and interpretable navigation systems, focusing on core mechanisms rather than solely architectural scaling.

**Guiding Model Development:** The detailed analysis of geometric decodability, including the observed asymmetry between rotation and translation recovery and the sensitivity to training parameters, provides actionable insights for future model development. Recognizing the distinct challenges in learning translational magnitude, for instance, can guide the design of improved self-supervision objectives or architectural inductive biases. The finding that data scale is crucial for robust geometric awareness underscores the importance of large, diverse datasets.

**Future Research Directions:** The paper also highlights limitations and opportunities for future work, particularly extending the formalism to dynamic scenes. Investigating how models differentiate between "changes of position" (camera motion) and "changes of state" (scene motion) in dynamic environments would probe deeper spatio-temporal symmetries and conservation laws, potentially bridging the gap to more profound world understanding.
