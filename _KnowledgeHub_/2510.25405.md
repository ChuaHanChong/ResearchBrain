# Sim-to-Real Gentle Manipulation of Deformable and Fragile Objects with Stress-Guided Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KTH Royal Institute of Technology and Institut de Robòtica i Informàtica Industrial developed a vision-based reinforcement learning framework for gentle manipulation of deformable and fragile objects. The system integrates explicit internal object stress into the reward function, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tasks like picking and pushing, successfully handling objects like tofu and foam without damage while maintaining high task success rates.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable and fragile objects (DFOM) is challenging due to complex, often unmodeled contact dynamics and the susceptibility of these objects to irreversible damage from excessive forces.
- Traditional methods (model-based, specialized hardware, advanced sensing) often require precise object models, significant engineering effort, or increase system complexity and cost, limiting real-world applicability and generalization.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for DFOM typically lack explicit integration of internal object stress, potentially learning policies that damage fragile objects, and face difficulties with real-world training due to the destructive nature of trial-and-error with fragile materials.

## Method

- The researchers introduce a **Stress-Guided RL** framework, built on ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)==, that explicitly penalizes ==internal object stress== in the reward function during simulation training.
- The framework combines a task success reward with a custom ==stress-penalized reward==, which averages the median of the top 10% stress values and the mean stress across all particles, to balance task achievement with damage avoidance.
- To facilitate stable learning and ==zero-shot sim-to-real== transfer, the framework integrates ==offline human demonstrations==, ==curriculum learning== (starting with rigid object training), and ==domain randomization== with perceptual noise.

## Results

- In simulation, the full framework (Ours-2) achieved the highest success rate (**98%** for pick-up, **88%** for push) and the lowest stress levels among RL variants (e.g., mean stress of **1747 Pa** for pick-up) compared to naive RL (**2584 Pa**), indicating gentler manipulation.
- Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was successfully demonstrated: for cubic foam pick-up, the method achieved **100%** success with significantly reduced water loss (**10.00%** for Ours-1), outperforming naive RL which caused **41.90%** water loss.
- Qualitative real-world experiments confirmed that policies learned with the proposed stress-guided framework successfully manipulated actual tofu without visible damage for both pick-up and push tasks, validating its practical applicability for fragile objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly penalizing internal object stress in the reward function is crucial for learning damage-aware, gentle manipulation policies, but it must be carefully balanced with task success through effective reward design.
> - Incorporating human demonstrations significantly improves sample efficiency and guides the RL agent towards gentle handling strategies, preventing overly conservative behaviors that might arise from pure stress minimization.
> - Curriculum learning, by initially training on rigid object surrogates, effectively bootstraps the learning process in computationally intensive soft-body simulations, accelerating convergence for complex deformable object interactions.
