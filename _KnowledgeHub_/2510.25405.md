# Sim-to-Real Gentle Manipulation of Deformable and Fragile Objects with Stress-Guided Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KTH Royal Institute of Technology and Institut de Robòtica i Informàtica Industrial developed a vision-based reinforcement learning framework for gentle manipulation of deformable and fragile objects. The system integrates explicit internal object stress into the reward function, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tasks like picking and pushing, successfully handling objects like tofu and foam without damage while maintaining high task success rates.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable and fragile objects (DFOM) is challenging due to complex, often unmodeled contact dynamics and the susceptibility of these objects to irreversible damage from excessive forces.
- Traditional methods (model-based, specialized hardware, advanced sensing) often require precise object models, significant engineering effort, or increase system complexity and cost, limiting real-world applicability and generalization.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for DFOM typically lack explicit integration of internal object stress, potentially learning policies that damage fragile objects, and face difficulties with real-world training due to the destructive nature of trial-and-error with fragile materials.

## Method

- The researchers introduce a **Stress-Guided RL** framework, built on ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)==, that explicitly penalizes ==internal object stress== in the reward function during simulation training.
- The framework combines a task success reward with a custom ==stress-penalized reward==, which averages the median of the top 10% stress values and the mean stress across all particles, to balance task achievement with damage avoidance.
- To facilitate stable learning and ==zero-shot sim-to-real== transfer, the framework integrates ==offline human demonstrations==, ==curriculum learning== (starting with rigid object training), and ==domain randomization== with perceptual noise.

## Results

- In simulation, the full framework (Ours-2) achieved the highest success rate (**98%** for pick-up, **88%** for push) and the lowest stress levels among RL variants (e.g., mean stress of **1747 Pa** for pick-up) compared to naive RL (**2584 Pa**), indicating gentler manipulation.
- Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was successfully demonstrated: for cubic foam pick-up, the method achieved **100%** success with significantly reduced water loss (**10.00%** for Ours-1), outperforming naive RL which caused **41.90%** water loss.
- Qualitative real-world experiments confirmed that policies learned with the proposed stress-guided framework successfully manipulated actual tofu without visible damage for both pick-up and push tasks, validating its practical applicability for fragile objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly penalizing internal object stress in the reward function is crucial for learning damage-aware, gentle manipulation policies, but it must be carefully balanced with task success through effective reward design.
> - Incorporating human demonstrations significantly improves sample efficiency and guides the RL agent towards gentle handling strategies, preventing overly conservative behaviors that might arise from pure stress minimization.
> - Curriculum learning, by initially training on rigid object surrogates, effectively bootstraps the learning process in computationally intensive soft-body simulations, accelerating convergence for complex deformable object interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The robotic manipulation of deformable and fragile objects (DFOM) constitutes a significant challenge in robotics, with implications across various domains such as food processing, agriculture, assistive care, and surgery. These objects are susceptible to irreversible damage, such as tearing, cracking, or bruising, when subjected to excessive forces or stresses. The difficulties inherent in DFOM stem primarily from two factors: the complex and often unmodeled contact and object dynamics that make internal stress assessment challenging, and the stringent safety constraints that mandate keeping applied stress below damage thresholds.

Traditional approaches to DFOM have typically fallen into several categories:
- **Model-based methods:** These leverage physical priors and analytical models to explicitly measure or predict stress levels. However, they frequently require precise object models and accurate parameter identification, which limits their practicality for real-world deployment where object properties can vary.
- **Specialized hardware:** This includes the development of soft or compliant end-effectors designed to inherently reduce the risk of object damage. While effective, these solutions often demand considerable engineering effort and task-specific expertise, hindering generalization.
- **Advanced sensing:** Methods employing tactile, force, or touch sensors can enable precise control and safe interaction. While promising, the integration of such sensors often increases system complexity and cost. Vision-based strategies exist for estimating interaction forces, but they introduce their own set of complexities.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for visuomotor control tasks, demonstrating success in sim-to-real transfer. However, its application to real-world DFOM, particularly with an explicit emphasis on ensuring safe stress levels for fragile objects, remains an area with limited exploration. Existing RL methods for deformable object manipulation often rely on extensive reward engineering or curriculum learning to guide behavior but do not typically integrate direct feedback on internal object stress or fragility.

This research addresses a recognized gap in the existing literature by proposing a vision-based reinforcement learning framework that explicitly incorporates object fragility into the learning process. Unlike prior work that either omits explicit stress considerations or relies on specialized hardware/sensors, this study integrates stress as a critical component of the reward function during simulation-based training. This allows for the learning of gentle, damage-aware manipulation policies that can be transferred directly to the real world using only visual input, without requiring complex object models or additional physical sensors. The work aims to bridge the gap between high-performance RL in simulation and its safe, effective deployment for fragile objects in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a vision-based reinforcement learning framework capable of performing gentle and reliable manipulation of deformable and fragile objects (DFOM) in a zero-shot sim-to-real manner. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and is motivated by the limitations of existing approaches.

1. **Develop a Damage-Aware Policy:** The policy must be capable of successfully performing manipulation tasks (e.g., picking up, pushing) while actively minimizing the internal stress applied to the object. This is crucial to prevent damage such as tearing, cracking, or bruising, which are common issues with fragile materials.
2. **Utilize Vision-Based Control:** The manipulation policy should rely solely on visual input, specifically point cloud observations from an RGB-D camera. This aims to reduce system complexity, eliminate the need for specialized tactile or force sensors, and remove the dependency on precise analytical object models.
3. **Enable Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A core objective is to ensure that policies trained entirely in a simulated environment can be deployed directly in the real world without any additional fine-tuning. This minimizes the risk and cost associated with real-world training, especially with fragile objects.
4. **Address RL Learning Challenges in DFOM:** The integration of a stress penalty can create a conflict with task success, as minimizing stress might lead to avoiding contact altogether, thus preventing task completion. The framework must incorporate mechanisms to facilitate stable and efficient learning, preventing policies from converging to suboptimal or non-performing behaviors.

**Motivation:**
- **Ubiquity of Fragile Objects:** Many real-world applications, spanning from food handling and agriculture to medical surgery and assistive robotics, involve objects that are inherently deformable and fragile. Current robotic solutions often struggle with the delicate nature of these materials.
- **Limitations of Current Methods:**
 - **Model-based control:** Requires highly accurate object models and material properties, which are often unavailable or difficult to obtain for deformable objects.
 - **Specialized hardware/sensors:** While effective, custom grippers or tactile sensors increase the cost, complexity, and specificity of robotic systems, limiting their broad applicability.
 - **Standard RL approaches:** Without explicit guidance, standard RL methods may learn policies that achieve task success but at the cost of damaging fragile objects, as stress minimization is not an intrinsic part of the reward structure. The "trial-and-error" nature of RL is also impractical for fragile objects in the real world due to potential for damage.
- **Potential of Simulation:** Modern soft-body simulators offer the fidelity required to compute internal stress, providing a valuable signal that is difficult or impossible to obtain directly in the real world without specialized instrumentation. Leveraging this simulation capability for learning can guide the robot towards gentle interactions.
- **Efficiency and Generalization:** A vision-based, sim-to-real approach offers the prospect of more generalized, adaptable, and cost-effective robotic systems for DFOM by reducing hardware dependency and the need for extensive real-world data collection.

By addressing these objectives and motivations, the research aims to contribute a robust and practical framework for robotic manipulation that inherently respects the fragility of objects, opening avenues for safer and more reliable automation in sensitive applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a stress-guided Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework designed for deformable and fragile object manipulation (DFOM), leveraging a standard off-policy RL algorithm, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC). The approach is structured to enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer using only visual input.

**Problem Formulation:**
The problem is modeled as a partially observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
- **State Space (S):** Includes robot joint positions and velocities, object particle positions and velocities, and internal object stress (measurable only in simulation).
- **Observation Space (O):** Comprises a low-dimensional encoding of the object's partial point cloud (obtained from an RGB-D camera), the centroid of the point cloud, the robot end-effector pose, and the gripper position.
- **Action Space (A):** Parameterizes the Cartesian displacement and orientation displacement (axis-angle) of the end-effector, along with the gripper position displacement.

**Stress Metrics (for Simulation):**
The framework utilizes a Material Point Method (MPM) simulation, where objects are represented by *N* particles. For each particle *i*, a Cauchy stress tensor **C**^(i) is computed. From this, the Von Mises stress σ^(i) (a scalar quantity indicating overall stress) is derived. To aggregate particle-level stress into a single scalar for reward computation, the following metrics are considered: mean stress (¯σ), median stress (ˆσ), top *K* percent mean stress (¯σ_top{k}), top *K* percent median stress, and maximum stress (σ_max).

**Core Components of the Stress-Guided RL Framework:**

1. **Stress-Penalized Reward Design:**
 - The reward function combines two terms: a task success reward (R_success) and a stress-penalized reward (R_stress).
 - **Task Success Reward (R_success):** Consists of intermediate dense rewards for achieving sub-goals (e.g., approaching the object, lifting) and a binary reward for task completion (e.g., reaching a target height or location).
 - **Stress-Penalized Reward (R_stress):** Designed to balance robustness and sensitivity to localized damage. Directly penalizing maximum stress (σ_max) was found to be too noisy, while global statistics like mean/median stress could mask critical localized high-stress regions. The proposed `R_stress` is:
 `R_stress = - (1/β) * (α * ˆσ_top10 + (1 - α) * ¯σ)^2`
 Where:
 - `ˆσ_top10` is the median of the top 10% stress values across particles.
 - `¯σ` is the mean stress across all particles.
 - `α ∈` is a balancing factor for the two stress components.
 - The quadratic term amplifies penalties for larger stresses, and `β` is a scaling factor that sharpens the penalty above a certain threshold.
 - This reward explicitly encourages gentle manipulation by penalizing actions that induce high stress.

2. **Policy Training and Architecture:**
 - **Observation Processing:** The object's point cloud is processed by a PointNet encoder. The robot's state (end-effector pose, gripper position, point cloud centroid) is processed by an MLP.
 - **Shared Embedding:** The latent representations from PointNet and the MLP are concatenated to form a shared feature vector.
 - **Actor-Critic Networks:** This shared feature vector serves as input to both the actor and critic networks, which are parameterized as MLPs.
 - **Optimization:** Policy optimization is performed using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).

3. **Offline Demonstrations:**
 - **Motivation:** The stress penalty can conflict with task achievement, as minimizing stress might lead to policies that avoid necessary contact. Human demonstrations provide prior knowledge of gentle handling.
 - **Framework:** Reinforcement Learning with Prior Data (RLPD) with SAC as the backbone.
 - **Data Collection:** 20 human-teleoperated demonstrations per task are collected in simulation.
 - **Sampling Strategy:** During training, each batch consists of 50% offline demonstrations and 50% online experience from robot rollouts, improving sample efficiency and guiding learning towards safe behaviors.

4. **Curriculum Learning:**
 - **Motivation:** Soft-body simulations are computationally intensive due to the need for fine-grained timesteps. Early learning stages, such as approaching an object, are less dependent on object deformability.
 - **Strategy:** The policy is first trained on a rigid object surrogate of the same shape. Once the policy converges for the rigid object, the object is switched to a deformable type, and training continues in the soft-body simulation. This accelerates initial learning.

5. **Mitigating the Sim-to-Real Gap:**
 - **Domain Randomization:** Key physical parameters of the object (friction coefficient, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) are randomized during training to enhance robustness to real-world material property variations. Initial poses of both the object and the robot end-effector are also randomized.
 - **Perceptual Noise:** Zero-mean Gaussian noise is added to all observational components (point cloud, centroid, end-effector pose, gripper position) to account for real-world sensor uncertainties.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Robot:** UFactory xArm 7 (7-DoF manipulator) with a parallel-jaw gripper.
- **Objects:** Tofu (cylindrical and cubic shapes, dyed blue for segmentation) as the primary fragile object. Absorptive foam block saturated with water for real-world quantitative evaluation of gentleness.
- **Simulator:** Genesis physics engine with the built-in Taichi soft body physical engine (MLS-MPM).
- **Camera:** Intel RealSense L515 LiDAR camera for real-world point cloud acquisition.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Pick-up:** Grasp and lift tofu at least 9 cm above the table.
 - **Push:** Move tofu to a 2 cm radius circular target area on the table.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Simulation:** Task success rate, mean stress (¯σ), mean of top 5% stress (¯σ_top5), and maximum stress (σ_max) observed over an episode.
 - **Real-world (Quantitative):** Pick-up success rate and water loss percentage (for foam, normalized by initial absorbed water) as an indicator of squeezing force.
 - **Real-world (Qualitative):** Visual assessment of tofu integrity (damage/no damage) and task completion.

The methodology is designed to systematically incorporate physical priors (stress information), leverage human expertise (demonstrations), and streamline training (curriculum learning) to enable robust and gentle manipulation in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to assess the effectiveness of the stress-guided RL framework in manipulating deformable and fragile objects, quantify the contribution of each design component, and demonstrate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Evaluation in Simulation (Cylindrical Tofu)**

1. **Pick-up Task (Table I):**
 - **Naive RL (without C, SPR, D):** Achieved a 90% success rate but induced high stress levels (mean stress: 2584 Pa, top 5% mean stress: 14974 Pa, max stress: 32879 Pa), indicating frequent damage to the simulated tofu.
 - **Stress reward (only SPR):** Resulted in 0% task success, as the policy became overly conservative, avoiding contact with the tofu to minimize stress. This highlights the conflict between stress minimization and task achievement.
 - **Curriculum (only C):** Improved the success rate to 97% and slightly reduced stress compared to Naive (mean stress: 2527 Pa, top 5% mean stress: 12705 Pa, max stress: 25427 Pa), suggesting its utility in bootstrapping learning.
 - **Demonstrations (only D):** Achieved a 95% success rate and showed some reduction in stress (mean stress: 2450 Pa, top 5% mean stress: 12934 Pa, max stress: 23888 Pa), but did not provide sufficient guidance for truly gentle manipulation.
 - **Ours-1 (SPR + D):** Demonstrated a 97% success rate and significantly reduced stress levels (mean stress: 1966 Pa, top 5% mean stress: 9707 Pa, max stress: 17774 Pa). This indicates that the stress-penalized reward is crucial for gentle manipulation, even when combined with human demonstrations. Compared to Naive, this represented a 36.5% decrease in mean stress.
 - **Ours-2 (C + SPR + D - full method):** Achieved the highest success rate of 98% and the lowest stress levels among all RL variants (mean stress: 1747 Pa, top 5% mean stress: 9271 Pa, max stress: 16915 Pa), demonstrating the complementary benefits of all three components.
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC):** Exhibited low task success (44%) but achieved relatively low stress levels (mean stress: 1910 Pa, top 5% mean stress: 8066 Pa, max stress: 13413 Pa). Its low success rate made it less effective for practical tasks.

2. **Push Task (Table II):** Similar trends were observed, with Ours-1 and Ours-2 generally outperforming baselines in balancing task success and stress minimization. Ours-1 (SPR+D) achieved 83% success with low stress (mean: 546 Pa), while Ours-2 (C+SPR+D) achieved 88% success with slightly higher stress (mean: 626 Pa), indicating that for pushing, the full curriculum might not always yield the absolute lowest stress compared to just SPR+D.

**B. Evaluation in Simulation (Cubic Tofu)**

- **Tables III & IV:** The results for cubic tofu generally mirrored those for cylindrical tofu, confirming the generalizability of the proposed method to different object geometries. Ours-1 and Ours-2 consistently achieved high success rates while reducing stress compared to the Naive baseline.

**C. Real-World Experiments**

1. **Quantitative Evaluation (Cubic Foam Pick-up, Table V):** To quantitatively assess gentleness in the real world, an absorptive foam block saturated with water was used, and water loss (due to squeezing) was measured.
 - **Naive:** Achieved 100% pick-up success but resulted in 41.90% water loss, indicating aggressive squeezing.
 - **Ours-1:** Achieved 100% success with significantly reduced water loss of 10.00%, demonstrating gentle manipulation.
 - **Ours-2:** Achieved 90% success with 21.69% water loss.
 - **BC:** Showed the lowest water loss (9.52%) but had a low success rate of 40%.
 - These results demonstrate successful zero-shot transfer, with Ours-1 and Ours-2 achieving high task success with gentle manipulation in the real world. The transfer from tofu-trained policies to foam suggests a degree of robustness to material variations.

2. **Qualitative Evaluation (Tofu Pick-up & Push, Figs. 5 & 6):**
 - Visual inspection confirmed that policies learned with Ours-1 and Ours-2 successfully completed tasks without visible damage to the tofu, contrasting with other methods that frequently caused tearing or excessive deformation. This further supports the effectiveness of the stress-guided approach and its zero-shot transfer capabilities.

**Overall Findings:**
- The stress-penalized reward is a necessary component for achieving damage-aware, gentle manipulation, as policies without it induce high stress or fail to make contact.
- Curriculum learning and offline demonstrations effectively bootstrap the learning process, improving sample efficiency and preventing convergence to suboptimal behaviors. Demonstrations specifically impart human knowledge for gentle handling.
- The proposed framework (particularly Ours-1 and Ours-2) successfully learns policies that balance high task success rates with significantly reduced stress on fragile objects in simulation.
- Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was successfully demonstrated for both pick-up and push tasks, validating the practical applicability of the learned policies in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for deformable and fragile objects, an area with substantial challenges and critical real-world applications. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling Safe and Gentle Robotic Manipulation:** The core contribution of this work is a framework that explicitly integrates object fragility into the learning process. By using a stress-penalized reward, the robot is guided to perform tasks gently, minimizing internal stress and preventing irreversible damage. This capability is paramount for applications involving delicate items where damage is costly or unacceptable.

2. **Broadening Robotic Applicability:** The ability to gently manipulate fragile objects opens doors for robotic automation in sensitive industries such as:
 - **Food Processing and Agriculture:** Handling delicate produce (e.g., fruits, vegetables, baked goods) without bruising, crushing, or tearing, leading to reduced waste and improved product quality.
 - **Assistive Care and Healthcare:** Robots interacting with humans or handling sensitive medical supplies, where gentle touch and safety are non-negotiable.
 - **Manufacturing and Packaging:** Assembling or packaging delicate components that are prone to damage under conventional robotic handling.

3. **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstration of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer using only visual input is a significant achievement. It mitigates the need for extensive, time-consuming, and potentially destructive real-world training with fragile objects. This reduces development costs and accelerates deployment cycles for robotic solutions in these domains.

4. **Cost-Effectiveness and Generalizability:** The vision-based approach, which avoids reliance on specialized tactile sensors or precise analytical models, offers a more generalized and cost-effective solution compared to hardware-centric or model-intensive methods. It makes advanced manipulation capabilities accessible to a wider range of robotic systems.

5. **Methodological Contributions to Reinforcement Learning:**
 - **Novel Stress-Penalized Reward:** The design of a stress-penalized reward that effectively balances the need to detect localized high-stress regions while maintaining robustness against simulation noise is a key methodological innovation. This approach can inform reward design in other safety-critical RL applications.
 - **Effective Learning Strategies:** The successful integration of curriculum learning and offline demonstrations for bootstrapping and stabilizing RL training in computationally demanding soft-body simulations provides a valuable blueprint for developing robust policies under challenging conditions.

6. **Addressing a Research Gap:** The work explicitly addresses the previously unexplored area of sim-to-real reinforcement learning for 3D deformable and fragile object manipulation with explicit consideration of object fragility. This lays a foundation for future research in this intersection of soft robotics, physics simulation, and machine learning.

7. **Future Impact and Standardization:** The identification of the need for a standardized benchmark in DFOM highlights a crucial area for future community effort. Such a benchmark would facilitate consistent evaluation and accelerate progress in this complex field. Future work can extend the approach to a broader range of objects and explore real-world policy fine-tuning to further close the sim-to-real gap.

In summary, this research provides a practical, robust, and generalizable framework for gentle robotic manipulation of fragile objects. Its ability to learn damage-aware policies through stress-guided reinforcement learning and transfer them zero-shot to the real world represents a substantial step forward in expanding the capabilities and safety of robotic systems in sensitive applications.
