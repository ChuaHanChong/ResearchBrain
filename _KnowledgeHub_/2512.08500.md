# Learning to Control Physically-simulated 3D Characters via Generating and Mimicking 2D Motions

> [!summary] Summary
> Mimic2DM is a framework that learns to control physically simulated 3D characters from abundant 2D video data, bypassing explicit 3D reconstruction by formulating motion imitation as a physics-based 2D motion tracking problem. The system enables characters to perform complex human-object interactions and animal locomotion, outperforming two-stage methods and exhibiting implicit 3D understanding from diverse 2D viewpoints.

## Problem

- The high cost and scarcity of 3D motion capture (MoCap) data limit the diversity and scalability of physics-based character control.
- Existing methods that reconstruct 3D motions from videos prior to imitation often produce physically implausible 3D trajectories, hindering robust policy learning.
- Directly leveraging abundant 2D video data for robust 3D control is challenging due to the inherent depth ambiguity of single-view inputs.

## Method

- Formulates motion imitation as an end-to-end physics-based 2D motion tracking problem, minimizing reprojection error under physical constraints, thereby unifying 3D reconstruction and imitation.
- Trains a 'view-agnostic' 2D tracking policy using reinforcement learning, designed to minimize reprojection error across arbitrary camera viewpoints without explicit camera information, fostering implicit 3D understanding.
- Introduces a hierarchical control framework that integrates the 2D tracking policy with a kinematic 2D motion generative model for novel motion synthesis and generative tasks.

## Results

- Successfully controls physically simulated characters to perform complex human-object interactions (e.g., soccer dribbling with **91.3%** success rate) and agile non-human locomotion solely from in-the-wild 2D video data.
- Achieves superior performance over two-stage baselines (3D reconstruction then imitation), demonstrating lower 2D tracking error and reduced motion jitters (e.g., **1.69** vs. **2.62** for soccer dribble jitters).
- Exhibits robust 3D tracking accuracy (e.g., **144.3** error on AIST++ training data with 3-view 2D training) closely matching methods trained directly on 3D data, and the hierarchical framework synthesizes novel, physically plausible motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physically plausible 3D character control can be learned directly from diverse 2D keypoint trajectories by leveraging physical constraints, implicitly resolving depth ambiguity and avoiding potentially flawed 3D reconstructions.
> - Training a 2D tracking policy with diverse camera viewpoints enables it to acquire a strong implicit 3D understanding of motion, achieving 3D tracking accuracy comparable to methods trained directly on ground-truth 3D data.
> - Adaptive State Initialization (ASI) significantly improves RL training efficiency and policy convergence for 2D-supervised tasks where accurate 3D initial poses are unavailable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper, Mimic2DM, addresses a fundamental and long-standing challenge in computer animation and robotics: controlling physically simulated 3D characters to perform realistic and plausible motions, especially in complex scenarios like human-object interaction (HOI) or animal locomotion. This field has historically relied heavily on high-quality 3D motion capture (MoCap) data, which, despite its effectiveness, is inherently costly, labor-intensive, and limited in availability for diverse scenarios.

The broader research landscape can be categorized into several key areas:

- **Physics-based Character Control (MoCap-driven):** Early and contemporary approaches in this domain, exemplified by works like DeepMimic and subsequent advancements, have successfully leveraged 3D MoCap data to train physics-based controllers. These methods excel at generating highly dynamic and physically realistic motions. However, their primary limitation is the fundamental dependency on expensive and scarce 3D MoCap datasets, which are particularly hard to acquire for human-object interactions or non-human characters. This dependency restricts the scalability and applicability of such systems.

- **Physics-based Character Control (RL-driven without MoCap):** Some research has explored using reinforcement learning (RL) to teach characters skills without direct MoCap imitation, focusing instead on designing complex reward functions. While offering flexibility, this often leads to "unnatural" motion patterns and requires extensive expert knowledge for reward engineering.

- **Learning Physically Plausible Skills from Videos (via 3D Reconstruction):** To circumvent the MoCap bottleneck, a significant line of research has turned to videos as a more accessible data source. Most existing methods in this category first employ off-the-shelf human motion reconstruction techniques to estimate 3D motions from videos. These reconstructed 3D trajectories are then used as "reference motions" for physics-based imitation. The key weakness here is the inherent unreliability and physical implausibility of 3D motions reconstructed from monocular videos, especially when dealing with ambiguous situations, human-object interactions, or non-human subjects where strong 3D priors are absent. These errors propagate through the pipeline, hindering robust policy learning and often necessitating extensive post-processing or manual corrections.

- **Direct 2D Supervision for 3D Tasks:** A smaller, but growing, body of work has explored using 2D data directly as supervision for various 3D tasks. While 2D keypoint data is highly accessible and accurately reflects the original movements, the crucial challenge has always been the missing depth information. Geometric constraints and 2D priors can yield visually plausible 3D poses, but they often lack the physical fidelity required for robust physics-based control.

Mimic2DM positions itself at the intersection of these fields, providing a novel solution to the limitations of existing approaches. Its core innovation lies in *directly learning physics-based character control solely from widely available 2D keypoint trajectories extracted from videos*, bypassing the problematic intermediate step of explicit 3D motion reconstruction. By formulating motion imitation as a unified physics-based 2D motion tracking problem (reprojection error minimization under physical constraints), the authors address the depth ambiguity inherent in 2D data and the physical implausibility of reconstructed 3D data. This direct 2D-to-physics approach offers a significant paradigm shift, enhancing accessibility, generalizability, and realism for physically simulated characters in novel and challenging domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel and generic motion imitation framework, named Mimic2DM, that can effectively learn a wide range of complex, physics-based character skills—including challenging human-object interactions (HOI) and agile non-human (animal) locomotion—by relying *solely on readily available 2D motion data extracted from "in-the-wild" videos*.

The motivation behind pursuing this objective stems from several critical limitations and challenges in the existing landscape of physics-based character control:

- **Cost and Scarcity of 3D Motion Capture (MoCap) Data:** High-quality 3D MoCap data is the gold standard for training realistic physics-based controllers. However, its collection is notoriously expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive, requiring specialized equipment and skilled performers. This prohibitive cost significantly limits the scale and diversity of motions that can be captured, especially for niche applications like complex human-object interactions or animal movements, where 3D MoCap data is particularly scarce.

- **Limitations of 3D Reconstruction from Videos:** While videos offer an abundant alternative data source, current methods that first reconstruct 3D motions from videos before imitation suffer from significant drawbacks:
 - **Dependency on 3D Training Data:** Many advanced 3D human motion reconstruction techniques themselves require extensive high-quality 3D data for training, creating a circular dependency and limiting their applicability in domains where such 3D data is unavailable (e.g., specific HOI or non-human motions).
 - **Generalizability Issues:** These methods struggle to generalize to challenging scenarios beyond standard human locomotion, such as intricate interactions with objects or the diverse anatomies and movement patterns of non-human characters.
 - **Physical Implausibility:** The ill-posed nature of 2D-to-3D inversion means that reconstructed 3D motions often lack physical plausibility. They may exhibit artifacts like foot sliding, unnatural joint limits, or incorrect contact dynamics. This "flawed 3D supervision" severely hinders the subsequent physics-based motion imitation process, often leading to learning failures or requiring extensive post-processing.

- **Unexploited Potential of Direct 2D Supervision:** 2D keypoint data, easily extracted from videos, is unbiased and accurately reflects the original movements. However, the fundamental challenge of missing depth information has largely prevented its direct use for robust physics-based 3D control. While 2D priors combined with geometrical constraints can generate visually plausible 3D poses, these are often physically limited and insufficient for high-quality motion imitation.

Mimic2DM is motivated to overcome these challenges by proposing an end-to-end framework that directly leverages the accessibility and ubiquity of 2D video data. By explicitly integrating physical constraints within a 2D reprojection minimization task, the aim is to implicitly infer physically plausible 3D motions, thus bypassing the need for explicit, potentially flawed, 3D reconstruction and making physics-based character control significantly more accessible and versatile across a broader spectrum of applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mimic2DM introduces a novel motion imitation framework that operates directly on 2D keypoint trajectories extracted from videos. Its methodology can be broken down into three main components: the core imitation learning formulation, the design of a view-agnostic tracking policy, and a hierarchical control framework for generative tasks.

#### 3.1. Imitation as Reprojection Minimization

The foundational idea of Mimic2DM is to unify 3D motion reconstruction and physics-based motion imitation into a single problem. Instead of performing a separate, potentially flawed, 3D reconstruction step, the framework directly learns a policy $\pi$ that controls a simulated character to perform physically plausible 3D motions such that their 2D projection precisely aligns with a given 2D motion reference. This is formulated as a physics-based 2D motion tracking problem:

$\min_{\pi} E_{s_0 \sim d(s_0)} \|\text{P}_{\pi}(\text{C}) - X\| \text{ s.t. } f_{\pi} = 0,$

where $X$ is the 2D reference motion sequence, $\text{P}_{\pi}(\text{C})$ is the 2D projection of the 3D joint positions synthesized by the policy $\pi$ under camera view $\text{C}$, $d(s_0)$ is the initial state distribution, and $f_{\pi} = 0$ represents the physical constraints and underlying MDP dynamics of the simulated character. This end-to-end approach leverages physical constraints to regularize the resulting 3D motions, ensuring their physical plausibility despite the depth ambiguity of 2D inputs. The optimization is performed using reinforcement learning (RL).

#### 3.2. View-agnostic 2D Tracking Policy

To address the inherent depth ambiguity of single-view 2D inputs and enhance generalization, Mimic2DM proposes training a "view-agnostic" 2D tracking policy. This policy learns to minimize the reprojection error for a given reference motion under *arbitrary camera viewpoints*. The rationale is that diverse viewpoints in the training data collectively provide sufficient information for the policy to implicitly acquire a 3D understanding of motion.

- **Policy Representation and Observations:** The tracking policy is a neural network that takes as input the character’s proprioceptive state ($\mathbf{s}^{\text{prop}}_t$) and an observation of the future 2D motion reference ($\mathbf{o}^{\text{2D}}_t$). Crucially, the policy's observations explicitly *omit any camera view information*. This design choice forces the policy to infer the viewpoint of the 2D motion reference solely from the character’s actual 2D projection within the simulation, promoting view-agnostic learning.
 $\mathbf{o}^{\text{2D|C}}_t = [\text{P}(\mathbf{x}^{\text{3D}}_t, \text{C}), \mathbf{x}_{t:t+L}],$
 where $\text{P}(\mathbf{x}^{\text{3D}}_t, \text{C})$ is the 2D projection of the simulated character's 3D keypoints, and $\mathbf{x}_{t:t+L}$ is a clip of the future 2D reference motion.

- **View Aggregation for 3D Motion Tracking:** The trained single-view policy can be adapted for robust 3D motion tracking through a "view-aggregation" technique, without requiring any fine-tuning. For multi-view scenarios, where $K$ multi-view 2D motions {$\mathbf{X}^k, \text{C}^k\}^K_{k=1}$ are available, the aggregated feature $\mathbf{o}^{\text{agg}}_t$ is computed by averaging the feature embeddings of the 2D observations from all views:
 $\mathbf{o}^{\text{agg}}_t = \frac{1}{N} \sum^{N}_{i=1} \phi(\mathbf{o}^{\text{2D}}_i_t),$
 where $\phi(\cdot)$ is the feature extractor for single-view 2D observations. This mechanism effectively combines information from multiple perspectives, significantly enhancing the policy's 3D controllability.

#### 3.3. Training for Single-view Tracking

The training of the view-agnostic 2D tracking policy uses a reinforcement learning paradigm (specifically PPO, as noted in the supplementary materials) with three key adaptations for 2D motion:

- **2D Tracking Rewards:** The reward function encourages minimization of the reprojection error. It consists of a distance-based 2D tracking reward $r^{\text{p}}_t$ (exponentially scaled inverse of the sum of squared reprojection errors across keypoints) and an energy consumption penalty $r^{\text{e}}_t$ for regularization.
 $r^{\text{p}}_t = \exp\left(-\alpha \sum^J_{j=1} \|\text{P}(\mathbf{x}^{\text{3D|j}}_t) - \mathbf{x}^j_t\|\right)$

- **Adaptive State Initialization (ASI):** Since accurate 3D reference poses are unavailable, conventional Reference State Initialization (RSI) is not directly applicable. Mimic2DM proposes an adaptive strategy: it maintains a buffer of initial states for each reference frame. During training, states from policy rollouts that achieve a high critic score are stored in the buffer. Sampling probability for these states is exponentially proportional to their score, prioritizing exploration of physically plausible and high-performing initial configurations.

- **Reprojection-based Early Termination:** To maintain training efficiency and prevent the simulator from entering unrecoverable states, an episode is terminated if the character's projected pose deviates significantly (exceeds a threshold of 100 pixels) from the 2D reference pose.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical Control via 2D Interface

To extend the framework for generative tasks (e.g., novel motion synthesis), a hierarchical control structure is introduced. The trained 2D tracking policy is integrated with a kinematic 2D motion generative model, where 2D motions serve as the interface.

- **Canonical Representation of 2D Motion:** Raw 2D keypoint coordinates exhibit high variance due to global movement (translation, scale). To facilitate stable generative model training, a canonical representation is used, decomposing 2D motion into root translation ($\mathbf{x}_{\text{root}}$), a scale factor ($s$), and local pose ($\bar{\mathbf{x}}$). Relative changes ($\delta\mathbf{x}_{\text{root}}, \delta s$) are used for the canonical sequence.

- **2D Motion Tokenizer:** A Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) is employed to compress and discretize the canonical 2D motion sequences into compact tokens (codebook indices). An additional absolute 2D reconstruction term is added to the VQ-VAE loss to ensure global translation and scale consistency. Causal convolutional layers are used for autoregressive token detokenization.

- **Autoregressive Generator:** A transformer-based (GPT-like) autoregressive model is trained to predict the sequences of discrete motion tokens generated by the VQ-VAE. This architecture enables on-the-fly, infinite-length motion synthesis while preserving high-quality motion characteristics. It can perform unconditional or conditional generation.

- **Interface for Hierarchical Control:** The generated 2D motion sequences from the autoregressive generator (in global coordinates) serve as the reference for the view-agnostic 2D motion tracking policy. The policy's view-agnostic training allows for arbitrary viewpoint selection during this process, making the controller highly versatile.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Mimic2DM demonstrates compelling results across a diverse range of challenging motion imitation and generation tasks, highlighting its effectiveness and versatility.

- **Versatility and Effectiveness in Challenging Domains:** The framework successfully learns complex, physics-based skills from *solely 2D motion sequences extracted from "in-the-wild" videos*. This includes intricate human-object interactions (HOI) like skillful soccer dribbling and agile movements of non-human characters (e.g., robotic dogs), which are traditionally difficult due to the scarcity of 3D data and the complexity of their dynamics.

- **Superiority over Two-stage Approaches:** Quantitative and qualitative comparisons against adapted two-stage baselines (e.g., Sfv*, which first reconstructs 3D poses) reveal Mimic2DM's superior performance (Table 1, Figure 4). For instance, on soccer dribble and animal datasets, Mimic2DM achieves significantly higher success rates (e.g., 91.3% vs. 47.8% for soccer dribble), lower 2D tracking error, lower object 2D tracking error, and lower motion jitters (e.g., 1.69 vs. 2.62 for soccer dribble jitters). The baseline often struggles with complex ball-foot interactions and produces unnatural quadruped locomotion due to the inherent inaccuracies and physical implausibility of the reconstructed 3D training data.

- **Universal Tracking Capabilities and Implicit 3D Understanding:**
 - The view-agnostic 2D tracking policy exhibits robust generalization to unseen test and generated motions.
 - Crucially, when trained on diverse viewpoints and combined with the proposed view-aggregation mechanism, the policy *implicitly acquires a strong 3D understanding of motion*. This allows it to achieve 3D tracking accuracy comparable to methods trained directly on ground-truth 3D data, as shown in the evaluation on the AIST++ dataset (Table 2). For example, with 3-view 2D training, Mimic2DM achieves a 3D tracking error of 144.3 (on training data) or 161.5 (on test data), very close to the 3D-trained baseline's 114.5 and 141 respectively. This demonstrates that learning to satisfy 2D reprojection constraints from multiple viewpoints effectively resolves depth ambiguity and enables robust 3D control.

- **Generative Potential through Hierarchical Control:**
 - The hierarchical control framework, integrating the tracking policy with a 2D motion generator, effectively supports generative tasks. Qualitatively, it synthesizes complex trajectories, demonstrating natural transitions between different dribbling styles and seamless composition of directional movements (Figure 5).
 - The proposed autoregressive (AR) 2D motion generator outperforms diffusion-based models (Table 3) in producing high-quality 2D guidance for the tracking policy, achieving lower FID scores (e.g., 5.16 vs. 6.29 for Soccer Dribble) and higher success rates (e.g., 0.71 vs. 0.13) in the generative setup. This highlights the effectiveness of the chosen generative model architecture.

- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **View Diversity:** An ablation study confirms that training on diverse viewpoints is critical for learning a robust 3D understanding and natural behaviors. A policy trained on homogeneous viewpoints yields unnatural motions and fails to execute complex interactions, underscoring the importance of viewpoint diversity in the input data (Figure 6).
 - **Adaptive State Initialization (ASI):** The adaptive state initialization strategy is shown to be highly effective. It significantly improves training efficiency and policy convergence by adaptively updating and refining physically implausible initial states into more feasible ones, particularly important when high-quality 3D initial poses are unavailable (Figure 7, and the learning curve in Supplementary Figure 8).
 - **Multi-view Guidance (Inference):** Further analysis (Supplementary Figure 9) demonstrates that during inference, multi-view guidance resolves depth ambiguity that plagues single-view tracking, leading to more stable and physically plausible 3D poses and preventing artifacts like foot sliding.

In summary, Mimic2DM successfully demonstrates a novel paradigm for physics-based character control, leveraging the abundance of 2D video data to learn versatile, physically plausible, and diverse motions without relying on scarce 3D MoCap or unreliable 3D reconstruction. Its robust performance in both imitation and generation tasks, across varied and challenging domains, marks a significant advancement in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Mimic2DM framework represents a significant step forward in the field of physically-based character animation and simulated robotics, with several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

- **Democratization and Accessibility of Physics-based Control:** By eliminating the reliance on expensive and labor-intensive 3D motion capture (MoCap) data, Mimic2DM democratizes access to robust physics-based character control. Researchers, developers, and animators can now leverage readily available "in-the-wild" video data (e.g., YouTube, personal recordings) to train controllers, drastically reducing the barrier to entry for creating complex and realistic simulations. This will enable a broader community to engage with and contribute to this field.

- **Expansion to Novel and Challenging Domains:** The ability to learn directly from 2D data makes it feasible to develop controllers for domains where 3D MoCap data is virtually non-existent or extremely difficult to obtain. This includes:
 - **Human-Object Interaction (HOI):** Mimic2DM's success in learning intricate soccer dribbling skills demonstrates its potential for synthesizing highly dynamic and interactive behaviors that are crucial for virtual agents, robotics, and immersive simulations.
 - **Non-Human Characters:** The framework's adaptability to animal movements (e.g., robotic dogs) opens up new avenues for simulating realistic quadruped locomotion and other non-human behaviors, with applications in animatronics, game AI, and robotics (sim-to-real transfer).

- **Enhanced Realism and Physical Plausibility:** By unifying 3D reconstruction and physics-based imitation into a single reprojection minimization task, Mimic2DM inherently promotes physically plausible motions. The physical constraints regularize the learned 3D trajectories, ensuring that characters move in a way that respects biomechanical limits and environmental interactions, even when supervised only by 2D data. This leads to more believable and high-fidelity animations.

- **Foundation for Generative AI in Animation:** The integration of the 2D tracking policy with an autoregressive 2D motion generator forms a powerful hierarchical control framework. This enables the synthesis of novel, diverse, and physically plausible motions from higher-level commands or even unconditional generation. This has direct applications in:
 - **Content Creation:** Accelerating the production of realistic character animations for games, films, and virtual reality.
 - **Interactive Agents:** Developing more intelligent and versatile AI characters that can respond dynamically to environments and user inputs.
 - **Robotics:** Providing a robust pipeline for generating reference motions for humanoid and animal-like robots, bridging the gap between perception (2D video) and control (physics simulation).

- **Advancement in 2D-to-3D Understanding:** The research provides compelling evidence that a policy can implicitly learn a strong 3D understanding from purely 2D observations, especially when exposed to diverse viewpoints and leveraging view aggregation. This is a significant theoretical and practical contribution to the broader problem of 2D-to-3D inference in computer vision and graphics, suggesting new directions for training robust 3D models from more accessible 2D data.

- **Robustness and Scalability:** The adaptive state initialization and reprojection-based early termination strategies contribute to the robustness and efficiency of the reinforcement learning process, making the system more scalable for learning from large datasets of diverse videos.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the reliance on solely reprojection-error-based rewards for fine-grained contact dynamics and potential issues with inaccurate camera parameter estimation, Mimic2DM lays a strong foundation. Future work building upon this framework could further enhance its capabilities, ultimately leading to a new era of highly realistic, versatile, and easily controllable physically-simulated characters across numerous applications.
