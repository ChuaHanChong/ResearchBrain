# Learning to Control Physically-simulated 3D Characters via Generating and Mimicking 2D Motions

> [!summary] Summary
> Mimic2DM is a framework that learns to control physically simulated 3D characters from abundant 2D video data, bypassing explicit 3D reconstruction by formulating motion imitation as a physics-based 2D motion tracking problem. The system enables characters to perform complex human-object interactions and animal locomotion, outperforming two-stage methods and exhibiting implicit 3D understanding from diverse 2D viewpoints.

## Problem

- The high cost and scarcity of 3D motion capture (MoCap) data limit the diversity and scalability of physics-based character control.
- Existing methods that reconstruct 3D motions from videos prior to imitation often produce physically implausible 3D trajectories, hindering robust policy learning.
- Directly leveraging abundant 2D video data for robust 3D control is challenging due to the inherent depth ambiguity of single-view inputs.

## Method

- Formulates motion imitation as an end-to-end physics-based 2D motion tracking problem, minimizing reprojection error under physical constraints, thereby unifying 3D reconstruction and imitation.
- Trains a 'view-agnostic' 2D tracking policy using reinforcement learning, designed to minimize reprojection error across arbitrary camera viewpoints without explicit camera information, fostering implicit 3D understanding.
- Introduces a hierarchical control framework that integrates the 2D tracking policy with a kinematic 2D motion generative model for novel motion synthesis and generative tasks.

## Results

- Successfully controls physically simulated characters to perform complex human-object interactions (e.g., soccer dribbling with **91.3%** success rate) and agile non-human locomotion solely from in-the-wild 2D video data.
- Achieves superior performance over two-stage baselines (3D reconstruction then imitation), demonstrating lower 2D tracking error and reduced motion jitters (e.g., **1.69** vs. **2.62** for soccer dribble jitters).
- Exhibits robust 3D tracking accuracy (e.g., **144.3** error on AIST++ training data with 3-view 2D training) closely matching methods trained directly on 3D data, and the hierarchical framework synthesizes novel, physically plausible motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physically plausible 3D character control can be learned directly from diverse 2D keypoint trajectories by leveraging physical constraints, implicitly resolving depth ambiguity and avoiding potentially flawed 3D reconstructions.
> - Training a 2D tracking policy with diverse camera viewpoints enables it to acquire a strong implicit 3D understanding of motion, achieving 3D tracking accuracy comparable to methods trained directly on ground-truth 3D data.
> - Adaptive State Initialization (ASI) significantly improves RL training efficiency and policy convergence for 2D-supervised tasks where accurate 3D initial poses are unavailable.
