# DeepPerception: Advancing R1-like Cognitive Visual Perception in MLLMs for Knowledge-Intensive Visual Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepPerception introduces an MLLM-based framework to advance R1-like cognitive visual perception for knowledge-intensive visual grounding through a two-stage training process. The framework achieves an average accuracy of 62.20% on the new KVG-Bench, an 8.08% improvement over direct fine-tuning, and establishes new state-of-the-art results on fine-grained visual recognition tasks.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs lack fine-grained visual discrimination when tasks require deep domain-specific knowledge and structured cognitive reasoning.
- Existing MLLMs tend to provide direct answers without integrating their vast knowledge bases with perceptual features through a step-by-step cognitive analysis.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, knowledge-aligned training data suitable for developing advanced cognitive visual perception in MLLMs.

## Method

- Defined the ==Knowledge-Intensive Visual Grounding (KVG)== task and introduced ==KVG-Bench==, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating cognitive visual perception.
- Developed DeepPerception, an MLLM-based framework with a two-stage cognitive training process combining ==Chain-of-Thought SFT (CoT-SFT)== and ==Perception-oriented Reinforcement Learning (GRPO)==.
- Implemented an ==automated data synthesis pipeline== to generate knowledge-aligned training samples, including composite images, to overcome data scarcity for KVG.

## Results

- DeepPerception achieved **62.20%** average accuracy on KVG-Bench, an **8.08%** increase over direct fine-tuning, and surpassed **70B**-scale MLLMs using a **7B**-scale base model.
- The framework demonstrated strong cross-domain generalization with **60.85%** accuracy on unseen categories and established state-of-the-art performance on standard Fine-Grained Visual Recognition tasks (**83.21%** average accuracy).
- General multimodal understanding and reasoning abilities were preserved, as shown by comparable performance to the base model on benchmarks like MMBench and MMMU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating cognitive reasoning processes (planning, analysis, comparison, conclusion) into MLLM training significantly enhances their ability to perform fine-grained visual discrimination.
> - A two-stage framework, combining CoT-SFT for establishing cognitive chains and GRPO for refining perceptual accuracy, effectively optimizes the synergy between MLLM perception and cognition.
> - Automated data synthesis, especially through composite image generation with visually similar entities, is an effective strategy to create data for knowledge-intensive visual tasks, reducing reliance on manual annotation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has demonstrated capabilities in tasks such as visual grounding and fine-grained visual recognition. These models typically integrate visual and textual information to localize objects within images based on linguistic descriptions. While MLLMs exhibit rich internal entity knowledge and strong generic visual grounding abilities, this research identifies a limitation: their performance often diminishes when confronted with specialized, domain-specific concepts that require fine-grained discrimination among visually similar entities.

Conventional visual grounding (VG) benchmarks, such as RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, primarily evaluate the ability to ground objects using generic referring expressions. More recent benchmarks like SK-VG and ReasonSeg have incorporated scene-level or world knowledge. However, these benchmarks do not specifically address the challenge of grounding fine-grained, subordinate-category entities using precise, domain-specific terminology, particularly when images contain multiple visually similar distractors.

This paper introduces "Knowledge-Intensive Visual Grounding (KVG)" to characterize this specific challenge. KVG emphasizes the integration of fine-grained entity knowledge with visual perception for accurate spatial localization. The work suggests that existing MLLMs, despite their advancements, suffer from a "knowledge–grounding gap" where their internal knowledge does not reliably translate into accurate grounding predictions for specialized concepts. This gap differs from issues of reasoning depth, which have been explored by approaches like UniVG-R1, Perception-R1, and Visual-RFT that use reinforcement learning to enhance general reasoning for grounding. Instead, this research focuses on the alignment between entity-level knowledge utilization and grounding decisions. By proposing a new task, benchmark, and training paradigm, this work positions itself as an effort to bridge this specific knowledge–grounding gap, thereby pushing MLLMs toward more expert-level visual perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an MLLM training paradigm that effectively addresses the "knowledge–grounding gap" observed in current models when performing Knowledge-Intensive Visual Grounding (KVG). This gap manifests as a discrepancy where MLLMs might correctly identify a specialized entity (e.g., "Clumber Spaniel") but fail to accurately localize it within an image, especially when visually similar distractors are present.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
- **Human Expert Behavior:** Human experts demonstrate superior domain-specific visual perception by flexibly leveraging specialized domain knowledge to refine discriminative features. Current MLLMs often lack this capability for fine-grained concepts.
- **MLLM Limitations:** While MLLMs possess substantial fine-grained knowledge and strong generic visual grounding capabilities, their performance substantially declines when required to ground specialized concepts (e.g., specific aircraft models, animal breeds). This suggests that internal entity knowledge is not consistently operationalized during grounding predictions.
- **Challenge of KVG:** The KVG task, as defined by the authors, requires models to localize objects using fine-grained, domain-specific entity names (e.g., "Boeing 747" instead of "aircraft"). Images in KVG typically contain multiple subordinate-category entities that are visually similar, necessitating knowledge-guided differentiation. This setup demands not only recognition of visual patterns but also precise alignment of domain-level knowledge with spatial evidence selection.
- **Ineffectiveness of Current Approaches:** Existing methods, including simply prompting MLLMs with step-by-step reasoning (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) or employing general reasoning-guided visual grounding frameworks, have not consistently yielded significant improvements on KVG. This indicates that the core difficulty is not merely insufficient reasoning depth but a fundamental misalignment between fine-grained entity knowledge and grounding decisions.

To overcome these limitations, the researchers aim to develop a training paradigm that explicitly encourages MLLMs to activate and integrate domain-relevant entity knowledge into the visual grounding process, thereby enhancing the alignment between knowledge utilization and grounding predictions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a two-stage knowledge-aware training framework, referred to as KARL, which integrates knowledge-guided reasoning with knowledge-aware reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Knowledge-Guided Grounding Data Construction (Stage 1)

The initial stage focuses on creating training data that encourages MLLMs to explicitly utilize internal entity knowledge for visual grounding, aiming to narrow the knowledge–grounding gap.

- **Multi-Entity Grounding Data Synthesis:**
 - **Source Data:** The training data is synthesized from five fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) datasets: FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford-Cars, iNaturalist2017 (for Reptilia and Aves), and Food101. These datasets provide entity annotations for five categories (Aircraft, Car, Reptilia, Bird, Food).
 - **Box Annotation:** Since original FGVR datasets lack bounding box annotations, a two-step procedure is employed. First, Qwen2-VL-7B is used to generate initial bounding boxes based on entity-specific prompts. Second, these predictions are selectively refined using SAM3, particularly for categories where initial quality is lower (e.g., food category). This process results in pseudo-annotations with over 95% accuracy on a random subset.
 - **Image Composition:** To increase grounding difficulty and prevent shortcut learning, composite images are synthesized. Each composite image contains 2 to 6 distinct fine-grained entities from the same category. Various layout strategies (horizontal/vertical concatenation, grid, random) are used, and bounding boxes are geometrically transformed. This ensures models must perform fine-grained discrimination rather than relying on coarse memorization. The synthesized training data is kept disjoint from KVG-Bench.
- **Knowledge-Guided Reasoning Generation (CoT-SFT):**
 - **Rationale Generation:** High-capacity MLLMs (Qwen2-VL-72B and Qwen3-VL-32B) serve as teacher models to generate knowledge-guided Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales. The teachers receive an image, target entity label, and ground-truth bounding box, along with a prompt instructing them to produce step-by-step reasoning before outputting the grounding result.
 - **Content of Rationales:** The prompts encourage explicit referencing of entity-related knowledge, including comparisons of fine-grained visual attributes, distinguishing characteristics among visually similar entities, and verification of these attributes against visual evidence.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (CoT-SFT):** The base MLLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) is then fine-tuned using these CoT rationales. This stage aims to provide a reasoning-aware initialization that integrates entity knowledge into the grounding process, serving as a stable foundation for the subsequent reinforcement learning.

#### 3.2. Knowledge-Aware Reinforcement Learning (Stage 2)

Following CoT-SFT, the model is further optimized using reinforcement learning, with a specific focus on adapting to the heterogeneous knowledge mastery across different entities.

- **Entity Knowledge Estimation:**
 - **Method:** For each entity in the training data, a knowledge mastery score is estimated. This is done by formulating a single-entity recognition task: randomly sampling up to 50 images containing the entity and converting them into four-choice multiple-choice questions. Distractors are drawn from semantically similar categories.
 - **Knowledge Levels:** The model's average accuracy on these questions determines the entity's knowledge mastery score. Entities are then assigned to one of five discrete knowledge levels (completely correct, mostly correct, partially correct, mostly incorrect, completely incorrect) based on these scores. This analysis revealed a skewed distribution, with a significant majority of entities falling into low-knowledge categories, underscoring the need for differentiated optimization.
- **Knowledge-Aware Optimization:**
 - **Reward Design:** The reinforcement learning reward combines a knowledge-aware Intersection over Union (IoU) reward (R_KA-IoU) and a format reward (R_format).
 - **R_KA-IoU:** This reward evaluates the spatial alignment between the predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes, but critically, it modulates the reward signal based on the entity's estimated knowledge level (k_e). Entities with higher knowledge levels receive smaller positive scaling for correct predictions and stronger negative scaling for incorrect predictions. Conversely, low-knowledge entities receive larger positive scaling for correct predictions and weaker negative scaling for incorrect predictions. This aims to balance learning pressure across entities.
 - **R_format:** A regular expression-based reward encourages the model to produce outputs with the expected structural format, including specific tags for reasoning traces (<think>, </think>) and final grounding predictions (<answer>, </answer> with a valid bounding box format).
 - **Optimization Algorithm:** The policy is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a method designed for enhancing reasoning in large language models.
- **Data Filtering:** Prior to Stage 2 reinforcement learning, the training data is filtered using the Stage-1 CoT-SFT model. Instances that are either trivially easy (4/4 correct predictions with stochastic sampling) or excessively difficult (0/4 correct predictions) are removed. Only samples with mixed outcomes (1-3 correct predictions) are retained, focusing the RL on instances where the model can still learn effectively (approximately 2K instances).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding KARL's performance on KVG-Bench and its general capabilities, supported by ablation studies and analyses.

#### 4.1. KVG-Bench Introduction and Human Evaluation

The authors introduce KVG-Bench, a benchmark designed for Knowledge-Intensive Visual Grounding. It comprises 1,336 test cases, 531 images, and 882 distinct entities across 10 diverse domains. Images in KVG-Bench frequently feature multiple subordinate-category entities, necessitating fine-grained visual discrimination. The benchmark is split into "seen" (5 categories) and "unseen" (5 held-out categories) to evaluate both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization. Human evaluation revealed that performance increased substantially from a "Closed-Book" setting (56.41% accuracy) to an "Open-Book" setting (78.83% accuracy), indicating that expert-level knowledge is crucial for the task.

#### 4.2. Overall Performance on KVG-Bench

(Refer to Table 1 in the original paper)
- **Superior Performance:** KARL consistently achieved the highest overall accuracy (68.41%) on KVG-Bench, outperforming a wide range of baseline models, including large-scale MLLMs, specialist grounding models, and other reasoning-guided MLLMs.
- **Seen Categories:** On categories present in the training data, KARL achieved 67.30%, surpassing strong 7B-scale MLLMs like Qwen3-VL-8B (56.94%) and even larger models such as Qwen3-VL-32B (63.38%). This suggests that explicit utilization of fine-grained entity knowledge can yield improvements beyond model scaling alone.
- **Unseen Categories (Generalization):** KARL demonstrated robust cross-domain generalization, achieving 70.04% on unseen categories. This performance significantly exceeded that of large-scale MLLMs and other reasoning-guided grounding models (e.g., UniVG-R1 at 58.46% and Visual-RFT at 38.24%). The improvements were observed across diverse semantic domains (e.g., Dog: 85.60%, Flower: 80.90%, Mammal: 69.64%).
- **Limitations of Generic Reasoning:** The study found that generic reasoning strategies, such as zero-shot CoT prompting or existing reasoning-guided grounding methods (e.g., Visual-RFT, UniVG-R1), did not consistently improve performance on KVG, with some even performing lower than their base models. This observation reinforces the premise that generic reasoning is insufficient for knowledge-intensive grounding.

#### 4.3. General Capability Preservation

(Refer to Table 2 in the original paper)
Evaluations on established general multimodal benchmarks (MMStar, RealWorldQA, AI2D, CV-Bench) indicated that KARL maintained performance comparable to the base Qwen3-VL-8B model. This suggests that the KVG-oriented training paradigm did not degrade the model's general multimodal capabilities.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

(Refer to Table 3 and 4 in the original paper)
- **Effectiveness of CoT-SFT (Stage 1):** Replacing standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Knowledge-Guided Reasoning (CoT-SFT) in Stage 1 led to a performance improvement on seen categories (from 61.49% to 67.05%). This indicates that explicit knowledge-guided reasoning supervision provides a more effective initialization.
- **Impact of Reinforcement Learning (Stage 2):** Both standard GRPO and KARL, when initialized from the CoT-SFT model, showed performance improvements over continued CoT-SFT alone. This highlights the benefit of reinforcement learning for refining grounding predictions.
- **KARL vs. GRPO:** KARL achieved comparable performance to standard GRPO on seen categories (67.30% vs. 67.29%) but significantly improved performance on unseen categories (70.04% vs. 66.36%). This result supports the hypothesis that knowledge-aware reward scaling helps rebalance optimization across entities with varied knowledge levels, leading to better generalization.
- **Reward Scaling Strategies:**
 - Removing negative reward scaling ("w/o Neg.") degraded performance on both seen (67.30% to 66.67%) and unseen (70.04% to 66.91%) categories, with a more pronounced decline on unseen categories. This indicates the importance of knowledge-guided penalties in avoiding spurious correlations and enhancing generalization.
 - Removing positive reward scaling ("w/o Pos.") resulted in a substantial decrease in unseen accuracy (70.04% to 66.36%), highlighting the role of reinforcing correct predictions for generalization.
 - Comparing KARL to vanilla GRPO ("w/o Pos. & Neg."), which uses fixed rewards, showed a notable degradation in unseen performance (70.04% to 66.36%). This confirms that the combined, adaptive positive and negative knowledge-aware scaling is crucial for achieving robust generalization beyond simple memorization.

#### 4.5. Knowledge-Level Analysis

(Refer to Figure 4 in the original paper)
Dividing entities into "high knowledge" and "low knowledge" groups based on estimated mastery, KARL demonstrated a significantly larger performance gain on low-knowledge entities (+2.10% over GRPO) compared to high-knowledge entities (where the gain was nearly nine times smaller). This suggests that the knowledge-aware reward scaling is particularly effective in enabling more efficient learning for concepts where the model initially possesses less mastery.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results

(Refer to Figure 5 in the original paper)
A case study illustrated KARL's structured reasoning process. For instance, in grounding a "Mississippi kite," KARL followed a pipeline of planning, detailed visual analysis, comparison of discriminative features (e.g., tail shape, coloration), and a conclusion, leading to an accurate bounding box. This contrasts with a baseline MLLM that made an incorrect prediction, suggesting it relied on superficial pattern recognition rather than integrated knowledge.

#### 4.7. Quantitative Analysis of the Knowledge-Grounding Gap (Appendix C.3)

(Refer to Table 8 in the original paper)
A quantitative analysis comparing the model's performance on a multiple-choice referring expression generation (REG) task (probing entity-level knowledge) versus the original referring expression comprehension (REC) grounding task on KVG-Bench instances provided direct evidence for the knowledge-grounding gap. The results showed that 22.90% of cases were correctly answered on the REG task but incorrectly grounded on the REC task. This proportion was substantially higher than the inverse (11.08%), where grounding was correct but knowledge prediction was wrong. This asymmetry indicates that MLLMs often possess the relevant entity knowledge but fail to translate it into accurate visual localization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Knowledge-Intensive Visual Grounding (KVG) as a distinct and challenging task, accompanied by the KVG-Bench benchmark. This contribution is significant as it provides a standardized evaluation framework to systematically assess MLLMs' ability to leverage fine-grained, domain-specific entity knowledge for precise visual localization, thereby highlighting a critical limitation in current models.

The identification and quantitative analysis of the "knowledge–grounding gap" represent a key insight. The finding that MLLMs often possess the necessary entity knowledge but struggle to effectively apply it during grounding decisions provides a targeted area for future research and development.

The proposed KARL framework offers a methodological contribution through its two-stage knowledge-aware training paradigm. The first stage, using knowledge-guided reasoning data construction and CoT-SFT, provides a strong initialization that encourages models to explicitly activate and align entity-level knowledge with visual evidence. This approach differs from prior reasoning-guided grounding methods by focusing specifically on entity-level knowledge integration rather than generic reasoning depth. The second stage, Knowledge-Aware Reinforcement Learning (KARL), further refines this capability by dynamically modulating optimization signals based on estimated entity-level knowledge mastery. This adaptive reward scaling is shown to promote more balanced optimization across entities with heterogeneous knowledge levels, leading to improved generalization, particularly on previously unseen categories and low-knowledge entities.

The demonstration of KARL's superior performance on KVG-Bench, especially in cross-domain generalization, while maintaining general multimodal capabilities, suggests a promising direction for developing MLLMs that are more capable of expert-level perception in specialized domains. The insights from this work could impact applications requiring precise object localization based on fine-grained concepts, such as scientific image analysis, medical imaging, specialized industrial inspections, or complex robotic interaction with specific objects. The release of the data, code, and models fosters reproducibility and further research in this emerging area.
