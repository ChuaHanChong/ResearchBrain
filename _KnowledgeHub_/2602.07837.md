# RLinf-USER: A Unified and Extensible System for Real-World Online Policy Learning in Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> The USER system provides a unified and extensible framework for real-world online policy learning in embodied AI, integrating a hardware abstraction layer, adaptive cloud-edge communication, an asynchronous learning pipeline, and a persistent data buffer. This infrastructure significantly boosts training efficiency, achieving up to 5.70x faster training throughput and reducing policy convergence time from over 8000 seconds to approximately 1500 seconds on manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Real-world online policy learning for embodied AI is hindered by the "sim-to-real gap" and the inability to scale data collection, resets, or replication like in simulators.
- Existing systems lack comprehensive orchestration for multi-robot, distributed learning, struggling with complex cloud-edge communication across heterogeneous network domains and relying on inefficient synchronous pipelines.
- Managing long-horizon, high-dimensional data in physical environments is problematic due to the lack of persistent storage, robust crash recovery, and efficient data reuse mechanisms.

## Method

- Implements a unified ==hardware abstraction layer== to manage heterogeneous robots and computational accelerators as first-class, schedulable resources.
- Develops an adaptive communication plane featuring ==UDP tunneling== for cross-domain networking, distributed data channels, and ==SM-aware weight synchronization== for efficient cloud-edge data exchange.
- Designs a fully ==asynchronous learning pipeline== with a persistent-cache-aware buffer and extensible interfaces for policies, algorithms, and rewards, enabling continuous robot operation and robust data management.

## Results

- Demonstrated high success rates on real-world manipulation tasks, with RLPD, SAC, and SAC-Flow achieving near-perfect scores within **2000 seconds**, and HG-DAgger fine-tuning a VLA model to **96%** success in **30 minutes** with ~200 online samples.
- Achieved efficient scaling for multi-robot training and successfully trained a unified policy for heterogeneous robots (Franka and ARX arms) on a button-pressing task, converging in approximately **two hours**.
- The asynchronous pipeline accelerated training convergence from over **8000 seconds** to around **1500 seconds** and improved data generation and training throughput by up to **1.55x** and **5.70x**, respectively, while distributed channels reduced cross-domain communication overhead by **3x**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective real-world online learning requires treating robots as schedulable hardware resources and abstracting heterogeneous deployments for unified management and scalable training.
> - Decoupling real-world data generation from training via asynchronous pipelines is essential for maximizing robot utilization and addressing the physical world's real-time constraints, accelerating learning convergence.
> - Robust cloud-edge communication, utilizing tunneling and distributed channels, is fundamental for deploying large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in geographically dispersed and challenging network environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied intelligence research has predominantly relied on training policies in high-parallel simulators, followed by deployment in the physical world. This simulation-centric approach, while enabling rapid iteration and massive data collection, is frequently hampered by the "sim-to-real gap." Discrepancies in dynamics, sensing, and interaction between simulation and reality often lead to performance degradation when policies are transferred to physical robots.

This limitation has motivated a growing interest in real-world online policy learning, where policies are trained directly on physical robots. However, current systems face significant challenges that differ fundamentally from simulation environments. Unlike simulators, real-world systems cannot be arbitrarily accelerated, cheaply reset, or massively replicated. This restricts data collection scalability, complicates deployments across diverse robot platforms, and hinders long-horizon training processes that are prone to interruptions and require robust data management.

Existing robot learning systems, such as those based on ROS2 and Zenoh, primarily offer connectivity but lack comprehensive learning orchestration. More specialized systems like SERL and Qt-Opt have typically focused on single-robot or small-model scenarios. They often employ synchronous pipelines, which are inefficient in real-world settings where physical interaction speed is a bottleneck, leading to robots pausing while waiting for training updates. Furthermore, the increasing adoption of large vision-language-action (VLA) models necessitates cloud-edge infrastructure, introducing complex communication challenges across heterogeneous and isolated network domains, characterized by bandwidth asymmetry and latency. Data management in these contexts is also challenged by the need for persistent, long-horizon data storage that can handle high-dimensional visual streams and support robust crash recovery and data reuse.

USER positions itself within this landscape by addressing these system-level challenges. It proposes a unified and extensible system that treats real-world online policy learning as a fundamental systems problem, rather than solely an algorithmic one. By abstracting heterogeneous hardware, optimizing cloud-edge communication, employing asynchronous learning pipelines, and developing a persistent data management system, USER aims to provide a robust infrastructure that facilitates scalable, efficient, and flexible real-world online policy learning, particularly for multi-robot fleets and large models. This work seeks to bridge the gap between algorithmic advances and the practical requirements of deploying and training embodied AI directly in physical environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified and extensible system, named USER (Unified and extensible SystEm for Real-world online policy learning), designed to overcome the system-level challenges inherent in direct physical world policy learning for embodied intelligence. The motivations stem from several identified limitations of current approaches:

1. **Challenges of Real-World Operation vs. Simulation:** Unlike simulation environments that allow for arbitrary acceleration, cheap resets, and massive replication, physical robots operate in real-time. This fundamental difference makes scalable data collection, deployment across heterogeneous robot platforms, and effective long-horizon training particularly difficult. The paper posits that these are not merely algorithmic issues but fundamentally system-level problems.

2. **Resource Management and Scheduling:** Current approaches often treat physical robots as external environments, complicating the joint scheduling and management of robots alongside computational accelerators (like GPUs) in distributed learning setups. This siloed view hinders efficient resource allocation and orchestration, especially for large-scale deployments involving multiple, diverse robots.

3. **Cloud-Edge Communication Bottlenecks:** The increasing complexity of embodied learning, particularly with large VLA models, necessitates cloud-edge infrastructures where data collection (rollout) occurs at the edge and training in the cloud. Such setups involve heterogeneous and often isolated network domains (e.g., NATs, campus networks, factory VLANs), leading to significant bandwidth asymmetry and cross-domain latency. Existing communication paradigms often struggle with these conditions, hindering stable and efficient data exchange.

4. **Data Efficiency and Asynchronous Execution:** Since the physical world cannot be accelerated, data collection is often the primary bottleneck in real-world online learning. Synchronous learning pipelines, common in simulation, lead to poor learning efficiency as robots may stall while waiting for training updates. There is a need for asynchronous frameworks that allow robots to operate continuously.

5. **Data Management for Long-Horizon Learning:** Embodied learning increasingly involves high-dimensional visual streams and long-duration experiments, generating massive volumes of data. Traditional memory-centric replay buffers are typically short-lived and lack mechanisms for persistence, robust crash recovery, and efficient reuse of historical data across long-running experiments or multiple training phases. This limits the ability to conduct experiments spanning days or weeks and to leverage valuable past experiences.

6. **Extensibility for Diverse Learning Paradigms:** The field of embodied AI is rapidly evolving with diverse policy architectures (e.g., CNN, MLP, generative, VLA models), learning algorithms (e.g., RL, imitation learning, human-in-the-loop), and reward mechanisms (e.g., rule-based, human feedback, learned models). Current systems often require significant re-engineering to support new combinations, hindering research flexibility.

To address these motivations, USER aims to provide:
- A unified abstraction that integrates physical robots as first-class hardware resources.
- An adaptive communication plane to manage data flow across cloud-edge domains.
- A fully asynchronous learning framework to maximize robot utilization and data throughput.
- A persistent, cache-aware buffer for robust, long-horizon data management.
- Extensible interfaces for policies, algorithms, and rewards within a unified pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

USER’s methodology is structured around a two-pronged design: a system architecture that virtualizes and connects heterogeneous resources, and a learning framework that organizes data and computation for real-world online policy learning.

#### 3.1. System Architecture Design

The system architecture aims to decouple learning logic from physical deployment specifics, enabling unified resource management and reliable data exchange in distributed, multi-robot environments.

**A. Unified Hardware Abstraction Layer (HAL)**
USER treats physical robots and accelerators (GPUs, TPUs) as first-class, schedulable hardware resources.
- **Node and Hardware Abstraction:** A deployment consists of nodes, each exporting hardware units. A unit is the smallest schedulable entity (e.g., a single GPU, a robot with peripherals). Units are described by typed descriptors and configuration metadata. Nodes can be heterogeneous, hosting different hardware types. Nodes are grouped (e.g., rollout, robot, training) to facilitate scheduling over homogeneous pools within a heterogeneous cluster.
- **Hardware Registration and Discovery:** A pluggable checker interface allows for extending new hardware types. HAL checkers define type identifiers, discovery methods (automatic for PCIe/USB devices, configuration-driven for IP-bound robots), and metadata attachment. At initialization, a global hardware inventory is constructed by probing nodes and validating hardware (e.g., network reachability, health checks for robots).
- **Hardware Scheduling:** Resources are scheduled via a single rank-based placement interface. Components select node groups and resource ranks, which the scheduler deterministically maps to specific hardware units. Processes are launched with explicit bindings to their assigned hardware, enabling heterogeneous placements within a single job.

**B. Adaptive Communication Plane**
This plane addresses communication challenges in cloud-edge distributed deployments, especially for VLA models.
- **Tunneling-based Cloud-Edge Networking:** USER establishes bidirectional connections across distinct administrative network domains (e.g., behind NATs, different campus networks) using UDP tunneling. Ray is used for control plane (cluster membership, worker placement), and TCP rendezvous for bootstrapping point-to-point data plane communication. All traffic is bound to the tunnel interface to ensure robustness against multi-homed hosts and network segmentation.
- **Distributed Data Channel:** Data exchange is abstracted by named, First-In-First-Out (FIFO) producer-consumer queues. These channels are hosted by lightweight channel services and accessed via asynchronous put/get APIs. Critically, channels are internally sharded based on data keys (e.g., robot IDs) to localize traffic within edge or cloud regions, reducing unnecessary cross-domain transfers. This design supports multiple producers and consumers, allowing robots and rollout nodes to stream data without synchronous coupling.
- **SM-Aware Weight Synchronization:** When using NVIDIA Collective Communications Library (NCCL) for GPU-to-GPU weight synchronization, the process consumes streaming multiprocessor (SM) resources, potentially degrading GPU performance for other tasks (e.g., policy inference). USER introduces a tunable configuration to cap the maximum number of NCCL Cooperative Thread Arrays (CTAs) used during weight transfer. This throttling mechanism prevents synchronization from monopolizing GPU resources, sustaining stable, low-latency rollouts under asynchronous weight updates.

#### 3.2. Learning Framework Design

Built upon the system architecture, the learning framework dictates how data and computation are organized during runtime.

**A. Fully Asynchronous Pipeline**
Recognizing data collection as the primary bottleneck in real-world learning, USER decouples data generation from training to ensure continuous robot operation.
- **Data Generation:** Multiple environment workers execute policies on physical robots via rollout workers, continuously streaming observations and actions. Human operators can intervene via teleoperation for corrections or demonstrations. A reward worker assigns supervision (rule-based, human-provided, or learned).
- **Data Ingestion:** All interaction data, including autonomous rollouts and human interventions, are asynchronously ingested into a persistent-cache-aware buffer.
- **Training and Synchronization:** Learning workers asynchronously sample mini-batches from these buffers to update model parameters, supporting both reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL). Updated weights are periodically synchronized back to rollout workers, closing the loop without interrupting robot execution.

**B. Persistent-Cache-Aware Buffer**
To support long-horizon, data-intensive learning with non-stationary policies and handle real-world interruptions, USER employs a persistent, index-based buffer.
- **Persistence:** Trajectories are asynchronously written to disk, allowing for arbitrarily large datasets and retaining historical data across evolving policies. The buffer stores lightweight indices with metadata (policy version, timestamps, episode IDs) to enable temporal and policy-aware sampling.
- **Caching:** A bounded in-memory cache with FIFO replacement handles new samples. When the cache is full, old entries are evicted to disk but remain indexed. Evicted samples are transparently reloaded when requested, balancing high-throughput sampling with large storage capacity and robustness against failures.

**C. Extensible Policies, Algorithms, and Rewards**
USER provides unified interfaces for diverse components, sharing a single execution and data pipeline.
- **Policies:** Supports lightweight models (CNN/MLP controllers, flow-matching policies) and large-scale VLA models (e.g., π0, π0.5). All are deployed through a unified rollout abstraction.
- **Algorithms:** Includes off-policy RL (SAC), sample-efficient RL for flow policies (SAC-Flow), human-in-the-loop methods (RLPD), and imitation-style updates for large models (HG-DAgger). Training workers interact via standardized sampling and update APIs.
- **Rewards:** Modular specification supports rule-based rewards, human-provided labels, and learned reward models. Rewards can be attached during rollout or computed offline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of USER was conducted across five groups of experiments in both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating the functionality and benefits of its core components.

**A. Extensibility of the Learning Framework**
Experiments were performed on five real-world manipulation tasks (Peg Insertion, Charger Plugging, Cap Tightening, Pick-and-Place, Table Clean-up) using a Franka robotic arm. Diverse policies (CNN, flow-based, π0 VLA model), algorithms (SAC, RLPD, SAC-Flow, HG-DAgger), and reward sources (rule-based, human-provided, learned models) were integrated.
- **RL Performance:** RLPD, SAC, and SAC-Flow achieved high success rates (near-perfect) on Peg-Insertion and Charger Plugging tasks within approximately 2000 seconds. RLPD also showed strong performance on Cap Tightening and Pick and Place, reaching success rates approaching 1.0. SAC's performance on Charger Plugging was noted to be worse, possibly due to the task's high precision requirement and dense rewards.
- **Imitation Learning with VLA Models:** HG-DAgger, used for online fine-tuning of the π0 VLA model, demonstrated significant performance gains. For Pick-and-Place, it achieved a 96% success rate in approximately 30 minutes with around 200 online samples, starting from a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) initialization.
- **Reward Models:** A learned reward model (binary classifier with ResNet18 backbone) provided supervision for the Peg Insertion task comparable to human labels, suggesting its potential for automated annotation.

**B. Advantages of a Unified Hardware Layer**
The unified hardware abstraction was evaluated for multi-robot and heterogeneous robot training.
- **Multi-Robot Training:** Two Franka robot arms concurrently trained policies for different manipulation tasks. Both tasks converged within approximately 2500 seconds, matching single-robot baselines, indicating effective scaling of real-world training through parallel data collection without degrading stability.
- **Heterogeneous Robot Training:** A unified CNN-based policy was trained to control a 7-DoF Franka arm and a 6-DoF low-cost ARX robotic arm on a multi-colored button-pressing task. The policy successfully converged after about two hours, demonstrating cross-embodiment learning despite variations in arm DoF, end-effector morphology, and camera parameters.

**C. Capability of the Communication Plane**
The adaptive communication plane was tested under cross-domain and same-domain network settings.
- **Cross-Domain Communication:** In a cloud-edge setup (master node in City A, rollout/robot nodes in City B), distributed data channels significantly reduced the communication overhead for transmitting observations and actions between the edge nodes. This resulted in a ~3x reduction in total episode generation time (from ~69s to ~22s) compared to a centralized baseline, while local rollout and interaction times remained constant.
- **Same-Domain Communication:** Even within a high-speed local network, distributed channels consistently reduced communication overhead, though the magnitude of reduction was less pronounced than in cross-domain scenarios.
- **Efficiency Comparison:** The cross-domain deployment, when utilizing distributed channels, achieved communication efficiency comparable to the all-same-domain setup, indicating effective exploitation of local high-bandwidth links.

**D. Validation for Persistent and Cache-aware Buffer**
The buffer's performance was analyzed regarding sampling throughput and capacity.
- **Throughput vs. Cache Ratio:** Increasing the ratio of in-memory cache size to total buffer size (r = c/s) improved sampling throughput.
- **Throughput vs. Buffer Size:** A pure in-memory buffer yielded the highest throughput but was memory-limited. A purely disk-based buffer offered large capacity but less than half the throughput. USER's persistent-cache-aware design demonstrated a balance, achieving higher throughput than standard disk buffers while retaining large capacity.

**E. Asynchronous Design**
The benefits of the fully asynchronous pipeline were demonstrated by profiling and convergence studies.
- **Throughput Improvement:** Compared to synchronous pipelines, USER's asynchronous pipeline improved generation throughput by 1.20x (for π0) and 1.55x (for CNN models), and training throughput by 5.70x (for π0) and 4.61x (for CNN models). This was attributed to pipeline overlapping, even with similar per-stage latencies.
- **Convergence Speed:** On the Peg Insertion task with a CNN policy and RLPD, the asynchronous pipeline significantly accelerated training, reducing convergence time from over 8000 seconds to approximately 1500 seconds.
- **Weight Synchronization Interval:** An ablation study showed that small synchronization intervals (e.g., 1 or 8 updates per synchronization) led to frequent in-episode weight updates, inducing policy non-stationarity and resulting in slower convergence or divergence. Larger intervals were found to ensure more stable updates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

USER presents a systems-level solution for real-world online policy learning in embodied AI, addressing key infrastructural limitations that hinder the widespread adoption and scaling of robot learning in physical environments.

The **unified hardware abstraction layer** provides a standardized approach to integrate and manage heterogeneous robots and computational accelerators as first-class resources. This can simplify deployment and orchestration for multi-robot fleets and diverse hardware setups, which is critical for scaling data collection and enabling broader research into cross-embodiment learning. By treating robots as schedulable units, USER facilitates more efficient resource utilization and joint scheduling, moving beyond isolated control paradigms.

The **adaptive communication plane** directly confronts the challenges of cloud-edge distributed learning, particularly relevant for large models like VLAs. Its tunneling-based networking and distributed data channels are designed to provide stable and efficient data exchange across geographically dispersed and heterogeneous network domains. The SM-aware weight synchronization mechanism further refines this by mitigating GPU resource contention during model updates, ensuring that inference capabilities remain robust during training. This enables the practical deployment of sophisticated, compute-intensive models in real-world settings with varying network conditions.

The **fully asynchronous learning framework** improves data efficiency by decoupling data generation from training. By allowing robots to operate continuously, it maximizes the utilization of expensive real-world interaction time, which is a bottleneck in physical robot learning. This design significantly accelerates training convergence, demonstrating substantial speed-ups compared to synchronous approaches.

The **persistent-cache-aware buffer** offers a robust solution for managing large-scale, long-horizon data generated in real-world experiments. Its ability to persist data to disk, support crash recovery, and enable efficient reuse of historical data addresses limitations of memory-centric buffers, which are inadequate for extended, continuous learning cycles. This allows for more stable and resilient long-duration experiments and facilitates off-policy learning across evolving policy versions.

Finally, the **extensible abstractions for policies, algorithms, and rewards** provide a flexible research platform. By supporting a range of policy architectures (from CNN/MLP to VLA models), learning algorithms (RL, imitation, human-in-the-loop), and reward mechanisms, USER reduces the engineering overhead for researchers. This unification allows for comparative studies and rapid iteration on diverse learning paradigms within a consistent infrastructural framework.

The open-source implementation of USER offers a reusable foundation that could accelerate research and development in real-world embodied AI. By providing robust solutions for resource management, communication, data handling, and training orchestration, USER has the potential to facilitate the transition of embodied AI from predominantly simulation-based research to scalable and practical real-world applications.
