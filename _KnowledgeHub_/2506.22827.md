# Hierarchical Vision-Language Planning for Multi-Step Humanoid Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT and EPFL introduced a three-layer hierarchical vision-language planning framework that enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to autonomously execute complex multi-step manipulation tasks. The integrated system achieved a 73% success rate in real-world obstructed bag-transfer trials, demonstrating effective coordination of learned skills with real-time VLM-driven monitoring.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots demonstrate dexterity in isolated, single-skill tasks, but struggle with complex, multi-step, long-horizon manipulation in unstructured environments.
- Reliably sequencing multiple learned skills and managing real-time transitions between them poses a significant challenge, often requiring human intervention or open-loop execution.
- Existing robot systems often lack integrated high-level reasoning modules capable of autonomously selecting, sequencing, and verifying the successful execution of complex skill chains.

## Method

- A three-layer hierarchical control framework is proposed, combining a low-level reinforcement learning (RL) based whole-body tracking policy, mid-level imitation-learned (IL) skill policies, and a high-level ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== for planning and execution monitoring.
- The high-level VLM layer utilizes GPT-4o for generating structured skill sequences from natural language instructions and a lightweight Gemini-2.0-Flash-Lite model for real-time visual verification of skill execution.
- Skill policies are trained via imitation learning from teleoperated RGB demonstrations, enabling autonomous motion generation for specific manipulation behaviors like picking and placing.

## Results

- The integrated hierarchical system achieved a **73%** success rate across 40 real-world trials on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot performing an obstructed bag-transfer task.
- Individual mid-level imitation policies demonstrated high success rates: **90%** for picking and **83%** for placing in autonomous trials, forming a robust base for complex tasks.
- Analysis of failures identified skill policy failures (e.g., grasping issues due to object variations) as most frequent, followed by VLM execution monitor misidentifications and less frequent VLM planner mis-grounding issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical approach effectively integrates high-level VLM reasoning with robust low-level control, allowing continuous closed-loop monitoring and adaptive transitions between skills.
> - The VLM execution monitor, operating at 1 Hz, can successfully verify skill completion in real-time without hindering the higher-frequency (25-200 Hz) control layers, demonstrating the viability of VLMs for deliberative robot control.
> - The modular design allows for independent training and integration of new skills into the library, providing flexibility and extensibility for the robot's capabilities without extensive system reconfiguration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has witnessed remarkable advancements, particularly with the advent of sophisticated reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL) techniques. Recent breakthroughs have enabled humanoid robots to execute complex *single-skill* tasks with impressive dexterity, such as dynamic walking, dancing, boxing, and basic object manipulation. Prominent examples include systems like ExBody, HumanPlus, and OmniH2O, which have showcased robust low-level control and motion generation from human demonstrations.

However, a significant chasm persists between these isolated skill demonstrations and the realization of truly general-purpose, autonomous humanoid robots capable of operating in unstructured human environments. Practical applications often demand *multi-step, long-horizon tasks* that involve sequences of interactions with multiple objects and dynamic environmental conditions. The challenge lies in seamlessly orchestrating these learned skills, managing transitions, and adapting to real-time feedback, all while maintaining the robot's inherent high-dimensionality and control complexity. Existing systems typically require human intervention to sequence skills or recover from errors, limiting their autonomy.

This paper addresses this critical gap by proposing a novel hierarchical framework that integrates high-level reasoning with robust low-level control. It positions itself within the evolving landscape where **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** are increasingly being leveraged to imbue robots with semantic understanding, planning capabilities, and robust task monitoring. The broader landscape sees two main trends in VLM integration for robotics:
1. **End-to-End Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Approaches like RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0.5 aim to map raw sensory input directly to robot actions using massive, paired datasets. While powerful, these models often lack interpretability and require vast amounts of data, making them complex to debug and adapt.
2. **Hierarchical VLM-based Approaches:** This category, where the presented work firmly resides, explicitly separates high-level semantic reasoning from low-level control. Methods like RT-H, NaVILA, and HAMSTER predict coarse geometric plans or leverage VLMs for spatial affordance prediction (e.g., KALM, RoboPoint, ManipGen), which are then refined by lower-level controllers. This approach offers greater interpretability, modularity, and often requires less task-specific training data.

This research distinguishes itself by building upon the established "two-layer humanoid control stack"—consisting of a low-level RL-based tracking policy and a mid-level imitation-learned skill generation module—and critically **adding a third, high-level VLM-based planning and execution monitoring layer**. This addition enables the autonomous selection, sequencing, and real-time verification of skills, thereby pushing the boundaries of autonomous multi-step manipulation for humanoids beyond what standalone skill learning or purely end-to-end VLA systems offer. It explicitly tackles the problem of "integrated high-level modules capable of autonomously selecting and sequencing skills, as well as verifying successful execution," which is identified as a key missing component in current humanoid systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to reliably and autonomously execute complex multi-step manipulation tasks in real-world, unstructured environments. This goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Autonomy Gap:** While humanoid robots possess morphology advantageous for human environments, their high dimensionality makes control and planning challenging. Current state-of-the-art methods excel at executing isolated, single-step skills (e.g., walking, picking up an object). However, real-world utility demands the ability to combine these skills into complex, long-horizon sequences involving multiple interactions with objects and surfaces. The primary motivation is to bridge this gap between singular skill execution and truly autonomous, multi-step operation.
2. **Addressing Sequencing and Transition Challenges:** Reliably sequencing multiple skills and managing transitions between them is a significant hurdle. This requires continuous real-time visual feedback and robust mechanisms to determine when one skill is completed and the next should begin. Traditional methods often rely on open-loop execution or human supervision for these transitions, limiting autonomy.
3. **Leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The emergence of powerful VLMs presents a unique opportunity to integrate high-level reasoning and task monitoring directly into robotic control. The authors are motivated to explore how VLMs can provide the semantic understanding necessary for planning and verifying complex multi-step tasks, thereby enhancing the robot's ability to operate effectively in human-centric environments.
4. **Enhancing Robustness and Interpretability:** Compared to purely end-to-end VLA models that can be opaque and data-hungry, a hierarchical approach with VLM-based planning and monitoring promises greater interpretability. This modularity allows for easier debugging, adaptation, and expansion of the robot's capabilities.

Based on these motivations, the paper outlines two primary contributions that serve as its key objectives:

1. **Development of a Novel Hierarchical Framework:** To design and implement a hierarchical vision-language planning and monitoring framework capable of dynamically sequencing and verifying humanoid manipulation skills. This involves defining distinct layers for low-level control, mid-level skill execution, and high-level VLM-driven reasoning.
2. **Validation on a Real Humanoid Robot:** To integrate this framework into a fully autonomous system and empirically validate its practical feasibility and effectiveness on a real-world humanoid robot performing complex multi-step tasks in realistic environments. This objective aims to demonstrate that the proposed system can achieve reliable performance outside of simulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system is a three-layer hierarchical control and planning framework designed for multi-step humanoid manipulation, leveraging a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Three-Layer Hierarchical Framework

The system operates with distinct temporal frequencies across its layers:
1. **Low-Level Controller (200 Hz PD, 50 Hz RL target):** A reinforcement learning (RL) policy ensures precise whole-body motion tracking.
2. **Mid-Level Skill Policies (25 Hz):** Multiple imitation-learned (IL) policies generate coherent motion targets for specific manipulation skills.
3. **High-Level Planning and Monitoring (1 Hz):** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) orchestrate skill sequences and monitor their real-time execution.

#### 3.2. A. Low-Level Tracking Policy: Robust Whole-Body Control

This foundational layer is an RL-based controller responsible for tracking motion goals provided by the mid-level.
- **Problem Formulation:** The policy, $\pi_T: G \times S \rightarrow A$, maps a motion goal ($G$) and the robot's current state ($S$) to feasible joint commands ($A$). The goal $G$ comprises a Root Motion Goal (desired base linear/angular velocity) and an Expression Goal (target joint angles and 3D keypoint positions for body landmarks). The state $S$ includes proprioceptive data (joint positions/velocities, IMU) and state history. The policy outputs target joint angles at 50 Hz, which are then fed to a high-frequency (200 Hz) Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller for execution.
- **Architecture and Training:** It employs a standard actor-critic architecture trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Both actor and critic are 3-layer MLPs. Training occurs in Isaac Gym with 4096 parallel environments, extensively randomized with parameters like gravity, friction, mass distribution, motor strength, external perturbations, and terrain irregularities to ensure robustness in real-world conditions. The reward function combines tracking accuracy (adapted from ExBody and ExBody2) with stability-promoting regularization terms specifically tuned for the Unitree G1.
- **Training Data:** A curated subset of the AMASS dataset and supplementary task-specific teleoperation demonstrations collected by the authors.

#### 3.3. B. Mid-Level Imitation Learned Skills: Autonomous Motion Generation

This module generates higher-level skill behaviors (e.g., "pick," "place") by translating sensory inputs into joint-motion targets for the low-level tracker. It comprises two main parts:

##### 3.3.1. Teleoperation for Data Collection

To acquire diverse demonstration data, an RGB-based teleoperation pipeline was developed, bypassing the need for specialized motion capture equipment.
- **Human Pose Estimation:** Uses HybrIK, a hybrid analytical-neural inverse kinematics model, to predict 3D human joint positions from single-view RGB inputs, refining them into smooth, anatomically plausible poses.
- **Retargeting:** A custom procedure translates human pose estimates onto the Unitree G1's morphology, accounting for differences in limb proportions, joint limits, and DoF.
- **Keypoint Generation:** Forward kinematics in simulation converts the retargeted robot joint configurations into 3D keypoints, matching the input format required by the low-level tracking policy.

##### 3.3.2. Imitation Learning Policy Details

- **Policy:** The IL policy, $\pi_{IL}: X \rightarrow A$, maps an observation state ($x_t$, comprising proprioceptive joint angles and binocular egocentric RGB images) to future joint configurations.
- **Architecture:** The Humanoid Imitation Transformer (HIT) is adopted, based on the Action Chunking Transformer (ACT). HIT is a decoder-only transformer that predicts multi-step future actions (action chunks of 50 joint-angle targets at 25 Hz) in a single forward pass. An auxiliary L2 loss on predicted visual embeddings enhances visual grounding.
- **Sensors:** Two externally mounted ELP high-speed RGB cameras with wide-angle lenses provide consistent binocular egocentric visual inputs.
- **Training:** Demonstration data is collected via teleoperation (30 successful sequences for each skill, e.g., picking and placing). These sequences are segmented into skill-specific datasets, and HIT policies are trained using these demonstrations.

#### 3.4. C. High-Level Vision-Language Planning and Execution Monitoring

This is the novel third layer responsible for dynamically selecting, sequencing, and verifying skill execution for multi-step tasks.

##### 3.4.1. Problem Formulation and Terminology

- **Skill:** An individual, short-duration manipulation capability (e.g., picking, placing).
- **Task:** A higher-level objective involving a sequence of at least two skills to achieve a goal (e.g., "Pick up the bag and place it on the table").
- **Goal:** Autonomously generate and execute a sequence of parameterized skills ($\sigma = [\pi^{(1)}, \dots, \pi^{(N)}]$) from a natural-language goal ($g$) and initial visual observation ($o_0$).

##### 3.4.2. System Architecture

This module consists of two VLM components:
- **VLM Planner (P):** Utilizes GPT-4o. It generates structured, interpretable skill sequences from initial visual context and textual task instructions. It uses a "PDDL-like" representation for each skill, including natural-language descriptions, preconditions, effects, and example verification queries (Fig. 3). The planner implicitly reasons about causal ordering and logical coherence from these descriptions.
- **VLM Skill Monitor (M):** Employs a lightweight Gemini-2.0-Flash-Lite model. Operating at approximately 1 Hz, it continuously evaluates the execution state of the current skill through real-time visual feedback. It assesses short video snippets (10-15 frames from 1.5-second segments) using natural-language verification queries to output a binary decision: "completed" or "in progress."

These two components form an iterative planning-monitoring loop, enabling closed-loop reasoning and coherent multi-step task execution.

#### 3.5. D. Integrated System Architecture and Robot Platform

The complete system is a harmonious integration of these three layers. The VLM Planner generates a skill sequence. The VLM Skill Monitor constantly checks the status of the current skill. A Skill Selector dynamically chooses the next skill based on the planned sequence and monitor feedback. The mid-level IL module activates the corresponding policy, generating joint targets from egocentric vision and proprioception. Finally, the low-level RL tracking policy, combined with the PD controller, translates these targets into motor torque commands.

A key advantage is the system's modularity: new IL skills can be trained independently, added to the skill library, and immediately become available for the high-level VLM planner to compose into more complex tasks without extensive system reconfiguration. The entire framework is validated on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot, equipped with two external ELP RGB cameras for binocular vision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of the proposed hierarchical vision-language planning and control framework through real-world experiments on a humanoid robot.

#### 4.1. A. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Task

Experiments were conducted on the 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot, equipped with external ELP RGB cameras. The robot operated in a controlled environment with tables and objects. The chosen multi-step manipulation task was an **obstructed bag-transfer task**, referred to as a "non-prehensile pick-and-place task" in the abstract and "pick-and-place" in the experiments section, involving:
1. **Pushing:** The robot first needed to push aside an obstacle (implicitly mentioned in the abstract and discussion, but not explicitly in Section IV.B, though Figure 1 shows this).
2. **Picking:** Grasping and lifting a bag from an initial table/surface.
3. **Placing:** Accurately placing the grasped bag onto a second designated surface.
A total of 40 independent trials were performed, with success defined by the robot correctly completing the entire sequence.

#### 4.2. B. Overall System Performance

- **Integrated System Success Rate:** The hierarchical system achieved a **73% success rate** in autonomously completing the full multi-step manipulation sequence over 40 real-world trials. This demonstrates practical feasibility and effective coordination of skills and visual feedback.
- **Real-time Operation:** The VLM execution monitor's mean verification latency of one second (at 1 Hz) comfortably coexisted with high-frequency skill policies (25 Hz) and the RL controller (50 Hz target, 200 Hz PD), indicating that large-model reasoning does not preclude high-rate control for deliberative motions.

#### 4.3. C. Individual Skill Performance

Before integration into the full hierarchical system, the mid-level imitation learning (IL) policies were evaluated independently:
- **Picking Skill:** Achieved a **90% success rate** over 30 autonomous trials.
- **Placing Skill:** Achieved an **83% success rate** over 30 autonomous trials.
These high individual success rates for the IL policies underscore the robustness of the mid-level controllers and provide a strong foundation for the VLM-orchestrated multi-step tasks.

#### 4.4. D. Qualitative Observations and Failure Analysis

Qualitatively, the robot exhibited stable and coherent manipulation behaviors during successful trials. Skill transitions were smooth and effectively guided by the VLM execution monitor, showcasing real-time adaptation. However, minor irregularities were observed due to executing multiple consecutive 50-step action chunks, which sometimes caused subtle positional resets between chunks, suggesting potential for improvement with smoothing functions.

A qualitative analysis of failures revealed three dominant modes, in order of frequency:
- **Skill Policy Failure (Most Frequent):** Occurred when a mid-level imitation policy failed to execute the desired skill. This was most often observed during the grasping phase, primarily attributed to variations in object positioning that fell outside the distribution of the training data. For example, partial occlusions or slightly novel object poses could cause the policy to stall before a successful grasp or placement.
- **VLM Execution Monitor Failure:** Instances where the visual verification component prematurely indicated task completion. This was typically due to subtle positioning inaccuracies or ambiguous visual cues, leading to a false positive for skill completion and an early transition to the next step.
- **VLM Planner Failure (Least Frequent):** Occasional "mis-grounding" issues where the VLM Planner generated incorrect skill sequences. This sometimes involved hallucinating objects, adding irrelevant plan steps, or formulating redundant transition questions, indicating limitations in its understanding of the environment or task requirements.

The low-level tracking policy's performance in simulation was quantitatively assessed, yielding a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.0593 rad (3.4°) for joint angles and 13.88 cm for keypoint positions, confirming its reliable tracking capabilities for real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a significant stride in the development of truly autonomous humanoid robots and has several profound implications for the field of robotics:

#### 5.1. A. Advancing Autonomous Humanoid Manipulation

The most direct impact is the demonstration that multi-step humanoid manipulation can be achieved reliably in the real world without continuous human intervention. By integrating robust RL-based whole-body tracking, imitation-learned skill policies, and a novel VLM-based planning and monitoring layer, the research effectively bridges the gap between isolated skill execution and long-horizon autonomy. This is crucial for the deployment of humanoids in complex industrial, household, and service environments where task sequences are the norm.

#### 5.2. B. Advantages of the Hierarchical VLM Approach

- **Closed-Loop Reasoning:** Unlike many prior humanoid systems that often relied on open-loop sequences, this framework introduces continuous closed-loop reasoning. The VLM execution monitor provides real-time verification of skill completion, enabling dynamic, adaptive transitions and recovery from minor deviations, which is vital for robustness in dynamic environments.
- **Interpretability and Debuggability:** Compared to opaque end-to-end VLA models that are challenging to interpret and debug, the explicit planner-monitor design offers clear introspection into the decision-making process. This transparency allows developers to easily diagnose failure modes (as evidenced by their failure analysis) and implement targeted improvements.
- **Flexibility and Extensibility:** The use of natural-language-based, PDDL-like skill descriptions simplifies the integration of new behaviors into the robot's repertoire. New skills can be trained independently and added to the library, making them immediately available for the high-level planner to compose. This modularity reduces the need for extensive system reconfiguration and domain-specific expertise, fostering rapid expansion of robot capabilities.

#### 5.3. C. Practical Feasibility and Real-World Validation

The successful validation of the integrated system on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot in 40 real-world trials (achieving 73% success) is a critical achievement. It moves beyond simulation-only results and demonstrates the practical feasibility and effectiveness of such complex hierarchical systems in realistic, physical environments. The finding that VLM reasoning, even at a lower frequency (1 Hz), can effectively coexist with high-rate motor control is particularly significant, showing that large language models are becoming viable tools for real-time robotic control by defining appropriate timescales of abstraction.

#### 5.4. D. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors candidly discuss limitations, providing clear pathways for future research:
- **Task Scope:** The evaluation task, while non-trivial, is confined to tabletop pick-and-place, undersampling the diversity of domestic manipulation.
- **Verification Rate:** The 1Hz VLM verification rate, while adequate for deliberative motions, may be insufficient for fast, reactive skills, indicating a need for faster VLM processing or different abstraction levels for rapid motions.
- **Error Detection and Adaptive Re-planning:** A critical future enhancement involves enabling the VLM execution monitor to explicitly detect execution failures or anomalous states, triggering adaptive replanning to improve robustness.
- **Enhanced Semantic and Contextual Conditioning:** Incorporating richer conditioning, possibly by replacing the ResNet backbone with a CLIP-like architecture, could provide finer-grained semantic control of skills via text conditioning.
- **Expanding the Skill Library:** Expanding the robot's skill repertoire to include more complex behaviors like non-prehensile bi-manipulation and tool use will enable more diverse and realistic long-horizon tasks, further challenging the planning and monitoring components in dynamic environments.

#### 5.5. E. Broader Implications for Robotics

This research provides a robust framework for integrating symbolic-like reasoning (through VLM planning) with learned sensorimotor policies, offering a powerful paradigm for future robotic systems. It paves the way for humanoids to become more versatile, adaptable, and truly autonomous agents capable of performing complex tasks in human-centric environments, ultimately contributing to the realization of general-purpose robots. The work highlights the transformative potential of combining large language models with established robotic control techniques, shaping the future of robot intelligence and physical embodiment.
