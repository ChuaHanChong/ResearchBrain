# Hierarchical Vision-Language Planning for Multi-Step Humanoid Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT and EPFL introduced a three-layer hierarchical vision-language planning framework that enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to autonomously execute complex multi-step manipulation tasks. The integrated system achieved a 73% success rate in real-world obstructed bag-transfer trials, demonstrating effective coordination of learned skills with real-time VLM-driven monitoring.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots demonstrate dexterity in isolated, single-skill tasks, but struggle with complex, multi-step, long-horizon manipulation in unstructured environments.
- Reliably sequencing multiple learned skills and managing real-time transitions between them poses a significant challenge, often requiring human intervention or open-loop execution.
- Existing robot systems often lack integrated high-level reasoning modules capable of autonomously selecting, sequencing, and verifying the successful execution of complex skill chains.

## Method

- A three-layer hierarchical control framework is proposed, combining a low-level reinforcement learning (RL) based whole-body tracking policy, mid-level imitation-learned (IL) skill policies, and a high-level ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== for planning and execution monitoring.
- The high-level VLM layer utilizes GPT-4o for generating structured skill sequences from natural language instructions and a lightweight Gemini-2.0-Flash-Lite model for real-time visual verification of skill execution.
- Skill policies are trained via imitation learning from teleoperated RGB demonstrations, enabling autonomous motion generation for specific manipulation behaviors like picking and placing.

## Results

- The integrated hierarchical system achieved a **73%** success rate across 40 real-world trials on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot performing an obstructed bag-transfer task.
- Individual mid-level imitation policies demonstrated high success rates: **90%** for picking and **83%** for placing in autonomous trials, forming a robust base for complex tasks.
- Analysis of failures identified skill policy failures (e.g., grasping issues due to object variations) as most frequent, followed by VLM execution monitor misidentifications and less frequent VLM planner mis-grounding issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical approach effectively integrates high-level VLM reasoning with robust low-level control, allowing continuous closed-loop monitoring and adaptive transitions between skills.
> - The VLM execution monitor, operating at 1 Hz, can successfully verify skill completion in real-time without hindering the higher-frequency (25-200 Hz) control layers, demonstrating the viability of VLMs for deliberative robot control.
> - The modular design allows for independent training and integration of new skills into the library, providing flexibility and extensibility for the robot's capabilities without extensive system reconfiguration.
