# Var-JEPA: A Variational Formulation of the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture -- Bridging Predictive and Generative Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Oxford introduce Var-JEPA, a variational formulation of the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), which re-interprets JEPA as a coupled latent-variable generative model derived from a unified Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective. This framework intrinsically prevents representational collapse in self-supervised learning and enables principled uncertainty quantification in learned representations.

## Problem

- Standard Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) frequently suffer from representational collapse, necessitating ad-hoc regularization techniques to produce meaningful latent representations.
- Traditional JEPA implementations lack a principled method for quantifying uncertainty within their learned latent spaces.
- The prevailing view of JEPA as a 'non-generative' self-supervised learning paradigm creates a conceptual divide with probabilistic generative models.

## Method

- Developed **Var-JEPA** by re-interpreting JEPA's predictive embedding steps as variational posteriors within a coupled Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework.
- Derived a unified Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective for Var-JEPA, whose intrinsic KL divergence terms naturally regularize latent distributions and prevent collapse without ad-hoc methods.
- Instantiated Var-JEPA for heterogeneous tabular data (Var-T-JEPA) using feature-level masking and transformer-based components, enabling practical evaluation.

## Results

- Simulation studies demonstrated that the ELBO's KL divergence terms effectively prevent representational collapse and enforce desirable latent distributional properties without explicit architectural heuristics.
- Var-T-JEPA achieved competitive downstream classification performance on diverse tabular datasets, consistently outperforming the deterministic T-JEPA baseline which often suffered from collapse.
- The framework provided principled per-sample uncertainty estimates, enabling effective selective prediction with a clear coverage-accuracy trade-off and strong alignment with ground-truth ambiguity in synthetic data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The architectural components of JEPA can be formally re-interpreted as variational posteriors and learned conditional priors within a coupled latent-variable generative model.
> - The per-sample KL divergence terms within the ELBO objective intrinsically regularize latent distributions, effectively preventing representational collapse without needing external heuristics like EMA or SIGReg.
> - Var-JEPA's probabilistic nature inherently provides meaningful, per-sample uncertainty estimates in the latent space, directly from the learned posterior distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of self-supervised learning (SSL), a machine learning paradigm that aims to learn meaningful data representations without requiring human-annotated labels. A prominent approach in SSL is the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), initially proposed by LeCun (2022). JEPA is characterized by its focus on predicting latent representations of data rather than reconstructing raw observations (e.g., pixels or raw features), often framed as a non-generative alternative to likelihood-based SSL methods like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs).

Existing JEPA implementations, such as I-JEPA for images (Assran et al., 2023), V-JEPA for video (Bardes et al., 2024), and T-JEPA for tabular data (Thimonier et al., 2025), have demonstrated effectiveness in learning rich representations. However, these architectures typically encounter a challenge known as "representational collapse," where the encoders produce trivial, uninformative latent vectors. To counteract this, standard JEPA implementations often rely on ad-hoc regularization techniques, such as Exponential Moving Average (EMA) for target encoder updates or auxiliary loss functions (e.g., variance-covariance penalties, Drozdov et al., 2024; Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) as proposed in LeJEPA, Balestriero & LeCun, 2025) to maintain representational diversity and prevent collapse.

This paper challenges the prevailing view of JEPA as fundamentally non-generative. It argues that the architectural patterns of JEPA, specifically its coupled encoders and context-to-target predictor, structurally resemble the variational posteriors and learned conditional priors found in specific classes of coupled latent-variable models. By establishing this structural equivalence, the authors propose a unified framework that connects predictive joint-embedding learning with probabilistic generative modeling. This re-framing positions JEPA not as an alternative to generative models, but as a specific configuration within the broader design space of latent-variable generative models. The work aligns with concurrent efforts to introduce probabilistic formulations to JEPA, such as Huang (2026), but distinguishes itself by deriving JEPA from a unified Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective of a coupled latent-variable generative model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the conceptual and structural gap between the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) and probabilistic generative self-supervised learning. The authors contend that the current distinction, which often labels JEPA as "non-generative," is primarily rhetorical rather than reflective of fundamental architectural differences.

The core motivation stems from two observations regarding existing JEPA implementations:
1. **Susceptibility to Representational Collapse:** Standard JEPA models are prone to learning trivial representations where encoders output constant vectors, minimizing the prediction loss without capturing meaningful information. This necessitates the integration of ad-hoc regularization mechanisms or auxiliary costs (e.g., EMA, variance-covariance penalties, SIGReg) to encourage diverse and useful embeddings. The authors seek a more principled, intrinsic mechanism to avoid collapse.
2. **Lack of Principled Uncertainty Quantification:** As a deterministic architecture, standard JEPA does not inherently provide measures of uncertainty in its learned latent space. Access to such uncertainty estimates could be beneficial for various downstream tasks, such as selective prediction or robust decision-making.

Building on these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper are:
- **Formal Reinterpretation of JEPA:** To establish a novel formal link between the canonical JEPA design (coupled encoders with a context-to-target predictor) and variational inference within a coupled latent-variable generative model. This involves "reverse-engineering" a probabilistic model whose Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective naturally yields the JEPA predictor as a learned latent-space conditional prior. The goal is to interpret standard JEPA as a deterministic specialization of this probabilistic framework.
- **Derivation of Variational JEPA (Var-JEPA):** To develop Var-JEPA, a generative JEPA formulation explicitly derived from maximizing a single, unified ELBO objective. This objective is intended to inherently prevent representational collapse by imposing explicit regularization through KL divergence terms, thereby eliminating the need for heuristic anti-collapse measures.
- **Enabling Principled Uncertainty Quantification:** To leverage the probabilistic nature of Var-JEPA to provide meaningful, per-sample uncertainty estimates in the latent space, derived directly from the learned posterior distributions.
- **Practical Instantiation for Tabular Data (Var-T-JEPA):** To demonstrate the feasibility and efficacy of the Var-JEPA framework by instantiating it for heterogeneous tabular data (Var-T-JEPA). This involves adapting the framework's components—such as encoders, decoders, and the predictor—to the specific characteristics of tabular modalities, including feature-level masking.
- **Empirical Validation:** To empirically evaluate Var-T-JEPA's performance in learning representations and its utility in downstream tasks, comparing it against both standard T-JEPA and strong raw-feature baselines, particularly focusing on its ability to prevent collapse and provide useful uncertainty signals.

In essence, the authors aim to show that JEPA’s design is not distinct from generative modeling but rather an implicit variant thereof, and by making this generative structure explicit, one can achieve more robust representation learning with added benefits like uncertainty quantification.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work involves re-framing the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) within a variational inference framework, specifically as a coupled latent-variable generative model.

#### 3.1. Variational Perspective and Problem Formulation

The authors hypothesize that JEPA's predictive embedding steps can be interpreted as variational posteriors of a coupled Variational Autoencoder (VAE). This leads to the derivation of Var-JEPA, which replaces JEPA's deterministic encoders and predictor with conditional probability distributions. The predictive pathway within JEPA is reinterpreted as a learned latent-space conditional prior.

The model aims to maximize the marginal log-likelihood of observed data pairs (x, y). A joint distribution $p_{\theta}(x, y, s_x, z, s_y)$ is defined based on a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) that includes context observations ($x$), target observations ($y$), context latent representations ($s_x$), target latent representations ($s_y$), and an auxiliary predictive variable ($z$).

#### 3.2. Generative Model Parameterization ($p_{\theta}$)

The joint distribution $p_{\theta}$ factorizes as:
$p_{\theta}(x, y, s_x, z, s_y) = p(s_x) \cdot p(z) \cdot p_{\theta}(x | s_x) \cdot p_{\theta}(s_y | s_x, z) \cdot p_{\theta}(y | s_y)$

Each component is parameterized as a Gaussian distribution:
- Priors: $p(s_x) = \mathcal{N}(s_x; 0, I)$ and $p(z) = \mathcal{N}(z; 0, I)$ (standard Gaussians).
- Decoders: $p_{\theta}(x | s_x) = \mathcal{N}(x; U_x^{\theta}(s_x), \sigma_x^2 I)$ and $p_{\theta}(y | s_y) = \mathcal{N}(y; U_y^{\theta}(s_y), \sigma_y^2 I)$. $U_x^{\theta}$ and $U_y^{\theta}$ are neural network decoders reconstructing observations from their latents.
- Conditional Prior (JEPA Predictor): $p_{\theta}(s_y | s_x, z) = \mathcal{N}(s_y; \mu_{s_y}^{\theta}(s_x, z), \Sigma_{s_y}^{\theta}(s_x, z))$. This is parameterized by a neural network that predicts the distributional parameters of $s_y$ from $s_x$ and $z$.

#### 3.3. Variational Posterior Parameterization ($q_{\phi}$)

Since the marginal log-likelihood is intractable, a variational posterior $q_{\phi}(s_x, z, s_y | x, y)$ is introduced to approximate the true posterior. It factorizes as:
$q_{\phi}(s_x, z, s_y | x, y) = q_{\phi}(s_x | x) \cdot q_{\phi}(z | s_x) \cdot q_{\phi}(s_y | s_x, z, y)$

This factorization ensures:
- $q_{\phi}(s_x | x)$ learns context representations independently of target information.
- $q_{\phi}(z | s_x)$ depends only on $s_x$ to prevent target information leakage.
- $q_{\phi}(s_y | s_x, z, y)$ uses the target observation $y$ for regularization, ensuring meaningful information encoding.

Each component is also parameterized as a Gaussian with learnable mean and covariance outputs from neural networks (inference networks or encoders).

#### 3.4. Var-JEPA: Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)

The ELBO is derived as a tractable lower bound on the marginal log-likelihood. Maximizing the ELBO is equivalent to minimizing a loss function ($L_{ELBO}$), which consists of five interpretable terms, each with a scalar weight ($\alpha$):
$L_{ELBO} = \alpha_{rec} L_{rec} + \alpha_{gen} L_{gen} + \alpha_{KL_{s_x}} L_{KL_{s_x}} + \alpha_{KL_z} L_{KL_z} + \alpha_{KL_{s_y}} L_{KL_{s_y}}$
- $L_{rec}$ (Context Reconstruction): Measures the reconstruction quality of $x$ from $s_x$.
- $L_{gen}$ (Target Generation): Measures the generation quality of $y$ from $s_y$.
- $L_{KL_{s_x}}$ (KL on $s_x$): Regularizes $q_{\phi}(s_x | x)$ towards its prior $p(s_x) = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
- $L_{KL_z}$ (KL on $z$): Regularizes $q_{\phi}(z | s_x)$ towards its prior $p(z) = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
- $L_{KL_{s_y}}$ (KL on $s_y$): Regularizes $q_{\phi}(s_y | s_x, z, y)$ towards the learned conditional prior $p_{\theta}(s_y | s_x, z)$. This term implicitly drives predictive accuracy and prevents collapse.

#### 3.5. JEPA vs. Var-JEPA Architectures

Var-JEPA extends JEPA by replacing deterministic encoders and predictors with probabilistic distributions and incorporating generative networks (decoders). This allows training under the unified ELBO objective, which inherently prevents collapse, unlike JEPA's reliance on surrogate costs.

#### 3.6. Reparameterization Trick and Sampling

The reparameterization trick is employed to enable backpropagation through stochastic latent variables, expressing samples as deterministic functions of noise. Single samples are used per forward pass for gradient estimation.

#### 3.7. Relationship to LeJEPA

The paper discusses how the per-sample KL divergence terms in Var-JEPA, particularly for latents with fixed priors ($s_x$ and $z$), relate to aggregated distributional regularizers like SIGReg in LeJEPA. These KL terms implicitly encourage properties such as isotropic Gaussian distributions, similar to the explicit regularization of SIGReg.

#### 3.8. Var-T-JEPA: Tabular Data Implementation

The framework is instantiated for tabular data as Var-T-JEPA.
- **Feature Masking:** Utilizes a feature-level masking strategy to create context ($x$) and multiple target ($y_k$) views from the complete feature vector ($w$).
- **Variational Network Components:**
 - **Context Encoder ($q_{\phi}(s_x | x)$):** Processes masked tabular features via tokenization (shared linear projection for numerical, learned embeddings for categorical, with type and positional embeddings) and a transformer encoder to output Gaussian parameters for $s_x$.
 - **Auxiliary Encoder ($q_{\phi}(z | s_x)$):** Infers auxiliary latents from pooled context representations ($s_x$) through a compact MLP, outputting Gaussian parameters for $z$.
 - **Target Posterior ($q_{\phi}(s_y | s_x, z, w)$):** Conditions on pooled context latents and auxiliary latents, concatenated with complete raw features ($w$), processed by a transformer, to output Gaussian parameters for $s_y$.
 - **Predictive Model ($p_{\theta}(s_y | s_x, z)$):** A transformer-based network that predicts target latent distributions from $s_x$ and $z$ using learnable mask tokens, without access to raw target features.
 - **Context and Target Decoders ($p_{\theta}(x | s_x)$ and $p_{\theta}(y | s_y)$):** Feature-specific MLPs reconstruct original tabular features from their respective latent representations. Numerical features use Gaussian likelihoods, while categorical features use categorical likelihoods (softmax).
- **Loss Computation:** The ELBO loss terms are adapted to the feature-level masking, averaging over masked features and multiple target predictions.
- **KL Divergence Annealing:** Linear annealing schedules are applied to the KL terms during training to stabilize learning.
- **Downstream Evaluation:** For downstream tasks, deterministic embeddings (posterior means) are used. Uncertainty estimates are derived from the standard deviations of the target latent posterior distribution, aggregated (e.g., mean or 90th percentile).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both theoretical insights from a simulation study and empirical validation on diverse tabular datasets.

#### 4.1. Simulation Study Findings

A controlled synthetic environment was utilized to examine the effects of latent regularization, isotropy, and information bottlenecks under a known ground-truth generative process.
- **Role of ELBO KL Terms:** The KL divergence terms within the ELBO were found to effectively enforce desirable distributional properties for latent variables ($s_x$ and $z$) that are regularized towards fixed standard-normal priors. Specifically, the per-sample KL regularization naturally led to aggregated latent distributions with properties (e.g., low KL divergence to N(0,I), low SIGReg-MSE, isotropic covariance) comparable to those obtained through explicit aggregated-distribution regularization methods like SIGReg (LeJEPA). This indicates that explicit architectural/training heuristics for collapse prevention may not be strictly necessary when a principled variational objective is employed.
- **Target Latent Behavior:** For the target latent $s_y$, which is regularized towards a *learned conditional prior* ($p_{\theta}(s_y | s_x, z)$) rather than a fixed prior, its aggregated distribution naturally deviates from an isotropic Gaussian. This outcome is consistent with the theoretical formulation, where $s_y$ is expected to encode information conditional on the context and auxiliary variables.
- **Collapse Prevention:** Removing the reconstruction (L_rec, L_gen) or KL divergence (L_KL_sx, L_KL_z, L_KL_sy) terms from the ELBO objective consistently led to representational collapse, evidenced by a significant drop in downstream probe accuracy and dramatic increases in distributional metrics (e.g., aggregated KL divergence to N(0,I), Frobenius norm of covariance deviation). This demonstrates the essential role of the ELBO's components in maintaining well-behaved latent distributions.
- **SIGReg as Compensation:** When all KL terms were removed, adding SIGReg partially mitigated the representational collapse, suggesting that aggregated-distribution regularization can compensate to some extent for the lack of per-sample variational regularization. However, the full ELBO objective generally remained optimal in preventing collapse and promoting robust representations.
- **Training Dynamics:** The evolution of distributional diagnostics during training revealed that the desired properties of the latent spaces emerged gradually and were stably maintained by the ELBO's KL terms.

#### 4.2. Downstream Evaluation on Tabular Data (Var-T-JEPA)

The Var-T-JEPA implementation was evaluated on five real-world tabular datasets (Adult, Covertype, Electricity, Credit Card Default, Bank Marketing), as well as a tabularized MNIST dataset and a fully synthetic dataset (SIM).
- **Competitive Representation Learning:** Var-T-JEPA produced competitive embeddings for various downstream classification tasks. Across multiple predictor families (MLP, DCNv2, ResNet, AutoInt, FT-Transformer, XGBoost), models trained on Var-T-JEPA embeddings generally achieved performance comparable to or better than models trained on raw features, and consistently improved over the deterministic T-JEPA baseline.
- **Superiority over T-JEPA:** The deterministic T-JEPA baseline frequently suffered from representational collapse on several datasets, leading to significantly reduced downstream performance and robustness compared to Var-T-JEPA. This highlights Var-JEPA's inherent ability to prevent collapse through its variational objective.
- **Principled Uncertainty Quantification:** Var-T-JEPA provided per-sample uncertainty estimates from the learned latent posterior distributions.
 - **Selective Evaluation:** Implementing selective evaluation (discarding the most uncertain 10%, 20%, or 50% of test samples) consistently demonstrated a clear coverage-accuracy trade-off. As more uncertain samples were removed, the accuracy on the remaining, more confident subset of data increased across all datasets and model families. This indicates that the learned uncertainty signals are meaningful and can be used for informed abstention.
 - **(Semi-)Synthetic Data Alignment:** On the MNIST and SIM datasets, where ground-truth uncertainty (simulated corruption/ambiguity) was controlled, the latent uncertainty estimates from Var-T-JEPA showed strong alignment. Risk-coverage curves illustrated the utility of abstention, and a positive correlation was observed between standardized latent uncertainty and the simulated uncertainty scores. ROC curves also indicated that latent uncertainty could reliably identify high-ambiguity samples, confirming the relevance of the uncertainty measures.

In summary, the results demonstrate that re-framing JEPA within a variational framework not only provides a principled method for preventing representational collapse but also enables robust uncertainty quantification, leading to competitive performance and improved robustness in practical applications like tabular data analysis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in self-supervised learning by re-interpreting the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) through a variational inference lens.

**Conceptual Significance:**
- **Unification of Predictive and Generative SSL:** The most prominent conceptual contribution is the argument that JEPA's design, often considered non-generative, can be understood as a specific instance of a coupled latent-variable generative model trained with variational inference. This bridges a perceived dichotomy in self-supervised learning, suggesting that the predictive architecture of JEPA can be rigorously derived from a unified probabilistic objective (the ELBO). This re-framing provides a deeper theoretical foundation for JEPA-style models.
- **Intrinsic Collapse Prevention:** By demonstrating that the ELBO's KL divergence terms inherently regularize latent distributions towards well-behaved priors, the work offers a principled mechanism to prevent representational collapse. This reduces reliance on ad-hoc heuristics (such as EMA or specific distribution-matching penalties like SIGReg), simplifying model design and training while potentially improving robustness.

**Practical Impact:**
- **Principled Uncertainty Quantification:** The variational formulation enables per-sample uncertainty quantification in the latent space, derived directly from posterior covariances. This is a crucial capability for real-world applications where model confidence is important, allowing for:
 - **Selective Prediction:** Models can abstain from making predictions on highly uncertain samples, leading to improved accuracy on the remaining, more confident predictions, as demonstrated by the risk-coverage trade-off in the experiments.
 - **Increased Robustness:** Uncertainty estimates can inform downstream decision-making, helping users understand when model outputs might be unreliable.
- **Improved Tabular Representation Learning:** The Var-T-JEPA implementation shows that the framework can be effectively applied to heterogeneous tabular data. It consistently outperforms the deterministic T-JEPA baseline, which often suffers from collapse, and remains competitive with strong raw-feature baselines. This provides a robust and uncertainty-aware method for learning representations in an important and challenging data modality.
- **Broader Applicability:** Although demonstrated on tabular data, the variational JEPA framework is modality-agnostic. The paper explicitly outlines how it can be extended to vision and video by adapting tokenization and encoder/decoder architectures, suggesting a potential for widespread adoption across diverse domains currently utilizing JEPA.

**Future Directions and Implications:**
The findings suggest new avenues for research, including:
- **Scaling to other modalities:** Applying Var-JEPA to image and video data would be a natural next step, potentially leading to more robust and interpretable models in these domains.
- **Conditional Generation:** The explicit generative formulation opens possibilities for leveraging Var-JEPA in scenarios where target observations are absent at test time, making conditional generation from context a central task.
- **Theoretical Analysis of Latent Space Properties:** The link between per-sample KL regularization and aggregated distributional properties (like isotropy) could inspire further theoretical work on designing effective regularizers for self-supervised learning.

Overall, this research deepens the theoretical understanding of JEPA while providing a practically advantageous formulation that enhances representation quality, prevents collapse, and enables robust uncertainty quantification.
