# AECNav: Active Evidence Consolidation for Efficient Zero-Shot Open-Vocabulary Object Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> AECNav is a training-free zero-shot open-vocabulary object-goal navigation pipeline. A single C-RADIOv4 encoder serves both frontier scoring and target confirmation, gating an expensive segmentation decoder on patch-level evidence; detections accumulate into 3D cluster log-odds beliefs separating goal from confuser and miss evidence; frontier choice maximizes information gain minus traversal cost. It reaches 84.7%, 57.3%, 51.3% success on HM3D-v2, HM3D-OVON, MP3D.

## Problem

- Prior value-map navigators run disjoint encoders for frontier scoring and target confirmation, redundantly processing every frame and fragmenting semantics, which inflates per-step inference latency.
- A high open-vocabulary confidence score is ambiguous: genuine target, visually similar distractor, or transient false detection. Prior one-shot or score-averaging confirmation cannot separate these.
- Exploration is ranked by semantic relevance or geometric frontier utility alone, without modeling expected new evidence relative to traversal cost, causing oscillation under weak semantic cues.

## Method

- **Evidence-gated perception**: one shared ==C-RADIOv4== forward pass yields a summary token for the scene value map and patch tokens; ==SAM3== segmentation fires only when max patch-goal similarity exceeds a threshold.
- **Evidence consolidation**: detections back-project into 3D clusters holding ==additive log-odds beliefs==, updated by a decisiveness-weighted semantic term (goal versus LLM-generated confuser scores) plus a visibility-weighted miss term for absent expected detections.
- **Active evidence acquisition**: frontier utility sums normalized semantic value, ray-cast ==information gain== sampled along the ==BFS== traversable path, minus path-length traversal cost, with stopping gated on belief stability plus proximity.

## Results

- State-of-the-art training-free success: **84.7%** success / **45.3%** SPL on HM3D-v2 (**+6.6** over TrajRAG), **51.3%**/**25.9%** on MP3D, **57.3%**/**30.5%** on HM3D-OVON (**+9.0** over MSGNav).
- Efficiency: **24.39s** and **108.63** steps per episode, **2.2x** faster than VLFM and **33%** fewer steps than ASCENT; the gate skips **62.5%** of segmentation calls at unchanged accuracy.
- Ablations: removing consolidation costs **12.8** points success, shared encoding **8.8**, active acquisition **2.9**; confuser and miss terms add **1.8** and **1.3** nearly additively; real robot scored **38/40** at **5Hz**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single agglomerative encoder can serve both global scene scoring and local target localization, so separate perception backbones per reasoning stage are avoidable overhead, not a requirement.
> - Accumulating additive log-odds evidence, including explicit negative evidence from distractors and expected-but-absent detections, beats averaging scores, which can never exceed the best single frame.
> - Information gain alone barely helps exploration; it becomes useful only paired with a traversal-cost penalty, otherwise utility chases large open regions and burns the step budget.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the field of Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation (ZSON) in open-vocabulary scenarios. ZSON tasks require a mobile robot to locate a specified, arbitrary object in an environment it has not previously encountered, without explicit prior training on the target object categories or specific scene layouts. This area is challenging due to the need for robots to interpret natural language goals and operate in novel settings.

Prior work in ZSON has typically relied on combining open-vocabulary localization models with classical frontier exploration techniques. Examples include CoW, which introduced a foundation for language-driven ZSON, and VLFM, which ranks frontiers based on semantic value maps derived from vision-language models. More recent methods have attempted to enhance target confirmation using scene graphs, VLM-based rejection, or occlusion-free viewpoint selection. Some approaches, like ApexNav, fuse target-centric observations over time. In terms of exploration, techniques have evolved from purely geometric heuristics to include semantic guidance and, in some cases, the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) for more nuanced decision-making.

However, existing ZSON methods often encounter several limitations:
- **Perceptual Redundancy and Latency:** Many approaches utilize separate, disjoint visual models for different tasks such as exploration guidance and target confirmation, leading to redundant processing of visual observations and increased inference latency. This fragmentation prevents a unified semantic understanding across visual reasoning stages.
- **Ambiguity in Target Confirmation:** High confidence scores from detection models may not reliably indicate the true target, potentially stemming from visually similar distractors or transient false positives. Previous methods often struggle to robustly distinguish genuine targets from confusers or accumulate evidence effectively over time. Simple averaging of confidence scores, as seen in some methods, can limit the growth of belief even with consistent observations.
- **Inefficient Exploration:** Frontier selection strategies often prioritize semantic relevance or geometric utility without adequately considering the expected information gain from exploring a path or the cost of traversing it. This can lead to inefficient exploration, particularly when semantic cues are weak or ambiguous, potentially causing the robot to oscillate or undertake long, unproductive detours.

AECNav aims to address these identified bottlenecks by introducing a training-free pipeline that reframes ZSON as an evidence-driven perception-to-decision problem. It integrates a unified and efficient perception architecture, a robust spatial evidence accumulation mechanism, and an active exploration strategy to enhance both the accuracy and efficiency of open-vocabulary object navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more accurate and efficient zero-shot object-goal navigation (ZSON) system capable of operating in open-vocabulary scenarios within unseen environments. The authors identify three critical problems in existing ZSON approaches that motivate the development of AECNav:

1. **Addressing Redundant Perception Pipelines and High Latency:** Existing ZSON methods frequently employ separate visual encoding pipelines for different stages of the navigation task, such as creating semantic value maps for exploration and performing instance segmentation for target confirmation. This architectural design leads to redundant processing of each visual observation by multiple models at every time step, resulting in substantial inference latency and preventing a unified semantic representation across the reasoning stages. The motivation is to establish a unified semantic basis for all reasoning stages and eliminate redundant computations to significantly reduce perceptual overhead and improve real-time performance.

2. **Enhancing Reliable Target Confirmation under Distractors:** In open-vocabulary settings, distinguishing the specified target object from visually similar distractors or transient false detections presents a significant challenge. A high confidence score for an object detection does not inherently guarantee it is the true target. Prior methods often rely on simple thresholding or averaging detection scores, which can be insufficient for robust target confirmation. The motivation is to develop a mechanism that explicitly separates genuine target support from the false confidence of visually similar distractors, accurately accumulates evidence over time and space, and utilizes the absence of expected detections as negative evidence to suppress unsupported hypotheses.

3. **Improving Productive Exploration under Weak Semantic Cues:** When semantic cues are weak, ambiguous, or distant, the robot's exploration strategy can become inefficient. Many existing frontier selection methods focus solely on semantic relevance or geometric reachability, without comprehensively evaluating how much new information a reachable frontier is expected to provide relative to its traversal cost. This can result in an agent oscillating between distant, low-yield frontiers or pursuing paths that consume excessive resources without gaining decisive evidence. The motivation is to devise an active exploration strategy that sustains productive exploration by selecting frontiers that maximize information gain at minimal traversal cost, thereby guiding the robot toward viewpoints most likely to reduce uncertainty and reveal decisive target evidence.

By addressing these three bottlenecks, AECNav aims to provide a structured, training-free pipeline that integrates efficient perception, reliable target confirmation, and productive exploration into a cohesive navigation framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AECNav reformulates the ZSON task as an evidence-driven perception-to-decision problem, integrating three core components: Evidence-Gated Perception, Evidence Consolidation, and Active Evidence Acquisition. The system receives egocentric RGB-D observations and its pose, with the goal category specified by an arbitrary name.

**A. Evidence-Gated Perception**
This module aims to provide a unified semantic understanding while optimizing perceptual efficiency.
- **Shared Visual Encoder:** AECNav utilizes a single C-RADIOv4 encoder to process RGB images. This encoder outputs a scene-level `summary token` ($z^t_{sum}$) and multiple patch-level `patch tokens` ($z^t_{patch}$). The goal category `g` is encoded once into a text embedding ($e_g$) using a SigLIP2 text encoder.
- **Scene-Level Relevance:** The `summary token` is adapted by a SigLIP2 adaptor `f` and its cosine similarity with the goal embedding ($f(z^t_{sum})$, $e_g$) is computed to generate a scene relevance score ($\sigma^t_{scene}$). This score is then projected onto a top-down `value map` ($V_t$) which accumulates scene-level relevance over the robot's trajectory, serving as a prior for exploration.
- **Patch-Level Target Indicator:** The `patch tokens` are similarly adapted by `f` to compute per-patch similarities with the goal embedding. The maximum of these similarities ($\sigma^t_{patch}$) provides a localized preliminary signal for target presence.
- **Evidence-Gated Segmentation:** To reduce computational overhead, the computationally expensive SAM3 segmentation decoder is activated only when the patch-level similarity $\sigma^t_{patch}$ exceeds a predefined threshold ($\tau_{gate}$). If the threshold is not met, the segmentation step is skipped entirely. This mechanism allows the shared visual features to be routed to the SAM3 decoder without re-encoding the image when a probable target region is identified, resulting in dynamic, on-demand instance-level detection.

**B. Evidence Consolidation**
This module is responsible for robustly confirming open-vocabulary targets by accumulating spatial evidence over time and explicitly handling distractors.
- **3D Cluster Management:** When the segmentation gate triggers SAM3, detected instances are back-projected using depth information to form 3D point clouds. These point clouds are then associated with the nearest existing 3D spatial clusters based on geometric proximity. New clusters are initialized for detections that do not match existing clusters.
- **Log-Odds Belief Model:** Each cluster $C_k$ maintains a scalar log-odds belief $l(C_k)$ representing the likelihood that $C_k$ corresponds to the goal object. The belief is initialized to 0 (neutral) for new clusters.
- **Additive Belief Update:** Upon new observations, the belief of each observed cluster is updated using an additive log-odds formulation: $l(C_k) \leftarrow l(C_k) + \Delta l_{sem}(C_k) + \Delta l_{miss}(C_k)$. The belief values are clamped within a defined range $[l_{min}, l_{max}]$ to prevent saturation.
- **Semantic Evidence ($\Delta l_{sem}$):**
 - An LLM is employed offline to generate a small set of "confuser" categories (e.g., "sofa" for "chair").
 - SAM3 is prompted with both the goal and confuser categories, providing a goal score ($s_g$) and a highest confuser score ($s_{conf}$) for each detection. Detections below a threshold ($\tau_{det}$) are discarded.
 - The semantic update $\Delta l_{sem}$ is calculated based on the comparison of $s_g$ and $s_{conf}$:
 - If $s_g \geq s_{conf} + \delta$ (goal clearly dominates): Positive evidence is added.
 - If $s_{conf} \geq s_g + \delta$ (confuser clearly dominates): Negative evidence is added, suppressing the cluster's belief.
 - Otherwise (ambiguous cases within a neutral margin $\delta$): No change.
 - A spatial weight $\rho_k$ (overlap between new instance and cluster) and a scaling factor $\alpha_{sem}$ are applied.
- **Missing Detection Evidence ($\Delta l_{miss}$):** If a cluster with a positive belief falls within the camera's field of view (visibility fraction $v_k$) but is not detected in the current frame, its absence is treated as negative evidence. This results in a negative update to its belief, scaled by $\alpha_{miss}$ and a miss prior $s_{miss}$.
- **Stopping Criterion:** The robot issues a stop action only when a cluster's belief exceeds a confidence threshold ($\tau_{stop}$) consistently over a temporal window (e.g., $\geq2$ of 5 consecutive frames) and the robot is sufficiently close (e.g., within 0.5 m) to the confirmed cluster.

**C. Active Evidence Acquisition**
This module guides the robot's exploration strategy when the accumulated evidence is insufficient for target confirmation.
- **Composite Utility Function:** Frontiers are selected based on a composite utility function $U_t(f; g)$ that balances three factors: semantic relevance ($S_t$), information gain ($G_t$), and traversal cost ($C_t$).
 $U_t(f; g) = \tilde{e^{S_t(f; g)}} + \lambda_{info} \tilde{e^{G_t(f)}} - \lambda_{dist} \tilde{e^{C_t(f)}}$
 where $\tilde{\cdot}$ denotes min-max normalization, and $\lambda_{info}, \lambda_{dist}$ are weighting coefficients.
- **Semantic Relevance ($S_t$):** This term is derived from the accumulated value map $V_t$ around each frontier, guiding the robot towards areas semantically likely to contain the target.
- **Information Gain ($G_t$):** For each candidate frontier $f$, the shortest traversable path $\pi_t(f)$ from the robot is computed via BFS on the occupancy grid. Viewpoints are sampled along this path, and rays are cast from these viewpoints to estimate the amount of unobserved space (unknown cells) that would be revealed by traversing to and observing from the frontier.
- **Traversal Cost ($C_t$):** This term penalizes long paths, calculated as the length of the shortest traversable path $\pi_t(f)$, discouraging expensive detours.
- **Navigation Loop:** The robot explores by navigating to the selected frontier. The new observations from this exploration then re-enter the perception and belief update stages, closing the loop until the target is confirmed. A low-level PointNav policy is used for waypoint navigation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AECNav was conducted on three benchmarks in the Habitat simulator: HM3D-v2, MP3D, and HM3D-OVON, using standard metrics of Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL). Efficiency was measured by average episode runtime and steps. Real-world deployment was performed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot.

**Quantitative Results on Navigation Accuracy (Table I):**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** AECNav consistently outperformed all previous methods across all three benchmarks without task-specific training.
- **HM3D-v2:** Achieved 84.7% SR and 45.3% SPL, an improvement of 6.6% SR and 5.1% SPL over the previous state-of-the-art (TrajRAG).
- **MP3D:** Reached 51.3% SR and 25.9% SPL, exceeding the previous best (WMNav) by 5.9% SR and 8.7% SPL. The significant SPL improvement suggests reduced unnecessary detours in complex environments.
- **HM3D-OVON:** Demonstrated 57.3% SR and 30.5% SPL, surpassing MSGNav by 9.0% SR on this challenging open-vocabulary benchmark. This improvement aligns with AECNav's explicit handling of visually similar distractors.

**Efficiency Comparison (Table II & III):**
- **Reduced Runtime and Steps:** On HM3D-v2, AECNav completed episodes in an average of 24.39 seconds, which is 2.2 times faster than VLFM (53.36 s), and took the fewest steps (108.63 steps), 33% fewer than the next best (ASCENT).
- **Perception Pipeline Analysis:**
 - Replacing multi-model perception with a unified C-RADIO backbone resulted in an 8.8% SR and 8.6% SPL gain, with a 1.6x reduction in latency (0.402 s/step down to 0.248 s/step). This indicates the benefit of a unified semantic basis.
 - The evidence-gated mechanism further enhanced efficiency. At $\tau_{gate}=0.08$, 62.5% of SAM3 calls were skipped, preserving full accuracy (84.7% SR) while achieving 2.3x speedup compared to the multi-model baseline.

**Ablation Studies (Table IV & V, Fig. 3):**
- **Contribution of Main Components (Table IV):**
 - "w/o Evidence-Gated Perception" (using separate BLIP-2, YOLOv7, MobileSAM): Led to an 8.8% drop in SR and 8.7% drop in SPL, highlighting the importance of a shared semantic basis.
 - "w/o Evidence Consolidation" (navigating to first detection): Caused the largest performance drop, 12.8% SR and 4.8% SPL, confirming the criticality of structured evidence accumulation for accurate target confirmation under interference.
 - "w/o Active Evidence Acquisition" (semantic relevance only for exploration): Resulted in a 2.9% drop in SR and 3.5% drop in SPL, indicating the value of considering observational value and reachability.
- **Evidence Consolidation Breakdown (Table V):**
 - Using only goal score-based log-odds accumulation yielded 81.9% SR, a 10% improvement over no consolidation, demonstrating the benefit of temporal evidence aggregation.
 - Adding the confuser term (explicitly suppressing distractors) further improved SR by 1.8% (to 83.7%).
 - Adding the missing detection term (correcting false positives) further improved SR by 1.3% (to 83.2%).
 - The nearly additive gains from these terms suggest they address distinct error modes.
- **Exploration Weights Ablation (Fig. 3):**
 - Including the traversal cost term ($ \lambda_{dist} $) alone significantly improved SR and SPL (from 81.8% to 84.3% SR, and 41.8% to 44.3% SPL).
 - Information gain ($ \lambda_{info} $) was effective when combined with the cost term but provided minimal benefit alone, suggesting it prevents inefficient long excursions. Over-weighting either term reduced performance.

**Real-World Deployment (Table VI & VII, Fig. 4):**
- **High Success Rate:** AECNav was successfully deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, achieving a 95% success rate (38 out of 40 episodes) across eight open-vocabulary targets in four indoor scenes.
- **Robustness to Distractors:** Trials involving targets like "chair" near "sofas" demonstrated the method's ability to distinguish targets from visually similar distractors.
- **Real-time Operation:** The system maintained a decision rate of approximately 5 Hz (197 ms per decision) in real-world settings.
- **Qualitative Observations (Fig. 4):** Case studies illustrated how confuser evidence effectively rejected distractors (e.g., sofas when searching for a chair) and how miss evidence corrected false positives by reducing belief when a detected object failed to reappear upon closer inspection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of AECNav marks an advancement in zero-shot open-vocabulary object navigation for mobile robots. By integrating a unified perception pipeline, a robust evidence consolidation mechanism, and an active information-aware exploration strategy, the research addresses key limitations of prior methods related to efficiency, accuracy, and reliability in challenging, unseen environments.

The achieved state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmark datasets, including significant improvements in both success rate and efficiency metrics, demonstrates the efficacy of the proposed components. The substantial reduction in inference overhead, partly due to the evidence-gated perception, and the fewer steps required per episode, attributed to active evidence acquisition, highlight AECNav's operational advantages.

Furthermore, the successful deployment and high success rate on a physical quadruped robot in real-world indoor environments underscore the practical viability and robustness of the system. The ability to distinguish targets from visually similar distractors and correct for transient false positives, as demonstrated by the evidence consolidation module, is particularly impactful for real-world scenarios where ambiguity is common. The sustained real-time decision rate of approximately 5 Hz on physical hardware suggests that AECNav is suitable for practical, real-time autonomous navigation applications.

This work contributes to the advancement of embodied artificial intelligence by enabling robots to navigate and locate arbitrary objects more reliably and efficiently in complex, dynamic, and previously unmapped environments. The framework's training-free nature and reliance on general-purpose vision-language models offer increased flexibility and generalization capabilities compared to methods requiring extensive task-specific training. Potential impacts include improved capabilities for service robotics, logistics, search and rescue operations, and general-purpose autonomous mobile manipulation platforms that require robust object search in diverse, unstructured settings.
