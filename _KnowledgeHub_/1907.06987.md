# A Short Note on the Kinetics-700 Human Action Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> Google and DeepMind released Kinetics-700, an expanded dataset for human action recognition featuring 700 classes and 650,317 YouTube video clips. This extension incorporates more diverse and fine-grained actions, presenting a more challenging benchmark for video understanding models, with baseline I3D-RGB achieving 57.3% Top-1 accuracy on the test set.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks require vast amounts of diverse, annotated video data for robust human action recognition.
- Existing datasets need continuous expansion to cover a wider range of actions, including fine-grained distinctions, and to counteract video link decay.
- The need for a standardized, challenging benchmark to evaluate and compare advanced video understanding models.

## Method

- Expanded the Kinetics dataset to 700 action classes and over 650,000 unique video clips from YouTube.
- Introduced a refined data collection pipeline, including sourcing new, more fine-grained, and "imaginative" action classes, along with "topping up" existing classes.
- Enhanced data diversity by using multi-language queries (English, French, Portuguese, Spanish) for video matching and crowdsourced human verification.

## Results

- Kinetics-700 contains 700 action classes and 650,317 video clips, a 30% increase in clip count from Kinetics-600, with 597 original classes retained.
- I3D-RGB baseline model achieved 57.3% Top-1 accuracy and 79.9% Top-5 accuracy on the Kinetics-700 test set, indicating a harder task than Kinetics-400 (68.4%) and Kinetics-600 (71.7%).
- The dataset shows improved geographic diversity, with clips sourced from Latin America increasing from 3% in K-400 to 8% in K-700.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling video datasets with increased class diversity and granularity provides a more challenging benchmark for state-of-the-art action recognition models.
> - Leveraging multi-language queries and advanced matching algorithms significantly enhances the cultural and geographical diversity of video datasets.
> - The transition to crowdsourced human verification, while efficient, may contribute to increased task difficulty or less "unimodal" class definitions compared to manual expert curation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of computer vision, specifically focusing on human action recognition in videos. The development of large-scale, curated datasets is fundamental for advancing deep learning research, as these datasets provide the necessary training and evaluation material for complex neural network architectures. The Kinetics dataset series (Kinetics-400, Kinetics-600, and now Kinetics-700) has established itself as a prominent benchmark, enabling researchers to develop and compare video understanding models.

The continuous expansion of the Kinetics dataset addresses several challenges in the research landscape:
1. **Data Scarcity for Deep Learning:** Deep neural networks require vast amounts of annotated data to learn robust features. Increasing the scale of video datasets directly facilitates the development of more capable and generalizable action recognition models.
2. **Dataset Diversity and Granularity:** As models become more sophisticated, there is a growing need for datasets that cover a wider and more diverse range of actions, including fine-grained distinctions and actions performed in varied contexts. Kinetics-700's inclusion of new and more specific classes aims to meet this need.
3. **Benchmarking and Comparison:** Standardized datasets with consistent splits (training, validation, test) are crucial for fair comparison of different algorithmic approaches. The Kinetics dataset, particularly through its use in ActivityNet challenges, serves this purpose.
4. **Addressing Dataset Decay:** Online video resources, such as YouTube, are dynamic. Videos can be deleted or unlisted over time, leading to dataset obsolescence. The ongoing effort to "top up" existing classes in Kinetics demonstrates a strategy to maintain the dataset's utility and longevity.
5. **Multicultural and Geographic Representation:** Efforts to source data using multiple languages contribute to a more geographically and culturally diverse dataset, which can help in developing models that are less biased towards specific demographics.

Overall, Kinetics-700 represents an incremental but significant step in providing the foundational data infrastructure necessary for pushing the boundaries of human action recognition and broader video understanding research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to describe and release the Kinetics-700 dataset, an extension of the existing DeepMind Kinetics human action dataset. This involves detailing the modifications from previous versions, providing comprehensive statistics, and presenting baseline performance results using a standard neural network architecture.

The motivation behind creating and extending the Kinetics dataset to 700 classes is multi-faceted:

- **Facilitating Neural Network Development:** The overarching goal of the Kinetics project is to provide a large-scale, curated dataset of video clips. This dataset is intended for training and exploring neural network architectures specifically designed for modeling human actions in video. The expansion to 700 classes and 650,000 clips provides a larger and potentially more challenging resource for this purpose.
- **Increasing Action Diversity and Granularity:** The researchers aimed to cover a more diverse range of human actions. This involved sourcing new action classes from recent human action datasets like EPIC-Kitchens and AVA. Furthermore, some existing general classes from Kinetics-600 were refined into more fine-grained variations (e.g., "picking fruit" was replaced by "picking apples" and "picking blueberries"), thereby increasing the specificity of action categories. The introduction of "imaginative" classes, such as "making slime" or "being in zero gravity," further broadened the scope of actions represented.
- **Maintaining Dataset Integrity and Scale:** YouTube videos, the source for Kinetics clips, are subject to deletion or unlisting. This natural decay rate (approximately 3% per year) necessitates an ongoing process to "top up" existing classes to maintain the desired number of clips per class. The Kinetics-700 release contributes to this continuous maintenance and expansion.
- **Advancing Towards a Larger Goal:** Kinetics-700 is explicitly presented as "another step towards our original goal of producing an action classification dataset with 1000 classes." This indicates a long-term vision for continuously scaling the dataset to support increasingly complex and comprehensive video understanding tasks.
- **Providing a Standardized Benchmark:** The dataset includes a standard validation set with publicly released labels and a held-out test set for challenge participation. This structured approach aims to provide a consistent and challenging benchmark for researchers to evaluate and compare the performance of their action recognition models.
- **Enhancing Cultural and Geographic Diversity:** By incorporating query texts translated into multiple languages (French, Portuguese, Spanish), the dataset collection process sought to access a broader range of YouTube content. This was motivated by the desire to promote greater dataset diversity by including a more well-rounded representation of cultures, ethnicities, and geographies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of Kinetics-700 involved an evolution of the data collection process established for Kinetics-400 and Kinetics-600, while adhering to the core principles of the dataset series.

**A. Dataset Principles and Statistics:**
- **Source:** All video clips are extracted from YouTube videos.
- **Clip Length:** Each clip is approximately 10 seconds long.
- **Resolution and Frame Rate:** Clips have variable resolution and frame rates.
- **Uniqueness:** For each action class, all clips originate from different YouTube videos.
- **Class Count:** The dataset contains 700 human action classes, an increase from 600 in Kinetics-600.
- **Clip Count:** Each class has a minimum of 600 video clips. The total number of clips increased by 30% from Kinetics-600, reaching approximately 650,317 clips in Kinetics-700, compared to around 500,000 previously.
- **Superset Relationship:** Kinetics-700 is almost a superset of Kinetics-600, retaining 597 out of 600 original classes. Three classes were renamed or split into more fine-grained subclasses.
- **Splits:** Kinetics-700 includes a standard training set (545,317 clips), a validation set (50 clips per class), and a held-out test set (100 clips per class). Labels for the validation set are public, while test set labels are held out for challenge evaluation.

**B. Data Collection Process (Evolved Pipeline):**
The overall pipeline comprises five steps: (1) action class sourcing, (2) candidate video matching, (3) candidate clip selection, (4) human verification, and (5) quality analysis and filtering. The main differences in Kinetics-700 compared to prior versions occurred in steps 1, 2, and 4.

1. **Action Class Sourcing:**
 - New classes were identified from existing human action datasets (e.g., EPIC-Kitchens, AVA).
 - General classes from Kinetics-600 were sometimes replaced or augmented with more fine-grained variations (e.g., "picking fruit" evolved into "picking apples," "picking blueberries").
 - New, "imaginative" classes were also introduced.
 - Existing Kinetics-600 classes were "topped up" with new clips to compensate for deleted or unlisted YouTube videos.

2. **Candidate Video Matching:**
 - **Class Name vs. Query Text:** A formal distinction was made between the official "class name" (e.g., "canoeing or kayaking") and the "query text" used for searching (e.g., "canoeing and kayaking" or "abseiling" / "rappelling").
 - **Multiple Languages:** English query texts were automatically translated into French, Portuguese, and Spanish. These four languages were then used to broaden the search for candidate videos on YouTube, aiming to increase linguistic, cultural, and geographical diversity.
 - **Advanced Matching Algorithm:** Instead of direct textual matching, a method leveraging weighted n-gram representations of video metadata (including titles of related videos) was employed. This approach, compatible with multiple languages, aimed to provide a robust similarity score between a query and YouTube videos, generating a larger candidate pool. Videos exceeding five minutes were generally discouraged.

3. **Candidate Clip Selection:**
 - **Image Classifiers:** Candidate clips within selected videos were identified using image classifiers. These classifiers were trained based on user relevance feedback from Google Image Search for specific action queries (e.g., "climbing tree").
 - **Temporal Localization:** The classifiers were run at the frame level on relevant videos. Clips were then extracted around the top two responses (k=2) indicating the presence of the action. If a direct classifier for the class name was unavailable, classifiers related to the query text were used.

4. **Human Verification:**
 - **Primary Annotation:** Human annotators evaluated whether a given 10-second candidate clip contained the specified action. This step remained consistent with previous Kinetics versions.
 - **Crowdsourced Curation:** A significant change involved crowdsourcing the final human annotation stage, which was previously a manual effort by the researchers. This stage involved reviewing animated-gif thumbnails for each class, resolving ambiguities between potentially confusing classes (e.g., differentiating "diving" from "jumping into pool"). To facilitate crowdsourcing, tasks were divided into panels of 16 elements.
 - **Yield Analysis:** The proportion of candidate clips positively verified by human annotators ("yield") was analyzed. Classes with high yield typically involved continuous actions or actions clearly defined by their names, while low-yield classes often involved transient actions, ambiguous queries, or sparse online content.

**C. Baseline Performance Evaluation:**
- **Model Architecture:** The I3D (Inflated 3D ConvNet) neural network architecture was used as a baseline.
- **Input Modality:** Only RGB video inputs were used; optical flow was not incorporated.
- **Training Protocol:** The model was trained from scratch on the Kinetics-700 training set. Hyperparameters were tuned based on validation set performance. Training utilized 32 P100 GPUs with a batch size of 5 videos, using 64-frame clips for training and 251-frame clips for testing. SGD with momentum was employed, with a learning rate of 0.1, decaying by a factor of 10 upon loss saturation.
- **Metrics:** Performance was reported using Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy. For the ActivityNet Kinetics challenge, the metric used was the average of Top-1 and Top-5 error.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Dataset Statistics and Characteristics:**
- **Scale:** Kinetics-700 comprises 700 action classes and a total of 650,317 video clips. This represents a 30% increase in clip count compared to Kinetics-600, and more than double the size of the original Kinetics-400.
- **Class Details:** 597 out of 600 classes from Kinetics-600 were retained. One class was renamed, and two were split into multiple subclasses.
- **Data Splits:** The training set contains 545,317 clips, with 50 clips per class in the validation set and 100 clips per class in the test set.
- **Overlap:** Minimal overlap (under 3%) was observed between the Kinetics-700 test set and the Kinetics-600 splits, indicating that models trained on Kinetics-600 can be safely evaluated on Kinetics-700's test set. However, training on Kinetics-700 and evaluating on Kinetics-600 is not recommended due to significant overlap where Kinetics-600 validation/test clips are present in Kinetics-700's training set.
- **Geographic Diversity:** The introduction of multi-language queries resulted in an increase in clips sourced from Latin America (from 3% in K-400 to 8% in K-700), indicating improved diversity. North America remained the dominant source (over 50%), while Africa remained the least represented continent (1% in K-700).

**B. Data Collection Process Observations:**
- **Yield by Class:** The human verification process revealed significant variations in "yield" (the proportion of candidate clips positively verified).
 - **Highest Yield Classes:** Actions like 'busking', 'spinning poi', 'rope pushdown' exhibited high yields (e.g., > 0.9). These were often continuous actions or actions easily and unambiguously captured in videos and described by queries.
 - **Lowest Yield Classes:** Classes such as 'opening letter', 'adding fish to aquarium', 'getting inside balloon' showed very low yields (e.g., < 0.005). These often corresponded to transient actions, ambiguous query texts (especially after machine translation), or actions for which abundant online content might be scarce. The paper notes that most low-yield classes were not included in the final dataset.

**C. Baseline Performance (I3D-RGB on Kinetics-700):**
- **Validation Set Accuracy:**
 - Top-1: 58.7%
 - Top-5: 81.7%
 - Challenge Metric (average of Top-1 and Top-5 error): 29.8%
- **Test Set Accuracy:**
 - Top-1: 57.3%
 - Top-5: 79.9%
 - Challenge Metric: 31.4%
- **Comparison with Previous Kinetics Versions:** The I3D-RGB baseline performance on Kinetics-700 (Top-1 test accuracy of 57.3%) indicates a notably harder task compared to Kinetics-400 (68.4%) and Kinetics-600 (71.7%).
- **Possible Reasons for Increased Difficulty:** The authors suggest potential factors contributing to the increased difficulty, including the shift to crowdsourced human verification (which might lead to less "unimodal" or pure classes) and possible minor distribution shifts between train and test sets (though validation and test performances were similar).
- **Easiest and Hardest Classes:** The paper provides lists of the 20 easiest and 20 hardest classes for the I3D-RGB model, offering insights into types of actions that are currently more or less challenging for models to recognize. Examples of easiest classes include 'playing accordion' and 'zumba', while hardest classes include 'hugging' and 'brushing teeth'.
- **Kinetics Challenge 2019:** The ActivityNet Kinetics challenge for 2019, utilizing Kinetics-700, featured 15 participating teams. The top team achieved a 17.9% error rate, substantially outperforming the reported baseline I3D-RGB model's 29.3% error, suggesting significant room for algorithmic improvement on the new dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The release of Kinetics-700 carries several significant implications for the field of computer vision and artificial intelligence:

- **Advancing Action Recognition Research:** By providing a larger dataset (650,000 clips) with a greater number of distinct action classes (700), Kinetics-700 furnishes researchers with an expanded resource. This is crucial for training more robust and generalizable deep learning models for video-based human action recognition, pushing the state-of-the-art beyond what was achievable with previous versions.
- **Stimulating Development of More Sophisticated Models:** The observation that Kinetics-700 presents a harder action recognition task, evidenced by the lower baseline accuracy compared to Kinetics-400 and Kinetics-600, indicates that existing models have substantial room for improvement. This increased difficulty acts as a catalyst for researchers to develop more sophisticated architectures, training methodologies, and data augmentation techniques.
- **Fostering Fine-Grained Action Understanding:** The inclusion of more specific and fine-grained action classes (e.g., 'picking apples' vs. 'picking fruit') encourages the development of models capable of nuanced action discrimination, moving beyond broad category recognition towards a deeper understanding of human activities.
- **Enhancing Dataset Diversity and Reducing Bias:** The proactive integration of multi-language queries in the data collection process contributes to a more geographically and culturally diverse dataset. This effort can help mitigate biases present in datasets primarily sourced from a single linguistic or cultural context, potentially leading to more universally applicable action recognition systems.
- **Standardized Benchmarking and Progress Tracking:** Kinetics-700 continues to serve as a critical benchmark for evaluating video understanding systems. Its integration into the ActivityNet Kinetics challenge ensures a competitive environment that drives innovation and allows for a standardized comparison of different research methodologies, providing clear metrics for progress in the field.
- **Informing Large-Scale Dataset Creation:** The detailed account of the data collection methodology, including challenges like managing yield rates for different action types and the transition to crowdsourced verification, offers practical insights and lessons for the creation and maintenance of future large-scale video datasets. This transparency is valuable for the broader research community.
- **Addressing Dataset Longevity:** The continuous effort to "top up" existing classes to counteract video link degradation addresses a practical problem faced by video datasets derived from online sources. This approach enhances the long-term usability and reliability of Kinetics as a research resource.
- **Pathway to More Comprehensive Datasets:** Positioned as a step towards a 1000-class dataset, Kinetics-700 signals a commitment to further expanding the scope and scale of action recognition resources, indicating a long-term vision for foundational data in video AI.
