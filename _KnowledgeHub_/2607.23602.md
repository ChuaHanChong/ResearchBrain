# Action from Adjacent Set in Physical Space Outperforms the Best Prediction in World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Adjacent Set Action Reconstruction (ASAR), a post-scoring method that leverages the local geometry of low-cost action prefixes to synthesize more reliable action sequences in latent world model control. ASAR addresses the issue of "proposal overgeneration" by demonstrating that action reconstruction from a dense prefix region can outperform simply selecting the best predicted existing action, achieving up to 28.0 percentage points improvement in event completion success on Cube manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Latent world models used in robotic control can fail to select physically feasible actions due to residual prediction errors, even when the terminal cost function itself is accurate.
- Increasing the number of candidate action sequences (proposal budget) can paradoxically degrade action selection reliability, as a larger pool provides more opportunities for infeasible sequences to have misleadingly low predicted costs, a phenomenon termed "proposal overgeneration."
- Current world model control methods typically evaluate candidate action sequences independently and do not explicitly leverage the spatial relationships or distances between early action prefixes within the generated proposal pool.

## Method

- **ASAR** identifies an "==adjacent set==" of action prefixes by first selecting the M lowest-cost candidates and then computing a ==local prefix isolation score== for each prefix based on its distance to K nearest neighbors within the pool.
- A new full action sequence is reconstructed by taking a ==weighted aggregation== of the full action sequences from this adjacent set, using weights derived from both prefix distance to the least isolated center and standardized terminal cost.
- The reconstructed sequence is lightly mixed with the sequence that had the absolute minimum cost in the eligible pool to anchor it to the task objective, then its first action block is executed in a ==receding-horizon== manner.

## Results

- Diagnostic audits empirically confirmed "proposal overgeneration," showing that increasing the proposal budget from **72** to **288** significantly increased the mean number of infeasible "ranking blockers" and reduced the top-k exposure of feasible sequences.
- On a 75-query Carry and Release task, Kernel ASAR improved event completion success rates by up to **28.0** percentage points over minimum cost selection (from **0.067** to **0.347** at 72 proposals with latent cost).
- Targeted tests demonstrated that reconstructing an action sequence provided a **13.7** percentage point gain in environment success (**0.630** vs **0.493**) compared to simply selecting the least isolated existing sequence, confirming the value of synthesis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simply increasing the computational budget (number of proposals) in world models with residual prediction error can be counterproductive for action selection, leading to "proposal overgeneration" where more opportunities arise for misleadingly low-cost infeasible actions.
> - The local geometric density of action prefixes provides valuable, previously unutilized information for identifying robustly feasible action sequences, which can mitigate the impact of residual prediction errors.
> - Synthesizing a new action sequence by aggregating nearby low-cost prefixes can be more effective than merely selecting the best existing candidate, especially when the true optimal action might lie in an interpolated space between sampled candidates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of robotic control and reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on planning with learned latent world models. Modern control strategies, such as those employing latent model predictive control (MPC) and various Dreamer models, rely on predicting future states in a learned latent space to evaluate candidate action sequences. These methods typically involve sampling a pool of actions, using a world model to predict their terminal latent states, assigning a cost based on proximity to a goal, and then executing the first action of the lowest-cost sequence in a receding-horizon manner.

The paper identifies a specific challenge within this paradigm: "proposal overgeneration." This refers to a scenario where increasing the number of candidate action sequences (proposal budget) can paradoxically lead to a decrease in the reliability of selecting a physically feasible action, even when the pool contains feasible options. This degradation occurs because residual prediction errors in the world model can assign anomalously low costs to infeasible sequences, and a larger pool provides more opportunities for such errors to outrank genuinely feasible alternatives.

This research distinguishes itself from prior work in several ways:
- **Addressing Model Error Post-Prediction**: Unlike methods that improve the world model's predictive accuracy (e.g., [Janner et al., 2019], [Lambert et al., 2020]), enhance proposal generation (e.g., CEM variants like [Pinneri et al., 2021], [Zhang et al., 2022]), or refine the terminal cost function (e.g., Trajectory Reachability Metrics (TRM) [Li et al., 2026a]), this work focuses on a post-scoring intervention. It assumes a meaningful terminal cost but addresses situations where residual prediction error leads to misleadingly low costs for infeasible sequences.
- **Leveraging Action Prefix Distances**: The novelty lies in explicitly using the spatial relationships (distances) between early action prefixes within the generated proposal pool. Traditional methods do not typically incorporate these prefix-level distances in their selection process after candidate generation and cost assignment.
- **Action Reconstruction**: Instead of merely selecting an existing candidate from the pool, the proposed method, Adjacent Set Action Reconstruction (ASAR), can synthesize a new action sequence by aggregating nearby prefixes, which may not have been directly sampled.

Therefore, this paper contributes to the robustness and reliability of latent world model control by introducing a mechanism to refine action selection that is complementary to existing efforts in model improvement, proposal generation, and cost function design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reliability of action selection in latent world model control, particularly in scenarios where an increased number of sampled action sequences (larger proposal budget) can lead to suboptimal decisions.

The key motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Failure of Minimum Cost Selection under Residual Prediction Error**: The core motivation stems from the observation that controllers selecting actions based solely on the minimum predicted terminal cost can fail, even when the terminal cost function itself accurately reflects the physical task objective. This failure occurs because the learned world model may have residual prediction errors. These errors can cause the model to assign an "anomalously low cost" to an action sequence that, if executed in the physical world, would be infeasible or perform poorly.
- **The Problem of Proposal Overgeneration**: A critical insight is that increasing the "proposal budget" (the number of candidate action sequences sampled) does not always lead to better control performance. While a larger budget increases the *coverage* of potentially feasible sequences, it also increases the likelihood of sampling *more infeasible sequences* that happen to have misleadingly low predicted costs due to model error. This phenomenon is termed "proposal overgeneration." The authors provide empirical evidence from Cube manipulation tasks where increasing the budget from 72 to 288 proposals significantly reduced the feasibility of selected actions, despite the larger pools always containing feasible sequences. This highlights a ranking problem rather than a coverage problem.
- **Neglected Information in Action Prefixes**: Current world model control methods, including those using CEM, typically treat each candidate action sequence independently for evaluation and ranking. They do not explicitly leverage the relationships or distances between the early action prefixes within the generated proposal pool. The authors hypothesize that a region of low cost in the space of full action sequences, if it corresponds to a "robust feasible neighborhood," might be identifiable by nearby early prefixes. This local regularity could provide a valuable signal that is currently unutilized.
- **Need for a Post-Scoring Intervention**: The study observes that improving the quality of the terminal cost (e.g., using trajectory reachability metrics) can help but does not entirely eliminate the issue of infeasible sequences being ranked highly. This indicates a need for an intervention *after* the terminal cost has been assigned, to address residual prediction failures where a chosen cost is meaningful but prone to error in specific instances.
- **Reconstruction for Better Actions**: By identifying an "adjacent set" of low-cost action prefixes, the research aims not just to select the *least isolated existing* sequence, but to *reconstruct* a new, potentially more robust, full action sequence through weighted aggregation. This allows for synthesizing an action that might lie between sampled candidates, potentially leading to a more stable and successful execution.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of minimum-cost selection in latent world models, particularly under proposal overgeneration, by introducing a novel post-scoring mechanism that uses local prefix geometry to identify and reconstruct more robust action sequences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the concept of "proposal overgeneration" and proposes Adjacent Set Action Reconstruction (ASAR) to mitigate it. The methodology involves diagnosing the problem, defining the control pattern, and detailing the ASAR mechanism.

#### 3.1. Control Pattern and Problem Definition

The standard control pattern involves:
- **Observation Encoding**: Current and goal observations ($o_t, o_g$) are encoded into latent states ($z_t, z_g$) using an encoder $e$.
- **Prediction**: A learned predictor ($\hat{f}_H$) takes the current latent state ($z_t$) and a candidate action sequence ($A_i$) to produce a predicted terminal latent state ($\hat{z}_{i,H}$).
- **Cost Assignment**: A terminal cost function $Q$ assigns a scalar cost $q_i = Q(\hat{z}_{i,H}, z_g)$. Two cost functions are considered:
 - **Latent Cost**: Euclidean distance, $Q(\hat{z}, z_g) = \parallel \hat{z} - z_g \parallel_2^2$.
 - **Reachability Cost**: A trajectory-based cost from Trajectory Reachability Metrics (TRM) [Li et al., 2026a].
- **Selection and Execution**: The controller traditionally selects the action sequence with the minimum cost and executes its first action block in a receding-horizon fashion.

The authors define a "feasible set" $E_\epsilon(x_t, o_g)$ for actions achieving task conditions within tolerance $\epsilon$. A "ranking blocker" is an infeasible proposal whose predicted cost ($q_b$) is lower than the minimum cost of any feasible proposal ($min_{i:Y_i=1} q_i$). The "blocker count" is the number of such infeasible proposals.

#### 3.2. Diagnosis of Proposal Overgeneration

To diagnose proposal overgeneration, the researchers conducted candidate execution audits in a simulated Cube manipulation task:
- **Independent Execution**: In these audits, every candidate action sequence in a given proposal pool was executed independently from the same initial state in the physical environment to determine its true feasibility ($Y_i$).
- **Metrics**: They measured "top-k exposure" (fraction of pools with a feasible sequence among the k lowest costs) and "mean blockers" (average number of infeasible sequences with lower cost than the best feasible one).
- **Scaling Analysis**: The audits observed how these metrics changed with increasing "total proposal budget" (number of candidates in a pool), showing that while feasible sequence *presence* might improve, *top-k ranking* and *mean blockers* can degrade.

#### 3.3. Adjacent Set Action Reconstruction (ASAR)

ASAR is a post-scoring rule that processes the final proposal pool to reconstruct an action sequence. It operates on sequences already identified as having low terminal cost.

#### 3.3.1. Adjacent Set Identification

1. **Candidate Restriction**: ASAR first considers a subset of candidates, $R_M(Q)$, which are the M candidates with the lowest terminal costs.
2. **Action Prefix Extraction and Standardization**: For each candidate in $R_M(Q)$, the first $h$ model action blocks are vectorized into an action prefix $p_i$. These prefixes are robustly standardized (using median and interquartile range) within the pool.
3. **Local Prefix Isolation Score ($u_i$)**: For each prefix $p_i$, a score $u_i$ is computed as the mean Euclidean distance to its K nearest other standardized prefixes (e.g., $K=3$). A smaller $u_i$ indicates that the prefix is less isolated and belongs to a denser region in the prefix space. This serves as a proxy for local prefix density.

#### 3.3.2. Full Action Sequence Reconstruction (Kernel ASAR)

1. **Center Identification**: The candidate $c$ with the smallest $u_i$ (least isolated prefix) is chosen as the "adjacent set center."
2. **Adjacent Set Formation**: An adjacent set $S$ is identified, consisting of a fixed number of prefixes (e.g., 12) nearest to $p_c$ among the least isolated candidates (e.g., within the 32 least isolated).
3. **Weighted Aggregation**: A weighted average of the full action sequences ($A_i$) from the adjacent set $S$ is computed. The weights $w_i$ are determined by a kernel function that considers both the distance of $p_i$ from $p_c$ and the robustly standardized terminal cost ($\tilde{q}_i$):
 $w_i \propto \exp \left( - \frac{\parallel p_i - p_c \parallel_2^2}{\tau_d} - \lambda \tilde{q}_i \right)$
 where $\tau_d$ is a kernel temperature and $\lambda$ is a weight for the terminal cost energy.
4. **Minimum Cost Anchor**: The reconstructed sequence $A_{rec}$ is then combined with the sequence $A_Q$ that had the absolute minimum cost in the eligible pool, using a light mixture coefficient $\alpha$:
 $A_{out} = (1 - \alpha) A_{rec} + \alpha A_Q$.
 This anchor retains some information from the task objective. The action parameterization must permit coordinate-wise interpolation.
5. **Fixed Parameters**: For Cube evaluations, Kernel ASAR typically uses 80 lowest-cost sequences, takes the first model action as prefix ($h=1$), considers $K=3$ neighbors for $u_i$, identifies 12 nearest sequences around the center from within the 32 least isolated candidates, and uses $\alpha=0.10$.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Environment**: Simulated Cube manipulation from OGBench [Park et al., 2024], using a fixed pretrained LeWM (Latent World Model) encoder, predictor, and CEM generator.
- **Proposal Budgets**: Total proposals of 72, 144, and 288 were used by drawing equally from three action noise scales.
- **Evaluation Sets**:
 - **Carry and Release**: 75 queries from 44 evaluation seeds, where the cube is already grasped and needs to be transported and released. Primary outcome: event completion success (environment success + final contact state consistent with release).
 - **Targeted Reconstruction**: 73 queries from 54 new seeds, designed to test reconstruction vs. selecting an existing candidate.
 - **Aggregate and Independent Model**: Larger 512-query evaluations, including one with independently trained world model weights.
- **Baselines**: Minimum cost selection (standard) and selection of the "least isolated existing sequence" (controls for prefix density use without reconstruction).
- **Comparisons**: ASAR (Kernel ASAR, Portfolio ASAR) was compared against baselines under both latent distance and TRM reachability costs, using matched random proposal draws for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across diagnostic audits and closed-loop evaluations:

#### 4.1. Proposal Overgeneration under Residual Model Error

- **Diagnostic Audit (Table 1, Figure 2)**: In an audit of 192 proposal pools, minimum latent cost selection failed in 132 pools. Among these failures, 58 pools (43.9%) contained a feasible alternative among the 80 lowest latent costs.
- **Budget-Induced Degradation**: In a separate evaluation set (32 pools for position targets, 32 for position+yaw targets), increasing the total proposal budget from 72 to 288 significantly reduced the "top-20 exposure" (fraction of pools with a feasible sequence among the 20 lowest costs). For position targets, it dropped from 0.969 to 0.656 under latent cost. For position+yaw targets, it dropped from 0.969 to 0.625. Concurrently, the mean number of infeasible "ranking blockers" with lower cost than the best feasible sequence substantially increased (e.g., from 4.31 to 16.91 for position targets under latent cost).
- **Closed-Loop Impact**: In closed-loop control, the feasibility of the selected sequence by minimum latent cost fell from 0.375 to 0.062 for position targets and from 0.344 to 0.031 for position+yaw targets when the budget increased from 72 to 288. Using the trajectory reachability cost improved these rates but did not eliminate the degradation. These results demonstrate that larger proposal pools can contain more prediction errors with misleadingly low costs, making minimum-cost selection less reliable.

#### 4.2. Adjacent-Set Reconstruction Improves under Both Terminal Costs

- **Carry and Release Evaluation (Table 2, Figure 4)**: On a 75-query Carry and Release evaluation set:
 - **Latent Cost**: Kernel ASAR improved event completion success over minimum cost selection by 28.0 percentage points at 72 proposals (from 0.067 to 0.347), 24.0 points at 144 proposals (from 0.053 to 0.293), and 18.7 points at 288 proposals (from 0.133 to 0.320).
 - **Reachability Cost**: Under the TRM reachability cost, Kernel ASAR showed gains of 18.7, 20.0, and 17.3 percentage points over minimum cost selection at 72, 144, and 288 proposals, respectively.
- **Control over Existing Candidate Selection**: Kernel ASAR also demonstrated improvement over the baseline that selects the "least isolated existing sequence" (which uses the same prefix-density proxy but no reconstruction). Under reachability cost, Kernel ASAR exceeded this baseline by 14.7 points at 72 proposals and 10.7 points at 144 proposals, providing evidence for the benefit of reconstruction beyond mere selection of a dense existing candidate.

#### 4.3. Reconstruction Improves beyond Selection of an Existing Candidate

- **Targeted 73-Query Test**: In a targeted test designed to compare reconstruction with selecting the least isolated existing sequence (on cases where kernel weight was spread across several adjacent candidates), Kernel ASAR achieved 0.630 environment success compared to 0.493 for selecting the least isolated existing sequence. This represents a gain of 13.7 percentage points, further supporting the value of synthesizing a new action sequence.
- **Independent World Model Replication**: The reconstruction advantage replicated with independently trained world model weights. At 72 proposals, the least isolated existing sequence reached 0.717 success, while Kernel ASAR reached 0.738, a paired difference of 2.1 points.

#### 4.4. Conditions and Limitations of ASAR Effectiveness

- **Aggregate Cube Gains**: On a larger 512-query aggregate Cube evaluation, Kernel ASAR showed a paired gain of 3.1 percentage points over minimum latent cost selection (0.742 vs 0.711 success).
- **Long-Horizon Complex Tasks**: For more complex "full pick-place" queries requiring composition of multiple distinct behaviors (grasp, lift, transport, lower, release), ASAR did not improve event completion success, suggesting that a single adjacent prefix set may not be sufficient to create sequences missing multiple distinct behaviors.
- **Domain Specificity**: Initial transfer tests to Reacher and Push-T domains with unchanged Cube parameters showed that the Cube prefix metric is not universally effective. ASAR results were neutral or negative in these domains, indicating that its positive impact appears when feasible sequences with low cost occupy a locally dense prefix region suitable for interpolation, implying a dependency on task-specific action space geometry and proposal distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of robotic control with latent world models, with potential impacts on the reliability and performance of such systems:

- **Novel Understanding of World Model Limitations**: The identification and detailed characterization of "proposal overgeneration" provide a critical insight into a failure mode not widely acknowledged in the literature. It highlights that simply increasing computational budget (more proposals) in world models with residual prediction error can be counterproductive for action selection. This reframes the challenge from merely achieving coverage to ensuring reliable ranking amidst noisy predictions.
- **Enhanced Reliability of Action Selection**: ASAR offers a complementary approach to robust control. By leveraging the local geometric relationships of action prefixes within a candidate pool, it provides a mechanism to filter out potentially misleading low-cost predictions and synthesize more reliable actions. This enhances the robustness of planning, particularly in tasks where precise manipulation and contact interactions are crucial, as demonstrated in the Cube manipulation environment.
- **Complementary to Existing Improvements**: ASAR is designed to be compatible with other advancements in world model control. It assumes a meaningful terminal cost (which can be improved by methods like TRM) and can work with existing world models and proposal generators. This modularity allows for potential integration into existing systems to further boost their performance and safety.
- **Improved Task Completion in Challenging Scenarios**: The demonstrated gains in "event completion success" for Carry and Release tasks show that ASAR can enable controllers to achieve physical objectives more reliably, even when standard minimum-cost selection fails. This is particularly relevant for tasks requiring object interaction and precise placement.
- **Foundation for Future Research**:
 - **Geometric-aware Planning**: The work establishes a new direction for research into "geometric-aware planning," where the spatial properties of candidate action sequences are explicitly considered during selection or reconstruction. This could lead to novel planning algorithms that are more sensitive to the structure of the action space.
 - **Adaptive Reconstruction**: The observation that ASAR's effectiveness is context-dependent (e.g., specific Cube tasks but not universally on Reacher or Push-T) suggests avenues for future work on adaptive ASAR variants. This could involve learning context-dependent parameters or choosing between different reconstruction strategies based on detected properties of the current proposal pool or task state.
 - **Theoretical Guarantees for Reconstruction**: The theoretical propositions (e.g., on radius support separation and reconstructed sequence containment) lay groundwork for developing more rigorous guarantees about when ASAR can reliably improve outcomes. Further research could focus on empirically verifying these conditions or developing planning-time diagnostics to assess their applicability.

While the study acknowledges limitations such as domain specificity and conditions for effectiveness, ASAR represents a significant step towards developing more reliable and nuanced decision-making processes in complex robotic control tasks governed by learned latent world models.
