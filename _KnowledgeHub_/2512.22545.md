# Self-Rewarded Multimodal Coherent Reasoning Across Diverse Visual Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-sen University and Alibaba Group developed Self-Rewarded Multimodal Coherent Reasoning (SR-MCR), a label-free framework that aligns Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by leveraging intrinsic process signals. This approach achieved an 81.4% average accuracy across diverse benchmarks, outperforming comparable open-source models, and demonstrated significantly improved reasoning coherence as preferred in 90.82% of blind evaluations.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently exhibit unreliable reasoning, lacking step-to-step coherence and sufficient visual grounding, despite producing fluent responses.
- Current MLLM alignment methods (Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback, Direct Preference Optimization) primarily focus on supervising the final output, neglecting the quality and coherence of the intermediate reasoning process.
- Existing reinforcement learning-based alignment techniques are costly due to their reliance on extensive human-labeled preference data or external evaluators, making them expensive and brittle.

## Method

- SR-MCR introduces a unified, label-free framework that leverages ==intrinsic process signals== to guide MLLM alignment.
- It computes a ==composite self-reward== based on five cues: semantic consistency, lexical fidelity, non-redundancy, visual grounding, and step-wise coherence, derived from external frozen tools.
- The framework employs a critic-free ==Generalized Reversed Policy Optimization (GRPO)== objective with a ==confidence-aware cooling mechanism== to stabilize training under noisy self-rewards and improve efficiency.

## Results

- SR-MCR-7B achieved an average accuracy of **81.4%** across a diverse suite of multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MMMU, MMBench), demonstrating a **+6.6%** improvement over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model and surpassing comparable open-source models.
- A blind A/B study with GPT-4o revealed that SR-MCR-7B's reasoning traces were preferred in **90.82%** of cases over those from the base MLLM, indicating enhanced clarity, logical soundness, and completeness of intermediate reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of all five self-reward components, especially visual grounding and step-wise coherence; external tool-based rewards consistently outperformed LLM-as-a-judge methods in preventing hallucination and improving grounding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly supervising the internal reasoning process of MLLMs using fine-grained, intrinsic signals leads to more coherent, reliable, and visually grounded outputs than solely supervising final answers.
> - Utilizing objective, tool-based evaluators for self-rewards is crucial for multimodal reasoning, effectively preventing "self-delusion" and enhancing factual grounding compared to LLM-as-a-judge approaches.
> - Online policy optimization with a critic-free algorithm like GRPO and confidence-aware regularization is effective for learning from continuous and potentially noisy self-rewards, bypassing the limitations of offline filtering or complex value functions.
