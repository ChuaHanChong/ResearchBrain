# V-GRPO: Online Reinforcement Learning for Denoising Generative Models Is Easier than You Think

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University, Tsinghua University, Amazon FAR, and the University of Pennsylvania developed V-GRPO, an online reinforcement learning method that uses ELBO-based surrogates to align denoising generative models with human preferences. This approach, designed for stability and efficiency, achieved state-of-the-art performance in text-to-image synthesis and demonstrated significant speedups over existing methods.

## Problem

- Aligning post-training denoising generative models (diffusion, flow matching) with human preferences or verifiable rewards using online reinforcement learning is challenging.
- Existing Markov Decision Process (MDP)-based methods for this task are often inefficient, inflexible in their sampling process, and lead to complex algorithmic designs.
- Alternative ELBO-based surrogate methods, while conceptually simpler, have historically shown significant underperformance and instability in visual generation tasks.

## Method

- **V-GRPO** integrates ==ELBO-based surrogates== into the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization== (**GRPO**) algorithm, treating multi-step generation as an atomic action for optimization based on a marginal likelihood surrogate.
- Three techniques are introduced to reduce surrogate variance: group-shared timestep-noise pairs, ==stratified timestep sampling==, and adaptive loss weighting.
- Gradient step control mechanisms are employed to prevent overly aggressive policy updates, including importance ratio clipping, a Kullback-Leibler (KL) penalty, and advantage soft-clipping.

## Results

- V-GRPO achieved state-of-the-art performance on FLUX.1-dev, outperforming all evaluated MDP-based baselines (DanceGRPO, BranchGRPO, MixGRPO) across all reward metrics, with a UnifiedReward of 3.45 compared to MixGRPO's 3.42.
- The method demonstrated a **2x** speedup in convergence time compared to MixGRPO on FLUX.1-dev and required approximately **3x** fewer gradient steps than DiffusionNFT on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium while matching its performance (e.g., 6.02 HPSv2.1 score vs. 6.01 for DiffusionNFT).
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three proposed surrogate variance reduction techniques were crucial for stable training and optimal performance, and that different gradient step control methods provided specific benefits in various training scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ELBO-based underperformance in visual generation stems from surrogate variance, not intrinsic limits; variance reduction (group-shared timesteps, stratified sampling, adaptive weighting) stabilizes training and unlocks superior performance.
> - Decoupling the optimization process from the sampling rollout in ELBO-based methods enables the use of more efficient higher-order or ordinary differential equation (ODE) samplers.
> - Strategic application of gradient control techniques can effectively manage policy deviation, preserve prior model capabilities, and stabilize training across various reward functions and sampling constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of post-training denoising generative models, such as diffusion and flow matching models, through online reinforcement learning (RL). The objective of such post-training is to align these models with human preferences or verifiable rewards, a challenge that has gained prominence following successes in large language models (LLMs) using similar RL techniques.

Existing online RL approaches for denoising generative models typically fall into two main categories:
1. **Markov Decision Process (MDP)-based methods:** These approaches frame the generation process as an MDP by imposing a stochastic sampling process. The joint probability of a sampling trajectory is factorized into a sequence of tractable Gaussian reverse transition kernels, which induces a sequential state-action space allowing for direct application of policy gradient methods. While theoretically sound, this formulation has several limitations:
 - **Inefficiency:** It often suffers from slow convergence during training.
 - **Inflexibility:** It restricts sampling to first-order stochastic differential equation (SDE) discretizations, preventing the use of more efficient higher-order or ordinary differential equation (ODE) solvers.
 - **Complexity:** It creates a tight coupling between optimization and rollout, leading to elaborate algorithmic designs in prior works (e.g., MixGRPO, BranchGRPO) to mitigate these issues.
2. **ELBO-based surrogate methods:** This alternative approach utilizes pretraining objectives, which are closely related to the diffusion Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), as tractable surrogates for the model log-likelihood within policy gradient methods. Despite its conceptual simplicity and connection to the model's variational roots, prior work (e.g., DDPO, FPO) has reported that this approach significantly underperforms in visual generation tasks.

This work directly addresses the reported limitations of ELBO-based methods in visual generation. It posits that the underperformance of these methods is not fundamental and can be overcome through specific technical advancements. By demonstrating that ELBO-based approaches can be made both stable and efficient, the paper aims to provide a simpler, yet more effective, alternative to the computationally intensive and complex MDP-based frameworks. This positions the research as an attempt to refine and re-establish a theoretically grounded and computationally lighter paradigm for aligning denoising generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an online reinforcement learning method, named Variational GRPO (V-GRPO), that effectively and efficiently aligns denoising generative models with desired rewards or human preferences by leveraging ELBO-based surrogates. The work is driven by several motivations:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of MDP-based Approaches:** Current state-of-the-art online RL methods for denoising generative models often rely on formulating the sampling process as a Markov Decision Process. As detailed in the introduction, these MDP-based methods exhibit slow convergence, restrict the use of efficient higher-order ODE samplers, and introduce complex algorithmic designs to manage their inherent inefficiencies and inflexibilities. The authors are motivated to circumvent these limitations by exploring an alternative framework.

2. **Re-evaluating ELBO-based Surrogates:** Prior research had indicated that using ELBO-based surrogates for model log-likelihoods in policy gradient methods, while conceptually simpler and aligned with pretraining objectives, significantly underperformed in visual generation tasks. The authors' key insight is that this performance gap is not an intrinsic limitation of the ELBO-based approach itself. They hypothesize that with appropriate modifications, these surrogates could be made effective. This motivation drives the exploration of techniques to stabilize and enhance their utility.

3. **Achieving Stability and Efficiency:** The core motivation is to demonstrate that an ELBO-based approach can, in fact, be both stable during training and computationally efficient in practice. This involves identifying and implementing specific techniques to address the root causes of instability, particularly high variance in surrogate estimations.

4. **Simplifying Implementation and Aligning with Pretraining:** A related objective is to create a method that is easy to implement. By directly utilizing pretraining objectives as surrogates, the proposed method naturally aligns with the model's initial training phase, which can lead to more stable and intuitive optimization. This contrasts with the sometimes disjoint objectives found in MDP-based methods.

5. **Establishing a New Paradigm:** Ultimately, the researchers aim to provide empirical evidence and methodological advancements that can establish ELBO-based online RL as a competitive, and potentially superior, default framework for post-training denoising generative models. This would shift the research focus towards further enhancing the robustness and scalability of these simpler, more aligned methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V-GRPO integrates ELBO-based surrogates directly into the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm for online reinforcement learning with denoising generative models. This approach distinguishes itself from MDP-based methods by treating multi-step generation as an atomic action, optimizing the policy based on a marginal likelihood surrogate of the final output, rather than per-step objectives along a sampling trajectory.

**Core Mechanism:**
The central idea is to replace the intractable log-likelihood $\log \pi_{\theta}(o_i | c)$ (where $o_i$ is a generated output and $c$ is a conditioning prompt) with a tractable surrogate derived from the negative pretraining objective (Eq. 2), conditioned on the generated output:
$\log \pi_{\theta}(o_i | c) \leftarrow - \mathcal{L}_w(\theta | o_i, c)$.
This surrogate is interpreted as a weighted diffusion ELBO, making it principled and amenable to optimization. The importance ratio $\rho_{\theta,i}$ for GRPO is then computed using the exponential of the difference between current and old policy surrogates (Eq. 8), which are estimated via Monte Carlo sampling (Eq. 9, 10).

**Diagnosis of Instability in Prior ELBO-based Methods:**
The authors hypothesize that the historical underperformance and instability of ELBO-based methods stem from excessive variance in the ELBO-based surrogates. The magnitude of the per-sample loss varies substantially across timesteps (Fig. 1). When timestep-noise pairs are sampled independently for each output, this leads to high-variance surrogates. This variance propagates to gradients, where noise in surrogate magnitudes can overshadow the reward signal and destabilize training (Fig. 2).

**Surrogate Variance Reduction Techniques:**
To address the identified instability, V-GRPO introduces three techniques:
1. **Group-shared timestep-noise pairs:** For a given prompt $c$, a single fixed set of $N_{MC}$ timestep-noise pairs $\{(t_j, \epsilon_j)\}_{j=1}^{N_{MC}}$ is sampled and applied across all outputs $o_i$ generated within that group. This reduces intra-group variance by ensuring all outputs share the same stochastic basis for likelihood estimation.
2. **Stratified timestep sampling:** Instead of uniform sampling, timesteps are drawn by partitioning the discretized time schedule into $N_{MC}$ equal-length intervals and sampling exactly one timestep $t_j$ from each interval. This ensures consistent and representative coverage of the noise schedule for every output.
3. **Adaptive loss weighting:** The model output is reparameterized as an x-prediction (e.g., $x_{\theta}(z_t)$), which empirically yields better performance. A self-normalizing adaptive weighting function is then applied to the loss (Eq. 14). This implicitly weights higher noise levels more and approximately aligns gradient magnitudes across varying per-sample losses.

These techniques collectively reduce the coefficient of variation of surrogate magnitude and decrease the sensitivity of gradient norms to surrogate magnitudes, enhancing training stability and performance (Fig. 2, 4a).

**Controlling Gradient Steps:**
V-GRPO also employs techniques to prevent overly aggressive policy updates, tailoring their application to specific training scenarios:
1. **Importance ratio clipping:** A standard technique in policy gradient methods (e.g., PPO) that clips the importance ratio $\rho_{\theta,i}$ within a range $[1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]$. It is generally sufficient for stable training in most settings.
2. **KL penalty:** A Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty is added to the objective to regularize policy deviation from the behavior policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$. A simplified form (Eq. 16), computed using the reparameterized x-prediction, is used. This is particularly beneficial for preserving capabilities acquired during prior training stages (Tab. 4).
3. **Advantage soft-clipping:** Applied using a hyperbolic tangent function (Eq. 17), this technique bounds extreme advantage values while retaining sensitivity for smaller advantages. It is useful for fully on-policy training (where $\theta \equiv \theta_{old}$) or when the number of sampling steps is limited (Fig. 4c, 4d).

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology is evaluated on two base rectified flow models: FLUX.1-dev and Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD 3.5 M, with Classifier-Free Guidance disabled). Reward functions include both rule-based (GenEval, OCR) and model-based (HPSv2.1, PickScore, ImageReward, UnifiedReward, Aesthetics, CLIPScore) metrics. Prompt datasets are HPDv2 for FLUX.1-dev and a combination of Flow-GRPO's sets and Pick-a-Pic/DrawBench for SD 3.5 M. Training is conducted with a second-order ODE sampler (DPM-Solver++) for rollouts and a first-order Euler sampler for evaluation. BF16 mixed precision is used during rollout, with FP32 for master weights, ELBO computations, and the backward pass. For SD 3.5 M, a multi-stage training curriculum with LoRA adaptation is employed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of V-GRPO.

**Overall Performance:**
V-GRPO achieves state-of-the-art performance in text-to-image synthesis, suggesting that properly stabilized ELBO-based methods can surpass more complex MDP-based approaches.

**FLUX.1-dev Main Experiments:**
- **Quantitative Performance:** In multi-reward text-to-image synthesis tasks on FLUX.1-dev, V-GRPO consistently outperforms all evaluated baselines, including DanceGRPO, BranchGRPO, and MixGRPO, across every reward metric (HPSv2.1, PickScore, ImageReward, UnifiedReward). For example, V-GRPO with 300 iterations and 16+4 sampling steps achieved a UnifiedReward of 3.45, compared to MixGRPO's 3.42 (Table 2).
- **Training Efficiency:** V-GRPO demonstrates a 2x speedup in convergence time compared to MixGRPO, as indicated by a lower number of total function evaluations (NFE) for similar reward levels (Table 1).
- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative comparisons reveal that V-GRPO generates images with superior alignment to text prompts, coherence, and aesthetic style (Fig. 3, Fig. 7). This includes strong text rendering capabilities without task-specific rewards or datasets.

**SD 3.5 M Main Experiments:**
- **Quantitative Performance:** In multi-stage, multi-reward experiments on SD 3.5 M, V-GRPO matches the performance of DiffusionNFT across various rule-based and model-based reward metrics (GenEval, OCR, HPSv2.1, PickScore, CLIPScore, Aesthetics, ImageReward, UnifiedReward). For instance, both V-GRPO and DiffusionNFT achieved similar top scores in HPSv2.1 (6.02 vs. 6.01) and ImageReward (1.52 vs. 1.49) (Table 3).
- **Training Efficiency:** V-GRPO achieves this performance while requiring significantly fewer gradient steps, approximately 3x fewer than DiffusionNFT (580 vs. 1.7K gradient steps), and a markedly lower NFE (Table 1, 5).
- **Qualitative Results:** Similar to FLUX.1-dev, V-GRPO produces images with improved alignment, coherence, and style in comparisons against Flow-GRPO and DiffusionNFT (Fig. 8).
- **Single-Reward Settings:** V-GRPO also exhibits competitive performance in single-reward scenarios for SD 3.5 M (Table 6).

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Surrogate Variance Reduction:** Ablation studies on FLUX.1-dev confirm that all three proposed variance reduction techniques (group-shared timestep-noise pairs, stratified timestep sampling, and adaptive loss weighting) are crucial for stable training and optimal performance (Fig. 4a). Removing any of these leads to training instability or performance degradation. On SD 3.5 M, while the techniques are collectively beneficial, the model shows more inherent robustness, meaning no single component is individually critical (Fig. 5).
- **Gradient Step Control:**
 - The KL penalty effectively preserves prior capabilities, such as GenEval performance, during training stages focused on other rewards like OCR (Table 4).
 - Importance ratio clipping is shown to be effective in preventing loss spikes during FLUX.1-dev training (Fig. 4b).
 - Advantage soft-clipping stabilizes fully on-policy training (SD 3.5 M Stage 1, Fig. 4c) and training with reduced sampling steps (FLUX.1-dev, Fig. 4d). However, it can underperform importance ratio clipping in scenarios with coarse-grained reward functions (SD 3.5 M Stage 2, Fig. 4e).
- **Effect of $N_{MC}$:** The number of Monte Carlo samples ($N_{MC}$) used for surrogate estimation exhibits a saturation effect. Too few samples (e.g., $N_{MC}=2$) hinder convergence, while increasing $N_{MC}$ beyond a certain point (e.g., $N_{MC}=4$ to $N_{MC}=8$) yields only marginal performance gains (Fig. 4f).
- **Prediction Parameterization for Adaptive Loss Weighting:** Using x-prediction for adaptive loss weighting yields the best performance. ε-prediction leads to training collapse, while v-prediction is stable but converges more slowly (Fig. 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of generative modeling and reinforcement learning:

1. **Re-establishment of ELBO-based RL:** The most significant finding is the empirical demonstration that ELBO-based methods for online reinforcement learning, when properly stabilized, can achieve state-of-the-art performance in visual generation tasks. This directly challenges previous reports that considered these methods to underperform significantly in this domain. This re-evaluation opens a promising avenue for research that was previously considered less effective.

2. **Increased Efficiency and Simplicity:** V-GRPO provides a framework that is both more efficient (e.g., 2x speedup over MixGRPO, 3x speedup over DiffusionNFT) and simpler to implement compared to complex MDP-based approaches. By directly leveraging pretraining objectives as likelihood surrogates, the method aligns naturally with the underlying model structure, avoiding the overhead and intricate designs required for inducing and optimizing MDPs over sampling trajectories. This simplification could lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners aiming to fine-tune generative models with RL.

3. **Flexibility in Sampling:** Unlike MDP-based methods that often tie optimization to first-order SDE discretizations, V-GRPO's decoupling of optimization from rollout allows the use of more efficient higher-order or ODE solvers (e.g., DPM-Solver++ during rollout). This flexibility can contribute to faster generation inference or improve output quality without compromising training efficiency.

4. **Novel Techniques for Stability:** The introduction and empirical validation of specific techniques—group-shared timestep-noise pairs, stratified timestep sampling, and adaptive loss weighting—to reduce surrogate variance are valuable contributions. Similarly, the detailed analysis and strategic application of gradient step control methods (importance ratio clipping, KL penalty, advantage soft-clipping) offer practical guidance for stabilizing online RL training in diverse scenarios. These techniques are transferable and could benefit other RL applications where surrogate variance or unstable gradients are concerns.

5. **Potential as a New Default:** The authors propose that these findings may establish ELBO-based methods as a new default for post-training denoising generative models. Should this paradigm be widely adopted, it could lead to more efficient and robust alignment of generative models with human preferences or complex reward functions, fostering advancements in areas like personalized content generation, creative AI, and fine-grained image control.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** By clarifying the conditions under which ELBO-based methods are effective, this work provides a strong foundation and guidance for future research. It encourages further exploration into the robustness, scalability, and theoretical underpinnings of these simpler, pretraining-aligned RL techniques for generative models. This could include investigating novel variance reduction strategies, adaptive hyperparameter tuning for gradient control, or extending the approach to other types of generative architectures.
