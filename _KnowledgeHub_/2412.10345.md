# TraceVLA: Visual Trace Prompting Enhances Spatial-Temporal Awareness for Generalist Robotic Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> TraceVLA enhances the spatial-temporal awareness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by introducing visual trace prompting, which overlays historical multi-point movement trajectories onto current visual observations. This approach improves performance by 7.5% in simulation and shows superior generalization across diverse manipulation tasks on a physical robot compared to OpenVLA.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limited spatial-temporal awareness, often making decisions based on instantaneous visual inputs.
- This lack of historical context hinders their performance in complex robotic manipulation tasks requiring understanding continuous motion, object trajectories, or effects of prior actions.
- Naive concatenation of raw historical frames as input is inefficient and can be distracting due to redundancy.

## Method

- Introduces ==visual trace prompting== where multi-point historical trajectories, extracted using ==Co-Tracker==, are visually overlaid onto the current observation image.
- The VLA model receives this trace-augmented image alongside the original observation, informed by an adjusted language prompt.
- A new dataset of 150K trajectories with visual traces was created to fine-tune state-of-the-art VLA models like OpenVLA and Phi-3-Vision.

## Results

- TraceVLA achieved an average success rate of **47.7%** in SimplerEnv simulations, an absolute gain of **7.5%** over OpenVLA, and demonstrated robustness to environmental variations.
- In real-world experiments on a WidowX-250 robot, TraceVLA consistently outperformed OpenVLA across diverse manipulation tasks, including achieving **6/10** successes on unseen generalization tasks like "Pickplace Banana" where OpenVLA failed.
- The method also showed superior multitask performance on LIBERO benchmarks, with an average success rate of **74.8%**, and incurs minimal inference overhead (approx. **0.03 seconds** per step).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual trace prompting is the main factor contributing to performance improvements, significantly outperforming simple finetuning or historical frame concatenation.
> - Explicitly encoding spatial-temporal information visually is more effective than textual descriptions of movements or raw historical image inputs for VLA models.
> - The length of the visual trace window (e.g., 6 steps) is critical, with optimal performance achieved at intermediate lengths.
