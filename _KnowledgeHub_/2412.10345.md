# TraceVLA: Visual Trace Prompting Enhances Spatial-Temporal Awareness for Generalist Robotic Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> TraceVLA enhances the spatial-temporal awareness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by introducing visual trace prompting, which overlays historical multi-point movement trajectories onto current visual observations. This approach improves performance by 7.5% in simulation and shows superior generalization across diverse manipulation tasks on a physical robot compared to OpenVLA.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limited spatial-temporal awareness, often making decisions based on instantaneous visual inputs.
- This lack of historical context hinders their performance in complex robotic manipulation tasks requiring understanding continuous motion, object trajectories, or effects of prior actions.
- Naive concatenation of raw historical frames as input is inefficient and can be distracting due to redundancy.

## Method

- Introduces ==visual trace prompting== where multi-point historical trajectories, extracted using ==Co-Tracker==, are visually overlaid onto the current observation image.
- The VLA model receives this trace-augmented image alongside the original observation, informed by an adjusted language prompt.
- A new dataset of 150K trajectories with visual traces was created to fine-tune state-of-the-art VLA models like OpenVLA and Phi-3-Vision.

## Results

- TraceVLA achieved an average success rate of **47.7%** in SimplerEnv simulations, an absolute gain of **7.5%** over OpenVLA, and demonstrated robustness to environmental variations.
- In real-world experiments on a WidowX-250 robot, TraceVLA consistently outperformed OpenVLA across diverse manipulation tasks, including achieving **6/10** successes on unseen generalization tasks like "Pickplace Banana" where OpenVLA failed.
- The method also showed superior multitask performance on LIBERO benchmarks, with an average success rate of **74.8%**, and incurs minimal inference overhead (approx. **0.03 seconds** per step).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual trace prompting is the main factor contributing to performance improvements, significantly outperforming simple finetuning or historical frame concatenation.
> - Explicitly encoding spatial-temporal information visually is more effective than textual descriptions of movements or raw historical image inputs for VLA models.
> - The length of the visual trace window (e.g., 6 steps) is critical, with optimal performance achieved at intermediate lengths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of generalist robotic policies, specifically at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and robotic control. Traditionally, robotic manipulation policies are trained for specific tasks, limiting their generalization to novel objects, environments, or robot embodiments. This contrasts sharply with the impressive generalization capabilities of foundation models in vision and language, such as CLIP, LLaVA, Phi-3-Vision, and GPT-4V, across diverse multimodal tasks.

The emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a crucial step towards bridging this gap. VLAs, exemplified by models like RT-1, RT-2, Octo, and OpenVLA, fine-tune pretrained VLMs on extensive robotic datasets, enabling them to generate robot control actions directly from visual and linguistic inputs. This approach leverages the powerful visual understanding and multimodal reasoning inherent in VLMs, paving the way for more adaptable generalist robot policies.

However, a key limitation identified in existing VLA models is their struggle with **spatial-temporal dynamics**. Current VLA systems tend to be overly reactive, making decisions based primarily on instantaneous visual inputs rather than a comprehensive understanding of their past movements and the resulting changes in the environment. This lack of historical awareness significantly hampers their performance in complex interactive manipulation tasks that require understanding continuous motion, object trajectories, and the effects of previous actions.

`TraceVLA` directly addresses this critical shortcoming. Instead of simply concatenating raw historical frames (which can be redundant and distracting), the authors propose `visual trace prompting`. This novel technique explicitly encodes state-action trajectories visually by overlaying the movement traces of key points directly onto the current image observation. By providing this visually rich, temporally aware context, `TraceVLA` enhances the VLA model's ability to reason about spatial and temporal relationships.

This work builds directly upon state-of-the-art VLA models like OpenVLA and also explores the use of more compact, efficient VLMs like Phi-3-Vision as backbones. While previous works have explored visual traces for different purposes (e.g., hand-drawn sketches for goal specifications in RT-Trajectory, predicting future traces in Robopoint, or 2D trace prediction as an auxiliary output in LLARVA), `TraceVLA` uniquely integrates *historical* visual traces as *input prompts* during the fine-tuning of VLMs. This input-centric approach aims to fundamentally improve the model's internal representation and understanding of its operational history, distinguishing it from methods that generate traces as outputs or rely on less structured historical information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to significantly enhance the spatial-temporal awareness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby improving their performance and generalization capabilities in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

The core motivation stems from an observed limitation in current VLA models: despite being pretrained on vast datasets and showing promising generalist capabilities, they often falter when tasks require a deeper understanding of continuous motion and historical context. The authors posit that simply feeding current image inputs as states to predict actions is insufficient for interactive robotics. This leads to policies that are reactive and prone to errors in scenarios demanding an understanding of how the robot's or objects' past movements influence the current state and future actions. For instance, successfully manipulating an object might require knowledge of how it was previously moved or positioned, or avoiding collisions might necessitate awareness of the robot's recent trajectory.

To overcome this, the paper introduces and validates the concept of "visual trace prompting." The key hypothesis is that by explicitly computing and visually overlaying multi-point temporal trajectories onto the robot's current visual observations, VLA models can gain the necessary spatial and temporal context to make more informed and robust action predictions. This visual encoding of state-action histories is designed to be a more effective and less distracting alternative to simply concatenating raw historical image frames, which can introduce visual redundancy.

Specifically, the key objectives articulated in the paper are:
1. **Methodological Innovation:** To introduce "visual trace prompting," a novel technique that meaningfully enhances VLA models' spatial-temporal reasoning for manipulation tasks.
2. **Dataset and Model Development:** To create a new dataset of robot manipulation trajectories annotated with visual traces (150K trajectories) and to use this dataset to fine-tune state-of-the-art VLA models (7B TraceVLA based on OpenVLA and a more efficient 4B TraceVLA-Phi3 based on Phi-3-Vision). This aims to provide both high-performance and computationally efficient models to the community.
3. **Rigorous Validation:** To extensively evaluate the proposed approach in both diverse simulated environments (SimplerEnv with 137 configurations on Google Robots) and real-world robotic tasks (4 tasks on a physical WidowX robot, including unseen scenarios). This validation aims to demonstrate superior generalization capabilities across varied embodiments and environmental conditions by leveraging the enhanced spatial-temporal information.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between reactive VLA policies and truly intelligent, generalist robotic agents that can understand and leverage the dynamics of their environment and their own actions over time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of `TraceVLA` lies in its novel `visual trace prompting` technique, designed to infuse VLA models with crucial spatial-temporal awareness. The methodology can be broken down into three main components: visual trace generation, model architecture design, and implementation details for training and inference.

#### 3.1. Visual Trace Generation

Instead of naive concatenation of historical frames, which can be redundant and distract the model, `TraceVLA` employs a targeted approach to extract and represent salient historical movement.
1. **Multi-Point Tracking:** At any given timestep `t`, and for a specified time window `N` (e.g., 6 steps), a sequence of historical image observations `(o_{t-N}..., o_t)` is used.
2. **Co-Tracker Application:** An off-the-shelf dense point tracking model, Co-Tracker (Karaev et al., 2023), is utilized. Co-Tracker works by partitioning the initial frame `o_t` into a `K x K` grid (e.g., `K=40x40`) and then tracking each of these grid cells across the `N` historical frames to construct dense point trajectories.
3. **Active Point Identification:** From the `K x K` dense trajectories, the method identifies "active points" – those that exhibit significant movement and are not static background points. This is achieved by computing the absolute pixel movement (`Δp_t'`) between adjacent frames for each point trajectory. Trajectories with a total movement exceeding a threshold (`κ`) over the `N` timesteps are deemed active.
4. **Sampling and Overlay:** From these active point trajectories, `M` points (e.g., `M=5`) are randomly sampled. Finally, these `M` sampled trajectories are visually overlaid onto the robot's *current* observation frame (`o_t`). This overlaid image serves as the visual trace prompt.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture Design

The enhanced VLA model, `TraceVLA`, takes a multi-image input approach to integrate the visual trace:
1. **Dual Image Input:** The model receives two images as input: the original robot observation (`o_t`) and the newly generated image with the overlaid visual trace (`image_overlaid`).
2. **Separator Token:** A special, learnable separator token is inserted between the visual tokens derived from these two images. This allows the VLA model to distinguish and process the original observation and its spatially-tempered historical context separately yet cohesively.
3. **Prompting with Language Instructions:** The textual prompt is adjusted to inform the VLA model about this dual visual input. For example, it might state: "You are given two images: one with the original robot observation, and another one marked with historical traces... What action should the robot take...?" This explicit instruction guides the model's interpretation of the multimodal input.
4. **Trace Dropout Mechanism:** To ensure robustness and adaptability when visual traces might be unavailable (e.g., due to poor lighting conditions or Co-Tracker failures), a dropout mechanism is implemented during training. With a probability `α`, the visual trace prompt image is replaced by the original image, and the corresponding text hint is removed. This encourages the model to learn to perform effectively even without the trace, making it more robust during inference.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Dataset Creation:** A significant effort was made to create a custom dataset of approximately 150,000 robot trajectories annotated with visual trace prompting. This dataset was constructed by applying the visual trace generation pipeline to existing datasets such as BridgeData-v2, Google RT1 Robot datasets, and an additional 120 demonstrations collected from a WidowX-250 robot.
- **VLA Model Backbones:**
 - `TraceVLA` (7B parameters) is fine-tuned from `OpenVLA`, which is itself based on the Prismatic vision-language model and pretrained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset.
 - `TraceVLA-Phi3` (4B parameters) is based on a custom-pretrained VLA model using Phi-3-Vision as its backbone. This compact model allows for testing the flexibility of visual trace prompting across different VLM architectures and offers a more computationally efficient alternative for fine-tuning on smaller GPUs.
- **Co-Tracker Efficiency during Inference:** To minimize computational overhead during real-time inference, dense `K x K` Co-Tracker queries are performed only at the start of a trajectory and then periodically (e.g., every 20 steps) to recalibrate. In between, Co-Tracker is queried only for the `M` previously identified active points, significantly reducing per-timestep computational cost while maintaining tracking accuracy.
- **Training Protocol:** Both `TraceVLA` and `TraceVLA-Phi3` are fine-tuned for an additional five epochs on the newly created visual trace-annotated dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of `TraceVLA` was rigorously evaluated across both simulated and real-world robotic environments, demonstrating significant improvements over existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation (SimplerEnv)

The SimplerEnv simulator, featuring Google Robots, allowed for testing across 137 diverse configurations, including "visual matching" (minimizing sim-to-real gap) and "variant aggregation" (evaluating robustness to varied backgrounds, lighting, distractors, table textures, and camera poses).

- **Overall Performance:** As summarized in Table 1, `TraceVLA` consistently outperformed `OpenVLA` (both 7B and 4B Phi3 versions) across all three tested tasks (`Move Near`, `Pick Coke Can`, `Open/Close Drawer`) and evaluation metrics. `TraceVLA` achieved an overall average success rate of 47.7%, significantly outperforming `OpenVLA`'s 40.2% (a 7.5% absolute gain). Similarly, `TraceVLA-Phi3` improved upon `OpenVLA-Phi3` by 4.1% (44.0% vs. 39.9%). This highlights the effectiveness of visual trace prompting across different model scales. `TraceVLA` also generally surpassed other baselines like Octo-Base and RT1-X, with RT1-X showing competitive performance in some specific tasks.
- **Environmental Variant Aggregation:** Figure 4 vividly illustrates `TraceVLA`'s robustness to environmental changes. It showed substantial performance enhancements, particularly under camera orientation changes, the presence of distractors, and background alterations, with average improvements exceeding 20% in these categories. This indicates that the visual trace effectively provides stable spatial trajectory information, helping the model maintain performance despite shifts in perspective, lighting, or scene clutter.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Experiments (WidowX-250)

Evaluations on a physical WidowX-250 robot, using a third-person view camera, further validated `TraceVLA`'s real-world applicability and generalization capabilities.

- **Diverse Manipulation Tasks:** `TraceVLA` consistently outperformed `OpenVLA` across a set of diverse manipulation tasks, including soft object manipulation (`Fold Cloth`), pick-and-place (`Pickplace Corn`), and object movement (`Swipe Corn Sink`, `Pickup Knife`). Notably, on the `Pickplace Corn` task, which was unseen during `TraceVLA`'s fine-tuning but had similar elements to a training task, `TraceVLA` achieved 8/10 successful trials compared to `OpenVLA`'s 1/10.
- **Unseen Generalization Tasks:** Four additional unseen tasks with novel objects, goals, and language instructions were designed to stringently test generalization. Results (Figure 6b) showed `TraceVLA`'s superior generalization. For example, in the challenging `Pickplace Banana` task, `TraceVLA` succeeded in 6/10 trials while `OpenVLA` failed in all 10. `OpenVLA` often struggled with correctly interpreting language instructions, highlighting `TraceVLA`'s enhanced language grounding and reduced susceptibility to spurious correlations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided key insights into the source of `TraceVLA`'s performance gains:
- **Finetuning vs. Visual Trace Prompting:** Simply fine-tuning `OpenVLA` on the new dataset *without* visual trace prompting yielded minimal (1.1%) or even negative (0.3% for Phi3) gains. The significant 7.5% gain for `TraceVLA` confirms that the improvement is primarily attributable to the visual trace prompting, not just additional finetuning on a subset of the data.
- **Historical Image Concatenation:** Appending 6 raw historical image observations to the VLA input *degraded* performance by 6%. This validates the authors' hypothesis that raw historical frames are redundant and distracting, whereas the curated visual traces provide focused, useful context.
- **Text Trace Prompting vs. Visual Trace Prompting:** Describing point movements verbally (text trace prompting) resulted in a 2.4% gain over baseline, confirming the utility of point tracking information. However, `TraceVLA` (visual trace prompting) achieved an *additional 6.4% gain* over the text-based approach. This underscores the effectiveness of leveraging the vision encoder for processing visual prompts, outperforming text-based descriptions which also incur higher token counts and computational costs.
- **Length of Visual Trace (N):** The optimal length for visual trace (`N`) was found to be 6 steps. Shorter traces (`N=3`) yielded smaller gains, while longer traces (`N=12`) slightly reduced performance, likely due to visual clutter or obscuring key scene elements.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis

- **Training Memory Cost:** While `TraceVLA` incurs some additional GPU memory cost compared to models without visual trace prompting, this cost is manageable (less than 10GB for a batch size of 32 on 8 H100 GPUs) and decreases with smaller batch sizes.
- **Inference Speed:** The additional computational overhead at inference time is minimal. The extra text and image tokens add negligible time (approx. 0.002 seconds). The Co-Tracker operations add about 0.03 seconds per step for 5 active points, with the amortized cost of dense tracking being very low (0.004 seconds per step).

#### 4.5. LIBERO Simulation Benchmarks (Appendix F)

`TraceVLA` demonstrated superior multitask performance across all four LIBERO suites (`LIBERO-Spatial`, `LIBERO-Object`, `LIBERO-Goal`, `LIBERO-Long`), which test spatial understanding, object recognition, goal generalization, and long-horizon planning. `TraceVLA` achieved an average success rate of 74.8% across these benchmarks, outperforming the fine-tuned OpenVLA baseline at 70.6%. This further cements `TraceVLA`'s robust generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The `TraceVLA` research represents a significant advancement in the field of generalist robotic policies, particularly in enhancing the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. By introducing `visual trace prompting`, the authors have successfully addressed a critical limitation of existing VLA systems: their struggle with maintaining spatial-temporal awareness and leveraging historical context for informed decision-making.

The key significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** `TraceVLA` consistently outperforms state-of-the-art VLA models in both diverse simulated environments and real-world robotic tasks. Its superior generalization capabilities across unseen objects, varied environmental conditions (lighting, backgrounds, distractors, camera angles), and novel task instructions are particularly impactful. This suggests that robots powered by `TraceVLA` can adapt more readily to unstructured and dynamic real-world scenarios, reducing the need for extensive task-specific training.

2. **Novel Methodological Contribution:** The `visual trace prompting` technique is a simple yet highly effective innovation. By converting complex state-action trajectories into an easily digestible visual format overlaid directly onto observations, it effectively provides "spatial memory" to the VLA model. This approach is demonstrated to be far more effective than naive historical frame concatenation or even textual descriptions of movements, highlighting the power of multimodal grounding in VLMs.

3. **Efficiency and Accessibility:** The development of `TraceVLA-Phi3`, a compact 4B-parameter model, is crucial for broader adoption. By rivaling the performance of larger 7B models while significantly improving inference efficiency and reducing computational requirements, this work makes advanced generalist robot policies more accessible to researchers and practitioners with more limited computational resources.

4. **Bridging the Perception-Action Gap:** `TraceVLA` effectively bridges the gap between visual perception, temporal understanding, and physical action execution. By enabling VLA models to reason about the dynamic interplay of objects and the robot's own movements over time, it pushes the boundaries of how intelligent agents can interact with the physical world. This is a step towards more nuanced and intelligent robotic control, moving beyond purely reactive behaviors.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of `visual trace prompting` opens several promising avenues for future research. The authors suggest incorporating multi-point spatial *trajectory prediction* to allow models to anticipate and plan actions with greater foresight. Additionally, leveraging 3D point cloud data for richer spatial representations could further enhance manipulation accuracy and robustness in highly complex scenes. These directions could lead to robots that are not only aware of their past but can also proactively plan for the future in a 3D environment.

In conclusion, `TraceVLA` represents a substantial contribution to generalist robotics, demonstrating that a focused injection of spatial-temporal context, through an elegantly designed visual prompting mechanism, can significantly unlock the potential of large vision-language-action models for more adaptable, robust, and intelligent robot manipulation.
