# Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Refine improves a large language model's output at test time with no training. One model generates an initial answer, critiques it with specific actionable feedback, and rewrites it, looping until a stop condition is met. Across seven tasks with GPT-3.5, ChatGPT and GPT-4, it beats one-step generation by about 20% absolute on average.

## Problem

- Iterative refinement methods usually need a separately trained refinement model with domain-specific data, so they do not transfer across tasks.
- Approaches based on reward models or human feedback need large training sets or expensive annotation, which is often infeasible to obtain.
- Single-pass LLM generation often misses multifaceted or hard-to-define goals such as dialogue quality or code readability, and lacks a general, training-free way to fix its own mistakes.

## Method

- **Self-Refine** alternates ==FEEDBACK== and ==REFINE== steps: the same model M critiques its latest output, then rewrites it using that critique, repeating until a ==stopping condition== is met.
- Three ==few-shot prompts== (initial generation, feedback, refinement) drive one ==frozen LLM==. Feedback prompts use ==input-output-feedback triples== that demand specific, ==actionable critiques== naming concrete phrases to change.
- Refinement conditions on the ==full history== of prior outputs and feedback, so the model can avoid repeating earlier mistakes. Multi-aspect tasks get ==numerical scores per aspect==, which are used to pick the best iteration.

## Results

- GPT-4 with Self-Refine raises Code Optimization from **27.3%** to **36.0%**. Dialogue Response preference jumps from **25.4%** to **74.6%**, and gains are largest on preference-based and Constrained Generation tasks.
- Feedback ablation on Sentiment Reversal: specific feedback scores **43.2**, generic feedback **31.2**, and no feedback fails at **0**. Code Optimization falls **27.5**, **26.0**, **24.8** and Acronym Generation **56.4**, **54.0**, **48.0**.
- More iterations help with diminishing returns: Constrained Generation rises **29.0** to **49.7** over three iterations. Humans prefer one refined output over four independent samples, and Math Reasoning gains stay modest.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feedback quality is the bottleneck: 94% of failures traced to wrong feedback (61% bad fix, 33% wrong location), only 6% to refinement. Better error detection matters more than better rewriting.
> - Refinement gains come from targeted critique, not extra sampling, since one refined output beat four independent samples. Specific, actionable feedback clearly beats generic advice.
> - Self-critique needs a capable base model and a detectable error signal. Vicuna-13B could not follow the feedback and refine formats, and math gains grow past 5% only when an external signal flags errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "SELF-REFINE" addresses a fundamental challenge in the burgeoning field of Large Language Models (LLMs): while these models can generate remarkably coherent text, their initial outputs often fall short of meeting complex or nuanced requirements. This limitation is particularly apparent in tasks demanding multifaceted objectives, such as generating nuanced dialogue responses, or tasks with hard-to-define goals, like enhancing code readability.

Historically, improving initial model outputs, or "iterative refinement," has typically involved training dedicated refinement models. These models often rely on domain-specific datasets, which can be costly and time-consuming to acquire. Other common approaches involve reinforcement learning (RL) from human feedback (RLHF) or external reward models. While effective, these methods necessitate extensive training sets, expensive human annotations, or significant computational resources for model parameter updates. These dependencies limit their broad applicability, especially for new tasks where data or fine-tuning infrastructure might be scarce.

Inspired by human cognitive processes, where individuals iteratively refine their thoughts, writing, or code based on self-generated feedback, SELF-REFINE positions itself as a novel, lightweight alternative. It leverages the inherent capabilities of modern LLMs themselves, particularly their proficiency in "in-context learning" or "few-shot prompting." Instead of training separate models or updating parameters, SELF-REFINE demonstrates that a single, powerful LLM can function as its own critic, generating feedback on its prior output and subsequently using that feedback to refine itself, all within the context of a prompt.

This work directly contrasts with several existing paradigms:
- **Learned Refiners:** Approaches like PEER, Self-critique, CodeRL, or Self-correction train specific models for refinement, requiring supervised data or distinct training phases for each task. SELF-REFINE, in contrast, is "supervision-free" in the sense that it doesn't require *additional* training data beyond the few-shot examples provided in the prompt, making it adaptable to new tasks without domain-specific training.
- **Prompted Refiners (Concurrent Works):** While some contemporary works like Augmenter, Re3, and Reflexion also explore using LLMs for feedback and refinement via prompting, SELF-REFINE distinguishes itself by using the *same LLM* for generation, feedback, and refinement, and by emphasizing *multi-aspect, structured feedback* for iterative self-improvement. It goes beyond simple "reflection" to structured, actionable critiques.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Approaches:** Methods like RLHF (e.g., Stiennon et al., 2020) aim to align model outputs with human preferences by optimizing a scalar reward function, requiring model parameter updates. SELF-REFINE operates purely at test-time (inference), avoiding the computational cost and complexity of RL training. It also offers explicit, interpretable natural language feedback on intermediate generations, unlike scalar rewards.

By demonstrating that even state-of-the-art LLMs like GPT-4 can be significantly improved at test-time through a simple, standalone iterative process, SELF-REFINE opens new avenues for enhancing LLM performance without the prohibitive costs associated with traditional training-intensive methods. It represents a shift towards more autonomous and self-correcting AI systems, moving beyond one-shot generation to a more human-like iterative problem-solving paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the SELF-REFINE research stem from a clear recognition of the current limitations and inefficiencies in leveraging large language models for complex generative tasks:

**Motivation:**

1. **Bridging the Gap between LLM Capabilities and Desired Output Quality:** While LLMs are highly capable of generating coherent text, their initial attempts often fall short of meeting intricate, multi-faceted, or hard-to-define requirements. For instance, a dialogue response might be grammatically correct but lack engagement or relevance; a piece of generated code might be functional but inefficient or unreadable. This "first-try" limitation means that even state-of-the-art LLMs frequently produce outputs that are not optimal, necessitating further improvement.
2. **Emulating Human Iterative Problem-Solving:** A powerful inspiration for SELF-REFINE is the human process of iterative self-refinement. When humans write, code, or solve problems, they rarely achieve perfection on their first attempt. Instead, they draft, reflect on their work, identify areas for improvement (self-feedback), and then revise. This intrinsic human ability to self-critique and refine is a cornerstone of creativity and problem-solving. The authors hypothesize that if LLMs could mimic this internal feedback loop, their output quality could be substantially elevated.
3. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Refinement Approaches:** Current methods for improving LLM outputs (e.g., fine-tuning, training dedicated refinement models, or reinforcement learning) suffer from significant drawbacks:
 - **Data Dependency:** They often require large, domain-specific supervised training datasets, which are expensive and time-consuming to acquire or annotate.
 - **Training Overhead:** They necessitate additional training or fine-tuning, involving substantial computational resources and expertise.
 - **Complexity:** Reinforcement learning, in particular, can be complex to set up and optimize, often relying on scalar reward signals that lack the explicit, actionable nature of natural language feedback.

**Key Objectives:**

Based on these motivations, the primary objectives of SELF-REFINE are to:

1. **Develop a Supervision-Free Refinement Approach:** Introduce a method that does not require any supervised training data, additional model training, or reinforcement learning. This aims to significantly reduce the cost and complexity associated with improving LLM outputs.
2. **Utilize a Single LLM for All Processes:** Design a system where the *same* LLM serves as the initial generator, the self-feedback provider, and the refiner. This simplifies the architecture and leverages the LLM's multifaceted capabilities.
3. **Enable Iterative Self-Correction:** Implement an iterative loop where the LLM continually generates feedback on its own previous output and uses that feedback to produce an improved version.
4. **Demonstrate Broad Applicability:** Evaluate the proposed SELF-REFINE approach across a diverse range of generative tasks (including natural language generation, code generation, and mathematical reasoning) to prove its versatility and generalizability.
5. **Achieve Substantial Performance Improvements:** Show that this iterative self-refinement process leads to significantly higher-quality outputs—preferred by humans and automatic metrics—compared to conventional one-step generation from the same LLM.

In essence, SELF-REFINE aims to unlock the full potential of existing powerful LLMs by enabling them to autonomously self-diagnose and self-correct, mirroring human iterative problem-solving without incurring additional training costs or external supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SELF-REFINE introduces a straightforward yet powerful iterative self-refinement algorithm that operates entirely within a single Large Language Model (LLM) at test-time. It requires no additional supervised training data, reinforcement learning, or parameter updates, relying solely on few-shot prompting to guide the LLM's behavior.

The core idea is an alternating sequence of two generative steps: **FEEDBACK** and **REFINE**, following an **Initial Generation**. This process is summarized in Algorithm 1 and Figure 1 of the paper.

**A. Initial Generation (y₀)**
The process begins with the LLM `M` generating an initial output `y₀` given an input `x`. This is achieved using a task-specific few-shot prompt, `p_gen`, which provides examples of input-output pairs for the desired task.
- **Prompt Structure:** `y₀ = M(p_gen || x)`
- **Example:** For code optimization, `p_gen` might include examples of slow code and their optimized counterparts.

**B. Iterative Loop (FEEDBACK and REFINE)**
After the initial generation, SELF-REFINE enters an iterative loop where the LLM continually evaluates and improves its own output.

1. **FEEDBACK (fb_t)**
 In this step, the *same LLM `M`* is prompted to provide feedback `fb_t` on its most recent output `y_t`. This feedback is crucial for guiding subsequent refinements.
 - **Prompt Structure:** `fb_t = M(p_fb || x || y_t)`
 - **Key Design Principle:** The prompt `p_fb` is meticulously designed to elicit "actionable" and "specific" feedback. This is achieved by providing few-shot examples of input-output-feedback triples `<x(k), y(k), fb(k)>` where `fb(k)` explicitly identifies concrete issues and suggests clear actions for improvement. For instance, in code optimization, feedback might pinpoint a slow loop and suggest a more efficient algorithm.
 - **Stopping Condition:** The feedback step can also incorporate a stopping condition (`stop(fb_t, t)`), allowing the process to terminate if the model determines no further refinement is necessary (e.g., by generating a specific "stop" indicator in the feedback or after a fixed number of iterations).

2. **REFINE (y_t+1)**
 Following the feedback step, the *same LLM `M`* uses the generated feedback `fb_t` to refine its previous output `y_t`, producing `y_t+1`.
 - **Prompt Structure:** `y_t+1 = M(p_refine || x || y₀ || fb₀ || ... || y_t || fb_t)`
 - **History Integration:** A critical aspect of the `p_refine` prompt is the inclusion of the *entire history* of previous inputs, outputs, and feedback (`y₀, fb₀, ..., y_t, fb_t`). This allows the LLM to learn from past mistakes, understand the refinement trajectory, and avoid repeating errors.
 - **Few-shot Examples:** `p_refine` includes examples of input-output-feedback-refined quadruples `<x(k), y(k)t, fb(k)t, y(k)t+1>` to guide the refinement process.

The loop (`FEEDBACK` -> `REFINE`) continues for a specified number of iterations (up to 4 in most experiments) or until the stopping condition is met. The final refined output `y_t` is then returned.

**C. Instantiation and Evaluation:**

- **Base LLMs:** The research uses state-of-the-art LLMs as the underlying `M`: GPT-3.5 (`text-davinci-003`, `gpt-3.5-turbo`/ChatGPT), GPT-4, and for code tasks, CODEX (`code-davinci-002`). This selection allows for evaluation across strong existing models.
- **Prompting:** All steps (initial generation, feedback, refinement) are implemented using few-shot prompts. For tasks where previous work provided prompts (e.g., Code Optimization, Math Reasoning), these were adapted; otherwise, new prompts were created. Greedy decoding with a temperature of 0.7 was used.
- **Diverse Tasks:** SELF-REFINE was evaluated on seven diverse tasks spanning different domains: Dialogue Response Generation, Code Optimization, Code Readability Improvement, Math Reasoning, Sentiment Reversal, Acronym Generation, and Constrained Generation (a harder variant).
- **Metrics:** A combination of metrics was used:
 - **Task-specific metrics:** Automated metrics from prior work (e.g., solve rate for Math Reasoning, optimization percentage for Code Optimization).
 - **Human-preference:** Blind A/B evaluations where human annotators chose preferred outputs.
 - **GPT-4 preference:** GPT-4 was used as a proxy for human evaluation, shown to have high correlation with human judgment in several tasks.

In essence, SELF-REFINE’s methodology is a clever application of in-context learning, transforming a powerful generative LLM into a self-contained, iterative improvement engine, mimicking human self-correction processes without the need for additional training or complex external machinery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SELF-REFINE across seven diverse tasks with state-of-the-art LLMs yielded consistent and compelling results, demonstrating its effectiveness in significantly improving output quality.

**A. Consistent and Substantial Improvements:**
- **Across all tasks and LLMs:** SELF-REFINE consistently improved performance over the base LLMs (GPT-3.5, ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Codex) using conventional one-step generation. The average absolute improvement in task performance was approximately 20%.
- **Quantitative Gains:** For instance, GPT-4 + SELF-REFINE improved Code Optimization by 8.7% (absolute), increasing the optimization percentage from 27.3% to 36.0%. In preference-based tasks, gains were even more dramatic; GPT-4's preference score in Dialogue Response Generation jumped by 49.2% (from 25.4% to 74.6%).
- **Statistical Significance:** A significant portion of the observed gains, especially for GPT-4, were statistically significant, confirming that the improvements were not merely due to chance.

**B. Impact of Task Complexity and Nature:**
- **Highest Gains in Complex Tasks:** Tasks like Constrained Generation, which require including many concepts (20-30 keywords), showed very high gains (e.g., GPT-4 + SELF-REFINE improved by 30% absolute). This is attributed to the increased opportunity for initial errors and the subsequent effectiveness of self-correction in catching and fixing these missed constraints.
- **High Gains in Preference-Based Tasks:** Dialogue Response Generation, Sentiment Reversal, and Acronym Generation, where human or AI preference is the metric, saw remarkable improvements. This suggests SELF-REFINE excels in tasks where output quality is subjective or multifaceted, allowing the LLM to refine nuanced aspects.
- **Modest Gains in Precise Tasks (e.g., Math Reasoning):** While still beneficial, gains in Math Reasoning were modest (e.g., GPT-4 improved by 0.2%). The analysis revealed that LLMs struggle to accurately identify subtle errors in mathematical reasoning without external verification. However, when an "Oracle feedback" (external correctness signal) was provided, the gains significantly increased (e.g., GPT-3.5 saw a 4.8% increase), highlighting the potential synergy with external validation mechanisms.

**C. Importance of Feedback Quality and Iterations:**
- **Actionable Feedback is Crucial:** Ablation studies clearly demonstrated that the quality of feedback matters significantly. Using specific, actionable feedback (as in SELF-REFINE) consistently outperformed using generic feedback or no explicit feedback at all. For example, in Sentiment Transfer, without actionable feedback, the task largely failed. This underscores the importance of the carefully designed `p_fb` prompt to guide the LLM's self-critique.
- **Benefits of Multiple Iterations:** Analysis showed that output quality generally improves with an increasing number of FEEDBACK-REFINE iterations, although with diminishing returns. Most significant gains were observed in the initial iterations. This validates the iterative nature of the approach. For multi-aspect tasks like Acronym Generation, the quality might not increase monotonically on all aspects, necessitating a scoring mechanism for best output selection.

**D. Comparison with Alternatives:**
- **Outperforming Multiple Samples:** SELF-REFINE outputs were consistently preferred over simply generating multiple initial outputs (e.g., 4 samples) and picking the best one. This indicates that the explicit feedback and refinement process is superior to mere diversified generation, highlighting the value of targeted self-correction.
- **Strength of Base LLM:** Stronger base models (like GPT-4) generally performed better even with SELF-REFINE, and SELF-REFINE seemed to unlock their full potential, even in cases where their initial, single-pass results were lower than other models.
- **Limitations with Weaker Models:** SELF-REFINE's effectiveness relies on the base LLM's sufficient few-shot modeling and instruction-following abilities. Weaker, conversation-tuned models like Vicuna-13B struggled to consistently follow the feedback and refinement prompts, often failing to refine effectively or generating unhelpful responses. This suggests a minimum capability threshold for the underlying LLM.

**E. Qualitative Insights:**
- **Feedback as the Bottleneck:** Qualitative analysis revealed that most failures of SELF-REFINE were attributable to erroneous or inappropriate feedback generated by the LLM itself, rather than the refiner failing to implement good feedback. This points to improving feedback generation as a key area for future work.
- **Resilience of Refiner:** The refiner module showed a surprising degree of resilience, capable of rectifying issues even when the feedback was partially incorrect.
- **Real-world Applicability:** Beyond benchmarks, SELF-REFINE was successfully applied to a complex, creative task like website generation, demonstrating its potential for iterative development of HTML, CSS, and JS.

In summary, SELF-REFINE stands as a robust and generally applicable method for enhancing LLM output quality through an internal, iterative self-correction loop, proving that LLMs can effectively serve as their own critics and editors without external supervision or fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SELF-REFINE marks a significant step forward in the capabilities and practical application of large language models. Its significance and potential impact can be understood across several dimensions:

**A. Novelty and Methodological Innovation:**
- **First of its Kind (Self-contained Iteration):** SELF-REFINE is the first approach that enables iterative self-feedback and refinement *within a single LLM*, without requiring any additional training data, reinforcement learning, or model parameter updates. This distinguishes it from prior work that either trains separate refiners or relies on complex RL setups.
- **Leveraging In-Context Learning:** By cleverly utilizing the few-shot prompting capabilities of modern LLMs, SELF-REFINE transforms a generative model into a self-aware, self-correcting agent. This showcases a deeper, more autonomous aspect of LLM intelligence.
- **Actionable Natural Language Feedback:** The emphasis on generating specific and actionable natural language feedback, rather than just scalar rewards or simple "critiques," provides a clear, interpretable signal for refinement, making the process more transparent and effective.

**B. Practicality and Accessibility:**
- **Cost-Effectiveness:** By eliminating the need for extensive supervised datasets, human annotations, and expensive fine-tuning or RL training, SELF-REFINE dramatically reduces the cost and computational burden associated with improving LLM outputs. This makes high-quality generative AI more accessible to researchers and developers with limited resources.
- **Ease of Use:** The approach is simple to implement, primarily requiring well-crafted prompts. This low barrier to entry means it can be easily integrated into existing LLM-based pipelines and applied to a wide variety of tasks without specialized machine learning engineering expertise for model training.
- **Broad Applicability:** Demonstrated success across 7 diverse tasks, including creative, logical, and code-based generation, highlights the method's generalizability. This suggests that SELF-REFINE can be a versatile tool for enhancing LLM performance in various real-world applications.

**C. Advancing LLM Capabilities and Research Directions:**
- **Unlocking Latent Potential:** The finding that even state-of-the-art models like GPT-4 can be significantly improved at test-time through SELF-REFINE suggests that LLMs possess latent capabilities for self-correction that are not fully expressed in single-pass generation. This could fundamentally change how we think about deploying and optimizing LLMs.
- **Towards More Autonomous AI:** SELF-REFINE moves LLMs closer to becoming truly autonomous problem-solvers. By enabling models to critique and refine their own work, it lays groundwork for AI systems that can learn from their "mistakes" and improve independently, mimicking human cognitive processes.
- **Future Research Avenues:** The work identifies key areas for future research, such as improving the accuracy and consistency of LLM-generated feedback (given that most failures stemmed from erroneous feedback) and enhancing the refiner's robustness to various types of feedback errors. It also opens doors to exploring multi-model feedback loops or hybrid approaches with external oracles.

**D. Societal and Economic Impact:**
- **Enhanced AI-Generated Content:** The ability to produce consistently higher-quality outputs from LLMs has immediate benefits for applications such as content creation (writing, marketing copy), code development (more efficient, readable code), conversational AI (more engaging and relevant dialogue), and educational tools (better explanations, problem-solving assistance).
- **Streamlining Creative and Development Processes:** By reducing the need for extensive human post-editing or manual refinement of AI-generated content, SELF-REFINE can significantly reduce the cost and time involved in creative and development workflows, augmenting human productivity.
- **Democratization of Advanced AI:** As LLMs become more performant and easier to use without specialized training, their benefits can be more widely disseminated, fostering innovation across various industries.

**Limitations to Consider:**
While impactful, the paper acknowledges certain limitations, including the reliance on sufficiently capable base LLMs (as weaker models struggled), the use of non-open-sourced models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Codex), and the exclusive evaluation on English datasets. These limitations suggest areas for further exploration and validation in broader contexts.

In conclusion, SELF-REFINE represents a pivotal conceptual and practical contribution, demonstrating that iterative self-feedback within a single LLM is a powerful, efficient, and broadly applicable paradigm for significantly enhancing generative AI quality. It pushes the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve autonomously, paving the way for more sophisticated and human-like AI systems.
