# Nymeria: A Massive Collection of Multimodal Egocentric Daily Motion in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Meta Reality Labs Research unveil Nymeria, a 300-hour multimodal egocentric dataset featuring synchronized full-body ground truth motion, device localization, and rich egocentric sensor streams from 264 participants across 50 diverse locations. The dataset uniquely includes over 310,000 hierarchical natural language descriptions, offering a comprehensive resource for advancing human motion understanding and multimodal AI.

## Problem

- Existing human motion datasets often lack scale, diversity, real-world 'in-the-wild' scenarios, and precise multimodal synchronization, particularly for egocentric perception.
- Obtaining long-term, globally accurate ground-truth human motion in dynamic, unconstrained environments is challenging due to limitations of traditional motion capture and IMU-based systems.
- Current motion-language datasets are limited in size, granularity, and contextual richness, hindering the development of AI models that can deeply connect human actions with natural language descriptions.

## Method

- Developed a multi-device hardware setup featuring XSens mocap suits, Project Aria glasses, and miniAria wristbands, all precisely synchronized with sub-millisecond accuracy using a custom universal time signal.
- Implemented a robust data processing pipeline for 6DoF localization of egocentric devices in a common, gravity-aligned 3D metric world, alongside accurate alignment of full-body mocap data to this global frame.
- Designed a hierarchical human-driven annotation system to generate rich, in-context natural language descriptions of motion, atomic actions, and activities, leveraging synchronized multi-view video and 3D motion playback.

## Results

- Nymeria is the largest 'in-the-wild' human motion dataset, comprising 300 hours of daily activities from 264 participants across 50 diverse indoor and outdoor locations, with 260 million body poses and 201.2 million egocentric images.
- The dataset features over 310,000 hierarchical natural language sentences (8.64 million words), making it the largest motion-language dataset with an average sentence length significantly longer than prior work.
- Baseline evaluations using state-of-the-art models confirm Nymeria's utility for motion tracking, synthesis, and language grounding, demonstrating both its rich data quality and the challenges it poses for advancing current AI capabilities in egocentric perception.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precise sub-millisecond synchronization and global 3D alignment of diverse wearable sensors are achievable and critical for creating high-fidelity multimodal egocentric datasets that accurately reflect real-world interactions.
> - Hierarchical and contextually rich natural language annotations, directly linked to synchronized motion and visual data, are essential for bridging the gap between physical actions and linguistic understanding, especially for multimodal large language models.
> - Real-world 'in-the-wild' egocentric data introduces significant noise and complexity that challenges existing state-of-the-art motion tracking and synthesis algorithms, pushing the boundaries for developing more robust AI models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The Nymeria dataset is positioned as a critical advancement within several interconnected research domains: human motion capture, egocentric perception, multimodal data fusion, and motion-language understanding.

**Limitations of Existing Datasets:** The paper meticulously outlines the shortcomings of prior work. Existing human motion datasets often suffer from limitations in scale, diversity, modality, or realism. Many rely on traditional motion capture (mocap) systems (e.g., optical markers), which are confined to controlled laboratory environments and struggle with "in-the-wild" outdoor or long-range scenarios due to line-of-sight issues. Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU)-based systems, while portable, traditionally suffer from accumulated drift, limiting global positioning accuracy. Datasets like AMASS provide diverse motion but lack scene context. Vision-based methods (monocular or multi-view) also face constraints on capture volume and require clear body observations.

Furthermore, integrating multiple sensing modalities (e.g., IMUs, cameras, eye-tracking) and ensuring their precise temporal and spatial alignment has been a significant technical hurdle for previous datasets. Many workarounds, like visual or audio cues, offer limited accuracy and reliability, especially for long recordings.

**Egocentric Perception and Human Motion:** The rise of smart glasses and other wearables (e.g., Apple Vision Pro, Meta Quest, Microsoft HoloLens, Project Aria) necessitates a shift towards "human-centric contextualized AI" that understands the wearer's body motion and actions from their own perspective. Existing egocentric datasets, such as Ego4D and EgoExo4D, provide large-scale video and action labels, but often lack ground-truth full-body parametric motion, precise device localization, or rich multimodal data streams (IMUs, eye tracking). Datasets focusing on hand-object interaction are more common (e.g., Epic-Kitchens), but comprehensive full-body egocentric motion datasets remain sparse. Nymeria aims to fill this specific gap by providing synchronized, localized multimodal egocentric data combined with full-body ground truth.

**Motion-Language Connection:** The integration of natural language descriptions with human motion is an emerging area, particularly with the advent of large language models (LLMs). While some datasets provide simple action labels or short descriptions (e.g., KIT, HumanML3D), they are often limited in scale, granularity, and contextual richness compared to the vast text corpora used for training LLMs. Nymeria addresses this by introducing a hierarchical narration schema, providing significantly longer and more detailed in-context language descriptions. This bridges a crucial gap for developing algorithms that can interpret, generate, and reason about human motion using natural language.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Nymeria is to **create the world's largest, most diverse, and richly annotated human motion dataset collected "in the wild," featuring synchronized and localized multi-device multimodal egocentric data, coupled with extensive natural language descriptions.**

The motivations behind this ambitious endeavor are multifaceted:

1. **Accelerate Research in Human-Centric Contextualized AI:** The paper posits that the next generation of AI assistants will be integrated into wearables, requiring an understanding of the wearer's body motion and actions as crucial context. Existing datasets are insufficient to train robust AI models for this paradigm. Nymeria aims to provide the necessary data scale and richness.
2. **Address Technical Challenges in Data Collection:** The authors identify three core challenges that limit existing datasets:
 - **Obtaining long-term ground-truth motion in the wild:** Traditional mocap systems are limited, and IMU-based solutions suffer from drift. Nymeria seeks to overcome this with a robust hardware and processing pipeline.
 - **Multi-device alignment:** Achieving accurate temporal and spatial synchronization across diverse hardware (mocap suit, egocentric glasses, wristbands) is difficult. Nymeria aims for sub-millisecond accuracy using a novel hardware solution.
 - **Comprehensive data processing and annotations:** Beyond raw sensor data, robust localization, scene representation, and, critically, rich natural language descriptions are needed. Nymeria strives for hierarchical and in-context narrations.
3. **Bridge the Reality-Simulation Gap:** While simulation offers scalability, it often lacks the realism and noise characteristics of real-world data. Nymeria provides high-quality real data to inform and validate simulations.
4. **Enable Novel Research Directions:** By combining full-body ground truth with multi-device egocentric data (videos, IMUs, eye-tracking) and detailed language descriptions in diverse real-world settings, Nymeria aims to unlock new avenues for research in body tracking, motion synthesis, action recognition, multimodal spatial reasoning, and language grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of Nymeria involved a sophisticated, multi-stage methodology encompassing hardware design, data collection protocols, advanced data processing, and human-driven annotation.

**Data Collection Setup:**
- **Hardware:** Each participant wore a **XSens MVN Link** mocap suit (17 inertial trackers) for full-body ground truth, **Project Aria glasses** (RGB, grayscale, eye-tracking cameras, IMUs, barometer, magnetometer, microphones) for egocentric multimodal data, and two **miniAria wristbands** (similar to Aria glasses but without microphones/barometer/magnetometer/ET cameras) to better constrain body tracking.
- **Synchronization:** A custom hardware device provided a universal time signal, ensuring sub-millisecond synchronization across all devices (XSens, Aria, miniAria), minimizing clock drift over long recordings.
- **Recording Protocols:** Data was collected by 2-3 onsite operators. A trained "observer" wearing Project Aria also recorded participants from a third-person perspective. Participants performed 15-20 minute unscripted activities in 4-8 recordings each.
- **Scenarios:** Twenty diverse indoor (cooking, working, cleaning, playing games) and outdoor (hiking, biking, sports, dining) scenarios were defined to capture natural, spontaneous actions and interactions. Participants were given high-level guidelines to encourage authentic behavior.
- **Privacy:** Strict protocols were followed, including obtaining informed consent from participants and homeowners. State-of-the-art de-identification (EgoBlur) was used to blur faces and license plates in all videos.

**Data Processing:**
- **Motion Capture and Retargetting:** XSens data (240Hz skeletal motion) was processed, and a novel algorithm was developed to retarget this motion onto a custom anatomically-inspired parametric human model (159 skeletal joints, 89 for body motion). This involved an inverse kinematics optimization problem to solve for identity and pose parameters, leveraging XSens anatomical landmarks. The process also included loss terms for parameter limits and body collision penalties to mitigate XSens artifacts.
- **6DoF Localization and Mapping with Global Alignment:** Project Aria MPS (using VIO, SLAM, and mapping algorithms) was employed to localize all Aria and miniAria devices with millimeter accuracy in a common, gravity-aligned 3D metric world. This generated high-accuracy 1KHz trajectories for the egocentric devices. The full-body mocap was then aligned to this same 3D world by correlating XSens's dead-reckoned trajectory with the precisely localized Aria device trajectory, using a HandEye calibration formulation to align local motion segments.
- **Eye Gaze and Point Cloud Maps:** MPS also provided calibrated eye gaze estimates and 3D point cloud maps of temporary static scene elements, triangulated from the moving Aria device.

**In-context Motion-Language Description:**
- **Hierarchical Annotation:** Human annotators generated textual descriptions using a coarse-to-fine hierarchical schema:
 1. **Motion Narration (finest):** Detailed descriptions of body posture, direction, velocity, interactions, and attention for <5s clips.
 2. **Atomic Action (middle):** Concise descriptions using verbs for <5s clips, focusing on actions rather than minute poses.
 3. **Activity Summarization (coarsest):** One-sentence summaries for 30s activity clips.
- **Annotation Tool:** Annotators used a custom tool that displayed synchronized playback videos of egocentric RGB, third-person RGB, and 3D human motion rendered with the scene. This multi-view context allowed for rich, accurate descriptions of human-object and human-environment interactions.

**Dataset Statistics:**
Nymeria comprises:
- **300 hours** of daily activities from **264 participants** across **50 locations**.
- **1200 sequences** with an average duration of 15 minutes.
- Total traveling distance of **399 Km** for the headset and **1053 Km** for both wristbands.
- **260 Million** body poses.
- **201.2 Million** egocentric images, **11.7 Billion** IMU samples, **10.8 Million** gaze points.
- **Language descriptions:** **310.5K sentences** (8.64 Million words) from a vocabulary of **6545** distinct words. The average sentence length (27.8 words) is significantly longer than previous motion-language datasets.
- Participant demographics cover diverse ethnicity, age, height, and weight, split by gender (48.5% female, 51.4% male). Locations include 47 houses (31 multi-floor, 201 rooms, 45 gardens) and a campus with cafeteria, office, and trails.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's main findings revolve around the unprecedented scale, diversity, multimodal richness, and comprehensive annotation of the Nymeria dataset, validated through benchmark evaluations.

**1. Unprecedented Scale and Diversity:**
- Nymeria is confirmed as the **world's largest human motion dataset collected in the wild**, surpassing previous benchmarks in total activity hours (300h), number of participants (264), and locations (50).
- Its "in-the-wild" nature, capturing 20 diverse, unscripted daily scenarios in various indoor and outdoor settings, offers a level of realism and naturalness rarely seen in prior controlled datasets.

**2. First-of-its-Kind Multimodal Egocentric Data:**
- Nymeria is the **first dataset to provide synchronized and localized multi-device multimodal egocentric data**. This includes full-body ground-truth motion, egocentric video (RGB, grayscale, eye-tracking), IMUs, magnetometer, barometer, and third-person observer views.
- The precise sub-millisecond synchronization and global 3D alignment of all modalities into a single metric world is a significant technical achievement, providing a robust foundation for complex multimodal perception research.

**3. World's Largest Motion-Language Dataset:**
- With over 310,000 sentences and 8.64 million words, Nymeria sets a new standard as the **largest motion-language dataset**. The hierarchical annotation (fine-grained motion narration, atomic actions, activity summarization) provides descriptions at varying levels of detail, with significantly longer and more contextual sentences than existing datasets. This rich linguistic context directly tied to synchronized motion and visual data is unique.

**4. Baseline Evaluations and Research Opportunities:**
The paper demonstrates the dataset's potential by evaluating several state-of-the-art (SOTA) algorithms across key research areas:

- **Motion Tracking and Synthesis from Sparse Inputs:**
 - **AvatarPoser (3-point regression):** Training on "real" device poses from Nymeria (SLAM output) resulted in slightly worse performance (higher MPJPE) compared to "synthetic" inputs mocked-up from body motion or models trained on AMASS. This highlights the inherent noise and complexity of real-world egocentric data, yet the gap was small, validating the quality of device tracking and the dataset's challenge. Nymeria's harder motions (e.g., hiking uneven terrain) contributed to higher MPJPE in lower-body tracking compared to AMASS.
 - **BoDiffusion (3-point diffusion) and EgoEgo (1-point diffusion):** Diffusion models for motion synthesis trained on Nymeria showed comparable performance to models trained on subsets of AMASS, with 3-point diffusion outperforming 1-point, as expected. This confirms Nymeria's utility for developing robust synthesis models.

- **Representing Human Motion Manifold (VQ-VAE):**
 - A Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) trained on Nymeria motion data achieved similar reconstruction quality (MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, ACC) to VQ-VAEs trained on AMASS. Ablation studies showed improvements with product quantization and increased codebook size. This validates Nymeria's ability to capture and represent the manifold of everyday human activities, offering a basis for motion tokenization for LLMs.

- **Motion and Language (MotionGPT, TM2T):**
 - Evaluations of motion-to-text tasks with a subset of Nymeria's narrations showed that models like MotionGPT, with strong language priors, performed better than TM2T. While initial results were slightly lower than those reported on simpler datasets like HumanML3D due to Nymeria's higher complexity and diversity, the authors expect significant improvements with the full dataset, emphasizing Nymeria's potential for grounding language in physical world interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Nymeria dataset represents a monumental contribution to the fields of computer vision, robotics, artificial intelligence, and human-computer interaction, with far-reaching implications for future technological development.

**Advancing Human Motion Understanding and Egocentric AI:**
Nymeria's primary significance lies in its ability to empower the development of robust AI models for understanding human motion from an egocentric perspective. This is crucial for:
- **Next-generation Wearable AI:** Enabling AI assistants in smart glasses and other wearables to interpret the user's actions, context, and intent based on their body movements and gaze, leading to more personalized and context-aware interactions.
- **Full-Body Tracking from Sparse Sensors:** The dataset is ideal for training and evaluating algorithms that reconstruct full-body pose from limited egocentric inputs (e.g., headset, wristbands), a key challenge for unobtrusive AR/VR devices.
- **Motion Synthesis and Forecasting:** Facilitating the generation of realistic human motion, conditioned by sparse sensor inputs, environmental context, or natural language commands.
- **Action Recognition and Human Behavior Analysis:** Providing rich, contextual data to train models that can accurately recognize complex daily activities and analyze human behavior in natural settings.

**Unique Research Opportunities:**
- **Multimodal Spatial Reasoning and Video Understanding:** The precise localization and synchronization of diverse modalities (video, IMU, gaze, 3D scene) enables novel research in understanding spatial relationships, object interaction, and the wearer's attention within a dynamic environment. It's particularly valuable for tasks like scene reconstruction, image retrieval, and relocalization.
- **Language Grounding and Multimodal LLMs:** The unparalleled scale and granularity of motion-language annotations offer an exceptional resource for training models that can generate motion from text, describe motion in natural language, and ground linguistic understanding in physical actions and perceived environments. This will drive advancements in multimodal large language models.
- **Simulation and Synthetic Data Generation:** By providing realistic, diverse, and comprehensively annotated real-world data, Nymeria can serve as a powerful ground truth for generating and validating more realistic synthetic datasets, helping to bridge the domain gap between simulation and reality.

**Limitations and Societal Impact:**
The authors transparently acknowledge certain limitations:
- The use of a mocap suit and wristbands, while providing ground truth, can appear unnatural in videos and might slightly restrict certain motions.
- XSens data quality can be affected by calibration and body measurements.
- The dataset, while large, covers only a portion of daily activities, leaving out some common public scenarios.

Crucially, the paper addresses the significant **social impact** of collecting personal human data. By emphasizing a commitment to responsible innovation, obtaining consent, employing state-of-the-art de-identification (EgoBlur), ensuring minimum data retention, and providing a permissive research license, Meta aims to mitigate privacy concerns and foster ethical research practices. This approach is vital for building trust and enabling the responsible development of technologies that deeply integrate with personal human experiences.

In conclusion, Nymeria is not merely a dataset; it is a foundational research infrastructure that promises to accelerate the progress of human-centric AI and egocentric perception, paving the way for more intuitive and intelligent interactions with future wearable computing platforms.
