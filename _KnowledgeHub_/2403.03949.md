# Reconciling Reality through Simulation: A Real-to-Sim-to-Real Approach for Robust Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The RialTo framework creates task-specific digital twin simulations from real-world data to enable robust robotic manipulation policies through reinforcement learning fine-tuning. It achieved substantially higher success rates for manipulation tasks under object pose randomization, visual distractors, and physical disturbances compared to imitation learning baselines.

## Problem

- Robotic policies trained via imitation learning often lack robustness to environmental variations and struggle to recover from unforeseen errors.
- Direct application of reinforcement learning in the real world is impractical due to prohibitive data collection times, safety concerns, and complexities in environment resets.
- The manual construction of geometrically, visually, and physically accurate simulation environments (digital twins) for complex real-world scenes is labor-intensive and difficult to scale.

## Method

![[2403.03949-method.png]]
*The RialTo pipeline consists of four main stages: (1) Real-to-Sim transfer to create a digital twin, (2) Inverse distillation to bring real-world knowledge into simulation, (3) Simulation fine-tuning for robustness, and (4) Sim-to-Real transfer to return the robust policy to the physical robot.*

- A four-step ==real-to-sim-to-real pipeline==, **RialTo**, that automatically constructs articulated digital twin simulation environments from real-world scene data using 3D reconstruction and a custom GUI.
- An "==inverse distillation==" process that transfers real-world, perception-based imitation policies into simulation, generating privileged low-level state-based trajectories for efficient reinforcement learning.
- Reinforcement learning (**PPO** with imitation loss) fine-tunes policies in these randomized digital twins, followed by ==teacher-student distillation== co-trained with a small set of real-world data to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- RialTo policies achieved average real-world success rates of **91%** for object pose randomization, **77%** for visual distractors, and **75%** for physical disturbances, outperforming imitation learning policies by up to 2.5 times with significantly fewer demonstrations.
- Co-training with real-world data during teacher-student distillation increased success rates for challenging tasks (e.g., **3.5x** for 'book on shelf' with disturbances), confirming its role in effective sim-to-real transfer.
- Training on target-specific reconstructed digital twins yielded a **90%** success rate on specific real-world drawers, whereas policies trained on generic synthetic assets achieved only **10%** success, highlighting the value of tailored environment reconstruction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining data-efficient real-world imitation learning with robustness-enhancing simulation-based reinforcement learning via task-specific digital twins effectively produces highly robust manipulation policies.
> - Inverse distillation allows existing real-world demonstrations to efficiently bootstrap state-based reinforcement learning in simulation, guiding exploration and ensuring physically plausible behaviors.
> - Co-training the teacher-student distillation process with even a small amount of real-world data is critical for addressing both perceptual shifts and dynamics mismatches when transferring policies from sim to real.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces significant challenges in developing policies that are both performant and robust to the inherent variability of real-world environments, including changes in object poses, physical disturbances, and visual distractors. Two prominent paradigms for learning robot control policies are imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL), each with distinct advantages and limitations that this work seeks to address.

Imitation learning, which involves learning from expert demonstrations, can effectively acquire basic robot control policies. However, IL-trained policies typically exhibit limited robustness, requiring extensive human supervision and large datasets to generalize to minor variations. Furthermore, IL struggles to learn recovery behaviors for unforeseen mistakes or out-of-distribution disturbances, as such scenarios are typically not present in demonstration data. This inherent brittleness limits the widespread deployment of pure IL methods in complex, uncontrolled real-world settings.

Reinforcement learning, conversely, offers the capability for autonomous exploration and the discovery of robust behaviors, including recovery from errors. However, directly applying RL in the real world is often impractical due to prohibitive data collection times, safety concerns, and complexities associated with environment resets and reward function specification.

Simulation-based RL addresses some of these issues by providing a safe, cheap, and scalable environment for data collection. Nevertheless, a major bottleneck for simulation-based approaches is the manual construction of geometrically, visually, and physically realistic simulation environments (digital twins), which is a time- and labor-intensive process, making it difficult to scale. Existing efforts in sim-to-real transfer often rely on techniques like domain randomization, system identification, improved simulator visuals, or teacher-student distillation to bridge the reality gap. While these methods have shown promise for specific tasks or object-level manipulation, creating entire articulated scenes that accurately reflect real-world kinematics and dynamics remains challenging.

Real-to-sim transfer of scenes has seen advancements through 3D reconstruction and inverse graphics. However, these often yield static, unified geometries lacking the necessary object separation, articulation, and accurate physical parameters required for robotic interaction. Prior work on real-to-sim-to-real transfer has primarily focused on leveraging the visual aspects of synthetic scenes without enabling physical interaction with reconstructed geometries or has simplified reconstructed shapes.

This research, RialTo, positions itself at the intersection of these challenges by proposing a novel real-to-sim-to-real pipeline. It aims to combine the data efficiency of real-world imitation learning with the robustness-enhancing capabilities of simulation-based reinforcement learning. The core contribution is to enable efficient training of robust policies by automatically constructing "digital twin" simulation environments from small amounts of real-world data and developing a robust mechanism ("inverse distillation") to leverage real-world demonstrations for efficient RL fine-tuning within these digital twins. This approach endeavors to overcome the limitations of individual learning paradigms and bridge the gap towards scalable, robust robot manipulation in diverse, real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that facilitates the programming of robots to perform manipulation tasks robustly under a wide array of environmental variations and disturbances. These variations encompass changes in object poses, the presence of visual distractors, and unexpected physical perturbations during task execution. The framework is intended to be scalable, task-agnostic, and applicable to numerous tasks without requiring extensive, task-specific engineering or human intervention.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current robotic learning paradigms:

- **Scalability of Robust Policies:** Traditional approaches attempting to train policies across a vast range of scenes and objects face challenges in data acquisition and synthesis. Creating diverse content for simulation is often difficult, and collecting varied real-world data is costly. Furthermore, overly general policies may compromise performance on specific deployment domains. The authors propose that for maximal user benefit, it is more critical for a robot to perform robustly within its specific deployment environment, responding to local disturbances and distractors.
- **Limitations of Imitation Learning (IL):** While IL can be a data-efficient method for initial skill acquisition, policies learned through imitation tend to lack robustness. They often fail when confronted with variations not explicitly present in the training demonstrations and cannot recover from errors or disturbances unless such recovery behaviors were explicitly taught. This inherent brittleness makes IL unsuitable for robust deployment in dynamic real-world settings.
- **Challenges of Real-World Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Direct application of RL in the real world is significantly constrained by practical issues. It is prohibitively slow, poses safety risks during exploratory phases, and is complicated by the need for frequent environment resets and the careful design of dense reward functions.
- **High Cost of Simulation Environment Creation:** Although simulation offers a safe and efficient platform for RL, the manual construction of geometrically, visually, and physically accurate simulation environments (digital twins) for complex robotic manipulation tasks, especially in unstructured settings like homes, is a time and labor-intensive process that does not scale well.

To address these motivations, the core insight driving RialTo is to construct task-specific, high-fidelity "digital twin" simulation environments quickly from real-world data. These environments serve as a sandbox for safely and efficiently fine-tuning policies using RL. The research aims to:
1. Enable the robustification of real-world imitation learning policies through large-scale RL fine-tuning in these dynamically constructed simulation environments.
2. Minimize human effort in environment design and reward engineering by leveraging real-world demonstrations to bootstrap efficient RL.
3. Develop a novel "inverse distillation" technique to transfer real-world, perception-based demonstrations into simulation, where they can be augmented with privileged low-level state information for more efficient RL.
4. Bridge the sim-to-real gap effectively, allowing fine-tuned policies to transfer back to the real world with improved success and robustness.

By doing so, RialTo seeks to provide a practical and scalable solution for learning robust manipulation behaviors that requires minimal human effort and avoids the dangers and inefficiencies of extensive real-world data collection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, RialTo, is a four-step real-to-sim-to-real pipeline designed to robustify real-world imitation learning policies through simulation-based reinforcement learning. The methodology assumes access to a small set of expert demonstrations (e.g., approximately 15) consisting of (observation, action) trajectories collected in the real world.

**Step 1: Real-to-Sim Transfer for Scalable Scene Generation**
The initial phase involves constructing accurate simulation environments that mirror real-world scenes.
- **3D Geometry Reconstruction:** Off-the-shelf 3D reconstruction tools (e.g., Polycam, ARCode, NeRFStudio) are employed to convert multi-view 2D images or video of a real environment into a textured 3D mesh. The choice of tool depends on scene size and detail requirements.
- **Scene Processing with GUI:** The raw, often unified, mesh is then processed using a custom, intuitive graphical user interface (GUI). Users can:
 - Separate the mesh into distinct objects or components ({G_i}).
 - Add kinematic articulations (K) between these components (e.g., defining joints for drawers, cabinets).
 - Specify physical parameters (P) such as mass and friction. For simplicity, default values (e.g., 0.5 friction, 0.41 kg mass) are initially set, with subsequent steps designed to compensate for potential sim-to-real discrepancies.
- **Output:** The result is a simulation-ready scene represented in a USD/URDF file, complete with separated geometries, kinematic relations, and physical properties.

**Step 2: Robustifying Real-World Imitation Learning Policies in Simulation**
This step focuses on leveraging the constructed simulation environment and real-world demonstrations to train a robust policy. It is broken down into two sub-procedures:

- **Inverse-Distillation from Real-to-Sim for Privileged Policy Transfer:**
 - A perception-based policy, π_real(a|o), is initially trained using a small dataset of real-world demonstrations (D_real), where `o` represents 3D point cloud observations and `a` represents delta end-effector pose actions.
 - This trained π_real(a|o) policy is then executed in the high-fidelity simulation environment. As the policy operates on simulated perceptual inputs (`o`), the corresponding low-level privileged state information (`s`), such as object poses and joint states, is simultaneously recorded.
 - This process generates a new dataset, D_sim = {(o, a, s)}, comprising successful trajectories with associated privileged state information, effectively transferring the real-world policy's behavior into the simulation's state space.

- **Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning in Simulation:**
 - The goal is to learn a robust state-based policy, π*_sim(a|s), using reinforcement learning. To simplify reward engineering, a sparse reward function is defined, which activates upon achieving the task's goal state (e.g., specific joint angle, object position).
 - The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is employed, augmented with an imitation learning loss term that biases the policy towards the behaviors observed in D_sim. This augmentation serves a dual purpose: it mitigates exploration challenges inherent in sparse-reward RL and encourages the learning of physically plausible actions that are likely to transfer well to reality.
 - During training, randomization is applied to initial robot, object, and goal poses, which is crucial for endowing the policy with robustness to various environmental configurations.

**Step 3: Teacher-Student Distillation with Co-Training on Real-World Data for Sim-to-Real Transfer**
The final step addresses the transfer of the robust, privileged state-based policy from simulation back to the real world, where only sensor observations are available.

- **Teacher-Student Distillation:** The privileged policy π*_sim(a|s) acts as a "teacher," guiding the learning of a perception-based "student" policy, π*_real(a|o). This typically involves the student policy imitating the teacher's actions given corresponding observations.
- **DAgger and Co-Training:** The distillation process is enhanced by the DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) objective, where the student policy's rollouts are relabeled by the teacher. Crucially, this training procedure is co-trained using a mixture of data: trajectories generated from the `π*_sim(a|s)` teacher in simulation (including scenarios with visual distractors) and the original small set of real-world demonstrations (D_real). This co-training step helps to bridge the sim-to-real gap, addressing both visual distribution shifts between simulated and real point clouds and discrepancies in physical dynamics.
- **Perceptual Input:** For real-world deployment, the robot relies on 3D point cloud observations obtained from a single calibrated depth camera. The robotic platform used is a Franka Panda arm, controlled through 6-DoF Cartesian end-effector position commands.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RialTo covered eight diverse robotic manipulation tasks, ranging from grasping and reorientation of free objects to interacting with articulated objects, in varying levels of environmental perturbation: object pose randomization, visual distractors, and physical disturbances.

- **Enhanced Robustness over Imitation Learning:** RialTo significantly improved policy robustness compared to standard imitation learning (Behavior Cloning - BC). Across tasks, RialTo achieved average success rates of 91% for object pose randomization, 77% with visual distractors, and 75% with physical disturbances. In contrast, BC with 15 demonstrations yielded average success rates of 25%, 11%, and 5% respectively for the same conditions. Even when scaling BC to 50 demonstrations (requiring more than double the human effort), RialTo with 15 demonstrations still achieved approximately 2.5 times higher success rates, demonstrating superior data efficiency and robustness. Qualitatively, RialTo policies exhibited error recovery capabilities not observed in BC.

- **Impact of Co-Training with Real-World Data:** Co-training the teacher-student distillation process with a small set of real-world demonstrations (15 demos) proved beneficial. For challenging tasks like "book on shelf" and "plate on rack" under disturbances, co-training with real-world data resulted in a 3.5x and 2x increase in success rate, respectively, compared to co-training with only simulated demonstrations. This suggests that real-world data helps bridge the sim-to-real gap by accounting for both perceptual shifts and dynamics mismatches, leading to more conservative and safer policy executions. The source of demonstrations for inverse distillation (real-world vs. simulation) had a negligible effect on final performance when real-world co-training was performed.

- **Necessity of Real-to-Sim Scene Transfer:** Training policies on generic, diverse sets of synthetic assets (e.g., 4 drawers from Objaverse) instead of target-specific reconstructed scenes proved less effective. A policy trained on multiple generic drawers achieved only a 10% success rate on a specific real-world target drawer, whereas RialTo trained on the digital twin of the target drawer achieved 90%. This indicates that targeted reconstruction of deployment environments is more effective for specialized performance than relying on broad generalization from diverse, untargeted synthetic data.

- **Scaling to In-the-Wild Environments:** RialTo demonstrated scalability to more uncontrolled and complex real-world environments, such as "open microwave in a kitchen," "put a cup in the trash," and "bring the plate from the sink to the dishrack." In these scenarios, RialTo consistently outperformed standard imitation learning techniques, showing an average improvement of 57% in success rate.

- **Further Ablations and Analysis:**
 - **Training with Distractors:** Explicitly training with visual distractors during teacher-student distillation significantly improved robustness in cluttered environments (e.g., 30% to 70% success for "mug on shelf" with distractors), and also positively influenced performance in non-distractor settings, indicating better sim-to-real transfer.
 - **RL from Scratch:** Reinforcement learning from scratch (PPO without demonstrations) largely failed, achieving 0% success in three out of five tasks. In tasks where it achieved non-zero success, the policies often exploited simulator inaccuracies (e.g., opening a toaster by pushing its bottom instead of the handle), leading to unsafe and non-transferable behaviors. This underscored the critical role of demonstrations in guiding RL towards robust and physically plausible solutions.
 - **RL from Vision vs. State:** Direct RL fine-tuning using visual observations (point clouds) was computationally inefficient and largely ineffective (1% success after 35 hours) compared to fine-tuning on compact state representations (96% success after 12 hours). This finding validated the "inverse distillation" approach to transform real-world demonstrations into a privileged state-based format suitable for efficient RL.
 - **Sim-to-Real Gap:** While minor performance differences were observed between simulation and real-world performance, a general sim-to-real gap was not consistently identified. In some instances, simulation performance was intentionally lower, indicating that the simulation was made harder to ensure robust policy recovery in the real world.

- **User Study on GUI Usability:** A user study involving six participants (five non-experts) demonstrated that the GUI for creating articulated digital twin scenes was intuitive and efficient. The average total time for a user to scan, process, and add articulations/objects to a scene was 25 minutes and 12 seconds, with an average active work time of 14 minutes and 40 seconds. This suggests that the real-to-sim scene transfer component of RialTo is not a significant bottleneck in terms of human effort.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RialTo presents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation by addressing several core limitations of existing learning paradigms and proposing a viable path towards scalable, robust robot deployment. The work has several key areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Bridging the Imitation-RL Gap:** The system effectively combines the benefits of data-efficient imitation learning and robust reinforcement learning. By using small amounts of real-world demonstration data to bootstrap simulation-based RL, RialTo reduces the dependency on large, expensive real-world datasets for robustness, while mitigating the safety and efficiency concerns of direct real-world RL.
- **Reduced Human Effort in Robot Programming:** RialTo's methodology minimizes the human effort traditionally required for robotic task programming. The intuitive GUI for creating digital twin environments allows non-expert users to quickly construct simulation assets, eliminating the need for extensive manual CAD modeling or complex scene engineering. Furthermore, the inverse distillation technique reduces the need for extensive reward engineering in simulation and simplifies the transfer of real-world knowledge.
- **Enhanced Policy Robustness and Generalization:** The primary impact is the significant improvement in the robustness of learned policies to real-world variations, including object pose changes, visual clutter, and physical disturbances. The policies developed with RialTo demonstrate error recovery capabilities and generalize more effectively than those learned through pure imitation, a critical factor for deployment in unstructured environments.
- **Scalability for Diverse Manipulation Tasks:** The demonstrated success across eight diverse manipulation tasks, including interactions with articulated objects and in-the-wild environments, suggests that RialTo is a generalizable framework. This indicates its potential applicability to a broad range of household, industrial, and service robotics problems where rapid, robust skill acquisition is desirable.
- **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The introduction of "inverse distillation" as a technique to transfer real-world, perception-based demonstrations into a privileged state-based format in simulation is a notable methodological contribution. This enables efficient leveraging of existing real-world data for simulation-based RL fine-tuning.
- **Practical Deployment Potential:** By lowering the barrier for creating robust robot skills, RialTo has the potential to accelerate the adoption and deployment of robots in everyday settings. Non-expert users could potentially "program" robots for custom tasks in their specific environments with relatively minimal overhead.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, including the reliance on accurate depth sensors (which may struggle with transparent/reflective objects), the current focus on rigid articulated bodies, the relatively quasi-static nature of the addressed problems (where precise physics identification is less critical), and the total training time (approximately 2 days per task). Future work could explore incorporating RGB-D data, handling deformable objects, improving physics parameter identification, developing faster controllers, and accelerating training for continual learning scenarios.

Despite these limitations, RialTo represents a significant step towards practical and robust robot manipulation by effectively reconciling the complexities of real-world data with the efficiency and safety of simulation.
