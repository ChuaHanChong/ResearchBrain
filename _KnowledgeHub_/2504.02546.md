# GPG: A Simple and Strong Reinforcement Learning Baseline for Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from AMAP, Alibaba Group developed Group Policy Gradient (GPG), a minimalist reinforcement learning method that directly optimizes the RL objective for large language models, significantly reducing computational costs by eliminating critic and reference models. GPG achieved superior reasoning performance across unimodal and multimodal tasks, including a 6.3% average improvement over state-of-the-art on mathematical benchmarks for 7B models and over 14% improvement on multimodal reasoning grounding tasks compared to GRPO.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) methods like PPO are computationally intensive, requiring complex critic and reference models, which increases resource demands and training complexity.
- Components of general RL algorithms, such as PPO, may be redundant when fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) that already possess strong representations from extensive pretraining and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- Current RFT approaches can suffer from reward and gradient estimation biases, and often rely on surrogate loss functions or explicit distributional constraints that can complicate the learning process.

## Method

- Proposed ==Group Policy Gradient== (**GPG**), a minimalist Reinforcement Learning approach that directly optimizes the original RL objective for LLMs, eliminating critic models, reference models, and explicit policy constraints.
- Developed an advantage function using group-level reward normalization and introduced Accurate Gradient Estimation (AGE) to correct for biases arising from samples yielding zero gradients within a group.
- Implemented a thresholding mechanism for minimal valid samples, accumulating them across batches when necessary to reduce variance and enhance the stability of policy updates.

## Results

- GPG-Zero-**7B** achieved an average score of **57.7%** across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the state-of-the-art Oat-Zero-**7B** by **6.3%** on average and requiring **0.45x** training cost and **0.39x** data cost compared to DAPO.
- On multimodal tasks, GPG significantly improved performance, surpassing GRPO by 3.85 percentage points on GEOQA, 7.1 percentage points on average across classification datasets, and 16.68 percentage points on CV-Bench.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Accurate Gradient Estimation and the thresholding mechanism for valid samples were critical for performance improvements, and GPG training did not degrade general capabilities, showing slight improvements on MMLU (+**0.22%**) and C-Eval (+**0.38%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Many auxiliary components in complex RL algorithms like PPO are unnecessary for fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs, which already have strong foundational representations, allowing for a simpler, more direct optimization approach.
> - Directly optimizing the core RL objective with proper gradient and reward normalization, and without explicit KL divergence constraints or surrogate losses, can lead to superior performance and efficiency.
> - Addressing gradient estimation biases by accounting for samples that yield zero gradients and managing high variance in low-valid-sample scenarios are crucial for stable and effective policy learning within a minimalist RL framework.
