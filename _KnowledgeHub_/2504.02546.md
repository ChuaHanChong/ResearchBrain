# GPG: A Simple and Strong Reinforcement Learning Baseline for Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from AMAP, Alibaba Group developed Group Policy Gradient (GPG), a minimalist reinforcement learning method that directly optimizes the RL objective for large language models, significantly reducing computational costs by eliminating critic and reference models. GPG achieved superior reasoning performance across unimodal and multimodal tasks, including a 6.3% average improvement over state-of-the-art on mathematical benchmarks for 7B models and over 14% improvement on multimodal reasoning grounding tasks compared to GRPO.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) methods like PPO are computationally intensive, requiring complex critic and reference models, which increases resource demands and training complexity.
- Components of general RL algorithms, such as PPO, may be redundant when fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) that already possess strong representations from extensive pretraining and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- Current RFT approaches can suffer from reward and gradient estimation biases, and often rely on surrogate loss functions or explicit distributional constraints that can complicate the learning process.

## Method

- Proposed ==Group Policy Gradient== (**GPG**), a minimalist Reinforcement Learning approach that directly optimizes the original RL objective for LLMs, eliminating critic models, reference models, and explicit policy constraints.
- Developed an advantage function using group-level reward normalization and introduced Accurate Gradient Estimation (AGE) to correct for biases arising from samples yielding zero gradients within a group.
- Implemented a thresholding mechanism for minimal valid samples, accumulating them across batches when necessary to reduce variance and enhance the stability of policy updates.

## Results

- GPG-Zero-**7B** achieved an average score of **57.7%** across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the state-of-the-art Oat-Zero-**7B** by **6.3%** on average and requiring **0.45x** training cost and **0.39x** data cost compared to DAPO.
- On multimodal tasks, GPG significantly improved performance, surpassing GRPO by 3.85 percentage points on GEOQA, 7.1 percentage points on average across classification datasets, and 16.68 percentage points on CV-Bench.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Accurate Gradient Estimation and the thresholding mechanism for valid samples were critical for performance improvements, and GPG training did not degrade general capabilities, showing slight improvements on MMLU (+**0.22%**) and C-Eval (+**0.38%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Many auxiliary components in complex RL algorithms like PPO are unnecessary for fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs, which already have strong foundational representations, allowing for a simpler, more direct optimization approach.
> - Directly optimizing the core RL objective with proper gradient and reward normalization, and without explicit KL divergence constraints or surrogate losses, can lead to superior performance and efficiency.
> - Addressing gradient estimation biases by accounting for samples that yield zero gradients and managing high variance in low-valid-sample scenarios are crucial for stable and effective policy learning within a minimalist RL framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) has seen significant advancements due to the development of Large Language Models (LLMs). A key strategy in enhancing LLMs' efficacy, particularly in domain-specific tasks such as mathematical reasoning, involves generating intermediate reasoning steps before producing final answers. This approach is largely attributed to Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) methods.

RFT, which employs Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques, allows models to iteratively refine their ability to generate coherent and logically structured reasoning paths, contrasting with the more rigid, label-dependent training of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). Prominent RFT algorithms include Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). PPO stabilizes policy updates by constraining significant deviations, while GRPO enhances this by evaluating performance across groups of actions, promoting consistent improvements in reasoning quality.

However, existing RFT methods, particularly PPO, pose considerable resource consumption challenges. PPO necessitates the integration of both a critic model and a reference model, which increases computational demands and complicates the training process. Recent efforts, such as ReMax, have aimed to simplify PPO by removing the critic model, and GRPO further eliminates the critic model while utilizing normalized rewards within a sample group. Concurrent work like Dr. GRPO has investigated reward and loss normalization details, identifying reward bias in the advantage function but without demonstrating substantial performance gains over GRPO.

The paper highlights that PPO, originally designed as a general RL algorithm for tasks like Atari games, involves learning both visual representations and control policies. In the context of LLMs, which already possess strong representations from pretraining and SFT, many of PPO's components may be redundant. Given that PPO itself is a simplification of TRPO, which builds on the policy-gradient algorithm, there is a recognized trend toward streamlining RL methods for reasoning tasks to improve scalability. The primary weakness of traditional policy gradients, high variance, can be mitigated through value-function baselines and increased trajectory sampling, practices often employed in LLM post-training. This context motivates the development of a simpler, yet effective, RL method for enhancing LLM reasoning.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing Group Policy Gradient (GPG), a minimalist RL approach that revisits the traditional Policy Gradient (PG) mechanism. GPG aims to address the complexity and resource intensity of methods like PPO and GRPO by directly optimizing the original RL objective, eliminating critic and reference models, avoiding KL divergence constraints, and addressing biases in advantage and gradient estimation. This positioning reflects a broader research trend towards developing more efficient, scalable, and simpler RL algorithms tailored for the specific requirements of fine-tuning large pre-trained models for complex reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a minimalist Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach, termed Group Policy Gradient (GPG), to directly enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) without extensive reliance on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).

The motivation behind this objective stems from several identified challenges and limitations in current Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) methods:

1. **Computational Cost and Complexity of Existing RFT Methods:** Methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) are computationally intensive. PPO, in particular, requires the development and integration of both a critic model and a reference model. This not only complicates the training pipeline but also significantly increases computational resource consumption during training. While GRPO has made strides in simplifying PPO by removing the critic model and using normalized group rewards, it still involves surrogate loss functions and policy constraints. The authors aim for further simplification.

2. **Redundancy of PPO Components for LLM Reasoning:** PPO was initially developed as a general RL algorithm for environments like Atari games, where the policy network often learns both visual representations and control policies from scratch. However, LLMs, through extensive pretraining and SFT, already possess robust representations. The authors hypothesize that many components deemed necessary for general RL (e.g., critic models, explicit KL divergence constraints) might be unnecessary or even detrimental for fine-tuning LLMs that already have strong foundational capabilities. This motivates a "rethinking simplified RL methods" approach.

3. **Limitations of Surrogate Loss Functions and Distributional Constraints:** Conventional RFT approaches often rely on surrogate loss functions (like PPO's clipped objective) and explicit distributional constraints (like KL divergence terms in PPO and GRPO) to ensure stable policy updates. The paper argues that directly optimizing the original RL objective, without these proxies or constraints, could lead to a more direct and potentially more efficient learning process.

4. **Reward and Gradient Estimation Biases:** Existing advantage functions in RFT methods, such as GRPO's normalization using the standard deviation of rewards within a group, can introduce reward bias. Furthermore, when training batches contain samples that yield "all right" or "all wrong" responses within a group, these samples may contribute zero gradients, leading to a bias in the overall gradient estimation. The authors aim to analyze and address these biases to achieve more accurate and robust policy updates.

In summary, the key motivation is to significantly simplify the RL training process for LLMs by stripping away unnecessary components and addressing inherent biases, thereby reducing computational costs, enhancing scalability, and achieving superior performance in reasoning tasks. The ultimate goal is to provide a "simple and strong" baseline that is both effective and resource-efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Group Policy Gradient (GPG), is a minimalist Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach designed to enhance the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). It deviates from conventional RFT methods like PPO and GRPO by directly optimizing the original RL objective and simplifying the training process.

#### 3.1. Preliminary and Task Formulation

The core RL problem is defined as maximizing the expected cumulative reward, $J(\theta) = \max_{\theta} E_{\pi_{\theta}}[\sum_{t=0}^{T} r_t]$. Policy gradient methods aim to estimate the gradient $\nabla_{\theta} J(\theta) = E_{\pi_{\theta}}[\nabla_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(a_t | s_t)Q_{\pi_{\theta}}(s_t, a_t)]$. To reduce variance, the advantage function $A_{\pi_{\theta}}(s_t, a_t) = Q_{\pi_{\theta}}(s_t, a_t) - V_{\pi_{\theta}}(s_t)$ is commonly used, leading to the update rule $\nabla_{\theta} J(\theta) = E_{\pi_{\theta}}[\nabla_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(a_t | s_t)A_{\pi_{\theta}}(s_t, a_t)]$. The paper adopts a one-step advantage estimation, finding it sufficient for model reasoning tasks where computational efficiency is critical.
The task formulation for LLMs is simplified: given a question and prompt $s$, the model samples an action $a$ from its policy $\pi_{\theta}$, obtaining a final reward signal $r$. The policy distribution $\pi_{\theta}$ is modeled autoregressively.

#### 3.2. Group Policy Gradient (GPG)

GPG is designed to address high variance in policy gradient estimation without requiring a value model. It leverages group-level rewards to stabilize learning.

- **Core Objective:** The GPG objective function is defined as:
 $J_{GPG}(\theta) = E_{(q,a)\sim D,\{o_i\}^G_{i=1}}[\frac{1}{\sum^G_{i=1} |o_i|} \sum^G_{i=1} \sum^{|o_i|}_{t=1} (-\log \pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t} | q, o_i, <t) \hat{A}_{i,t})]$
 where $o_i$ represents individual responses within a group $G$.

- **Advantage Function ($\hat{A}_{i,t}$):** The advantage for the $i$-th response is calculated by normalizing group-level rewards $\{R_i\}^G_{i=1}$:
 $\hat{A}_{i,t} = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(\{R_i\}^G_{i=1})}{F_{norm}}$
 This mechanism uses the mean reward within each group to normalize individual rewards, aiming to reduce variance without a traditional value model.
 The term $F_{norm}$ is an optional normalization technique. While standard variance normalization is common in other RL settings, the paper notes that for LLM reasoning tasks (e.g., math with 0.0 or 1.0 rewards), the reward signal is often well-defined, and $F_{norm}=1$ is adopted for simplicity, to avoid introducing reward bias. GRPO uses $F_{norm}=\text{std}\{R(o)\}$, which the paper identifies as a potential source of bias by making the advantage function dependent on the state (through the group samples).

- **Accurate Gradient Estimation (AGE):** The paper identifies that a proportion of samples in a batch might be "all right" (reward 1.0) or "all wrong" (reward 0.0) within a group. These samples yield zero gradients, leading to a biased gradient estimate if not accounted for.
 Given a batch size $B$ and $M$ invalid samples (all right/wrong), the standard backpropagation estimates the gradient as $g = \frac{\sum^B_{i=1} g_i}{B} = \frac{\sum^B_{i=M+1} g_i}{B}$.
 GPG proposes an "accurate gradient estimation" (AGE) by correcting this bias with a factor $\alpha$:
 $\hat{g} = \frac{\sum^B_{i=M+1} g_i}{B-M} = g \frac{B}{B-M} = \alpha g$, where $\alpha = \frac{B}{B-M}$.
 This correction ensures that the gradient is averaged only over valid, non-zero gradient samples. The final objective thus becomes $\hat{J}_{GPG}(\theta) = \alpha J_{GPG}(\theta)$. For multi-GPU training, an equivalent format that avoids extra communication overhead is derived.

- **Thresholding Minimal Partition of Valid Samples and Resampling:** To mitigate high variance when the proportion of valid samples ($1/\alpha$) is excessively low, GPG introduces a threshold $\beta_{th}$. If the proportion of valid samples falls below $\beta_{th}$, valid samples are accumulated into a subsequent batch until the proportion exceeds the threshold. This strategy aims to reduce variance and enhance stability.

- **Simplification of Components:** Compared to PPO, GRPO, and TRPO, GPG simplifies the RL framework by:
 - Eliminating the need for a critic model.
 - Eliminating the need for a reference model.
 - Not relying on a surrogate loss function.
 - Imposing no explicit policy distributional constraints (like KL divergence).

#### 3.3. Algorithm Summary

Algorithm 1 outlines the GPG process:
1. Collect samples, calculate $\hat{A}$ (Equation 6) and $\alpha$ (Equation 7) until $\alpha < 1/\beta_{th}$.
2. Calculate $\log \pi_{\theta}(o)$ (per-token log probabilities).
3. Compute the loss as $-\log \pi_{\theta}(o) \cdot \hat{A} \cdot \alpha$.

This methodology aims to provide a robust, efficient, and scalable RL approach by focusing on direct objective optimization, bias correction, and variance reduction within a minimalist framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental evaluations across unimodal and multimodal reasoning tasks, demonstrating the performance and efficiency of Group Policy Gradient (GPG) compared to existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Unimodal Task Evaluation (Mathematical Reasoning)

- **1.5B Model Performance (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B):** GPG-RS1 and GPG-RS3 models, trained on different open-source datasets, exhibited superior performance. GPG-RS1 achieved an average accuracy of 55.7%, surpassing other distilled 1.5B models (e.g., Open-RS1 at 53.1%). Notably, GPG-RS1 and GPG-RS3 achieved 77.5% and 80.0% on AMC23, respectively, exceeding Open-RS's 67.5% and 70.0%. They also showed competitive performance on MATH-500 (87.6% and 85.0%) and OlympiadBench (50.5% and 52.4%).

- **7B Model Performance (Qwen2.5-Math-7B):** GPG-Zero-7B achieved an average score of 57.7% across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming other baselines. It attained leading scores of 82.5% on AMC23 and 39.0% on Minerva, surpassing SimpleRL-Zero-7B by 22.3% and 11.4% respectively. GPG-7B also outperformed the recent state-of-the-art Oat-Zero-7B by an average of 6.3%. Robust evaluation across multiple runs (Table 16) confirmed GPG's consistent advantage over baselines across pass@1, pass@3, and pass@5 metrics, with lower standard deviations in some cases.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Task Evaluation

- **Geometry Reasoning (GEOQA):** GPG achieved a score of 51.33%, surpassing GRPO's 47.48% by 3.85 percentage points, indicating improved performance in complex geometric reasoning tasks.

- **Classification (Flower102, Pets37, FGVCAircraft, Car196):** GPG achieved an average score of 89.0% across four fine-grained classification datasets, exceeding GRPO by 7.1 percentage points and demonstrating consistent improvements across all datasets.

- **Visual Reasoning (CV-Bench):** GPG demonstrated a significant performance improvement, achieving 76.15% on CV-Bench, an increase of 16.68 percentage points compared to GRPO's 59.47%.

- **Reasoning Grounding (LISA):** GPG showed substantial enhancements over GRPO, improving all metrics by over 14.0 percentage points, indicating superior performance in object localization for reasoning tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study and Discussion

- **Case Study and Training Analysis:** A case study illustrated that GPG produces more comprehensive and accurate reasoning processes compared to GRPO, which exhibited errors in formula analysis. Real-time training metrics (loss, rewards, completion length) suggested GPG's effectiveness and stability.

- **Sensitivity on Group Size:** Increasing the group size from 2 to 16 generally led to progressive performance improvements across most metrics, with a group size of 8 chosen as a trade-off between training cost and performance.

- **Comparison with DAPO:** GPG achieved superior performance (57.7% average vs. DAPO-7B's 56.0%) with reduced training costs (0.45x), data costs (0.39x), and memory usage (24G vs. 28G) compared to DAPO. This suggests GPG is simpler, stronger, and more resource-efficient.

- **KL Constraint:** An ablation study showed that incorporating an explicit KL divergence constraint negatively impacted GPG's performance, supporting the hypothesis that direct optimization without such constraints is effective.

- **Reward Normalization:** Normalization within a group (as used by GPG) was found to be more effective than batch-level normalization for the chosen setup.

- **Gradient Debiasing and Variance Reduction:** The study showed that incorporating the Accurate Gradient Estimation (AGE) (alpha-scaling) significantly improved performance (from 43.9% to 47.8% average score with Fnorm=1). Further, adding the thresholding mechanism for minimal valid samples ($\beta_{th}=0.6$) yielded an additional improvement (from 47.8% to 48.3% average score), confirming its role in variance reduction.

#### 4.4. Evaluation on General Benchmarks

- **MMLU and C-Eval:** GPG training did not degrade general capabilities. The 1.5B model, after GPG training, showed slight improvements on MMLU (+0.22%) and C-Eval (+0.38%), suggesting it is a safe and broadly applicable method.

- **Zero-shot General QA and Coding:** GPG also outperformed GRPO on zero-shot code generation (MBPP, MBPP+) and general QA tasks (HellaSwag, TruthfulQA).

Overall, the main findings indicate that GPG, through its minimalist design, direct objective optimization, and bias correction techniques, consistently achieves superior reasoning capabilities across diverse unimodal and multimodal tasks while being more computationally efficient than prominent RFT baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Group Policy Gradient (GPG) presents a significant development in the field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly for enhancing their reasoning capabilities.

### 6. Significance

1. **Simplification of RL Fine-Tuning:** GPG significantly simplifies the architecture and process of reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT). By revisiting the traditional Policy Gradient (PG) mechanism, it eliminates the need for auxiliary components like critic models, reference models, and explicit KL divergence constraints that are common in PPO and GRPO. This simplification reduces the complexity of implementation and maintenance.
2. **Reduced Computational Overhead:** The minimalist design of GPG directly translates into reduced computational costs and memory usage during training. Experiments demonstrate that GPG achieves superior performance while requiring fewer computational resources compared to other methods like DAPO. This addresses a critical challenge in scaling RFT to larger models and datasets.
3. **Improved Reasoning Quality:** Despite its simplicity, GPG demonstrates superior performance across a wide range of unimodal and multimodal reasoning tasks, including mathematical reasoning, geometry reasoning, classification, visual reasoning, and reasoning grounding. This suggests that the simplified approach can be more effective than more complex existing methods.
4. **Addressing Core RL Challenges:** The work directly tackles fundamental issues in policy gradient methods, such as high variance and gradient estimation bias. The proposed Accurate Gradient Estimation (AGE) technique and the thresholding mechanism for valid samples contribute to more robust and accurate policy updates, which is crucial for stable and effective RL training.
5. **Broad Applicability:** GPG's demonstrated efficacy across diverse unimodal and multimodal tasks, and its maintenance or even slight enhancement of general capabilities on benchmarks like MMLU and C-Eval, indicates its broad applicability without degrading foundational model knowledge.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Facilitating Scalable LLM Training:** By providing a more efficient and less resource-intensive RFT framework, GPG can accelerate the development and fine-tuning of advanced LLMs capable of complex reasoning. This could enable more researchers and institutions with limited computational budgets to effectively utilize RL for improving LLM performance.
2. **Advancement Towards AGI:** Enhancing the reasoning capabilities of models is considered crucial for achieving advanced general intelligence. GPG’s ability to improve reasoning quality through an efficient and scalable method contributes directly to this long-term goal. The minimalist approach might unlock new avenues for building more robust and human-like reasoning processes in AI.
3. **New Baselines for Research:** GPG could serve as a "simple and strong baseline" for future research in RFT for LLMs. Its clear, direct approach might encourage the development of further simplified yet powerful RL algorithms, shifting focus from complex architectural designs to core optimization principles.
4. **Practical Applications:** Improved reasoning capabilities in LLMs, especially in mathematical and multimodal domains, have direct implications for practical applications such as automated problem-solving, advanced virtual assistants, intelligent tutoring systems, and enhanced image/video analysis with complex inference.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge that due to computational budget constraints, the method was not evaluated on "extremely large models." This suggests that while GPG has shown promise on 1.5B and 7B parameter models, its performance and scalability characteristics on models with hundreds of billions or trillions of parameters remain to be fully explored.
