# Efficient High-Resolution Deep Learning: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Aarhus University provide the first comprehensive survey on efficient high-resolution deep learning, categorizing existing methods into five groups and outlining their applications and datasets. The work offers a structured overview of techniques designed to address the significant computational and memory challenges presented by high-resolution visual data.

## Problem

- High-resolution input data causes substantial increases in computational cost, memory consumption, and inference latency for deep learning models, particularly Vision Transformers.
- Naive approaches like uniform downsampling or cutting images into patches compromise accuracy by losing crucial details or destroying global context, respectively.
- A systematic overview and categorization of existing methods to efficiently process high-resolution visual data was absent from the literature.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic literature review to identify and classify efficient high-resolution deep learning methods into ==five distinct categories==.
- Empirically demonstrated the limitations of naive baselines, such as ==uniform downsampling== and ==cutting into patches==, on a crowd counting task.
- Compiled a comprehensive list of ==high-resolution datasets== and described real-world applications where these methods are critical.

## Results

- The survey established a new taxonomy, categorizing efficient high-resolution deep learning methods into Non-Uniform Downsampling, Selective Zooming and Skipping, Lightweight Scanner Networks, Task-Oriented Input Compression, and High-Resolution Vision Transformers.
- Empirical evaluation showed that uniform downsampling increased Mean Absolute Error by **43-155%** and cutting into patches by **1-6%** on a crowd counting dataset.
- A comprehensive compilation of relevant high-resolution datasets and a detailed overview of applications in fields such as medical imaging, remote sensing, and surveillance were provided.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Specialized deep learning methods are essential to efficiently process high-resolution data while preserving accuracy, as simple baselines lead to significant performance degradation.
> - Techniques for handling high-resolution deep learning can be broadly classified based on how they manage input resolution, select regions, design lightweight architectures, compress inputs, or adapt Transformer attention mechanisms.
> - The choice of efficient high-resolution method depends on factors like task requirements, data saliency characteristics, and available hardware resources, each offering specific trade-offs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

**The Challenge of High-Resolution Data:**
The proliferation of high-resolution cameras in modern devices, ranging from smartphones and drones to medical equipment and satellites, has led to an explosion of high-resolution image and video data. Applications such as cancer detection, automated navigation, weather prediction, and surveillance increasingly rely on processing these detailed inputs with deep learning models. However, feeding such high-resolution data directly into deep neural networks presents significant technical challenges. These include a drastic increase in model parameters, computational cost (FLOPs), inference latency, and GPU memory consumption. Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures, in particular, suffer from quadratic scaling of inference speed and parameters with input size due to their self-attention mechanism. These issues are further exacerbated when processing needs to occur on resource-constrained edge devices or within time-critical applications like self-driving cars. While multi-GPU setups can mitigate some memory and latency issues, they are often cost-prohibitive or impractical in many real-world scenarios.

**Limitations of Baseline Approaches:**
The paper identifies two common, yet suboptimal, baseline approaches in the literature:
1. **Uniform Downsampling (UD):** This involves simply resizing images to a lower resolution. While simple, it typically leads to a significant decrease in accuracy because crucial details are lost. The authors empirically demonstrate this, showing a 43% to 155% increase in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for crowd counting when downsampling.
2. **Cutting Into Patches (CIP):** This method involves segmenting the high-resolution input into smaller, manageable patches, processing each independently, and then aggregating the results. This approach suffers from two main drawbacks: it destroys global contextual features that many deep learning models rely on, leading to accuracy degradation (though less severe than UD in their empirical test, +1% to +6% MAE increase); and it drastically increases inference time due to sequential processing of multiple patches.

**Distinguishing from Existing Surveys:**
The authors explicitly state that, "To the best of our knowledge, no other survey on the topic of high-resolution deep learning exists." This positions their work as a pioneering and much-needed comprehensive review in this specific niche. While they acknowledge other related surveys – on Transformer efficiency, histopathology, CT imaging, crowd counting, and remote sensing – they clarify that these works either cover broader aspects of efficiency (e.g., general Transformer efficiency) or are application-specific without a dedicated focus on the *efficiency challenges* posed by high-resolution inputs across diverse domains. Crucially, the survey deliberately excludes multi-scale or multi-column networks whose primary goal is accuracy improvement rather than efficiency for high-resolution inputs, unless they explicitly address the latter. This focused scope allows the survey to provide a deep dive into methods specifically designed to manage the computational burden of high-resolution data.

**Relevance to Emerging Technologies:**
The survey directly addresses a growing bottleneck in AI development. As sensor technology continues to advance (demonstrated by Figure 3 in the paper showing increasing camera resolutions over time), the demand for efficient processing of high-resolution data will only intensify. The work is highly relevant to areas like edge AI, real-time systems, and large-scale data analysis where computational resources and latency are critical factors. By categorizing and analyzing existing solutions, this survey provides a foundational reference for researchers and practitioners grappling with these contemporary challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this survey are:

1. **To address a critical gap in the literature:** The authors identify the lack of a comprehensive survey specifically dedicated to deep learning methods designed for efficient processing of high-resolution images and videos. This gap is significant given the increasing prevalence of high-resolution data and the inherent computational challenges it poses.
2. **To summarize and categorize existing efficient methods:** The core goal is to collect, describe, and systematically organize the diverse approaches developed to overcome the technical challenges (memory, computation, inference time) associated with high-resolution deep learning. This categorization aims to bring clarity to a fragmented field.
3. **To highlight real-world applications:** By detailing various applications across medical imaging, remote sensing, surveillance, and other scientific domains, the survey underscores the practical importance and broad utility of efficient high-resolution deep learning. It also implicitly motivates further research in these areas.
4. **To compile information on high-resolution datasets:** The survey provides a comprehensive list of available high-resolution datasets, which is crucial for reproducibility, benchmarking, and guiding future experimental work in the field.
5. **To provide practical guidance and future directions:** Through a discussion of the advantages and disadvantages of different method categories, the authors aim to offer recommendations for practitioners. Furthermore, by identifying open issues and unexplored research avenues, the survey seeks to stimulate new investigations and advancements in efficient high-resolution deep learning.
6. **To demonstrate the inadequacy of simple baselines:** The empirical evaluation of uniform downsampling and cutting into patches serves as a clear motivation, illustrating why more sophisticated methods are necessary to maintain accuracy while achieving efficiency.

In essence, the survey is motivated by the growing technical necessity to process high-resolution visual data effectively and efficiently using deep learning, an area where existing general-purpose solutions often fall short.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic survey methodology to analyze the landscape of efficient high-resolution deep learning. Their approach can be broken down into several key steps:

1. **Problem Definition and Scope Delimitation:** The survey begins by meticulously defining "high-resolution" in a task- and period-dependent manner, acknowledging that it refers to resolutions that create *technical challenges* for deep learning at a given time. It clearly establishes the scope by focusing exclusively on methods that explicitly address memory, computation, and inference time constraints, differentiating from methods primarily aimed at accuracy or general Transformer efficiency.

2. **Empirical Evaluation of Baselines:** To underscore the necessity for specialized efficient methods, the authors conduct a mini-experiment. They evaluate two common naive approaches – Uniform Downsampling (UD) and Cutting Into Patches (CIP) – on the Shanghai Tech Part B crowd counting dataset using a pre-trained SASNet model. This provides concrete evidence (Table I) of the significant accuracy and/or inference time penalties incurred by these simple strategies.

3. **Comprehensive Literature Review and Method Categorization:** The core of the methodology involves a thorough review of existing literature. Based on their analysis, the authors propose a novel categorization scheme for efficient high-resolution deep learning methods into five distinct groups:
 - **Non-Uniform Downsampling (NUD):** Methods that selectively sample more pixels from salient areas and fewer from non-salient ones.
 - **Selective Zooming and Skipping (SZS):** Approaches that dynamically zoom into important regions or skip unimportant patches.
 - **Lightweight Scanner Networks (LSNs):** Fully convolutional networks (FCNs) designed to efficiently scan entire high-resolution inputs with minimal parameters.
 - **Task-Oriented Input Compression (TOIC):** Methods that transform high-resolution inputs into lightweight, task-specific representations. This includes subcategories like frequency-domain DNNs.
 - **High-Resolution Vision Transformers (HR-ViTs):** Transformer-based architectures specifically optimized to mitigate the quadratic complexity of self-attention for high-resolution inputs.
 For each category, the authors describe the underlying principles, formalisms (where applicable, e.g., NUD equations), and provide illustrative examples from the literature (e.g., RAZN, VGG-720p, Slide Graph, ViL).

4. **Compilation of High-Resolution Datasets:** A dedicated section is devoted to listing and detailing popular high-resolution datasets (Table IV) used across various deep learning applications. This includes information on resolution, number of samples, annotations, splits, publication year, and availability. This resource aids researchers in selecting appropriate datasets for their studies and promotes standardized benchmarking.

5. **Discussion and Critical Analysis:** The survey includes a critical discussion of the advantages and disadvantages of each proposed method category. This analysis considers factors such as suitability for different tasks, impact of saliency quality, distortion effects, domain knowledge requirements, and architectural considerations. This provides a balanced perspective and helps readers understand the trade-offs involved.

6. **Identification of Open Issues and Future Research Directions:** The authors conclude by outlining current trends, identifying areas that are underexplored, and suggesting promising avenues for future research. This includes combining efficient high-resolution deep learning with other efficient DL paradigms (e.g., model compression, dynamic inference, split computing), exploring new architectures (e.g., MLP-based), and novel applications of existing concepts (e.g., frequency-domain ViTs, multimodal co-attention).

By systematically reviewing, categorizing, analyzing, and discussing methods, datasets, and future directions, the authors provide a structured and insightful overview of the efficient high-resolution deep learning landscape.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core findings of this survey revolve around the identification and comprehensive description of five main categories of efficient high-resolution deep learning methods, along with an overview of their applications and supporting datasets.

**A. Five Categories of Efficient High-Resolution Deep Learning Methods:**

1. **Non-Uniform Downsampling (NUD):**
 - **Principle:** Instead of uniformly reducing resolution, NUD methods sample more pixels from salient (important) areas of an image and fewer from non-salient regions. This often involves a preliminary saliency detection network and a non-uniform resampler (e.g., using transformations based on saliency maps or spatial transformer networks).
 - **Key Idea:** Maximize the preservation of task-relevant details while reducing overall resolution.
 - **Examples:** Learning to Zoom for gaze estimation and fine-grained classification; methods for semantic segmentation that sample near semantic boundaries; FOVEA for object detection using structured grids; SALISA for video object detection using spatio-temporal saliency.
 - **Efficiency:** Reduces input size to the main DNN, thus lowering computation and memory.

2. **Selective Zooming and Skipping (SZS):**
 - **Principle:** An evolution of the "cutting into patches" approach, SZS selectively processes only the most important regions or patches of an image, potentially at varying zoom levels, while entirely skipping others. This decision-making often leverages reinforcement learning or hierarchical network structures.
 - **Key Idea:** Focus computational resources only where they are most needed.
 - **Examples:** Reinforced Auto-Zoom Net (RAZN) for breast cancer segmentation in whole-slide images (WSIs); GigaDet for near real-time object detection in gigapixel videos by selecting top-K patch candidates; REMIX for pedestrian detection, partitioning frames and using networks of varying computational cost.
 - **Efficiency:** Reduces the number of computations by avoiding processing irrelevant areas.

3. **Lightweight Scanner Networks (LSNs):**
 - **Principle:** These are fully convolutional neural networks (FCNs) specifically designed to be lightweight and efficient for scanning entire high-resolution inputs. They are often task-specific and leverage the inherent efficiency of FCNs in a sliding-window setting by sharing computation in overlapping regions.
 - **Key Idea:** Design intrinsically efficient architectures capable of processing large inputs directly.
 - **Examples:** VGG-720p and VGG-1080p for real-time heatmap generation on drones; YOLT for rapid multi-scale object detection in satellite imagery; Fast ScanNet for cancer metastasis detection in WSIs; ICNet and ESPNet for real-time semantic segmentation using multi-scale processing and efficient convolutions. HR-NAS uses neural architecture search to design LSNs.
 - **Efficiency:** Achieved through simplified architectures, weight sharing, and efficient convolutional operations.

4. **Task-Oriented Input Compression (TOIC):**
 - **Principle:** This category involves compressing high-resolution inputs into compact, lightweight representations *before* feeding them to the main deep learning model. The compression is tailored to preserve task-relevant information rather than aiming for general image reconstruction quality.
 - **Key Idea:** Transform data into a more efficient domain for processing.
 - **Examples:** Slide Graph for cancer prognostics by building a graph representation of cellular architecture in WSIs; autoencoder-based compression for gigapixel histopathology images; MCAT for cancer survival prediction using multimodal co-attention to reduce spatial complexity. A subcategory is **frequency-domain DNNs**, which process inputs converted via Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) or Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT), like. MMNet performs object detection directly on H.264 compressed video streams by leveraging motion vectors.
 - **Efficiency:** Achieved by drastically reducing the dimensionality of the input feature space.

5. **High-Resolution Vision Transformers (HR-ViTs):**
 - **Principle:** Addresses the quadratic computational complexity of the self-attention mechanism in traditional Vision Transformers (ViTs) when applied to high-resolution images. These methods modify the attention mechanism or integrate convolutional operations to achieve linear or near-linear scaling.
 - **Key Idea:** Adapt the powerful Transformer architecture for high-resolution efficiency.
 - **Examples:** Vision Longformer (ViL) uses global tokens and windowed attention for linear complexity; High-Resolution Transformer (HRFormer) performs self-attention within local patches, augmented with depth-wise convolutions; Multi-Scale High-Resolution Vision Transformer (HRViT) uses cross-shaped attention and parameter sharing; Glance and Gaze Transformer (GG-Transformer) employs dilated partitions with parallel depth-wise convolutions; Hierarchical Image Pyramid Transformer (HIPT) processes gigapixel WSIs using nested ViTs at different scales.
 - **Efficiency:** Achieved by redesigning the self-attention mechanism to avoid quadratic complexity, often combined with multi-scale processing or convolutional inductive biases.

**B. Real-World Applications:**
The survey highlights the broad applicability of high-resolution deep learning across diverse fields, including:
- **Medical and Biomedical Image Analysis:** Cancer detection, semantic segmentation of tissues, COVID-19 detection in CT scans, neuropathology, wireless capsule endoscopy.
- **Remote Sensing:** Building detection, airplane detection, road network extraction, forest damage assessment, weed identification, crop monitoring, weather prediction.
- **Surveillance:** Person identification, pedestrian detection, crowd counting, social distancing enforcement.
- **Other:** Wheat ear density estimation in agriculture, material science segmentation, real-time background replacement in video calls.
The consistent theme across these applications is the necessity for high resolution to capture fine details crucial for accuracy, alongside the pressing need for computational efficiency.

**C. High-Resolution Datasets:**
The survey compiles a valuable list of popular high-resolution datasets (Table IV) that are frequently used in the field. These datasets span various applications like person segmentation (Supervisely Persons), crowd counting (PANDA, UCF CC 50, Shanghai Tech, UCF-QNRF, NWPU-Crowd), autonomous driving (CityScapes, SYNTHIA-RAND, ApolloScape, Argoverse-HD, BDD100K), scene understanding (PASCAL-Context, ADE20K, COCO-Stuff), land cover classification (DeepGlobe, Copernicus, fMoW), medical imaging (KID, CAD-CAP, CAMELYON16, TUPAC16, BACH, TCGA-BRCA, PCa-Histo, INbreast), and object detection (UA-DETRAC, ImageNet-VID, FAIR1M, COCO). This list serves as a practical resource for researchers, enabling standardized evaluation and fostering advancements.

In summary, the main findings include a novel taxonomy of efficient high-resolution deep learning techniques, a demonstration of the limitations of naive approaches, a comprehensive overview of real-world use cases, and a curated list of relevant datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The survey "Efficient High-Resolution Deep Learning" holds significant value and potential impact across several dimensions:

**A. Contribution to the Field:**
1. **First Comprehensive Taxonomy:** The most substantial contribution is the creation of the first dedicated and comprehensive survey on efficient high-resolution deep learning. By categorizing diverse approaches into five distinct groups (NUD, SZS, LSNs, TOIC, HR-ViTs), the authors bring much-needed structure and clarity to a previously fragmented research area. This taxonomy provides a common language and framework for understanding, comparing, and developing new methods.
2. **Highlighting Critical Challenges:** The paper effectively articulates the severe computational, memory, and latency challenges posed by high-resolution data, especially for modern deep learning architectures like Vision Transformers. The empirical demonstration of the shortcomings of simple baselines (uniform downsampling and cutting into patches) firmly establishes the necessity for the more sophisticated methods reviewed.
3. **Consolidated Knowledge Base:** By synthesizing knowledge from a wide array of papers, applications, and datasets, the survey serves as a vital single-point reference for researchers entering the field or seeking an overview of existing solutions. The detailed list of high-resolution datasets is particularly valuable for promoting reproducibility and standardized benchmarking.

**B. Practical Implications and Guidance:**
1. **Informed Method Selection:** The discussion section, which critically analyzes the advantages and disadvantages of each method category, is highly practical. It offers valuable guidance for practitioners and researchers in selecting the most appropriate efficient high-resolution deep learning technique based on their specific application, hardware constraints, saliency characteristics of the data, and task requirements. For instance, it highlights that NUD/SZS are best for small salient areas, LSNs for map-like outputs in uniform scenes, and TOIC for tasks with strong domain knowledge.
2. **Resource Optimization:** The survey directly addresses the practical need for optimizing computational resources in AI deployments. By understanding the principles behind these efficient methods, engineers and developers can design more performant and cost-effective deep learning systems, especially for edge computing, real-time applications (e.g., autonomous vehicles, drones), and large-scale data analysis (e.g., medical imaging, satellite imagery).
3. **Bridging Theory and Application:** By illustrating the theoretical methods with numerous real-world applications (medical, remote sensing, surveillance, etc.), the survey effectively connects research advancements with practical utility, motivating further innovation tailored to specific domain needs.

**C. Future Research Directions and Outlook:**
1. **Stimulating Novel Research:** The "Outlook" section is particularly forward-looking, identifying several unexplored avenues that could lead to significant breakthroughs. These include:
 - **Integration with Other Efficient DL Paradigms:** Proposing the combination of high-resolution methods with techniques like model compression, dynamic inference, collaborative inference, and continual inference opens up a vast space for hybrid solutions. The idea of combining NUD with early exiting is a concrete example.
 - **Exploring New Architectures:** Suggesting the use of attention-free MLP-based methods as lightweight alternatives for Transformers in high-resolution contexts points to a promising direction, given MLPs' typically lower computational overhead.
 - **Multimodal Applications:** Extending multimodal co-attention (as seen in MCAT) to other multimodal tasks, particularly those involving audio, vision, and language, could unlock new capabilities for complex data.
 - **Frequency-Domain ViTs:** Combining frequency-domain representations with Vision Transformers could lead to even more efficient processing than current frequency-domain CNNs.
 - **Edge-Cloud Collaboration:** Emphasizing the synergy between efficient high-resolution deep learning and edge computing paradigms (e.g., split computing where compressed representations are sent to the cloud) addresses a crucial challenge in distributed AI.
2. **Setting a Research Agenda:** By explicitly articulating these open issues, the survey effectively sets a research agenda for the coming years, guiding the efforts of individual researchers and funding bodies.

In conclusion, this survey is a timely and invaluable contribution to the deep learning community. It not only consolidates the current state of efficient high-resolution deep learning but also provides a clear roadmap for future innovation, enabling the development of more powerful, efficient, and broadly applicable AI systems. Its impact will be felt across academic research, industrial applications, and the continued advancement of AI at the edge and scale.
