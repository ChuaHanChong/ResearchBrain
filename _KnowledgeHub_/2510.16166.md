# Extending Prediction-Powered Inference through Conformal Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified framework connects prediction-powered inference (PPI) with conformal prediction, enabling PPI methods to systematically inherit guarantees like privacy, robustness, and validity under continuous distribution shifts for statistical inferences. The approach provides theoretical validity for mean, Z-estimation, M-estimation, and e-value based inference, while empirically demonstrating applicability in private healthcare data analysis and continuous risk monitoring.

## Problem

- Existing Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) methods primarily focus on statistical validity but require arduous, case-by-case constructions for additional guarantees like privacy, robustness, or validity under distribution shifts.
- The current PPI landscape is fragmented, lacking a general mechanism to systematically incorporate properties beyond basic statistical unbiasedness.
- Previous attempts to connect Conformal Prediction (CP) to non-parametric statistical inference, especially for 'batch' scenarios, faced challenges with scaling adjustments.

## Method

- The research integrates ==calibrated conformal set-predictors== directly into the ==Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI)== framework, using ==set-based imputation== instead of point predictions.
- A ==three-step procedure== is proposed for mean estimation, involving conformal set predictor fitting, one-sided confidence interval computation for expected infimum/supremum, and final confidence interval construction with a ==miscoverage penalty==.
- The methodology is extended to ==Z-estimation==, ==M-estimation== problems, and ==e-value-based inference==, systematically adjusting statistical estimators with a penalty based on the conformal predictor's miscoverage rate.

## Results

- **Conformal PPI** was competitive in mean estimation (phishing URL prevalence), providing **tightest confidence intervals** compared to **PPI++** and **FAB**, and showed competitive performance in median estimation (gene expression levels).
- The method successfully enabled privacy-preserving inference for thyroid cancer recurrence using differentially private conformal prediction, with only minor increases in interval sizes.
- For continuous risk monitoring, prediction-powered e-values rejected false null hypotheses significantly faster and with higher confidence than methods relying on occasional labeled samples, while maintaining low false positive rates in dynamic, time-series data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The framework allows PPI methods to inherit advanced guarantees (e.g., privacy, robustness) directly from the properties of the underlying conformal prediction method used for calibration, overcoming the need for bespoke derivations.
> - Formal proofs establish the validity of prediction-powered procedures across diverse statistical inference tasks, including mean, Z-estimation, M-estimation, and e-value-based hypothesis testing.
> - Statistical power and confidence interval sizes are quantified, demonstrating their dependence on the average size of conformal predictive sets, the range of the estimated function, and the miscoverage rate.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Csillag et al. operates at the intersection of two contemporary fields in machine learning and statistics: Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) and Conformal Prediction (CP).

**Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI)** is a recent methodology designed to improve the quality of statistical inference when a limited number of labeled samples are available, but a larger dataset of unlabeled samples exists alongside auxiliary data. It achieves this by utilizing a black-box machine learning (ML) model to impute missing data, while simultaneously applying a debiasing mechanism to ensure the validity of subsequent statistical inferences. Existing PPI methods primarily focus on achieving statistical validity, meaning that the inferences are unbiased. However, a limitation of the current PPI landscape is the necessity for bespoke constructions when additional, often critical, guarantees are required. These guarantees include properties such as differential privacy for sensitive data, robustness against outliers or adversarial distribution shifts, and validity under continuous changes in data distribution. Deriving PPI methods with these enhanced properties typically involves arduous, case-by-case analyses, leading to a fragmented approach across different application needs.

**Conformal Prediction (CP)**, on the other hand, is a framework for quantifying uncertainty in machine learning predictions. It produces prediction sets (or intervals) that are guaranteed to contain the true label with a user-specified probability (e.g., 1-alpha coverage), irrespective of the underlying data distribution, provided the data are exchangeable. A significant strength of conformal prediction is the extensive and growing body of literature that extends its guarantees to various challenging settings. This includes methods for ensuring privacy, robustness to different types of distribution shifts (strategic, adversarial, continuous), resilience to outliers, and handling censored or missing data.

The current work identifies a critical gap between these two fields: while CP offers a general mechanism for achieving robust, private, and adaptable uncertainty quantification, its direct application to *non-parametric statistical inference* problems (which is the domain of PPI) has been limited. Existing attempts to connect CP to statistical inference, particularly for "batch" scenarios involving multiple data points, often require scaling adjustments that become problematic as the number of samples increases.

Csillag et al. position their work as a resolution to this issue by establishing a general connection between PPI and CP. Their contribution aims to provide a unified framework for PPI that naturally inherits the additional guarantees from the rich CP literature, thereby overcoming the need for fragmented, case-specific derivations that characterize previous PPI approaches. This represents a foundational step towards broadening the applicability of PPI to more complex and demanding real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general and principled framework for Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) that can systematically incorporate desirable strong guarantees beyond mere statistical validity. Specifically, the authors aim to extend PPI to naturally achieve properties such as privacy, robustness to various data perturbations, and validity under continuous distribution shifts.

The motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations of existing PPI methodologies. While traditional PPI methods effectively leverage machine learning models to impute missing data and correct for bias in statistical parameter inference, they typically focus solely on ensuring the validity of these inferences under idealized conditions. In many practical applications, however, statistical inferences must meet additional stringent requirements. For instance:
- **Privacy:** When dealing with sensitive data, such as medical records, inferences must be made in a manner that preserves the privacy of individuals, often requiring guarantees like differential privacy.
- **Robustness:** In real-world environments, data may contain outliers, be subject to adversarial manipulation, or undergo unannounced distribution shifts. Inference procedures need to be robust to these challenges to maintain their reliability.
- **Validity under Continuous Distribution Shifts:** In dynamic settings, such as monitoring a deployed system, the underlying data distribution may change gradually over time. Methods are needed that can provide valid inferences continuously, without requiring active data collection or frequent recalibration.

Previous attempts to incorporate such strong guarantees into PPI have typically involved developing new methods from scratch for each specific guarantee or application context. This case-by-case approach is described as "arduous" and leads to a "fragmented" landscape of PPI methods.

The authors propose to resolve this issue by leveraging the extensive and well-developed body of research in Conformal Prediction (CP). They hypothesize that by using a calibrated conformal set-predictor for data imputation within the PPI framework, they can directly inherit the additional properties (e.g., privacy, robustness) that have been established for various conformal prediction methods.

The specific objectives of the paper include:
- Proposing a general framework for deriving prediction-powered methods with stronger guarantees by integrating imputation via conformal set-predictors.
- Instantiating this framework for common statistical inference tasks:
 - Inference of means.
 - General Z-estimation problems (e.g., medians, quantiles, regression coefficients).
 - General M-estimation problems (e.g., robust statistics, maximum likelihood estimates).
 - Inference based on e-values and e-value-based procedures, noting that this is the first general prediction-powered procedure for e-values that can operate offline.
- Providing theoretical proofs for the validity of their procedures under minimal assumptions and quantifying their statistical power, linking it to the characteristics of the conformal predictive sets.
- Demonstrating the practical advantages and applicability of their method in scenarios where previous PPI methods are insufficient, specifically private healthcare data analysis and continuous risk monitoring in time-series data.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to offer a unified, general solution that simplifies the development of PPI methods with strong guarantees, thereby expanding the scope and practical utility of prediction-powered inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology proposed by Csillag et al. involves integrating conformal prediction directly into the prediction-powered inference (PPI) framework. The central idea is to perform data imputation not with point predictions from a machine learning model, but with *calibrated conformal set-predictors*. This approach is designed to allow the PPI framework to inherit additional desirable properties, such as privacy and robustness, directly from the underlying conformal prediction method chosen for calibration.

The general setup assumes access to independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) data (X_i, Y_i), where X_i are observed, but Y_i are missing. A set-predictor, C: X → 2^Y, is fitted on a hold-out dataset. The miscoverage rate of this predictor, Err(C) = P[Y ∉ C(X)], is controlled through the conformal calibration process. The subsequent inference procedures are developed purely for scalar estimation, with a note on analogous extension to multivariate settings.

#### 3.1. Warmup: Mean Estimation

For inferring E[ϕ(Y)] for some bounded function ϕ: Y → R (i.e., ϕ(Y) ∈ [a, b] almost surely), the authors introduce **Lemma 2.1**. This lemma establishes that E[ϕ(Y)] can be bounded by terms involving the expected infimum and supremum of ϕ(C(X)) and a penalty term proportional to M * Err(C), where M = b - a (the range of ϕ(Y)). Specifically:
E[inf ϕ(C(X))] - M * Err(C) ≤ E[ϕ(Y)] ≤ E[sup ϕ(C(X))] + M * Err(C).
The proof relies on the law of total expectation, conditioning on whether Y belongs to the predictive set C(X), and utilizes the boundedness of ϕ(Y).

This lemma motivates the following three-step procedure for mean estimation:
1. **Conformal Set Predictor Fitting:** A conformal set predictor C is fitted on a hold-out dataset using a suitable conformal calibration method (e.g., split conformal prediction).
2. **One-Sided Confidence Interval Computation:** Using the unlabeled data (X_i), lower and upper one-sided (1-α/2)-confidence intervals are computed for E[inf ϕ(C(X))] and E[sup ϕ(C(X))], respectively. These can be obtained using standard off-the-shelf methods like CLT-based CIs, Hoeffding CIs, or e-value-based methods.
3. **Confidence Interval Construction:** The final (1-α)-confidence interval for E[ϕ(Y)] is constructed by adjusting the one-sided bounds from step 2 with the penalty term M * Err(C).

**Proposition 2.3** formally proves that this procedure yields a valid (1-α)-confidence interval. Furthermore, the framework allows direct inheritance of properties from the conformal predictor: if C satisfies privacy or robustness, the resulting confidence interval will also inherit these properties. **Proposition 2.4** quantifies the size of the confidence interval, relating it to the average size of the conformal predictive sets, the range M, the miscoverage rate Err(C), and the tightness of the one-sided confidence intervals.

#### 3.2. Z-Estimation and M-Estimation Problems

The methodology is extended to **Z-estimation** problems, where the estimand θ* is the solution to E[ψ(Y; θ*)] = 0. This covers a broad range of tasks including means, medians, quantiles, and regression coefficients. The assumption here is that ψ(Y; θ) is bounded in [a_θ, b_θ] for each θ.

The procedure for Z-estimation involves:
1. For each θ in the parameter space Θ, computing lower and upper one-sided (1-α/2)-confidence intervals for E[inf ψ(C(X); θ)] and E[sup ψ(C(X); θ)].
2. Constructing a confidence set for θ* by identifying all θ for which the interval [lower bound - M_θ * Err(C), upper bound + M_θ * Err(C)] contains zero.

**Proposition 2.5** proves the validity of this confidence interval for θ*. **Proposition 2.6** provides a bound on the size of this confidence interval, considering the smoothness of the one-sided confidence intervals and the behavior of ψ around θ*.

For **M-estimation** problems, where θ* = argmin_θ E[ℓ(Y; θ)] (with ℓ differentiable), the problem is reduced to Z-estimation by considering the gradient E[d/dθ ℓ(Y; θ*)] = 0. A similar procedure is applied, resulting in **Proposition 2.7**, which confirms the validity of the M-estimation confidence interval.

#### 3.3. Inference with E-values

The framework is also applied to inference tasks powered by e-values, which are non-negative random variables used for hypothesis testing. For a null hypothesis H_0, an e-value E is such that E[E] ≤ 1 if H_0 holds.

For an e-value E_n = product(e_i(Y_i)) with a predictable sequence of components (e_i), and assuming e_i(Y) is bounded in [a_i, b_i] with a_i > 0, the authors propose a conformal prediction-powered e-value:
E_ppi-(C)_n = product(rescale_eta_i(inf e_i(C_i(X_i)) - (b_i - a_i)Err(C_i))).
Here, `rescale_eta` is a function incorporating a predictable sequence (η_i) that ensures non-negativity and allows for power gains.

**Proposition 2.8** establishes that (E_ppi-(C)_n) forms a valid test supermartingale, and thus E_ppi-(C)_τ is a valid e-value for any stopping time τ. **Proposition 2.9** analyzes the power (expected growth rate) of these e-values, demonstrating that it approaches the power of the original e-value when conformal predictive sets are small and have low miscoverage. The methodology also extends to e-value-based procedures like confidence intervals/sequences and multiple testing.

In summary, the methodology systematically replaces point imputations with set imputations derived from calibrated conformal predictors. By adjusting statistical estimators with a penalty term based on the conformal predictor's miscoverage rate and the range of the function being estimated, the approach achieves statistical validity while inheriting advanced guarantees from the conformal prediction literature.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields both theoretical advancements and empirical demonstrations, establishing a general framework for Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) that incorporates strong guarantees.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Contributions

- **General Framework for Guaranteed PPI:** The central theoretical finding is the development of a unified framework that connects prediction-powered inference with conformal prediction. By performing imputation through a calibrated conformal set-predictor, the method allows PPI to inherit additional properties such as privacy, robustness, and validity under continuous distribution shifts. This resolves the previous challenge where such guarantees required arduous, case-by-case derivations for each specific PPI application.
- **Validity Proofs:** The authors provide formal proofs for the validity of their prediction-powered procedures across different statistical inference tasks:
 - **Mean Estimation (Proposition 2.3):** The proposed procedure yields a valid (1-α)-confidence interval for E[ϕ(Y)], given that ϕ(Y) is bounded. The interval construction directly integrates the miscoverage rate of the conformal predictor.
 - **Z-Estimation (Proposition 2.5):** The constructed confidence set for the Z-estimator θ* is proven to be valid at the (1-α) level. This extends the applicability to a wide class of problems including quantiles and regression coefficients.
 - **M-Estimation (Proposition 2.7):** By reducing M-estimation to Z-estimation of the loss function's derivative, the method is shown to provide valid confidence intervals for M-estimators.
 - **E-value-based Inference (Proposition 2.8):** The prediction-powered e-values constructed using conformal prediction are proven to form a test supermartingale, making them valid e-values for hypothesis testing. This is noted as the first general prediction-powered inference procedure for e-values that operates offline (without requiring active data collection).
- **Quantification of Statistical Power and Interval Size:** The research provides explicit quantification of the statistical power and confidence interval sizes:
 - **Mean Estimation (Proposition 2.4):** The Lebesgue measure of the confidence interval is shown to depend on the expected size of the convex hull of the predicted set of ϕ(C(X)), twice the product of the range M and Err(C), and the tightness of the one-sided confidence intervals used.
 - **Z-Estimation (Proposition 2.6):** The interval size is related to the average predictive interval size, M, Err(C), the tightness of one-sided CIs, and factors related to the derivatives of the bounds (steepness of ψ around θ*) and the smoothness of the confidence intervals.
 - **E-values (Proposition 2.9):** The expected growth rate of the prediction-powered e-values is shown to be close to that of the original e-value, with the reduction in power dependent on the average size of the log-transformed conformal predictive sets and terms involving Err(C) and the rescaling factor η_i.

#### 4.2. Empirical Demonstrations

The empirical section evaluates the method's performance in three distinct settings, highlighting its advantages over previous PPI approaches and its applicability to new problem domains.

- **Comparison with Previous Prediction-Powered Inference Methods:**
 - **Phishing URL Prevalence (Mean Estimation):** In estimating the prevalence of phishing websites, the proposed Conformal PPI method was compared against PPI++ and FAB. The results showed that Conformal PPI was competitive, providing the tightest confidence intervals among the tested methods, outperforming PPI++ and offering an improvement over using only labeled data.
 - **Gene Expression Levels (Median Estimation):** For inferring gene expression levels (a median estimation task), the Conformal PPI method proved competitive. It yielded tighter confidence intervals than PPI++ and the labeled-only baseline, although it was not as tight as FAB. Notably, Conformal PPI achieved the smallest variance in interval sizes across different data splits.

- **Prediction-Powered Inference with Private Labeled Data:**
 - The method was applied to analyze thyroid cancer recurrence using private patient data. By employing a differentially private conformal prediction method for calibration, the framework enabled prediction-powered inference for recurrence probabilities across various patient strata. The key finding was that a single, inference-agnostic, and differentially-private calibration resulted in only minor increases in interval sizes compared to a non-private calibration, significantly enhancing safety. This demonstrated the method's capability to address privacy concerns, a limitation of previous PPI methods.

- **Prediction-Powered Risk Monitoring via Online Conformal Prediction:**
 - For the task of continuously tracking the risk of a deployed model (forest cover type prediction), the method was combined with online conformal prediction to enable anytime-valid hypothesis testing on time-series data. This scenario represents an observational regime, unlike previous PPI methods which often require active data collection or static i.i.d. settings. Results indicated that when the null hypothesis (risk within safety level) was false, the prediction-powered e-values rejected it significantly faster and with higher confidence compared to using only occasional labeled samples, while maintaining a low false positive rate.

In summary, the findings indicate that integrating conformal prediction into PPI provides a theoretically sound and empirically effective approach to extending PPI with critical guarantees, enabling its application in complex, sensitive, and dynamic real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work by Csillag et al. presents a significant advancement in the field of prediction-powered inference (PPI) and its intersection with uncertainty quantification. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified Framework for Enhanced Guarantees:** The most prominent impact is the provision of a general and principled framework for PPI that naturally incorporates strong guarantees such as privacy, robustness, and validity under continuous distribution shifts. This directly addresses a critical limitation of previous PPI methods, which required bespoke, case-by-case derivations for each additional property. This unification simplifies the development and deployment of PPI techniques in complex real-world settings.
- **Leveraging Existing Conformal Prediction Literature:** By establishing a connection between PPI and conformal prediction, the research enables the immediate leveraging of the extensive and continuously evolving literature on conformal prediction methods with additional guarantees. This includes advancements in privacy-preserving CP, robust CP for adversarial or strategic shifts, online CP for dynamic environments, and CP for handling various data anomalies. This synergistic approach means future innovations in CP can directly translate into enhanced PPI capabilities.
- **Expansion of PPI Applicability to New Domains:** The empirical demonstrations highlight the method's ability to tackle problems previously out of reach for standard PPI.
 - **Private Data Analysis:** The application to private healthcare data for thyroid cancer recurrence analysis demonstrates that secure, privacy-preserving inferences can be conducted without significantly sacrificing statistical power. This opens avenues for PPI in sensitive domains like medical research, finance, and social sciences.
 - **Continuous Risk Monitoring:** Its utility in online risk monitoring for time-series data, without requiring active data collection, indicates its relevance for real-time systems, industrial process control, and continuous auditing. This addresses dynamic, non-i.i.d. settings where traditional PPI struggles.
- **Foundational Baseline for Future Research:** This work establishes a new theoretical and practical baseline for future research in prediction-powered inference, particularly concerning the integration of advanced guarantees. It is likely to inspire further exploration into how other properties of conformal prediction, such as efficiency under different shift types or guarantees for specific data structures (e.g., hierarchical data), can be integrated into the PPI paradigm.
- **Enhanced Trust and Deployability of ML Models:** By providing robust and privacy-preserving statistical inference guarantees, the framework can increase trust in machine learning models, especially in high-stakes applications. This can facilitate the safer and more widespread deployment of ML models for tasks involving missing data imputation and subsequent inference, knowing that the results are not only valid but also secure and reliable under adverse conditions.
- **First General Offline E-value PPI:** The development of the first general prediction-powered e-value procedure that operates offline is significant. E-values offer an alternative to p-values with desirable properties for sequential testing, and integrating them with PPI further enhances their utility in data-scarce or dynamically changing environments.

In conclusion, this research significantly broadens the scope and strengthens the foundations of prediction-powered inference. By providing a systematic and flexible approach to incorporate critical guarantees, it moves PPI closer to being a universally applicable tool for robust and responsible statistical inference in data-rich but label-poor environments.
