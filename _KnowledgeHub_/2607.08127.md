# Understanding and Mitigating the Video-Action Generalization Gap via Temporal Ratio

> [!summary] Summary
> Georgia Tech and Amazon FAR researchers characterized the "video-action generalization (VAG) gap," where finetuning robot policies on action data diminishes the compositional priors of underlying video foundation models. They developed Temporal Ratio (TR), an attention-based metric to quantify reliance on future predictions, and designed a TR-adaptive guidance method to enhance out-of-distribution compositional task success, improving it to 59.4% in simulation and 83.3% in real-world bimanual tasks.

## Problem

- Robot policies often lack compositional generalization, struggling to combine familiar components (objects, actions) into novel task sequences, often due to overfitting to training data.
- Generative video foundation models (VFMs) possess strong compositional priors, but existing World-Action Models (WAMs) and Video-Action Models (VAMs) frequently lose these capabilities after finetuning on robotic action data, creating a "video-action generalization (VAG) gap."
- A lack of mechanistic understanding limits the diagnosis and mitigation of the VAG gap, as policies may fail to effectively utilize plausible future video predictions for action generation.

## Method

- Developed a ==Video-Action Model (VAM)== architecture that integrates a Cosmos-Predict 2.5-2B DiT video backbone with a 300M Gemma Transformer action head, employing a **first-frame anchor** mechanism.
- Introduced **Temporal Ratio (TR)**, a novel attention-based metric, to quantitatively assess the action head's reliance on future-predictive latents versus the current-frame anchor.
- Proposed an inference-time ==adaptive guidance== method that dynamically scales language and plan guidance signals based on the observed Temporal Ratio, amplifying them during high-TR planning phases and relaxing them during precise manipulation.

## Results

- The best unguided VAM configuration achieved a **55.7%** average out-of-distribution (OOD) success rate in simulation, demonstrating a substantial improvement over prior VAMs (e.g., DiT4DiT at **6.4%**, Cosmos-Policy at **10.5%**).
- TR-adaptive guidance further improved the average OOD success rate to **59.4%** in simulation on the LIBERO benchmark and to **83.3%** in real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, up from **71.7%** without guidance.
- The optimal video noise level for feature extraction was found to be intermediate (e.g., **0.883**), indicating a trade-off between coherent temporal structure and vulnerability to hallucinatory artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The video-action generalization (VAG) gap is a fragmented issue across VAM design choices; low-rank adaptation (LoRA) and joint training with the video backbone yield better compositional generalization than full finetuning.
> - Temporal Ratio (TR) serves as an effective diagnostic tool, predicting a model's compositional generalization capacity and dynamically reflecting task phases (rising during planning, dropping during precise manipulation).
> - The action head's effective utilization of physically plausible video predictions is crucial; simply generating a correct future rollout is insufficient if the policy does not leverage it for action generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous manipulation increasingly relies on policies that can generalize compositionally, meaning they can recombine familiar components (objects, receptacles, primitive actions) into novel task sequences. Existing policies, particularly those trained with traditional methods, frequently exhibit brittle performance under such recompositions, often due to overfitting to visual correlations in training data.

An emerging direction to address this limitation involves leveraging generative video foundation models (VFMs). These models, trained on vast internet-scale video data, possess strong compositional priors, exhibiting an understanding of temporal coherence, object interactions, and physical plausibility. The primary challenge in robotics is effectively transferring these rich pretrained video priors into visuomotor control systems.

Current approaches include:
1. **World-Action Models (WAMs):** These models often predict future video or corresponding latent features, from which actions are then inferred using inverse dynamics.
2. **Video-Action Models (VAMs):** These systems typically jointly model video and action tokens within a unified transformer architecture.

However, a consistent observation across both WAMs and VAMs is that despite achieving high performance in their training domains, they frequently fail to preserve the compositional generalization abilities of their underlying video backbones after finetuning on robotic action data. This discrepancy is termed the "video-action generalization (VAG) gap." This paper positions itself within this landscape by systematically investigating the VAG gap in a specific class of VAMs that utilize latent features from pretrained VFMs and learn a separate latent inverse-dynamics action head. The work seeks to understand the mechanisms underlying this gap and propose a method to mitigate it, contributing to the development of robot policies that can robustly generalize to novel compositional tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to understand and mitigate the "video-action generalization (VAG) gap." This gap refers to the phenomenon where generative video foundation models (VFMs), despite possessing strong compositional priors, lose these capabilities when their features are integrated into or finetuned for robotic action policies (World-Action Models or Video-Action Models).

The motivation for this investigation is multifaceted:

1. **Requirement for Compositional Generalization:** For autonomous robots to operate effectively in dynamic and unstructured environments, they must exhibit compositional generalization. This means a robot should be able to perform novel tasks by combining known objects, receptacles, and primitive actions in new ways. For example, if a policy has learned "put the cream cheese in the bowl" and "put the bowl on top of the cabinet," it should ideally be able to execute "put the cream cheese on top of the cabinet." Existing policies often struggle with such recompositions, suggesting they overfit to specific visual correlations present in their training data.

2. **Potential of Video Foundation Models:** Generative VFMs trained on large-scale internet videos inherently capture complex temporal dynamics, object interactions, and physical plausibility. These learned priors are highly valuable for robot learning and offer a promising avenue to overcome the limitations of policies trained solely on robotic demonstrations. The challenge lies in effectively transferring and preserving these rich priors into a control policy.

3. **Observed Failure Mode (VAG Gap):** Despite the theoretical potential of VFMs, current WAMs and VAMs, after finetuning on robot action data, consistently demonstrate a significant drop in compositional generalization compared to the inherent abilities of their underlying video backbones. While these models might perform well on in-distribution (ID) tasks, their out-of-distribution (OOD) performance on compositional tasks is often severely compromised. This indicates a fundamental challenge in integrating video generative priors with action prediction without degrading the former's generalization capacity.

4. **Lack of Mechanistic Understanding:** Initial explorations into the design space of VAMs (e.g., backbone adaptation strategies, video-latent feature extraction parameters) revealed inconsistent and fragmented impacts on the ID-OOD performance gap. This suggested that simple design choices alone did not provide a clear explanation or consistent pattern for when compositional generalization succeeded or failed. This motivated the need for a more mechanistic diagnostic tool to interpret the underlying behavior of the action head.

5. **Addressing Video-Action Disagreement:** The authors observed instances where the video model might predict a correct future rollout, but the action policy would fail to follow it (or vice-versa). This "video-action disagreement" highlighted that the mere existence of a plausible future video prediction is insufficient; the action head must actually *utilize* this prediction for successful task execution. This insight motivated the development of a metric to quantify the action head's reliance on imagined futures.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives included:
- To systematically investigate the design space of VAMs to understand how different architectural and training choices influence the VAG gap.
- To introduce the Temporal Ratio (TR), a novel attention-based metric, to quantitatively assess the action head's reliance on future-predictive latents versus the current-frame anchor.
- To demonstrate that TR serves as a diagnostic tool, predicting a model’s compositional generalization capacity and reflecting task phase dynamics.
- To develop an inference-time adaptive guidance method, based on TR, to dynamically amplify compositional video conditioning signals, thereby mitigating the observed VAG gap and improving OOD performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research systematically investigates Video-Action Models (VAMs) and proposes a method to improve their compositional generalization. The approach consists of architectural design, systematic experimentation, introduction of a novel metric, and an inference-time guidance strategy.

#### 3.1. VAM Architecture Design

The core VAM architecture is based on previous designs and integrates two main components:
1. **Video Foundation Backbone:** This component uses Cosmos-Predict 2.5-2B DiT, a flow-matching video backbone.
 - It is trained to predict a velocity field `v_θ` that denoises noisy video latents `x_σv` to clean latents `x0` under a text condition `c`.
 - Features `F_θ^(k)` are extracted from the 20th block (k=20) of this DiT backbone.
 - A crucial feature is the **first-frame anchor mechanism**: the current observation at `t=0` is maintained as a clean, high-signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) conditioning frame throughout the video denoising process. This is achieved by an anchor channel, pre-network latent replacement, and post-network velocity replacement. Future frames (`t>0`) are denoised from noisy latent targets, representing the model's imagined scene evolution with lower SNR.
2. **Action Head:** A 300M Gemma Transformer policy, structured similar to π0.
 - It consumes flattened video tokens (derived from `F_θ^(k)`), a projected proprioceptive token, and noisy action tokens.
 - It is trained using a flow-matching objective (`L_action`) to denoise action sequences.
 - The action head uses a prefix-LM attention mask, bidirectional within the video prefix, and causal over action tokens.
 - The language instruction is only consumed by the video backbone; the action head sees video features, state, and the action timestep.

#### 3.2. Design Space Exploration

A comprehensive set of ablation studies was conducted across two primary groups of axes:
- **Training-time Configurations (Backbone Adaptation):**
 - **Finetuning Strategy:**
 - **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):** Trains 2–6% of the DiT backbone parameters.
 - **Full Finetuning:** Updates all 2.1B DiT parameters.
 - **Training Mode:**
 - **Joint Training:** Optimizes a weighted objective `L_total = L_action + λ L_video`, allowing action gradients to influence the DiT feature space.
 - **Two-Stage Training:** First, the backbone is finetuned on `L_video`, then frozen, and subsequently the action head is optimized on `L_action`.
- **Inference-time Parameters (Feature Extraction):**
 - **Video Noise Level (`σv`):** Features were extracted at varying `σv` values (e.g., 1.0, 0.953, 0.883, 0.562, 0.024) along the video denoising flow trajectory, influencing the noisiness and semantic structure of future latents.
 - **Prediction Horizon (`T`):** The temporal extent of the predicted rollout was varied (e.g., 5, 9, 24 latent frames). Training was always done with a `T=5` target, so larger `T` during evaluation primarily impacted the *quality* of the first 5 frames.

#### 3.3. Temporal Ratio (TR) Introduction

To diagnose the action head's reliance on predicted futures, the Temporal Ratio (TR) was introduced:
- **Definition:** `TR^(ℓ)_i = (Attention over future frames) / (Attention over current frame)`, where `Attention` is the head-averaged attention map at action-head layer `ℓ` from action tokens to video tokens. Video tokens are partitioned into current-frame (`V0`) and future-frame (`V+`) tokens.
- **Interpretation:** High TR signifies strong reliance on predicted future latents for action generation, while low TR indicates anchoring to the current observation.
- **Availability:** TR is available from the policy's normal forward pass, allowing for online tracking during execution.
- **Layer Selection:** Layer 15 of the 18-layer Gemma action head (`ℓ*=15`) was chosen for TR calculation due to its pronounced sensitivity to changes in video noise level, indicating its role in the routing decision between current and future frames.

#### 3.4. Adaptive TR Guidance

Based on the properties of TR, an inference-time adaptive guidance method was proposed:
- **Motivation:** TR was found to fluctuate with task phases (rising during planning, dropping during precise manipulation) and correlate with compositional generalization. This suggested dynamic intervention.
- **Guidance Components:**
 - **Language Guidance (`Δv_lang`):** Computed by subtracting the velocity derived from video features extracted without a text instruction (`c=∅`) from the conditional velocity (`v_cond`). This isolates instruction-dependent action velocity.
 - **Plan Guidance (`Δv_plan`):** Computed by subtracting `v_cond` from a velocity `v_plan` derived from video features generated with an extended horizon (`T' > T`) and then truncated. This isolates long-horizon temporal structure.
- **Combined Guided Update:** `v_guided = v_cond + w_lang,i Δv_lang + w_plan,i Δv_plan`.
- **Dynamic Weighting:** The guidance strengths `w_lang,i` and `w_plan,i` are not fixed but scaled by `s_i = clip(TR_i / TR_0 - 1, 0, s_max)`.
 - `TR_0` is the Temporal Ratio at the first replan of an episode, serving as an episode-specific baseline.
 - This scaling ensures guidance is amplified when TR increases (planning phase) and relaxed when TR drops (precise manipulation), concentrating intervention where it is most beneficial.

#### 3.5. Experimental Evaluation

- **Simulation:** Evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark, specifically its standard in-distribution (ID) suites and compositional out-of-distribution (OOD) suites from Li (spatial-OOD, object-OOD, goal-OOD).
- **Real-World:** Evaluated on a bimanual YAM robot platform using a multi-task dataset (5600 episodes, 24 tasks) and custom OOD scenes designed to test compositional generalization.
- **Baselines:** Included open-source VLAs (π0, π0.5), WAMs (Cosmos-Policy, Fast-WAM), and VAMs (Mimic-Video, DiT4DiT), many of which used the same Cosmos-Predict 2.5-2B video backbone for fair comparison.
- **Compute:** Real-world training for all methods was standardized to 1M optimizer steps on an equivalent of 32 NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings regarding the VAG gap, the properties of Temporal Ratio (TR), and the effectiveness of TR-adaptive guidance.

#### 4.1. Systematic Analysis of the Video-Action Generalization Gap

- **Fragmented Performance Landscape:** Initial systematic investigation across VAM design choices (finetuning strategies, training modes, video noise levels, and prediction horizons) revealed no consistent pattern for emergent compositional generalization. In-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) performance gaps were fragmented, indicating that standard design choices did not provide a clear explanation.
- **Finetuning Strategy:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) consistently outperformed full finetuning in terms of OOD performance, suggesting that restricting updates helps preserve the pretrained temporal structure of the video backbone. Joint training with LoRA yielded the strongest OOD performance.
- **Video Noise Level (`σv`):** The optimal `σv` for feature extraction was neither fully noisy (`σv` → 1.0) nor fully denoised (`σv` → 0.0). Performance peaked at an intermediate noise level (e.g., `σv` ≈ 0.883), implying a trade-off between coherent temporal structure and vulnerability to hallucinated trajectories.
- **Prediction Horizon (`T`):** While increasing the prediction horizon could qualitatively improve the video rollout's compositional plan by exposing more object-goal structure, it did not consistently translate to higher action success. Longer horizons increased the chance of implausible artifacts, and the action head did not always follow the correct plan.
- **Video-Action Disagreement:** Observations demonstrated that the action head could either correctly execute a memorized behavior despite an incorrect video prediction (in-domain) or ignore a correct compositional video prediction and follow an incorrect in-distribution trajectory (out-of-distribution). This highlighted that a plausible imagined future is only beneficial if the action policy utilizes it.

#### 4.2. Understanding the VAG Gap via Temporal Ratio (TR)

- **TR as a Predictor of Generalization:** TR was identified as a strong predictor of a model's underlying compositional generalization capacity. Successful compositional episodes were consistently characterized by higher TR values.
- **TR and Video Denoising:** TR increased as the video noise level `σv` decreased (i.e., more video denoising), indicating that the action head's reliance on future frames grew as their SNR and semantic structure improved.
- **TR as a Task Phase Indicator:** TR exhibited dynamic fluctuations within an episode:
 - It rose during "planning phases" (e.g., selecting objects, determining placement strategy), where integrating language and long-term goals through future predictions is critical.
 - It dropped during "precise local manipulation" (e.g., grasping, fine-grained placement), where anchoring to the current observation is essential for accuracy. This demonstrated that low TR is not inherently detrimental but context-dependent.

#### 4.3. Experimental Results for Mitigating the VAG Gap

- **Simulation (LIBERO Benchmark):**
 - **Baseline Performance:** Prior WAMs and VAMs showed high ID success rates (>95%) but severe collapse in compositional OOD success (e.g., DiT4DiT at 6.4%, Cosmos-Policy at 10.5%).
 - **Proposed Unguided VAM:** The best unguided VAM configuration (LoRA backbone adaptation, joint training, `σv=0.883`, `T=5`) achieved an average OOD success rate of 55.7%, representing a substantial improvement over baselines.
 - **TR-Adaptive Guidance:** Application of TR-adaptive guidance further enhanced the average OOD success rate to 59.4%. This performance gain was achieved while largely preserving ID performance (94.0% with guidance vs. 94.3% unguided), though a slight reduction was observed in LIBERO-long tasks, suggesting potential amplification of unreliable futures.
- **Real-World (Bimanual YAM Tasks):**
 - **Baseline Performance:** Baselines like π0 (36.7%), π0.5 (55.0%), and Cosmos-Policy (12.5%) showed varied but generally lower performance on compositional OOD bimanual tasks.
 - **Proposed Unguided VAM:** The proposed VAM achieved an average success rate of 71.7% on the real-world OOD tasks. The importance of partially denoised latents was evident, as extracting features at `σv=1.0` (pure noise) resulted in complete failure.
 - **TR-Adaptive Guidance:** TR-adaptive guidance further improved real-world performance from 71.7% to 83.3% average success.

In summary, the research demonstrates that the VAG gap is prevalent across existing VAMs and WAMs. The Temporal Ratio effectively diagnoses the action head's reliance on future predictions, correlating with compositional generalization. By leveraging TR, the proposed adaptive guidance method significantly mitigates the VAG gap, improving OOD compositional success in both simulation and real-world robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of robot learning and the understanding of generalization in visuomotor policies, with significant potential impact:

1. **Clarifying the Video-Action Generalization Gap:** The paper provides a systematic investigation into the "video-action generalization (VAG) gap," which is a critical bottleneck in deploying foundation-model-based policies for robotics. By demonstrating that policies often fail to inherit the compositional priors of their video backbones after finetuning, it highlights a fundamental challenge and motivates further research into the interface between generative models and action prediction.

2. **Introducing a Novel Diagnostic Metric (Temporal Ratio):** The Temporal Ratio (TR) offers a mechanistic and interpretable tool to understand the internal workings of VAMs. Unlike post-hoc success rates, TR provides an online signal quantifying how much the action head relies on imagined future rollouts versus the current observation. This ability to diagnose the policy's routing decisions in real-time is a significant advancement for analyzing and debugging complex robot behaviors. Its correlation with compositional generalization capacity provides a deeper insight into the conditions for successful out-of-distribution performance.

3. **Advancing Adaptive Control Strategies:** The observation that TR naturally fluctuates with task phases (high during planning, low during precise manipulation) is a key insight. This dynamic behavior can be exploited to develop more intelligent and context-aware control strategies. The proposed TR-Adaptive Guidance method, which dynamically amplifies compositional video conditioning signals during planning and relaxes them during precise manipulation, directly leverages this understanding. This represents a step towards more nuanced and adaptive robot policies that can balance high-level planning with low-level execution demands.

4. **Tangible Improvements in Generalization:** The significant empirical results, with TR-Adaptive Guidance mitigating the OOD compositional generalization gap on both the challenging LIBERO benchmark and complex real-world bimanual tasks, demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the proposed framework. Achieving a >5x improvement in OOD success over prior VAMs in simulation and substantial gains in real-world scenarios validates the approach's ability to create more capable and robust robotic systems.

5. **Guiding Future Research and Development:**
 - **Interface Design:** The work underscores that the *interface* between powerful video generative models and action heads is as crucial as the individual components themselves. Future research might explore alternative interface designs to better preserve and utilize video priors.
 - **Robust Video Generation:** The limitations noted, such as the reliance on physically plausible video model predictions and the potential for amplifying incorrect guidance signals under distribution shifts, highlight the ongoing need for more robust and controllable video foundation models, particularly for robotics applications where physical accuracy is paramount.
 - **Inference Efficiency:** The increased inference cost associated with guidance provides a clear direction for research into more efficient guidance mechanisms or hardware acceleration to maintain real-time performance.
 - **Beyond VAMs:** The concept of dynamically modulating attention or guidance based on a policy's internal reliance on different information sources (e.g., current observation vs. predicted future, language vs. visual cues) could be generalizable to other domains in multimodal or predictive control systems.

In conclusion, this research not only offers a deeper understanding of a critical generalization challenge in robot learning but also provides a practical and effective solution that measurably improves compositional generalization. Its insights into dynamic policy behavior and adaptive guidance hold significant promise for the future development of more intelligent and versatile robotic agents.
