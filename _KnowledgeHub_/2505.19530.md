# Heavy lifting tasks via haptic teleoperation of a wheeled humanoid

> [!summary] Summary
> A teleoperation framework enables a wheeled humanoid robot to perform dynamic mobile manipulation tasks, such as lifting and carrying objects up to 21% of its mass, by integrating height retargeting and haptic feedback; the system demonstrates that automatic robot compensation for payload-induced lean significantly reduces operator burden compared to manual compensation.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in "Dynamic Mobile Manipulation" (DMM) tasks involving heavy payloads in human-centered environments.
- Existing teleoperation systems for humanoids lack a unified approach for DMM, specifically integrating height variation, explicit handling of large payload disturbances, and immersive haptic feedback.
- Robots struggle with the intricate whole-body coordination and force regulation required for tasks like bending, lifting, and placing heavy objects while maintaining balance.

## Method

- Development of a teleoperation framework with a ==Human-Machine Interface (HMI)== that maps human whole-body motion (arms, height, pitch) to a height-adjustable wheeled humanoid robot.
- Implementation of two payload disturbance handling strategies: automatic robot-computed pitch compensation and pilot manual compensation via haptic feedback of external moments.
- Integration of two locomotion retargeting strategies: DCM dynamic similarity (pitch-only) for dynamic response and a velocity-based mapping for precise positioning.

## Results

- Pilots consistently preferred the Automatic Setpoint Estimation Mode, which autonomously adjusted robot lean for payload compensation, over manual compensation, due to reduced haptic feedback and decreased exertion.
- The manual compensation mode required excessive pilot leaning and effort, leading to decreased DCM tracking and unsustainability for prolonged heavy lifting.
- The robot successfully performed heavy lifting tasks, managing payloads up to **21%** of its own mass, demonstrating dynamic height adjustment and balance maintenance during manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automatic compensation of payload-induced lean by the robot significantly lowers the pilot's physical and cognitive load, improving the sustainability and performance of heavy lifting tasks.
> - Pilots benefit from a hybrid control approach, leveraging precise velocity mapping for critical sub-tasks like object release and more dynamic DCM mapping for general locomotion.
> - Tunable haptic feedback gains are crucial for balancing control transparency, system stability, and operator comfort, allowing for personalized task execution.
