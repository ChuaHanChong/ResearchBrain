# Heavy lifting tasks via haptic teleoperation of a wheeled humanoid

> [!summary] Summary
> A teleoperation framework enables a wheeled humanoid robot to perform dynamic mobile manipulation tasks, such as lifting and carrying objects up to 21% of its mass, by integrating height retargeting and haptic feedback; the system demonstrates that automatic robot compensation for payload-induced lean significantly reduces operator burden compared to manual compensation.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in "Dynamic Mobile Manipulation" (DMM) tasks involving heavy payloads in human-centered environments.
- Existing teleoperation systems for humanoids lack a unified approach for DMM, specifically integrating height variation, explicit handling of large payload disturbances, and immersive haptic feedback.
- Robots struggle with the intricate whole-body coordination and force regulation required for tasks like bending, lifting, and placing heavy objects while maintaining balance.

## Method

- Development of a teleoperation framework with a ==Human-Machine Interface (HMI)== that maps human whole-body motion (arms, height, pitch) to a height-adjustable wheeled humanoid robot.
- Implementation of two payload disturbance handling strategies: automatic robot-computed pitch compensation and pilot manual compensation via haptic feedback of external moments.
- Integration of two locomotion retargeting strategies: DCM dynamic similarity (pitch-only) for dynamic response and a velocity-based mapping for precise positioning.

## Results

- Pilots consistently preferred the Automatic Setpoint Estimation Mode, which autonomously adjusted robot lean for payload compensation, over manual compensation, due to reduced haptic feedback and decreased exertion.
- The manual compensation mode required excessive pilot leaning and effort, leading to decreased DCM tracking and unsustainability for prolonged heavy lifting.
- The robot successfully performed heavy lifting tasks, managing payloads up to **21%** of its own mass, demonstrating dynamic height adjustment and balance maintenance during manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automatic compensation of payload-induced lean by the robot significantly lowers the pilot's physical and cognitive load, improving the sustainability and performance of heavy lifting tasks.
> - Pilots benefit from a hybrid control approach, leveraging precise velocity mapping for critical sub-tasks like object release and more dynamic DCM mapping for general locomotion.
> - Tunable haptic feedback gains are crucial for balancing control transparency, system stability, and operator comfort, allowing for personalized task execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenge of enabling humanoid robots to perform physically demanding tasks, specifically "Dynamic Mobile Manipulation (DMM)," in human-centered environments such as warehouses. DMM involves the simultaneous coordination of locomotion, manipulation, and posture while interacting with dynamic forces, particularly when handling heavy payloads. Despite advancements in robotics, reliably executing such complex maneuvers remains a significant hurdle for autonomous systems.

The paper contextualizes its contribution by reviewing existing work:
- **Learned policies and imitation learning:** Recent studies have demonstrated the potential of learned policies and sim-to-real transfer for humanoid box loco-manipulation (e.g.,), often relying on human teleoperation to collect demonstration data for training (e.g.,). This highlights the increasing relevance of effective teleoperation frameworks.
- **Whole-body control for heavy lifting:** Prior research on bipedal humanoids has explored trajectory optimization for lifting, primarily for stationary tasks (e.g.,–).
- **Wheeled humanoids for mobility:** Wheeled platforms, such as Golem Krang, have shown capabilities in locomotion and height adjustment but generally lack manipulation demonstrations. The Ballbot platform demonstrated lean adjustment for payload compensation but at a fixed height.
- **Teleoperation systems:** Platforms like CENTAURO have explored lifting tasks, focusing on grasp force estimation, but typically with small payloads relative to robot mass and without dynamic posture reconfiguration. The Reflex teleoperated mobile robot demonstrated locomanipulation capabilities, including moving, bending, and lifting heavy bags, but this platform is fully actuated and does not explicitly address disturbance moments on the base from large payloads.

The authors identify specific gaps in the existing literature: the absence of a unified system that demonstrates teleoperated DMM with height retargeting, explicit handling of payload-induced disturbances, and immersive haptic feedback. This work aims to bridge these gaps by presenting a comprehensive teleoperation framework for wheeled humanoids performing heavy-lifting tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a teleoperation framework that enables a human pilot to control a height-adjustable wheeled humanoid robot for "Dynamic Mobile Manipulation" (DMM) tasks, specifically involving the lifting and carrying of heavy objects. This involves coordinating the robot's whole-body motion, including locomotion, manipulation, and dynamic posture adjustments, under external interaction forces.

The motivation behind this work stems from several factors:
- **Addressing physical demands in human-centric environments:** Humanoid robots possess the potential to assist human workers in physically demanding tasks within environments like warehouses or grocery stores, reducing human workload and improving safety. However, these tasks often require intricate whole-body coordination and force regulation that robots currently struggle with autonomously.
- **Enhancing robot capabilities for DMM:** Complex behaviors such as bending, lifting heavy objects, and placing them onto shelves require simultaneous control of locomotion and manipulation, along with the ability to manage interaction forces and contact states dynamically. This class of DMM tasks represents a significant challenge for robotic autonomy.
- **Leveraging human intelligence through teleoperation:** Teleoperation offers a method for embedding human cognitive and motor skills directly into the robot's control loop, allowing robots to perform adaptive tasks by becoming an extension of the human operator. This is also crucial for collecting high-quality human demonstrations needed for developing learned policies for future autonomous systems.
- **Closing identified gaps in existing teleoperation systems:** Previous teleoperation approaches for humanoid and mobile robots have demonstrated elements of whole-body control, but none have comprehensively addressed the combination of height variation, explicit compensation for large payload disturbances, and immersive haptic feedback within a unified DMM framework. Specifically, prior work often involved limited payload sizes, static postures, or lacked explicit accounting for disturbance moments generated by heavy objects on the robot's base.

To achieve these goals, the paper outlines three primary contributions:
- Development of human-motion retargeting capabilities for heavy-lifting tasks, incorporating height-varying control for the robot.
- Introduction of a pitch-based locomotion retargeting strategy that allows the pilot to regulate the linearized error dynamics of the robot, particularly during heavy-object lifting.
- An experimental comparison of two distinct disturbance rejection paradigms on hardware: one where the pilot manually compensates for payload-induced moments, and another where the robot's controller automatically provides compensation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a teleoperation framework designed for a height-adjustable wheeled humanoid robot, featuring a Human-Machine Interface (HMI) that enables whole-body motion retargeting from a human pilot to the robot.

**A. Human-Machine Interface (HMI) and Motion Retargeting:**
The HMI captures the pilot's body motion, including arm movements, height, pitch angle (θ_H, defined between ankle and CoM), and center of pressure. This human state information is retargeted to desired robot setpoints. Haptic feedback is delivered to the pilot, conveying end-effector wrenches and balance-related cues, aiming to close the perception-interaction loop. The pilot's body motion regulates robot posture and locomotion, while arm movements guide manipulation.

**B. Robot Modeling and Control:**
The robot is modeled as a wheeled inverted pendulum (WIP), with linearized dynamics along the x-axis given by `˙q_R = Aq_R + Bu_R + d_R`, where `q_R` includes wheel position, robot pitch (θ_R, between wheel axle and CoM), and their velocities. The term `d_R` accounts for external disturbances. The feedback controller employs a gain-scheduled Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR) to balance the robot at various heights, with gains `K_LQR(h_R)` linearly interpolated. Feedback linearization is included to compensate for disturbances (`d_w`) from leg forces affecting the robot base during height changes.

**C. Height Variation Locomotion Retargeting:**
To enable intuitive height control, the system maps changes in human height to corresponding changes in robot height. The deviation from nominal human height (Δh_H) is kinematically mapped to robot height (Δh_R) such that the percentage change is proportional: `Δh_R / h_nom_R = Δh_H / h_nom_H`. This translates to a desired robot height `h_des_R = h_nom_R + β_z (h_nom_R / h_nom_H) Δh_H`, where `β_z` is a tunable gain (set to 0.5 in experiments) to adjust the sensitivity of robot height to human bending.

**D. Payload Disturbance Handling and Desired Pitch Estimation:**
When lifting heavy objects, an external moment (M_y_ext) is induced around the robot's base. To maintain balance, the robot's Center of Mass (CoM) must remain above its wheelbase. This requires a backward lean. Two strategies are compared:
1. **Robot Desired Pitch Estimation (Automatic Compensation):** The system calculates a new desired lean angle (θ*_R) for the robot in static equilibrium. The robot arm is modeled as a planar manipulator, and the sum of moments around the wheel due to gravity (`F_g`) and the object (`F_obj`) is set to zero (`F_g h_R sin(θ*_R) = -F_obj x_ee`). The end-effector position `x_ee` is derived from the robot's arm joint angles and link lengths. The time derivative of θ*_R (`˙θ*_R`) is also computed to improve tracking of the dynamically changing lean. This `θ*_R` and `˙θ*_R` then serve as the setpoints for the robot's controller.
2. **Pilot Compensation (Manual Mode):** In this mode, no automatic pitch compensation is applied by the robot's controller. Instead, the external moment `M_y_ext` is scaled and directly conveyed as a haptic force feedback (`F_fb = (γ_H / γ_R) M_y_ext`) to the pilot, requiring the human to manually adjust their lean to maintain the robot's balance.

**E. Locomotion Retargeting Strategies:**
The paper evaluates two main locomotion retargeting strategies:
1. **DCM Dynamic Similarity (Pitch-only mapping):** This advanced strategy, building on previous work, aligns the robot's pitching behavior with the human's by equating their Divergent Component of Motion (DCM, ξ). To accommodate payload-induced lean, the robot's DCM is regulated towards a desired DCM (ξ*_R) computed from the desired pitch `θ*_R` and `˙θ*_R`. The pilot then controls the error between the robot's dynamics and this new reference (`(˙ξ_R - ˙ξ*_R) / ω_R = ˙ξ_H / ω_H`), resulting in new feedback (`F_fb`) and feedforward (`F_ff`) signals. A tunable gain `K_fb` on the haptic feedback allows adjustment of transparency versus stability.
2. **Velocity-based Mapping (Baseline):** This strategy maps human pitch (`θ_H`) to desired robot base velocity (`˙x_des_R`), providing a more conservative control interface suitable for precise positioning. This was used as a benchmark.

**F. Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted using a 2.5 kg payload (21% of robot mass), assuming its mass was known within 10% beforehand. Three control strategies were compared:
- Baseline (Velocity mapping with automatic pitch compensation).
- Automatic Setpoint Estimation Mode (DCM pitch-only mapping with automatic pitch compensation and haptic feedback reflecting DCM error).
- Manual Control Strategy (DCM pitch-only mapping with no automatic pitch compensation and haptic feedback reflecting the full payload-induced moment).
Tasks involved lifting barbells and boxes between elevated and lowered positions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to validate the robot's execution capabilities and assess pilot preferences across different control modalities for dynamic mobile manipulation (DMM) involving heavy payloads.

**A. Setpoint Estimation Preference:**
The study compared manual pilot compensation versus automatic robot controller compensation for payload-induced moments.
- **Manual Mode (Pilot Compensation):** When pilots were provided with full haptic moment feedback and had to manually compensate, they adopted a strategy of drawing the object closer to the robot's body. This reduced the external moment and the corrective force required from the pilot. However, DCM tracking performance decreased, and pilots were required to lean back excessively through the HMI. This scenario highlighted a limitation in the pilot's available range of motion to counterbalance heavier loads, rendering prolonged operation unsustainable due to constant exertion.
- **Automatic Setpoint Estimation Mode (Robot Controller Compensation):** This approach was consistently favored by pilots. The robot autonomously adjusted its lean to compensate for the payload, significantly reducing the haptic feedback amplitude that the pilot needed to counteract. This decreased the physical and cognitive load on the pilot, allowing for more sustained and less strenuous operation.
- **Tunable Haptic Feedback Gain:** The introduction of a tunable gain (`K_fb`) on the haptic feedback was found to be beneficial. While `K_fb = 1` provides high-fidelity, transparent force feedback, it could lead to oscillations if the pilot became overwhelmed. Intermediate gain values allowed pilots to adjust feedback strength, improving stability, comfort, and tracking performance.

**B. Mapping Preference for Heavy Box Lift:**
The research investigated pilot preferences between base velocity control and DCM pitch-only control when performing heavy box lifts, both utilizing automatic setpoint estimation.
- **Base Velocity Mapping:** This strategy provided a more conservative control interface, prioritizing tracking of the robot's wheel position while maintaining an upright posture. It exhibited reduced responsiveness, particularly for large desired pitch angles, but was initially preferred by pilots due to its predictability and the minimal adjustments required for precise grasping and box placement.
- **DCM Pitch-only Mapping:** This mapping offered a more dynamic response by directly commanding the robot's acceleration through its pitch. As pilots gained familiarity with the task, they increasingly transitioned to this mode to leverage its enhanced responsiveness for dynamic base motions.
- **Hybrid Control Strategy:** The findings suggested that pilots could benefit from a hybrid control strategy. They often reverted to the velocity mapping when releasing objects, a phase where precise positioning is critical, and neutralizing haptic feedback helped minimize unwanted oscillations. This indicates a preference for dynamically selecting between control strategies based on task phase, balancing precision during critical sub-tasks (grasping, releasing) with responsiveness during locomotion.

**C. Limitations:**
The study identified several limitations:
- **Pre-grasp Mass Estimation:** The method requires an estimate of the object's mass prior to grasping for accurate pitch equilibrium computation. While existing methods can estimate mass shortly after interaction, compensation *during* the grasp remains a challenge.
- **Object Modeling Simplification:** The object is modeled as a point mass at the end-effector. Objects with significant CoM offsets in the x-direction could lead to inaccurate compensation, and those with y-direction offsets could induce rolling, which is not explicitly addressed. Vision-based estimation of object inertial parameters before grasping is suggested as future work.
- **Grasping Inconsistency:** The robot's passive grippers rely on friction and protrusions, requiring precise alignment. The lack of depth perception from monocular first-person-view (FPV) cameras often resulted in alignment errors, contributing to grasping inconsistencies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of robotics and human-robot interaction, with implications for practical applications and future research directions.

**A. Advancement in Teleoperation Frameworks:**
The work develops a unified teleoperation framework that integrates height retargeting, explicit handling of payload mass, and haptic feedback for wheeled humanoid robots. This addresses previously identified gaps in the literature by demonstrating coordinated whole-body control, dynamic height variation, and effective disturbance handling under pilot guidance within a single system.

**B. Enabling Complex Robot Tasks:**
The developed framework significantly enhances the capability of wheeled humanoid robots to perform Dynamic Mobile Manipulation (DMM) tasks involving heavy payloads (up to 21% of the robot's mass). This moves robots closer to reliably executing physically demanding behaviors that require simultaneous locomotion, manipulation, and dynamic posture adjustment in dynamic environments.

**C. Improving Human-Robot Collaboration and Operator Experience:**
The Human-Machine Interface (HMI) with advanced motion retargeting allows human operators to intuitively control complex robot behaviors, effectively making the robot an extension of their own body. The finding that automatic lean compensation significantly reduces pilot effort and improves task performance is crucial for the long-term sustainability and usability of such systems, lowering the cognitive and physical burden on human operators. The investigation into tunable haptic feedback and different locomotion mappings highlights the importance of user-centric design for comfort, stability, and control fidelity.

**D. Foundation for Future Autonomous Systems:**
By providing a robust method for teleoperating complex DMM tasks, this work offers a valuable platform for collecting high-quality human demonstration data. Such data is essential for training deep imitation learning models, which can eventually enable humanoid robots to perform these tasks autonomously. The insights gained from evaluating different control paradigms and user preferences can inform the design of more effective learning algorithms.

**E. Practical Applications:**
The capabilities demonstrated, particularly the ability to lift and transport heavy objects while dynamically adjusting height and maintaining balance, have direct relevance to various industries. Potential applications include logistics, warehouses, manufacturing, and other service sectors where humanoids could assist in physically demanding tasks, improving efficiency, reducing human injury risk, and extending human capabilities in challenging environments.

**F. Directing Future Research:**
The identified limitations, such as the need for real-time object mass estimation and improved grasping consistency, provide clear directions for future research. This includes exploring vision-based inertial parameter estimation and developing more advanced gripper designs or multi-modal sensing to overcome current challenges in object interaction.
