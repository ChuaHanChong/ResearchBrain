# World-in-World: World Models in a Closed-Loop World

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Johns Hopkins University and collaborators introduce "World-in-World," the first comprehensive open platform to benchmark visual world models by their practical utility in supporting embodied agents in closed-loop interaction. The platform reveals that for embodied tasks, controllability and scaling post-training with action-observation data are more effective than relying on visual quality alone, and that increased inference-time computation leads to substantially improved closed-loop performance.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for generative world models (WMs) primarily focus on "open-loop" evaluations, assessing visual fidelity or plausibility in isolation without active interaction.
- A critical gap exists in evaluating the "embodied utility" of WMs, which is their functional effectiveness in helping an agent achieve tasks in interactive, closed-loop settings.
- The research community lacked a unified, standardized framework to test world models under conditions that truly mirror real agent-environment interactions.

## Method

- Introduced "**World-in-World**," a comprehensive open ==benchmark platform== for integrating diverse world models into concrete embodied tasks to evaluate their practical utility.
- Developed a unified closed-loop online planning strategy where WMs perform counterfactual rollouts of proposed action plans, enabling continuous re-planning and adaptation.
- Provided a standardized `Unified Action API` to seamlessly translate an agent's intended actions into specific control inputs compatible with various world model types (e.g., text, camera trajectory, low-level actions).
- Curated four diverse embodied tasks (Active Recognition, Image-Goal Navigation, Active Embodied Question Answering, Robotic Manipulation) utilizing established simulators and real-world datasets.

## Results

- Integrating a visual world model consistently improved the performance of base proposal policies across all four embodied tasks (Active Recognition, Image-Goal Navigation, A-EQA, Robotic Manipulation).
- Post-trained models demonstrated significant gains, with Wan2.1† increasing Active Recognition accuracy from **58.26%** to **62.61%** and Image-Goal Navigation success rate from **38.19%** to **45.14%**.
- A data scaling law was observed for world models in embodied settings, showing a positive correlation between increased post-training data and Active Recognition performance, with larger models benefiting more.
- Increasing the average inference count per episode from 3 to 11 improved SVD†'s Active Recognition success rate from **53.36%** to **60.98%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual quality alone does not guarantee task success in embodied settings; instead, a world model's controllability (reliable and precise response to low-level actions) is more crucial.
> - Scaling post-training with modest amounts of domain-specific action-observation data is more effective for improving embodied utility than merely upgrading to larger, general pretrained video generators.
> - Allocating more inference-time computation to world model queries substantially improves closed-loop performance by enabling the agent's planner to explore a wider range of hypothetical future states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed an extraordinary surge in the capabilities of generative models, particularly in visual synthesis. Recent advancements in diffusion models have led to systems that can create strikingly realistic images, videos, 3D scenes, and even 4D dynamic content. These "world generation systems" (e.g., Sora, LTX-Video, Hunyuan, Stable Video Diffusion) have captivated the AI community with their ability to produce complex and coherent visual narratives from simple prompts. This progress has naturally led researchers to hypothesize that these generative models, often referred to as "world models" (WMs), could serve as predictive perception systems for embodied agents, enabling them to anticipate future states and make informed decisions in complex environments.

However, a critical gap exists in the evaluation of these world models for embodied AI. The prevailing benchmarks, such as VBench (for visual quality), WorldModelBench (for visual plausibility), and WorldScore (for models taking camera trajectories), primarily focus on "open-loop" evaluations. This means they assess the visual fidelity, consistency, or plausibility of generated content in isolation, without truly testing whether these generated "worlds" are functionally useful for an agent trying to achieve a task in an interactive, closed-loop setting. The core issue of "embodied utility"—whether WMs actually help agents succeed at practical tasks—has remained largely unresolved due to this fragmented and visually-centric evaluation paradigm.

"World-in-World" directly addresses this fundamental limitation. It stands as the *first* comprehensive open platform designed to benchmark visual world models not by their aesthetic quality, but by their practical utility in supporting embodied agents in closed-loop interaction. By providing a unified evaluation framework that integrates diverse WMs into concrete embodied tasks and measures task success as the primary metric, this work shifts the research focus. It moves beyond passive visual generation to active, predictive capabilities essential for robust decision-making in physically grounded AI. This positions "World-in-World" as a crucial catalyst for advancing the development of WMs that are not just visually impressive, but genuinely intelligent and useful for embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "World-in-World" research is to introduce and establish a novel, comprehensive, and open benchmark platform for evaluating generative world models through the lens of embodied interaction. This platform, named "World-in-World," is explicitly designed to address the significant limitations of existing evaluation protocols, which predominantly focus on isolated visual quality metrics in open-loop settings.

The key motivations driving this work are:
1. **Resolve the Embodied Utility Gap:** The central motivation is to answer the critical question: "Do WMs actually help agents succeed at embodied tasks?" Current benchmarks emphasize visual realism, but the authors argue that this alone does not guarantee utility for decision-making in interactive environments.
2. **Establish a Unified Closed-Loop Evaluation:** The community lacks a standardized framework to test world models in conditions that mirror real agent-environment interactions. This fragmented evaluation hinders fair comparisons across heterogeneous world models and obscures true progress in embodied AI. "World-in-World" aims to provide this much-needed unified, closed-loop protocol.
3. **Shift Focus from Visual Quality to Task Success:** The authors contend that the ultimate measure of a world model's value for embodied agents should be its contribution to task success, not merely flawless visuals. The benchmark is designed to prioritize task success as the primary metric, providing a more relevant and rigorous assessment.
4. **Enable Integration of Diverse World Models:** Existing WMs employ various control interfaces (text, camera trajectories, low-level actions). A unified platform is needed to seamlessly integrate these diverse models and compare them on common embodied tasks.
5. **Uncover Principles for Effective Embodied World Models:** By systematically evaluating WMs in this new setting, the research aims to uncover fundamental insights and scaling laws related to their design and deployment for embodied agents, specifically examining factors like visual quality, controllability, data scaling, and inference-time computation.

In essence, "World-in-World" seeks to redefine how world models are assessed, moving from a passive observation of generated content to an active engagement within simulated environments, thereby guiding the development of more practically useful and intelligent embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "World-in-World" platform is meticulously designed to wrap generative world models within a closed-loop interface, enabling their evaluation based on practical utility for embodied agents. The methodology centers around three core components: a unified online planning strategy, a standardized action API, and a suite of comprehensive embodied tasks, complemented by a post-training protocol.

**A. Unified Strategy for Closed-Loop Online Planning:**
The core of "World-in-World" is a generic closed-loop decision-making framework that leverages visual world models. This strategy operates in a continuous cycle:
1. **Proposal (π_proposal):** Given the current observation (o_t) and task goal (g), the agent generates *M* candidate action plans, each with a horizon *L*. This can be instantiated by various policies, including vision-language models (VLMs), diffusion policies, or rule-based heuristics.
2. **Simulation (g_θ):** Each candidate action plan is transformed into appropriate control inputs (I^(m)_t) via the unified action API. The world model (g_θ) then performs a counterfactual rollout, predicting *L* future observations (O^(m)_t) corresponding to each plan.
3. **Revision (π_revision):** The agent evaluates all simulated rollouts and their corresponding candidate plans. A revision policy (π_revision), typically a scoring function (S) implemented by a VLM, assigns a score to each trajectory and selects the "best decision" (D*_t) that maximizes expected reward or utility. This chosen decision is then executed in the real environment.
This cycle repeats, allowing the agent to continuously re-plan and adapt to new observations, thus closing the interaction loop.

**B. Unified Action API (C):**
To ensure seamless integration of diverse world models, the platform introduces a standardized `Unified Action API` (denoted as `C`). This API translates an agent's intended action sequence (A) into the specific control inputs (I) expected by different world models. It supports three principal types of control information:
- **Text Prompt:** For image-and-text-to-video models, action sequences are converted into descriptive textual prompts.
- **Camera Trajectory/Viewpoint:** For models requiring explicit camera poses, actions are translated into sequences of camera positions and orientations.
- **Low-Level Actions:** For WMs that natively accept discrete or continuous low-level actions, the API maps the agent's action vocabulary to the model's expected input format, applying necessary transformations to maintain consistency.

**C. Comprehensive Embodied Tasks:**
The benchmark curates four diverse closed-loop environments, each designed to stress distinct capabilities of world models:
1. **Active Recognition (AR):** Agents must identify target objects under occlusion or extreme viewpoints while minimizing navigation cost, emphasizing perception and informed navigation.
2. **Image-Goal Navigation (ImageNav):** Agents navigate to a target viewpoint specified by a reference image, focusing on success rate and path efficiency.
3. **Active Embodied Question Answering (A-EQA):** Agents answer open-ended natural-language questions after actively exploring a 3D environment, requiring complex exploration strategies and reasoning.
4. **Robotic Manipulation:** Agents control a 7-DoF robotic arm to complete tasks (e.g., push, slide, insert, stack), evaluating a WM's ability to model precise object interactions and dynamics.

These tasks utilize established simulators like Habitat-Sim and RLBench, along with real-world datasets like Matterport3D and HM3D, ensuring realistic and challenging evaluation scenarios.

**D. Post-Training Protocol:**
To assess the adaptability of pretrained video generators, the authors introduce a lightweight post-training procedure. This involves fine-tuning these models on modest amounts of action-observation data specific to the target embodied domains (e.g., panoramic data from HM3D for Habitat-Sim tasks, task demonstrations from RLBench for manipulation). This protocol not only improves alignment with the target domain but also allows for the study of data scaling laws for world models in embodied settings. The post-training uses scenes disjoint from evaluation scenes to prevent memorization and test generalization.

By combining these elements, "World-in-World" provides a robust, fair, and practical framework to evaluate the true utility of generative world models for embodied AI, moving beyond superficial visual metrics to meaningful task performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "World-in-World" benchmark uncovers several surprising and crucial insights into the effectiveness of generative world models (WMs) for embodied AI, challenging prior assumptions centered solely on visual fidelity.

**1. Visual Quality Alone Does Not Guarantee Task Success; Controllability Matters More:**
Contrary to the common focus on generating visually stunning videos, the study found a weak correlation between high visual quality (e.g., aesthetic scores, image quality) and strong task success in embodied settings (Figure 2, 5a). For instance, off-the-shelf video generators like Wan2.1, despite producing high-quality visuals, offered only modest gains in embodied tasks without adaptation. In contrast, models that demonstrated better "controllability"—i.e., reliable and precise responses to low-level action controls—achieved substantially higher task success rates (Figure 5b). Post-training significantly improved this action-conditioned alignment, leading to better task performance, even if the raw visual generation quality wasn't the absolute highest. This finding underscores that for embodied agents, the ability to accurately simulate the consequences of specific actions is paramount, overshadowing purely aesthetic appeal.

**2. Scaling Post-Training with Action-Observation Data is More Effective than Upgrading Pretrained Video Generators:**
The research reveals that adapting pretrained video generators through a modest post-training phase on domain-specific action-observation data yields consistent and significant performance improvements. For example, Wan2.1† (post-trained) raised Active Recognition (AR) accuracy from 58.26% to 62.61% and Image-Goal Navigation (ImageNav) success rate (SR) from 38.19% to 45.14%, outperforming its zero-shot counterpart (Table 1). Furthermore, the study presents the first data scaling law for world models in embodied settings (Figure 6). It shows a clear positive correlation: increasing the amount of post-training data consistently improves AR performance. Notably, larger pretrained models (e.g., Wan2.1†, 14B parameters) benefited more from this scaling and saturated less than smaller models (e.g., SVD†, 1.5B parameters), indicating greater capacity to absorb action-conditioned supervision. This suggests that domain-specific fine-tuning on relevant interaction data is a more impactful strategy for improving embodied utility than merely using a larger, more general video generator out-of-the-box.

**3. Allocating More Inference-Time Compute Substantially Improves Closed-Loop Performance:**
Within the proposed online planning framework, the number of times the world model is queried to simulate potential futures (inference count) directly impacts task performance. The results demonstrate a clear positive correlation between increasing the average inference count per episode and higher task success rates (Figure 7). For instance, increasing the average inference count from 3 to 11 improved SVD†'s AR success rate from 53.36% to 60.98%. This indicates that leveraging the world model more extensively during decision-making, even at inference time, allows the agent's planner to make more informed choices by exploring a wider range of hypothetical future states.

**Additional Key Results:**
- **WMs Enhance Base Policy Performance:** Across all four embodied tasks (AR, ImageNav, A-EQA, Robotic Manipulation), integrating a visual world model consistently improved the performance of the base proposal policy (Tables 1, 2, 3), validating the effectiveness of the World-in-World online planning framework.
- **Challenges in Robotic Manipulation:** While improvements were observed, the gains were less pronounced for robotic manipulation tasks (Table 3). This is attributed to the inherent difficulty of accurately modeling contact-rich interactions, precise dynamics, and robot kinematics, which current visual world models still struggle to capture effectively.
- **Input Context Sensitivity:** The choice between panoramic and front-view input for world models did not consistently yield large gains across all settings (Table 4). This could be due to resolution loss during panorama-to-perspective conversion, which can degrade downstream perception and planning even with a richer global context.

These findings collectively provide critical guidance for the future development and application of generative world models in embodied AI, emphasizing the practical aspects of controllability, data adaptation, and inference-time utilization over sheer visual spectacle.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "World-in-World" research carries profound significance and is poised to have a substantial impact on the field of embodied AI and the development of generative world models.

**1. Establishing a Gold Standard for Embodied World Model Evaluation:**
The most immediate and impactful contribution of "World-in-World" is the introduction of the first comprehensive, closed-loop benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the *utility* of generative world models for embodied agents. By moving beyond isolated visual quality metrics to prioritize task success in interactive environments, this platform fills a critical void in the research landscape. It provides a standardized and rigorous framework that will enable fair comparisons between diverse world model architectures and guide the community towards truly effective embodied AI solutions. This benchmark is likely to become a de facto standard for assessing progress in this domain.

**2. Providing Actionable Insights for World Model Development:**
The "three surprises" uncovered by the study offer invaluable, actionable insights for researchers and practitioners:
- **Prioritizing Controllability:** The finding that precise controllability matters more than visual fidelity for task success shifts the focus of WM development. Future research will likely invest more in creating models that accurately respond to actions, rather than just generating photorealistic but uncontrollable futures.
- **Leveraging Post-Training:** The demonstration of the effectiveness of data scaling during post-training highlights a practical and efficient strategy for adapting general-purpose video generators to specific embodied tasks. This could accelerate the deployment of advanced WMs by reducing the need for extensive retraining from scratch.
- **Optimizing Inference-Time Compute:** The clear correlation between increased inference-time computation and task success provides a concrete pathway to improve agent performance without necessarily requiring larger or more complex WMs. This encourages the development of more intelligent planning strategies that effectively query and integrate world model predictions.

These insights challenge prevailing assumptions and will directly influence the design principles, training methodologies, and deployment strategies for next-generation embodied world models.

**3. Catalyzing Progress in Robust Embodied AI:**
By providing a platform to systematically evaluate and understand the strengths and weaknesses of WMs in real-world-like interactive scenarios, "World-in-World" will accelerate progress in building more robust, adaptive, and intelligent embodied agents. It offers a structured way to investigate critical challenges such as generalization to novel environments, long-horizon planning, and the precise modeling of physical interactions and dynamics – all of which are explicitly highlighted as future research directions by the authors. The open-source nature of the platform will further foster community engagement and collaborative development.

**4. Bridging Generative AI and Decision-Making:**
The work effectively bridges the gap between the advancements in pure generative AI (e.g., video synthesis) and the practical demands of decision-making in reinforcement learning and embodied control. It demonstrates how powerful generative models can be integrated into planning loops to enhance perception, prediction, and policy execution, thereby pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in model-based reinforcement learning and cognitive robotics.

In conclusion, "World-in-World" is not just a new benchmark; it is a foundational piece of infrastructure that redefines the evaluation paradigm for generative world models in embodied AI. Its significance lies in its capacity to shift research focus, provide crucial empirical guidance, and ultimately steer the development of world models towards achieving genuinely intelligent and functionally effective embodied agents.
