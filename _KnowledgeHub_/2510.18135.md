# World-in-World: World Models in a Closed-Loop World

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Johns Hopkins University and collaborators introduce "World-in-World," the first comprehensive open platform to benchmark visual world models by their practical utility in supporting embodied agents in closed-loop interaction. The platform reveals that for embodied tasks, controllability and scaling post-training with action-observation data are more effective than relying on visual quality alone, and that increased inference-time computation leads to substantially improved closed-loop performance.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for generative world models (WMs) primarily focus on "open-loop" evaluations, assessing visual fidelity or plausibility in isolation without active interaction.
- A critical gap exists in evaluating the "embodied utility" of WMs, which is their functional effectiveness in helping an agent achieve tasks in interactive, closed-loop settings.
- The research community lacked a unified, standardized framework to test world models under conditions that truly mirror real agent-environment interactions.

## Method

- Introduced "**World-in-World**," a comprehensive open ==benchmark platform== for integrating diverse world models into concrete embodied tasks to evaluate their practical utility.
- Developed a unified closed-loop online planning strategy where WMs perform counterfactual rollouts of proposed action plans, enabling continuous re-planning and adaptation.
- Provided a standardized `Unified Action API` to seamlessly translate an agent's intended actions into specific control inputs compatible with various world model types (e.g., text, camera trajectory, low-level actions).
- Curated four diverse embodied tasks (Active Recognition, Image-Goal Navigation, Active Embodied Question Answering, Robotic Manipulation) utilizing established simulators and real-world datasets.

## Results

- Integrating a visual world model consistently improved the performance of base proposal policies across all four embodied tasks (Active Recognition, Image-Goal Navigation, A-EQA, Robotic Manipulation).
- Post-trained models demonstrated significant gains, with Wan2.1† increasing Active Recognition accuracy from **58.26%** to **62.61%** and Image-Goal Navigation success rate from **38.19%** to **45.14%**.
- A data scaling law was observed for world models in embodied settings, showing a positive correlation between increased post-training data and Active Recognition performance, with larger models benefiting more.
- Increasing the average inference count per episode from 3 to 11 improved SVD†'s Active Recognition success rate from **53.36%** to **60.98%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual quality alone does not guarantee task success in embodied settings; instead, a world model's controllability (reliable and precise response to low-level actions) is more crucial.
> - Scaling post-training with modest amounts of domain-specific action-observation data is more effective for improving embodied utility than merely upgrading to larger, general pretrained video generators.
> - Allocating more inference-time computation to world model queries substantially improves closed-loop performance by enabling the agent's planner to explore a wider range of hypothetical future states.
