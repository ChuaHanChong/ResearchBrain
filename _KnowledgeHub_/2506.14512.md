# SIRI-Bench: Challenging VLMs' Spatial Intelligence through Complex Reasoning Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Sun Yat-sen University introduces SIRI-Bench, a new benchmark comprising 9,078 video-based 3D geometry problems, to evaluate Vision-Language Models' structural spatial intelligence. It reveals that state-of-the-art VLMs struggle with extracting implicit spatial information from videos and performing multi-step reasoning, with the best model achieving only 31% accuracy within a 40% error margin, significantly lagging behind text-only models and human performance.

## Problem

- Current VLM benchmarks for spatial abilities often focus on intuitive perception (e.g., distance, layout) rather than complex, multi-step structural reasoning.
- Existing reasoning benchmarks for LLMs/VLMs primarily use abstract textual descriptions or 2D diagrams, providing explicit conditions that do not necessitate sophisticated visual interpretation from realistic 3D scenes.
- There is a need for robust evaluation of VLMs' capacity to derive complex mathematical conditions and spatial relationships directly from visual input in realistic 3D environments, crucial for real-world applications like robotics.

## Method

- Introduces **SIRI-Bench**, a benchmark of 9,078 ==video-question-answer triplets== derived from solid geometry problems where mathematical conditions are implicitly embedded within realistic 3D scenes.
- Develops an ==Automatic Scene Creation Engine==, a ==multi-agent LLM system==, to automatically generate these 3D scenes and render them into videos, transforming abstract geometry problems into visual data at scale.
- Designs the benchmark to replace 2D diagrams and explicit textual conditions with ==dynamic 3D videos== and ==color-coded visual markers==, forcing VLMs to infer spatial information from visual input.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLMs achieved limited performance on SIRI-Bench, with the best model having only **31%** of predictions within a 0–**40%** error margin, and over **33%** exceeding **100%** error.
- Text-input LLMs significantly outperformed video-input VLMs, with text-only models achieving **89.2%** within a **20%** error margin vs. **18.4%** for vision models.
- VLMs frequently misidentified geometric types (e.g., GPT-4o misidentified in **~79%** of problems), indicating struggles with basic geometric recognition and inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLMs possess limited structural spatial intelligence, consistently underperforming on complex 3D spatially-grounded reasoning tasks compared to text-only models and human baselines.
> - The primary bottleneck for VLMs on such tasks is the difficulty in accurately extracting and interpreting implicit spatial information (like object types, dimensions, and relationships) from dynamic 3D visual inputs.
> - Explicit multi-step reasoning ("Deep-Thinking") offers some performance benefits for VLMs when processing complex visual inputs, but does not bridge the fundamental gap in spatial perception and inference.
