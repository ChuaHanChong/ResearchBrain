# EagleVision: A Dual-Stage Framework with BEV-grounding-based Chain-of-Thought for Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> EagleVision, a dual-stage framework, enhances the spatial reasoning capabilities of multimodal large language models by integrating macro-level perception with micro-level, verifiable BEV-grounded spatial reasoning. The framework achieved an average score of 63.5 on the VSI-Bench benchmark, positioning it as a leading open-source model.

## Problem

- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) often struggle with robust 3D spatial reasoning, frequently relying on weak 2D methods or opaque 3D reconstruction modules.
- Extending interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to spatial reasoning faces challenges due to limited token budgets preventing global spatial coverage and the lack of explicit 3D grounding for hypothesis verification.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, detailed supervision for training MLLMs on multi-step spatial CoT, making learning complex spatial reasoning difficult.

## Method

- A dual-stage framework is proposed, comprising ==Macro Perception== and ==Micro Verification==, to progressively refine spatial understanding in MLLMs.
- Macro Perception utilizes a ==Semantic-Perspective Fusion Determinantal Point Process (SPF-DPP)== to efficiently select a compact yet diverse and semantically relevant set of keyframes from raw video, balancing geometric diversity and query relevance.
- Micro Verification employs a ==BEV-grounded Chain-of-Thought (CoT) agent== capable of active pose querying and real-frame retrieval for iterative spatial reasoning, trained with a novel reinforcement learning reward that incorporates a ==spatial grounding penalty==.

## Results

- EagleVision achieved a state-of-the-art average score of **63.5** on the VSI-Bench benchmark among open-source Vision-Language Models, surpassing its Qwen3-VL-8B baseline by **4.1 points**.
- The framework demonstrated strong performance across various spatial tasks, including **74.9%** accuracy in numerical answers, **72.3%** in object size, and **84.7%** in route planning, even exceeding human accuracy in fine-grained distance estimation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual and synergistic effectiveness of the Multimodal CoT, BEV grounding, SPF-DPP for frame selection, and the novel spatial consistency reward in the reinforcement learning setup.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust spatial intelligence in MLLMs can be achieved by combining global scene understanding (macro perception) with active, verifiable, and iteratively refined local geometric reasoning (micro verification).
> - Explicitly grounding spatial hypotheses in a Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV) workspace allows MLLMs to query for specific viewpoints and retrieve concrete visual evidence, creating interpretable and consistent spatial reasoning chains.
> - Reinforcement learning with a custom spatial grounding reward function enables effective training of multi-step spatial CoT policies, reducing reliance on extensive and costly step-by-step human annotations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video-based spatial reasoning, encompassing tasks such as distance estimation, direction judgment, and layout understanding from multiple views, constitutes a core capability for multimodal understanding, autonomous driving, and various downstream applications. Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) demonstrate limitations in these areas. A significant challenge is that MLLMs are typically constrained by fixed token budgets, leading to the consumption of uniformly sampled frames. This approach often results in missing critical viewpoints that provide the necessary geometric parallax for reliable 3D inference. Furthermore, once initial frames are selected, existing models lack mechanisms to request additional views when the available evidence proves insufficient during the reasoning process.

Prior efforts in spatial intelligence for MLLMs have explored several directions:
1. **Augmenting 2D reasoning with 3D features**: This involves incorporating explicit 3D representations like point clouds or depth maps into language models. However, these methods generally lack the ability for the model to actively seek additional views when initial evidence is insufficient.
2. **Integrating 3D reconstruction modules**: Some approaches couple MLLMs with 3D reconstruction as a preprocessing step to generate dense geometric representations. The outputs of these modules are typically static and cannot be iteratively refined during reasoning.
3. **Interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) frameworks**: Agent-based CoT approaches, such as ChatGPT-o3 and DeepEyes, have shown the benefits of interleaving textual reasoning with active visual evidence acquisition. Nevertheless, these methods predominantly operate on single images (e.g., cropping or zooming) and do not address the complexities of multi-view, geometry-aware evidence gathering required for spatial tasks.

The present work identifies three specific challenges in extending the "thinking with images" paradigm to video-based spatial reasoning:
- **Token budget constraints**: Initial frame selection must optimize for both semantic relevance to the query and geometric diversity of camera viewpoints, which naive uniform sampling fails to achieve.
- **Spatial hypothesis verification**: Spatial statements (e.g., relative object positions) require verification against specific views within a shared coordinate system, necessitating a mechanism to map abstract spatial queries to concrete video frames.
- **Annotation impracticality**: Collecting human annotations for multi-step spatial reasoning trajectories is laborious, requiring methods that can learn when and where to query new views from answer-level supervision.

EagleVision aims to address these identified gaps by introducing a dual-stage framework that combines geometry-aware frame selection with active, Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV)-grounded reasoning, allowing MLLMs to dynamically acquire geometric cues and refine spatial judgments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of MLLMs in video-based scenarios. This involves enabling MLLMs to actively and intelligently acquire necessary visual information, moving beyond the passive consumption of a fixed set of input frames.

The key motivations and specific objectives are:

- **Overcoming limitations of fixed-input MLLMs**: Existing MLLMs typically process a predetermined set of frames, often uniformly sampled, which may not contain the most informative geometric cues for complex spatial questions. The motivation is to allow the model to dynamically gather relevant visual evidence.
- **Enabling active view selection during reasoning**: Spatial questions frequently require examining a scene from specific vantage points that might not be available in the initially provided frames. The objective is to equip the model with a mechanism to actively request and retrieve new frames based on its ongoing reasoning process, creating a closed-loop "hypothesize-look-verify" cycle.
- **Grounding spatial queries in a shared geometric representation**: Abstract spatial queries need to be translated into concrete visual evidence. The paper aims to use a Bird’s-Eye View (BEV) map as a shared coordinate system, allowing the model to predict poses for view retrieval and thus ground its spatial reasoning in a consistent geometric context.
- **Optimizing initial frame selection**: Given token budget constraints, it is crucial to select an initial set of keyframes that are both semantically relevant to the user query and geometrically diverse in terms of viewpoints. The motivation is to provide the MLLM with a high-quality, compact initial representation of the scene.
- **Learning active querying without extensive human supervision**: Annotating multi-step reasoning processes with explicit tool calls can be labor-intensive. A key objective is to train the active view selection policy purely through reinforcement learning with answer-level supervision, thereby reducing the dependency on detailed human-annotated reasoning traces.
- **Improving reliability and accuracy of spatial judgments**: By enabling dynamic evidence acquisition and grounding it in geometric reality, the research aims to significantly improve the accuracy and robustness of MLLMs in tasks requiring nuanced spatial understanding, such as estimating distances, judging relative directions, and comprehending room layouts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EagleVision is structured as a dual-stage framework designed for video-based spatial reasoning: macro perception for initial frame selection, and micro verification for iterative, active reasoning.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The framework begins with an offline preprocessing step: a frozen, off-the-shelf SLAM system (Vipe) processes the input video to recover per-frame camera poses and dense depth maps. This reconstruction forms a reusable spatial index, including a BEV map and a pose database. This one-time cost ensures subsequent per-query reasoning is efficient.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Macro Perception (Geometry-aware Frame Selection)

The first stage focuses on selecting a compact and informative set of keyframes under a fixed token budget. This is achieved using the Semantics–Perspective-Fusion Determinantal Point Process (SPF-DPP).

- **Task Definition**: Given a video with $N$ frames, each having a camera pose $T_i \in SE(3)$ and a semantic score $s_i$ (computed by FG-CLIP relative to the query), the goal is to select a fixed-size subset $X$ of $k$ frames that exhibit both viewpoint diversity and semantic representativeness.
- **Pose Distance and Sparse Viewpoint Graph**: Pairwise frame distance in $SE(3)$ is measured by combining translational and rotational components: $d_{ij}^2 = \|t_i - t_j\|^2/\sigma_t^2 + \beta^2 \theta(R_i, R_j)^2$. These distances are converted into affinities $w_{ij} = \exp(-\frac{1}{2}d_{ij}^2)$. A sparse adjacency matrix $W$ is constructed by retaining edges within a temporal window of bandwidth $b$, enhancing computational efficiency.
- **Diffusion Viewpoint Kernel**: To capture global geometric relationships from local affinities, a heat-kernel diffusion is applied to the graph Laplacian: $K_{view} = \exp(-\tau L)$, where $L = I - D^{-1/2} W D^{-1/2}$. This results in a positive semidefinite (PSD) kernel that quantifies geometric similarity between frames from a viewpoint coverage perspective.
- **Semantic Score and Quality Modulation**: FG-CLIP computes raw semantic scores $s_i$, which are then calibrated using a temperature-scaled softmax ($\tilde{s}_i$) to normalize scale effects. A diagonal quality matrix $Q = \text{diag}(q_1, \dots, q_N)$ is defined with $q_i = (1-\alpha) + \alpha \tilde{s}_i$. This formulation biases selection towards query-relevant frames while ensuring some baseline diversity.
- **L-ensemble Kernel and Fixed-Size Selection**: The SPF-DPP L-ensemble kernel is formed as $L_{dpp} = Q K_{view} Q$. For a fixed budget $k$, a greedy MAP approach with rank-one Cholesky updates is employed to maximize $\log \det(L_{dpp,X})$, selecting a subset $X$ of $k$ frames that jointly optimize for semantic importance and viewpoint diversity.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Micro Verification (Spatial MCoT with BEV-Grounded Pose Querying)

The second stage involves an iterative spatial reasoning process, termed Spatial Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (Spatial MCoT), where the model can actively acquire new visual evidence.

- **Iterative Reasoning Process**: At each step $t$, the model maintains a state comprising previously generated text tokens ($X_{\le t}$), retrieved visual observations ($I_{\le t}$), and a BEV pose buffer. The model can choose one of three actions: generate a text token, issue a pose query on the BEV plane, or terminate with an answer. This forms a "hypothesize → look → verify" cycle.
- **BEV-Grounded Pose Querying**: If a query action is chosen, the model predicts a 2D BEV pose $\hat{p}_t = (\hat{x}_t, \hat{y}_t, \hat{r}_t)$, where $(\hat{x}_t, \hat{y}_t)$ are BEV coordinates and $\hat{r}_t$ is the orientation. The system then calculates spatial similarity $s_{tj}$ between the queried pose and all stored video frames $j$. The frame with the highest similarity $j^\star$ is retrieved if $s_{max}^{(t)} \ge \tau_s$; otherwise, an error indicating no coverage is returned. The retrieved frame is appended to the model's context, and the total number of queries is capped.
- **Reward Function**: The trajectory reward $R(\tau)$ is composed of several terms:
 - $R_{acc}(\tau)$: Rewards correctness of the final answer.
 - $R_{format}(\tau)$: Encourages well-structured output.
 - $R_{tool}(\tau)$: Incentivizes appropriate invocation of the tool.
 - $R_{spatial}(\tau)$ (Ours): A spatial grounding reward that penalizes trajectories where any query's best retrieval similarity falls below the coverage threshold $\tau_s$. This term guides the model to query only within areas of actual camera coverage, preventing hallucinated viewpoints.
- **Optimization**: The querying policy is trained using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which estimates advantages within prompt-level trajectory groups to reduce variance. Importantly, the training is conducted directly via reinforcement learning without a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) cold start, meaning the model learns spatial querying purely from the reward signal, without human-annotated reasoning traces. Only model-generated text tokens contribute to the reward signal.

The framework is designed to be backend-agnostic regarding the SLAM system, allowing for replacement with other pose estimation methods without significant performance degradation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of EagleVision was evaluated on VSI-Bench and SQA3D benchmarks, comparing it against randomized heuristics, proprietary models, open-source VLMs, and task-specific spatial reasoning methods.

#### 4.1. Main Quantitative Results

- **VSI-Bench Performance**: EagleVision achieved an average score of 63.5, positioning it as the top-performing open-source VLM on this benchmark. This represents a 4.1-point improvement over its base model, Qwen3-VL-8B (59.4), and a 2.6-point gain over VLM-3R-7B (60.9), a model that incorporates an additional 3D reconstruction encoder. The results indicate that the dual-stage framework, combining geometry-aware frame selection and active BEV-grounded reasoning, contributes to enhanced performance.
- **SQA3D Performance**: For cross-benchmark generalization, EagleVision achieved an Exact Match (EM@1) score of 60.3% on SQA3D. This performance surpassed other recent spatial reasoning methods, including Struct2D (+1.8% over 58.5%), Spatial-MLLM (+4.4% over 55.9%), and Spatial-Mind (+14.0% over 46.3%). These results suggest the robustness and transferability of the RL-trained spatial querying paradigm.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on VSI-Bench using Qwen3-VL-8B as the base model to evaluate the contribution of individual components of EagleVision.

- **Overall Architecture**:
 - Adding only Spatial MCoT to the baseline (59.4) improved the score to 61.9, indicating the benefit of iterative multimodal reasoning.
 - Further incorporating BEV grounding lifted the performance to 62.7, highlighting the advantage of explicit geometric anchoring via active view selection.
 - The full EagleVision framework, including SPF-DPP, achieved 63.5, demonstrating that geometry-aware frame selection in macro perception further refines the initial evidence quality. The components were found to be complementary.
- **SPF-DPP Components**: On the MCoT+BEV baseline (62.7), using geometry-aware diffusion alone improved the score to 63.2 (+0.5), while semantic modulation alone achieved 63.0 (+0.3). The combination of both geometry and semantics in SPF-DPP yielded the full 63.5, confirming their synergistic effect.
- **SPF-DPP Hyper-parameter Sensitivity**: The SPF-DPP framework demonstrated robustness to variations in rotation weight ($\beta$) and temporal bandwidth ($b$), with performance remaining within 0.5 points. While diffusion scale ($\tau$) and quality mixing weight ($\alpha$) showed more sensitivity, SPF-DPP consistently outperformed naive time-based sampling across all tested configurations.
- **GRPO Reward Terms**:
 - Using only accuracy and format rewards resulted in a score of 61.1.
 - Adding the Tool bonus significantly boosted performance to 63.0 (+1.9), indicating the importance of incentivizing tool use.
 - The inclusion of the Spatial grounding reward further improved the score to 63.5 (+0.5), by discouraging queries to geometrically invalid viewpoints.

#### 4.3. Robustness to Pose Noise

The framework's robustness to pose estimation errors was assessed by injecting Gaussian noise into camera trajectories and by replacing the default Vipe SLAM system with VGGT (a learning-based alternative). Performance degraded gracefully: a medium noise level (5% translation, 5$^\circ$ rotation) led to only a 0.4% drop in accuracy. Replacing Vipe with VGGT resulted in a score of 63.2, a minor drop of 0.3% from the clean baseline, indicating that the framework's benefits are not tied to a specific SLAM system and that nearest-frame retrieval provides a degree of robustness.

#### 4.4. Visualizations

- **SPF-DPP**: Visualizations demonstrated how the diffusion kernel propagates local viewpoint affinities globally, favoring complementary viewpoints. Semantic scores were shown to peak only when query-relevant objects were visible, underscoring the necessity of integrating both geometric and semantic cues.
- **Model Response**: A qualitative example illustrated the model's iterative reasoning process for a spatial question. The model successfully anchored objects, performed BEV-grounded queries to retrieve specific views of target objects (e.g., refrigerator, plant), and iteratively updated its spatial estimates before providing a final, coherent judgment. This indicated the model's capacity for active view selection and evidence integration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EagleVision represents an advancement in multimodal large language models by enabling dynamic and geometry-aware acquisition of visual evidence for spatial reasoning tasks. Its significance stems from addressing critical limitations of existing MLLMs, particularly their passive consumption of visual input and lack of mechanisms for iterative spatial verification.

The introduction of a dual-stage framework, combining a macro perception stage for intelligent frame selection and a micro verification stage for active, BEV-grounded reasoning, allows MLLMs to "think with space" more effectively. The SPF-DPP in the macro stage offers an efficient method to select semantically relevant and geometrically diverse keyframes under token constraints, which is crucial for handling long videos.

A key impact lies in the methodology for training the active view selection policy. By utilizing reinforcement learning with a novel spatial grounding reward, the framework learns when and where to query new views without relying on labor-intensive human-annotated Chain-of-Thought traces. This approach contributes to the scalability of developing spatially intelligent MLLMs.

The demonstrated robustness to pose estimation errors and the ability to operate independently of a specific SLAM backend suggest that the core framework is adaptable to various real-world scenarios and underlying geometric reconstruction systems.

The improved performance on benchmarks like VSI-Bench and SQA3D indicates EagleVision's capacity for accurate spatial understanding, which has direct implications for applications in embodied AI, where agents need to navigate and interact with environments based on complex spatial relationships. Furthermore, its principles could extend to autonomous driving, robotics, and other domains requiring sophisticated perception and reasoning in 3D space. The research establishes a foundation for MLLMs to move beyond purely textual or fixed-visual reasoning towards more adaptive and intelligent interactions with dynamic visual environments.
