# VoxPoser: Composable 3D Value Maps for Robotic Manipulation with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VoxPoser enables robotic manipulators to execute open-set natural language instructions by having Large Language Models (LLMs) generate 3D value maps that guide a motion planner. This approach achieved an 88.0% success rate on real-world tasks and demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization compared to primitive-based approaches, addressing limitations in robot versatility and skill acquisition.

## Problem

- Existing language-guided robotic manipulation systems often rely on a rigid, pre-defined library of motion primitives, which limits versatility and requires extensive, task-specific data collection.
- Bridging the gap between high-level, abstract natural language instructions from LLMs and the fine-grained, continuous 6-DoF control signals required for robot end-effector trajectories remains a significant challenge.
- Traditional LLM-robot interfaces struggle with spatial-level composition of actions, meaning they can plan sequences but not precisely define spatial goals and constraints (e.g., 'grasp the top of the handle while avoiding the vase').

## Method

- **VoxPoser** prompts a ==Large Language Model (LLM)== to generate ==Python code== that orchestrates the creation of ==3D value maps== from visual observations and language instructions.
- The LLM-generated code invokes ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== for object detection and segmentation, then uses ==NumPy operations== to compose various 3D value maps (==affordance map==, ==avoidance map==, velocity, rotation, gripper action) in the robot's observation space.
- A ==model-based planner== utilizes these dynamically generated 3D value maps as ==objective functions== within an ==MPC framework== to synthesize full ==6-DoF end-effector trajectories== for zero-shot task execution.

## Results

- Achieved an **88.0%** average success rate for 5 representative everyday manipulation tasks in static real-world environments and maintained a **70.0%** success rate under dynamic disturbances, significantly outperforming a primitives-based baseline (**24.0%** static, **0.0%** disturbed).
- Demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization to unseen instructions and object attributes in simulation, consistently achieving higher success rates across various generalization categories compared to learning-based and primitives-based baselines.
- Accelerated learning for complex, contact-rich tasks by using zero-shot trajectories as priors for exploration, enabling high success rates (**80.0–91.7%**) with less than **3 minutes** of online interaction, where unguided learning often failed to converge within **12 hours**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can be leveraged to infer language-conditioned affordances and constraints and express them as executable code.
> - Composing spatial objectives via dynamic 3D value maps offers greater flexibility and zero-shot generalization for robot manipulation than relying on a fixed library of pre-defined motion primitives.
> - Combining LLM-generated code with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) allows abstract language to be grounded into continuous 3D robot observation space, enabling direct trajectory synthesis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the crucial intersection of robotic manipulation, large language models (LLMs), and vision-language models (VLMs), addressing the long-standing challenge of grounding abstract human language instructions into actionable robot behaviors.

Historically, grounding language instructions for robots involved classical methods like lexical analysis, formal logic, and graphical models to interpret commands. More recently, with the advent of deep learning, end-to-end approaches have emerged, where robots learn to associate language with actions through data-driven methods like imitation learning or reinforcement learning. However, these methods often require extensive, task-specific data collection, which is a major bottleneck in robotics due to the difficulty and cost of gathering diverse robotic interaction data.

The recent rise of LLMs has brought about a significant shift. Researchers have explored using LLMs for high-level reasoning and planning, enabling them to decompose complex tasks into textual sequences of steps (e.g., "Language Models as Zero-Shot Planners", "Socratic Models"). While LLMs excel at abstract planning and commonsense reasoning, a persistent challenge remains: how to translate these high-level textual plans into low-level physical robot actions, especially in an open-set environment where tasks and objects are not predefined. Existing LLM-robot interfaces commonly rely on a fixed library of "pre-defined motion primitives" or "skills" that the LLM can invoke and parameterize (e.g., "Code as Policies"). This reliance on pre-defined primitives severely limits the robot's versatility, as each new type of physical interaction requires manual skill acquisition or engineering.

VoxPoser directly confronts this limitation. It acknowledges that LLMs cannot directly output high-dimensional control signals, nor are they typically trained to compose actions at a *spatial* level. The key innovation is to leverage LLMs not just for sequential planning or primitive parameterization, but for inferring *language-conditioned affordances and constraints* and then grounding this abstract knowledge into the robot's *3D observation space* by composing "value maps." This moves beyond simply chaining pre-programmed skills; instead, the LLM dynamically specifies the *goal* and *constraints* in a spatially continuous manner.

The work builds upon and differentiates itself from:
- **Prior language grounding methods:** It moves beyond rigid lexical parsing or reliance on massive robot-specific datasets by leveraging the pre-trained, open-world knowledge of LLMs and VLMs.
- **LLMs for robotics:** Unlike methods that focus on high-level textual planning or parameterizing a fixed set of primitives, VoxPoser enables LLMs to generate code that directly defines the *spatial objective function* for a motion planner. This provides a more flexible and granular control mechanism than discrete primitives.
- **Learning-based trajectory optimization:** While many works learn cost functions from in-domain data, VoxPoser uses LLMs for *zero-shot* cost specification, achieving broad generalization without task-specific training data. It also introduces a novel way to use these zero-shot specifications as priors for efficient learning in contact-rich scenarios.

In essence, VoxPoser proposes a generalizable solution to the "last mile" problem of language-guided robotics, allowing abstract language instructions to directly shape the robot's real-time motion planning by creating dynamic, spatially-grounded objectives.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of VoxPoser is to enable robotic manipulators to execute a wide variety of everyday tasks specified through **open-set natural language instructions** and involving **open-set objects**. This means the system should be able to handle novel commands and interact with objects it has not been explicitly trained on.

The core motivation stems from the limitations of existing approaches in language-guided robot manipulation:

1. **Bottleneck of Pre-defined Motion Primitives:** Most state-of-the-art methods, even those utilizing LLMs for high-level reasoning, still rely on a pre-defined library of "skills" or "motion primitives" (e.g., "grasp," "push," "open drawer"). Acquiring and fine-tuning these individual skills is laborious, data-intensive, and limits the robot's ability to generalize to novel tasks or variations of known tasks. The paper seeks to overcome this "major bottleneck" by enabling more fluid and direct trajectory synthesis.
2. **Bridging Abstract Language to Fine-grained Actions:** While LLMs possess a "wealth of actionable knowledge" and excel at reasoning and planning in textual space, it is impractical for them to directly output the high-frequency, high-dimensional control signals required for robot end-effector trajectories (6-DoF waypoints). The challenge is how to effectively "ground" this abstract knowledge into the robot's continuous observation and action space.
3. **Need for Spatial-Level Composition:** The authors observe that while LLMs can compose *sequential* policy logic (e.g., first do A, then B), it's unclear if they can compose actions at a *spatial* level (e.g., "grasp the top drawer handle while staying away from the vase"). This spatial reasoning is crucial for flexible and robust manipulation in complex environments.
4. **Zero-Shot Generalization:** The ultimate goal is to achieve broad generalization without requiring laborious data collection or manual designs for each individual primitive or task. This necessitates a framework that can leverage the "open-world reasoning" capabilities of LLMs and the "generalizable visual grounding" of VLMs.

To address these motivations, VoxPoser aims to synthesize full 6-DoF end-effector trajectories directly, by enabling LLMs to infer affordances and constraints, and crucially, to leverage their code-writing capabilities to interact with VLMs and compose dense 3D value maps that ground this knowledge into the robot's real-time observation space. These value maps then serve as objective functions for a model-based planning framework, enabling zero-shot trajectory synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VoxPoser proposes a novel methodology for language-conditioned robot manipulation that shifts from relying on pre-defined motion primitives to dynamically generating 3D value maps, which guide a model-based planner. The core components are:

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The problem is framed as an optimization task for generating a robot motion trajectory ($\tau_r_i$) for a given sub-task instruction ($\ell_i$). The objective is to minimize a cost function comprising:
- `F_task(T_i, ℓ_i)`: Scores how well the environment state evolution (`T_i`) completes the instruction.
- `F_control(τ_r_i)`: Specifies control costs (e.g., minimizing effort or time).
This is subject to dynamics and kinematics constraints `C(T_i)` of both the robot and environment.

**B. Grounding Language Instruction via VoxPoser (The Core Innovation):**
The critical observation is that many manipulation tasks can be characterized by a 3D voxel value map (`V`) in the robot's observation space, which guides an "entity of interest" (e.g., robot end-effector, an object, or part of an object). `F_task` is approximated by accumulating values as the entity traverses `V`.

The ingenious part is *how* `V` is generated:
1. **LLM as Code Generator:** An LLM (specifically GPT-4) is prompted to generate Python code. This code, presented with the language instruction as a comment, orchestrates the entire grounding process.
2. **VLM for Perception:** The LLM-generated code makes calls to perception APIs (invoking Vision-Language Models like OWL-ViT for detection, Segment Anything for masking, and XMEM for tracking). These APIs provide spatial-geometric information (e.g., center position, occupancy grid, normal vectors) of relevant objects or parts identified by the LLM.
3. **NumPy for Value Map Composition:** The LLM's code then uses NumPy operations to manipulate 3D arrays, effectively "prescribing precise values at relevant locations" in the observation space. For instance, the region around a drawer handle might be assigned high values (affordance), while the vicinity of a vase might be assigned low values (avoidance).
4. **Types of Value Maps:** Beyond just positional cost, VoxPoser extends this to compose:
 - **Affordance Maps:** High values attract the entity (e.g., target object).
 - **Avoidance Maps:** Low values repel the entity (e.g., obstacles).
 - **End-effector Velocity Maps:** Specify target velocities.
 - **End-effector Rotation Maps:** Specify target orientations (e.g., gripper normal to a surface).
 - **Gripper Action Maps:** Specify open/close actions.
 These maps are then smoothed to encourage smoother trajectories.
5. **Hierarchical LLM Orchestration:** High-level LLMs (e.g., a "planner" LMP) decompose user instructions into sub-tasks. A "composer" LMP then takes a sub-task and invokes specific value map LMPs with detailed language parameters to generate the required 3D maps.

**C. Zero-Shot Trajectory Synthesis with VoxPoser:**
Once the 3D value maps (which constitute `F_task`) are obtained, a motion planner synthesizes the robot trajectory.
- **Planning Approach:** A simple zeroth-order optimization method is used, involving randomly sampling trajectories and scoring them against the objective functions derived from the value maps.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** The planning is implemented within an MPC framework, where the trajectory is iteratively replanned at 5 Hz using the current observation. This closed-loop execution provides robustness against dynamic disturbances and perception errors.
- **Heuristic Dynamics:** For most tasks, a simple heuristic-based dynamics model (or static scene assumption) is sufficient due to the dense rewards from value maps and frequent replanning. For tasks involving object manipulation, a simple planar pushing model is used.

**D. Efficient Dynamics Learning with Online Experiences (Optional Extension):**
For contact-rich tasks where simple heuristics are insufficient, VoxPoser can still be leveraged to improve learning efficiency:
- **Zero-Shot Trajectory as Prior:** VoxPoser's initial zero-shot synthesized trajectory (`τ_r_0`) serves as a powerful prior to bias the action sampling distribution for data collection.
- **Local Exploration:** Instead of exploring the full action space, actions are sampled in the vicinity of `τ_r_0` by adding small noise, encouraging focused and efficient exploration.
- **Dynamics Model Learning:** An MLP dynamics model is learned by collecting `(o_t, a_t, o_{t+1})` transitions and minimizing L2 loss, significantly speeding up the learning process compared to unguided exploration.

This methodology represents a paradigm shift, enabling LLMs to directly influence physical robot actions at a continuous, spatial level by generating dynamic, high-dimensional objectives, rather than being confined to discrete skill selection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for VoxPoser's efficacy and generalization capabilities across both simulated and real-world environments.

**A. VoxPoser for Everyday Manipulation Tasks (Real-World Evaluation):**
- **High Success Rate:** VoxPoser achieved a high average success rate of 88.0% for 5 representative everyday manipulation tasks in static real-world environments (e.g., "Take out bread from toaster," "Press down moisturizer pump," "Turn on lamp," "Open bottle," "Sweep trash into dustpan").
- **Robustness to Disturbances:** Even under dynamic disturbances (random forces, object displacement, reverting task progress), VoxPoser maintained a commendable 70.0% success rate, largely due to its fast, closed-loop replanning capabilities (5 Hz MPC).
- **Superior to Primitives-based Baseline:** A crucial comparison was made against a variant of "Code as Policies" that uses LLMs to parameterize pre-defined primitives. VoxPoser significantly outperformed this baseline, which achieved only 24.0% success in static environments and 0.0% with disturbances. This highlights VoxPoser's key advantage: the ability of LLMs to *spatially compose* objectives via value maps is more flexible and robust than merely chaining sequential, pre-defined skills.

**B. Generalization to Unseen Instructions and Attributes (Simulated Evaluation):**
- **Rigorous Generalization Study:** Conducted in a simulated block-world with 13 highly-randomizable tasks and 2766 unique instructions, allowing for robust evaluation of generalization to unseen instructions and attributes.
- **Outperformance Across Scenarios:** VoxPoser demonstrated superior generalization compared to two baselines:
 - **U-Net + MP:** A learning-based approach where a U-Net synthesizes 2D costmaps. This struggled significantly with generalization (e.g., 0.0% for unseen attributes, unseen instructions).
 - **LLM + Primitives:** Similar to the real-world baseline.
- **Strong Zero-Shot Generalization:** VoxPoser consistently achieved higher success rates across all categories (seen/unseen instructions, seen/unseen attributes). For instance, in "Object Interactions," VoxPoser achieved 64.0% (SI SA), 60.0% (SI UA), 65.0% (UI UA), showing its ability to maintain performance even when both instructions and object attributes are entirely unseen. This directly supports the claim of zero-shot generalization to open-set instructions and objects.
- **Benefit of Value Map Composition:** The results confirm that grounding LLM knowledge in robot perception through "value map composition" (VoxPoser) offers greater flexibility and generalization than directly specifying primitive parameters.

**C. Efficient Dynamics Learning with Online Experiences:**
- **Addressing Contact-Rich Tasks:** For complex, contact-rich tasks (e.g., opening doors, windows, fridges) in simulation, where zero-shot performance is inherently challenging (6.7% - 18.3% success), VoxPoser's approach offered a significant advantage.
- **Effective Exploration Prior:** Using VoxPoser's zero-shot synthesized trajectories as a prior for exploration dramatically sped up the learning process for an MLP dynamics model.
- **Rapid Learning:** With less than 3 minutes of online interaction, the system achieved high success rates (80.0% - 91.7%). In stark contrast, attempting to learn these dynamics without this intelligent prior ("No Prior" baseline) consistently exceeded the 12-hour time limit, underscoring the efficiency gain.

**D. Error Breakdown:**
- **Lowest Specification Error:** Analysis revealed that VoxPoser significantly reduced "specification error" (errors in defining the task objective for the planner) compared to baselines. This confirms the LLM's capability to accurately infer and ground affordances and constraints.
- **Remaining Challenges:** The primary sources of remaining errors were identified as "perception error" (e.g., VLM limitations in detecting fine-grained object parts or sensitivity to initial object poses) and "dynamics error" (limitations of the simple dynamics models or the need for learned models in contact-rich tasks). This points to future avenues for improvement.

In summary, VoxPoser demonstrates state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization for robot manipulation by enabling LLMs to generate spatial value maps, significantly outperforming primitive-based approaches and learning-based cost predictors. It also provides a robust method for accelerating learning in more complex, contact-rich scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VoxPoser represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, with the potential for broad impact:

1. **Breaking the Primitive Bottleneck:** The most significant contribution is the paradigm shift from reliance on pre-defined motion primitives to dynamically composed 3D value maps. This fundamentally addresses a major bottleneck in robotic skill acquisition, as it eliminates the need for laborious, task-specific data collection or manual engineering for every new manipulation skill. By allowing LLMs to directly influence the spatial objectives for motion planning, VoxPoser unlocks a vastly more flexible and generalizable approach to robot control.

2. **True Zero-Shot Generalization:** The demonstrated ability to perform a wide variety of everyday manipulation tasks with "open-set instructions" and "open-set objects" in a zero-shot manner is a crucial step towards truly versatile and intelligent robots. This means a robot can be given novel, free-form commands for unfamiliar objects and still attempt the task without prior training on that specific combination, greatly expanding the operational scope of robotic systems.

3. **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to directly interpret and act upon natural language instructions, VoxPoser facilitates more intuitive and natural human-robot collaboration. Users can describe tasks in common parlance without needing to know specific robot commands or programming interfaces. The emergent capabilities, such as handling fine-grained language corrections or behavioral commonsense, further enhance this interaction.

4. **Leveraging Foundation Models for Embodied AI:** The work effectively harnesses the vast, pre-trained knowledge embedded within large language models (for reasoning and code generation) and vision-language models (for open-vocabulary visual grounding). This demonstrates a powerful pipeline for translating the abstract capabilities of these foundation models into concrete physical actions in the real world, paving the way for more capable embodied agents.

5. **Accelerating Learning for Complex Tasks:** While excelling at zero-shot generalization for many tasks, VoxPoser also provides a powerful mechanism to accelerate learning for contact-rich or physics-intensive tasks where zero-shot execution alone might be insufficient. Using the zero-shot trajectories as intelligent priors for exploration significantly reduces the amount of online interaction time required to learn effective dynamics models, making complex skill acquisition much more efficient.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Wider Deployment of Robots:** By reducing the programming and data collection burden, VoxPoser could accelerate the deployment of robots in diverse, unstructured environments, such as homes, small businesses, and flexible manufacturing.
- **Democratization of Robotics:** Making robots easier to program and instruct through natural language could lower the barrier to entry for non-expert users, potentially democratizing access to robotic automation.
- **Foundation for Future AI-Robot Systems:** This approach lays a strong foundation for future research in embodied AI, particularly in areas like multi-modal reasoning, real-time adaptation, and lifelong learning for robots. The identified limitations (e.g., reliance on external perception, general-purpose dynamics models) also provide clear roadmaps for future research.

In essence, VoxPoser pushes the boundaries of robot autonomy by empowering language models to directly "imagine" and specify the spatial objectives for robot actions, marking a significant stride towards truly intelligent and adaptable robotic manipulation.
