# Physics-Aware Sparse Learning and Selective Online Adaptation for Euler-Lagrange Robot Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for Euler-Lagrange robot dynamics integrates physics-aware sparse learning and selective online adaptation to address unmodeled effects and time-varying conditions while preserving mechanical structure. This approach consistently yielded the lowest prediction error and superior trajectory tracking performance across five diverse robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Nominal first-principles Euler-Lagrange models become inaccurate due to unmodeled effects (e.g., payload variations, friction) and nonstationary operating conditions.
- Many learning-based dynamics correction methods disregard the fundamental mechanical structure of Euler-Lagrange systems, leading to physically inconsistent predictions (e.g., violating inertia matrix symmetry or positive-definiteness).
- Existing online adaptation techniques often incur high computational costs, unnecessarily modify mechanically structured components, or lack robustness for efficiently coping with time-evolving model mismatches.

## Method

- A structure-preserving ==residual decomposition== is introduced, explicitly correcting inertia to maintain symmetric positive-definiteness, inducing consistent Coriolis terms, and modeling generalized-force residuals.
- A sparse, history-dependent latent representation is utilized via a temporal encoder and soft-thresholding to capture a limited number of active interaction patterns for compact residual modeling.
- A selective Bayesian online adaptation mechanism is developed that updates only the disturbance-sensitive generalized-force component within its active latent subspace, keeping mechanically structured components fixed.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently achieved the lowest normalized prediction error across all five robotic platforms (differential-drive robot, 6-DoF manipulator, quadrotor, mobile manipulator, aerial manipulator) compared to Sparse SysID, PI-TCN, and DMRAC.
- It demonstrated superior trajectory tracking performance, yielding the best normalized RMSE on figure-eight trajectories across all platforms, outperforming nominal models and various learning-based baselines including DNN, Diffusion models, Meta-learning, and Active MLPs.
- Ablation studies confirmed that moderate sparsity improves prediction accuracy and sample efficiency, while the selective online adaptation facilitates faster error reduction and smoother convergence after disturbances like payload release, by dynamically activating relevant latent modes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decomposing and modeling residual dynamics to preserve the intrinsic Euler-Lagrange mechanical structure leads to physically consistent predictions and enhanced reliability in model-based control.
> - Employing sparsity in latent representations significantly improves data efficiency and computational tractability, enabling compact modeling and efficient online adaptation by focusing on relevant interaction patterns.
> - Selectively adapting only the generalized-force residual component, which is most susceptible to external disturbances, using uncertainty-aware Bayesian updates ensures stable and effective online performance without compromising learned mechanical structures.
