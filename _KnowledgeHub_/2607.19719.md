# Koopman Dreamer: Spectrally Constrained Latent Dynamics for Stable World-Model Imagination

> [!summary] Summary
> Koopman Dreamer integrates a spectrally constrained Koopman backbone into the deterministic latent dynamics of Dreamer-style world models, explicitly controlling modal properties for stable long-horizon imagination. This approach yields superior continuous control performance and significantly reduces prediction errors across diverse tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Dreamer-style world models use generic neural network transitions, offering limited explicit control over the dynamic properties of latent state propagation.
- Accumulated errors in long-horizon imagined trajectories bias policy updates due to implicit learning of dynamic behaviors.
- A discrepancy between observation-corrected training and posterior-free imagination exacerbates prediction errors during uncorrected rollouts.

## Method

- Replaced the generic ==RSSM== update with a ==spectrally constrained Koopman transition==, parameterizing autonomous dynamics using 2D rotation-scaling blocks with bounded modal radii.
- Integrated linear action terms, ==low-rank bilinear state-action interaction== terms, and stochastic-state modulation to adapt to controlled nonlinear dynamics.
- Designed Koopman-specific supervision objectives, including ==posterior-conditioned EMA teacher targets==, multi-step rollouts, and open-loop observation prediction, to reduce the train-imagination gap.

## Results

- Achieved the highest final score on **six of nine** DeepMind Control Suite tasks, surpassing DreamerV3's performance on **eight** tasks.
- Reduced mean deterministic-latent Mean Squared Error (MSE) by **89.4%** and mean proprioceptive observation MSE by **23.2%** in open-loop prediction over 64 steps.
- Improved the UAV-LiDAR autonomous navigation task completion rate to **73.8%** (compared to **53.8%** for DreamerV3), demonstrating more robust closed-loop control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly structuring latent dynamics with spectrally constrained operators allows direct control over modal amplification, decay, and oscillation, crucial for managing multi-step error growth.
> - A derived multi-step rollout error bound quantifies how spectral properties and other error sources contribute to error propagation, guiding the balance between error attenuation and information retention.
> - The bilinear state-action interaction term is critical for capturing state-dependent control effects, significantly improving nonlinear task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), specifically focusing on latent world models for continuous control. MBRL aims to improve sample efficiency by learning a model of environment dynamics and using this model to generate imagined trajectories for policy optimization. Latent world models, such as PlaNet and Dreamer, achieve this by compressing high-dimensional observations into compact hidden states and performing prediction and control in this lower-dimensional latent space.

A recurring challenge in existing latent world models, particularly those in the Dreamer family that employ Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSMs), is the reliable propagation of latent states over long horizons. Current deterministic latent transitions, typically parameterized by generic recurrent neural networks, offer limited explicit control over the dynamic properties of the latent space, such as modal persistence, contraction, and oscillation. Consequently, these properties emerge implicitly from data and optimization, making their long-horizon behavior difficult to regulate and diagnose. Accumulated errors in imagined trajectories can bias policy updates and compromise control performance, especially in tasks requiring extensive future planning.

The paper integrates concepts from Koopman operator theory to address these limitations. Koopman theory posits that nonlinear systems can be described by a linear operator in an appropriate observable-function space. This theoretical framework provides a means to structure learned latent dynamics around an explicit evolution operator, where spectral quantities directly control contraction, persistence, and oscillatory behavior. This offers a mechanism to analyze and regulate multi-step error growth. While previous research has explored Koopman representations for system identification, prediction, and control, and even integrated them into reinforcement learning for tasks like Q-learning, task-oriented control, and visual state representations, this work focuses on explicitly structuring the deterministic dynamics core *within a stochastic Dreamer-style latent world model*. The objective is to enhance the stability of long-horizon recursive imagination by embedding a spectrally constrained Koopman backbone, differentiating it from prior Koopman applications that may not directly target the recursive imagination stability in a Dreamer-like stochastic latent space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the stability and accuracy of long-horizon latent imagination in Dreamer-style world models, thereby enabling more robust and effective closed-loop continuous control.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current latent world models:
- **Limited Control over Dynamics**: Existing Dreamer-style models, while effective, use generic neural network transitions for deterministic latent state propagation. These networks do not inherently expose or parameterize modal properties like damping, persistence, or oscillation. As a result, critical dynamic behaviors are learned implicitly, making their long-horizon predictions susceptible to error accumulation and difficult to diagnose.
- **Error Accumulation**: In continuous control, policies are often optimized over imagined latent trajectories. Small, one-step errors in the latent transition, reward prediction, or continuation prediction can compound over long rollouts, leading to biased return targets and suboptimal policy updates. This limits the reliability of model-based planning for tasks requiring foresight.
- **Mismatch between Training and Imagination**: During training, stochastic states are corrected by observations (posterior-conditioned), while during imagination, they are generated purely from the prior without future observations (posterior-free). This discrepancy can exacerbate prediction errors in uncorrected rollouts.
- **Leveraging Intrinsic Dynamics**: Many continuous control tasks (e.g., locomotion, pendulum control, robotic reaching) exhibit structured dynamics that include smooth responses, damping, and near-periodic motion. Current generic neural transitions do not explicitly leverage or represent these patterns, potentially missing opportunities for more stable and interpretable latent dynamics.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of Koopman Dreamer are:
- **Structural Integration**: To incorporate a spectrally constrained, Koopman-inspired backbone into the deterministic latent dynamics core of a Dreamer-style world model. This involves parameterizing the autonomous dynamics using two-dimensional rotation-scaling blocks with bounded modal radii and learnable phases, allowing explicit control over damping, persistence, and oscillation.
- **Control and Modulation**: To adapt this spectral backbone to controlled nonlinear dynamics by integrating linear action terms, low-rank bilinear state-action interaction terms, and stochastic-state modulation. These components aim to capture global control effects, state-dependent action influences, and local observation-conditioned corrections, respectively.
- **Robust Training Framework**: To design a set of Koopman-specific supervision objectives. These include posterior-conditioned EMA teacher targets, one-step Koopman consistency, multi-step teacher and prior rollouts, and open-loop observation prediction. The goal is to reduce the mismatch between posterior-conditioned training and prior-only imagination, ensuring the structured transition functions effectively in both scenarios.
- **Theoretical Understanding**: To derive a multi-step rollout error bound that clarifies how the spectral properties of the Koopman backbone, bilinear interactions, stochastic-state mismatch, and modeling residuals contribute to error propagation. This analysis aims to illuminate the trade-off between error attenuation and the retention of long-term information.
- **Empirical Validation**: To evaluate Koopman Dreamer on a range of continuous control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite and a UAV-LiDAR autonomous navigation scenario, demonstrating that the spectrally structured latent dynamics lead to improved long-horizon prediction stability and enhanced closed-loop control performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Koopman Dreamer modifies the DreamerV3 world model by replacing its generic deterministic RSSM update with a spectrally constrained Koopman transition. The model retains the observation encoder, stochastic-state prior and posterior networks, prediction heads for observations, rewards, and continuation flags, and latent-space actor and value networks.

**Latent State Representation**: The latent state is represented as `y_t = (ϕ_t, s_t)`, where `ϕ_t ∈ R^D` is the deterministic Koopman evolution state and `s_t` is the stochastic state. `ϕ_t` maintains historical memory, while `s_t` provides local observation-conditioned correction and captures unmodeled factors. `s_t` consists of `N` groups of `K`-way one-hot discrete variables, smoothed with unimix to prevent collapse.

**Koopman Latent Transition (`F_K`)**: The core of Koopman Dreamer is the structured deterministic transition. The next deterministic state `ϕ_{t+1}` is computed by:
`ϕ_{t+1} = clip_{c_ϕ}(A_K ϕ_t + B_a ā_t + H_θ(ϕ_t, ā_t) + B_z z_t)`
where `clip_{c_ϕ}(x) = c_ϕ tanh(x/c_ϕ)` is an element-wise clipping function that bounds the magnitude of the deterministic state. The transition consists of four additive components:
1. **Autonomous Koopman Operator (`A_K`)**: This is the primary structural component. `A_K` is parameterized by `M` two-dimensional rotation-scaling blocks, where the deterministic dimension `D` is `2M`. Each block `i` transforms `(ϕ_{t,2i}, ϕ_{t,2i+1})` via:
 `[ (A_K ϕ_t)_{2i}, (A_K ϕ_t)_{2i+1} ]^T = ρ_i * R(θ_i) * [ϕ_{t,2i}, ϕ_{t,2i+1}]^T`
 where `R(θ_i)` is a 2D rotation matrix. The modal radius `ρ_i` is bounded within `[ρ_min, ρ_max]` (parameterized as `ρ_min + (ρ_max - ρ_min)σ(α_i)`), and the phase `θ_i` is `π tanh(ω_{K,i})`. This block-diagonal structure ensures that `||A_K||_2 = ρ(A_K) = max_i ρ_i ≤ ρ_max`, allowing explicit control over autonomous modal amplification, decay, and oscillation.
2. **Linear Action Term (`B_a ā_t`)**: `B_a` is a learnable linear map that projects the rescaled action `ā_t` into the latent space, representing a global control effect.
3. **Bilinear State-Action Interaction Term (`H_θ(ϕ_t, ā_t)`)**: This term introduces state-dependent action effects. It has a low-rank form: `β_b W_{b_o} ((W_{b_ϕ} ϕ_t) ⊙ (W_{b_a} ā_t))`. `W_{b_ϕ}` and `W_{b_a}` project the state and action into a shared low-rank interaction space, and `W_{b_o}` maps back to the Koopman state dimension. `β_b` controls its magnitude.
4. **Stochastic Modulation Term (`B_z z_t`)**: `z_t = g_z(s_t)` is a linear projection of the flattened stochastic state `s_t`. This term injects local posterior correction information and uncertainty into the deterministic propagation.

**Stochastic-State Prior and Posterior Networks**:
- **Prior**: `p_θ(s_t | ϕ_t)` generates `s_t` based solely on the deterministic state, used for observation-free imagination.
- **Posterior**: `q_θ(s_t | ϕ_t, e_t)` incorporates the current observation encoding `e_t = E_θ(o_t)` for observation-corrected training. Both use grouped categorical variables with unimix smoothing.

**Posterior-Conditioned Teacher Representation**: To provide stable and observation-corrected targets for the Koopman transition, a teacher representation is constructed:
`ϕ̄_t = clip_{c_ϕ}(ϕ_t + β_ϕ tanh(W_ϕ flat(s_t)))` and `z̄_t = W_z flat(s_t)`
where `W_ϕ` and `W_z` are teacher projection matrices, and `β_ϕ` bounds the posterior offset. These teacher parameters are updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) to reduce target drift, providing `sg(ϕ̄_{tar,t})` and `sg(z̄_{tar,t})` (stop-gradient targets).

**World-Model Training Objectives (`L_wm`)**: The overall objective is a combination of standard DreamerV3 losses and Koopman-specific supervision terms, linearly warmed up over training steps.
- **Base Losses (`L_base`)**: Include reconstruction losses for observations, rewards, and continuation flags, plus KL divergence terms (`max(KL(sg(q)||p), λ_free)` for dynamics and `max(KL(q||sg(p)), λ_free)` for representation) to align prior and posterior.
- **One-step Koopman Supervision (`L_koop`)**: Penalizes the squared L2 norm between the predicted `ϕ_{t+1}` from the Koopman transition using EMA teacher inputs and the EMA teacher target `ϕ̄_{tar,t+1}`. This focuses on local consistency.
- **Multi-step Teacher Rollout Supervision (`L_{tea_roll}`)**: Penalizes the L2 norm between `H`-step rollouts initiated from an EMA teacher state (`ϕ̂_t`) and subsequent EMA teacher targets (`ϕ̄_{tar,t+k}`). This directly supervises recursive accumulation of errors.
- **Posterior-free Rollout Supervision (`L_{free_roll}`)**: Similar to teacher rollout but uses `H`-step rollouts generated with stochastic states sampled from the prior (`t̃_{t+k}`), aligning prior rollouts with teacher targets to reduce the train-imagination gap.
- **Open-loop Observation Prediction (`L_pred`)**: Decodes observations (`D_m(t̃_{t+k})`) from prior rollouts and penalizes their reconstruction error against actual observations (`o_{m,t+k}`). This ensures the imagined latent states remain decodable and task-relevant.
- **Operator Regularization (`L_{opreg}`)**: L2 regularization on `B_a`, `B_z`, and the difference between teacher and online `ϕ` to limit their magnitudes.

**Latent Imagination and Policy Optimization**: Policies are optimized using an actor-critic framework in the latent space. Starting from posterior-sampled states, future trajectories are imagined by iteratively sampling actions from the policy and propagating latent states using the prior transition. Reward and continuation heads provide values for calculating `λ`-returns, which serve as targets for value and policy updates. The value network is trained with continuation-weighted two-hot cross-entropy and slow-value regularization. The actor uses continuation-weighted policy gradients based on normalized advantages, with gradients typically not flowing back to the world model.

**Theoretical Analysis**: The paper presents a controlled rollout error bound (`||d_ϕ_{t+H}|| ≤ κ_⋆^H ||d_ϕ_t|| + Σ_{i=0}^{H-1} κ_⋆^{H-1-i} δ_{t+i}`).
- `d_ϕ_t` represents the error in the deterministic state.
- `κ_⋆ = ρ(A_K) + L_{b_ϕ}` combines the spectral radius of the autonomous Koopman operator (`ρ(A_K)`) and the Lipschitz constant of the bilinear interaction term (`L_{b_ϕ}`).
- `δ_t` represents additive error sources, including stochastic modulation mismatch and modeling residuals.
This bound illustrates that `κ_⋆ < 1` leads to bounded error accumulation, `κ_⋆ = 1` to linear growth, and `κ_⋆ > 1` to geometric growth. This highlights the trade-off between attenuating errors and retaining long-term information, which the spectral parameterization directly regulates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation was conducted on nine proprioceptive continuous-control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) and a simulated UAV-LiDAR autonomous navigation task.

**A. DMC Proprioceptive Continuous Control**
- **Control Performance**: Koopman Dreamer achieved the highest final score on six of the nine DMC tasks and exceeded DreamerV3's performance on eight tasks. Notably, it showed considerable gains on tasks such as Acrobot and Hopper Stand. The method also demonstrated improved sample efficiency, reaching 90% of the task-best final return faster than other baselines on eight tasks. This suggests that the spectrally structured dynamics aid in more rapid and effective policy learning.
- **Open-loop Prediction**: When initialized from a 32-step observation context and rolled out for 64 steps without future observations, Koopman Dreamer exhibited substantially lower prediction errors. It reduced the mean deterministic-latent Mean Squared Error (MSE) by 89.4% across tasks and showed lower latent error on all nine tasks compared to DreamerV3. This improvement translated to observable metrics, with a 23.2% reduction in mean proprioceptive observation MSE (lower on 8/9 tasks) and an improvement in mean reward prediction MSE (from 0.9290 to 0.7318, lower on 8/9 tasks). These results indicate that the structured latent dynamics contribute to more accurate and stable long-horizon model predictions.

**B. UAV-LiDAR Autonomous Navigation**
- **Closed-loop Navigation**: In the UAV-LiDAR Forest scenario, Koopman Dreamer demonstrated improved task completion rates. It achieved a success rate of 73.8%, which was higher than DreamerV3 (53.8%) and D4PG (15.8%), and a lower overall failure rate (26.2%). The median final distance to the target was also lower for Koopman Dreamer (0.72), and it attained a higher median episode return (195.50 vs 182.19 for DreamerV3). Qualitative observations of trajectories suggested more direct and less circuitous paths to targets.
- **Open-loop Prediction**: Similar to DMC, Koopman Dreamer showed stronger open-loop prediction performance in the UAV-LiDAR environment. At a horizon of 64 steps, it reduced MSE for vector observations, LiDAR data, velocity, and deterministic-latent states compared to DreamerV3. Specifically, velocity, reward, and deterministic-state drift experienced the most significant and persistent reductions. This correlation between improved open-loop prediction accuracy and higher task completion rates supports the role of the structured transition in generating more reliable imagined trajectories for planning.

**C. Spectral Radius Sensitivity**
An analysis varying the maximum spectral radius (`ρ_max`) revealed a trade-off between error attenuation and the retention of task-relevant information. The smallest learned spectral radius (0.849) yielded the lowest latent MSE but not necessarily the lowest observable errors, suggesting that excessive contraction could lead to information loss even if latent states become numerically similar. Optimal observable prediction occurred at intermediate learned radii (e.g., 0.881 for vector, LiDAR, and velocity MSE; 0.943 for goal-relative and reward MSE). As the learned radius approached or exceeded the unit circle (e.g., 1.026), prediction errors, particularly in the deterministic latent state, increased substantially, confirming that unchecked amplification leads to unstable rollouts. This analysis supports the idea that the spectral constraint provides a tunable mechanism to balance these factors.

**D. Structural Ablation**
Ablation studies on the UAV-LiDAR task provided insights into the contributions of individual components:
- **"w/o spectral" (removing spectral constraint)**: This variant showed a comparable success rate but a 20.4% reduction in mean return and a substantial increase in median action variation on successful episodes. Open-loop prediction errors (for vector, LiDAR, velocity, and latent states) significantly increased. This indicates the spectral constraint primarily reduces long-horizon drift and enables smoother, less compensatory control.
- **"w/o teacher" (removing posterior-conditioned EMA teacher)**: This ablation resulted in reduced mean return, a lower success rate (68.8%), and an increased collision rate (from 15.0% to 28.7%). While average observation error remained similar to the full model, reward error was higher. This suggests that stable, slowly updated posterior-conditioned targets are crucial for robust closed-loop decision-making.
- **"w/o bilinear" (removing bilinear control term)**: This was the most detrimental ablation, leading to a drastic drop in success rate (8.8%) and a significant rise in collision rate (56.2%). Open-loop prediction errors for goal-relative position and reward increased considerably. This highlights the importance of the bilinear term in capturing state-dependent action effects necessary for effective nonlinear navigation.

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the Koopman Dreamer's structured dynamics consistently improve long-horizon latent propagation and lead to stronger closed-loop control performance across diverse continuous control tasks. The theoretical error bound and experimental analyses confirm the importance of the spectral backbone, supplementary training objectives, and specialized action terms in managing error accumulation and preserving task-relevant information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Koopman Dreamer research provides several contributions and potential impacts on the field of model-based reinforcement learning and continuous control:

- **Enhanced Stability and Reliability of World Models**: By introducing a spectrally constrained Koopman backbone, the work directly addresses the challenge of error accumulation and instability in long-horizon latent imaginations. This explicit control over modal properties (damping, persistence, oscillation) offers a more robust foundation for learned dynamics models, which is critical for planning and policy optimization in complex environments.
- **Improved Control Performance**: The experimental results on DMC tasks and UAV-LiDAR navigation demonstrate that the improved stability of latent imaginations translates into stronger closed-loop control performance and enhanced sample efficiency. This suggests that more accurate and reliable internal models can lead to more effective agents.
- **Theoretical Guidance for Dynamics Design**: The derived multi-step rollout-error bound provides a theoretical framework for understanding the trade-off between error attenuation and information retention in recursive latent dynamics. This analysis can guide the design of future world models and the selection of hyperparameters, such as the spectral radius, beyond purely empirical tuning.
- **Increased Diagnosability and Interpretability**: Explicitly structuring the latent dynamics around interpretable spectral properties offers a level of diagnosticity that is often lacking in generic neural network models. Understanding how latent modes behave can aid practitioners in debugging and improving model performance.
- **Practical Applicability for Continuous Control**: The approach is intended for continuous-control applications that rely on recursive multi-step prediction, including robotics and autonomous navigation. The note to practitioners emphasizes that the method provides a tunable mechanism for balancing stability and expressiveness, suggesting its relevance for real-world engineering challenges where reliable long-term predictions are crucial. The empirical success on UAV-LiDAR navigation, a complex task involving dense obstacles and goal-seeking, supports this potential.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research, such as developing adaptive spectral ranges that can dynamically adjust to different task phases or complexity, exploring broader evaluations across a wider range of environments and training runs, and integrating Koopman Dreamer with safety-aware or risk-sensitive policy objectives for enhanced reliability in safety-critical applications. This could lead to more trustworthy autonomous systems.

Overall, Koopman Dreamer offers a structured and controllable dynamics foundation that can improve the performance and reliability of latent world models, particularly for tasks demanding accurate long-horizon imagination.
