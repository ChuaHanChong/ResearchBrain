# Reinforcement Learning Meets Masked Generative Models: Mask-GRPO for Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Mask-GRPO integrates Group Relative Policy Optimization into masked generative models for text-to-image generation, introducing specific transition probability definitions and training enhancements to achieve a GenEval score of 0.73 with a 1.3B parameter model and an FID of 8.32 on MSCOCO-30K.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) had been applied to diffusion and autoregressive text-to-image models, but masked generative models (MGMs) remained largely unexplored in this context.
- Adapting RL to MGMs was challenged by the lack of a suitable definition for transition probability within their parallel unmasking mechanism.
- RL training for large generative models suffered from instability, high computational costs, and issues like "vanishing samples" (low reward variance).

## Method

- The iterative ==unmasking== process of MGMs was reframed as a multi-step Markov Decision Process to define states, actions, and rewards for RL.
- New definitions for transition probability (p_θ1 and p_θ2) were proposed, specifically accounting for newly unmasked and remasked tokens in MGMs.
- Training stability and efficiency were enhanced through strategies including removing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) constraint, applying computational and unmasking reduction, and implementing dynamic sample filtering.

## Results

- Mask-GRPO improved the base Show-o model's GenEval score from **0.53** to **0.73** (surpassing larger SOTA models zero-shot) and FID on MSCOCO-**30K** from 9.24 to 8.32, indicating enhanced image quality and diversity.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing the KL constraint and implementing sample filtering were key drivers for performance gains, with sample filtering improving GenEval from **0.60** to **0.73**.
- The framework demonstrated robustness across different reward models, successfully improving scores when fine-tuned with either CLIP or ImageReward.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A principled definition of transition probability, rather than naive adaptations, is essential for successfully integrating GRPO-based RL into masked generative models.
> - Removing the KL divergence constraint in GRPO can significantly improve performance for smaller generative models by allowing broader policy exploration.
> - Dynamic sample filtering, which resamples groups with low reward variance, is crucial for mitigating "vanishing samples" and ensuring stable, effective RL training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Text-to-Image (T2I) generation has advanced significantly, with existing models broadly categorized into three types: diffusion models, autoregressive (AR) models, and masked generative models (MGMs). Diffusion models synthesize images by iteratively refining noise, while AR models generate images token by token sequentially. MGMs, which are the focus of this work, operate as a hybrid. They predict masked tokens in parallel at each iteration, selectively unmasking the most confident predictions and remasking the rest for subsequent iterations. This iterative unmasking process can be viewed as a discrete diffusion process through an absorbing `[MASK]` state, blending parallel processing with an autoregressive-like refinement, and is noted for offering a trade-off between sampling quality and speed.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated efficacy in enhancing the capabilities of large language models (LLMs), particularly through methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Inspired by this success, RL techniques have been extended to the visual domain, including T2I generation, with GRPO-based methods being applied to both diffusion and AR models.

However, the application of RL to MGMs remained largely unexplored prior to this work. This paper addresses this gap by proposing the first method to integrate GRPO-based RL into the MGM paradigm for T2I generation. The primary challenge in applying RL to MGMs stems from defining the transition probability, which differs from the natural token-by-token or denoising step probabilities found in AR and diffusion models, respectively.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to integrate reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), into masked generative models (MGMs) to enhance their performance in text-to-image (T2I) generation. This is motivated by several identified challenges and opportunities:

1. **Addressing the Underexplored Paradigm of MGMs in RL**: While RL has shown promise in improving diffusion and autoregressive T2I models, MGMs have largely been overlooked in this context. The authors aim to fill this gap by proposing the first method to apply GRPO-based RL to MGMs, thereby potentially leveraging the computational efficiency and quality trade-offs offered by MGMs.

2. **Redefining Transition Probability for MGMs**: A core challenge in applying RL to MGMs is the absence of a straightforward definition for transition probability, which is fundamental to the Markov Decision Process (MDP) formulation in RL. In AR models, this naturally maps to next-token distributions, and in diffusion models, to reverse denoising distributions. For MGMs, which predict all masked tokens in parallel, a naive AR-style definition (product of all masked token probabilities) was found to yield unsatisfactory performance in initial experiments. This prompted a deeper investigation to identify a more appropriate and principled formulation for the transition probability that aligns with the iterative unmasking mechanism of MGMs. The motivation is to correctly model how the model transitions from one partial image state to the next.

3. **Enhancing RL Training Stability and Efficiency**: Reinforcement learning training, particularly for large generative models, can suffer from instability, computational overhead, and issues related to sample quality. The iterative nature of MGMs, requiring multiple steps to generate an image, introduces significant computational cost when revisiting all steps for GRPO objective computation. Furthermore, "vanishing samples," characterized by low reward variance within a group of samples, can impede learning. Therefore, a key motivation is to develop strategies that address these practical challenges, ensuring stable and efficient RL training for MGMs.

By achieving these objectives, the researchers aim to not only advance the state-of-the-art in T2I generation using MGMs but also to contribute foundational work on adapting RL principles to this specific class of generative models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Mask-GRPO, introduces GRPO-based reinforcement learning into masked generative models for text-to-image generation. The approach involves reformulating the unmasking process as a multi-step decision-making problem, defining appropriate transition probabilities, and incorporating several strategies to enhance training.

#### 3.1. Unmasking as a Multi-Step Markov Decision-Making Problem

The iterative unmasking process of MGMs is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **State (`s_t`)**: Defined as the current latent token sequence (`Y_t`) and the input prompt (`c`).
- **Action (`a_t`)**: The next latent token sequence (`Y_{t+1}`).
- **Initial State (`ρ_0`)**: A completely masked token sequence (`Y_0`).
- **Reward (`R(s_t, a_t)`)**: A reward `r(Y_t, c)` is assigned only at the final generation step (`t = T-1`), typically derived from a perceptual model like CLIP. For intermediate steps, the reward is effectively the same as the final step's reward.
- **Policy (`π_θ`)**: Represents the unmasking strategy at each iteration, mapping the current state and prompt to the next token sequence: `π_θ(Y_{t+1}|Y_t, c)`.

The learning objective for the policy `π_θ` is adapted from the GRPO framework, which aims to maximize the expected return by optimizing a clipped surrogate objective that incorporates a group-level normalized advantage function and a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence constraint against a reference policy (`π_ref`).

#### 3.2. Transition Probability Definitions

A central contribution is the redefinition of transition probability `p_θ(s_{t+1}|s_t, a_t)`, which was identified as problematic with a naive AR-style approach. The key insight is that only "newly unmasked tokens" (those chosen to be revealed at an iteration) are critical for defining the state transition, while "newly remasked tokens" (those predicted but not confident enough to be revealed, and thus remasked) are also considered. Two candidate definitions are proposed:

- **`p_θ1` (Mathematically Grounded)**: This definition considers both newly unmasked tokens and newly remasked tokens. It is calculated as the product of confidence scores for all newly unmasked tokens (`Y_CaM_t`) multiplied by the sum of probabilities for the *remasked* tokens (tokens in `Y_M_t \ Y_CaM_t`) where the sampled tokens have a confidence score lower than the minimum confidence score among the newly unmasked tokens. This formulation accounts for the possibility that different token samplings for remasked tokens could lead to the same next state.

- **`p_θ2` (Empirically Motivated)**: This is a simplification of `p_θ1`, focusing solely on the product of confidence scores for the newly unmasked tokens (`Y_CaM_t`), effectively ignoring the probabilities related to the remasked tokens.

Both definitions are used to compute the likelihood ratio `r_j_t(θ)` within the GRPO objective.

#### 3.3. Enhancing Strategies

To improve the training process of Mask-GRPO, several practical strategies are explored:

- **Removing Kullback–Leibler (KL) Constraint**: The `βD_KL(π_θ || π_ref)` term in the GRPO objective is removed by setting `β = 0`. This strategy is motivated by observations in LLMs where, for smaller models (like the 1.3B Show-o used here), the KL constraint might restrict policy exploration.

- **Reduction Strategy**: This addresses the computational cost associated with multi-iteration image generation in MGMs.
 - **Computational Reduction**: The GRPO objective is computed only over a subset of the total unmasking iterations (e.g., first or last 25 out of 50 steps), rather than all steps.
 - **Unmasking Reduction**: The total number of unmasking iterations during training is reduced (e.g., from T=50 to T=20), while the full schedule is maintained during evaluation for final image quality.

- **Sample Filtering ("Vanishing Samples")**: To counter training instability caused by low-quality samples and near-zero advantage values (due to similar rewards within a group), a dynamic sampling strategy is introduced. If a newly generated group of samples has a reward standard deviation below a predefined dynamic threshold (e.g., the lowest 10th percentile of historical standard deviations), that group is resampled. This aims to ensure sufficient reward variability to drive learning.

#### 3.4. Base Model and Reward Model

- **Base Model**: Show-o, a 1.3 billion parameter Masked Generative Model, serves as the foundation for Mask-GRPO.
- **Reward Model**: CLIP is the primary reward model used to evaluate the quality and alignment of generated images with text prompts. ImageReward is also used in ablation studies to test generality.

#### 3.5. Training Settings

Experiments are conducted on the LAION dataset, using prompts of varying lengths. The Adam optimizer is used with a learning rate of 3e-6 and a group size (G) of 6. The framework's default configuration employs `p_θ1`, includes sample filtering, and removes the KL constraint, while maintaining full iteration steps during both training and evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Mask-GRPO effectively enhances the performance of masked generative models for text-to-image generation.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- **GenEval Benchmark**: Mask-GRPO significantly improved the base model Show-o's GenEval score from 0.53 to 0.73. This score surpassed all other compared state-of-the-art methods, including those with larger parameter counts (e.g., SD3.5-L at 8B parameters, Emu3 at 8.5B parameters), despite Mask-GRPO utilizing a 1.3B parameter base model. The evaluation on GenEval was performed in a zero-shot setting, indicating strong generalization capabilities of the fine-tuned model.
- **MSCOCO-30K FID**: The Frechet Inception Distance (FID) score on the MSCOCO-30K dataset improved from 9.24 for Show-o to 8.32 with Mask-GRPO, indicating enhanced image quality and diversity.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Transition Probability Definitions

Both proposed transition probability definitions, `p_θ1` (mathematically grounded) and `p_θ2` (empirically motivated), were found to be effective in integrating GRPO into MGMs. `p_θ1` demonstrated faster convergence during training.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the impact of the proposed enhancing strategies:
- **Removing KL Constraint**: Removing the KL divergence term (`β=0`) in the GRPO objective led to a substantial performance boost in GenEval, from 0.62 (with KL) to 0.73 (without KL). This supports the hypothesis that the KL constraint may hinder exploration for models of this size (1.3B parameters), although it made the training more sensitive to hyperparameters like the learning rate.
- **Reduction Strategy**:
 - The "Unmasking Reduction Strategy" (reducing total iterations during training) yielded a GenEval score of 0.66, representing a good balance between computational efficiency (reducing requirements by approximately 3-4x) and performance.
 - The "Computational Reduction Strategy" (computing the objective over a subset of iterations) resulted in a GenEval score of 0.56, showing less effectiveness compared to unmasking reduction.
- **Sample Filtering**: Implementing the sample filtering strategy, which discards low-quality samples based on reward standard deviation, improved the GenEval score from 0.60 (without filtering) to 0.73. This finding suggests the critical role of maintaining high data quality and sufficient reward signal variability for stable and effective RL training in T2I generation.
- **Reward Model Robustness**: Mask-GRPO demonstrated robustness to different reward models. When replacing CLIP with ImageReward, the ImageReward score increased from 1.02 (Show-o) to 1.23 (Show-o + ImageReward), confirming the general applicability of the Mask-GRPO framework across different evaluation metrics.

#### 4.4. Further Discussion on CLIP's Relative Correctness

Despite CLIP's known limitations in tasks requiring precise counting or complex attribute binding, Mask-GRPO showed improvements in these areas on GenEval. The authors attribute this to GRPO's mechanism of using group-level normalized advantage, which relies on *relative correctness* within a sample group rather than absolute correctness. Experiments where CLIP scores were computed for images matching varying numerical counts confirmed that CLIP consistently assigned higher scores to images matching the correct count in the prompt, demonstrating its ability to capture relative distinctions relevant for GRPO's optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, Mask-GRPO, marks a notable contribution to the field of generative AI, particularly in text-to-image synthesis.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Methodological Contribution

Mask-GRPO represents the first successful integration of online reinforcement learning (specifically, a GRPO-based approach) into masked generative models (MGMs) for text-to-image generation. This addresses a previously underexplored area within T2I research. A key methodological advancement is the principled redefinition of transition probability for MGMs within an RL framework, moving beyond naive adaptations from autoregressive or diffusion models, which was shown to be critical for performance. The introduction of practical enhancing strategies—removing the KL constraint, implementing reduction strategies for efficiency, and dynamic sample filtering for stability—further contributes to the robustness and applicability of RL in T2I models.

#### 5.2. Performance and Efficiency

The achieved performance, with Mask-GRPO improving the base Show-o model's GenEval score to 0.73 and FID to 8.32, positions it competitively, even surpassing existing state-of-the-art methods that often employ larger models. This suggests that Mask-GRPO can extract more capabilities from existing MGMs, potentially leading to more efficient high-quality T2I generation with fewer computational resources compared to training larger models from scratch or fine-tuning other types of generative models. The strong zero-shot generalization capabilities demonstrated on GenEval underscore the effectiveness of the approach.

#### 5.3. Broader Applicability and Future Directions

The validated robustness of Mask-GRPO across different reward models (CLIP and ImageReward) indicates its general applicability. This research opens several avenues for future work.
- **Scaling**: Applying Mask-GRPO to larger base models could reveal further performance gains and scalability properties.
- **Multi-modal Generation**: The principles developed for T2I could be extended to more complex generative tasks, such as text-to-video generation, which requires maintaining temporal and contextual consistency.
- **Reward Model Development**: The paper highlights that the effectiveness of RL in T2I is still constrained by the quality and scope of available reward models. Future work could focus on developing more robust and specialized reward models for T2I generation or exploring joint training strategies for the generative model and the reward model to mitigate current limitations. Improving reward models would allow RL to drive more nuanced and accurate improvements in image quality and prompt alignment.

In summary, Mask-GRPO establishes a foundational framework for applying reinforcement learning to masked generative models, offering a path to enhance their performance, efficiency, and generalization in text-to-image synthesis, with significant implications for future research in multi-modal generative AI.
