# Long-Term Memory for VLA-based Agents in Open-World Task Execution

> [!summary] Summary
> ChemBot is a hierarchical lab-automation framework pairing an AI agent (Scene Describer + incremental Subtask Generator with reflection, backed by dual-layer memory) with Skill-VLA, a GR00T-based policy augmented with a progress-prediction head and future-state asynchronous inference; it outperforms VLA baselines on long-horizon chemical experimentation success rate.

## Problem

- Existing embodied planning frameworks (RoboMatrix, RoboChemist) decouple planning from execution, failing to sustain rigorous state-dependent closed-loop reasoning across chemical protocols.
- Prior VLA and agent systems operate in isolation with no mechanism to consolidate successful execution patterns into persistent, retrievable long-term memory across experiments.
- Standard VLA policies are memoryless and rely on fixed time-step or full-trajectory execution, causing distribution shift, compounding errors, and repetitive actions over long horizons.

## Method

- ==Multi-Agent Incremental Task Planning==: a Scene Describer builds a structured Dashboard via ==Set-of-Mark prompting== and tool calls, feeding an iterative ==Subtask Reasoner-Reflector== loop that generates and validates one atomic subtask at a time.
- ==Dual-Layer Memory Mechanism==: a short-term Dashboard compresses scene/tool/task-state context with immediate overwrite, while episodic long-term memory persists cross-session traces for semantic retrieval and preference adaptation.
- **Skill-VLA** with Progress Head: a ==cross-attention== module fused with GR00T's ==flow-matching== action head predicts a continuous task-progress scalar, triggering autonomous subtask termination without fixed-step or manual switching. Together these form **ChemBot**.

## Results

- On chemical task decomposition, the full ChemBot pipeline scores **0.766** normalized edit distance and **0.324** ROUGE-L with Qwen3-VL-Flash, beating ablations lacking Scene Describer (**0.915**), Subtask Chain (**1.195**), or Reflector (**0.881**).
- Across three real robot tasks (**11**-step Precipitation, **7**-step Heat-and-Dissolution, **6**-step Neutralization) on a UR3 arm, Skill-VLA achieves higher success rate than full-trajectory pi-0.5 and GR00T baselines evaluated within a **120s** window.
- The Chemical Manipulation Dataset comprises **5,459** expert trajectories (**872.2** frames average) and **51,580** atomic action segments across **12** procedure categories, used to train and validate Skill-VLA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling long-horizon reasoning into agent-level planning plus VLA-level progress estimation avoids the compounding drift that memoryless, fixed-step full-trajectory policies suffer.
> - Auxiliary progress-prediction heads trained jointly with flow-matching action heads let a policy self-signal subtask completion, removing dependence on brittle fixed time-step switching.
> - Asynchronous inference anchored to a predicted future robot state, rather than the current state, preserves trajectory smoothness under real inference latency without pausing control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) is increasingly relevant to scientific discovery, particularly in automating hazardous and repetitive laboratory tasks. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a significant development, leveraging the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to translate linguistic instructions into physical robot actions. This work operates within the context of advancing VLA models for complex, long-horizon tasks, specifically in chemical laboratory automation.

Previous research in embodied AI has seen LLMs employed for high-level planning in robotic tasks, with systems like RoboMatrix and RoboChemist demonstrating hierarchical planning and visual-semantic control. However, these frameworks often decouple planning from execution, which presents challenges in maintaining rigorous, state-dependent closed-loop reasoning essential for multi-stage chemical protocols. Furthermore, a common limitation is the absence of mechanisms for consolidating successful execution patterns into persistent, retrievable long-term memory, hindering knowledge accumulation across experiments. While AI agents such as OpenClaw have shown proficiency in continuous, tool-use-based desktop operations, they typically lack the embodied dexterity and physical grounding required for real-world laboratory interactions.

This paper addresses these limitations by developing a system that integrates hierarchical task decomposition, persistent memory mechanisms, and progress-aware execution to enable robust, long-horizon reasoning and autonomous operation in complex chemical environments. It aims to bridge the gap between abstract natural language instructions and reliable physical action sequences, thereby contributing to the "AI for Science" initiative by enhancing the autonomy and efficiency of chemical experimentation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges of achieving full autonomy in chemical laboratories. These environments demand strict adherence to rigid, multi-stage protocols, often involving long-horizon tasks. Current VLA models and LLM-based embodied agents face several limitations that restrict their application in such complex settings:
- **Limited Long-Horizon Reasoning:** Existing systems struggle to maintain coherence and accuracy over extended operational sequences.
- **Absence of Persistent Experience Accumulation:** Traditional frameworks often treat planning and execution as decoupled processes, failing to consolidate successful strategies. This results in inefficient trial-and-error, as robots do not learn from past experiences or accumulate knowledge across different experiments.
- **Difficulty in Grounding Abstract Instructions:** Translating abstract natural language instructions into robust action sequences that account for dynamic environmental states remains a core bottleneck.
- **Trajectory Discontinuities:** Latency in model inference can lead to control pauses and spatial inconsistencies during robot operation, posing safety risks and reducing precision.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this work were:
- **Develop ChemBot:** To propose and implement a dual-layer, closed-loop framework named ChemBot. This framework aims to integrate an autonomous AI agent with a progress-aware VLA model (Skill-VLA) for hierarchical task decomposition and execution in chemical laboratories.
- **Establish a Dual-Layer Memory Architecture:** To design and implement a memory system that consolidates successful trajectories into retrievable long-term assets while structuring real-time scene perception and task states in a short-term working memory. This aims to enable persistent learning and efficient information utilization.
- **Facilitate Sub-Agent and Tool Orchestration:** To utilize a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server to manage and orchestrate various sub-agents and tools effectively, ensuring seamless integration and communication within the framework.
- **Mitigate Trajectory Discontinuities:** To implement a future-state-based asynchronous inference mechanism to overcome the inherent limitations of VLA models regarding inference delays, thereby ensuring operational safety, precision, and smoothness in robotic movements.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** To empirically validate that ChemBot achieves superior operational safety, precision, and task success rates compared to existing VLA baselines when performing complex, long-horizon chemical experimentation on collaborative robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ChemBot framework operates on a hierarchical embodied intelligence architecture, described as "Agent-as-Planner, VLA-as-Skill." The high-level layer manages global planning, leveraging LLM comprehension capabilities, specialized task-decomposition sub-agents, and a dual-tier memory. The low-level VLA module orchestrates diverse motor skills.

**A. Multi-Agent Incremental Task Planning:**
The high-level planning involves two key modules:
- **Scene Describer:** This module autonomously employs custom tools to parse raw experimental bench images into a structured scene representation, termed "Dashboard." It utilizes Set-of-Mark Prompting to identify and enumerate objects, followed by visual assistance tools to analyze object properties. This includes evaluating interaction states (e.g., container openness), affordances (e.g., graspability, pourability), spatial positions, collision constraints, causal connections, operational logic, and physical commonsense relationships among items. This provides visual grounding for subsequent planning.
- **Subtask Generator:** Comprising a Subtask Reasoner and a Reflector, this module implements an incremental planning and reflection loop. The Reasoner integrates the Dashboard context to generate atomic subtasks sequentially. The Reflector then evaluates each proposed subtask for feasibility, rationality, and non-redundancy. Valid subtasks are added to an execution queue, while invalid ones are flagged for deletion, and feedback is provided to the Reasoner to trigger backtracking and replanning until the overall task sequence is complete.

**B. Dual-Layer Memory Mechanism:**
ChemBot incorporates a dual-layer memory system to enhance context management and knowledge retention:
- **Short-Term Working Memory (Dashboard):** This mechanism provides a structured, compressed representation of real-time scene and task states. It tracks the experimental phase, current reagents, and pending objectives. It consists of three sub-modules: the scene information module (generated by the Scene Describer), the tool index module (stores lightweight references to tool-generated information, loaded on-demand), and the task state module (records experimental planning progress). The Dashboard uses an immediate overwrite strategy for updates.
- **Episodic Long-Term Memory:** This component persistently stores historical dialogues and execution traces across different experimental sessions. It enables semantic retrieval of past experiences to facilitate rapid planning and verification of new tasks. Additionally, it extracts operational preferences and experimental archives from dialogue records to achieve long-term personalized adaptation for the agent.

**C. Progress-Aware VLA (Skill-VLA):**
The low-level execution is managed by Skill-VLA, an augmented VLA architecture:
- **VLA Policy:** The base VLA policy utilized is GR00T. At each environment step, the model predicts an action chunk [a_t..., a_{t+H-1}] conditioned on visual observations, language instructions, and the robot's current state. A Diffusion Transformer, trained via Flow Matching, is used to map Gaussian noise to the target action distribution.
- **Progress Head:** A lightweight neural network module is attached to the VLA policy to estimate real-time subtask execution progress. This module employs a cross-attention architecture, using the robot state as a query and the final hidden representations from the Eagle VLM (GR00T's backbone) as keys and values. Its output, a continuous progress scalar p_t ∈, is generated via a two-layer MLP with a Sigmoid activation. The Progress Head is trained jointly with the VLA policy using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, with ground-truth progress labels derived from normalized time-step positions within annotated subtask horizons. During inference, when p_t exceeds a predefined completion threshold, the system automatically signals the AI agent to terminate the current VLA skill and initiate the next subtask, creating a closed-loop execution cycle.

**D. Agent-as-Planner, VLA-as-Skill (Closed-Loop Chemical Manipulation):**
To ensure seamless interaction between the high-level planner and low-level executor, the VLA model's inference process is standardized into an Application Programming Interface (API) using the Model Context Protocol (MCP). The fine-tuned VLA model, encapsulated as an atomic action tool, receives disambiguated subtask instructions and continuous visual observations from the AI agent to drive the robot arm for precise operations. It returns binary execution states and diagnostic logs to the agent for visual verification. The integrated Progress Head provides fine-grained execution state feedback, enabling dynamic task termination and transition without relying on fixed time steps.

**E. Asynchronous Continuous Inference:**
To prevent hazardous control pauses and ensure smooth operation, model inference is decoupled from the control loop, enabling asynchronous execution. In this mechanism, if there's an inference delay, the system proactively truncates expired actions from the newly generated sequence and overwrites the remaining unexecuted portion of the old action sequence. To mitigate trajectory jumps caused by latency, Training-time RTC is employed, where the flow matching timestep for initial actions is fixed to 1.0, focusing denoising on the sequence suffix. Additionally, the predicted future state (s_{t+d}) is used as a boundary constraint and decision anchor instead of the current state (s_t), aligning generated actions with the robot's physical configuration at the moment of execution to maintain spatial continuity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Chemical Task Decomposition:**
- **Dataset and Metrics:** The evaluation used an extended CLARIFY dataset, enhanced with multimodal visual information (92 curated samples). Task decomposition was assessed using ROUGE (1, 2, L), BERTScore (P, R, F1), and an improved Levenshtein Distance (Edit Distance) that incorporates step-level semantic alignment and a positional penalty to account for the sequential dependency in chemical experiments.
- **Performance Comparison of Base Models (Table I):** Three VLM base models (Qwen3-VL-Flash, Qwen3-VL-Plus, and Doubao-1.6) were evaluated. Qwen3-VL-Flash demonstrated competitive performance in Edit Distance (0.766), BERTScore (F1: 0.694), and ROUGE scores, along with higher efficiency (98.8s total time). Qwen3-VL-Plus achieved slightly better semantic scores but with significantly higher execution time (298.8s). Doubao-1.6 showed the highest Edit Distance (1.364) and slowest execution (960.0s), attributed to cloud-based API delays.
- **Ablation Studies (Table III):** A series of ablation experiments highlighted the contribution of each ChemBot component (using Qwen3-VL-Flash as the base model).
 - Removing the **Scene Describer** (w/o SceneDescriber) led to degraded metrics across the board (Edit Dist. 0.915, BERTScore F1 0.556), underscoring the importance of visual scene understanding.
 - Eliminating the **Subtask Chain Reasoning** (w/o Subtask Chain) resulted in a substantial increase in Edit Distance (1.195) and lower semantic alignment, indicating the necessity of explicit chain reasoning for complex sequential tasks.
 - Disabling the **ReflectorAgent** (w/o Reflector) caused metrics to worsen (Edit Dist. 0.881, BERTScore F1 0.671), demonstrating its critical role in self-correction and error prevention.
 - Ablating the **Dual-Layer Memory** (w/o Memory) led to a slight increase in Edit Distance (0.791) and a notable increase in token length (28064 vs. 22401 for full model), validating its efficacy in reducing context overhead and mitigating hallucinations.
 These results collectively confirmed that the complete ChemBot architecture is essential for balancing reasoning accuracy and execution robustness.

**B. Real-World Evaluation:**
- **Experimental Setup and Dataset:** The ChemBot system was deployed on a UR3 collaborative robot. Experiments were conducted over 16 independent trials per task. A multimodal Chemical Manipulation Dataset was collected via teleoperation (5,459 expert trajectories, 51,580 atomic action segments across 12 chemical procedures), annotated with subtask start/end frames and fine-grained progress labels.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** A multi-level quantitative scoring mechanism was used: atomic success, subtask success, and end-to-end task success, culminating in an overall Success Rate (SR).
- **Task Performance (Fig. 8):** Skill-VLA, integrated with subtask decomposition and progress prediction, achieved significantly higher success rates across all three representative chemical experiments (11-step Precipitation, 7-step Heat and Dissolution, 6-step Neutralization) compared to full-trajectory baselines (π0.5, GR00T).
 - For **Precipitation**, Skill-VLA achieved an SR of approximately 85%, significantly higher than π0.5 (approx. 55%) and GR00T (approx. 45%).
 - For **Heat and Dissolution**, Skill-VLA achieved an SR of approximately 95%, outperforming π0.5 (approx. 60%) and GR00T (approx. 50%).
 - For **Neutralization**, Skill-VLA achieved an SR of approximately 90%, compared to π0.5 (approx. 70%) and GR00T (approx. 60%).
 The full-trajectory baselines exhibited lower performance due to distribution shifts over long horizons, compounding errors, and a memoryless nature leading to repetitive actions or skipped intermediate steps. The progress prediction mechanism in Skill-VLA enabled dynamic assessment of subtask completion in real time, eliminating reliance on fixed execution steps and improving autonomous closed-loop execution.

**C. Asynchronous Continuous Inference:**
- **Trajectory Smoothness (Fig. 9):** Comparison of robot joint trajectories (Joint 4 of UR robot) demonstrated that asynchronous inference resulted in substantially smoother motion compared to synchronous inference. This smoothness reduces end-effector jitter and enables continuous motion execution with minimal fluctuations, contributing to enhanced operational safety and precision in real-world deployments. Asynchronous inference also led to reduced execution time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ChemBot framework represents an advancement in the application of Vision-Language-Action models for complex, long-horizon real-world tasks, particularly in scientific automation. Its significance lies in addressing critical limitations of existing VLA systems, such as the lack of persistent memory, inadequate long-horizon reasoning, and vulnerability to trajectory discontinuities. By integrating a hierarchical planning agent with a progress-aware VLA executor and an asynchronous control mechanism, ChemBot demonstrates enhanced robustness and reliability in multi-stage chemical experimentation.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Accelerating Scientific Discovery:** ChemBot offers a pathway towards fully autonomous chemical laboratories, which can significantly accelerate research and development by automating hazardous and repetitive experimental tasks. This would free human chemists to focus on higher-level problem-solving and experimental design, potentially leading to faster discovery of new materials and chemical processes.
- **Advancing Embodied AI:** The framework's dual-layer memory, progress-aware execution, and asynchronous inference mechanisms contribute to the development of more capable and generalizable embodied AI agents. These agents can learn from experience, adapt to dynamic environments, and execute complex sequences of actions more reliably, pushing the boundaries of autonomous robotics in open-world settings.
- **Improving Robotic Control and Safety:** The implementation of asynchronous inference and future-state-based action generation enhances the smoothness and precision of robotic movements, reducing the risk of accidents (e.g., chemical spills) and improving overall operational safety in sensitive laboratory environments. This approach to control can be generalized to other domains requiring precise, uninterrupted robotic manipulation.
- **Enabling Adaptive Automation:** By decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level execution and incorporating real-time progress monitoring, ChemBot enables a more adaptive and resilient automation paradigm. This allows for dynamic adjustments during task execution, reducing the impact of unforeseen events and increasing task success rates in complex, unstructured environments.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding LLM planning reliability, visual perception of transparent glassware, and generalization across novel scenes, the established framework provides a foundation for future work. The stated goal of scaling to cross-device manipulation and enhancing agentic reasoning points towards the broader vision of realizing fully autonomous scientific assistants capable of end-to-end experiment orchestration and analysis.
