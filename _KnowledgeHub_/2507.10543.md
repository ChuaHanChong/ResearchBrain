# MP1: MeanFlow Tames Policy Learning in 1-step for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MP1 introduces a robot learning framework leveraging the MeanFlow paradigm for one-step action trajectory generation, achieving an average success rate of 78.9% and an inference time of 6.8 ms across 37 robotic manipulation tasks. This approach from Peking University outperforms prior diffusion and flow-based methods in both performance and speed while incorporating a novel Dispersive Loss for enhanced generalization.

## Problem

- Diffusion models, while effective for diverse action distributions, suffer from high inference latency due to their iterative denoising process, hindering real-time robotic control.
- Existing flow-based methods, designed for speed, often introduce architectural constraints or rely on iterative ODE solvers, which can lead to numerical errors and compromise trajectory precision.
- Policy learning struggles with generalization and can experience 'feature collapse,' where subtle environmental differences are not robustly captured, particularly in few-shot learning scenarios.

## Method

- **MP1** adapts the MeanFlow paradigm to robot learning, directly learning an *interval-averaged velocity field* to enable genuine one-step (1-NFE) action trajectory generation, bypassing iterative ODE solving.
- It leverages the '==MeanFlow Identity==,' a local differential equation, to train a network that predicts this average velocity, thus eliminating the need for explicit consistency constraints or numerical integration errors.
- A novel Dispersive Loss is introduced as a training-time regularizer, encouraging latent state embeddings to be more discriminative and improving generalization, especially in few-shot learning, without incurring inference overhead.

## Results

- MP1 achieved an average success rate of **78.9%** across 37 tasks (Adroit and Meta-World), outperforming FlowPolicy by **7.3%** and Diffusion Policy 3D (DP3) by **10.2%**, with notable gains on challenging tasks.
- It demonstrated state-of-the-art inference speed, averaging **6.8** ms, making it nearly **2x** faster than FlowPolicy (**12.6** ms) and 14-**19x** faster than DP3 (**132.2** ms).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Dispersive Loss contributed 4-5 percentage points to success rates, and real-world experiments on an ARX R5 robot showed MP1 achieved state-of-the-art success rates and faster task completion times across five manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The MeanFlow paradigm can effectively bridge the gap between high-performance generative models and real-time robotic control by enabling 1-NFE action prediction without explicit consistency constraints.
> - Learning an interval-averaged velocity field directly through the MeanFlow Identity simplifies the generative process and ensures precise trajectory generation at high speeds.
> - A contrastive-like Dispersive Loss, applied during training, significantly enhances policy generalization by fostering more discriminative latent representations, which is crucial for robust few-shot learning.
