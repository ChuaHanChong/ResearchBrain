# MP1: MeanFlow Tames Policy Learning in 1-step for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MP1 introduces a robot learning framework leveraging the MeanFlow paradigm for one-step action trajectory generation, achieving an average success rate of 78.9% and an inference time of 6.8 ms across 37 robotic manipulation tasks. This approach from Peking University outperforms prior diffusion and flow-based methods in both performance and speed while incorporating a novel Dispersive Loss for enhanced generalization.

## Problem

- Diffusion models, while effective for diverse action distributions, suffer from high inference latency due to their iterative denoising process, hindering real-time robotic control.
- Existing flow-based methods, designed for speed, often introduce architectural constraints or rely on iterative ODE solvers, which can lead to numerical errors and compromise trajectory precision.
- Policy learning struggles with generalization and can experience 'feature collapse,' where subtle environmental differences are not robustly captured, particularly in few-shot learning scenarios.

## Method

- **MP1** adapts the MeanFlow paradigm to robot learning, directly learning an *interval-averaged velocity field* to enable genuine one-step (1-NFE) action trajectory generation, bypassing iterative ODE solving.
- It leverages the '==MeanFlow Identity==,' a local differential equation, to train a network that predicts this average velocity, thus eliminating the need for explicit consistency constraints or numerical integration errors.
- A novel Dispersive Loss is introduced as a training-time regularizer, encouraging latent state embeddings to be more discriminative and improving generalization, especially in few-shot learning, without incurring inference overhead.

## Results

- MP1 achieved an average success rate of **78.9%** across 37 tasks (Adroit and Meta-World), outperforming FlowPolicy by **7.3%** and Diffusion Policy 3D (DP3) by **10.2%**, with notable gains on challenging tasks.
- It demonstrated state-of-the-art inference speed, averaging **6.8** ms, making it nearly **2x** faster than FlowPolicy (**12.6** ms) and 14-**19x** faster than DP3 (**132.2** ms).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Dispersive Loss contributed 4-5 percentage points to success rates, and real-world experiments on an ARX R5 robot showed MP1 achieved state-of-the-art success rates and faster task completion times across five manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The MeanFlow paradigm can effectively bridge the gap between high-performance generative models and real-time robotic control by enabling 1-NFE action prediction without explicit consistency constraints.
> - Learning an interval-averaged velocity field directly through the MeanFlow Identity simplifies the generative process and ensures precise trajectory generation at high speeds.
> - A contrastive-like Dispersive Loss, applied during training, significantly enhances policy generalization by fostering more discriminative latent representations, which is crucial for robust few-shot learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **robot manipulation**, which broadly encompasses enabling robots to perform complex physical tasks like grasping, moving, and manipulating objects. A cornerstone of this domain is **robot learning**, where robots learn to generate actions from various inputs, prominently visual data such as images or 3D point clouds, and increasingly, textual descriptions.

The broader landscape of robot learning policies has seen significant advancements driven by **generative models**, including Transformers, Diffusion Models, and Flow Matching. These models have expanded robots' capabilities in understanding complex environments and executing nuanced actions.

Within generative models, two primary paradigms dominate:
- **Diffusion Models:** Methods like Diffusion Policy (DP) and its 3D variant, DP3, have excelled at handling multimodal action distributions by representing action predictions as probability distributions. They achieve high success rates and robustness, especially when leveraging 3D point cloud features for richer environmental understanding. However, their fundamental limitation is **slow inference speed**. They require numerous iterative denoising steps to generate an action trajectory, making them unsuitable for real-time robotic control where millisecond-level latency is often critical.
- **Flow-based Methods:** Developed to address the speed bottleneck of Diffusion Models, approaches such as AdaFlow and FlowPolicy aim for faster, ideally single-step (1-NFE, one network function evaluation) inference. While achieving significant speedups, these methods often introduce a new challenge: they typically rely on **explicit consistency constraints** or necessitate solving Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) to integrate instantaneous velocities over time. These constraints can impose architectural limitations, require careful tuning, or introduce numerical errors, potentially compromising the precision or generality of the generated trajectories.

The presented work, **MP1**, directly targets this fundamental trade-off between the slow, iterative nature of Diffusion Models and the architectural/constraint-based limitations of existing Flow-based methods. It positions itself as a novel solution by being the "first adaptation of the MeanFlow paradigm to robot learning." MeanFlow, a recent advancement from image generation, offers a way to achieve genuine one-step sampling without requiring explicit consistency losses, by learning an *interval-averaged velocity field* rather than an instantaneous one.

Therefore, MP1 is a critical step forward in robot learning. It seeks to combine the high performance and multimodal capabilities of advanced generative models with the real-time inference speed demanded by practical robotic applications, while simultaneously simplifying the generative process by avoiding restrictive constraints and numerical integration issues. It leverages 3D point clouds as input, aligning with the trend towards more accurate and robust environmental representations in modern robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the MP1 research is to develop a robot learning policy that concurrently achieves **high efficiency** and **robust generalization** for robotic manipulation tasks. This core objective is motivated by addressing the significant limitations of current state-of-the-art generative models in the robotics domain:

1. **Overcoming Inference Latency in Diffusion Models:** A primary motivation is the prohibitively long inference time of Diffusion Models. While powerful in capturing complex action distributions, their multi-step denoising process fundamentally hinders their applicability in real-time robotic systems. MP1 aims for genuine one-step (1-NFE) inference, critical for responsive robot control.

2. **Eliminating Constraints and ODE-Solver Errors in Flow-based Methods:** Existing Flow-based methods, while faster than diffusion, often rely on explicit consistency constraints or the integration of instantaneous velocities via ODE solvers. These introduce architectural dependencies, potential numerical inaccuracies, and can complicate model design and training. MP1 aims to bypass these by directly learning an interval-averaged velocity through the "MeanFlow Identity," thereby removing the need for consistency constraints and iterative ODE solving.

3. **Enhancing Generalization, Especially in Few-Shot Learning:** Purely regression-based policy learning, even with powerful generative models, can struggle with learning robust representations. This can lead to "feature collapse" where distinct but subtly varied environmental states might map to similar latent embeddings, making it difficult for the robot to differentiate critical scene contexts. This issue is particularly detrimental in few-shot learning scenarios, where the model must generalize effectively from limited demonstrations. MP1 aims to explicitly address this by introducing a novel regularization technique.

In summary, the key objectives for MP1 are:
- To introduce the MeanFlow paradigm to robot learning for the first time.
- To achieve genuine one-step (1-NFE) action trajectory generation.
- To eliminate numerical ODE-solver errors during inference for more precise trajectories.
- To avoid any explicit consistency constraints, simplifying the model.
- To incorporate Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) for improved trajectory controllability without reintroducing structural constraints.
- To develop a lightweight Dispersive Loss that boosts generalization, particularly in few-shot learning, by encouraging discriminative latent state embeddings, all without impacting inference speed.
- To validate the method's superiority in both simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks across success rate and inference speed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MP1 method proposes a novel robot learning framework that pairs 3D point-cloud inputs with the MeanFlow paradigm, enhanced with a Dispersive Loss, to generate action trajectories efficiently and robustly.

#### 3.1. MP1: One-Step Trajectory Generation with MeanFlow

The core innovation lies in adapting the MeanFlow paradigm from image generation to robotics.
- **Input Processing:** The system takes historical observation point clouds (P) and robot states (S) as inputs. A 3D Projection module processes the point cloud into a visual feature vector ($\mathbf{f}_v$), while a state encoder processes the robot state into a state feature ($\mathbf{f}_s$). These are concatenated to form a conditional vector ($\mathbf{c}$).
- **MeanFlow Paradigm:** Unlike standard Flow Matching, which learns an *instantaneous velocity field* $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{z}_t, t)$ (requiring ODE solvers for sampling), MeanFlow learns an *average velocity field* $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{z}_t, r, t)$ over a time interval $[r, t]$. This average velocity is defined as the total displacement divided by time duration.
- **"MeanFlow Identity":** Directly learning $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{z}_t, r, t)$ from its integral definition is intractable. MP1 leverages the "MeanFlow Identity," a local differential equation derived from the integral definition, which allows training a network $\mathbf{u}_\theta$ to model this average velocity field. This is crucial as it bypasses iterative integration and eliminates numerical ODE-solver errors.
- **Training Objective:** The network $\mathbf{u}_\theta(\mathbf{A}_t, r, t|\mathbf{c})$ is trained to predict the average velocity that transforms an initial noise vector $\mathbf{A}_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ into an expert action trajectory $\mathbf{A}_0$. A simple regression objective $\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,r,x,\epsilon} ||\mathbf{u}_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, r, t) - \text{sg}(\mathbf{u}_{tgt})||_2^2$ is used, where $\mathbf{u}_{tgt}$ is constructed using the known instantaneous velocity and the network's own estimate of the total derivative, with a stop-gradient (sg) for stability.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** To improve controllability of the generated trajectories, MP1 integrates CFG. This involves training the network to predict a CFG-aware average velocity ($\mathbf{u}_{cfg}^\theta$), guided by a modified regression loss $\mathcal{L}_{cfg}(\theta)$ that blends conditional and unconditional predictions.
- **1-NFE Inference:** At inference time, given conditional features $\mathbf{c}$ and a random noise sample $\mathbf{A}_1$, the MP1 generates the entire K-step action $\mathbf{A}_0$ in a single forward pass: $\mathbf{A}_0 = \mathbf{A}_1 - \mathbf{u}_{cfg}^\theta(\mathbf{A}_1, 0, 1|\mathbf{c})$. This ensures the method's characteristic one-step (1-NFE) speed.

#### 3.2. Enhancing Representational Generalization with Dispersive Loss

To address the "feature collapse" problem and improve generalization, especially in few-shot learning:
- **Problem:** The primary MeanFlow objective focuses on temporal dynamics but provides only indirect signals for robustly mapping high-dimensional conditional inputs to actions. This can lead to distinct environmental states having ambiguous latent representations.
- **Dispersive Loss:** MP1 introduces a novel, self-contained regularizer called Dispersive Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{Disp}$). It acts as a "contrastive loss without positive pairs," encouraging the latent embeddings ($\mathbf{z}_A$) of different input samples within a training batch to spread out. This forces the model to learn a more discriminative feature space.
- **Implementation:** The loss is applied to the output features of each down-sampling block in the U-Net backbone of $\mathbf{u}_\theta$. Its formulation is $\mathcal{L}_{Disp}(\theta) = \log \mathbb{E}_{i,j \in \mathcal{B}} [\exp(-\frac{||\mathbf{z}_{A,i} - \mathbf{z}_{A,j}||_2^2}{\tau})]$, where $\mathcal{B}$ is a mini-batch and $\tau$ is a temperature hyperparameter.
- **No Inference Overhead:** Crucially, $\mathcal{L}_{Disp}$ is a training-time regularizer; it adds no computational cost during inference, thus preserving MP1's 1-NFE speed.

#### 3.3. Total Training Objective

The final training objective combines both goals: $\mathcal{L}_{total}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{cfg}(\theta) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{Disp}(\theta)$, where $\lambda$ (set to 0.5) balances the contribution of trajectory generation and representation regularization.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluated on 3 tasks from the Adroit benchmark and 34 tasks from the Meta-World benchmark (categorized into Easy, Medium, Hard, Very Hard).
- **Baselines:** Compared against leading Diffusion-based methods (DP3, Simple DP3) and Flow-based methods (AdaFlow, FlowPolicy), as well as other approaches like DP and CP. The number of Network Function Evaluations (NFE) for baselines (1-NFE for FlowPolicy/CP, 10-NFE for Diffusion methods) was carefully noted.
- **Data:** 10 expert demonstrations for simulation training, 20 human demonstrations for real-world tasks. Point cloud inputs were sampled to 512/1024 points.
- **Metrics:** Success rate and inference time (ms).
- **Hardware:** NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.
- **Real-world Validation:** Conducted on an ARX R5 dual-arm robot with a RealSense L515 camera for five manipulation tasks (Hammer, Drawer Close, Heat Water, Stack Block, Spoon).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MP1 demonstrates its superior performance across multiple dimensions, validating its design choices in addressing the limitations of prior methods.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Simulation Benchmarks

- **Overall Success Rate:** MP1 achieved an average success rate of **78.9% ± 2.1%** across 37 Adroit and Meta-World tasks. This significantly outperforms the previous best methods, with a **7.3% higher** average success rate than FlowPolicy (71.6% ± 3.5%) and a **10.2% higher** rate than DP3 (68.7% ± 4.7%).
- **Performance on Challenging Tasks:** MP1 showed notable improvements on Meta-World's more difficult categories, increasing success rates by 9.8%, 17.9%, and 15.0% on "Medium," "Hard," and "Very Hard" tasks, respectively, compared to FlowPolicy.
- **Stability and Robustness:** The consistently low standard deviation (e.g., 0-2.0% for Adroit Hammer, Door, and Meta-World Easy tasks, and an average fluctuation of 2.1%) indicates MP1's greater stability and reliability across different random seeds.
- **Training Efficiency:** MP1 exhibited faster convergence and achieved higher final success rates during training, as shown by success rate curves on various Meta-World tasks (Fig. 4).

#### 4.2. Exceptional Inference Speed

- **State-of-the-Art Latency:** MP1 achieved an average inference time of just **6.8 ms** across all evaluated tasks.
- **Significant Speedup:** This makes MP1 nearly **2× faster** than FlowPolicy (12.6 ms) and a remarkable **14× to 19× faster** than Diffusion-based methods like DP3 (132.2 ms).
- **Inference Stability:** MP1's inference speed was highly stable, with a standard deviation of only ±0.1 ms, demonstrating its robustness against GPU load fluctuations. These results confirm MP1's ability to achieve real-time performance.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study Insights

- **Impact of Dispersive Loss:** Removing the Dispersive Loss consistently led to a decrease in success rates across tasks, with average gains of 4-5 percentage points attributed to its inclusion. This confirms that Dispersive Loss effectively enhances policy performance by promoting a more discriminative latent feature space, boosting generalization.
- **MeanFlow Ratio Validation:** Experiments on varying the MeanFlow ratio (r vs. t) showed that performance significantly declines when the method degenerates to standard Flow Matching (ratio=0, i.e., r=t). Optimal performance was observed when r $\neq$ t, validating the core MeanFlow concept of learning an average velocity.
- **Few-Shot Learning Capability:** MP1 consistently outperformed FlowPolicy, especially when the number of demonstrations was limited (e.g., 2 or 5 demonstrations). As the number of demonstrations increased, MP1 maintained its lead, highlighting its strong generalization capabilities in few-shot learning scenarios.

#### 4.4. Real-world Validation

- **Practical Effectiveness:** MP1 achieved state-of-the-art success rates across all five real-world robotic manipulation tasks.
- **Fast Completion Times:** It also recorded the shortest average task completion times in real-world scenarios (e.g., 18.6s for Hammer vs. 22.3s for FlowPolicy and 31.1s for DP3).
- **Qualitative Success:** Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 3) visually demonstrated MP1's ability to successfully complete tasks where FlowPolicy failed, further underscoring its robustness and practical utility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MP1 framework represents a significant advancement in robot learning, offering solutions to several long-standing challenges and paving the way for more capable and practical robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Resolution of the Speed-Constraint Trade-off

The most profound impact of MP1 is its success in breaking the fundamental trade-off between inference speed and architectural constraints prevalent in generative models for robot learning. By effectively adapting the MeanFlow paradigm, MP1 achieves genuine 1-NFE (one-step) action generation, delivering millisecond-level inference latency without the need for explicit consistency constraints or iterative ODE-solver integrations. This means robots can now leverage powerful generative models for complex tasks in real-time.

#### 5.2. Enabling Real-time, High-Precision Robotic Control

The average inference time of 6.8 ms makes MP1 highly suitable for applications demanding real-time control, such as industrial automation, dexterous manipulation, human-robot interaction, and dynamic environments. The elimination of numerical ODE-solver errors leads to more precise and dynamically consistent action trajectories, which is crucial for delicate or complex manipulation tasks.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Generalization and Few-Shot Learning

The introduction of the Dispersive Loss is a key innovation for improving the robustness and generalization of learned policies. By encouraging discriminative latent representations, MP1 can better distinguish subtle environmental variations, which are critical in robotics. This, coupled with its demonstrated superior performance in few-shot learning scenarios, means that MP1 can learn effectively from limited expert demonstrations, significantly reducing the expensive and time-consuming data collection process. This has vast implications for deploying robots in new tasks or diverse environments quickly.

#### 5.4. Simplification of Generative Model Design

By directly learning the interval-averaged velocity through the "MeanFlow Identity" and avoiding complex consistency constraints or ODE integration, MP1 simplifies the generative process and potentially makes policy models easier to design, train, and understand. This could accelerate future research and development in this area.

#### 5.5. Broadening the Applicability of MeanFlow

As the first adaptation of the MeanFlow paradigm to robot learning, MP1 opens a new avenue for research. It demonstrates the versatility and power of MeanFlow beyond its original domain (image generation) and could inspire similar adaptations in other fields requiring efficient and precise trajectory or sequence generation.

#### 5.6. Strong Empirical Validation

The comprehensive experimental validation across diverse simulation benchmarks (Adroit, Meta-World) and real-world robotic tasks, including qualitative and quantitative comparisons against state-of-the-art methods, robustly supports MP1's effectiveness and practical utility. This strong empirical evidence increases confidence in its potential for widespread adoption.

In conclusion, MP1 represents a significant step forward in robot learning by offering a fast, robust, and data-efficient policy learning framework. Its ability to generate high-quality action trajectories in real-time, coupled with improved generalization from limited data, positions it as a powerful tool for advancing the capabilities of autonomous robotic manipulation systems in various real-world applications.
