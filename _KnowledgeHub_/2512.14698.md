# TimeLens: Rethinking Video Temporal Grounding with Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers enhanced multimodal LLMs for video temporal grounding (VTG) by systematically refining existing benchmarks and creating a high-quality training dataset, TimeLens-100K. The resulting TimeLens models establish new open-source performance standards on these refined benchmarks, surpassing several proprietary models through optimized textual timestamp encoding and a thinking-free reinforcement learning approach.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) demonstrate poor temporal awareness, struggling to precisely localize events ("when") in videos despite understanding "what" is happening.
- Existing Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) benchmarks suffer from critical flaws, including low-quality queries, erroneous timestamps, and unreliable annotations, leading to misleading evaluation results and misdirected research.
- A lack of systematic investigation into algorithmic designs for VTG with MLLMs prevents understanding of optimal timestamp representation, training paradigms, and effective training practices.

## Method

- Developed "**TimeLens**-Bench," a refined suite of VTG evaluation benchmarks with manually audited, high-quality queries and precise temporal annotations, addressing widespread errors in original datasets.
- Created "TimeLens-100K," a large-scale, high-quality training dataset comprising 100,000 VTG annotations generated via an automated re-annotation pipeline using advanced multimodal models.
- Systematically explored algorithmic components for MLLMs, including timestamp encoding strategies (e.g., interleaved textual encoding) and optimization paradigms (e.g., thinking-free reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards).

## Results

- TimeLens-Bench revealed significant error rates in original benchmarks, leading to a re-ranking where proprietary models like GPT-5 showed improved relative performance on refined data (e.g., 40.5 mIoU on Charades-TimeLens vs. 28.4 mIoU on original).
- TimeLens-**7B** and TimeLens-**8B** models established new open-source state-of-the-art VTG performance, surpassing frontier proprietary models like GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Flash (e.g., TimeLens-**8B** achieved 65.5 mIoU on QVHighlights-TimeLens).
- Models trained on the curated TimeLens-**100K** dataset demonstrated substantial performance improvements (e.g., 48.3 mIoU on Charades-TimeLens compared to 35.6 mIoU when trained on original noisy data), validating the impact of high-quality training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing VTG benchmarks contain high error proportions (e.g., 20.6% query uniqueness violations in Charades-STA), causing unreliable performance rankings where open-source models may appear to outperform proprietary models, a trend that reverses on quality-controlled data.
> - A simple interleaved textual encoding strategy, converting raw timestamps into text tokens inserted before visual tokens, is highly effective for MLLM temporal grounding, outperforming position-embedding based methods.
> - Pure thinking-free Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is more effective and efficient for VTG training than Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) or thinking-based RLVR, with early stopping and difficulty-based data sampling further optimizing training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated proficiency in various video understanding tasks, particularly in identifying "what" is happening in a video. However, the capability of these models to accurately localize "when" specific events occur within a video, a task known as video temporal grounding (VTG), remains an area requiring further development. VTG necessitates fine-grained, time-aware perception rather than coarse semantic aggregation, and it involves modeling long-term visual dynamics, which is challenging for both annotation and machine learning. As MLLMs are increasingly integrated into perception and reasoning systems, enhancing their temporal awareness has become a critical requirement.

Existing research in VTG has explored various datasets and algorithmic designs. Numerous VTG datasets have been proposed, with some aggregating multiple sources for training and using distinct benchmarks for evaluation. On the algorithmic front, studies have focused on model architectures, including token compression for long videos and timestamp encoding strategies, as well as training approaches like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). However, the paper identifies two primary gaps in the current landscape: a lack of systematic examination of existing dataset quality and inconsistent experimental settings that impede fair comparisons and the establishment of best practices. This work positions itself by systematically addressing these overlooked data quality issues and providing a structured investigation into algorithmic designs within a standardized, high-quality evaluation framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a systematic and essential baseline for video temporal grounding (VTG) by enhancing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) with robust temporal awareness. The authors motivate this work by highlighting a significant limitation of current MLLMs: while they can identify events, they often fail to precisely localize them in time, which is fundamental to VTG. This limitation is attributed to the inherent difficulty of fine-grained time-aware perception and the challenges in annotating and learning long-term visual dynamics.

The motivation is further detailed along two main dimensions:

1. **Addressing Data Quality Issues:** The authors contend that existing VTG benchmarks suffer from critical flaws, including low-quality queries and erroneous timestamps. These issues lead to unreliable evaluation results and can misguide research efforts. Specifically, they observe:
 - Absence of clear comparisons between leading proprietary and open-source models under consistent quality standards.
 - Prevalence of low-quality queries, inaccurate annotations, duplicate queries, and events that do not exist or are not unique within videos.
 - Potential for existing leaderboards to be misleading due to these data issues, inflating the performance of some models while underestimating others.
 - The need for high-quality training data, as existing training corpora are found to have even higher error rates than evaluation benchmarks.

2. **Systematic Exploration of Algorithmic Design:** Despite numerous algorithmic proposals for improving MLLMs' VTG capabilities, a systematic investigation into the core components and a fair comparison of their relative merits are lacking. The motivation here is to:
 - Investigate effective timestamp representation strategies to enable MLLMs to accurately perceive absolute and relative temporal positions.
 - Determine the optimal training paradigm (e.g., SFT, thinking-based RLVR, thinking-free RLVR) for VTG, considering both performance and efficiency.
 - Identify effective training recipes and practices for the chosen paradigm, such as optimal training duration and data sampling strategies.

By systematically addressing these data quality and algorithmic design dimensions, the research aims to provide a reliable foundation for future work in VTG, leading to the development of MLLMs with demonstrably strong temporal grounding capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a systematic investigative approach, dissecting its efforts along two primary dimensions: data quality and algorithmic design. The overall framework, named TimeLens, is designed to build MLLMs with enhanced VTG abilities.

#### 3.1. Data Quality Dimension

The methodology begins with a comprehensive overhaul of VTG datasets, focusing on both evaluation benchmarks and training data.

- **Annotation Criteria:** To ensure high-quality data, the authors established strict criteria for input videos, queries, and output temporal segments:
 - **Input Criteria:** Queries must be clear, precise, unambiguous, and specific. Events described must genuinely exist within the video and be unique within a single video. Queries should avoid information leakage (e.g., "ending credits") that allows shortcuts without true grounding.
 - **Output Criteria:** Annotated event boundaries must be precise, excluding irrelevant subsegments. Annotations must be exhaustive, meaning no other valid segments for the query should exist outside the annotated one.

- **Manual Auditing and Refinement:** A "diagnose-then-refine" workflow was implemented. Annotators reviewed video-query pairs against the criteria, identified error categories (e.g., multiple event occurrences, no event occurrence, duplicate queries, unclear queries, inaccurate annotations), and then revised the query or selected a new valid event, subsequently annotating precise temporal segments. Quality control involved cross-validation by different annotators and rejection of batches exceeding a predefined error rate. This meticulous process was applied to three popular benchmarks: Charades-STA, ActivityNet Captions, and QVHighlights, resulting in **TimeLens-Bench**.

- **Training Data Re-annotation:** Recognizing that existing training data also suffered from high error rates, an automated re-annotation pipeline was developed. Instead of refining existing labels, the pipeline re-annotated videos using advanced multimodal models (specifically Gemini-2.5-Pro). The model was prompted to identify distinct events, generate queries, output timestamps, and verify the quality of queries and annotations. This process led to the creation of **TimeLens-100K**, a large-scale, high-quality training dataset comprising approximately 20K videos and 100K VTG annotations.

#### 3.2. Algorithmic Design Dimension

With a refined data foundation, the research then systematically explored algorithmic design principles using Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the baseline model.

- **Experimental Setup:** Experiments were conducted using Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Video frames were sampled at 2 FPS, with adaptive spatial resolution based on video duration and raw resolution. For the final TimeLens models, the resolution budget was scaled up. RLVR experiments employed GRPO as the optimization method. TimeLens-Bench was used for evaluation, and TimeLens-100K for training.

- **Timestamp Encoding:** Three main strategies for aligning timestamps with video features were compared:
 - **Position-embedding based:** Adapting LLM position embeddings (e.g., MRoPE, 3D RoPE) to represent temporal positions.
 - **Visual overlay:** Directly overlaying timestamps or frame indices onto each frame, leveraging MLLM OCR capabilities.
 - **Textual encoding:** Converting timestamps into text tokens using the MLLM's text tokenizer. This included two variants: "Interleaved" (inserting timestamp tokens before visual tokens of each frame) and "Non-interleaved" (adding an instructional prefix to the prompt). Experiments compared raw timestamps (e.g., "10.2s") and frame indices (e.g., "1, 2, 3").

- **Optimization Paradigms:** The effectiveness and efficiency of different training paradigms for VTG were investigated:
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** A conventional approach.
 - **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):**
 - **Thinking-based RLVR:** Model first generates an explicit reasoning process ("think") and then produces the final answer ("answer"), with rewards computed on both accuracy and format compliance.
 - **Thinking-free RLVR:** Model directly generates the answer without explicit reasoning, with rewards based solely on grounding accuracy.
 - The study also examined whether a preceding SFT phase benefits RLVR.

- **Recipes for RLVR Training:** Building on the finding that thinking-free RLVR was effective, further explorations focused on optimizing its application:
 - **Training Duration (Early Stopping):** Monitored training reward and evaluation metrics to identify when performance peaked, suggesting an optimal training duration for RLVR.
 - **Data Sampling (Difficulty-based Sampling):** Employed offline inference to estimate sample difficulty (1-IoU) and then used Gaussian sampling to construct training subsets with varying average difficulty levels. This aimed to determine the impact of sample difficulty on performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning data quality, evaluation reliability, and algorithmic design for video temporal grounding (VTG).

#### 4.1. Findings on Data Quality and Evaluation

- **Finding 1: High Proportion of Errors in Existing Benchmarks.** Analysis of Charades-STA, ActivityNet Captions, and QVHighlights revealed a high proportion of errors across categories such as query uniqueness violations (e.g., 20.6% in Charades-STA) and annotation accuracy issues (e.g., 34.9% in Charades-STA). This indicated the unreliability of existing datasets for evaluation. The manual refinement resulted in TimeLens-Bench, which comprises 4,279 videos and 9,404 annotations across the three refined datasets.
- **Finding 2: Misleading Performance Rankings on Low-Quality Data.** Evaluation on original benchmarks showed open-source models often outperforming proprietary models (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro). However, on the refined TimeLens-Bench, this trend reversed, with proprietary models exhibiting superior performance and open-source models experiencing substantial degradation. This re-ranking confirmed that previous evaluation standards were unreliable due to data quality flaws. For example, on Charades-STA, GPT-5 scored 40.5 mIoU on TimeLens-Bench versus 28.4 mIoU on the original, while Time-R1-7B scored 36.6 mIoU on TimeLens-Bench versus 58.1 mIoU on the original.
- **Finding 3: Improved Training Data Enhances Grounding Ability.** Models trained on the automatically re-annotated TimeLens-100K dataset demonstrated substantial performance improvements on the refined evaluation benchmarks. This validated the enhanced quality of the curated training data. For example, models trained on TimeLens-100K showed an mIoU of 48.3 on Charades-TimeLens, compared to 35.6 mIoU when trained on original noisy data.

#### 4.2. Main Results of TimeLens Models

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The culmination of these efforts, the TimeLens models, demonstrated superior VTG capability. TimeLens-7B, built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieved 48.8 mIoU on Charades-TimeLens, 46.2 mIoU on ActivityNet-TimeLens, and 56.0 mIoU on QVHighlights-TimeLens, surpassing several strong open-source competitors (e.g., Time-R1-7B, MiMo-VL-7B) and proprietary models like GPT-4o and GPT-5.
- **Surpassing Proprietary Models:** TimeLens-8B, based on Qwen3-VL-8B, further pushed performance to 55.2 mIoU on Charades-TimeLens, 53.2 mIoU on ActivityNet-TimeLens, and 65.5 mIoU on QVHighlights-TimeLens, establishing a new state-of-the-art among open-source models and surpassing frontier proprietary models such as Gemini-2.5-Flash and GPT-5.

#### 4.3. Findings on Algorithmic Designs

- **Finding 4: Interleaved Textual Timestamp Encoding is Effective and Simple.** A comparison of timestamp encoding methods revealed that position-embedding based methods yielded unsatisfactory results. Instead, a simple interleaved textual encoding strategy that converts timestamps into text tokens and inserts them before visual tokens achieved the best performance. Specifically, using raw timestamps (e.g., "10.2s") with interleaved textual prefix achieved 48.3 mIoU on Charades-TimeLens, compared to 36.6 mIoU for position embeddings.
- **Finding 5: Thinking-Free RLVR is Superior for VTG.** Comparative experiments on training paradigms demonstrated that a pure thinking-free reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) approach achieved superior performance and efficiency compared to both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and thinking-based RLVR. A preceding SFT phase before RLVR was found to offer no significant additional performance gain. Thinking-free RLVR achieved 48.3 mIoU on Charades-TimeLens (1.0x training time), whereas SFT on 100K data achieved 48.6 mIoU (2.4x training time), and thinking-based RLVR achieved 42.7 mIoU (1.9x training time).
- **Finding 6: Early Stopping for RLVR Training.** Analysis of RLVR training dynamics showed that model performance peaked when reward metrics (temporal IoU reward and within-group reward standard deviation) plateaued. Continued training beyond this point led to performance degradation. This indicated that early stopping is an effective strategy for RLVR, saving computational cost and preventing over-training.
- **Finding 7: Importance of Difficulty-based Data Sampling.** For RLVR training, selecting training data with sufficiently high difficulty relative to the model was crucial for optimal performance. Model performance improved as the average sample difficulty increased, eventually plateauing when difficulty reached a high level (over 0.75 IoU difficulty).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes contributions by addressing fundamental challenges in video temporal grounding (VTG) for multimodal large language models (MLLMs), with potential implications across several aspects of video understanding research and application.

The primary significance lies in the rigorous **re-evaluation and improvement of data quality** for VTG. By exposing critical flaws in widely-used benchmarks and meticulously re-annotating them to create TimeLens-Bench, the authors have provided a more reliable and trustworthy evaluation suite. This directly impacts the integrity of model benchmarking, as demonstrated by the dramatic re-ranking of models on the refined datasets. The finding that prior evaluation standards were misleading suggests that a substantial portion of existing research might have been misdirected or incorrectly assessed. TimeLens-Bench is expected to serve as a more robust foundation for future VTG research, enabling fairer comparisons and more accurate insights into model capabilities.

Furthermore, the creation of **TimeLens-100K**, a large-scale, high-quality training dataset through an automated re-annotation pipeline, offers a valuable resource for training more performant MLLMs. The demonstrated performance gains attributed to this dataset validate the importance of high-quality data beyond just evaluation. This approach provides a scalable method for improving training data quality, which is critical for developing robust models in data-intensive domains.

On the **algorithmic design front**, the systematic exploration yielded several concrete and effective practices. The identification of interleaved textual encoding as a simple yet highly effective timestamp representation method provides a practical guideline for MLLM architecture design. The finding that a pure thinking-free reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) approach is superior for VTG, in terms of both performance and efficiency, challenges previous assumptions regarding the necessity of explicit reasoning or a preceding supervised fine-tuning phase. Additionally, the insights into RLVR training recipes, such as early stopping based on reward plateaus and difficulty-based data sampling, offer actionable strategies for optimizing training processes, potentially reducing computational costs and improving final model performance.

The **TimeLens models** themselves, demonstrating state-of-the-art VTG performance among open-source models and surpassing several proprietary models, serve as concrete exemplars of the effectiveness of the proposed data and algorithmic principles. By open-sourcing the code, data, and models, the research aims to facilitate broader adoption of these best practices and accelerate future advancements in building MLLMs with strong temporal video grounding capabilities.

The potential impact extends to various applications requiring precise temporal localization, such as video editing, content retrieval, surveillance, and human-computer interaction, where accurate "when" information is crucial. By establishing a solid foundation in both data and algorithmic design, TimeLens contributes to the development of more capable and reliable video understanding systems.
