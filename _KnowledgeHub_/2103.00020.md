# Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Here's a single-sentence breakdown of this paper followed by the requested format:

## Problem

- Existing computer vision models are limited by requiring task-specific training and labeled datasets
- Natural language supervision could enable more general and flexible visual models but has struggled to match supervised performance
- Current methods for learning from natural language supervision have been held back by scale

## Method

- Train a large model (==CLIP==) to predict accurate pairings of images and text from ==400M pairs== collected from the internet
- Use a ==contrastive learning== approach that is more efficient than other language-based training methods
- Enable ==zero-shot transfer== by having the model make predictions using natural language text descriptions

## Results

- Zero-shot CLIP matches or outperforms supervised baselines on many vision benchmarks
- Linear probes on CLIP features outperform supervised models on **21** of **27** datasets
- Significant improvements in robustness to distribution shifts (**75%** reduction in performance drop)
- Strong performance on diverse tasks including OCR, action recognition, and geo-location
- Effective zero-shot transfer across broad range of vision tasks

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language provides a more flexible and generalizable form of supervision than traditional labeled datasets
> - Training at large scale is critical for good performance - model capabilities improve smoothly with compute
> - Zero-shot CLIP models are more robust to distribution shifts than supervised models, suggesting more generalizable learning
> - Language supervision enables wider range of capabilities (OCR, geo-location, etc) in single model
