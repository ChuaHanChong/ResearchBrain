# Random Attention: Rethinking KV Cache Eviction for Efficient Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Random Attention is a KV cache eviction policy for long reasoning generations that never evicts prompt tokens and gives every other cached position an i.i.d. uniform score, keeping the top-K independently within each KV head, computing no importance score at all. Across four models and six reasoning tasks at about 4x compression it matches the strongest scored evictor while serving 32-43% higher vLLM throughput.

## Problem

- Every prior eviction method shares one paradigm: score each cached token by estimated future importance and keep the top-scorers, a premise never tested against a no-signal control.
- Baselines differ silently in whether they pin the prompt or leave it to the score, so cross-paper comparisons confound protection regime with selection quality.
- Earlier reports that random retention falls far behind scored selection used random baselines that lost the prompt, while scoring passes also cost throughput under paged serving.

## Method

- Protect the question: all prefill positions (system prompt, chat template, question) get ==score +infinity== and are never evicted, separating irreplaceable input from the self-restating generated trace.
- Scatter the rest per head: remaining positions get ==i.i.d. Uniform(0,1) scores== drawn independently in every ==KV head== each eviction, so cost is one rand plus one ==topk==.
- Two diagnostics: a ==prompt-protection retrofit== applied uniformly to all baselines, and a ==planted-fact probe== pinning a synthetic fact in chosen heads, scored by graded log-probability recall.

## Results

- At about **4x** compression Random Attention is significantly ahead in **31 of 60** baseline cells and behind in one; on Qwen3-4B MATH500 it scores **0.874** versus SnapKV **0.703**.
- vLLM serving on one H200 reaches **1.6-2.7x** full-attention throughput, **32-43%** above TriAttention, since one eviction round costs **0.30 ms** against **1.47-1.64 ms** with scoring.
- Forcing prompt protection lifts SnapKV up to **+22.5 points** and a recency window **+59.7**, collapsing baseline spread to **2.2 points**; a planted fact is retrieved **3%** from one head, **99%** from eight.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An evictor's accuracy is decided by what it protects, not by how it ranks the rest, so protection regime must be matched before crediting any selection signal.
> - Self-generated reasoning traces are redundant both in text and across attention heads, so independent per-head random draws preserve some usable copy of whatever the model restates.
> - Caution: signals still matter for facts stated once and never restated, where an attention-accumulating score retrieves a planted passcode 84% of the time versus 0% for random.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) often generate extended "chains of thought" for complex reasoning tasks, leading to very long output sequences. This process creates a significant memory bottleneck due to the Key-Value (KV) cache, which stores activations from previous tokens. As the generation length increases, the KV cache grows linearly, eventually exceeding available memory resources.

The prevailing paradigm for addressing this KV cache bottleneck has been through eviction strategies. Existing methods typically involve assigning a "score" to each cached token, estimating its future importance, and then retaining the top-scoring tokens while discarding the rest. This approach has led to a series of innovations, with researchers developing increasingly sophisticated scoring mechanisms based on factors such as accumulated attention, attention from recent windows, redundancy, value magnitude, and position-dependent key statistics. Each new method aims to improve accuracy under compression by refining these selection signals.

This work challenges the fundamental premise that a complex selection signal is necessary for effective KV cache eviction in reasoning tasks. It differentiates itself from prior work by proposing a signal-free approach and then providing mechanistic explanations for its effectiveness. The research also distinguishes between KV cache eviction (which permanently discards tokens to bound memory) and sparse-attention selection (which attends to a subset of tokens but keeps all in memory), focusing exclusively on eviction. It also draws a crucial distinction between long-context understanding (long input, short output) and long reasoning (short input, long output, which is the focus here).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to address the severe memory bottleneck imposed by the KV cache during the generation of long reasoning chains by LLMs. While sophisticated scoring methods have been developed, the authors hypothesize that the perceived importance of these selection signals might be overstated.

The key objectives of this study are:

1. **To empirically test the necessity of a selection signal:** The core objective is to directly evaluate whether complex scoring mechanisms, which form the basis of most existing KV cache eviction strategies, genuinely contribute to task accuracy under memory constraints. The authors aim to demonstrate that a simpler, signal-free approach can achieve comparable or superior performance.
2. **To propose and evaluate "Random Attention"**: The work introduces a novel, minimalist eviction policy, termed "Random Attention." This policy involves keeping the initial prompt tokens and then uniformly randomly evicting tokens within each attention head for the rest of the generation. A significant objective is to evaluate its performance against state-of-the-art signal-based evictors across a range of LLMs and reasoning tasks.
3. **To provide mechanistic explanations for the effectiveness of Random Attention**: Beyond demonstrating its performance, a crucial objective is to understand *why* a random eviction strategy, particularly when combined with prompt protection, is effective. This involves investigating two specific hypotheses:
 - The prompt is the "fragile" part of the cache, meaning its preservation is critical for task performance, irrespective of how other tokens are managed.
 - The model's internal reasoning trace (working state) is inherently redundant, both through textual restatement and across different attention heads, making it robust to random token loss.
4. **To assess the efficiency benefits in deployment**: A practical objective is to quantify the computational efficiency gains of a signal-free method like Random Attention, particularly in real-world serving environments (e.g., vLLM deployment), where avoiding a scoring pass could lead to significant throughput improvements.
5. **To redirect future research in KV cache eviction**: Based on its findings, the research aims to shift the focus of KV cache eviction studies from continually optimizing scoring signals to instead concentrating on areas where protection strategies truly matter, such as budgeting for very long prompts and recovering rare, non-redundant facts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study focuses on KV cache eviction during the decode phase, specifically for reasoning models generating long sequences from short prompts. Eviction decisions are made periodically: once the cache exceeds a budget `K` plus a buffer `r` (typically the `r` most recent tokens), tokens are scored, and the `K` highest-scoring candidates are retained. This process is independent across layers and KV heads.

**Baselines as Choices of Scoring Function (s):**
The research compares Random Attention against several prominent signal-based eviction methods:
- **StreamingLLM (H2O):** Keeps initial attention-sink positions and a recent window. H2O specifically scores by accumulated attention received since a token entered the cache.
- **SnapKV:** Uses attention from a recent window of queries (last `w` queries), max-pooled over neighboring positions.
- **R-KV:** Combines the SnapKV score with a redundancy term (inverse of mean cosine similarity to other keys) to discourage keeping duplicate information.
- **VaSE:** Scores values by their magnitude (max-min range) and fills remaining slots stochastically based on SnapKV scores.
- **TriAttention:** Scores positions based on their distance from the current query using a calibrated trigonometric series, combined with key norm.

**Random Attention Implementation:**
Random Attention is defined by two structural choices:
1. **Protect the question:** The entire prompt (positions 1 to `ℓp`) is explicitly pinned and never evicted.
2. **Scatter the rest, per head:** All other cached positions receive an independent and identically distributed uniform random score. Each KV head then independently keeps its `K` top-scoring (random) positions.
Algorithm 1 formally outlines this: generate random scores, force-set prompt scores to infinity, and then perform a `top-K` selection. This design makes Random Attention a "null hypothesis" for signal-based selectors.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B, and Phi-4-reasoning (14B).
- **Tasks:** Six reasoning tasks covering math (MATH500, AIME, HMMT), science (GPQA-Diamond), and code (LiveCodeBench-v6 medium).
- **Metrics:** Primary metric is accuracy (correct final boxed answer). For significance, paired, problem-clustered percentile bootstrap (95% CI) and an exact sign test are used. Serving throughput (tokens/s) is also measured.
- **Compression:** Tasks are evaluated at approximately 4x compression of typical traces (3x for LiveCodeBench), with a maximum generation length of 32,768 tokens. The per-head budget `K` varies by task.
- **Hardware:** Accuracy generation on NVIDIA H200 nodes; efficiency measurements exclusively on single H200 GPUs.

**Controlled Experiments for Explanation:**
1. **Prompt Protection Analysis (§5.1):** To isolate the effect of prompt protection, methods (SnapKV, R-KV, VaSE, Random Attention, Recency Window) are re-evaluated under a consistent rule: explicitly protect the entire prompt. The change in accuracy for each method is observed, correlating it with how much of the prompt each method's score *naturally* retained in its original form.
2. **Working State Redundancy Probe (§5.2):** A "planted-fact" experiment is conducted: synthetic facts are inserted into real MATH500 reasoning traces, with a question requiring the fact appended later. The experiment controls which KV heads retain the fact, while other heads and all other tokens undergo standard uniform eviction. Metrics include direct retrieval (fraction of traces reproducing the value) and a graded recall (normalized log-probability gain of correct value). This probes how the model utilizes redundancy across heads and if the shape of the surviving copies matters.
3. **Rare Fact Test (§5.3):** A "passcode" (a unique, once-stated fact) is planted far (57 compression rounds) before the question. The ability of various eviction policies to retrieve this non-redundant information is tested, measuring retrieval rate and log-probability. This assesses the "needle-finding" capability where redundancy is absent.

**Efficiency Evaluation (§6):**
- **vLLM with PagedAttention:** Compares Random Attention and TriAttention on one H200, simulating a serving environment with 128 concurrent requests, 1k-token prompts, and 32k-token generations. This setup uses vLLM's existing compression integration.
- **Batched Decoding (HuggingFace Transformers with FlashAttention-2):** Compares all methods by finding the largest batch size each method can fit on a single 143 GB H200 GPU at `K=3072` and 32k generations. This evaluates raw memory footprint and throughput without paging overheads.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Accuracy Performance (Main Results)

- **Overall Competitiveness**: Random Attention demonstrates performance comparable to or exceeding the strongest prior evictors across four models (Qwen3-4B, 14B, 32B, Phi-4-reasoning) and six reasoning tasks. It was significantly ahead in 31 out of 60 baseline comparisons in the main result table and significantly behind in only one instance (TriAttention on Qwen3-32B for LiveCodeBench).
- **Math and Science Tasks**: On MATH500 and GPQA-D, Random Attention consistently outperformed SnapKV and VaSE significantly and R-KV significantly on Qwen3-4B. No signal-based selector significantly surpassed Random Attention on these tasks. Similarly, on competition math tasks (AIME, HMMT), Random Attention maintained parity with the best baselines, outperforming SnapKV, R-KV, and VaSE in several instances, with no selector significantly ahead of it.
- **Code Reasoning (LiveCodeBench)**: This task exhibited larger performance gaps. Without explicit prompt protection, SnapKV and VaSE experienced substantial accuracy drops (20-35 points and ~29 points respectively). Random Attention, which inherently protects the full prompt, performed strongly here, tying with TriAttention on smaller models. TriAttention showed a slight, significant lead (approximately three points) over Random Attention on Qwen3-32B for LiveCodeBench, attributed by the authors to prompt length management rather than selection signal quality.
- **Compression Pressure**: As the compression factor increased (from 2x to 16x), Random Attention maintained its competitive edge, often performing comparably to TriAttention and widening its gap against VaSE and other baselines.

#### 4.2. Explaining the Effectiveness: The Role of Prompt and Redundancy

- **The Prompt is Fragile**: Experiments confirmed that the prompt is the most critical and vulnerable part of the KV cache. Methods that failed to adequately preserve the prompt (e.g., SnapKV, VaSE, Recency window, and Random Attention *without* explicit prompt pinning) experienced substantial performance degradation. Once all methods were given the explicit rule to keep the entire prompt, most of the performance differences between them diminished significantly. For instance, SnapKV and VaSE gained up to 22.5 and 10.2 points respectively on Phi-4-reasoning GPQA-D by protecting the prompt. This suggests that much of the accuracy variance among prior evictors was driven by their implicit handling of the prompt rather than the sophistication of their selection signals. With explicit prompt protection, Random Attention often performed as well as, or better than, other signal-based methods.
- **Working State Protects Itself via Redundancy**: The model's reasoning trace (working state) demonstrates inherent redundancy at two levels:
 - **Textual Redundancy**: LLMs often restate or rephrase intermediate reasoning steps, providing multiple textual copies of important values within the generated sequence.
 - **Cross-Head Redundancy**: Each KV head maintains its own copy of every token. Eviction decisions are made per head, meaning a token is truly lost only if *all* heads discard it.
 - **Planted-Fact Probe Results**: A synthetic fact insertion experiment revealed that copies of facts pool across heads in a strongly superadditive manner; a fact held in one head was rarely retrieved, but held in several, it almost always was (e.g., two heads yielding 60% retrieval vs. 1-3% for single best heads). The *shape* of the surviving copies (contiguous vs. scattered tokens) did not significantly impact retrieval or recall. This indicates that the model can utilize a needed value as long as *some* usable copy survives *somewhere* across the heads. This inherent redundancy makes the reasoning trace robust to random eviction, as a random draw is likely to retain enough copies across the numerous independent heads.

#### 4.3. Limitations: What a Selection Signal Still Buys

- While random eviction excels with redundant information, it fails when presented with a "needle-finding" task: a fact stated once, never restated, and needed much later. A passcode experiment showed Random Attention unable to retrieve such a fact (0% retrieval), whereas R-KV, which accumulates attention over history, retrieved it 84% of the time. This highlights that content-dependent signals can be superior for extremely rare, non-redundant information. However, the authors argue that such scenarios are rare in actual reasoning traces, as models tend to restate what they are actively using.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Performance

- **vLLM Serving Throughput**: In a vLLM deployment setting, Random Attention delivered 32–43% higher tokens per second throughput than TriAttention, the strongest baseline with a vLLM port. Random Attention achieved 1.6–2.7x full-attention throughput across models, primarily because it incurs no computational overhead for a scoring pass. This margin holds at different operating points and loads.
- **Equal Memory Throughput (Unpaged)**: When comparing methods at the largest batch size fitting a single H200 GPU (without paging), Random Attention achieved 8.8–10.0x full-attention throughput. It fit the largest batch and had the smallest peak memory footprint. The throughput advantage over other evictors in this setting directly correlates with the absence of a scoring pass.
- **Cost of Scoring Pass**: Although an individual eviction round's scoring pass is nominally fast (e.g., 1.47-1.64 ms for TriAttention vs. 0.30 ms for Random Attention compaction), in a batched serving environment with many concurrent requests, these small costs accumulate and are multiplied by synchronization points, leading to significant latency and reduced throughput. Content-dependent scoring is particularly expensive under paged serving as it requires additional passes or kernel modifications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this research have two main implications for the field of LLM efficiency and KV cache management:

#### 5.1. Practical Significance

- **Deployable and Efficient Baseline:** Random Attention emerges as a highly practical and deployable KV cache eviction method. It requires no calibration, tuning, or complex scoring passes, simplifying implementation and reducing computational overhead. Its measured efficiency, delivering 32-43% higher throughput than the strongest prior evictor in vLLM deployment while maintaining comparable accuracy, makes it a compelling default for serving reasoning models under memory constraints.
- **New Benchmark for Future Work:** By establishing that a signal-free, prompt-protected random eviction strategy can match or exceed the performance of sophisticated signal-based methods, Random Attention sets a new and challenging baseline. Any new eviction technique claiming superiority must now demonstrate significant gains over Random Attention at matched budget and prompt protection.

#### 5.2. Redirecting Research Focus

- **Shift from Scoring to Protection:** The research fundamentally challenges the long-held assumption that optimizing selection signals is the primary driver of eviction accuracy. Instead, it highlights that the accuracy of an evictor is predominantly determined by *what it protects* (primarily the prompt) rather than *how it ranks the rest*. This insight redirects research efforts away from incrementally improving scoring functions.
- **New Open Questions:** The work identifies critical areas for future investigation:
 - **Budgeting for Long Prompts:** Developing more nuanced strategies for protecting or compressing very long prompts, especially in code tasks where prompts can consume a substantial portion of the cache budget, rather than simply pinning them entirely.
 - **Recovering Rare, Non-Redundant Facts:** Exploring specific, content-dependent mechanisms or architectures that can effectively preserve rare, once-stated facts that are needed much later, as these are the only cases where a purely random eviction policy demonstrably falls short.
 - **Understanding Redundancy Mechanisms:** Further investigating how models generate and utilize redundancy in their reasoning traces, and how this redundancy can be leveraged in cache management.

In essence, this work simplifies the problem of efficient reasoning in LLMs by demonstrating the surprising efficacy of a random, yet structurally sound, KV cache eviction strategy, thereby streamlining deployment and refocusing future research on more impactful challenges.
