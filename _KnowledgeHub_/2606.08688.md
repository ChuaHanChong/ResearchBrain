# PhysAgent: Automating Physics-Based 4D Synthesis via Trajectory-Grounded Multi-Agent Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysAgent introduces a multi-agent framework that automates the configuration of environmental force fields for physics-based 4D synthesis, using large language models grounded by trajectory-based visual feedback from a Material Point Method simulator. This approach generates dynamic 3D scenes with enhanced physical plausibility and semantic alignment, outperforming prior methods in both quantitative metrics like CLIPsim and user preference for realism.

## Problem

- Existing physics-based 4D synthesis methods require manual configuration of complex environmental force fields, limiting scalability and diversity of generated content.
- Automated optimization techniques (e.g., Score Distillation Sampling) for force fields are computationally expensive, prone to local optima, and incapable of dynamically switching discrete force types.
- Directly applying Large Language Models (LLMs) to physical simulation without continuous feedback leads to 'physical hallucinations' due to a significant modality gap between discrete semantic instructions and continuous physical states.

## Method

- A '==simulator-in-the-loop==' multi-agent framework automates force field configuration by decoupling intrinsic material properties (from an image) from extrinsic dynamic parameters (from a text prompt).
- A Semantic Agent generates initial force field configurations guided by an externalized 'Force Field Skill module,' while Refine Agents iteratively optimize parameters using reasoning-driven updates.
- A trajectory-grounded visual perception module translates simulator outputs (2D video and depth) into structured textual descriptors (e.g., 3D trajectories, displacements) to provide continuous physical feedback to the LLM agents.

## Results

- Achieved the highest CLIPsim scores and User Preference Ratings across diverse force types (drop, stretching, sway), demonstrating superior semantic alignment and physical realism compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Generated dynamic 3D scenes with 'exceptionally smooth and physically plausible deformations' and 'highly realistic dynamic simulation sequences,' effectively mitigating severe physical distortions observed in other methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the Force Field Skill Library in preventing 'severe physical parameter hallucinations' and substantiated that Refine Agents with trajectory-grounded feedback are essential for high-fidelity physical synthesis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling intrinsic material properties from extrinsic dynamic environmental forces is crucial for focused and effective automation of complex scene dynamics.
> - Combining LLM's semantic reasoning with continuous, trajectory-based physical feedback effectively bridges the modality gap between discrete language and continuous physical states, mitigating 'physical hallucinations.'
> - LLM-driven 'zero-shot macroscopic leaps' in the force field parameter space enable dynamic switching of discrete force types and efficient escape from local optima, a limitation for gradient-based methods.
