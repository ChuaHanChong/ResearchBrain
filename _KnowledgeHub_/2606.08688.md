# PhysAgent: Automating Physics-Based 4D Synthesis via Trajectory-Grounded Multi-Agent Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysAgent introduces a multi-agent framework that automates the configuration of environmental force fields for physics-based 4D synthesis, using large language models grounded by trajectory-based visual feedback from a Material Point Method simulator. This approach generates dynamic 3D scenes with enhanced physical plausibility and semantic alignment, outperforming prior methods in both quantitative metrics like CLIPsim and user preference for realism.

## Problem

- Existing physics-based 4D synthesis methods require manual configuration of complex environmental force fields, limiting scalability and diversity of generated content.
- Automated optimization techniques (e.g., Score Distillation Sampling) for force fields are computationally expensive, prone to local optima, and incapable of dynamically switching discrete force types.
- Directly applying Large Language Models (LLMs) to physical simulation without continuous feedback leads to 'physical hallucinations' due to a significant modality gap between discrete semantic instructions and continuous physical states.

## Method

- A '==simulator-in-the-loop==' multi-agent framework automates force field configuration by decoupling intrinsic material properties (from an image) from extrinsic dynamic parameters (from a text prompt).
- A Semantic Agent generates initial force field configurations guided by an externalized 'Force Field Skill module,' while Refine Agents iteratively optimize parameters using reasoning-driven updates.
- A trajectory-grounded visual perception module translates simulator outputs (2D video and depth) into structured textual descriptors (e.g., 3D trajectories, displacements) to provide continuous physical feedback to the LLM agents.

## Results

- Achieved the highest CLIPsim scores and User Preference Ratings across diverse force types (drop, stretching, sway), demonstrating superior semantic alignment and physical realism compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Generated dynamic 3D scenes with 'exceptionally smooth and physically plausible deformations' and 'highly realistic dynamic simulation sequences,' effectively mitigating severe physical distortions observed in other methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the Force Field Skill Library in preventing 'severe physical parameter hallucinations' and substantiated that Refine Agents with trajectory-grounded feedback are essential for high-fidelity physical synthesis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling intrinsic material properties from extrinsic dynamic environmental forces is crucial for focused and effective automation of complex scene dynamics.
> - Combining LLM's semantic reasoning with continuous, trajectory-based physical feedback effectively bridges the modality gap between discrete language and continuous physical states, mitigating 'physical hallucinations.'
> - LLM-driven 'zero-shot macroscopic leaps' in the force field parameter space enable dynamic switching of discrete force types and efficient escape from local optima, a limitation for gradient-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 4D synthesis, which involves generating dynamic 3D scenes over time, is a significant area within computer graphics and generative AI. Recent advancements, particularly those leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have focused on data-driven approaches. These typically involve training generative models on 3D representations or distilling priors from 2D diffusion models. However, such methods often lack explicit physical modeling, leading to "physical hallucinations" – visually coherent but physically implausible dynamics.

To address this, a sub-field has emerged focusing on *physics-grounded* 4D Gaussian Splatting, often coupling physical simulators like the Material Point Method (MPM) with 3DGS. Early works in this area, such as PhysGaussian, demonstrated the potential but required manual parameter tuning, limiting scalability. Subsequent research employed Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to automate object material learning. While these methods improved the automation of intrinsic material properties, they typically suffer from high computational costs due to gradient backpropagation, are prone to local optima, and are mathematically constrained from dynamically switching discrete variables, such as different types of force fields. Crucially, these approaches predominantly focus on intrinsic material properties and largely overlook the configuration of extrinsic environmental force fields.

Concurrently, the development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has enabled autonomous agents for tasks like code generation and high-level 3D layout generation. However, applying LLMs directly to complex continuum mechanics, like MPM, has been challenging due to a significant modality gap between discrete semantic instructions and continuous physical states. Naive LLM prompting, without simulation feedback, has resulted in "physical hallucinations" as these "blind" agents cannot comprehend trajectory deviations or the complex numerical characteristics of MPM.

PhysAgent is positioned to bridge these gaps by introducing a simulator-in-the-loop multi-agent framework. It specifically targets the automation of complex environmental force fields, which existing physics-based methods largely neglect, and integrates LLM reasoning with continuous visual feedback to overcome the limitations of both SDS-based optimization and ungrounded LLM prompting. This work aims to achieve fully automated, physically plausible, and diverse 4D synthesis by decoupling intrinsic materials from extrinsic dynamics and leveraging a novel feedback mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to achieve fully automated and physically plausible 4D motion synthesis. The authors identify a critical bottleneck in existing physics-based 4D synthesis methods: the manual configuration of complex environmental force fields. While progress has been made in optimizing geometric and material properties of individual objects, the dynamic evolution of diverse scenes is fundamentally governed by these external forces, which currently rely heavily on manual expert intervention. This manual process severely limits the scalability and diversity of generated simulation data.

The motivation for PhysAgent stems from the identified limitations of current automated approaches when applied to the vast and complex space of force field optimization:
1. **Modality Gap and Technical Flaws in Existing Methods**:
 - **Score Distillation Sampling (SDS)-based methods**: These methods, while used for material optimization, are deemed insufficient for force field optimization. They rely on sluggish gradient backpropagation, are susceptible to local optima, and are inherently incapable of dynamically switching discrete force fields (e.g., toggling different types of wind or gravity).
 - **Naive Large Language Models (LLMs)**: Attempts to directly map high-level user intent to physical parameters using LLMs without simulation feedback fail to grasp the complex physical dimensions of Material Point Method (MPM) and its underlying mechanisms. This results in severe physical inaccuracies and distortions, as LLMs cannot interpret or respond to the physical consequences of their generated parameters.
2. **Need for Decoupled Optimization**: Existing methods often confound intrinsic material properties with extrinsic dynamic parameters. The authors argue for decoupling these two aspects, allowing the framework to focus specifically on automating the configuration of environmental force fields driven by text prompts, while leveraging pre-acquired material priors from images.
3. **Desire for Global Reasoning and Dynamic Adaptability**: Traditional gradient-based methods are limited to local updates, making it difficult to escape suboptimal configurations or make significant, non-continuous changes. The motivation is to introduce a mechanism that can leverage LLM's "commonsense reasoning" to execute "zero-shot macroscopic leaps," thereby bypassing local optima and dynamically switching discrete force field types.
4. **Requirement for Physically Grounded Feedback**: To overcome the "physical hallucinations" of ungrounded LLMs, the framework must incorporate a robust mechanism for obtaining continuous physical feedback from the simulation, translating explicit motion trajectories into structured descriptors that LLMs can process.

In summary, the primary objective is to develop a novel framework that can reliably translate user intent into dynamic environmental force field configurations for 4D synthesis, overcoming the computational rigidity of backpropagation and the physical inaccuracies of ungrounded LLMs by integrating a simulator-in-the-loop multi-agent system with multimodal inputs and trajectory-grounded feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysAgent operates as a "simulator-in-the-loop" multi-agent framework designed to convert multimodal inputs (an image and a text prompt) into physically grounded 4D motion videos. The methodology decouples intrinsic material properties from extrinsic dynamics, with a focus on automating environmental force field configurations.

The framework comprises several key components and a closed-loop optimization process:

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The system aims to find an optimal force field configuration (Θ*) that maximizes semantic and physical alignment with the user's text intent (P_text), given an initial image (P_image). P_image provides initial geometry and material priors (Φ_PhysGM), while P_text guides the dynamic evolution. Spatial anchoring (s0) links 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations to the Material Point Method (MPM) simulation grid. The optimization is semantic and gradient-free, formulated as `Θ* = arg max_Θ ALLM ( E_visual ( Sim(s0, Θ, Φ_PhysGM) ), P_text )`, where Sim is the MPM simulator, E_visual is the visual perception module, and A_LLM is the alignment function of the Refine Agents.

#### 3.2. Text-Driven Semantic Agent

This agent interprets the user's text instruction (P_text) and, guided by an externalized "Force Field Skill module," generates an initial, physically plausible force field configuration.
- **Force Field Skill Module**: This module explicitly defines the taxonomy, mathematical formulations, and operational constraints for various macroscopic forces (e.g., translation, scale, impulse, torque). It includes dimensional scaling factors (k_type) to bridge LLM outputs with physical units.
- **Semantic Compiler**: The Semantic Agent acts as a compiler, querying the skill library to produce a structured JSON output that describes discrete force types and their continuous parameters.
- **Deterministic Configuration Fusion**: The generated force field JSON is fused with four other components to create a complete initial simulation environment (Θ0): (1) 3DGS representation, (2) intrinsic materials (Φ_PhysGM) from P_image (via PhysGM), and (3) Gaussian spatial coordinate anchors (s0). This ensures a physically compliant and spatially accurate initial state.

#### 3.3. Forward Physical Simulation via Material Point Method (MPM)

MPM serves as the forward execution engine, simulating realistic 4D dynamics from the current configuration (Θ_k).
- **Hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian Approach**: MPM discretizes continuum bodies into Lagrangian particles (carrying physical properties like mass, position, velocity, deformation gradient) and uses an Eulerian grid for computation.
- **Particle-to-Grid (P2G) Transfer**: Particle mass and momentum are mapped to grid nodes using interpolation shape functions, employing the Affine Particle-in-Cell (APIC) transfer scheme to preserve angular momentum.
- **Grid Momentum Update**: Nodal velocities are updated based on Newton's second law, incorporating internal elastic forces (f_int) and external environmental forces (f_ext(Θ_k)) configured by the LLM agents.
- **Constitutive Models**: Supports various elastoplastic constitutive models (e.g., St. Venant-Kirchhoff, Neo-Hookean, Von Mises with Damage Softening, Drucker-Prager) to simulate diverse materials.
- **Grid-to-Particle (G2P) Transfer**: Updated grid velocities and positions are interpolated back to advance particle states, and the deformation gradient is advected.
- **2D Video Rendering**: The simulator drives the 3DGS to render a 2D physical video sequence (V).

#### 3.4. Trajectory-Grounded Multi-agent Feedback and Optimization

This is the core closed-loop refinement mechanism, iteratively updating the force field configuration.
- **Trajectory-Grounded Visual Perception**:
 - **Black-Box Simulator Treatment**: The simulator is treated as a 2D renderer to bridge the sim-to-real gap and avoid exposing dense 3D particle data directly to the LLM.
 - **Vision Foundation Models**: SAM 2 generates frame-wise binary masks. Depth Anything V3 extracts dense monocular depth maps. CoTracker3 tracks unprojected 2D features over time.
 - **3D Reconstruction**: Camera intrinsics (K) and depth maps are used to lift masked 2D features to 3D coordinates, yielding consistent 3D trajectories (T).
 - **Structured Motion Information (F_k)**: These macroscopic trajectories are synthesized into concise textual descriptors (e.g., centroid displacement, 3D rotation, bounding box volume scaling), serving as "visual loss" for the Refine Agents.
- **Reasoning-Driven Configuration Refinement (Refine Agents)**: This module acts as the "optimizer" and is composed of two agents using Qwen3.5 9B as their backbone.
 - **Refine Planning Agent**: Evaluates the alignment between the extracted motion (F_k) and the original text intent (P_text) using physical commonsense reasoning. If alignment is sufficient, simulation terminates; otherwise, it formulates targeted text-based feedback and optimization instructions.
 - **Refine Execution Agent**: Updates the configuration (Θ_k) based on the Planning Agent's feedback. Unlike gradient-based methods, it performs "zero-shot macroscopic leaps" via a semantic mapping function (G_LLM), allowing it to dynamically switch discrete force field types and escape local optima by evaluating causal relationships under physical constraints. A "Hard-Rule Prioritization protocol" is enforced during JSON rewrite to prevent physical hallucinations, mandating directional rigidity, skill-specific constraints, and a fallback protocol.
- **Iteration Limit**: The refinement loop is empirically capped at 3 iterations to balance physical fidelity with computational efficiency.

The overall approach emphasizes decoupling, physically grounded initialization, black-box simulation with visual perception, and LLM-driven causal reasoning for robust and efficient force field optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to demonstrate PhysAgent's superiority in generation diversity and physical accuracy compared to existing methods, as well as to validate the contribution of its core components through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Implementation Details

PhysAgent was implemented in PyTorch. 3DGS representations and intrinsic material properties were initialized using PhysGM. MPM simulations were performed on a 100³ background grid with up to 200,000 particles. Dynamic sequences consisted of 50 frames with a frame interval (∆t_frame) of 4 × 10⁻² seconds, while the MPM simulator operated at a finer sub-step (∆t_sub) of 2 × 10⁻⁴ seconds for numerical stability. Qwen3.5 9B served as the backbone for both Semantic and Refine Agents. Visual perception utilized pre-trained SAM 2, Depth Anything V3, and CoTracker3. All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 5000 GPU.

#### 4.2. Datasets

Two datasets were used for evaluation:
- **PhysGaussian**: Static scenes were used to provide a fair baseline comparison, with custom text prompts.
- **Objaverse**: A subset of 3D models was curated to evaluate generalization, accepting joint multimodal inputs (reference images and custom text prompts).

#### 4.3. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

PhysAgent was compared against recent physics-based 4D synthesis methods: PhysSplat, DreamPhysics, and OmniPhysgs. Given that baselines primarily focus on intrinsic material optimization, two evaluation strategies were employed:
1. **Expert-Crafted vs. Auto-Generated**: Comparing PhysAgent's autonomously generated force fields against manually crafted force field configurations from baseline domain experts (denoted as "-g").
2. **Adapted Optimization vs. Multi-Agent Reasoning**: Adapting baseline material optimization techniques (e.g., SDS) to the environmental force field optimization space (denoted as "-f").
A third setting, "+PhysAgent", integrated PhysAgent's force field generation into baselines.

**Metrics**:
- **CLIPsim**: Used to assess semantic alignment between the user’s text prompt and the generated dynamic video (higher is better).
- **User Preference Rating (UPR)**: A 3-Alternative Forced-Choice (3AFC) user study with 20 expert researchers to evaluate physical realism and visual naturalness (higher is better).

**Qualitative Results (Figure 3 & 4)**:
- PhysAgent successfully synthesized "highly realistic dynamic simulation sequences" from diverse multimodal inputs, exhibiting "exceptionally smooth and physically plausible deformations."
- Baseline methods, especially those relying on SDS or open-loop generation, showed significant limitations with complex multimodal instructions, frequently suffering from "severe physical distortions, manifesting as erroneous force applications or implausible physical phenomena." This was attributed to their lack of explicit physical constraints on force field parameters and difficulty with discrete decision attributes (e.g., force directions).

**Quantitative Results (Table 1)**:
- PhysAgent consistently achieved the highest CLIPsim and UPR scores across all evaluated force types (drop, stretching, sway).
- Notably, PhysAgent demonstrated substantial gains in complex interactive tasks like "Stretching" and "Sway," supporting the effectiveness of trajectory-grounded feedback in mitigating physical hallucinations.
- Baselines underperformed due to: (1) Expert annotations ("-g") falling into sub-optimal states due to human subjectivity and lack of iterative feedback. (2) Traditional SDS optimization's susceptibility to local optima for discrete force attributes. (3) Naive open-loop LLMs suffering from physical hallucinations without explicit simulator grounding. PhysAgent's LLM global reasoning and rigorous simulator feedback addressed these limitations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies investigated the contributions of the Force Field Skill Library and Refine Agents.

**Necessity of the Force Field Skill Library (Table 2)**:
- Removing the externalized Force Field Skill module ("w/o Skill") led to a "precipitous degradation" in overall system performance.
- This indicates that, despite LLM reasoning capabilities, the absence of explicit physical rule constraints and defined parameter spaces results in "severe physical parameter hallucinations," making the model unable to correctly configure MPM parameters.

**Critical Role of the Refine Agents (Table 2, Figure 5)**:
- Eliminating the Refine Agents ("w/o Refine Agents") degraded the framework to a naive open-loop generation paradigm.
- Qualitatively, relying solely on the Semantic Agent's initial predictions ("Before") often resulted in "anomalous kinematic drifting and suboptimal dynamics."
- Quantitatively, integrating the Refine Agent ("After") yielded substantial improvements across all evaluation metrics.
- This outcome substantiates that "trajectory-grounded feedback" is crucial for high-fidelity physical synthesis, enabling continuous rectification of initial semantic predictions within the continuous physical space.
- Furthermore, analysis of refinement iterations (Table 3) showed that performance gains plateaued after the 3rd iteration, justifying the empirically set limit of 3 iterations for computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysAgent represents a significant contribution to the field of 4D physics synthesis by directly addressing the automation of extrinsic environmental force fields. This capability fills a critical gap, as previous physics-based methods largely focused on intrinsic material properties, leaving the configuration of scene dynamics to manual intervention. By automating this aspect, the framework removes a major bottleneck for generating large-scale, diverse, and physically plausible simulation data, which is essential for applications like embodied AI and advanced graphics.

The work's core innovation lies in its "simulator-in-the-loop" multi-agent framework. This approach combines the semantic reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with precise visual feedback from a physical simulator. This integration allows PhysAgent to overcome key limitations of existing paradigms:
- **Bypassing Gradient-Based Limitations**: Unlike traditional Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) methods that rely on sluggish gradient backpropagation, PhysAgent's LLM-driven "zero-shot macroscopic leaps" enable it to escape local optima and dynamically switch discrete force field types. This capability significantly improves efficiency and flexibility in exploring the complex force field parameter space.
- **Mitigating LLM Hallucinations**: By grounding LLM reasoning with real-time, trajectory-based visual feedback, PhysAgent effectively addresses the "physical hallucinations" that arise when LLMs operate without an understanding of continuous physical states. The system's ability to convert explicit motion trajectories into structured textual descriptors for agent analysis creates a robust feedback loop that ensures physical accuracy.
- **Theoretical Rigor and Compatibility**: The framework's explicit decoupling of image-derived intrinsic material priors from text-driven extrinsic dynamics ensures theoretical rigor. This design choice also makes PhysAgent inherently compatible with existing material-centric methods, offering a pathway for more comprehensive 4D synthesis solutions.

The potential impact of PhysAgent is multifaceted:
- **Accelerated Content Creation**: It could significantly accelerate the creation of dynamic virtual content for gaming, film production, and virtual reality by automating complex physical simulations.
- **Enhanced Embodied AI and Robotics**: Generating diverse, physically realistic training data is crucial for embodied AI and robotics. PhysAgent's ability to create varied force field scenarios could provide richer environments for training agents to interact with the physical world.
- **Scientific Simulation and Research**: The automated generation of physically consistent simulations could benefit scientific research by allowing researchers to rapidly prototype and test hypotheses in various physical scenarios without extensive manual configuration.
- **Foundation for Future AI Models**: The authors envision distilling this "simulator-in-the-loop" framework into a unified, native "Physics-Vision-Language" foundation model. Such a model could enable real-time, large-scale dynamic generation in open environments, pushing the boundaries of multimodal AI.

While the current MPM grid resolution and the empirically set iteration limit represent trade-offs between fidelity and computational cost, the work lays a robust foundation for future advancements in granular force field control, adaptive optimization for complex multi-stage scenarios, and improved robustness against visual ambiguities like extreme occlusion or textureless surfaces. Overall, PhysAgent offers a promising direction towards truly autonomous and physically intelligent generative AI.
