# OpenHelix: A Short Survey, Empirical Analysis, and Open-Source Dual-System VLA Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive analysis and open-source implementation of dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models demonstrates improved robotic manipulation capabilities through prompt tuning and auxiliary task learning, achieving strong performance in dynamic scenarios while reducing computational costs compared to traditional VLA approaches.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models for robotics are computationally expensive and difficult to deploy in real-time applications
- Limited open-source implementations and standardized evaluation frameworks hinder research progress
- Direct fine-tuning of pre-trained VLAs on robotic data leads to performance degradation

## Method

- Development of OPENHELIX, a dual-system architecture combining large pre-trained models with lightweight action generators
- Implementation of prompt tuning strategy with frozen MLLM parameters
- Addition of auxiliary tasks to leverage multimodal reasoning capabilities

## Results

- Successful demonstration of language instruction generalization in enriched testing scenarios
- Improved robustness in dynamic object manipulation through integrated model layers, and enhanced task performance through auxiliary learning objectives
- Release of accessible open-source implementation with standardized evaluation framework

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Projector pre-alignment is critical for connecting upstream and downstream models, and prompt tuning achieves strong performance while maintaining generalization capability
> - Visual information significantly improves performance compared to language-only models
> - Current MLLM latent embeddings show limited sensitivity to environmental changes

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work directly addresses a critical need in the burgeoning field of Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) for robotics. VLAs have emerged as a promising approach to endow robots with enhanced generalization capabilities and the ability to follow complex, semantically rich instructions. However, the field faces several challenges that this paper attempts to tackle:

- **Computational Cost of VLAs:** Existing VLAs, especially those based on large language models (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), are computationally expensive, hindering their deployment in real-time robotic applications.
- **Lack of Open-Source Resources:** The limited availability of open-source implementations and standardized evaluation frameworks makes it difficult to compare different VLA architectures and hinders further research and development.
- **Domain Shift and Catastrophic Forgetting:** Directly fine-tuning pre-trained VLAs on robotic data can lead to performance degradation due to domain shift and catastrophic forgetting of previously learned knowledge.

The paper's approach of investigating "Dual-System VLAs" directly addresses the computational cost challenge by combining the strengths of large, pre-trained models (for reasoning and generalization) with lightweight, efficient models (for real-time action generation). This "System 1/System 2" architecture, inspired by cognitive science, allows for efficient inference while retaining the reasoning capabilities of large models.

The paper's emphasis on empirical evaluation and open-source development fills a significant gap in the research landscape. By systematically comparing different design choices in Dual-System VLAs and providing a low-cost open-source model, the authors aim to facilitate further research and accelerate the development of more practical and accessible robotic systems. This work aligns with the broader trend of promoting open science and reproducible research in the AI and robotics communities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives and motivation of this research are:

- **Summarize and Compare Dual-System VLA Architectures:** Provide a comprehensive overview of existing Dual-System VLA architectures, highlighting their structural designs and key differences.
- **Conduct Systematic Empirical Evaluations:** Investigate the core design elements of Dual-System VLAs through controlled experiments, focusing on the impact of different choices on performance, efficiency, and generalization.
- **Develop and Release a Low-Cost Open-Source Model:** Create and release a readily accessible open-source implementation of a Dual-System VLA model to facilitate further research and development in the field.
- **Address the Limitations of Existing VLAs:** Overcome the challenges of computational cost, domain shift, and lack of open-source resources that currently hinder the widespread adoption of VLAs in robotics.
- **Provide a Reference Framework:** Establish a standardized evaluation framework and benchmark to enable fair comparisons and guide future research in Dual-System VLA design.

The underlying motivation is to advance the field of embodied intelligence by making VLAs more practical, accessible, and reproducible. The authors recognize the potential of VLAs to revolutionize robotic skill acquisition and deployment but acknowledge the current limitations that need to be addressed through systematic research and open-source development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper employs a combination of literature review, empirical analysis, and open-source software development. The methodology can be broken down into the following steps:

- **Literature Survey:** A comprehensive review of existing VLA architectures, with a particular focus on Dual-System VLAs. The survey categorizes and compares different approaches based on key design elements, such as MLLM selection, policy selection, latent feature representation, training strategies, and integration strategies.
- **Empirical Evaluations:** Systematic experiments are conducted to evaluate the impact of different design choices on the performance of Dual-System VLAs. The experiments are performed in the CALVIN simulation environment, a standard benchmark for robotic manipulation tasks.
 - Standardized Experiment Setup: They standardized experimental conditions to ensure consistency, and focus primarily on evaluating MLLM training strategy, policy training strategy, and the integration strategy.
 - Harder Evaluation: For language instruction generalization, they used enriched language instructions for testing. For dynamic scenario testing, in grasping tasks, they made objects move in four different ways within the environment to examine the model’s robustness in dynamic scenarios.
- **Open-Source Model Development:** Based on the findings from the literature review and empirical evaluations, the authors develop a low-cost open-source Dual-System VLA model named OPENHELIX. The model is designed to be readily accessible and easily modifiable by other researchers.
- **Public Release and Documentation:** The authors release the source code, documentation, and experimental results on a public GitHub repository (https://openhelix-robot.github.com/OpenHelix/).
- **Prompt Tuning for the High-Level Model** Instead of fine-tuning the whole MLLM, they introduce one learnable token at the end of the instruction. During training, all parameters of the MLLM are frozen and only updates the embedding of the learnable token.
- **Multi-Modal Reasoning Learning:** They designed an auxiliary task to leverage the multimodal reasoning capability of the MLLM fully. The output embedding from the learnable prompt token is passed through linear layers to predict the action trajectories and gripper actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the research are:

- **Empirical Evaluations of Training Strategies:** The experiments provide insights into the impact of different training strategies for both the MLLM and the low-level policy. It was discovered that using a pre-trained policy can improve performance while reducing overall training time. When the MLLM is frozen, adding or omitting the CLIP loss does not significantly affect performance. In addition, prompt tuning achieves strong performance and generalization capability.
- **Importance of Projector Pre-alignment:** It was found that without prior projector pre-alignment, connecting upstream and downstream models based on frozen, fine-tuning and prompt tuning approaches directly fails. This demonstrates the importance of Projector pre-alignment in the connection process.
- **Relevance of Visual Information:** It was found that using only LLM produces results far inferior to MLLM, which demonstrates the inherent function of MLLM and shows it hasn’t degraded to simply functioning as an LLM.
- **Limitations of Current Latent Embeddings:** The analysis of latent embeddings revealed that current MLLMs are not sensitive to changes in the current environment. The action token has learned a semantic feature that remains constant and is unrelated to changes in the environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are:

- **Advancement of Dual-System VLA Architectures:** The systematic analysis of Dual-System VLA architectures provides valuable insights into the design choices and trade-offs involved in building these systems.
- **Facilitation of Research and Development:** The open-source release of OPENHELIX provides a readily accessible platform for researchers to experiment with and build upon Dual-System VLAs.
- **Promotion of Reproducibility and Open Science:** The authors contribute to the growing movement towards reproducible research in AI and robotics by making their code, data, and experimental results publicly available.
- **Acceleration of Robotic Skill Acquisition:** By addressing the limitations of existing VLAs, this research contributes to the development of more practical and versatile robotic systems capable of learning and executing complex tasks from natural language instructions.
- **Democratization of Robotics Research:** The low-cost nature of the OPENHELIX model makes it accessible to a wider range of researchers and developers, potentially democratizing access to advanced robotics technologies.
- **Improved Language Generalization:** Integrating the upper and lower layers of the model has significantly improved performance in language generalization scenarios.
- **Enhanced Task Performance:** Additional auxiliary tasks are beneficial for enhancing both standard task performance and generalization performance, mainly because they improve the model’s action capability.

In conclusion, the paper makes a valuable contribution to the field of embodied intelligence by providing a comprehensive analysis of Dual-System VLA architectures, addressing key limitations of existing VLAs, and releasing a low-cost open-source model to facilitate further research and development. The research has the potential to accelerate the development of more practical, accessible, and versatile robotic systems capable of learning and executing complex tasks from natural language instructions.
