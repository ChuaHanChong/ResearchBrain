# V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> FAIR at Meta developed V-JEPA 2, a self-supervised video model that learns a general world model from over 1 million hours of internet video, then adapts this model with a small amount of unlabeled robot data to enable zero-shot robot control and strong performance in video understanding and prediction tasks. The model achieves 80% success for pick-and-place tasks with a cup in novel environments and sets new benchmarks in video question-answering, demonstrating the efficacy of learning predictive world models in representation space.

## Problem

- AI agents struggle to understand and interact with the physical world primarily through observation, often relying on costly interaction data or explicit rewards.
- Traditional world models, particularly those based on generative approaches, are computationally expensive and may focus on unpredictable pixel-level details rather than robust underlying dynamics relevant for planning.
- Robotics faces data scarcity, requiring vast amounts of expensive, labeled interaction data for task-specific training, which limits scalability and generalization to novel environments.

## Method

![[2506.09985-method.png]]
*Multistage training. (Left) We first pretrain the V-JEPA 2 video encoder on internet-scale image and video data using a visual mask denoising objective. (Right) After pretraining, we freeze the video encoder and learn a new action-conditioned predictor, V-JEPA 2-AC, on top of the learned representation.*

- V-JEPA 2, a self-supervised video model, is pre-trained on over 1 million hours of diverse internet video using a ==mask-denoising objective== in a learned representation space.
- An ==action-conditioned predictor (V-JEPA 2-AC)== is then post-trained on a small amount (62 hours) of unlabeled robot video to learn the causal effects of actions for predicting future states.
- For robotic control, V-JEPA 2-AC is integrated into a ==Model Predictive Control (MPC) loop==, where it plans actions by minimizing a ==goal-conditioned energy function== defined by the L1 distance between imagined future state representations and goal image representations.

## Results

- V-JEPA 2 ViT-g achieved **77.3%** top-1 accuracy on Something-Something v2 (SSv2), outperforming other vision encoders and demonstrating superior motion understanding.
- The model attained state-of-the-art results for its model class on several video question-answering benchmarks (e.g., **84.0%** on PerceptionTest, **44.5%** on MVP) when aligned with an LLM, despite being pre-trained without language supervision.
- V-JEPA 2-AC demonstrated zero-shot robotic manipulation with high success rates (e.g., **80%** for pick-and-place with a cup) on Franka arms in novel environments, significantly outperforming baselines using only **62 hours** of unlabeled robot video for post-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale self-supervised pre-training on general internet video effectively builds a robust "world model" that captures predictable dynamics without explicit action or language supervision.
> - Predicting in a learned representation space (JEPA) is more efficient and robust for learning world dynamics compared to pixel-level generation, especially for planning.
> - A powerful visual foundation model, even without language pre-training, can achieve state-of-the-art video question-answering performance when aligned with a large language model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a central challenge in modern AI: enabling agents to understand and interact with the physical world primarily through observation. This aligns with a significant paradigm shift in AI research from task-specific supervised learning towards more generalist, self-supervised foundation models.

**Contrast with Traditional Approaches:**
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) & Behavior Cloning (BC):** Traditional robotic control often relies on large amounts of interaction data, explicit reward functions, or expert demonstrations (e.g., behavior cloning). This limits scalability and generalization to novel environments or tasks due to the high cost of data collection and the "sim-to-real gap." V-JEPA 2 aims to overcome this by largely learning from passive observation (internet video).
- **Generative Models:** Many "world models" attempt to learn by generating future pixel-level observations. While impressive visually, the authors argue that such generative objectives can be computationally expensive and may focus on unpredictable, high-frequency details (e.g., precise leaf movement in wind) rather than the underlying predictable dynamics relevant for action and planning. V-JEPA 2, as a JEPA, focuses on predicting in a *learned representation space*, aiming for more robust and efficient learning of invariant features crucial for dynamics.

**Advancement in Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):**
- **Vision Foundation Models:** The paper builds on the success of visual foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, MAE, V-JEPA 1) that learn powerful representations from vast unlabeled image/video data. V-JEPA 2 significantly scales up the self-supervised pre-training from video, pushing beyond previous V-JEPA formulations by increasing data, model size, and temporal/spatial resolution.
- **Bridging Observation and Action:** A key contribution is showing how a model pre-trained *without actions or language supervision* on general internet video can be effectively adapted with a *small amount* of robot interaction data to enable physical planning. This multi-stage approach aims to combine the richness of passive observation with the causality learned from limited active interaction.
- **Vision-Language Alignment:** The work also contributes to the rapidly evolving field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). By demonstrating that a video encoder pre-trained *without* language supervision can achieve state-of-the-art performance in video question-answering when aligned with an LLM, it challenges the conventional wisdom that language-aligned pre-training is necessary for strong VidQA performance.

In essence, V-JEPA 2 positions itself at the intersection of large-scale self-supervised learning, world modeling, and embodied AI, proposing a scalable and efficient pathway to build generalist agents capable of understanding, predicting, and planning in complex physical environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop **artificial agents that can understand the physical world, predict future states, and plan effectively in novel situations**, much like humans. This goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Learning from Observation:** Acknowledging that humans acquire a robust understanding of the world largely through passive observation (e.g., watching videos, interacting with the environment without explicit rewards), the authors aim to replicate this capability in AI. This contrasts with traditional approaches that heavily rely on explicit supervision, detailed rewards, or massive amounts of costly interaction data.
2. **Scalability and Data Efficiency for Robotics:** Real-world robot interaction data is expensive and limited. The paper is motivated by the need to bypass this data bottleneck. By leveraging internet-scale video data (over 1 million hours) for broad "world knowledge" pre-training, V-JEPA 2 aims to significantly reduce the dependency on large amounts of robot-specific interaction data for downstream robotic tasks. The ability to use "unlabeled" robot videos (without rewards or task labels) further enhances data efficiency.
3. **Limitations of Generative World Models:** The authors point out that previous attempts at world models, particularly those based on video generation, often emphasize pixel-level fidelity. While visually compelling, this can lead to learning computationally intensive models that struggle with unpredictable details and may not efficiently capture the predictable aspects of scene dynamics crucial for planning. The motivation here is to develop a more efficient and robust world model by predicting in a *learned representation space* rather than pixel space.
4. **Enabling Zero-Shot Generalization:** A critical goal is to enable AI systems, especially in robotics, to adapt and generalize to new environments and tasks without requiring new data collection or task-specific fine-tuning in those new settings. This "zero-shot" capability is demonstrated in the robotic manipulation experiments.
5. **Multimodal Understanding:** Beyond just robotics, the work aims to create a unified visual model that supports diverse downstream applications, including motion understanding, action anticipation, and complex video question-answering, by integrating visual representations with language models.

In summary, the core motivation is to build a foundation model for embodied AI that learns a comprehensive world model by combining the vastness of internet data with the causality of limited interaction data, leading to robust understanding, prediction, and crucially, effective planning for real-world agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The V-JEPA 2 framework employs a sophisticated two-stage training procedure: an initial large-scale, action-free self-supervised pre-training phase, followed by a more targeted action-conditioned post-training phase on a small amount of robot interaction data.

#### 3.1. V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised Video Pre-training (Action-Free)

This stage focuses on learning a general understanding of world dynamics from diverse visual data without explicit action labels.

- **Objective: Mask-Denoising in Representation Space (JEPA Principle):** V-JEPA 2 builds upon the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm. Unlike masked autoencoders that reconstruct pixels, JEPA models predict the learned *representation* of masked parts of an input (e.g., video frames) from unmasked parts. This is achieved via an **encoder** (Eθ) that extracts representations and a **predictor** (Pφ) that predicts masked representations. A stop-gradient (sg) operation and an exponential moving average (EMA) of the encoder weights (ema-encoder) are used to generate prediction targets, preventing representation collapse. The loss is an L1 distance between predicted and target representations of masked patches. This approach is designed to force the model to learn abstract, predictable aspects of the scene while ignoring irrelevant, unpredictable details.
- **Architecture:** Both the encoder and predictor are parameterized as Vision Transformers (ViTs). A 3D extension of Rotary Position Embeddings (3D-RoPE) is used instead of absolute embeddings for better stability in larger models and across spatial and temporal dimensions. Video clips are patchified into 2x16x16 (T x H x W) tubelets with a multi-block masking strategy.
- **Key Scaling Ingredients:**
 1. **Data Scaling:** The pre-training dataset, **VideoMix22M (VM22M)**, is significantly expanded to over 1 million hours of internet video (22 million videos), including ego-centric (SSv2), exo-centric (Kinetics, HowTo100M, YT-Temporal-1B), and image (ImageNet) data. A retrieval-based curation pipeline is applied to the massive YT-Temporal-1B dataset to reduce noise and align its distribution with higher-quality datasets, enhancing scalability and performance.
 2. **Model Scaling:** The encoder architecture is scaled from 300 million (ViT-L) to over 1 billion (ViT-g) parameters, demonstrating that larger capacities effectively leverage the self-supervised objective. The predictor size remains fixed.
 3. **Longer Training:** A warmup-constant-decay learning rate schedule allows for extended training durations (up to 252,000 iterations), fully leveraging the larger datasets and models.
 4. **Higher Resolution & Longer Videos (Progressive Training):** To efficiently handle high-resolution (up to 384x384 pixels) and longer video clips (up to 64 frames/16 seconds), a progressive training strategy is employed. Initial training uses shorter, lower-resolution clips, with resolution and clip length increased only during the final decay phase, significantly reducing computational overhead (e.g., 8.4x speedup for 64-frame, 384x384 inputs).

#### 3.2. V-JEPA 2-AC: Action-Conditioned World Model Post-training

This stage adapts the pre-trained V-JEPA 2 to understand the causal effects of actions for planning.

- **Objective: Autoregressive Feature Prediction:** The frozen V-JEPA 2 encoder is used as a fixed representation extractor. A new **action-conditioned predictor (V-JEPA 2-AC)**, a 300M-parameter transformer, is trained on top of these representations. This predictor learns to autoregressively predict the representation of the *next* video frame, conditioned on previous states (encoded frames), robot actions (change in end-effector state), and proprioceptive observations (end-effector pose).
- **Training Data:** Approximately 62 hours of *unlabeled* robot videos from the **Droid dataset** (teleoperated Franka Panda arm trajectories) are used. "Unlabeled" means no explicit reward signals or task labels, only raw video and end-effector state data.
- **Loss Functions:**
 - **Teacher-Forcing Loss:** Predicts the representation of the next timestep based on the actual current frame, action, and pose. This prevents error accumulation during training.
 - **Rollout Loss:** Encourages the model to predict several timesteps ahead by feeding its own outputs back as inputs, improving long-term prediction capabilities (though primarily used for 2-step rollouts in practice).
- **Architecture:** The V-JEPA 2-AC predictor uses a block-causal attention pattern, allowing each patch feature at a given timestep to attend to current and previous patch features, actions, and end-effector states.

#### 3.3. Planning with V-JEPA 2-AC (Model Predictive Control)

For robotic control, V-JEPA 2-AC is integrated into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) loop.

- **Energy Minimization:** Given a current observation (x_k) and a goal image (x_g), the model plans a sequence of robot actions (a*i) by minimizing a **goal-conditioned energy function**. This function is defined as the L1 distance between the world model's imagined future state representation (after executing the planned actions) and the representation of the goal image.
- **Optimization:** The Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is used to optimize the action sequence at each planning step. This involves iteratively sampling action trajectories, selecting the best ones, and updating a probability distribution over actions, until a satisfactory sequence is found. Only the first action of the optimal sequence is executed before the process is repeated (receding horizon control).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The V-JEPA 2 paper presents a comprehensive set of results across understanding, prediction, and planning tasks, demonstrating the model's versatility and strong performance.

#### 4.1. Understanding: Probe-based Classification

V-JEPA 2 excels at visual understanding, particularly for motion-centric tasks, while remaining competitive on appearance-based tasks. The evaluation uses an attentive probe trained on top of frozen V-JEPA 2 representations.

- **Motion Understanding (e.g., Something-Something v2, Diving-48, Jester):** V-JEPA 2 ViT-g achieves **77.3% top-1 accuracy on Something-Something v2 (SSv2)**, significantly outperforming other vision encoders (e.g., InternVideo2-1B at 69.7%, PEcoreG at 55.4%). This highlights V-JEPA 2's superior ability to capture fine-grained motion information from videos.
- **Appearance Understanding (e.g., Kinetics400, COIN, ImageNet):** V-JEPA 2 ViT-g also shows strong performance on these tasks, with **84.6% on ImageNet** (a +4.6 percentage point improvement over its predecessor, V-JEPA).
- **Overall Performance:** V-JEPA 2 ViT-g achieves the **best average performance of 87.5%** across all six classification tasks among tested vision encoders. The higher-resolution V-JEPA 2 ViT-g (384px) further improves this to 88.2%. The scaling ingredients (data, model size, training length, resolution) consistently contribute to these performance gains.

#### 4.2. Understanding: Video Question Answering (VidQA)

V-JEPA 2 representations prove highly effective for multimodal understanding when aligned with a large language model.

- **Performance without Language Pre-training:** Crucially, V-JEPA 2, pre-trained *without any language supervision*, demonstrates competitive and even state-of-the-art performance when its visual encoder is aligned with an LLM. In a controlled setup (Qwen2-7B-Instruct LLM, 18M alignment samples), V-JEPA 2 ViT-g (512px) outperforms DINOv2, SigLIP2, and Perception Encoder on most benchmarks, particularly those focused on temporal understanding (e.g., MVP, TemporalBench, TVBench).
- **State-of-the-Art (8B Model Class):** By scaling the alignment data to 88.5 million samples and using Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, V-JEPA 2 achieves **state-of-the-art results in the 8B model class** on several VidQA benchmarks: **84.0% on PerceptionTest**, **44.5% paired accuracy on MVP**, **76.9% multi-choice accuracy on TempCompass**, **36.7% on TemporalBench (MBA-short QA)**, and **40.3% on TOMATO**. This directly challenges the notion that vision encoders need language-aligned pre-training to excel in VidQA.

#### 4.3. Prediction: Probe-based Action Anticipation

V-JEPA 2 demonstrates superior capabilities in predicting future human actions.

- **Epic-Kitchens-100 (EK100):** V-JEPA 2 achieves state-of-the-art performance on the EK100 human-action anticipation task. The V-JEPA 2 ViT-g (384px) model achieves **39.7% recall-at-5**, which is a **44% relative improvement** over the previous best model, PlausiVL (27.6%), despite PlausiVL using an 8B parameter language model. This shows that powerful visual representations from self-supervised video pre-training are highly beneficial for anticipating complex human activities.
- **Scaling Behavior:** Performance on EK100 scales linearly with V-JEPA 2 model size, further confirming the benefits of increasing model capacity.

#### 4.4. Planning: Zero-shot Robot Control

This is a central result, showcasing V-JEPA 2-AC's ability to plan and control real robots in novel environments.

- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** V-JEPA 2-AC is deployed zero-shot (without collecting any data from or training on the specific environments) on Franka arms in two different labs.
- **Prehensile Manipulation Success Rates:**
 - **Single-goal Reaching:** The model consistently moves the end-effector within 4 cm of the goal, demonstrating a learned 3D spatial understanding from monocular RGB input. The energy landscape analysis shows a smooth, locally convex function, facilitating planning.
 - **Complex Tasks (Grasp, Reach w/ Object, Pick-and-Place):** V-JEPA 2-AC significantly outperforms behavior cloning-based Octo and video generation-based Cosmos models. For pick-and-place, V-JEPA 2-AC achieves **80% success rate with a cup and 65% with a box (average across labs)**, compared to Octo's 15% and 10%, and Cosmos's 0% (though Cosmos's planning time was prohibitive).
- **Efficiency in Planning:** V-JEPA 2-AC's representation-space planning is highly efficient. It requires only **16 seconds per action** for planning (with 800 samples, 10 refinement steps, and a 1-step horizon) compared to 4 minutes per action for Cosmos, enabling practical real-time robot operation.
- **Learning from Unlabeled Data:** The robot control is achieved using only 62 hours of *unlabeled* robot videos for post-training, emphasizing the data efficiency derived from the large-scale pre-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a significant step towards developing generalist AI agents capable of perceiving, understanding, predicting, and interacting with the physical world. Its significance and potential impact span several critical areas:

1. **Advancing Generalist AI and World Models:** V-JEPA 2 provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of self-supervised learning on massive observation data for building robust internal "world models." By focusing on prediction in representation space rather than pixel space, it offers a more computationally efficient and perhaps more fundamentally aligned approach to learning predictable world dynamics, addressing a key challenge identified by Yann LeCun. This moves AI closer to human-like intuitive physics and common sense understanding.

2. **Revolutionizing Robotic Learning Data Efficiency:** The demonstration of zero-shot robot manipulation, with impressive success rates, by post-training with *only 62 hours of unlabeled robot video* after internet-scale pre-training, is a breakthrough. This drastically reduces the data burden that has historically plagued robot learning, making it feasible to deploy capabilities in new environments without extensive, costly, and task-specific data collection or reward engineering. This could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in diverse, unstructured real-world settings.

3. **Unified AI Systems:** The paper showcases a single foundation model (V-JEPA 2) as a versatile backbone for disparate tasks: motion understanding, action anticipation, complex multimodal question-answering, and direct robot planning. This versatility underscores the potential for truly "generalist" AI systems built on powerful visual representations.

4. **Challenging Conventional Wisdom in Multimodal AI:** The finding that a video encoder pre-trained *without* language supervision can, when adequately aligned, achieve state-of-the-art performance in video question-answering, challenges the prevailing trend of requiring vision-language contrastive pre-training. This suggests that learning strong visual dynamics from observations alone provides a powerful foundation that can later be effectively grounded in language.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Accessible Robotics:** By reducing the need for bespoke robot data and environments, V-JEPA 2 could democratize access to advanced robotic capabilities, allowing smaller labs or individual researchers to develop and deploy more intelligent robots.
- **Safer and More Adaptive AI:** A deeper understanding of physical dynamics and the ability to predict consequences of actions can lead to safer and more adaptive AI systems, whether in autonomous vehicles, household robots, or industrial automation.
- **Foundation for Future AI Research:** The open-science approach (code release, detailed methodology) makes V-JEPA 2 a valuable foundation model for the broader AI community, enabling further research into hierarchical planning, long-horizon control, and more sophisticated multimodal reasoning.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that pave the way for future research:
- **Long-Horizon Planning:** The current model performs well for relatively short-horizon, greedy tasks (e.g., grasping, pick-and-place with sub-goals). Tackling longer, more abstract tasks without manual sub-goal specification will require innovations in modeling, perhaps via hierarchical approaches.
- **Input Modality for Goals:** Current planning relies on image-based goals. Integrating language-based goal specification would enhance natural interaction with AI agents.
- **Camera Sensitivity:** The implicit inference of action coordinate axes from monocular vision without explicit calibration leads to sensitivity to camera positioning, suggesting a need for more robust visual grounding.
- **Scaling Limits:** While scaled to 1B parameters, further exploration of even larger model sizes (e.g., 20B parameters) and their impact on performance remains an open question.

Despite these limitations, V-JEPA 2 is a landmark contribution, demonstrating a practical and scalable pathway towards building AI systems that learn rich, predictable world models from passive observation, enabling sophisticated understanding, prediction, and physical interaction.
