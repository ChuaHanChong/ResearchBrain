# A Closed-Form Geometric Retargeting Solver for Upper Body Humanoid Robot Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed SEW-Mimic, a closed-form geometric solver that retargets human upper body motion to 7-DoF robot arms by aligning limb orientations, achieving significantly faster inference times (median 0.587 ms) and superior pose similarity. This approach enhances real-time teleoperation and leads to robot policies with approximately three times higher success rates.

## Problem

- Existing teleoperation methods often suffer from high latency, leading to hesitant robot movements and poor-quality data for training autonomous policies.
- End-effector only retargeting for 7-DoF robot arms can result in uncontrolled null-space elbow motion, reducing pose similarity and increasing collision risks.
- Jacobian-based inverse kinematics (IK) solvers are prone to numerical instability and reduced speed near robot joint singularities.

## Method

- ==SEW-Mimic== formulates the retargeting problem as an orientation alignment task, minimizing error across human upper arm, lower arm, and wrist segments.
- It employs a closed-form geometric solver leveraging canonical subproblems to derive robot joint angles, ensuring a provably optimal and scale-independent kinematic solution.
- A real-time safety filter is integrated using capsule collision volumes and an XPBD-based approach to prevent self-collisions during bimanual teleoperation, utilizing SEW-Mimic's computational efficiency.

## Results

- SEW-Mimic achieved significantly faster inference (median **0.587** ms) and dramatically lower alignment error (median **1.57** × 10−13) compared to baseline methods.
- Robot policies trained on demonstration data collected with SEW-Mimic showed approximately 3× higher success rates and shorter task completion times than those trained on baseline data.
- The integrated safety filter reduced self-collision instances from nearly **50%** to approximately **1.3%** in bimanual tasks, albeit with a slight increase in alignment error and computation time (to ~**4.0** ms for both arms).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prioritizing orientational alignment of human limb segments enables a robust, scale-independent, and analytically solvable kinematic mapping to 7-DoF robot arms.
> - Closed-form geometric solutions can overcome the latency and numerical instability issues commonly found in iterative optimization or learning-based retargeting methods.
> - The high computational speed of the core retargeting algorithm provides sufficient overhead for implementing real-time safety measures like self-collision avoidance.
