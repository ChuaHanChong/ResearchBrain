# What Is The Best 3D Scene Representation for Robotics? From Geometric to Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive analysis of 3D scene representation methods for robotics, categorizing them from traditional geometric approaches to neural and foundation models. It evaluates their performance and suitability across core robotic functionalities like perception, mapping, localization, navigation, and manipulation.

## Problem

- Traditional geometric 3D scene representations lack the density, continuity, semantic richness, and photorealism required for advanced embodied intelligence tasks in robotics.
- The rapid development of neural representations (e.g., NeRF, 3DGS) and large-scale Foundation Models necessitates a unified and critical understanding of their strengths, weaknesses, and optimal applications within diverse robotic modules.
- Robotic systems require distinct properties from scene representations for various tasks, making it challenging to identify the "best" method for specific perception, mapping, localization, navigation, or manipulation functionalities.

## Method

- The paper systematically classifies 3D scene representations into geometric, neural (NeRF, 3DGS), and Foundation Model categories, detailing their formulations and properties.
- It conducts a comparative analysis of these representations across key dimensions including data form, continuity, memory efficiency, photorealism, flexibility, and geometric capability.
- The study evaluates the performance and applicability of each representation type across five fundamental robotic modules: perception, mapping, localization, manipulation, and navigation.

## Results

- Geometric representations remain efficient and interpretable for explicit geometric accuracy in many robotic tasks, while neural methods excel in high-fidelity, photorealistic reconstruction and dense mapping.
- 3D Gaussian Splatting provides a significant leap in real-time photorealistic rendering with editable primitives, offering a faster alternative to NeRF for dynamic scene reconstruction and SLAM.
- Foundation Models demonstrate superior performance in open-vocabulary perception, language-grounded manipulation, and robust localization by leveraging powerful learned features and semantic priors, pushing robotics towards more cognitive and generalized capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A clear evolutionary trend exists from explicit geometric representations towards implicit neural and large-scale Foundation Models, driven by increasing demands for photorealism, continuous scene modeling, and semantic understanding in robotics.
> - Neural representations like NeRF and 3DGS offer unparalleled photorealism and differentiable scene modeling but often face challenges related to memory consumption, rendering speed, and explicit geometric accuracy without specialized regularization.
> - Foundation Models show promising capabilities for integrating high-level semantic reasoning, language-based priors, and generalization into 3D scene understanding, enabling robust open-set perception and complex task execution for robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The capability for a robot to accurately perceive and represent its surrounding environment is foundational to achieving true autonomy and intelligence. This paper positions itself at the nexus of computer vision, computer graphics, and robotics, systematically charting the evolution of 3D scene representation methods. Historically, robotic systems relied on simpler 2D grid maps, which gradually progressed to 3D geometric representations like point clouds, voxels, and meshes with the advent of 3D sensing technologies (e.g., LiDAR, RGB-D cameras). While these traditional methods proved effective for basic tasks like SLAM and mapping, they inherently suffer from limitations such as discreteness, sparsity, and a lack of continuous appearance modeling, hindering their application in complex embodied intelligence tasks.

The broader research landscape has recently undergone a significant paradigm shift driven by advancements in deep learning. The introduction of neural representations, particularly Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), has revolutionized photorealistic rendering and dense scene reconstruction. Concurrently, the rise of large-scale pre-trained models, termed "Foundation Models," has begun to transform various AI domains, including robotics, by integrating rich semantic understanding and language-based reasoning.

This survey critically bridges these distinct eras of 3D scene representation. It recognizes the strengths and limitations of geometric methods while highlighting the disruptive potential of neural representations and foundation models. By providing a structured comparison across different robotic functionalities (perception, mapping, localization, navigation, manipulation), the paper addresses a crucial need for a unified understanding of which representation is "best" for specific robotic modules. It acts as a timely compass, guiding researchers through the rapidly evolving landscape, as evidenced by the growing trend in publications on neural scene representations shown in the paper's Fig. 2. The authors’ commitment to maintaining an open-source GitHub project further cements its role as a living resource in this dynamic field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research paper is to provide a comprehensive, up-to-date, and critically evaluative overview of existing 3D scene representation methods specifically for robotics. This overarching goal is distilled into the paper's provocative titular question: "What Is The Best 3D Scene Representation for Robotics?"

The motivations driving this extensive survey are multi-faceted:
- **Addressing the Gap in Embodied Intelligence:** Traditional geometric representations, while robust for basic SLAM and localization, often lack the density, continuity, and semantic richness required for advanced embodied intelligence tasks such as nuanced human-robot interaction, complex manipulation, and sophisticated navigation in cluttered or dynamic environments. The paper aims to explore how newer representations address this gap.
- **Synthesizing Rapid Advancements:** The fields of computer vision and graphics have witnessed an explosion of innovative neural scene representations (NeRF, 3DGS) and the emergence of powerful Foundation Models. These advancements have significant implications for robotics, but their strengths, weaknesses, and optimal applications within robotic systems are not yet fully understood or systematically cataloged. This survey seeks to provide that essential synthesis.
- **Guiding Module-Specific Choices:** Robotics systems are often modular, with distinct components for perception, mapping, localization, navigation, and manipulation. The paper aims to critically compare how different scene representations perform across these diverse functional requirements, offering guidance on which representation might be most suitable for a given module.
- **Integrating High-Level Reasoning:** A key motivation is to highlight the potential of neural and foundation models to integrate high-level semantic features and language-based priors directly into 3D scene understanding. This integration is crucial for enabling robots to not just "see" the environment geometrically, but to "understand" it semantically and interact based on natural language instructions.
- **Identifying Future Directions:** Beyond surveying existing methods, a core motivation is to identify current technical limitations, discuss emerging trends—particularly the potential for 3D Foundation Models to become a unified solution—and pinpoint promising future research avenues to inspire further advancements in the field. The paper explicitly aims to be a valuable resource for both newcomers and experienced researchers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and comprehensive literature review methodology to address their central question. Their approach is structured around several key pillars:

- **Categorization of Scene Representations:** The paper begins by broadly classifying 3D scene representations into two main categories:
 - **Geometric Representations:** These include traditional methods such as point clouds, voxels, meshes, Signed Distance Functions (SDF), and scene graphs, which have been mainstays in robotics for decades.
 - **Neural Representations:** This newer category encompasses Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), and the emerging class of Foundation Models (often employing tokenizer-based representations).
 The paper provides a brief mathematical formulation and background for each of these representation types in Section II.

- **Comparative Analysis Dimensions:** To evaluate the suitability of different representations, the authors establish a set of key dimensions for comparison, explicitly outlined and summarized in Fig. 4. These dimensions include:
 - **Data Form (Discrete vs. Continuous)**
 - **Continuity & Differentiability**
 - **Memory Efficiency**
 - **Photorealism**
 - **Flexibility**
 - **Geometric Representation Capability**
 Section III then delves into a detailed discussion of these critical properties, particularly focusing on memory efficiency, photorealism, and geometric representation capability, providing insights into various optimization and enhancement techniques for each representation type.

- **Application-Centric Evaluation:** The core of the methodology lies in evaluating how each category of scene representation performs across five fundamental robotic modules, as illustrated in Fig. 3:
 - **Perception:** Covering object detection and scene-level semantic understanding.
 - **Mapping:** Focusing on scene reconstruction, including static, large-scale, and dynamic environments.
 - **Localization:** Distinguishing between known map-based and relative pose regression localization.
 - **Manipulation:** Examining grasping frameworks and object interaction.
 - **Navigation:** Analyzing path planning and exploration strategies.
 For each module, the authors review representative methods from Geometric, NeRF-based, 3DGS-based, and Foundation Model paradigms, highlighting their respective advantages, limitations, and recent advancements.

- **Trend Analysis and Future Directions:** The paper integrates a historical timeline (Fig. 1) and a trend analysis of publication numbers (Fig. 2) to contextualize the evolution and increasing prominence of neural methods. It concludes by discussing overarching challenges (e.g., general-purpose vs. module-specific architectures, data scarcity, real-time performance) and proposing promising future research directions, particularly emphasizing the role of Foundation Models in unifying robotic intelligence.

- **Open-Source Resource:** A notable aspect of their approach is the provision of an open-source GitHub project, which compiles related articles and promises continuous updates. This initiative aims to serve as a living, dynamic resource for the research community, extending the impact of the paper beyond its publication date.

This systematic decomposition allows for a thorough and structured examination of a complex and rapidly evolving field, providing a valuable framework for understanding the current state and future trajectory of 3D scene representation in robotics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's extensive review yields several key findings and highlights significant trends in 3D scene representation for robotics:

- **Evolutionary Landscape:** The research confirms a clear evolutionary trajectory from traditional geometric representations (point clouds, voxels, meshes, SDFs, scene graphs, prominent from the 1970s-2010s) to neural representations (NeRF from ~2020, 3DGS from ~2023), and more recently, the emergence of Foundation Models. The paper's Fig. 1 timeline visually encapsulates this progression, while Fig. 2 demonstrates a sharp increase in research interest towards NeRF, 3DGS, and Foundation Models in robotics.

- **Comparative Strengths and Weaknesses (Fig. 4 Summary):**
 - **Geometric Representations:** Excel in explicit geometric accuracy, interpretability, and often memory efficiency (for sparse representations or structured formats like scene graphs). They are discrete and non-differentiable, limiting photorealism and flexibility for complex semantic tasks.
 - **Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF):** Offer unparalleled photorealism and continuous, differentiable scene modeling. However, they typically suffer from high memory consumption, slow rendering/training, and can produce blurry geometry if not explicitly regularized (e.g., by integrating SDFs).
 - **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** Represents a significant leap in real-time photorealistic rendering with explicit, editable primitives. It maintains differentiability and continuity but generally has high memory usage and can present geometric ambiguity (though recent works address this with surfels or opacity fields). Rendering speed is a major advantage over NeRF.
 - **Foundation Models (Tokenizer Representations):** Exhibit high flexibility, strong generalization (e.g., zero-shot capabilities), and the ability to integrate language-based priors. They are memory-efficient for tokens themselves but often require large network parameters. While promising for high-level understanding and interaction, their inherent geometric expressiveness can be limited, and real-time performance remains a challenge due to model size.

- **Module-Specific Suitability:**
 - **Perception:** Foundation Models (e.g., Grounding DINO, YOLO-World, Scene-LLM) are rapidly becoming dominant for open-vocabulary object detection and holistic scene-level semantic understanding due to their ability to ground language and generalize. Geometric methods remain efficient for traditional object detection.
 - **Mapping & Reconstruction:** Geometric methods (COLMAP, OctoMap, Voxblox, DeepSDF) provide robust, interpretable maps. NeRF (Neuralangelo, Block-NeRF) offers high-fidelity, photorealistic reconstruction but is computationally intensive. 3DGS (OctreeGS, CityGaussian) provides scalable, real-time photorealistic mapping. Foundation models (DuSt3R, VGGT) show potential for feed-forward dense 3D reconstruction, scaling with data volume.
 - **SLAM:** Geometric SLAM (ORB-SLAM2, F-LOAM) is mature and efficient but typically sparse. NeRF-based SLAM (iMAP, Nice-SLAM) enables dense, photorealistic mapping and tracking but with significant computational cost. 3DGS-based SLAM (SplaTAM, MonoGS) achieves real-time dense SLAM with explicit primitives and faster rendering. Foundation model-based SLAM (MASt3R-SLAM, SLAM3R) leverages powerful learned features and dense 3D priors from transformers, offering robustness.
 - **Localization:** Geometric methods (ICP, NDT) are efficient for known map localization. NeRF (Loc-NeRF) uses photometric losses, while 3DGS (iComMa, Gsplatloc) leverages differentiable rendering. Foundation Models (LEXIS, FM-Loc, Anyloc) provide robust, open-set localization by integrating semantic and language features, improving adaptability to varied environments.
 - **Manipulation:** Traditional geometric methods (Contact-GraspNet, VGN) are real-time for grasping but lack generalization. NeRF (Dex-NeRF) provides rich scene priors for teleoperation. 3DGS (GaussianGrasper, ManiGaussians) enables dynamic, differentiable scene representations for interactive manipulation. Foundation Models (SayCan, VoxPoser, NLMap) excel in language-grounded, zero-shot, and high-level task planning, pushing manipulation towards more cognitive abilities.
 - **Navigation:** Geometric maps (OctoMap, Voxblox) are efficient for path planning and collision avoidance. NeRF (NeRF-Navigation, CATNIPS) uses density fields for safe navigation and uncertainty-driven exploration. 3DGS (GaussNav, Splat-Nav) leverages semantic Gaussian maps for image-goal navigation and active reconstruction. Foundation Models (LM-Nav, SayCan, Reasoned Explorer) integrate VLMs/LLMs for open-set recognition, landmark-based navigation, and high-level task decomposition.

In essence, the paper finds a clear trend towards neural and foundation models for tasks requiring photorealism, dense continuous representations, semantic understanding, and language integration, while traditional geometric methods maintain an edge in computational efficiency and explicit geometric fidelity for certain applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this paper lies in its comprehensive synthesis of the rapidly evolving field of 3D scene representation for robotics, offering both a historical perspective and a forward-looking analysis. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Authoritative Resource for the Community:** By meticulously cataloging and comparing diverse methods, the paper serves as an invaluable and up-to-date reference for both newcomers and seasoned researchers. Its structured organization and clear articulation of pros and cons for each representation across various robotic modules demystify a complex landscape, facilitating informed design choices for future robotic systems. The commitment to an open-source GitHub project further amplifies its utility as a dynamic resource.

- **Guiding Research Directions:** The paper critically identifies the limitations of current approaches and proposes concrete avenues for future research. This includes pushing for greater memory efficiency, improving geometric accuracy in neural representations, and enhancing real-time performance for computationally intensive models. By highlighting these challenges, the authors provide a roadmap for future innovations.

- **Catalyzing the Shift to Foundation Models:** Perhaps the most significant impact is the paper's strong emphasis on the transformative potential of Foundation Models in robotics. By discussing their capabilities in generalization, zero-shot learning, and seamless integration of language with visual and geometric data, the paper advocates for a paradigm shift from specialized, modular robotic intelligence towards more unified, end-to-end systems. This encourages researchers to explore how these powerful models can unlock unprecedented levels of autonomy and intelligent interaction.

- **Rethinking Robotic Architectures:** The discussion on "General-purpose or Module Specify" challenges the long-standing modular design philosophy in robotics. Drawing inspiration from the plasticity of the human brain, the authors suggest that unified training of large foundation models could lead to emergent modularity and enhanced flexibility, potentially leading to more adaptable and robust robots. This philosophical inquiry could reshape how robotic systems are conceptualized and built.

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Real-time Constraints:** The paper squarely confronts practical deployment challenges like data scarcity (for training large models) and real-time performance. By discussing solutions like generative models, simulators, world models for data augmentation, and hardware-algorithm co-design for efficiency, it stimulates research into bridging the gap between theoretical potential and practical robotic deployment.

In conclusion, this paper is more than just a review; it is a strategic analysis that aims to inspire and direct the next generation of robotics research. By championing the integration of advanced scene representations, particularly Foundation Models, it envisions a future where robots possess a more profound understanding of their environments, enabling more intuitive human-robot interaction, robust navigation in complex spaces, and highly dexterous manipulation, ultimately driving the realization of truly intelligent and autonomous embodied AI.
