# Molt: A Scalable PyTorch-Native Training Framework for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA's Molt is a PyTorch-native framework for agentic reinforcement learning that prioritizes research agility while maintaining high performance. It offers a compact codebase of approximately 8.6K Python lines and achieves throughput parity with state-of-the-art Megatron-based stacks, demonstrating effective scaling up to 700B Mixture-of-Experts models.

## Problem

- Existing mainstream RL frameworks complicate algorithmic modifications due to multi-layered architectures, increasing development time and complexity for researchers.
- A significant trade-off exists between providing hyperscale training capabilities and maintaining ease of research for prototyping new agentic RL algorithms.
- Current infrastructure often lacks transparency, making it difficult for researchers and AI coding assistants to fully comprehend and modify the entire algorithm flow.

## Method

- **Molt** is a lean, PyTorch-native training framework that integrates Ray, vLLM, and NeMo AutoModel around a single ==disaggregated asynchronous loop==.
- It adheres to principles of readability, minimal single-backend code, performance parity, algorithmic modularity, and ==correctness invariants== to simplify development.
- The framework employs an ==Agent Contract== for various environments (Gym-aligned or Chat-based) and uses ==token-exact transport== with a single chat-format data path to ensure consistent, drift-free inputs.

## Results

- Molt's complete RL path comprises approximately **8.6K** Python lines, indicating a significantly smaller codebase compared to frameworks like verl (~**62K** lines) and slime (~**25K** lines).
- On a Qwen3-30B-A3B model, Molt achieved an average of **119.4** seconds per optimizer step, demonstrating statistically comparable throughput to a Megatron-based stack (slime, **109.5** seconds per step).
- Engine optimizations like speculative decoding yielded a nearly **5x** speedup in generation time, and the framework successfully ran end-to-end training for a **700B** MoE model at expert parallelism **256**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High throughput, comparable to complex hyperscale RL frameworks, can be achieved with a significantly smaller and more transparent codebase through effective composition of hardened, frontier-scale components.
> - Simplifying the codebase and aligning modularity with algorithmic concepts significantly reduces the cognitive load for researchers, accelerating the prototyping and iteration of new RL algorithms.
> - Scalability to very large models (e.g., 700B MoE) can be managed primarily through configuration rather than requiring fundamental architectural redesigns, demonstrating the framework's adaptability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) research involves continuous algorithmic modifications, including the development of new estimators, pipeline stages, and rollout schemes. In existing mainstream RL frameworks, implementing such changes often requires navigating through multiple layers of trainers, distributed backends, and rollout engine integration. This architecture, while designed for ultra-large-scale training workloads and hyperscale scalability, can impose a significant cost on researchers by increasing the complexity and time required for iterative development.

Current RL infrastructure often presents a trade-off between scalability and ease of research. On one end, frameworks like HybridFlow/verl, NeMo-RL, and Megatron-based systems offer extensive capabilities for large-scale training but introduce substantial complexity and a broad multi-backend surface. On the other end, frameworks such as OpenRLHF and TRL are designed to be lightweight and accessible but may not target agentic RL at frontier scales.

Molt positions itself in this landscape by addressing the need for research infrastructure that minimizes the barrier between an idea and a trustworthy experiment, while simultaneously retaining the performance necessary for large policies. It aims to provide a lean, PyTorch-native framework that supports the scale of state-of-the-art models without inheriting the architectural complexity typically associated with hyperscale systems. The work focuses on simplifying the codebase and development workflow for researchers, making it more readable and navigable, even for AI coding assistants. This approach seeks to demonstrate that capable RL infrastructure does not inherently require hyperscale-specific layering but can be achieved through effective composition of separately hardened, frontier-scale components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Molt is to reduce the overhead and complexity faced by researchers when prototyping new algorithms, estimators, or rollout schemes in agentic reinforcement learning. The authors identify that in conventional frameworks, such modifications often necessitate tracing changes across multiple layers of trainers, distributed backends, and rollout engine integrations, imposing a substantial cost on research iteration.

The core motivation is to create an RL training framework where the entire algorithm flow is compact and transparent, allowing a researcher to comprehend it fully and an AI coding assistant to reason about it comprehensively. This design is intended to enable end-to-end tracing and modification of the algorithm, fostering a more efficient research cycle.

Specifically, the framework's design is guided by five principles:

1. **Readability for Humans and AI Coding Assistants**: The codebase is designed to be clear and concise, enabling researchers to quickly understand control and data flow. It aims for navigability by AI coding assistants, treating unnecessary indirection as a defect.
2. **Minimal Code, Deliberately One Backend**: The framework prioritizes code deletion over addition and supports a single training backend (NVIDIA AutoModel) and a single serving engine (vLLM). This avoids the complexity of multi-backend abstractions and ensures that upstream improvements are immediately usable without rebaselining.
3. **Performance Parity with State of the Art**: Leanness is contingent on achieving throughput comparable to state-of-the-art Megatron-based stacks. This principle ensures that simplifications do not compromise training efficiency and that the optimizations of underlying engines remain accessible.
4. **Modularity Follows the RL Algorithm**: Components are designed to map directly to algorithmic concepts such (agent/environment interface, rollout, advantage estimator, loss) rather than infrastructure layers. This structure aims to localize algorithmic edits to a single component.
5. **Correctness in the Details**: The framework guarantees numerical fidelity between generation and training. This includes ensuring token identity (training on exactly generated token IDs), policy-version semantics (retaining behavior policy log-probabilities with explicit asynchronous corrections), and forward consistency (agreement on model semantics, including multimodal expansion and MoE routing). Silent divergence is treated as a bug.

These objectives collectively aim to develop an RL infrastructure that is both hackable for research and capable of scaling to large models, asserting that complexity is a design choice rather than an unavoidable cost for capable systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Molt is a PyTorch-native training framework that integrates Ray, vLLM, and NeMo AutoModel around a single disaggregated asynchronous loop. The system architecture comprises three primary components: an agent pool, vLLM rollout engines, and a single FSDP2 policy actor. Ray provides distributed placement and manages the asynchronous queue that connects these components.

**System Architecture and Design Principles Realization:**

- **One Asynchronous Loop (Principles 1 & 2)**: The entire runtime is composed of three components linked by a single asynchronous loop, contributing to a compact and readable codebase. No hybrid controller or per-backend adapter layers are used.
 - **Streaming Pool**: Maintains persistent prompt groups in flight, overlapping generation and training to prevent engine draining. It emits a training batch as soon as sufficient groups are complete.
 - **Partial Rollout**: Enables weight updates without discarding in-flight requests. Engines are paused, actor shards are broadcast, and retained requests are resumed. Each action token retains its sampled log-probability, and a per-token correction is applied for asynchronous use, maintaining policy-version semantics.
- **Agent Contract (Principle 4)**: The agent is defined as ordinary Python code, imposing a single requirement: the RL run names a Python module exporting an `AgentRunner`.
 - **Env Agent**: For Gym-aligned environments, the framework manages the LLM loop, tokenization, and multimodal accounting, invoking the user's `step()` method after each model action.
 - **ChatAgent**: For existing agent code using standard OpenAI or Anthropic SDKs, Molt deploys a loopback chat server. SDK traffic is captured as token IDs and log-probabilities (Token-In/Token-Out, TITO) without requiring integration code or retokenization.
 - **Context Compaction**: The chat server automatically detects context compaction (e.g., prefix rewrites), segments the current token-exact trajectory, and starts a new one, ensuring consistent trajectory data for long-horizon agents.
- **One Data Path (Principle 4)**: Both Env and ChatAgent forms utilize a single chat-format data path. The chat template is applied exactly once, either pre-rendered by the dataset for Env agents or by the chat server for ChatAgents, ensuring identical inputs to the rollout engine.
- **Token-Exact Transport (Principle 5)**: Molt avoids forking vLLM, ensuring compatibility with upstream improvements. Trainer-facing generation uses vLLM's token-level interface, transmitting token IDs and per-token log-probabilities directly to prevent retokenization drift. The request router ensures session-consistent request handling and multimodal image realignment.
 - **Weight Refit**: Parameters are broadcast directly from the actor to each rollout engine via NCCL, bypassing the request router.
- **Scalability as Configuration (Principle 3)**: Scaling features are integrated as local extensions rather than new architectural layers.
 - **Composable Parallelism**: FSDP2 integrates with AutoModel-native tensor, expert, and context parallelism. Large-scale MoE models (e.g., 1T-class) are configured via flags (e.g., `--fsdp.ep_size 256`), enabling scale changes without redesign.
 - **MoE Consistency**: Rollout routing replay is implemented, where the engine returns per-token expert choices, and the actor replays them during training. This addresses potential divergence in expert selection between rollout and training in MoE models.
 - **Engine Features**: Features like speculative decoding, prefix caching, CUDA graphs, and optimizer CPU offload are accessible via engine flags through the composed path.
- **Algorithm Layer (Principle 4)**: This layer is designed with estimators as plain functions.
 - **Estimators**: Advantage estimators are pure functions of rewards and groups, selected by name (e.g., REINFORCE++, RLOO, GRPO, GAE).
 - **Loss Normalization**: Losses are normalized by a global whole-batch token mean, making updates invariant to data-parallel size and gradient-accumulation depth.
 - **Off-Policy Correction**: For asynchronous rollout, a per-token importance correction with a sequence-level gate is applied to the loss.
 - **Batch Shaping**: Dynamic filtering (DAPO-style) removes degenerate groups, and a force-on-policy option maps multi-turn rollouts to single optimizer steps for strict on-policy training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation aimed to verify Molt's adherence to its design principles, particularly performance parity with state-of-the-art frameworks despite its lean design.

**Footprint and Workflow:**
The authors report that Molt's complete RL path, encompassing trainer, rollout, orchestration, experience/advantage/loss, and imported model/utility/parallelism code, comprises approximately 8.6K Python lines. This contrasts with approximately 62K lines for verl and 25K for slime, indicating a significantly smaller codebase, consistent with the "minimal code" principle. The workflow for new experiments involves subclassing an `Env` or `ChatAgent`, launching with a single CLI command, and observing real-time logging of reward statistics, response-length distributions, evaluation pass@n, and per-stage timing breakdowns.

**Engine Features and Optimizations:**
Measurements on a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B multimodal MoE policy across 2 nodes (8 training + 8 rollout GPUs) demonstrated the effectiveness of integrating upstream engine optimizations:
- **Prefix Caching**: Rerunning a growing conversation showed a re-prefill time of 0.05 seconds with a cache hit, indicating the functional integration of this vLLM feature.
- **Speculative Decoding**: Enabling speculative decoding via a configuration flag reduced per-step generation time from 329 seconds to 64 seconds. This nearly 5x speedup shifted the workload from being generation-bound to training-bound.
- **Optimizer CPU Offload**: Using the `--fsdp.offload optimizer` flag reduced actor peak GPU memory usage from 64.7 GB to 46.4 GB (a 18.3 GB reduction), allowing the model to fit on an 8-GPU training partition. This memory saving incurred an 18% increase in `policy_train` time (from 213 s to 251 s).

**Head-to-Head Throughput Parity with a Megatron-Based Stack:**
A head-to-head benchmark compared Molt (AutoModel + vLLM) against slime (Megatron-Core + SGLang) on a Qwen3-30B-A3B model using bf16 precision. Both frameworks ran fully asynchronously, with rollout and training disaggregated onto 8 + 8 H100 GPUs. The protocol ensured identical settings where possible (dataset, batch, context/response lengths, sampling, optimizer), while allowing each stack its recommended parallel layout (Molt: FSDP2 DP8, EP8, TP1; slime: Megatron-Core TP4+SP, CP1, EP8).
- **Result**: Molt achieved an average of 119.4 ± 2.3 seconds per optimizer step. Slime recorded 109.5 ± 10.3 seconds per optimizer step. The authors concluded that the two stacks exhibit statistically comparable throughput, with the mean difference falling within cross-run variability. No superiority was claimed for either direction.
- **Token Throughput**: Molt achieved 461 generated tokens per GPU-second, compared to slime's 502 tokens per GPU-second, based on a common workload of approximately 880K tokens per step across 16 GPUs.
- **Fairness Notes**: The authors acknowledge remaining asymmetries, including differences in training layout and rollout engines. For this specific benchmark configuration, an upstream distributed-MoE forward mismatch meant that throughput was measured without an effective policy update, as the batch was rejected by a sequence gate due to actor log-probabilities differing from a reference forward. However, other 35B workloads did not exhibit this gap.

**Large Scale Demonstrations:**
The paper reports that Molt has successfully run the full asynchronous loop, including rollout, weight refit, and optimizer step, end-to-end on a 700B MoE model at expert parallelism 256. This was achieved using the same lean loop as a 4B dense model, demonstrating that scaling is primarily a matter of configuration rather than a fundamental redesign.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Molt addresses a fundamental challenge in agentic reinforcement learning research: the high cognitive load and implementation complexity imposed by existing frameworks, which are often optimized for hyperscale production rather than rapid research iteration. Its core significance lies in demonstrating that a lean, readable, and PyTorch-native training framework can achieve performance parity with complex, state-of-the-art Megatron-based systems.

The framework's emphasis on **human readability and AI coding assistant navigability** has implications for research productivity. By reducing the complexity of the codebase and making algorithmic flow explicit, Molt aims to lower the barrier to entry for researchers and facilitate faster prototyping of new RL algorithms. The explicit design for AI assistant understanding suggests a forward-looking approach to research infrastructure, anticipating and integrating evolving developer tooling.

The **minimal and single-backend code** approach, achieved by composing hardened upstream components (Ray, vLLM, AutoModel) rather than forking or creating multi-backend abstractions, provides several benefits. It ensures that Molt immediately inherits upstream improvements, kernels, and model support, reducing maintenance overhead and accelerating the adoption of new technologies. This strategy challenges the assumption that broad coverage necessitates complex, layered architectures.

The **demonstrated performance parity** with leading distributed training stacks, despite Molt's significantly smaller codebase, validates the framework's design principles. It indicates that researchers can benefit from a more agile and transparent development environment without compromising on the throughput required for large-scale model training. The successful demonstration of scaling to 700B MoE models via configuration rather than redesign further highlights its capacity to handle frontier-scale workloads.

Molt's focus on **correctness invariants** (token identity, policy-version semantics, forward consistency, MoE consistency) is crucial for the reliability of RL experiments. By explicitly addressing silent failure modes, the framework aims to produce more trustworthy experimental results, which is essential for advancing RL research.

As an **open-source project**, Molt provides a concrete, high-performance substrate for the RL community. Its available recipes and containers can accelerate the adoption and exploration of agentic RL at scale. The framework's design approach, which prioritizes a compact, visible loop and algorithm-aligned components, could influence the design of future research infrastructure, particularly as AI coding assistants become more integrated into the research workflow. Future work involves comprehensive end-to-end convergence measurement for the largest models, pushing the boundaries of model size Molt can effectively train.
