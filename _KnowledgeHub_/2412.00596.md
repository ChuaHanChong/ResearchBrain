# PhyT2V: LLM-Guided Iterative Self-Refinement for Physics-Grounded Text-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyT2V, developed at the University of Pittsburgh, proposes an LLM-guided iterative self-refinement framework to enhance the physical realism and semantic alignment of videos generated by existing Text-to-Video models. It achieves up to 2.3x improvement in semantic adherence and 2.2x improvement in physical common sense without retraining the T2V models.

## Problem

- Text-to-Video (T2V) models often fail to adhere to fundamental physical rules and common sense, generating visually plausible but physically inaccurate videos.
- Existing T2V models exhibit poor generalizability to "out-of-distribution" scenarios and suffer from issues like flickering and inconsistent object appearances.
- Prior attempts to inject physical knowledge are limited by data dependency, architectural complexity, or a lack of robust feedback mechanisms for physical correction.

## Method

- An LLM-guided iterative self-refinement framework, **PhyT2V**, enhances physics-grounded T2V generation by intelligently refining text prompts.
- The framework uses local Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for the LLM to identify relevant physical rules and semantic mismatches between generated videos and prompts.
- Global step-back reasoning by the LLM integrates physical rules, mismatches, and quantitative feedback to iteratively generate new, refined T2V prompts.

## Results

- Achieved significant improvements in Physical Common Sense (PC) and Semantic Adherence (SA) scores, with gains up to **2.2x** for PC and **2.3x** for SA on models like CogVideoX-2B.
- Demonstrated high generalizability, enhancing video quality across diverse physical domains and various T2V models (e.g., CogVideoX-5B, OpenSora) without requiring additional training data.
- Outperformed existing prompt enhancers by at least 35% in PC and SA scores, validating the effectiveness of its explicit reasoning and video-to-text alignment mechanism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can serve as robust reasoning engines, applying Chain-of-Thought and step-back reasoning to embed complex physical knowledge into textual prompts for video generation.
> - A text-based feedback loop, achieved by translating generated video content into captions for LLM analysis, enables effective and automated self-correction for physical realism and semantic alignment.
> - Injecting physical rules and contextual details directly into prompts, rather than modifying model architectures or expanding datasets, provides a data-independent and model-agnostic approach to enhance T2V quality.
