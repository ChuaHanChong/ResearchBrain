# GeoGS-SLAM: Online Monocular Reconstruction Using Gaussian Splatting with Geometric Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoGS-SLAM introduces a closed-loop monocular SLAM system that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with learned geometric priors, achieving superior photorealistic dense reconstruction and state-of-the-art tracking accuracy. The system operates in real-time using only uncalibrated RGB input.

## Problem

- Existing 3DGS-based SLAM systems often require depth information from external sensors, leading to degraded performance with monocular RGB input.
- Current geometric prior-based SLAM methods frequently discard original RGB observations during optimization, resulting in photometric information loss and compromised reconstruction fidelity.
- Developing an online monocular dense reconstruction system capable of both robust tracking and high-fidelity mapping without relying on depth sensors or sacrificing visual evidence.

## Method

- **GeoGS-SLAM** leverages a pre-trained visual geometry model (==VGGT==) to predict camera and scene geometric priors (intrinsics, extrinsics, depth, points) from uncalibrated RGB input.
- Employs a two-stage probability assessment (==Difference of Gaussians== for placement and occupancy) to efficiently sample ==3D Gaussian primitives==, then refines camera poses and the map via a coarse-to-fine, rendering-based ==joint optimization== using both ==photometric loss== and ==geometric loss==.
- Integrates online ==loop closure detection== via global descriptors and ==pose graph optimization== to ensure global consistency and mitigate accumulated drift over extended sequences.

## Results

- Achieved superior rendering quality (PSNR improvements of **+4.94** on Replica, **+3.15** on TUM RGB-D, **+3.81** on Waymo vs. radiance field-based monocular SLAM) and state-of-the-art tracking accuracy among uncalibrated RGB-only methods, reducing ATE RMSE by **46.7%** on Replica, **40.7%** on TUM RGB-D, and **64.6%** on Waymo against leading geometric prior-based SLAM.
- Maintained real-time performance with an average processing time of **582.1 ms** per keyframe, validating its suitability for online applications.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of loop closure, primitive sampling, scene priors, and especially learned camera priors to the system's overall high performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A closed-loop pipeline combining feed-forward geometric priors for bootstrapping with 3D Gaussian Splatting for photometric optimization addresses both monocular SLAM paradigms' limits, with learned camera priors essential for resolving scale ambiguity and convergence challenges.
> - Direct sampling of Gaussian primitives based on image detail and map occupancy effectively expands the scene representation while avoiding redundancy and maintaining efficiency.
> - Joint optimization, integrating both photometric loss from rendered RGB and geometric loss from predicted depth priors, is critical for achieving high-fidelity reconstruction and accurate camera tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental problem in computer vision, forming the basis for technologies in robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality. Recent advancements in SLAM have been significantly influenced by two primary areas: radiance field rendering and feed-forward scene reconstruction.

**Radiance Field-based SLAM:** The introduction of neural radiance fields (NeRF) and, more recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has provided new approaches to scene representation in SLAM systems. 3DGS, in particular, offers efficient, photorealistic rendering through differentiable rasterization of 3D Gaussians. This has enabled the development of SLAM systems capable of high-quality tracking and mapping, such as MonoGS and SplaTAM. However, a limitation of many existing 3DGS-based SLAM methods is their reliance on external geometric measurements, typically from depth sensors (e.g., RGB-D cameras). Their performance often degrades when restricted to monocular RGB input, limiting their applicability in scenarios where depth information is unavailable.

**Geometric Prior-based SLAM:** Concurrently, feed-forward models, exemplified by architectures like DUSt3R and VGGT, have advanced 3D scene reconstruction by providing learned geometric priors from large-scale training. A number of contemporary SLAM systems leverage these models to achieve real-time pose estimation and dense scene reconstruction, including MASt3R-SLAM, SLAM3R, and VGGT-SLAM. These methods utilize the learned priors as a primary signal for optimization. A challenge with this approach is that it frequently omits the original RGB observations from the optimization loop. This omission can preclude closed-loop verification against visual evidence, potentially compromising the fidelity and consistency of the overall reconstruction.

**GeoGS-SLAM's Position:** GeoGS-SLAM addresses the limitations of both aforementioned paradigms. It aims to develop an online monocular dense reconstruction system that combines the strengths of 3DGS-based map representation (photorealistic rendering) with learned geometric priors (robust bootstrapping). By integrating these two components in a closed-loop framework, GeoGS-SLAM seeks to overcome the depth sensor dependency of 3DGS-based systems and the photometric information loss in geometric prior-based systems. This approach allows for robust geometric initialization while maintaining the visual fidelity and consistency that comes from optimizing against original RGB observations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of GeoGS-SLAM is to develop an online monocular dense reconstruction system that achieves robust tracking and high-fidelity mapping using only uncalibrated RGB input. This objective is motivated by specific limitations observed in existing SLAM methodologies:

1. **Addressing the Depth Dependency of 3DGS-based SLAM:** Current 3DGS-based SLAM systems often require depth information from external sensors (e.g., RGB-D cameras) to achieve their reported performance. When confronted with monocular RGB-only input, their mapping capabilities and tracking accuracy can decline significantly. The motivation here is to enable the benefits of 3DGS—such as efficient, photorealistic rendering and high-quality scene representation—to be realized in scenarios where depth sensors are impractical or unavailable, broadening the applicability of these advanced representations.

2. **Mitigating Photometric Information Loss in Geometric Prior-based SLAM:** Recent SLAM systems that leverage pre-trained feed-forward models for geometric priors can achieve real-time dense reconstruction and initial pose estimates. However, these methods frequently discard or de-emphasize the original RGB observations during the optimization process, relying predominantly on the learned geometric priors. This practice prevents the system from performing closed-loop verification against actual visual evidence, which can lead to a degradation in the overall reconstruction quality, texture accuracy, and long-term consistency. The motivation is to integrate the rich photometric information from RGB images directly into the optimization, allowing for visually driven refinement that enhances reconstruction fidelity.

3. **Synergistic Combination for Robustness and Fidelity:** The overarching motivation is to combine the complementary strengths of both approaches: leveraging learned geometric priors to provide a robust geometric bootstrap for initial camera poses and scene structure, while simultaneously utilizing 3DGS with photometric loss to refine this structure and appearance based on the original visual evidence. This synergy is intended to create a more resilient and accurate system for online dense reconstruction from monocular RGB input.

To achieve these motivations, GeoGS-SLAM sets the following key objectives:
- To employ a feed-forward visual geometry model to predict camera and scene priors (intrinsics, extrinsics, depth, point maps) from uncalibrated RGB input.
- To develop a mechanism for expanding the Gaussian scene map by directly sampling Gaussian primitives from both the RGB input and the predicted geometric priors.
- To implement a joint, rendering-based optimization strategy that refines camera poses and the scene map simultaneously, minimizing both photometric and geometric losses in a coarse-to-fine manner.
- To incorporate online loop closure detection and pose graph optimization to ensure global consistency and mitigate accumulated drift over extended sequences.
- To demonstrate that this integrated approach achieves superior rendering quality and tracking accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art monocular SLAM methods, while maintaining real-time online performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoGS-SLAM is structured as an online monocular dense reconstruction system that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with learned geometric priors. The methodology involves four core components: Geometric Prior Prediction, Map Expansion, Joint Optimization, and Online Loop Closure.

**A. 3DGS Scene Representation**
The system represents the scene using a collection of 3D Gaussian primitives. Each primitive $G_i$ is characterized by its 3D position $\mu_i$, color $c_i$, scale $S_i$, rotation $R_i$, and opacity $o_i$. The ellipsoidal shape of each Gaussian is defined by a covariance matrix $\Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T$. For rendering, these 3D Gaussians are projected onto the image plane, creating 2D Gaussians $G_i'$ with transformed means $\mu_i'$ and covariances $\Sigma_i'$. The final color and depth images are rendered at each pixel through $\alpha$-blending of these depth-sorted 2D Gaussians.

**B. Geometric Prior Prediction**
This component leverages a pre-trained visual geometry model, VGGT, to generate initial geometric priors from uncalibrated RGB input.
- **Keyframe Management:** A sliding window strategy is used to group keyframes. An RGB frame becomes a keyframe if its optical flow-based displacement from the last keyframe exceeds a threshold ($\tau_{disp}$). Overlapping windows ensure continuity, where the last keyframe of a previous window becomes the first of the current.
- **Prior Generation:** For each window $W_k$ containing keyframes $I_i^k$, the VGGT model predicts camera extrinsics ($^p T_i^k$), intrinsics ($^p K_i^k$), depth maps ($^p D_i^k$), and confidence maps ($^p Q_i^k$). Point maps ($^p X_i^k$) are generated by unprojecting the predicted depth maps using the predicted camera parameters, a method found to yield higher accuracy than direct point map regression.
- **Scale Alignment:** To maintain consistency across consecutive windows, the metric scale of predictions in the current window $W_k$ is aligned with the previous window $W_{k-1}$. This is achieved by computing a scale factor $\rho^{k-1,k}$ based on the ratio of depths of mutually high-confidence pixels in the overlapping keyframe ($I_{w}^{k-1} = I_{0}^{k}$). This scale factor is then applied to all metric predictions (translations of poses, depth maps, and point maps) within $W_k$.

**C. Map Expansion**
New Gaussian primitives are directly sampled and added to the 3D Gaussian map. This process employs a two-stage probability assessment to place primitives efficiently and avoid redundancy.
- **Placement Probability:** The Difference of Gaussians (DoG) operator is applied to the input image $I$ to identify regions with rich geometric detail, yielding a placement probability matrix $P_1$.
- **Occupancy Probability:** To prevent spawning Gaussians in already well-reconstructed areas, a synthetic view $^r I$ is rendered from the current 3D Gaussian map using the predicted camera pose. The DoG operator is applied to $^r I$ to compute an occupancy probability $P_2$.
- **Sampling Condition:** A new Gaussian is spawned at a pixel $(u, v)$ only if the difference between the placement and occupancy probabilities ($P_1(u,v) - P_2(u,v)$) exceeds a predefined threshold $\tau_{prim}$.
- **Initialization:** For each qualified pixel, the new Gaussian primitive's color $c$ is sampled from $I(u,v)$. Its 3D position $\mu$ is derived from the transformed point prior $^p X(u,v)$ in world coordinates. The scale $s$ for each dimension is initialized based on the predicted focal length $^p f$ and the point's position in the camera coordinate system.

**D. Joint Optimization**
Following map expansion, a coarse-to-fine, rendering-based joint optimization is performed to refine both the 3D Gaussian scene map ($G$) and the camera poses within the active keyframe window ($W$).
- **Photometric Loss ($L_{pho}$):** This loss enforces visual consistency between the rendered image $^r I$ and the input keyframe image $I$. It combines L1 loss and Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) loss.
- **Geometric Loss ($L_{geo}$):** This loss maintains consistency between the rendered depth map $^r D$ and the predicted depth prior $^p D$ from the visual geometry model, using an L1 loss.
- **Total Objective:** The overall optimization objective is the sum of photometric and geometric losses across all keyframes in the active window.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Strategy:** To accelerate convergence and enhance stability, optimization starts at the coarsest level of image and depth pyramids, progressively moving to finer resolutions at fixed iteration intervals.

**E. Online Loop Closure**
To counteract accumulated drift and enhance global consistency, GeoGS-SLAM integrates online loop closure detection and pose graph optimization.
- **Descriptor Extraction:** A global descriptor $d$ is extracted for each keyframe using the pre-trained MegaLoc model, providing robust place recognition.
- **Loop Detection:** For a query keyframe, the system searches for the most similar keyframe candidate from all previous windows by comparing global descriptors using cosine similarity. A genuine loop closure is validated if the similarity score exceeds a threshold $\tau_{sim}$.
- **Pose Graph Construction and Optimization:** Upon detecting valid loop closures, the active window is extended to include the loop frames. The feed-forward geometry model provides relative pose estimates between query and loop frames, which serve as loop closure constraints. These constraints are added to a pose graph, alongside odometry constraints from locally optimized keyframe poses. The complete pose graph is then optimized using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm, yielding pose corrections $\Delta T_i$ for each keyframe.
- **Map and Pose Adjustment:** Finally, these pose corrections are applied to update both the keyframe poses and the positions and rotations of associated Gaussian primitives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of GeoGS-SLAM was evaluated through extensive experiments across indoor and outdoor benchmarks, with comparisons against state-of-the-art monocular SLAM methods.

**A. Experimental Setup**
- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on Replica (Office, Room), TUM RGB-D (fr1 sequences), and Waymo (nine 200-frame sequences).
- **Metrics:** Rendering quality was assessed using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. Tracking accuracy was evaluated using the Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of the Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE).
- **Baseline Methods:** Comparisons included neural implicit representation-based (NI) methods (GO-SLAM, GlORIE-SLAM), 3DGS-based (GS) methods (MonoGS, Photo-SLAM, Splat-SLAM, DROID-Splat, S3PO-GS), and geometric prior-based SLAM methods (SLAM3R, MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM).
- **Implementation:** Implemented in PyTorch and CUDA, running on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU and Intel Core i9-13900K CPU.

**B. Results**

- **i) Rendering Quality:**
 - **Quantitative:** GeoGS-SLAM demonstrated superior rendering performance across all evaluated datasets and metrics (Table I). Compared to the best radiance field-based monocular SLAM methods, it achieved substantial improvements in PSNR: +4.94 on Replica, +3.15 on TUM RGB-D, and +3.81 on Waymo. It also exhibited the highest SSIM and lowest LPIPS scores, indicating better perceptual quality.
 - **Qualitative:** Visual comparisons (Figure 3) showed that GeoGS-SLAM produced photorealistic reconstructions for both indoor and outdoor scenes. For indoor environments, it accurately captured fine-grained textures, lighting variations, and geometric structures with minimal artifacts. In complex outdoor scenarios (Waymo), the method successfully handled varying illumination, dynamic content, and large-scale environments, preserving architectural structures and vehicle details.
 - **Conclusion:** These results indicate the benefits of integrating learned geometric priors into 3DGS-based SLAM frameworks.

- **ii) Tracking Accuracy:**
 - **Quantitative:** GeoGS-SLAM achieved state-of-the-art tracking performance compared to other methods operating on uncalibrated RGB input (Table II). It also maintained competitive accuracy with approaches that leverage calibrated camera parameters. Notably, it outperformed MASt3R-SLAM, a leading geometric prior-based SLAM method, by reducing tracking error (ATE RMSE) by 46.7% on Replica, 40.7% on TUM RGB-D, and 64.6% on Waymo.
 - **Qualitative:** Trajectory comparisons (Figure 4) illustrated that GeoGS-SLAM maintained accuracy and robustness across diverse indoor and outdoor environments.
 - **Explanation:** The paper attributes this superior tracking performance to its closed-loop optimization approach, which effectively fuses guidance from geometric priors with photometric refinement against actual image evidence. Geometric prior-based methods without photometric refinement tend to accumulate drift and show reduced accuracy in longer and more complex sequences.
 - **Conclusion:** The findings validate the effectiveness of GeoGS-SLAM's integrated optimization strategy.

- **iii) Real-time Performance:**
 - **Runtime Analysis:** GeoGS-SLAM demonstrated real-time performance, with an average processing time of 582.1 ms per keyframe (Table III). This was achieved by applying joint optimization only at keyframes, indicating the computational efficiency of the selective optimization strategy.

**C. Ablation Study**
An ablation study on the Replica dataset (Table IV) quantified the contribution of each core component:
- **Loop Closure:** Removal of loop closure detection and pose graph optimization led to a significant degradation in tracking accuracy (ATE increased from 0.024 to 0.034), confirming its role in enhancing global consistency.
- **Primitive Sampling:** Replacing the direct primitive sampling strategy with uniform sampling resulted in notable rendering quality deterioration (PSNR dropped from 37.56 to 35.62), validating the effectiveness of the proposed sampling method.
- **Scene Priors:** Eliminating feed-forward scene priors (using randomly initialized depth) caused a severe decline in both rendering (PSNR dropped to 22.28) and tracking (ATE increased to 0.052) performance. This underscores the importance of geometric constraints for monocular 3DGS-based SLAM.
- **Camera Priors:** Most significantly, removing learned camera priors (and relying on raw intrinsic estimates with the last keyframe's pose for initialization) led to catastrophic degradation in both rendering (PSNR dropped to 15.94) and tracking (ATE increased to 0.922) performance. This result highlights the critical role of learned camera priors in addressing the inherent scale ambiguity and convergence challenges associated with monocular SLAM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GeoGS-SLAM represents an advancement in monocular dense reconstruction by establishing a novel, closed-loop pipeline that effectively integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with learned geometric priors. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to address key limitations of existing SLAM methodologies and deliver enhanced performance.

**Addressing Core Challenges:**
- **Monocular 3DGS-SLAM:** The work demonstrates a viable approach to enable 3DGS-based SLAM systems to operate robustly and with high fidelity using only monocular RGB input, eliminating the dependency on external depth sensors. This expands the applicability of 3DGS, which is known for its efficient and photorealistic rendering capabilities, to a wider range of platforms and scenarios where depth sensors are not feasible or desired (e.g., small drones, mobile phones, low-cost robotics).
- **Improving Geometric Prior-based SLAM:** By incorporating photometric evidence into the optimization loop, GeoGS-SLAM overcomes the limitation of prior-based systems that often discard original RGB observations. This integration leads to significantly higher reconstruction quality and visual consistency, as demonstrated by the superior PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS scores.

**Performance Benchmarks:**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** GeoGS-SLAM achieves state-of-the-art rendering quality and tracking accuracy among monocular SLAM systems, particularly those relying solely on RGB input. Its ability to perform comparably to or surpass systems leveraging calibrated camera parameters or additional sensors demonstrates its robust design.
- **Real-time Operation:** Maintaining real-time performance, as evidenced by its keyframe processing speed, makes GeoGS-SLAM suitable for online applications requiring immediate feedback, such as interactive augmented reality, robotic navigation, and real-time mapping for autonomous systems.

**Broader Impact and Future Directions:**
- **New Paradigm for Visual SLAM:** The proposed "closed-loop pipeline that leverages feed-forward priors for geometric bootstrapping while preserving photometric evidence for radiance field rendering-based optimization" introduces a new paradigm. This approach effectively combines the strengths of data-driven learning models with differentiable rendering techniques, offering a template for designing more robust and high-performance SLAM systems.
- **Enabling Applications:** The ability to perform high-fidelity, dense 3D reconstruction from a single camera input with real-time performance holds significant potential for various applications. This includes enhanced environmental perception for autonomous vehicles, improved human-robot interaction through detailed scene understanding, and more immersive augmented/virtual reality experiences.
- **Research Directions:** The work suggests further research avenues in optimizing the interplay between learned geometric priors and differentiable rendering, potentially leading to even more accurate and efficient methods for real-time 3D reconstruction and localization in complex and dynamic environments. The successful integration of distinct computational paradigms highlights the potential for hybrid systems in advancing perception tasks.
