# Risk-Sensitive RL for Alleviating Exploration Dilemmas in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> An RL framework named RS-GRPO, developed by researchers from Tsinghua University, ETH Zurich, and ByteDance Seed, leverages risk-sensitive objectives to mitigate the exploration dilemma in large language model fine-tuning. This method consistently boosts multi-solution performance (Pass@k) and solution diversity on mathematical reasoning tasks, while preserving or enhancing single-solution accuracy (Pass@1).

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods often lead to an "exploration dilemma" in LLM fine-tuning, concentrating policy mass around limited, known solutions.
- This "sharpening" compromises solution diversity and Pass@k performance, preventing LLMs from discovering genuinely novel reasoning strategies beyond their pre-trained biases.
- Standard RL algorithms struggle to escape local optima in LLMs due to their sharply peaked initial policies, essentially distilling existing capabilities rather than expanding them.

## Method

- Introduced a ==Risk-Sensitive== Reinforcement Learning framework, instantiated as the **Risk-Sensitive GRPO** (RS-GRPO) algorithm, to promote risk-seeking exploration.
- The core of RS-GRPO is a risk-seeking objective function derived from an exponential utility, which dynamically re-weights optimization signals to emphasize high-reward outcomes.
- The algorithm acts as a "drop-in replacement" for existing GRPO-based RL methods by only modifying the advantage calculation, ensuring ease of integration and implementation.

## Results

- RS-GRPO consistently outperformed standard GRPO and base LLMs across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks and five different LLMs in Pass@k, sometimes by substantial margins.
- The method maintained or improved Pass@1 accuracy across tested models, demonstrating a superior balance between single-solution performance and multi-solution discovery compared to prior Pass@k optimization techniques.
- Analysis revealed RS-GRPO significantly increased the ratio of unique correct solutions found and improved accuracy on previously unsolved prompts, directly indicating enhanced solution diversity and exploration capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A risk-seeking objective function, controlled by the hyperparameter "beta", effectively encourages LLMs to explore low-probability regions of the solution space and discover novel reasoning paths.
> - The risk-sensitive advantage function dynamically amplifies learning signals for high-reward samples, particularly correct responses on challenging prompts, fostering deeper exploration.
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates that, for a sufficiently large risk-sensitivity parameter, this approach can guarantee an increase in the probability of optimal actions, enabling escape from local optima.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically in their fine-tuning using Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

**RLVR for LLMs:** The paper begins by acknowledging that RLVR has been "highly effective" in enhancing LLMs for complex reasoning tasks, leading to "superhuman performance" in some domains. This sets the stage by affirming the importance of RL in pushing LLM capabilities. RLVR is a prominent paradigm for aligning LLMs with human preferences or task-specific objectives, often relying on a reward model to evaluate generated responses.

**The "Exploration Dilemma":** The core problem identified is the "exploration dilemma," a critical failure mode of current RLVR methods. In contrast to traditional RL where agents learn from scratch with random policies, LLMs begin with "sharply peaked initial policies" inherited from pre-training. Standard RL algorithms, designed to optimize mean rewards, tend to exploit these existing biases, leading to a "sharpening" of the policy distribution around a limited set of known solutions. This boosts single-solution accuracy (pass@1) but simultaneously suppresses solution diversity and stagnates or degrades multi-solution performance (pass@k). Essentially, existing methods distill pre-trained capabilities rather than discovering genuinely "novel reasoning strategies." This bottleneck is a significant concern for the continued advancement of LLMs, especially in open-ended or complex reasoning tasks where diverse approaches might be necessary to find optimal solutions. The paper explicitly cites other works that have also identified this issue, demonstrating it's a recognized problem in the field.

**Broader Context of Exploration in RL:** The paper contrasts this LLM-specific exploration dilemma with traditional RL exploration challenges. In domains like game-playing, where policies are randomly initialized, intrinsic motivation techniques (e.g., count-based exploration, random network distillation) are widely used to encourage broad exploration. While some have adapted these to LLMs, they often involve auxiliary networks, adding complexity. The paper distinguishes its approach by focusing on directly optimizing for inference-time objectives.

**Pass@k Optimization:** The work also positions itself within a line of research specifically focused on optimizing for Pass@k performance, which is a key metric for evaluating an LLM's ability to find correct solutions given multiple attempts. Early efforts in this direction included "Best-of-N (BoN) alignment," which has evolved into policy gradient methods for pass@k optimization. The paper highlights its advantages over these prior methods, particularly its ability to handle continuous rewards and provide a denser advantage signal, which is crucial for balancing Pass@k and Pass@1 improvements.

**Risk-Sensitive Reinforcement Learning:** This paper innovatively introduces Risk-Sensitive RL as the solution. While historically, risk-sensitive RL focused on "risk-averse" strategies for safety-critical domains (e.g., finance, robotics), more recent work, particularly with distributional RL, has shown that "risk-seeking" behaviors can promote exploration. This paper adapts this latter insight, positing that a risk-seeking objective is precisely what LLMs need to escape initial policy biases and achieve broader exploration. This application of risk-seeking objectives to LLM fine-tuning for exploration is a novel contribution to the LLM-RL landscape.

In summary, this research is a significant contribution to the field of LLM alignment and fine-tuning, offering a principled solution to the "exploration dilemma" that limits the discovery of novel reasoning strategies. It bridges insights from risk-sensitive RL with the unique challenges of LLM policy landscapes, presenting a simple yet effective algorithm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research stem directly from the identified "exploration dilemma" in RL fine-tuning of Large Language Models:

1. **Overcome the Exploration Dilemma:** The most critical objective is to address the fundamental limitation of existing RLVR methods that often lead to "sharpening the policy distribution around a limited number of homogeneous solutions." This compromises solution diversity and hinders multi-solution performance (pass@k).
2. **Enable Discovery of Novel Reasoning Strategies:** Current methods are criticized for merely "distilling existing capabilities rather than discovering new reasoning strategies." The motivation is to enable LLMs to explore genuinely new, high-reward solutions that might be in low-probability regions of the initial policy distribution.
3. **Escape Local Optima Induced by Pre-trained Biases:** The authors argue that the sharply peaked initial policies of pre-trained LLMs create a challenging optimization landscape where standard RL algorithms struggle to "escape the gravitational pull of the pretrained model's biases." The goal is to develop a mechanism that allows the policy to move beyond these local optima.
4. **Improve Pass@k Performance without Sacrificing Pass@1:** A key desideratum is to boost multi-solution performance (pass@k) while maintaining or even enhancing single-solution accuracy (pass@1). This strikes a better balance than prior methods that often achieve pass@k improvements at the cost of pass@1 or vice versa.
5. **Develop a Simple and Flexible Solution:** The motivation is to introduce an approach that is easy to implement and integrate into existing RL pipelines. The paper aims for a "drop-in replacement" for advantage calculation, minimizing code modifications.
6. **Provide Theoretical and Empirical Justification:** The researchers aim to not only propose a practical algorithm but also provide a robust theoretical framework and empirical evidence (e.g., through bandit experiments) to explain *why* their risk-sensitive approach is superior for this specific problem.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock the full potential of RL for LLM fine-tuning by enabling more effective and diverse exploration, moving beyond merely refining known solutions to discovering truly novel and complex reasoning paths.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research lies in introducing a **Risk-Sensitive Reinforcement Learning framework** to address the exploration dilemma in LLM fine-tuning. The approach involves a novel objective function and a corresponding advantage calculation, instantiated as the **Risk-Sensitive GRPO (RS-GRPO)** algorithm.

#### 3.1. Risk-Sensitive Objective Function

- **Problem with Standard RL:** Traditional RL aims to maximize the *expected* (mean) reward, also known as a risk-neutral objective. When LLMs start with sharply peaked policies, this often leads to exploitation of nearby, suboptimal modes.
- **Risk-Seeking Objective:** To counter this, the authors propose a risk-seeking objective derived from an exponential utility function. For a policy $\pi_{\theta}$ and prompt $x$, the objective $J_{RS}(\pi_{\theta})$ interpolates smoothly between the mean and the maximum reward:
 $J_{RS}(\pi_{\theta}) = E_{x \sim D} \left[ \frac{1}{\beta} \log E_{y \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot|x)} \left[ e^{\beta r(y)} \right] \right]$
 Here, $\beta$ is a hyperparameter controlling risk-sensitivity:
 - $\beta \rightarrow 0$: Recovers the standard expected reward (risk-neutral).
 - $\beta \rightarrow +\infty$: Approaches the maximum reward (risk-seeking), encouraging exploration.
 - $\beta \rightarrow -\infty$: Approaches the minimum reward (risk-averse), promoting robustness.
- **Rationale:** For exploration in LLMs, a risk-seeking objective ($\beta > 0$) is chosen. As $\beta$ increases, the objective places greater emphasis on high-reward outcomes, pushing the model to find paths that yield the best possible results, even if they are initially rare.

#### 3.2. Risk-Sensitive Policy Gradient and Advantage Function

- **Derivation:** The authors derive the policy gradient for this risk-sensitive objective. The resulting gradient has a familiar policy gradient structure but with a modified advantage function, $A_{\pi_{\theta}}^{\beta}(y)$:
 $\nabla_{\theta} J_{RS}(\pi_{\theta}) = E_{x \sim D, y \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot|x)} \left[ A_{\pi_{\theta}}^{\beta}(y) \nabla_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(y | x) \right]$
 where $A_{\pi_{\theta}}^{\beta}(y) = \frac{1}{\beta} \left( \frac{e^{\beta r(y)}}{E_{y' \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot|x)}[e^{\beta r(y')}]} - 1 \right)$.
- **Empirical Implementation (RS-GRPO):** In practice, the gradient is approximated using $N$ sampled responses $\{y_i\}_{i=1}^N$. The empirical advantage is:
 $\hat{A}_{\pi_{\theta}}^{\beta}(y_i) = \frac{1}{\beta} \left( \frac{e^{\beta r(y_i)}}{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N e^{\beta r(y_j)}} - 1 \right)$.
- **Key Feature: Drop-in Replacement:** A significant design choice is that this formulation *only alters the advantage computation*, leaving the overall policy gradient structure intact. This allows RS-GRPO to be a "drop-in replacement" for existing GRPO-based RL algorithms (like GRPO itself, DrGRPO, etc.), simplifying implementation.

#### 3.3. Analysis of the Risk-Sensitive Advantage Function

- **Mechanism of Exploration:** The risk-sensitive advantage function dynamically re-weights optimization signals.
 - **Continuous Rewards:** As $\beta$ increases, the advantage function shifts from being linear with reward (standard RL) to a step-like curve, amplifying advantages for high-reward samples and suppressing those for low-reward ones.
 - **Binary Rewards (LLM context):** For binary rewards (common in RLVR), increasing $\beta$ makes the advantage function prioritize *correct responses on hard prompts* (low accuracy) while reducing penalties for incorrect ones on easy prompts (high accuracy). This means RS-GRPO focuses learning on the most challenging instances where the model performs poorly, thereby driving deeper exploration into under-explored regions of the solution space.
- **Denser Advantage Signal:** The method yields a denser advantage signal compared to prior Pass@k optimization methods, which often have their optimization weight vanish once prompt accuracy surpasses a certain threshold. RS-GRPO maintains a non-zero gradient even for high-accuracy prompts, facilitating a better trade-off between Pass@k and Pass@1.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Support

The paper includes a theoretical analysis using a multi-armed bandit problem:
- **Lemma 2 (Standard RL Flaw):** Demonstrates that standard policy gradient can decrease the probability of an optimal action if a suboptimal action is better than average, leading to convergence in local optima.
- **Lemma 3 (RS-GRPO Advantage):** Shows that for a sufficiently large $\beta$, the risk-sensitive update guarantees an increase in the probability of the optimal action, enabling escape from local optima.
- **Lemma 4 (Optimal $\beta$):** Suggests that while a large $\beta$ helps exploration, excessively large values can slow down convergence, providing guidance for hyperparameter tuning.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Five diverse LLMs were used, including Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-4B-Base, and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct.
- **Tasks:** Focus on mathematical reasoning, using six benchmarks (MATH500, AIME24/25, HMMT-Feb24/25, CMIMC25).
- **Training Framework:** Built upon VeRL and incorporates techniques from DAPO (dynamic sampling, clip-higher) for robust RL training. Uses Math_Verify for ground-truth rewards.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Pass@k (for $k \in \{1, \ldots, 1024\}$) is the primary metric, with Pass@1 as a crucial sub-metric.

This comprehensive methodology demonstrates a well-founded approach to address a known problem in LLM fine-tuning, combining theoretical derivations with practical algorithmic design and extensive empirical validation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a strong set of empirical and theoretical findings demonstrating the effectiveness of the Risk-Sensitive Reinforcement Learning framework, particularly the RS-GRPO algorithm.

1. **Consistent and Significant Pass@k Improvement:**
 - RS-GRPO consistently and significantly outperforms both the base LLM models and the standard GRPO baseline across all six mathematical reasoning benchmarks and five different LLMs tested (Fig. 4).
 - For several models (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen3-4B), standard GRPO actually underperforms the base model for higher values of $k$ (e.g., $k > 256$). This supports the "sharpening" hypothesis, where GRPO merely concentrates probability mass without true exploration. In contrast, RS-GRPO surpasses the base model's performance even at high $k$, indicating its ability to genuinely expand the model's exploratory boundaries.
 - While some models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct) still show RS-GRPO falling short of the base model at very high $k$, it still represents a substantial improvement over standard GRPO, suggesting that the optimal policy might be prohibitively distant from the initial distribution for these models.

2. **Maintenance or Enhancement of Pass@1 Accuracy:**
 - A crucial finding is that RS-GRPO achieves its pass@k gains while *maintaining or even improving* pass@1 accuracy (Table 2). This demonstrates a superior trade-off compared to existing methods, which often boost pass@k at the expense of pass@1.
 - RS-GRPO improves pass@1 by an average of about 2% across several models (Qwen2.5-7B-Math, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-4B) compared to GRPO.

3. **Empirical and Theoretical Validation of Exploration:**
 - **Bandit Experiment:** A 100-armed bandit problem clearly illustrates the exploration dilemma. A standard risk-neutral policy ($\beta=0$) and low-risk-sensitivity policies (e.g., $\beta < 4$) get trapped in a suboptimal local optimum. Policies with sufficient risk-seeking behavior ($\beta \geq 4$) successfully escape this trap and converge to the global optimum (Figure 3).
 - **Theoretical Lemmas:** Lemmas 2 and 3 formally explain why standard policy gradients can fail by decreasing the probability of optimal actions and why risk-sensitive gradients, for sufficiently large $\beta$, guarantee an increase in the probability of optimal actions, facilitating escape from local optima.

4. **Impact of Risk-Sensitivity Hyperparameter $\beta$:**
 - An ablation study on $\beta$ (Figure 5) reveals that increasing $\beta$ generally improves the cumulative solve rate on training data.
 - However, training reward growth slows with increasing $\beta$, which the authors suggest might be a regularization effect preventing overfitting.
 - On test benchmarks, $\beta=2$ offers an effective trade-off, leading to approximately 5% gain in pass@32 while also providing a 1-2% improvement in pass@1 for some models (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B). This guides practical hyperparameter selection.
 - Lemma 4 theoretically supports this, indicating that while larger $\beta$ ensures optimal action improvement, excessively large $\beta$ can slow down convergence speed.

5. **Superiority over Other Pass@k Optimization Baselines:**
 - Compared to other pass@k optimization methods, RS-GRPO generally matches or surpasses their pass@32 performance while consistently outperforming them in pass@1 (Figure 6, Table 2).
 - This is attributed to RS-GRPO's "denser advantage signals," which allow it to balance Pass@1 and Pass@k more effectively.
 - The method by Walder and Karkhanis performed poorly due to strictly positive advantage estimates, leading to rapid entropy collapse, consistent with prior findings on the importance of negative signals.

6. **Enhanced Solution Diversity:**
 - An analysis of unique answers ratio (Figure 7, left) shows that RS-GRPO significantly increases the number of unique correct solutions found compared to GRPO. This directly demonstrates that risk-sensitive RL enhances the diversity of reasoning paths, fulfilling a core objective.

7. **Accuracy Transition Dynamics:**
 - A heatmap illustrating prompt accuracy transitions from GRPO to RS-GRPO (Figure 7, right) shows that 8% of prompts with zero accuracy under GRPO achieve (0, 0.4] accuracy with RS-GRPO, while only 3% move in the opposite direction. This shift is the primary driver of pass@k improvements.
 - A smaller trade-off is observed: 3% of prompts with perfect accuracy (1) under GRPO shift to (0.6, 1) with RS-GRPO, while only 1% move oppositely. This explains why pass@1 improvements are more modest than pass@k gains; RS-GRPO solves more problems but occasionally errs on simpler ones.

8. **Entropy Analysis (Appendix):**
 - While not consistently increasing with $\beta$, the entropy analysis (Figure 9) suggests a relationship between optimizing the risk-seeking objective and an increase in entropy, with the lowest entropy levels observed for $\beta=0$. This implies that risk-seeking behavior fosters a less peaked, more diverse policy distribution.

In summary, the findings robustly support that RS-GRPO effectively addresses the exploration dilemma, leading to superior pass@k performance while maintaining strong pass@1 results, driven by its ability to foster diverse reasoning strategies through a risk-seeking objective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Risk-Sensitive Reinforcement Learning, specifically the RS-GRPO algorithm, holds significant implications for the future development and application of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in complex reasoning tasks.

1. **Breaking the "Exploration Dilemma" Bottleneck:** The most profound impact is the direct and effective solution to the "exploration dilemma" that has plagued RL fine-tuning of LLMs. By enabling LLMs to genuinely "discover new reasoning strategies" rather than merely "distilling existing capabilities," RS-GRPO moves beyond policy sharpening to fostering true capability expansion. This is critical for advancing LLMs beyond rote memorization and simple pattern matching towards more robust, generalized intelligence.

2. **Enhanced LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** For domains like mathematical reasoning, where this paper extensively validates its approach, the ability to explore diverse solution paths is paramount. RS-GRPO's demonstrated improvements in pass@k performance mean that LLMs can solve a higher proportion of challenging problems, even if they require multiple attempts. This makes LLMs more reliable and powerful tools for scientific discovery, engineering, and problem-solving.

3. **Improved Practical Utility and Robustness:** The ability to find more unique solutions and succeed on harder prompts translates directly into more capable and robust LLMs. In real-world applications, where optimal solutions might be non-obvious or require creative approaches, an LLM trained with RS-GRPO would be more likely to find a correct answer. The ability to maintain or even improve pass@1 while significantly boosting pass@k offers a better balance for practical deployment, ensuring that single-attempt accuracy isn't sacrificed for overall problem-solving capacity.

4. **Simplicity and Accessibility:** The design of RS-GRPO as a "drop-in replacement" for advantage calculation in existing GRPO-based RL algorithms is a major strength. This ease of implementation reduces the barrier to adoption for researchers and developers, allowing them to integrate risk-sensitive exploration into their LLM fine-tuning pipelines with minimal effort. This accelerates the dissemination and application of the technique.

5. **Principled Framework for Exploration:** The use of risk-sensitive RL provides a theoretically grounded framework for tackling exploration. Unlike heuristic-driven exploration methods, the risk-seeking objective offers a principled way to control the trade-off between exploration and exploitation using the $\beta$ parameter, with theoretical backing from bandit experiments and lemmas.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research. The paper itself notes limitations, such as the fixed $\beta$ parameter, suggesting dynamic adjustment strategies as an area for further investigation. The success of risk-sensitive objectives in LLMs could inspire their application to other generative modeling domains beyond text, such as image or code generation, where diverse outputs and novel solution discovery are also valuable. Furthermore, exploring the interplay between risk-sensitive RL and other advanced exploration techniques (e.g., intrinsic motivation, curiosity-driven learning) could lead to even more powerful LLM training paradigms.

In conclusion, this research provides a significant step forward in training more intelligent and versatile LLMs. By effectively addressing the exploration dilemma through a simple yet powerful risk-sensitive RL framework, it paves the way for LLMs that can truly discover novel solutions and tackle complex problems with greater depth and diversity.
