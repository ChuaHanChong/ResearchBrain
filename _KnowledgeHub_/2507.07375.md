# Bradley-Terry and Multi-Objective Reward Modeling Are Complementary

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed SMORM, a unified framework combining Bradley-Terry and multi-objective reward modeling with a shared embedding space. This approach effectively mitigates reward hacking in out-of-distribution scenarios and enhances the scoring performance of multi-objective reward models, leveraging limited fine-grained data.

## Problem

- Reward hacking is a critical vulnerability in RLHF, particularly in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) settings where existing mitigation methods struggle.
- Multi-objective reward models (MORMs) show promise for robustness but are limited by the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality human-annotated multi-attribute data, leading to weaker performance.
- Current approaches that combine single-objective and multi-objective reward models often involve separate inference passes, leading to computational inefficiency.

## Method

- Researchers propose **SMORM** (Joint Single and Multi-Objective Reward Model), which jointly trains a ==Bradley-Terry== single-objective head and a multi-objective regression head using a shared LLM backbone.
- The framework employs a combined loss function that optimizes both heads simultaneously, requiring only a single forward pass during inference for computational efficiency.
- SMORM allows the single-objective preference dataset and the multi-attribute preference dataset to be distinct, enabling flexible training without shared prompt-response pairs.

## Results

- SMORM consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in mitigating reward hacking across in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings for both PPO and Best-of-N scenarios.
- SMORM-F (single-objective head only) performs comparably to SMORM-M (averaged heads), demonstrating consistent improvements across all five fine-grained dimensions, supporting the theoretical finding of implicit multi-attribute effect.
- SMORM-L (multi-objective head only) substantially improves multi-objective scoring performance; for example, a **7B** SMORM-L model outperforms a **70B** baseline and matches a model trained on 15.9 times more multi-objective data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Theoretical analysis shows that SMORM's joint training provides a larger Fisher information matrix, leading to superior accuracy for both single- and multi-objective functions.
> - A high single-objective score from SMORM implicitly ensures a respectable level of fine-grained quality, making the single-objective head robust even without direct multi-attribute optimization.
> - The strong signal from abundant single-objective preference data can guide the multi-objective head, enabling competitive performance for multi-objective models even with limited multi-attribute data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities across various tasks, but their performance alone does not guarantee alignment with human values or safety. The field of LLM alignment primarily relies on two methods: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). RLHF is particularly effective as it learns a proxy reward model from human preference data, which then guides the LLM's optimization.

However, a critical vulnerability in RLHF is **reward hacking**, where the policy exploits imperfections in the reward function to maximize proxy scores without truly embodying the desired behavior. This leads to models generating repetitive, formulaic, or otherwise low-quality content that nevertheless scores highly. Prior research has attempted to mitigate reward hacking through several avenues:
- **Ensemble methods:** Combining multiple reward models to average out flaws. While effective, these are often resource-intensive, requiring training and inference of several models.
- **Constrained policy optimization:** Imposing constraints on the policy to prevent deviations from desired behavior. These methods often suffer from instability and hyperparameter sensitivity.
- **Regularization techniques:** Methods like GRM (incorporating text generation regularization) aim to prevent adversarial exploitation, but can introduce training instability due to conflicting objectives.
- **Disentangled reward models:** ODIN, for instance, trains separate reward functions for quality and length. However, this paper empirically shows that length alone is insufficient to prevent reward hacking effectively.

A crucial limitation across most existing studies is their predominant focus on **in-distribution (ID) scenarios**, where the training and testing data for the reward model share the same distribution. The effectiveness of these mitigation strategies in **out-of-distribution (OOD) settings**, where the policy might encounter prompts or generate responses from a different distribution than the reward model was trained on, remains largely unexplored. This gap is significant because real-world LLM deployment often involves varying and evolving user inputs, making OOD robustness paramount.

Recent advancements in **Multi-Objective Reward Models (MORMs)**, which leverage fine-grained attribute scores (e.g., helpfulness, correctness, verbosity), have shown promise for interpretability and steerability. The hypothesis is that by learning from multi-dimensional supervision, MORMs could capture nuanced distinctions in response quality, making reward hacking more difficult. However, MORMs face a major hurdle: the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality human-annotated multi-attribute data. While LLM-as-Judge approaches exist, they introduce their own biases and inconsistencies. Consequently, the scoring performance of MORMs often lags behind single-objective reward models (SORMs), which benefit from vast amounts of easier-to-collect binary preference data.

This paper addresses these critical gaps. It empirically demonstrates the failure of state-of-the-art reward hacking mitigation methods in challenging OOD settings. To overcome this, it proposes a novel unified framework that leverages the complementary strengths of both traditional Bradley-Terry (single-objective) and multi-objective (regression-based) reward models. By jointly training these components with a shared embedding space, the work aims to achieve both robust reward signals that generalize well in OOD scenarios and enhance the performance of multi-objective models despite limited fine-grained data, all while maintaining computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations driving this research are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Reward Hacking in OOD Settings:** The core motivation is to tackle the critical and largely overlooked problem of reward hacking in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) settings. The authors hypothesize that existing state-of-the-art methods, primarily evaluated in in-distribution scenarios, will fail when the policy operates on data distributions different from the reward model's training data. This highlights a significant limitation for real-world deployment where prompts and generated content can naturally drift from the training distribution.
- **Leveraging Fine-Grained Multi-Attribute Scores for Robustness:** The paper is motivated by the observation that multi-objective reward models (MORMs), which evaluate responses across several dimensions (e.g., helpfulness, correctness, coherence, verbosity), have the potential to mitigate reward hacking. By forcing the policy to optimize for multiple attributes simultaneously, it becomes harder for the policy to find "shortcuts" that maximize a single, potentially flawed, reward signal.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity for Multi-Objective Reward Models:** Despite their potential, MORMs are constrained by the limited availability of large-scale, high-quality human-annotated multi-attribute data. Human annotation is expensive and difficult to scale, while LLM-as-Judge annotations can introduce biases and inconsistencies. This data scarcity often leads to weaker performance in MORMs, making them a bottleneck if simply aggregated with single-objective models. The paper seeks to improve MORM performance without requiring additional, hard-to-obtain fine-grained data.
- **Developing an Efficient and Unified Framework:** Current approaches that combine single-objective and multi-objective reward models often involve separate inference passes, leading to computational inefficiency. The authors aim to propose a framework that is both efficient (requiring a single forward pass) and effective.
- **Establishing Theoretical Complementarity:** A key objective is to theoretically establish the connection and complementary benefits between the widely used Bradley-Terry (BT) loss for single-objective preference learning and the regression objective used for multi-objective reward modeling. This theoretical grounding provides insights into how jointly training these distinct objectives can lead to mutual enhancement. Specifically, they hypothesize that the regression task can refine the embedding space to improve the single-objective function's robustness in OOD settings, while BT-based training can enhance the scoring capability of the multi-objective function, especially with limited data.

In essence, the paper seeks to develop a robust, efficient, and theoretically sound reward modeling framework that addresses the persistent problem of reward hacking, particularly in challenging OOD environments, by intelligently integrating the strengths of single- and multi-objective reward signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Joint Single and Multi-Objective Reward Model (SMORM)**, is designed to efficiently mitigate reward hacking in OOD settings by leveraging fine-grained attribute scores without relying on additional costly multi-attribute preference data.

**Core Idea and Architecture:**
SMORM jointly trains two types of reward functions—a Bradley-Terry (BT) single-objective function and a multi-objective regression-based function—using a **shared embedding space**. This shared backbone is crucial for computational efficiency, requiring only a single forward pass during inference.

1. **Feature Extractor:** A pre-trained decoder-only Large Language Model (LLM) is used as the backbone (denoted `f_theta`), acting as a feature extractor. Given a prompt `x` and a response `y`, their concatenation `x ⊕ y` is passed through the decoder layers, and the hidden state of the final layer is extracted as a `d`-dimensional feature vector.
2. **Dual Heads:** Two distinct linear heads are attached on top of the shared feature extractor `f_theta`:
 - **Single-Objective Head (`w_S`):** This linear layer `w_S ∈ R^(d×1)` outputs a scalar reward score, representing the overall preference.
 - **Multi-Objective Head (`w_M`):** This linear layer `w_M ∈ R^(d×k)` produces a `k`-dimensional vector of attribute scores (e.g., helpfulness, correctness, coherence, complexity, verbosity), where `k` is the number of fine-grained attributes.

**Loss Function:**
SMORM is trained with a combined loss function that simultaneously optimizes both heads:
`min_(θ, w_S, w_M) -E_D_S [log σ(w_S^T ((f_θ(x_s, y_c) - f_θ(x_s, y_r))))] + E_D_M [||w_M^T f_θ(x_m, y_m) - r||^2_2]`
- The first term is the **Bradley-Terry loss** for the single-objective head, trained on `D_S` (chosen/rejected preference data). This encourages `w_S^T f_θ(x, y_c)` to be higher than `w_S^T f_θ(x, y_r)`.
- The second term is the **Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss** for the multi-objective head, trained on `D_M` (multi-attribute preference data). This encourages `w_M^T f_θ(x_m, y_m)` to predict the ground-truth attribute scores `r`.

**Inference Strategies:**
SMORM supports multiple ways to combine scores during inference:
- **SMORM-F:** Uses only the score from the first (single-objective) head.
- **SMORM-L:** Computes the mean of the scores from the second (multi-objective) head.
- **SMORM-M:** Averages the scores from both the first and second heads.

**Theoretical Grounding:**
The paper provides theoretical insights into why this joint training is beneficial:

- **Lemma 1 (Pairwise Preference Error to MSE Loss):** This lemma establishes a connection between the Bradley-Terry loss and the regression (MSE) objective. It shows that by minimizing the mean squared error of predicted scores, the expected pairwise preference error is bounded. This justifies the use of MSE for multi-objective training while relating it to the preference-based BT loss.
- **Theorem 1 (Implicit Multi-Attribute Effect):** This theorem states that when SMORM is trained to convergence, the average score from the multi-objective head (`r_m`) is lower-bounded by a positive linear function of the single-objective score (`r_s`). This means that a high single-objective score implicitly guarantees a respectable level of fine-grained quality, even if the multi-attribute head is not directly used for policy optimization. This explains why SMORM-F (using only the single-objective head) can perform comparably to SMORM-M. It also implies that the strong signal from the single-objective task can guide the multi-objective head, enabling competitive performance even with limited multi-attribute data.
- **Theorem 2 (Superior Scoring Performance):** This theorem proves that SMORM's joint training yields a larger Fisher information matrix compared to training either head alone. A larger Fisher information matrix implies a smaller asymptotic covariance for the estimated parameters, leading to lower asymptotic Mean Squared Error (MSE) for both the single-objective and multi-objective heads. This theoretically justifies SMORM's improved accuracy in predicting response quality.

**Experimental Setup:**
The authors conducted extensive experiments using:
- **Datasets:** `Skywork80K` and `Unified-Feedback` for single-objective preferences (`D_S`). `HelpSteer2` (human-annotated, 20K samples) and `UltraFeedback` (GPT-annotated, 240K samples) for multi-attribute preferences (`D_M`). A key aspect is that `D_S` and `D_M` do not need to share prompt-response pairs.
- **Base Models:** `gemma-2B-it` and `Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2` as backbone models for reward learning and policy models for RLHF.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Reward hacking mitigation is assessed via PPO and Best-of-N (BoN) sampling, tracking proxy vs. gold scores across increasing KL divergence or training steps. Scoring performance is evaluated on `RewardBench` and `RM-Bench` benchmarks.
- **Baselines:** Standard single-objective reward models (Baseline Classifier, Margin, Label Smooth, Ensemble), and reward hacking mitigation methods like `ODIN` and `GRM`. A `Baseline SM` (training SORM and MORM separately and aggregating) is also used to highlight SMORM's efficiency gains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental results validate SMORM's effectiveness in mitigating reward hacking and enhancing the scoring performance of reward models.

- **Failure of State-of-the-Art in OOD Settings:**
 - Experiments in both PPO and BoN settings under OOD conditions (Figure 2) clearly show that existing state-of-the-art methods like GRM and ODIN struggle. GRM shows initial gold score increases but then declines (BoN) or exhibits unstable behavior, while ODIN (relying on length as a bias) generally fails to prevent reward hacking effectively. This confirms a critical limitation of prior work.
 - The paper's interpretation (Appendix I) reveals that baseline single-objective models fail in OOD settings because they over-optimize for superficial attributes (e.g., complexity, verbosity) while neglecting genuine quality dimensions like helpfulness and correctness (Figure 7a).

- **Benefits of Incorporating Multi-Attribute Scores:**
 - The "Baseline SM" (training SORM and MORM separately and aggregating) generally outperforms GRM and ODIN in mitigating reward hacking (Figure 2), indicating that fine-grained multi-attribute scores do indeed contribute to robustness.
 - However, the Baseline SM also exposes a key weakness: the weaker performance of the MORM component can become a bottleneck, degrading the overall aggregated output quality (Figure 2d), thus motivating SMORM's joint training.

- **SMORM's Superiority in Mitigating Reward Hacking:**
 - Both SMORM-F (single-objective head only) and SMORM-M (averaged heads) consistently and significantly outperform all baselines in mitigating reward hacking across PPO and BoN experiments, in both in-distribution (ID) (Figures 3 & 4) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings (Figures 2 & 5).
 - Notably, SMORM-F performs comparably to SMORM-M. This empirical finding strongly supports **Theorem 1 (Implicit Multi-Attribute Effect)**, which theoretically establishes that a high single-objective score from SMORM implicitly ensures a respectable level of fine-grained quality, even when only the single-objective head is used for inference.
 - Further analysis (Appendix I) shows that SMORM-F, unlike the baseline, leads to consistent improvements across all five fine-grained dimensions (Helpfulness, Correctness, Coherence, Complexity, Verbosity), directly translating to higher gold scores (Figure 7b).

- **SMORM's Superior Scoring Performance:**
 - **Enhancing Single-Objective Reward Models:** Table 1 and Table 6 show that SMORM-F consistently achieves the highest average performance on the RewardBench benchmark compared to other single-objective reward models, across varying data scales and base models. This empirically validates **Theorem 2**, demonstrating that the joint training framework leads to superior overall performance for the single-objective function.
 - **Boosting Multi-Objective Reward Models:** Table 2 demonstrates that SMORM-L (using only the multi-objective head) significantly improves the scoring performance of its multi-objective component compared to a baseline MORM. For example, with Mistral-7B-Instruct and 40K samples, SMORM-L achieved 13.9 points higher on RewardBench and 12.4 points higher on RM-Bench. This confirms that the BT-based training successfully guides the multi-objective head to perform competitively, even with limited multi-attribute data, supporting **Theorem 1**'s assertion about the single-objective score guiding fine-grained quality.
 - **Competitive with Advanced, Larger Models:** SMORM-L (7B model trained on 20K multi-objective samples) outperforms a 70B baseline model (Table 3). Even more impressively, the SMORM-L 8B model matches the performance of `ArmoRM-Llama3-8B-v0.1`, despite ArmoRM being trained on 15.9 times more multi-objective data. This highlights SMORM's remarkable data efficiency and ability to leverage smaller, higher-quality datasets effectively.

- **Flexible Training and Stability:**
 - SMORM's design allows `D_S` and `D_M` datasets to be distinct, enabling flexible training (Table 4).
 - Hyperparameter analysis (Figure 6, Appendix H) shows that SMORM maintains consistent and stable performance across a wide range of weight ratios, unlike GRM, which exhibits significant performance fluctuations, underscoring the instability of next-token prediction regularization.

- **RM-Bench Specifics (Appendix J):**
 - The paper explains why existing MORMs underperform on "Easy" and "Normal" tasks in RM-Bench: their style biases (e.g., verbosity, complexity) can outweigh utility signals, especially when utility differences are subtle (Table 12).
 - SMORM's joint training corrects this by reducing the variance and impact of style-related biases, allowing utility attributes to dominate the decision-making, thus improving performance on these subtle tasks (Table 13).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers significant contributions to the field of LLM alignment and has several impactful implications:

- **Addressing a Critical, Overlooked Problem:** This work is among the first to systematically investigate and propose a robust solution for reward hacking in **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) settings**. Real-world LLM deployments inherently encounter OOD scenarios due to evolving user queries and generative outputs. By demonstrating that state-of-the-art methods fail in OOD and offering an effective mitigation, the paper provides a crucial step towards deploying more reliable and safe LLMs.

- **Novel and Efficient Unified Framework (SMORM):** The proposed SMORM framework is both theoretically grounded and practically efficient. By jointly training single-objective (Bradley-Terry) and multi-objective (regression-based) reward functions with a **shared embedding space and a single forward pass**, SMORM overcomes the computational overhead of prior ensemble or aggregation methods. This efficiency makes it more feasible for real-world application at scale.

- **Formalizing Complementary Benefits:** The paper theoretically establishes a novel connection between Bradley-Terry loss and regression objectives. **Theorem 1** shows that a strong single-objective score implicitly guarantees fine-grained quality, while **Theorem 2** demonstrates that joint training reduces prediction error for both heads. This dual benefit—robustness for single-objective models and improved performance for multi-objective models—highlights a powerful synergy previously underexplored.

- **Data Efficiency for Multi-Objective Reward Models:** One of the most impactful findings is SMORM's ability to significantly enhance the performance of multi-objective reward models **without requiring additional, costly human-annotated multi-attribute data**. By leveraging abundant binary preference data to guide the multi-objective head, SMORM enables smaller models (e.g., a 7B model) to outperform significantly larger baselines (e.g., a 70B model) and achieve performance comparable to models trained on orders of magnitude more fine-grained data. This drastically lowers the barrier to entry for developing high-quality, interpretable multi-objective reward models, democratizing their use.

- **Enhanced LLM Alignment and Safety:** By producing more robust and accurate reward signals, SMORM directly contributes to better LLM alignment. Policies trained with SMORM are less likely to "hack" the reward function and more likely to genuinely learn human-desired behaviors across multiple dimensions (helpfulness, correctness, coherence), leading to higher-quality, safer, and more reliable LLM outputs.

- **Improved Interpretability and Steerability:** While not the primary focus, the enhanced performance of the multi-objective head implicitly contributes to better interpretability and steerability of LLMs. By understanding how models score on various attributes, developers can gain finer control over the generated content and diagnose issues more effectively.

In summary, this research advances the state-of-the-art in reward modeling by providing a theoretically sound, empirically validated, and computationally efficient solution to crucial challenges in LLM alignment, particularly in generalizing to unseen data distributions and efficiently leveraging diverse human feedback signals. Its impact will likely be felt in the development of more robust, trustworthy, and performant LLMs for a wide range of applications.
