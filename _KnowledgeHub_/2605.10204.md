# 3DReflecNet: A Large-Scale Dataset for 3D Reconstruction of Reflective, Transparent, and Low-Texture Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> A large-scale hybrid dataset, 3DReflecNet, is introduced to benchmark and advance 3D reconstruction for reflective, transparent, and low-texture objects, combining over 12,000 synthetic instances and 1,000 real-world captures with comprehensive ground truth and textual annotations.

## Problem

- Existing 3D reconstruction algorithms fundamentally struggle with non-Lambertian materials, such as reflective, transparent, and low-texture objects, due to their reliance on photometric consistency and distinctive appearance features.
- Current benchmark datasets primarily focus on diffuse, textured objects, leading to a significant gap in data for developing and evaluating 3D vision models robust to real-world material complexities.
- Systematic failure modes arise from the violation of algorithmic assumptions by view-dependent appearances (reflections), light refraction (transparency), and insufficient high-frequency features (low-texture surfaces).

## Method

- The **3DReflecNet** dataset is a hybrid collection of over 12,000 ==synthetic instances== and 1,000 real-world captured objects, totaling over 22 TB and 7 million multi-view frames.
- Synthetic data is generated using physically-based rendering in Blender with 22 material types across diverse shapes and over 2,700 HDRI lighting conditions, providing ground truth for depth, normals, meshes, and camera parameters.
- Real-world data is captured using a specialized protocol with objects placed on detailed bases for robust camera pose estimation, followed by segmentation to isolate challenging objects; it includes rich textual annotations for generative 3D vision tasks.

## Results

- Image matching methods like ROMA showed significantly lower performance on 3DReflecNet (e.g., **32.1** AUC@5°) compared to standard datasets, indicating difficulties in establishing robust correspondences.
- Novel view synthesis methods achieved high PSNR (>**36** dB) on diffuse materials but severely struggled on transparent materials (**17**-21 dB PSNR) and metallic surfaces, with consistent degradation in surface reconstruction quality.
- Reflection removal and relighting benchmarks revealed modest performance from state-of-the-art methods (e.g., DSIT **24.07** PSNR for reflection removal, TensoIR **23.39** PSNR for relighting), confirming the challenges posed by the dataset's material complexities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction methods, including image matching, SfM, and novel view synthesis, exhibit substantial performance degradation on non-Lambertian materials, particularly transparent and highly reflective surfaces.
> - The observed performance drop highlights that current algorithms' implicit assumptions about light transport are insufficient for accurately processing complex optical phenomena.
> - The dataset underscores the critical need for developing material-aware and physically-informed 3D vision models that can explicitly account for reflection, refraction, and texture sparsity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-view 3D reconstruction is a foundational problem in computer vision, with applications in robotics, augmented/virtual reality, autonomous driving, and digital content creation. Recent advancements, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting, have significantly improved reconstruction quality and rendering efficiency, particularly for textured, Lambertian surfaces. However, a persistent challenge in this field is the accurate 3D reconstruction of objects featuring complex optical phenomena, specifically specular reflection, transparency, and low texture.

Existing state-of-the-art methods in Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) pipelines often rely on two core assumptions: photometric consistency across views and the presence of distinctive appearance features. These assumptions are typically violated by non-Lambertian materials. Reflective surfaces exhibit view-dependent appearance (Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function - BRDF), transparent objects introduce light transmission and refraction, and low-texture surfaces lack sufficient high-frequency features for robust correspondence matching. These conditions lead to appearance inconsistencies, undermining the reliability of pose estimation and dense geometry reconstruction.

Current benchmark datasets, such as DTU, CO3D, and MVImgNet, primarily consist of diffuse, textured objects, or offer data under relatively controlled conditions. While some datasets, like MV Reflectance, NeRO, and OpenMaterial, have explored material-aware 3D reconstruction, they often present limitations, such as being purely synthetic, lacking real-world data, providing limited object diversity, or supporting only a narrow range of tasks. This gap in available data has restricted the development and comprehensive evaluation of 3D vision models robust to real-world material complexities.

3DReflecNet addresses this deficiency by introducing a large-scale, hybrid dataset specifically curated to benchmark and advance 3D vision methods for reflective, transparent, and low-texture materials. It provides a comprehensive platform that combines high-fidelity synthetic data with real-world captures, bridging a crucial gap in the existing research landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce 3DReflecNet, a large-scale hybrid dataset engineered to facilitate the development and evaluation of 3D vision methods that can robustly handle reflective, transparent, and low-texture objects. The motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations of current 3D reconstruction algorithms and the deficiencies in existing benchmark datasets.

Detailed motivations include:

1. **Addressing Fundamental Algorithmic Limitations:** Existing 3D reconstruction algorithms fundamentally struggle with non-Lambertian materials because they implicitly assume photometric consistency and distinctive features across views.
 - **Reflective materials:** Violate photometric consistency due to view-dependent appearance governed by their BRDF, leading to misinterpretation of specular highlights as geometric features.
 - **Low-texture surfaces:** Lack the high-frequency features necessary for robust correspondence matching, resulting in ambiguous and often inaccurate reconstructions.
 - **Transparent materials:** Represent the most severe challenge, as light refraction invalidates both photometric consistency and the geometric assumption of linear light propagation inherent in epipolar geometry, often leading to complete reconstruction failure.
 The authors conducted an extensive experiment analyzing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) performance across various material parameters (metallic, roughness, IOR, transmission). This revealed systematic failure modes:
 - **Roughness Impact:** Reflective materials with smooth surfaces (roughness=0.0) experienced a significant degradation in reconstruction quality (e.g., metallic materials yielding approximately 19 dB PSNR compared to 35 dB for high-roughness non-metallic surfaces). Conversely, increasing roughness from 0.0 to 0.9 improved PSNR by 5 dB in non-metallic materials, demonstrating the reliance of correspondence-based methods on texture cues.
 - **Transparency Impact:** Transparent materials consistently caused a substantial 5.82 dB PSNR drop across all configurations, indicating a 19.3% quality loss. Higher refractive indices (IOR) progressively worsened performance, ranging from 19.9 dB (IOR=1.0) to 27.9 dB (IOR=1.9), confirming that stronger refraction severely compromises epipolar geometry.
 These observations underscore a fundamental mismatch between algorithmic assumptions and physical light transport, indicating an urgent need for reconstruction techniques that are inherently aware of material-light interactions.

2. **Bridging the Gap in Dataset Coverage:** Current datasets are predominantly focused on diffuse, textured objects and do not adequately represent the complexities introduced by reflective, transparent, or low-texture surfaces under diverse real-world lighting. This limits the training and evaluation of models capable of generalizing to realistic scenarios.

3. **Establishing Comprehensive Benchmarks:** The research aims to establish standardized benchmarks for five core tasks:
 - **Image Matching:** To evaluate the ability to find robust correspondences under photometric inconsistencies.
 - **Structure-from-Motion (SfM):** To assess camera pose estimation accuracy for non-Lambertian and low-texture surfaces.
 - **Novel View Synthesis (NVS):** To benchmark rendering quality under complex material conditions.
 - **Reflection Removal:** To evaluate methods for separating reflections from scene content.
 - **Relighting:** To test the decomposition of intrinsic material properties for rendering under novel illumination.
 By providing such benchmarks, the authors aim to identify systematic failure modes in existing state-of-the-art methods and catalyze the development of more resilient and physically aware 3D vision models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 3DReflecNet dataset is constructed as a large-scale, hybrid collection encompassing both synthetic and real-world data. It totals over 22 TB and comprises more than 7 million multi-view frames.

**Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline:**
The synthetic component of 3DReflecNet consists of over 120,000 instances derived from more than 12,000 unique shapes. The generation process leverages physically-based rendering (PBR) in Blender to achieve photorealism and control over material, lighting, and geometric properties.

- **Shapes:** A collection of over 10,000 high-quality shapes was gathered from existing object repositories and 3D asset databases spanning art, industry, and nature. Additionally, over 2,000 shapes representing everyday items were generated using a novel 2D-to-3D pipeline to enhance diversity.
- **Materials:** The dataset incorporates 22 distinct material types, categorized into five groups: Diffuse (e.g., concrete), Transparent (e.g., glass), Metallic (e.g., steel), Glossy-Textured (e.g., polished wood), and Glossy-Low-Texture (e.g., ceramic glazes). These materials are specifically chosen for their challenging properties in 3D reconstruction.
- **Lighting:** Over 2,700 high-dynamic-range image (HDRI) environment maps are utilized to simulate diverse lighting conditions, including indoor, outdoor, various times of day, and weather scenarios. To further enhance realism, local illumination is simulated by placing one to two finite-distance point lights in the upper hemisphere for indoor scenes.
- **Specular Reflection Simulation:** To capture complex specular reflection scenarios, a glass sheet is positioned between the object and the camera. This setup provides data from 60 different camera angles under hundreds of lighting conditions, comprehensively simulating the optical effects of reflections.
- **Rendering and Output:** For each synthetic instance, comprehensive ground truth annotations are provided. These include 60 multi-view RGB images (1000x1000 resolution), corresponding depth maps (EXR format), surface normal maps (EXR format), object segmentation masks, 3D geometry in point cloud and mesh formats, camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, and PBR scene files (.blend).

**Synthesis Data Generation with 2D Reference:**
To further enrich shape diversity, an automated pipeline generates 3D assets from 2D images. This process uses both real-world and Large Language Model (LLM)-generated 2D images as references. Diffusion-based methods (e.g., Hunyuan3D-2.1 or Stable3DGen) are employed to estimate normals and depth, reconstruct a mesh, and refine it to a canonical pose. These generated 3D models are then rendered in Blender with diverse PBR materials and HDR lighting, integrating them into the synthetic dataset.

**Real-World Capture:**
The real-world component comprises over 1,000 objects captured using consumer devices, specifically an iPhone 16 Pro, recording 1080x1920 video at 30 FPS. This reflects common acquisition scenarios.
- **Capture Protocol:** To overcome the challenge of standard camera pose estimation failing on reflective or low-texture objects, a specialized capture protocol was designed. Each target object is placed on a highly detailed base, which acts as a stable tracking marker. This entire assembly is then positioned on a rotating platform to ensure a smooth, stable 360-degree capture path.
- **Data Processing:**
 1. Robust camera poses are initially estimated from the video by tracking the detailed base of the object using RealityScan.
 2. Subsequently, SAM 2 is employed to segment and remove both the base and the background, isolating the challenging object.
- **Output:** The real-world data includes over 60 multi-view RGB images (1920x1080 or 3840x2160 resolution), corresponding mask images, point cloud files, and camera internal/external parameters.

**Annotations for Generative 3D Vision Tasks:**
Beyond perception tasks, 3DReflecNet provides detailed textual annotations for each instance, supporting generative 3D vision research. These annotations are generated using the Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct vision model and include:
- Detailed material descriptions (glossiness, roughness, reflectivity, texture scale, anisotropy, metallic hint).
- Lighting condition tags (indoor/outdoor, light type, intensity, key light direction, shadow hardness).
- Semantic object-level descriptions.
These structured annotations facilitate tasks such as text-to-3D generation, text-to-texture synthesis, and image editing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study established benchmarks for five core tasks: image matching, Structure-from-Motion (SfM), novel view synthesis (NVS), reflection removal, and relighting, using 3DReflecNet. Data was split into 80% for training, 10% for validation, and 10% for testing.

**Image Matching:**
Evaluation on a subset of 1,000 Roman statue instances using metrics like AUC@5°, AUC@10°, and AUC@20° revealed that state-of-the-art methods (e.g., ROMA, ELoFTR) exhibited significantly lower performance on 3DReflecNet compared to standard datasets like MegaDepth. For instance, ROMA achieved 32.1 AUC@5° on 3DReflecNet versus 62.6 on MegaDepth. This indicates that existing methods struggle to establish accurate correspondences on reflective, transparent, or low-texture surfaces due to inconsistent appearance, lack of distinctive texture, and view-dependent distortions. Similar modest scores were observed on real-world captures, with the top-performing method reaching 59.5 AUC@20°.

**Structure from Motion (SfM):**
The benchmark assessed the ability of methods to reconstruct sparse 3D point clouds and estimate camera parameters. Qualitative results demonstrated that most existing SfM approaches struggled to recover accurate camera parameters for objects with challenging materials, even when background features were removed to focus solely on object-intrinsic cues. Inaccuracies in pose estimation were shown to lead to geometric inconsistencies and visible artifacts in reconstructed models.

**Novel View Synthesis (NVS) and Surface Reconstruction:**
Six representative methods were evaluated across five material categories (Diffuse, Transparent, Metallic, Glossy-Textured, Glossy-Low-Texture).

- **Novel View Synthesis (PSNR):**
 - **Diffuse materials:** Methods performed well, achieving PSNR scores above 36 dB, which is expected given their reliance on photometric consistency.
 - **Metallic and Glossy-Low-Texture materials:** Performance degraded due to strong specular reflections violating view-consistency assumptions.
 - **Transparent materials:** All methods struggled severely, yielding PSNR scores between approximately 17-21 dB. Complex light transmission, refraction, and caustics were identified as fundamental challenges breaking color consistency.
 - **Glossy-Textured materials:** Achieved relatively higher PSNR (around 34 dB) due to the presence of diffuse texture patterns, although artifacts were still present in specular regions.
- **Surface Reconstruction (Chamfer Distance, Hausdorff Distance):**
 - Methods performed well on diffuse materials, where geometric features could be reliably triangulated.
 - However, reconstruction quality degraded significantly for materials with non-Lambertian appearances. For transparent and metallic materials, metrics like Hausdorff distance indicated catastrophic failures to reconstruct large parts of the geometry.
 These trends were consistent between synthetic and real-world captures, highlighting fundamental limitations of current NVS and surface reconstruction methods when dealing with complex light interactions.

**Reflection Removal:**
Four state-of-the-art reflection removal baselines were evaluated on 1,000 images from 3DReflecNet. The results, measured by PSNR and SSIM, showed modest scores (e.g., DSIT achieved 24.07 PSNR and 0.795 SSIM). These scores were comparable to those reported on other challenging real-world datasets, confirming that 3DReflecNet serves as a physically realistic and challenging benchmark for this task.

**Relighting:**
Four SOTA relighting methods were evaluated on 3DReflecNet. While TensoIR achieved the best performance with a PSNR of 23.39, all methods exhibited modest scores (below 24 dB PSNR). This indicated that the complex, non-Lambertian materials in 3DReflecNet pose a significant challenge for current relighting techniques that aim to disentangle intrinsic material properties from lighting.

**Material Parameter Impact (Detailed Analysis):**
Further analysis revealed that for opaque, non-metallic materials, roughness was the dominant factor influencing reconstruction quality; low roughness led to catastrophic failure due to a lack of features. The Index of Refraction (IOR) had minimal impact in this specific subset. Conversely, metallic materials severely degraded pixel-wise metrics (PSNR, SSIM), while transparency consistently degraded all metrics, particularly perceptual ones like LPIPS.

Overall, the extensive benchmarking consistently demonstrated that state-of-the-art methods, while proficient on diffuse objects, experience substantial performance degradation when confronted with the complex optical phenomena present in reflective, transparent, and low-texture materials.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of 3DReflecNet carries significant implications for the field of 3D computer vision, addressing critical gaps and charting new directions for research.

**Addressing a Critical Gap:** 3DReflecNet is the first large-scale, hybrid dataset specifically designed to address the challenges of 3D reconstruction for reflective, transparent, and low-texture objects. By combining over 12,000 physically-based synthetic objects with more than 1,000 real-world scans, it provides an unprecedented scale and diversity in materials, shapes, and lighting conditions that were previously unavailable. This directly fills a long-standing void in benchmark datasets, which predominantly focused on diffuse, textured surfaces.

**Revealing Systematic Failures in State-of-the-Art Methods:** The extensive benchmarking conducted across tasks such as image matching, SfM, novel view synthesis, reflection removal, and relighting consistently revealed that current state-of-the-art 3D vision models struggle substantially with non-Lambertian materials. The performance degradation on 3DReflecNet instances, particularly for transparent and highly reflective surfaces, highlights that these models rely on oversimplified assumptions about light transport that do not hold in real-world scenarios. This systematic exposure of limitations is crucial for identifying the fundamental weaknesses of existing approaches.

**Driving Future Research in Robust 3D Understanding:** By unambiguously demonstrating the failure modes of current algorithms, 3DReflecNet provides a clear impetus for the development of more generalizable and physically-aware 3D vision systems. The dataset's rich annotations (including depth, normals, masks, and camera parameters) offer invaluable ground truth for researchers to:
- Develop new feature descriptors resilient to photometric inconsistencies.
- Design SfM and MVS algorithms that explicitly model complex light interactions like reflection and refraction.
- Create novel view synthesis techniques that can faithfully render non-Lambertian materials without artifacts.
- Advance inverse rendering methods for accurate material and lighting decomposition.

**Enabling Advancements in Generative 3D Vision:** Beyond perception tasks, the detailed textual annotations (material properties, lighting conditions, semantic descriptions) for each instance open new avenues for generative 3D vision. This enables research in areas such as:
- **Text-to-3D generation:** Conditioning models to synthesize 3D assets with specific material properties and lighting responses.
- **Text-to-texture synthesis:** Generating realistic textures that account for roughness, metallic properties, and other material attributes.
- **Image-to-3D reconstruction:** Leveraging lighting and material cues to infer 3D geometry from 2D images more accurately.
- **Image editing and manipulation:** Performing material-consistent inpainting or lighting-aware object insertion in complex scenes.

**Broadening Real-World Applications:** The improved capability to reconstruct challenging materials has direct implications for a wide range of applications where such objects are prevalent. These include enhancing the realism and performance in robotics (grasping reflective objects), augmented and virtual reality (realistic rendering of transparent or glossy virtual objects), autonomous driving (better perception of vehicle windows, metallic surfaces), and digital content creation (more accurate 3D scanning and asset creation for film and gaming).

By releasing 3DReflecNet along with baseline implementations and evaluation tools, the authors aim to foster a collaborative research environment dedicated to building 3D vision methods that are robust and truly generalizable to the complexities of the physical world.
