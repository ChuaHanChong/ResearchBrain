# Persistent Visual Memory: Sustaining Perception for Deep Generation in LVLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed Persistent Visual Memory (PVM), a module designed to counteract visual signal dilution in autoregressive Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) during deep generation tasks. Integrating PVM led to an absolute improvement of 4.8% in average accuracy on eight multimodal benchmarks, reaching 71.5% with a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, and showed a 27.3% relative performance boost for long output sequences.

## Problem

- In autoregressive LVLMs, visual information, injected at the beginning, suffers from "Visual Signal Dilution" as textual context grows, leading to attenuated influence.
- Existing visual injection methods often interfere with the model's evolving semantic states, disrupting linguistic coherence and logical reasoning.
- LVLMs are prone to visual hallucinations and reduced fidelity in deep generation scenarios that require sustained visual grounding.

## Method

- Developed ==Persistent Visual Memory== (**PVM**), a lightweight, learnable module integrated as a parallel branch into the Transformer decoder block, structurally isolated from the main reasoning path.
- **PVM** employs a ==cross-attention== mechanism with independent attention normalization, confining attention exclusively to raw visual features to prevent dilution by textual history.
- Utilizes a learnable ==gated fusion== mechanism with a selective activation mask to integrate retrieved visual memory into the main stream only for textual tokens, preventing self-referencing by visual tokens.

## Results

- PVM-enhanced Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct models achieved an average accuracy of **71.5%** across eight multimodal benchmarks, an absolute improvement of **4.8%** over the baseline (**66.7%**).
- PVM demonstrated increased efficacy with sequence length, yielding a **27.3%** relative performance boost for "Long" sequences on MathVerseV, indicating stabilization of visual perception during deep generation.
- PVM introduces minimal computational overhead, with a 1.18 milliseconds increase in Time Per Output Token (**4.6%** throughput reduction) and approximately **0.32%** parameter overhead for the 8B model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual signal dilution in standard autoregressive LVLMs exhibits a power-law decay (O(t⁻¹)) of visual attention mass as textual history length (t) increases.
> - Structural isolation of visual retrieval, coupled with independent attention normalization within the visual domain, is crucial to mitigate dilution without perturbing the main reasoning backbone.
> - PVM structurally ensures that retrieved visual representations are locally invariant to the growing textual-history length, providing sustained, on-demand visual perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has advanced significantly, establishing a dominant paradigm in multimodal artificial intelligence. Models such as the Qwen3-VL series have demonstrated proficiency across a range of tasks, from fine-grained visual perception to complex reasoning. These models typically integrate vision encoders with Large Language Models (LLMs) through semantic projection, allowing visual tokens to be processed alongside textual sequences.

However, a challenge in this paradigm, particularly in "deep generation" scenarios involving extended dialogues or multi-step reasoning, is the "Visual Signal Dilution" phenomenon. In autoregressive LVLMs, visual tokens are often treated as fixed precursors, unlike textual tokens that self-replenish during generation. As the textual history accumulates, the attention mechanism's normalization process redistributes probability mass across an increasing number of tokens, leading to the attenuation of the initial visual signals. This can cause the model to enter a "Low-Attention Equilibrium" where visual cues are outweighed by textual priors, potentially resulting in visual hallucinations or a detachment from visual facts in longer generations.

Prior research has attempted to address this visual persistence issue through various strategies, broadly categorized as "Visual Injection" methods. These include inserting raw tokens, fusing processed features, hierarchical systems, retrieval-augmented frameworks, visual token compression, and attention optimization. While some of these approaches provide short-term recall, they often introduce their own limitations. Direct injection into the serial autoregressive pathway can disrupt linguistic coherence and perturb the evolving semantic states necessary for precise logical deduction. Other methods may necessitate complex external modules, structurally alter the backbone, or sacrifice fine-grained visual fidelity.

This work introduces Persistent Visual Memory (PVM) as an alternative approach. PVM aims to provide sustained, on-demand visual perception within the autoregressive flow without disrupting linguistic coherence or requiring extensive architectural modifications. It positions itself as a lightweight, parallel mechanism that directly addresses the structural conflict within the attention mechanism, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and faithful LVLMs for extended multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized architectural bottleneck in current autoregressive LVLMs, termed "Visual Signal Dilution." The paper highlights that while these models excel at multimodal tasks, their ability to maintain consistent visual fidelity degrades during deep generation, where responses are lengthy or involve multi-step reasoning. This degradation occurs because visual tokens, typically introduced at the beginning of a sequence, become progressively attenuated by the growing textual context in the Transformer's self-attention mechanism. As the generated text expands, the attention partition function distributes probability mass over more tokens, diluting the influence of the fixed visual signals. This leads to issues such as visual hallucinations and a decreased reliance on visual evidence, hindering the model's performance in tasks requiring persistent visual grounding.

To counteract this, the authors identify a need for mechanisms that can enable sustained, on-demand access to visual evidence, allowing the model to dynamically re-examine visual memory rather than passively retaining initial signals. Existing "Visual Injection" methods, which attempt to re-introduce visual information, are noted to often disrupt the model's linguistic coherence and reasoning logic by interfering with the autoregressive flow.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Formal Analysis and Empirical Validation of Visual Signal Dilution:** To mathematically characterize the asymptotic decay of visual attention and empirically demonstrate this phenomenon, thereby establishing the structural necessity for a sustained perception paradigm.
2. **Development of Persistent Visual Memory (PVM):** To propose and design a lightweight, learnable module that can provide sustained and distance-agnostic visual retrieval. This module is intended to structurally mitigate visual signal suppression caused by accumulating textual history.
3. **Architectural Integration for Interference-Free Operation:** To integrate PVM as a parallel branch alongside the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) in Transformer blocks. This design aims to create an independent retrieval pathway that preserves visual intensity without disrupting the autoregressive reasoning process of the main language model.
4. **Extensive Experimental Validation:** To demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of PVM across a comprehensive suite of multimodal benchmarks, including general understanding and complex scientific reasoning tasks. This involves showing consistent accuracy gains with minimal parameter overhead and across different model scales (e.g., 4B and 8B variants).
5. **Mechanistic Insight into PVM's Operation:** To conduct in-depth analysis (e.g., using LogitLens) to understand how PVM influences the model's internal predictive dynamics, particularly its impact on prediction convergence and robustness in longer generations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research encompasses a theoretical analysis of the problem, empirical validation, architectural design, and a rigorous experimental evaluation.

**Theoretical Analysis of Visual Signal Dilution:**
The authors formally analyze the attention mechanism in autoregressive LVLMs. They decompose the Softmax denominator (partition function) into aggregate unnormalized mass of visual context (Z_V) and textual history (Z_T). The Visual Attention Mass, Ω_V(t), is defined as the aggregate weight allocated to the visual set.
- **Phase I: Power-Law Dilution:** The paper posits that in early-to-mid generation stages, the growing textual history (t) actively competes for attention mass. A theorem is presented, stating that Ω_V(t) is structurally dominated by the growing textual history, decaying as O(t⁻¹). This suggests that the visual signal weakens inversely with the sequence length.
- **Phase II: High-Magnitude Saturation Trap:** In later stages, Z_T eventually saturates, but typically at a plateau orders of magnitude larger than Z_V. This leads to a "Low-Attention Equilibrium" where visual signals remain statistically insignificant, regardless of further textual growth.

**Empirical Verification:**
To validate the theoretical analysis, a "Blind Painter" Stress Test was designed using Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. The model was prompted to reconstruct an image textually in extreme detail, necessitating continuous visual retrieval.
- **Spatiotemporal Decay:** Log-scale analysis confirmed the O(t⁻¹) power-law decay of Visual Attention Mass (Ω_V), particularly in intermediate layers (8–27), which are crucial for multimodal reasoning.
- **Textual Dominance Dynamics:** Analysis of the Text-to-Visual Ratio (TVR) revealed a two-phase dynamic: initial linear growth of textual dominance, followed by saturation at a plateau where textual mass overwhelms visual mass by nearly two orders of magnitude. These empirical findings led to two key insights: the need for a parallel memory pathway isolated from the main reasoning backbone, and the requirement for independent attention normalization confined to the visual domain.

**Persistent Visual Memory (PVM) Architecture:**
Guided by these insights, PVM is designed as a lightweight, learnable module integrated parallel to the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) within the Transformer decoder block.
- **Parallel Bifurcation:** The hidden state (x) from the Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) layer is split into two paths:
 - **Reasoning Path:** Through the original, often frozen, FFN for static knowledge and logical patterns.
 - **Looking Path (PVM):** x acts as the Query for the PVM module to retrieve visual details.
- **PVM Computation:**
 1. **Projection:** Input hidden state (x) and original visual embeddings (V_img) are projected into a lower-dimensional latent space (d' < d) using learnable reducers (W_txt_down, W_vis_down).
 2. **Latent Retrieval:** Cross-Attention is performed, where the projected hidden state (x_lat) queries the projected visual features (V_lat) which serve as both Keys and Values. This step is crucial as attention is restricted *solely* to the visual set (V), ensuring independent attention normalization. A lightweight FFN further refines this latent feature.
 3. **Restoration:** The refined latent feature is projected back to the original high-dimensional space via an up-projection matrix (W_up), yielding h_pvm.
- **Gated Fusion with Selective Activation:** The PVM output (h_pvm) is integrated into the main stream via a residual connection controlled by a learnable scalar gate (λ). A Visual Silencing Mask (M_txt) is applied to activate PVM *only* for text tokens, preserving the integrity of visual representations during their initial processing. The final output is `y = x + h_ffn + (λ · h_pvm) · M_txt`.
- **Structural Analysis of PVM:** A theoretical analysis demonstrates that PVM's attention partition function is decoupled from the textual history length (t), meaning its retrieval representation h_pvm is locally invariant to t (∂∥h_pvm∥/∂t = 0). This confirms the structural isolation and independent attention normalization.

**Training Details:**
- **Backbone:** Qwen3-VL-Instruct (4B and 8B variants).
- **PVM Integration:** Modules inserted into intermediate Transformer layers (e.g., {8, 16, 24} for 8B). Latent dimension d' set to 512.
- **Two-Stage Training:**
 1. **Visual Memory Alignment (SFT):** Backbone frozen; PVM modules and gating scalars optimized to establish semantic mapping. Dataset: 526k samples from OpenMMReasoner-SFT.
 2. **Policy Refinement (GRPO):** LLM backbone and PVM modules unfrozen (Vision Encoder frozen); optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for complex reasoning. Dataset: 3.6k complex reasoning queries.

**Baselines and Evaluation:**
- **Baselines:** Included original Qwen3-VL, SFT/LoRA/RL-enhanced variants, visual injection methods (MemVR, ICoT, CoMemo re-implemented on Qwen3-VL), and recent RL reasoners (Euclid-8B, PEARL-8B, OneThinker-8B).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Eight diverse multimodal benchmarks were used: MMMU, MMBench-CN, MMBench-EN, MMStar, MMT, MathVerse, MathVision, and AI2D. Accuracy was reported as a 4-run average.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the "Visual Signal Dilution" phenomenon and the efficacy of Persistent Visual Memory (PVM).

**Validation of Visual Signal Dilution:**
- **Power-Law Decay:** Empirical analysis confirmed that the Visual Attention Mass (Ω_V) across Transformer layers in standard LVLMs exhibits a decay trajectory closely aligning with an O(t⁻¹) power-law, where t is the sequence length. This decay was particularly pronounced in the intermediate layers (Layers 8–27), which are critical for multimodal reasoning.
- **Textual Dominance:** The Text-to-Visual Ratio (TVR) consistently showed two phases: an initial linear growth, where textual history actively competes for attention, followed by a saturation plateau where textual mass significantly outweighs visual mass (by nearly two orders of magnitude). This illustrates that the visual signal becomes statistically insignificant in the shared attention partition function.

**Overall Performance of PVM:**
- **Superior Accuracy:** On the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, PVM-8B (SFT + GRPO) achieved an average accuracy of 71.5% across eight benchmarks, representing a 4.8% improvement over the baseline Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. This performance surpassed not only internal baselines (SFT, LoRA-SFT, and GRPO-enhanced variants) but also specialized visual injection methods (MemVR, ICoT, CoMemo) and leading open-source RL-tuned models (Euclid-8B, PEARL-8B, OneThinker-8B).
- **Scalability:** PVM demonstrated consistent improvements across model scales, delivering a 4.4% average accuracy gain on the 4B architecture, reaching an overall score of 68.4%.

**Robustness to Extended Generation:**
- **Length-Dependent Gains:** Evaluation on MathVerse_V showed a positive correlation between output token length and PVM's relative performance gain. While PVM provided a 6.1% improvement on "Very Short" sequences, this gain surged to 27.3% on "Long" sequences. This effect remained consistent even when controlling for problem difficulty, indicating that PVM's benefits are particularly relevant for deep generation where visual dilution is most severe.

**Mechanistic Analysis (LogitLens):**
- **Accelerated Prediction Convergence:** Using LogitLens to track prediction readiness, PVM-enhanced models exhibited a steeper descent in Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between intermediate layer representations and the final output distribution. A significant "Improvement Gap" emerged and widened across deeper layers after the initial visual injection point. This suggests that PVM helps accelerate the model's transition from perception to reasoning, speeding up internal prediction convergence by actively offloading visual retrieval to its parallel branch.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Necessity of Raw Visual Retrieval:** Replacing the raw visual embeddings in PVM with processed hidden states from the backbone resulted in a severe performance collapse (e.g., average accuracy dropped from 63.0% to 33.4%). This indicated that the gains from PVM derive from its ability to access and retrieve high-fidelity, *raw* visual evidence, rather than relying on potentially diluted processed states.
- **Injection Layer Selection:** Different strategies for placing PVM modules were tested. The "Strided Strategy" (Layers 8, 16, 24 for 8B model), which distributes PVMs across the network's depth, achieved the highest average score (70.6%) compared to "Peak Attention" (68.4%) and "Max Decay" (69.3%) strategies. This suggests that global coverage across diverse processing stages is more beneficial than targeting specific attention peaks or decay points.
- **Latent Dimension Sensitivity:** The default bottleneck latent dimension of 512 (d') was found to be optimal. Increasing d' to 1024 or 2048 led to slight performance regressions, attributed to potential data-capacity mismatch with the available fine-tuning data.
- **Iso-Parameter Control:** An MLP baseline with an identical parameter count to PVM, but lacking the visual cross-attention mechanism, consistently underperformed PVM by 2.5% on average. This confirmed that PVM's benefits are attributable to its active visual retrieval capabilities rather than merely an increase in model capacity.

**Computational Overhead:**
- The integration of PVM introduced a marginal increase in inference latency. The Time Per Output Token (TPOT) increased by 1.18 ms, leading to a 4.6% reduction in decoding throughput. This indicates that PVM provides substantial performance gains with a negligible impact on real-time inference speed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental architectural limitation in current autoregressive Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs): the "Visual Signal Dilution" phenomenon. By formally analyzing and empirically validating how visual attention degrades over extended generation, the paper provides a mechanistic understanding of why LVLMs struggle with sustained visual grounding and often exhibit hallucinations in long-context scenarios.

The proposed Persistent Visual Memory (PVM) module offers a structural solution to this problem. Its design, which integrates a parallel, dedicated pathway for visual retrieval with independent attention normalization, is significant for several reasons:

- **Enhanced Visual Fidelity:** PVM enables LVLMs to maintain access to high-fidelity visual evidence throughout extended generation. This directly mitigates the risk of visual hallucinations, where generated content diverges from visual facts, thereby increasing the reliability and trustworthiness of multimodal AI outputs.
- **Improved Long-Context Reasoning:** The demonstrated scaling of performance gains with sequence length indicates that PVM is particularly beneficial for complex reasoning tasks that require persistent visual perception over many steps. This is crucial for applications such as multi-turn dialogues, scientific problem-solving with diagrams, and in-depth visual analysis.
- **Lightweight and Efficient Integration:** PVM is a lightweight, learnable module with negligible parameter overhead (approximately 0.32% of an 8B model). Its integration as a parallel branch to the FFN allows it to operate without disrupting the existing autoregressive flow or requiring extensive structural changes to the backbone LLM, making it a practical and adaptable solution for existing LVLMs.
- **Accelerated Internal Processing:** The mechanistic analysis showing accelerated prediction convergence suggests that PVM not only improves external performance but also optimizes the internal dynamics of the model. By offloading visual retrieval to a dedicated branch, the main reasoning backbone can focus more efficiently on linguistic processing.
- **Foundation for Robust Multimodal Intelligence:** This work contributes to the development of more robust and reliable multimodal intelligence. By ensuring that LVLMs can consistently ground their responses in visual input, PVM paves the way for advanced applications requiring high degrees of accuracy and faithfulness to visual data.
- **Future Research Directions:** The findings highlight the importance of "sustained, on-demand perception" over passive retention. This paradigm shift could inspire further research into dynamic memory mechanisms, particularly for processing continuous or streaming visual inputs like long-form video, extending beyond static image contexts.

In summary, PVM represents a notable advancement in addressing a core challenge of LVLMs, enabling them to move towards more consistent, accurate, and visually grounded reasoning capabilities in complex and extended interaction scenarios.
