# Beyond Egocentric Limits: Multi-View Depth-Based Learning for Robust Quadrupedal Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel visual locomotion framework enhances quadrupedal robot agility and robustness by integrating both onboard and remote depth observations. Policies trained with stochastic remote-camera dropouts and positional perturbations demonstrated superior performance and adaptive fallback behavior when exocentric data was compromised.

## Problem

- Current legged robot locomotion systems are heavily reliant on egocentric (first-person) perception, limiting performance in complex, discontinuous terrains when the immediate viewpoint is occluded or insufficient.
- Most vision-based deep reinforcement learning policies for legged robots are restricted to single egocentric views, leading to performance degradation in visually ambiguous or occluded scenarios.
- While multi-view perception exists for mapping or planning, its direct integration into learned locomotion policies for dynamic control has been largely unexplored.

## Method

- A two-phase deep ==reinforcement learning== framework was developed for quadrupedal robots, fusing proprioception with both egocentric (onboard) and exocentric (remote) depth observations.
- A teacher policy with privileged information trains a student policy to imitate optimal actions using only realistic sensor inputs, including dual depth streams.
- Extensive domain randomization, such as stochastic remote-camera dropouts and 3D positional perturbations, was incorporated during training to enhance robustness against real-world sensing imperfections and communication unreliability.

## Results

- The combined vision model trained with random remote-camera dropouts achieved the best overall performance, maintaining a **63.86%** Traversal Success Rate (TSR) and **0.74** Mean X-Displacement (MXD) when the remote view was disconnected, closely matching egocentric baselines and significantly outperforming them when the remote view was active.
- Qualitative analysis showed the combined vision policy executing stable gap crossings by accurately estimating distance, a task where egocentric baselines failed.
- Policies trained with moderate remote camera displacement randomization (R_t_s = **0.3** m) maintained a TSR above **80%** and high MXD across various tested misalignment conditions (up to **0.5** m), demonstrating robustness to imprecise external camera positioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing egocentric and exocentric depth information significantly enhances a robot's environmental awareness, enabling more anticipatory and stable motion strategies in complex terrains.
> - Training policies with simulated remote-camera dropouts and positional errors is crucial for developing resilience against intermittent sensor data and imprecise external camera alignment in real-world deployments.
> - Learned policies can dynamically adapt their reliance on sensory inputs, demonstrating effective fallback to egocentric vision and proprioception when exocentric data becomes unavailable.
