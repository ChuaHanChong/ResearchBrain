# RynnVLA-002: A Unified Vision-Language-Action and World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from DAMO Academy, Alibaba Group, developed RynnVLA-002, a unified vision-language-action (VLA) and world model that integrates environmental dynamics learning with action planning. It achieved high success rates on simulation and real-world robot tasks, often outperforming extensively pretrained baselines without requiring large-scale pretraining itself.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models lack internal understanding of action dynamics, imagination (predicting future states), and explicit knowledge of physical interactions.
- Traditional world models, despite their predictive power, cannot directly generate actions, creating a functional gap for direct robot control.
- Autoregressive discrete action generation in VLA models suffered from error accumulation and poor generalization on limited real-world robotic data, leading to slow and non-smooth inference.

## Method

- **RynnVLA-002** unifies VLA and world model capabilities into a single ==autoregressive framework==, initialized from a Large Language Model (==Chameleon==), learning environmental dynamics and action planning jointly.
- It employs a novel ==attention masking strategy== for discrete action generation, ensuring each action relies only on visual and textual inputs to mitigate error propagation.
- A compact continuous ==Action Transformer head== generates entire ==action chunks== in parallel using ==learnable queries==, providing smoother trajectories, faster inference, and improved generalization.

## Results

- Achieved **97.4%** success rate with continuous actions on the LIBERO benchmark, matching or exceeding strong baselines without relying on large-scale pretraining.
- Demonstrated competitive real-world performance on LeRobot SO100 tasks, with superior robustness in cluttered multi-target environments (e.g., over **80%** success in 'Place the block' task).
- World model pretraining improved grasping performance, and ablation studies confirmed that the integrated world model significantly improved overall real-world task success rates by **50%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA and world models exhibit mutual enhancement: VLA's visual understanding improves world model's generation, while world model's dynamics understanding refines VLA's action generation.
> - Integrating world model training data is crucial for robust real-world robotic tasks, boosting success rates by 50% in ablation studies.
> - The continuous Action Transformer design significantly accelerates VLA training convergence, improves final success rates, and offers more efficient and smoother action execution compared to discrete autoregressive methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI has seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and world models as distinct paradigms for robotic control and environmental understanding. VLA models, often built upon pre-trained Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), translate language instructions and visual observations into robot actions. These models have demonstrated capabilities in perception and decision-making, leading to enhanced generalization in various robotic tasks. However, VLA architectures typically treat actions as outputs, limiting their internal representation of action dynamics, imagination, and explicit understanding of physics.

Conversely, world models focus on learning environmental dynamics by predicting future observations based on current images and actions. This provides agents with action-aware internal states, foresight, and physics-informed representations. Despite these advantages, traditional world models lack the ability to directly generate action outputs, which restricts their applicability in explicit action planning scenarios.

This work integrates these two paradigms into a unified framework, termed an "action world model." RynnVLA-002 aims to address the limitations of standalone VLA models by incorporating a world model's predictive capabilities and, simultaneously, enhance the world model's visual generation through the VLA component's refined visual understanding. This approach positions RynnVLA-002 within a growing trend of developing more comprehensive and mutually beneficial architectures for embodied AI, moving beyond separate or loosely coupled VLA and world model systems. It builds upon previous works that explored VLM-based VLAs, visual generation-based VLAs, and world-model-assisted VLAs, but distinguishes itself by internalizing both objectives within a single autoregressive backbone with a shared token space, rather than treating them as separate components or using world models solely for external supervision or pretraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and world model, named RynnVLA-002, that enables joint learning of environmental dynamics and action planning. The motivation stems from addressing three fundamental drawbacks observed in standard VLA architectures and a key constraint in world models:

1. **Limited Action Understanding in VLA Models:** Standard VLA models often treat actions solely as outputs, preventing the model from forming explicit internal representations of action dynamics. This limits the model's ability to fully comprehend and reason about the nature and consequences of actions.
2. **Lack of Imagination in VLA Models:** Current VLA models typically do not predict how the world might evolve given different candidate actions. This absence of foresight hinders counterfactual reasoning and proactive decision-making, as the model cannot anticipate future states resulting from its actions.
3. **Absence of Explicit Physics Understanding in VLA Models:** Without capturing physical dynamics, VLA models struggle to internalize object interactions, contact forces, or stability. This limits their ability to operate effectively in complex physical environments where such understanding is crucial for robust manipulation.
4. **Functional Gap in World Models:** While world models excel at forecasting future observations and learning environmental dynamics, they are inherently designed for prediction rather than direct action generation. This functional gap restricts their direct application in scenarios requiring explicit action planning and control.

RynnVLA-002 is motivated by the hypothesis that a unified framework can mutually enhance these two capabilities. The world model component is designed to learn environmental physics by generating visual representations from actions, which in turn refines the action generation process for the VLA. Conversely, the VLA component, by producing subsequent actions from visual observations, improves visual understanding and the precision of the world model's image generation. The aim is to create a single model that can both understand and generate actions and images, fostering a more robust and comprehensive understanding of embodied tasks. Furthermore, the paper aims to address practical challenges in robot control, such as error propagation in autoregressive action generation and limited generalization in real-world scenarios, by introducing specific architectural and training modifications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RynnVLA-002 proposes an autoregressive action world model that unifies action and image understanding and generation within a single framework. The architecture is initialized from Chameleon, a unified model for image understanding and generation.

**Overall Architecture and Unification:**
- RynnVLA-002 employs separate tokenizers for images, text, states, and actions. These tokens share a single vocabulary, enabling unified understanding and generation across modalities within a single Large Language Model (LLM) architecture.
- The model functions dually: as a VLA model, it generates actions based on language goals, proprioceptive states, and observation history; as a world model, it predicts future observations from past observations and actions.
- This functional unification means action prediction and action-conditioned visual prediction share the same modeling space, allowing for mutual enhancement.

**Data Tokenization:**
- **Image Tokenizer:** A VQ-GAN model with perceptual losses, inherited from Chameleon. It compresses images (e.g., 256x256 or 512x512) into a fixed number of tokens (256 or 1024) from a codebook of 8192.
- **Text Tokenizer:** A trained BPE tokenizer, also inherited from Chameleon.
- **State and Action Tokenizer:** Discretizes each dimension of continuous robot proprioceptive states and actions into 256 bins based on training data ranges.
- All token types (image, text, action, state) are mapped to a single token vocabulary of size 65536. Raw continuous actions generated by the Action Transformer bypass tokenization.

**Training Objective:**
- **VLA Model Data:** The input sequence is `{text} {state} {image-front-wrist}` followed by `{action}`. The model generates *K* actions conditioned on the language instruction, state, and *M* historical image observations. The text input format is a question, e.g., "What action should the robot take to <task>?". Loss is `L_dis_action` (cross-entropy for discrete action tokens).
- **World Model Data:** The input sequence is `{text} {images-front-wrist} {action}` followed by `{images-front-wrist}` for the next frame. The model predicts the next image frame given the current image and action. The text prefix is "Generate the next frame based on the current image and the action." This can repeat *N* times autoregressively. Loss is `L_img` (cross-entropy for discrete image tokens).
- **Joint Training:** RynnVLA-002 is trained by mixing VLA and world model data, typically with a 1:1 ratio, in a single finetuning stage without extensive large-scale robot manipulation pretraining. The overall loss for discrete actions is `L_dis = L_dis_action + L_img`.

**Inference Modes:**
- The model supports both action prediction (as a VLA) and future-image prediction (as a world model) but performs only one queried task per inference call.
- When acting as a VLA policy, it generates actions (discrete tokens or continuous via Action Transformer) without rolling out future images for control.
- When acting as a world model, it predicts future visual observations. This decoupling prevents compounding errors from autoregressive image prediction during real-time action generation.

**Specific Architectural Innovations:**
1. **Action Attention Mask for Discrete Action Chunk Generation:** To mitigate error propagation when autoregressively generating multiple discrete actions, a modified attention mask is introduced. This mask ensures that during action generation, current actions attend only to textual and visual input, explicitly preventing them from attending to prior actions within the same chunk. This aims to generate actions more independently, grounded in visual perception rather than relying on potentially faulty preceding action predictions.
2. **Continuous Action Transformer Head:** For improved generalization, smoother trajectories, and faster inference in real-world scenarios, a dedicated, smaller continuous Action Transformer head is added. This head processes the full context (language, image, state tokens) and uses learnable action queries to output an entire chunk of continuous actions in parallel. This compact design is less prone to overfitting on limited real-world data and accelerates inference compared to sequential autoregressive generation. The overall loss function becomes `L = L_dis_action + L_img + αL_conti_action`, where `L_conti_action` is an L1 regression loss for continuous actions and `α` is a weighting parameter (set to 10).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (LIBERO Benchmark)

RynnVLA-002 was evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark, comprising Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long tasks, using a dataset curated by removing unsuccessful trajectories and "no-operation" actions.

- **Discrete Actions:** RynnVLA-002-Discrete achieved a 93.3% average success rate. This performance was competitive with, and in many cases superior to, other non-pretrained discrete action methods.
- **Continuous Actions:** RynnVLA-002-Continuous achieved a 97.4% average success rate, surpassing its discrete counterpart. This performance was competitive with strong pretrained baselines like π0.5 (96.9%) and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and X-VLA (98.1%) despite RynnVLA-002 not using robot-data pretraining.

#### 4.2. Real-World Robot Results (LeRobot SO100)

Evaluation was conducted on a new LeRobot SO100 dataset for two pick-and-place tasks: "Place the block inside the circle" and "Place strawberries into the cup." Scenarios included single-target, multi-target, and instruction-following with distractors.

- RynnVLA-002 achieved competitive results against pretrained baselines (GR00T N1.5, π0, π0.5) without robot pretraining.
- For "Place the block inside the circle," RynnVLA-002 achieved 90% (single-target), 90% (multi-target), and 80% (with distractors) success rates.
- For "Place the strawberries into the cup," RynnVLA-002 achieved 80% (single-target), 80% (multi-target), and 50% (with distractors) success rates.
- The model demonstrated strong performance, improving over the strongest pretrained baseline by 20% in the multi-target "Place the block" task and matching its distractor robustness.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **World Model Benefits the VLA Model:**
 - In simulation (LIBERO discrete actions), incorporating world model data increased success rates from 62.8% to 67.2% (without action chunking/masking) and from 76.6% to 78.1% (with action chunking/masking).
 - For continuous actions in simulation, success rates increased from 91.6% to 94.6%.
 - In real-world robot experiments, the benefit was more pronounced: models without world model data had success rates below 30%, while augmenting with world model data boosted performance to over 80%.
 - Qualitative observation (Fig. 5) indicated that the jointly trained model exhibited improved target-oriented focus and recovery from failures, consistent with the world-modeling objective providing stronger supervision on object interaction dynamics.

- **VLA Model Enhances the World Model:**
 - The "Action World Model" (trained with VLA and world model data) achieved video generation results comparable to or better than a world model trained solely on world data across FVD, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics for all LIBERO task types (Table 6). For instance, on the Object task, FVD decreased from 1141.6 to 877.2, and LPIPS from 27.30 to 22.60.
 - Visualization (Fig. 8) showed the Action World Model consistently generated videos depicting successful grasps, unlike the baseline world model which often predicted failures or inconsistencies between camera views. This suggested VLA's image understanding capabilities strengthened the world model's generation performance.

- **Attention Mask for Discrete Action Chunk Generation:**
 - Naive autoregressive generation of discrete action chunks (without the attention mask) led to performance degradation, with success rates decreasing for longer chunk lengths (Fig. 6).
 - The proposed attention mask, which prevents actions from attending to prior actions, demonstrated superior performance, especially with longer chunk lengths, by mitigating error propagation.

- **Discrete Actions Accelerate Convergence:**
 - Training with discrete action tokens in conjunction with the continuous Action Transformer resulted in substantially faster convergence and a higher ultimate success rate for the VLA model (Fig. 7).

- **Ablation Study of Wrist Camera and Proprioceptive State:**
 - In simulation, the model achieved reasonable performance without these inputs, but incorporating them brought further gains (91.4% to 97.4% for continuous actions in Table 4).
 - In real-world experiments, the robot consistently failed without the wrist camera or proprioceptive state, indicating their critical importance for precise gripper-object pose feedback and accurate timing of manipulation actions.

- **Efficiency Analysis:**
 - Action chunking for discrete actions improved both inference speed and performance.
 - Continuous action generation was significantly faster due to its parallel nature, with frequency scaling almost linearly with chunk size (Table 7).

- **Discrete Action and Continuous Action Comparison:**
 - Continuous actions showed significantly faster convergence in simulation (Fig. 9).
 - In real-world experiments, continuous actions provided a substantial performance improvement over discrete actions (Table 5).

- **World Model Pretraining for VLA Model:**
 - Pretraining a VLA model with world model data (even from the same data source) led to notable improvements in grasping performance (Table 8), suggesting the benefit of prior exposure to general world knowledge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RynnVLA-002 introduces a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and world model framework, providing a methodology for the synergistic interplay between these two embodied AI paradigms. This work contributes to the development of robot learning systems that can both understand complex instructions and anticipate environmental dynamics.

The significance of RynnVLA-002 lies in several aspects:
- **Mutual Enhancement:** The research demonstrates that integrating VLA and world models within a single architecture leads to mutual enhancement. The world model improves the VLA's action planning by providing an internal representation of physics and imagination, while the VLA's visual understanding refines the world model's image generation capabilities. This suggests that a holistic approach to embodied intelligence, combining perception, action, and prediction, can yield more robust agents.
- **Addressing VLA Limitations:** By incorporating a world model, RynnVLA-002 directly addresses the limitations of traditional VLA models regarding action understanding, imagination, and explicit knowledge of physics, leading to more capable and adaptable robotic control.
- **Improved Action Generation:** The introduction of an action attention masking strategy for discrete actions mitigates error propagation, leading to more reliable action chunk generation. The addition of a continuous Action Transformer head further enhances generalization, provides smoother trajectories, and accelerates inference, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
- **Strong Performance Without Extensive Pretraining:** RynnVLA-002 achieves competitive performance on the LIBERO simulation benchmark and real-world robot tasks without relying on large-scale robot-data pretraining, a resource often unavailable. This suggests that the unified action-world objective provides a potent learning signal from more limited datasets, making the approach potentially more accessible.
- **Foundation for Multi-Modal Understanding:** By unifying text, vision, and action within a shared token space and autoregressive backbone (inspired by Chameleon), RynnVLA-002 contributes to laying groundwork for a more comprehensive foundation model for multi-modal understanding and generation in embodied AI.

The potential impact of RynnVLA-002 includes advancing the state of the art in robot manipulation by enabling robots to perform complex tasks with greater accuracy, robustness, and foresight. Its ability to learn effectively from more limited datasets, particularly in real-world scenarios, could democratize access to advanced robot learning techniques. Future work could focus on scaling the model to broader robot platforms, tackling long-horizon mobile manipulation and deformable-object tasks, and exploring more efficient image-token generation to reduce computational costs.
