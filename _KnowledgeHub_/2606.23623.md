# dVLA-RL: Reinforcement Learning over Denoising Trajectories for Discrete Diffusion Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The dVLA-RL framework enables reinforcement learning for discrete diffusion Vision-Language-Action (dVLA) models by formulating a tractable policy likelihood over multi-step denoising trajectories. This approach improved average success rates to 99.7% on LIBERO and 92.0% on RoboTwin 2.0, with an adaptive denoising strategy balancing performance and computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Discrete Diffusion VLAs (dVLAs) trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) are susceptible to distributional shift and compounding errors, limiting their potential task success.
- Standard policy-gradient RL algorithms require a tractable policy likelihood for the final action, which is combinatorially intractable for dVLAs' multi-step, iterative denoising process.
- Existing RL methods for discrete diffusion models in other domains are unsuitable for dVLAs due to distinct action token semantics and shorter denoising chains, posing challenges for direct application.

## Method

- The policy optimization objective is reformulated by modeling the multi-step action generation as a ==Markovian denoising trajectory== and leveraging its joint probability for tractable policy likelihood estimation.
- Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is adapted to use this trajectory-level log-probability, with gradients restricted to newly denoised tokens to implicitly optimize the Gumbel-TopK unmasking process without stability issues.
- A Hybrid denoising-step strategy is introduced, dynamically assigning 1, 2, or 4 denoising steps per task based on complexity, to balance action quality with training and inference efficiency.

## Results

- dVLA-RL boosted average success rates on LIBERO from **88.1%** (SFT-only) to **99.7%**, and on RoboTwin **2.0** from **61.4%** to **92.0%**, demonstrating substantial performance gains.
- The trajectory-level probability modeling consistently led to more stable and higher success rates throughout training compared to a 'last-step only' proxy, especially with increased denoising steps.
- The Hybrid dVLA-RL strategy achieved the highest peak success rate (**0.920**) on RoboTwin **2.0**, while reducing training time and inference FLOPs compared to consistently using high denoising steps, indicating superior efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling the joint probability of the full denoising trajectory is critical for stable and effective policy-gradient reinforcement learning in discrete diffusion VLAs, outperforming last-step only approximations.
> - Implicitly optimizing the scheduler's unmasking decisions by focusing gradients on token generation allows for robust PPO training for dVLAs, even with non-differentiable selection mechanisms.
> - An adaptive, hybrid approach to denoising steps enhances both sample efficiency during RL training and computational efficiency during inference by tailoring the action refinement level to specific task demands.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI increasingly focuses on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to enable generalist robotic manipulation by grounding physical control in the semantic reasoning capabilities of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models typically frame robotic control as a sequence generation task. Current VLA architectures broadly fall into categories based on their action modeling approach:
- **Continuous Action Models:** These often utilize diffusion or flow processes to represent complex, multimodal action distributions. Reinforcement Learning (RL) in these continuous spaces typically relies on likelihood approximations, variational objectives, or auxiliary stochastic formulations for policy gradient evaluation.
- **Discrete Action Models:** These generate actions either autoregressively (token-wise predictions) or through parallel decoding (single-step predictions). Standard policy-gradient algorithms can directly use their exact analytical action likelihoods.

Recently, **Discrete Diffusion VLAs (dVLAs)** have emerged as a distinct alternative. This paradigm unifies vision, language, and action into a single discrete token space through masked generative modeling. dVLAs combine iterative action refinement with unified representations and have demonstrated performance comparable to continuous counterparts when trained with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on demonstration data.

The broader research landscape for dVLAs, however, has primarily centered on supervised training. The application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for further policy refinement in native dVLAs has remained largely unexplored. This gap is significant because SFT methods are susceptible to distributional shift and compounding errors during real-world execution, limiting the upper bound of task success. RL, by optimizing directly for environmental rewards, offers a principled solution to these limitations, enabling policies to adapt and achieve higher success rates.

This work positions itself to address this specific gap by introducing an RL framework for dVLAs. It seeks to enable policy-gradient RL for discrete diffusion models, an area where existing methods for language and image diffusion models either use endpoint-conditioned surrogates or reweight timestep- and token-level gradients, approaches that are argued to be less suitable for the short denoising chains and distinct semantic nature of action tokens in dVLAs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable and facilitate Reinforcement Learning (RL) for native Discrete Diffusion Vision-Language-Action (dVLA) policies, thereby refining their performance beyond initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and addressing limitations associated with purely imitative learning.

The core motivation stems from a fundamental challenge in applying standard policy-gradient RL algorithms, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), to dVLAs:
- **Intractability of Marginal Action Probability:** dVLAs generate actions through an iterative, K-step Markovian denoising process. The final executable action ($a_t$) is the outcome of this multi-step process. Standard policy-gradient algorithms require a tractable policy likelihood for the *final action*, $\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)$. However, calculating this exact marginal probability for dVLAs necessitates marginalizing over all possible intermediate paths within the iterative denoising process. This marginalization is combinatorially expensive and computationally intractable. Simply approximating the likelihood using only the last denoising step's output is deemed insufficient as it fails to account for the iterative decision-making and intermediate states that dictate the rollout.

To overcome this intractability, the research establishes several key objectives:
1. **Formulate a Tractable RL Objective:** Develop a mathematical formulation that allows for the computation of a tractable policy likelihood for dVLAs, one that accurately reflects the multi-step action generation process used during rollout and deployment. This objective aims to shift from the intractable marginal action probability to a tractable representation that can be optimized with standard RL algorithms.
2. **Enable Policy-Gradient RL for dVLAs:** Integrate this tractable objective with established policy-gradient RL algorithms (specifically PPO) to allow for effective online policy refinement and maximization of environment rewards. This seeks to unlock the potential of RL for dVLAs, moving beyond the current SFT-only training paradigm.
3. **Unify Denoising Horizons:** Create a unified framework that can accommodate and optimize denoising processes of variable lengths (i.e., different numbers of denoising steps). This flexibility is motivated by the observation that different tasks may require different levels of iterative refinement for optimal performance and efficiency.
4. **Introduce an Adaptive Denoising-Step Strategy:** Leverage the unified formulation to propose a hybrid denoising-step scheduling approach. This strategy aims to dynamically assign denoising horizons based on task complexity and the initial policy's performance, thereby balancing action quality, sample efficiency, and computational cost during both training and inference.
5. **Empirically Validate Performance:** Demonstrate through extensive evaluation that the proposed RL framework consistently improves dVLA policies beyond their SFT initialization, achieving competitive or state-of-the-art performance on challenging robotic manipulation benchmarks, and validating the necessity of trajectory-level probability modeling.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the strong SFT performance of dVLAs and the potential for greater task success and robustness offered by RL, by addressing the unique challenges posed by their multi-step, discrete denoising action generation process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on reformulating the policy optimization objective for discrete diffusion VLAs (dVLAs) to enable standard policy-gradient Reinforcement Learning (RL). The approach involves modeling the multi-step denoising process as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and leveraging its intrinsic properties for tractable policy likelihood estimation.

#### 3.1. Modeling the Joint Probability of Markovian Denoising Trajectories

The core of the methodology is to address the intractability of the marginal action probability $\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)$ by shifting the optimization target to the joint probability of a *sampled denoising trajectory*, denoted as $\pi_\theta(\tau|s_t)$. A trajectory $\tau = (x_K, \dots, x_0)$ represents the sequence of action states from a fully masked state ($x_K$) to the fully denoised action ($x_0$).

- **Factorization of Trajectory Probability:** The iterative denoising process is treated as a parameterized Markov chain. The joint probability of a trajectory is factorized into a product of single-step transitions:
 $P_\theta(x_{k-1}|x_k, s_t) = P_{unmask}(U_k|p_\theta, k) \prod_{i \in U_k} p_\theta(x_i^{k-1}|x_k, s_t)$.
 Here, $x_k$ is the action sequence at denoising step $k$, $s_t$ is the current environment state, $U_k$ is the subset of tokens selected by the scheduler to be unmasked at step $k$, and $p_\theta(x_i^{k-1}|x_k, s_t)$ is the categorical probability distribution for newly generated tokens. $P_{unmask}(U_k|p_\theta, k)$ represents the probability of the scheduler's selection, which is driven by the network's output confidence $p_\theta$ via a Gumbel-TopK mechanism.
- **Trajectory-Level Log-Probability:** By taking the logarithm, the joint probability of the entire sampled path $\tau$ is expressed as a sum:
 $\log \pi_\theta(\tau|s_t) = \sum_{k=K}^1 \left[ \log P_{unmask}(U_k|p_\theta, k) + \sum_{i \in U_k} \log p_\theta(x_i^{k-1}|x_k, s_t) \right]$.
 This formulation provides a tractable expression for the likelihood of the executed denoising path.

#### 3.2. Implicit Optimization of the Unmasking Process

While the above formulation is mathematically complete, directly including the $\log P_{unmask}$ term in gradient calculations poses practical issues for PPO:
- **Gradient Stability:** The Gumbel-TopK scheduler involves discrete sorting operations, leading to non-differentiable step functions. Backpropagating through this term can generate high-variance gradient impulses that destabilize PPO training and violate its clip constraints.
- **Numerical Negligibility:** Under PPO's trust region constraint, the selection probabilities for already decoded tokens remain largely invariant, leading to numerically negligible gradient contributions from the $P_{unmask}$ term.

To mitigate these issues, the unmasking process (the scheduler's decision to select $U_k$) is treated as an *intrinsic, non-differentiable system dynamic*. The gradient computation is restricted exclusively to the newly denoised tokens.
- **Masked Objective:** The loss function is modified to focus gradients solely on tokens $M_k \subseteq U_k$ that transition from a `[MASK]` state to a specific token ID:
 $L_{dVLA-RL}(\theta) = E_\tau \left[ \sum_{k=K}^1 \sum_{i \in M_k} \log p_\theta(x_i^{k-1}|x_k, s_t) \cdot A_t \right]$.
 Here, $A_t$ is a chunk-level advantage estimate, uniformly weighting the unrolled generation steps.
- **Implicit Optimization:** The network's confidence $p_\theta$ is shared across both token generation and the Gumbel-TopK scheduler. As the policy gradient improves token prediction accuracy, the overall contextual representation is enhanced, implicitly improving the scheduler's ability to target uncertain regions without direct, high-variance gradients.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm and Backbone

- **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to optimize the policy. The PPO clipped surrogate objective is adapted to utilize the trajectory-level log-probability $log \pi_\theta(\tau|s_t)$ as defined above, effectively replacing the standard single-action log-probability.
- **Value Function:** A separate value head ($V_\phi(s_t)$) is trained to estimate state values, utilizing the first-step hidden state ($h^{(0)}$) of the action generation process. Advantage estimates ($A_t$) are computed using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
- **Reward Structure:** Sparse outcome rewards are used: 1 for successful task completion, 0 otherwise.
- **Backbone Model:** The framework is instantiated on MM-ACT (Liang et al., 2026), a pre-trained discrete diffusion VLA model. This backbone is initialized via multi-task Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using expert demonstrations (500 per suite for LIBERO, 1000 per task for RoboTwin 2.0).

#### 3.4. Unified Denoising-Step Strategy (Hybrid dVLA-RL)

The trajectory-level formulation naturally accommodates different denoising horizons (number of steps $K$). This flexibility is exploited by introducing a hybrid strategy:
- **Task-Specific Denoising:** Different tasks are assigned different denoising steps (1-step, 2-step, or 4-step) based on an assessment of their initial SFT performance and refinement needs. Easier tasks, where low-step decoding already yields high success, can use fewer steps to reduce computational cost. Harder tasks, requiring more iterative refinement, use more steps to provide better initial actions and richer online exploration signals under sparse rewards.
- **Efficiency and Quality Balance:** This adaptive approach aims to balance sample efficiency (faster learning with better initial policies) with training and inference efficiency (lower computational cost for easier tasks).

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations are conducted on the LIBERO (single-arm manipulation) and RoboTwin 2.0 (bimanual manipulation) simulation benchmarks.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Success rate (SR) is the primary metric, reported as the best online rollout success rate observed during RL training.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against the SFT-only MM-ACT backbone (MM-ACT\*), other VLA-based methods (e.g., OpenVLA, SimpleVLA-RL), and World Action Models (WAMs).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the LIBERO and RoboTwin 2.0 benchmarks yielded several findings concerning the effectiveness, necessity, and efficiency of dVLA-RL.

#### 4.1. dVLA-RL Improves Discrete Diffusion VLA Policies

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** dVLA-RL achieved a high average success rate of 99.7% across the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). This represented a substantial improvement over the SFT-only MM-ACT\* backbone, which achieved an 88.1% average success rate. Furthermore, dVLA-RL established competitive performance among VLA-based methods, surpassing benchmarks such as SimpleVLA-RL (99.1%) and also outperforming several World-Action Model (WAM) baselines.
- **RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark:** On the more challenging bimanual tasks of RoboTwin 2.0, dVLA-RL consistently improved performance. The average success rate increased from 61.4% (MM-ACT\*) to 92.0%, an absolute gain of 30.6 percentage points. Significant improvements were observed across all eight evaluated tasks, with particularly large gains on tasks like Handover Mic (+47.9 points), Move Can Pot (+47.5 points), and Lift Pot (+43.1 points). These results positioned dVLA-RL as a leading VLA-based method on RoboTwin 2.0, while also demonstrating competitive performance when compared to WAM-based reference results (acknowledging differences in training settings).

#### 4.2. Necessity of Trajectory-Level Probability Modeling

- A comparative analysis between the proposed trajectory-level log-probability objective and a "last-step only" proxy was conducted on LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Object under 2-step and 4-step denoising.
- The trajectory-level objective consistently demonstrated more stable and higher success rates throughout training. For instance, on LIBERO-Spatial with 2-step denoising, the last-step proxy showed initial improvement but became less stable, while the trajectory-level objective maintained higher and more stable performance. Under 4-step denoising, the trajectory-level objective continued to improve steadily, whereas the last-step proxy plateaued at a lower success rate. Similar degradation for the last-step proxy was observed on LIBERO-Object.
- These findings suggest that modeling the full denoising trajectory is critical for stable policy optimization, especially as the number of intermediate denoising steps increases.

#### 4.3. Effect of Denoising Steps on RL Optimization

- The study investigated how different denoising-trajectory lengths (1-step, 2-step, 4-step) influenced RL optimization on RoboTwin 2.0.
- Multi-step denoising variants (2-step and 4-step) generally started from higher initial SFT success rates and exhibited faster learning rates in the early and intermediate stages of training compared to 1-step parallel decoding. This indicates that more iterative refinement prior to RL provides a better initialization.
- The Hybrid dVLA-RL strategy, which adaptively assigns denoising steps per task, demonstrated the most favorable learning behavior. It maintained the highest success rate over most of the optimization process and reached high-success-rate regimes earlier than fixed-step alternatives. Hybrid dVLA-RL achieved the highest peak success rate (0.920), compared to 0.908 for fixed multi-step (2-step shown, 4-step similar trend) and 0.885 for 1-step decoding, suggesting superior sample efficiency.

#### 4.4. Hybrid Denoising Steps Improves Training and Inference Efficiency

- An efficiency comparison (Table 3) quantified the wall-clock training time, inference delay, and inference FLOPs for different denoising-step settings.
- Increasing denoising steps generally increased computational overhead proportionally: 4-step denoising, for example, required significantly more training time (845.95s) and inference FLOPs (70191.28 GFLOPs) than 1-step (259.33s, 17547.82 GFLOPs) or 2-step (452.06s, 35095.64 GFLOPs).
- Hybrid dVLA-RL achieved a balance by adaptively mixing 1-step, 2-step, and 4-step settings. Its training time (607.89s) and inference delay/FLOPs (387.24ms, 37289.12 GFLOPs) were lower than consistently using 4-step denoising, while still retaining the benefits of multi-step action refinement for tasks that required it. This suggests that the Hybrid strategy enhances both sample efficiency and computational efficiency, which are important for large-scale online rollouts and low-latency robotic deployments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents dVLA-RL, a framework that addresses a technical challenge in applying reinforcement learning to discrete diffusion Vision-Language-Action (dVLA) models. The research contributes to the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation through several aspects of significance and potential impact:

- **Enabling RL for Discrete Diffusion VLAs:** The primary significance lies in extending policy-gradient Reinforcement Learning to native discrete diffusion VLAs. Previously, dVLAs were predominantly trained via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). dVLA-RL provides a principled method to move beyond imitation learning, allowing dVLA policies to directly optimize for environmental rewards and overcome limitations such as distributional shift and compounding errors.
- **Addressing Intractability in Generative Models:** The proposed trajectory-level probability modeling offers a solution to the challenge of intractable marginal action probabilities in multi-step discrete generative processes. This mathematical formulation could have broader implications for other discrete diffusion models beyond robotics, where similar challenges in integrating with policy-gradient methods exist.
- **Improved Robotic Manipulation Performance:** The empirical results demonstrate that dVLA-RL consistently improves upon SFT-initialized dVLAs, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the LIBERO benchmark and robust, leading results among VLA-based methods on the complex RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark. This suggests that discrete diffusion models, when combined with effective RL optimization, can form a powerful foundation for high-performance generalist robotic policies.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency through Adaptive Strategies:** The introduction of the Hybrid denoising-step strategy is significant for practical applications. By dynamically adjusting the number of denoising steps based on task complexity, the framework balances action quality, sample efficiency, and computational cost. This adaptive approach makes large-scale online RL training and low-latency inference more feasible, which is crucial for real-world robotic deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for further exploration into RL-optimized discrete diffusion models in embodied AI. It opens avenues for research into more sophisticated trajectory-level RL algorithms, adaptive scheduling mechanisms, and novel reward designs tailored to the iterative nature of discrete diffusion action generation. The insights into the importance of modeling the full denoising trajectory versus just the final output can guide future architectural and algorithmic designs in this domain.
