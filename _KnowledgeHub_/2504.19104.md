# MISO: Multiresolution Submap Optimization for Efficient Globally Consistent Neural Implicit Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> MISO introduces a hierarchical optimization framework using multiresolution submaps to enable efficient and robust globally consistent neural implicit 3D reconstruction in large-scale environments, demonstrating superior computational efficiency and competitive accuracy.

## Problem

- Existing neural implicit SLAM systems struggle with scalability and efficiency for large-scale environments.
- Back-end optimization of high-dimensional neural implicit features is computationally expensive.
- Achieving robust and fast global consistency in submap-based neural SLAM is challenging, often requiring costly explicit geometry reconstruction.

## Method

- **MISO** represents environments using ==multiresolution feature grids== within local submaps, with a fixed ==pre-trained decoder== for SDF prediction.
- Applies a learning-based ==hierarchical initialization== for local submap features, predicted by neural network encoders based on residuals from coarser levels.
- Achieves global consistency by hierarchically aligning submaps directly in their ==implicit feature space==, avoiding explicit geometry decoding.

## Results

- MISO achieves up to **8x faster** local SLAM on ScanNet compared to iSDF and Neural Points, while maintaining competitive or superior reconstruction quality.
- The hierarchical feature-based submap alignment is up to **7x faster** and significantly more robust to large initial errors (e.g., **>5 deg** rotation, **>0.2m** translation) compared to SDF-based methods like MIPS and Vox-Fusion++.
- MISO demonstrates successful, globally consistent 3D reconstruction of large-scale indoor and outdoor environments, preserving fine details with fewer artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Residual information from coarser levels of implicit features can effectively inform the initialization of finer levels, enabling efficient optimization.
> - Aligning submaps directly in the multiresolution implicit feature space is more robust and faster than relying on explicit geometric reconstruction.
> - A coarse-to-fine hierarchical optimization strategy for both local mapping and global alignment enhances speed and accuracy in neural implicit SLAM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"MISO" is situated at the intersection of simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) and neural implicit representations, two rapidly evolving fields in robotics and computer vision.

**Evolution of Scene Representation in SLAM:** Traditionally, SLAM systems have relied on explicit scene representations such as point clouds, voxel grids, or mesh models. While effective, these methods often struggle with memory efficiency for large environments, produce discrete or piecewise continuous models, and can be sensitive to noise.

**Emergence of Neural Implicit Representations:** In recent years, neural fields, exemplified by works like DeepSDF and NeRF, have revolutionized 3D scene representation. These methods use neural networks (typically MLPs) to implicitly encode continuous 3D geometry (e.g., Signed Distance Functions - SDFs) or radiance fields. Their advantages include continuous and differentiable scene modeling, improved memory efficiency, and inherent robustness to measurement noise.

**Challenges of Neural SLAM:** Despite their promise, integrating neural implicit representations into real-time SLAM systems presents significant challenges. A primary limitation lies in the back-end optimization process: as the environment scale and mission time increase, the optimization problems become prohibitively large, hindering real-time performance and maintaining global consistency over long trajectories. Existing neural SLAM systems often either struggle with large-scale environments (e.g., iSDF) or adopt specific strategies (e.g., sparse features in PIN-SLAM) that may compromise fidelity or struggle with dense reconstruction.

**Role of Hierarchical Representations:** A powerful idea to enhance efficiency in SLAM is through hierarchical or multiresolution representations. This concept has a long history in conventional SLAM (e.g.,-) and has recently proven highly successful in neural fields (e.g., InstantNGP, Neuralangelo) for accelerating training and inference while capturing multi-level details. However, the authors identify a crucial gap: most existing neural SLAM back-end solvers do not fully leverage these hierarchical forms for optimization.

**Submap-based SLAM:** To address the scalability issue, many large-scale SLAM systems adopt a submap-based decomposition, partitioning the environment into smaller, manageable local maps that are then aligned and fused globally. While effective (e.g., MIPS-Fusion, Vox-Fusion++), current submap-based neural SLAM approaches typically rely on reconstructing explicit geometry (like SDF values) from the implicit features for global alignment. This geometric reconstruction is computationally expensive and can be prone to inaccuracies, especially under large initial misalignments.

"MISO" directly addresses these limitations by introducing a hierarchical optimization approach that operates directly on multiresolution implicit features. By doing so, it aims to deliver significant gains in efficiency and robustness for neural SDF SLAM, particularly in large-scale real-world environments, by avoiding the costly explicit geometric decoding for global consistency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MISO is to overcome the scalability and efficiency limitations of current neural implicit SLAM methods, particularly concerning back-end optimization and global consistency in large, complex environments.

The **key objectives** of MISO are:

- **Efficient and Scalable Neural Implicit Reconstruction:** To enable neural implicit representations to be effectively used for 3D reconstruction in large-scale environments without incurring prohibitive computational costs.
- **Improved Local SLAM Optimization:** To significantly reduce the time required to optimize neural implicit submap features within local regions, thereby accelerating the mapping process. This is motivated by the observation that existing methods often optimize features from scratch, which is computationally intensive.
- **Robust and Fast Global Consistency:** To develop a method for correcting estimation drift and achieving global consistency across multiple submaps that is both faster and more robust than existing approaches. This is motivated by the inefficiency and sensitivity of current methods that rely on explicit geometric reconstruction for submap alignment.
- **Leveraging Multiresolution Representations:** To fully exploit the benefits of multiresolution implicit features for both local and global optimization, enabling a coarse-to-fine approach that enhances speed and accuracy.

In essence, MISO seeks to provide a practical and high-performance solution for dense 3D mapping with neural implicit representations, making them more suitable for real-world robotic applications that demand both high fidelity and operational efficiency over extended periods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MISO proposes a hierarchical optimization framework for neural implicit SLAM built upon multiresolution submaps. The methodology is split into two main components: local SLAM within each submap and global alignment and fusion across submaps.

#### 3.1. Local SLAM with Multiresolution Feature Grid (Section IV)

- **Submap Representation:** Each local submap is represented as a multiresolution feature grid (Definition 1), comprising `L` levels of regular grids with increasing spatial resolution (from coarse to fine). Each grid vertex `z_l,i` stores a learnable feature vector `f_l,i`. Features at arbitrary query positions `x` are obtained via trilinear interpolation. These interpolated features from different levels are concatenated (`f_1:L(x)`) and passed through a pre-trained, fixed decoder network `D_theta` to predict the scene model (e.g., SDF values).
- **Pre-trained Decoder:** A crucial design choice is to pre-train the decoder `D_theta` offline over multiple diverse scenes (e.g., from Replica dataset). This pre-training addresses the computational challenge of training the decoder online with limited data and enhances generalization. During online SLAM, `D_theta` is fixed, and only the grid features `F` and robot poses `T^s_k` are optimized.
- **Local SLAM Optimization (Problem 1):** The core local SLAM problem is formulated as a joint optimization of the submap features `F` and robot poses `{T^s_k}`. The objective minimizes:
 - **Observation Costs:** Based on SDF supervision for near-surface points and bound enforcement for free-space points, following iSDF.
 - **Pose Regularization:** A trust-region-like penalty term that constrains pose updates, improving robustness.
 - This problem is approximately solved using local pose corrections (`Exp(epsilon_s_k)`) and optimized with Adam.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Feature Initialization for Local SLAM (Section IV-B)

- **Motivation:** Optimizing high-dimensional grid features `F` is computationally expensive. MISO introduces a novel learning-based initialization for `F`.
- **Theoretical Insight (Proposition 1):** For a simplified case (linear decoder, quadratic costs), the optimal feature `F*_l` at level `l` can be expressed as a linear function of the residuals from coarser levels `r_1:l-1(x)`. This suggests that residuals from prior levels carry information relevant to initializing finer levels.
- **Learning-based Initialization:** Inspired by Proposition 1, MISO replaces the linear mapping `E` with a neural network encoder `E_phi_l` for the general (nonlinear) case. A separate encoder `E_phi_l` is trained offline for each level `l` to predict `F_l` from the voxelized measurement residuals (`r_in_j`) computed from prior levels' predictions.
- **Algorithm 1 (HIERARCHICAL LOCAL SLAM):** The local SLAM process involves a coarse-to-fine initialization: For each level `l`, `F_l` is initialized using `E_phi_l` based on residuals computed from observed points and coarser features. After initialization, all `F` and `T^s_k` are jointly optimized.
- **Incremental Processing (Section IV-C):** For online scenarios, MISO can incrementally process sensor data by alternating between tracking (optimizing current pose) and mapping (optimizing submap features based on a sliding window of frames). In this setting, encoder initialization is typically disabled.

#### 3.3. Global Submap Alignment and Fusion (Section V)

- **Hierarchical Submap Alignment:** To correct drift between locally optimized submaps, MISO aligns them directly in the implicit feature space.
 - **Feature-based Pairwise Cost (c_feat_l):** For overlapping submaps `u` and `v`, this cost compares the concatenated multiresolution features `f_u_1:l(z_u_l,i)` and `f_v_1:l((T^w_v)^-1 T^w_u z_u_l,i)` at corresponding grid vertices using an L2 distance. This avoids decoding full geometry.
 - **Problem 2 (Level-l submap alignment):** All submap base poses `{T^w_u}` are jointly optimized based on these pairwise feature alignment costs and pose regularization.
 - **Algorithm 2 (HIERARCHICAL SUBMAP ALIGNMENT):** This alignment is performed hierarchically, progressively including features from coarser to finer levels. The solution from level `l` initializes the optimization for level `l+1`.
 - **SDF-based Finetuning (Problem 3):** After feature-based alignment, an optional final stage uses an SDF-based pairwise cost (`c_sdf`) that directly minimizes the squared difference of the *predicted SDF values* from overlapping regions. This stage runs for fewer iterations, providing fine-tuning without the high initial cost of full SDF alignment.

- **Submap Fusion:** MISO achieves global scene representation by averaging the interpolated multiresolution features from all overlapping submaps at a given world coordinate `x_w`, weighted by their bounding boxes. This averaged feature `f_w(x_w)` is then passed through the pre-trained decoder `D_theta` to obtain the global SDF prediction `h_w(x_w)`.
- **Optional Global Bundle Adjustment (Problem 22):** For further refinement, all submap features and poses can be jointly optimized using the global fused SDF prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MISO's evaluation on several real-world datasets demonstrates its superior computational efficiency and competitive accuracy compared to state-of-the-art neural implicit SLAM and submap alignment techniques.

- **Datasets Used:**
 - **Replica:** Used for offline pre-training of encoder and decoder networks.
 - **ScanNet:** Indoor RGB-D dataset for evaluating local SLAM and global submap alignment.
 - **FastCaMo-Large:** Large-scale indoor RGB-D dataset for qualitative evaluation of overall performance.
 - **Newer College:** Outdoor LiDAR dataset for evaluating large-scale, incremental performance.

- **Local SLAM Performance (ScanNet, Table I & II, Fig. 4 & 5):**
 - **Efficiency:** MISO significantly outperforms baselines (iSDF, Neural Points) in terms of GPU time. For example, on ScanNet scene 0000, MISO (noisy pose) takes 7.96 seconds, while iSDF (GT pose) takes 67.71 seconds, and Neural Points (GT pose) takes 57.87 seconds, despite MISO starting from noisy poses and baselines using ground truth.
 - **Accuracy:** MISO achieves competitive or superior reconstruction quality (Chamfer-L1 error and F-score) compared to baselines, even when starting from noisy poses and performing full SLAM.
 - **Learned Initialization:** Visualizations show that the learned hierarchical encoders (Fig. 5) effectively initialize the SDF, capturing the rough geometry at coarse levels and adding fine details at finer levels, significantly reducing the remaining optimization burden.
 - **SLAM vs. Mapping:** When using noisy odometry (Color ICP), MISO's local SLAM substantially improves pose estimates and map quality compared to mapping only.

- **Global Submap Alignment and Fusion (ScanNet, Table III, Fig. 6 & 7):**
 - **Robustness to Initial Error (Fig. 6):** MISO's hierarchical, feature-based alignment proves significantly more robust to large initial alignment errors (e.g., >5 deg rotation or >0.2m translation) compared to MIPS, Vox-Fusion++ (VFPP), and standard ICP, which often fail under such conditions.
 - **Efficiency:** MISO is substantially faster than MIPS and VFPP for submap alignment. For ScanNet scene 0011, MISO takes 10.53 seconds, while MIPS takes 59.23 seconds and VFPP takes 74.16 seconds. This speedup is attributed to direct feature-space optimization rather than costly SDF decoding.
 - **Accuracy:** MISO maintains competitive accuracy in rotation and translation errors.
 - **Qualitative (Fig. 7):** Demonstrates effective correction of misalignments, leading to clean and globally consistent 3D mesh reconstructions.

- **Qualitative Evaluation on FastCaMo-Large (Fig. 1 & 8):**
 - MISO successfully reconstructs large-scale indoor environments with fast camera motions, preserving fine details and producing cleaner meshes with fewer artifacts compared to iSDF.

- **Outdoor Evaluation on Newer College Dataset (Table IV & Fig. 9):**
 - **Performance:** MISO achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art LiDAR SLAM methods like PIN-SLAM and KISS-ICP on challenging outdoor sequences (Quad, Maths Institute).
 - **Global Consistency:** The "Full" MISO variant, which includes submap alignment and fusion, consistently improves accuracy over the "Odometry" variant (incremental tracking and mapping only), validating the effectiveness of the global optimization step.

- **Ablation Studies (Table V & VI, Fig. 10):**
 - **Encoder and Decoder Pre-training:** Pre-trained encoders and decoders significantly accelerate training loss convergence, particularly in early stages, and generally lead to better final performance.
 - **Hierarchical Alignment:** The full hierarchical alignment (coarse feature, fine feature, then SDF finetuning) yields the best accuracy for submap alignment, demonstrating the complementary benefits of each stage.
 - **Metric Function:** L2 distance is identified as the most effective metric for feature-based alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MISO makes significant contributions to the field of neural implicit SLAM by tackling the critical challenges of scalability, efficiency, and global consistency in large-scale environments.

- **Advancement in Neural SLAM Scalability:** By introducing a robust hierarchical optimization framework, MISO significantly extends the practical applicability of neural implicit representations to much larger and more complex scenes than previously feasible with existing neural SLAM systems. This is a crucial step towards deploying such high-fidelity mapping capabilities in real-world robotic systems.
- **Efficient Back-end Optimization:** The proposed learned hierarchical initialization for local submaps, inspired by a theoretical analysis of linear systems, drastically reduces the computational burden of optimizing high-dimensional implicit features. This makes local mapping faster and more robust, particularly in the initial phases of optimization.
- **Novel Global Consistency Approach:** MISO's hierarchical submap alignment in the implicit feature space is a key innovation. By avoiding the computationally expensive step of decoding full geometry for alignment, MISO achieves significantly faster and more robust global consistency correction, especially when dealing with large initial estimation errors. This is a clear advantage over prior work that relies solely on SDF-based alignment.
- **High-Fidelity and Globally Consistent Mapping:** The combination of multiresolution submaps, efficient local optimization, and robust global alignment enables MISO to reconstruct highly detailed and globally consistent 3D maps, even in challenging environments.
- **Paving the Way for Practical Deployment:** The substantial improvements in efficiency and robustness demonstrated by MISO bring neural implicit SLAM closer to real-time performance and practical deployment in applications such as autonomous navigation, augmented reality, and large-scale digital twinning.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its strengths, MISO identifies several avenues for future work. The reliance on dense feature grids, while offering advantages, could become memory-intensive for extremely large environments. Future research could explore integrating MISO's hierarchical optimization with sparse data structures (e.g., octrees) or tensor decomposition techniques to further improve memory efficiency. Additionally, while this work focuses on SDF reconstruction, extending the approach to model other scene properties (e.g., radiance, semantics, uncertainty) would enhance its utility. Finally, moving beyond offline batch optimization to fully online and dynamic SLAM settings represents a significant next step for practical real-world applications.

In conclusion, MISO represents a significant stride in neural implicit reconstruction for SLAM, addressing core limitations and setting a new benchmark for efficiency and robustness in large-scale 3D mapping.
