# PriorEye: Geospatial Visual Priors for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> PriorEye integrates geospatial visual priors into end-to-end autonomous driving systems, providing visual-spatial foresight that enables anticipatory behavior and improves robustness against sensor degradation across various driving tasks. This augmentation consistently improved driving performance across four E2E baselines, achieving up to a 31.2% relative EPDMS gain on the navhard-two-stage benchmark.

## Problem

- End-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving policies are typically reactive, relying solely on instantaneous sensor observations and lacking the anticipatory capabilities of human drivers.
- Current E2E systems are vulnerable to sensor degradation from severe weather or occlusions, as they lack persistent contextual information to stabilize perception.
- Existing methods incorporating prior knowledge into autonomous driving usually focus on upstream tasks or high-level decision-making, not directly integrating route-specific visual context into E2E planning.

## Method

- Introduces ==geospatial visual priors==, which are street-level visual contexts anchored to the intended driving route and stored in an offline ==memory bank==.
- Develops a model-agnostic ==memory augmentation module== that integrates these priors into an E2E system's intermediate state, acting as a generic ==state-editing mechanism==.
- Implements a ==dual-memory architecture==, consisting of input-dependent contextual memory and persistent learnable tokens, along with an ==adaptive memory gate== to dynamically regulate their influence.

## Results

- PriorEye consistently improved performance across all **four** tested E2E driving baselines (LTF, GTRS-DP, GTRS-Dense, DrivoR), with up to a **31.2%** relative EPDMS improvement on the navhard-two-stage benchmark.
- The system significantly enhanced robustness against synthetic camera corruptions, reducing average relative performance degradation from **20.6%** (baseline) to **13.9%**, and maintained stability even when priors were corrupted.
- The memory augmentation module introduced a low inference overhead of only **9.3 ms**, making it suitable for real-time deployment without substantial computational cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geospatial visual priors effectively provide visual-spatial foresight to E2E planners, enabling anticipatory driving actions beyond purely reactive responses.
> - A dual-memory architecture with a persistent fallback memory ensures robustness by adaptively shifting attention away from unreliable or corrupted contextual priors.
> - The adaptive memory gate, which models compatibility between the current state and memory, is crucial for dynamically weighting information sources and ensuring robust integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

End-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving is a prominent paradigm that seeks to directly map raw sensor observations to driving actions or trajectories. This approach offers advantages such as scalability, conceptual simplicity, joint optimization, and the potential to mitigate cascading errors inherent in traditional modular pipelines. Recent advancements, particularly with vision-language models, have further enhanced the performance and interpretability of E2E systems.

Despite these developments, a fundamental limitation in most existing E2E driving policies is their reactive nature. These systems typically rely solely on instantaneous sensor observations, usually spanning only a few seconds. This short-horizon perception restricts them from performing anticipatory actions that human drivers commonly employ, which are often informed by prior experience and foresight. For instance, human drivers can proactively respond to upcoming road conditions (like a lane merge or a sharp curve) even when these features are not yet visible, relying on a form of "driving memory." Furthermore, the exclusive reliance on real-time sensor data makes current E2E systems vulnerable to sensor degradation caused by severe weather or visual occlusions, as they lack persistent contextual information to stabilize perception.

Prior knowledge has been explored in various contexts within autonomous driving. This includes leveraging past driving experience for high-level reasoning, using spatial priors like maps and route information for structural guidance or online map construction, and employing visual priors (e.g., street-level images) to capture appearance and scene context. However, existing methods typically integrate priors for upstream tasks (e.g., HD map generation) or high-level decision-making, rather than directly incorporating them into the E2E planning process. Moreover, spatial and visual priors are often treated in isolation, preventing the planner from accessing route-specific visual context.

The work presented in "PriorEye" addresses this specific gap by introducing and directly integrating "geospatial visual priors" into E2E driving policies. By coupling offline visual context with route-level spatial information, this research aims to provide E2E planners with explicit visual-spatial foresight, thereby enabling anticipatory, efficient, and robust driving behavior, even when immediate onboard sensors are compromised. This positions PriorEye as a contribution to augmenting existing E2E architectures with a form of long-term memory, analogous to advancements seen in memory-augmented Large Language Models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a recognized limitation in contemporary end-to-end autonomous driving systems. These systems primarily operate reactively, making decisions based on immediate sensor observations. This approach contrasts sharply with human driving, where drivers utilize foresight and experience to anticipate future road conditions and execute proactive maneuvers. For example, a human driver might decelerate for a known sharp curve hidden by an obstruction or prepare to merge before a lane ends, relying on internalized knowledge rather than real-time visibility.

This reliance on instantaneous sensor data in existing E2E systems leads to two critical shortcomings:
1. **Lack of Anticipatory Behavior:** Without knowledge of upcoming environmental context beyond the immediate sensor view, E2E systems are confined to reactive responses, hindering efficient and smooth navigation in complex or partially observable scenarios.
2. **Vulnerability to Sensor Degradation:** When onboard sensors are compromised by factors such as severe weather (e.g., snow, heavy rain), occlusions, or corruption (e.g., mud splatter on cameras), systems solely dependent on these sensors become unreliable. They lack alternative, persistent contextual evidence to maintain stable perception and make informed decisions. Figure 1 in the paper illustrates this, showing how a stop sign becomes invisible due to camera occlusion but remains clearly discernible in a geospatial visual prior.

Inspired by these observations, the core objective of this research is to equip end-to-end driving policies with a mechanism for "visual-spatial foresight." This is achieved through the introduction of **geospatial visual priors**, which are defined as street-level visual context anchored to the intended driving route. These priors act as a form of "driving memory" that is independent of real-time sensor conditions.

To achieve this, the research sets forth several key objectives:
- **Introduce Geospatial Visual Priors:** Develop a formal representation that explicitly encodes visual and spatial foresight by anchoring offline-collected street-level images to the planned driving route.
- **Develop a Model-Agnostic Memory Augmentation Module:** Create a module that can be seamlessly integrated into diverse existing E2E driving policies without requiring modifications to their core architectures.
- **Design a Dual-Memory Architecture:** Implement a memory system comprising a "contextual memory" to store retrieved geospatial visual priors and a "persistent fallback memory" to ensure robustness against potential errors or unreliability in the retrieved contextual information.
- **Incorporate an Adaptive Memory Gate:** Design a mechanism to dynamically regulate the influence of the two memory sources (contextual and persistent) and real-time observations based on their compatibility and perceived reliability.
- **Validate Performance and Robustness:** Empirically demonstrate that integrating these priors consistently improves driving performance across various E2E baselines and enhances robustness, particularly against sensor degradation and even against corruption within the priors themselves.

By addressing these objectives, the researchers aim to advance E2E autonomous driving beyond purely reactive capabilities, imbuing systems with the anticipatory intelligence and resilience characteristic of human drivers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a generic memory augmentation module designed to integrate geospatial visual priors into existing end-to-end driving systems. This module operates as a state-editing mechanism, taking the intermediate state of an E2E model and enhancing it with retrieved priors before feeding it to the downstream decoder.

#### 3.1. Geospatial Visual Priors and Memory Bank

The foundation of the approach is the concept of geospatial visual priors, which are visual-spatial representations that anchor street-level visual context to the intended driving route. These priors are stored and retrieved from a global offline repository called the "memory bank."

- **Construction of the Memory Bank:**
 - For a target deployment area, street-level images are sampled along each lane centerline at a fixed spatial interval.
 - Each sampled image is encoded into an embedding using a frozen visual backbone (e.g., SigLIP2).
 - These visual embeddings, along with their corresponding locations (acting as spatial anchors), are stored as "lane-associated memory."
 - The framework is designed to be robust to coarsely distributed or noisy imagery, focusing on providing semantically meaningful visual cues rather than precise geometric reconstruction.

- **Querying Geospatial Visual Priors:**
 - During runtime (training or inference), a subset of relevant priors is retrieved.
 - Given the ego-vehicle's current state, the current lane is identified.
 - A Depth-First Search (DFS) is performed on the lane connectivity graph to generate candidate lane sequences extending forward.
 - Based on the high-level navigational intention (e.g., Turn Left, Turn Right, Straight), the corresponding lane sequence is selected.
 - The visual embeddings ($V$) and their relative (x,y) coordinates ($X$) stored along this selected lane sequence are retrieved, forming the geospatial visual priors: $\{(v_i, x_i)\}_{i=1}^N$, where N is the number of retrieved priors.

#### 3.2. Memory Augmentation Module

The memory augmentation module is responsible for integrating the queried geospatial visual priors into the E2E system's internal representation. It consists of a dual-memory architecture and an adaptive memory fusion mechanism.

- **Dual Memory Architecture:** This architecture treats priors as retrievable memories, decomposing them into two complementary components:
 - **Contextual Memory (C):** This is an input-dependent representation derived from the queried geospatial visual priors. It is constructed by projecting the visual priors $\phi(V)$ into the E2E model's embedding space and additively combining them with 2D sinusoidal positional embeddings $\psi(X)$ of the spatial priors. This combination allows the contextual memory to jointly capture semantic appearance information and coarse spatial layout.
 - **Persistent Memory (P):** This consists of a set of learnable tokens (independent of the current input and shared across all samples). It serves as a robust fallback mechanism, analogous to "attention sinks," to absorb attention mass when contextual memory is corrupted, misaligned, or unhelpful (e.g., due to seasonal changes or geometric mismatches in street-level imagery).

- **Memory Fusion:** This mechanism selectively integrates the dual memories into the current driving state.
 - The persistent memory (P) and contextual memory (C) are concatenated along the token dimension to form a combined memory representation M.
 - Multi-head cross-attention is applied, where the current driving state (S), represented as query tokens from the E2E model, attends to the combined memory M (acting as keys and values). This produces a memory-attended driving state $\tilde{S}$.

- **Memory Gate (G):** An adaptive gate is introduced to regulate the influence of the fused memories on the driving state.
 - The gate explicitly models the compatibility between the current state S and the memory-attended state $\tilde{S}$.
 - Compatibility is measured using both Euclidean distance (d) and cosine similarity (c) between S and $\tilde{S}$.
 - A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), $f_g$, processes the concatenated inputs [S; $\tilde{S}$; d; c]. The output is passed through a sigmoid function to produce the gate G.
 - The gate's bias is initialized to favor low values, ensuring it starts in a near-closed state for training stability.

- **Final State Update:** The fused driving state $S'$ is obtained via a gated residual update: $S' = S + G \odot \tilde{S}$, where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication. This enhanced state $S'$ then replaces the original state S and is fed into the downstream decoder of the E2E model for final trajectory prediction.

The entire module is designed to be jointly trained with each baseline E2E model using their original training objectives, without introducing additional loss terms, thus maintaining the integrity of the base planner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the proposed PriorEye framework across various end-to-end driving baselines on the NAVSIM-v2 benchmark, encompassing both quantitative performance metrics and robustness analyses.

#### 4.1. Core Performance Evaluation

The evaluation was conducted on the NAVSIM-v2 benchmark, utilizing the Extended Predictive Driver Model Score (EPDMS) as the primary composite metric, which aggregates several safety and comfort sub-metrics.
- **Consistency Across Baselines:** PriorEye consistently improved performance across all four tested end-to-end driving baselines (LTF, GTRS-DP, GTRS-Dense, and DrivoR), spanning regression-based, diffusion-based, and scoring-based paradigms. This indicates the model-agnostic nature and general effectiveness of the proposed memory augmentation.
- **Quantitative Gains on `navhard-two-stage` (Table 1):** On the `navhard-two-stage` benchmark, which includes a Stage 2 evaluation for robustness under controlled perturbations, PriorEye yielded significant relative EPDMS improvements:
 - LTF: +31.2%
 - GTRS-DP: +14.4%
 - GTRS-Dense: +8.2%
 - DrivoR: +1.4%
- **Quantitative Gains on `navtest` (Table 2):** Similar consistent improvements were observed on the `navtest` split, with EPDMS gains of 2.5, 0.3, 3.4, and 2.7 points for LTF, GTRS-DP, GTRS-Dense, and DrivoR, respectively. This confirms that the benefits extend beyond a single evaluation setting.
- **Qualitative Demonstration (Fig. 3):** A qualitative example illustrated PriorEye's ability to enable anticipatory driving. In a left-turn scenario with an upcoming pedestrian crosswalk obscured from immediate onboard sensors, the baseline maintained a higher speed. In contrast, PriorEye, using geospatial visual priors, anticipated the crosswalk and drove at a more cautious speed, demonstrating foresight-driven behavior.
- **Per-Metric Analysis (Supplementary Table 11):** Detailed analysis showed PriorEye primarily improved metrics related to route adherence (DAC, DDC, LK), ego progress (EP), and comfort (HC, EC). The improvements in comfort and progress were attributed to smoother motion and reduced hesitation due to anticipating road features. Slight declines in collision scores (NC, TTC) were observed, which the authors suggest might be due to the priors not capturing dynamic agent states, leading to more confident progress without necessarily improving dynamic safety margins.

#### 4.2. Robustness Analysis

The research specifically evaluated PriorEye's robustness in two critical areas:

- **Sensor Robustness (Table 3):**
 - Under synthetic camera corruptions (fingerprints, handprints, frost, mild mud, heavy mud), PriorEye consistently outperformed the baseline (GTRS-Dense).
 - The performance degradation for PriorEye was significantly less severe. For example, under "Mud (Heavy)" corruption, the baseline suffered a 53.2% performance drop, while PriorEye's drop was 32.5%.
 - On average, PriorEye reduced the relative performance degradation across all corruption types from 20.6% (baseline) to 13.9%. This confirms that priors, being independent of onboard sensors, provide a robust complementary information source.
 - Qualitative examples (Supplementary Fig. 8) visually demonstrate PriorEye maintaining proper lane-keeping under heavy mud occlusion where the baseline failed.

- **Geospatial Visual Prior Corruption (Table 4):**
 - To assess the dual-memory architecture, scenarios with corrupted geospatial visual priors (e.g., visual embeddings from a distant random location, spatially misaligned coordinates, or both) were simulated.
 - **Adaptive Attention Shift:** The full dual-memory model adaptively reallocated attention towards the persistent memory (P) when contextual memory (C) became unreliable. Under visual corruption, P's attention weight rose to 0.97, and under combined corruption, it reached 0.99, effectively suppressing misleading contextual cues.
 - **Graceful Degradation:** While a context-only model suffered severe performance degradation (e.g., 19.6% drop under visual corruption), the full dual-memory model remained robust, experiencing significantly smaller drops (e.g., 6.8% under visual corruption). Notably, the full model consistently outperformed the baseline even when its priors were corrupted, highlighting the persistent memory's role as a reliable fallback.
 - Qualitative examples (Supplementary Fig. 9) showed that even with corrupted priors, the planned trajectory remained largely consistent with normal retrieval, due to the module assigning full attention to persistent memory.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 5)

Extensive ablation studies using LTF as the baseline on a validation split validated key design choices:
- **Embedding Backbone:** SigLIP2 outperformed DINOv2 and SegFormer, suggesting its richer semantic representations are more effective for street-level visual priors.
- **Retrieval Strategy:** The intention-guided retrieval, which selects priors based on the planned maneuver, significantly outperformed a naive proximity-based strategy, emphasizing the importance of maneuver-relevant context.
- **Memory Components:** The dual-memory (P+C) architecture achieved the best performance, surpassing contextual memory alone (C-only) and persistent memory alone (P-only), supporting its design for both nominal performance and robustness.
- **Contextual Memory Components:** Combining visual encodings $\phi(V)$ and spatial encodings $\psi(X)$ yielded the largest gains, demonstrating that structural geometry and semantic appearance from priors are complementary.
- **Memory Gate Components:** Incorporating both Euclidean distance and cosine similarity as compatibility cues for the adaptive gate achieved the best performance, indicating their complementary roles in assessing state-memory consistency.

#### 4.4. Runtime Analysis (Table 6)

PriorEye introduced a low inference overhead. On the GTRS-Dense baseline, the memory augmentation module added only 5.6 ms of GPU latency and 3.7 ms of CPU-side memory retrieval, totaling 9.3 ms. This additional latency maintains the system comfortably within a 10 Hz runtime budget.

#### 4.5. Comparison with HD Map Priors (Supplementary Table 9)

An additional comparison demonstrated that PriorEye, which relies only on lane-center anchoring and street-level visual appearance, achieved larger EPDMS improvements (+3.7) than baselines augmented with richer rasterized (+2.2) or vectorized (+1.5) HD map content. This suggests that visual appearance along the route provides a more effective signal for E2E planning than structured map elements, and that geospatial visual priors offer a more practical solution with lighter mapping requirements than full HD maps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "PriorEye" addresses a fundamental limitation in end-to-end autonomous driving systems, marking a step toward more intelligent and resilient autonomous vehicles.

- **Enabling Anticipatory Driving:** The primary significance lies in moving E2E autonomous driving beyond purely reactive behavior. By providing spatially grounded visual foresight independent of instantaneous sensors, PriorEye enables vehicles to anticipate upcoming road conditions, leading to more proactive, smoother, and potentially more efficient navigation. This bridges a critical gap between current E2E systems and the anticipatory capabilities of human drivers.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Sensor Degradation:** The method inherently improves robustness against common real-world challenges such as sensor corruption (e.g., from weather, dirt, or occlusions). The geospatial visual priors serve as an uncorrupted, complementary source of information, maintaining scene context and driving capability even when real-time perception is degraded. This is a crucial step for deploying autonomous vehicles in diverse and challenging environments.
- **Reliable Operation through Dual-Memory Design:** The dual-memory architecture with an adaptive gate provides a safety mechanism. It ensures that the system can gracefully degrade and fall back to a reliable persistent memory when the retrieved contextual priors themselves are outdated, misaligned, or corrupted. This architectural choice enhances the trustworthiness and safety of the system.
- **Model-Agnostic and Integrable:** The proposed memory augmentation module is designed to be model-agnostic, demonstrating its compatibility and effectiveness across diverse E2E planning paradigms (regression, diffusion, scoring-based). This broad applicability means it can enhance a wide range of existing and future E2E architectures without extensive re-engineering.
- **Practical Deployment Considerations:** The low computational overhead (negligible additional parameters and latency) makes PriorEye suitable for real-world deployment. Furthermore, the reliance on street-level imagery and lane connectivity graphs, rather than full High-Definition (HD) maps, suggests a potentially more scalable and less costly approach to generating and maintaining priors. Existing fleet data could be leveraged to build and update the memory bank, integrating with current global routing systems.
- **Opening New Research Avenues:** The identified limitations, such as the reliance on ego-vehicle location for retrieval and the static nature of the memory bank, suggest promising future research directions. These include exploring appearance-based matching for more robust retrieval and developing mechanisms for memory update and forgetting to adapt to long-term environmental changes.

Overall, PriorEye contributes to making end-to-end autonomous driving systems more intelligent, robust, and reliable by endowing them with a form of long-term visual-spatial memory, which is a key component for robust autonomy.
