# Boundary-to-Region Supervision for Offline Safe Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology and Westlake University developed the Boundary-to-Region (B2R) framework, which enhances offline safe reinforcement learning by addressing the "symmetry fallacy" in cost signal conditioning. B2R employs region-level supervision through CTG realignment, leading to superior safety compliance in 35 out of 38 DSRL tasks and competitive reward performance.

## Problem

- Existing sequence-model-based offline safe RL methods (e.g., Constrained Decision Transformer) treat 'return-to-go' (RTG) and 'cost-to-go' (CTG) symmetrically, overlooking CTG's role as a rigid safety boundary.
- This 'symmetry fallacy' leads to unreliable constraint satisfaction, especially when policies encounter out-of-distribution cost trajectories.
- Current methods suffer from sparse supervision, learning primarily from trajectories with costs close to the safety threshold, which limits robust generalization across the entire safe operational region.

## Method

- The ==Boundary-to-Region== (**B2R**) framework first filters the offline dataset, retaining only trajectories whose total cumulative cost does not exceed the deployment-time safety threshold.
- It then performs Cost-To-Go (CTG) realignment for each safe trajectory, adding a constant offset to ensure all initial CTG tokens align with the fixed safety budget, thereby providing dense, region-wide supervision.
- Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) are integrated to effectively encode the fine-grained temporal structure and relative dependencies of the realigned cost signals within the Transformer architecture.

## Results

- B2R demonstrated superior safety compliance, satisfying safety constraints (normalized cost < 1) in **35** out of **38** safety-critical tasks on the DSRL benchmark.
- The framework achieved competitive reward performance, securing the highest rewards in **20** of the **38** tasks and ranking first in average scores across all three benchmark suites.
- Ablation studies confirmed that CTG realignment significantly lowers cumulative costs, trajectory filtering ensures stable safety compliance, and RoPE improves both cost adherence and reward performance by better modeling temporal dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - There is a fundamental asymmetry between flexible reward targets (RTG) and rigid safety boundaries (CTG) that existing sequence-model-based offline safe RL methods often conflate.
> - Transforming sparse boundary-level cost supervision into dense, region-wide supervision through intelligent CTG realignment enables more robust and reliable learning of safety constraints.
> - Careful temporal encoding of cost dynamics, such as with Rotary Positional Embeddings, is crucial for sequence models to effectively leverage realigned cost signals and ensure fine-grained safety adherence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of **Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL)** and **Safe Reinforcement Learning**, two critical and rapidly evolving subfields of AI.

**Offline RL** is crucial for real-world, safety-critical applications (e.g., autonomous driving, robotics, industrial control) where online interaction for data collection is risky, expensive, or impractical. It aims to learn optimal policies from static, pre-collected datasets without further environment interaction. A major challenge in offline RL is dealing with distributional shift, where the learned policy might try to exploit actions not well-represented in the training data. Recent advancements in "Reinforcement Learning via Supervised Learning" (RvS), exemplified by the **Decision Transformer (DT)**, have reframed offline RL as a sequence modeling problem, autoregressively predicting actions conditioned on desired returns and historical context.

**Safe RL** focuses on developing agents that can maximize rewards while adhering to predefined safety constraints, often formulated within the Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) framework. The integration of safety into offline learning (i.e., **Offline Safe RL**) is essential to ensure that policies learned from static data not only perform well but also rigorously comply with safety regulations in deployment.

Existing **sequence-model-based offline safe RL methods**, such as the Constrained Decision Transformer (CDT), typically extend the DT paradigm by symmetrically conditioning action generation on both "return-to-go" (RTG) and "cost-to-go" (CTG) tokens. The core limitation this paper addresses is a "symmetry fallacy" in these approaches. While RTG functions as a flexible performance *target* to be maximized, CTG represents a rigid safety *boundary* that must not be violated. Existing methods often conflate these distinct roles, leading to unreliable constraint satisfaction, particularly when policies encounter states or cost trajectories that are out-of-distribution (OOD) from the sparse "boundary-aligned" examples available during training. This lack of nuance in handling cost signals limits the robustness and reliability of safe policies.

The work presented in this paper directly intervenes in this landscape by proposing a **Boundary-to-Region (B2R) framework** that explicitly acknowledges and leverages the inherent asymmetry between reward and cost signals. By introducing "region-level supervision" through intelligent cost signal realignment, B2R aims to move beyond sparse supervision at constraint boundaries to leverage the full spectrum of safe behaviors within the dataset. This represents a significant conceptual and practical shift, seeking to make sequence-model-based offline safe RL more robust, reliable, and practically applicable by addressing a fundamental oversight in how safety constraints are modeled and learned.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a critical identified limitation in current sequence-model-based offline safe reinforcement learning (RL) approaches: the "symmetry fallacy."

**Key Problem Identified:**
Existing methods, particularly those building upon the Decision Transformer (DT) architecture like the Constrained Decision Transformer (CDT), treat "return-to-go" (RTG) and "cost-to-go" (CTG) signals symmetrically when conditioning action generation. This symmetrical treatment overlooks a fundamental difference in their intrinsic nature:
- **Return-to-Go (RTG):** Represents a flexible performance *target* that the policy strives to maximize. Its precise value can vary, and minor deviations are generally acceptable, impacting performance but not leading to catastrophic failure.
- **Cost-to-Go (CTG):** Represents a rigid safety *boundary* or budget that the policy absolutely *must not* exceed. Violations of the CTG can lead to unsafe, undesirable, or catastrophic outcomes.

This oversight leads to two significant challenges:
1. **Unreliable Constraint Satisfaction:** Treating CTG as a flexible target rather than a rigid boundary makes policies prone to violating safety constraints, especially when confronted with out-of-distribution (OOD) cost trajectories not extensively represented in the training data.
2. **Sparse Supervision:** Current methods typically learn from trajectories whose total costs are very close to the predefined safety threshold (boundary-aligned trajectories). This results in sparse training signals, limiting the model's ability to generalize robustly across the entire safe operational region and leading to brittle policies.

### 3. Key Objectives of the Work

To address these limitations, the authors set out to achieve the following objectives:
1. **Formalize the "Symmetry Fallacy":** Clearly identify and articulate the problem arising from the symmetric treatment of RTG and CTG in existing RvS methods.
2. **Introduce "Region-Level Supervision":** Propose a new paradigm for offline safe RL that moves beyond sparse boundary supervision to dense, region-wide supervision, effectively utilizing all feasible trajectories.
3. **Develop the Boundary-to-Region (B2R) Framework:** Design a logically coherent framework to implement this region-level supervision. This involves:
 - Enabling **asymmetric conditioning** of cost signals, redefining CTG as a boundary constraint.
 - **Unifying the cost distribution** of all feasible trajectories under a fixed safety budget through cost signal realignment.
 - **Preserving reward structures** while enhancing the exploration of the safe action space.
 - Integrating **Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE)** to better capture temporal dynamics.
4. **Provide Theoretical Analysis:** Establish initial theoretical foundations for B2R's safety compliance and performance superiority under simplified assumptions.
5. **Empirically Validate:** Demonstrate B2R's effectiveness in balancing reward maximization and safety constraint satisfaction across a diverse set of safety-critical tasks, outperforming baseline methods.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more robust and intrinsically safety-aware sequence model for offline RL by fundamentally rethinking how safety constraints are represented and learned.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The Boundary-to-Region (B2R) framework introduces a novel approach to offline safe reinforcement learning by explicitly addressing the inherent asymmetry between reward-to-go (RTG) and cost-to-go (CTG) signals. The methodology centers on transforming the CTG into a rigid safety boundary, enabling robust, region-wide supervision for sequence-model-based policies.

#### 4.1. Formalizing the Problem and Limitations

The paper first formalizes the offline safe RL problem within the Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) framework, where the goal is to maximize expected reward E[R(τ)] subject to an expected cost constraint E[C(τ)] ≤ κ. It then highlights how existing Reinforcement Learning via Supervised Learning (RvS) methods, like the Decision Transformer (DT), naively apply symmetric conditioning to both RTG and CTG. This leads to issues in token selection for deployment (balancing a flexible RTG target with a rigid CTG budget) and sparse supervision, as the model primarily learns from trajectories with costs close to the constraint boundary, rather than the entire safe region.

#### 4.2. The Boundary-to-Region (B2R) Framework

B2R is designed as a coherent system with three interlocking components:

1. **Trajectory Filtering:**
 - **Purpose:** To define the "safe region" by ensuring all training data is inherently feasible.
 - **Mechanism:** As a preprocessing step, B2R filters the raw offline dataset `D`, retaining only trajectories `τ` whose total cumulative cost `C(τ)` does not exceed the deployment-time safety threshold `κ`. This forms the `D_safe` dataset: `D_safe = {τ ∈ D | C(τ) ≤ κ}`. This step guarantees that the agent never observes or learns from unsafe behaviors during training.

2. **CTG Realignment:**
 - **Purpose:** To transform sparse boundary supervision into a dense, uniform, region-wide supervision signal. This is the core innovation for asymmetric conditioning.
 - **Mechanism (Default: Shift Strategy):** For each safe trajectory `τ` in `D_safe`, the Cost-To-Go (CTG) sequence `ˆC_t` is realigned. A constant offset `Δ = κ - C(τ)` is added to every CTG token in the trajectory: `ˆC'_t = ˆC_t + Δ`.
 - This ensures that the initial CTG token `ˆC'_0` for *every* realigned trajectory becomes exactly `κ` (the target safety budget).
 - Crucially, this `Shift` strategy preserves the *temporal profile* of the original cost decay within the trajectory while aligning its starting point to the fixed `κ`. This effectively unifies all feasible trajectories under a single cost boundary `κ` without altering their reward structure or state-action sequences. The model is thus consistently conditioned on `κ` but learns from the diverse behaviors originally scattered across the entire safe region.
 - *(The paper also discusses alternative strategies like Average, Random, and Scale in the appendix, with 'Shift' found to be the most balanced.)*

3. **Tokenization with Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE):**
 - **Purpose:** To effectively encode the temporal structure of the realigned cost signals within the Transformer architecture.
 - **Mechanism:** The input tokens (realigned `ˆC'_t`, `ˆR_t`, `s_t`, `a_t`) are augmented with RoPE. RoPE's relative positional encoding is better suited than standard absolute positional embeddings (APE) for capturing the fine-grained, step-by-step cost dynamics introduced by the CTG realignment strategy.

#### 4.3. Policy Training and Inference

- **Model Architecture:** B2R utilizes a standard Transformer-based autoregressive structure.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained via behavior cloning, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of actions (Eq. 6) on the filtered, CTG-realigned, and RoPE-encoded trajectories from `D_safe`.
- **Inference:** During deployment, the policy generates actions autoregressively, consistently conditioned on the desired initial return `ˆR_0` and the fixed safety threshold `ˆC_0 = κ`.

#### 4.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical insights into B2R's safety guarantees and performance:
- **Assumptions:** The analysis relies on assumptions such as "Safe-Aligned Data" (training trajectories adhere to the `ˆC'_0 = κ` principle) and "Prediction-Error Bound" (the agent's implicit cost predictions are sufficiently accurate during deployment, with a bounded error `σ`). "Optimal-Coverage" ensures a reward-optimal safe trajectory exists in the dataset.
- **Theorem 1 (Safety Guarantees):** Under these assumptions, B2R provides high-probability and expected-cost safety guarantees, ensuring that the generated policy's cumulative cost `C(τ)` remains below `κ` with high probability, and its expected cost stays below `κ` by a safety margin.
- **Theorem 2 (Performance Superiority):** B2R is theoretically guaranteed to achieve reward performance no worse than (and potentially superior to) methods relying solely on boundary-conditional supervision, while still satisfying safety constraints. This is attributed to its ability to leverage a broader set of safe, high-reward trajectories.

In summary, B2R ingeniously manipulates the cost signal in the training data to provide a consistent, dense, and safety-aligned supervision signal, overcoming the limitations of sparse boundary supervision and enabling more reliable offline safe RL with sequence models.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the B2R framework was conducted on the DSRL benchmark, a comprehensive suite of 38 safety-critical tasks spanning SafetyGymnasium, BulletSafetyGym, and MetaDrive environments. The key metrics focused on normalized reward (to be maximized) and normalized cost (to be kept below the constraint threshold of 1). Experiments were run across three cost limits and three random seeds for robustness.

#### 5.1. Overall Performance and Safety Compliance

- **Superior Safety:** B2R demonstrated remarkable safety compliance, satisfying safety constraints (normalized cost < 1) in **35 out of 38** tasks. This highlights its core strength in reliably adhering to safety budgets.
- **Competitive Rewards:** Alongside robust safety, B2R achieved superior reward performance, securing the highest rewards in **20** of the 38 tasks. It also ranked first in average scores across all three benchmark suites (SafetyGymnasium, Bullet Safety-Gym, and MetaDrive).
- **Robustness:** The framework proved robust across varying constraint levels. While some non-monotonic cost trends were observed (e.g., in MetaDrive due to filtering bias at stringent L1 levels), B2R generally maintained stable or improving rewards with decreasing costs under tighter constraints.
- **Limitations:** B2R struggled in a few exceptionally challenging environments (CarCircle1/2 and AntCircle). This was attributed to inherent dataset limitations where high-reward behaviors were tightly coupled with high-cost actions, and insufficient data existed for precise, long-horizon control within narrow safe margins.

#### 5.2. Comparison with Baseline Methods

- **vs. Transformer-based (CDT):** B2R consistently achieved lower costs than CDT in most environments while maintaining competitive rewards. This confirmed CDT's vulnerability to sparse boundary supervision and demonstrated B2R's advantage from region-wide supervision. Supplemental experiments further showed that simply buffering CDT's target cost (e.g., training with 70% of κ) often led to an unfavorable safety-performance trade-off and didn't match B2R's robustness.
- **vs. Filtering-based (BC-Safe, TraC):** While BC-Safe and TraC also filter unsafe trajectories, B2R's CTG realignment uniquely transforms the learning problem into a fine-grained temporal regression. This generates a denser, more effective supervision signal, enabling better learning of action-cost dynamics compared to sparse or coarse binary classification approaches.
- **vs. Recent Offline Safe RL Methods (FISOR, OASIS, LSPC, FAWAC):** B2R demonstrated the highest average reward while maintaining competitive or lower costs across diverse tasks. This suggests B2R's general applicability without requiring task-specific tuning, even outperforming more conservative methods like FISOR under stringent cost limits.

#### 5.3. Ablation Studies (Impact of B2R Components)

A detailed ablation study highlighted the critical contribution of each B2R component:
- **Without CTG Realignment:** Policies exhibited significantly higher cumulative costs and frequent constraint violations across almost all environments. Rewards remained identical, empirically confirming that realignment primarily impacts cost adherence.
- **Without RoPE (using Absolute Positional Embeddings):** Replacing RoPE with standard APE led to substantial performance drops (higher costs, lower rewards) in several tasks. This validated RoPE's effectiveness in modeling the fine-grained, relative temporal dependencies of cost dynamics.
- **Without Trajectory Filtering:** Removing filtering resulted in a marked increase in cost, unstable performance, and frequent constraint violations. This underscored filtering's crucial role in ensuring feasible supervision and stabilizing safety compliance, even if it might slightly limit reward in some cases by excluding higher-reward but unsafe data.

#### 5.4. Cost-Aware Action Selection

t-SNE visualizations of action distributions in the `easysparse` environment showed that B2R produced significantly fewer unsafe actions and more concentrated, constraint-compliant action clusters compared to a baseline. This indicates that B2R's cost-aligned training and region-wide supervision enable better generalization to the intended constraint threshold, leading to more reliable safe decision-making.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The Boundary-to-Region (B2R) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of offline safe reinforcement learning, particularly for approaches leveraging sequence models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 6.1. Addressing a Fundamental Limitation

The paper rigorously identifies and addresses the "symmetry fallacy" in existing sequence-model-based safe RL methods, where flexible reward targets and rigid safety boundaries are treated indistinguishably. By formalizing this oversight and proposing an asymmetric conditioning mechanism, B2R establishes a new theoretical and practical foundation for how safety constraints should be integrated into sequence modeling for RL. This fundamental conceptual correction enhances the reliability and interpretability of safe policies.

#### 6.2. Introducing Region-Level Supervision

B2R introduces a novel paradigm of "region-level supervision" by effectively transforming sparse, boundary-aligned data into a dense, robust learning signal. Instead of relying only on trajectories that narrowly meet the cost threshold, B2R's CTG realignment mechanism unifies *all* safe trajectories under the same desired safety budget. This richer training signal enables the model to learn a more comprehensive and nuanced understanding of the safe action space, leading to policies that are more stable and better generalize to diverse operational conditions.

#### 6.3. Enhanced Safety Compliance and Performance

Experimentally, B2R demonstrates superior safety compliance, consistently adhering to constraints in 35 out of 38 tasks, while simultaneously achieving competitive or leading reward performance. This strong empirical validation on a diverse benchmark (DSRL) highlights its practical efficacy in balancing the often-conflicting objectives of reward maximization and stringent safety.

#### 6.4. Robustness and Generalizability

The framework exhibits robustness across various constraint levels and even under conditions of data scarcity (as shown in supplementary experiments). The successful proof-of-concept for handling multiple constraint targets with a single B2R agent further underscores its potential for greater generalizability and reduced training overhead in real-world applications where safety requirements might vary. The detailed ablation studies confirm that each component of B2R (trajectory filtering, CTG realignment, and RoPE) contributes synergistically to its overall performance.

#### 6.5. Potential Impact on Real-World Applications

The ability to learn highly reliable and safe policies from offline data is paramount for deploying autonomous agents in high-stakes environments. B2R's advancements directly contribute to making RL systems more trustworthy for applications such as:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Ensuring vehicles operate within safety limits, avoiding collisions and respecting traffic laws.
- **Robotics:** Developing robots that can perform complex tasks safely alongside humans or in delicate environments.
- **Industrial Control Systems:** Optimizing processes without violating critical operational safety parameters.

#### 6.6. Future Directions

The paper acknowledges avenues for future research, including:
- **Adaptive CTG Realignment:** Exploring learned realignment strategies that can dynamically allocate safety margins based on state risk, offering more fine-grained control.
- **Addressing Extreme Data Scarcity:** Integrating B2R with data augmentation or generative modeling techniques to synthesize diverse, safe trajectories in environments where safe data is exceptionally rare.

In conclusion, B2R offers a principled and empirically validated solution to a critical problem in offline safe RL. By fundamentally re-conceptualizing how cost signals are treated, it paves the way for more robust, reliable, and practically deployable AI systems in safety-critical domains.
