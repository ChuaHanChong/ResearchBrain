# PhysMind: From Video to Executable Worlds for Training-Free Physical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysMind introduces a training-free framework that transforms video into an explicit, executable 3D physical world model, enabling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform grounded physical reasoning. The system integrates advanced perception and analytic system identification to model object dynamics and interactions, achieving 72.55% accuracy on CLEVRER and outperforming direct VLM reasoning, particularly for counterfactual tasks, while demonstrating significant cost efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit unreliable physical reasoning from video, struggling with complex dynamics, stability, latent material properties, and future/counterfactual predictions.
- Current methods often lack an explicit, inspectable state-transition model, making their physical predictions opaque and difficult to verify.
- Training-based approaches for physical reasoning are computationally expensive and data-intensive, potentially degrading general VLM capabilities.

## Method

- **PhysMind** converts video into a reusable, question-agnostic executable 3D physical world by integrating state-of-the-art perception tools and an ==analytic continuous-time system identification== process.
- The framework involves ==Dynamic Scene Reconstruction== to recover object identities, geometry, and 6D poses, followed by ==Executable Physical World Modeling== to fit dynamics and physical parameters.
- A VLM acts as an agent, interpreting queries, orchestrating tools (e.g., simulate_edit, check_contact), and formulating answers based on execution artifacts from the generated physical world.

## Results

- PhysMind achieved **72.55%** per-question accuracy on CLEVRER, an improvement of **38.23 points** over direct CoT reasoning with the same VLM backbone and exceeding GPT-5.5 by **5.31 points**.
- The framework demonstrated its most significant advantage in counterfactual reasoning, outperforming same-backbone CoT by **53.95 points** and GPT-5.5 by **19.25 points** on CLEVRER.
- PhysMind exhibited a strong cost-accuracy trade-off, achieving higher accuracy than GPT-5.5 on CLEVRER while utilizing **6.30 times** lower token-derived API cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Converting video into an explicit, executable 3D physical world significantly enhances VLM's physical reasoning, offering substantial gains in tasks like counterfactual reasoning by enabling direct simulation of interventions.
> - Analytic continuous-time system identification, modeling motion with segments and instantaneous collision updates, is critical for efficient and accurate dynamics fitting without relying on costly time-stepped simulator unrolling.
> - The systematic coordination of advanced visual perception (segmentation, 3D reconstruction, 6D pose tracking) with a rigorous physical model provides a robust foundation for grounding VLM reasoning in observed physics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of physical reasoning from video, a task where existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often exhibit limitations. While VLMs have demonstrated proficiency in image, video, and language understanding, their capacity to interpret physical dynamics and reliably reason about future and counterfactual outcomes remains constrained. Current VLM weaknesses include unreliable reasoning regarding stability, collisions, latent material properties, object permanence, and quantitative kinematics. These limitations restrict the deployment of VLMs in critical applications such as robotic manipulation and autonomous driving, where understanding world evolution is paramount.

Historically, approaches to video-based physical reasoning have involved training task-specific models to recover trajectories, learn interaction patterns, or predict future states. More recent advancements have focused on augmenting general-purpose VLMs beyond direct Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. These augmentations fall into two main categories:
1. **Training-based methods:** Supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning to induce physically grounded descriptions and reasoning traces.
2. **Training-free methods:** Supplying inference-time evidence through mechanisms like frame memories, perception tools, or simulators.

Despite this progress, a common characteristic of many existing methods is the absence of an explicit state-transition model for the observed scene. Predictions are often derived from latent features, textual reasoning, or query-specific evidence, rendering the underlying physical states and mechanisms opaque and uninspectable. Training-based approaches further necessitate substantial data and computational resources for adaptation, potentially compromising the general capabilities of VLMs.

PhysMind positions itself within this landscape as a training-free framework that grounds VLM reasoning in an explicit, executable model directly recovered from video. It leverages recent advancements in single-view and multi-view reconstruction, generation, and dynamic-scene recovery to establish a geometric foundation. Complementing this, system identification techniques are employed to estimate latent properties such as mass, friction, restitution, and initial motion.

Compared to other agentic 3D reconstruction efforts that recover dynamic scene representations for interaction, PhysMind distinguishes itself by reconstructing persistent object geometry and 6D motion specifically as observations for a rigorous system identification process, thereby coupling dynamic reconstruction to an executable physical model. Furthermore, in contrast to contact-aware physical system identification methods that optimize within specified physical systems or use black-box parameter search, PhysMind fits continuous-time analytic trajectories and models collisions as instantaneous state transitions, aiming to shorten the optimization path while preserving discontinuous interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of PhysMind is to enhance the reliability of physical reasoning from video by enabling Vision-Language Models to comprehend object movement, interaction, and responses to interventions more effectively. The motivation stems from several identified gaps and challenges:

1. **Overcoming VLM Limitations in Physical Reasoning:** Existing VLMs exhibit difficulty in interpreting complex physical dynamics and reasoning accurately about future and counterfactual scenarios. This deficiency hinders their applicability in domains requiring robust physical understanding.
2. **Developing Inspectable and Executable Models:** Many current approaches produce predictions from latent representations or textual reasoning, making the underlying physical states and mechanisms opaque. PhysMind aims to create an explicit, executable world model recovered from video, allowing for inspection and verification of reasoning processes. This promotes transparency and trust in AI systems.
3. **Implementing a Training-Free Approach:** The computational cost and data requirements associated with training or fine-tuning VLMs for specific physical reasoning tasks are substantial. A training-free methodology is motivated by the desire to avoid these resource demands and prevent potential degradation of general VLM capabilities.
4. **Enabling Reusability and Efficiency:** The framework is designed to construct a single, question-agnostic executable world for each video. This world can then be reused across various questions (explanatory, predictive, counterfactual), promoting efficiency and consistency by avoiding repeated reconstruction and dynamics fitting.
5. **Improving Accuracy on Complex Reasoning Tasks:** A key motivation is to achieve higher accuracy, particularly for counterfactual reasoning, which demands the ability to simulate hypothetical situations and their outcomes based on modified initial conditions or scene parameters.
6. **Orchestrating Diverse Tools into a Consistent Framework:** The challenge lies in coordinating various advanced tools—including perception, reconstruction, tracking, and dynamics estimation—into a unified, consistent executable world that can be leveraged by a VLM.
7. **Advancing System Identification:** To fit analytic continuous-time dynamics and latent physical parameters efficiently, PhysMind seeks to develop an approach that does not rely on unrolling a time-stepped simulator, thereby shortening the optimization path while accurately representing discontinuous interactions.

In essence, PhysMind is motivated by the need for transparent, accurate, efficient, and reusable physical reasoning capabilities derived directly from video, addressing a critical bottleneck in the advancement of embodied AI and real-world intelligent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysMind operates as a training-free agentic framework that systematically converts video data into a reusable executable physical world. This process is structured into two sequential stages: Dynamic Scene Reconstruction and Executable Physical World Modeling. A Vision-Language Model (VLM) serves as the agent, interpreting scene conditions, selecting tools, orchestrating operations, and formulating answers based on execution artifacts.

**Problem Formulation:**
PhysMind formulates the problem as a sequence of transformations:
1. **Dynamic Scene Recovery:** `S = F_scene(V)` – A dynamic scene `S` is recovered from an input video `V`. `S` comprises a shared camera model, gravity, support geometry, and persistent objects `O`. Each object includes an anonymous identity, semantic descriptor, conditioned mesh `M_i`, active interval, and 6D pose trajectory `X_i(t)`.
2. **Executable World Modeling:** `W = F_id(S)` – An executable world `W` is constructed by adding initial states and physical parameters `Θ` to the dynamic scene `S`.
3. **Query-Conditioned Execution:** `R_q = F_exec(W, q)` – A query-specific execution record `R_q` is generated by executing the world `W` in response to a question `q`.
4. **Artifact-Grounded Answering:** `ˆa = F_ans(V, q, W, R_q)` – The final answer `ˆa` is predicted from the video `V`, question `q`, world `W`, and execution record `R_q`.

A key aspect is that only `F_exec` and `F_ans` receive the question `q`, ensuring that `W` is a question-agnostic model reusable across various query types.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Scene Reconstruction

This stage aims to estimate persistent object identities, canonical geometry, and time-varying 6D poses within a shared 3D coordinate system. The VLM infers semantics and selects visually supported constraints, while specialized tools handle geometric recovery.

- **Object Segmentation and Tracking:**
 - SAM 3 (Carion et al. 2025) is applied with a broad object prompt to generate anonymous mask tracks.
 - For each track, a representative frame is selected, prioritizing isolated and larger visible mask regions.
 - A VLM assigns semantic attributes (color, material, geometry, appearance) to these anonymous identities, guiding mesh conditioning and language grounding.
 - Visible and fully observed portions of the tracks define active and pose-estimation intervals.
- **Geometry-Aware Object Reconstruction:**
 - MoGe-2 (Wang et al. 2025c) estimates camera intrinsics from sampled frames, aggregated into a fixed matrix `K`.
 - Video Depth Anything (Chen et al. 2025b) provides a temporally consistent depth sequence.
 - At each object's representative frame, the masked pixel information is back-projected using `K` and depth to form a point map.
 - SAM 3D Objects (Chen et al. 2025c) then reconstructs a colored canonical mesh, conditioned by the VLM-inferred object type (e.g., primitives for regular shapes, simplified meshes for irregular ones).
 - Visibility-aware rendering refines scale and translation of the mesh against the mask. The local frame is recentered at the mesh bounds for tracking and contact.
- **Spatiotemporal Pose Recovery and Alignment:**
 - FoundationPose (Wen et al. 2024) registers each conditioned mesh to RGB, depth, mask, and intrinsics, tracking the object in both temporal directions to form preliminary 6D pose trajectories.
 - The agent VLM determines if pose correction is necessary and selects visually supported constraints (e.g., shared-surface motion, stable camera roll, support geometry).
 - The default correction path aggregates up-direction estimates from GeoCalib (Veicht et al. 2024). When shared-surface motion is detected, support-constrained alignment is invoked. Roll constraints are applied only when supported.
 - Corrections are symmetry-aware (e.g., sphere rotation ignored, cylinder axes aligned with gravity). Positions are adjusted along camera rays to restore contact while preserving visible surface depth. This yields refined trajectories `{X_i(t)}` and support geometry `Π`.

#### 3.2. Executable Physical World Modeling

This stage converts the dynamic scene `S` into an executable 3D world `W` by fitting continuous-time analytic dynamics to the recovered trajectories and meshes.

- **Analytic Continuous-Time System Identification:**
 - The dynamic state `z(t)` of active objects and physical parameters `Θ` (initial velocities, ground friction, pairwise mass ratios, restitution) are defined.
 - Motion is modeled as analytic segments (`Φ_k`) separated by instantaneous collision updates (`∆_k+1`).
 - Candidate collision intervals are initialized by mesh proximity and refined from trajectories.
 - Motion modes (free flight, supported motion) are evaluated in closed form: ballistic solution for free flight, exact Coulomb friction for supported planar motion with explicit stopping-time handling.
 - Normal collisions apply an impulse (Eq. 4) based on restitution and velocities.
 - Contact-connected objects are optimized jointly, identifying mass ratios. New contacts leverage semantic priors and parameters from similar objects.
 - The identification problem minimizes a loss function (Eq. 5) that considers position, rotation, residual separation at contacts, and a soft-contact prior, while enforcing physical bounds.
 - Optimization uses cumulative temporal prefixes, fitting short trajectory segments first, then progressively introducing later collisions and extending the horizon. This avoids costly time-stepped unrolling.
- **Query-Conditioned Physical Rollout:**
 - The VLM interprets the question `q` after `W` is constructed, grounding linguistic references to object identities.
 - It then composes operations using a general world interface.
 - **Explanatory queries** involve inspecting states and contacts.
 - **Predictive queries** involve continuing the fitted dynamics beyond the observed video.
 - **Counterfactual queries** involve cloning, editing (e.g., removing an object), and comparing worlds to simulate interventions.
 - Execution typically extends 20% beyond the observed sequence. The base world is reused across multiple questions.
 - The process generates `R_q`, a structured record of trajectories, contacts, state changes, and interventions.
 - Seven schema-validated tools are exposed for VLM interaction with the world model (e.g., `simulate_edit`, `check_contact`, `compare_rollouts`).
- **Artifact-Grounded Answering:**
 - The answering VLM receives the video, question, and compact summaries of `W` and `R_q`.
 - It uses this evidence to map outcomes to the answer space, with semantic decisions remaining with the VLM and physical evidence provided by the generated artifacts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhysMind was evaluated on two benchmarks, CLEVRER and Physion++, against a range of foundation VLMs and specialized physical reasoning methods.

**Results on CLEVRER (Validation Subset):**
- **Overall Accuracy:** PhysMind achieved the highest overall accuracy, with 72.55% per-question accuracy and 87.22% per-option accuracy.
- **Improvement over Direct CoT (same VLM backbone):** PhysMind demonstrated substantial gains, improving overall accuracy by 38.23 points per question and 22.25 points per option compared to direct Chain-of-Thought reasoning with the same Gemini-3-Flash VLM.
- **Comparison to Strongest VLM Baseline (GPT-5.5):** PhysMind exceeded GPT-5.5 in overall accuracy by 5.31 points per question and 3.56 points per option.
- **Category-Specific Performance:**
 - **Counterfactual Reasoning:** PhysMind showed its most significant advantage, surpassing same-backbone CoT by 53.95 points per question and GPT-5.5 by 19.25 points. This suggests the executable world model is particularly effective for evaluating hypothetical scenarios.
 - **Explanatory Reasoning:** PhysMind performed comparably to GPT-5.5, with a minor gap of 0.24 points.
 - **Predictive Reasoning:** PhysMind trailed GPT-5.5 by 18.75 points. The paper posits this is because predictive tasks can often leverage motion already visible in the video, whereas counterfactuals require simulating unobserved interactions, where PhysMind's explicit execution capability provides a clearer advantage.

**Results on Physion++:**
- **Overall Accuracy:** PhysMind achieved 59.64% overall per-scene accuracy.
- **Improvement over Direct CoT (same VLM backbone):** PhysMind improved upon same-backbone CoT by 8.08 points.
- **Comparison to GPT-5.5:** PhysMind exceeded GPT-5.5 by 1.57 points in overall per-scene accuracy.
- **Category-Specific Performance:** Gains were concentrated on friction collision (+17.18 points) and mass collision (+10.93 points) over same-backbone CoT, indicating the effectiveness of executable collision modeling for pairwise interactions. Accuracy on friction-platform tasks decreased slightly (-1.56 points) relative to CoT, possibly due to direct observational evidence for platform friction. PhysMind also exceeded GPT-5.5 in four out of five categories, with gains ranging from 1.05 to 3.13 points, while trailing in friction collision by 1.56 points.
- **Matched-Pair Accuracy:** PhysMind led with 27.09% per-pair accuracy, exceeding GPT-5.5 by 4.69 points, suggesting improved discrimination between property-conditioned outcomes.

**Cost–Accuracy Trade-off:**
- On CLEVRER, PhysMind demonstrated a strong cost-accuracy trade-off. While improving overall accuracy over GPT-5.5 by 5.31 points, its token-derived API cost was 6.30 times lower. Compared to Gemini-3.1-Pro, PhysMind improved accuracy by 27.08 points while reducing cost by 24.7%.

**Ablation Studies (on 100 CLEVRER scenes):**
- **Analytic Continuous-Time System Identification:** Replacing this core component with fixed-step numerical integration resulted in the largest overall accuracy drop (42.30 points, from 73.10% to 30.80%), indicating its critical role, especially for explanatory and counterfactual reasoning.
- **Pose Correction:** Removing pose correction led to a 25.05-point decrease in overall accuracy, uniformly impacting all query types (23.87–26.48 point gains), underscoring the importance of temporally aligned geometry.
- **Executable Rollout:** Answering based only on trajectories (without execution) significantly reduced counterfactual accuracy (by 55.67 points), while slightly increasing predictive accuracy. This confirms that executable rollout is essential for evaluating interventions.
- **Agent-VLM Backbone:** PhysMind showed consistent overall accuracy gains over direct CoT (37.7 points with Gemini-3-Flash, 33.3 points with GPT-4o, 26.9 points with Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B), indicating the pipeline's generalizability across different VLM backbones.

**Error Analysis:**
- A manual analysis of errors on 100 CLEVRER scenes attributed 92.31% of failures to world-construction categories. The leading causes were perception and pose reconstruction errors (31.62%), followed by observed-dynamics fitting errors (28.21%), systematic trajectory precision limits (19.66%), and fallback due to limited visibility (12.82%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysMind represents a development in physical reasoning capabilities for Vision-Language Models, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Physical Reasoning for VLMs:** The framework addresses a known limitation of existing VLMs in understanding and reasoning about physical interactions, particularly for complex predictive and counterfactual scenarios. By converting video into an explicit executable world, PhysMind enables VLMs to move beyond superficial pattern recognition to a more grounded understanding of dynamics.

2. **Training-Free and Reusable Approach:** By being training-free and constructing a single, reusable, question-agnostic executable world per video, PhysMind offers an efficient and scalable solution. This reduces computational overhead associated with task-specific training and preserves the general capabilities of underlying VLMs, making it more practical for diverse applications.

3. **Inspectable and Transparent Reasoning:** Unlike black-box VLM approaches, PhysMind generates explicit physical models and execution artifacts. This transparency allows for inspection of the physical states, interactions, and mechanisms leading to an answer, which can increase trust, interpretability, and debuggability in AI systems.

4. **Novel Integration of Vision and Physics:** The approach effectively integrates state-of-the-art vision modules (for segmentation, 3D reconstruction, and 6D pose tracking) with a sophisticated analytic continuous-time system identification method. This systematic coordination is a step towards comprehensive visual-physical world understanding.

5. **Efficiency in Dynamics Fitting:** The use of analytic continuous-time dynamics and instantaneous collision updates, rather than time-stepped simulation unrolling, offers a more computationally efficient method for fitting long-horizon dynamics and handling discontinuities. This can lead to faster model construction and execution.

6. **Economic Advantage:** The demonstrated superior accuracy for complex reasoning types, especially counterfactuals, combined with a significantly lower API cost compared to leading foundation VLMs, highlights PhysMind's economic efficiency. This makes advanced physical reasoning more accessible for practical deployment.

7. **Potential for Real-World Applications:** Improved physical reasoning is fundamental for intelligent agents operating in dynamic environments. PhysMind's capabilities could significantly impact:
 - **Robotics:** Enabling robots to better predict the outcomes of their actions, understand object stability, and perform more sophisticated manipulation tasks.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Contributing to more reliable prediction of other agents' movements, anticipation of collisions, and informed decision-making in complex traffic scenarios.
 - **Simulated Environments:** Providing a robust method for creating executable digital twins of observed physical processes, useful for testing and training other AI systems.

Overall, PhysMind contributes to the field by providing a robust, interpretable, and efficient framework for physical reasoning from video, bridging the gap between perception and actionable physical understanding for AI systems.
