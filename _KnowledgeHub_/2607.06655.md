# Pelican-VLA 0.5: Attending Before Acting Benefits Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> Pelican-VLA 0.5 introduces a unified Vision-Language-Action model that develops manipulation-centric attention during pre-training through learnable Bottleneck Tokens, enhancing generalization by focusing on task-relevant visual information. The work identifies a "representation-to-action gap," where this strong attentional grounding requires further action-level fine-tuning for precise execution in novel scenarios.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limited generalization across novel objects, scenes, tasks, and robot embodiments, often requiring extensive task-specific data and fine-tuning.
- Existing VLA models' action pathways often display diffuse attention, spreading over task-irrelevant visual information (e.g., robot arm, background clutter) rather than focusing on manipulation-relevant regions.
- Achieving reliable zero-shot manipulation in unfamiliar environments remains a significant challenge for embodied intelligence systems.

## Method

- **Pelican-VLA 0.5** integrates vision-language understanding, future-frame generation, and action prediction into a single Transformer architecture, using a shared Qwen3-VL backbone.
- It introduces ==Bottleneck Tokens (BotTokens)== as a compact, learnable interface between dense perceptual input and the action pathway, enforced by a ==curriculum bottleneck mask== and an ==orthogonality regularizer==.
- The model is trained with a multi-task objective that includes ==flow-matching action loss==, ==future-frame generation loss==, and a ==BotTokens-language contrastive alignment== objective (InfoNCE) to promote task-relevant attention.

## Results

- Pelican-VLA 0.5 achieved the highest average success rate among compared open-source VLA baselines on the RoboTwin benchmark, with **91.4%** on Clean and **91.0%** on Randomized settings after fine-tuning.
- In strict zero-shot settings on RoboTwin 2.0 (unseen scenes, objects, and robot embodiments), the pre-trained policy demonstrated nascent manipulation behaviors, often approaching correct objects and producing coherent motions.
- When fine-tuned on teleoperated data, the model successfully deployed on a real-world TienKung humanoid, achieving an **80%** success rate on a tabletop-cleanup task by selecting, grasping, and placing objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Manipulation-centric attention can emerge during pre-training, allowing the model's action pathway to intrinsically focus on task-relevant objects and contact regions without explicit supervision.
> - Learnable Bottleneck Tokens (BotTokens) are critical for inducing this focused attention by acting as a constrained information bottleneck, routing task-relevant visual information to the action pathway.
> - A "representation-to-action gap" exists: while the model can identify what to attend to (attention-level generalization), translating this into reliable, precise action execution in strictly zero-shot novel scenarios requires additional action supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a central effort within embodied intelligence, aiming to enable robots to interpret free-form language instructions and manipulate objects effectively in open, unstructured environments. These models leverage large-scale multimodal pre-training to learn end-to-end mappings from visual observations and language instructions to robot actions.

Despite progress, a significant challenge for VLA models remains generalization. Current approaches often rely on extensive task- and environment-specific robot data, typically requiring additional data collection and fine-tuning when adapting to new objects, scenes, tasks, or robot embodiments. Reliable zero-shot manipulation in unfamiliar settings has largely remained an elusive goal.

Recent research has suggested that VLA generalization may not solely depend on the ability to fit low-level action trajectories. Instead, the manner in which task-relevant information is represented and effectively routed to the action-generation pathway is increasingly viewed as crucial. Analyses of existing VLA models reveal that their action pathways often exhibit diffuse attention, spreading over the robot arm, background clutter, and task-irrelevant objects. This diffuse attention pattern, rather than a focused concentration on manipulation-relevant target regions or potential contact areas, may contribute to their limited generalization by allowing reliance on visual shortcuts or environment-specific correlations.

This work positions itself within this landscape by specifically addressing the issue of diffuse attention in VLA models. It investigates whether models can inherently form more focused, manipulation-relevant attentional representations during pre-training, and if such representations can serve as a foundation for stronger generalization across novel scenarios. By focusing on "attention-level generalization" as a prerequisite for robust action-level generalization, this research contributes to understanding and mitigating one of the core limitations of current VLA systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to advance the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) by developing robotic models capable of improved generalization across diverse objects, scenes, tasks, and robot embodiments. The motivation stems from a recognized limitation in current VLA models: their heavy dependence on task- and environment-specific robot data, which necessitates substantial fine-tuning for new scenarios and hinders true zero-shot generalization.

A detailed analysis of existing VLA models revealed that their action pathways tend to distribute attention diffusely across the visual input, including the robot arm, background, and task-irrelevant objects. This pattern contrasts with a focused concentration on manipulation-relevant target regions or potential contact areas, which the authors hypothesize is beneficial for robust generalization. This diffuse attention might cause models to rely on superficial visual correlations rather than understanding the core elements pertinent to a manipulation task.

Motivated by this observation, the key objectives of this work are:

- **To develop a unified VLA model, Pelican-VLA 0.5, that integrates vision-language understanding, future-frame generation, and action prediction within a single architectural framework.** This aims to create a cohesive system where different aspects of robot intelligence can interact effectively.
- **To achieve "attention-level generalization" during pre-training without explicit supervision.** Specifically, the objective is for the model's action pathway to intrinsically focus on manipulation-relevant objects and contact regions. This capability should emerge without the need for object annotations, segmentation masks, attention supervision, or task-specific fine-tuning, and should persist across unseen scenes and robot embodiments.
- **To identify and verify the architectural mechanism responsible for inducing this manipulation-centric attention.** The research investigates whether a compact, learnable interface between perception and action, termed "Bottleneck Tokens" (BotTokens), can effectively route task-relevant visual information, thereby fostering focused attention patterns.
- **To evaluate the impact of this pre-formed, manipulation-centric attention on downstream task performance.** This includes assessing the model's performance on benchmark tasks after fine-tuning and exploring its capabilities in zero-shot settings involving unseen scenes, objects, and robot embodiments.
- **To clarify the distinction and potential gap between zero-shot representation (attention) and zero-shot control (action).** The research aims to understand if the ability to identify "what to attend to" directly translates into reliable execution in novel environments, or if a further "representation-to-action gap" remains.

Ultimately, the motivation is to address the fundamental challenge of generalization in embodied intelligence by building VLA models that can form robust and transferable internal representations of manipulation tasks, thereby reducing the reliance on extensive task-specific data collection and fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Pelican-VLA 0.5, a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed to integrate visual-language understanding, future-frame generation, and action prediction within a single Transformer architecture. The model is built upon a shared Qwen3-VL 4B backbone.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Input Sequence

The input sequence to Pelican-VLA 0.5 is structured into four contiguous segments:
- **Prefix:** Encodes current camera observations and language instructions using the Qwen3-VL vision encoder. Visual patch embeddings are inserted into the text-token sequence. This segment uses bidirectional attention to enable mutual contextualization between language and visual tokens.
- **Middle:** Incorporates a short visual history (two frames at t-15 and t) encoded by a frozen Cosmos-Tokenizer. This segment provides pixel-level and dynamics-aware information, complementing the semantic patch tokens. It attends bidirectionally to itself and the prefix.
- **Bottleneck (BotTokens):** Consists of K (default 32) learnable tokens, initialized from a normal distribution. These BotTokens serve as a fixed-capacity interface between the dense perceptual information and the action pathway.
- **Suffix:** Contains the robot's proprioceptive state (projected into a single token) and a noisy action chunk, which is embedded and fused with a sinusoidal embedding of the flow time. This segment comprises H (default 50) action tokens.

#### 3.2. Attention Geometry

The prefix, middle, and BotTokens segments employ internal bidirectional attention. The suffix uses causal or block-causal attention relative to its visible context. A key architectural design is a bottleneck attention mask: when enabled, the suffix (action pathway) can only attend to upstream perception tokens indirectly, through the BotTokens. This prevents direct attention from suffix tokens to the dense visual tokens of the prefix and middle segments.

#### 3.3. Bottleneck Tokens Mechanisms

To ensure the BotTokens function as an effective information bottleneck and a task-reasoning workspace, three mechanisms are implemented:
- **Curriculum Bottleneck Mask:** A hard bottleneck constraint (masking all direct suffix-to-perception attention edges) is applied probabilistically during training. The probability increases from 0 to 1 over a warm-up period (T_warm = 10k steps), allowing the model to initially learn with an easier, dense connection before gradually being forced to route perception through the BotTokens.
- **Orthogonality Regularizer (L_reg):** This regularizer encourages diversity among the BotTokens. It penalizes deviations of the BotTokens' Gram matrix from an identity matrix, promoting distinct information capture by each token and preventing them from collapsing into similar directions.
- **Bottleneck Tokens Gating of Generation:** The mean-pooled output of the BotTokens is used to compute a channel-wise gate that modulates the future-frame generation features. This mechanism ties the BotTokens representation to forward-predictive, action-relevant structures, encouraging them to encode information useful for predicting future visual changes in addition to action.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

Pelican-VLA 0.5 is trained with a multi-task objective:
- **Flow-matching action loss (L_action):** Uses conditional flow matching to predict the velocity field for action chunks, enabling denoising from Gaussian noise to a clean target action.
- **Future-frame generation loss (L_gen):** The middle outputs, modulated by BotTokens gating, are decoded into Cosmos latent space and supervised against future frame latents (I_t+15), providing a compact dynamics signal.
- **BotTokens-language contrastive alignment (L_task):** A symmetric InfoNCE objective aligns the trajectory embeddings (mean-pooled BotTokens) with language instruction embeddings. This encourages the BotTokens to capture information semantically aligned with the given task description.
- **Total Objective:** The final loss is a weighted sum of these components: L = L_action + λ_gen * L_gen + λ_task * L_task + λ_reg * L_reg.

#### 3.5. Training Data and Implementation

The model is pre-trained on a heterogeneous, cross-embodiment mixture of large-scale manipulation corpora, totaling over 6000 hours, including AgiBot World Alpha, InternData-A1, Galaxea Open-World Dataset, and self-collected teleoperation data. All data is unified using the LeRobot interface, with state and action vectors padded to a common 32-dimensional format. The model is trained using AdamW optimizer with a cosine decay learning rate schedule and specific hyperparameters (e.g., peak learning rate of 5e-5, 2k warmup steps). The current model uses approximately 2400 hours of this data (0.4 epochs).

#### 3.6. Cached Inference

During inference, BotTokens enable an efficient caching mechanism. Prefix and middle tokens are processed once and cached. For the iterative action denoising loop, only the compact BotTokens key-value pairs are retained as visual context, discarding the larger prefix and middle caches. This allows repeated denoising steps to depend only on the suffix length and number of BotTokens, supporting high-frequency control.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Methodology

The research employs a multi-faceted evaluation approach:
- **Qualitative Attention Visualization:** Visualizing attention maps of the action pathway on visual tokens to inspect focus.
- **Quantitative Grounding Analysis:** IoU-based metrics to measure attention alignment with manipulator-related regions and target objects.
- **Simulation Benchmarking:** Evaluation on the RoboTwin benchmark for seen tasks (Clean and Randomized settings).
- **Zero-shot Generalization Test:** Deployment on RoboTwin 2.0 (held-out scenes, objects, and robot embodiments) without fine-tuning.
- **Real-world Robot Evaluation:** Fine-tuning and evaluation on a tabletop-cleanup task using the TienKung humanoid.
- **Ablation Studies:** Controlled experiments to isolate the impact of the BotTokens and auxiliary losses by systematically varying architectural components and training objectives.
- **Attention Dynamics Analysis:** Tracking attention patterns across pre-training checkpoints.
- **Zero-shot vs. Fine-tuned Representation Comparison:** Analyzing attention maps and BotTokens representation similarity before and after fine-tuning to understand how adaptation affects internal representations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the generalization capabilities of Pelican-VLA 0.5:

#### 4.1. Attention-Level Generalization During Pre-training

A core finding is the emergence of manipulation-centric attention in Pelican-VLA 0.5's action pathway during pre-training. Without explicit object annotations, segmentation masks, attention supervision, or task-specific fine-tuning, the model's attention consistently focuses on manipulation-relevant objects and contact regions. This behavior was observed to be stronger and more concentrated than in other open-source VLA baselines and persisted across unseen scenes and robot embodiments (Figure 1).

#### 4.2. Role of Bottleneck Tokens (BotTokens)

Ablation studies and controlled comparisons indicated that this manipulation-centric attention primarily originates from the learnable BotTokens. These tokens, inserted as a compact interface between perception and action, appear to induce the focused attention patterns by routing task-relevant visual information through a constrained bottleneck. This effect was found to be consistent across different policy structures, including a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)-style architecture, suggesting its robustness and transferability (Figures 5, 6, 8). The orthogonality regularizer was identified as crucial for preventing BotTokens from collapsing and ensuring diverse information capture.

#### 4.3. Language-Conditioned Attention

The BotTokens-language contrastive alignment objective (L_task) was observed to enhance language-conditioned behavior in earlier training stages. For identical visual scenes, changing the language instruction led to a corresponding shift in attention to the newly specified target object (Figure 7). This suggested that while BotTokens induce attentional focus, the contrastive loss helps align this focus with language instructions. However, the stability of this language conditioning diminished in later training stages, attributed by the authors to potential noise and mismatch in the training data's language-action alignment.

#### 4.4. Simulation Performance and Robustness

After fine-tuning on the RoboTwin benchmark, Pelican-VLA 0.5 achieved a success rate of 91.4% on RoboTwin Clean and 91.0% on RoboTwin Randomized. This performance represented the highest average success rate among compared open-source VLA baselines (Table 1). The minimal performance gap (0.4 points) between the clean and randomized settings indicated the policy's robustness to visual variations, consistent with the hypothesis that routing perception through BotTokens encourages encoding task-relevant, transferable structure over low-level visual shortcuts.

#### 4.5. Early Zero-Shot Manipulation Capabilities

In strict zero-shot settings on RoboTwin 2.0 (featuring unseen scenes, objects, and robot embodiments not present during pre-training), Pelican-VLA 0.5 demonstrated nascent manipulation behavior. The pre-trained policy often approached the correct manipulation-relevant objects and produced coherent, goal-directed motions, such as picking up bottles or activating switches (Figure 3). However, the absolute success rates in this strict zero-shot regime remained low, with failures typically occurring at fine-grained stages of manipulation, such as stable grasping or precise placement, rather than initial target selection or approach.

#### 4.6. Representation-to-Action Gap

A comparison of attention maps before and after fine-tuning revealed that the manipulation-centric attention patterns remained highly similar, changing only marginally (Figure 10, Table 3). This suggested that fine-tuning primarily strengthens the mapping from these pre-formed, grounded attention regions to executable actions, rather than creating the foundational manipulation-centric attention from scratch. This observation highlighted a "representation-to-action gap": the model could largely identify what to attend to, but reliably executing actions based on that representation across new scenarios still required additional action supervision.

#### 4.7. Real-World Robot Evaluation

Pelican-VLA 0.5 was fine-tuned on teleoperated demonstrations and deployed on a real-world tabletop-cleanup task using the TienKung humanoid. The model achieved an 80% success rate, consistently selecting objects, executing stable grasps, and placing them into a target container (Figure 4). This demonstrated the model's ability to adapt its pre-trained representation for effective real-world manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research conducted on Pelican-VLA 0.5 contributes to the field of robotic learning by offering a perspective on achieving generalization in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The primary significance lies in the identification and demonstration of "attention-level generalization" as a distinct and early capability that can emerge during pre-training. This suggests that teaching a robot *what* to attend to for manipulation tasks can be separated from, and precede, teaching it *how* to act with precision.

The introduction of **learnable Bottleneck Tokens (BotTokens)** as a compact, constrained interface between perception and action represents a notable architectural contribution. The consistent ability of BotTokens to induce manipulation-centric attention across different VLA architectures, even without explicit supervision, implies a potentially transferable mechanism for improving how VLA models process and prioritize visual information. This could lead to more robust and less data-intensive approaches for perception-action grounding in robotics.

The research also provides clarity on the challenges of generalization by delineating the **"representation-to-action gap."** While Pelican-VLA 0.5 demonstrates strong attention-level generalization, its performance in strict zero-shot manipulation remains limited at fine-grained control stages. This distinction highlights that simply identifying the correct target and contact area (representation) does not automatically translate into reliable, precise execution (action) in entirely novel scenarios. This finding offers a roadmap for future research, suggesting that efforts should focus not only on improving perceptual grounding but also on scaling action experience and refining action representations to bridge this identified gap.

The proposed limitations, such as the scale and potential noise of the current 2400 hours of training data, and the use of embodiment-specific joint-position actions, are acknowledged as factors contributing to the remaining representation-to-action gap. These acknowledgments provide concrete directions for subsequent work, including the development of larger, higher-quality datasets and the exploration of more generalizable action representations (e.g., end-effector poses).

From a practical standpoint, Pelican-VLA 0.5's performance on the RoboTwin benchmark and its successful deployment on a real-world humanoid robot for tabletop cleanup suggest progress towards more capable and adaptable robotic systems. The ability to form robust manipulation-centric attention during pre-training could reduce the need for extensive task-specific data collection and fine-tuning, potentially accelerating the deployment of robots in diverse, unstructured environments.

Finally, the stated intention to release the code, model weights, and attention-visualization tools contributes to open science, fostering reproducibility and providing resources for the broader research community to build upon these findings and further investigate the mechanisms of VLA generalization.
