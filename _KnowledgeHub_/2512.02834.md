# Steering Vision-Language-Action Models as Anti-Exploration: A Test-Time Scaling Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> The TACO framework enhances the inference stability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by employing a test-time anti-exploration strategy that uses a lightweight pseudo-count estimator to filter out suboptimal action candidates. This method yields an average success rate improvement of 9.1% on RoboTwin 1.0 and 16% on a real-world robot platform, while reducing multi-sampling inference time by 73.2% through KV cache optimization.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly generative ones, exhibit significant inference-time fragility and instability after supervised fine-tuning.
- Success rates of VLA models can vary drastically based solely on initial noise sampling, indicating the policy retains "redundant action modes" that are suboptimal or irrelevant for a given task.
- Existing fine-tuning methods do not effectively constrain policies to stable, successful action distributions, leading to out-of-distribution behaviors during deployment.

## Method

- **TACO** (Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-COunts) implements a two-stage ==generate-then-verify== process during inference.
- The VLA generates multiple candidate action sequences, and a lightweight ==Coin Flipping Network (CFN)==, trained as a ==pseudo-count estimator== on successful demonstrations, scores each candidate.
- The system selects the action chunk with the highest pseudo-count, effectively steering the policy towards high-density, successful regions of the action space, enabled by ==KV cache== optimization for efficient parallel generation.

## Results

- Achieved consistent improvements in average success rates across diverse simulation benchmarks, including a **9.1%** increase on RoboTwin 1.0 and **7.5%** on Simpler-WindowX.
- Demonstrated a **16%** average success rate increase on a real-world dual-arm robot platform across five complex manipulation tasks.
- Reduced inference latency for parallel action sampling by **73.2%** through KV cache optimization, making multi-candidate verification computationally practical for real-time robotic control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA inference instability can be formally characterized as an out-of-distribution problem, where policies sample from undesired, low-density action modes.
> - Anti-exploration principles from offline Reinforcement Learning can be effectively adapted to generative VLA models by selecting actions with maximum visitation density (pseudo-counts) from successful demonstrations.
> - Leveraging the VLA's own internal representations, combined with a high-fidelity feature search, allows for an efficient and accurate pseudo-count estimator without modifying the VLA's parameters or requiring complex RL training.
