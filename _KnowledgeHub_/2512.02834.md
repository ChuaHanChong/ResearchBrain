# Steering Vision-Language-Action Models as Anti-Exploration: A Test-Time Scaling Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> The TACO framework enhances the inference stability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by employing a test-time anti-exploration strategy that uses a lightweight pseudo-count estimator to filter out suboptimal action candidates. This method yields an average success rate improvement of 9.1% on RoboTwin 1.0 and 16% on a real-world robot platform, while reducing multi-sampling inference time by 73.2% through KV cache optimization.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly generative ones, exhibit significant inference-time fragility and instability after supervised fine-tuning.
- Success rates of VLA models can vary drastically based solely on initial noise sampling, indicating the policy retains "redundant action modes" that are suboptimal or irrelevant for a given task.
- Existing fine-tuning methods do not effectively constrain policies to stable, successful action distributions, leading to out-of-distribution behaviors during deployment.

## Method

- **TACO** (Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-COunts) implements a two-stage ==generate-then-verify== process during inference.
- The VLA generates multiple candidate action sequences, and a lightweight ==Coin Flipping Network (CFN)==, trained as a ==pseudo-count estimator== on successful demonstrations, scores each candidate.
- The system selects the action chunk with the highest pseudo-count, effectively steering the policy towards high-density, successful regions of the action space, enabled by ==KV cache== optimization for efficient parallel generation.

## Results

- Achieved consistent improvements in average success rates across diverse simulation benchmarks, including a **9.1%** increase on RoboTwin 1.0 and **7.5%** on Simpler-WindowX.
- Demonstrated a **16%** average success rate increase on a real-world dual-arm robot platform across five complex manipulation tasks.
- Reduced inference latency for parallel action sampling by **73.2%** through KV cache optimization, making multi-candidate verification computationally practical for real-time robotic control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA inference instability can be formally characterized as an out-of-distribution problem, where policies sample from undesired, low-density action modes.
> - Anti-exploration principles from offline Reinforcement Learning can be effectively adapted to generative VLA models by selecting actions with maximum visitation density (pseudo-counts) from successful demonstrations.
> - Leveraging the VLA's own internal representations, combined with a high-fidelity feature search, allows for an efficient and accurate pseudo-count estimator without modifying the VLA's parameters or requiring complex RL training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the cutting edge of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for **robotic manipulation**, a rapidly evolving field aiming to create general-purpose robots capable of understanding and executing complex tasks from multimodal (vision, language) inputs.

**Evolution of VLA Models:** The paper acknowledges the progression of VLA models, from early works like RT-1 and Octo that trained policies from scratch, to later models like RT-2 and OpenVLA that leveraged pre-trained vision-language model (VLM) backbones. More recently, the field has seen a shift towards generative policies, particularly those based on **diffusion models** (e.g., RDT-1B, Dita) and **flow-matching models** (e.g., $\pi_0$, GR00T N1, SmolVLA). These generative approaches excel at capturing the multimodality and precision required for complex action sequences in large-scale datasets. This paper specifically targets these advanced generative VLA architectures.

**Identified Gap: Inference-Time Instability and Distribution Shift:** While VLA models exhibit strong generalization, this paper pinpoints a critical, often overlooked vulnerability: their **inference-time fragility and instability**, especially after supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on task-specific datasets. The authors illustrate this with drastic variations in success rates based solely on initial noise sampling. This is framed as a **distribution shift problem**, where the broad, multimodal policy learned during pre-training and fine-tuning retains "redundant action modes" that are irrelevant or suboptimal for the target downstream task. This issue is a significant hurdle for reliable deployment of VLA models in real-world scenarios.

**Connection to Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Anti-Exploration:** The paper draws a direct conceptual parallel between this VLA instability and the core challenge of **offline RL**: preventing a policy from generating actions or visiting states that are "out-of-support" relative to the fixed training dataset. It leverages the principle of **anti-exploration**, a technique from offline RL that constrains policies to stay within the reliable data distribution. This connection provides a theoretical grounding for their proposed solution, framing the problem not just as a practical bug but as a known challenge in data-driven policy learning.

**Novelty in Test-Time Scaling (TTS):** The paper positions its solution, TACO, as a form of **test-time scaling (TTS)**. While TTS has been explored in other domains, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), this work extends it to VLA models. The key innovation lies in using a *lightweight pseudo-count estimator* as a high-fidelity verifier, applied *only during inference*, without modifying the VLA's parameters or requiring complex RL updates (which are difficult for flow/diffusion models). This approach differentiates it from prior works that might use larger verifiers, require complex training, or rely on explicit reasoning traces. The emphasis on efficiency (KV cache optimization) makes it a practical solution for robotics.

In summary, this research addresses a critical, underexplored problem in state-of-the-art VLA models, proposing an elegant, theoretically-backed, and computationally efficient solution that bridges concepts from generative modeling, offline RL, and test-time inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from an observed **critical inference-time fragility** in state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly those trained with flow-matching or diffusion objectives, even after supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for specific downstream tasks. The authors demonstrate that the success rates of these models can vary drastically (e.g., from 0% to 80%) simply by sampling different initial noise vectors during inference for the same task.

The key objectives and underlying motivations are:

1. **Diagnose the Root Cause of Inference Instability:** The authors aim to understand *why* VLA models exhibit this instability. They attribute it to a **distributional shift** problem. Specifically:
 - VLA models, pre-trained on diverse, large-scale, multi-modal datasets, learn a broad spectrum of action modes.
 - Even after SFT on task-specific datasets, the policy often retains "redundant action modes" that are irrelevant or suboptimal for the desired task success.
 - SFT datasets themselves can be multimodal, containing demonstrations of suboptimal or undesirable strategies, further exacerbating the issue.
 - When sampling actions (especially in stochastic generative models like flow-matching or diffusion), the policy can inadvertently generate actions from these extraneous, unsuccessful modes, leading to task failure.

2. **Develop a Method to Constrain Actions to Successful Modes:** The primary objective is to develop a mechanism that, during inference, can steer the VLA model to execute actions only from the "stable success modes" present in the downstream task dataset, thereby preventing out-of-distribution (OOD) or suboptimal actions.

3. **Propose a Gradient-Free and Test-Time Only Solution:** Given the complexity of performing traditional reinforcement learning (RL) updates on flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models, a crucial objective is to design a solution that is:
 - **Gradient-free:** It should not require backpropagation through the VLA model to update its parameters.
 - **Test-time only:** The intervention should occur solely during inference, leaving the pre-trained and fine-tuned VLA policy weights unchanged. This preserves the VLA's generalization abilities and avoids costly retraining.

4. **Ensure Computational Efficiency and Practicality:** The proposed solution must be computationally lightweight enough for practical deployment, especially on real-world robotic platforms, despite potentially involving multiple action samples. This necessitates strategies to minimize inference latency.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to significantly improve the inference stability and success rates of general-purpose VLA models, making them more robust and reliable for real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **TACO (Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-COunts)**, is a test-time scaling (TTS) framework that leverages principles from anti-exploration in offline reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Reframing Instability as an Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Problem

The authors formally define the observed VLA inference instability as an OOD problem. They argue that fine-tuned VLA policies, $\pi_\theta$, approximate a dataset distribution $p_D(a|s)$ which is often a mixture of a desired success mode ($\pi^*$) and various suboptimal modes ($p_k$). When $\pi_\theta$ samples from an undesired $p_k$, it leads to failure. This is analogous to an offline RL agent sampling out-of-support actions. The goal, inspired by anti-exploration, is to constrain the policy to the support of the *successful modes* in the SFT dataset.

The core theoretical insight is that, under the assumption that the density of demonstrations in the SFT dataset correlates with task success (i.e., $P(r=1|o, l, a) \propto p_{D_{sft}}(o, l, a)$), the anti-exploration objective (maximizing Q-value while penalizing OOD actions) simplifies to finding the action chunk with the **maximum visitation count (density)**: $a^*_{1:H} = \text{arg max}_{a_{1:H}} [N_{D_{sft}}(o, l, a_{1:H})]$.

#### 3.2. Coupled Pseudo-Count Estimation for VLAs

To implement the density estimation (visitation count), TACO uses a **Coin Flipping Network (CFN)**, which estimates pseudo-counts.
- **Coupled Estimator Design:** Instead of a separate, heavy encoder, the CFN ($f_\phi$) is a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) head that takes the **VLA's own internal representations** ($h_\theta$) as input. This design is highly efficient as it reuses the VLA's pre-trained computational backbone and benefits from its rich representations.
- **High-Fidelity Feature Search (for Denoising-based VLAs):** A critical challenge for generative VLA models (diffusion/flow-matching) is that they are trained on *noised* actions and never explicitly see *clean* actions during training. To obtain an *in-distribution* feature that accurately represents a *clean* ground-truth action $a \in D_{sft}$, the authors propose a search procedure:
 1. For each $(o, l, a)$ in $D_{sft}$, the VLA is queried $N$ times with different noise levels ($\sigma_i$), generating predicted clean actions $a^{(i)}_{pre}$ and corresponding internal representations $h^{(i)}_\theta$.
 2. The representation $h^{(i^*)}_\theta$ whose predicted action $a^{(i^*)} _{pre}$ is closest (L2 distance) to the ground-truth $a$ is selected. This ensures the chosen feature is both in-distribution for the VLA and high-fidelity to the desired clean action.
- **Estimator Training:** The CFN $f_\phi$ is trained on these high-fidelity internal features $D_h$ by solving a simple regression problem: $\text{min}_\phi E_{(h_\theta, c) \sim D_h} \|f_\phi(h_\theta) - c\|^2$, where $c$ are random binary vectors (Rademacher trials). The pseudo-count for a feature $h_\theta$ is then inversely proportional to the squared norm of the CFN's output: $\hat{N}_{D_{sft}}(h_\theta) \propto 1/\|f_\phi(h_\theta)\|^2$.

#### 3.3. Test-Time Scaling as Anti-Exploration

During inference, TACO employs a two-stage generate-then-verify process:
1. **Generation Stage:** Given an observation ($o_t$) and instruction ($l$), the fine-tuned VLA ($\pi_\theta$) generates $M$ candidate action chunks ($\hat{a}^{(i)}_{t:t+H}$) by sampling $M$ distinct initial noise vectors (for generative VLAs). Crucially, the corresponding internal representations ($h^{(i)}_\theta$) are extracted simultaneously.
2. **Verification Stage:** The trained CFN ($f_\phi$) acts as a verifier. It scores each candidate action chunk $\hat{a}^{(i)}_{t:t+H}$ based on its internal feature $h^{(i)}_\theta$, providing a pseudo-count $\hat{N}_{D_{sft}}(o_t, l, \hat{a}^{(i)}_{t:t+H})$. The action with the *maximum pseudo-count* is selected for execution, directly realizing the anti-exploration objective.

#### 3.4. Enabling Efficient Inference via KV Cache Optimization

To mitigate the $O(M)$ computational overhead of generating $M$ candidates, a key optimization is introduced. The expensive VLA computations (e.g., transformer backbone for vision-language encoding) that depend on the shared context $(o, l)$ are computed only *once*. Their Key (K) and Value (V) caches are then *reused* across all $M$ parallel action generation processes, significantly reducing latency and making the marginal cost of additional candidates minimal.

In essence, TACO works by turning the stochasticity of generative VLA models into an advantage: instead of randomly picking one action, it samples multiple, and then intelligently filters them using a lightweight, task-specific verifier learned from the VLA's own representations and the SFT data distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and on a real-world dual-arm robotic platform demonstrate the significant effectiveness and efficiency of TACO.

#### 4.1. Performance Improvement Across Diverse Environments and VLA Policies (Q1)

- **Simulation Benchmarks:** TACO consistently improved success rates across four diverse simulation benchmarks:
 - **RoboTwin 1.0:** Achieved an average improvement of **9.1%** over the base $\pi_0$ policy (e.g., Block Handover from 41% to 62%, Container Place from 25% to 40%).
 - **Simpler-WindowX:** Showed an average improvement of **7.5%** over $\pi_0$ (e.g., Spoon on Towel from 36% to 52%, Eggplant in Basket from 80% to 88%).
 - **RoboTwin 2.0:** Improved $\pi_{0.5}$'s average success rate by **4.7%** across a large set of tasks (e.g., Move Can Pot from 42% to 57%, Stamp Seal from 26% to 38%).
 - **LIBERO-Long:** Even on already high-performing base policies, TACO further improved $\pi_{0.5}$ by **1.8%** (from 94.8% to 96.6% average success), with a notable gain in "Moka Pots on Stove" from 68% to 86%.
- **Generality Across VLA Architectures:** TACO was successfully applied to both flow-matching-based VLAs ($\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$) and an autoregressive VLA (OpenVLA), yielding a **6.0%** average improvement for OpenVLA on LIBERO-Long. This demonstrates the broad applicability of the framework beyond specific generative model types.
- **Underlying Mechanism Validation:** Analysis (Figure 3) showed a strong inverse correlation between the CFN's pseudo-count (1/Count) and the L2 distance of the predicted action to the ground truth. This confirms that selecting actions with higher pseudo-counts indeed steers the policy towards more accurate and in-support behaviors, effectively filtering out suboptimal actions.

#### 4.2. Test-Time Cost and Real-World Efficiency (Q2)

- **KV Cache Optimization:** The proposed Key-Value (KV) cache optimization dramatically reduced inference latency. For 32 parallel action samples, the inference time was reduced by **73.2%** compared to a naive multi-batch approach (Figure 4, left). This makes the multi-sampling approach computationally feasible for real-time robotic control.
- **Real-World Performance:** On a RealMan75 dual-arm robot platform with five complex tasks, TACO achieved an average **16%** increase in success rate over the base $\pi_0$ policy (Figure 5).
 - Notable gains in long-horizon tasks: 25% improvement in "Paper and Pen" and 15% in "Laptop."
 - **Qualitative Benefits:** Observations indicated that TACO not only improved precision but also mitigated policy failures caused by imperfect teleoperation data (e.g., unstable grasping, oscillations) by suppressing suboptimal trajectories (Figure 4, right).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Design Choices (Q3)

- **Importance of CFN Pseudo-Counting:** Replacing CFN with a direct fit of L2 distance (mapping features to action errors) led to a significant performance drop, suggesting that directly learning error is harder and prone to overfitting compared to density estimation.
- **Importance of Internal Features:** Using separate CNN/MLP encoders instead of the VLA's internal features resulted in a substantial performance drop, indicating that leveraging the VLA's pre-trained internal representations is crucial for effective and efficient pseudo-counting.
- **Importance of High-Fidelity Feature Search:** Visualization studies (Figure 8) highlighted that combining internal representations with the High-Fidelity Feature Search procedure was essential for the pseudo-counter to effectively distinguish between better and worse noise samples after denoising, successfully preserving ground-truth action information. Without it, the pseudo-counter's discriminative ability was limited.
- **CFN vs. RND:** A comparison with Random Network Distillation (RND) as an alternative pseudo-counter showed that CFN consistently yielded better average performance (Table 7), validating the choice of CFN for this specific problem.

In conclusion, TACO offers a robust, efficient, and widely applicable solution to the inference-time instability of modern VLA models, enhancing their performance and reliability across a spectrum of robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Steering Vision-Language-Action Models as Anti-Exploration" by Yang et al. holds significant implications for the development and deployment of robust robotic manipulation systems.

1. **Addressing a Critical but Overlooked Problem:** The paper successfully identifies and provides a solution for the prevalent, yet often unaddressed, issue of inference-time fragility in advanced VLA models. This instability, particularly in generative models, is a major barrier to their reliable deployment in real-world scenarios. By diagnosing it as an out-of-distribution problem rooted in redundant action modes, the authors lay a theoretical foundation for its mitigation.

2. **Principled and Efficient Anti-Exploration:** TACO offers a novel and elegant approach to anti-exploration within the context of generative VLA models. By leveraging the principles of offline RL and pseudo-counting, it provides a theoretically justified method to constrain policy outputs to observed successful behaviors. Crucially, its gradient-free, test-time-only nature, combined with lightweight components (CFN as an MLP head) and KV cache optimization, makes it highly efficient and practical. This overcomes the significant challenges of applying complex RL updates to diffusion or flow-matching models.

3. **Enhanced Reliability and Safety for Robotics:** The consistent and substantial improvements in success rates across diverse simulated and real-world tasks directly translate to more reliable and safer robotic operations. By filtering out suboptimal or risky actions, TACO makes VLA-controlled robots more trustworthy and predictable, which is paramount for deployment in human-centric environments.

4. **Robustness to Imperfect Data:** The ability of TACO to mitigate failures arising from suboptimal or multimodal fine-tuning data is a major advantage. Real-world robot datasets, whether collected via teleoperation or scripted policies, are rarely perfect. This research shows that VLA models can be made more resilient to such imperfections, reducing the stringent demands on data quality and collection processes.

5. **Broad Applicability and Generalizability:** The framework's demonstrated effectiveness across different VLA architectures (flow-matching, diffusion, autoregressive) and various robotic platforms (RoboTwin, Libero, Simpler, RealMan75) highlights its generalizability. This suggests that TACO could become a standard component or best practice for improving the robustness of a wide array of VLA systems.

6. **New Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research. While TACO effectively prunes out-of-support actions from a set of generated candidates, exploring how to synthesize entirely novel, yet in-support, actions could be a next step. Further refinements in pseudo-count estimation, alternative lightweight verifiers, or dynamic adaptation of the number of candidates based on task uncertainty could also be fruitful.

In conclusion, TACO represents a significant step forward in making generalist VLA models more stable, reliable, and practical for real-world robotic applications, addressing a fundamental limitation with an innovative and efficient solution.
