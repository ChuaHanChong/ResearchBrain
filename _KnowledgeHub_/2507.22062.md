# Meta CLIP 2: A Worldwide Scaling Recipe

> [!summary] Summary
> FAIR (Meta) researchers introduce a transparent, open-sourced methodology for pre-training CLIP on native worldwide web-scale image-text pairs. This approach overcomes the "curse of multilinguality," allowing a ViT-H/14 model to surpass its English-only counterpart by 0.8% on zero-shot ImageNet and establish new state-of-the-art results across multilingual benchmarks like CVQA (57.4%) and Babel-ImageNet (50.2%).

## Problem

- Most CLIP variants rely on English-only data, discarding over 50% of global web content and introducing challenges for worldwide scaling.
- Existing multilingual CLIP methods often rely on less robust approaches like distillation, machine translation, or proprietary datasets, leading to biases and limited transparency.
- Multilingual models typically exhibit degraded performance on English tasks, known as the "curse of multilinguality," hindering truly universal foundation models.

## Method

- Developed a ==three-step "worldwide scaling recipe"== based on English Meta CLIP, incorporating per-language metadata for 300+ languages from expanded sources like Multilingual WordNet and Wikipedia.
- Designed a novel, ==language-by-language curation algorithm== that uses language identification, language-specific substring matching, and ==adaptive concept balancing (t_lang)== for diverse data.
- Adapted the training framework by integrating a ==multilingual text tokenizer (XLM-V)==, scaling seen training pairs (2.3x), and identifying a crucial model capacity (ViT-H/14) to leverage global data.

## Results

- A Meta CLIP 2 ViT-H/14 model trained on worldwide data achieved **81.3%** accuracy on zero-shot ImageNet classification, outperforming its English-only counterpart by **0.8%**.
- Established new state-of-the-art performance on multilingual benchmarks, including **57.4%** averaged answer accuracy on CVQA, **50.2%** on Babel-ImageNet, and **64.3%** recall@1 for image-to-text retrieval on XM3600.
- Demonstrated improved understanding of cultural diversity, leading to better performance on geographically and culturally diverse benchmarks like Dollar Street, GeoDE, and GLDv2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "curse of multilinguality" can be overcome with sufficient model capacity (ViT-H/14) and scaled training, enabling non-English data to mutually benefit and even improve English performance.
> - Language-specific metadata and adaptive concept balancing are critical for effectively curating and processing the vast diversity and varying scales of multilingual web data.
> - A transparent and open-sourced recipe for training CLIP on native worldwide data is achievable, providing a generalizable framework without reliance on machine translation or distillation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP), introduced by OpenAI (Radford et al., 2021), revolutionized multimodal AI by enabling powerful zero-shot capabilities in tasks like image classification and retrieval, and serving as vision encoders for multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, a significant limitation of most CLIP variants, including the scalable Meta CLIP (Xu et al., 2024), is their reliance on English-only data. This English-centric approach discards a substantial portion of the worldwide web data (estimated at 50.9% non-English content) and introduces two major "worldwide scaling challenges":
1. **Lack of non-English data curation methods**: Existing approaches for multilingual CLIP often rely on less robust methods like distilling from English CLIP, machine translation, or proprietary/private datasets (e.g., WebLI used by mSigLIP and SigLIP 2). These methods introduce biases, limit transparency, and often fail to scale effectively.
2. **"Curse of multilinguality"**: Multilingual models typically exhibit degraded performance on English tasks compared to their English-only counterparts. This trade-off hinders the development of truly universal foundation models.

Meta CLIP 2 directly addresses these fundamental limitations. It positions itself as the **first recipe to train CLIP from scratch on *native worldwide web-scale image-text pairs*** without relying on outsourced resources, machine translation, or distillation. This approach aims to unlock the vast potential of non-English data, which is becoming increasingly critical as the availability of English internet data approaches exhaustion (Villalobos et al., 2022). By demonstrating that English and non-English data can mutually benefit each other, Meta CLIP 2 challenges the prevalent "curse of multilinguality" and pushes towards genuinely global, inclusive, and culturally diverse AI models. It also sets a new standard for transparent and reproducible multilingual CLIP training by making its metadata, curation algorithm, and training code fully open-sourced. This contrasts sharply with systems like mSigLIP and SigLIP 2, which leverage undisclosed datasets and processing pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of Meta CLIP 2 are to:

1. **Develop a foundational data curation method for non-English data at scale**: This addresses the critical gap in existing CLIP pipelines, which largely lack scalable and transparent techniques for processing diverse, non-English image-text pairs from the web. The motivation here is to move beyond methods that rely on distillation from English models or machine translation, which can introduce biases and reduce data quality.
2. **Overcome the "curse of multilinguality"**: The goal is to develop a CLIP model that not only performs strongly on multilingual tasks but also maintains or even improves English performance compared to English-only models. This aims to eliminate the performance trade-off between English and non-English capabilities, thereby enabling a single, universally applicable foundation model.
3. **Enable mutual benefits between English and non-English data**: The research hypothesizes that with proper scaling of data, metadata, curation, and model capacity, English and non-English data can complement each other, leading to improved performance across the board.
4. **Provide a transparent and open-sourced recipe**: A core motivation is to offer a generalizable, reproducible, and open framework for worldwide CLIP training, maximizing overlap with existing OpenAI/Meta CLIP architectures to isolate the impact of the proposed innovations. This fosters community adoption and further research.

The overarching motivation is to unlock the "next level of scaling" for CLIP by embracing the entirety of the worldwide web, not just its English portion. This is driven by the recognition that English internet data is finite and potentially soon to be exhausted, necessitating a broader data strategy for future AI advancements. Furthermore, leveraging native, culturally diverse data is crucial for developing robust, fair, and globally relevant AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Meta CLIP 2's methodology is encapsulated in a three-step "recipe" for scaling CLIP to native worldwide data and training, built upon the existing English Meta CLIP algorithm. The design emphasizes minimal architectural changes to ensure generalizability of findings.

#### 3.1. Worldwide Metadata

The first innovation involves scaling the English Meta CLIP metadata to cover non-English languages. This is achieved by:
- **Per-language metadata**: Instead of a monolithic metadata set, Meta CLIP 2 constructs independent metadata for each language, recognizing that concepts and their representations differ across languages.
- **Expanded sources**: It extends the four high-quality sources used by English Meta CLIP (WordNet Synsets, Wikipedia Unigrams, Bigrams, and Page Titles) to 300+ languages.
 - **Multilingual WordNet**: Incorporates synsets from 31 languages.
 - **Wikipedia Unigrams/Bigrams/Titles**: Processes Wikipedia dumps from May 2024 in 329 languages. For languages without space separation (e.g., some Asian languages), open-source tokenizers developed by local communities are used to maintain semantic integrity. Wikipedia titles are ranked by click-through traffic for each language.

#### 3.2. Worldwide Curation Algorithm

The core of Meta CLIP 2's data processing is a novel, language-by-language curation algorithm (Algorithm 1) designed for worldwide data.
- **Language Identification (LID)**: Each alt-text in an image-text pair is first classified by its language using a robust LID system (Grave et al., 2018).
- **Language-Specific Substring Matching**: Metadata corresponding to the identified language is used for substring matching within the alt-text. This ensures concepts are matched within their native linguistic context.
- **Adaptive Concept Balancing (`t_lang`)**: A critical innovation addresses the challenge of vastly different data sizes and concept counts across languages. Instead of a single global threshold `t` (used in English CLIP to balance head vs. tail concepts), Meta CLIP 2 introduces a language-specific threshold `t_lang`. This `t_lang` is dynamically calculated for each language by:
 1. First determining a global "tail proportion" `p` based on the English threshold `t_en`.
 2. Then, for each non-English language, finding `t_lang` that ensures the same `p` (percentage of matches from tail concepts) is maintained. This ensures a consistent balance of rare and common concepts across all languages, preventing tail concepts from being diluted in smaller language datasets.
- **Efficient Implementation**: To handle the computational demands of worldwide data, the algorithm employs:
 - **Aho-Corasick algorithm**: For 2000x faster substring matching with million-scale metadata.
 - **Lazy Metadata Loading**: Dynamically loads language-specific metadata automata to save memory and compilation time.
 - **Memory-mapped file loading (mmap)**: For efficient access to concept counts across all languages during sampling.
- **Safety and Deduplication**: NSFW content is filtered using a state-of-the-art classifier, and human biometrics are removed. Benchmark leakage is prevented by deduplicating against ImageNet evaluation sets using 64-bit hashes of feature embeddings.

#### 3.3. Training Framework

To address the "curse of multilinguality," Meta CLIP 2 adapts the training framework with three additions:
- **Multilingual Text Tokenizer**: Replaces the English tokenizer with a multilingual one. Ablation studies identified **XLM-V (Liang et al., 2023)** as providing the strongest performance.
- **Scaling Seen Training Pairs**: The number of seen image-text pairs during training is scaled proportionally to the increased data size from non-English content. This ensures that the exposure to English data remains consistent, preventing performance degradation. Specifically, the global training batch size is increased 2.3x (reflecting English pairs constituting 44% of the training data), while other hyperparameters are kept consistent with OpenAI/Meta CLIP.
- **Minimal Viable Model Capacity**: The study reveals that a larger model capacity is crucial to effectively learn from the increased data diversity and break the curse. While ViT-L/14 (the largest model in OpenAI CLIP) still suffered from the curse, **ViT-H/14** was identified as the inflection point, capable of leveraging worldwide data for mutual benefit.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Meta CLIP 2 demonstrates significant advancements in multilingual vision-language pre-training, primarily by overcoming the "curse of multilinguality" and setting new state-of-the-art (SoTA) benchmarks.

- **Breaking the Curse of Multilinguality**:
 - The paper's most critical finding is that with sufficient model capacity (ViT-H/14) and scaled training (2.3x seen pairs), the "curse of multilinguality" is broken. Meta CLIP 2 ViT-H/14 trained on worldwide data **surpasses its English-only counterpart by 0.8%** on zero-shot ImageNet classification (81.3% vs. 80.5%). This demonstrates that non-English data can *improve* English performance, achieving mutual benefits.
 - Conversely, smaller models like ViT-L/14, even with scaled seen pairs, still suffer from the curse, highlighting the importance of model capacity.

- **New State-of-the-Art in Multilingual Benchmarks**:
 - Meta CLIP 2 ViT-H/14 sets new SoTA performance across various multilingual benchmarks *without bespoke architectural changes or translation*:
 - **CVQA**: 57.4% (averaged answer accuracy, an improvement over mSigLIP's 49.8%/45.7%).
 - **Babel-ImageNet**: 50.2% (averaged zero-shot classification, an improvement over mSigLIP's 46.4%).
 - **XM3600 (Image-to-Text Retrieval)**: 64.3% recall@1 (an improvement over mSigLIP's 62.8%). Also 51.5% for Text-to-Image.
 - Outperforms mSigLIP and SigLIP 2 on most multilingual and English benchmarks, often with fewer seen pairs (72% of SigLIP series) and lower resolution (224px vs. 256px).

- **Ablation Studies Validate Methodological Choices**:
 - **Metadata and Curation**: Ablations on ViT-B/32 show the importance of:
 - **Language isolation**: Separating alt-texts and metadata by language before matching (improving IN accuracy from 66.9% to 67.5% when using English-only data/metadata, then showing how merging causes degradation).
 - **Language-specific `t_lang`**: Dynamically computing `t_lang` per language significantly improves performance (e.g., IN from 61.1% to 64.7%) by ensuring balanced concept distribution.
 - **Tokenizer**: XLM-V vocabulary (900k size) yields the strongest overall performance compared to mT5, Gemma, and XLM-Roberta tokenizers.

- **Enhanced Cultural Diversity**:
 - Meta CLIP 2 demonstrates improved performance on geographically and culturally diverse benchmarks.
 - On zero-shot classification for Dollar Street, GeoDE, and GLDv2, worldwide training (13B and 29B) consistently outperforms English-only training, showing better understanding of diverse contexts.
 - Similar improvements are observed in few-shot geo-localization tasks across Dollar Street, GeoDE, and XM3600, indicating more comprehensive cultural and socioeconomic coverage.

- **Embedding Quality**:
 - Evaluation of embedding alignment and uniformity (lower scores are better) on a 5k holdout dataset shows that Meta CLIP 2 models achieve good scores, suggesting high-quality, well-distributed representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Meta CLIP 2 represents a significant leap forward in developing truly global and inclusive foundation models, with several far-reaching implications:

- **Paving the Way for Worldwide AI**: By providing the first transparent and scalable recipe for training CLIP from scratch on native worldwide image-text pairs, this work lays a critical foundation for building AI models that can understand and interact with content from diverse languages and cultures globally. This is crucial for democratizing AI access and utility worldwide.

- **Overcoming Data Exhaustion**: As English internet data becomes saturated, Meta CLIP 2 offers a viable strategy to tap into the vast, underutilized reservoir of non-English content. This ensures the continued scalability of foundation models and mitigates the looming challenge of data depletion.

- **Eliminating the "Curse of Multilinguality"**: The demonstration that English and non-English data can mutually benefit each other fundamentally alters the paradigm for multilingual model development. This means future foundation models will not have to sacrifice English performance for multilingual capability, leading to more robust and versatile single models.

- **Enabling Truly Native-Language Supervision and Cultural Diversity**: By learning directly from native alt-texts and retaining the entire global distribution of images, Meta CLIP 2 models are less prone to biases introduced by translation or English-centric filtering. This leads to better geo-localization, region-specific recognition, and a richer understanding of diverse cultural nuances.

- **Open Science and Transparency**: The commitment to open-sourcing the metadata, curation algorithm, and training code empowers the broader research community. This transparency facilitates reproducibility, fosters innovation, and allows for scrutiny of data biases, contributing to more responsible AI development.

- **Broader Impact on Foundation Models**: The "foundational data algorithm" developed in this work extends beyond CLIP itself. It offers a paradigm for data curation that can benefit other areas of multimodal AI, including:
 - **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**: Providing better vision encoders with global understanding.
 - **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)**: Enhancing visual representation learning with diverse data.
 - **Generative AI Models (e.g., DALL-E, Diffusion Models)**: Offering richer, more culturally aware input and training data.

In conclusion, Meta CLIP 2's "Worldwide Scaling Recipe" is a landmark achievement, transforming CLIP from an English-dominant model into a genuinely worldwide foundation model. Its rigorous methodology and compelling results promise to accelerate the development of more inclusive, robust, and globally relevant AI systems.
