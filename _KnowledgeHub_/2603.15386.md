# RieMind: Geometry-Grounded Spatial Agent for Scene Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Riemann Lab, Huawei Technologies, developed RieMind, a geometry-grounded agentic framework that decouples perception from reasoning for 3D indoor scene understanding by having an LLM interact with a 3D Scene Graph via structured tools. This approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on the static questions of VSI-Bench, improving average accuracy by up to 16% over prior fine-tuned models.

## Problem

- Current Visual Language Models (VLMs) often struggle with precise metric and spatial reasoning in 3D indoor scenes, leading to inaccuracies.
- Existing VLM approaches tightly couple perception and reasoning, limiting metric consistency, interpretability, and robustness, especially with long-term visual contexts.
- A lack of explicit, persistent geometric representations hinders robust spatial understanding and leads to issues like 'hallucinations' in complex 3D environments.

## Method

- Developed **RieMind**, a ==geometry-grounded agentic framework== that explicitly decouples perception from reasoning in 3D indoor scene understanding.
- Constructed a ==3D Scene Graph (3DSG)== from ground-truth annotations to serve as a precise, persistent scene representation, isolating reasoning performance from perceptual errors.
- Designed a comprehensive, ==deterministic toolbox== enabling an LLM to query the 3DSG for specific geometric and semantic information, facilitating structured spatial reasoning.

## Results

- The agentic framework achieved state-of-the-art performance on VSI-Bench static questions with an average score of **89.5%** (GPT-4.1), an approximate **16%** increase over the previous best-performing fine-tuned model.
- Agentic GPT-4o showed a substantial average accuracy improvement of **49.9%** (from **3**5.3%** to 85.2%**) over its base VLM, with gains of **87.9%** for Absolute Distance questions.
- Even smaller models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B, when integrated into the RieMind framework, outperformed most fine-tuned models, achieving **64.1%** average accuracy without task-specific training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling perception from reasoning and grounding an LLM in an explicit 3DSG significantly improves spatial reasoning performance, particularly for metric-intensive tasks.
> - Explicit geometric grounding, provided by structured tools operating on a 3DSG, offers superior accuracy and interpretability compared to implicitly learned visual priors in end-to-end VLMs.
> - The underlying reasoning capability of the LLM is critical for composing complex spatial reasoning steps, with larger models demonstrating better performance on multi-tool inference chains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen substantial advancements with the rise of large language models (LLMs) and visual language models (VLMs), which aim to integrate linguistic and visual understanding. VLMs have become a prominent paradigm for comprehending indoor scenes. However, current VLMs frequently encounter difficulties with metric and precise spatial reasoning. This limitation becomes particularly pronounced in scenarios requiring long-term visual and spatial context, such as analyzing long-form videos, where performance can degrade, leading to inaccuracies or "hallucinations." Existing approaches to indoor spatial understanding typically fall into two categories: large-scale spatial instruction fine-tuning of VLMs or the augmentation of VLMs with geometric tools. Both methods, however, tend to tightly couple perception and reasoning processes. This inherent coupling can restrict metric consistency and interpretability, particularly in 3D indoor environments where persistent geometric representations are often considered more appropriate for robust reasoning.

This paper addresses this challenge by investigating an alternative approach that decouples perception from reasoning. It draws inspiration from 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs), which are metric-semantic scene representations commonly used in robotics for tasks requiring precise geometric and spatial information, such as task planning, exploration, and object manipulation. While 3DSGs have been applied in embodied question answering, their systematic and structured utilization for general indoor 3D spatial understanding benchmarks, particularly in a tool-augmented agentic framework, has been less explored. The work aims to bridge this gap by demonstrating that an explicit geometric grounding, provided by a 3DSG and accessed through structured tools, can enhance an LLM's spatial reasoning capabilities, thereby offering an alternative to purely end-to-end visual reasoning paradigms. This positioning suggests a shift from implicit, learned visual priors to explicit, deterministic geometric information for spatial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether decoupling the perception and reasoning processes leads to improved performance in spatial reasoning for 3D indoor scenes. This objective is motivated by the observed limitations of current Visual Language Models (VLMs), which, despite their progress in understanding visual information and natural language, frequently struggle with tasks requiring precise metric and spatial reasoning.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing VLM Limitations in Spatial Reasoning:** Current VLMs often exhibit a reduced focus on spatial intelligence, leading to difficulties in tasks involving metric information like distances, sizes, and geometries. Their reliance on end-to-end video understanding or large-scale spatial question answering fine-tuning inherently couples perception with implicit model representations. This coupling can result in a lack of robustness, grounded spatial understanding, metric consistency issues, and reduced interpretability, particularly when dealing with long-term visual and spatial context. The research aims to explore a framework that mitigates these issues.
- **Decoupling Perception and Reasoning:** The core conceptual approach of this paper is to separate the act of "seeing" (perception) from "thinking" (reasoning). Instead of VLMs directly ingesting videos as the basis for spatial reasoning, the paper proposes treating input videos primarily as a data ingestion and perception modality. The output of this perception phase is a persistent, structured representation that the reasoning system can then operate on.
- **Leveraging 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs):** Indoor environments are often static. The research posits that representing each scene using a 3DSG—a structure capable of unifying semantic, topological, and metric scene representations—provides a more suitable and unified understanding of inter-object and intra-scene relationships than raw visual streams. The objective is to demonstrate the utility of 3DSGs as a foundational representation for spatial reasoning.
- **Developing a Geometry-Grounded Agentic Framework:** The paper proposes "RieMind," an agentic framework where an LLM (rather than a VLM) interacts with the scene exclusively through structured geometric tools. These tools are designed to query an explicit 3DSG, retrieving precise geometric information (e.g., locations, distances, poses, dimensions, areas, volumes) and semantic relationships. This approach aims to provide interpretable metric and spatial reasoning capabilities to LLMs.
- **Isolating Spatial Reasoning Performance:** To specifically assess the agent's spatial reasoning capabilities, the researchers instantiated the 3DSG from ground-truth annotations. This methodological choice aimed to eliminate errors introduced by upstream perception (e.g., instance segmentation) and thereby provide an approximate upper bound on spatial reasoning performance under ideal perceptual conditions. This allows for a clearer evaluation of the reasoning component itself, independent of potential perceptual failures.
- **Evaluating Against Benchmarks:** A key objective is to demonstrate that this explicit geometric grounding approach yields improved spatial reasoning performance on established benchmarks like VSI-Bench, especially when compared to existing VLM-based or fine-tuned approaches.

In essence, the motivation stems from the belief that a structured, explicit representation of a 3D scene, coupled with an LLM that can query this representation via a well-defined set of tools, can overcome the inherent limitations of end-to-end, implicitly learned spatial reasoning in current VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a geometry-grounded agentic framework named RieMind, which explicitly decouples perception from spatial reasoning. This framework comprises two main components: a perception layer responsible for constructing a 3D Scene Graph (3DSG), and an agentic reasoning layer that interacts with a geometry-grounded toolbox.

#### 3.1. Perception Layer: 3D Scene Graph Construction

The perception layer is tasked with building the 3DSG, which serves as the explicit, persistent representation of the 3D indoor scene. The construction procedure generally follows established methods, typically taking RGB-D frames and associated camera parameters as input.

The 3DSG is formalized as a graph $G = (N, E)$, where:
- $N$ represents nodes, comprising four hierarchical layers: Building nodes ($N_B$), Floor nodes ($N_F$), Room nodes ($N_R$), and Object nodes ($N_O$).
- $E$ represents edges, denoting hierarchical connectivity (e.g., $E_{BF}$ between building and floor, $E_{FR}$ between floor and rooms, $E_{RO}$ between rooms and objects) and inter-object relational edges ($E_{OO}$). For this work, inter-object edges are restricted to "near" relationships.

Each node within the 3DSG stores detailed attribute information:
- **Building Node ($N_B$):** Describes the entire scene and stores class attributes.
- **Floor Nodes ($N_F$):** Group rooms on the same floor and store basic geometric information like areas and bounding box dimensions.
- **Room Nodes ($N_R$):** Represent bounded spatial regions and store bounding box-related and derived geometric information.
- **Object Nodes ($N_O$):** Store semantic information (from a closed set of classes) and comprehensive metric data, including bounding box dimensions, volume, surface area, orientation, and location.

Crucially for this research, to isolate the spatial reasoning capabilities of the LLM and establish an approximate upper bound, the 3DSG for the experiments was constructed directly from **ground-truth annotations**. This approach eliminates errors that would typically be introduced by an upstream instance segmentation or scene reconstruction pipeline, ensuring that the agent's reasoning is based on accurate geometric and semantic information. Each node is assigned a unique, persistent ID for referential consistency.

#### 3.2. Reasoning Layer: Agentic Interaction with Geometry-Grounded Toolbox

The reasoning layer employs an LLM within an agentic architecture to perform spatial understanding tasks. The LLM interacts with the 3DSG solely through a set of structured geometric tools.

**Agentic Architecture and Prompting:**
The architecture is structured around distributed Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers that expose the tools to the agent. Tools are semantically grouped into namespaces: `memory`, `scene`, `geometry`, and `location and orientation`. This categorization is designed to limit the types of tool calls and facilitate traceability of the reasoning process.

The agent's prompting system consists of two main parts:
1. **System Prompt:** A detailed prompt provided to the LLM, divided into several sections:
 - **Role Definition:** Establishes the agent's function as a spatial reasoning system that *must* delegate all geometric computations to tools.
 - **Available Tools:** A static schema listing the tools.
 - **Scene Context Cache:** A summary of the current 3DSG for the specific scene, obtained via the `mem_get_scene_context` tool. This provides the LLM with an overview of the scene's contents and hierarchy.
 - **Core Agent Behavior:** Six constraints are enforced: resolving user terminology to scene graph classes, searching for referenced objects before computation, avoiding duplicate tool calls, enforcing valid argument types, issuing one tool call per response, and maintaining an explicit reasoning plan.
 - **Tool Data Flow:** Guidelines on how to use tools, emphasizing the need to disambiguate object names from the question and resolve them to node IDs before calling tools.
 - **Tool Catalog:** Reference documentation for the four namespaces:
 - `sg_*`: Scene graph discovery and structural queries.
 - `geom_*`: Geometric measurements (dimensions, volumes, areas, distances).
 - `mem_*`: Scene context retrieval.
 - `loc_*`: Spatial reference frame construction and projection.
 - **Answer Format:** Requires a JSON output with a natural language summary, evidence of tools invoked, and a data dictionary of key results.
2. **User Question:** The specific question about the scene, sourced from the VSI-Bench dataset.

**Geometry-Grounded Toolbox:**
The tool framework is constructed based on three design principles:
1. **Minimal Geometric Primitives:** Tools implement atomic geometric or structural operations (e.g., accessing an object's volume, projecting an object into a frame) rather than composite functions.
2. **Explicit Grounding:** Tools operate on unique, persistent node IDs from the 3DSG (except for initial search tools that can use free-form text). This ensures referential consistency and aids entity, metric, orientation, frame, and geometric grounding.
3. **Determinism:** Tool outputs are solely dependent on the 3DSG state and are independent of the agent's internal reasoning.

The toolbox is further divided into semantic categories:
- **Memory Tools:** Provide a structured summary of the 3DSG.
- **Scene Tools (`sg_*`):** Enable direct query access to 3DSG nodes (buildings, floors, rooms, objects) and edges, supporting searches by string, ID, or distance. They facilitate entity grounding and scope restriction.
- **Geometric Tools (`geom_*`):** Expose precise metric properties from node geometries, including bounding box size, volume, surface area, and distance computations, handling both convex and concave geometries.
- **Location and Orientation Tools (`loc_*`):** Facilitate egocentric-allocentric transformation reasoning, crucial for VSI-Bench questions. These tools support constructing and manipulating coordinate frames and projecting object frames into different egocentric frames.

This comprehensive and deterministic toolbox enables the agent to perform a sequence of auditable and interpretable operations to understand symbolic, geometric, and frame-dependent questions about the environment.

#### 3.3. Experiments and Evaluation

The geometry-grounded agent was evaluated on the static spatial questions of **VSI-Bench**. This benchmark comprises approximately 5000 QA pairs derived from 288 real videos across ARKitScenes, ScanNet, and ScanNet++ datasets. For this work, dynamic questions (route planning, order appearance) were excluded, leaving 4185 static questions across six types: Object Count, Absolute Distance, Object Size, Room Size, Relative Distance, and Relative Direction.

The evaluation specifically used 3DSGs constructed from ground-truth annotations to isolate reasoning performance. The agentic framework was tested with three LLM backbones: Qwen2.5-VL-7B, GPT-4o, and GPT-4.1. Performance was compared against base VLMs (without tools) and various state-of-the-art proprietary, open-source, and fine-tuned models for spatial QA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several key findings regarding the efficacy of the geometry-grounded agentic framework.

#### 4.1. Impact of Agentic Reasoning

A significant performance increase was observed when comparing base VLMs (without tools or fine-tuning) to their agentic versions equipped with the RieMind tool framework.
- **Average Improvements:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B showed an average improvement of 32.9%, increasing its score from 31.2% to 64.1%. GPT-4o demonstrated an even more substantial average improvement of 49.9%, from 35.3% to 85.2%.
- **Category-Specific Gains:**
 - For questions related to `Object Count`, `Absolute Distance`, and `Object Size`, the improvements were substantial across both models. For instance, GPT-4o's `Absolute Distance` score increased from 5.3% to 93.2% (+87.9%), and `Object Size` from 43.8% to 96.5% (+52.7%). Qwen2.5-VL-7B also showed large gains in these categories (e.g., `Absolute Distance` from 14.8% to 90.3%, +75.5%). These categories typically require fewer tool invocations (1-2 tool calls).
 - `Room Size` questions also saw considerable improvements, with GPT-4o increasing from 38.2% to 83.5% (+45.3%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 10.7% to 31.9% (+21.2%).
 - For more compositionally complex questions like `Relative Distance` and `Relative Direction`, the performance varied more by model. GPT-4o showed strong improvements (e.g., `Relative Direction` from 41.3% to 67.5%, +26.2%), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B exhibited modest or even negative changes (e.g., `Relative Direction` decreased from 38.5% to 34.7%, -3.8%).
- **Reasoning Complexity and Model Capability:** The varying performance on complex questions like `Relative Direction` (which requires 5-6 sequential tool calls) indicates that the underlying reasoning capabilities of the LLM are critical. Smaller models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B struggled with these longer reasoning chains, leading to more frequent hallucinations and incomplete tool pipelines. This suggests that while geometric grounding provides necessary factual data, the LLM's capacity to compose complex reasoning steps is a bottleneck for certain tasks.

#### 4.2. Upper Bound Evaluation on VSI-Bench

The geometry-grounded agentic framework achieved state-of-the-art performance on the static questions of VSI-Bench, surpassing both proprietary and fine-tuned models without requiring additional task-specific training.
- **Overall State-of-the-Art:** The agentic GPT-4.1 achieved an average score of 89.5%, which represents a significant increase over the previous best-performing fine-tuned model, SpaceMind, which scored 73.6%. This constitutes an average increase of approximately 16% over competing fine-tuned spatial understanding models.
- **Superiority Across Categories:** GPT-4.1 demonstrated top performance across most subtasks: 94.9% for `Absolute Distance`, 97.9% for `Object Size`, and 92.7% for `Relative Distance`. Its performance on `Relative Direction` was 87.3%, significantly higher than other models.
- **Model Scaling Benefits:** The gap in performance between GPT-4o (85.2% average) and GPT-4.1 (89.5% average) was notable, especially in `Relative Direction` questions, where GPT-4.1 improved by nearly 20 points over GPT-4o. This observation further supports the conclusion that advanced LLM reasoning capabilities are crucial for complex spatial tasks, even with explicit geometric grounding.
- **Efficiency and Generality:** Even the smaller Qwen2.5-VL-7B, when used in the agentic framework, achieved an average score of 64.1%, outperforming most of the fine-tuned models presented in the comparison (only trailing VLM-3R and SpaceMind). This suggests the potential for broad applicability and resource efficiency, as the framework does not rely on extensive, scene-specific training.
- **Metric Reasoning Superiority:** The substantial performance differences observed in absolute questions (e.g., counting, distance, size estimation) between the agentic framework and fine-tuned models indicate that explicit geometric grounding is more effective for precise metric estimation and reasoning compared to relying on learned visual priors from end-to-end models.

These findings suggest that decoupling perception from reasoning and grounding an LLM in a precise 3DSG through a structured toolbox enables significantly improved spatial reasoning performance, particularly for metric-intensive tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on RieMind presents several significant contributions and indicates potential impacts across various domains.

- **Validation of Decoupled Architectures:** The most significant finding is the demonstration that explicitly decoupling perception from reasoning, by grounding an LLM in a 3D Scene Graph (3DSG) through a set of geometric tools, substantially improves spatial reasoning performance in 3D indoor scenes. This challenges the prevailing paradigm of tightly coupled, end-to-end visual reasoning in VLMs, particularly for tasks requiring precise metric and spatial understanding.
- **Establishment of New Performance Benchmarks:** By achieving state-of-the-art results on the static questions of VSI-Bench, the work sets an approximate upper bound for spatial reasoning performance under ideal perceptual conditions (using ground-truth 3DSGs). The significant average increase (up to 16% over leading fine-tuned models) highlights the efficacy of this approach.
- **Efficiency and Resource Optimization:** The framework achieves high performance without requiring task-specific fine-tuning, which distinguishes it from many existing methods that demand substantial computational resources for training. This suggests a more generalizable and resource-efficient approach to spatial intelligence.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Auditability:** The agentic framework, with its structured tool calls and explicit grounding in the 3DSG, offers a clear, auditable, and interpretable reasoning process. Unlike black-box VLMs, the sequence of tool invocations provides transparency into how the agent arrives at its conclusions, which is critical for debugging, understanding failures, and building trust in AI systems.
- **Implications for Structured Representations:** The success of RieMind underscores the value of structured, explicit representations like 3DSGs as a compelling alternative to purely implicit visual feature learning. For domains requiring quantitative and precise spatial reasoning, such as robotics, architectural design, and simulation, this approach provides a more reliable foundation.
- **Future Directions for Perception-Reasoning Integration:** While the current work uses ground-truth annotations for the 3DSG, the natural next step involves integrating robust perception modules that can construct 3DSGs from raw RGB-D data. This would allow for a full end-to-end system that still benefits from the decoupled reasoning advantages identified in this paper.
- **Potential for VLM Grounding:** The framework could potentially be applied to geometry-ground the visual reasoning processes of existing VLMs. By providing VLMs with access to similar structured geometric tools, their spatial understanding capabilities could be enhanced, leading to more robust and less hallucinatory outputs in complex 3D environments.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI and Robotics:** The precise spatial understanding enabled by RieMind has direct implications for embodied AI agents and robotics, where accurate geometric and relational reasoning is paramount for navigation, object manipulation, and task planning in dynamic or unknown indoor environments.

In summary, this research offers a foundational contribution by demonstrating the benefits of a geometry-grounded, agentic approach to spatial reasoning, positioning structured representations and explicit tool use as a powerful alternative or complement to purely end-to-end visual understanding models.
