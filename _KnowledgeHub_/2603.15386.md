# RieMind: Geometry-Grounded Spatial Agent for Scene Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Riemann Lab, Huawei Technologies, developed RieMind, a geometry-grounded agentic framework that decouples perception from reasoning for 3D indoor scene understanding by having an LLM interact with a 3D Scene Graph via structured tools. This approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on the static questions of VSI-Bench, improving average accuracy by up to 16% over prior fine-tuned models.

## Problem

- Current Visual Language Models (VLMs) often struggle with precise metric and spatial reasoning in 3D indoor scenes, leading to inaccuracies.
- Existing VLM approaches tightly couple perception and reasoning, limiting metric consistency, interpretability, and robustness, especially with long-term visual contexts.
- A lack of explicit, persistent geometric representations hinders robust spatial understanding and leads to issues like 'hallucinations' in complex 3D environments.

## Method

- Developed **RieMind**, a ==geometry-grounded agentic framework== that explicitly decouples perception from reasoning in 3D indoor scene understanding.
- Constructed a ==3D Scene Graph (3DSG)== from ground-truth annotations to serve as a precise, persistent scene representation, isolating reasoning performance from perceptual errors.
- Designed a comprehensive, ==deterministic toolbox== enabling an LLM to query the 3DSG for specific geometric and semantic information, facilitating structured spatial reasoning.

## Results

- The agentic framework achieved state-of-the-art performance on VSI-Bench static questions with an average score of **89.5%** (GPT-4.1), an approximate **16%** increase over the previous best-performing fine-tuned model.
- Agentic GPT-4o showed a substantial average accuracy improvement of **49.9%** (from **35.3% to 85.2%**) over its base VLM, with gains of **87.9%** for Absolute Distance questions.
- Even smaller models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B, when integrated into the RieMind framework, outperformed most fine-tuned models, achieving **64.1%** average accuracy without task-specific training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling perception from reasoning and grounding an LLM in an explicit 3DSG significantly improves spatial reasoning performance, particularly for metric-intensive tasks.
> - Explicit geometric grounding, provided by structured tools operating on a 3DSG, offers superior accuracy and interpretability compared to implicitly learned visual priors in end-to-end VLMs.
> - The underlying reasoning capability of the LLM is critical for composing complex spatial reasoning steps, with larger models demonstrating better performance on multi-tool inference chains.
