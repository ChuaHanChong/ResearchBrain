# AgiBot World Colosseo: A Large-scale Manipulation Platform for Scalable and Intelligent Embodied Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> AgiBot World Colosseo presents a large-scale platform and dataset, AgiBot World, comprising over 1 million real-world robot manipulation trajectories spanning diverse tasks and environments. The work introduces GO-1, a generalist policy leveraging vision-language models and latent action representations, achieving an average 32% performance improvement over prior generalist policies on complex manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality, large-scale, and diverse real-world robot manipulation data limits the development of general-purpose embodied systems.
- Existing robot datasets are often small, confined to controlled lab settings, lack task diversity, or suffer from inconsistent data quality.
- Current robotic systems struggle to generalize across various environments and scenarios, particularly for complex, long-horizon, bimanual, and dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Developed **AgiBot World Colosseo**, a full-stack, open-source platform including a 4,000-square-meter facility replicating five real-world domains and the versatile dual-arm ==AgiBot G1== robot.
- Curated the ==AgiBot World dataset== with over 1 million trajectories (2976.4 hours) across 217 tasks, 87 skills, and 106 scenes, utilizing a ==human-in-the-loop collection protocol== for high quality and diversity.
- Proposed **GO-1** (Genie Operator-1), a hierarchical ==Vision-Language-Latent-Action (ViLLA)== generalist policy that integrates web-scale vision-language models with learned ==latent action representations==.

## Results

- AgiBot World provides over **1 million** trajectories (**2976.4 hours**), covering five diverse real-world domains and over **3,000** unique objects, enabling training for complex, long-horizon manipulation.
- Policies pre-trained on AgiBot World demonstrated an average **30%** success rate improvement for in-domain scenarios and **29%** for out-of-distribution scenarios compared to Open X-Embodiment.
- The **GO-1** policy achieved over **60%** success rate on complex tasks and an average **32%** performance improvement over the RDT approach, exhibiting a predictable power-law scaling with dataset size (Pearson r = **0.97**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An order-of-magnitude increase in data scale, coupled with rigorous human-in-the-loop quality assurance and failure recovery data, is essential for training robust, generalist robot policies.
> - Integrating web-scale vision-language knowledge with robot-specific latent action representations through a hierarchical policy enables better scene understanding and robust control for complex tasks.
> - The performance of generalist robot policies scales predictably with increased dataset size, underscoring the importance of large-scale, high-quality data for future advancements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly progressing, yet the development of truly general-purpose intelligent embodied systems remains a formidable challenge. This paper directly addresses one of the most significant bottlenecks: the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale, and diverse real-world robot manipulation data. While natural language processing and computer vision have seen monumental advancements fueled by abundant, standardized datasets and foundation models (e.g., GPT-4, SAM 2), robotics has lagged due to the inherent difficulty and cost of collecting real-world interaction data.

Previous efforts in robot learning datasets, such as RoboTurk (2.1k trajectories), BridgeData (7.2k), and RT-1 (130k), have provided valuable contributions but are limited in scale, diversity of tasks, and environmental realism, often confined to simple pick-and-place scenarios in controlled lab settings. More recently, initiatives like Open X-Embodiment (OXE) attempted to address scale by aggregating existing datasets, reaching over 1.4 million trajectories. However, OXE suffers from significant variability in embodiments, observation perspectives, and inconsistent data quality, leading to policies constrained to naive, short-horizon tasks with weak generalization capabilities. DROID introduced crowd-sourced data from diverse real-life scenes but lacked robust data quality assurance and was limited by a constrained hardware setup (fixed, single-arm robots). These datasets often fail to capture the complexity of real-world manipulation, such as long-horizon tasks, bimanual operations, fine-grained dexterity, tool use, and multi-robot collaboration.

On the policy learning front, robotic foundation models are co-evolving with dataset scale. Approaches like Diffusion Transformers (e.g., RDT), VLM-based backbones (e.g., π0), and latent action models (e.g., LAPA) have shown promise in learning from diverse data and web-scale videos. However, these models still face limitations in effectively leveraging web-scale knowledge for robotic control due to the fundamental differences between human videos and robot action trajectories, and the relatively small scale of action-labeled robot datasets.

AgiBot World Colosseo directly intervenes in this landscape by presenting a full-stack platform that addresses these shortcomings comprehensively. It achieves an "order-of-magnitude increase in data scale" over existing datasets while critically focusing on data quality, real-world diversity, and complex task scenarios. By proposing a generalist policy (GO-1) that bridges web-scale vision-language models with latent action representations, this work aims to unlock the potential of heterogeneous data sources for robotic control, pushing the boundaries towards truly general-purpose robotic intelligence that can operate in unstructured, open-set environments. It positions itself as a pioneering effort in constructing a meticulously curated, large-scale, and high-quality dataset coupled with an advanced policy learning framework, designed to accelerate the development of next-generation generalist robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **effectively resolve real-world complexity in robotic manipulation by scaling up real-world robot data**, thereby advancing the pursuit of scalable and general-purpose intelligence for embodied systems.

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging the Data Gap in Robotics:** The most significant motivation stems from the recognition that robotics currently lags behind other AI fields like NLP and CV due to the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale datasets. Existing robotic datasets are often too small, too specific, or too inconsistent in quality to train robust, generalist policies capable of operating in diverse, real-world environments.
- **Addressing Limitations of Current Datasets:** Prior datasets are criticized for being confined to controlled lab settings, focusing on simple, short-horizon tasks (e.g., pick-and-place), lacking diverse scenarios and objects, and suffering from unstandardized collection procedures that lead to low quality and fragmented data. The authors explicitly aim to move beyond these limitations.
- **Enabling Generalization to Real-World Challenges:** Current robotic systems struggle to generalize across diverse environments and scenarios, particularly for complex tasks involving fine-grained object interaction, bimanual manipulation, tool usage, and mobile collaboration. The motivation is to create a platform that provides the data richness and complexity necessary for robots to acquire these "merits beyond the reach of current robotic systems."
- **Developing Scalable Generalist Policies:** With the increased scale and quality of data, there is a clear motivation to develop novel generalist policies that can effectively leverage this data. The authors seek a policy architecture that can not only learn from the large, diverse robot data but also integrate knowledge from web-scale vision-language models, demonstrating predictable performance scaling with data volume.
- **Democratizing Access and Fostering Innovation:** By open-sourcing the dataset, toolchain, and pre-trained models, a core motivation is to democratize access to advanced robot learning resources. This aims to foster community-wide collaboration, accelerate research, and enable researchers to explore a wider range of authentic and diverse applications, from household assistants to industrial automation.
- **Standardizing Data Collection:** A key motivation is to introduce a standardized, high-quality data collection pipeline with human-in-the-loop verification, which has been largely overlooked in previous fragmented data aggregation efforts, to ensure reliability and scalability.

In essence, AgiBot World Colosseo is motivated by the critical need to provide the foundational data and models necessary to unlock the next generation of general-purpose robotic intelligence, moving from narrow, lab-constrained capabilities to robust, adaptable performance in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "AgiBot World Colosseo" is comprehensive, spanning hardware development, large-scale data collection, and the introduction of a novel policy learning framework. The approach centers on building a full-stack, open-source ecosystem designed for scalable and intelligent embodied systems.

#### 3.1. The AgiBot World Platform

AgiBot World Colosseo is presented as a full-stack, open-source embodied intelligence ecosystem.
- **Physical Facility:** A 4,000-square-meter facility was constructed to replicate five major real-world domains: domestic, retail, industrial, restaurant, and office environments. This facility contains over 3,000 unique objects, ensuring broad diversity in scene configurations and interaction possibilities.
- **Hardware (AgiBot G1 Robot):** The platform is built around the AgiBot G1, a versatile dual-arm humanoid robot. Key features include:
 - Dual 7-DoF arms for bimanual manipulation.
 - A mobile chassis and adjustable waist for whole-body control and navigation.
 - Modular end-effectors, allowing for either standard grippers or 6-DoF dexterous hands, depending on task requirements.
 - Integrated visuo-tactile sensors on grippers for fine-grained interaction.
 - An extensive sensor suite comprising eight cameras: an RGB-D camera and three fisheye cameras for the front view, RGB-D or fisheye cameras on each end-effector, and two rear fisheye cameras.
 - Image observations and proprioceptive states are recorded at 30 Hz.
- **Teleoperation Systems:** Two systems are utilized for data collection:
 - **VR Headset Control:** Maps hand gestures to end-effector movements and provides control for the robot base and body.
 - **Whole-Body Motion Capture Control:** Records human joint data, including fingers, and maps it to robot posture, enabling more nuanced and dexterous control.

#### 3.2. Data Collection Protocol and Quality Assurance

The dataset, AgiBot World, comprises over 1 million trajectories (2976.4 hours) across 217 tasks, 87 skills, and 106 scenes. A rigorous, standardized, human-in-the-loop pipeline ensures high quality and diversity:
- **Three-Phase Collection:**
 1. **Preliminary Acquisition:** Validate task feasibility and establish collection standards.
 2. **Formal Data Collection:** Skilled teleoperators arrange scenes and collect data according to standards. Local validity checks ensure data completeness.
 3. **Post-processing:** Annotators verify episodes against standards and provide detailed language annotations for the overall task and individual sub-steps, including key-frame and instruction annotations for long-horizon tasks.
- **Failure Recovery Data:** Trajectories where teleoperators make errors but recover to complete the task are retained. These "failure recovery data" (approximately 1% of the dataset) are manually annotated with failure reasons and timestamps, considered invaluable for future policy alignment and failure reflection research.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Feedback:** An iterative feedback loop involves collecting small demonstration sets, training a policy, deploying it, and evaluating its performance. This feedback actively guides refinements to the data collection protocols (e.g., eliminating idle frames) to continuously enhance data utility for policy learning.
- **Comprehensive Annotation:** Beyond task-level, item, scene, and skill (sub-task segmented) annotations are provided, along with camera calibration information.

#### 3.3. GO-1: Genie Operator-1 Generalist Policy

To effectively utilize the AgiBot World dataset and improve generalization, the authors propose Genie Operator-1 (GO-1), a hierarchical Vision-Language-Latent-Action (ViLLA) framework with three training stages:
- **Stage 1: Latent Action Model (LAM):**
 - **Objective:** Learn universal action representations from internet-scale heterogeneous data (including human videos without explicit action labels) to bridge the gap between general image-text inputs and robotic actions.
 - **Mechanism:** An encoder-decoder model is trained to predict latent actions (inverse dynamics) between consecutive frames ($I_t$, $I_{t+H}$). The encoder uses a spatial-temporal transformer, and the decoder is a spatial transformer.
 - **Quantization:** Latent action tokens are discretized using a VQ-VAE objective with a codebook, making the action space orders of magnitude smaller than low-level actions.
- **Stage 2: Latent Planner:**
 - **Objective:** Establish strong scene/object understanding and general reasoning for embodiment-agnostic long-horizon planning.
 - **Mechanism:** Uses InternVL2.5-2B as a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone. Multiview image observations ($I^h_t, I^l_t, I^r_t$) and language instructions ($l$) are encoded. A latent planner (24 transformer layers) predicts latent action tokens ($z_t$), supervised by the LAM encoder's output ($I(I^h_t, I^h_{t+H})$). This efficiently adapts VLMs to robot policies.
- **Stage 3: Action Expert:**
 - **Objective:** Achieve high-frequency and dexterous manipulation by decoding low-level actions.
 - **Mechanism:** Shares the architectural framework with the latent planner but uses a diffusion objective to model continuous distributions of low-level action chunks ($A_t = [a_t..., a_{t+H}]$) over an interval of $H=30$ timesteps. It is conditioned hierarchically on preceding modules, including the latent planner and the VLM.
- **Inference:** During inference, the VLM, latent planner, and action expert synergistically combine to predict latent action tokens and then generate final control signals via a denoising process.

#### 3.4. Open-Source Release

All resources—the AgiBot World dataset (under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license), toolchains, and pre-trained models—are open-sourced to facilitate community-wide research and development.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate the efficacy of both the AgiBot World dataset and the GO-1 generalist policy, demonstrating significant advancements in robotic manipulation capabilities.

- **Unprecedented Data Scale and Diversity:** AgiBot World features over 1 million trajectories, accumulating 2976.4 hours of data across 217 tasks, 87 skills, and 106 distinct scenes. This represents an order-of-magnitude increase in scale compared to existing datasets like DROID and Open X-Embodiment. The dataset covers five diverse real-world domains (domestic, retail, industrial, restaurant, office) and includes over 3,000 unique objects, moving beyond simple tabletop tasks to challenging, long-horizon tasks, bimanual manipulation, dexterous hand use, and collaborative scenarios. Trajectories typically span 30 seconds, with some exceeding 2 minutes, in stark contrast to prior datasets where most trajectories lasted less than 5 seconds.
- **Superior Data Quality:** The human-in-the-loop data collection protocol, which includes meticulous verification, failure recovery data annotation, and iterative refinement based on policy deployment feedback, ensures exceptionally high data quality. An ablation study demonstrated that a smaller set of human-verified data (from "Wipe Table" task) yielded a 0.18 boost in completion score compared to a larger, unverified dataset, underscoring the critical importance of quality over mere quantity. Notably, the AgiBot World alpha dataset, despite being significantly smaller in volume (236 hours) than Open X-Embodiment (approximately 2000 hours), achieved a higher success rate in policy training, further highlighting its superior quality.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance with AgiBot World Dataset:** Policies (specifically, the RDT model) pre-trained on the AgiBot World dataset showed substantial performance gains compared to those trained on Open X-Embodiment (OXE). On average, an improvement of 30% in success rate was observed for in-domain scenarios and 29% for out-of-distribution scenarios. For instance, in the "Table Bussing" task, performance nearly tripled. Even with only 1/10th the data volume of OXE, the generalizability of pretrained policies was elevated by 18%, showcasing the dataset's efficiency.
- **GO-1 Outperforms Existing Generalist Policies:** The proposed Genie Operator-1 (GO-1) policy, leveraging latent action representations and pre-training on web-scale data, significantly outperformed prior generalist policies, including RDT-1B and a variant of GO-1 without the latent planner, as well as π0. GO-1 achieved over 60% success rate on complex tasks and an average performance improvement of 32% over the RDT approach. It demonstrated exceptional capabilities in tasks requiring robustness to object positions ("Pour Water") and strong instruction following ("Restock Beverage"), with the latent planner contributing an average improvement of 0.12 in task completion score.
- **Predictable Data Scalability:** GO-1's performance exhibits a robust and predictable power-law scaling relationship with increasing dataset size. An analysis using subsets of the AgiBot World dataset, ranging from 9.2k to 1M trajectories, yielded a Pearson correlation coefficient of r = 0.97, confirming that performance consistently improves as more data becomes available. This suggests a promising path for sustained advancement with larger datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "AgiBot World Colosseo" platform and the accompanying GO-1 policy represent a significant stride forward in robotic manipulation, addressing fundamental limitations that have hindered the development of general-purpose embodied intelligence.

- **Catalyst for Generalist Robotics:** The most immediate and profound impact lies in providing an unprecedented resource for generalist robot learning. By offering a large-scale, high-quality, and diverse dataset derived from real-world scenarios, AgiBot World breaks the long-standing data bottleneck in robotics. This enables researchers to train policies that can generalize far beyond narrow, controlled laboratory settings, accelerating the development of robots capable of operating in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Advancement in Policy Learning:** The introduction of GO-1, a novel generalist policy leveraging latent action representations and a hierarchical Vision-Language-Latent-Action (ViLLA) framework, marks a crucial advancement in policy learning. Its ability to effectively integrate web-scale vision-language knowledge with robot-specific action data, demonstrate predictable scaling with data volume, and outperform existing generalist policies in complex, long-horizon, and dexterous tasks, paves the way for more capable and robust robotic agents. The latent action approach offers an efficient mechanism to adapt powerful VLMs for real-world robotic control.
- **Democratization of Advanced Robotics Research:** The commitment to open-sourcing the dataset, toolchain, and pre-trained models is highly significant. It democratizes access to resources previously available only to well-funded labs or industry giants, fostering community-wide collaboration and innovation. This can accelerate the pace of research globally, allowing a broader range of researchers to tackle real-world robotic challenges and contribute to the shared goal of advanced embodied AI.
- **Enabling Real-World Applications:** The dataset's focus on reconstructing diverse real-world environments (domestic, retail, industrial, restaurant, office) and tasks (bimanual, dexterous, tool-use, multi-robot collaboration) directly targets practical applications. This work lays a strong foundation for developing intelligent robots that can perform complex everyday tasks, from household assistance and personal care to logistics, retail automation, and industrial manufacturing, bridging the gap between research and deployment.
- **Shaping Future Research Directions:** AgiBot World sets new benchmarks for data collection and policy evaluation. Its emphasis on detailed annotations, failure recovery data, and a human-in-the-loop quality assurance mechanism provides valuable insights for future data curation efforts. The framework for integrating VLMs with latent actions will likely influence subsequent research in robotic foundation models, pushing towards more unified and scalable approaches. The ongoing development of a corresponding simulation environment, as mentioned in the limitations, will further enhance reproducible research and rapid iteration.

In conclusion, AgiBot World Colosseo is more than just a dataset; it is a holistic ecosystem designed to be a cornerstone for future research in embodied AI, offering the essential ingredients—data, models, benchmarks, and a collaborative spirit—to empower robots to navigate and interact intelligently with the complexities of the real world.
