# Prompt Tuning Vision Language Models with Margin Regularizer for Few-Shot Learning under Distribution Shifts

> [!summary] Summary
> PromptMargin, developed by researchers at the Indian Institute of Science, introduces a prompt-tuning framework for adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP to few-shot learning scenarios with distribution shifts, without relying on source-domain data. The method integrates selective augmentation and a multimodal margin regularizer, demonstrating improved performance over baselines on 15 diverse datasets, including substantial accuracy gains of up to 10.79% on challenging domains.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often experience performance degradation when adapted to target datasets with limited labeled data (few-shot learning) and significant distribution shifts from their pre-training data.
- Traditional fine-tuning of large VLMs on few samples is prone to overfitting and catastrophic forgetting, while many few-shot adaptation methods require access to source-domain meta-training data, which is frequently unavailable in real-world applications.
- Effectively adapting VLMs in a 'completely source-free' manner, using only a few target samples and potentially unfamiliar class names, remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A ==prompt-tuning framework called PromptMargin== is introduced, which builds upon the MaPLe baseline by only fine-tuning learnable multimodal prompts (text and visual) while keeping the VLM backbone frozen.
- ==Selective Augmentation== is employed to mitigate overfitting in few-shot settings by dynamically filtering generated augmented images, retaining only those whose embeddings are sufficiently close to their corresponding class text embeddings.
- A ==Multimodal Margin Regularizer (MMReg)== is proposed, which enforces uniform separation not only for class text embeddings but also for class-wise image prototypes in the joint feature space, enhancing inter-class discrimination and multimodal consistency.

## Results

- PromptMargin achieved average accuracies of **72.00%** (1-shot) and **82.76%** (5-shot) across 15 datasets, outperforming the MaPLe baseline on 11 and 12 datasets, respectively.
- The method demonstrated substantial accuracy improvements, with gains up to **10.79%** on challenging datasets where class names were non-descriptive or unavailable (e.g., Plantae, Traffic Signs, ISIC with pseudo-classnames).
- An initial analysis revealed a strong negative Pearson correlation of **-0.713** between the proposed `diff_D_target` metric and zero-shot CLIP performance, indicating the utility of the metric for identifying adaptation opportunities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A quantifiable metric (`diff_D_target`) can estimate the semantic and distribution difference between a target dataset and CLIP's pre-training data, reliably predicting zero-shot performance and the potential for improvement via fine-tuning.
> - Filtering 'bad' or unhelpful augmentations through a selective process can reduce training time by approximately 40% and consistently improve or maintain classification accuracy in data-scarce few-shot learning.
> - Explicitly regularizing both text and image feature separation (multimodal margin) is critical for robust class discrimination and consistent performance gains, particularly in domains with weak or ambiguous class names.
