# Prompt Tuning Vision Language Models with Margin Regularizer for Few-Shot Learning under Distribution Shifts

> [!summary] Summary
> PromptMargin, developed by researchers at the Indian Institute of Science, introduces a prompt-tuning framework for adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP to few-shot learning scenarios with distribution shifts, without relying on source-domain data. The method integrates selective augmentation and a multimodal margin regularizer, demonstrating improved performance over baselines on 15 diverse datasets, including substantial accuracy gains of up to 10.79% on challenging domains.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often experience performance degradation when adapted to target datasets with limited labeled data (few-shot learning) and significant distribution shifts from their pre-training data.
- Traditional fine-tuning of large VLMs on few samples is prone to overfitting and catastrophic forgetting, while many few-shot adaptation methods require access to source-domain meta-training data, which is frequently unavailable in real-world applications.
- Effectively adapting VLMs in a 'completely source-free' manner, using only a few target samples and potentially unfamiliar class names, remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A ==prompt-tuning framework called PromptMargin== is introduced, which builds upon the MaPLe baseline by only fine-tuning learnable multimodal prompts (text and visual) while keeping the VLM backbone frozen.
- ==Selective Augmentation== is employed to mitigate overfitting in few-shot settings by dynamically filtering generated augmented images, retaining only those whose embeddings are sufficiently close to their corresponding class text embeddings.
- A ==Multimodal Margin Regularizer (MMReg)== is proposed, which enforces uniform separation not only for class text embeddings but also for class-wise image prototypes in the joint feature space, enhancing inter-class discrimination and multimodal consistency.

## Results

- PromptMargin achieved average accuracies of **72.00%** (1-shot) and **82.76%** (5-shot) across 15 datasets, outperforming the MaPLe baseline on 11 and 12 datasets, respectively.
- The method demonstrated substantial accuracy improvements, with gains up to **10.79%** on challenging datasets where class names were non-descriptive or unavailable (e.g., Plantae, Traffic Signs, ISIC with pseudo-classnames).
- An initial analysis revealed a strong negative Pearson correlation of **-0.713** between the proposed `diff_D_target` metric and zero-shot CLIP performance, indicating the utility of the metric for identifying adaptation opportunities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A quantifiable metric (`diff_D_target`) can estimate the semantic and distribution difference between a target dataset and CLIP's pre-training data, reliably predicting zero-shot performance and the potential for improvement via fine-tuning.
> - Filtering 'bad' or unhelpful augmentations through a selective process can reduce training time by approximately 40% and consistently improve or maintain classification accuracy in data-scarce few-shot learning.
> - Explicitly regularizing both text and image feature separation (multimodal margin) is critical for robust class discrimination and consistent performance gains, particularly in domains with weak or ambiguous class names.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving and critical areas in artificial intelligence: Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Few-Shot Learning (FSL), Prompt Learning, and robustness to Distribution Shifts.

The advent of large-scale Vision-Language foundation models like CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) and ALIGN, pre-trained on vast web-scale datasets, has revolutionized the field by demonstrating remarkable zero-shot generalization capabilities across diverse visual and textual domains. These models can classify unseen categories or perform tasks without explicit prior training for those specific tasks, merely by leveraging natural language descriptions.

However, despite their zero-shot prowess, adapting these powerful models to specific downstream tasks, especially those with limited labeled data (Few-Shot Learning) and significant deviations from their pre-training distribution (Distribution Shifts), remains a substantial challenge. Traditional fine-tuning of such large models on few samples often leads to overfitting or catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses its valuable generalized representations.

**Few-Shot Learning (FSL)** aims to enable models to learn new concepts from a very small number of examples. Existing FSL literature offers two main paradigms: meta-learning (training a model to learn how to learn across many tasks from a source dataset) and transfer learning (pre-training on a large source dataset and then fine-tuning on a small target dataset). Many current CLIP-based FSL methods, such as FDAlign and Wise-FT, involve full fine-tuning or parameter interpolation after meta-training on a source dataset (like mini-ImageNet). Other prompt-learning methods, like CoOp and MaPLe, also often involve meta-training prompts on a source dataset before adaptation.

**Prompt Learning** has emerged as an efficient alternative for adapting large pre-trained models. Instead of fine-tuning all model parameters, prompt learning introduces a small set of trainable parameters (prompts) that are prepended or inserted into the model's input or intermediate layers, allowing the frozen backbone model to be steered towards downstream tasks. Early works like CoOp focused on text prompts, while later developments like CoCoOp introduced conditional prompts, and MaPLe extended this to multimodal prompts, jointly optimizing both text and visual prompts.

This paper specifically addresses a relatively less explored but highly practical scenario: **few-shot learning under significant distribution shifts in a *completely source-free manner***. This means the model is directly adapted to a new target dataset using only a few examples from that dataset, without access to the large pre-training dataset of the VLM, and crucially, without any intermediate meta-training on a separate source domain. This is particularly relevant for real-world applications where target domains are specialized (e.g., medical images, satellite data) and distinct from common web data, and where source-domain meta-training data might not be available or suitable. The authors explicitly state that "no work in literature addresses the additional significant distribution shift problem in this context" using VLMs, highlighting the novelty and importance of their approach in filling this gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an effective method for adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically CLIP, to target datasets that exhibit significant distribution shifts and contain novel classes, using only a handful of labeled examples from the target dataset. This adaptation must be achieved in a "completely source-free setting," meaning without access to the original VLM pre-training data or any intermediate meta-training on auxiliary source datasets.

The motivations driving this research stem from several practical and theoretical challenges:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Distribution Shifts:** While VLMs generalize remarkably in zero-shot scenarios, their performance can degrade when target datasets have distributions very different from the pre-training data (e.g., specialized domains like medical images or satellite imagery) and contain categories unfamiliar to the model. Fine-tuning the entire large VLM on a few examples is prone to overfitting and loss of generalization, making efficient adaptation crucial.

2. **Unavailability of Pre-training Data and Source Domains:** The vast pre-training datasets for VLMs like CLIP are typically not publicly available. Furthermore, in many real-world FSL scenarios, a suitable source domain for meta-training (as often assumed in traditional FSL) might not exist or be accessible. This necessitates methods that can directly adapt the *original* pre-trained VLM using only the few target samples.

3. **Estimating Finetuning Potential:** Given the opaque nature of VLM pre-training data, it is unclear *when* fine-tuning might actually improve performance over zero-shot evaluation. The authors are motivated to first provide an empirical method to assess this potential by analyzing the common vision-language embedding space.

4. **Handling Unfamiliar or Unavailable Class Names:** Prompt tuning often relies on meaningful class names to construct textual prompts. However, for specialized target datasets, class names might be highly technical, generic, or even unavailable, making standard prompt construction ineffective. The research aims to develop a robust method that can learn discriminative classifiers even under such conditions.

In essence, the paper aims to enhance the practical utility and deployment of powerful VLMs by making them robustly adaptable to new, challenging, and data-limited real-world domains without relying on extensive auxiliary data or complex multi-stage training pipelines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **PromptMargin**, is a novel prompt-tuning method designed for few-shot learning under distribution shifts in a completely source-free setting. It builds upon multimodal prompt learning, using MaPLe (Khattak et al., 2023) as its baseline, and introduces two main modules: Selective Augmentation and Multimodal Margin Regularizer (MMReg).

**Initial Analysis of CLIP Representation Space:**
Before proposing PromptMargin, the authors first investigate whether one can estimate CLIP's performance and the need for fine-tuning given a target dataset. They propose a simple metric, `diff_D_target`, which approximates the combined semantic and distribution difference between a target dataset and CLIP's pre-training data. This is calculated based on the mean inter-class L2 distances of both text (`m_T`) and image (`m_V`) features in the frozen zero-shot CLIP embedding space. A high `diff_D_target` indicates poor initial class separation and thus potential for improvement through fine-tuning, while a low value suggests well-separated classes where fine-tuning might be less beneficial or even detrimental. This analysis guides the motivation for targeted adaptation.

**PromptMargin Framework:**
PromptMargin operates in an N-way k-shot episodic setting, directly fine-tuning only the learnable prompts on the few samples available in the support set of the target dataset, while keeping the main CLIP encoder parameters frozen. The core loss function is a cross-entropy loss (`L_CE`) on the cosine similarity between image and text embeddings.

1. **Selective Augmentation:**
 To mitigate overfitting due to data scarcity in the few-shot setting, PromptMargin employs a selective augmentation strategy. Instead of using all generated augmentations (e.g., HorizontalFlip, RandomRotation, ColorJitter), it selectively chooses a subset of augmented images. The selection criterion is based on the cosine similarity of the augmented image's embedding with its corresponding class text embedding in the joint representation space. Only those augmentations whose embeddings are "close enough" (i.e., have higher cosine similarity) to their respective text embeddings are retained. This approach aims to filter out "bad" augmentations that might distort class-relevant information, ensuring that only representative and helpful augmented examples contribute to training. This also helps reduce training time.

2. **Multimodal Margin Regularizer (MMReg):**
 This module addresses the challenge of handling unfamiliar or specialized categories, which may not be well-represented or meaningfully named in CLIP's latent space. MMReg aims to enforce better inter-class discrimination and consistent separation between class-wise image prototypes and text embeddings.
- It introduces a regularization term `R(X_T)` that forces the class text embeddings (`X_T`) to be uniformly separated by a mean distance (`μ_t`) in the feature space. This is based on the idea of spreading out classifier weights, adapted here for text representations.
- Crucially, it adds a second regularization term `R(X_V)` that enforces the class-wise image prototypes (`X_V` - mean of class-wise image embeddings) to also be separated by the *same* mean distance (`μ_t`) as the text embeddings. This ensures multimodal consistency and robust class discrimination.
- The final loss function becomes `L_total = L_CE + R(X_T) + R(X_V)`, jointly optimizing for classification accuracy and uniform multimodal inter-class separation.

**Implementation Details:**
The framework utilizes the CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. Learnable text prompts are initialized with a standard phrase ("A photo of a"). A linear layer projects text prompts to visual prompts, enabling coupled training. Deep prompting (adding learnable prompts after every transformer block) is used. The method is trained with SGD for 150 epochs. For 1-shot setting, 15 selective augmentations are used; for 5-shot, 3 selective augmentations per class (total 15 per class).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental results across fifteen diverse benchmark datasets, covering various distribution shifts (e.g., medical images, satellite images, handwritten characters), to demonstrate the effectiveness of PromptMargin.

1. **Analysis of CLIP Representation Space (Table 1):**
 The initial empirical analysis revealed a strong negative Pearson correlation of -0.713 between the `diff_D_target` metric (estimated distribution and semantic difference) and zero-shot CLIP performance. Datasets with a high `diff_D_target` (meaning poorly separated classes in CLIP's latent space) showed poor zero-shot performance but significant improvement after prompt-tuning with MaPLe. Conversely, datasets with a low `diff_D_target` (well-separated classes) had high zero-shot performance, with minimal or even adverse effects from further fine-tuning. This validates the need for adaptive methods and provides insight into when they might be most beneficial.

2. **Effectiveness of Selective Augmentation (Table 2):**
 The selective augmentation module was shown to reduce training time by approximately 40% compared to using a larger number of generic augmentations, while consistently maintaining or improving classification accuracy across several datasets. This demonstrates its efficiency and ability to filter out unhelpful or distorting augmentations.

3. **Impact of Multimodal Margin Regularizer (MMReg) (Tables 3, 6, Figs. 2, 6):**
- **Improved Discrimination:** MMReg significantly improved performance, especially for datasets where original class names were unavailable or semantically unmeaningful (e.g., Plantae, Traffic Signs, ISIC with pseudo-classnames), yielding absolute accuracy gains up to +2.79%.
- **Multimodal Consistency:** Ablation studies (Table 3) showed that simply regularizing text embeddings alone (`MaPLe + sel. augs + text reg`) did not consistently improve performance and sometimes even reduced it. The multimodal nature of MMReg, which jointly regularizes both text and image feature separation, was crucial for consistent gains.
- **Feature Space Visualization:** t-SNE visualizations (Fig. 6) and L2 distance heatmaps (Fig. 2) qualitatively illustrated that MMReg leads to more compactly clustered and uniformly separated class embeddings in the joint feature space, enhancing discriminability.

4. **Overall Performance Comparison (Table 4):**
 PromptMargin consistently outperformed the strong prompt-tuning baseline, MaPLe, across most datasets:
- **5-way 1-shot setting:** PromptMargin achieved an average accuracy of 72.00%, outperforming MaPLe (69.02%) on 11 out of 15 datasets. Notably, it showed substantial gains (e.g., 10.79% on Plantae and Traffic Signs) where class names were challenging.
- **5-way 5-shot setting:** PromptMargin achieved an average accuracy of 82.76%, outperforming MaPLe (81.92%) on 12 out of 15 datasets.
- **Comparison with Full Finetuning:** PromptMargin also generally outperformed or was competitive with full fine-tuning methods like Wise-FT and FDAlign, which require meta-training on source datasets. This highlights the efficacy of source-free prompt tuning.
- **Edge Cases:** For some datasets (e.g., mini-ImageNet, Aircraft), where the latent space was already well-separated, PromptMargin showed slight decrements, indicating that aggressive regularization can sometimes be counterproductive if the initial alignment is already strong.

5. **Generalizability to Other VLMs (Table 9):**
 Experiments with OpenCLIP B/16, an alternative VLM, further confirmed PromptMargin's effectiveness, consistently outperforming MaPLe across tested datasets, demonstrating the robustness of the proposed framework beyond a single CLIP backbone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Prompt Tuning Vision Language Models with Margin Regularizer for Few-Shot Learning under Distribution Shifts" offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact:

1. **Pioneering Source-Free Adaptation under Distribution Shifts:** This research is one of the first to effectively address the challenging problem of few-shot learning under significant distribution shifts using Vision-Language Models in a completely source-free setting. This is a critical advancement, as it removes the dependency on large source datasets for meta-training, making VLM adaptation more practical and accessible for real-world, niche applications where source data might be unavailable or irrelevant.

2. **Enhanced Practicality for Specialized Domains:** The ability to adapt VLMs to datasets with highly specialized or unfamiliar categories (e.g., medical images like Chest X-Ray or ISIC, satellite imagery like EuroSAT, or specific plant/animal species) without relying on meaningful class names or extensive domain-specific data is highly impactful. This expands the utility of powerful foundation models to domains traditionally challenged by data scarcity and domain expertise requirements.

3. **Robustness in Data-Limited Scenarios:** The proposed Selective Augmentation strategy directly addresses the problem of overfitting in few-shot settings by intelligently curating training data, ensuring that only high-quality, relevant augmentations contribute to learning. This leads to more robust prompt learning from very few examples.

4. **Improved Discriminability in Latent Space:** The novel Multimodal Margin Regularizer (MMReg) is a key innovation. By explicitly enforcing uniform separation between class-wise text and image embeddings, it tackles the issue of ambiguous or poorly separated classes in the VLM's latent space, especially when class semantics are weak or missing. This directly translates to better classification performance and model generalization.

5. **Efficiency and Accessibility:** As a prompt-tuning method, PromptMargin is parameter-efficient, requiring fine-tuning only a small number of parameters rather than the entire large VLM. This significantly reduces computational costs and memory requirements, making the adaptation process faster and more accessible on standard hardware, thus democratizing the use of powerful foundation models.

6. **Guidance for VLM Deployment:** The initial analysis of CLIP's representation space provides valuable insights into when and where fine-tuning is most likely to yield benefits. This analytical tool can guide practitioners in deciding whether further adaptation is necessary for a given target dataset, saving computational resources and effort.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong baseline and opens new avenues for future research in robust prompt learning, dynamic and adaptive augmentation strategies, more sophisticated multimodal regularization techniques, and mechanisms to handle scenarios where distribution shifts are so extreme that even prompt tuning struggles.

In conclusion, PromptMargin represents a significant step towards making large-scale Vision-Language Models more adaptable, robust, and practical for deployment in diverse, data-constrained, and distribution-shifted real-world scenarios, thereby broadening the impact of foundation models in artificial intelligence.
