# FastDSAC: Enhancing Policy Plasticity via Constrained Exploration for Scalable Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> FastDSAC, a lightweight variant of Distributional Soft Actor-Critic, enhances training stability and preserves policy plasticity for scalable humanoid locomotion in high-throughput environments. It employs a mean-centered truncation strategy for target actions, yielding higher returns and faster convergence across challenging control tasks.

## Problem

- High data volume and update frequency in massively parallel reinforcement learning can destabilize value-based methods due to noisy target Q-value estimates from extreme sampled actions.
- Policy networks often lose plasticity and adaptability over training, leading to performance regressions, particularly under aggressive optimization with high update-to-data ratios.
- Existing fast reinforcement learning approaches often rely on discrete value distributions, which may not provide the most accurate value estimation for complex continuous control tasks.

## Method

- Employs a ==diagonal Gaussian policy parameterization==, directly outputting mean and standard deviation without ==tanh squashing==, to improve gradient flow and numerical stability.
- Extends the ==Distributional Soft Actor-Critic (DSAC)== framework, modeling the state-action return as a Gaussian distribution with an adaptively learned variance.
- Introduces a novel ==mean-centered truncation mapping== for target actions, a′=μϕ(s′)+ctanh⁡(u′−μϕ(s′))a' = \mu_ϕ(s') + c \tanh(u' - \mu_ϕ(s'))a′=μϕ(s′)+ctanh(u′−μϕ(s′)), used only for target Q-value estimation to reduce target noise.

## Results

- Achieved the highest final returns across all **four** MuJoCo Playground tasks, demonstrating rapid early gains and stable late-stage learning without performance regressions.
- Exhibited comparable wall-clock training efficiency to FastTD3 and DSAC-T, while attaining higher returns earlier in training time across MuJoCo tasks.
- Demonstrated consistent and improved performance across **six** challenging HumanoidBench locomotion tasks, surpassing baselines like FastSAC and DSAC-T in stability and asymptotic returns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Regularizing target actions through mean-centered truncation effectively stabilizes critic learning by reducing variance in target Q-value estimates, especially in high-throughput settings.
> - The constrained exploration mechanism implicitly provided by target action truncation preserves policy plasticity, enabling efficient learning under high update-to-data ratios without performance degradation.
> - Utilizing a continuous Gaussian distributional critic with adaptive variance, combined with target action truncation, yields more accurate value estimation and higher, more stable rewards per transition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in solving high-dimensional continuous-control problems, including complex humanoid locomotion. A significant trend in this area is the adoption of massively parallel simulation, often GPU-accelerated, to increase data throughput and improve wall-clock efficiency during training. This approach enables rapid iteration and is increasingly used with both on-policy algorithms like PPO and off-policy methods such as SAC and TD3.

While parallel simulation accelerates training, it introduces challenges, particularly for off-policy actor-critic methods. The rapid increase in data volume and update frequency can destabilize value-based learning, making critic updates sensitive to noise in temporal difference (TD) errors. This sensitivity can erode "learning plasticity," a phenomenon where agents become less responsive to new data as training progresses, potentially leading to performance regressions. Prior research has identified the inaccuracy of target Q-value estimates as a key source of this instability, particularly when large mini-batches increase the likelihood of sampling extreme, low-probability actions that yield high-variance target Q-value estimates.

Existing strategies to address these issues include regularizing target value estimation through techniques like target policy smoothing (used in TD3), action-space smoothing, and ensemble-based stabilization methods such as REDQ and DroQ. Distributional critics, ranging from categorical to Gaussian approaches (e.g., DSAC) and truncation-based variants like TQC, also aim to refine value estimation. However, some of these methods introduce additional computational overhead, larger network ensembles, or more complex training objectives.

This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to improve the stability and performance of off-policy, entropy-regularized actor-critic methods under high-throughput, massively parallel training regimes. It builds upon the Distributional Soft Actor-Critic (DSAC) framework, aiming to develop a lightweight solution that stabilizes training and preserves policy plasticity without incurring substantial computational costs. The proposed method differentiates itself from prior fast RL approaches like FastTD3 and FastSAC, which often rely on discrete value distributions, by employing a continuous Gaussian representation with adaptive variance regulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing FastDSAC stem from the challenges encountered when scaling reinforcement learning to achieve fast and efficient humanoid locomotion:

- **Addressing Training Instability in High-Throughput Settings:** The increasing use of massively parallel simulation architectures significantly compresses training time by accelerating data collection. However, this also leads to a rapid increase in data volume and update frequency, which can destabilize value-based methods. Specifically, in off-policy actor-critic algorithms, target Q-value estimates, crucial for critic learning, become more susceptible to noise. When large mini-batches are processed, the probability of sampling extreme or out-of-distribution actions from the target policy increases, leading to higher variance in target Q-value estimates and, consequently, in TD errors. This noise can hinder stable critic learning and slow down overall policy improvement. FastDSAC aims to mitigate this by stabilizing target Q-value estimation.

- **Counteracting the Loss of Policy Plasticity:** A related challenge in scaled-up deep RL training is the "plasticity loss," where policy networks gradually lose their ability to adapt to new data or refine their behavior as training progresses. This phenomenon, linked to primacy bias and late-stage performance regressions, reduces the adaptability of the policy network, making it harder for the agent to achieve optimal performance or recover from suboptimal trajectories. FastDSAC is motivated by the need to preserve this plasticity, particularly when aggressive gradient updates are common under high update-to-data (UTD) ratios.

- **Improving Value Estimation Accuracy with Continuous Distributions:** Some fast reinforcement learning approaches, such as FastTD3 and FastSAC, utilize discrete value distributions. While effective, the authors hypothesize that a continuous Gaussian representation, particularly when equipped with adaptive variance regulation, can offer more accurate value estimation. By avoiding exploration of epistemically uncertain regions and retaining high-reward transitions, a more precise value function can guide policy learning more effectively. FastDSAC aims to leverage a continuous Gaussian value distribution to enhance this accuracy.

- **Achieving Scalable and Efficient Humanoid Locomotion:** The overarching goal is to enable scalable humanoid locomotion with improved asymptotic performance and faster convergence. This requires a method that not only addresses stability and plasticity but also maintains computational efficiency comparable to state-of-the-art baselines, making it suitable for practical, high-throughput training environments.

In summary, FastDSAC seeks to enhance the stability and adaptability of off-policy reinforcement learning in parallel sampling environments by introducing a constrained exploration mechanism. This mechanism is designed to stabilize target Q-value estimates, preserve policy plasticity, and improve the accuracy of value functions, ultimately leading to more robust and efficient learning for complex robotic control tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FastDSAC is presented as a lightweight variant of the Distributional Actor-Critic (DSAC) algorithm, tailored for high-parallel off-policy training. Its core methodological contributions revolve around a specific approach to policy parameterization and a novel mean-centered truncation strategy for target actions.

#### 3.1. A. Gaussian Policy Parameterization

The actor network in FastDSAC parameterizes a diagonal Gaussian policy, outputting the mean ($\mu_\phi(s)$) and standard deviation ($\sigma_\phi(s)$) for a given state $s$. Actions are sampled using the reparameterization trick: $a = \mu_\phi(s) + \sigma_\phi(s) \odot \epsilon$, where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$. The log-probability of an action is computed directly from this Gaussian distribution, avoiding the need for a tanh squashing function commonly used in other methods. This design offers two advantages:
1. **Gradient Flow:** It circumvents potential vanishing gradients when actions approach bounds, which can occur with tanh non-linearity.
2. **Numerical Stability:** It eliminates the log-likelihood correction factor associated with the change-of-variables rule for tanh-squashed policies, simplifying implementation and improving numerical stability.

To further enhance stability, the policy mean $\mu_\phi(s)$ is bounded within a predefined range ($[m_{\min}, m_{\max}]$), preventing excessively large mean actions early in training. Similarly, the policy standard deviation $\sigma_\phi(s)$ is initialized to a small value and constrained within ($[\ell_{\min}, \ell_{\max}]$).

#### 3.2. B. Distributional Actor-Critic (DSAC Foundation)

FastDSAC builds upon the DSAC framework, which models the conditional distribution of the state-action return ($Z^\pi(s, a)$) rather than just its expectation. The critic network parameterizes this value distribution as a Gaussian: $Z_\theta(\cdot | s, a) = \mathcal{N}(Q_\theta(s, a), \sigma^2_\theta(s, a))$. A target network with parameters $\theta'$ is used for stable target construction.

- **Policy Evaluation:** For a transition $(s, a, r, s')$, a next action $a'$ is sampled, and a target return $y_z = r + \gamma(Z' - \alpha \log \pi_\phi(a' | s'))$ is formed, where $Z' \sim Z_{\theta'}(\cdot | s', a')$. The critic is trained by maximizing the likelihood of $y_z$ under $Z_\theta(\cdot | s, a)$, using a loss function based on the squared error between $y_z$ and $Q_\theta(s, a)$, scaled by the variance $\sigma^2_\theta(s, a)$, along with a log $\sigma_\theta(s, a)$ term.
- **Policy Improvement:** The actor is trained to maximize the objective $E_{s \sim \mathcal{B}, a \sim \pi_\phi(\cdot | s)}[Q_\theta(s, a) - \alpha \log \pi_\phi(a | s)]$, using the mean value from the critic. An entropy regularization term controlled by $\alpha$ (temperature) is included.

#### 3.3. C. Mean-Centered Truncation for Target Actions

This is the central novel component of FastDSAC. The method decouples exploration from target Q-value estimation by applying a regularization exclusively to the target action used in the target Q-network.

1. **Pre-truncation Sampling:** Given the next state $s'$, a pre-truncation action $u'$ is sampled from the policy's diagonal Gaussian distribution: $u' = \mu_\phi(s') + \sigma_\phi(s') \odot \epsilon$.
2. **Mean-Centered Truncation Mapping:** The target action $a'$ is then derived by applying a mean-centered truncation mapping: $a' = \mu_\phi(s') + c \tanh(u' - \mu_\phi(s'))$. Here, $c$ is a truncation radius. This element-wise tanh function smoothly squashes the offset $(u' - \mu_\phi(s'))$, ensuring that each component of $a' - \mu_\phi(s')$ is strictly bounded within $[-c, c]$. This continuous mapping avoids discontinuities associated with hard clipping. This mapping is applied *only* when evaluating the target Q-value $Q_{\theta'}(s', a')$, not for the actions taken by the agent during interaction.
3. **Consistent Log-Probability Computation:** Since the target Q-values are evaluated at the truncated action $a'$, the entropy term in the Bellman backup must use the log-probability consistent with this induced action distribution. The change-of-variables rule is applied: $\log \pi_\phi(a' | s') = \log \pi_\phi(u' | s') - \log |\det(\frac{\partial a'}{\partial u'})|$. The Jacobian term involves $c[1 - \tanh^2(u'_i - \mu_{\phi,i}(s'))]$, which is reformulated using a softplus identity for numerical stability in PyTorch implementations.

By constraining the target action, FastDSAC aims to reduce the influence of rare, extreme next actions on target Q-values, thereby yielding lower-variance TD errors and improving training stability. The consistency in log-probability calculation ensures that the entropy regularizer remains properly aligned with the resulting action distribution, preserving necessary stochasticity for exploration around the policy mean. This implicit regularization is intended to alleviate plasticity loss, especially beneficial during early training phases with high UTD ratios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of FastDSAC was evaluated across ten locomotion tasks from two benchmark suites: MuJoCo Playground (4 tasks) and HumanoidBench (6 tasks). Experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, with results averaged over three random seeds. Baselines included FastSAC, FastTD3, DSAC-T, and PPO (on MuJoCo Playground).

#### 4.1. A. Sample Efficiency (MuJoCo Playground)

- **Comparison with Baselines:** FastDSAC consistently achieved the highest final returns across all four MuJoCo Playground tasks when measured against environment steps (Figure 1).
- **Performance Characteristics:** FastDSAC exhibited rapid early gains, similar to FastSAC, but maintained stable late-stage learning without the performance regressions observed in FastSAC. This behavior suggests improved learning plasticity preservation.
- **Challenging Tasks:** On tasks like G1JoystickRoughTerrain, FastDSAC matched FastSAC's early progress but continued to improve, demonstrating robust performance in more complex environments.
- **Off-policy vs. On-policy:** PPO, an on-policy baseline, showed significantly slower improvement and lower asymptotic returns compared to all off-policy methods, reinforcing the benefits of off-policy training in this context.

#### 4.2. B. Time Efficiency (MuJoCo Playground)

- **Wall-Clock Performance:** FastDSAC's overall wall-clock training time was comparable to FastTD3 and DSAC-T (Figure 2). FastSAC showed higher per-step latency, resulting in longer total wall-clock times.
- **Early Convergence:** FastDSAC attained higher returns earlier in wall-clock time than FastTD3. Although FastSAC was slightly faster initially on T1Joystick tasks, FastDSAC surpassed it and converged to a higher, more stable return. On G1Joystick tasks, FastDSAC matched FastSAC's real-time learning speed while achieving superior final returns.

#### 4.3. C. Transition Rewards (MuJoCo Playground)

- **Reward Stability:** FastDSAC achieved higher and more stable per-transition rewards during data collection compared to FastTD3, FastSAC, DSAC-T, and PPO across all four tasks (Figure 3).
- **Contributing Factors:** This improvement is attributed to two factors: the use of a Gaussian distributional critic with adaptively learned variance (improving value estimation accuracy by reducing overconfident updates in uncertain regions) and the mean-centered truncation applied to target actions (stabilizing Q-value learning and making policy updates more reliable).

#### 4.4. D. Cross-Suite Generalization (HumanoidBench)

- **Consistent Performance:** FastDSAC demonstrated consistent performance across six HumanoidBench locomotion tasks (Figure 4), a more challenging suite with whole-body tasks.
- **Stability and Plasticity:** Unlike FastSAC, which showed weaker and less consistent outcomes on HumanoidBench, FastDSAC maintained stable learning and better preserved plasticity, leading to more reliable improvements.
- **Comparative Advantage:** FastDSAC generally performed on par with or better than FastTD3 and substantially outperformed DSAC-T on these tasks.

#### 4.5. E. Robustness to Dynamics Perturbations

- **Qualitative Evidence:** A qualitative assessment under perturbed mass parameters and ground friction coefficients (Figure 5) suggested that the learned policy could maintain a stable, natural, and coordinated walking gait without significant posture degradation. This provides preliminary evidence of robustness.

#### 4.6. F. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Truncation Radius (c):** FastDSAC showed moderate sensitivity to the truncation radius $c$ (Figure 6). Varying $c \in \{10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-4}\}$ primarily affected early learning speed, with final returns remaining similar. A larger $c$ could speed up early learning by allowing more target action variability, while a smaller $c$ made targets more conservative. $c=10^{-3}$ was identified as a robust default.
- **Impact of Target Action Choice:** Using the mean-centered truncated action ($a'$) yielded the best learning behavior, especially on complex rough terrain (Figure 7). Raw Gaussian samples ($u'$) performed competitively but slower in early training on complex terrains due to noisier target Q-values. Using the deterministic policy mean ($\mu_\phi(s')$) generally underperformed, as it limited target-action diversity and exploratory coverage.
- **Impact of Parallelism and UTD Ratios:** Increasing the number of parallel environments (from 256 to 1024) consistently improved performance without late-stage regressions (Figure 8a, 8b), indicating effective noise suppression by the truncation mapping. Increasing the update-to-data (UTD) ratio (from 2 to 8) accelerated early learning to strong returns without causing late-stage decline (Figure 8c, 8d), supporting the hypothesis that truncation helps preserve learning plasticity under aggressive optimization.
- **Transfer to SAC:** Integrating the mean-centered truncation mapping into a raw Gaussian SAC variant consistently improved its performance over both the raw Gaussian SAC baseline without truncation and the tanh-squashed SAC baseline (Figure 9). This suggests the mechanism is not specific to FastDSAC and has broader applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FastDSAC method presents a contribution to the field of reinforcement learning, particularly for scalable robot locomotion in high-throughput environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Training Stability and Performance:** By introducing a mean-centered truncation to target actions, FastDSAC effectively mitigates the high variance in target Q-value estimates that typically arises from extreme actions in massively parallel sampling scenarios. This leads to more stable critic learning, which in turn facilitates faster convergence and higher asymptotic performance in policy optimization. The empirical results demonstrate that FastDSAC achieves superior performance and faster learning speeds compared to several state-of-the-art baselines on complex humanoid locomotion tasks.

- **Preservation of Policy Plasticity:** A key challenge in large-scale RL is the loss of policy plasticity, where networks become less adaptable over time. FastDSAC's constrained exploration, acting as an implicit regularization, addresses this by ensuring that policy updates are guided by more reliable value estimates. This allows for the effective use of high update-to-data (UTD) ratios, accelerating early training without the late-stage performance regressions often associated with aggressive updates, thereby improving the overall learning efficiency and robustness.

- **Computational Efficiency:** The proposed method is described as lightweight, involving a simple, continuous truncation mapping and consistent log-probability computation with negligible computational overhead. This is crucial for its applicability in high-throughput settings where wall-clock time is a critical performance metric. FastDSAC maintains comparable wall-clock efficiency to other strong off-policy baselines while delivering better performance.

- **Generalizability and Robustness:** The method's effectiveness is demonstrated across two distinct benchmark suites (MuJoCo Playground and HumanoidBench), suggesting good generalization properties. The ablation studies further confirm its robustness to variations in hyperparameters (truncation radius) and its benefits when integrated into other SAC variants, indicating broader applicability beyond the specific DSAC framework. The preliminary qualitative assessment of robustness to dynamics perturbations also suggests potential for real-world deployment.

- **Advancement in Off-policy RL for Robotics:** FastDSAC offers a practical recipe for stabilizing and improving off-policy reinforcement learning, which is a dominant paradigm for continuous control problems in robotics. Its focus on managing the challenges introduced by massively parallel simulation directly addresses a contemporary bottleneck in scaling RL for complex robotic tasks, particularly humanoid locomotion.

Future work outlined by the authors, including plasticity-specific diagnostics, representation-level analyses, qualitative behavior evaluation, and sim-to-real experiments, indicates avenues for deeper understanding and further validation of FastDSAC's benefits and underlying mechanisms. This continued research could solidify its impact on developing more robust and efficient learning agents for advanced robotic applications.
