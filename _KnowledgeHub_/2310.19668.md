# DrM: Mastering Visual Reinforcement Learning through Dormant Ratio Minimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DRM, a model-free visual reinforcement learning algorithm, improves sample efficiency, asymptotic performance, and robustness by actively minimizing the dormant neuron ratio within an agent's policy network. This approach enables the reliable solution of complex tasks such as DeepMind Control Suite's 'Dog' and Adroit's dexterous hand manipulation from pixel observations, which were previously intractable for model-free methods.

## Problem

- Existing visual RL algorithms suffer from low sample efficiency, requiring extensive environmental interactions to learn complex tasks.
- Achieving optimal asymptotic performance is challenging, especially in tasks with high degrees of freedom or sparse reward signals.
- A lack of robustness leads to performance sensitivity to hyperparameters and frequent 'broken seeds' where learning fails across different runs.

## Method

- **DrM**'s ==Dormant-Ratio-Guided Perturbation== periodically perturbs agent model weights, using a soft reset with a perturb factor dynamically controlled by the dormant ratio to encourage new behaviors.
- An ==Awaken Exploration Scheduler== adaptively adjusts exploration noise based on the dormant ratio, increasing noise when inactive and transitioning to a linear decay upon 'awakening'.
- ==Dormant-Ratio-Guided Exploitation== modifies target value calculation in actor-critic methods, prioritizing exploitation of successful trajectories when the dormant ratio is low.

## Results

- DRM reliably solves previously intractable model-free visual RL tasks, including DeepMind Control Suite's complex 'Dog' and 'Manipulator' domains and demonstration-free dexterous hand manipulation in Adroit.
- It achieved significant quantitative improvements in sample efficiency (e.g., **70%** fewer samples on DeepMind Control Suite) and asymptotic performance (e.g., **75%** improvement on Adroit) compared to leading baselines.
- The algorithm demonstrated enhanced robustness with notably reduced performance variance across random seeds, reporting 'no broken seeds' in **76** total runs on diverse benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sustained motor and neuronal inactivity in early training phases is a critical underlying cause of poor exploration and skill acquisition in visual RL.
> - The 'dormant ratio' (percentage of minimally active neurons within the policy network) serves as an intrinsic, reward-agnostic metric of an agent's activity level and behavioral diversity.
> - A sharp decline in the dormant ratio strongly correlates with the agent's transition from inactivity to meaningful action and skill acquisition, often preceding external reward increases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits Into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual Reinforcement Learning (RL), where agents learn to control systems directly from high-dimensional pixel observations, is a rapidly evolving but challenging field. Current algorithms, despite advancements, still face significant hurdles:
1. **Sample Efficiency:** They often require millions, if not billions, of environmental interactions to learn complex tasks.
2. **Asymptotic Performance:** Even with extensive training, the final performance can be suboptimal, especially in tasks with complex kinematics or sparse rewards.
3. **Robustness:** Performance can be highly sensitive to hyperparameter choices and random seeds, leading to "broken seeds" where agents fail to learn.
4. **Complex Tasks:** Challenges arise in environments with high degrees of freedom (DoFs), such as humanoid or dexterous hand manipulation, and those with sparse reward signals.

The existing landscape of visual RL methods can be broadly categorized into model-based and model-free approaches. Model-based methods (e.g., Dreamer series) aim to learn a world model to predict future states and rewards, often improving sample efficiency. Model-free methods, which are the focus of this paper, directly learn a policy without explicitly modeling the environment. Advancements in model-free visual RL have leveraged:
- **Data Augmentation:** Techniques like RAD and DrQ/DrQ-v2 to improve data efficiency and robustness.
- **Self-Supervised Representation Learning:** Methods like CURL, ATC, TACO, SPR, SR-SPR to learn meaningful state and action representations.
- **Regularization:** Approaches like A-LIX to stabilize temporal difference learning.
- **High Update-to-Data (UTD) Ratios** and **Ensemble Q-learning** to enhance sample efficiency.
- **Exploration Strategies:** Both uncertainty-oriented (UCB-based) and intrinsic motivation-oriented (prediction error, state novelty, entropy maximization) to guide agents in discovering useful behaviors. Parameter space noise and action noise injection are also common.

This paper, DRM, firmly positions itself within the model-free visual RL paradigm. It tackles the core problems of sample inefficiency, suboptimal asymptotic performance, and lack of robustness by identifying a novel underlying cause: **sustained motor and neuronal inactivity** during early training. This directly relates to the broader research on "loss of expressivity" or "dormant neurons" in deep RL, where neural networks tend to lose their capacity to fit new targets over time. DRM distinguishes itself by using the "dormant ratio" as an *intrinsic, reward-agnostic metric* to diagnose this inactivity and actively guide exploration and exploitation, rather than relying solely on external rewards or generic noise injection. By addressing this fundamental issue of "neural network dormancy," DRM aims to provide a more principled and robust solution to effective exploration and learning in complex visual environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly improve the sample efficiency, asymptotic performance, and robustness of visual reinforcement learning algorithms** for complex continuous control tasks, especially those with high degrees of freedom and sparse rewards.

The authors identify a critical shortcoming in existing visual RL methods: agents frequently exhibit **sustained inactivity, both motorically and neurally, during early training phases**. This inactivity severely limits their ability to explore the environment effectively and acquire meaningful skills. They observe that:
- Agents often remain physically immobile or engage in repetitive, non-productive actions early on.
- This motor inactivity is strongly correlated with a **high rate of dormant neurons** (neurons with minimal activation) within the policy networks.
- Conversely, a decline in this "dormant ratio" (percentage of dormant neurons) precedes and correlates with the acquisition of new skills, even before a significant increase in external reward signals.

This crucial insight motivates the development of a new framework. Traditional exploration strategies often rely on reward signals, which can be misleading in sparse reward settings or may not accurately reflect the agent's internal learning state. The dormant ratio, however, appears to be an **inherent gauge of an agent's activity level and behavioral diversity, independent of external rewards.**

Therefore, the **key motivation** is to leverage this discovered correlation to design a novel, intrinsic mechanism that actively minimizes the dormant ratio. By doing so, DRM aims to:
- Break agents out of early-stage inactivity.
- Promote more effective and purposeful exploration.
- Adaptively balance exploration and exploitation based on the agent's actual learning progress (as reflected by its neural network activity), leading to faster and more stable skill acquisition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DRM (Dormant Ratio Minimization) is a model-free visual RL algorithm built upon the insight that minimizing the dormant ratio within an agent's policy network improves exploration and skill acquisition. It integrates three core mechanisms:

#### 3.1. Dormant Ratio Definition and Observation

The paper adopts the definition of dormant neurons from Sokar et al. (2023). A neuron is considered $\tau$-dormant if its average absolute output over an input distribution is below a certain threshold relative to the average output of all neurons in its layer. The dormant ratio ($\beta_\tau$) is the total number of $\tau$-dormant neurons across all layers divided by the total number of neurons in the network.
The key empirical observation is that a sharp decline in the dormant ratio of the policy network is an early indicator of meaningful action and skill acquisition, often preceding any significant increase in reward. This suggests the dormant ratio is an intrinsic metric of behavioral variety and learning progress.

#### 3.2. Dormant-Ratio-Guided Perturbation

This mechanism aims to "wake up" dormant neurons and refresh the network's expressivity. Periodically (e.g., every 200,000 frames), when the dormant ratio is high, the agent's model weights are perturbed using a soft reset method:
$\theta_t = \alpha \theta_{t-1} + (1 - \alpha) \phi$, where $\phi$ are randomly initialized weights.
The perturb factor $\alpha$ is dynamically controlled by the dormant ratio $\beta$: $\alpha = \text{clip}(1 - k\beta, \alpha_{\text{min}}, \alpha_{\text{max}})$, where $k$ is the perturb rate. A higher dormant ratio (indicating inactivity) leads to a smaller $\alpha$, meaning a stronger perturbation towards random initialization, thus promoting more significant changes in the network weights to encourage new behaviors.

#### 3.3. Awaken Exploration Scheduler

Instead of a fixed or linearly decaying exploration noise, DRM dynamically adjusts the exploration noise standard deviation ($\sigma(t)$) based on the dormant ratio.
When the dormant ratio $\beta$ is high (agent is inactive), a larger exploration noise is applied to encourage diverse actions. As $\beta$ decreases and falls below a low dormant ratio threshold ($\hat{\beta}$, indicating the agent is "awakened" and performing meaningful actions), the exploration noise gradually shifts to a linear decay schedule, similar to conventional methods, but starting from the point of awakening ($t_0$).
The scheduler is defined as:
$\sigma(t) = \begin{cases} \max \left(\frac{1}{1+\exp(-(\beta-\hat{\beta})/T)}, \sigma_{\text{linear}}(t-t_0)\right) & \text{if awakened} \\ \frac{1}{1+\exp(-(\beta-\hat{\beta})/T)} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
where $T$ is the exploration temperature. This ensures that the agent receives adequate noise to explore when stuck, but transitions to more focused exploitation once active.

#### 3.4. Dormant-Ratio-Guided Exploitation

This mechanism, inspired by prior work on expectile regression for value functions, aims to prioritize exploitation when the agent is actively learning and has a low dormant ratio. It modifies the target value calculation in actor-critic methods:
$r(s, a) + \gamma[\lambda V(s') + (1 - \lambda) Q(s', \pi(s'))]$
Here, $V(s')$ is an expectile-based value function that quickly learns from historically successful trajectories, and $\lambda \in$ is the exploitation hyperparameter. A higher $\lambda$ means more reliance on the $V$ function to exploit past successes.
DRM makes $\lambda$ a function of the dormant ratio $\beta$:
$\lambda(\beta) = \frac{\hat{\lambda}}{1 + \exp((\beta - \hat{\beta})/T')}$
where $\hat{\lambda}$ is the maximum exploitation hyperparameter, and $T'$ is the exploitation temperature. When $\beta$ is high, $\lambda$ is low, emphasizing exploration (less reliance on the V function). When $\beta$ is low (agent is active and performing meaningful actions), $\lambda$ is high, prioritizing exploitation of learned successful behaviors.

These three mechanisms collectively ensure that DrM adaptively manages the exploration-exploitation trade-off by intrinsically monitoring the agent's neural activity level through the dormant ratio, leading to more robust and efficient learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DRM was extensively evaluated across three challenging visual continuous control benchmarks: DeepMind Control Suite, MetaWorld, and Adroit, encompassing 19 tasks of varying complexity, kinematics, and reward structures.

#### 4.1. Performance on DeepMind Control Suite (8 Hard Tasks)

- **Novel Solvability:** DrM is the first documented model-free algorithm capable of reliably solving complex "Dog" and "Manipulator" tasks from pixels. These tasks are notoriously difficult due to intricate kinematics, high DoFs, and sparse rewards.
- **Sample Efficiency and Asymptotic Performance:** DrM demonstrates significant improvements over baselines (DrQ-v2, ALIX, TACO). For example, in "Dog Run," DrM achieves substantially higher episode rewards earlier in training and reaches a much higher asymptotic performance.
- **Robustness:** DrM exhibits remarkably reduced variation across different random seeds (smaller shaded regions in plots) compared to baselines, which frequently encounter "broken seeds" where agents fail to learn. DrM had "no broken seeds" across 76 total runs.

#### 4.2. Performance on MetaWorld (8 Tasks)

- **Superior Sample Efficiency:** Across both dense and sparse reward tasks, DrM consistently outperforms baselines, especially in scenarios with sparse task completion rewards (e.g., Coffee Push, Soccer, Sweep Into, Hammer).
- **Sparse Reward Handling:** For sparse reward tasks, DrM achieves success rates comparable to baselines on dense reward tasks, demonstrating its remarkable advantage in exploration for challenging environments.

#### 4.3. Performance on Adroit (3 Dexterous Hand Manipulation Tasks)

- **Breakthrough in Dexterous Manipulation:** DrM is the first model-free visual RL algorithm to reliably solve dexterous hand manipulation tasks (Hammer, Door, Pen) in the Adroit domain without expert demonstrations. These tasks involve controlling a 24-DoF robotic hand from pixels.
- **Dramatic Improvements:** DrM shows substantial gains in both sample efficiency and asymptotic success rates compared to all baselines.

#### 4.4. Quantitative Summary of Improvements

- **Sample Efficiency:** DrM requires 70% fewer samples (DeepMind Control Suite), 45% fewer samples (MetaWorld), and 60% fewer samples (Adroit) to match the peak asymptotic performance of the best baseline.
- **Asymptotic Performance:** DrM achieves improvements of 65% (DeepMind Control Suite), 35% (MetaWorld), and 75% (Adroit) over the best-performing baseline.

#### 4.5. Dormant Ratio Analysis

Experiments show that DrM effectively and rapidly minimizes the dormant ratio in the agent's policy network compared to baseline algorithms. Baselines often fail to significantly reduce the dormant ratio, which correlates with their poorer performance and lack of meaningful exploration.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on the Adroit environment confirm that all three core mechanisms—dormant-ratio-guided perturbation, awaken exploration scheduler, and dormant-ratio-guided exploitation—are crucial for DrM's superior performance. Removing any single component leads to a significant performance drop (e.g., 20% for exploitation, ~40% for perturbation or awaken exploration). Comparing DrM to a baseline using fixed parameters for these mechanisms instead of dormant-ratio guidance also shows a substantial performance decrease, highlighting the importance of adaptive control via the dormant ratio.

#### 4.7. Comparison with RND and ReDo (from Appendix)

- **vs. RND:** An intrinsic reward-based exploration method like Random Network Distillation (RND) applied to DrQ-v2 improves performance over vanilla DrQ-v2 but is still insufficient to solve hard Adroit tasks and significantly underperforms DrM. This suggests that simply maximizing novelty or prediction error isn't enough; directly addressing neural inactivity is key.
- **vs. ReDo:** Comparing DrM to ReDo (Sokar et al., 2023), which only resets dormant neurons, shows DrM significantly outperforms it. This implies that perturbing non-dormant neurons, as done in DrM's soft reset, also contributes positively to exploration, and the dormant-ratio-guided exploration strategy is vital.

#### 4.8. Discussion on Dormant Ratio Interpretation (from Appendix)

The authors investigate an alternative hypothesis that dormant ratio merely reflects frame-to-frame velocity changes. Experiments using the DMC-Generalization Benchmark (dynamic backgrounds) and intrinsic rewards based on frame L1 distance refute this. The dormant ratio pattern in dynamic backgrounds mirrors static ones, and maximizing frame differences fails to solve tasks. Furthermore, DrM performs well on tasks requiring *minimal* motion (e.g., Cartpole Balance Sparse), indicating its effectiveness is not just from promoting rapid changes but from fostering meaningful activity regardless of its speed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DRM represents a significant advancement in model-free visual reinforcement learning, addressing several long-standing challenges in the field. Its primary significance lies in:

1. **Novel Insight into RL Dynamics:** By identifying and leveraging the "dormant ratio" as an intrinsic and reward-agnostic indicator of an agent's activity level and behavioral diversity, DRM introduces a fundamentally new perspective on understanding and enhancing RL learning dynamics. This neurological-inspired connection (mirroring arousal theory) provides valuable interpretability beyond typical reward signals.
2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** DrM sets a new benchmark in visual continuous control, consistently outperforming leading model-free baselines in sample efficiency, asymptotic performance, and robustness. Its ability to solve previously intractable tasks, such as Dog and Manipulator domains in DeepMind Control Suite and demonstration-free dexterous hand manipulation in Adroit, is a landmark achievement for model-free methods operating directly from pixels.
3. **Enhanced Robustness:** The substantial reduction in "broken seeds" and lower variance across runs indicates that DrM is significantly more stable and reliable. This addresses a critical barrier to deploying RL agents in real-world applications where consistent performance is paramount.
4. **Practical Applicability:** The method is described as "easy to implement" and "computationally efficient," making it a highly practical approach for researchers and practitioners. Its minimal hyperparameter tuning requirements across diverse domains further enhance its utility.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Robotics:** The ability to master complex dexterous manipulation and locomotion tasks from pixel observations without demonstrations opens new avenues for autonomous robotics, potentially leading to more versatile and general-purpose robots in manufacturing, healthcare, and exploration.
- **Benchmark for Future Research:** DrM's performance on previously unsolved tasks establishes new baselines, pushing the boundaries for future research in visual RL and challenging researchers to develop even more capable algorithms.
- **Theoretical Understanding of Deep RL:** The correlation between dormant neurons and behavioral variety could inspire further theoretical work into the functional role of neural network activity in learning, plasticity, and exploration in deep RL agents.
- **Beyond Continuous Control:** The authors suggest the core mechanisms could be adapted for discrete action tasks (e.g., DQN, Efficient Rainbow), implying a broader applicability of the dormant ratio concept across different RL paradigms.
- **Unsupervised RL:** The interpretability of the dormant ratio as an early indicator of skill acquisition, even before rewards, makes it a promising metric for unsupervised or reward-free reinforcement learning, where agents learn useful behaviors without explicit reward functions.

In conclusion, DRM offers a powerful and principled approach to master complex visual continuous control tasks by proactively addressing the fundamental issue of network inactivity. Its novel use of the dormant ratio not only yields superior empirical results but also provides fresh insights into the learning processes of deep reinforcement agents, promising significant advancements in the field.
