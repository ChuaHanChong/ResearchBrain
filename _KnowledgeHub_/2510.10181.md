# Dejavu: Towards Experience Feedback Learning for Embodied Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> The Dejavu framework introduces an Experience Feedback Network (EFN) to enable deployed Vision-Language-Action (VLA) agents to continuously improve performance by leveraging a growing memory of successful experiences, without retraining the large VLA backbone. This system achieved an average success rate of 87.0% on the LIBERO benchmark with OpenVLA, an 11.5% reduction in episode length, and significantly boosted real-world task success rates on the AgiBot-G1 platform, for instance, increasing success on the DrawerStore task from 5.3% to 58.7%.

## Problem

- Deployed Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, after extensive offline training, typically operate with frozen weights, preventing them from acquiring new knowledge or adapting to novel situations post-deployment.
- Existing retrieval-augmented reinforcement learning (RL) methods often involve trainable policies, operate on static offline corpora, or define retrieval over abstract states, which are not suitable for real-time adaptation of large, frozen VLAs with rich vision-language interfaces.
- The computational cost and risk of catastrophic forgetting associated with continuously fine-tuning or retraining large VLA models in dynamic real-world environments impede practical, continuously learning embodied agents.

## Method

- An ==Experience Feedback Network== (EFN) is introduced as a lightweight, fixed-weight controller that wraps a pre-trained, frozen VLA policy, predicting residual action corrections.
- The EFN utilizes a live, continuously growing "experience bank" storing successful action trajectories, employing instruction-filtered and efficiency-prioritized retrieval to identify relevant past experiences.
- EFN is trained with Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) using dense semantic similarity rewards, which compare the agent's progress to the successor states of retrieved experiences, shaped with anti-idling penalties.

## Results

- EFN consistently improved success rates and task efficiency across three VLA backbones (OpenVLA, UniVLA, GO-1) on the simulated LIBERO benchmark, for example, increasing OpenVLA success from **76.5%** to **87.0%**.
- On real-world manipulation tasks with an AgiBot-G1 robot, EFN achieved an average success rate of **70.2%** (vs. **25.8%** for base GO-1) and substantially boosted performance on challenging tasks like DrawerStore from **5.3%** to **58.7%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of SAC optimization, dense semantic rewards, instruction-aware retrieval, and anti-idling penalties for EFN's performance, while demonstrating that direct action prediction (instead of residual) was ineffective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embodied agents can achieve continuous post-deployment improvement by leveraging an external, dynamic memory of successful experiences without requiring internal parameter updates to the large VLA backbone.
> - Predicting a residual action, rather than a full action, is crucial for effectively improving a competent base VLA policy with a lightweight auxiliary network, as it guides small, experience-informed corrections.
> - Dense semantic similarity rewards, derived from comparing visual feature maps, provide more frequent and informative feedback for reinforcement learning than sparse task success signals, enabling efficient learning of the EFN.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied intelligence investigates agents that learn and operate through physical interaction with their environment. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated generalization capabilities across a range of tasks. These VLA models typically achieve their performance after extensive offline training on large, fixed datasets. A recognized limitation of these models, once deployed in real-world environments, is their inability to acquire new knowledge or adapt to novel situations without retraining their entire architecture. This means most deployed systems effectively cease learning.

The presented work addresses this post-deployment learning challenge. Existing literature includes retrieval-augmented reinforcement learning (RL), where external experience buffers guide policies or value functions by retrieving similar past trajectories. In embodied and robotic control, methods retrieve prior executions (e.g., state-action trajectories or policy snippets) to inform decision-making, either through fine-tuning on retrieved data or by conditioning policies directly on these trajectories. However, these methods often involve a trainable policy whose weights continue to change, operate on static offline corpora rather than live, growing memories, or define retrieval over abstract state representations rather than the rich vision-language interfaces of modern VLAs.

Separately, residual policy learning focuses on improving a base controller by predicting an additive correction rather than a full action, aiming for safer and more sample-efficient refinement. The use of dense, similarity-shaped rewards and world models has also been explored to stabilize RL by comparing observed or predicted future states to goal-like targets, offering more frequent feedback than sparse success/failure signals.

This research, titled "Dejavu," positions itself at the intersection of these domains. It proposes a framework for post-deployment learning in embodied agents that utilizes a frozen VLA backbone, a live and continuously growing memory bank, and a retrieval-conditioned residual correction mechanism. The objective is to enable adaptation and improvement through memory updates and a lightweight, fixed-weight auxiliary network, rather than requiring re-training or fine-tuning of the large base VLA policy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome a fundamental limitation in deployed embodied agents: their inability to continuously acquire new knowledge and improve task performance once their initial training is complete. Conventional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while powerful, typically deploy with frozen weights, meaning their knowledge remains static unless the entire model is retrained. This fixed-knowledge paradigm restricts their adaptability to dynamic real-world environments and novel challenges encountered after deployment.

The motivation for Dejavu stems from an analogy to human cognition. Humans frequently address new problems by recalling and reusing past experiences, rather than undergoing fundamental changes to their core knowledge. This concept of episodic memory, which underlies phenomena like "déjà vu," enables rapid adaptation through analogical reasoning without altering foundational knowledge representations. The authors investigate whether an artificial intelligence agent could similarly improve by recalling and reusing its own "experiences." Such a mechanism would allow an agent to enhance its capabilities over time by merely accumulating and exploiting new experiences at inference time, obviating the need for computationally intensive, gradient-based retraining of large models.

Specifically, the key objectives for the proposed Experience Feedback Network (EFN) within the Dejavu framework are:
- To develop a general post-deployment learning framework that augments a *frozen* VLA policy, ensuring that the substantial computational and data investment in pre-training is preserved without alteration.
- To identify contextually relevant prior action experiences from a continually updated memory. This involves designing a mechanism for efficient and semantically meaningful retrieval.
- To condition action prediction on this retrieved guidance. The system should learn how to integrate past successful behaviors to refine current decision-making.
- To train the EFN with reinforcement learning, utilizing dense semantic similarity rewards. This approach aims to provide frequent and informative feedback signals beyond sparse task success or failure, guiding the agent towards actions that align with successful past behaviors in similar contexts.
- To enable the agent to exhibit "learning from experience" during deployment by continuously expanding its memory with new successful trajectories. This online memory growth is intended to be the sole source of adaptation post-deployment, without requiring further gradient updates to the EFN's parameters.

In essence, the research aims to provide a practical and efficient method for embodied agents to continually improve their adaptability, robustness, and success rates in the real world, leveraging their own accumulated experiences through an external, lightweight, and frozen adaptation module.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Dejavu framework introduces the Experience Feedback Network (EFN) as a controller that wraps a pre-trained, frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy. EFN operates by recalling relevant past experiences and adjusting the current action to align with the outcomes observed in those experiences.

#### 3.1. Experience Bank Design

The system maintains a live "experience bank" storing full rollouts and individual steps. Each stored step `(s_t, a_t)` from a rollout `τ` includes:
- A fixed instruction embedding (`ℓ_τ`) obtained from the VLA's language model at the episode's start.
- VLA vision-encoder features (`F_t`) for the frame `s_t`.
- A compact key vector (`k_t`) derived from `F_t` for retrieval.
- The base policy's raw action (`a^(0)_t`) executed at that step.

**Key Construction and Retrieval:** Retrieval keys (`k_t`) are formed by a mean-max fusion of `ℓ_2`-normalized token features from `F_t`, followed by final normalization. At query time, a query vector (`q_t`) is similarly derived from the current frame. Cosine similarities are computed between `q_t` and all stored keys. A probabilistic top-k retrieval method is used: the top-k nearest keys are selected, and one key is then sampled from this shortlist using a similarity-biased softmax distribution, encouraging exploration among near-matches while favoring the most similar.

**Deployment-specific Retrieval:** During deployment, retrieval is refined in two ways:
- **Instruction-filtered candidate set:** Matches are restricted to rollouts whose instruction embeddings (`ℓ_τ`) are semantically close to the current task's instruction.
- **Efficiency prior:** Shorter successful trajectories are prioritized, as they are presumed to contain more efficient and high-quality behaviors. The combined score for candidates (`~s_i`) blends semantic similarity with a normalized length prior.

**Online Experience Growth:** After each successful episode during deployment, its entire trajectory (vision features, keys, and base actions) is appended to the experience bank. This mechanism enables continuous learning and adaptation solely through memory growth, without altering network weights.

#### 3.2. Learning with Residual Policy Optimization

EFN learns to predict a residual action (`Δa_t`) that is added to the base VLA policy's output (`a^(0)_t`) to produce the final executed action (`a_t = a^(0)_t + Δa_t`). This approach leverages the base policy's competence while providing experience-informed corrections.

**Dense Semantic Match Reward:** The core of EFN's training is a dense, similarity-based reward signal. After executing `a_t` and observing the next state `s_t+1` with features `F_t+1`, a semantic similarity reward (`r_sem_t`) is computed. This reward is the cosine similarity between a pooled representation of `F_t+1` and the pooled representation of the successor frame (`F_hat_+`) from the retrieved experience. This provides continuous feedback on how well the agent's actions are guiding it towards the retrieved trajectory's next state.

**Reward Shaping with Anti-Idling Penalty:** To encourage progress and prevent the agent from merely idling in visually similar states, the dense reward is further shaped:
- An absolute similarity bonus (`a_t`) for matching the retrieved next observation.
- A progress term (`[p_t]_+`) that rewards positive improvement towards the retrieved next state.
- A motion term (`m_t`) that penalizes staying static.
- An anti-idling penalty that activates if the current observation is already similar to the retrieved next state but shows negligible progress and little change between consecutive frames.
- A small per-step time penalty (`-λ_time`) to favor shorter successful trajectories.
The similarity function `sim(X, Y)` for these terms employs a Sinkhorn-regularized matching between the visual token feature maps of `X` and `Y`.

**Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) Objective:** EFN is trained using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), an off-policy maximum entropy deep reinforcement learning algorithm suitable for continuous action spaces. The actor (`π_φ`) and critics (`Q_θ`) are conditioned on a context representation (`c_t`) that concatenates information from the current state (`F_t`, `a^(0)_t`), the retrieved experience (`F_hat`, `a_hat`, `F_hat_+`), and the rollout instruction (`ℓ`). Critically, gradients flow only through EFN's actor, critics, and context encoder; they do not propagate back to the VLA backbone or the retrieval targets. SAC's entropy regularization facilitates exploration, and its off-policy nature supports efficient reuse of stored experiences.

#### 3.3. EFN Architecture and Implementation

EFN operates on the latent interface of the VLA, specifically on visual patch embeddings (e.g., 256 tokens of 4096 dimensions) and latent action tokens (e.g., 4 tokens of 4096 dimensions). It compresses long visual sequences into a few learned latent vectors using Multihead Attention Pooling blocks. The policy network combines these pooled visual latents from current and retrieved states, along with projected retrieved latent action tokens, into a context sequence. It then processes these through multihead attention and a two-layer Transformer encoder to predict the residual action (`Δa_t`). This residual is then added to the base VLA latent action tokens. The critic network shares inputs but has a simpler structure, summarizing global visual and token information to output a scalar value.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EFN was conducted across simulated tasks using the LIBERO benchmark and real-world manipulation tasks on an AgiBot-G1 platform. EFN's performance was compared against several baselines, including retrieval-only, residual-only, retrieval-augmented RL, and test-time training methods, all under identical experience banks and interaction budgets.

#### 4.1. Performance on LIBERO Benchmark (Simulation)

Across three VLA backbones (OpenVLA, UniVLA, and GO-1), EFN consistently demonstrated improvements in both success rate and task efficiency (average steps to completion):
- **OpenVLA:** EFN (Vol=1000) achieved an average success rate of 87.0% with 156.7 steps, outperforming the base OpenVLA (76.5% success, 160.2 steps) and all other baselines.
- **UniVLA:** EFN (Vol=1000) reached 97.2% average success with 151.3 steps, compared to UniVLA's 95.2% success and 164.2 steps.
- **GO-1:** EFN (Vol=1000) obtained 96.7% average success with 154.8 steps, against GO-1's 94.7% success and 163.1 steps.

**Comparison with Baselines:**
- **kNN-RAG (retrieval-only):** Generally degraded performance compared to the base VLA, suggesting that direct action copying from nearest neighbors is insufficient.
- **ResAct (residual-only):** Provided modest gains, indicating that residual refinement is beneficial but limited without episodic context.
- **R2A (retrieval-augmented RL):** While showing improvements, R2A, which requires updating the large VLA backbone, underperformed EFN under the same interaction budget.
- **GC-TTT (goal-conditioned test-time training):** Yielded small or sometimes negative improvements, reflecting the instability associated with aggressive test-time fine-tuning.

The results indicated that EFN with a moderate experience bank (Vol=300) already matched or surpassed all baselines, with a larger bank (Vol=1000) offering further, though diminishing, performance enhancements.

#### 4.2. Real-World Performance on AgiBot-G1 Platform

Experiments on real-world tasks (BottlePlace, ShelfSort, StockLift, DrawerStore) with the GO-1 backbone mirrored the simulation trends:
- EFN achieved the highest average success rate (70.2% with Vol=1000) while requiring fewer steps (435.1), significantly outperforming the base GO-1 (25.8% success, 491.8 steps).
- On the most challenging task, DrawerStore, EFN (Vol=1000) increased success from 5.3% (GO-1) to 58.7%, demonstrating its capability to substantially improve performance on difficult scenarios.
- The performance gap between Vol=300 and Vol=1000 was narrower in real-world settings, suggesting that a moderate bank size might adequately cover key task variations in real environments.
- Similar to simulation, kNN-RAG generally hurt performance, ResAct and R2A offered moderate gains, and GC-TTT was unstable, failing on the hardest task.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on LIBERO provided insights into EFN's components:
- **Without SAC:** Removing Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and using a value-only critic harmed both success and efficiency, supporting the use of entropy-regularized optimization.
- **Without Dense Rewards:** Relying solely on sparse task returns instead of similarity-shaped dense rewards reduced learning speed and final performance.
- **Without Instruction Embedding:** Disabling instruction-level filtering degraded performance, indicating the importance of instruction-aware retrieval.
- **Without Anti-Idle:** Removing the anti-idling term reduced performance (leading to longer episodes), suggesting the utility of penalties against static behavior.
- **Residual Action vs. Direct Action Prediction (Supplementary):** Replacing residual correction with direct action prediction led to a near-zero success rate, underscoring that learning small corrections around a competent base policy is more effective than attempting to relearn the entire action space with a lightweight network.

#### 4.4. Inference-Time Efficiency

EFN introduced a modest 4.2% increase in per-step latency. However, by reducing the average episode length by 11.5%, it resulted in an overall 7.9% reduction in total episode time, demonstrating that it improves performance while also enhancing end-to-end task completion speed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Dejavu framework introduces a mechanism for embodied agents to improve their performance continuously after deployment, addressing a core challenge in the field of embodied intelligence. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several aspects:

- **Novelty in Post-Deployment Learning:** EFN represents, to the authors' knowledge, the first post-deployment learning framework for *frozen* Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies that achieves behavior improvement through *online experience accumulation* and *retrieval-conditioned residual correction*. This approach diverges from common practices of continuous fine-tuning or full model retraining, which can be computationally intensive, prone to catastrophic forgetting, and difficult to manage in real-world settings.
- **Practicality and Efficiency:** By augmenting a frozen VLA backbone with a lightweight, fixed-weight Experience Feedback Network, EFN allows for adaptation without modifying the large, pre-trained model parameters. This significantly reduces the computational burden and memory requirements during deployment, making it feasible for resource-constrained robotic platforms. The demonstrated reduction in overall episode time, despite a slight per-step latency increase, highlights its practical efficiency.
- **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** EFN enables agents to adapt to new situations and recover from subtle failure modes by leveraging relevant past experiences. The consistent improvements in success rates and reduction in steps across diverse simulated (LIBERO) and real-world (AgiBot-G1) manipulation tasks suggest enhanced robustness and adaptability of the augmented agents. This is particularly valuable in dynamic real-world environments where training data might not fully capture all possible scenarios.
- **Conceptual Shift in "Learning":** The work promotes an "experience-centric" view of post-deployment learning. Instead of viewing learning as solely parameter updates, EFN emphasizes that an agent can "learn" by strategically growing and querying an external memory of successful actions and outcomes. This conceptual shift offers a pathway for deploying intelligent systems that evolve their capabilities through accumulated wisdom, rather than constant internal recalibration.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** Drawing inspiration from human episodic memory and the concept of "déjà vu" provides a biologically plausible and intuitive framework for AI agents to leverage past experiences for current decision-making. This connection could inspire further research into memory-augmented architectures for more human-like intelligence in embodied systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The EFN framework lays the groundwork for several future directions. These include exploring more sophisticated experience management strategies (e.g., dynamic memory scaling, coreset selection), integrating EFN with world models for predictive retrieval and uncertainty-aware guidance, and developing shared experience repositories across heterogeneous agents and domains to facilitate broad knowledge transfer. The ability to enhance a frozen VLA's performance without retraining the backbone opens avenues for building more generalist and continuously improving robotic systems.
