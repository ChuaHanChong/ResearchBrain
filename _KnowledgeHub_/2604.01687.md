# EvoSkills: Self-Evolving Agent Skills via Co-Evolutionary Verification

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoSkills enables large language model agents to autonomously generate and iteratively refine complex, multi-file skill packages through a co-evolutionary verification framework. This approach yields skills that surpass human-authored versions and exhibit strong transferability across different LLM architectures, effectively bridging the cognitive gap between human-designed workflows and agent execution.

## Problem

- Manual human authoring of 'agent skills' is labor-intensive, difficult to scale, and often leads to inconsistent performance due to 'human-machine cognitive misalignment'.
- Existing self-evolving methods for LLM agents focus primarily on atomic tools or function APIs, lacking the capability to generate and refine complex, structured, multi-file skill packages.
- Many self-evolution approaches require ground-truth supervision for failure diagnosis, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios where such detailed feedback is unavailable.

## Method

- The **EvoSkills** framework introduces a ==co-evolutionary approach==, employing a ==Skill Generator== and an information-isolated ==Surrogate Verifier== that iteratively refine skills and their verification mechanisms.
- The Skill Generator autonomously generates and refines multi-file ==skill bundles== by processing task instructions and structured failure diagnostics from the verifier.
- The Surrogate Verifier independently generates and refines a suite of ==deterministic test assertions==, providing dense, actionable feedback and escalating its tests when ground-truth failures occur despite surrogate success.

## Results

- **EvoSkills** achieved a **71.1%** pass rate on SkillsBench with **Claude Opus 4.6**, outperforming the no-skill baseline (**30.6%**) by **+40.5pp** and human-curated skills (**53.5%**) by **+17.6pp**, with self-evolved skills beating human-curated ones in 9 of 11 professional domains, including Finance (**+56.9pp**) and Cybersecurity (**+23.2pp**), and correcting degraded performance in Natural Science.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing the surrogate verifier led to a substantial drop in pass rate by **-30.0pp**, highlighting its critical role in targeted repairs.
- Skills evolved by **Claude Opus 4.6** showed strong transferability across six other LLM models, improving their respective no-skill baselines by **+36 to +44pp**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM agents can autonomously generate more effective and reliable skills than human experts, as these self-evolved skills better align with the agent's intrinsic reasoning and execution patterns.
> - A co-evolutionary framework, especially with an information-isolated surrogate verifier, provides crucial, actionable feedback for skill refinement without requiring direct ground-truth supervision.
> - Self-evolved skills demonstrate strong cross-model transferability, suggesting they encode reusable task structures rather than model-specific artifacts, making them broadly portable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Model (LLM) agents has progressed significantly through the integration of tools and Application Programming Interface (API) calls. These capabilities enable agents to interact with external environments and perform specific, self-contained functions. However, addressing complex, multi-step professional tasks—such as advanced software engineering, scientific analysis, or data pipeline orchestration—requires more than isolated tool invocations. Such tasks demand the orchestration of coherent procedures, including goal decomposition, tool coordination, error recovery, and validation of intermediate outputs, often under conditions of long-horizon decisions, tightly coupled instructions, and sparse environmental feedback.

Anthropic introduced the concept of "agent skills" to bridge this gap. Unlike simple tools, which are typically single functions, skills are defined as structured packages comprising workflow instructions, executable scripts, and domain references, often spanning multiple files. Systematic evaluations, such as those conducted on SkillsBench, have indicated that well-crafted skills can enhance agent performance across various professional domains.

Despite their demonstrated utility, the prevalent method for generating these skills relies on manual human authoring. This approach is labor-intensive, challenging to scale, and lacks systematic quality guarantees. Furthermore, human-authored skills can suffer from "human–machine cognitive misalignment," where workflows intuitive for human experts may not align with how LLM agents process context, reason, and operate within execution constraints, potentially leading to inconsistent or degraded performance.

Recent advancements have explored autonomous methods for LLM agents to evolve their own tools or tool libraries, moving away from pre-defined static resources. However, these existing self-evolving methods exhibit limitations when applied to the more complex structure of skills. They are typically designed for one-shot generation of simple, self-contained functions and are not inherently suited for creating structured, multi-file skill packages that coordinate various artifacts. Additionally, some prior self-evolution methods for agents depend on ground-truth supervision for diagnosing failures, which restricts their applicability in real-world scenarios where such detailed signals are unavailable.

EvoSkills addresses these limitations by proposing a framework for the autonomous generation and co-evolution of complex, multi-file agent skills. It differentiates itself from prior work by focusing on iterative refinement of structured skill packages and by employing a surrogate verification mechanism that does not rely on ground-truth feedback for internal evolution, thereby enhancing real-world applicability. This work contributes to the broader goal of developing more autonomous, capable, and generalizable LLM agents that can effectively tackle professional-level, open-ended tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable LLM agents to autonomously generate and refine complex, multi-file skill packages, thereby overcoming the limitations associated with manual skill authoring and the shortcomings of existing self-evolving tool methods. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Manual Authoring Limitations:** The current paradigm of human-authored skills is highly labor-intensive and not scalable. Manually creating and maintaining structured skill packages for a vast array of professional tasks consumes significant resources. Automating this process can lead to more efficient development and deployment of agent capabilities.

2. **Addressing Human–Machine Cognitive Misalignment:** Evaluations have indicated that human-curated skills can yield inconsistent performance, occasionally leading to degraded agent performance in specific domains. The hypothesis is that workflows and abstractions designed by human experts may not inherently match the reasoning patterns, context processing, and execution constraints of LLM agents. The motivation is to allow agents to discover and encode skills that align with their own operational modalities, potentially leading to more effective task-solving.

3. **Extending Self-Evolution Beyond Simple Tools:** Existing self-evolving methods for agents primarily focus on generating simple, single-file tools or API functions. These methods are inadequate for the complexity of "skills," which are structured bundles encompassing workflow instructions, executable scripts, and domain references across multiple interdependent files. A key motivation is to develop a framework capable of handling this increased complexity to unlock a broader range of applications.

4. **Mitigating Reliance on Ground-Truth Feedback:** Many self-evolving agent systems rely heavily on ground-truth supervision or detailed failure diagnosis for improvement. In real-world environments, such precise ground-truth signals are often unavailable, or only an opaque pass/fail signal is provided. Therefore, a critical motivation is to enable agents to self-evolve skills effectively even when detailed ground-truth feedback is absent.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of EvoSkills are:
- To design a framework that can iteratively generate and refine complex, multi-file skill packages.
- To develop a mechanism for providing informative and actionable feedback for skill improvement without direct access to ground-truth test content.
- To demonstrate that autonomously evolved skills can outperform human-curated skills by better aligning with agent reasoning.
- To evaluate the transferability of self-evolved skills across different LLM models and families, indicating that they capture reusable task structures rather than model-specific artifacts.
- To analyze how performance gains from self-evolved skills are distributed across various professional domains, particularly highlighting areas where human-curated skills may fall short.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to advance the capabilities of LLM agents to handle sophisticated, real-world tasks with greater autonomy and efficacy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvoSkills proposes a co-evolutionary framework designed to enable LLM agents to autonomously construct complex, multi-file skill packages and overcome the challenges of unreliable one-shot generation and the lack of ground-truth feedback during self-evolution. The framework operates through an iterative generate-verify-refine cycle involving two core components: a Skill Generator and a Surrogate Verifier.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task environment is defined as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) M = $\langle X, A, T, O, \Omega, R \rangle$. In this formulation, X represents the state space (e.g., filesystem, processes), A represents the agent's actions (e.g., terminal commands, file edits), T is the deterministic state transition function, O is the observation space (e.g., command execution results), $\Omega$ maps post-action states to partial observations, and R($x_T$) evaluates the final output against hidden ground-truth tests, returning a score in. The agent's policy $\pi_{\theta}$ for action selection is conditioned on a skill S, which is a structured bundle of domain-specific instructions, executable scripts, and reference materials. The objective is to discover an optimal skill $S^{*}$ that maximizes the expected terminal reward J(S), where J(S) = $E_{\tau \sim P(\tau|\pi_{\theta}, S, M)} [R(x_T)]$.
Since direct optimization against R(S) is intractable due to its computational expense and opaque pass/fail output, EvoSkills introduces a surrogate verifier reward $\tilde{R}(x, V)$. This reward is derived from a suite of deterministic test assertions V generated by an independent verifier, with $\tilde{R}(x, V) = \frac{1}{|V|} \sum_{k=1}^{|V|} 1[e_k(x)]$. This creates a coupled optimization problem where the skill must maximize a proxy reward that itself must be aligned with the hidden ground truth.

#### 3.2. EvoSkills Framework

The framework realizes the alternating optimization process (skill refinement and test escalation) through three informationally isolated components:

- **Skill Generator:**
 - **Purpose:** To iteratively generate and refine multi-file skill bundles.
 - **Mechanism:** It maintains a persistent conversation context C, initialized with the task instruction (I) and a domain-agnostic meta-skill ($S_{meta}$) that teaches skill creation. Each skill revision $S^{(i+1)}$ is produced by an LLM policy $\pi_{\theta}$, conditioning on the current skill $S^{(i)}$ and the accumulated high-fidelity feedback $F^{(i,j)}$ from the Surrogate Verifier appended to the context C.
 - **Execution:** The generator executes the current skill $S^{(i)}$ in the environment E to produce task outputs $x^{(i)}$, which are then passed to the Surrogate Verifier for evaluation. A context usage proportion threshold is implemented to prevent LLM context overflow.

- **Surrogate Verifier:**
 - **Purpose:** To provide dense, actionable feedback to the Skill Generator in the absence of detailed ground-truth signals.
 - **Mechanism:** It operates as a completely independent LLM session ($\pi^V_{\theta}$), observing only the task instruction I and the skill's output files $x^{(i)}$. Crucially, it remains blind to the Skill Generator's reasoning, code, and skill content to prevent confirmation bias. The verifier generates a suite of deterministic test assertions V.
 - **Feedback Generation:** If the surrogate reward $\tilde{R}$ indicates failure ($<1$), the verifier generates a structured failure diagnostic $F^{(i,j)}$. This diagnostic includes per-assertion results, root-cause analysis, and actionable revision suggestions, which are then fed back to the Skill Generator's context.
 - **Test Escalation:** The verifier iteratively refines its test suite V. Test escalation ($V^{(j+1)}$) is specifically triggered when the surrogate tests pass ($\tilde{R}=1$) but the ground-truth oracle returns a failure signal (opaque pass/fail bit). This forces the verifier to independently strengthen its tests without any access to the ground-truth test content, for instance, by generating more diverse or challenging test cases.

- **Co-evolutionary Loop:**
 - The Skill Generator and Surrogate Verifier co-evolve through iterative generate–verify–refine cycles, as formalized in Algorithm 1.
 - **Skill Refinement:** When the surrogate reward is less than 1, the verifier's test suite $V^{(j)}$ is held fixed, and the failure diagnostic $F^{(i,j)}$ drives the refinement of the skill $S^{(i+1)}$.
 - **Oracle Intervention:** If the surrogate tests pass ($\tilde{R}=1$), a ground-truth oracle test re-executes the skill in a fresh environment ($E'$) to provide an opaque pass/fail signal R($\hat{x}^{(i)}$). This oracle acts as the ultimate arbiter, but crucially, it provides no detailed test content or failure diagnostics to the agent.
 - **Verifier Escalation Trigger:** If the ground-truth oracle fails despite the surrogate passing, this opaque failure signal triggers the Surrogate Verifier to escalate its test suite. This mechanism ensures that the verifier continuously improves its ability to approximate the hidden ground-truth criteria.
 - **Convergence:** The co-evolutionary loop aims for convergence within a specified number of iterations (e.g., 5 evolution iterations, 15 surrogate retries), gradually improving skill quality. If the oracle passes with a score of 1, the skill is considered optimal and deployed. Otherwise, the best-performing skill is saved.

The methodology emphasizes strict informational isolation between the Skill Generator and Surrogate Verifier, preventing the verifier from inheriting the generator's biases and ensuring that the ground-truth oracle's feedback remains truly external and opaque.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on SkillsBench, a benchmark comprising 87 tasks across 11 professional domains, using deterministic verifiers and a binary pass/fail evaluation. The primary metric was the pass rate. Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.2 served as backbone models for evolution, with transferability evaluated on six additional LLMs from various providers.

#### 4.1. RQ1: Skill Quality Comparison

EvoSkills achieved a pass rate of **71.1%** on Claude Opus 4.6 with Claude-Code. This significantly surpassed the no-skill baseline (30.6%) by **+40.5 percentage points (pp)** and the human-curated skills baseline (53.5%) by **+17.6pp**.
Other self-generation baselines—including Anthropic’s Skill-Creator (34.1%), SkillsBench self-generated skills (32.0%), and a CoT-Guided Self-Generation variant (30.7%)—showed negligible improvements over the no-skill baseline. This indicated that skill generation alone, without co-evolutionary verification, was insufficient.

- **Takeaway 1: Agents can create better skills than humans.** Self-evolved skills demonstrated superior performance, effectively capturing agent-specific reasoning patterns, tool-use preferences, and decomposition strategies, which were more aligned with how LLM agents operate compared to human-designed procedures.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Ablation Studies

Ablation studies investigated the contribution of key components:
- Removing the **surrogate verifier** resulted in a substantial drop in pass rate from 71.1% to **41.1%** (-30.0pp). This indicated that structured diagnostic feedback from the surrogate verifier is critical for targeted skill refinement, as relying solely on the oracle's opaque pass/fail signal is inefficient.
- Providing **only background context documents** without any evolution yielded a **48.6%** pass rate, which was above the no-skill baseline (+18.0pp) but significantly below the full EvoSkills framework (-22.5pp). This highlighted that unstructured knowledge alone is insufficient without structured packaging and iterative evolution.
These results underscore the necessity of both iterative verification and structured skill packaging within the framework.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Skill Cross-Model Transferability

Self-evolved skills demonstrated strong generalization capabilities:
- Skills evolved by Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.2 showed substantial gains on their respective backbone models (+40.5pp and +40.2pp).
- Transferring Opus-evolved skills to six additional LLMs (GPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Haiku 4.5, Qwen3-Coder-480B, DeepSeek V3-671B, and Mistral Large 3-675B) consistently improved performance by **+36 to +44pp** over their respective no-skill baselines.
- While Opus-transferred skills improved GPT-5.2's performance (65.0%), its own self-evolved skills (69.8%) still provided a modest 4.8pp advantage.

- **Takeaway 2: Skills are portable across model families.** The evolved skills appear to encode reusable task structures rather than artifacts specific to the originating model, enabling broad transferability. Model-matched evolution offered a slight advantage but was not a prerequisite for substantial gains.

#### 4.4. RQ4: Domain-Level Analysis

A domain-level analysis on SkillsBench revealed varied performance distributions:
- Self-evolved skills outperformed human-curated skills in **9 out of 11 domains**, with the largest improvements observed in Finance (+56.9pp over human-curated) and Cybersecurity (+23.2pp).
- Notably, in domains like Natural Science, human-curated skills led to degraded performance, whereas self-evolved skills yielded substantial gains. In contrast, domains where human-curated skills already performed well (e.g., Energy, Robotics) showed diminishing returns from evolution.

- **Takeaway 3: Human–machine misalignment.** In some domains, human-curated skills were not only unhelpful but could actively degrade performance. This indicated a potential misalignment between human-designed workflows and LLM agent reasoning. Co-evolutionary optimization helped bridge this gap by allowing the model to discover procedures aligned with its own strengths.

#### 4.5. Evolution Dynamics

The pass rate of EvoSkills increased progressively over evolution rounds. Starting at a level comparable to the no-skill baseline at round 0, it surpassed human-curated skills by round 3 (63%) and converged at 75% by round 5. On average, each task required 4.1 verification cycles and 2.4 ground-truth oracle rounds to converge. Approximately 40% of iterations were resolved solely by the surrogate verifier, absorbing much of the iteration cost.

#### 4.6. Case Study: Exoplanet Transit Period Detection

A detailed case study for the Exoplanet Transit Period Detection task illustrated the iterative evolution process. The agent required four skill versions to achieve 100% pass rate. Initial versions, using Box Least Squares (BLS), failed due to precision issues (caught by the ground-truth oracle), prompting a shift to Transit Least Squares (TLS) with a two-stage refinement and alias checks. The surrogate verifier effectively caught early implementation bugs and regressions, but the ground-truth oracle was crucial for exposing fundamental algorithmic limitations (e.g., BLS precision ceiling) that the surrogate, lacking hidden test details, could not discern. The final self-evolved skill was more prescriptive, executable, and robust than the human-curated documentation, bundling 9 callable functions and a detailed procedure, leading to 100% pass rate in independent trials.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EvoSkills framework represents a notable advancement in the development of autonomous LLM agents, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Autonomous Skill Generation and Evolution:** EvoSkills provides a robust framework for LLM agents to autonomously generate and iteratively refine complex, multi-file skill packages. This addresses a critical bottleneck in LLM agent development, moving beyond manual, labor-intensive skill authoring towards scalable, self-improving agent capabilities.

2. **Addressing Human–Machine Cognitive Misalignment:** The research highlights that human-curated skills can sometimes degrade agent performance due to a misalignment between human expert reasoning and LLM agent operational patterns. EvoSkills demonstrates that agents can evolve skills that are better suited to their own intrinsic reasoning and tool-use strategies, leading to superior performance in various professional domains. This suggests a paradigm shift in how agent capabilities are developed, prioritizing agent-centric learning over human prescription.

3. **Overcoming Ground-Truth Dependency in Evolution:** By coupling a Skill Generator with an information-isolated Surrogate Verifier, EvoSkills effectively addresses the challenge of evolving skills without reliance on detailed ground-truth feedback. The surrogate verifier provides actionable diagnostics for internal refinement, while an opaque ground-truth oracle serves as an ultimate, unbiased arbiter. This design significantly enhances the applicability of self-evolving agents in real-world scenarios where comprehensive ground-truth information is often unavailable.

4. **Demonstrating Skill Portability and Generalizability:** The findings indicate that self-evolved skills are portable across diverse LLM model families from multiple providers. This suggests that the evolved skill packages encode reusable task structures and robust workflows rather than model-specific artifacts or prompt-level heuristics. This portability implies that skills developed with one advanced LLM could potentially benefit a wide range of other models, democratizing access to complex agent capabilities and reducing the need for model-specific training or fine-tuning.

5. **Advancing LLM Agents for Complex Professional Tasks:** By enabling agents to manage multi-step, open-ended professional tasks that require orchestration of multiple tools and artifacts, EvoSkills contributes to the development of more capable and general-purpose LLM agents. This has potential implications for applications in software engineering, scientific analysis, finance, cybersecurity, and other domains where complex procedural guidance is essential.

6. **Paving the Way for Continual Learning and Adaptation:** The iterative and co-evolutionary nature of EvoSkills lays groundwork for agents that can continuously learn, adapt, and improve their capabilities over time. This offers a path towards more resilient and intelligent autonomous systems.

In conclusion, EvoSkills presents a method for self-evolving agent skills that enhances performance, addresses current limitations in skill generation, and provides a foundation for future advancements in autonomous and adaptive LLM agents. Future work may extend this framework to multi-model skill evolution, potentially further broadening its applicability and impact.
