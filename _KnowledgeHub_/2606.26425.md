# A System for Fast, Resilient, and Adaptable Loco-Manipulation Behaviors on Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> An architecture developed by Duncan William Calvert with IHMC Robotics enables humanoid robots to perform fast, resilient, and adaptable loco-manipulation. The system, featuring robot-local, runtime-editable behaviors and behavior-time perception, achieved door traversal in as little as 14 seconds and reduced complex task authoring time to under two hours while demonstrating robust recovery from disturbances.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges performing complex loco-manipulation tasks in human-designed environments, requiring intricate coordination of locomotion, whole-body motion, and perception.
- Existing robot behavior systems often lack the speed, resilience, and efficient adaptability needed for dynamic real-world scenarios.
- Developing fast and reliable door traversal capabilities for bipedal humanoids, which involves precise foot placement, balance, and dynamic interaction, remains a significant hurdle.

## Method

- A robot-local, ==runtime-editable behavior architecture== was developed, enabling operators to modify and adapt robot behaviors directly on the robot.
- The system integrates an interactive operator interface (RDX) with synchronized UI state via Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs), concurrent action execution, and reactive behavior tree logic.
- Behavior-time semantic perception modules, such as YOLO and FoundationPose, dynamically configure and manage sensing based on task requirements, replacing fixed pipelines or external tracking.

## Results

- A Nadia humanoid robot achieved a door traversal speed of 14 seconds for a left push-bar door, and other complex tasks like multi-room exploration were completed in under 8 minutes.
- The system demonstrated high reliability, performing 11-12 consecutive successful door operations and robustly recovering from multiple human-induced disturbances during tasks.
- New, fully autonomous loco-manipulation behaviors could be authored from scratch in under 2 hours, and existing behaviors were adapted or extended to new environments/tasks within minutes to a few hours.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Runtime editability of behaviors and perception modules, combined with synchronized operator UI state, drastically reduces the iteration loop for creating and debugging complex robot actions.
> - Concurrent execution of locomotion and manipulation actions, alongside reactive behavior tree logic, significantly improves task speed and resilience to disturbances.
> - Integrating dynamically configured, behavior-time semantic perception directly within the behavior structure enhances flexibility, allowing robots to adapt to diverse objects and task contexts using only passive vision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenges of enabling humanoid robots to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks in human-designed environments, such as factories, shipyards, and construction sites. While humanoid robots offer the advantage of operating in spaces built for humans, coordinating their locomotion, whole-body motion, perception, and contact capabilities, especially under operator supervision, remains a significant challenge. The dissertation highlights a gap in existing literature, particularly concerning fast and reliable door traversal with bipedal humanoids, which often requires precise foot placement, balance management, and dynamic interaction with articulated objects.

The work builds upon and synthesizes several foundational architectural concepts:
- **Affordance Templates**: This framework, developed during the DARPA Robotics Challenge (DRC), provides a method for parameterizing and reusing robot behaviors based on environmental features that "afford" certain actions.
- **Behavior Trees (BTs)**: These data structures offer a means of coordinating low-level actions through structured and reactive task logic, including control nodes (sequence, fallback, parallel) and leaf nodes (actions, conditions).
- **Coactive Design**: This methodology emphasizes designing human-machine systems for interdependence, focusing on observability, predictability, and directability to enhance human-robot team performance.

The research differentiates itself from prior systems by combining several architectural desiderata:
- **Runtime Editability**: Unlike many systems that require offline coding, redeployment, or policy retraining for behavior modification, this work allows operators to edit and adapt behaviors directly on the robot at runtime.
- **Robot-Local Execution**: The system runs autonomy and perception processes onboard the robot, reducing dependence on external computing or continuous high-bandwidth communication, which enhances robustness in comms-degraded environments.
- **Behavior-Time Perception**: Perception modules are configured and managed dynamically within the behavior structure, rather than relying on fixed, global pipelines or external tracking infrastructure (e.g., motion capture, fiducial markers).
- **Integrated Loco-Manipulation**: The system is designed to coordinate both locomotion and manipulation, addressing tasks that require simultaneous walking and arm movements.

Compared to existing literature on door traversal systems, including classical model-based approaches (e.g., Jain & Kemp, Chitta, Axelrod & Huang, Banerjee et al.) and recent learning-based methods (e.g., Zhang et al., DoorMan, Sumo), this work uniquely demonstrates competitive speed and reliability on real humanoid hardware while maintaining a human-interpretable, editable, and composable behavior architecture. Many learned systems often rely on off-board inference or retraining-centered adaptation, which contrasts with the edit-centered workflow promoted here.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a behavior architecture that enables humanoid robots to perform physically demanding, hazardous, and repetitive tasks in human-centric environments. This system aims to facilitate the rapid creation, adaptation, extension, and combination of loco-manipulation behaviors.

The work is driven by three primary research questions:
1. **Behavior Architecture Sufficiency**: What specific behavior architecture can deliver fast and robust performance for a diverse range of humanoid loco-manipulation tasks?
2. **Comparative Performance**: How does this architecture quantitatively compare to prior IHMC baselines and state-of-the-art reinforcement learning systems in terms of task traversal time, reliability, and coverage of task variations?
3. **Operator Workflow Efficiency**: How does the runtime-editable behavior structure impact the time and effort required for expert operators to create new behaviors and to adapt, extend, and compose existing ones?

To address these questions, the dissertation establishes three core hypotheses, which also serve as the guiding principles and metrics for evaluation:
1. **Speed and Reliability (Hypothesis 1)**: Robot-local execution, synchronized user interface (UI) state, concurrent action layering, reactive tree logic, and behavior-time semantic perception will lead to door behaviors that are faster and more reliable than previous IHMC baselines and competitive with reported reinforcement learning systems on comparable door tasks.
2. **Fast Behavior Authoring (Hypothesis 2)**: The runtime-editable nature of behaviors and perception modules will significantly reduce the iteration loop needed for diagnosing failures, modifying logic, and re-testing on the robot, especially when compared to traditional redeploy, restart, or retrain development workflows.
3. **Fast Adaptation, Extension, and Combination (Hypothesis 3)**: By decomposing behaviors into reusable primitives, subtrees, and scene actions, new loco-manipulation variants, such as different door types, can be developed quickly by editing and extending existing components rather than requiring a complete rebuild from scratch.

These objectives are underpinned by a comprehensive set of desirable characteristics for a humanoid robot behavior architecture, including: high capability across tasks, feasibility within real-world constraints, high execution speed, support for parallelizable actions, strong reliability and robustness to disturbances, resilience to task failures (e.g., through retry mechanisms), independence from external systems, reliance on only passive color vision, and high adaptability of the operator-robot team (achieved via observability, predictability, and directability of the system). Furthermore, the system aims for learnability, understandability, usability, and strong debuggability, testability, and extendability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of the behavior system spanned roughly a decade, evolving from rudimentary teleoperation and hard-coded scripts to a sophisticated runtime-editable architecture.

**Evolutionary Path:**
- **DARPA Robotics Challenge Era (2014-2015)**: Initial efforts focused on teleoperation with a hybrid UI, employing virtual object placement and scripted, step-by-step actions that lacked full autonomy or runtime editability.
- **Hard-Coded State Machine Era (2016-2019)**: Behaviors were implemented as hard-coded Java state machines and pipelines, relying on direct whole-body controller APIs and basic perception (OpenCV, lidar). This era introduced A* footstep planning and planar region segmentation for rough terrain but suffered from slow iteration due to code-level modifications and split UI frameworks.
- **RDX and Early Behavior Tree Era (2020-2021)**: This period saw the development of Robot Data eXplorer (RDX), a unified sandbox for robot data exploration, algorithm development, and teleoperation, featuring integrated VR support, dockable panels, and 3D visualization. Perceptive locomotion (using RealSense L515, then Ouster OS0-128 lidar and Blackfly cameras) enabled continuous walking over rough terrain. Initial, hard-coded behavior trees were used for multi-task building exploration demos.
- **Runtime-Editable Sequences and Semantic Perception (2022-2024)**: The system transitioned to a fully runtime-editable behavior authoring pipeline. Behavior state synchronization between the robot and operator UI was achieved using Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs) over ROS 2 DDS. This enabled real-time editing and remote supervision. Key features included interactive footstep plan editing, a structured node decomposition (definition, state, executor, UI layers), and the introduction of the screw primitive action for constrained manipulation. Semantic perception via YOLO models replaced fiducial markers. An "execute after" concurrency mechanism allowed for overlapping actions, significantly improving speed. Hard-coded disturbance recovery heuristics were also implemented.
- **Advanced Manipulation and Reactive Logic (2024-2026)**: Further architectural enhancements focused on runtime-editable logic. Fallback, condition, and goto nodes were integrated to enable reactive behaviors. A dedicated "Behavior Scene" module was introduced, owned by the behavior system, managing instant and persistent object detections (YOLO, FoundationPose). "Scene actions" were developed to dynamically configure perception models (e.g., confidence thresholds for YOLO classes), set up and freeze scene objects (e.g., for grasp planning without occlusion), and derive complex environmental features (e.g., Door Panel, Door Frame, Approach Table). Five-finger anthropomorphic hands (PSYONIC Ability Hand) were integrated for improved manipulation reliability. Novel heuristic scene objects like the "Approach Table" were developed for accurate table-edge detection using depth data.

**Current Architecture (as of 2026):**
The system comprises three main domains: Behavior Coordination, Perception, and Whole-Body Control.
- **Process Structure**: The robot computer hosts two main processes: one for Perception and Autonomy (managing camera ingest, YOLO/FoundationPose detections, behavior tree, and scene updates) and another for Whole-Body Control (handling low-level hardware I/O, state estimation, and control loops). The operator computer runs a separate UI process. ROS 2 DDS facilitates communication, with CRDTs synchronizing behavior state bidirectionally.
- **Node Structure**: Each behavior tree node is decomposed into a persistent JSON definition, a synchronized runtime state, a robot-side executor, and an operator-side UI implementation, promoting modularity and maintainability.
- **Whole-Body Controller Integration**: The behavior coordination layer interfaces with a momentum-based whole-body and walking controller, capable of executing asynchronous trajectory requests for footsteps and body parts (arms, legs, spine, neck).
- **Operator Interface (RDX)**: The RDX framework provides a comprehensive UI with dockable panels, an interactive 3D scene, and integrated VR support, enabling behavior authoring, digital-twin visualization, perception debugging, and teleoperation.
- **Object-Centric Action Definition**: Actions (e.g., hand poses, footstep goals) can be defined relative to specific robot frames or dynamically perceived scene objects, enhancing reusability and robustness to accumulating errors.
- **Behavior Tree Structure**: The tree combines elements of traditional Behavior Trees and state machines, using a depth-first ordering of leaves and a "next execution index" that can be modified by operator input or reactive logic (fallback, condition, goto nodes).
- **Behavior Scene**: This robot-local, behavior-owned component manages persistent object detections and allows for authored "scene actions." It supports direct object types (YOLO, FoundationPose) and derived objects like the Door Panel (from mechanism and panel detections), Door Frame (inferred from panel state and depth), Composite/Hybrid Frames (for abstracting approach postures), and the Approach Table object (using depth-based capsule sweeps for table edge detection).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the system is structured around the three research hypotheses, supported by quantitative measurements from real-robot demonstrations.

**Support for Hypothesis 1: Fast and Reliable Loco-Manipulation Behaviors**

- **Speed and Capability**:
 - **Fastest Door Traversal**: A Nadia humanoid robot achieved a left push-bar door traversal in **14 seconds**, demonstrating continuous walking concurrent with arm actions. Other traversals on Nadia were completed in 27 seconds (right pull-handle), and on Alex in 34 seconds (left push lever) and 45 seconds (right pull lever). These speeds are competitive with, and in some cases faster than, reported state-of-the-art learned systems and significantly faster than classical model-based approaches (which typically exceed one minute).
 - **Complex Tasks**: The system enabled a 7-minute 45-second multi-room exploration "ONR Demo" on Nadia, involving five door passages, furniture manipulation, and blocked doorway recovery. On Alex, a reactive single-table ball sorting task was completed in 45.2 seconds, successfully sorting six balls under human disturbance. This was extended to a two-table loco-manipulation sorting task, where nine balls were sorted across two tables in 2 minutes and 8 seconds, involving locomotion between stations.
 - **Locomotion**: Early work on Nadia demonstrated autonomous rough-terrain locomotion at 0.079 m/s, 3.4 times faster than a contemporary baseline.
- **Reliability and Resilience**:
 - **Repeated Door Operations**: Alex achieved 11 consecutive successful left push door approach and opening trials, and 12 consecutive successful right pull door approach and opening trials. The Unitree H1-2 performed 32 consecutive successful standing right pull lever door openings. These tests demonstrated high reliability for core loco-manipulation sequences.
 - **Disturbance Recovery**: A Nadia pull door opening successfully recovered from five distinct human-induced disturbances (e.g., door re-closure, grasp slip, blocked reach), completing the full traversal. An Alex right pull door traversal similarly recovered from four disturbances during opening and waited for a blocked doorway to clear before proceeding, with the retry and wait logic explicitly authored in the behavior tree. The reactive ball sorting also recovered from a ball removal disturbance.

**Support for Hypothesis 2: Fast Behavior Authoring**

The runtime-editable nature of the system significantly reduced the iteration loop for behavior development.
- **Scratch Authoring Speed**: A new, fully autonomous left push door traversal on Alex was authored from an empty sequence to first successful execution in **1 hour, 59 minutes, and 48 seconds** of active authoring. Other sessions include a Unitree H1-2 standing door opening behavior created in 31 minutes 43 seconds.
- **Iteration Efficiency**: These sessions demonstrated that diagnosing failures, modifying logic (e.g., adjusting arm trajectories, adding fallbacks), and re-testing on the robot could be done rapidly, often within minutes, without requiring code redeployment, system restarts, or extensive retraining cycles, which contrasts with the multi-day pipelines often associated with learned systems.

**Support for Hypothesis 3: Fast Adaptation, Extension, and Combination of Behaviors**

The modular and hierarchical design enabled efficient reuse and modification of existing behaviors.
- **Behavior Composition**: An existing bottle pickup behavior was successfully composed with a door traversal behavior, allowing Alex to carry a bottle through a door in one autonomous run, taking 1 hour 13 minutes 49 seconds of adaptation time.
- **Environmental Adaptation**: A lab right pull door traversal behavior was adapted to a real-world break room left pull door in 1 hour 54 minutes 3 seconds. This involved mirroring the initial behavior and then tuning opening mechanics, fallbacks, and traversal footsteps for the new environment.
- **Task Extension**: An existing single-table ball sorting behavior was extended to a two-table loco-manipulation sorting task, incorporating inter-table locomotion and color-dependent routing, in 1 hour 50 minutes 41 seconds (including off-robot pre-authoring).
- **Reusable Primitives**: The ability to edit small parts of existing behaviors, leverage reusable primitives (e.g., screw primitive), and utilize scene actions for dynamic perception configuration facilitated rapid adaptation to novel tasks and variations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by demonstrating a system that achieves fast, resilient, and adaptable loco-manipulation behaviors through a novel architectural integration.

- **Architectural Innovation**: The development of a robot-local, runtime-editable behavior architecture that unifies task logic, synchronized operator UI state, and behavior-time perception is a key advancement. This architecture leverages the strengths of human-robot teaming by making the robot's internal state and planned actions highly observable and directable by an expert operator.
- **Performance Benchmark**: The system establishes new performance benchmarks for humanoid robot door traversals, with a record of 14 seconds for a push-bar door, placing it among the fastest reported results globally. This demonstrates that classical, model-based control approaches, augmented with advanced perception and human-centric design, can rival the speed of advanced learned policies, but with greater transparency and editability.
- **Efficiency in Development**: The most impactful finding may be the measured speed of behavior creation and adaptation. Demonstrating that new, fully autonomous loco-manipulation behaviors can be developed from scratch in under two hours, and adapted or extended in minutes to hours, represents a drastic reduction in development iteration time compared to traditional code-and-deploy or data-driven retraining workflows. This efficiency is critical for deploying robots in dynamic, real-world scenarios where tasks and environments are constantly changing.
- **Perceptual Flexibility**: The introduction of behavior-time perception modules, such as the visual door-state estimator and the depth-based table-edge detection, highlights a flexible approach to environmental sensing. By allowing behaviors to dynamically configure and utilize perception data, the system can adapt to diverse objects and task contexts without needing to rely on fixed external sensors or comprehensive world models.
- **Practical Applicability**: By demonstrating robust performance across various door types, multi-room exploration, and complex object sorting tasks, this work moves humanoid robots closer to practical deployment in dull, dirty, and dangerous environments. The ability for expert operators to quickly diagnose, modify, and refine robot behaviors on the fly is essential for real-world scenarios where unforeseen challenges are common.

The dissertation suggests a future where transparent, understandable, and tinkerable open-source robotic systems could enable widespread adoption of personal and industrial robots, allowing technical users to customize and extend robot capabilities without reliance on proprietary black-box solutions. This contrasts with the prevailing trend towards large-scale, end-to-end learned systems by emphasizing a human-in-the-loop, modular approach that maximizes both autonomy and operator control.
