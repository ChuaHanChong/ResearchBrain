# SC3-Eval: Evaluating Robot Foundation Models via Self-Consistent Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> SC3-Eval introduces a self-consistent video generation framework for evaluating generalist robot manipulation policies, adapting a unified dynamics model to enforce multiple forms of consistency. This approach achieved a Pearson correlation of 0.984 and an MMRV of 0.022 for in-distribution online evaluation, also demonstrating enhanced reproduction of policy failure modes.

## Problem

- Evaluating generalist robot manipulation policies in real-world settings is expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale.
- Current action-conditioned video world models suffer from compounding errors, maintaining multi-camera consistency, and generalizing to varied policy behaviors.
- Physics-based simulators require significant manual effort for asset creation and dynamics tuning, often leading to sim-to-real gaps.

## Method

![[2606.18610-method.png]]
*The SC3 model is trained jointly using forward dynamics, inverse dynamics, and cross-view inpainting objectives to ensure multi-faceted consistency.*

- Adapts a pre-trained unified dynamics model, functioning as a world simulator capable of forward dynamics, inverse dynamics, and video inpainting.
- Employs self-consistent training that enforces forward-inverse dynamics consistency through joint optimization and cross-view consistency via inpainting held-out camera views.
- Integrates test-time consistency using prediction-execution horizon decoupling and an uncertainty-driven early termination mechanism derived from forward-inverse consistency error.

## Results

- Achieved a Pearson correlation of 0.984 and an MMRV of 0.022 for in-distribution online evaluation, showing superior performance compared to existing video world models.
- Reproduced specific policy failure modes with an average rate of 0.56 in online evaluation, providing valuable diagnostic insights beyond aggregate success rates.
- Ablation studies confirmed that forward-inverse dynamics consistency, cross-view consistency, prediction-execution horizon decoupling, and uncertainty-driven early termination each contribute positively to evaluation faithfulness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly training a unified dynamics model for both forward and inverse dynamics implicitly regularizes forward generations, mitigating autoregressive drift and promoting physical plausibility.
> - Explicit cross-view supervision through inpainting significantly improves multi-camera coherence in generated sequences, which is critical for robot manipulation.
> - Reusing the inverse dynamics mode during inference provides a cost-effective, single-model uncertainty signal to detect off-manifold rollouts and enable early termination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Evaluating the performance of generalist robot manipulation policies in real-world environments presents challenges due to high costs, time consumption, and difficulties in scaling. Traditional real-world evaluations necessitate physical robot time, meticulous environment resets, and human supervision, which become prohibitive for assessing policies across a diverse range of tasks and environments.

Current approaches to address these limitations generally fall into three categories:
1. **Physics-based simulators:** Examples include SIMPLER and RoboLab. While offering a simulated environment, these often require extensive hand-engineered assets and careful dynamics tuning, which can introduce a "sim-to-real" gap, meaning the performance in simulation may not perfectly translate to the real world.
2. **Real-to-sim approaches:** Methods like PolaRiS aim to reconstruct real scenes using techniques such as Gaussian splatting. However, these often require a new reconstruction for each specific scene, limiting their scalability.
3. **Video-model-based evaluators:** Recent work, including Ctrl-World, IRASim, and Cosmos-Predict, proposes using action-conditioned video world models to simulate policy rollouts. These models generate plausible future frames conditioned on initial observations and action sequences, allowing policy evaluation without direct physical robot interaction. SC3-Eval extends this family of evaluators.

This research specifically addresses three persistent challenges within video-model-based evaluation for real-world manipulation:
- **Compounding errors:** Autoregressive generation, where each predicted frame serves as input for the next, can lead to the accumulation of small errors over long horizons, causing generated rollouts to drift from reality.
- **Multi-camera consistency:** Robot policies often rely on observations from multiple synchronized cameras. Maintaining visual coherence and mutual consistency across these views is crucial, as inconsistencies can mislead the policy.
- **Generalization to out-of-distribution policies:** An evaluator must be capable of accurately predicting the performance of policies whose behaviors or task semantics differ from the data used to train the world model.

Furthermore, this work builds upon and contributes to the domain of unified dynamics models, such as UVA, which integrate both forward and inverse dynamics within a single architecture. The use of an inverse dynamics mode for an uncertainty signal connects to broader research in model-based reinforcement learning (RL) on addressing distribution shift and quantifying predictive uncertainty, often through ensembles or calibrated generative uncertainty, but SC3-Eval proposes a method derived from the model's inherent architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-consistent video generation framework, named SC3-Eval, that adapts a pre-trained video foundation model into a faithful and accurate closed-loop policy evaluator for generalist robot manipulation. This development is motivated by the significant resource demands and inherent scalability limitations of real-world policy evaluation.

The specific objectives addressed by SC3-Eval include:
- **Mitigating autoregressive drift:** To counteract the accumulation of errors in long autoregressive rollouts generated by video world models, which typically lack a direct mechanism to penalize physically implausible frames.
- **Ensuring multi-camera observation coherence:** To maintain mutual consistency across observations from multiple synchronized camera views, thereby preventing the policy from being misled by visually incoherent simulated scenes.
- **Improving generalization capabilities:** To enable the policy evaluator to generalize effectively to policies whose behaviors or task variants lie outside the distribution of its training data, facilitating evaluation of diverse or novel policies.
- **Providing diagnostic capabilities:** To allow for fine-grained diagnostic comparisons by reproducing specific policy failure modes observed in real-world rollouts, rather than merely providing aggregate performance metrics. This enables a deeper understanding of policy shortcomings (e.g., distinguishing a grasping issue from a perception issue).
- **Developing a cost-efficient uncertainty signal:** To integrate a reliability indicator into the evaluation process without requiring additional training costs or complex model ensembles, thereby enhancing the trustworthiness of simulated rollouts.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to provide a scalable, reliable, and diagnostically informative alternative to physical robot testing, thereby accelerating the development and iteration cycle for robot manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SC3-Eval operates as a self-consistent video generation recipe built upon a pre-trained video foundation model. It adapts a unified dynamics model architecture, similar to UVA, which functions as a single transformer capable of processing both video and action tokens. This architecture allows the model to perform forward dynamics (predicting frames from actions), inverse dynamics (recovering actions from frames), and video generation (infilling unobserved frames) by selectively masking different token subsets during training.

The methodology incorporates three complementary forms of consistency:

**Self-Consistent Training (Figure 2):**
During training, SC3-Eval enforces two types of consistency:
- **Forward-Inverse Dynamics Consistency:** This objective addresses the issue of physically implausible frames in forward-only models. The model is jointly trained to predict future frames given an action sequence (forward dynamics) and to recover the action sequence given video frames (inverse dynamics). Since both modes share the same underlying parameters, the forward mode is implicitly regularized to generate frames from which the inverse mode can accurately reconstruct the commanded actions. This regularization anchors generated rollouts to a physically plausible action manifold, effectively counteracting autoregressive drift.
- **Cross-View Consistency:** To maintain coherence across multiple camera views (e.g., two third-person and one wrist camera), a cross-view inpainting mode is introduced. In this mode, one camera view is randomly selected and "held out," and the model is tasked with inpaining this missing view from the remaining views and the action sequence. This explicit supervision ensures that multi-camera observations remain mutually consistent over long autoregressive rollouts without requiring an explicit memory mechanism.

The training procedure initializes the model with pre-trained weights from Cosmos3-Nano, a variant of the Cosmos3 family. The three objectives (multi-view forward dynamics, cross-view inpainting, and inverse dynamics) are jointly trained by randomly sampling each training instance into one of these modes with fixed probabilities (0.8 for forward dynamics, 0.1 for cross-view inpainting, 0.1 for inverse dynamics). The loss function is a flow matching objective applied to noised tokens. Training augmentations include pseudo-action augmentation (replacing policy commands with pseudo-actions from real-world end-effector poses) and multi-FPS training (sampling at both 10 Hz and 20 Hz) to broaden the model's exposure to diverse temporal statistics.

**Inference with Test-Time Consistency (Figure 1c, Algorithm 1):**
During policy evaluation, SC3-Eval implements a closed-loop rollout schedule that mimics real-world robot operation:
- **Prediction-Execution Horizon Decoupling:** The world model is trained to predict frames over a longer horizon ($l'=24$ frames) than the policy's execution horizon ($l=16$ frames). At inference, the policy proposes an action chunk, the forward mode generates $l'$ frames, but only the first $l$ frames are appended to the observation context for the next planning step. This decoupling preserves the pre-training prior of the backbone (trained on longer video clips) and provides richer per-chunk supervision during training, leading to improved generation quality.
- **Uncertainty-Driven Early Termination:** This mechanism leverages the inverse dynamics mode at inference. For each generated action chunk, the forward mode ($W_{fd}$) renders frames based on the commanded actions ($a_{t:t+l}$). Subsequently, the inverse mode ($W_{id}$) attempts to recover the actions ($\hat{a}_{t:t+l}$) from these *generated* frames. A per-chunk consistency error $U_{chunk}(t)$ is calculated as the mean squared error between the commanded and recovered actions. If this error exceeds a predefined threshold $\tau$ (tuned to 0.02 on a held-out dataset), the rollout is terminated. This functions as a per-action-chunk uncertainty signal, terminating rollouts when generated frames drift significantly from the requested actions, preventing the accumulation of compounded errors.

**Dataset:** The model is trained on a 381-hour real-world table bussing manipulation dataset, comprising trajectories collected in a single physical scene with 12 distinct object categories. This dataset includes synchronized streams from two third-person cameras and one wrist camera, recorded at 20 Hz. Actions are represented as 7-dimensional delta end-effector poses. For evaluation of generalization, a "reverse table bussing" task, absent from the training data, is used as an out-of-distribution scenario.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SC3-Eval focused on its ability to faithfully evaluate seven vision-language-action (VLA) policies across in-distribution (table bussing) and out-of-distribution (reverse table bussing) tasks. Performance was measured using Pearson correlation ($r$) for linear agreement and Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) for pairwise policy ranking consistency, comparing predicted performance against real-world robot rollouts. Baselines included Ctrl-World, IRASim, and Cosmos-Predict 2.5, all relying solely on forward dynamics generation.

**Overall Policy Evaluation Performance:**
- SC3-Eval demonstrated superior performance across multiple evaluation settings. In the most demanding online (closed-loop) in-distribution scenario, it achieved a Pearson correlation of 0.984 and an MMRV of 0.022. Across all four settings (offline/online, in-distribution/out-of-distribution), SC3-Eval consistently attained the best MMRV, indicating high fidelity in preserving policy rankings. It also achieved the best Pearson correlation in three of the four settings.
- Generalization to the out-of-distribution task variant, reverse table bussing, showed a degradation in performance across all evaluators, including SC3-Eval, as expected due to the task-semantic shift. However, SC3-Eval maintained higher correlation ($r=0.870$) and lower MMRV ($0.171$) compared to the baselines in the online out-of-distribution setting, demonstrating improved generalization.

**Offline vs. Online Evaluation:**
- Counter to the expectation that online evaluation would be less faithful due to policy interaction with imperfect generated frames, SC3-Eval's online performance on the in-distribution task (Pearson r = 0.984) slightly exceeded its offline performance (Pearson r = 0.959). This suggests that the closed-loop interaction allows the policy to react to generated frames, potentially compensating for some minor drift.
- The observed degradation in online evaluation was concentrated on the out-of-distribution split, indicating that when the world model itself is off-distribution, closed-loop correction has limitations.

**Reproduction of Policy Failure Modes:**
- Beyond aggregate success rates, SC3-Eval was evaluated on its ability to reproduce specific failure modes (language following, object lifting, object placing) observed in real-world trajectories.
- SC3-Eval achieved the highest average reproduction rate for outcome categories under online (closed-loop) evaluation, and led in all four outcome categories (no failure, language, lifting, placing failures). This indicates that SC3-Eval not only predicts if a trajectory will fail but also *why* it will fail, providing diagnostic information. Baselines exhibited a sharper drop in reproduction rates in the closed-loop setting.

**Ablation Study:**
A comprehensive ablation study confirmed the contribution of SC3-Eval's key design choices:
- **Forward-Inverse Dynamics Consistency:** Removing the inverse dynamics grounding objective resulted in degraded closed-loop metrics (Pearson r = 0.842, MMRV = 0.175) and offline visual quality (PSNR). The degradation was more pronounced in out-of-distribution scenarios, highlighting its role in anchoring rollouts to a physically plausible action manifold, especially when commanded actions push the model away from its training distribution.
- **Cross-View Inpainting:** Eliminating cross-view inpainting also degraded closed-loop metrics (Pearson r = 0.802, MMRV = 0.199) and offline visual quality. Qualitatively, this ablation particularly impacted wrist-view scene re-entry, where the model struggled to restore the correct workspace state after the wrist camera turned away and returned, confirming its importance for multi-view consistency.
- **Uncertainty-Driven Early Termination:** Disabling the early termination criterion (allowing all rollouts to complete) led to a degradation in closed-loop metrics (Pearson r = 0.871, MMRV = 0.151). The gains from early termination were more consistent and substantial on out-of-distribution tasks, where drift is more common. The per-chunk consistency error signal ($U_{chunk}$) derived for early termination also proved useful as context for VLM-based automatic annotation, improving VLM-derived policy-level success rates.
- **Prediction-Execution Horizon Decoupling:** Training the world model at the policy's execution horizon directly (without decoupling) degraded both closed-loop metrics (Pearson r = 0.807, MMRV = 0.177) and offline visual quality. This indicated that aligning the training horizon with the longer pre-training horizon of the backbone and providing richer per-chunk information during training is beneficial.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SC3-Eval represents a advancement in the field of robot policy evaluation, offering a more accurate, scalable, and diagnostically informative approach compared to previous methods. Its significance stems from several key contributions:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Fidelity:** By incorporating three forms of consistency—forward-inverse dynamics, cross-view, and test-time uncertainty—SC3-Eval addresses fundamental challenges in video world models, such as autoregressive drift and multi-view inconsistency. This results in more faithful policy simulations that closely align with real-world robot performance. The closed-loop Pearson correlation of 0.929 and MMRV of 0.119 indicate a high degree of agreement with real-world outcomes.
- **Cost-Effective Diagnostic Tool:** The ability of SC3-Eval to reproduce specific policy failure modes (e.g., language following, object lifting, object placing) rather than merely providing aggregate success rates offers diagnostic capabilities. This granular insight can aid robot policy developers in identifying and addressing the root causes of policy failures more efficiently, accelerating the iterative development cycle.
- **Improved Generalization:** Demonstrated performance on a held-out task semantic (reverse table bussing) suggests that SC3-Eval can generalize to out-of-distribution task variants, which is crucial for evaluating generalist robot policies in diverse scenarios without extensive real-world data collection for every new task.
- **Novel Uncertainty Quantification:** The reuse of the inverse dynamics mode to derive a per-action-chunk uncertainty signal for early termination provides a computationally efficient method to improve closed-loop reliability. This approach avoids the overhead associated with ensemble methods typically used for uncertainty estimation in model-based RL, as it is intrinsically linked to the unified dynamics model architecture.

**Limitations:**
Despite its contributions, SC3-Eval currently faces certain limitations:
- **Computational Speed:** Closed-loop online evaluation operates at 2.3 seconds per chunk on a single GB200 GPU, which is slower than real-time physics-based simulators. This speed constraint may limit its utility for rapid, routine checkpoint selection during the early stages of policy development.
- **Horizon Length:** The evaluator has been trained and validated on short-horizon manipulation tasks, with episodes typically lasting around 20 seconds. Its performance at substantially longer horizons may degrade due to accumulated local drift and potential visual incoherence stemming from the temporal coherence prior of the pre-trained backbone.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
SC3-Eval has the potential to substantially impact the development of robot manipulation policies by providing a reliable and scalable evaluation framework. Future work could focus on addressing the identified limitations, such as exploring diffusion-sampler acceleration techniques and caching intermediate representations to improve inference speed. Extending the evaluator to longer task horizons through methods like longer training trajectories or hierarchical sub-goal supervision could also enhance its applicability to more complex, multi-stage robotic tasks. The integration of the inverse dynamics-derived uncertainty signal with VLM-based automatic annotation also suggests avenues for further automation and scalability in policy evaluation.
