# Intern-S1-Pro: Scientific Multimodal Foundation Model at Trillion Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Laboratory introduced Intern-S1-Pro, the first scientific multimodal foundation model with one trillion parameters, which achieved superior performance on over 100 specialized scientific tasks and competitive results in general AI tasks. The model validated the "specializable generalist" concept, demonstrating that a large generalist model can outperform specialized counterparts in several scientific domains.

## Problem

- Developing scientific foundation models requires immense capacity to master diverse specialized tasks while maintaining general capabilities, facing challenges like training instability and load imbalance at trillion-parameter scales.
- High-quality, densely aligned scientific image-text pairs are scarce, impeding effective multimodal training for scientific domains.
- Integrating highly structured, logically deterministic scientific data with diverse general data often creates distribution shift and negative transfer, leading to training conflicts.

## Method

- Implemented ==Grouped Routing== and ==Straight-Through Estimator== for stable trillion-parameter ==Mixture-of-Experts== training, alongside a ==Native Vision Transformer== with ==Fourier Position Encoding== and a dedicated time-series encoder for multimodal data.
- Developed a specialized ==caption pipeline== to generate dense, aligned scientific image-text data from PDFs and applied strategies to transform structured scientific data and isolate system prompts to mitigate data conflicts.
- Applied stable mixed-precision reinforcement learning, including FP8 quantization and operator-by-operator precision comparison, to ensure stability and performance during post-training of sparse MoE models.

## Results

- Intern-S1-Pro achieved higher performance on several scientific benchmarks, including SciReasoner (**55.5**), SmolInstruct (**74.8**), and MatBench (**72.8**), surpassing some proprietary models.
- The model demonstrated competitive performance in general AI tasks, such as AIME-2025 (**93.1**) and MMLU-Pro (**86.6**), and exhibited robust agent capabilities, scoring **77.4** on GAIA (Text-Only) and **93.6** on ScreenSpot V2.
- Its dedicated time series processing module achieved an F1 score of **99.5** on the EAU01 task, and in a biology case study, the model significantly outperformed a specialized biology model (**52.45** vs. **39.24**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A sufficiently large generalist model, trained jointly on both general and specific tasks, can achieve superior performance compared to specialized models in various scientific domains, validating the "Specializable Generalist" hypothesis.
> - Architectural innovations like Grouped Routing and Straight-Through Estimator are effective in overcoming training instability and load imbalance challenges in ultra-large-scale Mixture-of-Experts architectures.
> - The development of a specialized caption pipeline for scientific figures and targeted strategies to resolve data conflicts are essential for effectively integrating diverse scientific multimodal data into large foundation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual Language Models (VLMs) has influenced artificial intelligence capabilities in areas such as reasoning, generation, and multimodal understanding. In the domain of AI for Science (AI4S), these foundation models are being utilized to support scientific discovery across various challenges, including protein structure prediction and materials design. Such large models can integrate diverse scientific literature, experimental data, and domain-specific knowledge, aiming to bridge gaps between disciplines.

A notable challenge in developing effective scientific foundation models is the diversity and specialization inherent in scientific domains, each possessing distinct notations, knowledge, and reasoning patterns. Prior research in multilingual machine translation has indicated that increasing the number of language pairs necessitates a proportional increase in model parameters to maintain performance. This principle suggests that a scientific foundation model requires substantial capacity to master a broad array of scientific tasks while retaining general text and vision capabilities.

This work builds upon previous efforts in multimodal foundation models, specifically Intern-S1, by exploring further scaling of model parameters. It addresses the technical and computational challenges associated with creating models of unprecedented size, particularly in the context of scientific applications. The paper posits that a sufficiently large generalist model, when trained on both general and specific tasks, can achieve or surpass the performance of specialized models, a notion that sometimes runs counter to the expectation that specialized models inherently perform better on niche tasks. The research contributes to the ongoing discourse on model scaling, architectural innovations for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, and the integration of diverse scientific modalities and data types within a unified framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is the introduction of Intern-S1-Pro, positioned as the first one-trillion-parameter scientific multimodal foundation model. The work aims to achieve a comprehensive enhancement of capabilities across both general and scientific domains by scaling the model to this size.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Enhancing Core Intelligence:** The model aims to exhibit stronger reasoning capabilities and improved image-text understanding. This objective is driven by the general trend in AI research to develop more capable and versatile foundation models.
- **Augmenting with Advanced Agent Capabilities:** Intern-S1-Pro is designed to incorporate advanced agent capabilities, allowing it to autonomously plan and execute complex scientific workflows. This seeks to move beyond passive knowledge retrieval to active problem-solving within scientific contexts.
- **Expanding Scientific Expertise:** A core objective is to significantly expand the model's scientific expertise to master over 100 specialized tasks across critical science fields. These fields include chemistry, materials science, life sciences, and earth sciences, reflecting the diverse and specialized nature of scientific inquiry. The motivation here is to create a "Specializable Generalist" that can excel in both broad and deep scientific applications.
- **Validating the "Specializable Generalist" Hypothesis:** The research is motivated by the hypothesis that a sufficiently large generalist model, when subjected to joint training on general and specific tasks, can outperform models explicitly designed for specialization in niche scientific tasks. This challenges conventional approaches that often favor highly specialized architectures for specific domains.
- **Addressing Scaling Challenges:** Scaling models to a trillion parameters introduces architectural and engineering challenges. The work aims to address issues such as:
 - **Training instability:** Specifically, load imbalance among a massive number of experts in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, which can lead to memory spikes.
 - **Router optimization difficulty:** Ensuring efficient learning and optimization of router embeddings, particularly with an expanded expert pool, is a critical technical objective.
- **Achieving Efficient Trillion-Parameter Training:** A practical motivation is to achieve massive-scale training with high efficiency and precision consistency between training and inference. This is supported by leveraging robust infrastructure such as XTuner for training and LMDeploy for inference, to enable efficient Reinforcement Learning (RL) at the 1-trillion parameter level.

In summary, the motivation behind Intern-S1-Pro is to demonstrate the feasibility and benefits of extreme scaling in multimodal foundation models for scientific applications, while simultaneously addressing the technical complexities involved in such an undertaking.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of Intern-S1-Pro involved architectural enhancements, a specialized pre-training pipeline, and a stable post-training reinforcement learning strategy.

#### 3.1. Architecture

Intern-S1-Pro is an expansion of its predecessor, Intern-S1, primarily through an increased number of experts.

- **Grouped Routing:** To address training instability and load imbalance in ultra-large-scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, a Grouped Routing mechanism was introduced. In this approach, experts are uniformly partitioned into `G` mutually disjoint groups based on device mapping. For each group, only the top-`K/G` experts with the highest scores are selected. The final set of activated experts is the union of these intra-group top experts. For Intern-S1-Pro (k=8, EP8 strategy), this involves dividing experts into 8 groups and selecting the Top-1 expert within each group, aiming for absolute load balancing across devices. This design aims to enhance training stability and eliminate Out-of-Memory (OOM) risks.
- **Straight-Through Estimator (STE) for Sparse Expert Routing:** The discrete selection in Top-K routing, where only selected experts receive gradient updates, can impede router optimization. STE is implemented to decouple the forward and backward passes. During the forward pass, standard sparse Top-K selection is preserved. In the backward pass, gradients flow through the full dense softmax distribution, ensuring all expert routing logits receive a gradient regardless of their selection status. A temperature parameter `τ` is used to adjust gradient sharpness.
- **Vision Encoder:** A Native Vision Transformer (ViT) serves as the vision encoder, processing images at their native resolution. This design allows for flexible handling of varying input resolutions and preservation of fine-grained spatial information. Visual tokens are projected into the language model's embedding space via a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) projector. The encoder is trained using contrastive learning on approximately 300 million image-text pairs, sourced from English (CC12M, LAION-COCO, SBU Caption) and Chinese (LAION-2B-Multi, Wukong) datasets, to improve visual representation and cross-modal alignment.
- **Fourier Position Encoding (FoPE):** FoPE is introduced to address limitations of traditional positional encodings (e.g., RoPE) in modeling continuous, wave-like, and spectral characteristics inherent in physical signals. FoPE models each dimension as a Fourier series of different frequency components, aiming to separate information more effectively and mitigate spectral damage. It also involves clipping inadequately trained frequency components.
- **Time-series Encoder:** An enhanced time-series module, building on Intern-S1, expands coverage to include domains like physiological signal analysis and bioacoustics. The architecture comprises an adaptive subsampling module and a time series encoder. The adaptive subsampling partitions continuous signals into local segments (patches), whose size and stride are dynamically determined based on the signal and its sampling rate. This normalizes heterogeneous time series into a uniform representation space, allowing the encoder to handle sequences from 10^0 to 10^6 time steps while preserving structural features.

#### 3.2. Pre-training

Intern-S1-Pro underwent continued pre-training on 6T tokens of image-text and text data. A key focus was on improving understanding of scientific images.

- **Caption Pipeline:** To address the scarcity and low quality of scientific image-text pairs in existing datasets, a dedicated caption pipeline was developed.
 - **PDF Extraction:** MinerU2.5 is used for layout analysis and structural recognition to extract and crop figures, formulas, and tables from massive PDF corpora.
 - **Content Deduplication:** Perceptual hashing (pHash) is applied for deduplication of visual content.
 - **Topic Classification and Model Routing:** Scientific sub-images are captioned using InternVL3.5-241B for domain-specific captions, while non-scientific images are processed by CapRL-32B, a Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) trained model designed for dense image captioning.
 - **Quality Filtering:** A 0.5B-parameter text quality discriminator filters out low-quality text.
 - This pipeline generated approximately 270B tokens of scientific image-text caption data.
- **Resolving Conflicts between Scientific and Textual Data:** To mitigate "distribution shift" and "negative transfer" between logically deterministic scientific data and semantically diverse general data, three strategies were employed:
 - **Structured Scientific Data Transformation:** Highly structured scientific data (e.g., tabular information from PubChem) is converted into grammatically correct, narrative text using template construction and task form transformation. This maps numerical symbols to descriptive answers with scientific meaning.
 - **Scientific Data Diversification:** Prompt Diversification generates varied instructions for the same scientific concept to prevent overfitting from data repetition. The Rollout mechanism leverages a base model and scientific prior knowledge to generate complete reasoning chains, enhancing zero-shot reasoning.
 - **System Prompt Isolation:** Mutually exclusive system-level prefixes are injected for scientific and general data during training to create independent contextual processing environments, aiming to reduce conflicts and improve stability.

#### 3.3. Post-Training

- **Stable Mixed-Precision Reinforcement Learning for Sparse MoE Models:** Scaling RL to trillion-parameter MoE models creates memory challenges, particularly with FP8 quantization. A comprehensive stabilization framework was implemented:
 - **Operator-level Precision Discrepancies:** Systematic comparison between the LMDeploy rollout engine and XTuner training engine identified numerically sensitive components (RMSNorm, router softmax, positional embedding). Precision gaps in these kernels were minimized.
 - **Rollout Router Replay:** Expert indices selected during rollout are recorded and replayed during policy updates to ensure consistency between rollout and training. Ray object references are used for efficient trace transmission.
 - **Targeted Mixed-Precision Scheme:** Expert MLP layers are quantized to FP8 due to their large memory footprint and relative tolerance to reduced precision. Non-expert components are kept in BF16, and an FP32 Language Modeling (LM) head is used to improve numerical accuracy of log-probabilities.
 - **REINFORCE Objective with Dual Importance Sampling Ratios:** The REINFORCE objective is modified with two importance sampling ratios to calibrate for training-inference distribution mismatch and off-policy bias. A masking function `M` suppresses tokens with excessively large training–rollout discrepancies.
 - **Leave-One-Out (LOO) Baseline:** An LOO baseline is used for advantage estimation across sampled responses.

These methodological components collectively aim to enable the training and deployment of a trillion-parameter multimodal model with strong performance, stability, and broad scientific utility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Intern-S1-Pro encompassed a wide array of scientific and general-purpose benchmarks, covering both text-only and multimodal tasks, using OpenCompass, VLMEvalKit, and AgentCompass toolkits. Evaluations were conducted under "thinking" and "non-thinking" configurations.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

Intern-S1-Pro demonstrated competitive capabilities among open-source models and exhibited notable performance in scientific reasoning, often surpassing leading closed-source models.

- **Scientific Tasks:** Intern-S1-Pro achieved higher scores compared to proprietary models such as Gemini-3-Pro and GPT-5.2 on several benchmarks. For example, it scored 55.5 on SciReasoner, which was considerably higher than Gemini-3-Pro (14.7) and GPT-5.2 (13.6). It also secured the top position on benchmarks including SmolInstruct (74.8), MatBench (72.8), Mol-Instructions (48.8), Biology-Instruction (52.5), and XLRS-Bench (52.8).
- **General Tasks:** The model maintained strong performance in general tasks, achieving 93.1 on AIME-2025 and 86.6 on MMLU-Pro, performing comparably to or exceeding open-source models such as Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking.

#### 4.2. Improvements over Intern-S1

Compared to its predecessor, Intern-S1, Intern-S1-Pro showed advancements in three main areas:

- **General Capabilities:** Performance improvements were observed across general benchmarks; for instance, AIME-2025 scores increased from 86.0 to 93.1, and MMLU-Pro scores rose from 83.5 to 86.6.
- **Scientific Task Coverage:** The model's coverage of scientific tasks expanded to include new domains like SciReasoner, Mol-Instructions, and Biology-Instruction, while maintaining performance in foundational scientific benchmarks.
- **Agent Capabilities:** Intern-S1-Pro demonstrated enhanced agent capabilities, achieving scores of 77.4 on GAIA (Text-Only), 80.9 on τ2-Bench, and 93.6 on ScreenSpot V2, indicating advancements in multi-step planning and environmental grounding.

#### 4.3. Time Series Results

Evaluation on a subset of the SciTS benchmark indicated that Intern-S1-Pro, with its dedicated time series processing module, exhibited performance superior to both Text LLMs (e.g., GPT-4.1-mini, DeepSeek-V3) and Vision-Language LLMs (e.g., GPT-5-mini, Gemini2.5-Flash). For instance, on the EAU01 task, Intern-S1-Pro achieved an F1 score of 99.5, compared to 67.0 for GPT-4.1-mini and 67.6 for GPT-5-mini. Similar improvements were observed on tasks such as BIU03 and PHU04.

#### 4.4. Specializable Generalist Case Study in Biology

A case study comparing Intern-S1-Pro against a specialized domain model, Biology-Instruction, on biological sequence and structure tasks showed Intern-S1-Pro achieving higher overall performance. Both models were trained on the same core biological information, with Intern-S1-Pro's data benefiting from more fluent text expression. Intern-S1-Pro scored 78.14 on Protein-Fluorescence compared to Biology-Instruction's 2.57, and 72.70 on Protein-FunctionEC compared to 19.79. These results suggest that the larger model size and broader general reasoning capabilities of Intern-S1-Pro allowed for more effective utilization of specialized data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Intern-S1-Pro represents an advancement in the field of large-scale scientific multimodal foundation models. Its development at the trillion-parameter scale demonstrates the feasibility of constructing models of this size for scientific applications.

The architectural innovations, including Grouped Routing and the Straight-Through Estimator for sparse expert routing, contribute to managing the complexities of training such large Mixture-of-Experts models. These methods aim to enhance training stability and efficiency by addressing issues like load imbalance and gradient sparsity. The incorporation of Fourier Position Encoding (FoPE) suggests an approach to model continuous and wave-like characteristics in data, potentially refining how transformer models process complex signals. The dedicated time-series encoder, with its adaptive subsampling, addresses the challenges of diverse temporal data, expanding the model's applicability across various scientific fields that rely on time-series analysis.

The specialized caption pipeline for scientific imagery and the strategies for resolving conflicts between scientific and general data are significant contributions to data preparation for multimodal AI in science. These methods aim to produce high-quality, domain-aligned training data, which is crucial for a model's understanding and reasoning abilities in complex scientific contexts.

The evaluation results indicate that Intern-S1-Pro exhibits competitive performance across general AI benchmarks and achieves high performance in a range of scientific tasks, sometimes surpassing proprietary models. The concept of the "Specializable Generalist," wherein a large generalist model can outperform specialized models even on domain-specific tasks, has implications for future model development paradigms in AI for science. This suggests that broad intelligence, when sufficiently scaled and appropriately trained, can generalize effectively to specialized knowledge domains.

The potential impact of Intern-S1-Pro lies in its capacity to accelerate scientific discovery by providing a unified interface for processing and reasoning over diverse scientific data and literature. Its advanced agent capabilities could enable more autonomous execution of scientific workflows. This work contributes to the capabilities of open-source models at a scale previously less explored for scientific applications, potentially fostering further research and development in AI for science.
