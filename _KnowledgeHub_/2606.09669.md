# SpatialWorld: Benchmarking Interactive Spatial Reasoning of Multimodal Agents in Real-World Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialWorld establishes a unified, simulator-agnostic benchmark to evaluate the interactive spatial understanding of multimodal agents in real-world tasks, operating under vision-only partial observability and a text-based action interface. Evaluation of 15 state-of-the-art MLLMs revealed limited reliability, with the top model achieving only 17.4% average task success rate, underscoring critical vulnerabilities in dynamic physical environments.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for MLLM spatial reasoning are either passive, failing to capture dynamic, interactive real-world challenges under partial observability.
- Current embodied AI benchmarks are often simulator-specific, providing privileged information or low-level interfaces that hinder assessment of general interactive spatial reasoning.
- A unified evaluation framework was needed to assess MLLMs' high-level spatial understanding using only egocentric vision and language-based actions across diverse 3D environments.

## Method

- Developed SPATIALWORLD, a benchmark operating under vision-only ==partial observability== with only egocentric RGB input, mimicking human perception.
- Implemented a unified, high-level, text-based action interface, allowing MLLMs to express decisions directly in language across diverse environments.
- Integrated eight heterogeneous simulation backends (e.g., AI2-THOR, CARLA, Block3D) into a simulator-agnostic protocol for consistent evaluation of interactive spatial reasoning.

## Results

- State-of-the-art MLLMs achieved an average Task Success Rate (TSR) of only **17.4%** (GPT-5) across the benchmark, and **14.4%** for physical tasks, showing limited reliability.
- Models with higher TSR did not consistently exhibit higher Step Efficiency (SE), suggesting successful agents often achieve goals through extensive exploration rather than efficient planning.
- Performance leadership varied by domain: GPT-5 excelled in household/travel, Qwen-3.5-**397B**-A17B in physical entertainment, and Gemini-3.1-Pro in digital games.
- Hybrid navigation-interaction tasks proved most challenging with a mean TSR of **4.2%**, significantly lower than pure interaction tasks (**50.2%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs demonstrate limited interactive spatial reasoning, achieving low task success rates and often relying on inefficient trial-and-error strategies.
> - The performance of MLLMs is highly domain-specific, with no single model universally superior across household, travel, digital games, or other environments.
> - Critical vulnerabilities exist in handling multi-step reasoning, active exploration, long-horizon planning, and robust action execution, especially in hybrid navigation-interaction tasks.
