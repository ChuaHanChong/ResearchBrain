# DragAnything: Motion Control for Anything using Entity Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Kuaishou Technology, Zhejiang University, and NUS Show Lab developed DragAnything, a framework for precise, user-friendly motion control of any entity in generated videos using a novel entity representation. It achieved an FID of 33.5 and an FVD of 494.8, outperforming previous trajectory-based methods like DragNUWA, and was preferred by human evaluators for both motion control and video quality.

## Problem

- Existing controllable video generation methods often rely on labor-intensive dense guidance signals or imprecise pixel-level trajectory control.
- Trajectory-based approaches frequently misinterpret user intent, leading to camera movement instead of desired object motion.
- Manipulating objects via single pixel points can cause undesirable object deformation rather than cohesive entity movement.

## Method

- Introduces a novel Entity Semantic Representation Extraction (**ESRE**) method, deriving compact entity embeddings from ==latent diffusion== features of the initial frame and a user-provided mask.
- Utilizes a complementary 2D Gaussian representation to ensure coherent, non-deforming object motion by emphasizing the entity's central region.
- Employs a dedicated 3D ==U-Net== encoder to process and inject these combined entity and Gaussian representations as conditional guidance into a Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) base model.

## Results

- Achieved improved video quality (FID 33.5 vs 39.8 for DragNUWA) and temporal coherence (FVD 494.8 vs 519.3) on the VIPSeg validation set.
- Demonstrated superior object motion control (ObjMC 305.7 vs 324.6) compared to DragNUWA.
- Human evaluators preferred DragAnything for motion control (63% vs 37%) and overall video quality (56% vs 44%), and ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of both entity and Gaussian representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Previous trajectory-based methods for video motion control often misinterpret user intent because single pixel points do not adequately represent an entire semantic entity.
> - Directly dragging pixels disproportionately influences nearby areas, causing object deformation rather than unified, coherent movement.
> - Leveraging deep latent diffusion features enables the extraction of robust semantic representations for diverse entities, facilitating more precise and intuitive control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "DragAnything" operates within the rapidly advancing field of generative AI, specifically focusing on **controllable video generation**. This area has witnessed significant breakthroughs recently, with models like OpenAI's SORA, RunwayML's Gen-2, PikaLab, Stability AI's Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), and Google's Imagen Video demonstrating remarkable capabilities in generating high-quality, realistic videos from text prompts.

However, as the authors rightly point out, while general video generation has seen rapid progress, **controllable video generation** has lagged. The challenge lies not just in creating visually plausible videos but in precisely manipulating their content and, crucially, their temporal dynamics and motion. This contrasts with controllable image generation (e.g., ControlNet), where spatial manipulation is often more straightforward.

Within controllable video generation, several paradigms exist:
- **Dense Guidance Signals:** Many methods rely on detailed input like masks, human poses, or depth maps (e.g., AnimateDiff, Control-A-Video, Follow Your Pose). While effective, these signals are often labor-intensive and not user-friendly to obtain.
- **Trajectory-based Motion Control:** This approach, which DragAnything falls into, is favored for its user-friendliness. Users merely draw a line (trajectory) to guide motion. Early works in this category used optical flow or recurrent neural networks. More recently, prominent methods like **DragNUWA** and **MotionCtrl** have advanced this paradigm. DragNUWA encodes sparse strokes into a dense flow space, while MotionCtrl converts trajectory coordinates into a vector map, both serving as guidance signals for diffusion models.

DragAnything directly addresses a critical oversight in these existing trajectory-based methods. The core innovation stems from the authors' observation that previous approaches, by merely dragging pixels or pixel regions, fail to accurately represent and control a *semantic entity*. This often leads to unintended consequences, such as:
1. **Misinterpretation of intent:** A single pixel point may not convey the user's desire to move an entire object, sometimes causing the camera to move instead (Insight 1).
2. **Object deformation:** Pixels closer to the drag point receive disproportionately greater influence, distorting the object rather than moving it coherently as a whole (Insight 2).

The paper positions itself as a significant advancement by moving beyond "pixel-level motion" to achieve "entity-level motion control." It builds upon the success of diffusion models (using SVD as its foundation) and draws inspiration from works that leverage latent features for object representation and customization (e.g., Anydoor, VideoSwap, DIFT). By introducing a novel "entity representation" derived from the diffusion model's latent features, DragAnything aims to provide a more intuitive, precise, and semantically aware control mechanism, thereby pushing the boundaries of user-friendly and effective controllable video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of DragAnything is to develop a highly intuitive and precise motion control mechanism for any object within the domain of controllable video generation. This is driven by a desire to overcome the limitations of existing trajectory-based methods, which, despite their user-friendliness, lack true semantic understanding of the objects being manipulated.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhance User-Friendliness through Intuitive Interaction:** The primary motivation is to simplify the interaction process for users. The authors emphasize that drawing a simple trajectory line is significantly less labor-intensive and more user-friendly than generating complex guidance signals like masks or depth maps for each frame. DragAnything aims to maintain this ease of interaction while dramatically improving control precision.

2. **Achieve True Entity-Level Motion Control:** This is the central technical objective. The authors critically re-evaluate previous trajectory-based methods (like DragNUWA and MotionCtrl) and identify a fundamental flaw: a single pixel or a small pixel region cannot adequately represent an entire object. This "point representation paradigm" leads to:
 - **Insight 1: Inadequate Object Representation:** Trajectory points on an object often fail to represent the entire entity, leading to scenarios where the model misinterprets the user's intent, such as moving the camera instead of the desired object (e.g., a cloud).
 - **Insight 2: Undesirable Object Deformation:** Pixels closer to the drag point are disproportionately influenced, causing objects to deform and distort rather than moving cohesively as a single entity.
 DragAnything aims to solve these issues by enabling the model to "perceive" the entire entity the user wishes to control.

3. **Enable Open-Domain Control for Any Object:** A significant objective is to allow motion control for diverse entities, including:
 - **Foreground Objects:** Standard objects that are typically the focus of control.
 - **Background Elements:** Challenging elements like skies, clouds, or entire landscapes, which are often irregular in shape and hard to define with simple bounding boxes.
 - The proposed entity representation is designed to be "open-domain," meaning it can represent and control *anything* visible in the video frame.

4. **Facilitate Simultaneous and Distinct Multi-Object Motion Control:** The system is designed to allow users to control the motion of multiple objects independently and simultaneously within the same video, adding another layer of flexibility and creative possibility.

By achieving these objectives, DragAnything seeks to bridge the gap between user-friendly interaction and precise, semantically aware motion control, thereby making controllable video generation more powerful and accessible for various applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DragAnything builds upon the foundation of latent video diffusion models, primarily Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), and introduces novel mechanisms for entity representation and conditional control. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Base Architecture:** The core generative model is SVD, which consists of a denoising diffusion model (a 3D U-Net) for learning the denoising process in latent space, an encoder, and a decoder for converting between pixel and latent spaces. This provides a robust and efficient framework for high-quality video generation.

2. **Conditional Control Inspired by ControlNet:** Similar to ControlNet for image generation, DragAnything adopts a separate 3D U-Net as an encoder specifically for processing guidance signals. The latent features generated by this control encoder are then injected into the decoder blocks of the main denoising 3D U-Net of SVD, allowing the guidance to steer the video generation process.

3. **Novel Entity Semantic Representation Extraction (ESRE):** This is the core innovation of DragAnything.
 - **Latent Feature Extraction:** Given the first frame image ($I$) and an initial entity mask ($M$) (which can be interactively provided by tools like SAM), the image is first passed through a diffusion inversion process to obtain its latent noise ($x_t$). A single forward pass through the denoising U-Net ($\epsilon_\theta$) then extracts deep latent diffusion features ($F \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$) that semantically characterize the image content. The choice of a single forward pass and one time step is highlighted for its efficiency and performance.
 - **Entity Embeddings:** Based on the coordinate indices provided by the entity mask ($M$), the corresponding semantic features are extracted from $F$. These features are then aggregated, typically using average pooling, to obtain a compact entity embedding ($e_k$) for each identified entity ($k$). These embeddings capture the semantic essence of each object.
 - **Spatio-Temporal Representation:** To associate these entity embeddings with motion, a zero matrix ($E \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$) is initialized. The entity embeddings ($e_k$) are then inserted into this matrix at the spatial locations corresponding to the entity's trajectory points for each frame. This creates a trajectory sequence of entity representations ($\hat{E}_i$) across the video length ($L$).

4. **Complementary 2D Gaussian Representation:** To further refine motion control and prevent deformation, a 2D Gaussian representation is employed.
 - **Motivation:** This representation ensures that pixels closer to the entity's center receive greater influence, thereby promoting coherent object movement rather than pixel-level distortion. This addresses "Insight 2" from the motivation.
 - **Generation:** From the calculated center coordinates ($x_i, y_i$) and radius ($r_k$) of each entity's incircle (derived from the initial mask), a sequence of 2D Gaussian distribution maps ($G_i$) is generated for each frame, emphasizing the central region of the moving object.

5. **Encoder for Guidance Signals:** A dedicated encoder ($E$) is designed to process both the entity representation ($\hat{E}_i$) and the 2D Gaussian map ($G_i$). This encoder comprises four convolutional blocks, each downsampling the input feature resolution by a factor of 2, resulting in a final output resolution of 1/8 of the original. The encoded latent features from both representations are combined:
 $R_i = E(\hat{E}_i) + E(G_i) + Z_i$
 where $Z_i$ is the latent noise of the $i$-th frame. This combined feature $R_i$ then serves as the conditioning signal, which is input into the encoder of the SVD's denoising 3D U-Net, effectively guiding the generation process at different resolutions.

6. **Training Data Generation:** To train the model, a specific procedure is used to generate ground truth labels from video segmentation datasets like VIPSeg:
 - For each entity's mask in the first frame, its incircle (center coordinates $(x,y)$ and radius $r$) is calculated.
 - **Co-Tracker** is employed to track these center points across the video sequence, generating accurate motion trajectories for each entity.
 - These trajectories, along with the entity embeddings and Gaussian distributions, form the comprehensive guidance signals used for training.

7. **Loss Function:** The model is optimized using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss. A crucial aspect is the incorporation of a **mask ($M$)** in the loss calculation:
 $L_\theta = \sum_{i=1}^L M (\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_{t,i}, E_\theta(\hat{E}_i), E_\theta(G_i)))^2_2$
 This mask ($M$) is applied to the entities that are intended for motion control. This ensures that the loss backpropagates primarily through the selected areas, optimizing their motion while preserving the quality and stability of other objects or the background.

8. **Inference with User Interaction:** During inference, DragAnything is highly interactive. Users:
 - Use a segmentation tool like SAM to select the region they wish to control in the first frame.
 - Draw a desired trajectory line for any point within that selected region.
 DragAnything then leverages its entity representation to generate a video where the entire selected entity follows the specified trajectory precisely.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DragAnything demonstrates significant advancements in controllable video generation, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple quantitative metrics and superior qualitative results, particularly in precise object motion control.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

The evaluation was conducted on the VIPSeg validation set, comparing DragAnything against DragNUWA, a leading trajectory-based method built on the same SVD base architecture.

- **Video Quality (FID - Frechet Inception Distance):** DragAnything achieved an FID of **33.5**, significantly outperforming DragNUWA's 39.8. A lower FID indicates higher perceptual quality and realism of the generated videos.
- **Temporal Coherence (FVD - Frechet Video Distance):** DragAnything recorded an FVD of **494.8**, a notable improvement over DragNUWA's 519.3. Lower FVD signifies better temporal consistency and fluidity in the generated video sequences.
- **Object Motion Control (ObjMC):** This metric measures the Euclidean distance between predicted and ground truth object trajectories, with lower values indicating more accurate motion control. DragAnything achieved **305.7**, surpassing DragNUWA's 324.6, confirming its superior precision in controlling object movement.

#### 4.2. User Study (Human Voting)

To assess user perception, a human evaluation involving 30 cases (input images with trajectories and masks) and three professional evaluators was conducted, focusing on video quality and motion matching.

- **Motion Control:** DragAnything received 63% of human votes for superior motion control, indicating a **26% improvement** over DragNUWA (which received 37%). This is a strong validation of the paper's core hypothesis regarding entity-level control.
- **Video Quality:** For overall video quality, DragAnything was preferred by 56% of users, demonstrating a **12% improvement** over DragNUWA (44%). This suggests that the improved motion control does not come at the cost of visual fidelity but rather enhances it.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The authors conducted crucial ablation studies to validate the contribution of their core proposed components:

- **Effect of Entity Representation ($\hat{E}$):** Removing the entity representation led to a significant increase in ObjMC (from 305.7 to 410.7), highlighting its critical role in accurate object motion control.
- **Effect of 2D Gaussian Representation:** Similarly, excluding the 2D Gaussian representation also degraded ObjMC performance (from 305.7 to 339.3). This confirms that the Gaussian representation helps focus control on the object's center, preventing undesirable deformation.
- **Combined Effect:** The best performance (ObjMC 305.7) was achieved when both Entity Representation and 2D Gaussian Representation were utilized, demonstrating a synergistic effect between the two.
- **Effect of Loss Mask (M):** Employing the loss mask during training yielded improvements across metrics, particularly for ObjMC (from 311.1 to 305.7). This confirms that constraining the loss to the controlled entity regions effectively helps optimize motion without negatively impacting other parts of the video.

#### 4.4. Diverse Motion Control Capabilities (Qualitative Results)

Qualitative examples and visualizations (Figures 6, 7, 9) vividly demonstrate DragAnything's flexibility and precision:

- **Foreground Motion Control:** The model accurately controls the movement of distinct foreground objects like a sun or a horse, avoiding the distortions seen in previous methods.
- **Background Motion Control:** Crucially, DragAnything effectively controls amorphous background elements such as cloud layers, demonstrating the "open-domain" nature of its entity representation.
- **Simultaneous Multi-Object Control:** It can handle complex scenarios involving simultaneous and independent motion control of multiple entities, including both foreground and background objects (e.g., a cloud, a sun, and a horse moving distinctly).
- **Camera Motion Control:** The system also supports basic camera movements like zooming in/out or panning, by selecting the entire image and manipulating specific points.

In summary, DragAnything successfully addresses the limitations of pixel-level motion control, establishing a new paradigm for entity-level control that is both significantly more precise and user-friendly, as validated by both objective metrics and human perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DragAnything represents a significant step forward in the field of controllable video generation, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Semantic Precision in Video Manipulation:** The most critical contribution is its shift from rudimentary pixel-level manipulation to **true entity-level motion control**. By semantically understanding and representing objects, DragAnything allows for intuitive and precise control that was previously elusive. This fundamentally enhances the quality and reliability of video editing and generation tasks, making the output more consistent with user intent and visually plausible.

2. **Enhanced User Experience and Accessibility:** The method champions a user-friendly interaction paradigm. By requiring only a simple trajectory draw after an initial selection (via SAM), it significantly lowers the barrier to entry for complex video motion editing. This makes sophisticated video manipulation accessible to a wider audience, including artists, content creators, and casual users, without requiring specialized technical skills or labor-intensive mask annotations for every frame.

3. **Open-Domain and Multi-Object Control:** The ability to control *any* entity, including challenging background elements like skies and clouds, and to simultaneously control multiple distinct objects, vastly expands the scope of applications. This opens up new creative possibilities for dynamic scene composition, special effects, and realistic environmental animation in videos.

4. **Foundation for Future Generative AI:** DragAnything's success in robust entity representation using latent diffusion features provides a valuable blueprint for future generative AI models. It emphasizes that semantic understanding at a deeper, feature-level is key to achieving fine-grained, controllable generation, moving beyond merely mimicking visual patterns. This could inspire similar approaches in other generative tasks beyond motion control.

5. **Applications Across Industries:** The potential impact spans various industries:
 - **Media and Entertainment:** Film production, animation, and video game development can leverage DragAnything for rapid prototyping of motion, creating dynamic scenes, and precise object choreography.
 - **Advertising and Marketing:** Generating customized video content with specific brand elements or product movements can become more efficient and impactful.
 - **Education and Training:** Creating interactive educational videos where objects move along prescribed paths to illustrate concepts.
 - **Creative Content Creation:** Empowering independent artists and content creators to produce professional-quality animated content with ease.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly acknowledges limitations, such as the current restriction to 2D motion and the foundational model's capacity for very large motions. These limitations implicitly lay out compelling directions for future research:
 - **3D Motion Control:** Integrating depth information to enable control of 3D rotations and movements, adding another dimension of realism and control.
 - **Robustness to Large Motions:** Driving the development of even more powerful foundational video diffusion models (e.g., inspired by advancements like OpenAI's SORA) that can handle highly dynamic and extensive movements without quality degradation.
 - **Complex Interactions:** Further research into more intricate interactions between controlled entities and the environment.

7. **Ethical Considerations:** The authors responsibly address potential negative impacts, including the reinforcement of biases from training data, the misuse of generated content for misleading visuals (deepfakes), and privacy concerns. This highlights the importance of ethical guidelines and responsible implementation in the deployment of such powerful generative technologies.

In conclusion, DragAnything significantly advances the state-of-the-art in controllable video generation by providing a user-friendly, semantically precise, and flexible solution for object motion control. Its innovations pave the way for more intuitive and powerful AI-assisted video creation tools, while also setting important precedents for future research in general generative AI and its responsible deployment.
