# Overcoming Statistical Bias in Action-Controllable World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The research from Xi an Jiaotong University and China Mobile developed the Counterfactual Consistency (CoCo) framework, which enhances action controllability in world models by enforcing temporal and spatial consistency under counterfactual action and scene changes. This approach reduced action-independent motion by up to 90% and improved visual planning success rates by 3.0 points, leading to more reliable predictive simulations for visual planning and model-based reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Action-conditioned world models often exploit statistical biases like visual inertia, producing visually plausible but not causally action-driven futures.
- Models fail to establish meaningful causal dependencies, leading to similar predictions for different actions or motion persisting under zero action, hindering effective planning.
- Prior work relies on observational supervision, which cannot distinguish shortcut predictions from genuine action-driven transitions because training data lacks counterfactual outcomes.

## Method

- Introduced the **Counterfactual Consistency (CoCo)** framework, which enforces action-dependent dynamics through two types of constraints on the world model.
- ==Multi-Step Counterfactual Consistency (MSC2)== ensures temporal consistency via rollouts with original, inverse, and zero actions, imposing cycle and stability losses.
- ==Action-Spatial Counterfactual Consistency (ASC2)== maintains consistent predictions under spatial transformations (e.g., mirroring) of both the scene and corresponding actions.

## Results

- CoCo reduced Drift Energy (action-independent motion) by **17.07%** on Mini-SSMB and by up to **90%** on continuous action video prediction benchmarks (BAIR, RoboNet) compared to baselines.
- The framework achieved the highest average success rate in visual planning tasks on the VP^2 benchmark (**73.1%**), outperforming previous methods by **3.0 points**.
- CoCo-trained world models improved MBPO's aggregate success rate and learning speed in MetaWorld RL tasks, while maintaining or improving visual prediction quality (e.g., FVD, LPIPS, PSNR) across datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Overcoming statistical bias in world models requires explicit counterfactual supervision, moving beyond observational learning to enforce consistency under interventions on actions or scenes.
> - Combining temporal constraints (MSC2) like action reversibility and zero-action stability with spatial equivariance constraints (ASC2) enhances pixel-level action controllability.
> - New metrics (ARC and DE) are essential for directly quantifying action-response fidelity and action-independent motion, addressing limitations of traditional visual quality metrics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Action-conditioned world models aim to develop learned predictive simulators that enable intelligent agents to forecast future visual environments based on their actions. These models are fundamental for tasks such as visual planning and model-based reinforcement learning, where agents need to evaluate the consequences of alternative actions. Recent advancements in autoregressive token models, diffusion models, and large-scale interactive simulators have improved the fidelity and scale of visual world modeling.

However, a prevalent issue in data-driven learning, particularly for world models, is the exploitation of statistical biases. Future frames are often highly predictable from visual inertia and recurring motion patterns alone, rather than being causally driven by an agent's actions. When optimized solely to minimize empirical prediction error, models tend to learn the easiest-to-predict correlations in the training data. This can lead to a situation where the model generates visually plausible videos but fails to function as an effective interactive simulator: different actions may produce similar futures, or motion may persist even under zero action. Consequently, planners relying on such models cannot reliably compare the outcomes of distinct actions.

Previous efforts to address this problem have focused on strengthening how actions are represented and injected into world models (e.g., via concatenated tokens, latent controls, cross-attention, feature modulation, or motion prompts) or improving representations through disentanglement and causal modeling. While these methods can increase apparent action responsiveness and improve prediction accuracy on observed trajectories, they often do not fully remove statistical bias at its source. This limitation arises because their supervision remains observational; training data typically contains only the future produced by the *executed* action, not the futures that would have resulted from *alternative* or *zero* actions. Standard factual losses struggle to distinguish shortcut predictions from genuinely action-driven transitions in such scenarios.

This work contributes to the landscape by identifying the absence of explicit counterfactual supervision as a key limitation in prior work. It proposes that overcoming statistical bias requires enforcing consistency under *counterfactual changes* to actions and observations, moving beyond purely observational learning towards a more causal understanding of action effects. The paper positions itself within the growing body of research on statistical bias in world models and counterfactual consistency, aiming to provide a structural principle for enhancing action controllability at the pixel level.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a recognized limitation in current action-conditioned world models: their susceptibility to statistical biases. Despite generating visually realistic future frames, these models frequently fail to establish a meaningful causal dependency between the agent's actions and the predicted environmental evolution.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Address Statistical Bias in Action-Conditioned Prediction:** The primary objective is to overcome the phenomenon where world models exploit "statistical shortcuts" – such as visual inertia and recurring motion patterns – to make predictions. This allows models to achieve high factual prediction accuracy without genuinely encoding how actions influence outcomes.
- **Enhance Action Controllability:** The core aim is to ensure that the dynamics predicted by a world model are meaningfully controlled by the agent's actions. This implies that different actions should lead to distinct, predictable futures, and that specifying a zero action should result in a stable, non-drifting state. The paper argues that effective action control necessitates more than simply incorporating action features; it requires *enforcing consistency under counterfactual changes* to actions and observations.
- **Develop a Counterfactual Consistency Framework (CoCo):** Based on the insight that action-driven models should produce predictably transformed futures in response to interventions on actions or scenes, the authors aim to introduce a framework that integrates this principle. This framework is designed to enforce consistency through two complementary types of constraints: temporal and spatial.
- **Introduce Novel Evaluation Metrics:** Current world model metrics predominantly assess visual quality (e.g., FVD, LPIPS) but do not directly quantify action controllability. A crucial objective is to develop new metrics that can specifically assess whether generated motion follows the action (Action Response Consistency - ARC) and whether the model remains stable under zero actions (Drift Energy - DE).
- **Construct a Dedicated Evaluation Dataset:** To systematically evaluate discrete action control under counterfactual conditions, the authors aimed to construct a specialized dataset (Mini-SSMB) that allows for evaluating multiple actions from the same initial state.
- **Validate Framework Across Diverse Tasks:** The research seeks to validate the proposed framework's effectiveness across various tasks, including video prediction, visual planning, and model-based reinforcement learning, demonstrating its ability to improve action controllability while maintaining or enhancing visual fidelity and downstream performance.

In essence, the motivation is to transform world models from passive observational predictors into active, controllable simulators that accurately reflect the causal impact of an agent's interventions, thereby increasing their utility for intelligent decision-making systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **CoCo (Counterfactual Consistency framework)**, built on the principle that an action-driven world model should produce a consistently transformed future when an intervention alters the action, the scene, or both. This framework enhances action controllability through two main complementary constraints: Multi-Step Counterfactual Consistency (MSC2) and Action-Spatial Counterfactual Consistency (ASC2).

**Problem Definition and Model Backbone:**
The core of the system is an action-conditioned world model, represented by a conditional generative mapping $f_{\theta}$, which autoregressively predicts future frames $\hat{S}_{t+1:t+H}$ from historical context $S_{\le t}$ and future action sequence $A_{t:t+H-1}$. For models operating on discrete video tokens, frames are first encoded into latent variables $Z_{1:T} = E(S_{1:T})$, predicted by an autoregressive Transformer $p_{\theta}(Z_{t+1:t+H} | Z_{\le t}, A_{t:t+H-1})$, and then detokenized back to pixel space $\hat{S}_{t+1:t+H} = D(\hat{Z}_{t+1:t+H})$. The paper states that existing models typically optimize for maximum likelihood or reconstruction, which primarily encourages plausible futures under observational data, not necessarily genuinely action-controlled outcomes. The objective of CoCo is to address this by moving towards an interventional distribution $p_{\theta}(Z_{t+1:t+H} | Z_{\le t}, \text{do}(A_{t:t+H-1}))$.

**Multi-Step Counterfactual Consistency (MSC2):**
MSC2 aims to enforce temporal consistency by constraining the model to learn causal action-state relationships through multi-step rollouts involving original, inverse, and zero actions. Given a current state $S_t$ and an action sequence $A_{t:t+K-1}$, MSC2 evaluates the model under three counterfactual rollout branches:

1. **Original-action (Reference) Branch:** Predicts future frames $\hat{S}_{\text{ref}}^{t+1:t+K} = f_{\theta}(S_t, A_{t:t+K-1})$. This branch is constrained by a multi-step prediction loss $L_{\text{ref}} = \sum_{k=1}^K d(\hat{S}_{\text{ref}}^{t+k}, S_{t+k})$, which aligns predicted motion with real dynamics. The forward reference rollout is generated greedily with a stop-gradient.
2. **Inverse-action (Rollback) Branch:** Starting from the predicted endpoint of the reference rollout $\hat{S}_{\text{ref}}^{t+K}$, the model performs a backward counterfactual rollout $\tilde{S}_t = f_{\theta}(\hat{S}_{\text{ref}}^{t+K}, I(A_{t:t+K-1}))$, where $I(A_{t:t+K-1})$ is the inverse of the original action sequence. The multi-step cycle constraint $L_{\text{cyc}} = d(\tilde{S}_t, S_t)$ enforces that the inverse rollout should approximately recover the initial state, testing action reversibility. The inverse rollout is optimized with teacher forcing.
3. **Zero-action (Stability) Branch:** This branch predicts future frames $\hat{S}_{\text{zero}}^{t+1:t+K} = f_{\theta}(S_t, 0_{1:K})$, where $0_{1:K}$ represents a sequence of K zero actions. A loss $L_{\text{zero}} = \sum_{k=1}^K d(\hat{S}_{\text{zero}}^{t+k}, S_t)$ penalizes state drift, ensuring the model remains stable when no action is applied.

The overall objective for MSC2 is a weighted sum: $L_{\text{MSC2}} = \lambda_{\text{ref}} L_{\text{ref}} + \lambda_{\text{cyc}} L_{\text{cyc}} + \lambda_{\text{zero}} L_{\text{zero}}$.

**Action-Spatial Counterfactual Consistency (ASC2):**
ASC2 ensures that the model's predictions transform consistently when the visual scene and corresponding actions undergo the same spatial transformation. For this work, the spatial transformation $g$ is horizontal image mirroring. For an action-conditional world model, there should exist a corresponding action-space transformation $g_a$ such that $g (f_{\theta}(S_{\le t}, A_{t:t+K-1})) \approx f_{\theta}(g(S_{\le t}), g_a(A_{t:t+K-1}))$. In MiniGrid, $g_a$ swaps left and right actions.

ASC2 uses two parallel branches with shared parameters:
1. **Original Branch:** Takes $(S_{\le t}, A_{t:t+K-1})$ as input, producing continuous hidden representations $H_{\text{fut}}$.
2. **Mirrored Branch:** Takes $(g(S_{\le t}), g_a(A_{t:t+K-1}))$ as input, producing continuous hidden representations $\tilde{H}_{\text{fut}}$.

The constraint is applied by aligning the continuous hidden representations. After reshaping the hidden states into spatial grids, a flipping operator $F_w$ is applied along the width dimension of the original branch's representations. The mirror-equivariance loss is defined as $L_{\text{mir}} = \ell(F_w(H_{\text{fut}}), \tilde{H}_{\text{fut}})$, using SmoothL1 loss for $\ell$. The final objective for ASC2 is $L_{\text{ASC2}} = L_{\text{CE}} + \lambda_{\text{mir}} L_{\text{mir}}$, where $L_{\text{CE}}$ is the standard cross-entropy loss for autoregressive token prediction in the original space.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
To directly assess action controllability, the authors introduce:
- **Action Response Consistency (ARC):** Measures whether the generated motion aligns with the specified action.
- **Drift Energy (DE):** Quantifies the degree of action-independent motion or instability when a zero action is applied.
Standard visual quality metrics (FVD, LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM) are also used.

**Mini-SSMB Dataset:**
A new dataset, Mini-SSMB (MiniGrid Same-State, Multi-Action Branches), was constructed based on MiniGrid. It contains 30,000 training and 3,000 test states. For each state, it records next-frame observations for multiple distinct actions (left, right, forward), enabling direct counterfactual evaluation.

**Experimental Setup:**
The framework was evaluated on three types of tasks:
- **Video Prediction:** Using BAIR (robotic pushing with 4-D continuous actions) and RoboNet (multi-robot manipulation with 5-D continuous actions) datasets.
- **Visual Planning:** On the VP$^2$ benchmark (RoboDesk and robosuite tasks) using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to optimize action sequences for goal image matching.
- **Model-based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL):** On six MetaWorld operation tasks, augmenting MBPO (Model-Based Policy Optimization) with the CoCo-trained world model and comparing it against model-free (DrQ-v2) and MBPO(iVideoGPT) baselines.

The backbone world model used is iVideoGPT. Experiments were conducted using 50K optimization steps, batch size 16, a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-5}$, cosine scheduling, and 64x64 resolution, on 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate whether the proposed counterfactual constraints enhance action controllability and benefit downstream tasks, without sacrificing visual quality.

**Action Controllability in Mini-SSMB (Discrete Actions):**
- All tested models exhibited basic action-state correspondence (ARC scores above random baseline).
- The baseline model (iVideoGPT) showed significant drift under zero actions (DE of 7.82).
- The MSC2 component alone reduced DE to 6.75 (13.7% reduction).
- The ASC2 component alone further reduced DE to 4.19 (46.4% reduction from baseline).
- The full CoCo model (Ours) achieved an ARC$_{inv}$ of 0.417, ARC$_{ref}$ of 0.483, and reduced DE to 6.49, representing a 17.07% reduction compared to the baseline. These results indicate that CoCo improves control over predicted actions and suppresses action-independent variations.

**Video Prediction (Continuous Actions on BAIR & RoboNet):**
- **Action Controllability:**
 - On BAIR, the full CoCo model obtained ARC$_{inv}$ of 0.100 and ARC$_{ref}$ of 0.119, outperforming iVideoGPT by 35.1% and 25.3% respectively. It reduced DE from 0.176 (iVideoGPT) to 0.018, demonstrating a substantial decrease in static drift.
 - On RoboNet, CoCo reduced DE from 0.945 (iVideoGPT) to 0.091. It achieved the highest ARC$_{inv}$ (0.055) and ARC$_{ref}$ (0.064) among action-conditioned models, suggesting better alignment between generated transitions and conditioned actions.
 - MSC2 was shown to directly improve zero-action stability (lower DE), while ASC2 improved directional action response (higher ARC$_{inv}$).
- **Visual Prediction Quality:**
 - On BAIR, CoCo reduced FVD from 73.46 to 71.27, LPIPS from 5.88 to 4.84, and increased PSNR from 23.33 to 28.35 compared to iVideoGPT.
 - On RoboNet, CoCo achieved the best FVD (43.81) and LPIPS (1.32) among the compared models.
 - The results suggest that CoCo effectively mitigates statistical bias while maintaining competitive, and often improved, visual generation quality.

**Visual Planning (VP$^2$ Benchmark):**
- The full CoCo model achieved an average success rate of 73.1% across various RoboDesk and robosuite tasks, which was the highest among all tested models. This performance surpassed iVideoGPT (70.1%) by 3.0 points and SAMPO (72.2%) by 0.9 points.
- Improvements were observed particularly in tasks requiring stable directional execution and long-horizon control (e.g., robosuite Push, Open Slide, Blue Button, Green Button, Upright Block).
- The study attributes these gains to the cycle constraint reducing rollout drift and enforcing action reversibility, as well as the equivariance constraint aligning action direction with spatial action.

**Model-based Reinforcement Learning (MetaWorld):**
- CoCo improved the aggregate success rate of MBPO (Model-Based Policy Optimization) compared to both the iVideoGPT-based world model and the model-free DrQ-v2 baseline.
- The gains were most notable in learning speed and final success rate on tasks like Button Press Topdown Wall and Handle Pull Side.
- CoCo remained competitive on tasks such as Hammer and Door Lock, indicating that the counterfactual constraints did not compromise performance.
- On Coffee Push, all methods faced challenges, and CoCo did not show a distinct advantage. Task-dependent gains were indicated by overlapping confidence intervals on several tasks.
- These results suggest that counterfactual constraints can benefit model-based policy learning, particularly for tasks necessitating reliable action-conditioned state transitions.

Overall, the findings indicate that explicit counterfactual consistency training enhances the controllability of action-conditioned world models, leading to improved performance on both direct controllability metrics and downstream decision-making tasks, while maintaining or improving visual prediction quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental challenge in the development of action-conditioned world models: their tendency to rely on statistical shortcuts rather than learning the genuine causal effects of actions. By introducing the CoCo framework, the paper makes several contributions with potential impacts on the fields of robotics, reinforcement learning, and broader artificial intelligence.

**1. Enhanced Model Reliability and Controllability:** The primary significance lies in the development of a principled method to enforce action-dependent dynamics. By ensuring that world models are truly sensitive to actions, CoCo can lead to more reliable predictive simulations. This is crucial for applications where agents must accurately anticipate the consequences of their interventions, reducing the risk of unexpected behavior stemming from misinterpretations of causal relationships.

**2. Improved Decision-Making in Downstream Tasks:** The demonstrated improvements in visual planning and model-based reinforcement learning tasks indicate that more controllable world models directly translate to better agent performance. For robotic systems, this could mean more robust model-predictive control, enabling robots to execute complex manipulation tasks more effectively and adaptively. In reinforcement learning, a more accurate and causally aligned world model can significantly improve sample efficiency, allowing agents to learn complex behaviors with less real-world interaction.

**3. Advancement in Causal AI and World Modeling:** The work explicitly tackles the problem of moving beyond observational prediction towards interventional understanding. By formalizing counterfactual consistency through MSC2 and ASC2, the paper contributes to the broader research agenda of building AI systems that learn causal models of the world. This approach aligns with the pursuit of more generalizable and interpretable AI.

**4. Novel Evaluation Methodology:** The introduction of Action Response Consistency (ARC) and Drift Energy (DE) metrics, along with the Mini-SSMB dataset, provides a standardized and direct way to evaluate action controllability. This enables researchers to more precisely diagnose model limitations and measure progress in an area where traditional visual quality metrics are insufficient. These tools could foster further research into the causal properties of learned models.

**5. Generalizability Across Model Settings:** The finding that these gains preserve video prediction quality and transfer across discrete and continuous control settings, as well as different model backbones, suggests the framework is broadly applicable. This indicates that counterfactual consistency could be a valuable regularization technique for various world model architectures.

**Potential Future Directions Highlighted by the Authors:**
The authors acknowledge that constructing multiple counterfactual branches and spatially transformed inputs introduces additional computational cost. Furthermore, defining inverse actions and spatial transformations can be challenging for highly irreversible physical interactions or complex semantic actions. Future research could focus on:
- **Learning Transformations from Data:** Investigating methods to learn valid inverse actions and spatial transformations directly from data rather than relying on predefined rules.
- **Improving Computational Efficiency:** Developing more efficient ways to implement counterfactual training to reduce computational overhead.
These identified future directions underscore the ongoing challenges and opportunities in building genuinely controllable and causally informed world models, where this work marks a significant step.
