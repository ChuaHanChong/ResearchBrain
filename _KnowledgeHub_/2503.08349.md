# LiPS: Large-Scale Humanoid Robot Reinforcement Learning with Parallel-Series Structures

> [!summary] Summary
> LiPS integrates detailed multi-rigid-body dynamics of humanoid robot parallel ankle mechanisms directly into large-scale GPU-accelerated reinforcement learning simulations. This approach enables policies to learn parallel-aware control strategies, significantly reducing the sim-to-real gap and allowing direct, robust deployment on physical humanoid robots such as the Tien Kung.

## Problem

- Current large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) simulators often simplify humanoid robot models, neglecting complex parallel mechanical structures like ankle mechanisms, leading to a significant sim-to-real gap.
- Training policies on these simplified serial models necessitates computationally intensive or imprecise series-to-parallel conversions during real-world deployment, hindering performance and robustness.
- Existing methods for handling parallel structures either introduce high computational load and instability or result in diminished robot performance and robustness due to oversimplification.

## Method

- A detailed mathematical framework for the inverse kinematics, velocity, and acceleration of the humanoid robot's parallel ankle mechanism is derived.
- The **LiPS** method integrates this parallel dynamics modeling directly into the large-scale parallel simulation environment during the RL training loop.
- The policy learns to generate parallel-aware actions, which are internally transformed into serial torques for the simulator, enabling direct deployment to the physical robot without runtime series-to-parallel conversion.

## Results

- The Tien Kung humanoid robot, developed by the Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics, successfully performed robust omnidirectional walking using the LiPS method.
- Qualitative comparisons indicate superior control performance and robustness against joint noise compared to conventional series-to-parallel conversion methods and passive ankle approaches.
- The approach significantly reduces the computational load during real-world deployment, supporting a stable 100 Hz control frequency on an NVIDIA Orin by eliminating complex real-time dynamic conversions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating accurate multi-rigid-body dynamics of parallel mechanisms directly into large-scale RL simulation environments effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap for complex humanoid robots.
> - Policies trained with parallel-aware dynamics achieve superior control performance and robustness on physical robots compared to methods relying on post-training conversion or dynamic simplification.
> - Learning control for parallel structures within a serial-based simulator is feasible and beneficial by strategically incorporating dynamic transformations directly into the training pipeline.
