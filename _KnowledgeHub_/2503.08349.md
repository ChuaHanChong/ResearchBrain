# LiPS: Large-Scale Humanoid Robot Reinforcement Learning with Parallel-Series Structures

> [!summary] Summary
> LiPS integrates detailed multi-rigid-body dynamics of humanoid robot parallel ankle mechanisms directly into large-scale GPU-accelerated reinforcement learning simulations. This approach enables policies to learn parallel-aware control strategies, significantly reducing the sim-to-real gap and allowing direct, robust deployment on physical humanoid robots such as the Tien Kung.

## Problem

- Current large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) simulators often simplify humanoid robot models, neglecting complex parallel mechanical structures like ankle mechanisms, leading to a significant sim-to-real gap.
- Training policies on these simplified serial models necessitates computationally intensive or imprecise series-to-parallel conversions during real-world deployment, hindering performance and robustness.
- Existing methods for handling parallel structures either introduce high computational load and instability or result in diminished robot performance and robustness due to oversimplification.

## Method

- A detailed mathematical framework for the inverse kinematics, velocity, and acceleration of the humanoid robot's parallel ankle mechanism is derived.
- The **LiPS** method integrates this parallel dynamics modeling directly into the large-scale parallel simulation environment during the RL training loop.
- The policy learns to generate parallel-aware actions, which are internally transformed into serial torques for the simulator, enabling direct deployment to the physical robot without runtime series-to-parallel conversion.

## Results

- The Tien Kung humanoid robot, developed by the Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics, successfully performed robust omnidirectional walking using the LiPS method.
- Qualitative comparisons indicate superior control performance and robustness against joint noise compared to conventional series-to-parallel conversion methods and passive ankle approaches.
- The approach significantly reduces the computational load during real-world deployment, supporting a stable 100 Hz control frequency on an NVIDIA Orin by eliminating complex real-time dynamic conversions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating accurate multi-rigid-body dynamics of parallel mechanisms directly into large-scale RL simulation environments effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap for complex humanoid robots.
> - Policies trained with parallel-aware dynamics achieve superior control performance and robustness on physical robots compared to methods relying on post-training conversion or dynamic simplification.
> - Learning control for parallel structures within a serial-based simulator is feasible and beneficial by strategically incorporating dynamic transformations directly into the training pipeline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has witnessed rapid advancements, particularly with the integration of deep learning and reinforcement learning (RL). Humanoid robots are considered the "ultimate robotic solution" due to their anthropomorphic form, which allows them to adapt to human environments and leverage human-centric data for learning. However, their inherent complexity—featuring multi-rigid-body structures, floating bases, and intricate series-parallel mechanisms—presents formidable control challenges.

Traditional model-based control methods for humanoids often demand highly precise models and extensive recalibration for different tasks, making them cumbersome. This has propelled researchers towards deep reinforcement learning, which has shown significant promise in handling complex, non-linear, and time-varying control problems.

A critical bottleneck in RL-based robotics is the **sim-to-real gap**, the discrepancy between simulated training environments and real-world physics. While simulators are crucial for rapid, safe, and scalable training, they come with trade-offs between speed and accuracy. GPU-based physics engines like NVIDIA's Isaac Gym have revolutionized large-scale parallel training by accelerating simulations, but they often rely on simplified robot descriptions, such as URDF (Unified Robot Description Format). URDF, while easy to use and widely adopted, primarily supports open-loop kinematic chains and often neglects the dynamic characteristics of closed-loop parallel structures.

This limitation means that many existing RL approaches for humanoids train policies on a simplified serial representation of the robot in simulation. The complex series-to-parallel conversion, necessary for robots with mechanisms like parallel ankles (which enhance stiffness and load-bearing), is often deferred until the "sim2real phase" – i.e., during deployment on the physical robot. This post-training conversion introduces significant challenges:
- **Increased Sim2Real Gap:** The policy learns on a model that fundamentally differs from the physical robot, making transfer difficult.
- **Computational Burden:** Real-time analytical solutions for series-to-parallel conversion on the robot side are computationally intensive and can be unstable or yield no solutions.
- **Reduced Performance:** Approximations or imprecise encoder data can lead to jitter and sub-optimal utilization of the parallel mechanism's design advantages.

LiPS directly addresses this critical issue. Instead of bypassing the parallel dynamics during training, it proposes a method to integrate sophisticated multi-rigid-body dynamics modeling, specifically for parallel structures, directly into the large-scale parallel simulation environment. This work fits into the landscape by enhancing the fidelity of simulation for complex robot architectures, thereby reducing the sim2real gap and facilitating more robust and efficient learning for actual humanoid robots. It leverages the speed of GPU-based simulators while overcoming their limitations in handling complex kinematic and dynamic structures, positioning itself as a vital advancement for enabling more capable and reliable humanoid robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the LiPS research is to overcome the limitations of current large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) training environments for humanoid robots, particularly concerning the accurate modeling of complex series-parallel mechanical structures.

The key motivations driving this research are:
1. **Addressing Physics Engine Limitations:** Current GPU-based physics engines, crucial for large-scale parallel RL training (e.g., Isaac Gym with URDF), often have limited capabilities in simulating multi-rigid-body closed-loop topologies. This forces existing RL algorithms to train on simplified open-loop robot models, especially for components like parallel ankle mechanisms common in modern humanoids (e.g., Wukong, Tesla Optimus, Fourier GR-1, Tien Kung).
2. **Reducing the Sim2Real Gap:** The disparity between the simplified simulation model and the intricate mechanical structure of physical humanoid robots creates a significant "sim2real gap." When policies are trained on open-loop models and then require conversion to series-parallel structures during real-world deployment, this gap leads to performance degradation, increased deployment difficulty, and less robust control.
3. **Improving Training and Deployment Efficiency:** The complex structure and dynamics of humanoid robots mean RL training is inherently slow. High parallel sampling efficiency is crucial. By enabling the RL policy to learn directly with parallel-aware dynamics in simulation, the authors aim to improve both the efficiency of training and the robustness/speed of inference on the physical robot, eliminating the need for computationally expensive real-time series-to-parallel conversions during deployment.
4. **Enhancing Robot Performance and Robustness:** Incorporating accurate dynamics modeling during training allows the policy to learn control strategies that fully leverage the advantages of parallel structures (e.g., stiffness, load-bearing capacity). This leads to more robust, precise, and high-performing locomotion and manipulation in real-world applications, capable of handling complex tasks and environments.

In essence, the LiPS method aims to provide a novel training framework that seamlessly integrates complex multi-rigid-body dynamics, particularly for parallel mechanisms, directly into large-scale GPU-accelerated RL simulations. This integration is designed to yield policies that are immediately applicable to physical humanoid robots, bridging the sim2real gap and unlocking the full potential of these advanced robotic platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the LiPS methodology lies in its novel approach to integrating complex multi-rigid-body dynamics modeling, specifically for parallel mechanisms, directly into the reinforcement learning (RL) training pipeline, even when using simulators that predominantly support simpler, open-loop topologies (like URDF in Isaac Gym).

**A. Dynamics Modeling of Humanoid Robots with Multi-Rigid-Body and Floating Base:**
The paper begins by meticulously modeling the kinematics and dynamics of a humanoid robot, focusing on its key characteristics: a multi-rigid-body structure, a floating base, and, crucially, parallel ankle mechanisms. Modern humanoid robots widely adopt parallel structures in their ankles to enhance stiffness and load-bearing capacity, facilitating dynamic balance and adaptation to uneven terrain.

The authors detail the local kinematics of a closed-loop linkage parallel ankle. Using a coordinate system fixed to the lower leg, they derive:
- **Inverse Position Kinematics:** Equations (1-7) determine the required active joint angles ($q_1, q_2$) for a given footplate posture ($\chi = [\phi, \theta]^T$), accounting for the lengths of active ($L_1$) and passive ($L_2$) arms and hinge points. Constraints on rotation range and rod length are applied to select the correct solution from multiple possibilities.
- **Inverse Velocity Kinematics:** Equations (9-12) derive the angular velocities of the main joints ($\dot{q} = [\dot{q}_1, \dot{q}_2]^T$) as a function of the footplate's angular velocities ($\dot{\chi}$). This involves calculating the inverse Jacobian matrix ($J$) for the parallel mechanism, which maps end-effector velocities to joint velocities.
- **Inverse Acceleration Kinematics:** Equations (13-20) extend the analysis to accelerations, determining the angular accelerations of the main joints ($\ddot{q}$) based on the footplate's angular accelerations ($\ddot{\chi}$) and velocities ($\dot{\chi}$). This involves calculating the time derivative of the Jacobian matrix ($\dot{J}$).

This detailed dynamic modeling provides the essential mathematical framework for precisely understanding and predicting the behavior of the parallel ankle mechanism.

**B. Formulation in Large-Scale Parallel Reinforcement Learning Environment (LiPS Method):**
The LiPS method fundamentally alters how humanoid robots with parallel structures are treated during RL training and deployment, contrasting it with the "currently widely used method."

- **Currently Widely Used Method:** This approach simplifies the robot as a serial structure during RL training in simulation. The complex series-to-parallel conversion is postponed until the "sim2real phase" when deploying the trained policy to the physical robot. This conversion typically involves methods like torque equivalence (computationally intensive, prone to real-time issues) or position equivalence (suffers from encoder precision issues, leading to jitter). This creates a significant sim2real gap and limits the robot's performance.

- **LiPS Method (Our Proposed Method):** As illustrated in Figure 4 and Algorithm IV.1, LiPS integrates the detailed parallel ankle dynamics modeling directly into the *training loop* within the large-scale parallel simulation environment (e.g., Isaac Gym).
 1. **Parallel-Aware Policy Output:** The reinforcement learning policy, trained on observations from the *serial representation* of the robot in simulation (`s_t(Series)`), learns to output actions (`a_t`) that are directly interpreted as desired control signals for the *parallel ankle mechanism* (`a_t(Parallel)`).
 2. **Internal Dynamics Computation:** Within each simulation step during training, LiPS utilizes the derived parallel dynamics (Jacobian `J`) to perform a critical transformation. It converts these parallel-aware desired actions (e.g., desired torques `τ_p_d` from a PD controller acting on `a_t(Parallel)`) into equivalent serial torques (`τ_s_d`) that the underlying serial-based simulator can process (`τ_s_d ← J_transpose τ_p_d`).
 3. **Simulation with Transformed Commands:** These computed serial torques (`τ_Series`) are then applied to the simulation environment. This means the policy effectively learns to control the robot *as if it were parallel*, even though the low-level simulator interface is serial.
 4. **Direct Real-World Deployment:** Crucially, once the policy is trained, it can be *directly applied* to the physical humanoid robot, which possesses the actual parallel mechanisms. Since the policy has learned to generate parallel-aware actions, no further complex series-to-parallel conversion is required during real-world deployment, significantly reducing the computational load and errors at the robot's end.

By effectively creating a "virtual parallel robot" within a serial simulator for training, LiPS significantly reduces the sim2real gap and the challenges associated with deploying policies on complex hardware. This approach allows for more accurate simulation of dynamic behaviors, leading to more robust and efficient control strategies in practice.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted experiments to validate the LiPS method, focusing on its ability to enable robust humanoid robot locomotion with complex series-parallel structures.

**A. Training and Implementation Details:**
- **Training Environment:** The training was performed on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU, utilizing 4096 parallel environments within NVIDIA IsaacGym. This massively parallel setup is key to the efficiency of modern deep reinforcement learning for robotics.
- **Codebase and Network:** They used Legged Gym as their codebase and integrated their series-parallel training module. An MLP network with layers of sizes was employed for the policy. No historical data or pre-trained models were used, indicating learning from scratch.
- **Hardware Platform:** The validation was performed on the **Tien Kung humanoid robot**, developed by the Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics Co. Ltd. This robot is a sophisticated platform:
 - Dimensions: 163 cm tall, 56 kg weight.
 - Degrees of Freedom (DoF): 42.
 - Sensors: Multiple visual perception sensors, six-dimensional force sensors, an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU), and 3D vision sensors.
 - Computational Power: 550 trillion operations per second (TOPS).
- **Real-time Deployment:** For real-time inference on the physical robot, an NVIDIA Orin was used, maintaining a control frequency of 100 Hz, ensuring smooth and responsive operation.

**B. Comparison with Other Series-Parallel Approaches:**
The paper qualitatively compares LiPS against two existing approaches for handling parallel ankle mechanisms in humanoid robots:

1. **Series-to-Parallel Conversion During Deployment:**
 - **Description:** This is the "currently widely used method" where a policy trained on a serial model in simulation is converted to control a parallel physical robot. This typically involves either "torque equivalence" (analytically converting serial torques to parallel actuator torques) or "position equivalence" (controlling parallel joint positions directly).
 - **Problems:**
 - **Computational Load:** Torque equivalence requires solving analytical solutions at high frequencies (robot control frequency), consuming significant computational resources and potentially leading to no solutions in real-time.
 - **Accuracy/Robustness:** Position equivalence suffers from amplified errors if encoder precision is low, causing robot jitter (as shown in Figure 5, illustrating joint velocity and position spikes from a high-performance actuator). This method also fails to fully utilize the sophisticated design of parallel ankles.
 - **Table I Assessment:** Low computational load (during *training*), Medium robustness, High control noise.

2. **Passive Ankles:**
 - **Description:** An attempt by other researchers (e.g., Gu et al.) to simplify ankle control by setting the proportional gain (kp) to 0, achieving only passive damping.
 - **Problems:**
 - **Performance Degradation:** This method oversimplifies the dual-degree-of-freedom performance of the ankle. The coupling of damping and stiffness terms in parallel ankles leads to a decline in overall robot performance.
 - **Instability and Damage Risk:** In the authors' experiments, setting kp to 0 caused severe robot tilting, leading to a high risk of damage.
 - **Table I Assessment:** High computational load (due to instability leading to high energy consumption), Low performance, Low robustness.

3. **LiPS Method (Our Proposed Method):**
 - **Description:** LiPS integrates parallel dynamics modeling into the simulation training loop, allowing the RL policy to learn parallel-aware control, which is then directly deployed to the physical robot without complex runtime conversion.
 - **Advantages:**
 - **Training Efficiency:** Fully utilizes the computational speed of GPU simulation engines, significantly increasing exploration in reinforcement learning.
 - **Deployment Efficiency:** Eliminates the need for high-frequency analytical solution computations during real-world deployment, greatly reducing the computational load on the robot's end.
 - **Control Performance & Robustness:** By learning on a model that accounts for the true parallel dynamics, the policy achieves superior control performance and robustness against joint noise.
 - **Demonstration:** The Tien Kung humanoid robot successfully performed **robust omnidirectional walking** using the LiPS method (Figure 6). The authors qualitatively state that LiPS outperforms other methods across metrics.
 - **Table I Assessment:** Low computational load, High performance, High robustness.

Due to the significant risk of damage and the extensive tuning required for other methods, the authors note that full ablation results across all conditions were not presented. However, based on their dynamic modeling and observed experimental waveforms (Figure 5), they qualitatively conclude that LiPS offers superior performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LiPS method represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robot reinforcement learning, with broad implications for both research and practical applications. Its core contribution lies in effectively bridging a critical gap between the capabilities of high-performance simulation environments and the mechanical complexities of advanced robotic platforms.

The key areas of significance and potential impact include:

1. **Reduced Sim2Real Gap and Enhanced Reliability:** By incorporating detailed multi-rigid-body dynamics modeling, specifically for complex parallel structures like ankle mechanisms, directly into the reinforcement learning training phase, LiPS substantially reduces the sim2real gap. This means policies learned in simulation are much more faithful to the physical robot's behavior, leading to increased reliability, robustness, and predictable performance when deployed in the real world. This is crucial for humanoid robots operating in unstructured or dynamic environments.

2. **Improved Training and Deployment Efficiency:** LiPS optimizes both stages of robot learning. During training, it leverages the speed of GPU-accelerated simulators (like Isaac Gym) to conduct large-scale parallel learning, while effectively handling complex dynamics. During real-world deployment, it eliminates the need for computationally intensive, real-time series-to-parallel conversions, thereby reducing the computational load on the robot's onboard systems and enabling faster, more responsive control at high frequencies (e.g., 100 Hz on Tien Kung).

3. **Unlocking Full Potential of Parallel Mechanisms:** Humanoid robots increasingly rely on parallel structures for their inherent advantages in stiffness, load-bearing capacity, and precision. By enabling RL policies to learn control strategies that explicitly account for these dynamics, LiPS allows robots to fully exploit the mechanical design benefits of these complex mechanisms, leading to more agile, stable, and powerful locomotion and manipulation capabilities.

4. **Generalizability Across URDF-based Robots:** The methodology proposed in LiPS is not limited to humanoid robots or ankle mechanisms. The authors claim its generalizability to "other URDF-based robots." This means that researchers working with various complex robots whose dynamics are poorly represented by simplified URDF models in GPU-based simulators can potentially apply LiPS to improve their RL training and deployment processes. This could benefit legged robots, manipulators, and other complex robotic systems.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** By providing an effective tool for large-scale reinforcement learning training of complex URDF-based robots, LiPS is poised to assist the broader robotics research community. The authors' commitment to releasing their code and framework will enable other researchers to explore new configurations, algorithms, and dynamic models in reinforcement learning for humanoid robotics. This will foster innovation and a deeper understanding of the interplay between dynamic modeling and advanced robot control.

In conclusion, LiPS addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in humanoid robotics: effectively combining the power of data-driven reinforcement learning with the precision of detailed dynamics modeling for complex mechanical systems. Its impact will likely be seen in the development of more capable, robust, and autonomous humanoid robots that can seamlessly transition from simulation to real-world operation.
