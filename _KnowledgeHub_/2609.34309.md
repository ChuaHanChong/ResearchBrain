# MaLiang-Harness: A Programmable Path to Image and Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> MaLiang-Harness generates images and videos by having a multimodal LLM write executable visual programs (Canvas, SVG, Three.js) that renderers execute, then revising those programs from rendered visual feedback. A persistent program state, a traceable operation log, and revision-tied verification close the gap between runnable code and visual intent. GPT-6-Astra reaches 100% generation success, meeting all quality thresholds on 96% of image and 76.9% of video tasks.

## Problem

- Diffusion and flow-based generators synthesize pixels directly, leaving construction implicit, so composition, targeted edits, and the history of how an artwork was built cannot be inspected.
- Program-driven visual methods treat successful execution as success, yet code can run cleanly while misplacing objects, mistiming events, or violating requested appearance, a Program-to-Visual gap.
- Prior executable-visual pipelines lack persistent state and revision-linked verification, so edits can silently break previously satisfied requirements while stale assessments are trusted for changed outputs.

## Method

- ==Persistent Executable Generation (PEG) state==: a ==revisioned tuple== of program, backend, hashed assets, spatiotemporal composition and task context, where requirements can be appended but never weakened.
- ==Traceable Generation Process (TGP)==: logs every operation with inputs, results, and source and resulting revision indices, linking renders, sampled timestamps, and crops to the exact revision evaluated.
- ==Revision-aware Editing and Verification (REV)==: per-requirement pass, fail, or uncertain reviews bound to one revision; delivery requires export checks, checkpoints, and fresh passing reviews on the current revision. Together they form **MaLiang-Harness**.

## Results

- On MaLiang-IBench's **50** image prompts, GPT-6-Astra succeeds on **all** tasks and meets every quality threshold on **48**, while GPT-5.6-Luna succeeds on **46** but only **22** qualify.
- On MaLiang-VBench's **13** video prompts, Astra completes all **13** with **10** fully qualifying, motion coherence being the binding criterion; GPT-5.6-Sol succeeds **53.8%**, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash **0%**.
- General capability index correlates only moderately with drawing pass rate (Spearman **0.65**); GPT-5.6-Luna and GPT-6-Luna share index score **37** yet reach **44%** versus **88%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Execution success is a weak proxy for visual correctness; programmatic generators need rendered-evidence verification tied to the exact version being delivered, not to earlier passing checks.
> - General reasoning and coding benchmarks poorly predict translating spatial and appearance constraints into code, so visual program generation needs direct evaluation rather than inherited leaderboard rankings.
> - Iterative code refinement can introduce new defects and stall; separating budget-bounded termination from verified completion keeps agent loops honest, while photorealism remains an open limitation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Consider an animation request: a pixel-art kitten carries a star across a keyboard-and-book "railway" toward a desk lamp. An image or video program can run and still put something in the wrong place, look wrong, or make an event happen at the wrong time. Executable programs offer explicit ways to specify and edit composition and motion, but execution alone does not establish that the requested visual result was achieved.

MaLiang-Harness responds with a loop. The model plans and writes drawing or animation code, and a backend renders it. The model then inspects the result against the request, revises the code, and checks the new version.

Case (a), Star Delivery: a pixel cat carrying a star along a keyboard railway, unfolding books and crossing a tea flood toward a desk lamp. Each model row contains five uniformly sampled frames in left-to-right temporal order; a white × on black marks an unsuccessful run.

In this case, the paper describes Astra's setting as detailed, with its kitten traversing keyboard and books toward the lamp. Kimi's scene is simpler, and its action sequence is reduced. Five sampled frames show rendered outcomes, but they cannot prove continuity between frames.

### 2. One revision reference

The distinguishing move is to keep the artwork, its edit history, and the visual evidence used to review it linked to the same revision. Three designs support this:

- **PEG** (Persistent Executable Generation) retains the executable program, assets, task requirements, plan, and revision.
- **TGP** (Traceable Generation Process) records operations and their results against revisions.
- **REV** (Revision-aware Editing and Verification) ties visual reviews to the revision that produced their evidence.

A commit creates a new revision. The model renders and inspects evidence for that revision and uses discrepancies to plan edits. It must then review the new current revision before delivery. Historical content can be restored, but restoration is also a new revision. A review of an old version cannot certify a changed one.

These reviews are model self-assessments, not an independent measure of perceptual quality. Temporal checks use sampled frames and cannot establish what happens between them.

The upper part routes prompt, reference images, requirements, and optional visual materials through model reasoning and coding toward task decomposition, component planning, backend selection (scene2d, Canvas, SVG, Three.js), and temporal planning. The lower part shows task context and PEG states moving through planning and construction, rendering, evidence associated with a revision, review/revision, and feedback records.

### 3. What the benchmark establishes

The 50-prompt MaLiang-IBench image benchmark tests whether running code means the visual request was satisfied. GPT-6-Sol judges prompt alignment, aesthetics, and composition on a five-point scale. Each threshold requires a score of at least 4. GPT-6-Astra generated successfully on all 50 tasks, but only 48 met all three thresholds. This rate is reported against the full 50-task set.

Video shows the same pattern. Astra completed all 13 MaLiang-VBench tasks, and 10 of its videos met all four thresholds, including motion coherence. Video review used 12 temporally ordered frames per successful video.

Task completion and visual satisfaction are therefore different outcomes. These results do not come from a controlled test, so they do not isolate which harness mechanism caused them.

Left: AA Intelligence Index score versus drawing-quality pass rate (%) for 11 labeled models, Spearman ρ = 0.65. A dashed annotation marks the 44-percentage-point difference between the Luna models with identical displayed AA score 37. Right: shares of the same 50 image tasks with all criteria met, successful but criteria unmet, or failed.

General capability scores do not fully predict these results. The rank correlation is positive. Even so, GPT-5.6-Luna and GPT-6-Luna both have displayed AA scores of 37, while their drawing-quality pass rates are 44% and 88%. This association is exploratory because budgets and feedback differed. In addition, the public DeepSeek-V4-Pro score may not match the evaluated checkpoint.

### 4. Closing

The authors frame the contribution as a systematic way to study how models turn programs into visual outcomes. In this framing, general capability scores are an imperfect predictor. A renderer can turn a plan into an image or video, but only inspecting the rendered result shows whether the visual requirements were met. The paper reports no controlled ablation separating PEG, TGP, and REV. The workflow is therefore a proposed framing, not a demonstrated guarantee of quality.
