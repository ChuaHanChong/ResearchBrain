# Learning Representations by Maximizing Mutual Information Across Views

> [!summary] Summary
> Augmented Multiscale Deep InfoMax (AMDIM) is a self-supervised learning approach that maximizes mutual information between features extracted from independently augmented, multiscale views of input data. The method attained state-of-the-art results on several computer vision benchmarks, including a 68.1% linear accuracy on ImageNet, exceeding prior self-supervised methods by over 12%.

## Problem

- The high cost and labor intensity associated with creating large, manually annotated datasets for traditional supervised learning.
- A need for effective methods to learn robust data representations from unlabeled data to broaden the applicability of deep learning models.
- Challenges in fairly evaluating self-supervised methods, distinguishing performance gains from the objective versus architectural improvements.

## Method

- **AMDIM** (Augmented Multiscale Deep InfoMax) extends local **Deep InfoMax** by maximizing ==mutual information== across multiple independently augmented views of a single input image.
- The mutual information objective is applied simultaneously across multiple feature scales (e.g., global-to-local and local-to-local) to capture hierarchical relationships within the data.
- A ==Noise-Contrastive Estimation (NCE)== lower bound is used to maximize mutual information, involving distinguishing true feature pairs from negative samples, with stability regularization applied.

## Results

- Achieved **68.1%** linear accuracy on ImageNet, outperforming prior self-supervised methods by over **12%** and concurrent methods by **7%**, using a relatively smaller model that is reproducible on standard hardware.
- Demonstrated strong performance on other benchmarks, with **94.2%** linear accuracy on STL10, **91.2%** on CIFAR10, and **70.2%** on CIFAR100.
- Showcased robust transfer learning capabilities, attaining **55.0%** accuracy on the ImageNet→Places205 task, surpassing the best prior result by **7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maximizing mutual information between features derived from diverse, augmented views of a shared context compels the learned representations to capture robust, high-level semantic factors.
> - Data augmentation is the most impactful component for improving performance in contrastive self-supervised learning, significantly more so than multiscale prediction or regularization.
> - The combination of the mutual information objective with mixture-based representations can lead to emergent properties, such as segmentation-like behavior, without explicit supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper contributes to the field of self-supervised representation learning, an area of machine learning focused on training models to learn useful data representations without requiring explicit human-provided labels. This field has gained prominence due to the high cost and labor associated with creating large, manually annotated datasets, particularly in domains like computer vision, natural language processing (NLP), and robotics.

Historically, unsupervised learning methods aimed to discover underlying structures in data. Self-supervised learning distinguishes itself by relying on "pretext tasks" or procedurally generated supervised learning problems. These tasks are designed such that solving them requires the model to capture meaningful information about the data. Examples in computer vision include predicting spatial structure (e.g., Doersch et al., 2015; Noroozi and Favaro, 2016), colorizing grayscale images (Zhang et al., 2016), or predicting image rotations (Gidaris et al., 2018). In NLP, methods like word embeddings (Mikolov et al., 2013; Pennington et al., 2014) and context-sensitive encoders (Peters et al., 2018; Devlin et al., 2019) have demonstrated the utility of pre-trained models.

This work specifically builds upon mutual information maximization principles, a concept explored in various forms for representation learning. Earlier work, such as Vondrick et al. (2018) and Eslami et al. (2018), showed that objectives relying on mutual information could lead to the emergence of behaviors like object tracking or scene rendering. Deep InfoMax (DIM, Hjelm et al., 2019) is a direct predecessor, maximizing mutual information between global and local features of a single input. Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC, van den Oord et al., 2018) similarly uses noise-contrastive estimation to maximize mutual information between different parts of a sequence or image, and its approach to bounding mutual information is adopted here.

A recurring challenge in evaluating self-supervised methods is distinguishing performance gains due to the self-supervised objective itself from those attributable to improved model architectures or training practices. The authors acknowledge this by following evaluation protocols established by Kolesnikov et al. (2019), which involve re-implementing and optimizing existing methods with up-to-date architectures for fair comparison. The present work aims to advance the state-of-the-art in self-supervised representation learning by proposing an enhanced mutual information-based approach that yields notable performance improvements while maintaining computational feasibility.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective method for self-supervised representation learning that can learn useful representations from unlabeled data. The core motivation stems from the high cost and labor involved in generating large, manually annotated datasets required for traditional supervised learning. By minimizing dependence on such datasets, the proposed method seeks to broaden the applicability of deep learning models and reduce development overhead.

The central hypothesis guiding this work is that maximizing mutual information between features extracted from multiple "views" of a shared context will compel the learned representations to capture high-level, semantic factors. These factors, such as the presence of specific objects or the occurrence of events, exert a broad influence across different perspectives or modalities of the same underlying context. For instance, observing an object from different camera angles or through different sensory modalities (e.g., visual, auditory, tactile) should yield features that are highly mutually informative if the underlying representation accurately captures the object's identity.

Specifically, the authors aim to:
1. **Advance mutual information-based self-supervised learning:** By extending the local Deep InfoMax (DIM) framework to incorporate multiple, independently-augmented views of an input and simultaneously learn representations across multiple feature scales.
2. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance:** Develop a model capable of learning image representations that significantly outperform prior and concurrent self-supervised methods on standard benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet, STL10) when evaluated using standard linear classification protocols.
3. **Ensure computational practicality:** Design a model and training procedure that remains reproducible on standard hardware, addressing concerns about the computational demands of some prior methods.
4. **Explore emergent properties:** Investigate whether extending the model to use mixture-based representations leads to additional desirable behaviors, such as segmentation, as a natural byproduct of the mutual information objective.

The underlying motivation is that by forcing the model to find commonalities across diverse, but related, observations of a single context, it will distill robust and abstract features that are highly valuable for downstream tasks, even when those tasks involve entirely different data distributions or objectives.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Augmented Multiscale Deep InfoMax (AMDIM)**, an extension of the local Deep InfoMax (DIM) model (Hjelm et al., 2019). AMDIM enhances local DIM through three primary modifications and introduces an additional component for mixture-based representations.

#### 3.1. Local Deep InfoMax (DIM) Foundation

Local DIM maximizes mutual information between a global summary feature vector, `f1(x)`, derived from the entire input `x`, and a collection of local feature vectors, `{f7(x)ij}`, extracted from an intermediate layer of the encoder. Here, `f1` represents antecedent features (conditioning information), and `f7` represents consequent features (predicted information). The mutual information quantifies how much knowing `f1(x)` improves the prediction of `f7(x)ij`.

#### 3.2. Noise-Contrastive Estimation (NCE)

The mutual information is maximized using a Noise-Contrastive Estimation (NCE) lower bound, a method adapted from NLP applications (van den Oord et al., 2018). The objective minimizes a loss function:
`LΦ(f_antecedent, f_consequent, N_consequents) = -log (exp(Φ(f_antecedent, f_consequent)) / Σ~f_consequent∈N_consequents∪{f_consequent} exp(Φ(f_antecedent, ~f_consequent)))`
where `Φ(f_antecedent, f_consequent)` is a scalar matching score, typically a dot product between transformed features (`φ1(f_antecedent)^T φ7(f_consequent)`). The `N_consequents` set consists of "distractor" negative samples drawn from the marginal distribution of consequent features, forcing the model to distinguish the true consequent from many plausible alternatives. To mitigate instability during training, regularization (`λ(score)^2`) and soft clipping of scores (`s' = c tanh(s/c)`) are applied.

#### 3.3. Augmented Views

AMDIM introduces data augmentation to generate multiple views of a single input image. For each input `x`, two independently augmented versions, `x1` and `x2`, are generated (`x1 ~ A(x)`, `x2 ~ A(x)`). The mutual information is then maximized between features extracted from these distinct augmented views: `I(f1(x1); f7(x2)ij)`. Standard data augmentations used include random resized crop, random jitter in color space, random conversion to grayscale, and random horizontal flip. This modification aims to make the learned representations invariant to specific data augmentations, thereby capturing more fundamental, higher-level information.

#### 3.4. Multiscale Mutual Information

AMDIM extends the mutual information objective to operate across multiple feature scales simultaneously. Instead of only between global (`f1`) and local (`f7`) features, the model also maximizes mutual information between `f1` and `f5` (features from a 5x5 spatial dimension layer), and between `f5` and `f5` (local-to-local prediction at the 5x5 scale). This multiscale approach enables the model to capture hierarchical relationships within the input and integrate information at various levels of abstraction. The objective becomes a sum of these n-to-m infomax costs.

#### 3.5. Encoder Architecture

The model employs an encoder architecture based on ResNet (He et al., 2016a,b), specifically adapted for DIM objectives. Key design considerations include controlling receptive fields to prevent the pretext task from becoming too trivial due to excessive overlap, and avoiding padding to maintain stationary feature distributions. The encoder consists of a sequence of residual blocks. The first layer in each block uses mean pooling and a convolution with a specific stride, followed by ReLU and a 1x1 convolution. Subsequent layers are standard 1x1 residual layers. Different feature maps (`f1`, `f5`, `f7`) are extracted from different layers within this encoder structure.

#### 3.6. Mixture-Based Representations

As an extension, the model incorporates mixture-based features. For each antecedent feature `f1`, a set of `k` mixture features `{f^1_1, ..., f^k_1}` are computed. The objective is modified to maximize a weighted sum of NCE scores between these mixture features and consequent features, regularized by an entropy maximization term `αH(q)`. The weighting `q(f^j_1(x1)|f^i_7(x2))` is computed as a softmax over the NCE scores, incorporating a temperature parameter `τ`. This approach is designed to allow the model to learn multiple, distinct representations for a given context, potentially leading to a richer understanding of the input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The AMDIM model was evaluated on several standard datasets, including CIFAR10, CIFAR100, STL10, ImageNet, and Places205, following the linear evaluation protocol (Kolesnikov et al., 2019). The encoder was trained on unlabeled data, and then linear or MLP classifiers were trained on top of the fixed encoder features.

#### 4.1. Performance on Standard Benchmarks

- **CIFAR10 & CIFAR100:**
 - Large AMDIM models achieved 91.2% linear accuracy and 93.1% MLP accuracy on CIFAR10.
 - On CIFAR100, these models reached 70.2% linear accuracy and 72.8% MLP accuracy.
 - These results are reported to be comparable to slightly older fully-supervised models and surpass other self-supervised feature learning methods.
- **STL10:**
 - AMDIM achieved 94.2% linear accuracy. This significantly improved upon prior self-supervised results and compared favorably with semi-supervised methods that fine-tune the encoder using labeled data.
- **ImageNet:**
 - With a large model and batch size of 1008, AMDIM reached 68.1% linear accuracy. This outperformed the best prior self-supervised result by over 12% and concurrent results by 7%.
 - With MLP evaluation, the model achieved 69.5% accuracy.
 - Both linear and MLP ImageNet results surpassed the original AlexNet model (trained end-to-end) by a substantial margin.
 - Notably, the AMDIM model is described as significantly smaller than competing models that produced similar or inferior results and is reproducible on standard hardware. A smaller model trained for a shorter duration (50 epochs on 4 GPUs) achieved 62.7% accuracy.
- **ImageNet → Places205 Transfer Task:**
 - AMDIM achieved 55.0% accuracy on the Places205 task when trained on ImageNet, beating the best prior result by 7%. This indicated strong transferability of the learned features.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation tests on STL10 and ImageNet (using smaller models and shorter training schedules) revealed the relative importance of different AMDIM components:
- **Data Augmentation:** Had the most substantial impact on performance. Removing color jitter, random grayscale, or random crop individually led to significant drops in accuracy. Applying "strong augmentation" (e.g., Fast AutoAugment) further improved accuracy.
- **Stability Regularization:** The regularization applied to the NCE cost was the second most impactful factor, contributing to performance improvements.
- **Multiscale Prediction:** Maximizing mutual information across multiple scales also yielded performance gains, though less pronounced than data augmentation.

#### 4.3. Emergent Behaviors

- **Mixture-Based Representations:** When AMDIM was extended with mixture-based representations, segmentation-like behavior emerged as a natural side-effect, as visualized by the posterior distributions over mixture components for different feature locations. While not fully optimized for ImageNet, this qualitative finding on STL10 demonstrated a new capability and showed a modest performance boost (approximately 1%) with a simple bag-of-features approach during evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work introduces Augmented Multiscale Deep InfoMax (AMDIM), which represents a significant advancement in self-supervised representation learning.

**Quantitative Impact:** AMDIM establishes new state-of-the-art performance on several prominent computer vision benchmarks, including ImageNet, STL10, and CIFAR. The reported gains of over 12% on ImageNet compared to prior methods and 7% compared to concurrent work are substantial. The strong transfer learning performance on the ImageNet→Places205 task further underscores the generality and robustness of the learned representations.

**Reduced Data Dependency:** By achieving high performance with self-supervised learning, the approach reduces the reliance on large, manually labeled datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to produce. This has implications for developing models in domains where labeled data is scarce or expensive, making advanced deep learning more accessible.

**Computational Efficiency and Reproducibility:** A key aspect of AMDIM's significance is its computational practicality. The model is described as significantly smaller than some competing methods and is reproducible on standard hardware (e.g., 4-8 Tesla V100 GPUs). This makes the research more accessible to a wider community and facilitates further research and development.

**Generalizability of Approach:** The core principle of maximizing mutual information across multiple views of a shared context is broadly applicable beyond static images. The authors suggest extensions to other domains such as video, audio, and text, indicating a potential for wide-reaching impact across different data modalities and tasks. For example, capturing spatio-temporal relationships in video through multiple views could immediately improve the model's capabilities.

**Emergent Capabilities:** The observation that segmentation-like behavior emerges as a natural side-effect of incorporating mixture-based representations is an intriguing finding. This suggests that the mutual information objective, when combined with architectural innovations, can lead to the discovery of richer, more structured representations without explicit supervision for segmentation. This could open avenues for multi-task learning or specialized feature extraction in a self-supervised context.

**Future Research Directions:** The authors identify several areas for future work, including adapting AMDIM objectives to standard architectures, improving scalability, further developing mixture-based representations, and formally analyzing the role of regularization in NCE-based mutual information bounds. These directions suggest continued potential for refinement and broader application of the contrastive self-supervised learning paradigm.
