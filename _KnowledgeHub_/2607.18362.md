# FARO: Feasibility-Aware Robot Motion Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> FARO, a framework for robot motion optimization, introduces a hierarchical strategy using progressively expressive feasibility tests to efficiently prune infeasible contact decisions for humanoid loco-manipulation. This method accelerates the search for dynamically consistent multi-contact motion plans by up to two orders of magnitude in kinematic checks and enhances solution discovery in tree search while maintaining a low false-negative rate for proposed plans.

## Problem

- Efficiently planning novel robot behaviors in previously unseen scenarios, particularly for high-dimensional, hybrid, and underactuated systems like humanoid robots performing loco-manipulation, remains a challenge.
- Trajectory optimization methods face computational expense due to the combinatorial growth of the search space, often requiring simplified models or heuristics.
- Reinforcement learning struggles with exploration for complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks, frequently relying on tracking pre-existing demonstrations rather than discovering new behaviors.

## Method

- **FARO** employs a nested hierarchy of optimization problems, including ==Kinematic Sequence Optimization (KSO)== and full ==Trajectory Optimization (TO)==, to perform progressively more expressive feasibility checks on candidate contact sequences.
- The framework integrates these feasibility tests into a ==feasibility-guided tree search== algorithm that explores discrete contact-mode sequences, using ==Upper Confidence bounds applied to Trees (UCT)== with ==progressive widening==.
- It incorporates ==caching== of feasibility outcomes for contact modes and transitions to avoid redundant computations, thereby accelerating the exploration process.

## Results

- Kinematic Sequence Optimization (KSO) achieved an average speedup of approximately **two orders of magnitude** (**0.60s** vs. **64.70s**) compared to full Trajectory Optimization (TO) on human-designed contact sequences, reducing decision variables by **74.8x**.
- Feasibility-guided tree search variants utilizing FARO's filters found significantly more solutions and expanded more nodes than a TO-only baseline in box-placement tasks (e.g., M,E,KSO found **26.4 solutions** vs. **0** for TO-only in a challenging scenario).
- When filtering LLM-generated contact plans, KSO provided speedups (up to **15.5x** in harder tasks) with a false-negative rate near zero (**0.0%** to **1.3%**), indicating it rarely prunes truly feasible plans while efficiently filtering infeasible ones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical approach to feasibility checking, starting with computationally cheaper kinematic tests (like KSO), can effectively prune infeasible contact decisions early in the planning process without generating a high number of false negatives.
> - Kinematic Sequence Optimization (KSO) serves as an efficient proxy for full Trajectory Optimization, offering up to two orders of magnitude speedup while retaining sufficient constraint coverage to guide the search towards dynamically feasible paths.
> - Integrating such hierarchical feasibility checks with a tree search and caching mechanism significantly enhances the discovery of solutions and the efficiency of exploration for complex multi-contact motion planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The problem of fast planning for novel robot behaviors in previously unseen scenarios, particularly for high-dimensional, hybrid, and underactuated systems like humanoid robots performing loco-manipulation, represents a significant challenge in robotics. Current approaches primarily fall into two categories: trajectory optimization (TO) and reinforcement learning (RL).

Trajectory optimization methods often combine discrete search over contact sequences with continuous trajectory optimization to manage the hybrid nature of contact interactions. While these methods can improve contact reasoning and behavioral diversity, the combinatorial growth of the search space renders them computationally expensive. This often necessitates restricting the search through simplified models, task-specific heuristics, or hand-designed high-level actions, which limits the complexity and diversity of discoverable dynamic behaviors.

Reinforcement learning has demonstrated success in locomotion tasks, often attributed to the cyclic nature of these tasks and extensive reward shaping. However, RL remains less effective for complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation skills. Recent advancements in RL-based humanoid loco-manipulation often rely on tracking pre-existing demonstrations or retargeting human motions to bypass the exploration problem inherent in discovering novel behaviors in unknown situations. These approaches are effective when suitable reference motions are available but do not address the fundamental problem of *discovering* behaviors.

This research addresses the central obstacle of exploration in multi-contact motion planning. It aims to develop a strategy that enables efficient searching of motion and contact interaction spaces for humanoid loco-manipulation. By rapidly pruning infeasible contact decisions, the proposed framework seeks to mitigate the combinatorial burden of search without relying on predefined behavioral abstractions or reference motions, thereby offering a complementary approach to current TO and RL paradigms for discovering novel, complex dynamic behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the efficient, on-the-fly generation of complex loco-manipulation behaviors for humanoid robots in real-world environments. To achieve this, the authors propose a framework, Feasibility-Aware Robot Motion Optimization (FARO), designed to address the challenges of exploration and computational complexity in multi-contact motion planning.

The detailed objectives and motivations include:

1. **Developing a Nested Kino-Dynamic Framework:** The core motivation is to create a hierarchical system capable of rapid feasibility checking and dynamically consistent trajectory generation for a given candidate contact sequence. This aims to reduce the computational cost associated with full dynamic optimization by filtering out infeasible plans early.
2. **Improving Search Process Efficiency:** The high-dimensional and combinatorial nature of humanoid loco-manipulation planning makes search computationally expensive. The authors aim to substantially improve the search process by integrating the feasibility-checking module with a feasibility-guided tree search and a Large Language Model (LLM)-based contact plan sampling strategy. This is motivated by the need to explore a broader range of behaviors faster.
3. **Generating High-Quality, Executable Trajectories:** A key objective is to ensure that the trajectories produced by the optimization framework are dynamically consistent and of sufficient quality for execution on real humanoid robots. This addresses the practical requirement for deploying planned behaviors in physical systems, which often necessitates robust and stable motions.
4. **Enabling Efficient Exploration of Multi-Contact Motion:** The authors are motivated by the limitations of existing TO (which restricts search scope due to complexity) and RL (which often avoids exploration through imitation). Their goal is to overcome these limitations by introducing a general exploration strategy that efficiently navigates the space of motion and contact interaction. By pruning infeasible contact decisions, the framework intends to reduce combinatorial burden without relying on pre-defined abstractions or reference motions.

The specific contributions of the paper, reflecting these objectives, are:
- The introduction of FARO, a novel optimization-based pruning strategy for multi-contact motion planning, designed to accelerate search without generating a significant number of false-negative feasibility classifications.
- The application of this FARO pruning strategy to a feasibility-guided tree search algorithm for multi-contact motion planning, demonstrating its superior performance compared to direct trajectory-optimization-based exploration.
- A systematic evaluation of the FARO approach across three distinct scenarios: a model-based tree search, an LLM-guided contact-plan generation, and human-input contact plans.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, FARO (Feasibility-Aware Robot Motion Optimization), operates on the principle of hierarchical, progressively more expressive optimization-based feasibility tests. It aims to efficiently explore discrete contact-mode sequences while preserving a range of dynamically feasible behaviors.

**A. Contact-Explicit Optimization**
The method centers on contact-explicit optimization, where a discrete contact-mode sequence is provided, and the corresponding continuous state, control, and timing variables are optimized. This is complemented by a hierarchy of feasibility checks to identify promising contact sequences.

1. **Contact Mode Sequence Definition:**
 - A scene consists of contact interfaces, `I`.
 - A contact state for an interface `a` is a pair `(a, b)`, where `b` is another interface or `∅` (free).
 - A `contact mode` `c` is a complete, consistent assignment of contact pairs for all interfaces.
 - A `contact-mode time sequence` `C` is a series of `K` contact modes.

2. **State and Control Spaces:**
 - The state `x` includes robot (configuration `q_r`, generalized velocity `v_r`, centroidal momentum `h_r`) and object components (pose `q_o`, body velocity `V_o`).
 - The control `u` includes robot generalized acceleration `v_r_dot` and end-effector wrenches `λ_r_e`, and object body acceleration `V_o_dot` and external environment wrenches `W_o_ℓ_env`.
 - Robot configuration is in `SE(3) × R^n`, object pose in `SE(3)`.

3. **Constraint Definitions:** The optimization problems share a common set of constraints:
 - **Contact:** Modeled as rectangular patch-to-patch unilateral contact. Constraints include:
 - Patch normal alignment and zero normal separation.
 - In-plane position within admissible region (half-extents).
 - Pyramidal approximation of Coulomb friction for forces.
 - Torsional friction and center-of-pressure bounds for moments.
 - No-slip condition for sticking contacts (fixed in-plane position and rotation across timesteps).
 - **Collision:** Avoidance is imposed using a signed-distance approximation for convex geometries, queried with the GJK algorithm.
 - **Dynamics:**
 - **Robot Dynamics:** Integrates robot configuration, velocity, and centroidal momentum using backward Euler discretization. Centroidal momentum rate is governed by net external wrench (gravity, end-effector wrenches), consistent with whole-body robot dynamics via the centroidal momentum matrix.
 - **Object Dynamics:** Integrates object pose and twist using backward Euler discretization. Object twist-wrench dynamics are driven by total external wrenches (environment, gravity, contact forces).
 - **Limits:** Joint position, velocity, and a linear torque-speed envelope are enforced for actuated robot joints.

**B. Hierarchy of Optimization Problems (Feasibility Checks)**
FARO employs a nested hierarchy of optimization problems, progressively increasing in computational cost and constraint expressiveness:

1. **Contact Mode and Edge Feasibility (MEF):**
 - An inverse kinematics (IK) optimization problem.
 - A `contact mode` `c` is deemed infeasible if this optimization fails to converge.
 - A `transition edge` between `c1` and `c2` is checked by replacing `c` with `c1 ∪ c2`.
 - Optimizes only `q` (robot and object configurations) to satisfy contact, collision, and configuration limits. It is the cheapest check.

2. **Kinematic Sequence Optimization (KSO):**
 - Given a sequence of `K` contact modes, KSO solves for `K+1` robot-object configurations (`q_0` to `q_K`).
 - `q_0` satisfies the initial condition; `q_K` can constrain the terminal condition.
 - Each intermediate `q_s` represents a transition between `c_(s-1)` and `c_s`.
 - Enforces geometric contact constraints, sticking constraints (for persistent contact or contact release), collision avoidance, and configuration limits along the sequence.
 - It tests geometric feasibility without imposing time-dependent dynamics, making it computationally less expensive than full trajectory optimization.

3. **Trajectory Optimization (TO):**
 - Given a contact-mode sequence, TO solves for the full state trajectory `x_(0:N)`, control trajectory `u_(0:N-1)`, and a dimensionless time-scaling factor `T_bar_s` for each contact-mode stage.
 - Enforces full robot-object dynamics, contact feasibility (including friction and no-slip), collision avoidance, and state/control limits at each knot point of the discretized trajectory.
 - It is the most computationally expensive check, providing dynamically consistent motions.

**C. Feasibility-Guided Tree Search (Algorithm 1)**
The framework integrates these feasibility checks into a tree search over discrete contact-mode sequences.
- The tree is rooted at an initial mode. Each node represents a contact-mode sequence.
- Node selection uses a cost-based variant of Upper Confidence bounds applied to Trees (UCT) with progressive widening to balance exploitation and exploration.
- Candidate successor modes are proposed and appended to a sequence.
- **Verification (VERIFY):** The proposed sequence is subjected to a selected set of feasibility filters (`F`), applied from cheaper to more expensive (M, E, KSO, TO). Verification terminates at the first failed filter.
- **Caching:** Reusable outcomes (feasible/infeasible) are stored in caches to avoid recomputing previously checked contact modes and transitions, reducing redundant exploration.
- If all active filters succeed, the candidate is added to the tree with a cost from the final filter. Goal-reaching sequences are stored.
- Four search variants are defined based on the filter sets used: `F1` (KSO only), `F2` (M, E, KSO), `F3` (M, E, KSO, TO), and `F4` (TO only as baseline).

**D. Implementation:**
The KSO and TO are implemented using direct multiple-shooting transcription. Collision detection uses `coal` and the GJK algorithm. Robot kinematics and dynamics are handled by `Pinocchio`. Nonlinear programs are formulated in `CasADi`. KSO is solved with `acados` (SQP solver), TO with `Hippo` (SQP solver), and MEF with `Ipopt`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FARO was conducted across three distinct settings: human-specified contact sequences, feasibility-guided tree search, and LLM-based contact plan sampling.

**A. Qualitative Analysis on Human-Defined Contact Sequences:**
- Eight human-designed contact-mode sequences of varying lengths and complexities were tested.
- The **Kinematic Sequence Optimization (KSO)** demonstrated significantly faster computation times compared to the full **Trajectory Optimization (TO)**, with approximately two orders of magnitude speedup (average KSO time: 0.60s vs. average TO time: 64.70s).
- KSO achieved this speedup by reducing the number of decision variables by an average factor of 74.8x.
- Despite the reduction in complexity, KSO retained a high average constraint coverage of 70.0% of the constraint types present in TO, primarily omitting dynamics-related constraints.
- Selected trajectories generated by the framework were successfully executed on a real humanoid robot using a pre-trained RL-based trajectory-tracking controller, indicating the practical quality of the generated motions.

**B. Feasibility-Guided Tree Search:**
- The feasibility-guided tree search (Algorithm 1) was evaluated on two box-placement tasks (easy and hard variants) over five runs with a two-hour time budget.
- **Improved Solution Discovery and Tree Expansion:** Across both tasks, search variants employing FARO's feasibility filters (KSO, M,E,KSO, M,E,KSO,TO) consistently outperformed the TO-only baseline in terms of the number of solutions found and total tree nodes expanded.
- **KSO as an Effective Proxy:** Filters that excluded TO during node expansion (KSO and M,E,KSO variants) explored substantially faster and invoked TO only after reaching a discrete goal. This indicated that KSO can effectively guide the search, identifying promising kinematic paths without the full computational cost of dynamics.
- **Benefits of Mode and Edge Filtering:** In the "hard task," the `M,E,KSO` variant found 26.4 solutions and expanded 814.6 nodes on average, significantly outperforming the `KSO` variant (0.4 solutions, 218.2 nodes). This difference was attributed to the caching of infeasibility results for contact modes and transitions, which reduced redundant exploration.
- **Challenging Scenarios:** In the hard task, the TO-only baseline failed to find any solutions and expanded only 12.8 nodes on average, highlighting the framework's advantage in complex, challenging scenarios.

**C. LLM-based Contact Plan Sampling:**
- KSO was evaluated as a feasibility filter for 100 contact plans generated by GPT-5.5 across four task scenarios (each with an easy and hard variant).
- **Computational Speedup:** KSO filtering provided a speedup in all tasks, with larger gains observed in the harder variants (e.g., up to 15.5x speedup for task 1-b).
- **Low False-Negative Rate (FNR):** The false-negative rate for KSO (i.e., rejecting a plan that is actually feasible under TO) remained near zero (0.0% or 1.3%). This indicates that KSO rarely prunes truly feasible contact plans, preserving the potential for discovering valid behaviors.
- **False-Positive Rate (FPR):** A false-positive rate (KSO accepting a plan that TO later finds infeasible) was observed (ranging from 4.2% to 41.4%). This is expected, as KSO omits dynamic constraints, meaning a kinematically feasible plan might not be dynamically feasible.
- The low FNR is consistent with the shared constraint structure between KSO and TO, where KSO preserves most geometric and contact constraints, while primarily omitting dynamic aspects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FARO framework represents a step towards addressing a fundamental challenge in robotics: the fast and efficient planning of novel, complex behaviors for humanoid loco-manipulation in previously unseen environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Accelerated Discovery of Dynamic Behaviors:** By introducing a hierarchical pruning strategy, FARO substantially accelerates the search for dynamically feasible multi-contact motion plans. This efficiency gain, particularly the two-orders-of-magnitude speedup offered by the Kinematic Sequence Optimization (KSO) compared to full Trajectory Optimization (TO), allows for broader exploration of candidate contact plans within a given time budget. This capacity for faster discovery of behaviors is crucial for real-time or near real-time planning in dynamic environments.
2. **Bridging the Gap in Planning Paradigms:** FARO's approach addresses a core limitation of both traditional Trajectory Optimization (combinatorial complexity) and Reinforcement Learning (difficulty with exploration for long-horizon, novel tasks). By efficiently pruning infeasible contact decisions, it reduces the computational burden of search without relying on task-specific heuristics or pre-existing demonstrations, enabling the discovery of behaviors that might otherwise be inaccessible.
3. **Enhanced Search Algorithms:** The integration of FARO's feasibility checks into a feasibility-guided tree search algorithm has demonstrated superior performance in solution discovery and tree expansion compared to baseline methods. The ability to cache feasibility results and prune early without expensive full dynamics computations significantly improves the scalability and effectiveness of the search, especially in challenging scenarios where baseline methods fail to find solutions.
4. **Practical Real-World Application:** The framework generates trajectories that are not only dynamically consistent but also of sufficient quality for execution on real humanoid robots, as demonstrated by the accompanying video. This indicates the practical viability of the approach for deploying autonomous loco-manipulation skills in physical systems.
5. **Synergy with High-Level Planning:** The successful application of KSO as a filter for LLM-generated contact plans highlights FARO's potential for integration with high-level AI systems. LLMs can propose diverse, high-level contact plans, which FARO can then rapidly filter for kinematic feasibility, significantly speeding up the overall planning process and reducing the burden of dynamically inconsistent proposals.

Looking forward, the authors identify several directions for further development, including extending the formulation to richer contact geometries, formally proving the nested property of the feasible sets, and integrating FARO with higher-level task and motion planning or program search to manage combinatorial complexity for substantially longer-horizon tasks. These advancements could further broaden the applicability and impact of FARO, making it a foundational component for increasingly autonomous and versatile humanoid robots. The work contributes to the objective of enabling robots to reason about their environment and plan novel situations, a hallmark of intelligent behavior.
