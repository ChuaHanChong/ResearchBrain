# Multi-Resolution Flow Matching: Training-Free Diffusion Acceleration via Staged Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beihang University and collaborating institutions developed MrFlow, a training-free multi-resolution acceleration strategy for flow-matching text-to-image diffusion models. This method reduces inference time by up to 10.3x for 1024x1024 images while maintaining generation quality within a 1-2% gap compared to native inference.

## Problem

- High computational cost and inference time of high-quality text-to-image diffusion models, especially for 1024x1024 image generation, hindering real-time applications.
- Existing training-free acceleration methods often provide limited speedup (e.g., <4x) or introduce visual artifacts and blurring at higher accelerations.
- Training-dependent acceleration techniques, such as timestep distillation, require extensive and costly fine-tuning, limiting their broad applicability.

## Method

- **MrFlow** proposes a staged ==low-to-high-resolution pipeline==, starting with low-resolution ==latent-space sampling== to establish global image structure.
- ==Pixel-space super-resolution== is performed using a pre-trained ==GAN-based network (Real-ESRGAN)== to upscale the image from low to target resolution.
- ==Low-strength noise injection== is applied to the super-resolved latent, followed by a ==single high-resolution latent-space sampling step==, to refine details and correct potential artifacts.

## Results

- MrFlow achieved up to **10.3x speedup** on Qwen-Image-20B and **8.25x** on FLUX.1-dev for 1024x1024 image generation, maintaining quality (Geneval/OneIG-Bench) within a **1-2% gap** of native 50-step inference.
- The method consistently outperformed other training-free acceleration baselines, which exhibited substantial quality degradation or collapse at comparable speedups.
- MrFlow combines orthogonally with pre-trained timestep distillation methods (e.g., Pi-Flow), yielding up to **25.1x speedup** on Qwen-Image while preserving generation quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Images exhibit structure preservation across resolutions, allowing efficient coarse-to-fine generation where global structure can be established rapidly at low resolutions with fewer sampling steps.
> - Pixel-space super-resolution using GANs is more robust for introducing sharper high-frequency details, with errors concentrated in this frequency band, making them amenable to subsequent refinement.
> - A single high-resolution denoising step with low-strength noise is sufficient for detail refinement because the velocity field near the clean image endpoint has a 'straighter' trajectory, resulting in low single-step discretization error.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the computational cost associated with high-quality text-to-image diffusion models, particularly those built upon Transformers and flow matching paradigms, such as Qwen-Image-20B and FLUX. These models can require substantial inference times (e.g., 47 seconds for a 1024x1024 image on an Nvidia A100), posing a challenge for widespread adoption and real-time applications.

Existing acceleration strategies generally fall into several categories:
- **Hardware/System-level optimizations:** This includes methods like quantization and efficient attention mechanisms, which typically offer limited speedup (e.g., less than 4x) and rely on specialized hardware or low-level system changes.
- **Diffusion-specific timestep reduction:** This involves techniques like timestep distillation, which can significantly reduce the Number of Function Evaluations (NFEs) from 50-100 down to 1-4, achieving high speedups (10x or more). However, these methods typically require extensive and costly fine-tuning training.
- **Feature caching:** These strategies exploit the similarity between adjacent timesteps to reuse historical features, offering moderate speedups (around 4x) without requiring training.
- **Token pruning/merging:** These methods dynamically reduce the number of computed tokens, but often yield limited acceleration (less than 1.5x).
- **Multi-resolution generation:** This class of methods leverages the spatial characteristics of images, generating structures at lower resolutions and refining details at higher resolutions. Some training-free multi-resolution approaches have shown speedups exceeding 5x. However, they often rely on dynamic identification of image regions during runtime, or perform upsampling in the latent space, which can introduce visual artifacts or blurring.

MrFlow positions itself within the training-free multi-resolution acceleration landscape, specifically aiming to overcome the limitations of existing multi-resolution methods related to blurring and artifacts. It seeks to provide a high-speedup solution that does not require additional training, runtime dynamic identification, or hardware-specific optimizations, thereby offering a more accessible and robust acceleration strategy for modern flow-matching diffusion models. Furthermore, the paper explores its orthogonal combinability with pre-trained timestep distillation strategies to achieve even higher acceleration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a training-free multi-resolution acceleration strategy for pre-trained flow-matching models that significantly reduces inference time while preserving high generation quality. The method aims to achieve this by addressing specific limitations observed in existing acceleration techniques:

1. **Overcoming Artifacts and Blurring in Existing Multi-Resolution Methods:** Current training-free multi-resolution strategies, particularly those that perform upsampling in the latent space or selectively modify partial regions, are noted to often produce noticeable blurring or artifacts in the generated images. MrFlow aims to mitigate these issues by designing a pipeline that performs super-resolution in the pixel space and employs a precise detail refinement mechanism.

2. **Achieving High End-to-End Acceleration without Training:** Many highly effective acceleration methods, such as timestep distillation, necessitate costly fine-tuning training, which limits their applicability. Other training-free methods, such as feature caching or token pruning, often yield more limited speedups (typically less than 4x). The motivation for MrFlow is to achieve a substantial end-to-end acceleration (more than 10x) without requiring any additional training or complex runtime adaptations.

3. **Exploiting Multi-Resolution Properties Efficiently:** The work is motivated by the observation that images naturally possess structure preservation at different resolutions, allowing for an efficient coarse-to-fine generation process. The objective is to design a staged pipeline that leverages the faster structure-generation capabilities of low-resolution sampling and then efficiently adds high-frequency details. This involves:
 - Maximizing speedup from low-resolution processing, which benefits from quadratic token reduction and fewer required timesteps.
 - Employing an effective super-resolution technique that preserves structure and introduces high-frequency signals without significant semantic errors.
 - Developing a mechanism to refine high-frequency details and correct potential errors introduced by super-resolution, ensuring quality preservation at high resolutions.

4. **Ensuring Flexibility, Simplicity, and Generalization:** A key motivation is to propose a method that is simple to implement (no specific hardware, complex code adaptation, or runtime statistics), flexible in balancing speedup and quality, and generalizable across different pre-trained flow-matching models and even combinable with other acceleration methods like timestep distillation.

In essence, the research seeks to fill a gap by providing a training-free, highly efficient, and high-quality acceleration solution for text-to-image diffusion models, specifically targeting the common resolution of 1024x1024.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MrFlow proposes a staged low-to-high-resolution pipeline for training-free diffusion acceleration. The overall procedure comprises four main stages: low-resolution latent-space sampling, VAE decoding, pixel-space super-resolution, VAE encoding, low-strength noise injection, high-resolution latent-space sampling, and VAE decoding. A typical configuration involves 12 low-resolution sampling steps, a noise strength of 0.1 for injection, and one high-resolution denoising step.

#### 3.1. Low-Resolution Structure Generation

- **Formulation:** This stage begins by sampling initial noise ($z_{LR_1}$) in the low-resolution latent space. It then performs ODE sampling using the pre-trained flow matching velocity network ($v_\theta$) and text-condition embedding ($c$) to obtain a clean low-resolution latent ($z_{LR_0}$). Typically, $K_L = 12$ Euler steps are used for this sampling. Finally, the clean latent is decoded into the pixel space ($x_{LR}$) using the pre-trained VAE decoder.
- **Analysis:** The acceleration here stems from two factors. First, the per-step inference cost scales nearly linearly with the number of image tokens; a 2x reduction in each side's dimension leads to a 4x reduction in tokens and approximately 4x speedup per step. Second, fewer sampling steps are required at low resolution to establish the global structure. This is attributed to higher text-to-image semantic utilization at low resolution (image tokens depend more strongly on text conditions), and a shorter ODE path for the low-frequency skeleton (only about 58% of the total path length is needed for low-frequency information).

#### 3.2. Pixel-Space Super-Resolution

- **Formulation:** The low-resolution image ($x_{LR}$) obtained after VAE decoding is upsampled in the pixel space by a pre-trained super-resolution (SR) network ($U$) to the target high resolution ($x_{SR}$). The paper specifically adopts the Real-ESRGAN model for 2x upsampling.
- **Analysis:** This stage involves two key design choices. Firstly, performing upsampling in the pixel space is considered more robust because edges, textures, and power-spectrum priors of natural images are inherently pixel-based. This allows for the reuse of pre-trained SR knowledge and benefits from the VAE encoder regularizing high-frequency components during re-encoding. Secondly, a GAN-based SR model (Real-ESRGAN) is chosen over interpolation or regression-based methods (like SwinIR). GANs produce sharper, more natural-image-like details, leaving mainly local high-frequency residuals. Empirical ablation shows that the high-resolution refinement stage is primarily effective at correcting high-frequency biases, making GAN-based SR more suitable as its errors are concentrated in this frequency band.

#### 3.3. Low-Strength Noise for High-Frequency Resampling

- **Formulation:** The super-resolved pixel-space image ($x_{SR}$) is re-encoded into the high-resolution latent space ($z_{SR_0}$) using the VAE encoder ($E$). To enable high-frequency resampling and correct potential artifacts from the GAN-based SR, low-strength noise is injected into $z_{SR_0}$ to obtain the noised SR latent ($z_{HR_t}$). The noise level ($\sigma_t$) is typically set in the range [0.1, 0.15].
- **Analysis:** The injected noise primarily targets high-frequency errors, as the global structure is already established. A formal characterization based on the posterior mean (Tweedie formula) shows that the refinement process combines the SR estimate with the prior. The Wiener gain, which determines the weight of the SR estimate, is high for low-frequency (high SNR) and low for high-frequency (low SNR). A lower bound for the noise level ($\sigma_t^* = \sqrt{\lambda_{hf}} / (1 + \sqrt{\lambda_{hf}})$) is derived, where $\lambda_{hf}$ is the power of the clean latent's high-frequency band. This ensures that the injected noise amplitude is not lower than the clean signal in high-frequency directions, allowing the high-resolution prior to correct potential SR errors. Measurements show $\sigma_t^* \approx 0.075$ for high-frequency bands, justifying the empirical choice of [0.1, 0.15].

#### 3.4. High-Resolution Detail Refinement

- **Formulation:** The noised high-resolution latent ($z_{HR_t}$) serves as the initial value for a final high-resolution sampling step, typically using $K_H=1$ Euler step, to produce the refined clean latent ($z_{HR_0}$). This latent is then decoded by the VAE decoder ($D$) to yield the final high-quality image ($x_{HR}$).
- **Analysis:** The single-step sufficiency of this stage is explained by the characteristics of the velocity field near the clean image endpoint (t=0). Under low-strength noising, the trajectory segment is "straighter," meaning the velocity field magnitude is flatter, and adjacent-step differences are smaller. This results in a lower single-step Euler discretization error. Numerical experiments confirm that with $\sigma_t = 0.1$, a single step achieves a CLIP consistency of 0.9974 relative to an 8-step reference, very close to higher step counts. This phenomenon is consistent with observations in other diffusion models that schedule segments near the clean endpoint have low cost. The overall pipeline's division of labor is confirmed by seed-sensitivity experiments, showing that low-resolution sampling primarily determines low-frequency structure, while high-resolution refinement mainly impacts high-frequency details.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of MrFlow across various metrics and model backbones.

#### 4.1. Training-Free Acceleration Performance

- **Speedup and Quality:** On FLUX.1-dev and Qwen-Image-20B (generating 1024x1024 images), MrFlow consistently outperforms other training-free acceleration strategies.
 - With a configuration of 20 low-resolution steps and 1 high-resolution step, MrFlow achieved a speedup of approximately 5.78x on FLUX and 6.98x on Qwen-Image, while keeping generation quality (Geneval metric) within a 2% gap relative to the native 50-step inference.
 - An aggressive configuration of 12 low-resolution steps and 1 high-resolution step further pushed speedup to 8.25x on FLUX and 10.3x on Qwen-Image. Even at this speed, MrFlow maintained Geneval scores of 0.63 (FLUX) and 0.86 (Qwen-Image), with OneIG-Bench loss within a 1% gap to the native models. This performance significantly surpassed other training-free methods like Teacache, DB-Taylor, RALU, and SPEED, which exhibited substantial quality degradation or collapse at comparable speedups.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Token-pruning (ToMA):** Achieved minimal speedup (<1.1x) with noticeable image collapse despite stable metrics.
 - **Feature-caching (Teacache, DB-Taylor):** Performed well around 4x speedup but suffered significant collapse at 8x or 9x, especially on Qwen-Image.
 - **Other Multi-resolution (RALU, SPEED):** Showed noticeable accuracy degradation at higher speedups (around 8-10x), with visual inspection revealing blurring or artifacts. MrFlow demonstrated superior perceptual fidelity.

#### 4.2. Combination with Training-Dependent Methods

- MrFlow can be orthogonally combined with pre-trained timestep distillation strategies without additional training, leading to even higher acceleration.
 - When combined with Pi-Flow (MrFlow†), it achieved a speedup of 11.3x on FLUX and 25.1x on Qwen-Image. Notably, on Qwen-Image, this combination maintained OneIG-Bench loss within 1% of native inference while significantly exceeding the speedup of native Pi-Flow.
 - Combining with FLUX-schnell (MrFlow‡) yielded 19.5x on FLUX, comparable to native FLUX-schnell but with slightly higher DPG.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Step Configurations:** Trade-off curves showed MrFlow consistently outperforming naive acceleration strategies across various low-resolution (10, 12, 16, 20 steps) and high-resolution (1, 2, 3 steps) configurations. Increasing low-resolution steps significantly improved performance, while additional high-resolution refinement steps beyond one yielded only marginal changes, supporting the single-step refinement design.
- **Super-Resolution Network:** Comparing different SR methods (Interpolate, SwinIR, OSEDiff, Real-ESRGAN) revealed that GAN-based Real-ESRGAN offered the best balance between clarity, semantic accuracy, and efficiency. While automatic metrics were often close, visual inspection showed interpolation and SwinIR remained blurry, and OSEDiff introduced character inaccuracies, underscoring the importance of GAN-based SR for effective high-frequency detail provision.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis (Runtime Breakdown)

- A stage-wise runtime breakdown for MrFlow (12, 1 configuration) on Qwen-Image (total 4.77s) confirmed that the dominant costs were low-resolution sampling (3.24s) and high-resolution refinement (1.03s). Fixed overheads like VAE operations and Real-ESRGAN were minimal (around 0.30s), demonstrating that the acceleration primarily comes from converting expensive high-resolution sampling steps into cheaper low-resolution ones.

#### 4.5. Generalizability to Other Models

- Extended experiments on FLUX.2 Klein (4B and 9B variants) and Z-Image (Base and Turbo) showed similar trends, confirming MrFlow's applicability to newer flow-matching backbones and their distilled variants, maintaining high speedups and quality across different model scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MrFlow represents a notable advancement in the field of diffusion model acceleration due to several key aspects:

1. **Training-Free High-Speedup:** The ability to achieve over 10x end-to-end acceleration without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning is a significant contribution. This eliminates the substantial computational resources and time typically associated with training-dependent acceleration methods, making diffusion models more accessible and cost-effective for users and developers.
2. **Quality Preservation:** Unlike many existing training-free acceleration methods that suffer from noticeable quality degradation at high speedups, MrFlow demonstrates consistent generation quality, often within a 1% gap on complex metrics like OneIG-Bench, compared to native, slower inference. This indicates that its acceleration does not come at the expense of fidelity or semantic coherence.
3. **Robustness and Simplicity:** The proposed staged sampling pipeline avoids reliance on runtime dynamic region identification or complex hardware-specific optimizations. Its design, incorporating pixel-space super-resolution and targeted low-strength noise injection, addresses the artifact and blurring issues present in prior multi-resolution approaches. This makes MrFlow a robust and relatively simple-to-implement solution.
4. **Flexibility and Orthogonal Combinability:** MrFlow offers flexibility in trading off speed and quality through configurable low-resolution step counts. Furthermore, its demonstrated ability to combine orthogonally with existing pre-trained timestep distillation models (achieving up to 25x speedup) indicates its potential as a foundational acceleration component that can synergize with other techniques for even greater efficiency gains.
5. **Broad Applicability:** The effectiveness across different advanced flow-matching backbones (FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image, FLUX.2 Klein, Z-Image) suggests that MrFlow is a generalizable strategy, enhancing its potential impact across the diverse ecosystem of text-to-image models.

The potential impact of MrFlow is significant for various applications requiring fast and high-quality image generation, including creative content generation, rapid prototyping, gaming asset creation, and more. By significantly reducing inference latency without compromising quality or requiring costly re-training, MrFlow contributes to making advanced text-to-image diffusion models more practical, efficient, and widely deployable in real-world scenarios. It also provides a new direction for future research in training-free multi-resolution diffusion acceleration.
