# Inference-Time Enhancement of Generative Robot Policies via Predictive World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harvard University developed Generative Predictive Control (GPC), a framework that enhances pretrained behavior cloning robot policies at inference time through a learned predictive world model and online planning. This approach improves policy robustness and adaptability by providing foresight for error correction, yielding up to a 25% improvement in IoU over baseline diffusion policies on vision-based tasks and achieving a 0.8 success rate on real-world pushing.

## Problem

- Behavior cloning (BC) policies are often brittle at deployment, lacking explicit mechanisms for test-time correction or recovery from out-of-distribution states.
- Cumulative errors and performance degradation occur when BC policies encounter deviations from the training distribution, as they rely solely on learned past behaviors.
- Integrating traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC)'s look-ahead planning with modern generative policies is challenging due to MPC's reliance on carefully engineered models and objectives.

## Method

- The **Generative Predictive Control (GPC)** framework combines a pretrained ==diffusion-based behavior cloning== policy trained on expert demos for short-horizon action chunks, with a learned ==action-conditioned world model== and lightweight ==online planning==.
- An ==action-conditioned diffusion world model==, trained on both expert and random exploration data, predicts future visual observations to evaluate candidate actions, using a ==frozen-noise mechanism== for stable gradient computation.
- Online planning strategies (**GPC-RANK** for selecting best actions from proposals, and **GPC-OPT** for gradient-based refinement) utilize a ==reward model== (including Vision-Language Models) to enhance policy output at inference time.

## Results

- **GPC** improved performance over BC baselines, achieving up to **0.934 IoU** (vs. **0.812**) on state-based pushing tasks, and a **25%** IoU gain (**0.882** vs. **0.642**) on vision-based tasks via GPC-RANK+OPT, albeit with higher inference cost.
- The framework demonstrated robust operation in real-world environments, achieving a **0.8 success rate** for real-world Push-T tasks and superior performance in clothes folding, surpassing the BC baseline's **0.5 success rate**.
- The diffusion-based visual world model generated visually realistic and physics-accurate future predictions, achieving an average **SSIM of 0.983** for the Push-T task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frozen behavior-cloning policies can be augmented at inference time with MPC-style foresight from learned world models, without retraining; combining the policy's generative prior with model-based planning's evaluative foresight outperforms planning-only or policy-only approaches.
> - Training the world model with additional random exploration data is crucial for enriching its learned dynamics, enabling it to predict consequences of non-expert actions and support corrective behaviors.
> - The "frozen-noise" mechanism stabilizes gradient-based optimization within stochastic diffusion world models, making continuous action refinement feasible and robust.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of robot policy learning, generative models, and model predictive control (MPC). Behavior cloning (BC) with generative models has become a prominent paradigm for training robot policies to imitate expert demonstrations and generalize across diverse manipulation tasks. Diffusion models, in particular, have been widely adopted for representing policies due to their ability to model complex action distributions. However, a known limitation of standard BC policies is their brittleness at deployment; they often lack explicit mechanisms for test-time correction, leading to performance degradation when encountering deviations from the training distribution.

In contrast, model predictive control (MPC) offers robustness through explicit look-ahead planning, where candidate actions are evaluated by simulating their future consequences under a predictive dynamics model. While effective for online adaptation, traditional MPC often relies on carefully engineered models and objectives, making its direct integration with modern generative policies challenging.

The paper aims to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It addresses the challenge of endowing pretrained, frozen BC policies with MPC-style foresight and test-time adaptability, without requiring retraining or fine-tuning of the policy itself. This work contributes to the area of inference-time planning methods that enhance existing policies through imagined rollouts in learned world models.

The proposed Generative Predictive Control (GPC) framework distinguishes itself from related work in several ways. Unlike methods that train learning-based policies within imagined world models (e.g., IRIS, Dreamer-v3, TDMPC-2) or those that focus on latent-space optimization during inference (e.g., IRIS, LaDi-WM, Dino-WM), GPC employs an explicit, action-conditioned diffusion world model that directly predicts future observations in image space. This direct visual prediction facilitates interpretable evaluation of predicted outcomes. Furthermore, GPC introduces a "frozen-noise" inference mechanism for stable gradient-based optimization within the diffusion world model and unifies both action proposal ranking and refinement strategies. It also incorporates vision-language models (VLMs) as direct reward surrogates, expanding its applicability. This modular design, decoupling policy and world model learning, allows for independent training and potentially different datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and adaptability of pretrained, frozen behavior cloning (BC) policies at deployment time, without engaging in costly retraining or fine-tuning processes. The authors are motivated by the inherent brittleness of standard BC policies, which often struggle with out-of-distribution states or cumulative errors during execution, as they lack mechanisms for self-correction or recovery.

Specifically, the paper seeks to answer the question: "Can we endow pretrained, frozen BC policies with test-time adaptability by incorporating MPC-style foresight through learned world models—without retraining or fine-tuning the policy itself?" This objective is inspired by the human ability to combine retrospective experience (akin to BC's reliance on past demonstrations) with prospective mental simulation (analogous to MPC's look-ahead planning).

The authors aim to develop a lightweight inference-time framework that unifies these two modes of reasoning. This framework, termed Generative Predictive Control (GPC), is designed to be adaptive and interpretable by combining the generative flexibility of BC policies with the predictive foresight offered by learned world models. The motivation extends to enabling robots to generalize and perform more reliably in dynamic and uncertain environments, mitigating the common issue of performance degradation in real-world deployment for policies trained solely on expert demonstrations.

To achieve this, the paper outlines three core objectives for the GPC framework:
1. **Develop a generative policy training component:** Train a diffusion-based policy on expert demonstrations to provide a generative prior of plausible, short-horizon action chunks conditioned on past observations. This policy serves as the foundation to be enhanced.
2. **Establish a predictive world modeling component:** Learn an action-conditioned world model capable of forecasting future observations given candidate action chunks. A key motivation here is to overcome the limitations of world models trained solely on expert data, which may be too narrow to support corrective actions. Thus, the world model is intended to be trained on both expert demonstrations and supplementary random exploration data to enrich its learned dynamics and facilitate robust predictions, even for non-expert behaviors.
3. **Implement online planning strategies for inference-time enhancement:** Design lightweight planning mechanisms that utilize the pretrained generative policy and the learned world model to rank and/or refine action proposals. These strategies should enable real-time adaptation and improvement over raw policy outputs. The motivation is to provide flexible options, including simple ranking (for broad applicability) and gradient-based optimization (for continuous refinement with differentiable rewards).

Overall, the central motivation is to create a modular, effective, and computationally feasible approach to enhance the robustness and performance of pre-trained robot policies in real-world deployment scenarios by integrating predictive reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Generative Predictive Control (GPC) framework is comprised of three primary modules: Generative Policy Training, Predictive World Modeling, and Online Planning.

#### 3.1. Generative Policy Training

The first component involves training a behavior cloning policy using a diffusion model.
- **Data:** Expert demonstrations, denoted as $D_P^{expert} = \{I_t^i \leftrightarrow a_{t:t+T}^i\}_{i=1}^{N_{expert}}$, where $I_t$ represents the information vector (history of states or visual observations) and $a_{t:t+T}$ is an action chunk of length $T+1$.
- **Policy Type:** A diffusion-based policy framework, specifically following the Diffusion Policy learning approach. This involves training a network to parameterize the score function of $p(a_{t:t+T}|I_t)$, enabling the gradual denoising of Gaussian noise vectors into expert action chunks.
- **Architecture (Vision-based):** A ResNet18 visual encoder processes visual observations, and a UNet diffusion backbone models the action distribution.
- **Training Details:** The policy is trained using a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) formulation for 300 epochs with the AdamW optimizer (learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-6}$). Inference involves 100 diffusion denoising steps.
- **Temporal Abstraction:** An observation horizon $H=2$, a prediction horizon $T=16$, and an action horizon of 9 are used, with control executed in a receding-horizon manner. Images are resized to 96 pixels in height.

#### 3.2. Predictive World Modeling

This module focuses on learning an action-conditioned dynamics model that forecasts future observations.
- **Purpose:** To predict the future sequence of observations $I_{t+1:t+T+1}$ given the current information $I_t$ and a proposed action chunk $a_{t:t+T}$. This model aids in evaluating the consequences of action proposals.
- **Data:** The world model is trained on a combined dataset $D_W = D_W^{expert} \cup D_W^{explore}$.
 - $D_W^{expert}$: Consists of pairs $(I_t^i, a_{t:t+T}^i) \leftrightarrow I_{t+1:t+T+1}^i$ derived from expert demonstrations, similar to the policy training data.
 - $D_W^{explore}$: Crucially, this dataset is augmented with random exploration trajectories where human operators (or other controllers) perturb the system without necessarily solving tasks. This inclusion aims to enrich the learned dynamics and enable the world model to predict outcomes beyond expert behavior, facilitating corrective actions.
- **Architecture (State-based tasks):** For tasks where $I_t$ is a low-dimensional state, Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) are employed to learn the dynamics.
- **Architecture (Vision-based tasks):** For visual observations, a conditional diffusion model is used to build a visual world model.
 - **Recursive Single-step Prediction:** The visual world model is designed as a recursive application of single-step image predictors $o_{t+1} = f_{vision}(o_{t-H:t}, a_t)$. Each $f_{vision}$ is itself a conditional diffusion model that iteratively denoises a noisy image sample.
 - **Denoiser:** The denoiser $D_\phi$ within $f_{vision}$ is a neural network with convolutions, action embedding, and a U-Net architecture. It is trained to predict the noise added to clean images.
 - **"Freeze the Noise" Mechanism:** During inference for GPC-OPT (gradient-based optimization), the initial noise $o_{N_d}^{t+1}$ in the diffusion process is fixed to zero. This makes the world model deterministic, generating the most likely future prediction, which is critical for stable gradient computation during action optimization.
- **Two-phase Training (Visual World Model):**
 - **Phase One:** Train the single-step image predictor $f_{vision}$ with supervision from only a single future image $o_{t+1}$.
 - **Phase Two:** Recursively apply the single-step predictor for $T$ times to generate a sequence of future images $o_{t+1:t+T+1}$, and jointly supervise this sequence with ground truth images.
 - **Parameters:** Observation horizon $H=4$, $N_d=3$ diffusion steps, using an EDM-based diffusion model for output quality.

#### 3.3. Online Planning

GPC employs two complementary online planning strategies: GPC-RANK and GPC-OPT, which can also be combined. Both strategies assume access to a reward model $R(\cdot): I_{t+1:t+T+1} \rightarrow r_t$ that predicts reward based on future state information. Rewards can be learned via a small neural network or provided zero-shot by a Vision-Language Model (VLM).

- **GPC-RANK:**
 - **Mechanism:** Samples $K$ action proposals from the pretrained policy $P(I_t)$. Each proposal is unrolled through the world model $W(\cdot)$ to predict its future outcome. The action chunk corresponding to the highest predicted reward $R(W(I_t, a_{t:t+T}^{(k)}))$ is selected for execution.
 - **Characteristics:** Simple, easily parallelizable, requires minimal hyperparameter tuning, and accommodates non-differentiable or VLM-based rewards.

- **GPC-OPT:**
 - **Mechanism:** Aims to directly optimize the action chunk to maximize reward $R(W(I_t, a_{t:t+T}))$. To address the non-convexity of this problem, it warm-starts the optimization with a single action proposal $\hat{a}_{t:t+T}$ sampled from $P(I_t)$.
 - **Refinement:** This warm-started action is then refined through $M$ gradient-based optimization steps, using automatic differentiation to compute gradients of the reward through the world model ($\nabla_{a_{t:t+T}} R(W(I_t, \hat{a}_{t:t+T})))$. Optimizers like ADAM can be used.
 - **Characteristics:** Resembles the classical single shooting method, enables continuous action refinement, and is particularly effective for tasks with reliable numerical rewards where gradients are meaningful.

- **GPC-RANK+OPT:** This combined strategy involves sampling $K$ action proposals from $P(\cdot)$, applying $M$ gradient optimization steps to each, and then selecting the best of these $K$ optimized action chunks based on their predicted rewards. This explores a broader search space but at increased computational cost ($K \times M$ world model evaluations).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GPC framework was evaluated across a range of robot manipulation tasks, encompassing both state-based and vision-based scenarios, in simulation and on real hardware. The core finding is that GPC consistently enhanced the performance of pretrained behavior cloning policies compared to the standalone baseline.

#### 4.1. State-based Planar Pushing in Simulation

- **Task:** Pushing an object from an initial to a target pose, with low-dimensional state information.
- **World Model:** MLPs were used for state prediction.
- **Reward:** Defined by a differentiable registration loss (ℓ2 distance) between predicted and target object poses.
- **Results (Table I):** All GPC variants (GPC-RANK, GPC-OPT, GPC-RANK+OPT) consistently outperformed the pure behavior cloning baseline (scoring 0.812 IoU). GPC-RANK with K=5000 achieved 0.934 IoU, while GPC-OPT with K=1 and M=30 achieved 0.886. The combined GPC-RANK+OPT (K=20, M=30) reached 0.914 IoU. Notably, the best GPC variant approached the performance of planning with a groundtruth simulator (0.952 IoU), indicating effective learned world modeling.

#### 4.2. Vision-based Tasks in Simulation

- **Tasks:** Four vision-based tasks: Push-T, Triangle Drawing, Block Stacking, and Cube & Sphere Swapping, utilizing high-dimensional visual observations.
- **Policy:** Vision-based diffusion policy (ResNet18, UNet).
- **World Model:** Diffusion-based visual world model, recursively applying a single-step image predictor.
- **Quality of Visual World Modeling (Fig. 3, Fig. 4):** The diffusion-based world model generated visually realistic future predictions with accurate object interactions. Quantitative evaluation using Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) showed that diffusion-based approaches (GPC's world model and AVDC) outperformed a CNN/LSTM-based baseline (Deep Visual Foresight), with GPC's recursive model producing predictions closely aligned with ground-truth. The average SSIM for GPC's world model across 5 evaluation seeds was 0.983 for Push-T, demonstrating high visual fidelity.
- **Reward Strategies:**
 - **Learned Reward Predictor:** A ResNet18+MLP network was trained for tasks with numerically definable rewards (e.g., registration loss for Push-T, cube distance for Block Stacking).
 - **VLM as Reward Surrogate:** For tasks where numerical rewards were difficult to define, a VLM (ChatGPT-4o) was used zero-shot to select the most promising action proposal by evaluating predicted image sequences against a task description.
- **Planning Performance (Table II):** GPC-RANK, using either a learned reward predictor (with K=100 candidates) or a VLM (with K=10 candidates), achieved strong performance across all four vision-based tasks. It consistently outperformed the Diffusion Policy baseline (BC) and compared favorably to other inference-time enhancement methods such as V-GPS, LaDi-WM, and DreamerV3. For Push-T, GPC-RANK (learned reward) scored 0.739 IoU compared to Diffusion Policy's 0.642.
- **Ablation Studies (Vision-based Push-T, Table III):**
 - **Impact of K and M:** Increasing the number of action proposals (K) in GPC-RANK and gradient steps (M) in GPC-OPT or GPC-RANK+OPT consistently improved performance. GPC-RANK (K=100) improved by ~10% over BC, GPC-OPT (M=25) yielded ~15% gain, and GPC-RANK+OPT (K=10, M=25) achieved up to ~25% improvement (0.882 IoU vs 0.642 IoU). However, this came with increased inference-time cost (e.g., GPC-RANK+OPT took 374.102s per decision cycle compared to BC's 0.457s).
 - **Importance of Generative Prior:** Planning-only methods (MPPI, CEM, pure gradient ascent) without the diffusion policy warm-start exhibited significantly lower success rates (below 0.2) on vision-based Push-T, underscoring the necessity of integrating a generative prior.
 - **Importance of Random Exploration (Fig. 5):** World models trained with additional random exploration data consistently led to about a 10% performance improvement for both GPC-RANK and GPC-OPT variants. This highlights the value of collecting diverse data to capture robust dynamics for corrective predictions.

#### 4.3. Real-world Vision-based Tasks

- **Tasks:** Real-world Push-T and Clothes Folding.
- **Rewards:** For real-world Push-T, rewards were derived from learned registration losses using AprilTags-based state information from expert demos. For clothes folding, a progressing reward (increasing with successful grasp-and-move operations) was designed and used to train a reward predictor.
- **Results (Fig. 8):** GPC demonstrated effective operation in real-world environments, which involve complex dynamics and non-rigid objects. For real-world Push-T, GPC-RANK (K=10, M=0) achieved a 0.8 success rate, surpassing the BC baseline (0.5 success rate). For Clothes Folding, GPC-RANK also showed superior performance. The policy and world model were fully vision-based during inference, although low-level state information was optionally used for reward predictor training.

In summary, GPC successfully enhanced frozen BC policies across diverse settings. GPC-RANK offered simplicity and broad applicability, while GPC-OPT provided continuous refinement, and GPC-RANK+OPT explored the full potential at a higher computational cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Generative Predictive Control (GPC) framework represents a significant advancement in enhancing the robustness and adaptability of robot policies without requiring extensive retraining. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that pretrained, frozen behavior cloning policies, especially those based on diffusion models, can be effectively augmented at inference time by coupling them with a learned predictive world model and lightweight online planning.

The significance of this work can be understood through several key aspects:
1. **Bridging Policy Learning and Control:** GPC effectively unifies the strengths of generative behavior cloning (for learning complex, diverse behaviors from demonstrations) with the foresight and adaptive capabilities of model predictive control (for online error correction and robustness). This hybrid approach addresses a critical limitation of traditional behavior cloning, which often struggles with distributional shifts or cumulative errors in dynamic environments.
2. **Inference-Time Adaptability:** By operating solely at inference time, GPC offers a computationally efficient way to improve policy performance without the need for costly data collection, retraining, or fine-tuning of the base policy. This is particularly valuable in real-world robotics, where data acquisition can be expensive and retraining time-consuming. It allows for immediate adaptation to unforeseen circumstances or deviations.
3. **Modular and Flexible Design:** The decoupled nature of policy learning and world model learning enhances the modularity of GPC. This allows both components to be trained independently, potentially on different datasets, which increases flexibility and reduces interdependencies. The framework's ability to incorporate various reward mechanisms, including learned differentiable rewards and zero-shot VLM-based rewards, further broadens its applicability to tasks where explicit reward engineering might be challenging.
4. **Robust Visual World Modeling:** The employment of diffusion-based visual world models demonstrates a capability to generate high-fidelity, physics-accurate future predictions from raw visual observations. This is crucial for vision-based control, enabling robust planning in complex visual environments. The "freeze the noise" mechanism is a practical innovation that stabilizes gradient-based optimization within stochastic generative models, making GPC-OPT feasible.
5. **Empirical Validation across Diverse Tasks:** The consistent performance gains observed across state-based and vision-based tasks, in both simulation and real-world manipulation, underscore the generalizability and effectiveness of GPC. Its ability to handle non-rigid objects like clothes in real-world scenarios further highlights its practical utility.

The potential impact of GPC is substantial for the field of robotics:
- **More Robust Robot Deployment:** GPC could lead to the deployment of more reliable and adaptable robots in real-world settings, reducing instances of policy failure due to unexpected environmental changes or execution errors.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By minimizing the need for policy retraining or fine-tuning, GPC could lower the development and maintenance costs of robotic systems, making advanced manipulation capabilities more accessible.
- **Enhanced Autonomy:** The framework's ability to correct and adapt in real-time contributes to higher levels of robot autonomy, allowing robots to operate effectively in less structured and more dynamic environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** GPC provides a foundation for future work on accelerating world model inference (e.g., via diffusion distillation or hardware acceleration), integrating more complex reasoning, and exploring diverse combinations of generative policies and predictive models.

While GPC has a limitation regarding inference-time cost, especially for diffusion-based world model rollouts, the authors acknowledge this and suggest future directions for efficiency improvements. Overall, GPC represents a significant step towards enabling robots to combine learned skills with proactive foresight, fostering more capable and resilient autonomous agents.
