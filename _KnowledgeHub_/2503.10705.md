# Enhanced Continual Learning of Vision-Language Models with Model Fusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and BIGAI introduce Continual Decoupling-Unifying (ConDU), a novel model fusion framework for continual learning of Vision-Language Models that mitigates catastrophic forgetting and enhances zero-shot capabilities. ConDU achieves 0.5-1.4% higher average accuracy and transfer scores than previous state-of-the-art methods in Multi-domain Task Incremental Learning while significantly reducing storage overhead.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer from catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned sequentially on multiple downstream tasks, degrading performance on previously learned knowledge.
- Existing VLM continual learning methods often rely on additional reference datasets, require extensive hyperparameter tuning, or are restricted to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), limiting their practical applicability.
- Many current approaches struggle to preserve or even enhance the VLM's crucial zero-shot generalization capabilities across continuously learned tasks.

## Method

- **ConDU** proposes a "Decoupling-Unifying" framework that continually fuses "delta models" (differences from a pre-trained VLM) for each new task.
- The framework iteratively fine-tunes a task-specific expert, computes its delta model, decouples previously unified delta models using task triggers, and then unifies all current delta models through an election process.
- For inference with unknown task IDs or zero-shot scenarios, a semantic-based aggregating mechanism combines predictions from selected task experts based on pre-trained VLM prototype similarities, eliminating the need for task-specific oracles.

## Results

- ConDU (FT) achieved an average transfer score of **70.8%** and average accuracy of **78.8%** in MTIL, surpassing the best baselines by 0.7-**1.4%** and 0.5-**1.0%** respectively, demonstrating improved zero-shot generalization and knowledge retention.
- In task-agnostic MTIL, ConDU (FT) achieved **78.1%** average accuracy and **86.4%** last task accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (DPeCLIP) by **2.0%** and **1.8%** respectively, confirming its robustness without task ID knowledge.
- ConDU significantly reduced storage overhead by over 4.9 GB for FT and 360 MB for LoRA in an 11-task scenario compared to storing individual models, while maintaining competitive training and inference times.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model fusion, specifically of delta models, can effectively serve as a continual learning strategy for VLMs, enabling knowledge consolidation without storing previous task data or requiring complex trade-off hyperparameters.
> - The decoupling and unifying mechanism, guided by task triggers (binary masks and rescalers), allows for accurate reconstruction of individual task experts from a single unified model.
> - Utilizing the frozen pre-trained VLM's features to compute prototypes for semantic aggregation at inference time provides a robust and generalizable approach for task identification and prediction in zero-shot or task-agnostic settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) frequently encounter the issue of "catastrophic forgetting," where sequentially learning new tasks significantly degrades performance on previously learned ones. Continual learning (or lifelong learning) aims to address this by enabling models to acquire new knowledge while retaining old.

Recently, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP have demonstrated robust zero-shot capabilities by integrating visual and textual modalities. However, despite their successes, VLMs are also susceptible to catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned sequentially on multiple downstream tasks. Traditional continual learning methods often fall short in VLM contexts, particularly in preserving the essential zero-shot capabilities.

Existing continual learning approaches for VLMs present several limitations:
1. **Reliance on Additional Data**: Some methods require supplementary reference datasets for knowledge distillation from pre-trained models. The effectiveness of these methods can be sensitive to the choice of the reference dataset.
2. **Hyperparameter Tuning**: Many approaches necessitate careful adjustment of multiple handcrafted hyperparameters to balance competing objectives: mitigating forgetting, preserving zero-shot performance, and optimizing current task performance.
3. **Limited Scope**: A subset of methods is restricted to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) paradigms, such as adapters or LoRA, and struggles to adapt to full fine-tuning scenarios.

This work introduces model fusion as a novel strategy to overcome these limitations within the continual learning paradigm for VLMs. Model fusion, a technique for combining multiple models into a single unified model without requiring original training data, is positioned as a suitable approach for sequential learning, allowing the maintenance of a single model that can represent multiple task-specific experts. The paper aims to develop a robust, flexible continual learning method that addresses the shortcomings of existing VLM-specific approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel continual learning approach for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that effectively acquires new knowledge from sequential tasks while simultaneously mitigating catastrophic forgetting, preserving, and ideally enhancing, the VLM's inherent zero-shot capabilities.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in current VLM continual learning methods:

1. **Catastrophic Forgetting in VLMs**: VLMs, despite their advanced zero-shot capabilities, suffer from catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned on a sequence of downstream tasks. This performance degradation on previous tasks limits their utility in dynamic, real-world environments requiring continuous adaptation.
2. **Inadequacy of Conventional Continual Learning for VLMs**: Standard continual learning techniques, developed for unimodal neural networks, often fail to maintain the unique and critical zero-shot generalization abilities of VLMs when directly applied to their sequential fine-tuning.
3. **Limitations of Existing VLM-specific CL Methods**:
 - **Data Dependency**: Many current VLM continual learning methods, particularly distillation-based ones, depend on external reference datasets. The performance of these methods can be highly sensitive to the quality and relevance of these datasets, posing a practical challenge.
 - **Hyperparameter Complexity**: Balancing the trade-offs between retaining old knowledge, learning new tasks, and preserving zero-shot capabilities often involves tuning numerous hyperparameters. This makes these methods complex to implement and optimize.
 - **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) Restriction**: Some methods are exclusively designed for PEFT modules (e.g., LoRA, adapters). While efficient, this limits their applicability to scenarios requiring full model fine-tuning or where full model capacity is desired.
 - **Degradation of Zero-Shot Transfer**: A key motivation is to prevent the degradation of zero-shot capabilities, which are fundamental to the value of VLMs. Existing methods often struggle to maintain or improve this aspect.

To address these, the authors propose leveraging model fusion, a technique that allows combining multiple models into a single entity without requiring access to their original training data. This property is particularly appealing for continual learning, where access to past task data is often restricted. However, direct iterative application of model fusion can lead to performance degradation. Therefore, a key motivation is to design a refined "Decoupling-Unifying" framework that carefully integrates model fusion to circumvent these issues.

Furthermore, the research aims to develop a method that:
- Is compatible with both parameter-efficient and full fine-tuning paradigms, offering greater flexibility.
- Eliminates the need for adjusting trade-off hyperparameters, incorporating reference datasets, or maintaining replay exemplars, simplifying the continual learning process.
- Provides a robust inference strategy for both scenarios where the task ID is known and crucial zero-shot scenarios where it is unknown or belongs to an unseen task. The motivation here is that aggregating predictions from multiple specialized models for unseen tasks could outperform a single general model.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to unlock the full potential of VLMs for lifelong learning by providing a continually adaptable and high-performing solution that overcomes the current limitations in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, Continual Decoupling-Unifying (ConDU), introduces model fusion to address continual learning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). ConDU maintains a unified model along with task triggers and prototype sets throughout the continual learning process. The framework is divided into training and inference stages.

**A. DELTA MODELS CONTINUALLY FUSION AT TRAINING STAGE**

At each session `t` (representing a new task), ConDU follows a three-step iterative process:

1. **Tuning Individually**:
 - A pre-trained VLM, denoted `f(·; θ₀)`, is fine-tuned on the data for the current task `t` (`Dᵗ`). This results in a task-specific expert model `θt`.
 - The "delta model" for task `t`, `δt`, is then computed as the difference between the fine-tuned expert parameters and the pre-trained VLM parameters: `δt = θt - θ₀`. The process focuses on unifying these delta models, rather than the full models, aligning with advanced model fusion techniques.

2. **Decoupling Unified Model**:
 - Before unifying the current task's delta model, the *current unified delta model* (`δ₁:t-1`) is decoupled to retrieve approximations of the delta models for all previously seen tasks (`˜δ_i` for `i = 1..., t-1`).
 - This decoupling uses previously stored "task triggers" (`M_i` and `λ_i`). Specifically, each `˜δ_i` is reconstructed as `λ_i · M_i ⊙ δ₁:t-1`, where `⊙` denotes element-wise multiplication.
 - These reconstructed `˜δ_i` (along with the newly obtained `δt`) are then used in the subsequent unification step.

3. **Unifying Models**:
 - All available delta models (the reconstructed `˜δ_i` for `i=1..., t-1` and the new `δt`) are combined to form an updated unified delta model, `δ₁:t`.
 - The `j`-th dimension of `δ₁:t` is determined by an "election process":
 - If the sum of the `j`-th dimensions of all current delta models `(Pᵗᵢ=₁ δ_ij)` is positive, `δ₁:tj` is set to the maximum `j`-th value among all `δ_i`.
 - If the sum is negative, `δ₁:tj` is set to the minimum `j`-th value.
 - This strategy aims to retain the largest magnitude while preserving the dominant sign information across the delta models.
 - The unified model `θ₁:t` is then constructed by adding the unified delta model to the pre-trained VLM: `θ₁:t = θ₀ + δ₁:t`.
 - Crucially, `t` new **task triggers** are also generated for each task `i ∈ {1..., t}`:
 - A binary mask `M_i` is created, where `M_ij = 1` if `δ_ij` and `δ₁:tj` have the same sign, and `0` otherwise. This indicates which parameters of task `i` are aligned with the unified model.
 - A rescaling scalar `λ_i` is computed as `sum(abs(δ_i)) / sum(abs(M_i ⊙ δ₁:t))`. This rescaler ensures that the reconstructed `δ_i` preserves the average magnitude of the original `δ_i`.
 - These updated task triggers and the new unified model are stored for subsequent sessions.

The decoupling and unifying procedures are designed to be training-free, contributing to their efficiency compared to the fine-tuning step.

**B. SEMANTIC-BASED AGGREGATING MECHANISM AT INFERENCE STAGE**

ConDU supports multiple inference scenarios:

1. **Known Task ID**: If the test sample's task ID is known, the corresponding task expert `˜θ_i` is reconstructed from the unified model using its specific task triggers and `θ₀`, then used for prediction.

2. **Unknown Task ID / Zero-Shot Scenario**: For test samples from unseen tasks or when the task ID is not provided, ConDU employs a "Semantic-based Aggregating Mechanism":

 - **Computing Prototypes**: During the training stage, for each category `k` within each task `i`, a prototype `P_ik` is computed. This prototype is the mean of the image feature vectors (from `Dᵗk`) plus the text feature vector (for category `y`), all extracted using the *pre-trained VLM* (`f(·, θ₀)`). These prototypes are stored.

 - **Aggregating Predictions**:
 - For a test image `x`, its feature is extracted using the pre-trained VLM (`f(·, θ₀)`).
 - The cosine similarity between this test image feature and all stored prototypes `P_ik` is calculated.
 - For each task `i`, the highest similarity score obtained across its categories is selected as a candidate weight for task expert `i`.
 - The `K` task experts with the highest candidate weights are selected, and their weights are set to 1, while others are set to 0. (K is the only hyperparameter, shown to be insensitive via ablation).
 - The outputs (logits) from these `K` selected and decoupled task experts are then aggregated (summed with their respective binary weights) to produce the final prediction.

This approach ensures that ConDU is flexible, efficient, and addresses the critical need to maintain zero-shot capabilities in dynamic learning environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of ConDU across various continual learning benchmarks for VLMs, encompassing Multi-domain Task Incremental Learning (MTIL), task-agnostic MTIL, and few-shot MTIL.

**A. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods**

ConDU was evaluated against numerous state-of-the-art baselines, including Zero-shot, Individual FT, Continual FT, LwF, iCaRL, ZSCL, MoE, and others. Key metrics included "Transfer" (zero-shot transfer performance on subsequent tasks), "Average" (average accuracy across all seen tasks), and "Last" (average performance at the end of the continual learning process).

- **Multi-Domain Task Incremental Learning (MTIL)**:
 - ConDU (FT) achieved an average "Transfer" score of 70.8% and ConDU (LoRA) 70.3%. These figures surpass the best baseline (MulKI, Dual-RAIL, Primal-RAIL at 70.1%/69.4%) by 0.7-1.4% and significantly exceed the original pre-trained VLM (Zero-shot) by 5.5%. This indicates improved zero-shot generalization.
 - For the "Average" metric, ConDU (FT) reached 78.8% and ConDU (LoRA) 78.3%, outperforming the best baseline (Dual-RAIL, 77.8%) by 0.5-1.0%. This highlights ConDU's ability to retain knowledge across all tasks.
 - The "Last" metric showed ConDU (FT) at 87.1% and ConDU (LoRA) at 86.2%, surpassing the best baseline (DPeCLIP, 86.9%) by a smaller margin of 0.2% for ConDU (FT).
 - Experiments with an alternative task order also confirmed ConDU's superior performance.

- **Task-Agnostic MTIL**: In scenarios where task IDs are unknown during inference, ConDU continued to outperform baselines.
 - ConDU (FT) achieved an "Average" score of 78.1% (best baseline DPeCLIP, 76.1%).
 - ConDU (FT) achieved a "Last" score of 86.4% (best baseline DPeCLIP, 84.6%).
 - These results emphasize the robustness of ConDU's semantic-based aggregation mechanism without relying on task oracle information.

- **Few-Shot MTIL**: Even with limited training data (five samples per category), ConDU demonstrated strong performance.
 - ConDU (FT) achieved a "Transfer" score of 70.0% and ConDU (LoRA) 70.3%, exceeding the best baseline (Dual-RAIL, 69.4%) by 0.6-0.9%.
 - For the "Average" metric, ConDU (LoRA) reached 72.7% and ConDU (FT) 72.3%, outperforming the best baseline (MoE, 71.4%) by 0.9-1.3%.
 - The "Last" metric showed ConDU (LoRA) at 77.4% and ConDU (FT) at 76.6%, surpassing the best baseline (MoE, 76.1%) by 0.5-1.3%. In few-shot settings, ConDU (LoRA) occasionally surpassed ConDU (FT), attributed to reduced overfitting with limited data.

**B. Ablation Studies**

- **PTM vs. Task Expert Features for Prototypes**: Using the frozen Pre-Trained Model (PTM) features for prototype-sample similarity calculation consistently outperformed using task-specific expert features. This suggests that the PTM provides a more unified and reliable representation for cross-task semantic matching.
- **The Effect of Rescalers**: The rescalers (`λ_i`) in task triggers were found to be critical. Eliminating them led to substantial performance degradation, confirming their role in maintaining the magnitude alignment of reconstructed delta models.
- **Choice of K in Aggregating Predictions**: The performance of the semantic-based aggregation mechanism was relatively insensitive to the choice of `K` (the number of highest-scoring task experts to aggregate). This indicates robustness of the selection process. Directly using the unified model for unseen tasks performed significantly worse, validating the aggregation mechanism.

**C. Other Analyses**

- **Hardware Robustness**: Experiments on both NVIDIA RTX 4090 and Huawei Ascend 910B showed negligible performance discrepancy, indicating hardware independence.
- **Computational Cost and Storage**:
 - **Parameters**: ConDU (LoRA) required significantly fewer learnable parameters than SOTA methods while maintaining superior performance. ConDU (FT) was comparable in parameter count to other full-parameter update methods.
 - **Storage**: ConDU significantly reduced storage overhead compared to storing individual task-specific models (e.g., saving over 4.9 GB for FT and 360 MB for LoRA in an 11-task scenario), with efficiency increasing with more tasks and fine-tunable parameters.
 - **Time**: Training time was competitive with `Continual FT` and 62% faster than `ZSCL`. Inference time was comparable to a single model, with negligible overhead from model selection and parallel aggregation.
- **t-SNE Visualization**: Visualizations of feature spaces showed that task experts reconstructed by ConDU retained their discriminative abilities close to their initial fine-tuned state, even after multiple unification and decoupling rounds.
- **Convergence of Delta Models (Theorem 1)**: Theoretical analysis suggests that the change in each task expert parameter is monotonically non-increasing as the number of sessions approaches infinity, implying stability and convergence of the delta models.
- **Accuracy During Sessions**: Performance across previous tasks showed minimal degradation even when encountering highly Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) tasks like MNIST, demonstrating ConDU's robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ConDU represents a notable contribution to the field of continual learning for Vision-Language Models, offering several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Novel Paradigm for VLM Continual Learning**: ConDU pioneers the integration of model fusion into continual learning specifically for VLMs. This introduces a new methodological direction, moving beyond traditional replay, regularization, or architecture-based methods, and offering a distinct way to manage knowledge acquisition and retention.

2. **Addressing Key Practical Limitations**: The method directly tackles several practical challenges associated with existing VLM continual learning approaches. By eliminating the reliance on additional reference datasets, removing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning for trade-off objectives, and avoiding the storage of replay exemplars, ConDU simplifies the deployment and management of continually learning VLMs in real-world scenarios.

3. **Enhanced Zero-Shot Capabilities**: A critical finding is that ConDU not only preserves but *enhances* the zero-shot capabilities of VLMs relative to the original pre-trained model. This is a substantial improvement over many existing methods that often suffer from zero-shot degradation during continual learning. The ability to generalize to unseen tasks is a core strength of VLMs, and ConDU's capacity to boost this trait makes it particularly valuable.

4. **Flexibility Across Fine-Tuning Paradigms**: ConDU's compatibility with both parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and full fine-tuning (FT) paradigms expands its applicability. This versatility allows researchers and practitioners to choose the appropriate fine-tuning strategy based on computational resources, data availability, and desired performance trade-offs, without needing to switch to an entirely different continual learning framework.

5. **Efficiency in Storage and Computation**: The method significantly reduces storage overhead compared to maintaining separate models for each task, a crucial factor for deploying large VLMs in resource-constrained environments. It also maintains competitive training times and achieves inference speeds comparable to a single model, demonstrating practical efficiency.

6. **Theoretical Foundations**: The inclusion of a theoretical proof regarding the convergence of delta models adds a foundational layer to the empirical successes. This theoretical backing provides insights into the stability and long-term behavior of the ConDU framework.

The potential impact of ConDU is broad. It could accelerate the development of more adaptable and robust AI systems capable of continuous learning from dynamic data streams without suffering from catastrophic forgetting. This is particularly relevant for applications leveraging VLMs in evolving domains such as medical imaging analysis, autonomous driving, personalized content recommendation, and general-purpose AI assistants, where continuous adaptation and strong generalization are paramount. ConDU's ability to maintain and improve zero-shot transfer further positions it as a significant step towards truly lifelong learning systems that are both efficient and performant.
