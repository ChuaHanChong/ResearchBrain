# TypeTele: Releasing Dexterity in Teleoperation by Dexterous Manipulation Types

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sun Yat-sen University and Stanford University developed TypeTele, a type-guided dexterous teleoperation system that uses a library of manipulation types and MLLM-assisted retrieval to enable robots to fully utilize their dexterity. The system significantly improves task success rates and collects higher-quality demonstration data for training autonomous policies, especially for tasks unachievable by traditional hand retargeting.

## Problem

- Traditional hand retargeting constrains dexterous robots to human-feasible motions, preventing them from fully utilizing their intrinsic capabilities and unique morphology.
- Morphological differences between human and robotic hands often lead to unstable, suboptimal, or self-colliding retargeted poses, hindering fine manipulation and resulting in poor quality demonstration data.
- Existing teleoperation systems struggle to provide the high-quality, stable, and diverse expert demonstration data crucial for effectively training robust autonomous manipulation policies via imitation learning.

## Method

- **TypeTele** is built around a hierarchical ==Dexterous Manipulation Type Library==, including both general and robot-exclusive grasp types, each annotated with semantic attributes and defining 'stretched' and 'contracted' postures.
- An ==MLLM-assisted== module (using GPT-4o) was integrated to interpret user voice commands and visual input, infer task attributes, and autonomously retrieve the most suitable manipulation type from the library.
- An ==interpolation mapping strategy== was designed to intuitively control the robotic hand within the bounds of a selected manipulation type by projecting human hand movements onto the robot's predefined stretched and contracted postures.

## Results

- **TypeTele** significantly improved data collection efficiency, achieving higher success rates (e.g., **95.2%** vs. **60.6%** for 'Collect and Store') and shorter average demonstration durations compared to a retargeting baseline.
- The system successfully executed complex tasks previously unachievable by retargeting methods (**0%** success), such as 'Use Scissors' (**91.1%** success) and 'Use a Heavy Kettle' (**85.0%** success).
- Autonomous policies trained on TypeTele's collected data showed consistently superior performance, achieving much higher success rates (e.g., **10/10** vs. **3/10** for 'Collect and Store') than those trained with baseline data.
- A user study confirmed improved usability, with TypeTele achieving an **88%** task success rate compared to the baseline's **20%**, alongside higher subjective ratings for accuracy, ease of use, and user confidence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Moving beyond direct human hand mimicry by leveraging predefined 'dexterous manipulation types' allows robotic hands to execute actions and postures optimal for their unique morphology, including those infeasible for humans.
> - Integrating multi-modal large language models with a structured manipulation type library enables intelligent, context-aware selection of appropriate robot actions, simplifying complex teleoperation tasks for operators.
> - Mapping human input to an interpolation range defined by robot-specific 'stretched' and 'contracted' postures for each manipulation type significantly enhances control stability and effectiveness compared to direct joint-to-joint retargeting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is advancing rapidly, particularly with the advent of learning-based methods that rely on large-scale datasets. Dexterous robots, equipped with multi-fingered hands, are increasingly capable of performing intricate and diverse tasks. Teleoperation serves as a foundational tool within this landscape, primarily for two critical functions: collecting high-quality real-world robotic demonstration data and enabling remote control of robots in challenging or distant environments.

Previous approaches to dexterous teleoperation have largely focused on "hand retargeting." This paradigm involves capturing human hand postures, often using motion capture devices, and then mapping these poses to the robotic hand. The goal is typically to preserve the spatial relationships between keypoints (such as fingertips) of the human hand and their robotic counterparts, often through optimization or inverse dynamics techniques.

However, the paper identifies several inherent limitations within this retargeting paradigm. First, it fundamentally constrains the robotic hand to motions and postures that are physically feasible for human hands. This restriction prevents dexterous robots, which often possess structural advantages and greater degrees of freedom, from fully utilizing their intrinsic capabilities to perform actions unique to their design, such as specific non-anthropomorphic grasps that might be more stable or efficient for certain objects. Second, significant morphological differences between human and robotic hands frequently lead to suboptimal or unstable retargeted poses. This can manifest as unstable grasps, self-collisions between robot fingers, or undesirable contact points that hinder fine manipulation and object stability. These issues contribute to lower success rates, increased task completion times, and reduced quality of collected demonstration data.

Within the broader context of dexterous manipulation, achieving autonomous and generalizable policies remains a long-term objective. Imitation learning, particularly with the rise of transformer-based, diffusion-based, and vision-language-action architectures, has shown promise in this area. However, the effectiveness of these learning methods is heavily dependent on the quality and scale of expert demonstration data. Existing teleoperation systems, due to their limitations, often struggle to provide the high-quality, stable, and diverse demonstrations needed to train robust autonomous policies.

TypeTele positions itself as a development aimed at addressing these specific shortcomings in dexterous teleoperation. By introducing a "type-guided" approach that moves beyond simple human hand mimicry, the work seeks to unlock the full dexterity of robotic hands. This advancement intends to improve teleoperation performance, enhance the efficiency and quality of data collection, and ultimately contribute to the development of more capable and generalizable autonomous manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a dexterous teleoperation system, named TypeTele, that effectively addresses the limitations of traditional retargeting-based methods. The central motivation stems from the observation that existing teleoperation systems often fail to fully leverage the inherent dexterity of advanced robotic hands, despite their structural advantages over human hands.

Specifically, the authors identify two key challenges that motivate the development of TypeTele:

1. **Restriction to Human Motion Patterns:** Current retargeting paradigms enforce a close correspondence between human and robotic hand postures. This means that if a human hand cannot perform a certain pose, the robotic hand is similarly constrained, even if it is physically capable of executing that posture and if that posture would be more advantageous for a specific manipulation task. This limitation prevents the robot from performing actions that are better suited for its unique morphology and actuation capabilities. The authors highlight that fully actuated dexterous hands can perform poses that are physically infeasible for humans but highly effective for robotic manipulation.
2. **Unreasonable Retargeting Poses Due to Morphological Differences:** The kinematic and morphological differences between human and robotic hands frequently lead to issues even when attempting to mimic human motions. Direct alignment of corresponding vectors or keypoints often results in unstable postures, such as weak or undesired contact directions with objects, self-collisions between fingers, or poses that are generally unstable and prone to dropping objects. These issues compromise the effectiveness and reliability of teleoperation, particularly for fine-grained or heavy-load tasks.

To overcome these challenges, TypeTele proposes introducing "dexterous manipulation types" into the teleoperation system. This fundamental shift is motivated by two anticipated benefits:
- **Enabling Robot-Exclusive Actions:** By defining and utilizing specific manipulation types, the robot can execute actions and achieve postures that are beyond the capabilities of human hands but are optimal for the robotic hand's structure. This allows the robot to fully exploit its unique dexterity.
- **Improving Posture Effectiveness and Rationality:** Discretizing dexterous actions into predefined types helps to ensure that the robotic hand adopts stable, effective, and rational postures tailored for specific tasks and objects. This approach aims to circumvent the problems of instability, self-collision, and poor contact often encountered with direct retargeting.

The broader motivation for this work is to enhance the efficiency and quality of real-world data collection for imitation learning, a critical component in training autonomous robotic policies. By providing a more intuitive and capable teleoperation system, the researchers aim to generate higher-quality demonstration data, which can, in turn, lead to improved performance and robustness of learning-based robotic manipulation policies. The system is also motivated by the goal of making dexterous teleoperation more accessible and effective for operators, even those without extensive robotics expertise.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TypeTele is structured as a type-guided dexterous teleoperation system comprising several interconnected modules. The overall system operates in two main stages: type retrieval and action execution.

#### 3.1. Dexterous Manipulation Type Library

The foundation of TypeTele is a comprehensive Dexterous Manipulation Type Library. This library is inspired by existing human grasp taxonomies and augmented with postures specifically designed for dexterous robotic hands. It is organized using a hierarchical taxonomy to cover a broad range of manipulation actions. The library classifies types into two primary categories:
- **Single-hand types:** Further subdivided into:
 - **Grasp types:** These include `robot-exclusive grasp types` (for tasks exceeding human hand capabilities) and `general grasp types` (derived from human grasp taxonomies).
 - **Non-grasp types:** For manipulation without grasping.
- **Bimanual collaborative types:** Subdivided into `symmetric` and `asymmetric` types based on the hands' relative positions and roles.
In total, the library consists of 4 sub-categories and 30 distinct types. Each dexterous type is annotated with a "stretching" and a "contracting" posture, which define the range of motion for that type. Additionally, each type is enriched with semantic annotations (manipulation attributes) describing its suitability for certain objects, tasks, object-centric information (e.g., object categories, contact parts, geometry, grasp direction), and posture-centric information (e.g., posture description, manipulation intention). These annotations facilitate autonomous type retrieval.

#### 3.2. MLLM-assisted Type Retrieval

To select the appropriate manipulation type for a given task, TypeTele incorporates a Multi-modality Large Language Model (MLLM)-assisted retrieval module, utilizing models such as GPT-4o. The process involves:
1. **Prompt Generation:** All annotated manipulation types from the library are converted into language prompts.
2. **Task Decomposition and Attribute Inference:** The MLLM receives the user's task command (e.g., via a voice control program using Whisper for transcription) and real-time camera images. It is prompted to decompose the task into sequential steps, infer the objects involved in each step, and determine the interaction method.
3. **Type Recommendation:** Based on this reasoning, the MLLM infers the desired manipulation attributes for each hand at each step. These inferred attributes are then used to retrieve the most suitable manipulation type from the library. The system provides voice control for interaction, allowing operators to issue commands even when their hands are engaged.

#### 3.3. Type Adjustment Strategy

The system includes a type adjustment strategy to enhance versatility. While the library covers many common tasks, users can explicitly apply offsets to the position or orientation of specific fingertips to refine a selected type for unique cases or object variations. This adjustment is performed by:
1. Obtaining the initial fingertip position and orientation via forward kinematics (FK).
2. Specifying the desired adjustment either through capturing the user's 6-DOF fingertip motion or manual input of transformation values.
3. Applying the offset transformation to the fingertip pose and computing the adjusted joint angles via inverse kinematics (IK). The IK solver is initialized with the original type's joint angles to maintain proximity and prevent undesirable deviations.

#### 3.4. Interpolation Mapping Strategy

For intuitive control of the robotic hand's actions within a chosen type, an interpolation mapping strategy is designed:
1. The human hand's fully stretched and fully contracted postures are associated with the corresponding stretched and contracted postures of the robotic hand for the selected manipulation type.
2. Given the operator's current human hand posture, a normalized projection ratio ($p_{ratio}$) is calculated for each fingertip. This ratio quantifies the current fingertip's position along the 3D vector defined by its stretched and contracted positions, clipped within.
3. This $p_{ratio}$ is then used to linearly interpolate between the robotic hand's stretched and contracted joint angles, yielding the target joint angles for the robotic hand ($\theta_{current}$).

#### 3.5. Hardware and Robot Control

The TypeTele system employs a multi-component hardware setup:
- **Motion Capture:** Rokoko Gloves capture 3-DOF finger positions, and a Meta Quest 3 VR controller captures 6-DOF wrist poses.
- **Robots:** Two Kinova Gen3 arms (6 and 7 DOF) are used, each equipped with a LEAP dexterous hand (16 DOF).
- **Vision:** A Realsense L515 LiDAR Camera provides single-view RGB-D observations.
For robot control, joint position PD control is used for the dexterous hands, while the arms utilize a high-frequency Cartesian velocity control interface from Kinova. Arm motion is predefined with uniform acceleration/deceleration for smoothness, and rotational velocity is dynamically adjusted based on orientation error, with bounds for safety. Kalman filtering is applied to smooth velocity signals. The system also features multi-threaded device communication, uniformly accelerated motion profiles, dynamic speed control for rotation, and a dedicated asynchronous robot control thread to ensure stable and fluid operation.

#### 3.6. Imitation Learning Setting

To evaluate the quality of data collected, the paper employs a state-of-the-art diffusion-based policy, iDP3, as the imitation learning algorithm. This policy takes single-view 3D point clouds (4096 points) and robot proprioceptive inputs (44 dimensions including arm Cartesian poses and hand joint angles) as conditional inputs. The policy outputs the desired Cartesian position of the robot arm's end-effector and dexterous hand joint angles. Different observation and action horizons are used depending on task length. The policy is trained using a denoising score matching loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness and advantages of the TypeTele system across various metrics, including teleoperation efficiency, task success rates, data quality for imitation learning, and system applicability.

#### 4.1. Improved Data Collection Efficiency

Comparative teleoperation experiments with a retargeting-based baseline showed that TypeTele significantly improves data collection efficiency.
- **Overall Collection Time ($T_{all}$):** TypeTele consistently achieved shorter total times for completing tasks, including time spent on failures. For example, "Collect and Store" took 616.8 seconds with TypeTele versus 1231.6 seconds with the baseline.
- **Task Success Rate ($Suc$):** TypeTele achieved higher success rates across all evaluated tasks. For "Collect and Store," TypeTele recorded a 95.2% success rate compared to the baseline's 60.6%.
- **Average Demonstration Duration ($T_{single}$):** Successful demonstrations using TypeTele were generally shorter. In "Handover," TypeTele had an average duration of 11.64 seconds, while the baseline was 18.38 seconds.
These results indicate that the type-guided approach facilitates more convenient object manipulation and more stable grasping, enhancing teleoperation performance.

#### 4.2. Enabling Previously Unachievable Tasks

A notable finding is TypeTele's ability to successfully execute tasks that were unachievable using the retargeting-based baseline (which recorded 0% success). These challenging tasks included:
- "Use Scissors"
- "Spray Water"
- "Use a Heavy Kettle"
- "Open a Large Box"
- "Grasp Two Objects"
For these tasks, TypeTele achieved substantial success rates (e.g., 91.1% for "Use Scissors," 85.0% for "Use a Heavy Kettle"). The paper attributes the baseline's failure to issues like unstable grasping, undesired contact, or inability to achieve necessary postures (e.g., wide hand opening for a large box or two objects). TypeTele's type-guided approach enabled precise manipulation and stable grasping required for these complex scenarios.

#### 4.3. Higher Quality Data for Imitation Learning

The quality of collected demonstrations was evaluated by training an autonomous policy (iDP3) on datasets from both TypeTele and the baseline. The results showed that the policy trained with data collected by TypeTele achieved consistently higher success rates in autonomous execution (evaluated over 10 attempts).
- For tasks where both systems could collect data, TypeTele-trained policies performed better (e.g., "Collect and Store": 10/10 successes for TypeTele vs. 3/10 for baseline; "Pouring from Pan": 9/10 for TypeTele vs. 1/10 for baseline).
- For tasks where the baseline could not collect any successful demonstrations, the TypeTele-trained policy achieved high success rates (e.g., "Use Scissors": 9/10, "Grasp Two Objects": 8/10).
This demonstrates that TypeTele collects higher-quality demonstration data, which is beneficial for training more effective and robust autonomous policies.

#### 4.4. Applicability of the TypeTele System

The system demonstrated broad applicability:
- **Generalizability of Types:** A single dexterous manipulation type was shown to generalize across various objects with similar geometric structures or functional properties (e.g., a type for trigger sprayers working for different instances; a type for thin handles adapting to various sizes).
- **Complex Task Handling:** TypeTele effectively managed long-horizon, multi-object, and multi-stage manipulation tasks, capable of selecting and executing appropriate types at each stage.
- **Hardware Compatibility:** Experiments showcased TypeTele's applicability to different dexterous robotic hands, including real-world manipulation with the Inspire Hand and simulation evaluations with the Shadow Hand and Allegro Hand.

#### 4.5. Efficiency of TypeTele System Components

The system demonstrated practical operational efficiency:
- Data recording occurred at 15 FPS.
- Imitation policy inference ran at 11 FPS.
- Robotic arm control maintained a stable 25 FPS.
While the MLLM-assisted retrieval module had an average query time of 4.8 seconds, this step occurs only once per task, minimizing its impact on real-time teleoperation performance.

#### 4.6. Effectiveness of MLLM-assisted Retrieval Module

Evaluations on 50 test environments (40 single-object, 10 multi-object) confirmed the high accuracy of the MLLM-assisted retrieval module:
- 91.89% success rate for single-object tasks.
- 92.00% success rate for multi-object tasks.
This indicates the module's reliability in identifying appropriate manipulation types for diverse tasks.

#### 4.7. User Study Results

An additional user study involving five participants compared TypeTele against a retargeting-based baseline for grasping a frying pan handle.
- **Objective Metrics:** TypeTele achieved an 88% task success rate, significantly higher than the baseline's 20%. Participants also completed tasks faster with TypeTele.
- **Subjective Ratings:** TypeTele received higher average scores across all subjective dimensions on a 0-10 scale: accuracy (9.4 vs 3.4), responsiveness (8.6 vs 5.4), ease of use (8.4 vs 4.8), and user confidence (9.4 vs 3.4). These results suggest that TypeTele not only improves performance but also enhances the user experience.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of TypeTele represents a notable advancement in dexterous teleoperation, moving beyond the constraints of direct human hand retargeting. Its significance and potential impact span several areas within robotics and machine learning:

#### 5.1. Enhanced Dexterous Robot Capabilities

By introducing and leveraging "dexterous manipulation types," TypeTele enables robotic hands to perform actions and achieve postures that are not limited by human hand kinematics. This allows the full range of a dexterous robot's structural and actuation advantages to be utilized, unlocking new capabilities for manipulation that were previously impractical or impossible with conventional teleoperation methods. This directly translates to robots being able to complete more complex and diverse tasks.

#### 5.2. Improved Data Collection for Robot Learning

A major impact of TypeTele is its contribution to the field of robot learning. The system significantly improves the efficiency and quality of real-world demonstration data collection. Higher success rates, shorter demonstration durations, and the ability to execute previously unachievable tasks mean that imitation learning algorithms can be trained on richer, more stable, and more diverse datasets. This is crucial for developing robust and generalizable autonomous policies, addressing a key bottleneck in current learning-based robotics. The experimental results clearly show that policies trained with TypeTele data achieve superior performance, directly accelerating progress towards truly autonomous dexterous manipulation.

#### 5.3. Broader Task Versatility and Applicability

The comprehensive dexterous manipulation type library and the MLLM-assisted retrieval module provide a flexible framework for handling a wide array of manipulation scenarios. The demonstrated generalizability of types across objects with similar characteristics and the system's capacity for long-horizon, multi-object tasks underscore its versatility. Furthermore, its applicability to various dexterous robotic hands (LEAP, Inspire, Shadow, Allegro) suggests that the core principles of TypeTele are transferable and could benefit different hardware platforms, potentially making advanced teleoperation more accessible.

#### 5.4. Reduced Operational Complexity and Increased User Confidence

The MLLM-assisted type retrieval, voice control, and intuitive interpolation mapping contribute to a more user-friendly teleoperation experience. By abstracting away the complexities of precise joint-level control and providing intelligent task guidance, TypeTele can lower the entry barrier for operators. The positive results from the user study, particularly in terms of ease of use and user confidence, indicate that the system can empower a broader range of users, potentially allowing non-expert operators to effectively control advanced dexterous robots.

#### 5.5. Foundation for Future Research

TypeTele's novel approach of "type-guided" teleoperation establishes a new paradigm for human-robot interaction in dexterous manipulation. This work provides a foundation for future research into how semantic understanding and structured manipulation knowledge can enhance robot control. Further exploration could involve dynamic type switching, real-time adaptation of types, or integrating more sophisticated learning directly into the type generation or adjustment process, further bridging the gap between human intent and robotic execution.

In summary, TypeTele represents a significant step forward in making dexterous teleoperation more capable, efficient, and user-friendly. Its impact is expected to extend from practical applications requiring fine motor skills in remote environments to advancing the underlying data infrastructure for the next generation of autonomous robotic manipulation systems.
