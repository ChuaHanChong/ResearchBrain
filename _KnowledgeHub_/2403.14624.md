# MathVerse: Does Your Multi-modal LLM Truly See the Diagrams in Visual Math Problems?

> [!summary] Summary
> MATHVERSE, a new benchmark from CUHK MMLab, Shanghai AI Lab, and UCLA, rigorously evaluates Multi-modal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) visual mathematical reasoning by systematically varying information modalities. The benchmark reveals that current MLLMs frequently rely on textual cues over visual diagram interpretation, with some models even showing improved accuracy when visual input is absent.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks contain textual redundancies that allow models to solve problems primarily from text, bypassing true diagram interpretation.
- Current evaluations rely on binary (correct/incorrect) answers, failing to assess the quality and coherence of intermediate reasoning steps in complex math problems.
- Many benchmarks include peripheral tasks or focus on overly complex college-level problems, diluting the focus on core visual mathematical reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- **MathVerse** transforms 2,612 visual math problems into ==six distinct versions==, systematically varying textual and visual information content to compel models to rely on diagram interpretation.
- Developed a ==two-phase Chain-of-Thought (CoT) evaluation== strategy using GPT-4(V) to adaptively extract and score fine-grained reasoning steps, providing a more granular assessment.
- Curated a specialized dataset of high-quality high-school level problems across plane geometry, solid geometry, and functions, focusing on ==multi-modal mathematical reasoning==.

## Results

- Some MLLMs, like Qwen-VL-Max and InternLM-XComposer2, achieved **5.1%** and **5.6%** higher accuracy respectively when visual input was entirely removed compared to text-dominant versions.
- Incorporating Essential Conditions (EC) visually within diagrams led to notable performance drops, such as **-5.8%** for GPT-4V, highlighting difficulty in perceiving precise numerical information from visuals.
- GPT-4V consistently achieved the best overall performance among tested models, while CoT-evaluated scores were often significantly higher than final answer accuracy (e.g., **+16.1%** for GPT-4V), indicating partial reasoning success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit a strong reliance on textual information, often struggling to genuinely interpret and utilize mathematical diagrams for problem-solving.
> - A primary cause of MLLM failure in visual math problems is deficient visual encoding and inaccurate perception of mathematical diagrams, particularly for essential conditions.
> - Fine-grained Chain-of-Thought (CoT) evaluation reveals that models frequently produce correct intermediate reasoning steps even when the final answer is incorrect.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and their multimodal extensions (MLLMs) have led to impressive performance across various visual and linguistic tasks. However, the authors argue that their capabilities in visual mathematical problem-solving remain inadequately evaluated. Existing benchmarks, such as GeoQA, UniGeo (geometry-specific), MathVista (broader, but with many peripheral tasks), and MMMU (college-level complexity), are identified to have three key shortcomings that MATHVERSE aims to address:

1. **Textual Redundancy and True Diagram Interpretation:** Current benchmarks often embed redundant visual information within textual questions. This redundancy allows MLLMs to potentially deduce answers primarily from text, bypassing the genuine interpretation of diagrams. MATHVERSE directly investigates this by systematically varying the textual and visual information content across six problem versions, forcing models to rely more heavily on visual cues for mathematical reasoning. This directly challenges the assumption that MLLMs "see" diagrams in existing evaluations.
2. **Insufficient Fine-grained Evaluation:** Most existing benchmarks rely on a simple binary (correct/incorrect) evaluation of the final answer. The authors contend that mathematical problem-solving requires intricate, step-by-step reasoning. A binary judgment fails to capture the nuanced quality of intermediate reasoning steps. MATHVERSE introduces a novel Chain-of-Thought (CoT) evaluation strategy that adaptively extracts and scores crucial reasoning steps, offering a more granular assessment of MLLMs' logical deduction processes.
3. **Specialization in Mathematical Reasoning:** While some benchmarks like GeoQA focus narrowly on geometry, others like MathVista include a wide array of "peripheral tasks" (e.g., natural images, statistic plots) that do not directly assess professional math skills, potentially skewing evaluation emphasis. MMMU, while math-focused, targets college-level problems requiring extensive domain-specific knowledge, which might hinder MLLMs from fully demonstrating their reasoning capacity if their knowledge base is insufficient. MATHVERSE, in contrast, specializes in a diverse range of high-school level mathematical problems (plane geometry, solid geometry, functions), ensuring the evaluation truly reflects multi-modal mathematical reasoning without being overly constrained by advanced domain knowledge or diluted by unrelated tasks.

By addressing these limitations, MATHVERSE carves out a unique and critical niche in the MLLM evaluation landscape, aiming to provide a more equitable, in-depth, and specialized assessment of how MLLMs genuinely process and reason with visual mathematical diagrams.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of MATHVERSE are to:

- **Investigate True Diagram Understanding:** The core motivation is to determine whether MLLMs genuinely interpret and utilize information presented in diagrams for mathematical problem-solving, or if they primarily rely on redundant textual information. The authors hypothesize that current benchmarks might inadvertently enable MLLMs to "cheat" by reading text instead of seeing visuals.
- **Develop an Equitable and In-depth Evaluation Benchmark:** Create a robust and comprehensive benchmark that allows for a fair and thorough assessment of MLLMs' visual mathematical reasoning abilities, overcoming the limitations of existing datasets.
- **Assess Multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Capabilities:** Go beyond simple final answer correctness to evaluate the quality and coherence of MLLMs' intermediate reasoning steps in visual math problems. This is crucial for understanding *how* models arrive at their answers, not just *if* they are correct.
- **Guide Future MLLM Development:** Provide insights and detailed error analysis that can inform and steer the development of more capable and genuinely multimodal large language models, particularly in their ability to integrate visual and textual information for complex reasoning.

In essence, the authors are motivated by the need for a more transparent and diagnostically valuable benchmark to push the boundaries of MLLM research in the challenging domain of visual mathematics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of MATHVERSE involved a meticulous, multi-faceted approach, encompassing data collection, problem transformation, and a novel evaluation strategy.

#### 3.1. Data Composition and Collection

- **Dataset Size and Scope:** MATHVERSE comprises 2,612 high-quality visual math problems, resulting in 15,672 total test samples after transformation.
- **Subject Diversity:** The problems span three fundamental math subjects: plane geometry (1,746), solid geometry (332), and functions (534). These subjects were chosen for their rigorous demands on multimodal reasoning.
- **Difficulty Level:** Problems are primarily high-school level, avoiding overly complex college-level theorems to focus on reasoning capacity rather than domain-specific knowledge limitations.
- **Categorization:** Expert annotators further subdivide problems into twelve fine-grained categories (e.g., Length, Area, Angle for Plane Geometry; Coordinate, Property, Expression for Functions), allowing for nuanced skill assessment.
- **Sources:** Data was collected from both pre-existing benchmarks (GeoQA, GEOS, Geometry3K) and new public question repositories, ensuring diversity in question types and diagram styles. Newly collected problems (1,236) include detailed explanations.
- **Quality Control:** A rigorous review process verified answer accuracy, consistency between questions and diagrams, and relevance to defined categories. Problems with multiple diagrams or visual solutions were excluded.

#### 3.2. Problem Transformation for Visual Interpretation Assessment

To investigate how MLLMs truly "see" diagrams, each problem in MATHVERSE is transformed into six distinct versions with varying multi-modal information content. This relies on categorizing textual information:
- **Descriptive Information (DI):** Directly observable content in diagrams (e.g., basic shapes, spatial arrangements). This is often redundant with visual input.
- **Implicit Property (IP):** Information requiring higher-level visual perception but less mathematical knowledge (e.g., parallelism, perpendicularity, similarity). Theoretically extractable from diagrams alone.
- **Essential Condition (EC):** Specific numerical or algebraic measurements indispensable for solving the problem (e.g., angles, lengths, function expressions). Cannot be derived from visuals alone.

Based on these categories, six problem versions are created:
1. **Text-dominant:** Full textual content (DI + IP + EC + Question), diagram as supplementary. Serves as baseline.
2. **Text-lite:** DI removed from text, assuming MLLMs observe it from the diagram.
3. **Text-only:** Diagram input discarded, MLLM relies solely on text (DI + IP + EC + Question).
4. **Vision-intensive:** IP removed from text (EC + Question), challenging MLLMs to derive strong visual conditions from diagrams.
5. **Vision-dominant:** EC removed from text and instead annotated visually in diagrams (IP + Question). Demands MLLMs recognize EC from visuals.
6. **Vision-only:** Entire textual input stripped, problem conveyed solely through diagram (including EC, IP, and question rendered visually). The ultimate test for visual reasoning.
This systematic variation allows for a comprehensive ablation study on the impact of different information modalities.

#### 3.3. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Evaluation Strategy

Recognizing the limitations of binary evaluation for complex math problems, the authors propose a two-phase CoT evaluation strategy using GPT-4(V):
1. **Key-step Extraction (using GPT-4):** Given an MLLM's output, GPT-4 (language-only) is prompted to extract N pivotal reasoning steps (s1, s2..., sN, plus final answer sA). Crucially, GPT-4 receives only the MLLM's output, without the original question, diagram, or ground truth, to avoid bias towards GPT-4's own problem-solving abilities. This adaptive extraction handles varying reasoning lengths and styles.
2. **Multi-step Scoring (using GPT-4V):** GPT-4V (multi-modal) is then used to evaluate each extracted step. It receives the extracted steps, original question, diagram, ground-truth answer, and (for function problems) additional diagram annotations. Each step is scored 0 (incorrect) or 1 (correct) for logical coherence, visual perception, and numerical computation. The final score is a weighted sum: `Score_final = α * (Avg_step_score) + (1 - α) * Score(s_A)`, with α (default 0.7) emphasizing intermediate reasoning. This strategy provides fine-grained scoring and detailed error analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments on MATHVERSE with popular closed-source and open-source MLLMs revealed several critical insights:

- **MLLMs Rely Heavily on Text, Struggle with Diagrams:** A surprising key finding is that most existing MLLMs struggle to understand mathematical diagrams, relying heavily on textual questions. Some models, like Qwen-VL-Max (+5.1%) and InternLM-XComposer2 (+5.6%), even achieved *higher* accuracy when the visual input was entirely removed (Text-only vs. Text-dominant/Lite versions). This suggests that for these models, the visual input can be a "distraction" or is inadequately processed, leading to performance degradation.
- **Deficient Visual Encoding for Mathematical Diagrams:** The error analysis using the CoT strategy confirms that a primary cause of failure is "visual perception error" – inaccurate interpretation of mathematical diagrams. This includes inaccurate identification of mathematical symbols and insufficient grasp of domain-specific knowledge to associate identified measurements with concepts.
- **MLLMs are Moderately Effective at Perceiving Implicit Properties (IP):** Removing Implicit Property information from text (Text-lite to Vision-intensive) caused only a negligible decline in accuracy for most MLLMs. This indicates that MLLMs have decent visual perception skills for non-mathematical geometric elements.
- **MLLMs are Challenged by Essential Conditions (EC) from Diagrams:** Incorporating Essential Conditions within diagrams (Text-lite to Vision-dominant) led to a notable performance drop (e.g., -5.8% for GPT-4V, -3.9% for InternLM-XComposer2). This highlights their difficulty in recognizing precise numerical or algebraic measurements exclusively from visual inputs.
- **MLLMs Struggle with Vision-Only Problems:** When problems are conveyed solely through diagrams (Vision-only version), MLLMs' problem-solving capacity further diminished, indicating a significant challenge in simultaneously understanding conditions, questions, and figures from visual input alone.
- **Closed-source MLLMs Outperform Open-source Models:** GPT-4V consistently achieved the best overall performance across different problem versions and subjects, though still with a gap to human performance. InternLM-XComposer2 and SPHINX-MoE were the best open-source models, but significantly lagged behind closed-source counterparts.
- **Text-only LLMs are Competitive:** Text-only LLMs like GPT-4 and ChatGPT achieved competitive or even superior accuracy to most MLLMs in text-rich versions (Text-dominant, Text-lite). GPT-4 even surpassed human performance in the Text-only version, demonstrating strong mathematical reasoning when visual context is absent. This emphasizes that MLLMs' weakness lies specifically in *visual interpretation* for math.
- **Mathematical Training Benefits Performance:** Models that underwent math-specific tuning (e.g., SPHINX-MoE, InternLM-XComposer2, G-LLaVA) showed improved performance, but sometimes at the cost of catastrophic forgetting in other domains if tuning data was too narrow.
- **CoT Evaluation Reveals Nuances:** The "CoT-E" (CoT-evaluated) scores were often significantly higher than "Acc" (final answer accuracy) scores (e.g., +16.1% for GPT-4V), indicating that models frequently performed many correct intermediate reasoning steps despite arriving at an incorrect final answer. This validates the importance of fine-grained evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MATHVERSE represents a significant contribution to the field of MLLM evaluation and development, with several potential impacts:

- **Diagnosing MLLM Weaknesses:** The benchmark clearly identifies a critical bottleneck in current MLLMs: their inability to robustly and accurately interpret mathematical diagrams. This precise diagnosis is invaluable, moving beyond general "multimodal reasoning" to pinpoint "mathematical visual perception" as a key area for improvement.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By explicitly demonstrating that visual input can sometimes *harm* performance or that MLLMs rely on text redundancies, MATHVERSE provides a clear roadmap for future research. It strongly suggests the need for:
 - **Stronger Math-specific Vision Encoders:** Developing visual encoders specifically designed to understand mathematical elements (symbols, relationships, geometric properties) within diagrams, rather than relying solely on general visual understanding.
 - **Improved Multi-modal Fusion and Reasoning:** Enhancing the mechanisms by which MLLMs integrate visual and textual information to prevent visual input from distracting or misleading the reasoning process.
- **Promoting Fine-grained Evaluation:** The proposed CoT evaluation strategy sets a new standard for assessing complex reasoning tasks in MLLMs. Its ability to provide detailed error analysis and capture nuances in intermediate steps will be highly beneficial for model debugging and iterative improvement.
- **Fostering Data Diversity and Quality:** The meticulous data collection and transformation process for MATHVERSE highlight the importance of high-quality, diverse, and well-structured datasets for training and evaluating advanced AI models. The comprehensive nature of the benchmark encourages more holistic testing across subjects and information modalities.
- **Bridging Human-AI Performance Gaps:** By comparing MLLM performance against human baselines and revealing areas where MLLMs struggle (e.g., perceiving essential conditions from diagrams), MATHVERSE contributes to understanding the remaining challenges in achieving human-level visual mathematical reasoning in AI.

In conclusion, MATHVERSE is not just another benchmark; it is a diagnostic tool that unveils fundamental limitations in current MLLMs' ability to truly "see" and reason with mathematical diagrams. Its findings and methodology are poised to significantly influence the next generation of MLLM architectures and training paradigms, driving the development of more intelligent and genuinely multimodal AI systems.
