# SEAOTTER: Sensor Embedded Autoencoding with One-Time Transcode for Efficient Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> The SEAOTTER framework from The University of Texas at Austin introduces a method for efficient image compression in cloud robotics by integrating a lightweight sensor-side encoder with a cloud-based, task-aware transcoding process to standard JPEG. It achieves up to 12.80 percentage points higher ImageNet top-1 accuracy compared to the base autoencoder, while enabling sensor-side encoding over 7 times faster and consumer-side decoding 3.5 times faster than AVIF.

## Problem

- Robotics systems struggle with high-resolution visual data due to extreme on-device compute limitations and restricted wireless communication bandwidth for cloud offloading.
- Existing learned compression techniques (asymmetric autoencoders) suffer from either prohibitive decoding costs or proprietary data formats, making them incompatible with standard infrastructure and inefficient for common "encode-once, decode-many" scenarios.
- Standard image codecs are optimized for human perception and often degrade performance on downstream machine vision tasks due to their fixed color transforms and quantization matrices.

## Method

- A frozen, lightweight FRAPPE encoder is used on the sensor to generate compact latent representations that are transmitted losslessly to the cloud.
- In the cloud, these latents are processed by a fine-tuned FRAPPE decoder and then fed into a "learned JPEG sandwich" which includes de novo learned color transforms and DCT-domain quantization matrices.
- The cloud transcode generates a standard JPEG file, allowing efficient consumer-side decoding using vanilla JPEG decoders, with an optional inverse color transform to recover displayable RGB or for direct use in JPEG-domain learning by machine vision models.

## Results

- SEAOTTER-FT achieved up to +12.80 percentage points (pp) higher ImageNet top-1 accuracy over the FRAPPE baseline and +8.00 pp over AVIF-max-speed, operating at matched or lower bitrates.
- The sensor-side encoding throughput exceeded 250 MPx/s (over 7 times faster than AVIF), and consumer-side decoding was approximately 3.4 times faster than AVIF and 100 times faster than a pure FRAPPE decoder.
- The framework successfully cleared all tested deployment tiers (Bluetooth Low Energy, 5G, Wi-Fi) at various low-bitrate operating points, demonstrating superior efficiency and compatibility compared to AVIF and WaLLoC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating lightweight learned encoders with a cloud-based, task-aware transcoding step into a standard image format (JPEG) effectively bridges the gap between sensor-side efficiency and consumer-side compatibility.
> - End-to-end optimization of both learned color transforms and quantization matrices for downstream machine perception tasks, rather than just pixel-level reconstruction, can significantly improve AI model accuracy.
> - By specifically targeting the standard JPEG format for the final artifact, the framework leverages decades of existing infrastructure for efficient storage, processing, and decoding, while still benefiting from learned, task-optimized representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern robotics systems are increasingly equipped with high-resolution, low-cost, and low-power image sensors capable of generating vast amounts of visual data. However, the effective utilization of this data is often hampered by severe limitations in on-device compute resources and wireless communication bandwidth. Cloud robotics, which offloads intensive computation to remote datacenters, emerges as a potential solution, but it necessitates extreme data compression before transmission to the cloud.

The current landscape of image compression techniques presents a dilemma. Conventional codecs, such as JPEG and MPEG, can achieve high compression ratios but often at the cost of significant perceptual degradation, which negatively impacts downstream machine perception tasks. Newer, more advanced codecs like AV1 and AVIF offer improved rate-distortion trade-offs but demand substantially greater computational resources for encoding, often requiring custom Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) that are impractical for many resource-constrained robotic platforms.

A recent area of focus in learned compression involves asymmetric autoencoders (AAEs). These codecs are designed to optimize either encoding or decoding efficiency. Decoding-efficient AAEs (DE-AAEs) provide high quality under tight power and bandwidth constraints but incur prohibitive decoding costs and utilize proprietary data formats, rendering them incompatible with established infrastructure built around standards like JPEG. Conversely, encoding-efficient AAEs (EE-AAEs), such as FRAPPE, WaLLoC, and LiVeAction, employ lightweight encoders suitable for on-device processing and pair them with more complex, often DNN-based, decoders. While solving the sensor-side bottleneck, these EE-AAEs introduce a new challenge: their DNN decoders are computationally expensive for consumer-side applications and their bespoke latent formats are incompatible with the extensive ecosystem of ML frameworks, fast dataloaders, web browsers, and hardware codecs that rely on standard image formats. The "encode-once, decode-many" lifecycle prevalent in cloud robotics, where data is ingested once but accessed repeatedly for training, inference, and auditing, exacerbates the issue of high decoding costs.

SEAOTTER addresses this specific gap by proposing a framework that bridges the efficiency of learned sensor-side compression with the universal compatibility and efficient decoding requirements of consumer-side applications. It leverages the strengths of EE-AAEs for on-device compression while introducing a mechanism to transform the learned latent representation into a standard JPEG file in the cloud, thereby circumventing the high decoding cost and format incompatibility of purely learned codecs on the consumer end. This work aims to integrate advanced learned compression into practical cloud robotics deployments without disrupting existing data pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a compression framework for cloud robotics that effectively reconciles the disparate resource constraints and functional requirements across the sensor, cloud, and consumer stages of the data pipeline. The specific motivations and detailed objectives are as follows:

1. **High-Throughput Sensor-Side Encoding Under Extreme Resource Constraints:** Robotic, wearable, and remote-sensing platforms operate under strict budgets for power, thermal dissipation, and uplink bandwidth. For instance, a 1080p 30 frames-per-second (fps) stream over a 25 Mbps Wi-Fi channel demands a compression ratio of approximately 60:1, while a 480p stream over a 1 Mbps Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) channel requires about 288:1. Conventional codecs severely degrade image quality at such ratios, and newer codecs like AVIF have per-pixel encoding costs that are impractical for on-device processing without dedicated hardware. The motivation is to provide a sensor-side encoder that is exceptionally lightweight (e.g., 10-100 MAC/pixel), enabling real-time high-resolution data capture and transmission within these tight resource constraints.

2. **Learning Specialized Representations via End-to-End Optimization for Task Adaptability:** Robotic applications are highly diverse, involving a wide range of sensors (e.g., RGB, depth, IR, multispectral), environmental conditions (e.g., high motion, poor lighting, aerial views, lens distortions), and downstream machine perception tasks (e.g., global classification, dense segmentation, vision-language understanding). Standard codecs use fixed color transforms and quantization matrices that are optimized for human perception and may not be optimal for machine vision tasks. The objective is to enable the codec to learn data-driven, specialized representations that are optimized end-to-end for specific downstream tasks and sensor characteristics, thereby improving machine perception accuracy, while still adhering to standard output formats.

3. **Flexible and Efficient Decoding with Universal Consumer-Side Compatibility:** A significant impediment to the widespread adoption of many asymmetric autoencoders is their requirement for custom, computationally intensive DNN decoders and their use of proprietary latent formats. This renders them incompatible with the existing decades-old infrastructure, including common ML frameworks, fast dataloaders, web browsers, and hardware codecs embedded in ASICs and SoCs, all of which are built around standards like JPEG. Furthermore, in an "encode-once, decode-many" paradigm typical for training datasets or archival, the high computational cost of a DNN decoder becomes prohibitive, as it is multiplied by every read access. The motivation is to produce a decoded artifact that is universally compatible with existing JPEG infrastructure, can be decoded efficiently (ideally faster than conventional learned decoders and comparable to or faster than standard codecs), and offers flexibility for both legacy human-perception applications and modern machine-vision pipelines (e.g., by allowing direct operation in a learned color space).

SEAOTTER aims to address these motivations by combining a lightweight, pre-trained encoding-efficient autoencoder (FRAPPE) on the sensor, a one-time, task-aware transcoding process in the cloud that leverages a learned JPEG codec, and outputs a standard JPEG file for efficient and compatible consumption.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEAOTTER's methodology is structured around a three-stage pipeline (sensor, cloud, consumer) designed to optimize for efficiency at each stage while ensuring compatibility.

**A. Overall Workflow and Design:**
The core idea is to use a compact learned latent representation transmitted from the sensor, which is then transcoded once in the cloud into a standard JPEG file. This JPEG file serves all subsequent consumers. The overall transformation is represented by:
`^x = F^-1 ◦ J_Q ◦ F ◦ G_S ◦ C ◦ G_A(x)`
where `x` is the input RGB image, `G_A` is the sensor-side encoder, `C` is the lossless transmission channel, `G_S` is the cloud-side decoder, `F` and `F^-1` are the learned color transform and its inverse, and `J_Q` is the standard JPEG encode-decode round-trip with learned quantization matrix `Q`.

**B. Sensor Stage: Sensor-Embedded Encoding with Frozen FRAPPE Encoder (`G_A`)**
- **Component:** The analysis transform `G_A` is a frozen FRAPPE encoder. FRAPPE is selected for its high encoding efficiency, estimated at 10-100 MAC/pixel, which is two orders of magnitude lower than other learned codecs.
- **Variable Rate Compression:** FRAPPE’s residual training procedure sorts latent channels in coarse-to-fine order. This allows the sensor to select its operating point (bitrate) by transmitting a prefix of these channels without re-encoding the entire image.
- **Resource Constraint Adherence:** Freezing `G_A` reflects the hard budget on sensor-side compute, akin to approaches in WaLLoC and LiVeAction.
- **Transmission:** The output `int8` latents are losslessly compressed (using a codec like JPEG-LS) and transmitted over the wireless uplink to the cloud.

**C. Cloud Stage: One-Time Transcode with Fine-tuned FRAPPE Decoder (`G_S`) and Learned JPEG Sandwich**
- **Fine-tuned FRAPPE Decoder (`G_S`):**
 - **Component:** The synthesis transform `G_S` is a FRAPPE decoder (approximately 57 million parameters).
 - **Application-Specific Enhancement:** Unlike `G_A`, `G_S` is fine-tuned against the specific downstream task loss (e.g., classification accuracy), with `G_A` remaining frozen. This process allows `G_S`'s RGB output to "drift" towards a distribution that a JPEG-pretrained consumer-side backbone can interpret more accurately, potentially sacrificing pixel-domain reconstruction PSNR for improved downstream task accuracy.
 - **Flexibility:** Because `G_A` is frozen, different fine-tuned `G_S` snapshots can be interchanged at runtime, enabling a single uplink stream to serve multiple downstream tasks simultaneously, each optimized with a different `G_S`.
- **Learned JPEG Sandwich:** The RGB output from `G_S` is passed through a sequence of learned operations before being encoded into standard JPEG.
 - **De Novo Learnable Wrapper Filter and Color Transform (`F` and `F^-1`):**
 - **`F` (Forward Transform):** Composed of three operators:
 1. A 3x3 wrapper filter (`Conv_W`) with a learnable kernel `W`. This convolution spatially filters and mixes the RGB channels, defining the learned color space.
 2. Per-channel softsign companding (`σ_s`) with learnable scale `s`. This confines each channel’s value to a signed 8-bit range (−127, 127).
 3. A per-channel affine transform (`A_α,β`) with learnable scale `α` and offset `β`. This maps the result into the unsigned 8-bit range required by JPEG.
 - **`F^-1` (Inverse Transform):** Mirrors `F`, using an independently learned wrapper-filter kernel (`f_W`) and algebraic inverses for companding and affine transforms. The parameters `α, β, s` are shared between `F` and `F^-1`.
 - **Initialization and Bias:** `F` and `F^-1` are initialized to approximate the algebraic identity, rather than the JFIF RGB→YCbCr matrix. This inductive bias allows the network to discover an optimal color transform from data, guided by the rate-distortion loss, rather than starting from a human-perception-tuned transform.
 - **Standard Compatibility:** `F`’s three-channel output is written byte-for-byte into the JPEG file with `subsampling=0` (true 4:4:4). The JPEG decoder at the consumer must skip the standard YCbCr→RGB color conversion, a standard setting in compliant JPEG implementations.
 - **De Novo Learnable DCT-Domain Quantization Matrices (`Q_k`):**
 - For each of `K` rate points, an unconstrained 3x8x8 parameter tensor (`Q_raw^(k)`) is reparameterized via a softsign-plus-affine function to generate a JPEG quantization matrix `Q^(k)` in the legal range (1, 256).
 - This parameterization ensures finite gradients during training. Each `Q^(k)` is learned independently, specializing to its operating point, while `F` and `F^-1` are shared.
 - These learned matrices replace the standard ITU T.81 tables.
 - **Improved JPEG Rate Proxy:** A differentiable, sparsity-aware run-length surrogate is used during training to estimate the JPEG file size. It models JPEG's zigzag AC coding, incorporating a smooth nonzero gate for AC coefficients and a fixed Huffman overhead constant. A per-rate scalar calibrates this proxy to real JPEG bits-per-pixel.
- **Training Methodology:**
 - The shared (`F`, `F^-1`) pair and the `K` rate-specific `Q^(k)` matrices are trained jointly, end-to-end, against a multi-rate rate-distortion objective.
 - The loss function `L_total` combines `log_10(MSE)` and the calibrated `bpp` for each rate, weighted by Lagrange multipliers `λ_k` and loss weights `w_k`.
 - Training is *de novo*: `F`/`F^-1` are identity-initialized, `Q^(k)` from random Gaussian noise, and standard Huffman tables are used. This avoids warm-starting from existing JPEG structures.

**D. Consumer Stage: Flexible and Efficient Decoding**
- **Standard Decoding:** The deployed consumer-side decoding involves a vanilla JPEG decode.
- **Optional `F^-1`:** The learned inverse color transform `F^-1` is then applied to recover the displayable RGB image. Crucially, `F^-1` is optional:
 - **Legacy Systems:** Applications requiring sRGB inputs (e.g., human perception, pre-trained foundation models) apply `F^-1`. The overhead is a single post-filter (∼81 MACs/pixel).
 - **JPEG-Domain Learning:** Downstream machine vision applications that can train or fine-tune their own models can skip `F^-1` and operate directly on the JPEG-decoded coefficients, analogous to prior work on JPEG-domain learning. This integrates the inverse-convolution into the first layer of the downstream model.
- **Compatibility:** This approach ensures compatibility with both traditional JPEG pipelines and specialized learning systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SEAOTTER focused on the rate-distortion-complexity trade-off, comparing it against AVIF, WaLLoC, and FRAPPE. Performance was assessed through standard distortion metrics, downstream task accuracy, and processing throughput.

**A. Downstream Accuracy Improvements:**
- **Classification (ImageNet):** At a matched transmit bit-per-pixel (bpp) of 0.109 (compression ratio CR=221:1), SEAOTTER-FT (fine-tuned for task) achieved 69.02% ImageNet top-1 accuracy, representing a +12.80 percentage point (pp) increase over FRAPPE alone (56.22%). This margin widened at lower bitrates, reaching +19.85 pp at 0.038 bpp. Even SEAOTTER-ZS (zero-shot, no task-aware fine-tune) showed a +4.03 pp improvement over FRAPPE at n=12.
- **Segmentation (ADE20K):** SEAOTTER-FT demonstrated a +3.68 pp improvement in mIoU over FRAPPE at n=12.
- **Vision-Language (SigLIP-2):** SEAOTTER-FT yielded a +6.71 pp gain in zero-shot top-1 accuracy compared to FRAPPE alone.

**B. Pareto Dominance on Machine-Perception Tasks:**
- SEAOTTER-FT achieved higher ImageNet top-1 accuracy than AVIF and AVIF-max-speed by +7.87 pp and +8.00 pp, respectively, despite both AVIF baselines using more bits per pixel.
- On SigLIP-2 zero-shot classification, SEAOTTER-FT led AVIF by +5.63 pp and AVIF-max-speed by +4.03 pp.
- For ADE20K segmentation, SEAOTTER-FT performed comparably to or slightly better than AVIF baselines (+0.02 pp over AVIF, +0.26 pp over AVIF-max-speed), while also leading FRAPPE alone by +3.68 pp.

**C. Storage-Side Compression Ratio:**
- The SEAOTTER-FT artifact, at n=12 transmit-bpp, was found to be 13.7% smaller than an architecturally comparable FRAPPE followed by a vanilla ITU T.81 transcode. This smaller file size was achieved while also yielding +8.19 pp higher ImageNet top-1 accuracy.

**D. Sensor-Side Encoding Throughput:**
- SEAOTTER variants utilize the frozen FRAPPE encoder, inheriting its high speed. At matched-rate operating points, the shared encoder was over an order of magnitude faster than AVIF default-speed and 5-8 times faster than AVIF max-speed across all three tasks.
- Encoding throughput exceeded 250 MPx/s for `n≤9`, indicating suitability for 1080p 30 fps video streams over Wi-Fi, without additional encode-time overhead from the SEAOTTER sandwich.

**E. Downstream Consumer Decoding Throughput:**
- The steady-state consumer-side decode process for a SEAOTTER artifact (vanilla JPEG decode followed by `F^-1`) was approximately 3.4 times faster than AVIF's decoding cost and 100 times faster than the FRAPPE codec without the transcode step. The option to skip `F^-1` further enhances decoding efficiency for specific machine vision applications.

**F. Deployment-Tier Suitability:**
- SEAOTTER-FT cleared all three tested deployment tiers (BLE, 5G, Wi-Fi) at low-bitrate operating points (`n ∈ {3, 6, 9}`) and the 5G and Wi-Fi tiers at `n=12`. In contrast, AVIF cleared no tier at any evaluated quality, and WaLLoC cleared only the BLE and 5G tiers at its lowest quality setting.

**G. Standalone Learned JPEG Codec Performance:**
- When evaluated as a standalone system (without the FRAPPE encoder upstream) on the Kodak dataset, the learned JPEG sandwich consistently dominated ITU T.81 4:4:4 in PSNR. At matched bpp, gains of +0.27 dB, +1.40 dB, and +1.27 dB were observed, indicating that the learned representation itself offers distortion benefits. The learned color space was found to approximate YCgCo.

**H. Explanation for Transcode Effectiveness:**
- The fine-tuning and learned JPEG sandwich (specifically the softsign companding and DCT-domain `Q_k` matrices) deliberately reduced pixel-domain reconstruction PSNR in exchange for higher downstream accuracy. This is hypothesized to be due to "distribution calibration," where the consumer's input is conditioned to better match the standard JPEG distribution that its JPEG-pretrained backbone expects, even if the intermediate FRAPPE output may differ significantly from typical JPEG images.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SEAOTTER framework introduces a method for image compression that addresses several challenges in cloud robotics and learned compression, demonstrating potential for practical impact across various applications.

**A. Reconciliation of Conflicting Demands:**
SEAOTTER's primary significance lies in its ability to reconcile the conflicting requirements of extreme encoding efficiency on resource-constrained sensors, task-specific optimization for machine perception, and universal compatibility with existing data infrastructure for diverse consumer applications. This integrated approach offers a practical pathway for deploying advanced compression in complex, multi-stage systems.

**B. Enhanced Machine Perception Performance:**
The framework demonstrates substantial improvements in downstream machine perception accuracy (e.g., up to +12.80 pp in ImageNet top-1 accuracy over the base autoencoder, and +8.00 pp over AVIF-max-speed) while operating at competitive or lower bitrates. This indicates that task-aware compression and transcoding can significantly enhance the utility of compressed data for AI models, potentially leading to more accurate and robust robotic systems.

**C. Superior Efficiency and Compatibility:**
SEAOTTER achieves a favorable trade-off across the rate-distortion-complexity spectrum. It retains the high sensor-side encoding throughput of efficient autoencoders (7x faster than AVIF), offers significantly faster consumer-side decoding (3.5x faster than AVIF and 100x faster than a pure autoencoder decoder), and produces standard JPEG files. This compatibility is crucial as it allows seamless integration into existing ML frameworks, data loaders, web browsers, and hardware codecs without requiring costly infrastructure overhauls or specialized hardware for decoding.

**D. Broad Applicability in Cloud Robotics:**
The ability to compress high-resolution sensor data efficiently on-device, transmit it at low bandwidths, and then convert it into a widely usable and efficiently decodable format in the cloud makes SEAOTTER highly relevant for various cloud robotics applications. This includes remote inference, collaborative inference, teleoperation, auditing, and debugging, all of which benefit from data that is both high-fidelity for machine tasks and easily accessible for human operators or legacy systems. Its suitability across various deployment tiers (BLE, 5G, Wi-Fi) further broadens its practical utility.

**E. Advancing Learned Compression Deployment:**
By tackling the critical issue of consumer-side compatibility and decoding cost, SEAOTTER provides a model for how learned compression techniques can move beyond research prototypes into broader deployment. It demonstrates that the benefits of learned, task-optimized representations can be combined with the pragmatism of standard formats, potentially influencing future codec design and standardization efforts.

**F. Foundation for Future Research:**
The work identifies several avenues for future research, including expanding modality coverage beyond RGB (e.g., depth, IR, multispectral), conducting detailed component ablations to isolate the contributions of different learned elements, characterizing the behavior of the learned color transform across diverse sensor and lighting conditions, and evaluating the impact on human perception given the nonstandard color space. These areas suggest SEAOTTER can serve as a foundational framework for further advancements in practical, learned compression.
