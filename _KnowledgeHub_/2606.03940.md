# SEAOTTER: Sensor Embedded Autoencoding with One-Time Transcode for Efficient Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> The SEAOTTER framework from The University of Texas at Austin introduces a method for efficient image compression in cloud robotics by integrating a lightweight sensor-side encoder with a cloud-based, task-aware transcoding process to standard JPEG. It achieves up to 12.80 percentage points higher ImageNet top-1 accuracy compared to the base autoencoder, while enabling sensor-side encoding over 7 times faster and consumer-side decoding 3.5 times faster than AVIF.

## Problem

- Robotics systems struggle with high-resolution visual data due to extreme on-device compute limitations and restricted wireless communication bandwidth for cloud offloading.
- Existing learned compression techniques (asymmetric autoencoders) suffer from either prohibitive decoding costs or proprietary data formats, making them incompatible with standard infrastructure and inefficient for common "encode-once, decode-many" scenarios.
- Standard image codecs are optimized for human perception and often degrade performance on downstream machine vision tasks due to their fixed color transforms and quantization matrices.

## Method

- A frozen, lightweight FRAPPE encoder is used on the sensor to generate compact latent representations that are transmitted losslessly to the cloud.
- In the cloud, these latents are processed by a fine-tuned FRAPPE decoder and then fed into a "learned JPEG sandwich" which includes de novo learned color transforms and DCT-domain quantization matrices.
- The cloud transcode generates a standard JPEG file, allowing efficient consumer-side decoding using vanilla JPEG decoders, with an optional inverse color transform to recover displayable RGB or for direct use in JPEG-domain learning by machine vision models.

## Results

- SEAOTTER-FT achieved up to +12.80 percentage points (pp) higher ImageNet top-1 accuracy over the FRAPPE baseline and +8.00 pp over AVIF-max-speed, operating at matched or lower bitrates.
- The sensor-side encoding throughput exceeded 250 MPx/s (over 7 times faster than AVIF), and consumer-side decoding was approximately 3.4 times faster than AVIF and 100 times faster than a pure FRAPPE decoder.
- The framework successfully cleared all tested deployment tiers (Bluetooth Low Energy, 5G, Wi-Fi) at various low-bitrate operating points, demonstrating superior efficiency and compatibility compared to AVIF and WaLLoC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating lightweight learned encoders with a cloud-based, task-aware transcoding step into a standard image format (JPEG) effectively bridges the gap between sensor-side efficiency and consumer-side compatibility.
> - End-to-end optimization of both learned color transforms and quantization matrices for downstream machine perception tasks, rather than just pixel-level reconstruction, can significantly improve AI model accuracy.
> - By specifically targeting the standard JPEG format for the final artifact, the framework leverages decades of existing infrastructure for efficient storage, processing, and decoding, while still benefiting from learned, task-optimized representations.
