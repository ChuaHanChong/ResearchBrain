# Wider or Deeper? Scaling LLM Inference-Time Compute with Adaptive Branching Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> An inference-time framework named Adaptive Branching Monte Carlo Tree Search (AB-MCTS) was introduced by Sakana AI to dynamically balance exploration and exploitation in large language model problem-solving. This method improved performance on complex coding, abstract reasoning, and machine learning engineering tasks, demonstrating more effective utilization of LLM inference budgets compared to existing search strategies.

## Problem

- Existing LLM inference-time scaling methods, such as repeated sampling, primarily focus on exploration without a principled mechanism for exploiting or refining promising solutions based on external feedback.
- Traditional Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) approaches for LLMs typically use a fixed branching factor, limiting the search's ability to fully capitalize on the vast and diverse output space of LLMs.
- There is a need for an adaptive method that can intelligently allocate LLM computational resources by dynamically deciding whether to explore new candidates ('go wider') or refine existing ones ('go deeper') during complex problem-solving.

## Method

- The ==Adaptive Branching Monte Carlo Tree Search (AB-MCTS)== framework was developed, generalizing repeated sampling by integrating ==multi-turn exploration and exploitation== within an MCTS structure.
- Special ==GEN nodes== are introduced to enable ==unbounded branching==, allowing dynamic generation of new child nodes, while ==Thompson Sampling== is used with ==Bayesian posterior updates== to adaptively guide the search strategy (wider vs. deeper).
- Two variants, **AB-MCTS-M** (Mixed Model) and **AB-MCTS-A** (Node Aggregation), are proposed to model and update the ==posterior predictive distributions== for decision-making.

## Results

- AB-MCTS variants consistently achieved higher average ranks across competitive programming (LiveCodeBench, CodeContest), abstract reasoning (ARC-AGI), and ML engineering (MLE-Bench) benchmarks compared to baselines, utilizing GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3.
- The method demonstrated more efficient use of LLM inference budgets, reaching peak performance earlier or achieving superior ultimate success rates, particularly as computational budgets increased.
- Analysis revealed that AB-MCTS generally generates wider search trees compared to standard MCTS, demonstrating flexible exploration while simultaneously excelling on tasks that benefit from sequential refinement, showcasing its adaptive balance of width and depth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bayesian decision-making through Thompson Sampling provides a principled way to dynamically balance exploration (generating new solutions) and exploitation (refining existing solutions) in LLM-driven tree search.
> - Allowing 'unbounded branching' via specific node types (e.g., GEN nodes) is crucial for leveraging the full diversity of LLM outputs, which fixed-width tree search methods cannot achieve.
> - The ability to adapt search strategy based on external feedback enables more robust performance across varied tasks, outperforming static or purely explorative methods that may struggle with specific problem structures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and rapidly evolving area within Large Language Model (LLM) development: **inference-time scaling**. As LLMs become increasingly powerful, the focus is shifting from merely training larger models to effectively utilizing their capabilities during deployment, especially for complex reasoning tasks. The paper situates its contribution within three broad categories of inference-time scaling: (1) post-training fine-tuning, (2) reward-guided chain-of-thought (CoT) generation, and (3) multiple answer generation. The authors explicitly focus on the third category, emphasizing that their approach is complementary and can be combined with the others.

The "multiple answer generation" paradigm, often implemented through **repeated sampling** (e.g., best-of-n, majority voting, self-consistency), has gained significant traction. It leverages the inherent stochasticity of LLM generation at non-zero temperatures to produce a diverse set of candidate outputs, increasing the likelihood of finding a high-quality solution without additional training. This approach has demonstrated remarkable success in challenging domains like coding competitions and abstract reasoning tasks (ARC-AGI). However, the authors point out a crucial limitation: repeated sampling is purely explorative and lacks an explicit mechanism for exploitation, meaning it doesn't systematically refine promising candidates based on external feedback.

This limitation opens the door for methods that combine exploration and exploitation. Previous attempts in this direction, such as those leveraging Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) (e.g., LATS, RAP, SWE-Search), were often designed before the full power of inference-time scaling was realized and typically use a *fixed branching factor*. This fixed width, the paper argues, constrains the search and prevents fully capitalizing on the vast and diverse output space of LLMs—a strength that repeated sampling inherently exploits.

The proposed Adaptive Branching Monte Carlo Tree Search (AB-MCTS) bridges this gap. It generalizes repeated sampling by introducing principled multi-turn exploration and exploitation within an MCTS framework. By allowing "unbounded branching" and dynamically deciding whether to "go wider" (explore new candidates) or "go deeper" (refine existing ones), AB-MCTS addresses the inflexibility of standard MCTS and the purely explorative nature of repeated sampling. This positions AB-MCTS as a significant advancement, offering a more adaptive and efficient way to allocate inference-time compute for complex problem-solving. It moves beyond static search strategies to a dynamic, feedback-driven approach that is particularly relevant for tasks where external validation or feedback (e.g., code execution results, test cases) is available. The work also distinguishes itself from progressive widening techniques by using reward-based decisions rather than mere visit counts to inform branching.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of Adaptive Branching Monte Carlo Tree Search (AB-MCTS) stem from identified limitations in current inference-time scaling strategies for Large Language Models (LLMs).

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Develop an inference-time framework that effectively integrates multi-turn exploration and exploitation:** The primary goal is to create a principled system that can both generate diverse candidate solutions ("go wider" for exploration) and systematically refine promising ones based on external feedback ("go deeper" for exploitation).
2. **Generalize repeated sampling:** The method aims to extend the success of repeated sampling, which is highly effective due to its ability to leverage LLM output diversity, by embedding it within a more structured, adaptive search framework.
3. **Address the challenge of "unbounded branching" in tree search:** Unlike traditional MCTS, where the branching factor is fixed, LLMs can theoretically generate an infinite number of responses from a single prompt. AB-MCTS seeks to enable this unbounded branching dynamically, ensuring the search can fully harness LLM diversity without being constrained by static hyperparameters.
4. **Dynamically balance exploration and exploitation based on external feedback:** The framework should adaptively decide the optimal search direction (wider or deeper) at each step, leveraging available feedback signals (e.g., test scores, validation metrics) to guide its decisions in a principled manner, formalized via Bayesian posterior updates.
5. **Achieve superior performance on complex tasks with limited computational budgets:** The ultimate objective is to significantly boost LLM performance on challenging benchmarks, such as coding and machine learning engineering tasks, under the constraint of a predefined number of LLM calls, outperforming existing baselines.

**Motivation:**

1. **Limitations of Pure Exploration (Repeated Sampling):** While successful, repeated sampling only explores. In many real-world tasks (e.g., coding, engineering), external feedback is available. Repeated sampling cannot effectively use this feedback to refine solutions, leaving performance on the table.
2. **Limitations of Fixed-Width Search (Standard MCTS):** Traditional MCTS methods applied to LLMs often use a fixed branching factor. This design prevents the search from fully capitalizing on the LLM's vast and diverse output space, which has been shown to be crucial for performance through the success of repeated sampling. A fixed width can hinder discovering optimal solutions by prematurely pruning promising but initially low-scoring branches or by not exploring enough diverse alternatives.
3. **Desire for Adaptive and Principled Resource Allocation:** There's a clear need for a method that can intelligently allocate inference-time compute. A static strategy (always wide or always deep) is suboptimal for diverse problem types. An adaptive approach that can learn from the search process and external signals to adjust its strategy offers a more efficient and powerful way to utilize LLM capabilities.
4. **Enhancing LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** The overarching motivation is to further unlock and scale the reasoning and problem-solving abilities of LLMs without requiring costly retraining, thereby making them more capable agents for complex, multi-step tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is **Adaptive Branching Monte Carlo Tree Search (AB-MCTS)**, a novel inference-time framework designed to dynamically balance exploration ("going wider") and exploitation ("going deeper") in LLM-based problem-solving, particularly when external feedback is available.

**General MCTS Framework for LLM Answer Generation:**
The method uses a search tree, `T`, where each non-root node represents an LLM-generated answer. The goal is to populate `T` with high-scoring answers within a limited budget of LLM calls (`n_nodes`). Each iteration involves three steps, similar to standard MCTS:
1. **Selection:** A node `N` is chosen for expansion.
2. **Expansion:** A new answer is generated from `N` (either a fresh candidate if `N` is the root, or a refinement of `N`'s answer if `N` is a non-root node), creating `N_new` as a child.
3. **Score Backup:** The score of `N_new` is propagated upwards through `T`.

**Key Innovations for Adaptive Branching:**

1. **Unbounded Branching and Non-Leaf Expansion:** Unlike standard MCTS, AB-MCTS relaxes the constraint that only leaf nodes are expanded and that each node can only be expanded once. This allows for repeated sampling from the same prompt (effectively "unbounded branching") and revisiting existing nodes for further refinement.
2. **The GEN Node:** To explicitly manage the action of generating new child nodes (going wider), a special **GEN node** is introduced as a child to every regular node `N`. When the GEN node of `N` is selected, it triggers the generation of a *new* child node for `N`, reflecting an exploration step.
3. **Thompson Sampling for Node Selection:** The decision of whether to "go wider" (select a GEN node) or "go deeper" (select an existing child node for further refinement) is made adaptively using **Thompson Sampling**. This Bayesian approach draws scores from posterior predictive distributions for each potential action (GEN or existing child) and selects the action with the highest sampled score. This replaces UCT (Upper Confidence Trees) scores, which are not suitable for dynamic arm generation.

**Two Variants of AB-MCTS:**

The paper proposes two distinct algorithms for modeling and calculating the posterior predictive distributions required for Thompson sampling:

1. **AB-MCTS-M (Mixed Model):**
 - **Modeling:** At each node `N` during the selection step, a *node-specific mixed linear model* is fitted. Each subtree under `N`'s existing children, `T_sub(N_j)`, is treated as a distinct group `j`. The GEN node (`a_0`) is treated as a newly introduced group without direct observations.
 - **Equation:** `r_N_new = α_j + σ_y ϵ_N_new`, where `α_j = μ_α + σ_α ϵ_j`. `α_j` captures the base solution quality, and `σ_y ϵ_N_new` is per-instance noise. `μ_α` and `σ_α` are shared parameters across all groups, allowing "statistical strength" to be shared.
 - **Posterior Updates:** Priors are placed on hyperparameters (`μ_α`, `σ_α`, `σ_y`), and Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) is used to sample from their posterior distribution. The GEN node's `α_0` is inferred from the posterior of `μ_α` and `σ_α`, leading to higher uncertainty and encouraging exploration.
 - **Score Backup:** Observed scores are added to the histories of the newly generated node and its ancestors, influencing the shared parameters of the mixed model.

2. **AB-MCTS-A (Node Aggregation):**
 - **Modeling:** This variant is simpler and more computationally lightweight, akin to standard UCT-based MCTS but with dynamic branching. There are no shared model parameters among different actions.
 - **CONT Node:** A special **CONT node** is introduced at the same tree level as all GEN nodes. This node represents the action of *continuing refinement* from the current answer at node `N` by selecting an *existing* child. This explicitly separates generating new answers (GEN) from refining existing ones (CONT).
 - **Posterior Updates:** Each node's score probability is modeled using exponential family distributions with conjugate priors, allowing for efficient, analytical posterior updates. Two sub-variants are presented:
 - **Gaussian:** Uses a normal-inverse-χ² prior for unbounded scores.
 - **Beta:** Uses a Beta prior for scores normalized to.
 - **Score Backup:** The score of an expanded node is backed up to the GEN node that led to its expansion, and then to the GEN node's ancestors (which include generated nodes and CONT nodes). This updates the parameters of the prior probability distributions directly associated with these nodes.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methods were evaluated on complex coding and ML engineering tasks: LiveCodeBench, CodeContest, ARC-AGI, and MLE-Bench, using frontier models like GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3. The performance metric was typically success rate (Pass@1 or Pass@2), and a maximum generation budget (LLM API calls) of 128 (2^7) or 512 (2^9) was used. Baselines included Repeated Sampling (best-of-n), Sequential Refinement (pure exploitation), and Standard MCTS (with fixed branching factor 5, inspired by LATS).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations across diverse benchmarks and LLMs consistently demonstrate the superior performance and adaptability of AB-MCTS compared to established baselines.

1. **Overall Superiority:**
 - AB-MCTS variants (AB-MCTS-M, AB-MCTS-A Gaussian, AB-MCTS-A Beta) consistently achieved higher average ranks across all experimental settings (Table 1), outperforming Repeated Sampling, Sequential Refinement, and Standard MCTS.
 - AB-MCTS-M, in particular, demonstrated robust performance, securing the best average rank of 2.3 across GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 evaluations on LiveCodeBench, CodeContest, and ARC-AGI, and also showing strong results on MLE-Bench (Table 2).

2. **Performance on Competitive Programming (LiveCodeBench, CodeContest):**
 - On both LiveCodeBench and CodeContest with GPT-4o (Figure 4), AB-MCTS algorithms generally outperformed baselines. On LiveCodeBench, AB-MCTS started to pull ahead even with smaller budgets (2^3). On CodeContest, its superiority became more pronounced at larger budgets (2^5 and beyond).
 - With DeepSeek-V3 (Appendix Figure 8), while Standard MCTS performed relatively well on LiveCodeBench, AB-MCTS methods still achieved comparable or superior success rates, especially on CodeContest.

3. **Performance on Abstract Reasoning (ARC-AGI):**
 - ARC-AGI is a challenging benchmark where repeated sampling is known to be a strong baseline, emphasizing broad exploration. AB-MCTS achieved performance comparable to repeated sampling (Figure 4).
 - Crucially, when the generation budget was significantly increased up to 512 LLM calls (Figure 6), AB-MCTS methods continued to show substantial performance improvement, whereas repeated sampling's improvement rate began to plateau. This indicates AB-MCTS's superior ability to scale effectively and direct its search towards promising branches at larger computational scales.

4. **Performance on Machine Learning Engineering (MLE-Bench):**
 - On MLE-Bench, which features practical ML tasks, the best-performing baseline varied significantly across different competitions (Table 2, Figure 10). This variability underscores that different tasks benefit from different exploration-exploitation trade-offs.
 - In contrast, AB-MCTS-M consistently delivered strong performance across all three MLE-Bench tasks, demonstrating its inherent strength in adapting its search strategy to varying problem structures.

5. **Analysis of Search Behavior (Width vs. Depth):**
 - Quantitative analysis of search tree shapes (Figure 5, Figure 9) revealed that AB-MCTS methods tend to generate *wider* trees compared to Standard MCTS. This is attributed to their adaptive decision-making, allowing them to select the GEN node (exploring wider) from any existing node, providing more flexible exploration.
 - Despite favoring wider exploration, AB-MCTS also performs strongly on benchmarks where sequential refinement (deep exploration) excels, suggesting its capability to effectively identify and exploit promising branches by refining existing solutions. This adaptability allows it to dynamically combine the strengths of both exploration and exploitation.

6. **Efficiency and Robustness:**
 - AB-MCTS variants could reach the peak performance of repeated sampling substantially earlier in many cases (Figure 4), demonstrating more efficient use of the generation budget.
 - Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (Appendix A.6) showed that AB-MCTS is robust to the initialization of its prior hyperparameters, with performance primarily driven by data-driven posterior updates during the search.
 - An ablation study on Standard MCTS's fixed branching factor (Appendix C.8) highlighted its sensitivity to this hyperparameter, further emphasizing the advantage of AB-MCTS's dynamic approach.

7. **Multi-LLM Extension (Appendix D):**
 - A preliminary evaluation of Multi-LLM AB-MCTS on ARC-AGI-2, using a combination of o4-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and DeepSeek-R1-0528, showed further performance improvements over single-LLM AB-MCTS and repeated sampling (Figure 13).
 - Analysis (Figure 14, 15, 16) indicated effective collaboration between different LLMs, with more proficient models allocated to easier problems and synergistic interactions occurring where one LLM's incorrect solution provided useful hints for another to refine.

In summary, AB-MCTS successfully integrates a principled Bayesian decision-making process into an MCTS framework, enabling LLMs to dynamically allocate inference-time compute to either explore diverse new solutions or exploit promising existing ones. This adaptive strategy results in superior and more robust performance on complex tasks compared to fixed or purely explorative/exploitative approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Adaptive Branching Monte Carlo Tree Search (AB-MCTS) represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) inference-time scaling, with substantial potential impact on how LLMs are utilized for complex problem-solving.

**Significance:**

1. **Principled Integration of Exploration and Exploitation:** AB-MCTS offers a principled, Bayesian framework for dynamically balancing "going wider" (exploration) and "going deeper" (exploitation) during LLM inference. This is a critical improvement over prior methods, which either focused solely on one aspect (e.g., repeated sampling for exploration) or used static, fixed-width search strategies (e.g., standard MCTS). The ability to adaptively adjust the search strategy based on real-time feedback is a key innovation.
2. **Addressing Unbounded Branching:** The paper successfully tackles the challenge of unbounded branching inherent in LLM generation, where a single prompt can yield a theoretically infinite number of diverse outputs. By integrating the "GEN node" and dynamic branching decisions, AB-MCTS effectively harnesses this diversity, which is crucial for achieving high performance in complex tasks.
3. **Robustness and Adaptability:** The empirical results demonstrate that AB-MCTS consistently outperforms baselines across a wide range of tasks (coding, abstract reasoning, ML engineering) and with different frontier models (GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3). This robustness, especially on MLE-Bench where optimal baseline strategies varied, highlights its adaptability to diverse problem characteristics without extensive hyperparameter tuning.
4. **Efficiency in Inference-Time Compute:** While search algorithms incur overhead, AB-MCTS proves to be more efficient in many cases, reaching peak performance earlier or achieving higher ultimate performance than repeated sampling, especially at larger computational budgets. This implies a more intelligent allocation of expensive LLM API calls.
5. **Foundation for Future AI Agents:** AB-MCTS provides a robust mechanism for LLMs to act as more intelligent and adaptive agents. Its ability to iterate, reflect, and refine solutions based on external feedback closely mimics human problem-solving processes, paving the way for more capable and autonomous AI systems in domains like software engineering, scientific discovery, and complex decision-making.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Enhanced LLM Problem-Solving Capabilities:** By optimizing inference-time computation, AB-MCTS can significantly boost the success rates of LLMs on tasks requiring multi-step reasoning, code generation, and iterative refinement. This directly translates to more reliable and powerful LLM applications.
2. **Broader Applicability of LLMs:** The framework's adaptability across various problem types means LLMs can be deployed more effectively in new and complex domains where external validation is available, reducing the need for domain-specific fine-tuning of search strategies.
3. **Catalyst for Agentic AI Systems:** The principles of adaptive exploration and exploitation are fundamental to agentic AI. AB-MCTS offers a concrete, performant approach that can be integrated into larger agent architectures, enabling them to navigate complex environments, debug solutions, and learn from interactions more effectively. The preliminary work on Multi-LLM AB-MCTS further expands this by showing how different LLMs can collaboratively contribute to solving problems, mimicking team problem-solving.
4. **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper highlights the importance of dynamic decision-making in tree search for LLMs. This could inspire further research into more sophisticated Bayesian models for decision-making, novel ways to incorporate diverse types of feedback, and optimizations for parallelizing adaptive search strategies. The exploration of collaborative multi-LLM search is particularly intriguing, suggesting avenues for combining the strengths of various models.
5. **Practical Tool for Developers:** With the code publicly available, developers and researchers can readily implement and extend AB-MCTS in their own LLM-powered applications, potentially leading to immediate improvements in task performance and efficiency.

While the current limitations include the reliance on a reliable score evaluator and a focus on API call counts as the sole cost metric, these are common challenges in AI research. Addressing these in future work, perhaps by integrating more nuanced cost factors or developing robust reward models, will further solidify AB-MCTS's position as a foundational technique for pushing the Pareto frontier of LLM inference-time scaling.
