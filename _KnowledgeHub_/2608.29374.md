# Think, Look, and Revise: Inconsistency-Aware Visual Self-Correction in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> ReVISE is a verification-driven framework for tool-augmented multimodal reasoning. It trains MLLMs on a reflection-rich cold-start dataset of five trajectory types, then applies GRPO with self-correction and grounding rewards, so the model validates tool outputs against the image, reissues refined tool queries, or falls back to intrinsic perception. On Qwen2.5-VL it lifts average accuracy to 73.15% at 7B.

## Problem

- Existing tool-augmented MLLMs run open-loop: intermediate detector or zoom outputs are trusted as correct, so perception errors propagate into the final answer.
- Supervised fine-tuning data for tool use lacks trajectories that show failure detection and revision, giving models no pattern for distrusting a returned observation.
- Reinforcement learning objectives reward only final-answer accuracy, incentivizing tool calling over verification, which weakens intrinsic grounding when tools are unavailable or unreliable.

## Method

- ==Cold-start SFT== on Gemini-2.5-Pro-synthesized multi-turn trajectories spanning five verification patterns: positive tool-verify, tool-retry, negative tool-verify with intrinsic fallback, and positive or negative tool-free verify.
- GRPO with a **self-correction reward** keyed to an emitted re-examine cue, scoring +1 when reflection precedes a correct answer and -1 when reflection still ends wrong.
- A **grounding reward** summing static ==box IoU== with ==r_IoU++==, which credits only positive localization improvement from the initial box prediction to the final refined one.

## Results

- ReVISE-7B averages **73.15%** across nine benchmarks, above ReVPT-7B at **71.81%**, with CountBench **92.89%** versus CodeDance-7B **91.20%** and CV-Bench Count **79.12%** versus **74.11%**.
- ReVISE-3B averages **67.89%** versus ReVPT-3B **66.77%**, gaining **11.28** points on CV-Bench and **9.94** on BLINK over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct backbone.
- Ablations: the correction reward lifts BLINK-HARD from **56.80%** to **61.20%** and MMVP to **67.30%**; the grounding reward lifts CV-Bench from **79.24%** to **82.10%**; combined reaches **83.83%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verification is learnable rather than a decoding heuristic: supervising inconsistency detection in trajectories and rewarding it in RL beats inference-time retry loops.
> - Rewarding improvement in localization across a trajectory, not just absolute IoU, gives a dense signal for self-correction that pure accuracy rewards cannot supply.
> - Specializing for fine-grained grounding costs knowledge-heavy performance, and recovery stays bounded by the tool ceiling when detections are ambiguous or missing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in various vision tasks, including grounding, counting, and spatial relation understanding. Recent advances in training methodologies, particularly the combination of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), have led to performance improvements in models like Qwen VL and InternVL. However, these models often rely on purely textual reasoning chains, which can lead to insufficient visual interpretability at intermediate steps.

To address these perceptual limitations, the field has increasingly explored tool-integrated multimodal reasoning. This approach augments MLLMs with external vision tools, such as object detection, zooming, and cropping, to provide visual evidence for intermediate decisions. Existing tool-augmented methods, including ReVPT, Thyme, and CodeDance, typically focus on how to invoke these tools and incorporate their outputs into reasoning traces.

This work identifies a critical gap in the current tool-augmented MLLM landscape: the lack of mechanisms for verifying the reliability of tool outputs. Existing systems often operate in an "open-loop" manner, blindly trusting intermediate tool-derived evidence. This can lead to the propagation of errors if a tool provides noisy, imprecise, or misaligned outputs, limiting the model's ability to detect inconsistencies and recover from failures. Furthermore, this over-reliance on external tools can diminish the MLLM's intrinsic visual reasoning capabilities.

The present research aims to advance tool-augmented MLLMs by introducing explicit verification and self-correction mechanisms, moving beyond simple tool invocation to a more robust, inconsistency-aware reasoning paradigm.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to equip Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with the ability to verify and dynamically recover from errors in tool-augmented reasoning. This addresses the limitation of existing tool-augmented MLLMs that operate in an open-loop manner, often propagating incorrect intermediate tool outputs without detection or correction.

The motivation for ReVISE stems from several identified issues:

- **Perceptual Bottlenecks in MLLMs:** While MLLMs are proficient in reasoning, they often struggle with perception-heavy tasks requiring precise visual grounding or verification of visual evidence. External tools are integrated to address these, but their outputs are not always reliable.
- **Lack of Tool Output Verification:** Current tool-augmented MLLMs primarily focus on *how* to use tools to obtain answers, rather than *how* to evaluate and revise unreliable tool outputs. This leads to blind trust in intermediate tool results.
- **Error Propagation:** If a tool misidentifies a visual target or provides imprecise information, the error propagates through the reasoning chain, leading to incorrect final answers. For example, a generic "photo" detection instead of a specific "dark cave wall decor photo" can lead to spatial reasoning failures.
- **Deficiencies in Training Data:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) datasets, which form the foundation for MLLM behaviors, largely lack explicit supervision for detecting and correcting tool failures.
- **Limitations of RL Objectives:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) objectives in current systems often emphasize only final answer accuracy, without incentivizing intermediate verification or revision behaviors. This fails to train models to reflect on and improve their reasoning process.
- **Over-reliance on External Tools:** Without self-correction capabilities, models can become overly dependent on tools, diminishing their intrinsic visual reasoning abilities and causing performance degradation when tools are unavailable or unreliable.

To address these motivations, ReVISE proposes a framework that:
1. Enables MLLMs to critically evaluate intermediate visual observations.
2. Triggers dynamic self-correction when tool feedback is noisy, contradictory, or missing.
3. Rejects noisy observations and refines tool execution to acquire correct visual matching before generating a final answer.
4. Incorporates mechanisms for validating tool-derived evidence against the model's direct interpretation of the image.

The framework aims to foster reflective behaviors within MLLMs, allowing them to reformulate queries when visual mismatches occur and fall back to intrinsic grounding when external tools are unreliable.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The ReVISE framework implements a verification-driven approach for tool-augmented multimodal reasoning, formulated as a sequential decision process. This process is optimized through both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) using reflection and grounding-oriented rewards.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Reasoning is conceptualized as a multi-step inferential process. An MLLM, denoted as $\pi$, iteratively makes decisions and, if required, invokes external visual tools to gather evidence before producing a final answer. For an input $(i, q)$ (visual input and textual query), the reasoning follows a think–execute–feedback trajectory: $\tau = ((s_1, a_1, o_1), \ldots, (s_T, a_T, o_T))$, where $s_t$ is the state (including image, query, and history), $a_t$ is an action (tool invocation or terminal answer), and $o_t$ is the observation from tool execution.

#### 3.2. Toolset Collection

The framework integrates a specialized suite of four visual perception tools:
- **Object Detection:** Utilizes LLMDet, an open-vocabulary object detector, to provide bounding boxes and confidence scores for queried objects.
- **ZoomIn:** Crops and resizes a specified bounding box region in an image, allowing for fine-grained inspection. The tool caps the zoom factor at 4.0 and adjusts aspect ratio.
- **Edge Detection:** Applies OpenCV's Scharr operator to compute horizontal and vertical gradients, generating a binary edge map for boundary-aware spatial reasoning.
- **Depth Estimation:** Employs Depth Anything V2 to estimate scene depth, assisting in interpreting spatial relationships and occlusions.

#### 3.3. Training Data Curation

A high-quality, "reflection-rich" supervised dataset is constructed to teach the model verification and self-correction behaviors. Gemini-2.5-pro is used to synthesize multi-turn tool-interaction trajectories, followed by manual filtering to ensure quality. The dataset includes five types of trajectories, illustrated in Figure 2:
- **(i) Positive Tool-Verify:** Tool output is correct and verified by the MLLM before answering.
- **(ii) Tool-Retry:** Initial tool output is noisy, prompting the MLLM to detect inconsistency and re-invoke the tool with refined arguments or a different tool.
- **(iii) Negative Tool-Verify:** Tool fails (e.g., missing detections), and the MLLM verifies the failure, falling back to intrinsic perception.
- **(iv) Positive Tool-Free Verify:** Initial intrinsic reasoning is correct, and the MLLM confirms it without tool use.
- **(v) Negative Tool-Free Verify:** Initial intrinsic reasoning is incorrect, and the MLLM performs verification and revision without tool use.

#### 3.4. Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The MLLM's parameters ($\theta$) are initially optimized using this curated dataset. SFT teaches the model basic patterns of tool use, verification, and self-correction by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of actions given the state, based on the optimal trajectories. This provides a strong initialization for sequential visual tool use and self-correction.

#### 3.5. Policy Optimization via Reflection and Grounding Rewards

Following SFT, the model policy is further optimized using Reinforcement Learning (RL), building on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This stage introduces specific reward components tailored to verification and spatial refinement. The task-specific reward $r_k$ is a sum of four sub-rewards:

- **Answer-Accuracy Reward ($r_{accuracy}$):** A binary reward (1 for correct, -1 for incorrect) based on the final answer's correctness.
- **Reasoning Format Reward ($r_{format}$):** Ensures adherence to predefined generation formats (e.g., `<think>`, `<tool_call>`, `<answer>` tags). A reward of 1 for correct format, -1 otherwise.
- **Self-Correction Reward ($r_{correction}$):** Encourages dynamic verification and revision. A reward of 1 is given if the final answer is correct and the reasoning trace includes a "re-examine" token, indicating an internal reflection phase. A reward of -1 is given if the token is emitted but the answer is incorrect, and 0 otherwise.
- **Grounding Reward ($r_{grounding}$):** Improves intrinsic grounding capability by penalizing imprecise bounding box predictions. This reward consists of two parts:
 - $r_{IoU}$: The mean Intersection over Union (IoU) between the final predicted boxes and ground-truth boxes.
 - $r_{IoU++}$: Measures localization improvement over the reasoning trajectory, rewarding only positive IoU gains from initial to final box predictions. If no boxes are produced or updated, $r_{IoU++} = 0$. The total grounding reward is $r_{IoU} + r_{IoU++}$.

The GRPO objective maximizes a clipped surrogate objective with group-normalized advantages, encouraging reward improvement while stabilizing updates around a reference policy.

### 4. Main findings and results

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

Experiments utilized the Qwen-VL series (Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B) as the backbone model, initialized with pre-trained weights for SFT and further optimized with RL. The training data was compiled from SAT, TACO, and TallyQA datasets, filtered to retain challenging samples that the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model answered incorrectly. Ground-truth bounding boxes for the grounding reward were generated using Gemini-2.5-Pro.

For evaluation, the model was tested on a diverse set of multimodal benchmarks:
- **Perception-intensive tasks:** CountBench (counting), CVBench (spatial reasoning), BLINK, BLINK-HARD (perception tokens), MMVP.
- **General multimodal evaluations:** MMSTAR, MMMU, MMBench.
- **Math reasoning:** MathVista.

Baselines included proprietary MLLMs (Gemini-2.0-Flash, GPT-4.1), strong open-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct), and recent tool-augmented methods (TACO, ReVPT, Thyme, CodeDance). Internal baselines (Qwen2.5-VL-SFT and Qwen2.5-VL-SFT-GRPO) were also included to isolate the impact of the proposed data and reward design.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

Table 1 demonstrates that ReVISE consistently improves performance across a wide range of perception-intensive benchmarks, for both 3B and 7B model variants, compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL and its SFT/GRPO variants.

- **3B Model:** ReVISE-3B showed improvements over Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct on CountBench (5.86% gain), CVBench (11.28% gain), and BLINK (9.94% gain). These gains were particularly notable in tasks requiring explicit grounding and tool verification, such as counting, spatial/depth understanding, and relation understanding. Performance degradations were observed on knowledge-intensive MMMU and MathVista.
- **7B Model:** ReVISE-7B generally outperformed other tool-augmented methods like ReVPT and CodeDance on datasets such as CountBench and several CVBench subtasks. This indicates the benefits of verifying intermediate evidence and refining spatial predictions in multi-step inference. Small performance drops were noted on BLINK (-4.04%), MMVP (-0.09%), and MMSTAR (-0.83%).
- **Domain Specificity:** The study noted that ReVISE's focus on explicit visual grounding leads to stronger gains in perception-intensive tasks. MMMU and MathVista, which demand more abstract problem solving and external knowledge, showed less improvement or slight degradation, suggesting a specialization effect.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

- **Effect of Model Size:** Both 3B and 7B models benefited from the ReVISE framework. The smaller 3B backbone showed more substantial gains, suggesting that tool invocation and the grounding/reflection rewards effectively compensate for its more limited intrinsic reasoning capacity. The stronger 7B model also improved, demonstrating that verification-driven tool use and self-correction are beneficial across scales.
- **Tool Usage Analysis (Figure 3):** Comparing tool invocation frequency on CVBench, the study found that SFT stage models frequently invoked Object Detection. In contrast, the RL stage models exhibited a more diverse and optimized tool invocation policy. The error recovery rate, defined as the proportion of SFT-stage tool-induced failures rectified by the RL model, showed that self-correction mechanisms led to more efficient and robust tool use. However, some failures remained unrecoverable due to inherent ambiguities, low-resolution regions, or limited tool coverage.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 2)

- **Importance of ReVISE Cold-Start Dataset:** Comparing "Ours w/o RL" with "REVPT Cold-Start w/o RL*", the ReVISE SFT model, trained on data incorporating explicit verification and revision patterns, achieved notable gains (e.g., +3% on CVBench, +2.06% on MMVP). This suggests that training models to verify and reflect, rather than blindly trusting, improves reasoning performance. Some performance drop on MMSTAR was attributed to differences in reasoning trajectories.
- **Validation of Reward Design:** The ablation study systematically evaluated the contribution of each reward component.
 - Adding the `r_correction` reward alone improved performance on perception-heavy tasks (e.g., BLINK-HARD: 56.80% to 61.20%, MMVP: 62.85% to 67.30%).
 - Introducing the `r_grounding` reward further benefited spatial reasoning benchmarks (e.g., CountBench: 73.15% to 74.45%, CVBench: 79.24% to 82.10%).
 - Combining both rewards yielded the best results, indicating that spatial grounding and reflective reasoning are complementary. RL training curves showed stable reward increases, confirming the effectiveness of the reward design.
- **Robustness to Noisy Grounding Supervision (Appendix E.1):** Manual inspection showed 98% correctness in Gemini-generated bounding boxes. Experiments with 5% Gaussian jitter on bounding boxes caused only a minor degradation of 0.63 points on average, indicating robustness to moderate noise. A 10% perturbation led to a larger, though empirically less frequent, drop.
- **Effect of Mixed-Domain Training (Appendix E.2):** Augmenting SFT and RL with math and science reasoning data (1K tool-free verification trajectories for SFT, 10K for RL) significantly recovered performance on MMMU and MathVista (by 1.71 and 4.35 points, respectively), while preserving strong CVBench performance. This suggests that the observed performance degradation on general reasoning tasks was due to domain specialization and can be mitigated.
- **Effect of Cold-Start SFT (Appendix E.3):** Direct application of RL without cold-start SFT resulted in substantially worse performance. SFT was found to be crucial for establishing structured multi-turn interaction protocols, while RL further refined these behaviors.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results (Figure 4)

Qualitative comparisons illustrated ReVISE's ability to self-correct where previous tool-based MLLMs failed. For instance, ReVISE successfully rejected ambiguous initial detections and actively invoked `zoom_in` to reliably locate objects. In scenarios where initial `zoom_in` crops missed crucial context, ReVISE recognized the error and refined bounding box arguments to capture all target elements simultaneously, leading to accurate spatial grounding.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research introduces ReVISE, a framework that enhances the reliability of tool-assisted reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) through visual self-correction. This work addresses a critical limitation in existing tool-augmented MLLMs, which often operate in an "open-loop" manner, implicitly trusting and propagating potentially erroneous tool outputs.

The significance of ReVISE lies in several aspects:

- **Improved Robustness:** By introducing explicit mechanisms for tool-output verification and dynamic error recovery, ReVISE enhances the robustness of MLLMs, particularly in perception-intensive tasks. This allows models to identify inconsistencies between tool-derived evidence and the visual query, leading to more accurate and reliable final predictions.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** The framework fosters a more reflective reasoning process. Models learn to critically evaluate intermediate visual observations, reformulate tool queries, or fall back to intrinsic visual grounding when external tools prove unreliable. This moves MLLMs towards more autonomous visual reasoning, rather than merely acting as tool callers.
- **Novel Training Paradigm:** The construction of a reflection-rich supervised training dataset, coupled with correction-oriented reinforcement learning rewards (including self-correction and grounding rewards), represents a novel approach to training MLLMs for error detection and recovery. This provides a blueprint for developing more sophisticated and trustworthy multimodal agents.
- **Better Generalization on Perception-heavy Tasks:** Experimental results demonstrate consistent improvements on challenging perception-heavy benchmarks, such as counting, spatial reasoning, and depth estimation. This suggests that the verification-driven approach can lead to better generalization and performance on tasks requiring precise visual understanding.

**Potential Impact:**

The development of inconsistency-aware MLLMs has several potential impacts:

- **Increased Reliability in Real-world Applications:** In fields requiring high accuracy and trustworthiness, such as autonomous systems, medical image analysis, or intricate data interpretation, MLLMs equipped with self-correction capabilities could perform more reliably and safely.
- **Foundation for More Sophisticated Agents:** The ability to verify and self-correct is a crucial step towards building more intelligent and autonomous multimodal agents that can adapt to unexpected tool failures or ambiguous visual inputs.
- **Advancement in Multimodal AI Research:** This work establishes a new direction for research in tool-augmented MLLMs, shifting the focus from mere tool invocation to critical evaluation and iterative refinement. Future research can explore adaptive revision policies, stronger perception modules, and extending verification to video understanding and interactive agents.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

Despite its advancements, the approach has limitations:
- **Reliance on Synthetic Data and Proprietary Models:** The framework currently relies on high-quality synthetic trajectories and bounding boxes generated by proprietary models (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro). This might limit robustness in open-domain scenarios if real-world reasoning dynamics are not fully captured.
- **Performance Trade-off:** While optimizing for fine-grained spatial grounding, the model exhibits a slight performance degradation on abstract, knowledge-intensive tasks (e.g., MMMU, MathVista). This suggests a current specialization bias that might require domain-adaptive strategies or broader training data.
- **Bounded by Tool Capabilities:** The policy is fundamentally limited by the capabilities of the predefined external vision tools. Ambiguous or catastrophic tool failures can still derail the revision process if the self-correction mechanism cannot provide sufficient alternatives or context.
- **Scalability for Larger Models:** Due to computational constraints, RL evaluations were primarily conducted on 3B and 7B models. How these verification behaviors scale to, or interact with, the intrinsic capabilities of much larger MLLMs remains an area for future investigation.

Future research directions include exploring iterative self-correction strategies, adaptive revision policies guided by model uncertainty, integrating stronger perception modules, and extending verification-driven reasoning to video understanding and interactive multimodal agents.
