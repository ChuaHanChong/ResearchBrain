# VerlTool: Towards Holistic Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> VERLTOOL introduces a unified, open-source framework for Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT), addressing fragmentation and inefficiency in current systems. It achieves near 2x speedup in rollout execution through asynchronous processing and demonstrates competitive performance across six diverse tasks, including mathematical reasoning, knowledge QA, and visual reasoning, while supporting multimodal tool integration.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLMs operates in closed, single-turn settings, limiting interaction with the external world.
- Prior LLM tool-use approaches rely on supervised imitation learning, lacking adaptive agentic autonomy from environmental feedback.
- The Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT) landscape suffers from fragmented, task-specific codebases, synchronous execution bottlenecks leading to inefficient resource utilization, and limited support for multimodal tool interactions.

## Method

- VERLTOOL provides a modular, open-source framework extending RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to ==Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT)==, allowing multi-turn, feedback-rich interactions.
- It introduces an ==asynchronous rollout mechanism== to eliminate synchronous execution bottlenecks, enabling independent tool interactions for each trajectory and improving computational efficiency.
- The framework implements a ==plugin-based design== for tool management with standardized APIs, supporting diverse tools and multimodal interactions, alongside a consistent tokenization strategy.

## Results

- VERLTOOL-trained models achieved competitive performance, matching or surpassing specialized baselines across **six** diverse ARLT tasks, including mathematical reasoning, knowledge QA, and software engineering.
- The asynchronous rollout mechanism delivered substantial efficiency gains, resulting in nearly **2x speedup** in rollout execution and improved computational resource utilization.
- The framework demonstrated robust support for multimodal tools (vision processing and system-level tools, beyond text-centric RL), and agents exhibited emergent behaviors such as self-correction, iterative refinement, and strategic tool selection in complex, multi-turn scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT) necessitates combining feedback-driven learning with external tool interaction to foster robust agentic behaviors.
> - Infrastructure limitations - fragmented codebases, synchronous execution, single-modality support - hinder ARLT adoption; asynchronous rollout mechanisms fix the synchronous bottleneck by enabling concurrent tool interactions and reducing idle computation time.
> - Tool usage patterns in ARLT are highly dynamic and task-specific, with effective deployment requiring appropriate reward design and careful initialization strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly large language models (LLMs), has witnessed unprecedented advancements in recent years, largely driven by increasingly powerful models and sophisticated training paradigms. A central driver of this progress, particularly in enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities, has been **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. This paradigm enables LLMs to refine their long-context reasoning through feedback, fostering emergent cognitive behaviors such as reflection, backtracking, and multi-step reasoning. However, current RLVR systems predominantly operate in a "closed, single-turn setting" without direct interaction with the external world, effectively acting as a "brain in a vat." This isolation limits their practical applicability and can lead to brittle behaviors like overthinking or hallucination.

A parallel and complementary line of research has focused on **augmenting LLMs with tool-use capabilities**. Early efforts, such as TOOLFORMER and OPENHANDS, primarily relied on supervised training on synthetic tool-use data. While these approaches enabled LLMs to interact with external resources like code interpreters, search engines, or APIs, they were largely based on imitation learning, lacking the "agentic autonomy" to learn adaptively from environmental feedback.

VERLTOOL emerges at the intersection of these two critical research directions, addressing a significant gap by pioneering the paradigm of **Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT)**. ARLT aims to combine the strengths of RLVR (feedback-driven learning, long-context reasoning) with the practical utility of tool use (external interaction, access to real-world information). This integration transforms LLM training into a multi-turn, feedback-rich process, promising improved efficiency, reduced token usage, and more robust agentic behaviors.

The authors identify several system-level challenges that have hindered the widespread adoption and innovation within the nascent ARLT landscape:
1. **Fragmentation:** Existing ARLT codebases are often "task-specific," tightly coupling tool interaction logic with the core RL training loop. This makes them difficult to extend, reproduce, or adapt to new tools or domains.
2. **Synchronous Execution Bottlenecks:** Multi-tool trajectories often involve asynchronous tool responses, leading to idle waiting times and inefficient utilization of computational resources, especially GPUs, in synchronous batch-by-batch execution models.
3. **Limited Extensibility and Multimodal Support:** Most existing RL frameworks for LLMs are narrowly focused on text. The emergence of multimodal reasoning agents necessitates unified handling of diverse tool outputs, including images, videos, and structured data, which current frameworks largely lack.

VERLTOOL positions itself as a principled, open-source, user-friendly, and efficient framework designed to systematically overcome these barriers. By building upon and aligning with VERL (an established RLHF framework), VERLTOOL extends the capabilities of RLVR to robustly support multi-turn, stateful agentic training with diverse tool use, including multimodal interactions. It directly contrasts with other agentic RL training frameworks like OPENRLHF, SLIME, AREAL, and SKYRL, which either offer limited tool support, lack multimodal capabilities, or introduce significant deployment overheads (as highlighted in Table 1 of the paper). In essence, VERLTOOL aims to provide the foundational infrastructure that unifies and accelerates research in LLMs as versatile, interactive, and autonomous agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VERLTOOL stems from the inherent limitations of existing approaches to enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs) with reasoning and interaction capabilities. While Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has significantly advanced LLM reasoning, it remains confined to single-turn, closed-world interactions. Similarly, previous tool-use augmentations for LLMs, primarily based on supervised imitation learning, lack the adaptive and agentic autonomy crucial for complex, open-ended environments.

The emergence of Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT) seeks to bridge this gap, allowing LLMs to actively engage with external tools and learn from feedback in a multi-turn fashion. However, the authors identify critical system-level challenges that impede the progress and broader adoption of ARLT:
- **Fragmentation:** Current ARLT solutions are often bespoke, "task-specific codebases" designed for a single tool or domain. This leads to redundant development efforts, difficulty in reproducing results, and a high barrier to entry for researchers wanting to explore new tools or multi-tool scenarios.
- **Inefficiency and Bottlenecks:** Multi-turn tool interactions are inherently asynchronous, with varying response times from different tools. Existing synchronous execution models result in "idle waiting time" and "inefficient utilization of computational resources," particularly GPUs, during rollouts.
- **Limited Extensibility and Multimodality:** Most RL frameworks for language models are text-centric. The growing need for agents to process multimodal observations (images, videos) from tools is inadequately supported, hindering the development of more sophisticated, general-purpose agents.

To address these significant limitations, the key objectives of VERLTOOL are precisely articulated through its four core contributions:
1. **Upstream Alignment:** To simplify maintenance and accelerate framework evolution, VERLTOOL aims to inherit and remain compatible with VERL, an established RL training framework, ensuring a stable and continuously updated foundation.
2. **Unified Tool Management:** To overcome fragmentation and enhance extensibility, VERLTOOL seeks to provide a dedicated tool server with "standardized interaction APIs" that can support a diverse array of tools across various modalities (code execution, search, SQL, vision utilities). The goal is to enable the addition of new tools with minimal development overhead, requiring only "lightweight Python definitions."
3. **Asynchronous Rollouts:** To eliminate synchronous execution bottlenecks and improve efficiency, VERLTOOL aims to implement a fully "asynchronous rollout" mechanism. This design allows individual trajectories to interact with tool servers independently, thereby achieving significant speedups by reducing idle times.
4. **Diverse ARLT Task Support:** To demonstrate its generality and competitive performance, VERLTOOL's objective is to be comprehensively evaluated across "six diverse ARLT tasks" covering mathematical reasoning, knowledge QA, SQL generation, visual reasoning, web search, and software engineering.

In essence, VERLTOOL's overarching motivation is to provide a "holistic," "unified," and "modular" open-source infrastructure that makes Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use more accessible, efficient, and scalable for the research community, ultimately enabling the development of LLMs that can truly act as interactive and intelligent agents in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VERLTOOL proposes a systematic and modular framework for Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT), building upon the foundation of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The methodology is designed to overcome the fragmentation, inefficiency, and limited multimodal support prevalent in existing ARLT systems.

**Conceptual Foundation: Extending RLVR to ARLT**
The paper first formalizes RLVR, where a policy LLM ($\pi_\theta$) is optimized using a verifiable reward function ($R_\phi$) for single-turn outputs ($y$) given an input ($x$). The optimization typically employs algorithms like GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization).
VERLTOOL extends this to ARLT by introducing multi-turn trajectories: $\tau = \{a_0, o_0, \ldots, a_{n-1}, o_{n-1}, a_n\}$. Here, $a_i$ represents LLM-generated action tokens, and $o_i$ represents observation tokens returned by a tool call. A key distinction is the explicit handling of observation tokens, which are "off-policy" and therefore masked out during policy optimization to prevent destabilization, focusing the GRPO loss (Equation 5) only on the LLM's generated action tokens. Tool invocation is determined by specific stop tokens ($s \in S_k$) for each tool $T_k$.

**Framework Design: Modular and Decoupled Architecture**
VERLTOOL's core design philosophy is modularity and decoupling, consisting of two main components:
1. **VeRL Workflow:** Handles all reinforcement learning aspects, including multi-turn rollouts and actor updates. The LLM actor generates action sequences, some of which trigger tool interactions.
2. **Tool Server:** Manages tool execution. It receives tool-invoking actions from the VeRL Workflow, executes the appropriate tool, and returns observations.

These components communicate via a unified API, allowing for independent management and efficient interaction.

**Key Architectural Innovations:**

- **Asynchronous Rollout Design:** This is a crucial innovation to address efficiency bottlenecks. Unlike synchronous batch-based frameworks where tool calls block the entire batch, VERLTOOL enables each trajectory to interact with the Tool Server independently and immediately upon completing its action generation. This eliminates idle waiting times, allowing the actor and environment to evolve concurrently, and achieving "near 2× speedup" during rollout execution (Table 2).

- **Modular Tool-as-Plugin Design:** To combat fragmentation and enhance extensibility, VERLTOOL implements a plugin system. Each tool is developed as a subclass of a `BaseTool` interface, which defines methods like `parse_action` (to identify tool calls), `load_env` (to retrieve trajectory state), `conduct_action` (to execute the tool), `update_env`, and `delete_env`. This design significantly reduces the overhead for integrating new tools, requiring only a "lightweight Python definition file." The framework dynamically manages tool execution across threads or distributed workers.

- **Consistent Tokenization Strategy:** A practical challenge in multi-turn ARLT is the tokenization of tool observations alongside LLM actions. VERLTOOL adopts a strategy of tokenizing action and observation strings *separately* before appending their token sequences. This "On-Policy" approach prevents inconsistencies that can arise from joint tokenization (merging boundary symbols into different token IDs), ensuring stable alignment across multiple rollout turns (Figure 4).

- **Parallel Tool Server Backend:** To ensure high-throughput and scalable execution of diverse tools, the Tool Server offers two parallel execution backends:
 - **Multi-threading:** For lightweight tool calls, utilizing Python's `ThreadPoolExecutor`.
 - **Ray-based Asynchronous Execution:** For resource-intensive or computationally heavy tools, supporting distributed and fault-tolerant execution across machines or GPU nodes via Ray.

**Experimental Setup:**
VERLTOOL's methodology includes a comprehensive evaluation across six diverse ARLT tasks to demonstrate its adaptability and competitive performance:
- **VT-Math:** Mathematical reasoning with a Python interpreter (training on DeepMath, evaluating on MATH-500, OLYMPIAD, MINERVA, GSM8K, AMC, AIME24/25). Reward combines accuracy and tool usage incentives.
- **VT-Search:** Knowledge QA with a FAISS-based search retriever (training with E5 retriever on Wikipedia, evaluating on NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue, Bamboogle). Reward is accuracy-based.
- **VT-SQL:** Multi-turn NL2SQL generation with a SQL Executor (training on SkyRL-SQL, evaluating on SPIDER-1.0, SPIDER-DK, SPIDER-SYN). Reward is execution accuracy.
- **VT-VisualReasoner:** Visual reasoning with image operation tools (training on Pixel-Reasoner, evaluating on V-Star). Reward includes accuracy, curiosity, and penalty components.
- **VT-DeepSearch:** Agentic web search using Google Search API (training on SimpleDeepSearcher and Web-Sailor, evaluating on GAIA and HLE). Reward combines accuracy and tool-usage incentives.
- **VT-SWE:** Software engineering benchmark with bash terminal and code execution tools (training on R2E-Gym scaffold, evaluating on SWE-Verified). Reward is task completion accuracy.

These diverse tasks showcase VERLTOOL's ability to integrate text-based and multimodal tools (Table 8) and adapt various reward designs and interaction patterns, validating its claim as a holistic framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with VERLTOOL across six diverse Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT) tasks yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the framework's effectiveness, efficiency, and versatility.

#### 4.1. Competitive Performance Across Diverse ARLT Tasks

VERLTOOL-trained models consistently achieved performance comparable to, and often surpassed, specialized baselines across all six domains. This is a crucial validation of its unified framework approach, demonstrating that a single infrastructure can train competitive agents for varied tasks without requiring fragmented, task-specific codebases.
- **Mathematical Reasoning (VT-Math):** Achieved an average performance of 62.2% on mathematical benchmarks like AIME24, AMC23, and Olympiad Bench, outperforming several expert models and specialized fine-tuned LLMs such as Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct-TIR, SimpleRL-Zoo-7B, and ToRL-7B.
- **Knowledge QA (VT-Search):** Reached 45.9% accuracy on knowledge QA tasks (e.g., NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA), significantly outperforming Search-R1 (a specialized search agent) by 10.9%.
- **NL2SQL Generation (VT-SQL):** Matched or slightly exceeded the performance of specialized systems like SkyRL-SQL on SPIDER benchmarks, achieving 83.9% on the Dev split and 81.3% on the Realistic split.
- **Visual Reasoning (VT-VisualReasoner):** Achieved 82.7% accuracy on the V* Bench, a benchmark for MLLM visual search capabilities, showing strong performance in multimodal tasks.
- **Agentic Web Search (VT-DeepSearch):** Reached 34.0% on GAIA, a challenging benchmark for general AI assistants requiring real-time web search and multi-step reasoning.
- **Software Engineering (VT-SWE):** Achieved 19.5% on the SWE-Verified benchmark, significantly improving upon baselines like OpenHands-7B-Agent (11.0%) and SkyRL-v0 (14.6% with OpenHands-7B-Agent based).

#### 4.2. Robust Multimodal Tool Support and Extensibility

VERLTOOL's modular, plugin-based design proved highly effective in seamlessly integrating a wide array of tools and supporting diverse modalities. Beyond standard text-based tools like Python and SQL interpreters, local retrievers, and web search engines, the framework successfully incorporated visual processing tools for image and video manipulation (e.g., zoom, frame selection) and system-level tools like bash terminals and file operations. This demonstrates a significant advancement over existing single-modality RL frameworks, enabling complex multimodal workflows and agent behaviors previously unsupported.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Gains from Asynchronous Rollouts

The asynchronous rollout mechanism implemented in VERLTOOL effectively addressed the "synchronous execution bottlenecks." By processing tool calls independently per trajectory, the framework eliminated idle waiting times, leading to significant speedups. Experiments showed "near 2× speedup" in rollout execution (specifically 1.32x for Math-TIR, 1.22x for SQL, and 1.97x for DeepSearch), demonstrating improved throughput and computational resource utilization, particularly on GPUs.

#### 4.4. Dynamics of Task-Specific Tool Usage

The study revealed that tool usage patterns are highly task-dependent and evolve dynamically during training.
- **Task Variability:** Mathematical tasks typically required 1-4 tool interactions, while software engineering tasks often needed over 100 turns, reflecting the varying complexity and iterative nature of different domains.
- **Reward-Shaped Learning:** Models do not inherently develop effective tool-use capabilities; rather, "appropriate reward design and initialization strategies" are crucial. For instance, without sophisticated reward mechanisms in VT-VisualReasoner, tool action frequency could diminish to zero.
- **Memorization vs. Intrinsic Need:** In VT-SQL, tool usage decreased rapidly as the model learned to memorize results for simpler queries, reducing the need for explicit SQL executor verification. Conversely, in VT-DeepSearch, tool usage dramatically increased, as problem-solving fundamentally relied on continuous search capabilities, which cannot be easily memorized. This highlights how the intrinsic value of tool assistance directly shapes learning dynamics.

#### 4.5. Emergence of Sophisticated Agentic Behaviors

Agents trained within the VERLTOOL framework exhibited advanced agentic problem-solving behaviors, extending beyond simple function-calling. These included:
- **Self-correction:** Mathematical agents learned to verify computations and backtrack from errors.
- **Iterative Refinement:** Search agents developed strategies to refine queries based on retrieved information.
- **Strategic Tool Selection:** Agents demonstrated the ability to choose and utilize the most appropriate tool for a given sub-problem.
- **Debugging:** Software agents combined code analysis with incremental fixes, showcasing genuine problem-solving capabilities.

These findings collectively underscore VERLTOOL's success in providing a robust, efficient, and extensible infrastructure that facilitates advanced research in ARLT, moving closer to the goal of holistic agentic LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VERLTOOL represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly by addressing critical infrastructure limitations in Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Tool use (ARLT). Its potential impact spans both research and practical applications, paving the way for more capable and robust AI agents.

#### 5.1. Unifying and Standardizing ARLT Research

Perhaps the most profound impact of VERLTOOL is its potential to unify and standardize the fragmented landscape of ARLT research. By providing a single, modular, and open-source framework, it directly tackles the issue of "task-specific codebases" and "hard-coded tool integrations." This significantly reduces the development overhead for researchers, enabling them to:
- **Rapidly Prototype:** Integrate new tools with minimal effort using the lightweight Python plugin interface.
- **Reproduce and Compare:** Facilitate easier reproduction of results and fair comparison across different algorithmic innovations in ARLT.
- **Accelerate Algorithmic Innovation:** Researchers can focus on novel RL algorithms, reward designs, and agentic strategies rather than spending time on re-implementing basic infrastructure.

#### 5.2. Enhancing Efficiency and Scalability of ARLT Training

The asynchronous rollout design is a crucial innovation for large-scale RL training. By eliminating synchronous execution bottlenecks and achieving "near 2× speedup," VERLTOOL makes ARLT training more computationally efficient. This efficiency is vital for:
- **Scaling Up:** Training more complex models and tackling tasks requiring extensive multi-turn interactions and larger datasets.
- **Resource Optimization:** Better utilization of expensive GPU and CPU resources, reducing the cost and time associated with research and development.
- **Enabling Long-Horizon Tasks:** The ability to handle diverse tool latencies and long trajectories makes complex, real-world tasks (e.g., software engineering, web browsing) more feasible for RL training.

#### 5.3. Fostering Multimodal Agent Development

VERLTOOL's comprehensive support for diverse tool modalities, including vision processing, is a critical step towards developing truly generalist AI agents. Most existing RL frameworks for LLMs are text-centric. By seamlessly integrating multimodal observations and actions, VERLTOOL:
- **Unlocks New Capabilities:** Enables agents to reason and interact with the world through various senses, expanding the scope of problems LLMs can solve.
- **Promotes Multimodal ARLT:** Facilitates research into how LLMs can effectively combine linguistic reasoning with visual or other sensory inputs for tasks like visual reasoning and robotics.

#### 5.4. Bridging the Gap Towards Holistic Agentic Intelligence

By extending RLVR to full-fledged ARLT, VERLTOOL pushes LLMs beyond "isolated reasoning" towards "interactive agentic intelligence." The observed emergence of sophisticated behaviors like self-correction, iterative refinement, and strategic tool selection demonstrates that VERLTOOL helps train agents that can:
- **Adapt and Self-Improve:** Learn from environmental feedback and dynamically adjust their strategies.
- **Robustly Solve Problems:** Handle uncertainties, recover from errors, and achieve goals in complex, open-ended environments.
- **Exhibit True Agency:** Move closer to the definition of intelligent agents that reason, act, and interact in multi-turn environments, maintaining state and revising plans.

#### 5.5. Open-Source Contribution and Community Impact

The decision to open-source VERLTOOL (https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/verl-tool) is highly significant. An open, accessible framework will empower a broader community of researchers and developers, leading to:
- **Faster Progress:** Collaborative development and shared infrastructure will accelerate advancements in ARLT.
- **Increased Adoption:** Lowering the barrier to entry will encourage more researchers to explore agentic RL.
- **Diversified Applications:** The community can adapt and extend VERLTOOL for novel applications beyond the six tasks evaluated in the paper.

In conclusion, VERLTOOL offers a timely and robust solution to the systemic challenges facing ARLT research. By providing a unified, efficient, and extensible framework, it not only streamlines current research but also lays a scalable foundation for future advancements, significantly contributing to the development and practical deployment of intelligent, tool-using LLM agents.
