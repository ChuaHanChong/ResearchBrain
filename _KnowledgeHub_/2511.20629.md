# MapReduce LoRA: Advancing the Pareto Front in Multi-Preference Optimization for Generative Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Tech and Adobe developed MapReduce LoRA and Reward-aware Token Embedding (RaTE) to enable generative models to improve simultaneously across multiple human-aligned preferences without performance trade-offs. MapReduce LoRA iteratively trains and merges LoRA experts, achieving gains like 36.1% on GenEval and 55.7% on OCR for Text-to-Image generation, while RaTE provides efficient inference-time preference control.

## Problem

- Generative models struggle to align with multi-dimensional human preferences simultaneously, often encountering an 'alignment tax' where optimizing one preference degrades others.
- Existing multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) methods frequently lead to easily optimized objectives dominating harder preferences or offer modest, inflexible performance gains.
- Current post-training alignment techniques lack a unified, scalable framework that can robustly and efficiently improve models across multiple preferences with flexible test-time control.

## Method

- **MapReduce LoRA** is proposed as an iterative, progressive ==souping== framework that parallelly trains preference-specific LoRA experts (Map phase) and then merges them to refine a shared base model (Reduce phase) using Group Relative Policy Optimization.
- Reward-aware Token Embedding (RaTE) is introduced as a complementary method, learning unique embedding vectors for pseudo-tokens representing specific reward preferences, distilled from LoRA experts, for flexible inference-time control.
- Both methods leverage Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, allowing adaptation of large pre-trained models across diverse modalities.

## Results

- MapReduce LoRA achieved substantial improvements on Text-to-Image tasks using SD **3.5 M**, increasing GenEval by **36.1%**, PickScore by **4.6%**, and OCR by **55.7%**, while also improving out-of-domain metrics like VQAScore by **1.85%**.
- For Text-to-Video generation, MapReduce LoRA advanced Visual Quality by **48.1%** and Motion Quality by **90.0%** on HunyuanVideo, outperforming individually trained experts and Rewarded Soup.
- RaTE provided additional gains when combined with MapReduce LoRA (e.g., +**4.20%** on GenEval, +**1.03%** on PickScore) with a training cost approximately **0.1579** times that of MapReduce LoRA, demonstrating efficient inference-time control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative decomposition and merging of preference-specific LoRA experts effectively advance the Pareto front in multi-preference optimization, mitigating the 'alignment tax' by progressively converging towards a joint optimum.
> - Distilling reward preferences into lightweight, special token embeddings enables flexible and composable control over generated content at inference time, offering a low-cost method for dynamic customization without retraining.
> - The framework demonstrates cross-modal generality, successfully applying to Text-to-Image, Text-to-Video, and Language tasks, indicating a broadly applicable approach for aligning diverse generative models with complex human preferences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generative models, particularly flow-based diffusion models for Text-to-Image and Text-to-Video generation, has resulted in high-fidelity outputs. A subsequent focus in the field involves aligning these generated outputs with complex human preferences and judgments. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), often employing reward models, has become a standard post-training method for this alignment.

However, human perception of quality is inherently multi-dimensional, leading to the use of multiple reward models (e.g., for text-image alignment, aesthetic quality, text rendering). A significant challenge in this area, referred to as the "alignment tax," arises when jointly optimizing for multiple preferences: improving performance on one metric often results in a degradation of performance on others.

Existing approaches to multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) attempt to address this by combining rewards (e.g., through weighted sums) or by approximating a Pareto set. However, these methods often exhibit limitations. A priori scalarization methods (e.g., weighted sums) can lead to easily optimized objectives dominating harder preferences, potentially causing under-training or regression on certain dimensions. A posteriori methods, such as Rewarded Soup, while offering test-time control, may still underperform models specifically fine-tuned for individual rewards. Therefore, the development of a unified framework that can robustly and efficiently improve generative models across multiple human-aligned preferences without significant trade-offs remains an active area of research. This work positions itself as an advancement in addressing these multi-preference alignment challenges for generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop methods that allow generative models to simultaneously improve across multiple human-aligned preferences without incurring the "alignment tax," where gains in one preference dimension lead to losses in others. The authors aim to overcome limitations observed in current multi-objective post-training methods.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the "Alignment Tax":** The core challenge is the inherent conflict in multi-objective optimization for generative models. While single-reward RLHF methods perform effectively for isolated preferences, they tend to either degrade performance on unoptimized metrics or face competing gradients when attempting joint optimization. This suggests a need for a more robust multi-preference optimization strategy.

- **Improving upon Existing MORL Limitations:**
 - **Weighted Mixtures:** Current MORL approaches that combine rewards through weighted mixtures often result in easily optimized objectives dominating harder preferences. This can lead to certain quality dimensions being undertrained or even regressing during the optimization process.
 - **Calibrated Preference Optimization (CaPO):** While CaPO attempts to balance multiple Text-to-Image preferences, its performance gains are noted as modest, and it is limited by fixed weighting schemes, lacking flexible test-time control.
 - **Rewarded Soup:** This method offers a posteriori weight selection for combining models but has been observed to underperform specialized models fine-tuned for individual rewards, indicating a sub-optimal approximation of the Pareto front.

- **Achieving Unified and Robust Multi-Preference Alignment:** The overarching goal is to create a unified framework that can efficiently and robustly enhance generative models across diverse reward signals. This framework should be capable of producing models that generalize well across multiple preferences and can be customized after training.

- **Introducing Complementary Approaches:** To achieve this, the authors propose two complementary methods:
 - **MapReduce LoRA:** This method is designed to decompose the multi-objective optimization problem. It aims to iteratively train preference-specific LoRA experts in parallel (Map phase) and then merge these experts to refine a shared base model (Reduce phase). This iterative process is intended to advance the Pareto front by progressively converging toward the joint optimum.
 - **Reward-aware Token Embedding (RaTE):** This method focuses on providing lightweight, flexible control over preferences at inference time. It involves learning reward-specific token embeddings that can be composed and appended to input prompts, enabling *a posteriori* customization without necessitating model retraining.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived inefficiency and limitations of current multi-preference optimization techniques, with the objective of developing a scalable, generalizable, and controllable framework that can effectively align generative models with complex and often conflicting human preferences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates two complementary methods: MapReduce LoRA for multi-preference training and Reward-aware Token Embedding (RaTE) for inference-time preference control. Both methods leverage Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter efficiency.

#### 3.1. MapReduce LoRA

MapReduce LoRA is designed as an iterative, progressive souping process to optimize generative models across multiple reward objectives.

- **Preliminaries:**
 - **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):** LoRA is employed to efficiently adapt large pre-trained models. It learns a low-rank update matrix (ΔW = BA) to frozen pre-trained weights (W). During fine-tuning, only matrices A and B are trained, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters. At inference, ΔW can be merged into W.
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** This PPO-style preference optimization algorithm is utilized for fine-tuning. For a given prompt, GRPO samples multiple results, computes group-normalized advantages by z-scoring rewards within that group, and updates the policy using a clipped likelihood-ratio objective regularized by a KL penalty relative to a frozen reference policy.

- **MapReduce LoRA Process:**
 1. **Initialization:** A base model `M` with initial parameters `θ^(0)` is established, and a reference policy `π_ref` is initialized from this base model.
 2. **Iterative Optimization (k iterations):** For each iteration `k` from 0 to `K-1`:
 - **Map Phase (Parallel Expert Training):** For each of `n` distinct reward objectives `R_i`, a new LoRA adapter `ϕ^(k)_i` is initialized. The base model weights `θ^(k)` are frozen, and only `ϕ^(k)_i` is trained for a fixed number of GRPO steps (`T_GRPO`). This training optimizes the generative model with respect to its specific reward `R_i`. This parallel training is conducted for each reward, creating `n` reward-specific LoRA experts.
 - **Reduce Phase (Expert Merging and Base Update):** After all `n` LoRA experts `ϕ^(k)_i` are trained in the Map phase, their weights are averaged. User-controlled interpolation weights `μ_i` are applied (defaulting to uniform averaging), resulting in a merged adapter `¯ϕ^(k)`. This `¯ϕ^(k)` is then merged into the current base model parameters `θ^(k)` to produce `θ^(k+1)`.
 - **Iteration Update:** The LoRA adapters `ϕ^(k)_i` are reset to zero, and the reference policy `π_ref` is updated to `M(θ^(k+1))`, preparing for the next iteration with the refined base model.
 3. **Final Model:** After `K` iterations, the resulting model `M^(K)` is the multi-preference aligned model.

- **Theoretical Basis:** The authors formalize this process as progressive souping, where `θ^(k+1)` is the average of `n` proximal operators applied to `θ^(k)`. They demonstrate that this iterative application of the averaged proximal map converges to a stationary point of the aggregated objective (maximizing the average of individual reward objectives). The geometric contraction bound `||F(θ^(m)) - F*|| <= (1 - cημ)^m ||F(θ^(0)) - F*||` suggests that each progressive-soup iteration further contracts towards the joint optimum, providing a theoretical advantage over one-shot merging methods like Rewarded Soup.

#### 3.2. Reward-aware Token Embedding (RaTE)

RaTE is designed to provide flexible, composable, and lightweight control over reward preferences at inference time.

- **Concept:** Inspired by Textual Inversion, RaTE learns a unique embedding vector for a pseudo-token that represents a specific reward preference.
- **Training Process:**
 1. **Teacher Setup:** For each preference `i`, the corresponding LoRA expert `ϕ_i` (trained via MapReduce LoRA) is attached to the frozen base model `M` to create a "teacher model" `M_teacher`. This teacher model generates preference-specific latents.
 2. **Student Setup:** The student model is the frozen base model `M` *without* any LoRA adapters. The prompt provided to the student includes a special token `<RaTE_i>`, whose embedding `θ^token_i` is the *only* trainable parameter.
 3. **Distillation:** The training uses a Flow Matching objective. An initial noise vector `ϵ_0` is sampled, and the teacher model `M_teacher` generates a target latent `z^teacher_0,i`. A timestep `t` is sampled, and `z_t` is created by mixing `z^teacher_0,i` with Gaussian noise `ϵ`. The target velocity `v_target` is `ϵ - z^teacher_0,i`. The student model `M` then predicts a velocity `v_pred` from `z_t` and the conditioned prompt `p'` (original prompt concatenated with `<RaTE_i>`). The loss function is the Mean Squared Error between `v_pred` and `v_target`. Only `θ^token_i` is updated during backpropagation.
- **Inference:** Once trained, these expert-infused token embeddings `θ^token_i` can be appended as special tokens to user prompts during inference. This allows for flexible and composable control over desired preferences without modifying the underlying generative model or its LoRA adapters.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The methodology was validated across three modalities:
- **Text-to-Image:** Using Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD 3.5 M) and FLUX.1-dev as base models. Reward models included GenEval (text-image alignment), PickScore (human preference), and OCR (text rendering quality). Additional evaluation metrics were VQAScore, MPS, and VILA.
- **Text-to-Video:** Using HunyuanVideo as the backbone. Reward models were Visual Quality (VQ) and Motion Quality (MQ) from VideoAlign.
- **Language Tasks:** Using Llama-2 7B for Helpful Assistant (with Helpful and Harmless rewards) and Reddit Summary (with Faithful and Preference1 rewards).

Baselines for comparison included individual experts (fine-tuned for a single reward), Calibrated Preference Optimization (CaPO), Rewarded Soup, and two variants of Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL-D for data mixture and MORL-DR for data and reward mixture). Ablation studies were conducted on MapReduce LoRA's merging iterations and RaTE's number of appended tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of MapReduce LoRA and Reward-aware Token Embedding (RaTE) across multiple generative modalities.

#### 4.1. MapReduce LoRA Performance

- **Text-to-Image Generation:**
 - **Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD 3.5 M):** MapReduce LoRA resulted in substantial improvements on in-domain rewards: GenEval by 36.1%, PickScore by 4.6%, and OCR by 55.7%. Furthermore, the method also improved out-of-domain metrics, with VQAScore increasing by 1.85%, MPS by 6.49%, and VILA by 19.96%. These gains surpassed those reported for CaPO on comparable metrics.
 - **FLUX.1-dev:** Similar trends were observed, with improvements of 32.7% on GenEval, 4.3% on PickScore, and 67.1% on OCR.
 - **Comparison to Baselines:** MapReduce LoRA consistently maintained competitive performance across all targeted rewards, a contrast to individual experts that performed well only on their targeted metrics and MORL variants (MORL-D, MORL-DR) which often exhibited instability or objective collapse (e.g., PickScore being dominated by other rewards during joint training). The method also demonstrated improved visual aesthetics, positional relationships, and text rendering quality compared to the base model and Rewarded Soup in qualitative assessments.

- **Text-to-Video Generation (HunyuanVideo):** MapReduce LoRA advanced both Visual Quality (VQ) and Motion Quality (MQ), achieving improvements of 48.1% and 90.0% respectively. This outperformed individually trained DanceGRPO experts (which showed +34.55% VQ and +74.10% MQ) and Rewarded Soup (+27.37% VQ and +50.42% MQ), establishing a new performance benchmark among post-trained diffusion systems for video generation.

- **Language Tasks (Llama-2 7B):** On the Helpful Assistant task, MapReduce LoRA yielded improvements of 43.4% on helpfulness and 136.7% on harmlessness. The framework demonstrated state-of-the-art performance when compared to baselines such as Llama2 (base and SFT), Rewarded Soup, and Bone Soup, indicating its cross-modal generality.

- **Ablation Study on Merging Iterations:** An analysis with fixed total training steps showed that increasing the number of merging iterations (`k`) in MapReduce LoRA consistently improved final performance and reduced performance degradation associated with merging multiple experts. For instance, comparing `k=10` to `k=1` showed gains of +11.84% on GenEval, +1.56% on PickScore, and +10.72% on OCR, supporting the theoretical claim of progressive convergence towards the joint optimum.

- **Scalability to More Rewards:** The framework was extended to optimize for five rewards (GenEval, PickScore, OCR, VQAScore, MPS) on SD 3.5 M. MapReduce LoRA demonstrated improvements across all five rewards (e.g., +22% GenEval, +3% PickScore, +44% OCR, +6% VQAScore, +7% MPS), outperforming a one-shot Rewarded Soup. While scaling to more rewards did not introduce diminishing returns in performance, it required 1.66 times more training steps, reflecting increased optimization complexity.

#### 4.2. Reward-aware Token Embedding (RaTE) Performance

- **Text-to-Image Generation (SD 3.5 M):** RaTE proved effective in enabling per-reward control and improving multiple rewards at inference time. When combined with MapReduce LoRA, RaTE contributed additional gains (e.g., +4.20% on GenEval, +1.03% on PickScore, and +2.17% on OCR), suggesting orthogonal benefits from token-level and transformer-level tuning.
- **Efficiency:** RaTE's training cost was approximately 0.1579 times that of MapReduce LoRA, indicating a lightweight approach for inference-time control.
- **Architectural Dependence:** RaTE was found to be more effective on models with explicit cross-attention for text conditioning, such as the Stable Diffusion series. For models performing joint text–image sequence modeling (e.g., FLUX.1-dev), its effectiveness was less stable due to the way modified token embeddings could perturb both text and image tokens across layers.
- **Ablation Study on Appended Token Count:** Varying the number of appended RaTE tokens revealed that optimal performance varied by reward metric (e.g., GenEval saturated at 2-3 tokens, PickScore peaked at 1, and OCR at 3), providing insights into the nuanced control capabilities.

In conclusion, the results indicate that MapReduce LoRA consistently advances the Pareto front in multi-preference optimization across diverse modalities and generative models, demonstrating robust performance and generalization. RaTE complements this by offering an efficient and flexible mechanism for inference-time preference control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents MapReduce LoRA and Reward-aware Token Embedding (RaTE) as methods designed to address the challenges of multi-preference optimization for generative models. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing the "Alignment Tax":** The work provides a systematic approach to mitigate the "alignment tax," a fundamental issue where optimizing for one human preference often compromises others. By iteratively merging preference-specific LoRA experts, MapReduce LoRA demonstrates a capability to improve performance across multiple, potentially conflicting, objectives simultaneously without significant degradation.

- **Advancing Multi-Preference Pareto Fronts:** The framework consistently pushes the Pareto front, yielding models that achieve higher performance across multiple reward dimensions compared to existing multi-objective optimization strategies and single-expert fine-tuning. This represents an advancement in the ability to align generative model outputs with complex, multi-faceted human judgments.

- **Scalability and Cross-Modal Generalization:** The effectiveness of MapReduce LoRA is demonstrated across diverse generative tasks and modalities, including Text-to-Image (Stable Diffusion 3.5 M, FLUX.1-dev), Text-to-Video (HunyuanVideo), and Language (Llama-2 7B). This broad applicability suggests that the proposed recipe is generalizable and scalable, offering a unified approach for various generative AI applications.

- **Efficient and Iterative Optimization:** By utilizing LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and an iterative merging strategy, MapReduce LoRA provides a more robust and efficient alternative to one-shot model merging or unstable multi-objective reinforcement learning methods. The iterative refinement process allows for progressive convergence towards a more balanced optimum.

- **Flexible Inference-Time Control:** RaTE introduces a lightweight and modular mechanism for *a posteriori* control of model preferences at inference time. Users can append specific reward-aware tokens to prompts to influence the generated output towards desired qualities without requiring further model retraining. This enhances the practical utility and customizability of generative models for end-users, enabling dynamic preference adjustments.

- **Robustness and Generalization to Untargeted Rewards:** The observed improvements in untargeted reward metrics (e.g., VQAScore, MPS, VILA in Text-to-Image) indicate that the method achieves a robust cross-preference alignment rather than merely overfitting to the explicitly optimized rewards. This suggests the models developed with this framework possess a more holistic understanding of quality.

- **Practical Implications for Generative AI Development:** This research offers a practical and scalable recipe for post-training alignment, which is crucial for deploying generative models in real-world applications where outputs must meet a complex array of human expectations. It could lead to the development of more versatile, controllable, and human-aligned generative AI systems across various industries, from content creation to automated assistants.

In summary, this work contributes to the field by providing a principled and empirically validated framework for multi-preference optimization, offering improved alignment, flexibility, and control over generative model outputs.
