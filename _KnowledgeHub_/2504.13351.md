# Chain-of-Modality: Learning Manipulation Programs from Multimodal Human Videos with Vision-Language-Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind and Stanford researchers develop a system that learns manipulation programs from multimodal human demonstrations, using a Chain-of-Modality prompting strategy with vision-language models to process visual, muscle activity, and audio data, enabling robots to execute complex manipulation tasks across different embodiments and environments.

## Problem

- Traditional vision-only approaches struggle to capture crucial force and contact information needed for manipulation tasks
- Current methods lack effective ways to process and reason about multiple modalities of demonstration data simultaneously
- Traditional learning-from-demonstration methods rely on explicit programming or teleoperation, which is time-consuming and requires expert knowledge, limiting scalability

## Method

- **Chain-of-Modality** (CoM) prompting strategy analyzes each modality sequentially to extract comprehensive task information
- Integration of multiple sensor inputs (RGB video, EMG signals, audio) to capture force and contact details
- VLM-based code generation pipeline that converts task plans into robot-executable programs

## Results

- Successfully generates executable robot control programs from single human demonstrations, enabling cross-embodiment deployment through abstracted API calls
- Demonstrates strong generalization to new objects and task setups
- Outperforms vision-only baselines in task plan recognition and parameter extraction accuracy

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Processing modalities separately rather than merging them leads to better VLM understanding
> - Force information from EMG/audio significantly improves task plan extraction compared to vision-only approaches
> - Hand pose data is crucial for fine-grained manipulation parameter extraction

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several active areas in robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Learning from Demonstrations (LfD):** The paper addresses the challenge of teaching robots new skills by learning from human demonstrations, which is a core area of LfD. Traditional LfD methods often rely on explicit programming or teleoperation, which can be time-consuming and require expert knowledge. This work explores a more scalable approach by leveraging human video data.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The research capitalizes on the recent advancements in VLMs, which have shown remarkable capabilities in understanding and reasoning about visual and textual information. By using VLMs as a core component of their system, the authors aim to bridge the gap between human demonstrations and robot control.
- **Multimodal Learning:** The paper tackles the limitations of vision-only approaches by incorporating additional modalities such as muscle activity (EMG) and audio. This aligns with the broader trend of multimodal learning, where combining information from multiple sources can lead to more robust and accurate perception and reasoning.
- **Robot Manipulation:** The ultimate goal of this research is to enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks, which is a fundamental challenge in robotics. The paper focuses on tasks that require fine-grained control and force application, which are often difficult to learn from visual data alone.
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** This work contributes to the growing field of using large pre-trained foundation models to solve problems in robotics. By demonstrating how VLMs can be used to generate robot control programs from multimodal video data, the authors showcase the potential of foundation models to accelerate robot learning and generalization.

The work differentiates itself from existing approaches by:

- **Focusing on multimodal human videos:** Most prior work on learning from human videos relies solely on visual information. This research explicitly incorporates additional modalities to capture details that are not readily visible, such as force and contact information.
- **Introducing Chain-of-Modality (CoM):** The CoM prompting strategy provides a novel way to leverage VLMs for multimodal reasoning. By analyzing each modality sequentially and progressively refining the answer, CoM improves the accuracy and robustness of task plan extraction.
- **Generating robot-executable code:** The paper presents a complete pipeline for transforming human demonstrations into robot control programs, which can be directly executed on real-world robots. This allows for cross-embodiment generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- To develop a system that can learn manipulation programs from single multimodal human video demonstrations.
- To leverage sensing devices to capture the details in the human manipulation process that visual data alone cannot capture
- To enable robots to extract task plans and control parameters to perform the same task as a human demonstration
- To introduce a prompting strategy, Chain-of-Modality (CoM), that enables Vision Language Models to reason about multimodal human demonstration data
- To improve the accuracy of task plan recognition and control parameter extraction compared to vision-only baselines.
- To achieve strong generalization to new task setups and objects in real-world robot experiments.

The main motivations behind this research are:

- The limitations of vision-only approaches for learning manipulation skills, particularly those requiring precise control of force and contact.
- The potential of VLMs to bridge the gap between human demonstrations and robot control, but the need for effective methods to leverage multimodal data.
- The desire to create more scalable and generalizable approaches to robot learning, reducing the need for explicit programming or teleoperation.
- The potential of cross-embodiment generalization by generating code API that can abstract away the robot embodiment

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a three-stage approach:

1. **Multimodal Human Demonstration Video Collection:** The authors collect multimodal data consisting of RGB images, human muscle signals (EMG) or object interaction sounds, and hand pose information. They use armbands equipped with muscle sensors and sports cameras with microphones to capture force information, and a vision-based method (HaMeR) to estimate hand pose.

2. **Chain-of-Modality (CoM) for Multimodal Video Understanding:** The core of the methodology is the CoM prompting strategy. It involves querying VLMs to analyze each modality sequentially, extracting key information and progressively aggregating results to produce the final answer. This approach addresses the challenges of directly processing long-context multimodal inputs. The CoM prompt includes descriptions of each modality, the available action set, and an example of a video-to-analysis pair.

3. **Robot Code Generation and Execution:** Based on the task plan descriptions extracted by CoM, the authors use VLMs to generate robot-executable code with low-level API calls. These API calls provide benefits of cross-embodiment generalization. The prompts for code generation include the video analysis from CoM, descriptions of robot APIs, and the required output format. The generated code is then executed on real-world robot platforms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings of the research are:

- **CoM improves VLMs' understanding of multimodal human videos:** CoM consistently outperforms baseline methods that either merge the modality inputs or generate a single merged answer. Processing and analyzing each modality separately is more effective for current VLMs.
- **Force information enhances learning from human videos:** Methods with force inputs significantly outperform those without, indicating that extracting key information for manipulation purely from images is still challenging. Force inputs help VLMs better segment the video into different stages.
- **Hand pose aids fine-grained manipulation:** Hand pose plays a crucial role in tasks requiring fine-grained information about fingertip rotation direction and grasping/releasing actions.
- **CoM enables VLMs to extract control parameters from multimodal human videos:** The system can successfully identify task plans and target objects, as well as extract detailed control parameters such as motion direction, force intensity, and timing of each action.
- **VLMs can generate manipulation programs based on CoM analysis:** The generated programs can control robots to perform tasks with strong generalization to novel objects and environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research has several significant implications:

- **Improved Robot Learning:** The CoM approach offers a more effective way to teach robots new skills from human demonstrations, reducing the need for explicit programming or teleoperation.
- **Enhanced Robot Manipulation Capabilities:** By incorporating multimodal data and extracting detailed control parameters, the system enables robots to perform complex manipulation tasks that require fine-grained control and force application.
- **Greater Generalization:** The system demonstrates strong generalization to new task setups, objects, and robot platforms, making it more adaptable to real-world scenarios.
- **Cross-Embodiment Deployment:** The generated code API allows for smooth deployment across different robots, leveraging cross-embodiment generalization.
- **Advancements in VLM Research:** The CoM prompting strategy provides a valuable technique for leveraging VLMs in multimodal reasoning tasks, which can be applied to other domains beyond robotics.

The potential impact of this research includes:

- **Accelerating the development of more capable and versatile robots** that can perform a wider range of tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Lowering the barrier to entry for robot programming**, allowing non-expert users to easily teach robots new skills through human demonstrations.
- **Enabling robots to work more effectively alongside humans** in collaborative tasks, by understanding and responding to human actions and intentions.
- **Advancing the state of the art in multimodal learning and VLM research**, leading to new applications in various fields such as computer vision, natural language processing, and human-computer interaction.
