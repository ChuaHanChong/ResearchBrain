# FunRec: Reconstructing Functional 3D Scenes from Egocentric Interaction Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> FunREC introduces a training-free, optimization-based pipeline that reconstructs functional 3D digital twins of indoor scenes directly from egocentric RGB-D interaction videos. This system captures both static geometry and the dynamic, articulated behaviors of objects, outperforming previous methods and yielding physically consistent, interactable digital twins suitable for simulation and robotics.

## Problem

- Current 3D scene reconstruction primarily focuses on static environments, lacking the dynamic, interactive, and functional information critical for embodied AI and robotics applications.
- Previous methods for functional scene reconstruction are often labor-intensive, rely on manual annotation, infer functionality from static geometry, or require controlled setups and pre-existing CAD models, limiting their real-world scalability and accuracy.
- Dynamic 3D scene tracking from egocentric video struggles with complex articulations, occlusions, and producing coherent, simulation-ready functional models that include newly exposed geometry.

## Method

- A training-free, optimization-based pipeline processes egocentric RGB-D interaction videos, using a video-language model to classify fragments as static or dynamic and predict articulation types (prismatic/revolute).
- The system performs articulation-aware motion clustering, dense pixel-aligned part segmentation, and jointly optimizes part poses and kinematic parameters for articulated objects.
- It reconstructs static geometry and articulated parts in canonical coordinate frames using separate TSDF volumes, followed by global fragment alignment to create a unified, functional digital twin with defined motion ranges.

## Results

- FunREC achieved superior articulated motion estimation, with axis direction errors of approximately 5° and joint state errors of 5° or 0.02m on OmniFun4D, significantly outperforming baselines with a 1.7% failure rate.
- The method demonstrated high moving part segmentation accuracy (e.g., 77.9 mIoU on OmniFun4D) and 6D part pose estimation (e.g., 79.0% ADD-S on OmniFun4D), showing substantial improvements over prior work.
- It produced high-fidelity articulated 3D reconstructions with low Chamfer Distance (e.g., 0.7 cm on HOI4D), indicating superior surface quality, and introduced two new egocentric interaction datasets, RealFun4D and OmniFun4D.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric human interaction videos provide direct and rich signals for identifying articulated parts, inferring kinematic parameters, and exposing occluded geometry, which is challenging to derive from static observations.
> - Integrating geometric reasoning with semantic and motion priors from foundational models within a training-free, optimization-based pipeline enables robust functional 3D scene reconstruction without extensive training data.
> - Reconstructing articulated components in their canonical coordinate frames and accurately estimating their feasible motion ranges allows for the creation of physically consistent digital twins compatible with interactive simulation environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D scene understanding has seen advancements in reconstructing static environments, supported by large-scale RGB-D datasets. However, these datasets primarily represent scenes in a single, fixed state, failing to capture the dynamic and interactive nature of real-world environments. This limitation is significant for applications in embodied AI, robotics, and virtual reality, where agents need to perceive, plan, and act in physical spaces that change through interaction. The absence of functionality—how objects move and articulate—remains a barrier to creating truly functional 3D digital twins.

Previous research efforts have approached the challenge of functional scene reconstruction with various methodologies, each presenting specific limitations:
- **Multi-state captures:** Methods like MultiScan involve manually aligning multiple scans of a room in different states (e.g., cabinet open and closed). This approach is labor-intensive and relies on manual annotation, which is not scalable for complex, dynamic scenes.
- **Static scene annotation:** SceneFun3D and Articulate3D enrich static LiDAR scans with fine-grained functional and affordance annotations. While providing detailed information, these methods infer functionality from static geometry rather than directly observing causal and kinematic properties during interaction.
- **Digital cousins/proxy retrieval:** Approaches aimed at creating "digital cousins" or interactive digital replicas often involve retrieving CAD models that resemble static reconstructions. This method relies on similarity-based substitution rather than capturing the actual physical interaction and geometry of the observed scene.
- **Object-level articulated reconstruction:** Numerous works focus on reconstructing individual articulated objects from demonstrations. However, these typically operate under controlled setups, using static cameras, or requiring known CAD models. They are generally limited to object-centric or synthetic scenarios and do not offer an end-to-end solution for scene-scale, physically grounded digital twins from unconstrained, egocentric interaction videos.
- **Dynamic 3D scene tracking:** While methods exist for tracking objects and scene changes from egocentric videos, they often focus on rigid transformations, assume pre-scanned 3D scenes with pre-labeled part motions, or struggle with occlusions and depth inconsistencies common in real-world interactions. Monocular 4D reconstruction methods typically lack an understanding of object semantics and articulation parameters and can suffer from artifacts in the presence of occlusions.

FunREC addresses these limitations by introducing a method that leverages direct human interaction captured through egocentric RGB-D videos. This approach shifts from inferring articulation from static geometry or relying on controlled setups to reconstructing scene functionality and kinematic properties directly from observed, unconstrained interactions. The work aims to provide an end-to-end pipeline for creating coherent, articulated 3D digital twins of real environments, bridging the gap between static 3D reconstruction and dynamic, functional scene understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reconstruct functional 3D digital twins of indoor scenes directly from egocentric RGB-D interaction videos. This involves capturing not only the static geometry of an environment but also the dynamic behaviors of its articulated components, enabling interaction and simulation.

The motivation for FunREC stems from the observation that human interaction provides a direct and informative signal for understanding scene functionality. As individuals interact with their surroundings, egocentric observations naturally reveal critical information that is difficult to infer from static scans or controlled setups. This includes:
- **Identification of articulated parts:** Observing how objects move during interaction directly indicates which components are movable.
- **Kinematic parameter estimation:** The motion trajectories reveal the underlying joint types (e.g., prismatic for sliding, revolute for rotating) and their specific parameters (e.g., rotation axis, pivot point, translation axis).
- **Exposure of occluded geometry:** Interactions, such as opening a drawer or a cabinet door, expose previously occluded interior volumes, which are essential for a complete 3D digital twin.
- **Affordance inference:** The manner in which humans interact with objects can implicitly suggest their affordances.

Specifically, the key objectives of the FunREC system are to:
- **Automate articulation discovery:** Detect articulated parts within a scene without prior knowledge or manual labeling.
- **Estimate kinematic parameters:** Precisely determine the joint type (prismatic or revolute) and their associated parameters (e.g., axis, pivot, range of motion).
- **Track 3D motion:** Accurately track the 3D poses of both the static scene and the individual moving parts across the video sequence.
- **Reconstruct geometry in canonical space:** Generate clean 3D models for both the static background and each articulated part in its canonical coordinate frame, including newly exposed interiors.
- **Produce simulation-compatible representations:** Output a 3D scene representation that is physically consistent and allows for continuous, interactive manipulation of articulated elements, suitable for use in simulation environments.
- **Operate on "in-the-wild" data:** Achieve these objectives using a single egocentric RGB-D interaction video captured in unconstrained real-world settings, without relying on controlled setups, multi-state captures, or pre-existing CAD models.
- **Develop a training-free pipeline:** Implement the system as an optimization-based pipeline that integrates geometric reasoning with motion and semantic priors from foundational models, avoiding the need for extensive training data.

By addressing these objectives, FunREC aims to enable the creation of functional digital twins that can support a wide range of applications in robotics, embodied AI, and virtual reality, where understanding and interacting with dynamic environments is crucial.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FunREC is designed as a training-free, optimization-based pipeline that leverages geometric reasoning alongside semantic and motion priors from foundation models to reconstruct functional 3D digital twins from egocentric RGB-D interaction videos. The method models articulated parts with either prismatic (sliding) or revolute (rotating) joints, each defined by specific kinematic parameters and time-varying transformations.

The overall approach involves several sequential stages:

#### 3.1. Fragment Construction

The input RGB-D video is initially segmented into temporally contiguous fragments. A video-language model (VLM) is employed to classify each fragment as either static (no interaction) or dynamic (containing an interaction with an articulated part). For dynamic fragments, the VLM also provides a prediction of the articulation type (prismatic or revolute). Each fragment is then processed independently.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Fragment Reconstruction

This is the core stage for fragments identified as dynamic, where articulated motion is estimated, and geometry is reconstructed.

- **Camera Pose Estimation:**
 - Dense 2D-2D point correspondences are established between consecutive RGB-D frames using RoMA.
 - These matches are filtered: points within estimated hand masks (from VISOR) are discarded, and those within interacted-object masks (also from VISOR) are down-weighted to reduce noise from interaction.
 - Surviving 2D correspondences are lifted to 3D, and relative camera motion is estimated using SupeRANSAC.
 - A fragment-level pose graph optimization ensures global consistency of camera poses throughout the fragment.

- **Sparse 3D Trajectories:**
 - Sparse 3D point trajectories are obtained directly in the world frame using TAPIP3D.
 - New tracks are periodically initialized on a uniform 2D grid to capture surfaces that become visible during interactions.

- **Articulation-Aware Motion Clustering:**
 - Tracks with minimal motion are discarded.
 - For each remaining track, an articulation hypothesis (a 3D line for prismatic or a 3D circle for revolute motion) consistent with the VLM-predicted fragment articulation type is fitted.
 - Tracks are filtered based on their average fitting error to these hypotheses.
 - The filtered tracks are clustered using HDBSCAN, grouping them based on the similarity of their fitted joint parameters (axis, pivot, motion pattern). These clusters represent candidate articulated components.
 - To identify the actually manipulated part, a consistency score is computed for each cluster by evaluating its overlap with 2D interacted-object masks (from VISOR). The cluster with the highest score is designated as the interacted (moving) part, separating tracks into moving and static sets.

- **Pixel-Aligned Part Segmentation:**
 - To obtain dense, pixel-accurate segmentation masks for the articulated part, a hybrid approach combining geometric evidence and image-level semantics is used.
 - Keyframes are selected across the fragment. On each keyframe, SAM's automatic mask generator provides an over-segmentation into semantic regions.
 - The previously identified moving and static 3D tracks are projected onto each keyframe. For each semantic region, a "motion ratio" is calculated based on the count of projected moving and static tracks within it.
 - Regions with a motion ratio exceeding a threshold are classified as belonging to the articulated part, forming keyframe motion masks.
 - These keyframe masks are then propagated across the entire fragment using SAM2's video propagation module to produce a temporally consistent sequence of dense articulated part masks.

- **Part Pose and Articulation Estimation:**
 - Given the sparse moving tracks and their visibility, 3D-3D correspondences are established between visible points across pairs of frames. Relative transformations of the part between frames are estimated using SupeRANSAC.
 - These relative transforms form a pose graph for the part poses.
 - A non-linear optimization objective function is minimized to jointly recover the absolute part poses for all frames and the articulation parameters (e.g., axis, pivot) of the joint. The objective includes terms for consistency with relative transformations and loop-closure regularization.
 - Articulation parameters are initialized via least-squares fitting to the observed tracks and refined using Ceres Solver with manifold optimization.

- **Reconstruction and Interactive Manipulation:**
 - Two separate Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) volumes are used for geometry reconstruction: one for the static background and one for the articulated part.
 - The static TSDF is integrated in the world coordinate frame, excluding pixels identified as hand or moving part.
 - The articulated part is reconstructed in its canonical coordinate frame. For each frame, the depth map (filtered by the part mask) is transformed into the part's canonical frame and fused. This process yields a clean 3D model of the part, including occluded interiors.
 - Meshes are extracted from both TSDF volumes. The complete scene at any time is a union of the static scene and the canonically reconstructed part transformed by its estimated pose.
 - The feasible motion range (min/max displacement or angle) for the articulation parameters is derived from the tracked motion, enabling interactive manipulation and simulation of the reconstructed digital twin.

#### 3.3. Global Fragment Alignment

After processing all fragments, each yields a local submap (static geometry and articulated parts). For static fragments, only camera pose estimation is performed. To form a globally consistent scene, all local submaps are aligned into a shared coordinate frame. Geometric correspondences are extracted between submap pairs, and relative rigid transformations are estimated using PREDA-TOR. A scene-level pose graph is optimized to achieve global consistency, and the aligned submaps are fused into a unified TSDF volume to produce the final complete static scene and all articulated part meshes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FunREC was evaluated on its ability to perform articulated motion estimation, 6D part pose estimation, moving part segmentation, and 3D reconstruction. The method was benchmarked against several baselines, including 4D reconstruction pipelines (MonST3R, SpatialTrackerV2), 6D object pose tracking (BundleSDF), and articulated object reconstruction (ArtGS).

#### 4.1. Datasets

The authors introduced two new egocentric interaction datasets to facilitate evaluation in realistic settings:
- **RealFun4D:** Comprises 351 real-world egocentric RGB-D interaction videos captured across 60 apartments in four countries. It includes synchronized RGB-D at 15 FPS, annotated interaction intervals, per-frame 2D hand and part masks, and articulation joint labels with 2D part tracks.
- **OmniFun4D:** Consists of 127 photorealistic sequences rendered in 12 OmniGibson scenes. These sequences feature scripted interactions, high-quality RGB-D, and masks, with simulated camera motion perturbations.
Additionally, the HOI4D dataset was adapted for evaluation, focusing on 30 interactions involving articulated objects (laptop, cabinet, safe, trash can).

#### 4.2. Articulated Motion Estimation

FunREC demonstrated superior performance in articulated motion estimation across all datasets and motion types (revolute and prismatic joints).
- On **OmniFun4D**, FunREC achieved axis direction errors of 5.3° (revolute) and 5.4° (prismatic), an axis position error of 0.03m, and joint state errors of 5.0° (revolute) and 0.02m (prismatic), with a failure rate of 1.7%. This significantly outperformed BundleSDF, which had errors over 30° for axis direction and an order of magnitude higher for axis position.
- On **HOI4D** and **RealFun4D**, FunREC maintained low state prediction errors (9.1°/0.02m and 8.4°/0.03m, respectively) and a 0% failure rate.
- In contrast, existing dynamic reconstruction pipelines (MonST3R, SpatialTrackerV2) and articulated modeling methods (ArtGS) exhibited substantially larger deviations and frequent failures.

#### 4.3. Moving Part Segmentation

FunREC achieved the highest segmentation accuracy for moving parts.
- **OmniFun4D:** 77.9 mIoU.
- **HOI4D:** 76.4 mIoU.
- **RealFun4D:** 74.8 mIoU.
These scores represent a substantial improvement compared to baselines like MonST3R (23.6-26.8 mIoU) and SpatialTrackerV2 (5.8-13.4 mIoU), indicating accurate delineation of dynamic parts across diverse scene types.

#### 4.4. 6D Part Pose Estimation

FunREC consistently achieved the best results in 6D part pose accuracy.
- **OmniFun4D:** 79.0% ADD-S and 71.3% ADD.
- **HOI4D:** 79.4% ADD-S and 69.9% ADD.
- **RealFun4D:** 75.6% ADD-S and 68.1% ADD.
These metrics represent more than a twofold improvement over methods such as BundleSDF and a considerable margin over dynamic trackers, suggesting that the joint optimization of part pose and articulation parameters contributes to stable and temporally consistent motion trajectories.

#### 4.5. Articulated Reconstruction

FunREC delivered high-fidelity articulated 3D reconstructions with low Chamfer Distance (CD).
- **OmniFun4D:** 3.2 cm CD.
- **HOI4D:** 0.7 cm CD.
- **RealFun4D:** 6.1 cm CD.
This indicates superior surface reconstruction quality compared to all baselines. Qualitative examples demonstrated that FunREC accurately reconstructs both static geometry and articulated parts with correct motion ranges, facilitating continuous re-simulation of interactions in 3D.

In summary, the experimental results indicate that FunREC significantly surpasses prior work across multiple quantitative metrics for functional 3D scene reconstruction from egocentric interaction videos. The training-free, optimization-based approach appears to generalize effectively across synthetic, controlled, and real-world egocentric recordings, generating physically consistent and interactable digital twins.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FunREC research offers several contributions and has implications for various fields:

#### 5.1. Advancements in Functional 3D Scene Reconstruction

FunREC represents a notable step forward in the reconstruction of functional 3D scenes. Unlike previous methods that often rely on controlled environments, multi-state captures, or proxy models, FunREC operates directly on "in-the-wild" egocentric interaction videos. Its training-free, optimization-based pipeline, which integrates geometric reasoning with semantic and motion priors from foundation models, demonstrates the feasibility of reconstructing complex articulated digital twins from less constrained data. The system's ability to automatically discover articulated parts, estimate kinematic parameters, track 3D motion, and reconstruct both static and moving geometry (including occluded interiors) in canonical space is a significant technical achievement.

#### 5.2. New Benchmarking Resources

The introduction of RealFun4D and OmniFun4D datasets addresses a critical gap in available benchmarks for functional 3D scene reconstruction from egocentric videos. RealFun4D, with its diverse real-world human-scene interactions, and OmniFun4D, with its photorealistic simulated environments, provide standardized and challenging platforms for evaluating future research in this area. These datasets are instrumental for quantitative assessment and foster further development in functional scene understanding.

#### 5.3. Quantitative Performance Improvement

The experimental results demonstrate that FunREC substantially outperforms existing state-of-the-art baselines across key metrics. The improvements in part segmentation (up to +50 mIoU), articulation and pose errors (5-10x lower), and overall reconstruction accuracy (significantly lower Chamfer Distance) indicate the robustness and precision of the proposed method. This enhanced performance can enable more reliable and accurate digital twins for various applications.

#### 5.4. Practical Applications and Potential Impact

The functional digital twins generated by FunREC have several practical applications with significant potential impact:

- **Simulation-Ready Export:** The system can export reconstructed geometry and articulation information in standard formats like URDF or USD. This enables the direct loading of these functional scenes into physics simulators (e.g., Isaac Sim). The ability to infer physical properties like mass and inertia via vision-language models further enhances their utility for realistic physical simulation. This capability is crucial for developing and testing robots in virtual environments that closely mirror real-world dynamics.
- **Hand-Guided Affordance Mapping:** By integrating 3D hand mesh estimation with the functional digital twin, FunREC facilitates the localization of hands in 3D and the recovery of contact regions on objects during interaction. This allows for joint reasoning about hand motion and interacted object motion, contributing to a more nuanced understanding of affordances—how objects can be interacted with. This has implications for human-robot interaction and intuitive control interfaces.
- **Robot-Scene Interaction from Human Demonstration:** The functional scene models can be transferred to mobile manipulators, allowing robots to reproduce human-demonstrated interactions. As illustrated with a Boston Dynamics Spot robot, the inferred contact points, articulation parameters, and interaction trajectories enable robots to perform tasks such as opening cabinets reliably and stably. This capability is fundamental for learning from demonstration in robotics, allowing robots to acquire new skills by observing human actions in complex, dynamic environments.
- **Embodied AI and Virtual Reality:** By providing rich, interactive 3D scene representations, FunREC can contribute to advancements in embodied AI, where intelligent agents need to perceive, plan, and act in dynamic physical worlds. It also holds potential for creating more immersive and interactive virtual reality experiences that accurately reflect real-world functionality.

In conclusion, FunREC contributes a novel and effective method for creating functional digital twins from egocentric videos, accompanied by new datasets for evaluation. Its performance improvements and demonstrated applications suggest a significant impact on fields requiring a detailed and interactive understanding of 3D environments, particularly robotics and embodied AI.
