# Embodied Agents Take Control: Minimal-Interface Zero-Shot Agents Rival Industrial-Scale Policies in Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Australian Institute for Machine Learning demonstrated that minimal-interface zero-shot agents, empowered by frontier Vision-Language Models (VLMs), can autonomously manage complex interaction loops in vision-and-language navigation. This agentic embodied control approach achieved success rates competitive with specialized trained policies (70.7% SR on R2R-CE) by allowing the VLM to decide when to observe, act, and self-correct, and showed that optional tool use enhances both performance and efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI systems delegate control of the interaction loop (perceiving, acting, verifying, self-correcting) to external environment loops or human-authored pipeline code.
- Current trained policies and zero-shot workflows typically embed navigation capability within model weights or assemble it via fixed human-authored pipelines, rather than allowing the general-purpose model itself to direct the high-level interaction.
- A critical gap exists in understanding if and how frontier VLMs can autonomously manage complex decision-making processes for sustained embodied tasks without extensive task-specific fine-tuning or external navigation scaffolding.

## Method

- The authors proposed and implemented **agentic embodied control**, a paradigm where a general-purpose ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== directly assumes authority over the ==high-level interaction loop==, making decisions on observations, actions, error recovery, and termination.
- They used off-the-shelf software engineering ==agent harnesses== with an unmodified VLM, providing only a ==minimal interface==: a monocular RGB camera input and a set of ==primitive actions== (forward, left, right, stop) without any navigation-specific training, maps, memory, or search algorithms.
- Systematic ==ablations== were conducted on model choice, reasoning effort, agent harnesses, and interface design (fixed vs. optional ==waypoint tools==) to localize the sources of navigation capability and evaluate the effectiveness of different scaffolding approaches.

## Results

- Minimal-interface zero-shot agents achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 7`opus-5`.7% on R2R-CE with `opus-5` (**68.3%** with `fable-5` at default effort), competitive with or surpassing prior engineered zero-shot systems and within the range of industrial-scale trained navigation policies.
- A hybrid interface, offering both primitive actions and an optional waypoint module, enabled the `fable-5` agent to reach **76.7%** SR, outperforming both fixed primitive (**68.3%** SR) and fixed waypoint (**69%** SR) interfaces and demonstrating a more efficient "coarse-to-fine" strategy.
- Performance sharply degraded on longer-horizon tasks (**75%** SR on R2R-CE to **26%** SR on RxR-CE for primitive control), primarily due to unbounded context growth (median **33k tokens** on R2R-CE, doubling on RxR-CE) and common failure modes like misinterpreting instructions or poor stop-decision logic.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The VLM's inherent capabilities in spatial scene understanding, multi-turn multimodal interaction, and context retrieval are the primary drivers of embodied agent performance, rather than the general-purpose agent harness.
> - Scaffolding, such as a trained waypoint predictor, is most effective when offered as an *optional tool* that the agent can choose to invoke and compose with primitive actions, leading to superior performance and efficiency compared to fixed control paths.
> - Achieving sustained, open-ended autonomy requires addressing critical limitations, including unbounded context growth and associated latency, as well as the agent's lack of persistent spatial memory and awareness of its own body.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous embodied agents capable of sustained operation without frequent human intervention represents a significant challenge in artificial intelligence. Current research primarily approaches this goal through two main streams:

1. **Trained Policies:** This stream focuses on developing specialized navigation policies where a model directly maps observations to actions. Examples include systems like NaVid and StreamVLN, where navigation capability is internalized within the model's weights. These systems typically operate within an external environment loop that dictates the overall interaction flow.
2. **Zero-Shot Workflows:** This stream utilizes a general-purpose, often frozen, large language model (LLM) or vision-language model (VLM) and augments its capabilities with external navigation-specific scaffolding. Systems like NavGPT and MapGPT employ fixed workflows, spatial representations, and planning modules that are external to the core model. Here, capability is assembled through a human-authored pipeline that determines the sequence of operations.

More recently, industrial systems have combined aspects of these two streams through hierarchical or "dual-brain" architectures, such as ABot-N1 and InternVLA-N1. These systems couple a deliberative reasoning model with a trained action expert. However, the control flow in such architectures remains fixed and human-authored; the slow reasoning stage emits a subgoal, and the fast stage executes it, thereby constituting a trained workflow rather than an agent that autonomously controls its interaction loop.

This paper identifies a critical gap in these existing approaches: the interaction loop—the sequence of perceiving, acting, verifying, and self-correcting—is typically directed by an external environment loop or human-authored pipeline code. The authors propose and investigate a third paradigm: **agentic embodied control**. In this paradigm, the general-purpose model itself assumes authority over the high-level interaction loop, deciding when to observe, which actions to take, how to recover from errors, and when to terminate. This approach is motivated by the increasing capabilities of frontier VLMs in perception, spatial reasoning, progress tracking, and long-horizon tool use, as well as the success of generic agent frameworks (like ReAct) in software engineering tasks.

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is chosen as a controlled testbed for this study due to its inherent requirements for language grounding, spatial reasoning, progress tracking, error recovery, and timely stopping over extended interactions, while using discrete actions to simplify low-level control complexities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate the feasibility and capabilities of agentic embodied control in autonomous navigation. The authors aim to demonstrate that a general-purpose agent, using a minimal interface and off-the-shelf components, can autonomously manage complex decision-making processes typically handled by specialized policies or human-designed workflows.

The detailed motivations and objectives are:

1. **To challenge the conventional control paradigm:** Current embodied AI systems typically delegate control of the interaction loop to external, human-authored policies or workflows. The authors are motivated to explore whether the decision-making authority for these high-level actions can reside directly within the general-purpose model itself, shifting from externally directed loops to model-directed loops.
2. **To leverage advanced VLM capabilities:** Frontier vision-language models have demonstrated enhanced abilities in perception, spatial reasoning, and tool use across multiple turns. The research seeks to determine if these inherent model capabilities can be directly applied to complex embodied tasks like navigation without extensive task-specific fine-tuning or external scaffolding.
3. **To establish a baseline for minimal-interface agentic control:** A core objective is to instantiate and evaluate agentic embodied control under strictly minimal conditions. This involves equipping unmodified software-engineering agent harnesses with only a monocular RGB camera and a set of primitive actions, without any navigation-specific training, maps, memory, search algorithms, or policies. This setup serves as a diagnostic probe to assess the intrinsic embodied control capability of VLMs.
4. **To localize the sources of navigation capability:** Through systematic ablations, the study aims to determine the relative contributions of the VLM itself, the general-purpose agent harness, and the interface design to the overall navigation performance. This includes investigating how different levels of reasoning effort within the VLM and alternative action interfaces (e.g., waypoint control) influence outcomes.
5. **To understand the role of optional versus imposed scaffolding:** The research seeks to clarify whether traditional navigation scaffolding (such as waypoint predictors) is more effective when explicitly incorporated into a fixed workflow or when offered as an optional tool that the agent can choose to invoke and compose with primitive actions.
6. **To characterize the limitations for sustained autonomy:** Beyond achieving episodic success, the authors aim to identify the practical limitations that prevent these agentic systems from achieving long-term, open-ended autonomy in real-world environments. This includes analyzing performance on longer-horizon tasks, resource constraints like latency and context growth, common failure modes in simulation, and the transferability of capabilities to physical robots.

By addressing these objectives, the paper aims to provide insights into the potential of model-directed interaction for embodied agents and to inform future research directions toward truly autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study adopted an experimental approach to evaluate agentic embodied control in vision-and-language navigation (VLN).

**Core System Design: Agentic Embodied Control**
The central tenet of the methodology is to remove navigation-specific scaffolding and empower a general-purpose agent harness, coupled with a Vision-Language Model (VLM), to autonomously manage the interaction loop. This means the VLM decides when to observe, what actions to take, how to recover from errors, and when to terminate an episode, rather than being directed by an external policy or fixed workflow.
- **No Training/Prior State:** Agents received no navigation training or in-domain demonstrations. Each episode started in a fresh session without carrying over state or experience.
- **No External Components:** The system explicitly excluded external navigation memory, map builders, state estimators, planners, search procedures, learned policies, or verification stages.

**Experimental Environment**
- **Simulator:** Habitat-Sim 0.1.7 using Matterport3D scenes.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **R2R-CE:** The primary benchmark, using the fixed `rand100` subset of `val-unseen` (n=100 episodes), a standard for zero-shot systems.
 - **RxR-CE:** Used for long-horizon evaluation (n=100 English random samples).
 - **VLNVerse:** Used for additional interface ablation studies.
- **Success Criterion:** An episode was considered successful if the agent issued a `STOP` action within 3 meters (geodesic distance) of the goal. Episodes exceeding the budget without stopping were counted as failures.
- **Metrics:** Standard VLN-CE measures: Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), Navigation Error (NE), and Oracle Success Rate (OSR).

**Minimal Perception-Action Interface**
The agent interacted with the environment through only two tools:
- `observe()`: Returns a single 512x512 pixel front-facing RGB image with a 90° horizontal field of view. This action does not advance the environment state.
- `step(actions)`: Executes an ordered sequence of up to 50 discrete primitives. The available primitives were: `FORWARD` (advances 0.25 m), `LEFT` (rotates 15°), `RIGHT` (rotates 15°), and `STOP` (terminates the episode). The tool reported the number of executed primitives and the remaining budget.
- **Information Exclusion:** Crucially, the agent received no pose, odometry, depth information, panoramas, maps, waypoint candidates, collision feedback, or privileged simulator state. It had to infer all navigation-relevant information solely from its monocular observation history.
- **Budget:** A maximum of 500 low-level simulator steps and 200 LLM calls per episode. Episode timeout was 2400 seconds.

**Agent Harnesses and Models**
Three off-the-shelf agent harnesses, originally designed for software engineering, were used to sustain interaction:
- `mini-swe-agent`
- Claude Agent SDK
- Codex CLI (OpenAI's local software-agent harness)
A variety of frontier VLMs were evaluated, including models from the Qwen series (e.g., `qwen3.5-4b`, `qwen3.5-9b`, `qwen3.5-plus`), GPT series (e.g., `gpt-5.5`, `gpt-5.6`), and Claude series (e.g., `sonnet-5`, `opus-4.8`, `fable-5`, `opus-5`).

**Ablation Studies**
To localize the sources of capability, controlled interventions were performed:
- **Model Ablation:** Compared performance across different VLMs while holding the harness and interface fixed.
- **Reasoning Effort Ablation:** Varied the vendor-defined reasoning effort (e.g., default vs. max/xhigh) for specific models within a fixed harness and interface to assess its impact on SR and efficiency.
- **Harness Ablation:** Compared the performance of different agent harnesses using the same VLM and interface.
- **Interface Ablation (Fixed Waypoint):** Replaced the primitive `step()` tool with a `step(waypoint_id)` tool. This waypoint module was a *trained depth-based waypoint predictor* that proposed up to five candidate waypoints. This forced the agent to operate at a higher level of abstraction.
- **Hybrid Interface (Optional Capability):** Exposed *both* the primitive `step()` tool and the trained waypoint module simultaneously, allowing the agent to choose between fine-grained primitive control and coarse-grained waypoint guidance.

**Failure Analysis**
A detailed qualitative analysis was conducted on all 25 failed episodes from one `fable-5` run on R2R-CE. Each failure was reconstructed frame-by-frame from its raw log to diagnose the primary failure mechanism and categorize it.

**Physical Robot Deployment**
The minimal agent (using `fable-5` and Claude SDK) was qualitatively deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot in an office environment. The robot's low-level control was wrapped behind the same monocular RGB view and primitive `step` actions as in the simulator. The physical runs were assessed through archived frames and motion reports, focusing on the transferability of reasoning and the emergence of new failure modes in a real-world setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the capabilities and limitations of agentic embodied control:

**Minimal-Interface Performance (R2R-CE)**
- Under the strictly minimal conditions (monocular RGB, four discrete actions, no navigation-specific scaffolding), replicated default-effort runs achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 70.7 ± 3.5% with `opus-5` and 68.3 ± 1.5% with `fable-5`.
- With maximum reasoning effort, `fable-5` reached 78% SR (65.27 SPL).
- These results position minimal-interface agentic agents competitively with prior engineered zero-shot systems (e.g., AgenticNav at 55 SR with more scaffolding) and within the performance range of recent industrial-scale trained navigation policies (e.g., Qwen-RobotNav at 72 SR, albeit on different test splits and with extensive training).

**Ablations of Model, Harness, and Interface**
- **Model Capability:** Model choice was the most significant factor influencing performance, with SR spanning from 5% (qwen3.5-4b) to 72% (fable-5) when the harness and interface were held constant. This suggests that the inherent capabilities of the VLM, particularly in spatial scene understanding, multi-turn multimodal interaction, and context retrieval, are central to the agent's performance.
- **Reasoning Effort:** Varying vendor-defined reasoning effort showed model-specific rather than uniform effects. `fable-5` demonstrated a notable gain of 9.7 SR at maximum effort, while other models showed smaller, inconsistent shifts ranging from -5 to +6 SR.
- **Harness Effects:** Differences in SR across the three general-purpose agent harnesses (`mini-swe-agent`, Claude Agent SDK, Codex CLI) were minor (1.7–7.3 SR points) and lacked a consistent direction. This indicates that these harnesses primarily facilitate interaction rather than conferring substantial navigation capability themselves.
- **Interface Augmentation (Fixed Waypoint):** The impact of a fixed waypoint interface was dependent on the primitive control ability of the VLM.
 - For weaker models (e.g., Qwen series, GPT-5.5/5.6), forcing a waypoint interface provided substantial SR gains (ranging from +17 to +38 SR points on R2R-CE), compensating for their weak primitive grounding.
 - For stronger models (e.g., `fable-5`, `opus-5`), the gains were marginal (+0.7 to +1.3 SR on R2R-CE).
 - On VLNVerse, the waypoint interface *reduced* SR by 4–6 points for strong models and significantly increased collision rates (four- to six-fold), indicating that it could impose a mismatched action abstraction.

**Hybrid Interface: Optional Scaffolding**
- When both primitive actions and the trained waypoint module were offered as *optional capabilities* to the `fable-5` agent, it achieved 76.7 ± 0.6% SR at default effort. This surpassed the performance of both fixed primitive (68.3 SR) and fixed waypoint (69 SR) interfaces at the same effort.
- The hybrid agent adopted a "coarse-to-fine" strategy, using waypoints for initial localization and long-distance travel, then switching to primitives for fine-grained control near the target.
- This approach also demonstrated improved efficiency, achieving nearly the same SR as maximum-effort primitive runs with half the environment steps and less than one quarter of the wall time. This suggests that scaffolding is most effective when the agent controls its invocation.

**Long-Horizon Limitations**
- **Performance Degradation:** Performance dropped sharply on longer-horizon tasks; `fable-5` SR fell from 75% on R2R-CE to 26% on RxR-CE with primitive control, and to 39% with the waypoint interface.
- **Latency and Context Growth:** The minimal loop re-sends its entire observation history on every call, leading to unbounded context growth (median 33k tokens, max 169k tokens on R2R-CE). This resulted in high wall times (median 206s per episode) and made sustained operation impractical, especially on RxR-CE where context and wall time further doubled or tripled.

**Simulator Behavior and Failure Analysis**
- **Silent Failures:** 23 of 25 failures ended with a voluntary `STOP`, 22 of which included explicit claims of success, irrespective of the large final distance to goal (3.1m to 44.6m).
- **Route-Level Issues:** Most failures were due to early divergence from the correct route (only 4/25 trajectories ever entered the 3m success region).
- **Failure Categories:**
 - **Wrong Referent / Branch (12/25):** Misinterpreting ambiguous instructions or binding referents to incorrect visual instances.
 - **Stop-Decision Failures (6/25):** Route was largely correct, but stopping was anchored to the named object rather than the true annotated endpoint.
 - **Runaway Search (4/25):** Agent continued searching indefinitely when expected landmarks did not appear, rather than re-evaluating its committed decisions.
 - **Geometry / Simulator Traps (3/25):** Failures due to environmental geometry (e.g., unseen obstacles) combined with the lack of collision feedback, leading to physical wedging or loss of pose.
- **Cognitive Failure Signature:** "Self-diagnosis without self-correction" was a recurring pattern, where the agent correctly identified doubts or contradictions but proceeded with the wrong decision.

**Physical Robot Deployment (Unitree Go2)**
- **Reasoning and Perception Transfer:** Instruction-level reasoning (e.g., conditional logic, multi-stage routes), object recognition, and person disambiguation (e.g., by shoe color) transferred effectively to the physical robot. The agent could also correct for under-executed turns.
- **Embodiment Gap:** The agent lacked awareness of its own body, leading to collisions (e.g., turning before its physical body cleared a doorway). Coarse actuation and the lack of precise feedback on action execution further compounded these issues.
- **Lack of Spatial Memory:** The absence of a persistent map meant the agent struggled with tasks requiring integrated views over time, such as counting objects across revisits, distinguishing similar targets, or accurately judging distance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI by demonstrating the viability and current limitations of **agentic embodied control**, a paradigm where general-purpose models themselves direct the interaction loop.

**Reconsideration of Control Authority:** The primary significance is the empirical evidence that minimal-interface, zero-shot agents, powered by frontier VLMs, can achieve navigation performance competitive with specialized, engineered zero-shot systems and even industrial-scale trained policies. This finding suggests that a substantial portion of embodied decision-making capability already resides within general-purpose models, challenging the traditional reliance on external, human-authored policies or workflows for high-level control. This shifts the focus from building rigid architectures around a model to empowering the model to control its own interactions.

**Model-Centered, Composable Systems:** The ablation studies highlight that while the VLM is the primary source of capability, the surrounding system (harness and interface) significantly shapes how that capability is expressed. The discovery that a trained navigation module (waypoint predictor) becomes substantially more beneficial, faster, and cheaper when offered as an *optional tool* for the agent to compose, rather than as a fixed control path, is particularly impactful. This indicates a future where diverse task-specific policies, planners, and sensors could be organized as callable capabilities under the agent's flexible control, leading to more adaptable and efficient embodied systems.

**Clear Roadmap for Autonomous Agents:** The detailed characterization of limitations—including sharp performance degradation on longer horizons, unbounded context growth, high latency, and specific failure modes (e.g., self-diagnosis without self-correction, lack of body awareness, absence of persistent spatial memory)—provides a clear roadmap for future research. These issues underscore the need for embodied-native harnesses that can manage persistent, resource-bounded state, and for models that are trained to effectively utilize such structures.

**Bootstrapping Cycle Hypothesis:** The paper proposes a "bootstrapping cycle" as a potential path towards robust autonomous embodied agents. This cycle envisions scaffolds (like maps, waypoint modules, or engineered harnesses) generating verifiable trajectories, which in turn train better models through techniques like supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. These improved models could then simplify or modify the scaffolds they require, creating a continuous loop of progress. This hypothesis offers a framework for integrating domain-specific knowledge and general intelligence.

**Impact on Robotics and AI:** The findings imply that future embodied agents could be more general-purpose and less task-specific. Instead of requiring extensive re-engineering for each new task or environment, a core agentic system might adapt by composing available tools and skills. This has potential implications for household robots, industrial automation, and other domains requiring adaptable, long-duration autonomous operation, though current limitations in sustained, open-ended autonomy still need to be addressed. The work suggests that the critical question is not merely how much scaffolding to add, but whether it is imposed by the designer or placed under the agent's intelligent control.
