# ETR: Entropy Trend Reward for Efficient Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Toronto and McMaster University developed the Entropy Trend Reward (ETR) to guide Large Language Models toward more efficient Chain-of-Thought reasoning. ETR shapes the trajectory of predictive uncertainty, leading to significantly shorter reasoning chains while maintaining or improving accuracy, for example, boosting DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B's accuracy by 9.9% while reducing CoT length by 67%.

## Problem

- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs frequently generates excessively long, repetitive, or contradictory sequences, known as "overthinking," increasing inference latency and computational overhead.
- Existing methods to reduce CoT length, such as content-blind length penalties or global entropy minimization, can truncate useful steps, compromise accuracy, or misalign with effective human reasoning dynamics.
- Prior entropy reduction strategies often lack trajectory awareness, assuming uniformly low uncertainty is ideal throughout reasoning rather than acknowledging an initial exploratory phase followed by progressive uncertainty reduction.

## Method

- The **ETR** (Entropy Trend Reward) is introduced as a ==trajectory-level reward== that employs a ==momentum-based accumulation== of step-wise entropy changes, designed to encourage a global trend of uncertainty reduction.
- **ETR** assigns higher rewards to earlier uncertainty reductions, promoting rapid convergence of the belief state and discouraging oscillatory self-reflection.
- Integrated into the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== framework, ETR acts as an ==efficiency shaping term== that refines reasoning trajectories only when the final answer is correct, prioritizing accuracy while improving conciseness.

## Results

- **ETR** consistently achieved the highest Accuracy–Efficiency Trade-off Score (AES) across all evaluated models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B) and benchmarks (AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, GPQA Diamond).
- For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B, ETR improved overall accuracy by **+9.9%** (from **58.1%** to **68.0%**) and reduced average CoT length by **67%** (from 8.5k to 2.8k tokens).
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of momentum-based entropy trend aggregation combined with correctness supervision for optimal performance, while alternative entropy-based and length-based baselines showed limitations in jointly optimizing accuracy and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient reasoning is characterized by a global trend of progressive uncertainty reduction, rather than static low uncertainty at every step, allowing for productive local exploration within a converging trajectory.
> - Empirical analysis revealed a strong correlation: CoTs with a greater prevalence of downward entropy trends were substantially shorter, motivating the design of a reward focused on this dynamic.
> - The momentum-based ETR implicitly enables instance-adaptive termination, allowing LLMs to adjust reasoning length based on problem difficulty by penalizing steps that increase uncertainty while rewarding productive refinement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently employ Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to address complex tasks by breaking them down into intermediate steps. This approach has been observed to enhance answer accuracy, interpretability, and robustness. However, CoT often presents practical challenges, notably the generation of excessively long, repetitive, or sometimes contradictory reasoning sequences, referred to as "overthinking." This verbosity significantly increases inference latency and computational overhead.

Current research efforts to mitigate this inefficiency can be broadly categorized. Training-free methods involve heuristic prompt design or early stopping criteria to reduce reasoning length. Examples include DEER and NoThink. However, these methods may exhibit reduced effectiveness with smaller models or struggle with content-blind truncation. Training-based approaches include supervised fine-tuning, which uses datasets with varied reasoning trace lengths but often has limited generalization, and reinforcement learning (RL). RL-based methods, such as those employing length-based reward designs (e.g., LCPO, O1-Pruner), encourage concise reasoning by penalizing long trajectories. A limitation of length-based rewards is their content-blind nature, potentially discouraging informative intermediate steps necessary for accuracy.

More recent work has explored the relationship between CoT length and predictive uncertainty, proposing to reduce global or instantaneous entropy (e.g., CGRS, PEAR). This perspective implicitly assumes that uniformly low uncertainty is desirable throughout the reasoning process. The presented work identifies a gap in this assumption, contending that efficient human reasoning is not characterized by constant low uncertainty, but rather by an initial exploratory phase followed by a progressive narrowing of plausible solutions. Consequently, this paper posits that existing entropy-reduction strategies, which lack trajectory awareness, may not fully capture or optimize the dynamics of efficient reasoning. The contribution of this work lies in addressing this gap by shifting the focus from static entropy measures to the trajectory-level dynamics of uncertainty reduction during CoT generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed inefficiency of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs). While CoT enhances performance on complex tasks, it frequently leads to unnecessarily long, repetitive, and sometimes contradictory reasoning sequences, a phenomenon termed "overthinking." This verbosity contributes to increased inference latency and computational resource consumption.

Existing methods designed to reduce CoT length, such as explicit length penalties or global entropy minimization, have demonstrated limitations. Length penalties can be content-blind, potentially truncating useful intermediate steps and compromising final accuracy. Approaches based on global entropy reduction implicitly assume that consistently low uncertainty is ideal at every stage of reasoning. The authors argue that this assumption misaligns with the nature of effective human reasoning, where initial steps might involve exploration and higher uncertainty, followed by a gradual and progressive reduction of uncertainty as a solution emerges. Directly suppressing all uncertainty may inadvertently penalize productive self-reflection or necessary exploratory steps.

To validate this intuition, the authors conducted an empirical analysis of step-wise entropy dynamics in generated CoTs, particularly on the MATH500 dataset. This analysis involved quantifying the directionality of uncertainty evolution using the Spearman rank correlation (ρ) between the reasoning step index and the entropy at each step. A smaller ρ value indicated a stronger global entropy descent. The findings revealed a monotonic relationship: CoTs with a greater prevalence of downward entropy trends (smaller ρ) were substantially shorter, whereas those exhibiting frequent entropy increases were considerably longer. This empirical observation motivated the central hypothesis that reasoning efficiency is closely linked to the *global trend* of uncertainty reduction, rather than its instantaneous magnitude.

The key objective of this research is to develop a novel reward mechanism, the Entropy Trend Reward (ETR), that explicitly shapes the trajectory of uncertainty during CoT generation. ETR is designed to encourage reasoning behaviors characterized by steady, coherent uncertainty reduction over time, while allowing for limited, productive local exploration. This trajectory-aware objective aims to provide dense feedback throughout the reasoning process, enabling LLMs to differentiate between efficient exploration and inefficient uncertainty oscillation, ultimately leading to shorter, more decisive reasoning traces without compromising answer quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces the Entropy Trend Reward (ETR), a trajectory-level reward designed to enhance the efficiency of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. ETR is integrated into the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework to guide policy updates.

#### 3.1. Entropy Trajectory of Chain-of-Thought

For a given input question, the model generates a CoT response `o = {C_1, C_2..., C_T}`, where each `C_t` represents a reasoning step. In practice, reasoning steps are delimited by the "\n\n" separator. For each step `t`, a scalar entropy measure `H_t` is defined as the Shannon entropy of the model's next-token predictive distribution, `H_t = H(p_θ(· | C_{1:t}))`. This measure provides an internal estimate of predictive uncertainty at each step. The step-wise entropy change, `Δ_t = H_{t-1} - H_t` for `t = 2..., T`, quantifies whether uncertainty has reduced (`Δ_t > 0`) or increased (`Δ_t < 0`).

#### 3.2. Momentum-Based Entropy Trend Reward (ETR)

To address the limitation of naive total entropy reduction (which only considers initial and final entropy), ETR employs a momentum-based accumulation of entropy changes. A latent trend variable `S_t` is defined recursively as `S_t = γS_{t-1} + Δ_t`, with an initial value `S_1 = 0` and a momentum coefficient `γ ∈ (0, 1)`. The entropy reward for a CoT, `R_entropy(o)`, is then calculated as the sum of these trend variables over all steps: `R_entropy(o) = Σ_{t=2}^T S_t`.

Unrolling this recurrence relation reveals that `R_entropy(o) = Σ_{t=2}^T α_t Δ_t`, where `α_t = (1 - γ^(T-t+1)) / (1 - γ)`. This formulation assigns a gradient signal to every step, allowing each entropy drop or increase to influence the reward. A key property is that `α_t` is strictly decreasing with `t`, meaning that uncertainty reductions occurring earlier in the reasoning trajectory receive a higher reward than those occurring later. This design encourages rapid convergence of the belief state rather than postponing critical deductions. In the experiments, `γ` was set to 0.9.

#### 3.3. Instance-Adaptive Termination via Entropy Trend Shaping

ETR implicitly promotes instance-adaptive termination without explicit length constraints. The reward accumulates `S_t`, which benefits from additional steps only if they contribute to a positive average uncertainty reduction. Steps that increase entropy (`Δ_t < 0`) negatively impact `S_t` and are penalized, thereby discouraging oscillatory self-reflection. This mechanism results in shorter CoTs for easy problems, where uncertainty drops quickly, and allows for more steps in harder problems only when they contribute to gradual belief refinement. This approach contrasts with global entropy penalties by tolerating limited local exploration while enforcing a coherent global descent.

#### 3.4. Integration with GRPO

ETR is incorporated into GRPO by decomposing the overall reward function. The total reward `R(q, o)` is structured to prioritize correctness while shaping efficient reasoning:
`R(q, o) = -1`, if the final answer is incorrect.
`R(q, o) = 1 + λR_entropy(o)`, if the final answer is correct.
Here, `R_corr(q, o)` evaluates the correctness, and `λ > 0` controls the influence of the entropy-based efficiency shaping. This design ensures that correctness is a hard constraint, and ETR only refines correct reasoning trajectories. GRPO's relative advantage normalization further enhances comparative efficiency among valid CoTs within each rollout group.

#### 3.5. Training Details

Training was conducted using the open-source VeRL framework on 8 NVIDIA H-100 GPUs. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) was employed with a rank of 32 and alpha of 64 to reduce memory footprint. Key hyperparameters included a batch size of 32, a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁵, a maximum response length of 16,384 tokens, and 5 rollouts per sample.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated ETR across multiple reasoning models and benchmarks, demonstrating its efficacy in improving the accuracy-efficiency trade-off for CoT reasoning.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset**: 7,000 problems sampled from DeepMath-103K (difficulty levels 5-10), with verification against evaluation benchmarks to prevent overlap.
- **Benchmarks**: Mathematical reasoning (AIME24, AMC23, MATH500) and general knowledge-intensive reasoning (GPQA Diamond).
- **Reasoning Models**: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B.
- **Baselines**: Training-free (DEER, NoThink) and RL-based (LCPO, O1-Pruner, PEAR).
- **Metrics**: Accuracy (Acc), Response Length (Len), Compression Rate (CR), and Accuracy–Efficiency Trade-off Score (AES) with `ς = 5` (prioritizing accuracy).

#### 4.2. Main Results

ETR consistently achieved the highest AES across all evaluated models and benchmarks (Table 1).
- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B**: ETR improved overall accuracy by +9.9% (from 58.1% to 68.0%) and reduced average CoT length by 67% (from 8.5k to 2.8k tokens).
- **Qwen3-4B**: ETR increased overall accuracy from 69.5% to 77.1% and decreased average length from 8.7k to 4.4k tokens.
- **Qwen3-8B**: ETR raised overall accuracy from 74.0% to 79.1% and shortened average length from 8.9k to 5.1k tokens.

Compared to baselines:
- Training-free methods (DEER, NoThink) struggled to jointly optimize accuracy and efficiency, often preserving accuracy at the expense of length or aggressively truncating with significant accuracy loss.
- Length-based RL methods (LCPO, O1-Pruner) achieved length reduction but often with notable accuracy degradation.
- Entropy-based RL (PEAR) generally preserved accuracy better than length-based methods but produced longer reasoning chains than ETR.
ETR demonstrated robust performance across different model families and sizes, indicating its generalizability beyond specific architectures.

#### 4.3. Empirical Analysis

- **Ablation Study on Entropy Rewards (Table 2)**:
 - Direct optimization of absolute entropy (minimization or maximization) was found to be suboptimal. "Min. H" shortened CoTs but with a lower AES than ETR, while "Max. H" collapsed reasoning.
 - Removing momentum ("No γ") in the reward formulation failed to effectively capture the overall entropy trend, resulting in longer CoTs.
 - Removing correctness supervision ("No R_corr") led to very short CoTs but substantial accuracy degradation.
 - These results collectively support the necessity of combining momentum-based entropy trend aggregation with correctness supervision for optimal performance.
- **Analysis on Entropy Trend (Figure 5)**: The Spearman rank correlation (ρ) between reasoning step index and step-wise entropy showed a consistent shift towards negative values after ETR training. This indicates that ETR successfully promoted *convergent reasoning*, with uncertainty progressively reducing across steps.
- **Behavioral View of CoT Reduction (Figure 6)**: ETR reduced CoT length primarily by maintaining low per-step verbosity while preserving a moderate degree of multi-step reasoning and self-verification. In contrast, direct entropy minimization aggressively suppressed exploratory and reflective processes, leading to fewer steps and reflection markers but higher tokens per sentence. This suggests ETR prevents oscillatory "overthinking" without enforcing uniformly low uncertainty.
- **Model Scale Analysis (Table 7)**: ETR scaled effectively to a larger DeepSeek-R1-Distill-14B model, improving accuracy and significantly reducing length. It also proved effective on a smaller DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B model, enhancing both reasoning quality and efficiency.
- **Step-Level Entropy After ETR (Figure 7)**: ETR-trained models exhibited more efficient convergence of entropy trajectories, with necessary self-reflection occurring productively without the frequent "entropy spikes" observed in baseline models.
- **Alternative Segmentation Strategies (Table 8)**: Token-level segmentation, while dramatically reducing length, led to accuracy degradation due to its overly fine-grained nature. ETR's step-level segmentation maintained a better balance.
- **Broadening Task Scopes (HumanEval, Table 9)**: ETR transferred effectively to an out-of-distribution coding benchmark (HumanEval), improving or maintaining accuracy while reducing reasoning length. This suggests ETR targets general reasoning convergence rather than domain-specific patterns.
- **Influence of ETR on Final Answer (Appendix F.7)**: ETR models produced more concise and information-dense final answers, reducing the verbosity often carried over from internal CoTs in traditional models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Entropy Trend Reward (ETR) as a contribution to the field of efficient Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for Large Language Models (LLMs). The work introduces a novel perspective by demonstrating that reasoning efficiency is primarily determined by the global *trajectory* of predictive uncertainty, rather than its absolute or instantaneous magnitude. This insight represents a shift from static entropy measures to the dynamic characteristics of reasoning.

The development of ETR offers a principled approach to shaping LLM reasoning. By providing a trajectory-aware objective, ETR encourages a progressive reduction in uncertainty over time while accommodating limited, beneficial local exploration. This mechanism guides models towards more coherent and decisive reasoning paths, addressing the issue of "overthinking" that often leads to unnecessarily long and inefficient CoTs.

The empirical results indicate that ETR consistently achieves a superior accuracy–efficiency trade-off compared to existing methods. This is evidenced by significant reductions in CoT length while either maintaining or improving answer accuracy across various models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill, Qwen3) and diverse benchmarks (mathematical and general reasoning). For instance, ETR improved DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B's accuracy by 9.9% while reducing CoT length by 67% across four benchmarks.

The potential impacts of this work include:
- **Reduced Inference Latency and Computational Overhead**: Shorter CoT traces directly translate to faster LLM inference and lower computational resource consumption, enhancing the practical deployment and scalability of LLMs.
- **Improved Reasoning Quality**: By promoting convergent reasoning and managing self-reflection efficiently, ETR contributes to more focused and effective problem-solving, potentially leading to higher-quality outputs.
- **Enhanced Interpretability**: More concise and targeted reasoning paths, which avoid excessive oscillation or redundancy, may be easier for human users to understand and verify.
- **Instance-Adaptive Behavior**: The implicit instance-adaptive termination mechanism means that the model naturally adjusts its reasoning length based on problem difficulty, without requiring explicit length constraints or manual tuning.
- **Generalizability**: The demonstrated effectiveness of ETR across different model sizes, families, and even out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., coding) suggests its broad applicability and robustness to various reasoning domains.

A stated limitation of the work is the computational constraint that restricted comparisons to models up to 8B parameters using LoRA-based training. The authors indicate that scaling the method to larger models and conducting broader comparisons is a direction for future research.
