# ETR: Entropy Trend Reward for Efficient Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Toronto and McMaster University developed the Entropy Trend Reward (ETR) to guide Large Language Models toward more efficient Chain-of-Thought reasoning. ETR shapes the trajectory of predictive uncertainty, leading to significantly shorter reasoning chains while maintaining or improving accuracy, for example, boosting DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B's accuracy by 9.9% while reducing CoT length by 67%.

## Problem

- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs frequently generates excessively long, repetitive, or contradictory sequences, known as "overthinking," increasing inference latency and computational overhead.
- Existing methods to reduce CoT length, such as content-blind length penalties or global entropy minimization, can truncate useful steps, compromise accuracy, or misalign with effective human reasoning dynamics.
- Prior entropy reduction strategies often lack trajectory awareness, assuming uniformly low uncertainty is ideal throughout reasoning rather than acknowledging an initial exploratory phase followed by progressive uncertainty reduction.

## Method

- The **ETR** (Entropy Trend Reward) is introduced as a ==trajectory-level reward== that employs a ==momentum-based accumulation== of step-wise entropy changes, designed to encourage a global trend of uncertainty reduction.
- **ETR** assigns higher rewards to earlier uncertainty reductions, promoting rapid convergence of the belief state and discouraging oscillatory self-reflection.
- Integrated into the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== framework, ETR acts as an ==efficiency shaping term== that refines reasoning trajectories only when the final answer is correct, prioritizing accuracy while improving conciseness.

## Results

- **ETR** consistently achieved the highest Accuracy–Efficiency Trade-off Score (AES) across all evaluated models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B) and benchmarks (AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, GPQA Diamond).
- For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B, ETR improved overall accuracy by **+9.9%** (from **58.1%** to **68.0%**) and reduced average CoT length by **67%** (from 8.5k to 2.8k tokens).
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of momentum-based entropy trend aggregation combined with correctness supervision for optimal performance, while alternative entropy-based and length-based baselines showed limitations in jointly optimizing accuracy and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient reasoning is characterized by a global trend of progressive uncertainty reduction, rather than static low uncertainty at every step, allowing for productive local exploration within a converging trajectory.
> - Empirical analysis revealed a strong correlation: CoTs with a greater prevalence of downward entropy trends were substantially shorter, motivating the design of a reward focused on this dynamic.
> - The momentum-based ETR implicitly enables instance-adaptive termination, allowing LLMs to adjust reasoning length based on problem difficulty by penalizing steps that increase uncertainty while rewarding productive refinement.
