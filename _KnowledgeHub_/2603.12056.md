# XSkill: Continual Learning from Experience and Skills in Multimodal Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> XSKILL introduces a dual-stream framework that enables multimodal agents to continually learn from visually-grounded task-level skills and action-level experiences without explicit retraining. This approach consistently improves agent performance, showing Average@4 gains of 2.58 to 6.71 points over baselines across various benchmarks, by enhancing tool-use efficiency and flexibility.

## Problem

- Multimodal agents exhibit inefficient tool use, often consuming excessive computational steps for simple problems or failing to conduct deep multi-turn exploration for complex queries.
- Tool orchestration in existing systems is inflexible, typically confined to single-path execution with limited ability to compose tools in a generalizable manner across varied tasks.
- Current continual learning methods for agents predominantly rely on textual trajectory logs, which are insufficient for multimodal settings where critical decision signals are visually grounded.

## Method

- **XSKILL** employs a ==dual-stream framework== to maintain two complementary forms of knowledge: 'Skills' (task-level structured workflows and reusable tool templates) and 'Experiences' (action-level tactical prompts for specific contexts and failure patterns), both visually grounded.
- Two specialized MLLM instances are used: an ==MLLM_exec== for tool-use inference and an ==MLLM_kb== for knowledge base operations like extraction, consolidation, and adaptation, enabling cross-model knowledge transfer.
- The framework operates in two phases: accumulating knowledge from multi-path rollouts via visually-grounded summarization, cross-rollout critique, and hierarchical consolidation, then dynamically retrieving and injecting adapted knowledge (skills and experiences) into the agent's prompt during inference.

## Results

- XSKILL consistently outperformed tool-only and learning-based baselines across five benchmarks and four MLLM backbones, achieving Average@4 gains ranging from **2.58 to 6.71 points**.
- The framework demonstrated cross-model transferability, where knowledge accumulated by one backbone (e.g., Gemini-3-Flash) improved the performance of other models (e.g., GPT-5-mini) by **2.58 to 4.16** Average@4 points.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both skills and experiences are essential and complementary, with removing either leading to significant performance drops, and demonstrated strong zero-shot cross-task transferability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continual learning in multimodal agents benefits from a dual-stream approach that explicitly separates and visually grounds task-level skills (structured procedures) and action-level experiences (context-specific tactical advice).
> - Skills mitigate inefficient tool use by providing structured workflows and reusable templates, reducing common execution errors like syntax or tool name mistakes.
> - Experiences enable flexible tool orchestration by capturing context-aware tactical knowledge, influencing adaptive tool selection patterns such as increased use of code interpreters or image search calls.
