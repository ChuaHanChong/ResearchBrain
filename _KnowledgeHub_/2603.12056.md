# XSkill: Continual Learning from Experience and Skills in Multimodal Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> XSKILL introduces a dual-stream framework that enables multimodal agents to continually learn from visually-grounded task-level skills and action-level experiences without explicit retraining. This approach consistently improves agent performance, showing Average@4 gains of 2.58 to 6.71 points over baselines across various benchmarks, by enhancing tool-use efficiency and flexibility.

## Problem

- Multimodal agents exhibit inefficient tool use, often consuming excessive computational steps for simple problems or failing to conduct deep multi-turn exploration for complex queries.
- Tool orchestration in existing systems is inflexible, typically confined to single-path execution with limited ability to compose tools in a generalizable manner across varied tasks.
- Current continual learning methods for agents predominantly rely on textual trajectory logs, which are insufficient for multimodal settings where critical decision signals are visually grounded.

## Method

- **XSKILL** employs a ==dual-stream framework== to maintain two complementary forms of knowledge: 'Skills' (task-level structured workflows and reusable tool templates) and 'Experiences' (action-level tactical prompts for specific contexts and failure patterns), both visually grounded.
- Two specialized MLLM instances are used: an ==MLLM_exec== for tool-use inference and an ==MLLM_kb== for knowledge base operations like extraction, consolidation, and adaptation, enabling cross-model knowledge transfer.
- The framework operates in two phases: accumulating knowledge from multi-path rollouts via visually-grounded summarization, cross-rollout critique, and hierarchical consolidation, then dynamically retrieving and injecting adapted knowledge (skills and experiences) into the agent's prompt during inference.

## Results

- XSKILL consistently outperformed tool-only and learning-based baselines across five benchmarks and four MLLM backbones, achieving Average@4 gains ranging from **2.58 to 6.71 points**.
- The framework demonstrated cross-model transferability, where knowledge accumulated by one backbone (e.g., Gemini-3-Flash) improved the performance of other models (e.g., GPT-5-mini) by **2.58 to 4.16** Average@4 points.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both skills and experiences are essential and complementary, with removing either leading to significant performance drops, and demonstrated strong zero-shot cross-task transferability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continual learning in multimodal agents benefits from a dual-stream approach that explicitly separates and visually grounds task-level skills (structured procedures) and action-level experiences (context-specific tactical advice).
> - Skills mitigate inefficient tool use by providing structured workflows and reusable templates, reducing common execution errors like syntax or tool name mistakes.
> - Experiences enable flexible tool orchestration by capturing context-aware tactical knowledge, influencing adaptive tool selection patterns such as increased use of code interpreters or image search calls.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Phase I: Accumulation of Task Experience & Skills

This phase focuses on distilling and consolidating knowledge from agent interaction trajectories.
1. **Rollout Summary**: For each training task, `MLLM_exec` performs multiple independent rollouts, generating a set of diverse trajectories. `MLLM_kb` then conducts visually-grounded rollout summarization. This process takes the trajectory set, task images, query, ground truth, and any adapted skills as input. The summarization is designed to integrate interleaved image observations with trajectory text, analyzing each image in conjunction with its local generation context (e.g., tool calls, intermediate outputs). The output includes a trajectory summary, detailing key decision points, tool usage patterns, and failure reasons, and also extracts skill fragments. Skill extraction identifies structured workflows and reusable tool templates, replacing specific entities with variable placeholders to promote generalization.
2. **Cross-Rollout Critique**: Following summarization, `MLLM_kb` performs a contrastive analysis across the trajectory summaries, comparing successful and failed attempts against the ground truth and any experiences used during the rollouts. This critique aims to identify causal factors for different outcomes and generate structured experience updates. These updates can either introduce a new experience (`add` operation) or refine an existing one (`modify` operation). Experiences are constrained in length and must be generalizable.
3. **Knowledge Consolidation**: To ensure the scalability and quality of the knowledge base, a hierarchical consolidation mechanism is employed:
 - **Experience Consolidation**: Before adding new experiences, the system checks for high cosine similarity with existing entries. If similar experiences are found, `MLLM_kb` merges them into a single, comprehensive entry. The system also actively manages the total number of experiences, pruning redundant or low-quality items when the bank exceeds a predefined size, with `MLLM_kb` assessing generalizability and actionability.
 - **Skill Consolidation**: Extracted skill fragments are integrated into the global skill document (K). `MLLM_kb` is responsible for updating, merging, or removing content within the skill document. A skill manager monitors the document length, triggering refinement by `MLLM_kb` to assess generalizability, workflow correctness, and conciseness, and replacing concrete instances with reusable placeholders.

### 2. Phase II: Solving Task with Experience & Skills

During inference for a test task, XSKILL employs a dynamic retrieval and injection mechanism:
1. **Task Decomposition Retrieval**: To overcome visual-semantic gaps and query specificity, `MLLM_kb` decomposes the task query and images into several abstract subtasks. For each subtask, a specific textual query is generated based on both textual description and visual context. This subtask-specific query is then used to retrieve the top-k most relevant experiences from the Experience Bank based on semantic similarity. This multi-aspect retrieval aims for more precise and comprehensive knowledge access.
2. **Task Adaptation & Injection**:
 - **Experience Rewrite**: Retrieved experiences are generic. `MLLM_kb` rewrites these experiences, adapting their conditions to match the current task and visual state, instantiating actions with task-relevant details, and filtering out inapplicable entries. This ensures the guidance is actionable and visually grounded.
 - **Skill Adaptation**: The global skill document is adapted by `MLLM_kb` using the current images and task description. This involves pruning irrelevant sections, integrating the rewritten experiences into workflow steps, and adjusting code templates to be task-relevant. The resulting `K_adapted` is injected into the agent's system prompt as a non-prescriptive reference.

The framework is designed as a continual learning loop: the usage history (adapted skills and retrieved experiences) from Phase II is fed back into Phase I, serving as a reference to refine future rollout summaries and cross-rollout critiques, thereby allowing the knowledge base to evolve over time.

#### 2.1. Main Findings and Results

The XSKILL framework was evaluated across five benchmarks spanning visual agentic tool use (VisualToolBench, TIR-Bench), multimodal search (MMSearch-Plus, MMBrowseComp), and comprehensive multimodal reasoning (AgentVista). Four backbone models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3-Flash, GPT-5-mini, o4-mini) were used, with additional analysis on open-source models (Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct, Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) presented in the appendix. Performance was measured by Success Rate (SR), specifically Average@4 (average success rate over four rollouts) and Pass@4 (proportion of tasks with at least one successful rollout).

### 3. Primary Performance Enhancements

- **Consistent Outperformance of Baselines**: XSKILL consistently outperformed both tool-only and learning-based baselines across all evaluated benchmarks and backbone models. Average@4 gains over the tool-only baseline ranged from 2.58 to 6.71 points. On challenging settings, such as TIR-Bench with Gemini-3-Flash, XSKILL achieved a 47.75% Average@4, surpassing the strongest learning-based baseline, Agent-KB, by 11.13 points. This indicates the dual-stream knowledge capture effectively supports task resolution.
- **Cross-Model Transferability**: The framework demonstrated positive performance gains (2.58 to 4.16 points in Average@4) for models (GPT-5-mini, o4-mini) that used knowledge accumulated by Gemini-3-Flash, suggesting that the externalized knowledge structures are effective across different model architectures without requiring model-specific accumulation.

### 4. Ablation Study Insights (on VisualToolBench with Gemini-2.5-Pro)

- **Complementary Roles of Experiences and Skills**: Removing either experiences or skills individually led to performance drops of 3.04 and 3.85 points, respectively, validating the necessity and complementary nature of both knowledge streams.
- **Dominance of Accumulation Phase**: Components of Phase I (knowledge accumulation, e.g., Experience Manager, Skill Manager) contributed more significantly to overall performance than Phase II components (retrieval and adaptation, e.g., Task Decomposition, Task Adaptation), with drops of 4.09 and 3.62 points compared to 1.28 and 1.52 points, respectively. This suggests that the quality of accumulated knowledge is a critical factor.
- **Skills Mitigate Inefficient Tool Use**: Analysis of execution errors showed that skills substantially reduced structural mistakes. The overall error rate decreased from 29.9% (Experience Only) to 15.3% (Skill Only). Specifically, syntax errors decreased from 20.3% to 11.4%, and tool name errors were nearly eliminated (2.85% to 0.32%). This indicates that skills provide robust guidance for tool execution.
- **Experiences Enable Flexible Orchestration**: Experiences were observed to induce shifts in tool usage patterns. For example, on VisualToolBench, the 'Experience Only' setting increased code interpreter usage from 66.63% to 74.49%. On MMSearch-Plus, experiences doubled code interpreter usage (6.18% to 13.21%) and increased image search calls (15.43% to 24.63%). These shifts suggest experiences capture tactical, context-aware knowledge for tool selection.

### 5. Further Analysis

- **Impact of Rollout Values**: Performance, measured by both Average@4 and Pass@4, consistently improved as the number of rollouts (N) increased from 1 to 4. This indicates that a greater diversity of trajectories during the accumulation phase leads to the extraction of higher-quality, more generalizable knowledge.
- **Cross-Task Transferability**: Zero-shot transfer experiments, where knowledge accumulated on one benchmark was applied to another, demonstrated XSKILL's superior generalization. The method consistently outperformed baselines with average improvements of 2 to 3 points, attributed to its hierarchical consolidation and task-adaptation mechanisms.
- **Open-Source Model Evaluation (Appendix A.1)**: Evaluation with Qwen3-VL models showed mixed results. While gains were observed on MMSearch-Plus, there were negative effects on Average@4 for VisualToolBench compared to the tool-only baseline. However, Pass@4 generally improved, suggesting increased exploratory behavior. This indicates that the efficacy of knowledge transfer may depend on the foundational capabilities of the base model.

#### 5.1. Significance and Potential Impact

This work addresses a limitation in multimodal agents concerning the effective utilization of knowledge from past interactions. By introducing XSKILL, the authors present a framework that unifies task-level skills and action-level experiences, explicitly grounding both knowledge extraction and retrieval in visual observations. This approach moves beyond reliance on static documentation or text-only knowledge accumulation, which has been insufficient for multimodal reasoning tasks.

The empirical findings suggest that XSKILL consistently improves multimodal agent performance over existing baselines across various domains and backbone models. The ablation studies provide evidence for the distinct yet complementary advantages of the dual knowledge streams: skills contribute to robust tool execution by mitigating common errors, while experiences facilitate strategic tool selection based on task-specific contexts. The demonstrated zero-shot transferability suggests that XSKILL captures generalizable reasoning principles, potentially enabling agents to adapt to novel tasks more effectively.

From a practical perspective, XSKILL offers a non-parametric and scalable pathway for continuous agent improvement, as it does not necessitate computationally intensive parameter retraining. The externalization of knowledge into structured, human-readable representations could enhance the transparency and interpretability of agent decision-making. The explicit separation of skills and experiences may also allow human operators to audit, modify, or remove specific pieces of accumulated knowledge, potentially facilitating human-in-the-loop oversight.

However, the increased capabilities of agents, particularly concerning sensitive visual data and cross-model knowledge transfer, also present potential risks. More capable agents could be misused for malicious automation or might inadvertently inherit and amplify biases present in historical data. The demonstrated transferability further implies that biased knowledge from one model could propagate across others. The authors acknowledge these concerns, recommending human oversight, periodic bias auditing of accumulated knowledge bases, and access control policies for cross-model knowledge transfer as mitigating measures. The architecture's compatibility with iterative refinement as new tasks are encountered suggests a pathway toward continuously evolving autonomous systems in dynamic real-world environments.
