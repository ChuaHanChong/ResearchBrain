# VLA-InfoEntropy: A Training-Free Vision-Attention Information Entropy Approach for Vision-Language-Action Models Inference Acceleration and Success

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-InfoEntropy introduces a training-free inference acceleration method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, dynamically selecting important tokens using visual and attention entropy guided by timestep information. This approach reduces inference latency by 39.8% and achieves a 1.53x speedup while simultaneously boosting the average task success rate to 76.4% on the LIBERO benchmark.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models incur substantial computational costs and latency during inference, hindering real-time deployment in applications like robotics.
- Existing VLA acceleration methods often reduce redundancy in isolated dimensions (visual, linguistic) or rely on static token selection, failing to dynamically adapt to evolving task contexts.
- Current token informativeness criteria, such as attention scores, may not fully capture a token's intrinsic information content or its dynamic relevance to a task over time.

## Method

- **VLA-InfoEntropy** quantifies token informativeness using two ==training-free metrics==: ==visual entropy== for intrinsic visual content and ==attention entropy== for semantic relevance to the language prompt.
- A ==dynamic selection strategy== adaptively combines these entropy measures with temporal information (==timestep==) to adjust the focus from global visual context to task-specific semantic details as the action sequence progresses.
- Important tokens identified by this strategy are recomputed, while low-information tokens are efficiently reused via a ==Key-Value (KV) cache==, minimizing redundant computations.

## Results

- **VLA-InfoEntropy** achieved a **1.53x** speedup, reducing inference latency by **39.8%** and FLOPs by **34.9%** compared to the OpenVLA baseline.
- The method demonstrated an average success rate of **76.4%** across all LIBERO tasks, surpassing the OpenVLA baseline (**75.0%**) and other state-of-the-art acceleration methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the dynamic, timestep-conditioned integration of both visual and attention entropy yields superior performance over individual or statically combined entropy metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining visual, semantic, and temporal information in a dynamic, unified framework is critical for effective and efficient multimodal reasoning in VLA models.
> - Significant informational redundancy exists in VLA inference, and intelligently pruning these redundant features can improve both computational efficiency and task performance.
> - A training-free acceleration approach that avoids model modification and extensive fine-tuning offers a practical and readily deployable solution for VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, integrating visual perception, natural language understanding, and action decision-making into a unified framework. These models hold potential for applications in fields such as autonomous driving, robotics, and intelligent assistants, enabling agents to comprehend instructions and interact with dynamic environments.

However, the primary challenge in deploying VLA models in real-time and at scale is their substantial computational cost during inference. The joint processing of high-dimensional visual features, complex linguistic inputs, and continuous action sequences leads to significant computational overhead and latency, which can hinder system reliability and responsiveness.

Existing research efforts to improve VLA model efficiency generally focus on reducing redundancy within visual, linguistic, or action representations. Approaches include dynamic token pruning (e.g., LightVLA, FLASH-VLA, SP-VLA), and reusing static visual tokens through caching mechanisms (e.g., VLA-Cache). These methods primarily fall into two categories: training-based acceleration, which often requires significant computational resources for model modification (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT, PD-VLA, FAST, CEED-VLA), and training-free methods, which accelerate inference without altering model weights.

The current work builds upon training-free methods, particularly VLA-Cache, which reuses intermediate features. A recognized limitation of many existing approaches is their tendency to address visual redundancy, semantic relevance, and temporal coherence in isolation. For instance, visual pruning might inadvertently remove instruction-critical visual cues, while attention-based pruning can be susceptible to noise, leading to focus on irrelevant regions. Furthermore, the absence of explicit timestep information prevents models from dynamically adapting their focus to task-relevant details over the course of an action sequence. This paper aims to address this gap by proposing a unified, training-free framework that concurrently leverages visual, semantic, and temporal information for more effective and efficient token selection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the inference efficiency and success rate of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models without requiring additional training or modification of model weights. The authors aim to achieve this by developing a novel, dynamic token selection strategy that judiciously identifies and preserves critical information while reducing redundant computations.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations regarding current VLA inference pipelines:

1. **High Computational Overhead:** VLA models process large volumes of high-dimensional data, leading to significant computational costs. This overhead hinders their real-time deployment and widespread application, as inference latency directly impacts system reliability and responsiveness in embodied intelligence tasks.
2. **Informational Redundancy:** A considerable portion of the visual, linguistic, and action representations processed by VLA models contains redundant or low-information content. Indiscriminate processing of all tokens, as in traditional inference, leads to inefficient resource utilization.
3. **Limitations of Existing Acceleration Methods:**
 - **Isolated Redundancy Reduction:** Current methods often address redundancy in visual, semantic, or temporal dimensions separately. This fragmented approach can lead to suboptimal performance; for example, visual pruning might remove critical cues, or attention-based pruning might be misled by noise.
 - **Static Token Selection:** Many approaches employ a static token selection strategy that does not adapt to the evolving context of sequential decision-making. Without incorporating timestep information, the model's focus remains fixed, failing to adjust from global understanding to task-specific details as an action sequence progresses.
 - **Incomplete Informativeness Criteria:** While methods like VLA-Cache use attention scores to identify tokens for reuse, attention alone may not fully capture the intrinsic information content or dynamic task relevance of tokens. A more comprehensive measure is needed.
4. **Need for Training-Free Solutions:** Training-based acceleration methods incur additional computational costs and time for fine-tuning. A training-free approach would offer a more practical and readily deployable solution for improving VLA model efficiency.

To address these motivations, the authors propose VLA-InfoEntropy, a framework designed to:
- Quantify the intrinsic information content of visual tokens using visual entropy.
- Capture the semantic relevance of visual tokens to task-related language using attention entropy.
- Integrate these two entropy-based metrics with timestep information to enable a dynamic, adaptive token selection strategy.
- Facilitate the reuse of low-information tokens through a Key-Value (KV) cache mechanism, thereby reducing redundant computation and accelerating inference.
- Provide a unified strategy for efficient spatial, semantic, and temporal token selection that improves inference efficiency while maintaining or enhancing task performance.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to make VLA models more amenable to real-time, large-scale deployment in critical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VLA-InfoEntropy method is a training-free inference acceleration framework that integrates visual, semantic, and temporal information to dynamically select important tokens and enable efficient reuse of computation. The approach builds upon the concept of Key-Value (KV) cache reuse in VLA models, enhancing it with entropy-based metrics.

**A. Cache for Vision-Language-Action Models (Foundation)**

The proposed method extends the VLA-Cache approach. VLA-Cache partitions images into patches and calculates the cosine similarity between corresponding patches across successive frames. Patches with low similarity over time are considered part of a "static token" set ($P_{static}$). The core idea is to reuse the Key (K) and Value (V) representations of these static tokens from the previous timestep, avoiding recomputation. Crucially, VLA-Cache also defines a set of "important tokens" ($P_{important}$) that are *excluded* from $P_{static}$ because their recomputation is deemed essential. The reusable tokens ($P_{reuse}$) are then defined as $P_{static} \setminus P_{important}$. For any visual token $i$:
- If $i \in P_{reuse}$, then $K_t(i), V_t(i)$ are reused from $K_{t-1}(i), V_{t-1}(i)$.
- Otherwise, $K_t(i), V_t(i)$ are recomputed from the current image input $X_t(i)$.

The VLA-InfoEntropy method focuses on optimizing the identification of these "important tokens" to balance acceleration with performance.

**B. Visual Entropy and Attention Entropy**

The paper introduces two complementary, training-free entropy metrics to quantify token informativeness:

1. **Visual Entropy ($H_{img}$):** This metric quantifies the intrinsic information content of each visual token (image patch). It is calculated based on the grayscale distribution of pixels within a token. A more dispersed grayscale distribution (e.g., in textured regions or areas with sharp edges) indicates higher entropy and suggests that the token carries more visual information.
 The formula for visual entropy for the $i$-th visual token is:
 $H_{img}(i) = -\sum_{g=0}^{G-1} p_i(g) \log_2 p_i(g)$
 where $G$ is the number of grayscale levels (typically 256), and $p_i(g)$ is the probability of grayscale level $g$ in the $i$-th visual token. To unify with attention entropy, this is normalized as $eH_{img}(i) = H_{img}(i) / \log_2 G$. Tokens with higher $eH_{img}$ are considered visually salient.

2. **Attention Entropy ($H_{attn}$ and $eI_{attn}$):** This metric captures the semantic relevance of visual tokens to the task-related language prompt. It leverages the cross-attention matrix from the VLA model, which indicates how much each visual token attends to each text token.
 - For each visual token $i$, the text-to-vision attention scores ($A^{(l,h)}_{w \to i}$) across all layers $l$ and heads $h$ for each text token $w$ are extracted and averaged.
 - These averaged scores are then normalized using a softmax operation along the text-token dimension to yield $q(w, i)$, representing the distribution of visual token $i$'s attention over the set of text tokens $W$.
 - The attention entropy for the $i$-th visual token is then defined as:
 $H_{attn}(i) = -\sum_{w \in W} q(w, i) \log_2 q(w, i)$
 A low $H_{attn}$ indicates that the visual token's attention is highly concentrated on a few specific text tokens, implying strong semantic relevance. Conversely, high $H_{attn}$ suggests diffuse attention and less task relevance.
 - The "amount of information" or entropy reduction (akin to mutual information) relative to a uniform prior is normalized as:
 $eI_{attn}(i) = 1 - H_{attn}(i) / \log_2 |W|$
 Tokens with higher $eI_{attn}$ are considered more semantically relevant.

**C. VLA-InfoEntropy (Dynamic Selection Strategy)**

The core of VLA-InfoEntropy is a dynamic selection strategy that adaptively identifies important tokens by combining visual entropy, attention entropy, and temporal information. This strategy is inspired by human visual perception, which initially captures global context and then focuses on local, task-relevant details.

The method dynamically adjusts the number of tokens selected based on visual entropy ($k_{vis}(t)$) and attention entropy ($k_{attn}(t)$) over the course of $T$ action execution timesteps, denoted by $t$:
$k_{vis}(t) = \lfloor k_2 - (k_2 - k_1) \cdot \alpha \rfloor$
$k_{attn}(t) = \lfloor k_1 + (k_2 - k_1) \cdot \alpha \rfloor$
Here, $k_1$ and $k_2$ are hyperparameters defining the base number of visual and task-related tokens, respectively, and $\alpha = t/T$ is a temporal parameter ranging from 0 to 1. This formulation means that at early timesteps ($t$ close to 0, $\alpha$ close to 0), more tokens are selected based on visual entropy ($k_2$) to capture global visual context. As the task progresses ($t$ approaches $T$, $\alpha$ approaches 1), the number of attention-entropy-selected tokens increases ($k_1$) while visual-entropy-selected tokens decrease, shifting the focus towards task-specific semantic cues.

The set of important tokens at timestep $t$ ($P_{important}(t)$) is then formed by taking the union of the top $k_{vis}(t)$ tokens with the highest normalized visual entropy and the top $k_{attn}(t)$ tokens with the highest normalized attention information:
$P_{important}(t) = \text{Top}_{k_{vis}(t)}(\tilde{H}_{img}(i)) \cup \text{Top}_{k_{attn}(t)}(\tilde{I}_{attn}(j))$
These identified important tokens are then excluded from the set of static tokens for KV-cache reuse, ensuring that essential visual and semantic information is recomputed at each step, while low-information tokens are efficiently reused.

The overall pipeline involves:
1. Patching the input image.
2. Computing visual entropy for each patch.
3. Extracting cross-attention scores and computing attention entropy for each patch.
4. Applying the dynamic selection strategy (VLA-InfoEntropy) using the current timestep $t$ to determine the set of important tokens.
5. Evicting these important tokens from the static token set.
6. Reusing the KV cache for the remaining, less informative tokens.
7. Feeding the updated representations to the VLM and action decoder to predict actions.

This methodology provides a principled way to quantify multimodal information importance and adaptively allocate computational resources, aiming for more efficient multimodal reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of VLA-InfoEntropy was evaluated through extensive experiments on the LIBERO benchmark, a standard for VLA model evaluation.

**A. Datasets and Experimental Settings**
- **Dataset:** LIBERO benchmark, which includes four task suites: LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Long. These suites assess diverse capabilities such as semantic grounding, spatial reasoning, goal understanding, and multi-step decision-making.
- **Experimental Parameters:**
 - The cross-attention matrix for attention entropy calculation was taken from the computation of the last frame, a choice validated to not significantly degrade performance.
 - The dynamic scheduling parameter $T$ was set to 100, facilitating a gradual transition in focus over 100 action steps.
 - Hyperparameters $k_1=40$ and $k_2=60$ were chosen to balance the selection of spatially salient and semantically relevant tokens.
 - Experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, and results were averaged over multiple repetitions.

**B. Main Results (Table I)**
VLA-InfoEntropy was compared against the OpenVLA baseline and several state-of-the-art VLA acceleration methods, including SparseVLM, FastV, VLA-Cache, SP-VLA, and Spec-VLA.

- **Success Rate:** VLA-InfoEntropy achieved an average success rate of 76.4% across all LIBERO tasks, which was the highest among all compared methods. It particularly excelled on LIBERO-Spatial (86.4%) and LIBERO-Object (87.6%) tasks, outperforming the OpenVLA baseline (75.0% average) and VLA-Cache (74.7% average).
- **Inference Efficiency:**
 - **Latency:** VLA-InfoEntropy recorded the lowest inference latency at 31.25 units, representing a 39.8% decrease compared to the OpenVLA baseline (51.91 units) and an improvement over VLA-Cache (34.38 units).
 - **FLOPs:** The method demonstrated the lowest FLOPs at 1.214, indicating a 34.9% reduction compared to the OpenVLA baseline (1.864 FLOPs) and an improvement over VLA-Cache (1.355 FLOPs).
 - **Speedup:** VLA-InfoEntropy achieved the highest speedup factor of 1.53x relative to the baseline, surpassing other methods like VLA-Cache (1.38x), SP-VLA (1.35x), and Spec-VLA (1.32x).

These findings suggest that traditional VLA inference contains considerable informational redundancy, which the entropy-guided selection in VLA-InfoEntropy effectively mitigates. By preserving high-information, task-relevant multimodal cues and suppressing redundant features, the method enables more compact evidence usage and reduces error propagation, leading to both efficiency gains and improved task performance.

**C. Ablation Study (Table II)**
An ablation study investigated the individual contributions of visual entropy, attention entropy, and the dynamic timestep mechanism. With the total number of selected important tokens fixed at 100, four settings were evaluated:
- **Visual-Entropy only:** Achieved an average success rate of 69.8%, with 1.42x speedup. While efficient, it missed task semantics.
- **Attention-Entropy only:** Achieved 73.9% average success rate, with 1.39x speedup. Performed better than visual-only but ignored some important visual information.
- **Visual+Attention-Entropy (static combination):** Achieved 75.8% average success rate, with 1.49x speedup. Improved performance but lacked temporal adaptability.
- **Ours (VLA-InfoEntropy - dynamic combination with timestep):** Achieved the highest average success rate of 76.4% and the best speedup of 1.53x.
This study demonstrated that the full VLA-InfoEntropy method, with its dynamic, timestep-conditioned integration of both visual and attention entropy, yielded superior results, highlighting the importance of jointly considering spatial, semantic, and temporal cues.

**D. Sensitivity Analysis (Figure 4, Table III)**
The sensitivity of VLA-InfoEntropy to key hyperparameters was analyzed:
- **Temporal Parameter (T):** Performance, measured by average success rate, peaked at $T=100$, indicating that an appropriate duration for the dynamic transition is crucial.
- **Ratio of Visual to Attention Tokens ($k_1/k_2$):** The optimal ratio was found to be approximately 40/60. This suggests a balance where spatial tasks might benefit more from visual cues, while goal-oriented tasks favor semantic information, and the selected ratio effectively accommodates this balance.
- **Total Number of Important Tokens ($k_1+k_2$):** Increasing the total number of important tokens beyond 100 led to a substantial increase in latency without a proportional gain in success rate (Table III). This implies a saturation point where adding more tokens becomes redundant, reinforcing the trade-off between efficiency and performance.

**E. Qualitative Visualizations (Fig. 3)**
Qualitative results illustrated that VLA-InfoEntropy effectively distinguishes task-critical regions from redundant background areas. Important tokens (highlighted in purple) tended to cluster around manipulable objects and interaction points, while reusable tokens (green) were distributed over less informative regions, confirming the method's ability to concentrate computation on relevant visual evidence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA-InfoEntropy research presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied intelligence, particularly in addressing the computational bottlenecks of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to simultaneously enhance inference efficiency and maintain or improve task performance through a training-free, dynamic information-theoretic approach.

**Key aspects of its significance include:**

1. **Enhanced Inference Efficiency:** The method achieves substantial reductions in inference latency (39.8% decrease) and FLOPs (34.9% reduction), alongside a notable 1.53x speedup. These improvements are critical for deploying VLA models in real-time applications such as autonomous robotics, where responsiveness and low latency are paramount. By making VLA inference more computationally tractable, VLA-InfoEntropy helps bridge the gap between powerful multimodal models and their practical, real-world deployment.

2. **Improved Task Performance:** Contrary to typical efficiency-focused methods that might trade off performance for speed, VLA-InfoEntropy demonstrates an overall improvement in task success rates across diverse benchmarks (76.4% average success rate). This suggests that intelligently pruning redundant information can not only accelerate computation but also enhance the model's focus on critical cues, leading to more robust and accurate decision-making.

3. **Training-Free Nature:** The proposed approach does not require additional training or modification of existing model weights. This is a practical advantage, as it avoids the substantial computational resources, time, and expertise typically associated with fine-tuning large multimodal models. Its plug-and-play compatibility with existing VLA architectures makes it readily adoptable for practitioners.

4. **Unified Information-Theoretic Framework:** By integrating visual entropy, attention entropy, and timestep information, VLA-InfoEntropy offers a principled and comprehensive method for quantifying multimodal information importance. This unified approach addresses the limitations of prior methods that treated visual, semantic, or temporal aspects in isolation, providing a more holistic understanding of information flow in VLA tasks. The dynamic adaptation based on timestep mirrors human cognitive processes, transitioning from global context to task-specific focus.

5. **Redundancy Mitigation:** The findings explicitly expose the substantial informational redundancy within standard VLA pipelines. VLA-InfoEntropy's success underscores the importance of intelligent redundancy mitigation for both computational efficiency and decision-making accuracy. By effectively suppressing redundant or misleading features, the method contributes to more compact evidence utilization and reduced error propagation.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Real-time Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** The acceleration capabilities of VLA-InfoEntropy can enable VLA models to be integrated into robots and autonomous agents operating in dynamic environments, where quick decision-making and low latency are crucial for safety and effectiveness.
- **Broader Accessibility of VLA Models:** By reducing computational requirements, the method could make advanced VLA capabilities more accessible, potentially allowing their deployment on devices with limited computational resources.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a foundation for future research into adaptive and efficient multimodal reasoning. The entropy-based metrics and dynamic selection strategy could inspire new approaches for information gating, token management, and resource allocation in other large-scale AI models.
- **Understanding Multimodal Information Processing:** The framework offers insights into how different modalities (vision, language) contribute to task-relevant information over time, advancing the understanding of multimodal information processing in AI systems.

In summary, VLA-InfoEntropy represents a significant step towards practical and efficient embodied intelligence, offering a robust, training-free solution to a fundamental challenge in the deployment of advanced VLA models.
