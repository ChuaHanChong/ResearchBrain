# ViC-Bench: Benchmarking Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought Capability in MLLMs with Free-Style Intermediate State Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> ViC-Bench introduces a new benchmark for evaluating Multi-modal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) capabilities, featuring free-style intermediate visual state representations across four diverse tasks. The comprehensive evaluation strategy uncovers MLLMs' strengths and weaknesses in dynamically utilizing visual feedback for complex reasoning, revealing substantial performance gaps and areas for improvement.

## Problem

- Existing Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) benchmarks provide models with fixed Intermediate Visual States (IVS), potentially misrepresenting their true reasoning abilities.
- There is a lack of systematic exploration into how various IVS representations (positive, negative, or neutral) influence Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) reasoning performance.
- Current evaluation methods often fail to capture MLLMs' intrinsic capacity for dynamic visual thinking, which is crucial for human-like complex problem-solving.

## Method

- ==ViC-Bench== is introduced, featuring four diverse tasks (maze navigation, jigsaw puzzle, embodied planning, complex counting) that inherently demand dynamic interaction with visual contexts.
- Dedicated data construction pipelines enable ==free-style Intermediate Visual State (IVS) generation==, allowing models to interact with adaptive visual feedback via function calls.
- A progressive ==three-stage evaluation strategy== with novel metrics, including ==ThinkGain==, systematically assesses Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) performance and the gains from free-style IVS.

## Results

- Commercial proprietary MLLMs consistently outperform open-source models, particularly in complex reasoning and leveraging free-style Intermediate Visual States (IVS).
- A significant performance drop is observed from multiple-choice to open-ended question answering, especially in embodied planning (e.g., GPT-4o's ACC dropped from **57.2%** to **0.04%**), indicating a struggle with autonomous, unguided reasoning.
- Free-style IVS yields substantial 'ThinkGain' in tasks requiring sequential verification (e.g., maze navigation, embodied planning), yet surprisingly leads to negative ThinkGain for jigsaw puzzles, suggesting potential distraction or misinterpretation of visual feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Free-style Intermediate Visual States (IVS) act as crucial visual anchors, significantly enhancing reasoning in sequential tasks like maze navigation and embodied planning, but can sometimes distract or lead to irrational decisions in tasks requiring fine-grained semantic understanding.
> - There is a notable performance disparity between commercial proprietary MLLMs and open-source models, especially in their ability to leverage complex reasoning and free-style IVS.
> - Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) face considerable challenges in open-ended reasoning without explicit options, often exhibiting 'embodied hallucinations' and a lack of grounded physical world understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "ViC-Bench" is strategically positioned at the forefront of Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) research, specifically addressing critical limitations in evaluating their reasoning capabilities. The field of artificial intelligence has seen a rapid evolution from Large Language Models (LLMs) to MLLMs, which integrate various modalities like vision with language. A key advancement in LLMs has been the "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) prompting, enabling models to break down complex problems into intermediate steps, significantly enhancing their reasoning.

Inspired by CoT's success, its application to visual-language contexts led to "Visual CoT." Early Visual CoT approaches primarily used vision as input, with rationales generated purely in language, often overlooking explicit visual feedback updates. This paradigm was deemed misaligned with human cognitive processes, which continuously update understanding based on visual information. This gap led to the emergence of "Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought" (VI-CoT), where models interact with and update their reasoning based on step-wise intermediate visual states (IVS).

Current VI-CoT methods broadly fall into two categories: those that autonomously generate IVS (often limited by current MLLM generative capabilities) and those that rely on external tools or human intervention to provide IVS. While several benchmarks have emerged to evaluate VI-CoT, this paper identifies two significant shortcomings:
1. **Fixed IVS Representations:** Existing benchmarks often provide models with relatively fixed IVS, which can forcibly distort the models' original thinking trajectories. This fails to evaluate their intrinsic reasoning ability, as the visual "thoughts" are pre-determined rather than dynamically generated or chosen by the model.
2. **Lack of Systematic Impact Factor Exploration:** Previous benchmarks have largely neglected to systematically explore how different IVS representations (positive, negative, or neutral) actually impact MLLMs' reasoning performance.

ViC-Bench directly addresses these gaps by introducing a benchmark with **free-style IVS representations** and a comprehensive evaluation suite designed to **systematically explore the impact factors of IVS**. By doing so, this work aims to provide a more authentic and thorough assessment of whether MLLMs can truly leverage human-like VI-CoT for superior reasoning. This pushes the research landscape beyond mere performance measurement to deeper insights into MLLM cognitive processes and limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind introducing ViC-Bench stems from the recognition that current evaluation paradigms for Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are insufficient and potentially misleading. The authors identify two critical limitations in existing benchmarks:
1. **Constrained Intermediate Visual States (IVS):** Many existing benchmarks provide models with pre-defined or fixed IVS, rather than allowing for dynamic, free-style generation. This artificial constraint can force models into specific reasoning paths, thereby failing to accurately assess their intrinsic, untamed reasoning capabilities and potentially distorting their natural thinking trajectories. The authors argue that a true evaluation of VI-CoT requires models to interact with IVS that are adaptive and chosen by the model itself, mirroring human-like visual thinking.
2. **Neglected Impact Factor Analysis of IVS:** There is a lack of systematic investigation into how the provision of IVS (whether positive, negative, or neutral) actually influences an MLLM's reasoning performance. Understanding this impact is crucial for developing MLLMs that can effectively leverage visual feedback.

To overcome these limitations, the key objectives of this research are:
- **To introduce ViC-Bench:** A specialized benchmark featuring four representative tasks (maze navigation, jigsaw puzzle, embodied long-horizon planning, and complex counting) that inherently require dynamic interaction with visual contexts and continuous understanding updates based on step-wise IVS.
- **To enable Free-Style IVS Generation:** Develop dedicated data construction pipelines for each task, incorporating free-style IVS generation workflows that support adaptive function calls, allowing models to generate and interact with IVS more naturally.
- **To propose a Thorough Evaluation Suite:** Design a novel, progressive three-stage evaluation strategy with targeted new metrics (ACC, Recall_o, Legality, ThinkGain) to meticulously examine VI-CoT performance, especially the in-depth thinking gains brought by free-style IVS.
- **To explore Prompting Factors:** Introduce the Incremental Prompting Information Injection (IPII) strategy to ablatively analyze how different levels of global-aware prompting affect VI-CoT capabilities.
- **To Provide Comprehensive Insights:** Conduct extensive evaluations on a diverse set of 18 advanced MLLMs (proprietary and open-source) to reveal key observations and insights into their current VI-CoT capabilities, performance gaps, and areas for future improvement.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond superficial evaluations to truly understand if MLLMs can inherently achieve better reasoning performance by leveraging VI-CoT in a way that closely aligns with human cognitive behavior, especially when confronted with dynamic, free-form visual information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the construction of ViC-Bench and the design of a comprehensive evaluation suite to assess Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) capabilities in MLLMs.

**Benchmark Construction (ViC-Bench):**
The benchmark comprises four diverse, representative tasks, each requiring dynamic interaction with visual contexts and step-wise updates based on IVS:
1. **Maze Navigation:** Models must find the shortest path in a rendered maze. Data generation uses the Maze library with DFS, filtering for unique paths and specific lengths.
2. **Jigsaw Puzzle:** Models are tasked with reassembling a masked image by placing selected patches into vacant regions. Images are sourced from multiple advanced Text-to-Image models (DALLE-3, FLUX.1, etc.) to ensure diversity and prevent data leakage, then filtered based on consistency, reasonableness, and realism.
3. **Embodied Long-Horizon Planning:** Models plan and execute actions in a simulated environment (AI2-THOR) to achieve complex goals, requiring sequential, context-dependent actions. Trajectory lengths are filtered to ensure appropriate difficulty, and search-oriented tasks are excluded.
4. **Complex Counting:** Models count specific objects (e.g., human heads) within irregularly segmented regions of an image. Data is derived from existing crowd-counting datasets (JHU-CROWD++, CoMT) and manually annotated with irregular lines to define regions.

For each task, a dedicated construction workflow involves:
- **Raw Data & Pre-processing:** Task-specific data generation, filtering, and preparation.
- **Three-Stage Construction:** Data is prepared for each of the three evaluation stages.
- **IVS Generation:** Crucially, this involves a **free-style IVS generation pipeline supporting adaptive function calls**. For Maze Navigation and Jigsaw Puzzle, simulated functions mark agent positions or apply patches. For Embodied Planning, a rendering engine executes actions dynamically. For Complex Counting, coarse-grained region-aware masks are generated based on model predictions to reflect counting behavior without overly fine-grained detail.
- **Human Recheck/Collaboration:** Human experts are engaged throughout construction and evaluation to ensure data feasibility, quality, plausibility of options, and for qualitative assessment in real-world embodied planning scenarios (human-in-the-loop executor).

**Evaluation Suite:**
The evaluation employs a progressive three-stage strategy with targeted new metrics:
1. **Stage 1: Multiple-Choice QA:** Models receive visual input and select the correct final answer from given options. This stage establishes a baseline for initial visual CoT performance. The metric is **Accuracy (ACC)**.
2. **Stage 2: Open-Ended QA:** Models receive visual input and must generate the final answer in an open-ended format without options. This stage challenges models' autonomous reasoning. Metrics include **ACC**, **Recall_o** (measures informative steps towards the ground truth, indicating reasoning rigor), and **Legality** (measures compliance with task-specific rules, penalizing invalid actions).
3. **Stage 3: Open-Ended QA with Free-Form IVS:** This is the core VI-CoT evaluation, where models operate in an agent-form and receive free-style IVS feedback after each step/action via function calls. This stage investigates the "untamed" VI-CoT capabilities. Metrics include **ACC**, **Recall_o**, **Legality**, and a newly introduced metric, **ThinkGain**.
 - **ThinkGain:** Defined as `Recall_o - Recall_d`, where `Recall_d` is the Recall metric calculated based on the decision states *before* retrospection (i.e., before IVS feedback or self-correction). ThinkGain quantifies the performance improvement directly attributable to the model's ability to leverage IVS and subsequent self-correction.

**Incremental Prompting Information Injection (IPII) Strategy:**
To explore the influence of prompting on VI-CoT, the authors designed a three-level IPII strategy, applied ablatively:
- **Level 1 (P_L1):** Baseline, uses only original basic instructions.
- **Level 2 (P_L2):** Incorporates implicit VI-CoT prompts (e.g., "update your internal intermediate visual state"), guiding models to mentally simulate IVS.
- **Level 3 (P_L3):** Further augments prompts with external knowledge (e.g., explicit coordinates for maze navigation) to enhance visual perception and reasoning.

**Experimental Setup:**
18 advanced MLLMs (10 commercial proprietary, 8 open-source) are evaluated. Standard hyperparameters are used, consistent with VLMEvalKit.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluations on 18 advanced MLLMs across ViC-Bench's four tasks and three evaluation stages yielded several key findings:

1. **Performance Gap between Proprietary and Open-Source MLLMs:** Generally, commercial proprietary MLLMs (e.g., OpenAI's GPT-4o, Google's Gemini series) consistently exhibit superior performance compared to open-source models across most tasks and stages. This gap is particularly evident in complex reasoning scenarios and when leveraging free-style IVS. Most MLLMs, regardless of origin, still show substantial disparities compared to human-level proficiency.

2. **Challenges of Open-Ended Reasoning (Stage 1 vs. Stage 2):**
 - For Maze Navigation, Jigsaw Puzzle, and Embodied Long-Horizon Planning, models show a significant performance drop from Stage 1 (multiple-choice QA) to Stage 2 (open-ended QA). This indicates that the presence of options provides critical hints, and their removal exposes inherent limitations in MLLMs' ability to perform autonomous, unguided spatial or sequential reasoning. Models often struggle with precise, formatted outputs and direct problem-solving without explicit choices.
 - In Embodied Long-Horizon Planning, this drop is particularly severe (e.g., GPT-4o from 57.2% to 0.04% ACC), highlighting MLLMs' "embodied hallucinations" and lack of grounded physical world understanding when deprived of structured options.

3. **Impact of Free-Style IVS (Stage 3 and ThinkGain):**
 - **Maze Navigation:** All models consistently achieved gains in ACC and Recall_o in Stage 3, leading to impressive ThinkGain scores. This strongly suggests that free-style IVS acts as a critical visual anchor, enabling MLLMs to verify intermediate states and significantly enhance spatial-aware reasoning. However, weaker models sometimes showed a decline in Legality, indicating that external visual feedback can confuse their thinking trajectories if their internal reasoning capabilities are insufficient.
 - **Jigsaw Puzzle:** While most models showed global-level gains in ACC and Recall_o in Stage 3 compared to Stage 2, many surprisingly exhibited **negative ThinkGain** and drops in Legality. This counter-intuitive result implies that for tasks requiring fine-grained semantic understanding and spatial coherence (where AIGC semantics can be challenging), IVS might distract or lead to irrational decisions rather than guide. It reveals an "immature VI-CoT capability" where visual feedback can be counterproductive.
 - **Embodied Long-Horizon Planning:** The performance collapse observed in Stage 2 was generally reversed in Stage 3, with substantial ThinkGain scores. This demonstrates that free-style IVS is vital for transforming "blind hallucinations" into verifiable actions, reactivating embodied reasoning potential. Proprietary models showed robust reasoning-for-planning and self-correction, while open-source models struggled with long-horizon dependencies, exhibiting invalid planning, task deviation, and recursive behaviors.
 - **Complex Counting:** Some models (e.g., Doubao-1.5, Hunyuan-Vision, Qwen-Omni-Turbo) showed ACC declines and negative ThinkGain in Stage 3, including Gemini-2.5-pro with null ThinkGain. This points to persistent issues with object hallucination and basic perception deficiencies in MLLMs, where low-quality or misinterpreted IVS can accumulate errors rather than provide valid references.

4. **Correlation with Response Length:** For Maze Navigation and Complex Counting, models with relatively better performance tend to produce shorter, more concise responses. Conversely, excessive verbosity often correlates with uncertainty or error accumulation, especially in weaker models (e.g., SkyWork-R1V1 in Jigsaw Puzzle with lengthy chains but performance wipe-out). This suggests that efficient reasoning involves focused and succinct communication.

5. **Insights from Incremental Prompting Information Injection (IPII):**
 - **Stage 1:** Proprietary models exhibited superior stability, while some open-source models showed performance decline with increasing difficulty. SkyWork-R1V1's sharp accuracy surge at Level 3 indicates higher sensitivity to detailed prompts.
 - **Stage 2:** A widespread failure to achieve significant performance gains across models at higher IPII levels suggests that current MLLMs largely lack the capability to implicitly update their internal IVS through textual CoT in open-ended QA formats. This underscores the necessity of *explicit* free-style IVS as external knowledge.
 - **Stage 3:** The introduction of external free-style IVS led to clear performance divergence. Advanced MLLMs (e.g., Gemini-2.5-pro) successfully leveraged visual contexts to boost planning accuracy. Weaker MLLMs, however, often struggled with information overload, leading to confusion and decreased performance.

Overall, the results highlight the promise of free-style IVS in enhancing MLLM reasoning, especially for tasks requiring sequential decision-making and verification (like maze navigation and embodied planning). However, they also reveal significant limitations in MLLMs' ability to effectively integrate and interpret IVS in more nuanced tasks (like jigsaw puzzles and complex counting), where visual feedback can sometimes be detrimental.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ViC-Bench research makes significant contributions to the field of multi-modal artificial intelligence, specifically in the critical area of evaluating and advancing reasoning capabilities in MLLMs. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Refined Evaluation of VI-CoT:** By introducing free-style Intermediate Visual States (IVS) and a progressive three-stage evaluation strategy, ViC-Bench moves beyond the limitations of previous benchmarks that offered fixed or constrained IVS. This provides a more authentic and comprehensive assessment of MLLMs' intrinsic Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) capabilities, aligning more closely with human cognitive processes involving dynamic visual feedback.

2. **Systematic Analysis of IVS Impact:** The proposed metrics, particularly **ThinkGain**, enable a systematic and quantitative exploration of how IVS influences reasoning. This allows researchers to pinpoint whether visual feedback is positive, negative, or neutral, offering crucial insights into the strengths and weaknesses of different MLLM architectures in leveraging visual information for complex problem-solving. This is vital for debugging and improving MLLM design.

3. **Benchmark for Future MLLM Development:** ViC-Bench serves as a challenging and diverse benchmark for the next generation of MLLMs. The four selected tasks (maze navigation, jigsaw puzzle, embodied long-horizon planning, complex counting) cover a range of visual-spatial, logical, and embodied reasoning challenges, encouraging the development of more robust and versatile models. Its public availability (at Huggingface) ensures widespread adoption and accelerates research.

4. **Insights for Training Paradigms:** The findings regarding negative ThinkGain in certain tasks (like jigsaw puzzles) and the performance divergence with IPII strategies provide critical guidance for future MLLM training. They suggest that simply providing IVS is not enough; models need to be specifically trained to *effectively and discerningly* utilize visual feedback, avoiding distraction, overthinking, or hallucination. This could lead to new architectures or training methodologies that better align visual generation with reasoning goals.

5. **Foundations for Downstream AI Systems:** The improvements and insights gained from ViC-Bench are crucial for the progress of several high-impact AI domains:
 - **Unified MLLMs:** Understanding how models integrate different modalities for reasoning is fundamental for building truly unified multimodal models.
 - **Multi-modal Agents:** The embodied long-horizon planning task directly addresses capabilities required for intelligent agents that interact with dynamic environments.
 - **Embodied AI:** Better VI-CoT means more capable embodied agents that can perceive, reason, and act in physical or simulated worlds.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Tasks requiring spatial reasoning, planning, and continuous environment updates are directly relevant to autonomous systems.

In summary, ViC-Bench is not just another dataset; it's a meticulously designed diagnostic tool that delves into the "how" and "why" of MLLM reasoning with visual interleaving. By uncovering significant performance gaps, highlighting the dual nature of IVS (beneficial vs. distracting), and providing a framework for systematic exploration, it will undoubtedly inspire novel research directions and foster the development of more sophisticated, human-like multimodal AI systems.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that the current ThinkGain metric involves black-boxing the retrospection process of MLLMs. Future work will aim to delve deeper into this aspect for more detailed investigations, which could further refine the understanding of how MLLMs self-correct and learn from IVS.
