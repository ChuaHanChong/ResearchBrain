# ViC-Bench: Benchmarking Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought Capability in MLLMs with Free-Style Intermediate State Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> ViC-Bench introduces a new benchmark for evaluating Multi-modal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) capabilities, featuring free-style intermediate visual state representations across four diverse tasks. The comprehensive evaluation strategy uncovers MLLMs' strengths and weaknesses in dynamically utilizing visual feedback for complex reasoning, revealing substantial performance gaps and areas for improvement.

## Problem

- Existing Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) benchmarks provide models with fixed Intermediate Visual States (IVS), potentially misrepresenting their true reasoning abilities.
- There is a lack of systematic exploration into how various IVS representations (positive, negative, or neutral) influence Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) reasoning performance.
- Current evaluation methods often fail to capture MLLMs' intrinsic capacity for dynamic visual thinking, which is crucial for human-like complex problem-solving.

## Method

- ==ViC-Bench== is introduced, featuring four diverse tasks (maze navigation, jigsaw puzzle, embodied planning, complex counting) that inherently demand dynamic interaction with visual contexts.
- Dedicated data construction pipelines enable ==free-style Intermediate Visual State (IVS) generation==, allowing models to interact with adaptive visual feedback via function calls.
- A progressive ==three-stage evaluation strategy== with novel metrics, including ==ThinkGain==, systematically assesses Visual-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VI-CoT) performance and the gains from free-style IVS.

## Results

- Commercial proprietary MLLMs consistently outperform open-source models, particularly in complex reasoning and leveraging free-style Intermediate Visual States (IVS).
- A significant performance drop is observed from multiple-choice to open-ended question answering, especially in embodied planning (e.g., GPT-4o's ACC dropped from **57.2%** to **0.04%**), indicating a struggle with autonomous, unguided reasoning.
- Free-style IVS yields substantial 'ThinkGain' in tasks requiring sequential verification (e.g., maze navigation, embodied planning), yet surprisingly leads to negative ThinkGain for jigsaw puzzles, suggesting potential distraction or misinterpretation of visual feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Free-style Intermediate Visual States (IVS) act as crucial visual anchors, significantly enhancing reasoning in sequential tasks like maze navigation and embodied planning, but can sometimes distract or lead to irrational decisions in tasks requiring fine-grained semantic understanding.
> - There is a notable performance disparity between commercial proprietary MLLMs and open-source models, especially in their ability to leverage complex reasoning and free-style IVS.
> - Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) face considerable challenges in open-ended reasoning without explicit options, often exhibiting 'embodied hallucinations' and a lack of grounded physical world understanding.
