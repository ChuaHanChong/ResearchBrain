# Diffusion Forcing for Multi-Agent Interaction Sequence Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> MAGNet, a unified autoregressive diffusion framework, enables realistic multi-human interaction motion generation across diverse tasks and variable group sizes. Developed by Sony Group Corporation, UC Berkeley, and Meta, it scales to polyadic groups of up to four agents and generates ultra-long, coherent motion sequences, demonstrating up to 89% improvement in Fréchet Distance for long-horizon tasks over previous methods.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent motion generation methods were largely fragmented and task-specific, requiring separate models for different interaction tasks (e.g., prediction vs. inpainting).
- Many prior dyadic (two-person) methods failed to scale naturally to polyadic (three or more) interacting groups due to architectural constraints like fixed cross-attention mechanisms.
- Models struggled to maintain temporal coherence and consistency over long temporal horizons (hundreds of frames) in multi-agent interactions, leading to motion degradation and interaction breakdown.

## Method

- A novel relational motion representation uses per-frame local canonical coordinates and pairwise inter-agent transforms, making it agnostic to the number of participants and enabling natural scalability.
- ==The Multi-Agent Generative Network (MAGNet)== employs a Diffusion Forcing Transformer (DFoT) with a conditional VQ-VAE for discrete motion tokenization, modeling the joint distribution of multi-agent interactions.
- Flexible sampling strategies, enabled by independent per-token noise levels, allow a single trained MAGNet model to perform diverse tasks such as partner inpainting, various prediction modes, and agentic generation without re-training.

## Results

- MAGNet outperformed specialized baselines across various dyadic tasks, achieving an FD of **0.067** vs. **0.181** and MPJPE of **0.218** vs. **0.580** for partner prediction on DuoBox compared to Ready-to-React.
- The model scaled gracefully to polyadic interactions (up to 4 agents) using a single trained model, achieving the best Fréchet Distance (e.g., **0.590** for N=4 vs. **2.143** for NN baseline) on the Embody3D dataset.
- MAGNet significantly improved ultra-long motion generation, demonstrating an **89%** improvement in Fréchet Distance (**2.781** vs. **24.810** for Ready-to-React) over 1800 frames on DuoBox while maintaining real-time inference speed of up to 56 FPS.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding pairwise relative transforms within a world-agnostic, local canonical frame is crucial for achieving natural scalability to arbitrary group sizes in multi-agent motion generation.
> - Utilizing diffusion forcing with independent per-token noise levels in an autoregressive transformer enables a single model to implicitly learn conditional distributions for diverse interaction tasks.
> - Discrete motion tokenization via a VQ-VAE is essential for regularizing motion quality, significantly reducing artifacts like foot skating and improving overall motion fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The modeling and generation of multi-person interactions represents a significant challenge in computer vision and graphics, with applications spanning robotics, virtual reality, and social computing. Existing research in motion generation has largely been fragmented and task-specific.

Historically, single-agent motion generation has evolved from Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) to diffusion models and transformers. While these methods have improved temporal coherence and motion quality, they are restricted to individual agents.

Dyadic (two-person) motion generation methods have attempted to model inter-agent dependencies, often employing cross-attention mechanisms or diffusion models. Examples include text-conditioned generation of synchronized motions (e.g., InterGen), future prediction (e.g., ExPI), and partner inpainting (e.g., ReMoS, ReGenNet). Some approaches like ARFlow offer multimodal diffusion for both inpainting and prediction, typically for short clips. Others, such as Human-X, focus on low-latency reactive generation. Ready-to-React introduced a unified approach for partner prediction using vector quantization, diffusion, and autoregressive generation in an agentic manner.

However, a fundamental limitation across many of these dyadic methods is their inability to scale beyond two agents. Cross-attention mechanisms, commonly used to encode inter-agent relationships, are often designed for fixed agent pairs, making extension to polyadic (three or more agents) groups non-trivial. Most architectures assume a two-agent scenario, leading to a lack of generality. This fragmentation necessitates separate models for different tasks (e.g., reacting to motion vs. joint prediction) and restricts inference modes, leaving a gap for a single, unified model capable of handling a broad spectrum of multi-agent generation tasks, variable group sizes, and long temporal horizons.

The present work addresses this gap by introducing a unified framework designed to support a wider range of interaction tasks and scale naturally to polyadic scenarios, thereby advancing the field beyond specialized dyadic models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework capable of understanding and generating realistic motion for multiple interacting humans, addressing the limitations of existing task-specific approaches. The motivation stems from several core challenges and requirements in multi-agent interaction modeling:

- **Long Temporal Horizons:** Human interactions often unfold over extended periods, requiring models to maintain temporal coherence and consistency for hundreds of motion steps. Current methods frequently struggle with temporal drift over long sequences.
- **Strong Inter-Agent Dependencies:** Interacting agents exhibit complex, coordinated behaviors that necessitate explicit modeling of their mutual influences and spatial relationships. Capturing both tightly synchronized activities (e.g., dancing) and loosely structured social interactions is crucial.
- **Variable Group Sizes:** Social situations are not limited to dyadic pairs; they frequently involve polyadic groups of three or more individuals. Existing methods are often architecturally constrained to two agents, failing to generalize to larger groups without modifications or re-training.
- **Diversity of Generation Tasks:** Applications in robotics, virtual reality, and content creation demand flexibility in how motion is generated. This includes tasks such as predicting an agent's future motion based on others' past actions (partner prediction), completing an agent's motion given others' complete trajectories (partner inpainting), jointly predicting multiple agents' futures, and enabling turn-taking or distributed generation (agentic sampling). Most prior work offers only a subset of these capabilities, requiring multiple specialized models.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a single model, MAGNet (Multi-Agent Generative Network), that unifies these diverse requirements. The motivation is to provide a comprehensive solution that supports flexible conditioning and sampling, scales to arbitrary group sizes, and maintains coherent coordination across agents, thus offering a foundation for more versatile multi-agent generation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Multi-Agent Generative Network (MAGNet) is a unified autoregressive diffusion framework designed for flexible motion generation among multiple interacting agents. Its methodology is built upon a novel relational motion representation and a transformer-based diffusion model trained with diffusion forcing.

#### 3.1. Motion Representation

MAGNet models a sequence of `T` timesteps involving `P` interacting people. Each person's motion is parameterized by body shape ($\beta$) and joint rotations ($\Theta_t$). To achieve spatial relationship modeling invariant to absolute world positioning, two coordinate frames are defined per agent: a root frame at the pelvis and a per-timestep canonical frame.

Rigid transforms are represented in 9D (6 for rotation, 3 for translation). Key transforms include:
- `T_can→root_t`: Transform from canonical to root frame at timestep `t`.
- `ΔT_can_t`: One-timestep intra-agent temporal transition in the canonical frame.
- `T_self→partner_t`: Pairwise inter-agent transform from self to each partner in the canonical frame at time `t`. For `P` agents, each agent has `P-1` such transforms. This design allows the model to handle arbitrary group sizes without architectural modification.

A latent-space approach is adopted using a conditional VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder).
- **VQ-VAE Encoding:** A VQ-VAE compresses the single-agent pose information (joint rotations $\Theta_t$ and root height/tilt from `T_can→root_t`) into a latent representation. It is conditioned on body shape ($\beta$) and `ΔT_can_t`. The temporal stride is $\omega=4$. The VQ-VAE focuses on learning a high-fidelity dictionary of spatial body poses and per-frame root placements.
- **VQ-VAE Loss:** The VQ-VAE is trained using a loss function combining geodesic distance for rotations on SO(3) and a smooth L1 (Huber) loss for translations, supervising the reconstruction of joint rotations and root transformations.
After training, the VQ-VAE parameters are frozen, and its encoded motion forms the tokens for the generative model.

#### 3.2. Multi-Agent Generative Network (MAGNet)

The core of MAGNet is a Diffusion Forcing Transformer (DFoT) that models the joint distribution of multi-agent interactions in the latent space.

- **Motion Tokens:** For the `p`-th agent at token timestep `i` (after VQ-VAE compression, `T'` = `T/ω`), a motion token `m_p_i` is constructed. This token concatenates the VQ-VAE latent encoding (`h_z_p_i`), the intra-agent temporal transition (`ΔT_can_p_i`), and all `P-1` pairwise inter-agent transforms (`T_self→partner_k_i`). This formulation explicitly includes inter-agent relationships within each token.
- **Forward Process:** To enable flexible conditioning, each clean latent token `m` is perturbed with an independent, discrete noise level `k` using a cosine schedule. Gaussian noise is applied to generate noisy tokens `m_p_i(k_p_i)`, where `k_p_i` is sampled independently for each token `(i, p)`.
- **Transformer Denoiser:** A transformer denoiser (`f_ϕ`) processes the sequence of noised motion tokens `M^(k)`. For each token `(i, p)`, the input embedding `e_p_i` is constructed by combining the noised token `m_p_i(k_p_i)`, a sinusoidal embedding of the noise level `SinEmb(k_p_i)`, Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) for temporal positioning, and a learned agent-identity embedding `ψ(p)`. The transformer predicts the clean motion token sequence `M_0`.
- **Training Objective:** MAGNet is optimized using an `M_0`-prediction objective with a smooth L1 (Huber) loss across all tokens. The total loss `L_Total` is a weighted sum of:
 - `L_pose`: Latent pose reconstruction.
 - `L_cont`: Temporal continuity (for `ΔT_can`).
 - `L_inter`: Inter-agent spatial relationships (for `T_self→partner`).
 - `L_c`: A consistency loss that penalizes deviations between the predicted pairwise transform and its kinematically propagated counterpart, enforcing interpersonal velocity consistency.
- **Inference:** During inference, DFoT iteratively denoises the sequence from `k = K` to `0` to produce the clean token sequence `M_0`. The VQ-VAE decoder then reconstructs the physical motion sequence, using body shape `β` and the predicted intra-agent temporal transforms `ΔT_can`.

#### 3.3. Sampling Strategies

The independent per-token noise levels and flexible conditioning allow a single trained MAGNet model to perform various generation tasks without re-training or architectural changes. Observed tokens (context) are treated as "clean" (not denoised), while target tokens are "noisy" (denoised).

- **Partner Inpainting:** Given one agent's complete trajectory, the model generates the motion for another agent.
- **Partner Prediction:** Predicts an agent's future motion conditioned on both agents' past motions.
- **Joint Future Prediction:** Generates the future motion for all agents simultaneously from a shared past context, ensuring coordinated predictions. This naturally extends to `n ≥ 2` agents.
- **Agentic Motion Sampling:** Enables distributed generation. In synchronous mode, all agents generate motion in parallel at each timestep. In asynchronous (turn-taking) mode, agents generate sequentially, with one agent's newly generated motion serving as immediate context for another.
- **Ultra-long Motion Generation:** An autoregressive windowed strategy is used, where overlapping segments are predicted iteratively. The model conditions on the final frames of the previously generated segment to ensure temporal continuity, enabling arbitrarily long sequence generation.
- **In-Betweening:** Generates continuous motion between a set of predefined keyframes for multiple agents.
- **Motion Control:** One agent's motion is predicted based on its own past and the partner's current motion, enabling responsive control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MAGNet was evaluated across various multi-agent motion datasets including DuoBox (contact sports), ReMoCap and DD100 (dance), and Embody 3D and Inter-X (social interactions). Evaluation metrics included Fréchet Distance (FD), Diversity (DIV), Foot Skating (FS), Inter-penetration (IP), Motion Interaction (MI), Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE), and Mean Per Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE).

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Partner Inpainting:**
 - On ReMoCap, MAGNet achieved competitive performance with the specialized ReMoS model. For instance, FS was 0.513 (MAGNet) vs. 0.469 (ReMoS), IP 0.176 vs. 0.162, and MPJPE 0.074 vs. 0.026. While ReMoS showed a lower FD, it also exhibited lower diversity (0.000 vs. 0.028 for MAGNet), suggesting MAGNet offers a more balanced trade-off.
 - On DD100, MAGNet achieved an FD of 0.048, significantly outperforming Duolando (18.180, without music conditioning) and showing competitive performance across other metrics. MAGNet also achieved a lower MI on both datasets.
- **Partner Prediction:**
 - On the DuoBox dataset, MAGNet outperformed Ready-to-React (R2R) across most metrics, achieving an FD of 0.067 (vs. 0.181 for R2R), MI of 0.020 (vs. 0.071), IP of 0.019 (vs. 0.309), MPJPE of 0.218 (vs. 0.580), and MPJVE of 0.016 (vs. 0.029).
- **Dyadic Prediction:**
 - In the more challenging dyadic motion generation task on DuoBox, MAGNet showed superior FD (0.118 vs. 0.337 for R2R) and DIV (5.622 vs. 0.395 for R2R). It also produced lower Interpenetration (0.101 vs. 0.162) and Foot Skating (0.407 vs. 0.249, though R2R was lower in FS) errors compared to R2R.
- **Polyadic Future Prediction:**
 - MAGNet, using a single trained model, extended to 2, 3, and 4 agents on the Embody3D dataset. It achieved the best FD across all group sizes (e.g., N=4: 0.590 vs. 2.143 for NN) and maintained low MPJPE and FS. While Nearest Neighbor (NN) and Random Sample (RS) baselines showed sharp degradation in FD with increasing agents due to data sparsity, MAGNet's performance degraded gracefully.
- **Ultra-Long Generation:**
 - MAGNet significantly outperformed R2R on DuoBox over 1800 frames, achieving an FD of 2.781 (vs. 24.810 for R2R), MI of 0.021 (vs. 0.062), and IP of 0.116 (vs. 0.345). Qualitative analysis and cumulative FD plots indicated that MAGNet maintained superior stability and coherence, whereas R2R exhibited exponential FD accumulation and breakdown of interaction.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **VQ-VAE:** Removing the VQ-VAE and using raw joints/transforms led to catastrophic foot skating (FS: 5.682) and an 86x degradation in FD, demonstrating the importance of discrete tokenization for motion quality regularization.
 - **`T_self→partner`:** Removing the explicit `T_self→partner` (inter-agent spatial encoding) from motion tokens nearly tripled interpenetration (IP: 0.116 to 0.342) and degraded FD and MPJPE, confirming its criticality for coordinated and physically plausible interactions.
 - **Positional Encodings (PE):** Ablating Agent PE degraded FD by 11x and foot skating by 2.5x, indicating loss of agent distinction. Removing Time PE had a larger impact (FD: 2.032), suggesting a loss of temporal ordering.
 - **Noise Sampling Strategy:** For joint future prediction, "Joint Sampling" (agents at each timestep share noise level) substantially outperformed "Independent Sampling" on FD (0.052 vs. 0.716), diversity, and interaction quality (IP: 0.116 vs. 0.231), highlighting the importance of preserving spatiotemporal correlations.
- **Inference Speed:** MAGNet demonstrated real-time performance, achieving up to 56 FPS for partner and dyadic future prediction, which is faster than competing methods (e.g., 54 FPS vs. 49 FPS for Duolando in inpainting; 56 FPS vs. 16 FPS for R2R in partner prediction).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative results consistently supported the quantitative findings. MAGNet generated plausible boxing exchanges with timely reactive motions (e.g., counter-punches). It sustained coherent two-person boxing over very long horizons (1800 frames) where other models exhibited interaction breakdown. The model demonstrated the ability to generate coordinated polyadic interactions with three and four agents using a single trained model without architectural modifications. Diverse tasks such as boxing, casual interaction, polyadic generation, and in-betweening were handled by the unified model, with results indicating realistic temporal dynamics and inter-agent coordination.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents MAGNet as a significant contribution to the field of multi-agent motion generation. The primary significance lies in its introduction of a unified framework that consolidates tasks previously requiring separate, specialized models. This unification is achieved through two core design elements:
1. **Relational, World-Agnostic Motion Representation:** By embedding pairwise relative transforms in per-frame local canonical coordinates, the model becomes agnostic to the number of participants. This design choice enables natural scaling to polyadic scenarios (three or more agents) without architectural modifications, addressing a key limitation of prior cross-attention-based methods.
2. **Diffusion Forcing over Multi-Agent Tokens:** The use of independent per-token noise levels within an autoregressive diffusion framework allows MAGNet to implicitly learn conditional distributions over arbitrary subsets of agents and timesteps. This flexibility underpins its ability to perform diverse tasks like partner inpainting, various prediction modes (partner, joint future), agentic generation, and ultra-long synthesis within a single model.

The empirical results demonstrate that MAGNet is competitive with, and often outperforms, specialized baselines across multiple benchmarks for dyadic tasks while uniquely extending to polyadic interactions. Its ability to generate ultra-long, coherent motion sequences (e.g., 89% improvement in FD over Ready-to-React for long-horizon generation) and scale gracefully to groups of up to four agents represents an advancement in robustness and generality. The fast inference speed also suggests its potential for practical applications.

The potential impact of MAGNet is broad, providing a more versatile foundation for:
- **Robotics:** Enabling robots to generate natural, coordinated movements in response to or in collaboration with multiple humans.
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Creating more realistic and dynamic virtual agents that can engage in complex social interactions within virtual environments.
- **Social Computing and Animation:** Facilitating the generation of diverse and temporally consistent multi-person interactions for character animation and social simulation.

By offering a unified, scalable, and robust approach to multi-agent motion generation, MAGNet contributes a versatile tool for future research and development in fields requiring sophisticated human-like multi-agent behaviors. Future work could explore incorporating physical constraints to mitigate artifacts like inter-agent penetration and scaling to swarm-scale scenarios with hundreds of interacting agents.
