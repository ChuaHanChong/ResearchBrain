# Diffusion Forcing for Multi-Agent Interaction Sequence Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> MAGNet, a unified autoregressive diffusion framework, enables realistic multi-human interaction motion generation across diverse tasks and variable group sizes. Developed by Sony Group Corporation, UC Berkeley, and Meta, it scales to polyadic groups of up to four agents and generates ultra-long, coherent motion sequences, demonstrating up to 89% improvement in Fréchet Distance for long-horizon tasks over previous methods.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent motion generation methods were largely fragmented and task-specific, requiring separate models for different interaction tasks (e.g., prediction vs. inpainting).
- Many prior dyadic (two-person) methods failed to scale naturally to polyadic (three or more) interacting groups due to architectural constraints like fixed cross-attention mechanisms.
- Models struggled to maintain temporal coherence and consistency over long temporal horizons (hundreds of frames) in multi-agent interactions, leading to motion degradation and interaction breakdown.

## Method

- A novel relational motion representation uses per-frame local canonical coordinates and pairwise inter-agent transforms, making it agnostic to the number of participants and enabling natural scalability.
- ==The Multi-Agent Generative Network (MAGNet)== employs a Diffusion Forcing Transformer (DFoT) with a conditional VQ-VAE for discrete motion tokenization, modeling the joint distribution of multi-agent interactions.
- Flexible sampling strategies, enabled by independent per-token noise levels, allow a single trained MAGNet model to perform diverse tasks such as partner inpainting, various prediction modes, and agentic generation without re-training.

## Results

- MAGNet outperformed specialized baselines across various dyadic tasks, achieving an FD of **0.067** vs. **0.181** and MPJPE of **0.218** vs. **0.580** for partner prediction on DuoBox compared to Ready-to-React.
- The model scaled gracefully to polyadic interactions (up to 4 agents) using a single trained model, achieving the best Fréchet Distance (e.g., **0.590** for N=4 vs. **2.143** for NN baseline) on the Embody3D dataset.
- MAGNet significantly improved ultra-long motion generation, demonstrating an **89%** improvement in Fréchet Distance (**2.781** vs. **24.810** for Ready-to-React) over 1800 frames on DuoBox while maintaining real-time inference speed of up to 56 FPS.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding pairwise relative transforms within a world-agnostic, local canonical frame is crucial for achieving natural scalability to arbitrary group sizes in multi-agent motion generation.
> - Utilizing diffusion forcing with independent per-token noise levels in an autoregressive transformer enables a single model to implicitly learn conditional distributions for diverse interaction tasks.
> - Discrete motion tokenization via a VQ-VAE is essential for regularizing motion quality, significantly reducing artifacts like foot skating and improving overall motion fidelity.
