# Limited Preference Data? Learning Better Reward Model with Latent Space Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> A research team from University of Wisconsin-Madison and Nanyang Technological University introduced LENS, a framework that synthesizes preference data directly in the latent embedding space of large language models, enhancing reward model accuracy and reducing computational generation time by 18x compared to text-based methods.

## Problem

- Aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences requires vast amounts of human feedback data, which is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to collect.
- Existing methods that synthesize preference data in the textual space using other LLMs are computationally intensive due to the costs of text generation and quadratic scaling of LLM-based annotation.
- Limited initial preference data restricts the generalization capabilities and robustness of reward models, hindering effective LLM alignment.

## Method

- LENS employs a ==Variational Autoencoder (VAE)== to learn a structured latent representation from existing LLM embeddings of preferred and non-preferred responses.
- A ==divergence-aware learning objective== is integrated into the VAE training, which maximizes the separation between the latent distributions of positive and negative samples.
- New preference data is synthesized by ==perturbing latent codes== in this learned space and decoding them into new embeddings, which are then used to train a lightweight reward model.

## Results

- LENS consistently outperforms all textual synthesis methods and simpler latent space baselines across HH-RLHF and TL;DR summarization benchmarks, improving reward model performance.
- The framework achieves an **18x** faster sample generation time and a **13x** reduction in total processing time on a single A100 GPU, using a generator model with **16,000x** fewer parameters (0.5M vs 8B) compared to text-based synthesis.
- Reward models trained with LENS-augmented data lead to better downstream Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) outcomes, with SFT models achieving a **61%** win rate against those trained with text-synthesis-derived rewards when evaluated by GPT-4.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthesizing preference data directly in the latent embedding space can significantly reduce computational overhead while effectively expanding the dataset for reward model training.
> - A carefully designed VAE with a divergence-aware loss function is crucial for learning a discriminative and smooth latent space that preserves underlying preference relationships.
> - Theoretical analysis supports that synthetic data generated by LENS can preserve preference ordering and reduce the estimation error of the reward model, improving its learnability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs): aligning their behavior with human preferences. This alignment is fundamental for deploying LLMs safely and effectively in real-world applications. The dominant paradigm for achieving this is Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which relies on a reward model to quantify human preferences. However, the efficacy of reward modeling is severely constrained by the high cost and time-consuming nature of collecting large-scale human preference data.

Historically, researchers have tackled this data bottleneck through several approaches:
1. **Direct Human Annotation:** This is the "gold standard" where human annotators provide pairwise preferences (e.g., "response A is better than response B"). While highly effective, it is prohibitively expensive and slow for the vast amounts of data needed for modern LLMs.
2. **Textual Space Synthesis with LLMs:** To alleviate human annotation costs, recent methods generate synthetic preference data by using powerful LLMs themselves. This typically involves a two-stage process:
 - **Response Generation:** A large LLM generates multiple diverse responses to a given prompt.
 - **Preference Annotation:** Another (or the same) LLM acts as an "AI judge" to evaluate these generated responses and create pairwise preference labels. Examples include "Self-rewarding" models, "Self-evolved" reward learning, and methods leveraging "IPO" (Implicit Preference Ordering).
 - **Limitations:** This approach, while reducing human involvement, is still computationally intensive. Generating diverse, high-quality responses from LLMs with billions of parameters is costly, and the subsequent LLM-based annotation scales quadratically with the number of responses, demanding significant computational resources. It also often requires complex prompt engineering.
3. **Embedding-based Reward Modeling:** This approach improves computational efficiency by training lightweight reward models (e.g., MLPs) on frozen LLM embeddings, rather than fine-tuning the entire LLM backbone. While this makes the reward model training itself faster, it does not inherently solve the problem of *expanding* the preference dataset efficiently.
4. **Active Learning:** Some methods focus on intelligently selecting which preference queries to solicit from humans or AI, aiming to maximize data efficiency. While competitive, these still rely on *collecting* data, not *generating* synthetic data to expand the existing set without additional labeling efforts.

This paper, by proposing **LENS (Latent EmbeddiNg for Synthesis)**, introduces a novel paradigm shift. Instead of synthesizing data in the expensive textual space, it synthesizes preference data directly in the **latent embedding space** of LLMs. This positions LENS as a crucial bridge technology, aiming to offer the data expansion benefits of textual synthesis while circumventing its immense computational overhead. It directly addresses the shortcomings of both costly human data and computationally prohibitive LLM-based textual synthesis, making advanced LLM alignment more scalable and accessible. By leveraging generative models (VAEs) on learned embeddings, it opens up a new avenue for efficient data augmentation in the field of LLM alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, efficient, and scalable framework for synthesizing preference data to improve reward modeling for Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in scenarios with limited initial preference data.

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical challenges in current LLM alignment methodologies:

1. **High Cost of Human Preference Data:** Aligning LLMs with human values and intentions through reward modeling is paramount for their safe and beneficial deployment. However, acquiring high-quality, large-scale human preference labels is an extremely expensive, labor-intensive, and time-consuming process. This bottleneck significantly impedes the development and deployment of aligned AI systems.
2. **Computational Expense of Textual Synthesis:** Existing approaches that attempt to automate data generation, primarily textual space synthesis using other LLMs, introduce their own set of challenges. These methods require substantial computational resources for two main reasons:
 - **Text Generation:** Generating diverse and high-quality responses to prompts necessitates running powerful, often multi-billion-parameter LLMs, which is computationally expensive.
 - **Preference Annotation:** Evaluating and ranking these generated responses to form preference pairs also requires additional LLM inference, scaling quadratically with the number of candidate responses. This makes textual synthesis impractical for many research labs and resource-constrained environments.
3. **Leveraging Semantic Structure:** LLM embeddings inherently capture rich semantic information. The authors are motivated to explore whether this semantic structure can be directly leveraged for data augmentation, rather than repeatedly processing and generating full text.
4. **Improving Generalization and Robustness:** A core goal of data augmentation is to expose the reward model to a wider variety of preference examples, thereby enhancing its generalization capabilities and making it more robust to unseen data. The challenge is to generate diverse yet semantically consistent synthetic data that accurately preserves the underlying preference relationships.
5. **Addressing Resource Constraints:** The current landscape of LLM alignment often favors institutions with massive computational resources. The authors are motivated to democratize AI alignment research by providing a significantly more efficient alternative that can be adopted by a broader range of researchers and organizations.

In essence, the paper aims to answer the question: "Given limited preference data, can we efficiently expand the dataset to improve reward modeling without incurring the prohibitive computational costs of traditional text-based synthesis or extensive human annotation?" LENS seeks to provide a scalable and effective solution that bypasses these limitations by operating directly in the latent space.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **LENS (Latent EmbeddiNg for Synthesis)**, introduces a novel approach to synthesize preference data directly in the latent embedding space of language models, rather than the textual space. This paradigm shift aims to bypass expensive text generation and annotation, leveraging the semantic structure already captured by LLM embeddings. LENS comprises three main stages:

#### 3.1. Variational Autoencoder with Divergence Learning

1. **Embedding Extraction:** The process begins by extracting LLM embeddings for preferred and rejected responses. Given a preference dataset `D = {(x_i, y+_i, y-_i)}`, where `x_i` is the prompt and `y+_i` / `y-_i` are preferred/non-preferred responses, the LLM's embedding extraction function (`LLM_embed`) is used to obtain `e+/-_i = LLM_embed(x_i, y+/-_i)`. These embeddings represent the semantic content of the responses.

2. **Learning Latent Representations with Divergence-aware VAE:** A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is employed to learn a probabilistic latent representation of these preference embeddings. The VAE is chosen for its ability to learn a smooth and structured latent space, which is crucial for generating diverse yet semantically coherent synthetic data.
 - **Encoder:** The VAE encoder (`phi`) maps each `d`-dimensional LLM embedding `e` to parameters (`mu_phi|e`, `sigma^2_phi|e`) of a Gaussian posterior distribution `q_phi(z|e)` in a `d_VAE`-dimensional latent space. Separate posterior distributions are parameterized for positive (`e+`) and negative (`e-`) embeddings.
 - **Decoder:** The decoder (`g_theta`) reconstructs the embeddings from samples (`z`) in the latent space back into the original embedding space (`e^hat = g_theta(z)`).
 - **VAE Loss:** The standard VAE loss `L_VAE(e) = L_recon(e, e^hat) + beta * D_KL(q_phi(z|e) || p(z))` is used, balancing reconstruction accuracy and regularization towards a standard Gaussian prior `p(z)`.
 - **Divergence Learning:** A key innovation is the introduction of a `L_divergence` term, which is the negative Wasserstein distance (`-W_2`) between the latent distributions of positive and negative samples: `L_divergence = -1/N * sum_i W_2(q_phi(z+|e+_i), q_phi(z-|e-_i))`. This term explicitly encourages the VAE to learn discriminative representations, maximizing the separation between latent spaces of preferred and non-preferred responses.
 - **Total Objective:** The overall objective is `L_total = 1/N * sum_i [L_VAE(e+_i) + L_VAE(e-_i)] + gamma * L_divergence`, where `gamma` controls the importance of the divergence term.

#### 3.2. Latent Space Synthesis

1. **Latent Space Sampling:** Once the VAE is trained, new preference data is synthesized by performing controlled perturbations in the learned latent space. For each original preferred/non-preferred embedding, multiple noisy variants are generated:
 - An original latent vector `z+/-_i` is sampled from its posterior.
 - Gaussian noise `eta+/-_i,j ~ N(0, sigma^2_noise * I)` is added to `z+/-_i`.
 - The noisy latent vector (`z+/-_i + eta+/-_i,j`) is then decoded by `g_theta` back into the LLM embedding space to create a synthetic embedding `e^hat+/-_i,j`.
 - To maintain quality, only the top-k synthetic latent codes (based on their likelihood under the learned distributions) are selected for decoding. `sigma_noise` is a hyperparameter controlling the noise level.

2. **Forming Synthetic Preference Pairs:** To expand the training data maximally, a compositional pairing strategy is used. Each synthesized or original preferred embedding is paired with both synthesized and original non-preferred embeddings, and vice versa. This forms an augmented dataset `E_aug = {(e~+, e~-) | e~+ in E+ U E+synth, e~- in E- U E-synth}`, where `E+synth` and `E-synth` are the sets of synthesized positive and negative embeddings.

#### 3.3. Reward Modeling with the Synthesized Preference Data

1. **Reward Model Training:** A lightweight MLP (`r_o`) is trained on the combined original and augmented preference dataset `E_aug`. This MLP maps the embeddings to a scalar reward score.
2. **Objective:** The reward model is optimized using the standard Bradley-Terry model objective: `L_E_aug_RM = -E_{(e~+, e~-) in E_aug} [log sigma(r_o(e~+) - r_o(e~-))]`. This objective encourages `r_o` to assign higher scores to preferred embeddings.
3. **Efficiency:** This approach utilizes embedding-based reward modeling, which is significantly more efficient than fine-tuning an entire LLM. By training on both real and synthesized pairs, the reward model benefits from a wider range of data, leading to better generalization.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical guarantees for LENS:
- **Theorem 1 (Synthesis Quality):** It proves that synthetic preference pairs generated by LENS approximately preserve the original preference ordering. The reward difference between synthesized positive and negative pairs, evaluated by an ideal reward function, is lower-bounded by the original reward difference minus an error term dependent on VAE reconstruction quality (`epsilon_rec`) and noise level (`sigma_noise`). This ensures synthetic data maintains fidelity.
- **Theorem 2 (Reward Model Learnability):** It demonstrates that augmenting the original training set with these synthetic samples reduces the estimation error upper bound of the reward model. This is achieved by effectively increasing the sample size while maintaining preference consistency, provided the original dataset size `N` is above a certain threshold and reconstruction error decays sufficiently with `N`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of LENS demonstrates its significant advantages in both performance and computational efficiency for reward modeling.

#### 4.1. Superior Reward Model Performance

- **Consistent Outperformance:** LENS consistently outperforms all textual space synthesis methods and simpler latent space baselines (direct embedding perturbation, Gaussian sampling) across two diverse benchmarks: HH-RLHF (helpfulness and harmlessness) and TL;DR summarization. This holds true across various data augmentation scales (2x, 4x, 8x).
 - For instance, at 4x augmentation on HH-RLHF, LENS achieves a reward score of 1.94, significantly higher than the strongest textual synthesis baseline (Fully fine-tuned at 1.78). Similar gains are observed on TL;DR (1.44 for LENS vs. 0.97 for textual synthesis at 4x).
 - Simpler latent space baselines, like direct perturbation or Gaussian sampling, either show inconsistent results or significant performance degradation, highlighting the critical role of the VAE in learning a structured latent representation.
- **Generalizability Across Models and Tasks:** LENS demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, improving reward model performance for various base LLMs from different families and scales, including Gemma-2B, Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen-2.5-14B. This indicates its broad applicability.

#### 4.2. Significant Computational Efficiency Gains

- **Massive Reduction in Model Size:** LENS requires a generator model with only 0.5M parameters (the VAE), compared to 8B parameters for text-based synthesis using LLMs. This is a 16,000x reduction in model size.
- **Faster Generation and Total Runtime:** This smaller model size translates into substantial speedups:
 - **18x faster sample generation time** (0.2 hours vs. 3.6 hours for 8x augmentation on HH-RLHF).
 - **13x reduction in total processing time** (0.4 hours vs. 5.2 hours on a single A100 GPU).
These efficiency gains are critical for scalability and practical deployment, especially in resource-constrained environments.

#### 4.3. Improved Downstream SFT Performance

- **Better Rejection Sampling Targets:** Reward models trained with LENS-augmented data lead to better outcomes for downstream Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) through rejection sampling.
- In a comparison using GPT-4 as a judge, the SFT model trained with targets selected by the LENS-trained reward model achieved a 61% win rate against the SFT model trained with targets from a textual synthesis-trained reward model (which achieved 39%). This indicates that LENS not only accelerates reward modeling but also enables the selection of higher-quality data for subsequent fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Insights

- **Effect of Divergence Loss Weight (γ):** A moderate weight for the `L_divergence` term (e.g., `γ = 0.1`) is optimal. It ensures a discriminative latent space where positive and negative samples are well-separated yet diverse. Too high a `γ` leads to over-separation, diminishing the benefit by creating trivially easy synthetic pairs.
- **Effect of Synthesis Noise (σ^2_noise):** Moderate noise levels (e.g., `σ^2_noise = 0.01`) are crucial. Too little noise limits diversity, while excessive noise violates the preference preservation condition, generating unrealistic embeddings and degrading performance.
- **Impact of Original Training Set Size:** LENS consistently boosts reward model performance across all initial training set sizes (from 0.1k to 50k samples). Its benefits are particularly pronounced in low-data regimes, validating its utility as an effective data augmentation strategy.
- **Combined Data Superiority:** Training with a combination of original and synthetic data yields the best performance, outperforming models trained on either original data alone or synthetic data alone. This empirically validates Theorem 2, showing that augmented data reduces statistical error while retaining the benefits of original samples.
- **VAE Architectural Design:** Optimal performance is achieved with a shared encoder/decoder architecture but separate distribution parameters for positive and negative samples. This balances shared representation learning with the need for distinct preference modeling.

#### 4.5. Empirical Verification of Theoretical Guarantees

- The appendix details empirical estimations of constants in the theoretical theorems. For instance, the reconstruction error (`epsilon_rec`) was observed to decay as `O(N^-p)` with `p ≈ 0.26`, supporting Assumption 1.
- The estimated threshold for original sample size `N_0 ≈ 66` suggests that LENS is beneficial even for relatively small initial datasets, validating Theorem 2.
- Empirical checks showed that synthetic samples largely preserved reward ordering, with 93.9% consistency and an average reward gap of 2.64 (synthetic) vs. 2.86 (original), supporting Theorem 1's claim about synthesis quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LENS framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment, carrying substantial significance and potential impact:

1. **Democratization of AI Alignment Research:** Perhaps the most profound impact of LENS is its potential to democratize access to advanced AI alignment techniques. By drastically reducing the computational and data acquisition costs associated with reward modeling (18x faster generation, 16,000x smaller model), LENS lowers the barrier to entry for smaller research labs, startups, and academic institutions that lack access to supercomputing clusters or vast annotation budgets. This could accelerate innovation and diversify the community contributing to safer and more aligned AI.

2. **Enhanced Scalability and Efficiency in LLM Development:** As LLMs continue to grow in size and complexity, the methods for aligning them must also scale. LENS offers a highly scalable solution for expanding preference datasets without proportionate increases in computational demands. This efficiency is critical for faster iteration cycles in LLM development, allowing researchers to train and evaluate reward models more rapidly and frequently.

3. **Improved LLM Performance and Safety:** The core purpose of reward modeling is to guide LLMs towards desired behaviors. By enabling the training of better-performing reward models—demonstrated by LENS's superior empirical results and its ability to improve downstream supervised fine-tuning outcomes—this work directly contributes to the development of more helpful, harmless, and generally higher-quality LLMs. This has direct implications for various applications, from more reliable conversational agents to better content moderation and educational tools.

4. **Novel Research Direction at the Intersection of Generative Models and Alignment:** LENS is highlighted as the first work to explore latent-space synthesis specifically for reward modeling of LLMs. This opens a new and promising research avenue, encouraging further exploration into how generative models, particularly VAEs, can be leveraged to efficiently manipulate and expand data representations in latent spaces for complex AI tasks. This could inspire similar approaches for other types of human feedback or data augmentation challenges in AI.

5. **Robustness in Low-Data Regimes:** The empirical evidence showing LENS's consistent benefits even with small initial training datasets underscores its value in low-resource scenarios. This is particularly important for niche applications or domains where human preference data is inherently scarce, making it possible to develop aligned LLMs even under severe data constraints.

6. **Theoretical Foundation for Data Augmentation:** The provision of theoretical guarantees, demonstrating that synthetic pairs preserve preference ordering and improve reward model generalization under certain conditions, strengthens the scientific foundation of data augmentation techniques. This provides a clearer understanding of when and why latent space synthesis is effective, guiding future methodological improvements.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the current reliance on offline embeddings, it explicitly points towards future work in online or dynamic synthesis. LENS therefore not only offers an immediate practical solution but also lays a strong foundation for future advancements in making LLM alignment more adaptive and pervasive.
