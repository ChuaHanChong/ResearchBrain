# Self-Improving VLA Policies: Selected Diffusion Noise for Spurious-Robust Action Smoothing

> [!summary] Summary
> The Selected Diffusion Noise (SDN) framework improves the robustness and motion quality of diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies by implementing a training-free, two-stage inference-time selection process for initial diffusion noise. This approach leads to an 18.33% absolute increase in real-world task success rates and reduces kinematic jerk, addressing spurious visual dependencies and unstable movements.

## Problem

- VLA policies exhibit fragility in out-of-distribution scenarios, often overfitting to spurious correlations and leading to 'hallucinations' where robots misinterpret scenes or states.
- Generated actions frequently prioritize semantic goals over physical feasibility, resulting in kinematically unstable, high-jerk, or 'jerky' motions that cause mechanical wear and tear.
- Existing methods for improving VLA robustness often require retraining multi-billion-parameter models or rely on external evaluators, which are computationally expensive or lack generalizability.

## Method

- **SDN** introduces a training-free, two-stage hierarchical selection algorithm that refines VLA action distributions at inference time by treating the initial diffusion noise as a controllable degree of freedom.
- Stage 1, the '==Grounding Filter==,' constructs positive (original observation) and negative (object-masked observation) action candidate sets, then uses kNN-based contrastive grounding to select actions robust to visual perturbations.
- Stage 2, 'Kinematic Stability Refinement,' further filters the grounded candidates by minimizing the JerkRMS metric over extended action chunks to ensure physical feasibility and reduce jerky motions.

## Results

- Achieved an **18.33%** absolute increase in average success rate on real-world ALOHA robot tasks over the vanilla Gr00t N1.5 model (from **30.00%** to **48.33%**).
- Consistently improved motion quality by reducing Jerk and Action Total Variation (ATV) below vanilla baselines in real-world experiments, a unique benefit compared to other guidance methods.
- Demonstrated a 'do-no-harm' property in simulation, yielding non-negative gains across all tasks for both Gr00t N1.6 (**+5.1%** avg. success) and π0 (**+8.7%** avg. success), confirming its robust and architecture-agnostic nature.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The initial diffusion noise space in VLA models can be leveraged as a controllable degree of freedom for test-time self-improvement without modifying model parameters.
> - A contrastive grounding filter, by generating 'confused' samples from object-masked observations, effectively identifies and mitigates spurious visual correlations in VLA policies.
> - Explicitly optimizing for kinematic smoothness (e.g., minimizing JerkRMS) during action selection is crucial for ensuring physically feasible and stable robot motions, complementing semantic grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Generalist Robot Policies, such as $\pi_0$ and GR00T, has advanced robotic manipulation capabilities through the application of diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models trained on extensive datasets. These models demonstrate versatility across multiple tasks. However, existing VLA policies often exhibit fragility in out-of-distribution scenarios. This fragility stems from several factors: a tendency to overfit to spurious correlations and non-essential behaviors in human demonstrations, an inability to consistently differentiate critical task logic from incidental expert variance, and a susceptibility to local distribution shifts (e.g., novel lighting or tool wear) where retraining multi-billion-parameter models is computationally prohibitive and risks catastrophic forgetting. This context highlights a need for test-time self-improvement mechanisms that allow VLA models to refine their action distributions during inference without altering model parameters.

Current research on enhancing diffusion-based VLA policies explores three main directions: (i) noise-steering methods, which inject guidance signals (e.g., classifier-free, classifier-based gradients, action-coherence vectors) into denoising steps; (ii) best-of-N sampling, which generates multiple rollouts and selects the most plausible; and (iii) non-Gaussian priors, which bias the latent space with environment-specific memories. While these methods show promise, they often rely on external evaluators like vision-language models or auxiliary memories to select trajectories.

The authors identify two specific limitations prevalent in current VLA models that these existing methods often overlook:
1. **Reliance on Spurious Visual Cues**: VLA models can suffer from "hallucinations" where they incorrectly infer object states or attend to irrelevant visual information. For instance, a robot might continue a "place carrot onto plate" task as if it successfully grasped the carrot, even after a grasp failure, due to reliance on background cues.
2. **Prioritization of Semantic Likelihood over Physical Feasibility**: Policies frequently generate actions that achieve semantic goals but involve kinematically unstable, high-jerk, or "jerky" motions, leading to mechanical wear and tear or brittle behavior.

This work, "Selected Diffusion Noise (SDN)," positions itself as a training-free, test-time method designed to address these identified gaps. Unlike existing approaches, SDN does not require external evaluators or model parameter updates. It leverages the inherent diffusion noise space as a controllable degree of freedom to simultaneously enhance visual grounding and kinematic stability, offering a plug-and-play improvement for existing VLA architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the robustness and success rate of diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, particularly in the face of spurious visual correlations and noisy action generation. This objective is driven by two main motivations:

1. **Mitigating Spurious Visual Correlations and Hallucinations**:
 - **Problem**: Existing VLA policies, despite their generalization capabilities, often exhibit brittleness in out-of-distribution settings. They can overfit to non-essential visual cues in training data, leading to "hallucinations" where the robot misinterprets the scene or its own state. For example, a robot might continue a "grasp and place" task even if a grasp failed, moving towards the target placement location while holding nothing. This behavior arises from the policy's dangerous reliance on spurious background cues or an inability to distinguish critical task elements from incidental visual information.
 - **Motivation**: There is a need for a mechanism that enables VLA models to autonomously detect and filter out actions derived from such ungrounded visual interpretations. This would make robotic behaviors more reliable and less susceptible to minor visual perturbations or changes in the environment. Traditional guidance methods often struggle here because the "unconditional" model (used for contrast) is not explicitly designed to capture structured failure modes like hallucinated grasps.

2. **Enhancing Kinematic Stability and Reducing Jerky Motions**:
 - **Problem**: VLA policies frequently prioritize semantic goal achievement over the physical feasibility of the action trajectory. This can result in motions characterized by sudden, high-jerk rotations or oscillations. While these trajectories might technically reach the goal, they can cause significant mechanical wear and tear on the robot and lead to unstable behavior, especially under physical perturbations. Current methods for addressing smoothness often involve modifying model parameters during training or relying on heuristic augmentations, which can be sensitive and non-generalizable.
 - **Motivation**: There is a critical requirement for test-time refinement that can ensure physical viability and smoothness of generated actions without complex retraining or specialized hardware. Such a mechanism would contribute to the longevity and stable operation of robotic systems.

To address these motivations, the key objective of SDN is to introduce a simple, training-free test-time method that:
- Dynamically samples and selects initial diffusion noise vectors to mitigate reliance on spurious visual cues (improving visual grounding).
- Selects candidate actions that yield kinematically coherent and smooth trajectories (reducing action jitter and ensuring physical feasibility).
- Combines these dual objectives to encourage stable behavior even under challenging observations, such as object-masked scenes.
- Operates as a plug-and-play enhancement for various VLA architectures without requiring auxiliary networks, parameter updates, or expensive real-time operations like image inpainting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Selected Diffusion Noise (SDN) framework introduces a training-free, two-stage selection strategy that refines the VLA action distribution during inference by treating the initial diffusion noise as a controllable degree of freedom. This approach aims to filter task-relevant actions based on visual grounding and then refine them for physical stability.

**Core Principle:**
Instead of traditional stepwise guidance of a single action sample, SDN performs a global search over trajectories. It generates multiple candidate action chunks from different initial noise seeds (via flow matching) and selects the most reliable one based on a dual-objective evaluation. The policy output distribution is conceptually modeled as a mixture of the expert data distribution ($p^*$) and a failure distribution ($p_{fail}$), where failure cases can overlap significantly with correct actions, making them difficult to distinguish via standard sampling. SDN aims to select samples that are consistent with $p^*$ and maximally separated from $p_{fail}$.

**Two-Stage Hierarchical Selection Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**

**Stage 1: Grounding Filter (Contrastive Grounding)**
This stage aims to identify and prioritize initial noise seeds that lead to "grounded" actions, i.e., actions that are robust to object-level visual perturbations and less reliant on spurious correlations.

1. **Constructing Selection Sets:** For each inference step, SDN generates two sets of action chunk candidates:
 - **Positive Set (G)**: Consists of `N` action chunks ($A_i$) generated by the VLA policy using the original observation ($o$) and language instruction ($\ell$). This set represents the policy's standard output.
 - **Negative Set (B)**: Consists of `N` "confused" action chunks ($\tilde{A}_i$) generated by the same policy but with a perturbed observation ($o_{neg}$). The perturbation involves **zero-masking** pixels in the region of the target object. This is achieved by first detecting task-relevant objects using Grounding-DINO and then tracking their regions across timesteps with SAM-2. This masking technique is highlighted as computationally cheaper than inpainting methods used in prior work. The purpose of this set is to capture "hallucinated" or spurious behaviors that persist even when the target object is visually absent.

2. **Contrastive Grounding Score ($R^{(i)}_{ground}$):**
 - A grounded action chunk should be consistent with the original observation (high density under $p_G$) and unlikely under the corrupted observation (low density under $p_B$). This is formulated as a log density ratio problem.
 - Since $p_G$ and $p_B$ are unknown, they are estimated using k-nearest neighbor (kNN) density estimation from the finite samples in sets G and B.
 - Local scale estimates, $d^{(i)}_G$ and $d^{(i)}_B$, are computed as the average squared Euclidean distance from a candidate $A_i$ to its k nearest neighbors within G (excluding $A_i$ itself) and B, respectively.
 - The grounding score for a candidate $A_i$ is then defined as $R^{(i)}_{ground} = d^{(i)}_B - d^{(i)}_G$. A higher score indicates that $A_i$ is relatively farther from the "confused" (negative) behaviors and closer to the "grounded" (positive) behaviors.
 - **Selection:** From the initial `N` candidates, the top-M candidates with the highest $R^{(i)}_{ground}$ scores are retained, forming a reduced set $G_M$. This step effectively filters out actions likely driven by spurious visual correlations.

**Stage 2: Kinematic Stability Refinement (Smoothness Filtering)**
This stage further refines the selection from $G_M$ to ensure physical feasibility and reduce jerky motions.

1. **Kinematic Stability Evaluation:** The `M` candidates from Stage 1 are evaluated for smoothness.
 - **Metric:** The JerkRMS metric is used to quantify smoothness. For an action chunk $A_i = [a^{(i)}_1, \ldots, a^{(i)}_L]$, it calculates the root mean square of the third-order finite difference of the action sequence, which represents discrete jerk.
 - **Extended Action Chunks:** To better assess temporal consistency and expose delayed oscillations, action chunks longer than the executed horizon are generated for this evaluation.
 - Minimizing $S(A_i)$ (JerkRMS) suppresses dynamically unstable candidates.

2. **Final Action Selection:** The final action chunk $A^*$ is selected from the $M$ grounded candidates as the one that minimizes the JerkRMS score: $A^* = \arg \min_{A_i \in G_M} S(A_i)$.

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Training-Free**: SDN operates exclusively at inference time and does not require any additional training of the base VLA model or auxiliary networks.
- **Plug-and-Play**: It can be integrated as a wrapper for various diffusion-based VLA backbones (e.g., $\pi_0$, GR00T).
- **Efficiency**: Object masking is used instead of computationally intensive inpainting, and kNN estimation is used for density approximation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SDN was conducted across simulated and real-world environments using multiple VLA policies. The findings indicate consistent improvements in success rates, enhanced motion quality, and a robust, "do-no-harm" property compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Performance on Gr00t N1.6 (SimplerEnv)**:
 - SDN consistently improved performance over the base Gr00t N1.6 model. Notable gains included tripling the success rate on the "Open Drawer" task (from 4.0% to 14.0%).
 - On the WidowX platform, SDN achieved a 54.5% average success rate, representing a 5.1% absolute improvement over the baseline (49.5%).
 - SDN demonstrated a "do-no-harm" property, yielding non-negative gains across all tasks, unlike some baselines (e.g., CFG, PCD) which occasionally degraded performance.

- **Performance on $\pi_0$ (SimplerEnv)**:
 - SDN achieved a substantial 8.7% average gain in success rate over the base $\pi_0$ model.
 - It consistently improved success rates, particularly on high-precision WidowX tasks like "Spoon Towel" (90.0%) and "Stack Cube" (78.0%), where some baselines (ACG, WNG) degraded performance.
 - These results confirm SDN's architecture-agnostic nature and its ability to provide robust, additive improvements to different foundation-scale models.

#### 4.2. Real-world Experiments

- **Performance on Gr00t N1.5 (ALOHA robot)**:
 - SDN achieved an average success rate of 48.33% across "Cube2Drawer" and "Banana2Pot" tasks, marking an 18.33% absolute improvement over the vanilla Gr00t N1.5 model (30.00%). This significantly outperformed other baselines (CFG, ACG, PCD).
 - **Motion Quality**: Beyond success rates, SDN was the only method that simultaneously improved motion quality. It reduced Jerk and Action Total Variation (ATV) below the vanilla baseline (e.g., Jerk decreased from 12.45 to 11.8 on "Cube2Drawer"). In contrast, other guidance baselines often significantly increased Jerk and ATV, suggesting unstable motions resulting from pushing the policy towards out-of-distribution regions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Stage 1 vs. Stage 2**:
 - SDN-Grounding (Stage 1 alone) improved average performance by +13.33%.
 - SDN-Smoothness (Stage 2 alone) yielded an even larger average gain of +16.67%.
 - Combining both stages (Full SDN) achieved the best overall performance (48.33% average success rate in real-world tasks), demonstrating a strong synergy between semantic grounding and motion stability.

- **Effect of Masking Observation**:
 - Masking the target object in observations led to a catastrophic performance collapse in base models: $\pi_0$ success rates dropped by 39.5%, and Gr00t by 45.3%. This result validated the hypothesis that VLA models heavily rely on ungrounded "visual shortcuts," underscoring the necessity and effectiveness of the Stage 1 grounding filter.

- **Effect of Different Corruption Strategies**:
 - Comparing visual (object masking), semantic (text), and proprioceptive (state) corruption, visual guidance consistently yielded the most significant gains. For example, visual grounding provided the highest success rates across both Google Robot and WidowX platforms, outperforming text-based (+3.6% on Google Robot) and state-based (+6.1% on WidowX) anchors. This established visual object masking as the most potent grounding signal for SDN.

- **Efficiency Comparison**:
 - SDN achieved a higher success rate (48.33%) while operating 1.53 times faster than Policy Contrastive Decoding (PCD), which requires computationally intensive image inpainting. SDN's direct, modality-specific masking avoids generative artifacts and significant latency.

- **Effect of Sample Sizes (N) and k-NN (k)**:
 - A grid search indicated N=12 candidates provided an optimal trade-off between grounding strength and inference speed.
 - SDN remained robust for k-NN neighborhood sizes up to k=10; performance declined with larger k due to noise in the contrastive signal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, introducing the Selected Diffusion Noise (SDN) framework, contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a practical and effective method for improving the reliability and performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies.

**Key Significance Points:**

- **Generalizable Test-Time Enhancement**: SDN provides a simple, training-free, and architecture-agnostic mechanism to enhance diffusion-based VLA policies. This means it can be readily applied as a "plug-and-play" wrapper to existing foundation models like $\pi_0$ and GR00T without requiring costly retraining or modification of their parameters. This significantly lowers the barrier to deploying more robust robotic systems.
- **Addressing Core VLA Limitations**: The framework effectively tackles two critical and prevalent failure modes in VLA policies:
 1. **Mitigation of Spurious Visual Correlations/Hallucinations**: By leveraging a contrastive grounding filter with object-masked observations, SDN helps VLA models become less susceptible to relying on irrelevant visual cues. This leads to more robust task execution even under visual perturbations or when objects are partially occluded.
 2. **Reduction of Kinematic Instability**: Through a smoothness refinement stage, SDN actively selects action trajectories that are kinematically stable and smooth, directly addressing the problem of "jerky" motions. This not only improves task success but also contributes to the mechanical longevity and safer operation of robotic hardware.
- **Demonstrated Performance Gains in Diverse Settings**: The consistent improvements in success rates (8-10% in simulation, 10-18% in real-world settings) across various VLA backbones and benchmarks (Google Robot, Widow-X, ALOHA robot) underscore the method's effectiveness and generalizability. Crucially, SDN achieves these gains while also improving motion quality, unlike some other guidance methods that may increase jerkiness.
- **Efficiency and Practicality**: SDN's training-free nature and reliance on computationally efficient observation augmentation (zero-masking instead of inpainting) make it suitable for real-time inference. This efficiency allows for immediate deployment of frozen foundation models in dynamic environments without significant computational overhead or latency, which is a key advantage for practical robotics.
- **Conceptual Contribution to Diffusion Model Steering**: The work highlights the initial diffusion noise space not merely as a source of randomness, but as a controllable degree of freedom that can be strategically optimized. This perspective opens avenues for further research into leveraging the latent space of generative models for robustness and control in robotics.

**Potential Impact:**

The potential impact of SDN lies in its ability to make VLA-powered robots more reliable, efficient, and safer for real-world deployment. By reducing failure modes related to visual misinterpretation and unstable movements, SDN could accelerate the transition of advanced VLA policies from research labs to practical applications in logistics, manufacturing, service robotics, and other domains where robust and precise manipulation is critical. The "do-no-harm" property ensures that its integration does not negatively impact existing functionalities, fostering greater confidence in its adoption.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as noted by the authors):**

The authors acknowledge that SDN, despite its gains, might have limitations. Its reliance on object-centric visual shortcuts might not capture hallucinations induced by other factors like dynamic lighting or texture shifts (corruption specificity). Additionally, the current fixed number of noise candidates at inference time limits adaptivity to varying task complexity. Future research could explore adaptive candidate allocation and richer multimodal corruption modeling to further enhance robustness, efficiency, and generalization of diffusion-based robotic policies.
