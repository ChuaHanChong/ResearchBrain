# DexImit: Learning Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation from Monocular Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> DexImit is an automated framework that generates physically plausible bimanual dexterous manipulation data for robots directly from monocular human videos, overcoming data scarcity without requiring depth information. It achieves high success rates in simulation and facilitates zero-shot real-world deployment for complex tasks through robust data generation and comprehensive augmentation.

## Problem

- Collecting large-scale datasets for bimanual dexterous hands is expensive and labor-intensive, leading to data scarcity.
- Direct use of human videos for robot learning suffers from a significant "embodiment gap" between human and robotic hands.
- Prior 3D reconstruction methods from videos often require precise depth information or perfect reconstruction accuracy, limiting their scalability and robustness for complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- A four-stage **DexImit** pipeline performs ==4D hand-object trajectory reconstruction== from monocular human videos using hand-size-based scale approximation and robust 6D pose estimation.
- An ==action-centric scheduling algorithm== decomposes and coordinates bimanual subtasks, followed by structured action generation via ==force-closure-based grasp synthesis== and keyframe motion planning.
- Comprehensive data augmentation, including object pose, scale, camera, and observation noise, enhances policy robustness and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- Achieved an **82%** success rate in 4D object trajectory reconstruction on 100 short-horizon tasks using a combination of SpatialTracker v2 and FoundationPose++.
- Policies trained on DexImit data consistently outperformed baseline methods in simulation, achieving high success rates across six tasks, including **100%** for 'Put Cup', 'Grapefruit', and 'Fruits'.
- Enabled successful zero-shot real-world deployment on bimanual robotic systems, with ablation studies showing that object scale and visual observation augmentations were critical for robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Near-metric scale reconstruction of hand-object interactions from monocular videos is achievable by leveraging human hand size as a consistent scale prior.
> - Decomposing long-horizon bimanual tasks into subtasks with an action-centric scheduling algorithm effectively manages complex coordination and reduces error accumulation.
> - Force-closure based grasp synthesis, combined with keyframe motion planning and robust 6D object pose tracking, yields physically plausible robot trajectories for intricate manipulations.
> - Strategic data augmentation, including scale randomization that adjusts finger articulation instead of re-generating grasps, is crucial for successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic dexterous manipulation faces a fundamental challenge: data scarcity. Collecting large-scale datasets for bimanual dexterous hands is expensive and labor-intensive due to the complexity of teleoperation and hardware costs. In contrast, human manipulation videos are abundant, available from the internet or increasingly through video generation models. These videos inherently contain rich information about task concepts and manipulation actions.

Previous efforts to leverage human videos for robot learning generally fall into two categories:
1. **Direct pretraining with human hands as heterogeneous embodiments:** These methods directly use human hand data for policy pretraining, but encounter a significant "embodiment gap" due to discrepancies in visual observations and action spaces between human and robotic hands. This gap severely limits cross-embodiment learning and generalization.
2. **3D reconstruction from videos:** This paradigm involves reconstructing 3D hand-object keypoint flows or object trajectories from videos and then reproducing these demonstrations using motion planning or reinforcement learning. While this approach effectively bridges the embodiment gap, many existing methods rely on absolute depth information, or require highly accurate reconstructions to prevent training failures, which fundamentally limits their scalability and robustness in challenging scenarios like fast motions, occlusions, or complex interactions. Furthermore, prior approaches often struggle with object scale variations, trajectory noise, and sim-to-real transfer issues, particularly for long-horizon bimanual tasks.

DexImit positions itself within the 3D reconstruction paradigm, specifically aiming to overcome the scalability and robustness limitations of existing methods. It focuses on developing an automated, depth-free framework that can convert monocular human manipulation videos from arbitrary sources into physically plausible robot data, addressing the data scarcity problem for bimanual dexterous manipulation. By mitigating compounding errors during data generation and incorporating comprehensive data augmentation, DexImit seeks to enable zero-shot real-world deployment of policies trained on this synthetic data, thus advancing the practical application of robot learning from human video demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an automated framework, named DexImit, that can generate large-scale, physically plausible bimanual dexterous manipulation data for robots directly from monocular human videos. This framework aims to operate without requiring additional information such as depth or camera parameters, thus facilitating scalability.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges in dexterous manipulation robotics:
- **Data Scarcity:** Real-world data collection for bimanual dexterous robots is highly resource-intensive and expensive. This scarcity fundamentally limits the generalization capabilities of learned policies.
- **Leveraging Human Knowledge:** Human manipulation videos represent a vast, untapped source of manipulation knowledge. They encode both high-level task concepts and low-level actions, making them highly promising for scaling robot learning.
- **Bridging the Embodiment Gap:** A significant challenge arises from the inherent differences between human and robotic hands (embodiment gap). Directly applying human video data for robot training often leads to poor performance due to disparities in visual appearance and action spaces. Existing 3D reconstruction methods often require precise depth information or perfect reconstruction accuracy, which are difficult to achieve at scale from arbitrary monocular videos.
- **Handling Complex Tasks:** Many existing methods struggle with complex manipulation scenarios, including tool-using, long-horizon tasks, and fine-grained interactions, especially those requiring bimanual coordination.
- **Achieving Zero-Shot Real-World Deployment:** Policies trained on synthetic data often suffer from a sim-to-real gap, limiting their direct applicability in real-world environments without further fine-tuning or adaptation.

DexImit is specifically motivated to address these challenges by providing a scalable solution that:
- Reconstructs hand-object interactions from arbitrary monocular human videos with near-metric scale.
- Enables dynamic bimanual coordination for long-horizon tasks through subtask decomposition and scheduling.
- Synthesizes robust manipulation actions using force-closure constraints and motion planning.
- Incorporates comprehensive data augmentation to facilitate zero-shot transfer to real-world robot systems.

By achieving these objectives, DexImit aims to unlock the potential of large-scale human video data, including those from video generation models, for advancing the capabilities of bimanual dexterous robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexImit is structured as a four-stage data generation pipeline: (1) 4D trajectory reconstruction of hand-object interactions, (2) subtask decomposition and bimanual scheduling, (3) structured action generation to produce robot manipulation trajectories, and (4) comprehensive data augmentation.

#### 3.1. Reconstruction of 4D Hand-Object Interactions

This stage converts raw monocular video into 4D hand-object trajectories in a unified world coordinate system.
- **Video Processing and Task Understanding:** The input video is sampled to a constant frame rate. A Vision-Language Model (VLM), Qwen3-VL, is used to understand the video content and identify objects involved in the manipulation.
- **Segmentation:** Grounded Sam2 generates three types of masks frame-by-frame: object masks for 3D generation and 6D pose estimation, hand masks for hand trajectory estimation (left and right hands), and a table mask for defining the world coordinate system.
- **Objects and Hands Reconstruction:**
 - **Depth Estimation and Scale Approximation:** SpatialTracker v2 estimates unscaled depth for each frame. An initial metric scale is estimated by leveraging the relatively consistent size of human hands: a hand point cloud from the first frame (derived from the hand mask and unscaled depth) is scaled to match a hand mesh (from Wilor). This scale factor is then applied to all depth frames to obtain near-metric depth.
 - **3D Mesh Generation:** SAM3D generates object meshes with approximate initial pose and scale. The align-render-align procedure, similar to hand scaling, is used to precisely align object meshes with the metric-scaled depth. Hand poses are directly used from Wilor without further scale adjustment.
- **6D Pose Estimation:** FoundationPose++ (a tracking variant) is employed for robust 6D pose estimation of objects, ensuring temporal continuity. For hands, only the translation component is estimated using the align-render-align procedure, as orientation is provided by Wilor. This yields continuous hand and object trajectories.
- **World Coordinate Transformation:** To map reconstructed trajectories from arbitrary camera viewpoints into a fixed world coordinate system:
 - **Axis Estimation:** The world z-axis is determined by the surface normal of the table plane (from the table mask). The world x-axis is derived from the first-frame perpendicular bisector direction of the two hands, projected orthogonal to the z-axis. The y-axis is then determined by the right-handed coordinate system rule.
 - **Origin Estimation:** The world-frame origin is set to the center of the axis-aligned bounding box enclosing all manipulated objects in the first frame, translated to a predefined manipulation region in the robot workspace.

#### 3.2. Subtask Decomposition and Task Scheduling

This stage enables coordination for bimanual and long-horizon tasks.
- **Task Representation:** Tasks are defined with an embodiment set (e.g., left/right hand), an associated object, an ordered list of subactions, and a current subaction index. Subactions include types like `pregrasp`, `grasp`, `motion`, and `release`, each with a start frame.
- **Task Annotation:** Qwen3-VL is used for video understanding, subtask decomposition, and structured labeling. For long-horizon tasks, manual annotations can optionally be incorporated.
- **Action-Centric Scheduling Algorithm:** This algorithm dynamically schedules subtasks across multiple robot embodiments over time. It prioritizes tasks based on their start times and manages an action queue for each embodiment, ensuring conflict-free execution of concurrent or sequential bimanual actions.

#### 3.3. Source Data Generation

After trajectories and schedules are established, low-level robot actions are generated.
- **Grasp Synthesis:** For each required grasp on an object, a candidate generation-and-selection strategy is used:
 - **Initialization:** Grasp candidates are initialized by sampling contact points on the object's convex hull. For unimanual, one contact point; for bimanual, two on opposite sides. Hands are oriented towards the object.
 - **Optimization:** The BODex formulation is used to minimize a cost function involving wrench error, contact distance, hand-object collision, and hand-hand penetration, yielding physically feasible grasps. The number of active fingers involved in the grasp can be conditioned on VLM inference from the human video.
 - **Ranking and Stability Check:** Candidates are ranked based on their distance (translation and rotation error) to the reconstructed human hand pose. The most consistent candidates are then evaluated for physical stability via a simulation rollout, moving the object from the grasping pose to its target. A grasp is stable if the object's final pose is close to the planned target. The first stable grasp is selected.
- **Motion Generation:** After a grasp is established, the hand and object are treated as a single rigid body. Relative transformations between keyframe object poses are applied to the end-effector pose of the robot hand(s) to generate the target end-effector configurations for motion planning.

#### 3.4. Data Augmentation

To facilitate robust policy learning and zero-shot real-world deployment, comprehensive data augmentation is applied to the generated source trajectories.
- **Object Pose Augmentation:** Randomization of object position and translation to improve spatial generalization.
- **Object Scale Augmentation:** Reconstructed object scales (considered 1.0) are augmented with factors like [0.8, 1.2]. Crucially, grasps and motions are *not* regenerated for each scale; instead, only finger articulation is adjusted to maintain consistent supervision signals, which stabilizes training.
- **Camera Pose Augmentation:** Randomization of camera orientation and position for viewpoint generalization.
- **Observation Augmentation:** For 3D point cloud observations, 30% of points are randomly removed, and noise (30% perturbation) is added to the normals of remaining points to simulate real-world depth sensor imperfections (e.g., sparsity, noise).

A 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) is then trained on this augmented dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to evaluate DexImit's capability to scale data generation, handle task difficulty, and enable zero-shot real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Data Usability

- **Object Trajectory Estimation:** The accuracy of 4D object trajectory reconstruction was evaluated using different combinations of depth estimation models (VGGT, SpatialTracker v2 (ST2), Trace-Anything (TA), Depth-Anything v3 (DA3)) and 6D pose estimation methods (RANSAC, ColorPCR (PCR), FoundationPose++ (FPose)). The combination of ST2 for depth estimation and FPose for 6D pose estimation (ST2+FPose) achieved the highest success rate of 82% on 100 short-horizon tasks, indicating its superior accuracy and temporal consistency compared to other combinations (e.g., TA+RANSAC at 38%, ST2+PCR at 76%).
- **Usability under Varying Quality and Difficulty:** The usability of generated data was assessed across four levels of input video quality (sentence-level generation, in-the-wild, custom-captured, manually corrected) and four levels of task difficulty (unimanual short-horizon, cooperative bimanual short-horizon, independent bimanual short-horizon, cooperative bimanual long-horizon).
 - For **simple tasks**, all input data sources, including synthesized videos from models like Veo3 and in-the-wild videos, showed high usability rates. Veo3 demonstrated stronger temporal consistency than Wan2.2.
 - As **task complexity and temporal horizon increased**, the usability of in-the-wild and lower-quality generated videos degraded significantly due to factors like occlusions and suboptimal camera viewpoints.
 - **Custom-captured videos** (recorded with reconstruction in mind) significantly improved reconstruction quality, yielding high usability for simple tasks and considerable success for challenging long-horizon tasks.
 - With **manual correction** of reconstruction errors, even the most complex long-horizon and fine-grained tasks (e.g., cooking, multi-cup stacking) could be effectively processed into training-ready data.

#### 4.2. Data Quality

DexImit's data quality for policy learning was evaluated by training a DP3 policy on 100 augmented demonstrations and testing its performance in simulation against two re-implemented baselines: RigVid and DexMan.
- **Baselines:** RigVid (adapted for bimanual dexterous hands) estimates object poses and uses grasp synthesis/motion planning. DexMan (re-implemented) uses reinforcement learning to track video-derived trajectories.
- **Tasks:** Six representative tasks were used: Put Cup, Grapefruit, Fruits, Pour, Pot, and Stack Cups.
- **Results:** DexImit achieved the highest success rate across all tasks (e.g., 100% for Put Cup, Grapefruit, Fruits; 78% for Pour; 100% for Pot; 52% for Stack Cups). RigVid performed well only on unimanual, short-horizon tasks, failing on bimanual and interaction-intensive scenarios. DexMan was sensitive to trajectory quality and unstable for tasks with multiple sequential actions, succeeding only on short-horizon tasks. DexImit's explicit subtask decomposition, scheduled execution, stable grasp synthesis, and keyframe-based motion planning contributed to its robust performance on both short-horizon and challenging long-horizon tasks like `Pot` and `Stack Six Cups`.

#### 4.3. Task Difficulty

DexImit's capabilities were further assessed on four complex long-horizon tasks:
- **Make-Beverage:** An ultra-long-horizon task involving multiple pouring and mixing steps.
- **Cook:** A long-horizon cooperative bimanual task involving placing items and bimanual carrying.
- **Cut-Apple:** A tool-using task requiring precise knife manipulation.
- **Stack-Cup:** A long-horizon fine-grained task requiring stacking six cups into a pyramid.
Qualitative results indicated that DexImit successfully generated robust action sequences and physically plausible data for these complex tasks, leveraging its force-closure-based grasp synthesis, keyframe motion planning, and Action-Centric Scheduling Algorithm to manage multi-step hand-object interactions and mitigate compounding errors.

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Real World Deployment

DexImit's effectiveness for zero-shot real-world deployment was tested using two UR5e arms with XHands and a Microsoft Azure Kinect depth camera.
- **Meta-tasks:** Four tasks were used: Unimanual Place Apple, Independent bimanual Place Potato&Pepper, Bimanual grasping Place Pot, and Cooperative bimanual Pour Water.
- **Ablation Study:**
 - **`w/o scale aug`:** Disabling scale augmentation led to a significant drop in success rates, indicating the importance of exposing the policy to a distribution of metric-consistent scales for robust real-world execution.
 - **`regen grasp`:** When scale augmentation was applied, but grasps and motions were re-generated for each scaled instance, performance degraded drastically, even below the no-augmentation setting. This was attributed to inconsistent supervision signals introduced by re-generated motions at different scales.
 - **`w/o obj pcd noise`:** Removing visual augmentations (camera pose and object point cloud noise) also reduced real-world performance, highlighting the need to simulate real-world sensor noise and variability in training.
- **Overall Results:** DexImit achieved consistently high zero-shot success rates across all meta-tasks, demonstrating both the physical plausibility of the reconstructed data and the effectiveness of the proposed data augmentation pipeline for sim-to-real transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexImit framework offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of robotic dexterous manipulation:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** DexImit provides an automated, scalable solution to the long-standing problem of data scarcity in bimanual dexterous manipulation. By converting readily available monocular human videos (both "in-the-wild" and synthetically generated) into physically plausible robot data, it significantly expands the potential training data pool beyond costly real-world robot demonstrations. This could accelerate research and development in dexterous manipulation by democratizing access to diverse manipulation skills.

- **Bridging the Embodiment Gap and Enhancing Robustness:** The method effectively bridges the embodiment gap by reconstructing 4D hand-object interactions and then synthesizing robot-specific actions based on stable grasp principles and motion planning. This approach, combined with explicit subtask decomposition and scheduling, helps mitigate compounding errors inherent in video-to-robot data generation, leading to more robust and reliable robot trajectories compared to prior methods.

- **Handling Complex Manipulation Tasks:** DexImit demonstrates capabilities for handling a broad range of challenging tasks, including tool-using, long-horizon multi-step tasks, and fine-grained bimanual manipulations. This expands the complexity of behaviors that robots can learn from human demonstrations, moving beyond simple pick-and-place to more intricate real-world scenarios.

- **Enabling Zero-Shot Real-World Deployment:** The comprehensive data augmentation pipeline, particularly the nuanced approach to object scale and visual observation randomization, is crucial for improving the sim-to-real transferability of learned policies. The demonstrated zero-shot success rates on real robots without any real-world fine-tuning represent a significant step towards practical, deployable robotic systems.

- **Advancing Human-to-Robot Skill Transfer:** By enabling robots to learn complex skills directly from human videos, DexImit contributes to the broader goal of intuitive and efficient human-to-robot skill transfer, reducing the need for expert teleoperation or extensive manual programming.

Despite its strengths, the paper also identifies limitations, such as the current inability to handle soft or articulated objects, mobile manipulation, complex in-hand manipulation, and the potential for error propagation in long videos without manual intervention. Future work aiming to address these limitations, possibly through end-to-end data generation approaches or more advanced 3D generation models, could further enhance the framework's practicality and broaden its applicability.

Overall, DexImit represents a step towards making dexterous manipulation more accessible and capable by effectively leveraging the vast repository of human manipulation knowledge available in videos.
