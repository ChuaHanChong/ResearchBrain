# SM2ITH: Safe Mobile Manipulation with Interactive Human Prediction via Task-Hierarchical Bilevel Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The SM²ITH framework presents a unified control approach for mobile manipulators, integrating hierarchical task prioritization with interactive human motion prediction and safety guarantees for operations in dynamic, human-populated environments. It achieved enhanced performance on high-priority tasks and improved human-robot proxemics compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Coordinating multiple prioritized tasks for mobile manipulators is challenging, as traditional methods struggle to leverage kinematic redundancy or guarantee strict adherence to task importance.
- Ensuring safe and efficient navigation in dynamic, interactive human environments is difficult, since human motions are influenced by robot actions, and existing approaches often lead to overly conservative or deadlocked robot behaviors.
- Current robotic solutions typically address either task prioritization in static settings or interactive navigation without considering the full complexity of mobile manipulation, leaving a gap for unified control in human-centered spaces.

## Method

- The SM²ITH framework unifies Hierarchical Task ==Model Predictive Control== (HTMPC) with interactive human motion prediction via bilevel optimization.
- It employs bilevel optimization to embed the Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance (ORCA) problem as lower-level constraints, modeling closed-loop human predictions that adapt to the robot's planned movements.
- Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions (DT-CBFs) are integrated to enforce robust collision avoidance constraints for both humans and static obstacles, ensuring safety throughout the prediction horizon.

## Results

- SM²ITH demonstrated superior adherence to high-priority tasks in varying human densities, exhibiting significantly lower end-effector tracking errors in high-density human environments compared to weighted-sum baselines.
- The framework achieved a better balance between navigation efficiency and safe human-robot proxemics (e.g., reduced time in humans' intimate space) compared to open-loop (Constant Velocity Motion Model) and reactive human prediction baselines.
- In adversarial interaction scenarios, SM²ITH enabled the robot to adapt and continue towards its target by anticipating human reactions, unlike open-loop models which caused the robot to continuously retreat when blocked.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strict lexicographic task prioritization effectively manages conflicting objectives for mobile manipulators, leveraging kinematic redundancy to fulfill lower-priority tasks without compromising higher-priority ones, outperforming weighted-sum methods in managing task hierarchies.
> - Modeling reciprocal human-robot interaction through bilevel optimization allows the robot to anticipate and negotiate with human movements, leading to more fluid and predictable co-existence compared to open-loop or purely reactive human models.
> - The integrated framework provides a comprehensive solution for safe and efficient mobile manipulation, balancing task execution, kinematic feasibility, and guaranteed collision avoidance in dynamic human-populated environments.
