# SM2ITH: Safe Mobile Manipulation with Interactive Human Prediction via Task-Hierarchical Bilevel Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The SM²ITH framework presents a unified control approach for mobile manipulators, integrating hierarchical task prioritization with interactive human motion prediction and safety guarantees for operations in dynamic, human-populated environments. It achieved enhanced performance on high-priority tasks and improved human-robot proxemics compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Coordinating multiple prioritized tasks for mobile manipulators is challenging, as traditional methods struggle to leverage kinematic redundancy or guarantee strict adherence to task importance.
- Ensuring safe and efficient navigation in dynamic, interactive human environments is difficult, since human motions are influenced by robot actions, and existing approaches often lead to overly conservative or deadlocked robot behaviors.
- Current robotic solutions typically address either task prioritization in static settings or interactive navigation without considering the full complexity of mobile manipulation, leaving a gap for unified control in human-centered spaces.

## Method

- The SM²ITH framework unifies Hierarchical Task ==Model Predictive Control== (HTMPC) with interactive human motion prediction via bilevel optimization.
- It employs bilevel optimization to embed the Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance (ORCA) problem as lower-level constraints, modeling closed-loop human predictions that adapt to the robot's planned movements.
- Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions (DT-CBFs) are integrated to enforce robust collision avoidance constraints for both humans and static obstacles, ensuring safety throughout the prediction horizon.

## Results

- SM²ITH demonstrated superior adherence to high-priority tasks in varying human densities, exhibiting significantly lower end-effector tracking errors in high-density human environments compared to weighted-sum baselines.
- The framework achieved a better balance between navigation efficiency and safe human-robot proxemics (e.g., reduced time in humans' intimate space) compared to open-loop (Constant Velocity Motion Model) and reactive human prediction baselines.
- In adversarial interaction scenarios, SM²ITH enabled the robot to adapt and continue towards its target by anticipating human reactions, unlike open-loop models which caused the robot to continuously retreat when blocked.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strict lexicographic task prioritization effectively manages conflicting objectives for mobile manipulators, leveraging kinematic redundancy to fulfill lower-priority tasks without compromising higher-priority ones, outperforming weighted-sum methods in managing task hierarchies.
> - Modeling reciprocal human-robot interaction through bilevel optimization allows the robot to anticipate and negotiate with human movements, leading to more fluid and predictable co-existence compared to open-loop or purely reactive human models.
> - The integrated framework provides a comprehensive solution for safe and efficient mobile manipulation, balancing task execution, kinematic feasibility, and guaranteed collision avoidance in dynamic human-populated environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of mobile manipulators capable of operating in human-centered environments presents a multifaceted challenge, encompassing both complex task coordination and safe, efficient navigation among dynamic human agents. This work addresses existing limitations in current robotics research by integrating two distinct yet complementary areas: hierarchical task planning for mobile manipulation and interactive human motion prediction.

Previous research in mobile manipulation has focused on enabling robots to execute multiple tasks with varying priorities. Early approaches, such as decoupled base and arm planning, often resulted in slow execution and failed to exploit kinematic redundancy. Weighted-sum formulations allowed simultaneous task execution but required precise tuning of weights to manage conflicting objectives, often failing to enforce strict priorities. Hierarchical Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) advanced this field by introducing lexicographic optimization, allowing strict task priorities and efficient utilization of robot redundancy. However, these HTMPC applications have primarily been validated in static or structured environments, lacking consideration for human presence.

Concurrently, the field of human-robot interaction has focused on enabling robots to navigate safely among humans. Initial strategies treated humans as dynamic obstacles, using reactive avoidance mechanisms based on occupancy grids or geometric local planners. While these methods provided collision avoidance, they did not account for the mutual influence between robot and human motions. More advanced approaches incorporated open-loop human trajectory predictions, but these often led to overly conservative or "frozen" robot behaviors in dense crowds. A more recent direction involves modeling the interaction between humans and robots by integrating predictive human models into the robot controller, treating collision avoidance as a cost term or as constraints. However, these interactive formulations have largely been applied to mobile navigation tasks, without extending to the complexities of mobile manipulation, which involves coordinating both base and arm movements with varying priorities.

This research aims to bridge this gap by proposing SM²ITH, a framework that unifies the capabilities of HTMPC for prioritized mobile manipulation with interactive human motion prediction. By doing so, it extends the applicability of advanced task-hierarchical control to dynamic, human-populated environments, addressing the limitations of existing methods that either focused solely on task prioritization in static settings or on interactive navigation without considering the full mobile manipulation context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing demand for versatile service robots in human-centered environments. Such robots, exemplified by a hypothetical "robot waiter," are envisioned to perform complex sequences of navigation and manipulation tasks, such as retrieving dishes, transporting them across crowded spaces, and delivering them, all while ensuring safety and efficiency. These scenarios highlight two interconnected technical challenges that current robotic systems struggle to fully address:

1. **Seamless Coordination of Multiple Prioritized Tasks:** Mobile manipulators must coordinate a diverse set of tasks (e.g., precise end-effector positioning, stable object transport, base navigation) that often have different, sometimes conflicting, priorities. Traditional methods for task prioritization often fall short, either by executing tasks sequentially without leveraging robot redundancy or by using weighted objectives that require extensive tuning and may not guarantee strict adherence to priorities.
2. **Safe and Efficient Navigation in Dynamic, Interactive Human Environments:** Robots operating alongside humans must anticipate and react to human movements to avoid collisions. Crucially, human motions are not independent; they are influenced by the robot's actions, and vice-versa. Current approaches often either reactively avoid humans without prediction or use open-loop predictions, which can lead to conservative behaviors or deadlocks, failing to capture the reciprocal nature of human-robot interaction.

Against this backdrop, the key objective of this work is to develop a unified control framework, SM²ITH, that enables mobile manipulators to operate safely and efficiently in dynamic human-centered environments. Specifically, the framework aims to generate real-time robot actions that concurrently achieve the following:

- **Collision Avoidance:** Prevent collisions with both humans and static obstacles at all times.
- **Kinematic Feasibility:** Ensure robot motions remain within its kinodynamic limits.
- **Prioritized Task Execution:** Execute multiple tasks for both the base and end-effector with strict prioritization, while minimizing the violation of the associated task costs. This involves leveraging the robot's kinematic redundancy to fulfill lower-priority tasks without compromising higher-priority ones.
- **Interactive Human Prediction:** Jointly predict human trajectories and generate robot commands, explicitly modeling how humans react to the robot's actions within shared spaces. This closed-loop prediction is intended to enable more fluid and efficient human-robot co-existence.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to advance the capabilities of mobile manipulators to perform complex operations reliably and safely in dynamic, human-populated settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Safe Mobile Manipulation with Interactive Human Prediction via Task-Hierarchical Bilevel Model Predictive Control (SM²ITH) framework integrates several advanced control concepts to address the challenges of prioritized mobile manipulation and interactive human-robot navigation. The architecture (Figure 2) comprises three main blocks: a planning block, a human state estimation block, and the core SM²ITH motion generation block.

The **planning block** is responsible for defining the robot's tasks and organizing them into a strict priority hierarchy, subsequently generating the corresponding references for the robot to track. The **human state estimation block** detects humans in the environment and provides estimates of their positions and velocities, primarily through Kalman Filters, utilizing external motion capture systems in the presented work, though the framework is designed to be adaptable to onboard sensors.

The central contribution lies in the **SM²ITH motion generation block**, which computes robot control commands by accounting for future human trajectories. This block unifies Hierarchical Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) with interactive human motion prediction via bilevel optimization and incorporates Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for safety.

**A. Hierarchical Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) Extension:**
SM²ITH builds upon the HTMPC framework to handle a sequence of tasks ordered by strict priorities, denoted as [J1, J2..., JL]. At each control step, the controller computes optimal state and input trajectories over a finite prediction horizon (N) by solving a lexicographic optimization problem. This problem minimizes a series of task costs (Jℓ) subject to robot dynamics, state and input limits, initial conditions, and self-collision avoidance constraints. To enforce the strict hierarchy, the problem is decomposed into a sequence of Single Task Model Predictive Control (STMPC) problems. The highest priority task is optimized first. Subsequent STMPC problems for lower priority tasks are then solved, with an additional constraint that ensures the tracking error of already-optimized higher priority tasks does not increase. This approach effectively leverages the mobile manipulator's kinematic redundancy to achieve multiple objectives according to their defined priorities.

**B. Interactive Human Motion Prediction via Bilevel Optimization (SICNav Integration):**
To incorporate interactive human motion prediction, SM²ITH adopts and extends the Safe and Interactive Crowd Navigation (SICNav) framework. This involves embedding the Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance (ORCA) problem as a set of lower-level constraints within the robot's HTMPC formulation, thereby creating a bilevel optimization problem.
In this formulation, the upper-level problem optimizes the robot's trajectory (state `x(r)` and input `u`) and its task costs, while the lower-level ORCA constraints define the predicted human actions `a(j)` (velocities for each human `j`). These human actions are derived from an optimization problem (Equation 4) that minimizes the deviation from a desired velocity, subject to maximum velocity, acceleration limits, and collision avoidance constraints with other humans, the robot, and static obstacles. Slack variables are introduced to guarantee feasibility, ensuring a solution always exists. By formulating this as a bilevel problem, the robot's planning (`u`) and the human predictions (`a(j)`) are inherently coupled; the human predictions are closed-loop, reflecting how humans are assumed to react to the robot's planned movements. The bilevel optimization problem is then converted into a single-level optimization problem by incorporating the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions of the lower-level ORCA problems as additional constraints (Equations 5h-5k).

**C. Safety Assurance with Control Barrier Functions (CBFs):**
To ensure robust collision avoidance for both humans and static obstacles, Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions (DT-CBFs) are integrated into the SM²ITH formulation as constraint (5f). A safe set `C` is defined as the superlevel set of a function `h(x) ≥ 0`, where `h(x)` represents the minimum signed distance to obstacles or humans. The CBF constraint `h(x_{k+1}) ≥ (1 - γ)h(x_k)` ensures that if the system starts in a safe state, it will remain in a safe state throughout the prediction horizon (forward invariance). The hyperparameter `γ ∈ [0, 1)` controls the rate at which the system can approach the boundary of the safe set, allowing adjustment of the robot's cautiousness. For human-robot and human-obstacle collision avoidance, humans are modeled as cylinders, and `h(x)` concatenates individual CBFs for collision avoidance between the robot's base and arm links with each human and static obstacle. This provides an additional layer of safety by preventing the robot from entering unsafe states.

The overall SM²ITH optimization problem (Equation 5) combines the lexicographic minimization of task costs with the robot's dynamic and kinematic constraints, the KKT conditions for interactive human motion prediction, and DT-CBF constraints for safety. The implementation utilizes the `acados` library in Python for solving the Model Predictive Control (MPC) problem. The framework was evaluated on two mobile manipulator platforms: the Hello-Robot Stretch 3 (7-DOF, nonholonomic base) and the Ridgeback-UR10 (9-DOF, holonomic base).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SM²ITH framework was evaluated through three sets of experiments on two mobile manipulator platforms: the Hello-Robot Stretch 3 and the Ridgeback-UR10.

**A. Shaping Robot Behavior Through Task Prioritization (Stretch 3):**
This set of experiments assessed SM²ITH's ability to manage simultaneous tasks with strict priorities compared to a weighted-sum MPC baseline. The robot performed a base navigation task and an end-effector (EE) positioning task while avoiding 1, 2, or 3 humans. Two priority scenarios were tested:
- **EE Task Higher Priority (E≥B - "Banner Carrying"):** Robot prioritizes holding its arm extended over base navigation.
- **Base Task Higher Priority (B≥E - "Cup Carrying"):** Robot prioritizes base navigation over keeping its arm extended.
Results (summarized in Figure 3) indicated that SM²ITH's strict lexicographic prioritization provided performance advantages. In the "Banner" scenario, SM²ITH achieved comparable average EE tracking errors at lower human densities (1-2 humans) but exhibited significantly lower average and maximum EE errors at high human density (3 humans) compared to the weighted-sum baseline. In the "Cup" scenario, SM²ITH demonstrated greater efficiency in terms of both average base error and navigation time. The weighted-sum method performed less effectively on the high-priority task, particularly as human density increased, due to the challenges in consistently tuning weights to approximate strict task hierarchies across varying scenarios.

**B. Human Motion Prediction in Pick-and-Place Tasks (Stretch 3 & Ridgeback-UR10):**
This experiment evaluated the impact of different human motion prediction models on the robot's behavior during sequential pick-and-place tasks involving 1 or 2 crossing humans. SM²ITH was compared against a Constant Velocity Motion Model (CVMM) baseline (open-loop prediction) and a Reactive baseline (no prediction).
Quantitative results (Figure 6) showed:
- **Navigation Efficiency:** CVMM consistently led to the largest deviations from the optimal path and longer total runtimes on both platforms. The Reactive approach remained closer to the optimal path but frequently compromised safety. SM²ITH demonstrated a balance between efficiency and safety, adapting predictions to minimize detours.
- **Human-Robot Proxemics (Ridgeback-UR10):** The Reactive method resulted in longer durations spent in humans' intimate space (defined as a 0.45m radius). CVMM reduced this intimate space duration but at the cost of degraded navigation efficiency. SM²ITH effectively balanced these metrics, enabling safer proxemics without excessive detours.
- **Human-Robot Proxemics (Stretch 3):** Differences in proxemics metrics were less pronounced for the Stretch 3. This was attributed to the Stretch 3's lower maximum speed (0.3m/s) and nonholonomic base, which required more complex maneuvers and led to increased close interactions irrespective of the prediction model, unlike the more agile Ridgeback-UR10 with its holonomic base and greater redundancy.
The framework also successfully generalized to randomized pick-and-place targets on both robots, demonstrating its robustness in varied scenarios.

**C. Adversarial Human Interaction (Stretch 3):**
A qualitative experiment was conducted to assess SM²ITH's behavior in an adversarial scenario where a human purposefully moved to block the robot. Compared to the CVMM baseline (Figure 7), SM²ITH's interactive prediction allowed the robot to reason about coupled human-robot trajectories. When the human approached, the CVMM model, extrapolating the human's straight-line motion, caused the robot to continuously retreat. In contrast, SM²ITH anticipated that the human could adapt to the robot's presence, leading to a joint plan where both agents proceeded while avoiding each other, allowing the robot to continue towards its target. This demonstrated the benefit of interactive prediction in negotiating complex, dynamic human-robot encounters.

In summary, the experimental results indicate that SM²ITH effectively combines strict task prioritization with interactive human motion prediction. It generally achieved better performance on high-priority tasks than weighted-sum approaches, particularly in denser human environments, and facilitated a safer and more efficient balance between navigation and human-robot proxemics compared to open-loop or purely reactive human models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SM²ITH framework contributes to the field of mobile manipulation by addressing the recognized challenge of operating robotic systems safely and efficiently in dynamic, human-centered environments. By integrating Hierarchical Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) with interactive human motion prediction, this work provides a unified solution for managing complex multi-task execution alongside human presence.

The significance of this research lies in several key aspects:
- **Unified Framework for Complex Scenarios:** It offers a comprehensive approach that simultaneously considers strict task prioritization, kinematic redundancy, interactive human dynamics, and safety constraints. This integration moves beyond solutions that address these challenges in isolation, providing a more holistic and practical framework for real-world applications.
- **Enhanced Safety and Efficiency:** The use of bilevel optimization for interactive human prediction, coupled with Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions, allows robots to anticipate human reactions and guarantee safety. This approach reduces the likelihood of overly conservative or deadlock behaviors often observed in systems relying on open-loop human models or purely reactive strategies. The ability to balance task efficiency with human-robot proxemics is crucial for effective deployment in shared spaces.
- **Context-Dependent Robot Behavior:** The strict lexicographic task hierarchy enables the robot to exhibit context-dependent behaviors. As demonstrated in the task prioritization experiments, the robot can prioritize different objectives (e.g., maintaining end-effector position versus base navigation) based on the current task, a capability that is difficult to achieve consistently with weighted-sum methods. This flexibility is vital for robots to adapt to diverse service roles and human expectations.
- **Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction:** The framework's capacity to model and account for the reciprocal nature of human-robot interaction, as evidenced in the adversarial interaction experiments, represents an advancement over non-interactive prediction methods. This allows for more fluid and predictable co-existence, fostering greater acceptance and utility of robots in human environments.

The potential impact of SM²ITH is substantial for the deployment of mobile manipulators in various service industries and public spaces. Robots equipped with such capabilities could perform tasks like delivery, assistance, or logistics in crowded environments (e.g., hospitals, warehouses, retail spaces) with increased reliability and safety. By enabling robots to adapt to and even anticipate human behavior, the framework could lead to a new generation of service robots that are more collaborative, less disruptive, and more integrated into human daily life.

Future work identified by the authors includes incorporating active perception using onboard sensors, such as 6D pose estimation of human bodies, to enhance anatomical modeling and refine collision avoidance constraints. Additionally, leveraging more advanced models for complex social behaviors, including group dynamics, human intent, or learning-based generative models for motion prediction, could further enrich the robot's interactive capabilities. These extensions would contribute to making robotic systems even more sophisticated and robust in navigating the complexities of human-populated environments.
