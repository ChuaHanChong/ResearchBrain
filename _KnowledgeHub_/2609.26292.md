# RoboTwin-Phys: Do WAMs and VLAs Understand the Physical World?

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboTwin-Phys is a physics-diverse manipulation benchmark built on RoboTwin-2.0's 50 bimanual tasks that continuously samples 13 physical attributes (mass, friction, center of mass, joint damping, table tilt, camera pose) per episode within task-calibrated plausible ranges, verified feasible by an expert planner. It ships over 5,000 expert demonstrations with ground-truth physical parameters. Five representative WAMs and VLAs drop from roughly 87-93% to 32-44% success under physical variation.

## Problem

- Existing manipulation benchmarks randomize appearance, layout, pose, lighting and viewpoint, but hold underlying physical parameters fixed at nominal values.
- No standardized protocol measures how performance shifts as mass, friction, mass distribution or joint damping vary, so physical robustness stays unquantified.
- Prior physical-factor studies use isolated factors and small ablations, and lack per-sample physical ground truth needed to train or audit condition-aware modules.

## Method

- **Physical-condition sampling**: 13 attributes drawn independently at episode initialization from continuous ranges, held fixed through the rollout, seed-reproducible, and independent of visual and layout randomization.
- **Task-aware physical validity**: nine physics-sensitive tasks routed to dedicated empirically calibrated domains (narrowed ==center-of-mass offsets==, restricted ==joint damping==, halved camera magnitudes) instead of the global configuration.
- **Feasibility filtering** plus **physics-annotated training set**: expert planner pre-verifies each sampled condition, and retained episodes ship as RoboTwin-format HDF5 trajectories with per-episode 13-dimensional physical metadata.

## Results

- Success collapses under Physical Random: Fast-WAM **44.24%**, Motus **39.60%**, FACT **39.14%**, Galaxea-VLA **37.82%**, pi-0.5 **31.60%**, versus **82.74-93.70%** on the clean setting.
- Visual and layout randomization costs little by comparison: **0.10 points** for Fast-WAM, **1.64** for Motus, **1.80** for FACT, **0.90** for Galaxea-VLA, **5.98** for pi-0.5.
- Task-level failures are structured, not random: beat_block_hammer, pick_diverse_bottles, move_stapler_pad and put_bottles_dustbin stay near **8-25%** across models, while grab_roller and shake_bottle hold **65-91%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robustness to visual and layout randomization does not transfer to physical-condition variation; the two are distinct evaluation axes that must be measured separately.
> - Degradation is consistent across WAM and VLA architectures, indicating a shared blind spot in current training paradigms rather than a model-specific design flaw.
> - Physical ranges must be calibrated per task; a global domain can place a center of mass outside a slender object, testing artificial failure instead of genuine diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A grasp can fail even when the scene looks familiar, because the object's physical behavior has changed. Robot-manipulation benchmarks often vary what a robot sees, but they do not systematically vary the physical properties that govern contact and motion. In real settings, changes in mass, friction, center of mass, or joint dynamics can change whether an otherwise familiar manipulation succeeds. RoboTwin-Phys varies these properties as well as the scene's appearance. Under this physically randomized setting, the authors report pronounced degradation for all five tested models.

Author illustration of changed physical behavior, not a model result: an off-center center of mass makes a rolling pin swing down mid-grasp. Source: [RoboTwin-Phys project page](https://yefeng00.github.io/RoboTwinPhysTest/).

### 2. What the benchmark changes

RoboTwin-Phys builds on [[2506.18088|RoboTwin 2.0]], whose paper describes domain randomization across clutter, backgrounds, lighting, tabletop height, and language instructions. The new benchmark adds continuously sampled physical conditions to RoboTwin 2.0's existing 50-task bimanual suite. It also annotates demonstrations with the physical parameters in effect. Tabletop height already appears among RoboTwin 2.0's axes, so the contribution is an explicit, task-aware evaluation dimension for physical conditions. It is not the first variation of every parameter that could affect interaction.

The 13 attributes fall into five groups:

- **Object properties:** mass, center of mass, geometry scale.
- **Contact properties:** friction, restitution.
- **Damping properties:** object, arm, and gripper damping.
- **Task environment:** table tilt, table height, external force.
- **Camera configuration:** distance, angle.

Each attribute is sampled independently from a continuous range at episode initialization. It stays fixed during the rollout and is reproducible from the episode seed. Physical sampling can be studied alone or together with visual and layout variation.

Taxonomy of benchmark factors: object, contact, and damping properties, task environment, and camera configuration.

The benchmark also screens out infeasible test cases. Nine physics-sensitive tasks use dedicated calibrated configurations, and the others use the global configuration. For example, the global center-of-mass range could put a slender hammer's center outside its body. The expert planner is run in advance, and instances it cannot complete are excluded.

The companion training set contains more than 5,000 expert episodes. Each rollout carries its 13-dimensional ground-truth physical attributes. These labels could support physical-attribute estimation or condition-aware policy training, but the paper does not evaluate those uses. The [GitHub repository](https://github.com/yefeng00/RoboTwin-Phys) lists the dataset download link as "TBD."

### 3. What the results show, and what they leave open

The evaluated models come from two families: World-Action Models (WAMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The metric is aggregate success rate across the 50-task suite, and each Physical Random task is evaluated with 100 rollouts. The two settings differ as follows:

- **Official Random** enables RoboTwin 2.0's original visual and layout randomization and keeps physical parameters nominal.
- **Physical Random** retains that randomization and additionally samples the 13 physical attributes.

Fast-WAM is reported at **91.78%** under Official Random and **44.24%** under Physical Random. This is a comparison across settings, not a same-run, physics-only causal estimate. The Official Random scores come from the corresponding public benchmark evaluations, while the authors evaluated Physical Random themselves. The authors report the same effect across all five models, spanning both WAM and VLA families.

Results also vary by task. Some tasks remain difficult across models, while others are relatively robust. The authors suggest that behaviors depending on contact dynamics, object stability, mass distribution, or articulated motion may be especially stressed. The paper does not isolate which physical factor caused a particular task outcome, and it reports no factor-by-factor ablations.

### 4. Takeaway

A scene that looks the same can produce different contact and motion, so visual randomization alone can miss a robustness gap. RoboTwin-Phys provides a repeatable, task-feasible, labeled way to test physical-condition diversity. The paper measures task success. It does not show whether models can perceive, diagnose, or adapt to physical changes.
