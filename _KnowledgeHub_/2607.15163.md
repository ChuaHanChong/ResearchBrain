# Scaling Behavior Foundation Model for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Lab and collaborating institutions systematically investigated the scaling of Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) for humanoid robots, developing a principled recipe that integrates global-frame motion tracking, a balance of data quantity and diversity, and a Humanoid Transformer architecture. This approach led to a reduction in Mean Per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE) on the test set by over 10% in local mode and 82% in global mode compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots require effective whole-body control to achieve natural, responsive, and robust behaviors across a variety of tasks and environments.
- Prior humanoid whole-body control systems are often task-specific, relying on extensive, individualized reward engineering, which limits their adaptability and generalization.
- A systematic understanding of how to coordinate key factors like the learning paradigm, behavioral data, and model architecture for effective scaling of Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) was lacking.

## Method

- Reformulated diverse humanoid control problems as ==whole-body motion tracking== in the ==global frame==, providing dense guidance and mitigating behavioral ambiguity.
- Established a strategic synergy between the quantity of ==on-policy rollouts== and the diversity of ==reference motions== to enhance scalable BFM pretraining.
- Developed the **Humanoid Transformer**, an expressive and scalable model architecture, designed to facilitate the natural emergence of structured behavioral representations.

## Results

- The proposed BFM consistently achieved higher success rates and lower tracking errors compared to existing whole-body controllers (GMT, TWIST, SONIC) and ablation baselines.
- The method demonstrated a reduction in **Mean Per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE)** on the test set by over **10%** in local control mode and **82%** in global control mode against existing controllers.
- A medium-sized Humanoid Transformer (**3.00M parameters**) exhibited performance comparable to or superior to a substantially larger MLP (**11.86M parameters**), indicating more effective utilization of model capacity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective scaling of on-policy data collection for PPO optimization necessitates a balanced increase in both the width (parallel environments) and depth (rollout horizon) of environmental interaction.
> - The utility of scaling reference motions is critically dependent on expanding behavioral coverage through diverse data sources, rather than merely increasing the quantity of homogeneous data.
> - The Humanoid Transformer architecture naturally induces structured and robust latent representations of behavioral intentions within its hypersphere-normalized latent space, without requiring auxiliary objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are recognized for their potential in human-centric environments due to their human-like morphology and rich actuation capabilities. Realizing this potential necessitates effective whole-body control that can manage numerous degrees of freedom to produce natural, responsive, and robust behaviors across a variety of tasks and environmental conditions. Prior research has demonstrated progress in humanoid whole-body control, enabling diverse motor skills, but these systems often remain task-specific, relying on extensive, individualized reward engineering. This task-specificity limits their adaptability and generalization.

To address these limitations, Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) have emerged as a paradigm for general-purpose humanoid control. BFMs aim to leverage large-scale behavioral data to learn robust and versatile control policies that can respond to diverse behavior specifications. This approach shifts from learning isolated tasks to learning holistic behaviors. Existing BFM research, such as BFM4Humanoid, BeyondMimic, BFM-Zero, and SONIC, has explored various pretraining strategies and control mechanisms. While initial steps have been taken to scale BFMs, a systematic understanding of how to coordinate key factors like the learning paradigm, behavioral data, and model architecture for effective scaling has remained largely unexplored. This paper contributes to the field by providing a systematic investigation into these factors, aiming to establish a principled scaling recipe for BFMs to enhance their capabilities. It seeks to bridge the gap between initial explorations of BFM scaling and concrete design principles for achieving substantial performance gains and broader generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the scaling behavior of Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) for humanoid robots and to develop a principled scaling recipe. The authors identify that, despite growing interest in scaling BFMs to improve capabilities, it has been unclear how key factors—the learning paradigm, behavioral data, and model architecture—should be coordinated for effective scaling.

The motivation stems from the recognition that humanoid control requires:
1. **Natural Whole-Body Coordination:** The robot's many degrees of freedom must be coordinated to produce fluid, human-like movements.
2. **Precise Real-Time Responses:** The controller must react accurately and promptly to control signals.
3. **Robust Generalization:** The robot should be able to perform effectively across diverse environmental contexts and tasks without extensive re-engineering.

To address these challenges, the authors propose revisiting current BFM pretraining pipelines to coordinate three core components:
1. **Learning Paradigm:** Reformulating diverse humanoid control problems as motion tracking, specifically the reproduction of integrated whole-body behaviors in the global frame. This approach aims to provide dense whole-body guidance, mitigate behavioral ambiguity caused by ignoring root translation, and avoid decoupling global root evolution from local pose tracking. This formulation is also designed to preserve flexibility for both global and local control modes.
2. **Behavioral Data:** Establishing a strategic synergy between the quantity of on-policy rollouts and the diversity of reference motions. The motivation here is to move beyond simply increasing the number of reference motions to considering how the *amount* of on-policy experience (governed by parallel environments and rollout horizon) interacts with the *breadth* of behavioral patterns in the reference motion corpus.
3. **Model Architecture:** Developing an expressive and scalable model architecture, termed the Humanoid Transformer, that facilitates the natural emergence of structured behavioral representations. The goal is to investigate whether more expressive architectures exhibit stronger scaling behavior compared to widely used MLPs, and whether structured latent representations can emerge without reliance on auxiliary objectives or human priors. Such representations could support flexible utilization and efficient transfer of learned behavioral knowledge.

Overall, the research aims to achieve significant improvements in control fidelity, execution robustness, and generalization capability, positioning BFMs as a foundation for scalable and general-purpose humanoid control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a systematic investigation of BFM scaling through the coordination of a revised learning paradigm, enhanced training data management, and a new model architecture.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Humanoid control is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The state `s_t` includes a proprioceptive state `s_p_t` and a goal state `s_g_t`. Actions `a_t` represent desired joint angles executed by a low-level PD controller. Humanoid behavior `B` is defined as a trajectory of proprioceptive states and actions. BFM pretraining is cast as a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL) problem, maximizing expected return. To create a unified learning objective, whole-body motion tracking is selected as a proxy task, where the reward function evaluates fidelity to reference motions. This differs from general motion trackers in that BFMs use motion tracking for *behavior learning* rather than as the sole control objective, allowing for diverse behavioral specifications. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for optimization.

#### 3.2. Learning Paradigm

The motion tracking recipe for BFM pretraining includes:
- **Proprioceptive State Design:** An asymmetric actor-critic design is employed. The actor's state `s_p_t` includes base angular velocity, projected gravity, current joint positions, and velocities. The critic's privileged state `ˆs_p_t` includes root height, link positions/orientations, and linear/angular velocities, all in the humanoid's heading frame.
- **Control Interface Design:** A flexible control interface uses masked whole-body target poses in root-relative Cartesian space. `x_t` represents target link poses and their deviations from current poses in the humanoid root frame. The actor's goal state `s_g_t` is `(x_t ⊙ m_i, m_i)`, where `m_i` is a randomly sampled link-wise mask from eight predefined control modes (e.g., Root Mode, Whole-Body Mode) to enable versatile control and behavior inpainting. The critic receives complete, unmasked target information for accurate value estimation.
- **Reward Design:** The reward is a weighted sum of tracking rewards (global root height, body position, rotation, linear velocity, angular velocity), penalty terms (action rate, joint position limits), and a survival reward. Critically, the formulation requires the robot to reproduce reference motions as *integrated whole-body trajectories in the global frame*, which is distinct from prior work that might omit or decouple root-position tracking. This aims to provide coherent guidance and reduce ambiguity.
- **Domain Randomization:** To enhance robustness and generalization, physical parameters (static/dynamic friction, default joint positions, base COM offset, hand mass) are randomized at the start of training. Periodic random perturbations are applied to root velocities to simulate external forces.

#### 3.3. Training Data

The authors differentiate between reference motions and effective training data in PPO (on-policy rollouts). Scalable BFM pretraining necessitates a synergy of data quantity (on-policy rollouts) and diversity (reference motions).
- **Human Motion Collection:** A large-scale human motion collection of over 102 million frames at 50 FPS is curated from various open-source datasets (LAFAN, AMASS, OMOMO, GRAB, SnapMoGen, FineDance, BONES-SEED, Embody3D) to ensure broad behavioral coverage.
- **Motion Retargeting:** A two-stage pipeline is used. First, human and humanoid skeletons are aligned by optimizing human shape coefficients (for SMPL) or local skeletal offsets (for BVH). Second, human motions are retargeted to the humanoid sequentially using inverse kinematics to minimize weighted pose discrepancy.
- **Early Termination and Reference State Initialization (RSI):** Episodes terminate if any humanoid link deviates by more than 0.5m from the reference. Upon termination, RSI reinitializes the humanoid to the reference state *next to the termination point*, bringing it back to the desired trajectory manifold.
- **Adaptive Sampling:** A sampling weight `w_t` is assigned to each motion sequence. Periodically, the model is evaluated; if a trajectory fails, its weight increases (`w_t-1 / β^T_eval`); if it succeeds, its weight decreases (`w_t-1 * β^T_eval`). Weights are clamped and normalized, biasing training towards challenging motions while maintaining coverage.

#### 3.4. Model Architecture

The proposed **Humanoid Transformer** is designed for expressiveness and scalability:
- **Temporal Windows:** Proprioceptive states, goal states, and actions are extended into finite temporal windows to provide richer temporal information.
- **Tokenization:** Modality-specific tokenizers encode proprioceptive states and actions. These are interleaved to form a context sequence.
- **Query Token:** A learnable query token is appended for action or value prediction, allowing it to attend to the entire context while preventing context tokens from attending to it.
- **Goal Conditioning:** Goal states, with temporal offsets, are tokenized and injected via cross-attention, providing a flexible conditioning mechanism.
- **Latent Space:** RMSNorm is employed to normalize goal embeddings onto a continuous and bounded hypersphere, naturally inducing a structured latent representation space for behavioral intentions without auxiliary regularization.
- **Future Window (Actor/Critic):** The actor's future window includes the next five frames plus one randomly sampled from for robustness to real-time delays. The critic uses fixed exponentially spaced indices {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32} for fine-grained short-term dynamics and long-horizon outcomes.
- **Implementation Details:** Single linear layers for tokenizers and projection head, SwiGLU for feed-forward modules.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the scaling behavior of BFMs from data, architecture, and latent space perspectives, along with benchmarking results.

#### 4.1. Scaling with Training Data

- **Scaling On-policy Data Collection:** Experiments demonstrated that jointly scaling the width (number of parallel GPUs) and depth (rollout horizon) of environmental interaction substantially improved performance across diverse control modes. The largest configuration (64 GPUs and a rollout horizon of 64) consistently achieved the best performance. This is attributed to more reliable estimates for PPO optimization due to increased on-policy experience. However, scaling either dimension in isolation did not always yield consistent improvements, suggesting that an appropriate balance between width and depth is necessary for effective scaling.
- **Scaling Reference Motions:** The study categorized reference motion scaling into two regimes based on behavioral coverage analysis (occupancy rate of clusters in feature space):
 - **Homogeneous Scaling (XXS to S):** Increased the amount of reference motions from the same domain (BONES-SEED) without a significant expansion in measured behavioral coverage. This yielded only marginal improvements on both BONES and Ours Test Sets.
 - **Heterogeneous Scaling (S to L):** Increased both the amount of reference motions and significantly broadened measured behavioral coverage by incorporating diverse datasets (e.g., Embody3D). This regime produced little additional benefit on the BONES Test Set (in-domain) but led to substantial performance gains on the Ours Test Set (novel data sources).
 These findings indicate that the effectiveness of scaling reference motions depends not just on quantity, but critically on whether the scaling expands behavioral coverage and if the new behaviors are relevant to the evaluation benchmark. For domain-specific improvements, fine-grained coverage of domain-specific behaviors is suggested over mere quantity increase. For general capabilities, incorporating diverse complementary data sources is proposed.

#### 4.2. Scaling with Model Architecture

- The Humanoid Transformer architecture generally achieved higher success rates and lower tracking errors compared to the MLP architecture across experiments.
- A medium-sized Humanoid Transformer (3.00M parameters) demonstrated performance comparable to or superior to a substantially larger MLP (11.86M parameters), suggesting more effective utilization of model capacity.
- Further increasing the Humanoid Transformer's size (from M to L to XL) resulted in diminishing returns. Some control modes continued to benefit, while others exhibited early saturation, potentially due to an imbalance in learning difficulty between modes, leading to optimization trade-offs within the shared latent space.

#### 4.3. Latent Analysis

- **Structured Representations:** The research demonstrated that structured latent representations of behavioral intentions emerged naturally within the Humanoid Transformer's hypersphere-normalized latent space, without requiring auxiliary objectives.
 - **Locality and Global Organization:** Visualizations showed that temporally close commands mapped to nearby latent representations, forming continuous trajectories. Distinct behavioral intentions occupied distinguishable regions, preserving underlying directional relationships (e.g., walk forward/backward on opposite sides).
 - **Robustness:** Quantitative evaluation showed the learned latent space to be robust, maintaining strong performance even with significant perturbations applied to latent representations.
- **Impact of Scaling on Latent Space:** Increasing model capacity led to a convergence of latent representations across different control modes. This observation may contribute to explaining the diminishing returns and trade-offs seen when scaling model architecture, suggesting a strong coupling among control modes within the shared latent space.

#### 4.4. Model Performance Benchmarking

- The proposed BFM (3M-parameter variant) was benchmarked against existing whole-body controllers (GMT, TWIST, SONIC) and an ablation baseline (BFM-Bym, using BeyondMimic's reward design).
- The proposed BFM consistently achieved higher success rates and lower tracking errors across both local and global control modes, outperforming all competing methods.
- The proposed reward formulation, which emphasizes tracking integrated whole-body trajectories in the global frame, consistently yielded lower global tracking errors compared to the BFM-Bym ablation, indicating its efficacy in providing coherent behavioral guidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a systematic investigation into the scaling behavior of Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) for humanoid robots, deriving a scaling recipe that coordinates the learning paradigm, behavioral data, and model architecture. The demonstrated improvements in control fidelity, execution robustness, and generalization capability are substantial, with reductions in Mean Per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE) on the test set exceeding 10% in local mode and 82% in global mode compared to existing controllers.

The establishment of motion tracking as a unified and scalable learning paradigm, coupled with strategic data management that emphasizes both the quantity of on-policy rollouts and the diversity of reference motions, provides practical guidance for future BFM development. The introduction of the Humanoid Transformer architecture, which demonstrates superior performance and facilitates the natural emergence of structured and robust latent behavioral representations, contributes to the architectural design of future humanoid controllers. The analysis of latent space, revealing its locality, global organization, and robustness, offers insights into how BFMs learn and represent complex behaviors.

The findings contribute to the realization of general-purpose humanoid control, moving beyond task-specific solutions towards models that can generalize across diverse scenarios and specifications. The ability of the BFM to perform effectively in both simulation and real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 robot underscores its practical relevance.

However, the authors also acknowledge limitations, including the ongoing question of the most appropriate abstraction for the control interface and how it should integrate with future high-level policies. The current infrastructure for BFM pretraining is noted as preliminary, facing constraints in scalability due to factors such as multi-GPU communication, simulator stability, and computational limits for real-time onboard inference. Addressing these limitations will require coordinated efforts from the broader research community. By open-sourcing resources, this work aims to provide a foundation for systematic development, fostering collective progress toward advanced humanoid intelligence.
