# 3D Diffuser Actor: Policy Diffusion with 3D Scene Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Carnegie Mellon University researchers introduce 3D Diffuser Actor, a novel robot manipulation policy that integrates diffusion models with tokenized 3D scene representations. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like RLBench and CALVIN, exhibiting strong generalization and learning capabilities from limited real-world demonstrations.

## Problem

- Traditional robot policies often struggle with the inherent multimodality of actions, where multiple valid action sequences can achieve a task goal.
- Policies trained on 2D image inputs exhibit limited generalization to novel camera viewpoints or scene configurations due to implicit learning of 2D-to-3D mappings.
- Developing robot manipulation policies that are both highly capable and data-efficient for learning from limited human demonstrations.

## Method

- A novel 3D denoising transformer is employed as a conditional diffusion probabilistic model for robot end-effector pose trajectories.
- Visual observations are lifted into tokenized 3D scene representations using sensed depth, which are then fused with language instructions and robot proprioception.
- The architecture incorporates relative 3D attentions using rotary positional embeddings to ensure translation equivariance and enhance spatial reasoning among all 3D tokens (scene, proprioception, trajectory).

## Results

- Achieved an **81.3%** average success rate on RLBench multi-view tasks, an absolute improvement of **+18.1%** over the previous state-of-the-art Act3D.
- Attained the highest average completed sequential tasks (**3.35** Avg. Len) on the CALVIN zero-shot long-horizon benchmark, surpassing GR-1 by a **9%** relative increase.
- Demonstrated practical applicability by learning multi-task manipulation in the real world from just 15 demonstrations per task, achieving high success rates on various scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D scene representations are crucial for robust generalization in robot manipulation, significantly outperforming 2D-based policies.
> - Using relative 3D attentions within a transformer architecture provides translation equivariance, which is important for generalization across varying viewpoints and scene configurations.
> - Diffusion models effectively capture the multimodality of robot actions, leading to more robust and flexible policies that can handle diverse task executions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself at the intersection of two rapidly evolving and impactful areas within robotics and machine learning: **diffusion models for policy learning** and **3D scene representations for robot control**. The authors argue that while both fields have shown significant progress independently, their unification holds the key to overcoming existing limitations in robot manipulation from demonstrations.

Historically, robot policy learning from demonstrations has often relied on deterministic policies (e.g., behavior cloning), which struggle with the inherent multimodality of robot actions—meaning, there can be multiple valid ways to complete a task. To address this, recent research has explored probabilistic models like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Mixture of Gaussians, and Energy-Based Models (EBMs). Diffusion models have emerged as a particularly powerful class of generative models for learning complex data distributions, and their application to modeling state-conditioned action distributions (diffusion policies) has recently demonstrated superior performance in mode coverage and action fidelity. Previous diffusion policies, such as Diffusion Policy, typically utilize low-dimensional or 2D image-based representations of the scene.

Concurrently, another line of research has focused on the importance of 3D scene representations for robot policies. Unlike 2D policies that implicitly learn the 2D-to-3D mapping, 3D policies (e.g., PerAct, Act3D, RVT) explicitly "lift" visual features from camera views into a 3D robot workspace, often leveraging sensed depth information. This approach has shown improved generalization across camera viewpoints and robustness to novel camera positions, as the robot's actions and visual scene elements are inherently represented in a common 3D space.

The "3D Diffuser Actor" paper proposes to unify these two successful paradigms. It aims to harness the expressive power of diffusion models for learning multimodal action distributions while simultaneously benefiting from the strong spatial reasoning and generalization capabilities offered by 3D scene representations. The paper explicitly states that "3D robot policies have not yet been combined with diffusion objectives," highlighting a gap in the literature that this work seeks to fill. While acknowledging concurrent work like "3D Diffusion Policy", the authors differentiate their approach by emphasizing the use of a *tokenized* 3D scene representation (similar to Act3D), arguing it offers better robustness to scene changes compared to holistic 1D embeddings used by alternatives. This distinction underlines the paper's contribution beyond a mere combination, focusing on *how* the 3D representation is processed for optimal performance within a diffusion framework. The work represents a significant step towards developing more robust, versatile, and data-efficient robot manipulation policies for complex tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the "3D Diffuser Actor" paper is to develop a novel robot manipulation policy that achieves state-of-the-art performance by effectively integrating the strengths of **diffusion models** and **3D scene representations**.

The primary motivations behind this unification are:

1. **Handling Action Multimodality:** Many robot manipulation tasks are inherently multimodal, meaning there are multiple valid sequences of actions that can achieve the desired goal. Traditional deterministic policies struggle to capture this diversity, often leading to brittle behavior. Learning the entire distribution of possible actions, rather than just a single deterministic outcome, is crucial. Diffusion models have recently proven exceptionally effective at learning complex, multimodal distributions, outperforming other generative approaches in policy learning. The objective is to leverage this capability to enable more robust and flexible robot behavior.

2. **Enhancing Generalization and Spatial Reasoning:** Policies trained on 2D image inputs often struggle to generalize across varying camera viewpoints or to novel scene configurations because they must implicitly learn the 2D-to-3D geometric mapping. 3D robot policies, which explicitly lift visual features into a 3D workspace, offer superior generalization by representing both the scene and robot actions in a common, viewpoint-invariant 3D space. The motivation here is to harness these inherent geometric advantages to improve policy robustness and transferability.

3. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance in Complex Tasks:** The authors observe that while both diffusion policies and 3D policies have individually advanced the field, their combination has not been thoroughly explored. The key objective is to demonstrate that a policy that marries these two paradigms can significantly surpass the performance of existing methods on challenging, multi-task, and long-horizon benchmarks, bridging the gap between high-fidelity action prediction and robust spatial understanding.

4. **Learning from Limited Demonstrations:** A practical motivation is to develop a policy that can learn complex manipulation skills from a relatively small number of human demonstrations, even those containing natural noise and diverse execution styles. This addresses a significant bottleneck in real-world robot deployment.

In essence, the "3D Diffuser Actor" aims to create a more capable and generalizable robot policy by designing a novel architecture that explicitly reasons about 3D space while simultaneously modeling the inherent multimodality of action trajectories using the powerful generative capabilities of diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of "3D Diffuser Actor" is a **novel 3D denoising transformer** designed as a conditional diffusion probabilistic model for robot end-effector pose trajectories. This method combines a tokenized 3D scene representation with a language instruction and proprioceptive information to predict the noise in future robot pose trajectories.

**A. Input Representation and 3D Tokenization:**
The model takes three main types of conditioning information:
- **Visual Observations (o):** One or more posed RGB-D images. Each image is processed by a pre-trained CLIP ResNet50 2D image encoder to obtain 2D feature maps. Crucially, sensed depth information and camera intrinsics/extrinsics are then used to "lift" these 2D features into 3D scene tokens. Each 3D scene token comprises a latent embedding (the patch feature vector) and a corresponding 3D position (X, Y, Z coordinates). For multi-view setups, 3D tokens from all views are aggregated.
- **Language Instruction (l):** A language description of the task is encoded into language tokens using a pre-trained CLIP language encoder. These tokens provide high-level task context.
- **Proprioception (c):** The robot's current end-effector pose (position and rotation) is represented as a 3D scene token with a learnable latent embedding and its current 3D coordinates.

The robot's future actions (`a_t`) are represented as an end-effector pose, decomposed into a 3D location (`a_loc` in R^3), rotation (`a_rot` in 6D representation to avoid discontinuities), and a binary open/close state (`a_open`). The model aims to predict a trajectory `τ_t = (a_loc_t:t+T, a_rot_t:t+T)` of future locations and rotations over a temporal horizon `T`.

**B. Conditional Diffusion Model:**
The 3D Diffuser Actor is a conditional diffusion model that learns the distribution `p(τ | o, l, c)`. The core idea is to reverse a gradual noise-adding process. During training, a clean ground-truth trajectory `τ_0` is iteratively noised (`τ_i`) according to a variance schedule. The model, `ϵ_θ(τ_i; i, o, l, c)`, is trained to predict the noise component (`ϵ`) added to `τ_0` given the noised trajectory `τ_i`, the current diffusion step `i`, and the conditioning information (visual observation `o`, language `l`, proprioception `c`).

**C. 3D Relative Denoising Transformer:**
This is the central architectural component. It processes the various tokenized inputs:
- **Noised Trajectory Tokens:** Each noisy pose `a_i` from `τ_i` is mapped to a latent embedding via an MLP, with its 3D position derived from `a_loc,i`. These become "noised trajectory tokens."
- **Fusion with Attention:** The transformer fuses information from:
 - 3D scene tokens (from visual observations)
 - Language tokens
 - Proprioception token
 - Noised trajectory tokens
- **Relative 3D Attentions:** A key innovation is the use of **rotary positional embeddings** in the self-attention layers among all 3D tokens (scene, proprioception, and trajectory tokens). This encodes relative positional information, achieving translation equivariance, which is crucial for robust spatial reasoning and generalization. The attention weight between a query `q` and key `k` incorporates a matrix function `M` dependent only on their relative 3D positions (`p_q - p_k`).
- **Language Integration:** Language tokens are fused using normal attention mechanisms, as 3D coordinates are not applicable to them. The model can either allow visual tokens to cross-attend to language tokens (standard version) or interleave cross-attention layers from visual and pose estimate tokens to language tokens (enhanced version for CALVIN).
- **Output Prediction:** The contextualized trajectory tokens are fed to MLPs to predict:
 - The noise `ϵ_loc_θ` for 3D translations.
 - The noise `ϵ_rot_θ` for 3D rotations.
 - The end-effector opening state `f_open_θ` (binary classification).

**D. Training and Inference:**
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a composite loss function:
 - L1 loss for reconstructing the noise in 3D locations and rotations.
 - Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss for the end-effector open/close state.
 - Separate noise schedulers are used for `a_loc` (scaled-linear) and `a_rot` (square cosine), which is found empirically to improve performance.
- **Inference:** Starting with a sample `τ_N` from a standard Gaussian distribution, the model iteratively denoises it over `N` steps using the learned `ϵ_θ` function and a specified sampling schedule. The final denoised `τ_0` represents the predicted action trajectory.
- **Keypose-based Execution:** For benchmarks like RLBench, the model predicts the next end-effector *keypose*, which a low-level motion planner then uses to reach the target. For CALVIN and real-world tasks, the model predicts full pose trajectories and replans after each predicted trajectory segment is executed. Keyposes are extracted from demonstrations using heuristics (e.g., changes in gripper state, significant motion changes).

This comprehensive approach allows 3D Diffuser Actor to leverage the strengths of both 3D understanding and generative action modeling, leading to robust and precise manipulation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments across three distinct setups: two simulation benchmarks (RLBench and CALVIN) and real-world robot manipulation tasks. The results consistently demonstrate that 3D Diffuser Actor achieves state-of-the-art performance and significantly outperforms existing methods.

**A. Evaluation on RLBench:**
- **Setup:** Tested on 18 multi-view and 10 single-view manipulation tasks. Policies are trained to predict the next end-effector keypose, with a low-level motion planner handling the trajectory to that keypose. Success is measured by task completion rate.
- **Multi-view Results (Table 1):** 3D Diffuser Actor achieved an average success rate of **81.3%**, an **absolute improvement of +18.1%** over Act3D, the previous state-of-the-art. It showed significant gains on challenging, long-horizon, high-precision, and multimodal tasks like "stack blocks," "stack cups," and "place cups," where most baselines performed poorly.
- **Single-view Results (Table 2):** On single-view tasks, 3D Diffuser Actor achieved an average success rate of **78.4%**, outperforming GNFactor by **+46.7%** and Act3D by **+13.1%**. This highlights the importance of the diffusion objective even when the visual input is constrained.
- **Ablation Studies (Table 3):**
 - **2D Diffuser Actor vs. 3D Diffuser Actor:** The 2D counterpart achieved only 47.0% success (multi-view), demonstrating that **3D scene representations are crucial for performance**, even when using diffusion.
 - **3D Diffuser Actor w/o RelAttn. vs. 3D Diffuser Actor:** Removing relative 3D attentions (using absolute attentions) dropped performance to 71.3%, emphasizing that **translation equivariance through relative attentions is important for generalization**. Notably, even this ablated version still outperformed all prior arts in Table 1, showcasing the general effectiveness of combining 3D representations with diffusion.
- **Robustness to Depth Noise (Table 6 in Appendix):** With mild Gaussian noise (σ=0.01) added to 3D pixel locations, 3D Diffuser Actor maintained an average success rate of 72.4% (only a 6% absolute drop from clean data), showing reasonable robustness to noisy depth.

**B. Evaluation on CALVIN:**
- **Setup:** Evaluated on a zero-shot long-horizon generalization setting, where models are trained on environments A, B, C and tested on D. All models predict full robot pose trajectories (no motion planner provided).
- **Results (Table 4):** 3D Diffuser Actor achieved the highest average number of completed sequential tasks (Avg. Len) at **3.35**, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art GR-1 (3.06) by a **9% relative increase**. It also significantly outperformed concurrent 3D Diffusion Policy (0.31 Avg. Len), supporting the authors' claim about the superiority of tokenized 3D scene representations over holistic 1D embeddings. ChainedDiffuser also performed poorly, indicating challenges with its deterministic keypose prediction module on diverse human demonstrations.

**C. Evaluation in the Real World:**
- **Setup:** Tested on 12 multi-task manipulation scenarios using a Franka Emika robot with an Azure Kinect RGB-D sensor, learning from only 15 demonstrations per task.
- **Results (Table 5):** 3D Diffuser Actor effectively learned real-world manipulation from a handful of demonstrations, achieving high success rates on various tasks (e.g., "close box" 100%, "put duck in bowl" 100%, "press stapler" 90%, "put block in triangle" 90%). This demonstrates the practical applicability and data efficiency of the method.

**D. Runtime:**
- 3D Diffuser Actor's inference speed on CALVIN (NVIDIA 2080 Ti) was 600ms for 6 end-effector poses (10 Hz control frequency), which is competitive with other diffusion policies like 3D Diffusion Policy (581ms for 4 poses, 5.2 Hz) and faster than ChainedDiffuser (1170ms for 6 poses, 5.1 Hz).

In summary, the main findings robustly confirm that combining 3D scene representations with diffusion objectives, specifically through a 3D relative denoising transformer, yields significant performance gains in diverse robot manipulation tasks, setting new state-of-the-art benchmarks and demonstrating real-world applicability with limited data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "3D Diffuser Actor" paper presents a significant advancement in the field of robot manipulation, with several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging Key Research Lines:** The most direct impact is the successful unification of two powerful and previously somewhat separate research trajectories: diffusion models for probabilistic policy learning and 3D scene representations for robust spatial understanding. By demonstrating that their combination yields superior performance, the work opens new avenues for future research that can jointly leverage these complementary strengths.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** By achieving new state-of-the-art results on challenging and widely recognized benchmarks like RLBench and CALVIN, and demonstrating strong performance in real-world scenarios, 3D Diffuser Actor pushes the boundaries of what is possible with learning-from-demonstrations policies. The substantial performance gains (e.g., +18.1% absolute on RLBench multi-view) are not incremental but represent a significant leap.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The empirical results, particularly the ablations, strongly support the authors' hypotheses:
 - **3D representations are critical:** The clear superiority over the 2D Diffuser Actor counterpart highlights the fundamental importance of explicit 3D spatial reasoning for generalization across viewpoints and complex scene interactions.
 - **Relative attentions improve equivariance:** The better performance of 3D Diffuser Actor with relative attentions suggests that designing models with translation equivariance (or similar geometric inductive biases) is vital for robust generalization in 3D space.
 - **Diffusion handles multimodality:** The success on complex, multimodal tasks where deterministic or regression-based policies falter showcases the power of diffusion models in capturing the diverse ways a task can be accomplished.

4. **Real-World Applicability and Data Efficiency:** The ability to learn multi-task manipulation in the real world from a "handful of demonstrations" (15 per task) is highly significant. It addresses one of the major bottlenecks in deploying robot learning systems: the need for massive datasets. This data efficiency makes the approach more practical for real-world applications where data collection can be expensive and time-consuming.

5. **Future of Robot Learning:** This work contributes to the broader trend of designing more "generalist" robot policies that can perform a wide range of tasks and generalize to novel scenarios. It points towards a future where sophisticated generative models, combined with rich, geometrically grounded representations, enable robots to learn complex skills with less supervision and adapt more flexibly to varied environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to fertile ground for future research:
- **Reliance on Calibrated Depth:** Like other 3D policies, 3D Diffuser Actor requires camera calibration and accurate depth information. Research into methods that can infer 3D structure more robustly or from uncalibrated/less-accurate sensors could further enhance its applicability.
- **Quasi-static Tasks:** The current application focuses on quasi-static manipulation tasks. Extending the method to dynamic tasks and direct velocity control would be a crucial next step for real-world robustness.
- **Inference Speed:** While competitive among diffusion policies, it is generally slower than non-diffusion counterparts due to the iterative denoising process. Future work could explore techniques to reduce inference steps (e.g., through knowledge distillation or faster sampling schedules) to achieve higher control frequencies.
- **Scaling Data and Suboptimal Demonstrations:** The authors explicitly mention scaling up training data in simulation and the real world, and learning from suboptimal demonstrations, as important future directions. This aligns with the broader push towards foundation models in robotics, where large and diverse datasets, possibly including imperfect human data, are leveraged.

In conclusion, "3D Diffuser Actor" is a landmark paper that effectively unifies critical advancements in deep learning for robotics. Its methodological innovation and empirical success suggest a promising path towards more capable, generalizable, and deployable robot manipulation systems.
