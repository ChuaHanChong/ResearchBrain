# NAVSIM: Data-Driven Non-Reactive Autonomous Vehicle Simulation and Benchmarking

> [!summary] Summary
> NAVSIM offers a data-driven non-reactive simulation and benchmarking framework for autonomous vehicles, designed to evaluate end-to-end driving policies against challenging real-world scenarios. It introduces a comprehensive scoring function, the PDM Score, which demonstrates better alignment with full closed-loop simulation compared to conventional open-loop metrics, and reveals that simpler models can achieve competitive performance against more complex architectures.

## Problem

- Existing open-loop AV evaluation using real data fails to accurately reflect closed-loop performance, leading to unfair penalties for valid alternative trajectories.
- Full closed-loop simulations are computationally intensive, exhibit a substantial 'domain gap' from real-world data, and often lack robust sensor simulation for vision-based models.
- Many autonomous driving datasets contain an abundance of 'trivial' scenarios, which do not effectively test sophisticated planning and perception algorithms.

## Method

- **NAVSIM** implements a ==non-reactive simulation== approach where the driving policy plans a ==4-second trajectory== once per scene, assuming other agents' behavior is unaffected, allowing for interactive evaluation with ==open-loop efficiency==.
- It introduces the ==PDM Score (PDMS)==, a composite metric inspired by nuPlan, which combines ==strict penalties for safety== (collisions, drivable area compliance) with ==weighted averages for driving quality== (progress, time-to-collision, comfort).
- The framework curates challenging scenarios from the ==OpenScene dataset== by systematically filtering out trivial cases and unreliable human trajectories, resulting in distinct `navtrain` and `navtest` splits for robust evaluation.

## Results

- The PDM Score shows a significantly better correlation (up to **0.73**) with full closed-loop simulation scores compared to traditional open-loop metrics like Average Displacement Error (**0.28**).
- Leading models such as UniAD and PARA-Drive achieved PDMS scores of **83.4%** and **84.0%** respectively, which were closely matched by the more efficient TransFuser at **84.0%**, despite requiring considerably less training compute.
- A performance gap of approximately **10%** persists between the best-performing models (around **84% PDMS**) and human expert drivers (**94.8% PDMS**), particularly in drivable area compliance and ego progress.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - NAVSIM's PDM Score demonstrates a substantially higher correlation with full closed-loop simulation scores than traditional open-loop metrics, offering a more reliable assessment of AV performance.
> - The benchmark reveals that computationally efficient end-to-end driving models, such as TransFuser, can achieve performance comparable to significantly larger and more complex state-of-the-art architectures on challenging scenarios.
> - The curated datasets successfully eliminate trivial scenarios, forcing models to rely on sensor-based perception and complex planning, as evidenced by the poor performance of 'blind' agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and reliable autonomous vehicles (AVs) hinges on effective evaluation methods. However, the current landscape of AV benchmarking is fraught with challenges, which NAVSIM directly addresses.

Historically, AV evaluation has primarily fallen into two categories:
- **Open-loop evaluation with real data:** This involves running a driving policy on recorded sensor data and comparing its planned trajectory to the human driver's recorded path. While easy to conduct and based on real-world conditions, these methods suffer from a critical flaw: they do not reflect "closed-loop" performance. The policy's actions do not influence the environment or other agents, meaning a safe but different trajectory might be unfairly penalized by simple displacement errors (e.g., Average Displacement Error - ADE), as illustrated in Figure 1. Moreover, many existing datasets (like nuScenes) were originally designed for perception tasks, leading to a predominance of "trivial" scenarios that can be "solved" by simple kinematic extrapolation, without relying on sensor data, thereby failing to genuinely test sophisticated planning algorithms.
- **Closed-loop evaluation in simulation:** This allows the AV policy to interact with a dynamic environment, enabling assessment of safety, comfort, and traffic rule compliance. However, these simulations are computationally intensive and, more critically, exhibit a significant "domain gap" to real-world data. Synthetic sensor data often lacks the fidelity and complexity of real-world inputs, making it difficult for models trained in simulation to generalize to real driving conditions. Data-driven simulators (e.g., nuPlan, Waymax) exist but often lack sensor simulation for camera/LiDAR inputs, limiting their use for vision-based end-to-end driving models.

NAVSIM positions itself as a crucial "middle ground" between these two paradigms. It aims to combine the benefits of real-world data evaluation with simulation-based metrics that better reflect closed-loop performance, all while circumventing the challenges of full reactive sensor simulation. This addresses the long-standing need for a standardized, scalable, and reliable benchmark that can meaningfully compare the rapidly growing body of end-to-end autonomous driving research. The paper also highlights issues with inconsistent metric implementations and "label leakage" in previous open-loop benchmarks, further emphasizing the need for a principled approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of NAVSIM are:

- **Bridge the gap between open-loop and closed-loop evaluation:** The core motivation is to overcome the limitations of both traditional open-loop (lack of closed-loop relevance, flawed metrics like ADE) and closed-loop simulations (high computational cost, large domain gap from real data). NAVSIM seeks to create an evaluation method that offers better alignment with closed-loop performance while leveraging real-world data at scale.
- **Enable large-scale real-world benchmarking:** To facilitate robust and comparative analysis of diverse AV algorithms, NAVSIM aims to provide a platform for benchmarking on a large, curated set of challenging real-world scenarios.
- **Develop principled, simulation-based metrics:** Traditional displacement-based metrics are insufficient for evaluating complex driving behavior. NAVSIM seeks to introduce a comprehensive scoring function (PDM Score) that objectively assesses safety, comfort, and progress, drawing inspiration from rule-based planning.
- **Standardize evaluation protocols and data curation:** The project aims to establish standardized training and testing splits, along with clear protocols, to ensure fair and consistent comparisons across different research efforts. This includes filtering for challenging scenarios to prevent "blind" policies from appearing state-of-the-art.
- **Foster community engagement and accelerate research:** By providing an accessible framework, an official evaluation server, and hosting challenges, NAVSIM intends to lower the barrier to entry for AV research and encourage innovation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NAVSIM's methodology centers on a novel "non-reactive simulation" paradigm combined with sophisticated data curation and a comprehensive scoring system.

- **Non-Reactive Simulation:** This is the cornerstone of NAVSIM. Instead of simulating the full, reactive interaction between the ego vehicle and its environment at high frequencies (e.g., 10Hz), the driving agent is queried only once at the beginning of each scene. The policy commits to a planned trajectory for a short horizon (fixed at 4 seconds). During this horizon, the agent's actions are assumed *not* to influence the behavior of other agents in the scene. This simplifying assumption allows for "open-loop" metric computation while still incorporating interactive elements.
 - **Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) Abstraction:** To simulate the consequences of the planned trajectory without requiring expensive sensor rendering, NAVSIM uses a simplified BEV abstraction of the scene. The expected motion of all agents (ego and background) over the 4-second horizon is simulated in this abstract space.
 - **Trajectory Execution:** An LQR controller calculates steering and acceleration, and a kinematic bicycle model propagates the ego vehicle at 10Hz over the 4-second horizon, enabling precise simulation of the planned path.

- **PDM Score (PDMS):** This composite metric is derived from the Predictive Driver Model (PDM) used in nuPlan and serves as the primary evaluation metric. It aggregates several subscores, categorized as "penalties" or "weighted averages."
 - **Penalties:** These enforce strict compliance with fundamental safety and legal requirements.
 - **No Collisions (NC):** Punishes collisions with other road users (vehicles, pedestrians, bicycles). A full collision results in `score_NC = 0`, leading to an overall PDMS of 0 for that scene. Collisions with static objects incur a softer penalty (`score_NC = 0.5`).
 - **Drivable Area Compliance (DAC):** Penalizes infractions of driving outside designated drivable areas, also resulting in `score_DAC = 0` if violated.
 - **Weighted Average:** This combines subscores reflecting driving quality and efficiency.
 - **Ego Progress (EP):** Measures the agent's progress along its route center, normalized by an estimated safe upper bound from a privileged rule-based planner.
 - **Time-to-Collision (TTC):** Ensures the agent maintains safe margins to other vehicles, setting the subscore to 0 if a collision is imminent within the horizon.
 - **Comfort (C):** Evaluates the smoothness of the trajectory based on acceleration and jerk profiles.
 - **Aggregation:** PDMS combines these, multiplying the penalty scores and taking a weighted average of the others: `PDMS = (Penalty Scores) * (Weighted Average of EP, TTC, C)`. This hierarchical structure ensures safety and rule compliance are prioritized.

- **Data Curation for Challenging Scenarios:**
 - **Dataset Source:** NAVSIM utilizes OpenScene, a redistribution of nuPlan, which is a large real-world driving dataset.
 - **Filtering Strategy:** To overcome the issue of trivial scenarios in raw datasets, NAVSIM employs a filtering strategy. It removes scenes where a simple "constant velocity and heading" agent achieves a PDMS above 0.8 (indicating an easy scenario) or where the human trajectory itself yields a PDMS below 0.8 (indicating annotation errors or inherent difficulty for even human drivers). This ensures the `navtrain` (103k samples) and `navtest` (12k samples) splits consist of genuinely challenging and solvable scenarios.
 - **Goal Definition:** Navigation goals are derived from the lane graph rather than the human trajectory endpoint to prevent "label leakage."

- **Evaluation Server:** An official, publicly accessible evaluation server is hosted on the HuggingFace platform, providing a standardized, low-maintenance platform for submissions and leaderboard tracking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NAVSIM provides several key findings, both in validating its own methodology and in benchmarking state-of-the-art autonomous driving models.

- **Alignment with Closed-Loop Metrics:**
 - A critical finding is that NAVSIM's PDMS exhibits a significantly *better correlation* to full closed-loop simulation (CLS) scores than traditional open-loop metrics like Average Displacement Error (OLS). This correlation holds across various types of planners (learned and rule-based) and is robust to changes in CLS duration, planning frequency, and PDMS horizon.
 - The highest correlation is observed when the closed-loop simulation duration (d=4s) matches the NAVSIM horizon, reinforcing the non-reactive simulation's validity for short-term planning.

- **Performance of End-to-End Driving Models:**
 - **"Blind" Agents Fail:** On NAVSIM's curated challenging scenarios, "blind" agents (e.g., Constant Velocity baseline, Ego Status MLP) perform poorly (PDMS of 20.6% and 65.6% respectively). This contrasts sharply with results on unfiltered datasets like nuScenes, where such agents could achieve high displacement error scores, demonstrating that NAVSIM successfully identifies scenarios requiring sensor-based perception and planning.
 - **Surprising Performance of Simpler Models:** State-of-the-art models like UniAD and PARA-Drive, known for their complexity and high computational demands, achieve PDMS scores (83.4% and 84.0%) that are only marginally better than or even matched by more efficient architectures like TransFuser and Latent TransFuser (84.0% and 83.8%). TransFuser, for example, required significantly less training compute (1 GPU for 1 day) compared to PARA-Drive (80 GPUs for 3 days). This suggests that for the current set of challenging scenarios and evaluation horizon, efficiency might be as important as sheer model size, or that the current benchmark doesn't fully exploit the capabilities of the more complex models.
 - **Persistent Gap to Human Performance:** Even the best-performing models on NAVSIM's `navtest` split achieve a PDMS of around 84%, while the human expert scores 94.8%. This 10% gap highlights the significant challenges posed by the benchmark, particularly in `drivable area compliance (DAC)` and `ego progress (EP)`.
 - **Comfort Subscore Mastery:** Interestingly, all sensor-based agents achieve near-perfect comfort scores (around 100%), indicating that smooth acceleration and jerk profiles are effectively learned through imitation.

- **Insights from the CVPR 2024 NAVSIM Challenge:**
 - **Revival of Trajectory Sampling:** The winning entry, Hydra-MDP, extended TransFuser with a trajectory sampling and scoring strategy. This result prompts a reassessment of these classical methods, which had been somewhat overshadowed by end-to-end approaches predicting a single trajectory.
 - **Emergence of VLMs:** The second-place team utilized a Vision Language Model (VLM) for driving, reflecting the growing interest in incorporating large foundation models into autonomous systems.
 - **Community Engagement:** The challenge attracted 143 teams and 463 submissions, demonstrating NAVSIM's effectiveness in catalyzing research and providing a platform for diverse approaches.

- **Ablation Studies on TransFuser:** These revealed insights into model sensitivity, such as the importance of ego status inputs (velocity, acceleration, navigation goal) and the impact of camera field-of-view and LiDAR range on performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NAVSIM represents a significant step forward in the benchmarking of autonomous driving systems, with several potential impacts on the research community and the development of AV technology:

- **Standardized and Principled Evaluation:** By providing a standardized framework, carefully curated datasets of challenging scenarios, and a robust, simulation-based metric (PDMS) that correlates well with closed-loop performance, NAVSIM addresses many inconsistencies and shortcomings of previous benchmarks. This enables fairer comparisons and more accurate conclusions about the true capabilities of AV algorithms.
- **Accessibility and Scalability:** The non-reactive simulation paradigm makes evaluation computationally tractable and scalable, allowing researchers to benchmark their models on large real-world datasets without the prohibitive costs of full closed-loop sensor simulation. The public evaluation server on HuggingFace further enhances accessibility.
- **Focus on Meaningful Progress:** By filtering out trivial scenarios and penalizing unsafe or non-compliant behaviors, NAVSIM pushes researchers to develop genuinely intelligent and robust planning algorithms that effectively use sensor data, rather than models that merely extrapolate simple human behavior.
- **Re-evaluation of Model Architectures:** The finding that simpler models like TransFuser can compete with larger, more complex ones (e.g., UniAD, PARA-Drive) on this benchmark has a substantial impact. It prompts the community to reassess the trade-offs between model complexity, computational requirements, and real-world performance, potentially fostering research into more efficient and deployable solutions.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The success of the NAVSIM challenge, highlighting trajectory sampling and Vision Language Models, demonstrates its ability to stimulate innovation and identify promising new directions in AV research. It provides a platform for testing novel ideas and methodologies.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** By using real-world sensor data for policy input while employing simulation-based metrics, NAVSIM offers a pragmatic approach to evaluate real-world performance without the full complexities of reactive simulation, thereby helping to bridge the challenging sim-to-real gap.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as acknowledged by authors):**
The authors are transparent about NAVSIM's limitations, primarily the lack of full reactive simulation. The non-reactive nature means the system does not account for the compounding accumulation of errors or the ego vehicle's influence on other agents' future behavior. It also currently doesn't fully evaluate all traffic rules (e.g., stop signs, traffic lights) or concepts like transit and fuel efficiency. Future work will explore more comprehensive subscore definitions, potentially integrating data-driven sensor/traffic simulation as these technologies mature, and supporting a wider range of datasets to address inherent biases or missing classes in current datasets. The authors emphasize that NAVSIM should be seen as a *complementary* benchmark to fully reactive, graphics-based simulators like CARLA, rather than a replacement.

In conclusion, NAVSIM offers a timely and impactful contribution to the autonomous driving research community, providing a robust, scalable, and accessible framework for benchmarking sensor-based driving policies that is better aligned with real-world closed-loop performance.
