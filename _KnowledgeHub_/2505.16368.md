# SATURN: SAT-based Reinforcement Learning to Unleash LLMs Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SATURN framework introduces a reinforcement learning approach that leverages Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) problems to improve large language model reasoning. It achieves substantial performance gains, with SATURN-1.5B showing a +33.6% improvement on specific SAT tasks and a +4.9 average score increase across math and programming benchmarks, demonstrating generalization and enhanced self-verification in reasoning trajectories.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for large language models (LLMs) face scalability issues, requiring extensive human annotation or expensive LLM synthesis for high-quality training data.
- Verifying the correctness of LLM outputs for complex reasoning tasks is challenging, making it difficult to design accurate and reliable reward signals for RL.
- Prior RL tasks for LLMs often lack fine-grained control over problem difficulty, hindering the implementation of effective curriculum learning strategies.

## Method

- **SATURN** proposes a multi-stage curriculum learning framework that utilizes programmatically generated ==Boolean Satisfiability== (SAT) problems as the foundation for RL tasks.
- The framework employs an iterative process with a Curriculum Estimation Loop to adaptively adjust task difficulty based on LLM performance and an LLMs Training Loop that optimizes models using Generalized Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- It features a `SAT_Construction` method for scalable SAT instance generation with precise control over structural parameters (n, k, l) and a reward function encouraging both logical correctness and proper output formatting.

## Results

- SATURN significantly improved LLM performance on SAT tasks, with SATURN-**1.5B** showing a +**33.6%** `pass@1` gain on `SAT-(3,5,5)` and a +**14.0%** average `pass@3` gain on unseen, harder SAT test sets.
- The reasoning capabilities generalized across benchmarks, with SATURN-**1.5B** improving its average score across AIME, AMC, MATH-500, GPQA Diamond, and LiveCodeBench by +4.9 points.
- SATURN enhances self-verification in LLM reasoning trajectories and serves as a complement to existing RL training, providing additional performance gains even on models already fine-tuned on math and programming tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SAT problems provide a scalable, verifiable, and controllable foundation for LLM reasoning training due to their programmatic generatability, objective verifiability, and adjustable difficulty parameters.
> - An analytical difficulty estimator, D(n, k, l), effectively correlates with LLM solvability trends for SAT tasks, enabling precise curriculum control tailored to human-like logical reasoning processes.
> - Training on abstract logical SAT problems fosters the acquisition of generalizable meta-reasoning abilities, such as self-verification, that transfer effectively to diverse domains like mathematics and programming.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of reinforcement learning (RL) with large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a strategy for developing advanced reasoning capabilities in domains such as mathematics, programming, and logical problem-solving. Existing methodologies for designing RL tasks, while demonstrating some success, face inherent challenges. These include issues with **scalability**, where generating sufficient high-quality training data often necessitates extensive human annotation or expensive LLM synthesis. **Verifiability** poses another challenge, as automatically and reliably assessing the correctness of LLM outputs can be difficult. Furthermore, **controllable difficulty** is frequently absent, making it challenging to implement curriculum learning approaches that gradually introduce more complex reasoning tasks.

Prior RL tasks for LLMs can broadly be categorized into two groups. The first involves solving math or programming problems, with rewards based on the correctness of final answers or code. While verifiable, these tasks often depend on human-annotated ground truths, lack high-quality problem diversity, and offer limited control over reasoning difficulty. The second category focuses on manually designed reasoning tasks, such as natural language logic puzzles (e.g., Knights & Knaves). These tasks often struggle with scalability due to reliance on LLM sampling or handcrafted templates, face verification difficulties despite using LLMs for cross-validation, and typically lack fine-grained difficulty control.

The SATURN framework addresses these identified limitations by introducing Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) problems as the foundation for RL task design. SAT problems offer a unique combination of features that address the limitations of existing approaches. Their programmatic generation capability ensures scalable data production, their NP-complete nature allows for clear, verifiable solutions, and their configurable parameters (number of variables, clauses) provide precise control over problem difficulty. By leveraging SAT problems, SATURN aims to establish a more robust, scalable, and adaptable paradigm for training LLMs to develop and generalize reasoning skills.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether a reinforcement learning task can be designed that simultaneously supports scalability, verifiability, and controllable difficulty, thereby effectively enhancing the reasoning capability of LLMs. This overarching goal is motivated by critical limitations observed in existing RL task designs for LLMs.

Specifically, the motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Scalability Limitations:** Current RL approaches for LLM reasoning often require substantial amounts of training data, which is typically acquired through labor-intensive human annotation or computationally expensive synthesis by other LLMs. This dependence limits the scale at which LLMs can be trained and iteratively improved. The objective is to devise a task where instances can be generated programmatically and in virtually unlimited quantities, removing the bottleneck of data acquisition. SAT problems, being algorithmically generatable, inherently offer this scalability.
- **Ensuring Verifiability:** For effective RL, reward signals must be accurate and unambiguous. Many complex reasoning tasks produce LLM outputs that are difficult to verify automatically and reliably. The motivation is to integrate a task where the correctness of an LLM's solution can be objectively and efficiently validated. SAT problems are well-suited here because their solutions (satisfying assignments) can be verified in polynomial time, providing clear, rule-based feedback for RL.
- **Enabling Controllable Difficulty for Curriculum Learning:** The development of sophisticated reasoning capabilities in LLMs is often a progressive process, suggesting the utility of curriculum learning—training from easy to hard tasks. However, most existing RL tasks lack the mechanisms for fine-grained control over problem difficulty. The objective is to design a task that allows precise adjustment of difficulty levels, enabling a structured curriculum. SAT problems offer this control through parameters like the number of variables, clauses, and clause-to-variable ratios, which can be manipulated to create a continuous spectrum of difficulty.
- **Enhancing Generalizable Reasoning:** Beyond merely solving specific tasks, a core motivation is to foster generalizable reasoning capabilities in LLMs. The paper hypothesizes that training on foundational logical reasoning problems, such as SAT, can instill domain-agnostic reasoning patterns (e.g., self-verification, backtracking) that transfer to other complex tasks like mathematics and programming.
- **Developing a Robust RL Framework:** To operationalize these objectives, the research aims to propose SATURN, a multi-stage curriculum learning-based RL framework. This framework is designed to continuously improve LLMs' reasoning by constructing progressively difficult SAT tasks and providing a principled mechanism for controlling difficulty transitions, ensuring stable and effective curriculum advancement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SATURN employs a multi-stage curriculum learning framework that alternates between two interconnected loops: the **Curriculum Estimation Loop** and the **LLMs Training Loop**. This iterative process is designed to adaptively challenge LLMs with SAT problems of increasing difficulty.

#### 3.1. SATURN Learning Loop Framework

The framework operates as follows:

1. **Curriculum Estimation Loop:**
 - This loop begins by constructing a validation set of SAT instances at the current difficulty level, determined by parameters (n, k, l).
 - The LLM's performance on this validation set is evaluated using the `pass@1` metric.
 - If the `pass@1` score exceeds a predefined threshold (`ϵ`), the curriculum controller increments the estimated difficulty parameters (n, k, l) to advance to a harder stage.
 - If the performance does not meet the threshold, the process proceeds to the LLMs Training Loop at the current difficulty, indicating that the model needs further training at this level. This adaptive mechanism ensures the LLM is consistently trained at the frontier of its current reasoning capability.

2. **LLMs Training Loop:**
 - For the current difficulty level, a new set of training SAT instances is generated (distinct from the validation set).
 - These instances are used to train the LLM using **Generalized Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, an RL algorithm.
 - The reward function for GRPO is designed to encourage outputs that are both **logically correct** and **properly formatted**. Specifically, an invalid output (e.g., missing a `\boxed{}` wrapper) receives a reward of -1, a well-formatted but incorrect answer receives 0, and a perfectly correct and formatted answer receives 1.
 - The training loop continues until the LLM's `pass@1` performance on the validation set at the current difficulty exceeds the `ϵ` threshold. Upon reaching this criterion, control returns to the Curriculum Estimation Loop to potentially advance the curriculum.

This alternating process iterates until a maximum number of curriculum stages or iterations is reached.

#### 3.2. SAT Instances Construction

The core of SATURN's data generation lies in `SAT_Construction(n, k, l, m)`, a programmatic constructor for Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) instances.
- A SAT instance is defined in Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF) with parameters: `n` (number of literals per clause), `k` (total number of variables), and `l` (total number of clauses).
- The constructor uniformly samples `m` satisfiable SAT instances.
- The procedure involves:
 1. Randomly generating a Boolean solution for `k` variables.
 2. Randomly selecting `n` variables to form a clause.
 3. Negating `n` variables randomly such that the clause is satisfied by the pre-generated solution.
 4. These (solution, clause) pairs are grouped by their solutions.
 5. `l` clauses are randomly selected from a solution group and shuffled to form a complete SAT instance guaranteed to be satisfiable.
 6. `m` diverse SAT instances are uniformly sampled across these solution clusters, with duplicates removed.
This method ensures scalability and precise control over the structural parameters (n, k, l) to generate varying difficulty levels.

#### 3.3. Estimation of Task Difficulty

For curriculum learning, SATURN defines an analytical estimator `D(n, k, l)` to quantify the difficulty of SAT tasks for LLMs. Unlike traditional SAT solver-centric phase transition theory (which observes peak difficulty around a clause-to-variable ratio `αc ≈ 4.26`), this estimator is designed to align with human-like logical reasoning processes.
The difficulty `D(n, k, l)` is estimated as:
`D(n, k, l) = log2(k) + 2 log2(l) - n + k/n`
This formula incorporates:
- **Sparsity-Based Estimate (`log2(k) + log2(l) - n`):** This component approximates difficulty by considering the ratio of symbolic search cost (proportional to `k * l`) to the expected solution space size (approximately `2^n`). A higher cost-to-solution ratio implies greater difficulty.
- **Structural Complexity Adjustment (`log2(l) + k/n`):** This component accounts for the structural properties of the SAT formula. `log2(l)` reflects the complexity introduced by longer clauses, while `k/n` (the inverse of average variable reuse across clauses) captures the increased interdependency and entanglement between variables.
Empirical validation (Figure 3) demonstrates that LLM `pass@3` scores generally decrease as `D(n, k, l)` increases, supporting the estimator's effectiveness in correlating with observed LLM solvability trends.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning with GRPO

Within the LLMs Training Loop, the LLMs are optimized using GRPO (Generalized Policy Optimization). The GRPO objective function aims to optimize the policy `πθ` with a KL divergence penalty to `πref`, encouraging efficient learning while maintaining proximity to a reference policy. The objective is defined as:
`L_GRPO(θ) = E[q ~ P(Q), {o_i}^G_{i=1} ~ π_{θold}(O|q)] [ (1/G) Σ_{i=1}^G (1/|o_i|) Σ_{t=1}^{|o_i|} min(r_{i,t}(θ) Â_{i,t}, clip(r_{i,t}(θ), 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ) Â_{i,t}) - βD_KL[π_θ || π_ref] ]`
where `r_{i,t}(θ)` is the ratio of new to old policy probabilities, `Â_{i,t}` is a normalized advantage estimate, `q` is a SAT instance, `o_i` is a generated reasoning trajectory, and `G` groups instances of identical difficulty.
The reward `r_i` for a generated output `o_i` is:
- `-1` if the output is invalid (e.g., missing `\boxed{}`).
- `0` for well-formatted but incorrect answers.
- `1` for both correct formatting and a verifiable satisfying assignment.

#### 3.5. SATURN-2.6k Dataset

The research introduces **SATURN-2.6k**, a dataset comprising 2,660 SAT problems. This dataset is structured to support comprehensive evaluation:
- 1,500 training instances.
- 160 test instances at the same difficulty as the training set.
- 1,000 test instances across 10 harder, previously unseen difficulty levels (100 instances per level).
The dataset, coupled with open-sourced construction scripts, allows for scalable generation of SAT instances and systematic evaluation of LLM reasoning across a range of difficulties.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding SATURN's effectiveness in enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities across SAT, math, and programming tasks. The DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B models were utilized to create SATURN-1.5B and SATURN-7B, respectively.

#### 4.1. RQ1: SATURN Substantially Improves Performance on SAT Tasks

SATURN demonstrates notable improvements in LLM performance on SAT problems, particularly on unseen, harder difficulty levels.
- **SATURN-1.5B:** Achieved a `pass@1` improvement of +33.6% (from 36.7% to 70.3%) on the `SAT-(3,5,5)` difficulty level from Iteration-1 to Iteration-2. On the unseen harder test set, its average `pass@3` improved from 10.1% to 24.2%, representing a +14.0% gain.
- **SATURN-7B:** On the `SAT-(3,5,13)` difficulty level, its `pass@1` increased from 53.9% to 89.5% by Iteration-2. On the unseen harder test set, its average `pass@3` improved from 36.1% to 64.2%, a +28.1% gain.
These results indicate that SATURN effectively enhances LLM reasoning for both seen and progressively more challenging SAT difficulties. Furthermore, the `pass@3` accuracy on the SATURN-2.6k benchmark correlates strongly with the estimated SAT difficulty `D(n, k, l)`, with R² values of 0.746 for SATURN-1.5B and 0.5011 for SATURN-7B, confirming the validity of the difficulty estimation and the benchmark's reliability.

#### 4.2. RQ2: SATURN Demonstrates Strong Generalization to Math and Programming

The reasoning capabilities acquired through SATURN training exhibited transferability to broader math and programming benchmarks.
- **Overall Improvement:** SATURN-1.5B improved its average score across AIME, AMC, MATH-500, GPQA Diamond, and LiveCodeBench by +4.9. SATURN-7B achieved an average improvement of +1.8 across the same benchmarks.
- **Specific Examples:** SATURN-1.5B outperformed z1-7B by 8.3 and s1.1-7B by 21.7 on AIME 24/25. SATURN-7B showed an increase from 93.2% to 95.0% on Math500 and from 35.4% to 37.7% on LiveCodeBench.
- **Comparison with SFT:** Unlike supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which showed an "alignment tax" (performance drop on LiveCodeBench from 16.4% to 14.6% after specializing on math-focused data), SATURN consistently improved performance across all benchmarks. SATURN-1.5B outperformed its SFT counterpart by 3.3 points on average, suggesting better generalization.

#### 4.3. RQ3: SATURN Serves as a Complement and Further Enhances LLM Reasoning

SATURN was found to complement existing RL training on math and programming tasks and to outperform other constructing RL task approaches.
- **Complementary Role:** When applied to DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview, a model already trained with 40k math and programming examples, SATURN still yielded additional improvements, raising the average score from 49.9% to 52.3%. This included specific gains of +5.0 on AIME and +6.0 on GPQA-D.
- **Outperformance:** Using only 1k training examples, SATURN on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M improved the average score from 32.5% to 36.2%. This represents a +8.8% relative improvement over Logic-RL, which used 5k examples.

#### 4.4. RQ4: SATURN Enhances Self-verification in LLMs’ Reasoning Trajectories

Analysis of reasoning trajectories indicated that SATURN influences LLMs' reasoning patterns, particularly by promoting self-verification.
- **Response Length:** Consistent with prior work, a gradual increase in response length was observed during SATURN training, indicative of more elaborate reasoning steps.
- **Self-Verification in SAT:** Solving SAT problems naturally encourages rechecking all clauses during variable assignment, thereby reinforcing self-verification behaviors.
- **Generalization to Math:** Case studies on Math500 problems showed that SATURN-trained models exhibited improved self-verification capabilities. For instance, SATURN-7B demonstrated the ability to verify intermediate conclusions in small scenarios, identify inconsistencies, and correctly re-evaluate solution paths, whereas baseline models sometimes failed to recheck even when inconsistencies were detected. This suggests that the self-verification patterns learned from SAT tasks transfer to math and programming, leading to more structured and robust reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SATURN framework represents a contribution to the field of LLM reasoning by addressing several fundamental limitations of existing reinforcement learning task designs. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that **Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) problems can serve as an effective, scalable, and controllable foundation for training LLMs to acquire and generalize complex reasoning skills.**

The key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Scalable and Verifiable Task Generation:** SATURN overcomes the data scarcity and verification challenges inherent in many reasoning tasks. The programmatic generation of SAT instances allows for an essentially unlimited supply of training data, eliminating reliance on expensive human annotation or LLM synthesis. The rule-based verifiability of SAT solutions provides clear, objective reward signals for RL, fostering more reliable learning.
- **Effective Curriculum Learning:** The precise control over SAT problem difficulty, enabled by parameters like the number of variables and clauses, facilitates robust curriculum learning. This allows LLMs to progressively develop reasoning abilities from simpler to more complex problems, a crucial aspect for mastering intricate logical steps.
- **Generalization of Reasoning Capabilities:** A significant finding is that the reasoning skills learned from SAT tasks generalize effectively to diverse domains such as mathematics and programming. This suggests that training on abstract logical problems can foster domain-agnostic meta-abilities (like self-verification and structured problem-solving) that are transferable across different intellectual challenges. This broadens the applicability of RL for LLM enhancement beyond domain-specific datasets.
- **Improved Reasoning Trajectories:** SATURN's training encourages LLMs to generate more detailed and self-verifying reasoning trajectories. This aligns with observed behaviors of expert human reasoners and contributes to developing more robust and reliable LLMs that can identify and correct their own errors.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By releasing the SATURN-2.6k dataset, construction scripts, and models, the authors provide a resource for the research community to further explore and build upon this approach. This facilitates comparative studies and the development of new methods for LLM reasoning.

**Limitations identified by the authors include:**
- **Plateauing Performance:** Improvements in math and programming tasks tend to plateau with increased training iterations, indicating that SATURN, while enhancing formal logical reasoning, does not provide domain-specific knowledge supervision.
- **Context Window Bottlenecks:** The exponential growth in reasoning length required for increasingly difficult NP-complete SAT problems can strain LLMs' context windows, limiting performance on highly complex tasks.
- **Limited Plasticity and Forgetting:** Like other continual learning scenarios, LLMs may face limitations related to plasticity and catastrophic forgetting, hindering continuous improvement across many training stages.

Despite these limitations, the paper argues that SATURN retains significant potential, especially for stronger models. Even frontier LLMs still exhibit common reasoning errors on complex SAT problems, suggesting that SATURN can continue to be a promising avenue for enhancing their reasoning capabilities with sufficient computational resources. Future work plans to apply SATURN to larger-scale LLMs and explore paths toward continuous self-evolution capabilities, moving beyond reliance on human-annotated data.
