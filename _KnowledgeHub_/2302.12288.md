# ZoeDepth: Zero-shot Transfer by Combining Relative and Metric Depth

> [!summary] Summary
> ZoeDepth unifies relative and metric depth estimation by pre-training a MiDaS-style encoder-decoder on relative depth across many datasets, then attaching lightweight domain-specific metric bins heads with attractor-based refinement and automatic routing, achieving state-of-the-art metric accuracy plus strong zero-shot cross-domain generalization.

## Problem

- Metric depth models trained on single datasets overfit to that domain's scale and camera setup, generalizing poorly to unseen indoor or outdoor environments.
- Relative depth methods generalize well by factoring out scale but produce depth without metric meaning, limiting use in mapping, planning, and 3D reconstruction.
- Existing architectures lack an established way to exploit relative depth pre-training for metric depth, and jointly training on multi-domain metric datasets typically degrades performance.

## Method

- ==Metric bins module== predicts all bin centers at the bottleneck and refines them via lightweight ==attractor layers== at each decoder stage instead of splitting bins progressively.
- ==Inverse and exponential attractor== formulations pull bin centers toward predicted attractor points per pixel, contracting the estimate without local sum constraints like splitting.
- ==Log-binomial probability head== and a trained per-image router select scene-appropriate metric heads (indoor/outdoor experts) atop a shared ==MiDaS==-pretrained relative depth backbone.

## Results

- ZoeD-X-N without relative pre-training beats prior SOTA NeWCRFs by **13.7%** REL on NYU Depth v2; ZoeD-M12-N with pre-training improves it further to **21%** over SOTA.
- ZoeD-M12-NK jointly trained on NYU and KITTI improves absolute relative error by **24.3%** over comparable jointly-trained baselines, while AdaBins and PixelBins fail to converge in that setting.
- Zero-shot evaluation on eight unseen datasets shows up to **976.4%** relative improvement on DIML Outdoor, topping baselines on **7 of 8** datasets without any fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling generalization (relative depth pre-training) from scale calibration (lightweight metric heads) lets one backbone serve many domains without the usual multi-dataset performance collapse.
> - Bin-based, ordinal-aware depth heads (attractors plus log-binomial) consistently outperform naive regression and softmax heads, suggesting ordinality should be modeled explicitly in dense regression tasks.
> - Routing images to domain-expert heads via a learned classifier, rather than requiring labels at inference, can recover most of the benefit of oracle domain knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Single-image depth estimation (SIDE) is a fundamental computer vision task with applications ranging from augmented reality to autonomous driving. However, existing approaches face a critical trade-off: metric depth estimation (MDE) models provide absolute depth measurements but struggle to generalize across diverse environments, while relative depth estimation (RDE) models generalize well but lack metric meaning.

ZoeDepth, developed by researchers from King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST) and Intel, addresses this challenge by combining the strengths of both approaches. This novel framework achieves both excellent generalization across diverse environments and maintains metric scale accuracy, making it suitable for real-world applications where depth perception is crucial.

### 2. Background and Context

The field of single-image depth estimation has evolved significantly in recent years. Traditional approaches fall into two categories:

1. **Metric Depth Estimation (MDE)**: These models are trained on datasets with absolute depth measurements, providing metric information needed for tasks like navigation and object manipulation. However, they typically overfit to specific environments and struggle with generalization.

2. **Relative Depth Estimation (RDE)**: These models, exemplified by MiDaS, excel at generalizing across diverse scenes by learning scale-invariant depth relationships. However, they lack absolute scale information necessary for many applications.

Previous attempts to bridge this gap have not succeeded in achieving both high metric accuracy and strong generalization capabilities simultaneously. The persistent challenge has been how to leverage the generalization power of relative depth learning while maintaining metric information.

### 3. The ZoeDepth Architecture

ZoeDepth employs a two-stage approach that synergistically combines relative and metric depth estimation:

The architecture consists of:

1. **Encoder-Decoder Backbone**: Based on MiDaS, this component uses transformer backbones (BEiT or Swin) and is pre-trained on diverse relative depth datasets to learn robust features that generalize well across different environments.

2. **Domain-Specific Heads**: Multiple metric depth prediction heads based on the novel "Metric Bins Module" are fine-tuned on specific datasets (e.g., NYU Depth v2 for indoor scenes, KITTI for outdoor driving scenes) to learn absolute scale information.

3. **Automatic Router**: A latent classifier that automatically directs input images to the appropriate domain-specific head during inference, enabling seamless handling of diverse environments without manual intervention.

The combination of these components enables ZoeDepth to perform effectively across varied environments while maintaining metric accuracy. The architecture can be configured with different transformer backbones, with BEiT-Large (345M parameters) providing the best performance.

### 4. The Metric Bins Module

A key innovation in ZoeDepth is the Metric Bins Module, which represents a significant advancement over previous binning-based approaches like AdaBins and LocalBins:

The module works as follows:

1. **Initial Bin Prediction**: Rather than splitting bins (as in LocalBins), the module predicts all bin centers directly at the bottleneck of the network.

2. **Attractor Layers**: These novel layers refine bin centers by moving them toward "attractor points" predicted from multi-scale features in the decoder. This is a contracting process that concentrates predictions in areas of depth complexity.

3. **Log-Binomial Distribution**: Instead of softmax, a binomial distribution is used to predict probabilities for bin centers, promoting ordinal relationships between bins and improving performance.

The mathematical formulation for the final depth prediction at each pixel is:

$$D(p) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{bins}} P_i(p) \cdot c_i$$

Where $P_i(p)$ is the probability of bin $i$ at pixel $p$, and $c_i$ is the corresponding bin center. This approach enables more accurate depth estimation, particularly at depth discontinuities and complex scenes.

### 5. Training Methodology

ZoeDepth employs a sophisticated training methodology:

1. **Relative Depth Pre-training**: The encoder-decoder backbone is pre-trained on a diverse collection of relative depth datasets, learning scale-invariant depth relationships that generalize well across environments.

2. **Metric Depth Fine-tuning**: Domain-specific heads are fine-tuned on metric depth datasets to learn absolute scale information for particular environments.

3. **Joint Training Strategies**: Different configurations are explored:
 - Single-head models (ZoeD-M12-N, ZoeD-M12-K) fine-tuned for specific domains (NYU or KITTI)
 - Multi-head models (ZoeD-M12-NK) jointly trained on multiple domains with automatic routing

The loss function combines scale-invariant and scale-dependent terms to ensure both relative depth relationships and metric accuracy:

- Scale-invariant gradient matching loss
- Scale-dependent L1 loss
- Virtual normal loss for planar surfaces
- Classification loss for the router (in multi-head models)

This comprehensive training approach enables ZoeDepth to effectively combine the generalization capabilities of relative depth estimation with the metric accuracy of domain-specific fine-tuning.

### 6. Performance and Results

ZoeDepth demonstrates significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods across multiple benchmarks:

On the NYU Depth v2 benchmark for indoor scenes, ZoeD-M12-N achieves a 21% improvement in relative absolute error (REL) over the previous state-of-the-art method (NeWCRFs). The performance gains are even more pronounced on challenging scenes with complex geometry and depth discontinuities.

The multi-head model (ZoeD-M12-NK) maintains strong performance on both NYU Depth v2 and KITTI datasets without significant degradation compared to the single-head models, demonstrating the effectiveness of the automatic routing mechanism.

Qualitative results show that ZoeDepth produces more detailed and accurate depth maps, particularly for fine structures and depth boundaries. The error visualization confirms that ZoeDepth's predictions are consistently closer to ground truth across diverse scenes.

### 7. Zero-Shot Generalization

One of the most significant contributions of ZoeDepth is its unprecedented zero-shot generalization performance:

When evaluated on eight unseen datasets spanning both indoor and outdoor environments, ZoeD-M12-NK demonstrates substantial improvements over previous methods, with some metrics improving by up to 976%. This remarkable generalization capability is attributed to:

1. The effective transfer of knowledge from relative depth pre-training
2. The robust feature extraction capabilities of the transformer backbone
3. The automatic routing mechanism that correctly identifies the appropriate domain

The zero-shot performance is particularly impressive for complex environments that differ significantly from the training data, such as indoor scenes with unique lighting conditions or outdoor scenes with unfamiliar objects.

### 8. Practical Applications

The advancements introduced by ZoeDepth have significant implications for numerous real-world applications:

1. **Robotics**: Improved depth perception enables more precise navigation and object manipulation in diverse environments without requiring environment-specific training.

2. **Augmented Reality**: Better depth estimation facilitates more realistic integration of virtual objects into real scenes with accurate occlusion and lighting effects.

3. **Autonomous Driving**: Enhanced zero-shot generalization capabilities allow vehicles to accurately perceive depth in new environments, improving safety and reliability.

4. **3D Reconstruction**: More accurate depth maps lead to higher-quality 3D reconstructions from single images, useful for digital twins and virtual environment creation.

The public availability of the code and pre-trained models further accelerates the adoption of ZoeDepth for these applications, allowing researchers and developers to build upon this work.

### 9. Conclusion

ZoeDepth represents a significant advancement in single-image depth estimation by successfully bridging the gap between relative and metric depth estimation. By combining relative depth pre-training with metric fine-tuning through a novel Metric Bins Module and automatic routing mechanism, ZoeDepth achieves both excellent generalization across diverse environments and high metric accuracy.

The key contributions of ZoeDepth include:

1. A novel framework that effectively leverages the strengths of both relative and metric depth estimation
2. The Metric Bins Module with attractor layers for more accurate bin center prediction
3. An automatic routing mechanism for seamless handling of diverse environments
4. State-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks with a 21% improvement over previous methods
5. Unprecedented zero-shot generalization capabilities across unseen datasets

These advancements make ZoeDepth particularly valuable for real-world applications that require robust depth perception across varying environments. As computer vision continues to evolve, the principles demonstrated in ZoeDepth—effectively combining complementary approaches and leveraging pre-training for generalization—will likely influence future research in depth estimation and related fields.
