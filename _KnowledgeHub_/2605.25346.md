# Parallel Differentiable Reachability for Learning and Planning with Certified Neural Dynamics and Controllers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Georgia Institute of Technology developed DiffReach, a framework that unifies Taylor model-based reachability with CROWN-style neural network verification. This enables certified training of neural dynamics and controllers, and facilitates robust, reachability-aware online planning for robotic systems operating under uncertainty.

## Problem

- Providing reliable safety guarantees for robotic systems that use neural network dynamics and controllers is challenging due to their black-box nature and inherent uncertainties.
- Existing reachability analysis and neural network verification tools are often computationally expensive, non-differentiable, and difficult to integrate into online learning and planning workflows.
- Formal verification typically functions as an inefficient post-hoc step, requiring iterative retraining and re-verification when safety properties are not met.

## Method

- The **DiffReach** framework integrates parallel and differentiable reachability by unifying ==Taylor model-based flowpipe construction== with ==CROWN-style neural network verification== within a single JAX computation graph.
- Reachable sets are represented using fixed-shape Taylor Models, which are propagated through continuous-time analytical dynamics, and through neural network components using a CROWN-based approach that preserves affine dependencies.
- **DiffReach-Robotics** applies this engine to enable certified training of neural dynamics and controllers via ==reachability regularization==, and for robust online planning through a reachability-aware ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== scheme.

## Results

- The DiffReach engine is approximately **100 times** faster than existing discrete-time tools like CROWN while maintaining comparable tightness, and it produces substantially tighter reachable sets than continuous-time tools like immrax at similar runtimes.
- Certified training with reachability regularization yields neural dynamics and controllers that maintain nominal performance while consistently producing tighter and more verifiable reachable sets compared to non-certified models.
- Reachability-aware MPC, utilizing certified models, achieves higher success rates under significant dynamics and control disturbances compared to vanilla MPC, with practical online planning rates of around **10 Hz** on hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying Taylor models for continuous dynamics with CROWN-style verification for neural networks within a single, differentiable JAX framework allows for efficient, sound, and GPU-accelerated reachability analysis.
> - Exposing differentiable reachable-set objectives enables direct integration of formal safety guarantees into the training of neural dynamics and controllers, leading to 'verified-by-construction' models.
> - Reachable-set information can be effectively incorporated into Model Predictive Control for online planning, enabling robust decision-making that explicitly accounts for bounded uncertainty and avoids unsafe regions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The presented work addresses the integration of formal safety guarantees with neural network (NN) based dynamics models and control policies in robotics. Robotic systems frequently employ NNs due to their capacity to model complex, nonlinear dynamics, especially in environments where analytical models are difficult to derive. While NNs demonstrate strong empirical performance, providing sound robustness and safety guarantees under uncertainty for NN-in-the-loop systems remains a significant challenge. This difficulty arises from the high-dimensional nonlinearities introduced by NNs, which complicate worst-case analysis.

Classical reachability analysis offers formal guarantees by computing over-approximations of reachable sets, encompassing all possible states a system might occupy under given initial conditions and disturbances. However, these classical methods often suffer from scalability issues, struggle with NN components, and can produce overly conservative bounds.

Concurrently, modern neural network verification (NNV) techniques, such as CROWN and abstract interpretation, have advanced the scalability of bound propagation through large networks. Nevertheless, NNV methods are primarily designed for static verification tasks and do not inherently handle continuous-time dynamical systems. Recent efforts have combined reachability analysis with NNV to support NN-controlled systems, but these integrations typically operate offline, are non-differentiable, and are challenging to integrate into contemporary robot learning and online planning pipelines. Consequently, formal verification often acts as a post-hoc certification step, leading to iterative cycles of non-certified training, failed verification, and subsequent retraining, which is inefficient.

This research aims to bridge these gaps by developing a parallelizable and differentiable reachability framework. It seeks to enable efficient, integrated, and continuous verification throughout the learning and planning processes, moving beyond the traditional offline, post-hoc application of reachability analysis. By making reachability differentiable and efficient, the authors intend for verification to directly inform the design and training of certified models, thereby fostering "verified-by-construction" robot learning and planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges of providing reliable safety guarantees for robotic systems that increasingly rely on neural network (NN) dynamics models and control policies. Despite the empirical performance of NNs in complex robotic tasks, their black-box nature and the presence of uncertainty (e.g., sensing errors, modeling errors, external disturbances) make formal safety assurances difficult. The limitations of existing reachability and NN verification tools—specifically their non-differentiability, computational expense, conservatism, and incompatibility with online learning and planning—hinder their practical application in developing certifiable robotic systems.

To address these issues, the authors define three interconnected problems that guide their research:

**Problem 1: Reachability-aware planning.**
The objective is to enable high-level planning that considers bounded uncertainty. This involves computing sound over-approximations of reachable sets over finite horizons for discrete-time predictive dynamics, where these sets account for initial uncertainty propagation. The planner must optimize a sequence of actions to achieve task objectives while ensuring that these reachable sets satisfy robustness and safety constraints (e.g., collision avoidance). A crucial aspect is the computational efficiency of reachability analysis, as it must support repeated online replanning within a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework. The motivation is to move beyond nominal trajectory planning, which can fail under real-world disturbances, towards robust planning that formally guarantees safety envelopes.

**Problem 2: Certified learning of dynamics and control.**
This objective focuses on developing methods to train NN components (dynamics models and controllers) that are not only performant but also robust to bounded uncertainty. The goal is to incorporate reachable-set information directly into the learning process, using differentiable summaries of reachable sets as regularizers or constraints during training. This aims to produce models that are inherently "reachability-friendly" or "verified-by-construction," thereby reducing the need for costly post-hoc verification and retraining cycles. The motivation is to embed formal guarantees into the model acquisition phase, ensuring that learned behaviors are robust from their inception.

**Problem 3: Differentiable reachability engine.**
This is the foundational objective: to design a robust, efficient, and versatile reachability engine that underpins the solutions to Problems 1 and 2. The engine must be capable of handling both continuous-time and discrete-time systems, accommodate NN-based dynamics and controllers, and support exogenous reference inputs. It needs to provide sound reachable-set over-approximations with sufficient efficiency for iterative learning and online planning. Critically, the engine must expose differentiable reachable-set objectives to facilitate gradient-based optimization in downstream tasks like model training and MPC. The motivation is to create a core computational tool that overcomes the scalability and differentiability limitations of prior methods, enabling the practical integration of formal verification into modern robot learning and control pipelines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, termed DiffReach, integrates parallel and differentiable reachability into robot learning and planning by unifying Taylor-model (TM) based reachability with CROWN-style neural network verification within a single JAX computation graph. This approach consists of a core reachability engine (DiffReach) and robotics-specific applications (DiffReach-Robotics).

**IV. DiffReach: A GPU-Parallelized and Differentiable Reachability Engine**

The core engine is designed to perform efficient, sound, and differentiable reachability analysis.

**A. Representation of Reachable Sets:**
Reachable sets are represented using Taylor Models (TMs), which combine a polynomial approximation with an interval remainder to soundly bound truncation errors. To enable GPU acceleration and automatic differentiation, the authors adopt a fixed-shape TM representation. This restricts the polynomial component to linear or quasi-quadratic forms, expressed as `F(z) = c + Az ⊕ I` or `F(z) = c + Az + Bᵀz ⊕ I`. This design maintains a fixed `O(n²)` representation complexity, facilitating GPU-batched computation and JIT compilation. The affine term `Az` preserves first-order correlations, while the quasi-quadratic term captures limited curvature efficiently.

**B. Propagation under Continuous-Time Analytical Dynamics:**
For continuous-time (CT) analytical dynamics, DiffReach employs flowpipe construction. This method propagates the initial uncertain state set through a sequence of TMs over small time intervals. Each interval's solution is rigorously bounded using truncated Picard iteration, which computes a k-th order polynomial approximation and refines an interval remainder until it is self-consistent and contractive. This iterative procedure generates a certified reachable tube over a finite horizon. Techniques like affine normalization and reparameterization are used to mitigate the accumulation of over-approximation errors over long horizons.

**C. Neural Components via CROWN:**
Direct TM propagation through neural networks (NNs) can be overly conservative for linear TMs or computationally prohibitive for higher-order TMs. To address this, DiffReach leverages CROWN (Complete Verification using Randomized Optimization for Neural networks), a backward bound propagation method known for yielding tighter affine bounds by considering downstream network layers.
A key insight is that a linear TM (`p(z) ⊕ I`) can be viewed as a pair of affine bounds with a shared slope. DiffReach enforces this shared-slope constraint within CROWN to maintain the linear TM representation for NN outputs. For NN evaluation on set-valued TM inputs, a reparameterization technique is used: the TM input `g(z) = c_g + A_g z ⊕ I_g` is transformed into an equivalent box-constrained input for CROWN by treating `z` and the interval remainder `r ∈ I_g` as separate inputs to an augmented network `f_NN(c_g + A_g z + r)`. This approach allows CROWN to act as a drop-in module for neural components while preserving affine dependencies and end-to-end differentiability.

**D. Closed-Loop Reachability for Continuous-Time Systems:**
For CT systems under neural feedback control, DiffReach alternates between two steps over each control interval `[iδ, (i+1)δ]`:
1. **Controller certification:** At the start of an interval, the TM-CROWN interface is applied to the TM enclosure of the current state `x(iδ)` to obtain a certified linear-TM enclosure of the controller output `u_ctl = f_ct,ctl(x(iδ))`.
2. **Dynamics propagation:** With the certified control input treated as constant (zero-order hold), the system dynamics `f_ct,dyn` are propagated over `[iδ, (i+1)δ]` using the TM flowpipe method. This involves augmenting the state with `u_ctl` and setting its derivative to zero. This procedure ensures a unified TM-based representation for both controller and dynamics, avoiding intermediate interval conversions that discard crucial affine dependencies.

**E. Discrete-Time Systems:**
For discrete-time (DT) systems, reachability simplifies to stepwise propagation. At each time step `k`, the TM-CROWN interface is applied to certify the one-step map `x_{k+1} = f_dt,dyn(x_k, u_k)`, yielding a linear-TM enclosure of the next reachable set. This removes the inner-loop integration of CT systems, resulting in a more efficient scheme suitable for batched rollouts in planning and trajectory optimization.

**V. DiffReach-Robotics: Reachability-Aware Model Learning and Planning**

Building upon the differentiable reachability primitive, DiffReach-Robotics applies this engine to two key areas:

**A. Certified Training of Neural Dynamics and Controllers:**
The framework introduces a certified training method that augments standard prediction or tracking losses with reachability regularization.
- **Training DT Neural Dynamics:** A prediction loss (`L_pred`) is minimized, emphasizing long-horizon consistency with time-increasing weights. This is combined with a reachability-aware loss (`L_reach`) that penalizes the "tube-volume proxy" (sum of widths of reachable sets) computed by the DT reachability engine. The total objective `L_total = L_pred + λ L_reach` guides the model to produce compact certified reachable sets.
- **Curriculum Learning:** To stabilize training for large horizons and perturbation radii, a curriculum strategy is employed. The rollout horizon `T_h` is progressively increased, and the perturbation radius `ϵ` is gradually decreased from a larger initial value. This ensures robust and reachability-friendly dynamics.
- **Training CT Neural Controllers:** Similarly, CT neural controllers are trained using a tracking loss (`L_track`) augmented by `L_reach`, leveraging the CT NN-control reachability engine.

**B. Reachability-Aware MPC:**
DiffReach-Robotics integrates the DT reachability engine into a sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme.
- **Planning:** For each sampled action sequence, the nominal dynamics are rolled out, and the corresponding reachable sets are computed using the DT reachability engine. The objective function for MPC includes both task completion cost and a penalty (`max(0, -G(R_t))`) for unsafe reachable sets or large reachable-set sizes.
- **Optimization:** Parallelization facilitates efficient evaluation of many candidate trajectories. Gradient-based refinement is applied to the top candidates in the final iteration to further reduce reachable-set size.
- **Receding Horizon:** The planner operates within a receding-horizon MPC loop, passing planned actions and states as references to a low-level CT neural controller. The approach offers guarantees with respect to the learned dynamics, with potential for incorporating model-error bounds for real-world application.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the DiffReach framework and DiffReach-Robotics applications was conducted across four main areas, demonstrating its efficiency, scalability, and ability to improve robustness in learning and planning.

**A. Reachability Tool Evaluations:**
- **Runtime and Tightness:** Benchmarking against existing JAX-based reachability tools showed that DiffReach offers competitive or superior performance.
 - **Discrete-Time (DT):** Compared to CROWN (a DT reachability tool), DiffReach achieved comparable reachable-set tightness while being approximately 100 times faster. CROWN exhibited slightly tighter tubes due to joint propagation across time steps, whereas DiffReach uses truncated propagation.
 - **Continuous-Time (CT):** Against immrax (a CT reachability tool), DiffReach produced substantially tighter reachable sets with comparable runtime. immrax frequently yielded infinite-volume tubes (up to 95% of trials), indicating numerical instability or severe over-approximation, even after input partitioning. DiffReach provided more stable and tighter over-approximations.
- **Refinement Strategies:** The differentiable and GPU-parallel implementation enabled practical refinement strategies. Input splitting significantly reduced reachable-set volume with a modest increase in runtime. Gradient-based refinement further improved tightness without altering the core algorithm, suggesting its utility beyond just verification.

**B. Scalability on Higher-Dimensional Systems:**
- **6-Quadrotor Swarm (72D state, 18D action):** DiffReach effectively handled this higher-dimensional system, producing reasonable over-approximations (average 1.37x expansion on position dimensions compared to dense sampling) with efficient runtime. The runtime for 6 quadrotors remained close to that of a single quadrotor, indicating favorable scaling in practice despite the `O(n²)` memory complexity of the affine representation.
- **10-Joint Planar Arm (40D state, 10D action):** For this articulated system, reachable sets were initially more conservative without splitting. However, parallelized input splitting (e.g., 64 or 512 splits) substantially improved end-effector reachable widths while maintaining practical runtime. These results suggest the framework's scalability to moderately high-dimensional systems, with tightness benefiting from refinement strategies.

**C. Certified Training:**
- **Nominal Performance vs. Reachable-Set Tightness:** Certified training, applied to both CT and DT dynamics models and controllers for T-pushing and quadrotor tasks, maintained nominal prediction or tracking performance comparable to standard (non-certified) training. This indicates that the reachability regularization did not lead to significant performance degradation.
- **Tighter Reachable Sets:** Models trained with reachability regularization consistently produced substantially tighter and more verifiable reachable sets, aligning more closely with sampled lower-bound volumes. In contrast, models trained without this regularization yielded looser and less verifiable predictions. Visualizations confirmed that realized trajectories closely aligned with the certified reachable regions.
- **Long-Horizon Growth:** On the quadrotor task, DT reachability demonstrated relative stability in reachable-volume growth over longer horizons. CT reachability, however, experienced more substantial growth in conservativeness. Parallelized splitting of critical states (roll, pitch, yaw) improved tightness and reduced the fraction of unstable CT trajectories (from 42% to 24%), ultimately decreasing the long-horizon (5x) volume ratio from 18.6154 to 6.2407.

**D. Reachability-Aware MPC:**
- **Robustness under Disturbances:** Reachability-aware MPC, utilizing certified models, achieved higher success rates under larger dynamics and control disturbances compared to vanilla MPC with non-certified models. While performance was similar under no or small disturbances, the vanilla MPC baseline degraded significantly with increased uncertainty, particularly on the quadrotor task. This demonstrates the framework's ability to improve robustness without excessive conservatism in nominal settings.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations of MPC rollouts on T-pushing and quadrotor tasks (Figure 1) showed that the planner successfully navigated towards goals while respecting state constraints and maintaining tight certified reachable-set over-approximations. An example of vanilla MPC failure (Figure 7) underscored the importance of integrating reachable-set information to avoid unintended contacts and trajectory deviations under uncertainty.
- **Hardware Demonstration:** A qualitative hardware rollout on a Kuka iiwa platform for the T-pushing task demonstrated the practical feasibility of online planning (approximately 10 Hz, 5 Hz with gradient refinement) and smooth execution with the differentiable reachability framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a parallel, differentiable reachability framework that significantly advances the integration of formal verification into modern robot learning and control pipelines. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified Framework for Certified Systems:** The work unifies Taylor-model (TM) flowpipe construction for continuous-time analytical dynamics with CROWN-style linear bound propagation for neural network components. This integration, achieved within a single JAX-based representation that preserves affine dependencies, allows for efficient, GPU-accelerated, and automatically differentiable reachability analysis for both continuous and discrete-time systems with NN-based dynamics and controllers. This unification is a key step towards building verifiable-by-construction learning systems.

- **Enabling Certified Learning:** By exposing differentiable reachable-set objectives, the framework facilitates certified training methods for neural dynamics models and controllers. This means that formal guarantees can directly inform the model learning process, rather than being a post-hoc verification step. This can lead to the development of inherently more robust models that are less prone to safety violations under uncertainty, potentially reducing costly cycles of training and re-verification.

- **Robust Online Planning:** The integration of the differentiable reachability engine into a reachability-aware Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme allows for online planning that actively considers and mitigates uncertainty. By incorporating reachable-set penalties into the optimization objective and leveraging parallel evaluation and gradient-based refinement, the MPC can generate trajectories that are formally safe and robust, even in the presence of bounded disturbances. This has a direct impact on the reliability and deployability of autonomous robotic systems in safety-critical applications.

- **Scalability and Practicality:** The experimental results demonstrate that the framework can scale to moderately high-dimensional robotic systems (e.g., 72D quadrotor swarm, 40D robotic arm) while maintaining practical runtimes and providing reasonable over-approximations. The hardware demonstration further validates the real-time applicability of the approach in robotic control, moving formal verification closer to practical deployment.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** While the framework currently focuses on smooth or learned dynamics and faces challenges with long-horizon conservatism and hybrid or contact-rich dynamics, it lays a foundation for future research. It motivates further exploration into scalable differentiable reachability, tighter integration with formal verification techniques, and application to more complex, real-world robotic scenarios. This includes extending the methods to address non-smooth dynamics, incorporating more sophisticated uncertainty models, and developing adaptive refinement strategies for highly complex systems.

In essence, this work represents a step towards creating more reliable and provably safe autonomous systems by making formal verification an integral, rather than a peripheral, part of the robot learning and planning paradigm.
