# 3D AffordanceNet: A Benchmark for Visual Object Affordance Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from South China University of Technology, I2R (A*STAR), Pazhou Laboratory, and Peng Cheng Laboratory introduce 3D AffordanceNet, the first large-scale 3D point cloud benchmark for visual object affordance understanding. This dataset, comprising 22,949 shapes with 56,307 fine-grained probabilistic affordance annotations across 18 classes, reveals that current 3D deep learning models struggle significantly with partial views and arbitrary rotations, while demonstrating the efficacy of semi-supervised learning for this challenging task.

## Problem

- Existing 2D and 2.5D visual affordance datasets lack crucial 3D geometric information for functional understanding, often failing to capture specific geometric properties vital for interactions, and no longer pose significant challenges to modern computer vision models.
- A dedicated large-scale 3D point cloud benchmark for visual affordance understanding was absent, despite growing interest in 3D data and its relevance to robotics and embodied AI.
- Existing 3D datasets like PartNet focus on part segmentation, which often does not perfectly align with human-perceived affordances, requiring a new approach for functionally-oriented annotations.

## Method

- A custom web-based 3D Graphical User Interface (GUI) was developed to annotate 22,949 ==3D point cloud== shapes from PartNet with 56,307 ==probabilistic point-wise affordance labels== for 18 distinct affordance classes, utilizing a ==label propagation== method to generate dense ground truth from sparse keypoint annotations.
- Three challenging benchmarking tasks were defined: ==full-shape==, ==partial-view== (synthesized from four camera viewpoints), and ==rotation-invariant== (z/z and SO(3)/SO(3)) affordance estimation, using state-of-the-art networks (**PointNet++**, **DGCNN**, **U-Net**) as baselines.
- A ==semi-supervised learning== benchmark was established, using ==Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT)== with DGCNN, to explore mitigating the high cost of 3D data annotation by leveraging vast amounts of unlabeled data.

## Results

- State-of-the-art point cloud networks (PointNet++, DGCNN, U-Net) achieved relatively low performance on 3D AffordanceNet (e.g., **~19% aIoU** for full-shape estimation), indicating that 3D affordance estimation remains a challenging problem that current models struggle to solve comprehensively.
- Performance decreased for partial-view affordance estimation and dropped significantly by **5-10%** for SO(3)/SO(3) arbitrary rotations, highlighting that existing 3D networks are not inherently robust to arbitrary 3D object orientations for fine-grained functional understanding.
- The semi-supervised approach, trained with only **1%** labeled data, significantly outperformed a fully supervised baseline on the same limited data, demonstrating its practical value in leveraging unlabeled data to improve performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-perceived affordances often do not align perfectly with predefined semantic parts, as empirically validated by low Intersection Over Union (IoU) scores between annotated affordances and PartNet parts (e.g., 6.8% for 'wrap-grasp').
> - 3D geometric information, including details from self-occluded parts, is indispensable for comprehensive affordance understanding, as models trained on full 3D data significantly outperformed those using multi-view or single-view partial data.
> - Semi-supervised learning, leveraging unlabeled data, can significantly improve performance and reduce the substantial cost of 3D annotation for affordance estimation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The concept of "affordance," originally introduced by psychologist J.J. Gibson, refers to the possibilities for action that an environment offers an animal. In the context of robotics and computer vision, visual affordance understanding—the ability to infer potential interactions with objects from visual cues—is a critical capability for intelligent agents operating in dynamic and complex environments. This understanding supports a wide range of applications, including anticipating future actions, recognizing agent activities, understanding object functionality, interpreting social scenes, and deciphering the hidden values of objects.

Prior research in visual affordance understanding has primarily focused on 2D (RGB images) and 2.5D (RGB-D images) domains. Datasets such as UMD, CAD120, and IIT-AFF have facilitated the development of algorithms for affordance recognition, detection, and segmentation using deep neural networks. However, these 2D and 2.5D datasets suffer from inherent limitations. They often lack the rich 3D geometric information vital for a truly functional understanding of object interactions. For instance, the "grasp" affordance is intimately tied to specific 3D structures (e.g., a vertical handle with a small perimeter), and "sittable" relates to flat surfaces with particular dimensions. These detailed geometric properties are either not captured adequately by 2D images or are represented with insufficient depth variation in 2.5D data, especially when objects are partially occluded or viewed from limited angles. Consequently, existing datasets no longer pose significant challenges to modern computer vision techniques, impeding further progress in real-world robotic applications.

The recent surge in 3D point cloud data (from LiDAR, Structure-from-Motion, etc.) and its applications in robotics and autonomous driving has shifted research focus towards the 3D domain. Large-scale 3D model repositories like ShapeNet and its derivatives, such as PartNet, have emerged, offering fine-grained semantic segmentation capabilities for 3D shapes. While PartNet acknowledged affordance as a potential application, no dedicated benchmark specifically for visual affordance prediction on 3D data existed. Previous attempts at 3D shape functionality understanding were limited in scope and lacked a direct connection to the well-established visual affordance understanding in 2D/2.5D domains.

This paper addresses this crucial gap by introducing 3D AffordanceNet, the first large-scale 3D point cloud benchmark explicitly designed for visual affordance understanding. By providing a comprehensive dataset with probabilistic affordance annotations on 3D object shapes, this work aims to catalyze research into 3D-native affordance estimation, which is essential for advancing embodied AI and robotics towards more intelligent and human-like interaction capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a comprehensive benchmark for visual object affordance understanding in the 3D physical domain. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the 2D/2.5D to 3D Gap:** The authors contend that a truly functional understanding of object affordance, crucial for vision-guided robotics, necessitates learning and prediction in the 3D domain. Current 2D and 2.5D datasets are inadequate as they fail to capture the geometric properties essential for nuanced interactions. The aim is to move beyond mere visual recognition to a deeper, physically grounded comprehension.
2. **Addressing the Absence of 3D Affordance Benchmarks:** Despite the growing interest in 3D data and its relevance to affordance, a large-scale, explicit 3D dataset specifically tailored for affordance understanding has been conspicuously absent. Existing 3D datasets like PartNet focus on part segmentation, which, as the authors demonstrate, does not perfectly align with human-perceived affordances (e.g., the 'pour' affordance on a mug might not correspond to a single, neatly defined part).
3. **Providing a Challenging and Diverse Benchmark:** The goal is to create a dataset and associated tasks that genuinely challenge state-of-the-art 3D perception models. This includes evaluating performance not only on full 3D shapes but also under more realistic conditions such as partial views and arbitrary object rotations, mimicking real-world robotic scenarios where objects are often incompletely observed or arbitrarily oriented.
4. **Enabling Probabilistic and Point-wise Affordance Estimation:** The benchmark aims to support the prediction of point-wise probabilistic scores for affordances, moving beyond simple categorization or coarse segmentation. This fine-grained understanding is critical for precise robot manipulation and interaction.
5. **Exploring Semi-Supervised Learning:** Recognizing that 3D annotation is an expensive and labor-intensive process, a key motivation is to investigate the potential of semi-supervised learning techniques to leverage vast amounts of unlabeled data, thereby reducing the reliance on costly, fully supervised annotation.

In essence, the authors are motivated to lay a new foundation for 3D visual affordance research by providing the necessary data and evaluation protocols, thereby facilitating the development of more robust and intelligent robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers around the construction of the 3D AffordanceNet dataset and the design of several challenging benchmarking tasks.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction: 3D AffordanceNet

1. **Data Source:** The foundation of 3D AffordanceNet is derived from 3D shapes present in PartNet, which itself collects models from ShapeNet and 3D Warehouse. This provides a diverse collection of common indoor objects. The final dataset includes 22,949 shapes from 23 semantic object categories.
2. **Affordance Type Definition:** To ensure relevance and consistency, the authors reviewed existing visual affordance research and selected 18 affordance categories suitable for 3D objects in PartNet. These include 'grasp', 'lift', 'contain', 'open', 'lay', 'sit', 'support', 'wrap-grasp', 'pour', 'display', 'push', 'pull', 'listen', 'wear', 'press', 'cut', 'stab', and 'move'. Specific affordances were associated with each object category (e.g., a chair is 'sittable' but not 'layable').
3. **Annotation Process:** A critical component was the development of a custom web-based 3D Graphical User Interface (GUI) to facilitate annotation. A team of 42 professional annotators was hired. The annotation followed a guided question-answering workflow (illustrated in Fig. 2 and detailed in Appendix A). For each shape, annotators first identified supported affordance classes, then selected at least three keypoints on the object's mesh surface that exemplify each affordance. Importantly, annotators also indicated whether an affordance should propagate beyond the part containing the labeled keypoint, acknowledging that affordances often transcend predefined part boundaries (confirmed by low PartNet-affordance IoU in Appendix B). Each shape was annotated by three annotators to capture diversity and address subjectivity.
4. **Ground-Truth Construction via Label Propagation:** To obtain dense, point-wise probabilistic affordance scores from sparse keypoint annotations, a label propagation method was employed. For each shape, N points were densely sampled from its mesh surface. A k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN) graph was constructed on these points, forming an adjacency matrix. This matrix was then normalized and used in a closed-form solution (S = (I - α * ˜W)^-1 * Y, where Y is a one-hot label vector for keypoints) to propagate labels to all sampled points. The resulting scores S were linearly normalized to a range, representing probabilistic affordance scores.
5. **Dataset Statistics:** The final 3D AffordanceNet comprises 56,307 affordance annotations across 18 classes and 23 object categories. A key feature is its multi-label nature, where a single point can be associated with multiple affordances.

#### 3.2. Benchmarking Tasks

The dataset was split into training, validation, and testing sets (70%/10%/20% ratio) based on semantic object categories. Three core tasks were defined:

1. **Full-Shape Affordance Estimation:** This task evaluates a model's ability to predict point-wise probabilistic affordance scores and types given the complete 3D point cloud of an object.
2. **Partial-View Affordance Estimation:** Mimicking real-world scenarios, this task assesses performance when only a partial view of an object is available. Partial point clouds were synthesized by observing shapes from four affine camera viewpoints, followed by furthest point sampling and up-sampling if necessary to maintain 2048 points.
3. **Rotation-Invariant Affordance Estimation:** This task evaluates model robustness to object orientation, a critical aspect for robotics. Two settings were established:
 - **z/z rotation:** Objects are rotated only along the z-axis during both training and inference.
 - **SO(3)/SO(3) rotation:** Objects undergo arbitrary 3D rotations (around x, y, and z axes) during training and inference.

#### 3.3. Baseline Networks and Training

Three state-of-the-art point cloud deep learning networks were selected as baselines:
- **PointNet++**
- **DGCNN**
- **U-Net** (specifically, its architecture pre-trained with PointContrast for 3D understanding, then fine-tuned).

For all tasks, the segmentation branch of these networks served as shared backbones to extract point-wise features. For each of the 18 affordance types, multiple classification heads (FC layers followed by sigmoid) were used to output posterior probabilistic scores. The loss function combined Cross-Entropy (L_CE) and Dice Loss (L_DICE) to effectively handle the class imbalance inherent in affordance data (many points have zero affordance score). Standard training strategies and optimizers (Adam for PointNet++, SGD for DGCNN/U-Net) were employed, with specific learning rate schedules.

#### 3.4. Semi-Supervised Affordance Estimation

To explore mitigating annotation costs, a semi-supervised learning (SSL) benchmark was established.
- **Setup:** Only 1% of the training data was assumed to be labeled, with the rest considered unlabeled.
- **Method:** DGCNN was used as the backbone. Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT), a state-of-the-art SSL framework, was applied. VAT encourages consistency between a model's predictions for an unlabeled sample and its adversarially perturbed augmentation. The total loss function combined L_CE and L_DICE for labeled data with MSE consistency losses for both labeled and unlabeled data.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

Performance across all tasks was quantified using four standard metrics:
- **Mean Average Precision (mAP):** Evaluates overall classification accuracy across different confidence thresholds.
- **Area Under ROC Curve (AUC):** Measures the ability to distinguish between positive and negative classes.
- **Mean Squared Error (MSE):** Quantifies the difference between predicted and ground-truth probabilistic scores.
- **Average Intersection Over Union (aIoU):** Assesses segmentation quality by averaging IoU scores across various binarization thresholds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using the 3D AffordanceNet dataset provided several key insights into the current state and challenges of 3D visual affordance understanding.

- **Affordance Estimation as a Challenging Task:** Across all tasks, the overall performance of state-of-the-art point cloud networks, particularly in terms of aIoU, was relatively low (e.g., around 19% for PointNet++ in full-shape estimation). This indicates that 3D affordance estimation remains a challenging problem that existing networks struggle to solve comprehensively. The analysis of training, validation, and testing set performances (Table 5) showed that both PointNet++ and DGCNN still underfit, suggesting that the task is inherently complex and demands more sophisticated models or learning strategies.
- **Full-Shape Affordance Estimation:** The three baseline networks (PointNet++, DGCNN, U-Net) achieved comparable performance. Qualitatively, some affordances were successfully localized (e.g., 'grasp' on a bag's handles), demonstrating the networks' ability to learn basic interactions. However, significant failures were observed, such as predicting 'pour' on a bottle's lid instead of its body edge, highlighting limitations in understanding fine-grained functional areas.
- **Partial-View Affordance Estimation:** As expected, performance quantitatively decreased compared to full-shape estimation due to the inherent loss of geometric information from partial observations. Despite this, the networks demonstrated reasonable qualitative results, often inferring affordances correctly even from partial views (e.g., 'move' on partially visible table legs). This suggests some capacity for robust inference from incomplete data, although errors occurred when critical parts for an affordance were entirely occluded (e.g., 'contain' on storage furniture).
- **Rotation-Invariant Affordance Estimation:**
 - **z/z Rotation:** A modest performance drop (around 1% across metrics) was observed when objects were rotated only along the z-axis. Qualitatively, most affordances were still correctly identified, though specific errors emerged (e.g., missing the pour tip of a bottle or misidentifying the open area of a microwave).
 - **SO(3)/SO(3) Rotation:** This proved to be a significantly more challenging task, with performance dropping by 5-10% across all metrics. This substantial decline underscores the difficulty of achieving true rotation invariance for complex 3D functional understanding. Qualitative results showed more pronounced errors, such as a complete failure to detect 'contain' on storage furniture, likely due to the highly complex geometric structures interacting with arbitrary rotations. This suggests that existing 3D networks are not inherently robust to arbitrary 3D rotations for fine-grained affordance understanding.
- **Semi-Supervised Affordance Estimation:** The semi-supervised approach using Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT) with DGCNN, when trained with only 1% labeled data, significantly outperformed a fully supervised baseline trained on the same limited labeled data. The gains were particularly notable for affordance categories that initially showed low metrics. This finding is crucial, demonstrating the practical value of leveraging unlabeled data to improve performance and potentially reduce the substantial cost of 3D annotation.
- **Affordance vs. Part Segmentation (Appendix B):** An important finding was the low Intersection Over Union (IoU) between annotated affordances and the best combination of fine-grained parts from the PartNet dataset (e.g., 6.8% for 'wrap-grasp' and 3.5% for 'stab'). This empirically validates the paper's initial motivation: human-perceived affordances often do not perfectly align with predefined semantic parts, thus necessitating dedicated affordance annotations rather than relying on existing part labels. The relatively small variance in annotations across different human annotators also confirmed the learnable patterns in affordance definitions.
- **Importance of 3D Data (Appendix H):** A comparative experiment confirmed the necessity of 3D information. Models trained on full 3D data (3DV) significantly outperformed those trained on multi-view (MTV) or single-view (SGV) partial data. This quantitative evidence supports the argument that 3D geometric properties, including those from self-occluded parts, are indispensable for comprehensive affordance understanding.
- **Benefit of Pre-training (Appendix G):** Fine-tuning a U-Net architecture pre-trained with PointContrast on ScanNet dataset yielded better results than training the U-Net from scratch. This suggests that pre-training on large, diverse 3D datasets can effectively bootstrap performance for downstream tasks like affordance estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of 3D AffordanceNet marks a significant contribution to the fields of computer vision, robotics, and artificial intelligence, offering a novel and much-needed resource for advancing research in visual object affordance understanding.

1. **First Large-Scale 3D Affordance Benchmark:** This work fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale dataset of 3D point clouds annotated with fine-grained, probabilistic affordance information. This directly addresses the limitations of previous 2D and 2.5D benchmarks and the absence of dedicated 3D affordance datasets, thereby establishing a new standard for research in this domain.
2. **Enabling Advanced Robotic Interaction:** Affordance understanding is fundamental for developing truly intelligent robots that can interact effectively and safely with the physical world. By providing a 3D-native benchmark, 3D AffordanceNet will accelerate the development of more capable vision-guided robotic systems, leading to advancements in areas such as manipulation, task planning, human-robot collaboration, and autonomous navigation in complex environments.
3. **Driving New Research Directions:** The benchmark tasks—full-shape, partial-view, and rotation-invariant affordance estimation—highlight significant challenges for current 3D deep learning models. The low performance in certain categories and under arbitrary rotations (SO(3)) will stimulate research into more robust and geometrically aware 3D feature learning architectures, explicit affordance reasoning mechanisms, and better strategies for handling incomplete or varied object observations.
4. **Promoting Practical AI Solutions:** The successful demonstration of semi-supervised learning techniques for affordance estimation is highly impactful. It offers a promising avenue for reducing the prohibitively high cost of data annotation, making it more feasible to create larger and more diverse datasets for real-world applications. This can democratize access to advanced affordance learning for researchers and practitioners.
5. **Validation of 3D Importance:** The empirical evidence demonstrating the superior performance of 3D-based affordance understanding over 2.5D approaches rigorously validates the necessity of rich 3D geometric information. This reinforces the paradigm shift towards 3D-centric perception for tasks requiring a deep functional understanding of objects.
6. **Foundation for Human-Centric AI:** A robot that understands affordances can better predict human intentions and actions, fostering more intuitive and cooperative human-robot interaction. This dataset can therefore serve as a foundation for developing AI systems that are more attuned to human behavior and needs.

In conclusion, 3D AffordanceNet is not just a dataset; it is a meticulously constructed benchmark that will serve as a cornerstone for future research in 3D visual affordance understanding, propelling the field towards more intelligent, adaptive, and human-aware robotic systems.
