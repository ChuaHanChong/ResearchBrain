# Transformers without Normalization

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Dynamic Tanh (DyT), a simple element-wise function designed as a drop-in replacement for normalization layers in Transformers. Models equipped with DyT achieve performance comparable to or exceeding their normalized counterparts across a wide range of tasks including computer vision, large language models, and speech processing, often without requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning.

## Problem

- Modern deep neural networks, especially Transformers, are widely believed to require normalization layers (e.g., Layer Normalization, RMSNorm) for stable and effective training, which can complicate architectures and potentially limit optimization.
- Previous attempts to train deep models without normalization often involve complex initialization schemes, architectural modifications, or extensive hyperparameter tuning, frequently resulting in performance trade-offs or fragile training processes.
- Understanding the precise functional role that makes normalization layers indispensable beyond statistical stabilization remained an open question, hindering simpler alternatives.

## Method

- The paper introduces ==Dynamic Tanh (DyT)==, a simple element-wise operation defined as `DyT(x) = γ * tanh(αx) + β`, where `α` is a ==learnable scalar== and `γ`, `β` are learnable per-channel vectors.
- DyT serves as a direct ==drop-in replacement== for normalization layers like Layer Normalization (LN) and RMSNorm within Transformer architectures, requiring no statistical computation.
- The `tanh` function provides the observed ==S-shaped squashing effect== on extreme values, while the learnable `α` parameter dynamically scales the input to adapt to varying activation ranges.

## Results

- Transformers equipped with **DyT** achieve performance comparable to or slightly surpassing normalized counterparts across diverse tasks, including supervised and self-supervised computer vision (ViT, ConvNeXt, MAE, DINO), diffusion models (DiT), large language models (LLaMA **7B**-**70B**), speech (wav2vec 2.0), and DNA sequence modeling, often without extensive hyperparameter re-tuning.
- **DyT** consistently outperforms other normalization-free methods like Fixup, SkipInit, and σReparam in empirical evaluations on ViT and MAE, demonstrating superior performance with greater simplicity.
- Ablation studies confirm that both the `tanh` squashing function and the learnable `α` parameter are essential for stable training and strong performance, with `α` values correlating with the inverse of input activation standard deviations in trained models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Layer Normalization (LN) and RMSNorm, despite their statistical computation, collectively exhibit an S-shaped, tanh-like input-output mapping across activations in trained deep networks, particularly in deeper layers.
> - The learnable scalar parameter 'α' in DyT dynamically scales input activations similar to how normalization layers adjust magnitudes based on variance, effectively learning to track the inverse of the standard deviation (1/std) of inputs.
> - The functional squashing of extreme activation values via a smooth non-linearity like `tanh`, combined with adaptive scaling, appears to be a critical mechanism for stable training that DyT successfully emulates, rather than complex statistical normalization.
