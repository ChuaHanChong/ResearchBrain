# Prediction with Action: Visual Policy Learning via Joint Denoising Process

> [!summary] Summary
> The Prediction with Action Diffuser (PAD) framework unifies image prediction and robot action generation through a joint denoising process, achieving a 26.3% relative improvement on MetaWorld tasks and demonstrating strong generalization in real-world robot manipulation.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to learn directly from visual observations due to high-dimensional data and complex observation-action mappings.
- Traditional visual policy learning methods either separate state prediction and action generation, leading to compounding errors, or lack explicit understanding of physical dynamics.
- Limited availability of large-scale robotic demonstration data hinders training robust policies.

## Method

- Introduces Prediction with Action Diffuser (PAD), a diffusion-based framework that simultaneously denoises future images and robot actions.
- Utilizes a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) as a backbone to jointly process multiple modalities, including RGB images, robot pose, language instructions, and optional depth data.
- Implements a co-training strategy that leverages both limited robot demonstrations and abundant large-scale video datasets by handling missing modalities via masked attention.

## Results

- Achieved a 26.3% relative improvement in success rate on the MetaWorld benchmark compared to the strongest baseline methods.
- Demonstrated strong generalization capabilities in real-world robot manipulation, notably achieving over 40% success rates on the most challenging unseen tasks.
- Showed that performance scales with increased computational capacity, and generated higher quality, more consistent future visual predictions than prior approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying image prediction and action generation through a joint denoising process allows the model to capture deep correlations and learn underlying physical dynamics.
> - Effective multi-modal integration within a transformer architecture enables the policy to condition on diverse inputs, including natural language instructions.
> - Co-training with general video data provides rich visual dynamics knowledge, significantly enhancing future prediction quality even when action data is absent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Enabling robots to learn directly from visual observations remains a significant challenge in robotics. Traditional approaches often separate the processes of predicting future states and determining actions, potentially missing critical connections between these interdependent tasks. In this paper, researchers from Tsinghua University, Shanghai Qizhi Institute, Shanghai AI Lab, and UC Berkeley introduce Prediction with Action Diffuser (PAD), a framework that unifies image prediction and robot action generation through a joint denoising process.

As shown in the figure above, PAD (c) differs fundamentally from traditional diffusion generative models (a) and standard diffusion policies (b) by jointly denoising multiple modalities including images, robot poses, and potentially other inputs like depth data. This integrated approach leverages the inherent connection between predicting future states and determining appropriate actions, leading to significant performance improvements across various robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Background and Context

Recent advances in diffusion models have revolutionized generative AI, particularly for image and video generation. Meanwhile, in robotics, visual policy learning—teaching robots to act based directly on pixels—has remained challenging due to the high-dimensional nature of visual data and the complex mapping between observations and actions.

Prior approaches typically fall into two categories:
1. Methods that first predict future states and then determine actions based on those predictions
2. Techniques that directly map observations to actions without explicit future prediction

Both approaches have limitations: the former often suffers from compounding errors between modules, while the latter might lack an understanding of physical dynamics. PAD addresses these limitations by unifying both processes in a single framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The PAD Framework

PAD employs a diffusion-based approach that simultaneously denoises future images and robot actions. The core architecture is illustrated below:

The framework consists of several key components:

1. **Multi-Modal Encoders**: Different encoders process various input modalities, including:
 - A pre-trained VAE encoder for images
 - MLP networks for robot pose data
 - A frozen CLIP encoder for language instructions

2. **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)**: The backbone of the system uses transformer blocks with masked multi-head attention to process all modalities jointly.

3. **Denoising Process**: Both images and actions undergo a joint denoising process, where the model progressively removes noise to generate coherent future predictions and action sequences.

The mathematical foundation of PAD builds on the denoising diffusion probabilistic model, where the objective is to learn a model that can reverse a gradual noising process. For a given data point x, noise is added according to:

$$x_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\epsilon$$

Where $\epsilon$ is Gaussian noise, $t$ is the timestep, and $\alpha_t$ is a schedule of noise levels. The model is trained to predict the added noise at each step, allowing it to denoise corrupted inputs.

#### 3.2. Joint Denoising Process

The key innovation of PAD is its joint denoising process that simultaneously handles multiple modalities. Unlike previous approaches that process different modalities separately, PAD treats them as part of a unified system governed by the same underlying physical dynamics.

For each timestep, PAD takes the noised versions of future images and actions and jointly denoises them. This approach has several advantages:

1. It allows the model to capture correlations between visual changes and robot actions
2. Information from one modality can help denoise the other
3. The joint representation learns the physical constraints and dynamics that govern both visual states and actions

The denoising objective can be expressed as:

$$L = E_{x_0, \epsilon, t}[w_{img}||\epsilon_{img} - \epsilon_{\theta}^{img}(x_t, t)||^2 + w_{act}||\epsilon_{act} - \epsilon_{\theta}^{act}(x_t, t)||^2]$$

Where $w_{img}$ and $w_{act}$ are weights balancing the image and action denoising losses, and $\epsilon_{\theta}^{img}$ and $\epsilon_{\theta}^{act}$ are the predicted noise for images and actions respectively.

#### 3.3. Multi-Modal Integration

A critical aspect of PAD is its ability to integrate multiple modalities effectively. The system processes:

1. **RGB Images**: Current visual observations of the environment
2. **Robot Pose**: Current state of the robot's joints and end effector
3. **Language Instructions**: Natural language descriptions of the task
4. **Depth Images**: Additional 3D structure information (when available)

Each modality is encoded into a latent representation and then combined through the DiT architecture. The transformer's self-attention mechanism allows different modalities to interact with each other, capturing complex relationships between them.

This multi-modal approach allows PAD to be conditioned on language instructions, enabling a single policy to solve diverse tasks specified through natural language. As shown in the example below, the model can accurately predict future states based on current observations and language prompts:

The figure demonstrates PAD's ability to predict both RGB and depth images accurately, with predictions closely matching ground truth future states.

#### 3.4. Co-training Strategy

One of the most significant innovations in PAD is its co-training strategy, which allows the model to learn from both robotic demonstrations and large-scale video datasets. This addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: the limited availability of robot demonstration data.

The co-training process works as follows:

1. When training on robot demonstrations, all modalities (images, actions, depth, etc.) are used
2. When training on video data, only the image modality is present, and the action modality is treated as missing
3. The transformer's masked attention mechanism allows it to handle missing modalities gracefully

This approach enables the model to learn rich visual dynamics from abundant video data while still learning action policies from the more limited robotic demonstrations. The effect is particularly noticeable in the quality of future predictions, as shown in the figure above, where co-training with videos produces more accurate and detailed predictions.

The training objective during co-training can be expressed as:

$$L_{robot} = L_{img} + L_{act} + L_{depth} \quad \text{(for robot data)}$$
$$L_{video} = L_{img} \quad \text{(for video data)}$$

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Conclusion

The Prediction with Action Diffuser (PAD) represents a significant advancement in visual policy learning for robotics. By unifying image prediction and action generation through a joint denoising process, PAD captures the deep connections between these tasks and achieves state-of-the-art performance across various benchmarks.

Key contributions of this work include:

1. **Joint Denoising Framework**: A unified approach to visual prediction and action generation that captures underlying physical dynamics
2. **Effective Multi-Modal Integration**: Seamless incorporation of diverse inputs including images, robot states, language instructions, and depth data
3. **Co-training Strategy**: A novel approach to leverage both limited robotic demonstrations and abundant video data
4. **Demonstrated Scalability**: Evidence that performance improves with increased model capacity

PAD's strong generalization capabilities, particularly on unseen tasks, suggest that this approach could be valuable for deploying robots in real-world settings where they will inevitably encounter novel situations. Furthermore, the framework's ability to be conditioned on language instructions enables flexible task specification without requiring retraining for each new task.

As shown in the real-world experiments, PAD can successfully handle complex manipulation tasks involving various objects and scenarios:

This work opens up promising directions for future research, including scaling to even larger models, incorporating additional modalities, and extending the framework to more complex robotic tasks and environments.
