# Fine-R1: Make Multi-modal LLMs Excel in Fine-Grained Visual Recognition by Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University introduce FINE-R1, a Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) that achieves state-of-the-art performance in Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR) with only 4-shot training data per category. This MLLM surpasses specialized discriminative models like SigLIP-L by +5.16% on unseen categories and shows a +23.75% semantic similarity improvement in open-world settings, demonstrating strong generalization and efficient knowledge deployment for subtle visual distinctions.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) underperform specialized computer vision models in Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR) tasks, which require distinguishing subtle visual differences.
- Adapting MLLMs for FGVR is data-inefficient, demanding vast amounts of expensive, fine-grained annotated data and often leading to overfitting and poor generalization to unseen categories.
- FGVR is inherently challenging due to high intra-class variance (diverse appearances within a category) and low inter-class variance (subtle differences between similar categories).

## Method

- FINE-R1 employs a ==two-stage training framework== starting with ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== to teach structured, human-like reasoning for open-world FGVR.
- It then uses ==Triplet Augmented Policy Optimization (TAPO)==, a novel reinforcement learning algorithm that augments each image with positive (same category) and negative (most similar distinct category) examples.
- TAPO incorporates both ==intra-class augmentation== (hybrid sampling for diversity) and ==inter-class augmentation== (KL divergence loss to enhance discriminative ability between similar categories) to refine knowledge deployment with few-shot data.

## Results

- FINE-R1-7B achieved state-of-the-art average accuracy of **91.71%** on seen categories and **85.70%** on unseen categories in closed-world FGVR, surpassing general MLLMs, reasoning MLLMs, and specialized CLIP models.
- In open-world evaluation, FINE-R1-7B established a new state-of-the-art with an average relative semantic similarity of **74.80%**, representing a **+23.75%** improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- The model demonstrates strong generalization to unseen categories, with significant gains (**+15.59%** over SFT in closed-world for FINE-R1-3B), and also improved performance on related non-classification questions (e.g., **+3.60%** on ImageWikiQA) while preserving general VQA capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs do not necessarily lack fine-grained subcategory knowledge but rather struggle with its effective deployment for specific FGVR tasks, as FINE-R1 shows improved knowledge deployment without altering visual features or subcategory encoding.
> - Structured Chain-of-Thought reasoning, when learned through SFT, provides a robust foundation for MLLMs to integrate domain knowledge and perform meticulous visual comparisons, outperforming zero-shot or in-context learning CoT strategies.
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly with triplet-based augmentation, is highly effective in addressing the high intra-class and low inter-class variance challenges of FGVR, leading to superior generalization with limited data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), enabling them to perform various vision-language tasks, such as image captioning and visual question answering, with considerable success. However, existing research indicates that MLLMs typically exhibit a performance degradation when confronted with Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR) tasks. FGVR involves distinguishing subtle differences among visually similar sub-categories, a challenge that often requires extensive domain knowledge and fine-tuned discriminative capabilities. Examples include differentiating between bird species, car models, or plant varieties.

Current approaches to adapting MLLMs for FGVR largely fall into two categories: fine-tuning with classification data or employing training-free methods. Fine-tuning often demands substantial amounts of meticulously annotated data, which is costly and labor-intensive to acquire. Furthermore, MLLMs fine-tuned on such data frequently overfit to categories seen during training, leading to poor generalization when encountering novel or "unseen" sub-categories. This limitation is particularly critical in real-world scenarios where new categories emerge frequently. Consequently, general-purpose MLLMs, including advanced models like GPT-4 and GeminiPro, often underperform compared to specialized contrastive models, such as CLIP, which are explicitly designed for discriminative visual tasks.

Concurrently, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has emerged as a method to enhance the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and MLLMs by providing explicit intermediate steps. CoT strategies, applied through prompting or supervised fine-tuning (SFT), have been shown to improve performance in complex tasks, including visual math reasoning. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has also demonstrated potential in enhancing reasoning and reducing reliance on large annotated datasets for both LLMs and MLLMs. Prior work has explored policy optimization in image classification for MLLMs, but a specific investigation into its application for fine-grained challenges, characterized by high intra-class variance and low inter-class variance, has been less explored.

This work addresses the identified performance gap of MLLMs in FGVR, specifically focusing on data efficiency and generalization to unseen categories. It integrates structured CoT reasoning with a novel reinforcement learning framework to enable MLLMs to effectively deploy their intrinsic knowledge for FGVR while also improving generalization capabilities, even with limited training data. By doing so, this research aims to bridge the performance disparity between general MLLMs and specialized discriminative models in the context of fine-grained recognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capability of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR) tasks, particularly addressing the challenges of data inefficiency and poor generalization to unseen categories.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations regarding the performance of current MLLMs in FGVR:

1. **Struggle with Fine-Grained Distinctions:** While MLLMs demonstrate strong performance on coarse-grained visual tasks, they often struggle with the nuanced distinctions required for FGVR. This task demands identifying subtle visual differences among visually similar sub-categories, which necessitates a deeper level of visual understanding and domain-specific knowledge.

2. **High Data Annotation Costs:** Adapting general-purpose MLLMs to excel in FGVR typically requires large quantities of annotated data. Obtaining such fine-grained annotations is often costly, time-consuming, and resource-intensive, making widespread deployment challenging. This limits the practical applicability of MLLMs in domains where expert annotations are scarce or difficult to obtain for every possible sub-category.

3. **Performance Gap with Specialized Models:** State-of-the-art generative MLLMs frequently underperform when compared to contrastive models like CLIP, which are specifically designed for discriminative tasks. This suggests a fundamental limitation in how general MLLMs process and leverage visual information for highly discriminative classification.

4. **Overfitting and Poor Generalization:** Existing MLLM fine-tuning approaches for FGVR tend to overfit to the sub-categories encountered during training. This results in limited utility in real-world scenarios where models must generalize effectively to recognize novel or "unseen" concepts that were not part of their training data. The ability to identify new categories is crucial for practical applications.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a framework designed to enable MLLMs to better leverage their intrinsic knowledge for FGVR while simultaneously improving generalization to unseen categories with only limited data. Specifically, the research aims to:

- **Develop a structured reasoning process:** Introduce a human-like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning framework (visual analysis, candidate subcategories, comparison, and prediction) to guide MLLMs in FGVR, thereby establishing a foundation for knowledge-integrated reasoning.
- **Enhance robustness and discriminative ability:** Implement a policy optimization algorithm tailored to overcome the inherent challenges of FGVR, namely high intra-class variance (variations within the same sub-category) and low inter-class variance (subtle differences between distinct sub-categories).
- **Improve data efficiency:** Achieve strong FGVR performance and generalization capabilities with only few-shot training samples per category, mitigating the need for extensive annotated datasets.
- **Demonstrate open-world classification:** Transition MLLMs into effective open-world classifiers capable of identifying both seen and unseen sub-categories.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to advance MLLM capabilities in knowledge-intensive domains where fine-grained recognition is critical but data acquisition is arduous.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a two-stage training framework for enhancing MLLMs in fine-grained visual recognition, named Fine-R1. This framework integrates Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-tuning (CoT SFT) with Triplet Augmented Policy Optimization (TAPO).

#### 3.1. Overview

The training process initiates with CoT SFT, which aims to instill foundational FGVR capabilities by training the MLLM on synthesized CoT reasoning data, imitating a human-like analytical process. Following this supervised learning stage, the model undergoes further optimization using Triplet Augmented Policy Optimization (TAPO), a reinforcement learning (RL) approach. TAPO is designed to refine the model's reasoning process, making it robust to intra-class variance and discriminative towards inter-class variance.

#### 3.2. Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-tuning (CoT SFT)

The first stage focuses on enabling the MLLM to perform open-world FGVR by teaching it a structured, human-like reasoning pathway.

- **Open-world CoT Data Construction:**
 - **MLLM for Data Generation:** Qwen2.5-VL-32B is utilized to generate the open-world FGVR CoT data.
 - **Image-level Visual Concept Selection:** For each image and its ground-truth sub-category, the MLLM's captioning ability is employed to produce multiple descriptions highlighting diverse visual attributes. These descriptions are aggregated to approximate discriminative features, and an information bottleneck strategy is applied to retain only the most relevant concepts. This process transforms visual cues into textual representations for reasoning.
 - **Structured CoT Prompt:** The extracted visual concepts are concatenated with the image-question pair. A structured CoT prompt is designed, inspired by human cognition, to decompose the reasoning process into four explicit stages: "visual analysis," "candidate subcategories," "comparison," and "final prediction." The prompt guides the MLLM to first propose likely candidate subcategories and then use textual knowledge to compare them in detail before making a prediction.
 - **Data Reliability Strategies:** To ensure high-quality data (404 samples in total), several strategies are implemented: 1) Multiple responses are sampled until the CoT precisely matches the ground-truth subcategory. 2) Mixed-language CoT responses are manually corrected to English. 3) Predicted subcategories within the CoT rationales are manually checked to ensure they are consistent with the ground truth and included in the candidate subcategories.

- **CoT SFT Process:** The MLLM is fine-tuned on this curated CoT dataset. This process enables the model to integrate domain knowledge effectively when generating candidate subcategories and to perform detailed comparisons, leading to more accurate predictions. The resulting model serves as a robust base for the subsequent RL optimization.

#### 3.3. Triplet Augmented Policy Optimization (TAPO)

Building upon the structured reasoning foundation established by CoT SFT, the second stage employs a modified policy optimization algorithm, Triplet Augmented Policy Optimization (TAPO), derived from DAPO (Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization). The core principle of TAPO is to enhance the model's discriminative ability for low inter-class variance while maintaining robustness against high intra-class variance in FGVR. This is achieved by augmenting each anchor image with a positive and a negative image, forming a triplet T = (x, x_pos, x_neg).

- **Triplet Formulation:**
 - **Anchor Image (x):** The primary image for which a prediction is sought.
 - **Positive Image (x_pos):** Another image from the *same* subcategory as the anchor.
 - **Negative Image (x_neg):** An image from the *most visually similar but distinct* subcategory.

- **Intra-class Augmentation:**
 - **Objective:** Improve robustness to significant intra-class variations.
 - **Mechanism:** For an input pair (x, q), a positive image x_pos from the same category is randomly sampled. The old policy (π_θ_old) generates two sets of rollouts: n1 responses conditioned on (x, q) and n2 responses conditioned on (x_pos, q). All rollouts are then aggregated into a single pool for reward computation.
 - **Policy Update:** Critically, the policy update step remains conditioned only on the anchor (x, q), promoting effective exploration of relevant features.
 - **Advantages:** This approach enhances intra-class diversity modeling by exposing the policy to varied visual perspectives within a class. Discrepancies in rewards between anchor and positive predictions guide the model to focus on fine-grained, image-specific cues, strengthening category-level distinctions.

- **Inter-class Augmentation:**
 - **Objective:** Encourage the policy to generate distinct responses for visually similar images from different subcategories, addressing low inter-class variance.
 - **Mechanism:** The model's ability to distinguish matched from mismatched image-category pairs is quantified by a ratio, `g_inter(θ) = π_θ(o | q, x*) / π_θ(o | q, x_neg)`. A higher ratio indicates that the model assigns a significantly lower probability to the correct output under the negative image, implying learned discriminative features.
 - **Loss Term:** An additional loss term is introduced into the DAPO objective by maximizing the KL divergence between the output distribution conditioned on the anchor/positive image (π_θ(o|q, x*)) and that conditioned on the negative image (π_θ(o|q, x_neg)). This encourages the model to differentiate between similar concepts.
 - **Regularization:** Double entropy regularization is applied to constrain both π_θ and π_neg_θ, ensuring stable and low-entropy distributions.

- **Combined TAPO Objective:** The final TAPO objective combines the augmented policy optimization with the KL-divergence term for inter-class distinction and entropy regularization terms. This holistic objective guides the MLLM to refine its reasoning process, making it both robust and discriminative for FGVR tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments across six FGVR datasets (CaltechUCSD Bird-200, Stanford Car-196, Stanford Dog-120, Flower-102, Oxford-IIIT Pet-37, and FGVC-Aircraft) under a few-shot base-to-new generalization setting.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Closed-world Evaluation:** Fine-R1 demonstrates superior performance in the closed-world setting, where the model must select from a predefined list of categories.
 - Fine-R1-7B achieved an average accuracy of 91.71% on seen categories, which represents a 7.73% improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (83.98%) and a 3.38% improvement over SigLIP-L (88.33%), a strong contrastive CLIP model.
 - For unseen categories, Fine-R1-7B achieved 85.70% accuracy, showing even larger improvements: 9.28% over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (76.42%) and 5.16% over SigLIP-L (80.54%). These results indicate that Fine-R1 effectively leverages knowledge for FGVR and generalizes well to novel categories.
- **Open-world Evaluation:** In the open-world setting, where the model generates categories without constraint, Fine-R1-7B achieved a new state-of-the-art with an average relative semantic similarity of 74.80% with only 4-shot training samples per sub-category. This is a substantial improvement of 23.75% over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (51.05%). The model also demonstrated strong base-to-new category generalization in this setting. The superior performance in both settings is attributed to the CoT guidance, which enhances the model's ability to discern subtle discriminative features and integrate intrinsic knowledge.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A series of ablation studies were conducted, primarily using Qwen2.5-VL-3B as the base model, to validate the effectiveness of the proposed design components.

- **Training Methods:**
 - Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) alone improved accuracy on seen categories (+3.98% over zero-shot baseline) but significantly harmed unseen categories (-6.12%), indicating overfitting.
 - CLS-RL (Li et al., 2025) reduced the unseen performance drop but still underperformed the zero-shot baseline (71.13% vs. 71.94%) and degraded seen category accuracy by 5.92%.
 - No-Thinking-RL (Li et al., 2025) improved seen category performance but lagged behind SFT.
 - Fine-R1-3B, with its two-stage framework, significantly outperformed SFT on seen categories (+7.56%) and surpassed No-Thinking-RL on unseen categories (+10.05%), demonstrating a balanced improvement.
- **Inference Strategies:** Direct CoT prompting without specialized training showed limited impact on FGVR. In-Context Learning (ICL) with one demonstration per candidate for seen categories was also evaluated, with Fine-R1-3B outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-3B with ICL by 17.90%. Fine-R1 also surpassed CLIP-like models, even when those employed prompt ensembling.
- **Key Components of Fine-R1:**
 - CoT SFT improved relative semantic similarity by 13.30%, establishing a foundation for reasoning.
 - Adding DAPO after CoT SFT yielded an additional 1.60% performance gain.
 - Both Intra-class and Inter-class Augmentation, when added individually, outperformed CoT SFT + DAPO.
 - Combining both augmentations (TAPO) achieved the best results, with 67.32% relative semantic similarity, confirming their complementary benefits.
- **Anchor-to-Positive Ratio:** An anchor-to-positive rollout ratio (n1:n2) of 1:1, for a total of 10 rollouts, achieved the best performance. This indicates that the gain from intra-class augmentation stems from increasing the diversity of rollouts rather than simply generating more rollouts.
- **CoT Generalization:** Performance on unseen categories increased with the quantity of CoT data, confirming that the model did not overfit to the synthetic patterns in the limited training set. The study also suggested that data quality outweighed quantity.
- **Cross-model Evaluation:** The training pipeline consistently improved performance on openPangu-VL-7B, demonstrating the generality of TAPO across different model architectures.

#### 4.3. Performance Gain Analysis

To understand the source of Fine-R1's performance gains, three hypotheses were investigated:

- **H1: Improved visual cue extraction:** Linear probing on image features showed negligible differences in performance between Fine-R1 and the base model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B). This suggests that Fine-R1 does not produce more effective visual embeddings for FGVR.
- **H2: Enhanced subcategory knowledge:** Evaluating the model's taxonomy-aware knowledge (e.g., distinguishing between species within the same genus versus different genera) showed little difference between Fine-R1 and the base model. This indicates that Fine-R1 does not fundamentally alter the encoding of subcategory knowledge.
- **H3: Better deployment of subcategory knowledge:** A PCA analysis of the last hidden state representations of positive and negative image-category pairs (e.g., "Is the bird species {correct/incorrect name}?") revealed that these pairs were more linearly separable in Fine-R1's representations compared to Qwen2.5-VL-3B. This finding suggests that Fine-R1 improves the ability to effectively deploy existing fine-grained subcategory knowledge in the context of FGVR tasks, leading to genuinely different representational states when the task requires such differentiation.

#### 4.4. Broader Benefits

Fine-R1 also demonstrated improvements in related tasks:

- **Classification-based VQA:** It raised performance on the ImageWikiQA benchmark from 54.85% (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) to 58.45% (Fine-R1-3B), indicating that enhanced FGVR is foundational for more advanced capabilities.
- **General VQA Benchmarks:** Fine-R1 maintained competitive performance on general VQA benchmarks like MME, MMBench, and SEED-Bench, even showing slight improvements on MMBench and SEED-Bench. This occurred despite being post-trained solely on FGVR data, suggesting that the RL-driven gains are not due to answer memorization but to a more robust reasoning mechanism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Fine-R1, a Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) specifically designed to address key challenges in Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR), namely data inefficiency and the generalization gap to unseen categories. The proposed two-stage framework, integrating Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-tuning (CoT SFT) with Triplet Augmented Policy Optimization (TAPO), represents a method for enhancing MLLMs' ability to leverage intrinsic knowledge for detailed visual tasks.

The work demonstrates that MLLMs can achieve superior FGVR performance by adopting a structured "human-like" reasoning process and by employing a policy optimization strategy tailored to the nuances of fine-grained distinctions (high intra-class and low inter-class variance). The observed performance gains, including state-of-the-art results in both closed-world and open-world evaluations, and the outperformance of dedicated contrastive CLIP models, indicate that Fine-R1 offers an effective approach for fine-grained tasks.

A significant aspect of this work is the demonstration of robust generalization to unseen categories with only few-shot training samples. This data-efficient learning capability addresses a critical limitation of previous MLLM adaptation methods, which often required extensive, costly annotated datasets. The analysis confirming that Fine-R1's improvements stem from a better deployment of existing subcategory knowledge, rather than a fundamental alteration of visual feature extraction or knowledge encoding, provides insights into the mechanisms underlying its enhanced performance.

The potential impact of Fine-R1 extends to several areas:

- **Practical Applications in Knowledge-Intensive Domains:** By enabling MLLMs to excel in FGVR with limited data, Fine-R1 can facilitate the deployment of AI in specialized fields where expert annotations are scarce or constantly evolving (e.g., biological species identification, medical imaging, quality control of manufactured goods).
- **Advancement of MLLM Capabilities:** This work contributes to the broader understanding of how MLLMs can be trained to perform more sophisticated reasoning and discriminative tasks, pushing the boundaries of their applicability beyond coarse-grained recognition.
- **Development of Specialized AI Assistants:** Fine-R1's ability to provide detailed, accurate answers to fine-grained visual questions, while maintaining general VQA performance, suggests its utility in developing specialized AI assistants that can cater to specific domain expertise.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The methodology, particularly the integration of structured CoT and triplet-augmented RL, offers a framework that could be adapted and extended to other complex, data-constrained multimodal reasoning tasks. The findings on knowledge deployment also open avenues for further investigation into how MLLMs utilize and reason with their internal representations.

Overall, Fine-R1 represents an advancement towards more capable and generalizable MLLMs for fine-grained visual understanding, with implications for a wide range of real-world applications.
