# Dynamics as Prompts: In-Context Learning for Sim-to-Real System Identifications

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed CAPTURE, an in-context learning framework for online system identification that dynamically adjusts simulation environment parameters. This method achieved faster and more accurate alignment between simulated and real-world dynamics, improving performance by approximately 20% compared to leading baselines in real-world air hockey tasks.

## Problem

- The persistent sim-to-real gap, caused by differences between simulated and real-world dynamics, hinders the deployment of simulation-trained robotic policies.
- Traditional Domain Randomization (DR) often captures only average dynamics, limiting precision for tasks requiring fine-grained control.
- Conventional System Identification (SysID) methods are iterative, time-consuming, and inefficient for online adaptation in dynamic real-world scenarios.

## Method

![[2410.20357-method.png]]
*The CAPTURE pipeline. It involves generating transition histories (left), training a causal transformer (center), and using that transformer online to predict simulation parameters ϵ for a task policy (right).*

- A ==causal transformer==, named **CAPTURE**, is trained to model the distribution of ==simulation parameters== conditioned on ==multi-episodic interaction histories==.
- An ==environment-conditioned reinforcement learning== task policy is trained to adapt to varying environment parameters, providing actions for both simulated and real-world rollouts.
- A dataset of ==source-to-target adaptation transitions== is generated using a ==randomized binary search== process, allowing the transformer to learn a robust search algorithm for parameter estimation without iterative optimization.

## Results

- In sim-to-sim object scooping, CAPTURE achieved a **50%** higher success rate than other SysID methods and converged to the correct center of mass within approximately **4** adaptation iterations.
- In real-world table air hockey, CAPTURE steadily improved, achieving the lowest trajectory differences of **0.27 m** at **9** iterations, approximately **20%** better than leading baseline methods.
- The method demonstrated robust performance and generalization to unseen objects, complex parameter spaces, and noisy observations in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing the System Identification problem as an in-context learning task allows a causal transformer to learn the dynamics adaptation process itself, rather than a direct mapping.
> - Leveraging multi-episode interaction histories as context enables the model to predict optimal environmental parameters more accurately and efficiently, eliminating traditional iterative optimization loops.
> - A randomized binary search strategy used for data generation helps distill a robust parameter search algorithm that generalizes well and avoids overfitting to linearly interpolated transition histories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the persistent challenge of sim-to-real transfer in robotics, which arises from discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics. This gap can lead to performance degradation or catastrophic failures when policies trained in simulation are deployed in physical environments.

Traditional approaches to bridging the sim-to-real gap include Domain Randomization (DR) and System Identification (SysID). DR involves randomizing simulation parameters during training to create robust policies. While DR can be effective in some contexts, it often captures only average dynamics, which can limit its precision for tasks requiring fine-grained control. Traditional SysID methods aim to align simulation and real-world performance by iteratively adjusting simulation environment parameters. These methods typically require repeated training cycles or model updates, making them time-consuming and inefficient, especially for online adaptation in dynamic real-world scenarios. The paper highlights that current offline SysID methods involve learning new models for each parameter update, which is often slow. Online SysID approaches, such as RMA and IIDA, have emerged to address these limitations by inferring environment dynamics without requiring continuous model retraining.

Concurrently, in-context learning (ICL) has gained prominence in fields like Natural Language Processing (NLP) and computer vision for its ability to adjust model behavior without explicit gradient updates, by leveraging contextual information. In robotics, ICL has been applied to improve generalization by adapting policies when rewards or expert demonstrations change. However, existing ICL applications in robotics generally assume fixed environment dynamics, or frame different dynamics as separate tasks, which can be impractical in high-dimensional, continuous parameter spaces.

This research introduces a novel application of in-context learning by adapting simulation environment parameters, rather than task policies, to address the sim-to-real gap. It seeks to eliminate the iterative optimization loop common in traditional SysID, thereby aiming for faster and more accurate alignment between simulated and real-world dynamics. By framing the SysID problem as an in-context learning task and utilizing multi-episodic interaction histories, the work positions itself as a development in online system identification that leverages advanced sequence modeling capabilities to learn the dynamics adaptation process itself.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and accurate method for bridging the sim-to-real gap in robotics by dynamically adjusting simulation environment parameters online. The motivation stems from several limitations of existing sim-to-real transfer techniques:

- **Discrepancies between Simulation and Reality:** The inherent sim-to-real gap, caused by differences in physical dynamics between simulated and real environments, is a fundamental barrier to deploying simulation-trained robotic policies. These discrepancies can lead to unpredictable robot behavior and task failures in the real world.
- **Limitations of Domain Randomization (DR):** While DR helps create robust policies by randomizing environment parameters during training, it often only captures average dynamics. This characteristic makes it less suitable for tasks that demand precise control or fine-grained adaptation to specific real-world conditions. For example, scooping an irregularly shaped object requires accurate knowledge of its center of mass, which DR may not adequately address.
- **Inefficiency of Traditional System Identification (SysID):** Conventional SysID methods, particularly offline ones, necessitate iterative refinement and often involve learning new models for each parameter update. This process is time-consuming and inefficient for dynamic scenarios where rapid adaptation is crucial. The paper illustrates this with an example of a robot scooping celery, where iterative, offline SysID would be impractical.
- **Lack of Online Adaptation Capabilities in Current In-Context Learning (ICL) Robotics Applications:** While ICL has been successfully applied in robotics for policy adaptation based on new tasks or demonstrations, these applications typically assume fixed environment dynamics. Adapting simulation parameters using ICL represents an unexplored direction for enhancing generalization and efficiency.
- **Need for Accelerated Parameter Estimation:** The paper proposes to accelerate the parameter estimation process by leveraging the in-context learning ability of transformer models. This approach aims to eliminate the optimization loop traditionally required in SysID, thereby enabling faster alignment of simulated and real dynamics.
- **Learning the SysID Search Process:** A core objective is to distill the complex SysID parameter update process itself, rather than simply learning a single-step mapping from observations to parameters. By incorporating multi-episode interaction histories as "context," the method seeks to enable the causal transformer to learn more comprehensive dynamics properties and search strategies. This is hypothesized to lead to smoother and more accurate predictions of environmental parameters and dynamic behaviors, ultimately advancing the deployment of robots in dynamic real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named inContext AdaPTation modUle for sim-to-REal system identification (CAPTURE), utilizes in-context learning to dynamically adjust simulation environment parameters online. The approach operates on the assumption that aligning simulation and real-world environment parameters will consequently reduce the sim-to-real performance gap.

The CAPTURE pipeline consists of three main modules:
- **Problem Formulation:**
 - **Environment Parameter Space ($\epsilon$):** The method defines a finite and bounded environment parameter space $\epsilon \in E$, encompassing task-related properties such as center of mass or sliding friction. These parameters are modifiable through the Robosuite API.
 - **Interaction Histories ($h_i$):** Previous SysID iterations are treated as context. An interaction history $h_i$ at the $i$-th iteration includes the current simulation parameters $\epsilon^{sim}_{i-k}$, the action $a_{i-k}$, simulated state trajectories $\tau^{sim}_{i-k}$, and real-world state trajectories $\tau^{real}_{i-k}$, extending back $k$ iterations.
 - **SysID Causal Transformer ($P_\theta$):** A causal transformer, $P_\theta$, is trained to model the distribution of simulation parameters conditioned on this history. The objective is to distill the SysID algorithm by learning to predict the next iteration's environment parameters, $\epsilon^{sim}_i$, from the history $h_{i-1}$. The training minimizes the Mean-Square-Error (MSE) loss between predicted and ground-truth next-iteration parameters.

- **Environment-Conditioned RL Training:**
 - An environment-conditioned reinforcement learning (RL) task policy $\pi(a | s_0, \epsilon)$ is trained to adapt to varying environment parameters $\epsilon$.
 - During training, $\epsilon$ is sampled uniformly from the parameter space $E$ for each episode.
 - The policy selects an action $a$, which is executed in the simulation, generating state trajectories and rewards.
 - The policy is updated using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) to refine actions and facilitate adaptation to predicted parameters.

- **Source-to-Target SysID Iteration Generation (Data Generation):**
 - This module generates a dataset of "source-to-target adaptation transitions" that mimic the sim-to-real adaptation process.
 - Each iteration $i$ in a transition sequence includes: the current simulation environment parameter $\epsilon_i$, the rollout action $a_i$ (obtained from the environment-conditioned task policy), the simulated trajectory $\tau^{source}_i$, and the target environment trajectory $\tau^{target}_i$ (collected from a "target" simulation using the same action $a_i$).
 - Instead of learning a direct single-step mapping, the method aims to learn a *search algorithm* that navigates the parameter space to find the target environment parameter.
 - **Randomized Binary Search:** To achieve this, a randomized binary search process is emulated for generating transition sequences. This involves dynamically adjusting upper and lower search bounds and selecting the next environment parameter using a beta distribution, which promotes exploration. This contrasts with linear interpolation or standard binary search. The duration of this parameter iteration history, denoted as $L$, is a predefined length (e.g., $L=7$).

- **SysID Causal Transformer Structure:**
 - The architecture is based on the Decision Transformer (DT), using a GPT-2 backbone.
 - The model is trained on the generated SysID parameter transition histories $D$. It learns to predict $\hat{\epsilon}_{i+1}$ at iteration $i$ using a next-token prediction framework with a shifted input setup.
 - During training, a multi-episode window of size $k$ is sampled from $D$. Each iteration block fed to the transformer consists of tokens representing the action, environment parameter, and state trajectories for both simulated and real rollouts (2 + 2T tokens).
 - During inference (in-context SysID), the model uses relative timestep embedding to predict $\epsilon_{i+1}$ from the current history. The environment-conditioned policy then generates actions in the newly estimated simulation, and real-world rollouts provide new trajectories, which are appended to the history for subsequent predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated CAPTURE across two robotic tasks: object scooping and table air hockey, in both sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer settings.

**Sim-to-Sim SysID Evaluation:**

- **Object Scooping:** In identifying an object's center of mass (a 1D parameter), CAPTURE achieved a success rate 50% higher than other SysID methods and 70% higher than Domain Randomization (DR). CAPTURE converged to the correct center of mass within approximately 4 adaptation iterations. Baselines, lacking historical interaction data, exhibited lower success rates because their identification was limited to the current scooping point. Ablation studies showed that linear interpolation converged slower due to restricted exploration, while the randomized binary search (used in CAPTURE) promoted a robust adaptation process without hindering performance compared to a standard binary search.
- **Table Air Hockey:** For this task involving a higher-dimensional parameter space (5 parameters, including friction and damping), CAPTURE demonstrated more stable and accurate adaptation curves for parameter estimation. It reached normalized parameter differences of approximately 0.2 after 7 adaptation iterations, whereas baseline methods generally converged around 0.35. The baselines struggled with dynamic environments due to their inability to leverage historical interactions effectively. CAPTURE improved trajectory differences by roughly 40% compared to identification baselines and 50% compared to DR. This reduction in trajectory difference correlated with improved parameter estimation. Ablations confirmed that linear interpolation struggled to establish a robust search due to overfitting to linearly interpolated transition histories.

**Sim-to-Real SysID Evaluation:**

- **Object Scooping:** CAPTURE was tested on three objects (eggplant, celery, carrot) with varying mass distributions. While DR performed well for objects with centralized centers of mass (e.g., eggplant, with over 90% success rate), its performance declined significantly for objects with more complex mass distributions (celery and carrot). CAPTURE adapted to these varied objects, achieving at least a 70% success rate by the 7th iteration. Unlike one-step adaptation methods that might randomly sample parameters after an initial success, CAPTURE consistently maintained high success rates in subsequent iterations, demonstrating its ability to generalize to unseen target-to-target adaptations by leveraging context history.
- **Table Air Hockey:** In real-world air hockey experiments with adjustable fan voltages controlling surface friction, CAPTURE showed consistent improvement over multiple adaptation iterations. While an Expert Distillation (ED) baseline achieved the best trajectory difference at 1 iteration (0.40 m), CAPTURE steadily improved, achieving the lowest trajectory differences of 0.29 m at 7 iterations and 0.27 m at 9 iterations. This represented approximately 20% better performance than the leading baseline methods in the final iterations.

**Additional Findings (from Appendix):**

- **Transition Sequence Length (L):** An ablation study on $L$ (number of iterations to generate a complete transition sequence) showed minimal performance variation for $L$ between 7 and 9, with some degradation at $L=5$ and $L=13$.
- **Transformer Window Sizes (k):** Experiments on window size $k$ (length of history context) indicated that $k=2$ and $k=3$ performed better than larger window sizes. Larger $k$ was observed to potentially overfit the training data sequence length, leading to poorer out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Comparison with COMPASS:** When compared to COMPASS, an offline SysID baseline, CAPTURE demonstrated superior performance, converging faster in object scooping and performing better in table air hockey.
- **Robustness to Non-Monotonicity and Noise:**
 - Even when the assumption of a monotonic relationship between environment parameters and state trajectories was challenged (e.g., by observing distance to center of mass in scooping), CAPTURE, after initial struggles, gradually decreased error and outperformed baselines.
 - Under noisy observations (random angle labels for scooping, uniform noise for air hockey state trajectories), CAPTURE maintained robust performance and generally outperformed baselines, highlighting its resilience in imperfect real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics by introducing a novel application of in-context learning to the sim-to-real problem, specifically targeting the dynamic adjustment of simulation environment parameters. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By reframing System Identification as an in-context learning problem, the paper proposes a mechanism for online adaptation that moves beyond traditional iterative optimization or policy-centric generalization. This is a substantive step toward reducing the persistent sim-to-real gap, which remains a key barrier to widespread robot deployment.
- **Enhanced Efficiency in System Identification:** CAPTURE's ability to adjust simulation parameters online without requiring gradient updates results in faster and more efficient system identification. This efficiency is critical for robotic systems that operate in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments where rapid adaptation is necessary, contrasting with the time-consuming nature of many existing SysID methods.
- **Improved Accuracy and Robustness:** The method demonstrates superior accuracy in aligning simulated and real-world dynamics, as evidenced by its performance in parameter estimation and trajectory matching across diverse tasks. Its validated robustness to unseen objects, complex parameter spaces, and noisy observations further enhances its practical applicability.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** By distilling the SysID search process through multi-episode interaction histories, CAPTURE exhibits a stronger capacity for generalization. This allows robots to adapt to varied real-world objects and scenarios (e.g., different types of food in scooping) without requiring extensive retraining or explicit model updates for each new dynamic.
- **Foundation for Future Robotics Autonomy:** The framework offers a more efficient and accurate solution for deploying simulation-trained robotic systems in the real world. This could accelerate the development and adoption of autonomous robots in fields such as manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics, where adaptability to changing physical properties is crucial.
- **Novel Application of In-Context Learning:** The work extends the utility of in-context learning beyond policy adaptation to the domain of environment parameter estimation. This indicates a broader potential for ICL to address fundamental challenges in robot learning by treating dynamic environment properties as part of the context for adaptation.

While the paper notes that further alignment between state trajectories and environment parameter spaces is required for multi-task settings, its demonstrated ability to achieve efficient and smooth system identification through historical interaction data marks a notable advance in addressing the practical challenges of deploying robots in dynamic real-world scenarios.
