# World-Ego Modeling for Long-Horizon Evolution in Hybrid Embodied Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces World-Ego Modeling, a new paradigm for embodied AI that decomposes future predictions into distinct "world" (environment) and "ego" (agent) components. This approach, instantiated in the World-Ego Model (WEM), achieves state-of-the-art performance on hybrid navigation-manipulation tasks within a new long-horizon benchmark called HTEWorld, leading to more consistent scene evolution and instruction-aligned interactions.

## Problem

- Existing embodied world models struggle to simultaneously predict both the evolution of the environment and the behavior of the agent, often entangling these heterogeneous dynamics within a single predictive stream.
- This entanglement leads to performance degradation in long-horizon scenarios and hybrid tasks (interleaving navigation and manipulation), particularly in maintaining long-term scene consistency during navigation and accurately simulating contact-rich physical dynamics during manipulation.
- Prior work often targets specific aspects like action conditioning or local object dynamics without systematically investigating the fundamental distinction between "world" and "ego" dynamics or the optimal level of disentanglement.

## Method

- Introduces World-Ego Modeling, a conceptual paradigm that explicitly decomposes the prediction of future embodied evolution into distinct "world" (persistent scene regularities) and "ego" (robot-centric, instruction-conditioned dynamics) components.
- Proposes the World-Ego Model (**WEM**), an architecture featuring a vision-language state predictor with role-conditioned attention and a cascade-parallel ==mixture-of-experts== ==diffusion== generator with full disentanglement, guided by dynamically predicted semantic masks.
- Develops HTEWorld, a new benchmark tailored for long-horizon hybrid navigation-manipulation tasks, complete with fine-grained annotations and specialized metrics, to rigorously evaluate world-ego modeling capabilities.

## Results

- WEM achieved state-of-the-art performance with an EWMScore of 61.48 on the HTEWorld benchmark, outperforming a PAN-style baseline by 3 points and other methods by larger margins, indicating improved coherence and instruction alignment.
- The model showed consistent gains across 16 WorldArena metrics and all 6 newly introduced HTEWorld-specific metrics, demonstrating stronger chunk continuity, instruction alignment, layout preservation, phase-matched motion, and long-horizon stability for continuous and hybrid tasks.
- Qualitative comparisons affirmed WEM's ability to better preserve scene geometry, object consistency, and instruction alignment over complete trajectories, mitigating physical implausibility, hallucinations, and placement failures observed in baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly separating "world" and "ego" dynamics at both the conceptual and architectural levels significantly enhances embodied world model performance for complex, long-horizon, and hybrid tasks.
> - A semantic-based definition for the world-ego boundary, which differentiates between the robot/manipulated objects (ego) and the background/unmanipulated objects (world), proves most effective for assigning predictive responsibility.
> - Full disentanglement, involving specialized expert routing and output recomposition based on a dynamically predicted world-ego mask within a cascade-parallel mixture-of-experts generator, yields superior performance compared to partial disentanglement or monolithic approaches.
