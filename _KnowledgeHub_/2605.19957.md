# World-Ego Modeling for Long-Horizon Evolution in Hybrid Embodied Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces World-Ego Modeling, a new paradigm for embodied AI that decomposes future predictions into distinct "world" (environment) and "ego" (agent) components. This approach, instantiated in the World-Ego Model (WEM), achieves state-of-the-art performance on hybrid navigation-manipulation tasks within a new long-horizon benchmark called HTEWorld, leading to more consistent scene evolution and instruction-aligned interactions.

## Problem

- Existing embodied world models struggle to simultaneously predict both the evolution of the environment and the behavior of the agent, often entangling these heterogeneous dynamics within a single predictive stream.
- This entanglement leads to performance degradation in long-horizon scenarios and hybrid tasks (interleaving navigation and manipulation), particularly in maintaining long-term scene consistency during navigation and accurately simulating contact-rich physical dynamics during manipulation.
- Prior work often targets specific aspects like action conditioning or local object dynamics without systematically investigating the fundamental distinction between "world" and "ego" dynamics or the optimal level of disentanglement.

## Method

- Introduces World-Ego Modeling, a conceptual paradigm that explicitly decomposes the prediction of future embodied evolution into distinct "world" (persistent scene regularities) and "ego" (robot-centric, instruction-conditioned dynamics) components.
- Proposes the World-Ego Model (**WEM**), an architecture featuring a vision-language state predictor with role-conditioned attention and a cascade-parallel ==mixture-of-experts== ==diffusion== generator with full disentanglement, guided by dynamically predicted semantic masks.
- Develops HTEWorld, a new benchmark tailored for long-horizon hybrid navigation-manipulation tasks, complete with fine-grained annotations and specialized metrics, to rigorously evaluate world-ego modeling capabilities.

## Results

- WEM achieved state-of-the-art performance with an EWMScore of 61.48 on the HTEWorld benchmark, outperforming a PAN-style baseline by 3 points and other methods by larger margins, indicating improved coherence and instruction alignment.
- The model showed consistent gains across 16 WorldArena metrics and all 6 newly introduced HTEWorld-specific metrics, demonstrating stronger chunk continuity, instruction alignment, layout preservation, phase-matched motion, and long-horizon stability for continuous and hybrid tasks.
- Qualitative comparisons affirmed WEM's ability to better preserve scene geometry, object consistency, and instruction alignment over complete trajectories, mitigating physical implausibility, hallucinations, and placement failures observed in baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly separating "world" and "ego" dynamics at both the conceptual and architectural levels significantly enhances embodied world model performance for complex, long-horizon, and hybrid tasks.
> - A semantic-based definition for the world-ego boundary, which differentiates between the robot/manipulated objects (ego) and the background/unmanipulated objects (world), proves most effective for assigning predictive responsibility.
> - Full disentanglement, involving specialized expert routing and output recomposition based on a dynamically predicted world-ego mask within a cascade-parallel mixture-of-experts generator, yields superior performance compared to partial disentanglement or monolithic approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the development of "world models." World models are computational systems designed to learn the physical dynamics of an environment, enabling them to predict future consequences, generate synthetic data, and serve as simulators for policy learning. Early advancements in this area primarily focused on learning compact latent dynamics for imagination. More recently, with the emergence of diffusion models and advanced video generation techniques, the capability of world models has expanded to generate realistic, pixel-level visual rollouts.

Existing embodied world models face challenges in simultaneously predicting both the evolution of the environment ("the world") and the behavior of the agent ("the ego"). A common practice is to combine these two heterogeneous dynamics into a single predictive stream. The "world" typically encompasses persistent, instruction-agnostic scene regularities such as spatial layout and object permanence. In contrast, the "ego" involves robot-centric, instruction-conditioned dynamics, including robot motion and object interactions. This entanglement of world and ego dynamics within a single model architecture has been observed to lead to a degradation in performance, particularly in long-horizon embodied scenarios and hybrid tasks that involve interleaved navigation and manipulation behaviors. Challenges include maintaining long-term scene consistency during navigation and accurately simulating contact-rich physical dynamics during manipulation.

Prior research has explored related concepts of disentangled modeling. For instance, JEPA-style architectures separate environmental evolution from action proposals in representation space, and ego-vision world models factor future video into ego motion, object dynamics, and scene composition to gain controllability. However, these approaches often target specific aspects like action conditioning or local object dynamics without systematically investigating the fundamental distinction between world and ego dynamics or the optimal level of disentanglement.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a systematic framework for "World-Ego Modeling." It aims to explore the theoretical and architectural implications of explicitly decomposing future evolution into world and ego components, thereby enhancing the stability, consistency, and instruction alignment of embodied world models over long horizons and across diverse task types. The introduction of a new benchmark, HTEWorld, further contributes to the research landscape by enabling rigorous evaluation of these capabilities, which were previously underserved by existing short-horizon or manipulation-only benchmarks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and investigate **World-Ego Modeling**, a conceptual paradigm that decomposes the prediction of future embodied evolution into distinct "world" and "ego" components. This paradigm seeks to address the limitations of conventional world models, which typically entangle these two heterogeneous dynamics within a single predictive stream, leading to performance degradation in complex, long-horizon scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations regarding the inherent differences between the "world" and "ego" in embodied AI:
1. **Heterogeneous Dynamics:** The "world" component is characterized by persistent, instruction-agnostic scene regularities, such as stable layouts and object permanence. Its evolution is largely governed by underlying physical laws independent of immediate agent actions. Conversely, the "ego" component pertains to robot-centric dynamics, including the agent's motion, physical interactions with objects, and instruction-conditioned behaviors. These dynamics are transient, goal-oriented, and directly influenced by the current task.
2. **Entanglement Challenges:** When these fundamentally different dynamics are collapsed into a single predictive stream, existing world models often struggle with:
 - **Long-Horizon Degradation:** Maintaining scene consistency over extended periods (crucial for navigation) becomes difficult, as the model may over-prioritize transient ego dynamics.
 - **Hybrid Task Inefficiency:** In tasks combining navigation and manipulation, the model is overloaded with the responsibility of simultaneously predicting stable scene changes and precise, contact-rich physical interactions, leading to reduced fidelity in both aspects. For instance, navigation requires stable scene consistency, while manipulation demands accurate object interaction. A single predictive stream may compromise one for the other.
 - **Reduced Interpretability and Controllability:** A monolithic model makes it challenging to understand which parts of the prediction are driven by environmental regularities versus agent intentions, limiting fine-grained control over specific aspects of the generated future.
3. **Alignment with Reality:** Decomposing world and ego dynamics aligns the model's predictive structure more closely with the underlying physical reality, where environmental properties and agent behaviors are distinct yet interacting entities. This structural alignment is hypothesized to yield more stable, consistent, and instruction-aware future predictions.

To systematically explore this paradigm, the research aims to answer fundamental questions:
- **Definition of World and Ego:** How should the "world" and the "ego" be conceptually defined and delineated within an embodied context? The paper explores three perspectives: motion-based (source of visual motion), semantic-based (embodied role of scene entities), and intention-based (source of conditioning information).
- **Necessity of Disentanglement:** To what extent does embodied world modeling benefit from, or even require, explicit world-ego disentanglement, particularly for long-horizon and composite tasks? This involves analyzing different strategies for disentangling these components within a model architecture.

In addition to these core objectives, the paper also aims to:
- **Instantiate a Concrete Model:** Design and implement a unified embodied world model, named World-Ego Model (WEM), that incorporates the most effective world-ego definition and disentanglement strategy.
- **Develop a Rigorous Evaluation Benchmark:** Construct the Hybrid-Task Embodied World Benchmark (HTEWorld), specifically tailored for long-horizon world modeling in hybrid navigation-manipulation tasks, complete with comprehensive metrics. This benchmark is crucial for empirically validating the benefits of World-Ego Modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on proposing, instantiating, and evaluating the World-Ego Modeling paradigm.

#### 3.1. Conceptual Framework for World-Ego Modeling

The core idea is to decompose the prediction of the next video chunk ($ \hat{V}_k $) into two complementary latent states: a world state ($ S_w_k $) and an ego state ($ S_e_k $). These states are inferred by a vision-language state predictor ($ \Phi_\phi $) from multimodal history ($ O_0, V_{<k}, a_{\le k} $) and then utilized by a video generator ($ D_\theta $).

#### 3.2. Defining the World-Ego Boundary

The paper explores three operational definitions for delineating the world and ego, each serving as a criterion for assigning predictive responsibility:
- **Motion-based view:** Separates based on the source of visual motion. Pixels whose motion is explained by camera egomotion (viewpoint change) are assigned to the world, while pixels whose motion deviates (due to contact-driven object dynamics from the embodiment) are assigned to the ego. Object residual flow serves as the proxy.
- **Semantic-based view:** Separates based on the embodied role of scene entities. The robot and any currently manipulated objects constitute the ego region, capturing instruction-conditioned dynamics. The background and unmanipulated objects form the world region, capturing persistent regularities. A semantic mask serves as the proxy. This view was adopted as the default for WEM.
- **Intention-based view:** Separates based on the conditioning information source. The world reflects information from visual history, while the ego reflects instruction-conditioned dynamics. This view partitions conditioning sources, allowing the predictor to implicitly learn how to integrate information from each.

#### 3.3. Disentanglement Strategies

To implement the world-ego concept, a general framework featuring a cascade-parallel mixture-of-experts (CP-MoE) diffusion generator is designed, supporting three disentanglement strategies:
- **Pre-disentanglement:** Separates tokens at the input of the generator's rear stage. A proxy partitions tokens into world and ego groups, which are then processed by a single rear module with restricted cross-attention (world tokens attend only to $ S_w_k $, ego tokens to $ S_e_k $).
- **Post-disentanglement:** Duplicates the generator's rear stage into a World Expert and an Ego Expert, both processing the full token sequence. The proxy acts as a soft mask to fuse their outputs.
- **Full disentanglement (WEM's default):** The proxy first routes preceding-expert tokens to dedicated World and Ego Experts. After computation, an unrouting module recomposes their per-token outputs into a single sequence using the same proxy. This strategy enforces separation across input routing, branch processing, and output fusion.

#### 3.4. World-Ego Model (WEM) Architecture

WEM instantiates the semantic-based view with full disentanglement, built upon a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) for state prediction and a pretrained video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for generation.

- **State Predictor:** Uses a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct backbone augmented with learnable ego and world queries.
 - **Asymmetric Query Budgets:** Allocates different numbers of queries (192 for world, 64 for ego) based on the expected information content for each role.
 - **Role-Conditioned Attention (RCA):** Restricts the attention horizon for each query group. World queries attend to the entire visual history and past instructions, excluding the current instruction and ego queries. Ego queries attend to the current instruction and recent instruction-video pairs, masking out distant history and world queries. This ensures distinct conditioning sources for $ S_w_k $ and $ S_e_k $.

- **World-Ego Generator:** Restructures a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B DiT backbone into a CP-MoE structure.
 - **Preceding Expert:** Early DiT blocks act as a shared encoder, conditioned on both $ S_w_k $, $ S_e_k $, and text instruction. It integrates these into a common visual representation and predicts the semantic world-ego mask.
 - **Role Experts:** Later DiT blocks are duplicated into a World Expert and an Ego Expert. Each expert operates strictly under its corresponding single state ($ S_w_k $ or $ S_e_k $), while preserving text conditioning.
 - **Semantic Head, Routing, and Unrouting:** A lightweight dense prediction transformer (semantic head) processes intermediate features from the preceding expert and $ S_e_k $ to output a world-ego mask $ M $ over video patches. This mask routes tokens to the respective role experts, with neighbor-expanded routing to mitigate seam artifacts. An unrouting module then recomposes their outputs into a single sequence for the final video chunk generation.

#### 3.5. Training Objectives

WEM is trained end-to-end with two objectives:
- A **flow-matching loss ($ L_{flow} $)** on the recomposed latents, inherited from the pretrained DiT.
- A **mask-prediction loss ($ L_{mask} $)** on the Semantic Head's output, combining a class-balanced binary cross-entropy term and a Dice term. The total loss is $ L = L_{flow} + \lambda L_{mask} $.

#### 3.6. HTEWorld Benchmark Construction

Recognizing the limitations of existing benchmarks, the authors constructed the **Hybrid-Task Embodied World Benchmark (HTEWorld)** using BEHAVIOR-1K.
- **Data:** 125K video clips (over 4.5M frames) for training, with 300 multi-turn evaluation trajectories (over 2K instructions).
- **Annotations:** Fine-grained annotations include semantic world-ego masks (from simulator instance segmentation), decomposed optical flow (separating camera-induced from contact-induced motion), and action-centric language captions (generated using a dynamic, context-rich prompting pipeline with Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct).
- **Metrics:** It incorporates the 16-metric EWMScore from WorldArena and introduces 6 HTEWorld-specific metrics:
 - **Multi-Turn Continuous Generation:** Rollout Chunk-Boundary Dynamics (RCBD), Late-Prefix State Alignment (LPSA), and Chunk Instruction-Step Retrieval (CISR).
 - **Unified Navigation-Manipulation Generation:** Phase-Matched Motion Profile Alignment (PMPA), Cross-Phase Discriminative Margin (CPDM), and Frontier Phase-Hop State Consistency (FPHS).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of World-Ego Modeling (WEM) and its components yielded several findings:

#### 4.1. Design Study I: World-Ego Boundary Definition

A comparison of the three world-ego boundary definitions (motion-based, semantic-based, intention-based) was conducted using the EWMScore on HTEWorld.
- The **semantic-based view** achieved the highest EWMScore of 61.48.
- The motion-based view scored 59.36.
- The intention-based view scored 58.69.
- This result supported the adoption of the semantic-based world-ego assignment as the default in WEM, indicating its effectiveness in directly separating interaction regions from persistent scene regions.

#### 4.2. Design Study II: World-Ego Disentanglement Strategies

With the semantic-based boundary fixed, different architectural disentanglement strategies were evaluated.
- **Full disentanglement (WEM's configuration)** achieved the highest EWMScore of 61.48.
- Post-disentanglement with a semantic proxy scored 61.09.
- Post-disentanglement without a semantic proxy (constant assignment) scored 58.59.
- Pre-disentanglement scored 58.85.
- A baseline without explicit disentanglement scored 58.40.
- These results indicated that full disentanglement, which employs the semantic proxy for both routing tokens to specialized experts and recomposing their outputs, provides the most consistent and effective separation, leading to performance improvements.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Prior Arts on HTEWorld

WEM was compared against representative baselines (WoW-7B, Cosmos-Predict 2.5-2B, Cosmos-Predict 2.5-14B, and a PAN-style baseline) on HTEWorld using both the WorldArena metric suite and the 6 HTEWorld-specific metrics. All models were fine-tuned under the same protocol.
- WEM achieved state-of-the-art performance with an **EWMScore of 61.48**, outperforming the PAN-style baseline (58.40) by 3 points and other methods by larger margins.
- WEM showed notable gains across **motion, consistency, 3D, control, and physics-related metrics**, suggesting that world-ego disentanglement contributes to more coherent scene evolution and instruction-aligned interaction beyond local visual quality.
- Consistent gains were observed across all **6 HTEWorld-specific metrics**:
 - RCBD (0.31), LPSA (0.87), and CISR (0.57) indicated stronger chunk continuity, instruction alignment, and layout preservation for multi-turn generation.
 - PMPA (0.54), CPDM (0.52), and FPHS (0.89) demonstrated improved phase-matched motion, camera/object coordination, and long-horizon stability for unified navigation-manipulation tasks.
- Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5) further supported these findings, showing that WEM better preserved scene geometry, object consistency, and instruction alignment over complete trajectories, mitigating artifacts such as physical implausibility, hallucinations, and placement failures observed in baselines.

#### 4.4. Compatibility with Manipulation-Only Benchmarks

WEM was evaluated on the original WorldArena benchmark to assess its compatibility with conventional manipulation-oriented tasks.
- WEM achieved an EWMScore of 58.10, remaining competitive with top-performing models on this benchmark (e.g., CtrlWorld at 59.70, IRASim at 58.12), despite being optimized for hybrid navigation-manipulation tasks. This suggests a degree of generalizability.

#### 4.5. Role Expert Specialization and Ablation Study

- **Role Expert Specialization:** Visualizations confirmed that the ego expert primarily focused on robot body parts and manipulated objects, while the world expert reproduced stable background structure. The Semantic Head accurately localized the boundary between these roles.
- **Ablation Study:** Removing key components of WEM (asymmetric query budgets, role-conditioned attention, neighbor-expanded routing) each resulted in a degradation of performance (EWMScore dropping to 60.50, 60.64, and 59.57 respectively). This confirmed the importance of role-aware state extraction and boundary-aware expert routing for effective world-ego separation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "World-Ego Modeling for Long-Horizon Evolution in Hybrid Embodied Tasks" presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI, particularly in the domain of world modeling. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Conceptual Advancement in World Modeling

The introduction of the World-Ego Modeling paradigm offers a novel conceptual framework for understanding and building embodied world models. By explicitly advocating for the decomposition of future evolution into persistent "world" regularities and robot-centric "ego" dynamics, it addresses a fundamental limitation of monolithic world models that often struggle with the entanglement of these heterogeneous processes. This paradigm provides a more structured and interpretable approach to predicting complex embodied interactions.

#### 5.2. Architectural Innovation for Robust Long-Horizon Prediction

The proposed World-Ego Model (WEM) provides a concrete and effective architectural instantiation of this paradigm. Its design, incorporating a vision-language state predictor with role-conditioned attention and a CP-MoE diffusion generator with full disentanglement, demonstrates how to achieve more stable and coherent long-horizon, multi-turn predictions. The architectural choices, such as asymmetric query budgets and semantic-mask-guided expert routing, contribute to improved scene consistency for navigation and more accurate physical simulation for manipulation tasks.

#### 5.3. Establishment of a Rigorous Hybrid-Task Benchmark

The creation of HTEWorld is a substantial contribution to the research community. By providing a large-scale dataset and evaluation protocol specifically designed for long-horizon hybrid navigation-manipulation tasks, it fills a critical gap in existing benchmarks. This enables more thorough and realistic assessment of embodied world models, pushing the boundaries beyond short-horizon or manipulation-only evaluations. The HTEWorld-specific metrics further provide fine-grained insights into various aspects of continuous and hybrid task generation.

#### 5.4. Demonstrated Performance Improvements

WEM's state-of-the-art performance on HTEWorld, significantly outperforming existing baselines, empirically validates the benefits of explicit world-ego separation. The observed gains across metrics related to visual quality, motion, 3D consistency, control, and physics indicate that disentanglement leads to higher fidelity and better instruction alignment in complex scenarios. The model's competitive performance on existing manipulation-only benchmarks also suggests its general applicability and compatibility with diverse embodied tasks.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Research and Applications

This work lays foundational groundwork for several promising future research directions:
- **Weakly/Self-Supervised Boundary Estimation:** The current reliance on semantic masks suggests a path toward developing methods for inferring world-ego boundaries directly from raw video, language, and interaction signals, reducing dependence on explicit annotations.
- **Expanded Design Space Exploration:** The paper acknowledges that its exploration of boundary definitions and disentanglement strategies is an initial step. Future work can investigate alternative definitions (e.g., 3D geometry, affordances, causality) and architectural designs (e.g., adaptive routing, recurrent memory, structured latent states) to refine world-ego separation.
- **Broader Applications:** The principles of World-Ego Modeling extend beyond video prediction. They could potentially benefit downstream tasks such as policy learning, robot planning, autonomous navigation, interactive simulation, and embodied reasoning, where disentangling environmental stability from agent-induced dynamics could lead to more robust and controllable intelligent systems. For instance, in autonomous driving, separating road scene regularities from ego-vehicle behavior could be impactful.

In conclusion, this research presents a method for addressing critical challenges in embodied world modeling, providing both a novel conceptual framework and a high-performing architectural instantiation. Its contributions have the potential to advance the development of more structured, robust, and controllable AI agents capable of operating effectively in complex, dynamic environments over extended time horizons.
