# SA-VLA: State-aware tokenizer for improving Vision-Language-Action Models' performance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from East China University of Science and Technology, Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Xi’an University of Electronic Science and Technology, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced SA-VLA, a state-aware tokenizer that integrates robot proprioceptive state into discrete action tokenization for Vision-Language-Action models. This approach achieved up to 40% higher average success rates in simulation and up to 25% higher in real-world zero-shot tasks compared to state-agnostic baselines.

## Problem

- Existing discrete action tokenizers for VLA models map each discrete action code to a fixed, continuous action prototype, neglecting the robot's current proprioceptive state.
- This state-agnostic approach creates a 'compression gap,' where identical discrete tokens may correspond to widely varying continuous actions depending on contextual factors like joint configurations or object poses.
- The inability to generate fine-grained, state-dependent actions based on discrete tokens degrades VLA policy performance, particularly in complex manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **SA-VLA**, a ==state-aware action tokenizer==, was developed to integrate robot proprioceptive state information directly into the decoding of discrete action tokens to continuous robot commands.
- Two state injection methods were explored: a ==cross-attention mechanism== within the ==VQ-VAE=='s Transformer layers and a ==lightweight state adapter== that uses an MLP to predict action scaling factors.
- The SA-VLA tokenizer (specifically the lightweight state adapter method) was integrated into an ==LLM-based VLA== architecture, enabling state-conditioned action decoding for both autoregressive and parallel generation.

## Results

- SA-VLA (Method B) achieved an average success rate of **56%** across 12 RoboTwin simulation tasks, marking a **23% to 40%** improvement over existing state-agnostic tokenizers like Binning, FAST, and VQ-BET.
- In zero-shot real-world experiments on an AgileX Cobot Magic platform, SA-VLA (Method B) demonstrated an average success rate of **33%**, outperforming baselines by **18% to 25%** on three manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that integrating state information into the tokenizer consistently improved decoding accuracy and overall task performance, with SA-VLA showing finer-grained action differentiation compared to state-agnostic methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating robot proprioceptive state directly into the action tokenization process is crucial for mitigating the 'compression gap' and improving the precision of continuous action decoding.
> - A lightweight state adapter can effectively modulate discrete tokens, allowing a finite codebook to represent a dynamic and continuous family of state-dependent actions rather than static prototypes.
> - State-aware tokenization significantly enhances the expressiveness of discrete action representations, leading to more robust and adaptable VLA policies in robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotics research, aiming to enable robots to interpret visual observations and language instructions to generate action sequences. These models generally adopt one of two approaches: either directly generating continuous actions or producing discrete action tokens that are subsequently decoded into continuous robot controls via an action tokenizer. This paper focuses on the latter, specifically addressing limitations within the discrete action tokenization framework.

Existing discrete action tokenizers, as used in models like OpenVLA and VQ-VLA, typically map each discrete action code to a fixed, continuous action prototype. This approach neglects the robot's current proprioceptive state, such as joint configurations, object poses, or contact conditions. This disregard for contextual state information presents a challenge, particularly in manipulation tasks, where identical discrete action tokens may necessitate distinct continuous controls depending on the robot's immediate environment and internal state. For instance, a "grasp" action might require different joint movements based on the current position of the gripper relative to an object. Prior work, such as uniform binning in OpenVLA, has been noted for its coarse granularity. FAST attempts to improve compression but can lead to inconsistent sequence lengths, complicating training. VQ-VLA employs residual VQ-VAE for mapping actions to tokens, but its action tokenization granularity has been identified as an area for improvement.

The presented work introduces "State-aware VLA" (SA-VLA) to address this "compression gap"—the discrepancy between discrete action tokens and the continuous, state-dependent actions required for accurate robot control. By integrating state information directly into the action decoding process, SA-VLA aims to enhance the precision and adaptability of discrete action tokenization, thereby improving the performance of VLA policies. This positions the research as an advancement in making discrete action representation more robust and contextually aware for robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized bottleneck in current VLA models that rely on discrete action tokenization: the challenge of accurately converting discrete action tokens into precise continuous robot actions. While discrete action tokens offer a compact interface for autoregressive VLA policies, existing tokenizers often map each discrete code to a static, fixed continuous action prototype. This fixed mapping ignores critical contextual information derived from the robot's proprioceptive state.

The authors highlight that in manipulation tasks, the same discrete action token can correspond to different physical outcomes depending on the robot's current joint configurations, the exact pose of an object, or contact conditions. For example, a single "delta action" intended to advance a gripper might require varying continuous motor commands depending on whether the gripper is far from an object, close to it, or already in contact. If diverse state-dependent actions are all compressed into an identical discrete action token, the model's ability to execute fine-grained, successful manipulations is inherently limited, leading to degraded performance. This limitation is termed the "compression gap."

To address this, the key objectives of the SA-VLA research are:
1. **Introduce State Information:** To incorporate robot proprioceptive state information directly into the action tokenizer. This aims to move beyond fixed action prototypes, allowing the tokenizer to decode continuous actions in a state-conditioned manner.
2. **Improve Decoding Accuracy:** To enhance the accuracy with which continuous robot actions are recovered from discrete tokens by making the decoding process context-aware.
3. **Expand Codebook Expressiveness:** To enable a finite discrete codebook to represent a wider, more flexible range of continuous actions. This is envisioned as allowing each discrete token to represent a "family" of state-dependent continuous actions, rather than a single fixed action.
4. **Maintain Compatibility and Efficiency:** To achieve these improvements while preserving the benefits of discrete action modeling, such as computational efficiency and compatibility with existing LLM-based VLA policy interfaces for both autoregressive and parallel decoding.
5. **Demonstrate Performance Gains:** To empirically validate the effectiveness of state-conditioned action decoding through comparative experiments in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, showcasing improved success rates over state-agnostic tokenizers.

The motivation is further informed by observations of human behavior, where individuals often predict and adapt future actions based on their current state, suggesting a similar mechanism could benefit robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper introduces SA-VLA, a state-aware action tokenizer, designed to integrate robot proprioceptive state information into the process of decoding discrete action tokens into continuous robot commands. The work builds upon a base VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder) architecture and explores two distinct mechanisms for state injection.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to train a policy $\pi_{\theta}(a_{t:t+k} | o_t, s_t, L)$ that predicts a block of $k$ future actions ($a_{t:t+k}$) conditioned on the current observed RGB image ($o_t$), the robot's state ($s_t$, e.g., joint angles), and a language instruction ($L$).

#### 3.2. State-aware Action Tokenizer

The core of SA-VLA involves modifying a standard VQ-VAE-based action tokenizer. The base VQ-VAE uses a CNN and Transformer encoder to extract temporal action features, quantizes these features using a codebook, and then reconstructs actions via a symmetric decoder. SA-VLA enhances this by incorporating state information through two proposed methods:

- **Method A (Cross-Attention):**
 - This approach integrates state information by employing cross-attention mechanisms within the Transformer layers of both the encoder and decoder.
 - Given an input continuous action sequence ($a$), an encoder (comprising a CNN and two Transformer layers) extracts intermediate features. In the Transformer layers, the keys and values are directly derived from the raw robot state input.
 - The intermediate features are then quantized using a VQ-VAE codebook, yielding discrete action embeddings.
 - A decoder, structurally symmetric to the encoder, reconstructs the action. The decoder's Transformer layers also utilize the raw state data for keys and values in their cross-attention mechanisms.
 - This method directly influences the feature transformation and reconstruction based on the robot's state.

- **Method B (Lightweight State Adapter):**
 - This approach is inspired by the concept of humans modulating actions based on current state.
 - A lightweight adapter, consisting of an MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) and an activation function (e.g., sigmoid), processes the robot's state information to predict a scaling factor ($w$) for each dimension of the input action sequence.
 - The original action ($a$) is then transformed into a "state-scaled action" ($a_{trans} = a \div w$).
 - This $a_{trans}$ then passes through an encoder (CNN + Transformer layers) *without* any direct state input to its Transformer components.
 - After quantization via the VQ-VAE codebook, the quantized features are passed to a symmetric decoder (also *without* state input to its Transformers) to reconstruct the transformed action ($\hat{a}_{trans}$).
 - Finally, $\hat{a}_{trans}$ is multiplied by the state-derived scaling factor ($w$) to obtain the final reconstructed action ($\hat{a}$).
 - This method effectively allows a discrete token to represent a class of actions, with the state-dependent scaling factor providing the specific continuous action within that class, thereby extending the effective continuous action space representable by a finite codebook.

#### 3.3. Loss Function

Both methods are trained using a standard VQ-VAE loss function, which is a weighted combination of:
- Reconstruction loss: $L_{res} = ||\hat{a} - a||_2^2$
- Vector Quantization (VQ) loss: $L_{codebook} = \lambda_1 \cdot ||sg(x) - q(x)||_2^2$ (where $sg(\cdot)$ is stop-gradient, $x$ is encoder output, $q(x)$ is quantized features)
- Commitment loss: $L_{commit} = \lambda_2 \cdot ||x - sg(q(x))||_2^2$

The total loss is $L = L_{res} + L_{codebook} + L_{commit}$.

#### 3.4. Integrating State-aware Action Tokenizer in VLA

The SA-VLA tokenizer is integrated into an LLM-based VLA architecture.
- **Input Modalities:**
 - **Language ($L$):** Processed by the base LLM's native tokenizer.
 - **Robot State ($s_t$):** Discretized into 256 bins per joint dimension, following an approach similar to FAST, producing discrete state tokens.
 - **Observation Image ($o_t$):** Resized to 224x224 and tokenized using SigLIP-SO400M-patch14-224, generating 256 continuous image tokens.
 - **Action Sequence ($a'$):** Continuous action sequences are transformed into discrete action tokens ($\tilde{a}$) by the proposed SA-VLA tokenizer (specifically Method B, given its superior performance).
- **Serialization:** All input tokens (text, state, image, action) are serialized into a single sequence using modality-specific boundary tokens (e.g., `[t bos]`, `[i eos]`) to enable autoregressive learning.
- **Decoding Strategies:** The VLA model operates under two decoding paradigms:
 - **Autoregressive Decoding (AD):** Action tokens are generated sequentially, predicting the next token conditioned on previous tokens and the full context.
 - **Parallel Decoding (PD):** The fixed-length discrete action sequences produced by the tokenizer allow for parallel generation of the entire action block in a single forward pass, utilizing bidirectional attention and placeholder embeddings.
The LLM itself remains largely unchanged, with minimal modifications to its input interface and attention mask to accommodate the state-aware action tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both simulation and real-world experiments, along with ablation studies, to evaluate the SA-VLA tokenizer.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments on RoboTwin

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on 12 RoboTwin manipulation tasks, using a dataset of 19,200 trajectories (1,600 per task). A Qwen2.5-based VLM was used in conjunction with various action tokenizers for comparison. Baselines included Binning (from OpenVLA), FAST, and VQ-BET (from VQ-VLA). SA-VLA was evaluated using both Method A and Method B, under both Autoregressive Decoding (AR) and Parallel Decoding (PD) strategies. Success rates were computed from 100 rollouts per task.
- **Main Results:**
 - SA-VLA methods consistently demonstrated significant improvements over existing tokenization approaches.
 - **Method B (AR) achieved the highest average success rate of 56%** across the 12 tasks. This represents a 23% improvement over the Binning tokenizer (0.24 average success rate), a 40% improvement over the FAST tokenizer (0.17), and a 28% improvement over the VQ-BET tokenizer (0.29).
 - Method A (AR) also performed strongly, achieving an average success rate of 55%.
 - Parallel decoding (PD) versions of SA-VLA (Method A PD: 52%, Method B PD: 56%) also showed high performance, often comparable to their autoregressive counterparts and notably superior to baseline tokenizers.
- **Results Analysis:** The authors attribute Method B's superior performance to its ability to modulate the discrete codebook tokens with state-derived scaling factors. This mechanism allows a single discrete token to represent a continuous family of actions, thereby circumventing the fixed action correspondence limitation of traditional VQ-VAE and enabling a more flexible, continuous action space.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Role of State Information:**
 - Experiments compared VLA models with and without state information integrated into the action tokenizer.
 - Models *without* state information achieved average success rates of 43% (PD) and 51% (AR).
 - In contrast, SA-VLA (Method A: 52% PD, 55% AR; Method B: 56% PD, 56% AR) consistently showed higher success rates. This indicates that incorporating state information directly into the action tokenizer enhances decoding accuracy and overall task performance.
- **Model Generalization:**
 - Evaluated on four tasks: two unseen tasks ("Shake Bottle Horizontally" and "Place Empty Cup" in a clean environment) and two tasks in unseen scenarios ("Handover Mic" and "Place Container Plate" in a random environment).
 - Results showed varying generalization capabilities: "Shake Bottle Horizontally" achieved a high success rate (0.95), while others like "Handover Mic" (0.10) and "Place Container Plate" (0.16) in random environments were lower, suggesting task and environment dependency.
- **Tokenization Granularity:**
 - On the "Beat Block Hammer" task, a state-agnostic tokenizer mapped four similar action timesteps to identical discrete indices.
 - SA-VLA, however, was able to distinguish these four actions, showing cosine similarity differences as fine as 0.001. This demonstrates SA-VLA's capability for finer-grained action tokenization by leveraging state context.

#### 4.3. Real-world Experiments (Zero-shot Sim-to-Real)

- **Setup:** Performed on an AgileX Cobot Magic mobile platform with an Aloha setup (AgileX Piper arms and RealSense D435 camera). The VLA models, trained solely on RoboTwin RGB images, were directly applied to three real-world tasks: "Click Bell," "Place Container Plate," and "Pick Diverse Bottles." Each task involved 20 trials. Method B tokenizer was used for these experiments due to its stronger simulation performance.
- **Main Results:**
 - SA-VLA (Method B) demonstrated improved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities compared to baselines.
 - **SA-VLA (AR) achieved an average success rate of 33%** across the three real-world tasks. This was an improvement of 23% over the Binning tokenizer (10%), 25% over FAST (8%), and 18% over VQ-BET (15%).
 - Task-specific performance for SA-VLA (AR) was: "Click Bell" (10/20 successes), "Place Container Plate" (7/20 successes), and "Pick Diverse Bottles" (3/20 successes). Baseline tokenizers often yielded 0 successes for "Place Container Plate" and "Pick Diverse Bottles."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research demonstrates that integrating robot proprioceptive state information into the action tokenization process can significantly enhance the performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly those relying on discrete action representations.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addressing the Compression Gap:** The work provides an effective mechanism, SA-VLA, for mitigating the "compression gap" inherent in discrete action tokenization. By conditioning action decoding on the robot's state, SA-VLA moves beyond the limitation of fixed continuous action prototypes for each discrete token.
- **Improved Action Decoding:** The proposed methods, particularly Method B with its lightweight state adapter, facilitate more precise and context-aware decoding of continuous robot actions. This approach effectively expands the expressive power of a finite codebook, allowing discrete tokens to represent a dynamic family of state-dependent actions.
- **Empirical Performance Gains:** The consistent and substantial improvements observed in both extensive simulation experiments (up to 40% higher average success rate than baselines) and zero-shot real-world tasks (up to 25% higher average success rate) validate the practical utility of state-aware tokenization.
- **Compatibility with VLA Architectures:** SA-VLA seamlessly integrates into existing LLM-based VLA policies, supporting both autoregressive and parallel decoding strategies with minimal architectural modifications.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Robotic Manipulation:** The increased precision and adaptability of action generation could lead to more robust, reliable, and generalized performance for robotic manipulation tasks, especially those requiring fine-grained control or operating in variable environments.
- **Advancement of VLA Models:** The concept of state-aware action decoding could serve as a foundational improvement for future VLA architectures, offering a pathway to bridge the gap between high-level language commands and low-level robot control more effectively.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The authors identify several directions for future exploration, which highlight the potential impact of this work:
 - **Scalability:** Investigating the performance and scaling laws of SA-VLA on larger datasets and with increased model parameters.
 - **Alternative Generative Models:** Exploring the integration of other generative models, such as diffusion models, in place of VQ-VAE within the tokenizer framework to potentially further improve action generation quality and fidelity.
 - **Embodiment Transfer:** Extending the applicability and performance validation of state-aware tokenization to different robot embodiments, such as dexterous hands, beyond the current focus on robotic arms.

In summary, this research contributes a principled and empirically validated approach to making discrete action representations more capable and contextually intelligent, thereby advancing the development of more effective and generalizable VLA models for complex robotic tasks.
