# Spanning the Visual Analogy Space with a Weight Basis of LoRAs

> [!summary] Summary
> LoRWeB introduces a framework for visual analogy learning that dynamically generates task-specific LoRA adapters from a learned basis, enabling models to apply diverse transformations from example pairs. This approach achieves superior generalization to unseen analogy tasks, producing higher quality and more consistent visual outputs compared to methods using single, fixed-capacity adapters.

## Problem

- Text-based image editing models struggle to articulate complex, nuanced, or abstract visual transformations precisely, limiting creative control for users.
- Existing visual analogy learning methods relying on single Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules face a fundamental capacity bottleneck, hindering generalization to a vast and diverse range of unseen transformations.
- There was a need for a stable and efficient mechanism to dynamically specialize generative models for individual analogy tasks at inference time, avoiding the instability and training difficulties of hypernetworks.

## Method

- ==LoRWeB== proposes a novel architecture that dynamically composes a task-specific LoRA from a ==learned basis of multiple low-rank LoRA modules==, each associated with a unique, learnable key vector.
- A lightweight encoder (router) processes input analogy images using frozen ==CLIP embeddings== to generate a query vector, which then computes scalar coefficients via a softmax function to combine the basis LoRAs.
- The resulting dynamically composed "Mixed LoRA" is injected into a pre-trained conditional flow model (FLUX.1-Kontext) to generate analogously transformed images, specializing the model for each specific analogy task.

## Results

- Qualitative evaluations demonstrate LoRWeB's superior generalization to a wide variety of novel analogy tasks, accurately applying diverse transformations with enhanced detail preservation and consistency.
- Quantitative metrics, including VLM-based assessments (Gemma-3), show LoRWeB achieves higher 'Edit Accuracy' and 'Preservation' scores, significantly improving the Pareto front compared to all baselines.
- In 2-alternative-forced-choice (2AFC) evaluations, both Gemma-3 and human users strongly preferred LoRWeB's outputs over all baselines, with Gemma-3 preferring LoRWeB over Edit Transfer **83.6%** of the time and over the r=128 LoRA baseline **57.6%** of the time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dynamically composed basis of low-rank LoRAs can effectively span the visual analogy space, overcoming the generalization limitations of single, fixed-capacity adapters.
> - Separately encoding analogy images (`a`, `a'`, `b`) with a powerful Vision-Language Model (CLIP) provides richer semantic context for the router, leading to better editing accuracy compared to encoding a composite image.
> - Using a softmax function for combining basis LoRAs is crucial for stability and performance, preventing overly large LoRA norms that can arise with alternative coefficient-weighting functions.
> - A larger basis of smaller-rank adapters (e.g., N=32, r=4) is more effective for generalization across diverse transformations than a smaller basis of higher-rank adapters or a single high-capacity LoRA.
