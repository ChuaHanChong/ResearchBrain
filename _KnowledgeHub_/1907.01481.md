# HOnnotate: A method for 3D Annotation of Hand and Object Poses

> [!summary] Summary
> HOnnotate automatically annotates RGB-D sequences of a hand manipulating an object with 3D poses of both, by jointly optimizing MANO hand and rigid object poses over all frames at once through a differentiable renderer, with silhouette, depth, 2D-joint, interpenetration and temporal terms. It produced HO-3D, the first markerless real-image hand+object 3D pose dataset (77,558 frames, 68 sequences, 10 subjects, 10 YCB objects), plus a single-RGB hand pose baseline.

## Problem

- Single-image 3D hand and object pose estimators break under the mutual occlusions of grasping, and no real annotated hand+object dataset exists to train them.
- Prior annotation relies on frame-by-frame tracking needing manual initialization and drifting over time, or on magnetic sensors taped to the hand that visibly corrupt color images and bias learning.
- Synthetic grasp datasets give perfect labels but suffer a domain gap and cannot render complex realistic manipulation, so real images with accurate annotation remain necessary for evaluation.

## Method

- Global energy minimization over a whole sequence, bundle-adjustment style, combining a silhouette discrepancy term, a Tukey-robust depth residual, a 2D hand-joint heatmap term, and a point-cloud 3D distance term via a ==differentiable renderer==.
- Constraint energies: per-parameter joint-angle limits instead of ==MANO== PCA space (too weak for complex grasps), an exponential repulsion term penalizing hand-object interpenetration, and a 0th/1st-order temporal consistency prior, optimized with ==Adam== in 20-frame batches.
- Staged initialization pipeline: ==DeepLabV3== hand/object segmentation from synthetic overlays, ==CPM== heatmaps plus per-frame tracking for multi-camera; for a single camera, a constant-grasp-pose assumption relaxed through grasp-pose, object-pose, then joint refinement stages.

## Results

- Against manual multi-camera annotation, the method reaches under **8mm** average joint error with AUC **0.79**, on par with FreiHAND's **0.791** despite larger occluding objects and no green screen.
- Ablation shows silhouette or depth alone are weak (**4.20cm** and **1.17cm**); all terms with multi-frame joint optimization cut hand-joint error from **0.92cm** to **0.77cm**, roughly **15%** better than per-frame optimization.
- Single-camera annotation matches multi-camera within **0.77cm** hand and **0.45cm** object mesh error; the RGB baseline predicting 2D joints plus direction vectors gets **1.06cm** mesh error, F@5mm **0.51**, **3.04cm** joint error, beating direct MANO parameter regression at **1.10**/**0.46**/**3.18**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Batch optimization over an entire sequence exploits temporal consistency far more strongly than frame-to-frame tracking, avoiding drift and manual reinitialization, essentially bundle adjustment applied to poses rather than points.
> - Differentiable rendering lets deep-learning optimizers minimize classic geometric energies, making automatic annotation a gradient-descent problem rather than a hand-crafted tracking pipeline.
> - Predicting 2D keypoints plus joint directions and fitting a parametric model beats regressing model parameters directly; interpenetration penalties improve grasp plausibility without improving pose accuracy numbers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D pose estimation for hands and rigid objects from monocular images has experienced progress, particularly due to advancements in Deep Learning and the availability of large datasets. However, a significant challenge persists when a hand interacts with an object. This challenge primarily stems from extensive mutual occlusions between the hand and the object, combined with a lack of datasets specifically designed for 3D hand-object interaction pose estimation. Existing methods often fail to accurately estimate poses under these conditions.

Prior research on joint hand and object pose estimation has typically relied on tracking algorithms that leverage temporal and sometimes physical constraints. While these approaches can yield results, they often require manual initialization and are susceptible to tracking drift over extended sequences. There is a recognized need for data-driven methods that can estimate 3D hand and object poses from a single image, which would mitigate the issues associated with tracking. However, developing such data-driven methods necessitates large datasets of real images accurately annotated with 3D poses of both hands and objects.

The creation of such annotated data presents its own difficulties. Existing methods for generating 3D annotations for real images either produce noisy "ground truth" through automatic tracking, or involve visible sensors on the hand, which can bias learning algorithms. Synthetic image generation offers precise 3D poses but faces challenges in achieving realistic rendering, physically accurate grasps, and complex manipulation, leading to a domain gap that can hinder generalization to real-world data. Real images with accurate 3D annotations are also essential for evaluating the performance of methods trained on synthetic data.

Against this background, this work contributes to the landscape by addressing the fundamental problem of acquiring accurate 3D annotations for real images of hand-object interactions. By proposing an automatic annotation method, the authors aim to bridge the data gap that currently limits the development of robust, single-image data-driven methods for 3D hand and object pose estimation. The resulting dataset, HO-3D, aims to provide a markerless benchmark with both 3D hand and object pose annotations, filling a specific void compared to datasets that are synthetic, sensor-laden, or only provide partial annotations (e.g., hand only, object only, 2D only, simple objects, or limited interaction scenarios). The subsequent development of a single RGB image-based prediction method using this dataset further illustrates the potential for advancing data-driven approaches in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized gap in the availability of annotated real images for the problem of 3D hand and object pose estimation during interaction. The authors identify two main challenges:
1. **Mutual Occlusions:** When a hand manipulates an object, there are often significant occlusions where the hand covers parts of the object and vice versa. These occlusions make accurate 3D pose estimation difficult for both components.
2. **Lack of Specific Datasets:** Existing datasets for 3D hand pose estimation often do not include interactions with diverse objects, or lack corresponding 3D object pose annotations. Datasets for general 3D object pose estimation do not typically feature hands interacting with the objects, and those that do have limitations such as being synthetic, relying on intrusive markers, or offering only partial annotations.

To address these challenges, the paper outlines several key objectives:
1. **Develop an Automatic 3D Annotation Method:** The central objective is to propose a method capable of automatically annotating images of a hand manipulating an object with accurate 3D poses for both the hand and the object. This method is designed to be robust against large mutual occlusions by exploiting temporal consistency across image sequences.
2. **Create a Novel Dataset (HO-3D):** Using the developed annotation method, a major objective is to construct HO-3D, described as the first markerless dataset of color images featuring 3D annotations for both the hand and the object during interaction. This dataset aims to provide a resource for training and evaluating data-driven methods.
3. **Demonstrate Dataset Utility through Baseline Prediction:** To validate the practicality and effectiveness of the HO-3D dataset, an objective is to develop and evaluate a baseline method for predicting 3D hand pose from a single RGB image, even under severe occlusions with interacting objects. This demonstration also seeks to show the generalization capability of the method to objects not seen during training.
4. **Investigate the Role of Multi-Camera vs. Single-Camera Capture:** The method aims to be flexible, working with a single RGB-D camera while also leveraging multiple cameras for enhanced robustness and accuracy. This provides options for dataset expansion by other researchers.
5. **Leverage Differentiable Rendering and Optimization:** The method is motivated by the potential of differentiable rendering and gradient descent techniques to jointly optimize complex objective functions over entire sequences, drawing parallels to bundle adjustment in SLAM.

In essence, the research aims to overcome the data bottleneck in hand-object interaction analysis by providing a scalable, automatic annotation solution and a foundational dataset, thereby enabling the development of more robust single-image 3D pose estimation techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method is a fully automatic approach for annotating RGB-D image sequences of hand-object interactions with 3D poses of both the hand and the object. This is achieved through a multi-stage, global optimization framework that minimizes a complex energy function over entire sequences.

### 4. 3D Hand and Object Pose Representation

- **Hand Model:** The **MANO hand model** is utilized to represent the hand. This model consists of 45 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) for finger articulation (3 DoF per of 15 finger joints) and 6 DoF for the global rotation and translation of the wrist joint. Hand shape parameters (10 DoF) are also estimated and fixed for each person.
- **Object Model:** Objects from the **YCB-Video dataset** are used, for which 3D models are available. Each object's pose consists of 6 DoF for global rotation and translation.

### 5. Cost Function Formulation

The problem is formulated as an energy minimization problem, jointly optimizing all 3D hand ($p_h^t$) and object ($p_o^t$) poses over all $N_F$ frames of a sequence:
$P = \arg \min_P \sum_{t=1}^{N_F} (E_D(p_h^t, p_o^t) + E_C(p_h^t, p_o^t))$
The total energy comprises data terms ($E_D$) and constraint terms ($E_C$).

#### 5.1. Data Terms ($E_D$)

The data term aggregates information from RGB-D cameras and 2D joint predictions:
$E_D = \sum_{c=1}^{N_C} (\alpha E_{mask}(I_c^t, p_h^t, p_o^t) + \beta E_{dpt}(D_c^t, p_h^t, p_o^t) + \gamma E_{j2D}(I_c^t, p_h^t)) + \delta E_{3D}({D_c^t}_{c=1..N_C}, p_h^t, p_o^t)$
- **Silhouette Discrepancy Term ($E_{mask}$):** Compares the rendered silhouettes of the hand and object models with their segmentation masks obtained from DeepLabv3 (trained on synthetic hand-object overlay images). This term utilizes a differentiable renderer for gradient computation.
- **Depth Residual Term ($E_{dpt}$):** Compares the rendered depth maps of the hand and object models with the captured depth map. A robust Tukey function is used to handle noise and small model deviations. Also uses a differentiable renderer.
- **2D Joint Error Term ($E_{j2D}$):** Measures the squared Euclidean distance between projected 3D hand joint locations and their 2D predicted locations. These 2D predictions are obtained from a CNN (based on) trained on an initial semi-automatic hand-object dataset and augmented data.
- **3D Error Term ($E_{3D}$):** Calculates the squared Euclidean distance between points in the merged 3D point cloud (from all RGB-D cameras) and their closest vertices on the hand and object meshes. This term guides optimization and speeds up convergence.

#### 5.2. Constraint Terms ($E_C$)

The constraint term introduces regularization and physical plausibility:
$E_C = \iota E_{joint}(p_h^t) + \zeta E_{phy}(p_h^t, p_o^t) + \eta E_{tc}(p_h^t, p_o^t, p_h^{t-1}, p_o^{t-1}, p_h^{t-2}, p_o^{t-2})$
- **Joint Angle Constraint ($E_{joint}$):** Penalizes unnatural hand poses by enforcing limits on the 45 joint angle parameters of the MANO model, empirically derived. This differs from PCA-space regularization to allow for more complex grasps.
- **Physical Plausibility Term ($E_{phy}$):** A repulsion term that prevents the hand model from interpenetrating the object model. It is based on the penetration amount between hand vertices and their closest object vertices, projected onto the object's surface normal.
- **Temporal Consistency Term ($E_{tc}$):** Applies 0-th and 1-st order motion models to both hand and object poses across frames, effectively constraining poses in a sequence to be smooth and consistent over time.

### 6. Optimization Strategy

Due to the high dimensionality and non-convexity of the problem, a multi-stage optimization process is employed, with variations for multi-camera and single-camera setups (illustrated in Fig. 2).

#### 6.1. Multi-Camera Setup Optimization

1. **Initialization:**
 - Hand pose for the first frame is estimated by minimizing $E_{j2D}$ and $E_{joint}$ using the Dogleg optimizer.
 - Object pose for the first frame is estimated using a robust 6D object pose estimator.
2. **Single-frame Joint Pose Optimization (Tracking):** Hand and object poses for subsequent frames are estimated sequentially by minimizing the combined $E_D + E_C$ term, initialized with the previous frame's estimates. This acts as a tracking step.
3. **Multi-frame Joint Pose Optimization (Refinement):** A global optimization of the full energy function (Eq. 1) is performed simultaneously over all frames, or batches of 20 frames, using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.01 for 100 iterations. This strong temporal consistency ensures refined, globally consistent poses.

#### 6.2. Single-Camera Setup Optimization

This setup assumes marginal variation in grasp pose over a sequence.
1. **Initialization:**
 - Hand poses are initialized by minimizing $E_{j2D}$ and $E_{joint}$ over a random subset of frames, assuming constant joint angle parameters and only varying wrist rotation/translation.
 - Object poses for the subset are initialized as in the multi-camera setup.
 - An initial constant grasp pose in the object coordinate system is computed as the average of grasp poses from the subset.
2. **Grasp Pose Estimation:** The constant grasp pose and object poses for the subset frames are refined by minimizing $E_D + E_{phy} + E_{joint}$.
3. **Object Pose Estimation:** Object poses for all frames are estimated independently by minimizing $E_D$, with hand pose derived from the refined constant grasp pose.
4. **Multi-frame Joint Hand+Object Pose Refinement:** Finally, the full energy function (Eq. 1) is optimized over all frames, allowing grasp pose variations and introducing temporal constraints, similar to the multi-camera refinement stage.

### 7. Monocular RGB-Based 3D Hand Pose Prediction Baseline

To demonstrate the dataset's utility, a baseline method for 3D hand pose prediction from a single RGB image is developed:
- A CNN architecture based on Convolutional Pose Machine (CPM) predicts 2D hand joint locations.
- An additional stage is added to the CPM to predict root-relative hand joint direction vectors.
- A MANO model is then fitted to these 2D joint predictions and 3D joint direction vectors by minimizing a loss function that includes terms for 2D joint error, cosine similarity for direction vectors, joint angle constraints, and shape regularization.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The research yields an annotation method and a new dataset, HO-3D, and demonstrates its application through a single-image hand pose prediction baseline.

### 9. HO-3D Dataset Characteristics

- **Scale:** The HO-3D dataset comprises 77,558 frames across 68 sequences.
- **Diversity:** It includes interactions from 10 different persons manipulating 10 distinct objects selected from the YCB-Video dataset.
- **Capture:** Sequences were captured using either a single RGB-D camera or a multi-camera setup (5 synchronized RGB-D cameras).
- **Annotations:** It provides 3D pose annotations for both the hand and the object in real, markerless color images.

### 10. Evaluation of the Annotation Method

- **Multi-Camera Setup Accuracy:** The annotation method was validated against manually annotated 3D joint locations from a consolidated point cloud of 5 cameras.
 - It achieved an average joint error of **7.7 mm** (standard deviation 2.9 mm) with an Area Under the Curve (AUC) metric of 0.79. This is reported to be comparable to the FreiHAND dataset (AUC=0.791), despite HO-3D having larger occlusions and not using green screens.
- **Ablation Study of Cost Function Terms (Table 2):**
 - Individual `E_mask` or `E_dpt` terms alone did not provide good pose estimates.
 - Combining `E_mask` and `E_dpt` terms yielded better estimates.
 - The `E_3D` term provided a minor improvement (0.98cm to 0.92cm) and accelerated convergence.
 - The `E_phy` term did not improve pose accuracy but resulted in more natural grasps.
 - The multi-frame optimization (including `E_tc`) improved accuracy by approximately **15%** compared to single-frame optimization (0.92cm to 0.77cm).
- **Single-Camera Setup Accuracy (Table 3):**
 - The accuracy of the single-camera annotation method was evaluated by comparing its output against the multi-camera annotations, treated as ground truth.
 - The estimated poses showed consistency, with an average mesh error of **0.77 cm for the hand** and **0.45 cm for the object**.
 - The final multi-frame refinement stage in the single-camera setup improved accuracy by 15%.
 - These results suggest that the single-camera method can achieve comparable annotation quality to the multi-camera setup.

### 11. Evaluation of Hand Pose Prediction Method (Baseline)

- **Training and Test:** The proposed single-frame hand pose prediction method was trained on 66,034 frames from HO-3D and evaluated on a test set of 11,524 frames, including subjects and objects not present in the training set.
- **Performance Metrics (Table 4):**
 - **Mean Joint Error:** 3.04 cm (after aligning root joint and global scale).
 - **Mesh Error:** 1.06 cm (average Euclidean distance between aligned meshes).
 - **F-score:** 0.51 @ 5mm, 0.94 @ 15mm.
- **Impact of Joint Direction Predictions:** Predicting joint direction vectors in addition to 2D joint locations significantly improved hand pose estimation accuracy (e.g., Mesh Error from 1.14 cm to 1.06 cm).
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art:** The method of predicting 2D hand joint locations and fitting a MANO model, particularly with joint direction predictions, performed more accurately than directly predicting MANO parameters as done in (e.g., Mesh Error 1.06 cm vs. 1.10 cm, Mean Joint Error 3.04 cm vs. 3.18 cm).
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects (Fig. 3, 8):** Qualitative results indicate that the method can robustly predict hand poses even when interacting with objects not seen in the training dataset.

In summary, the research successfully developed an automatic, markerless 3D annotation method for hand-object interactions, validated its accuracy across multi-camera and single-camera setups, and created the HO-3D dataset. The subsequent baseline hand pose prediction method demonstrated the dataset's value for training robust models, particularly highlighting the benefit of predicting joint directions and model fitting over direct parameter regression.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental limitation in the development of robust data-driven methods for 3D hand and object pose estimation in interactive scenarios: the scarcity of accurately annotated real-world data. The significance and potential impact of this work can be categorized as follows:

1. **Enabling Data-Driven Research:** The primary impact is the provision of a fully automatic, markerless method for generating 3D annotations for hand and object poses, even under significant mutual occlusions. This method can potentially scale the creation of complex interaction datasets. By overcoming the challenges of manual annotation, intrusive markers, and the domain gap of purely synthetic data, the proposed method offers a practical solution for data acquisition.
2. **Introduction of HO-3D Dataset:** The creation of HO-3D, the first markerless dataset of real color images with simultaneous 3D hand and object pose annotations, is a direct and substantial contribution. This dataset fills a critical void for benchmarking and training algorithms in 3D hand-object interaction. Its characteristics (diverse objects, multiple users, challenging occlusions, multi-camera capture) make it a valuable resource for the computer vision and robotics communities. The public availability of such a dataset can accelerate research in this area.
3. **Advancement in Hand-Object Pose Estimation:** The research directly contributes to advancing single RGB image-based 3D hand pose estimation. By providing a baseline method trained on HO-3D, the paper demonstrates that accurate predictions are possible even with severe occlusions, and that leveraging 2D keypoints and joint direction vectors for MANO model fitting can be more effective than direct parameter regression. The observed generalization to unseen objects is also a notable aspect, suggesting practical applicability.
4. **Applications in Augmented Reality and Robotics:** Accurate 3D hand and object pose estimation is crucial for various applications.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR):** Understanding how users interact with real-world objects is fundamental for seamless AR experiences, enabling realistic virtual object interactions or overlaying contextual information.
 - **Robotics:** For learning-by-imitation, robots require precise understanding of human manipulation. The detailed 3D pose data provided by this work can inform robotic grasping, manipulation planning, and human-robot collaboration.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a foundation for several future research directions. It provides a benchmark for evaluating new algorithms for joint hand-object pose estimation from single RGB frames. It also highlights areas for further improvement, such as enhancing segmentation accuracy and integrating more sophisticated physics constraints (e.g., attraction terms), which could lead to even more accurate annotations and robust pose estimators. The flexibility of the annotation method (single vs. multi-camera) also allows other researchers to expand the dataset with their own objects and grasping poses.

In conclusion, this research significantly contributes by providing a solution to the data bottleneck in 3D hand-object interaction analysis, offering a valuable dataset and demonstrating its utility for developing robust data-driven models with practical implications across various fields.
