# UFM: A Simple Path towards Unified Dense Correspondence with Flow

> [!summary] Summary
> A single transformer-based model, Unified Flow & Matching (UFM), integrates optical flow and wide-baseline matching, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both domains. The model demonstrates significant error reduction, such as 62% less End-Point Error compared to RoMa for wide-baseline matching, and improved efficiency through joint training on diverse datasets.

## Problem

- Dense image correspondence has been traditionally split into two separate domains: optical flow estimation (small temporal displacements) and wide-baseline matching (large viewpoint changes).
- Existing specialized models are suboptimal for real-world scenarios that often present mixed challenges, limiting their generalization across both small and large displacement conditions.
- Previous attempts to unify these tasks had limited success, failing to match the performance of state-of-the-art specialized methods in their respective domains.

## Method

- The ==Unified Flow & Matching (UFM) model== employs a simple, end-to-end transformer-based architecture that directly regresses ==dense pixel displacements and covisibility masks==.
- It utilizes shared ==DINOv2 ViT-L encoders== for feature extraction, followed by 12 layers of self-attention transformers for feature fusion, with separate ==Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT) heads== for flow and covisibility prediction.
- The model is trained on a comprehensive dataset compiled from **12 diverse sources** covering both optical flow and wide-baseline domains, using robust loss functions and supervising only co-visible pixels.

## Results

- UFM560 achieves a **6**2%** reduction** in End-Point Error and is **6.**7x** faster** compared to RoMa on wide-baseline dense correspondence benchmarks (ETH3D, DTU, TA-WB), and **6**0x** faster** than MASt3R with comparable precision.
- UFM980 achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on the Sintel-Final and KITTI datasets for optical flow, and competitive performance on Sintel-Clean.
- The model demonstrates state-of-the-art accuracy for relative pose estimation on ETH3D and TA-WB benchmarks, indicating its learned correspondences are robust for 3D reconstruction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint training on diverse optical flow and wide-baseline data leads to mutual performance improvements, with optical flow benefiting from wide-baseline data and wide-baseline matching benefiting from optical flow data.
> - The UFM architecture demonstrates superior scalability compared to existing specialized methods, showing significant performance gains with increased computational resources when trained on unified datasets.
> - Analysis of DINOv2 layers reveals that intermediate layers (L9-L11) encode the most effective features for correspondence, supporting the decision to fine-tune the encoder during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dense image correspondence, the task of finding where each pixel in one image appears in another, is a foundational problem in computer vision, with critical applications ranging from visual odometry and 3D reconstruction to object association and augmented reality. Historically, this problem has been bifurcated into two distinct sub-fields:

- **Optical Flow Estimation:** Focuses on small displacements between temporally adjacent frames, typically in dynamic scenes. Methods often rely on motion priors and temporal consistency.
- **Wide-Baseline Matching:** Deals with large viewpoint, illumination, or temporal changes between images, often assuming static scenes and requiring robust geometric and semantic cues.

This division has led to specialized models that excel in one domain but struggle to generalize to the other, creating a significant challenge for real-world scenarios where both small and large motions can co-occur.

Prior attempts to unify these tasks, such as GLUNet and RGM, have explored common frameworks. GLUNet was an early effort to show that geometric, optical flow, and semantic correspondence could be tackled by a single network. RGM scaled a RAFT-like architecture on a comprehensive dataset but notably "failed to show that the generalist model, trained on all data, outperforms the specialized model, trained on in-domain data only." Other methods like COTR proposed point-wise query formulations but were prohibitively expensive for dense flow. These prior efforts highlighted the potential for unification but also revealed the difficulties in achieving generalist models that are both effective and efficient across domains.

UFM positions itself as a critical advancement in this landscape by directly confronting the limitations of previous unified approaches and specialized models. The authors argue that existing architectures suffer from biases that make them either inefficient for learning from diverse, large-scale data or ill-suited for dense, high-resolution outputs. UFM's core contribution is to demonstrate, *for the first time*, that a truly unified model, trained on a comprehensive dataset spanning both optical flow and wide-baseline matching, can *mutually benefit* from this shared training and *outperform specialized state-of-the-art methods* in both domains.

The paper also connects to the broader trend of "scaling correspondence" through larger datasets and more generic architectures. Works like MatchFlow and SEA-RAFT have utilized pre-training on wide-baseline datasets for optical flow tasks, showing benefits in generalization. Similarly, there's a growing interest in generic transformer architectures (e.g., Perceiver IO, CroCoV2) for diverse vision tasks, including optical flow. UFM builds on this by employing a simple, generic transformer architecture and scaling it with an unprecedented 12 diverse datasets, arguing for its superior efficiency and generalizability compared to more specialized designs or those requiring computationally intensive sliding windows for high-resolution output. By achieving mutual benefit and SOTA performance, UFM represents a significant step towards creating "foundation models" for dense correspondence, analogous to large language models in NLP.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **Unified Flow & Matching model (UFM)** that can robustly and accurately estimate dense pixel correspondence across a wide spectrum of visual scenarios, encompassing both traditional optical flow and challenging wide-baseline matching. The authors are driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridge the Performance Gap:** Address the historical bifurcation of dense correspondence tasks, where specialized models excel in one domain but fail to generalize to others. The goal is to create a single model that not only generalizes but also *outperforms* these specialized approaches across both domains.
2. **Overcome Architectural Biases:** Identify and rectify the limitations of existing specialized architectures (e.g., coarse-to-fine cost volumes) which are often inefficient when learning from large, diverse data or are not designed for direct, high-resolution dense output. The aim is to find an architecture that is simpler, easier to train, and more accurate for large flows.
3. **Demonstrate Mutual Benefit of Unified Training:** A central hypothesis is that simultaneously training on both optical flow and wide-baseline matching data will lead to mutual improvements, enabling the model to learn more robust and generalizable features. This directly addresses the shortcomings of previous unified attempts that failed to show this mutual benefit.
4. **Achieve Efficiency and Accuracy:** Develop a model that provides both state-of-the-art accuracy and high inference speed, making it practical for real-time and compute-limited applications.
5. **Spur Future Research:** Introduce a novel, challenging benchmark dataset, TartanAir-Wide Baseline (TA-WB), to encourage further research and rigorous evaluation of dense wide-baseline correspondence methods.
6. **Enable New Applications:** Pave the way for general-purpose correspondence that can benefit multi-modal, long-range, and real-time vision tasks critical for fields like robotics and augmented reality.

In essence, the authors sought to answer: "Can we develop a unified model that benefits from shared training on both optical flow and wide-baseline matching data? Specifically, what architecture, data, loss, and training scheme do we need to unify flow & matching?"

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UFM's methodology is characterized by a "simple path" towards unification, focusing on a generic transformer architecture, comprehensive data aggregation, and tailored training objectives.

#### 3.1. UFM Architecture

The core of UFM is an **end-to-end transformer-based regression model** designed to directly predict pixel displacement (flow) and a covisibility mask.
- **Feature Encoding:** Two input images are processed by a shared DINOv2 ViT-L encoder, which produces patch tokens. DINOv2 is a large-scale self-supervised visual features model, chosen for its optimality in encoding robust visual features.
- **Global Attention Transformer:** The patch tokens from both images are concatenated, infused with view-index positional encodings, and then processed by 12 successive self-attention transformer layers. The use of self-attention across concatenated features, accelerated by Flash-Attention, is chosen for better training and inference efficiency compared to cross-attention blocks, while maintaining similar performance.
- **Prediction Heads:** Separate DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer) heads are applied to the encoded patch tokens (utilizing features from intermediate transformer layers) to directly regress the forward pixel displacement map (φ₁) and logits for the covisibility mask (C₁). This direct regression approach contrasts with prior methods that often construct initial solutions at a patch level and progressively upsample.
- **Refinement by Classification (UFM-Refine):** To boost precision, especially for sub-pixel accuracy, a refinement module is introduced. It combines globally aligned features from the UFM backbone with local fine features from a separate U-Net encoder. Instead of global matching, this module leverages the UFM base model's regressed flow to guide a local feature matching process within a small 7x7 neighborhood. Attention is computed between source features and interpolated neighborhood features, and their weighted sum of coordinates serves as a residual to refine the initial flow.

#### 3.2. Training Objective

UFM's training is meticulously designed to optimize both flow accuracy and covisibility.
- **Supervision on Covisible Pixels:** Crucially, flow supervision is *restricted exclusively to co-visible pixels*. This encourages the model to ground its correspondence predictions in visual evidence rather than inferring 3D geometry or extrapolating into occluded regions.
- **Robust Regression Loss:** For flow (L_EPE), a generalized Charbonnier loss (α=0.5, c=0.24) is employed. This robust loss focuses gradients on inlier predictions with small errors (1-2 pixels), while exhibiting vanishing gradients for large errors caused by unmatchable pairs, thus reducing the impact of noisy ground truth.
- **Binary Cross-Entropy Loss:** For the covisibility mask (L_BCE), standard binary cross-entropy loss is used, with a weighting factor of 10 to prioritize accurate covisibility prediction. The final loss is L = L_EPE + 10 × L_BCE.

#### 3.3. Combining Flow and Matching Datasets

A major innovation is the compilation of a **unified dataset** comprising 12 diverse datasets spanning both optical flow and wide-baseline matching domains.
- **Dataset Diversity:** This collection includes sources like BlendedMVS, MegaDepth, TartanAirV2, ScanNet++ V2 (static wide-baseline), and FlyingThings, FlyingChairs, Spring, Monkaa, HD1K (dynamic optical flow), as well as Kubric4D (dynamic and wide-baseline).
- **Data Vetting and Pairing:** Datasets were rigorously vetted for depth consistency and geometric correctness. A custom geometric sampler was developed to curate image pairs with explicit control over viewpoint differences and ensure sufficient co-visibility, especially for static wide-baseline datasets. Covisibility and correspondence targets are computed specifically for each dataset type, accounting for static scenes, optical flow labels, and rigid posed objects.
- **Simultaneous Training:** Both optical flow and wide-baseline data are trained simultaneously within a single gradient update, fostering cross-domain generalization.

#### 3.4. TA-WB Training & Benchmarking Dataset

To address the lack of challenging wide-baseline benchmarks, UFM introduces the **TartanAir-Wide Baseline (TA-WB)** dataset. This benchmark is curated from TartanAirV2 environments using a specialized geometric sampler that controls viewpoint differences (0-120 degrees) and filters for matchability (rejecting occluded, textureless, or unsolvable pairs via Superpoint + Lightglue matching).

#### 3.5. Training Details

- **Multi-resolution Training:** The model is initially trained at a resolution of 560 (UFM 560) for 48 epochs. For high-resolution downstream tasks, a model (UFM 980) is bootstrapped from UFM 560 by training on optical flow data for 15 additional epochs at 980 resolution, with a lower learning rate and full-pixel supervision (standard for OF).
- **Encoder Finetuning:** Unlike some prior works (e.g., RoMa) that freeze the DINOv2 encoder, UFM finetunes it with a significantly lower learning rate (5e-6) to preserve pre-training while allowing adaptation to correspondence tasks.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with a cosine decay learning rate schedule, 10% linear warmup, and weight decay is used. Batches are symmetrized for bidirectional correspondence, yielding an effective batch size of 96 pairs. Training UFM 560 takes 4 days on 8 H100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence supporting UFM's efficacy and its capacity to unify dense correspondence tasks.

- **Mutual Improvement from Unified Training:** An ablation study (Table 5) definitively demonstrates that training UFM on a merged dataset of optical flow (OF) and wide-baseline (WB) data leads to mutual benefits. Adding wide-baseline data resulted in a 20-80% decrease in EPE for optical flow tasks (especially on KITTI), while incorporating optical flow data led to a 3.2% relative decrease in 1-2 pixel outlier rates for wide-baseline tasks. This is a crucial finding, as it validates the core hypothesis of the paper and distinguishes UFM from previous unified attempts.

- **State-of-the-Art Wide-Baseline Correspondence:**
 - **Accuracy:** On zero-shot wide-baseline evaluations (ETH3D, DTU, TA-WB) at all covisible pixels (Table 2), UFM significantly outperforms all other dense methods. It achieves, on average, 62% less EPE and 6.7x better runtime compared to the best dense baseline, RoMa.
 - **Speed & Precision:** UFM achieves precision comparable to MASt3R (a robust sparse matcher) on its *confident* pixels, despite MASt3R's advantage in selective evaluation. Crucially, UFM is 60x faster than MASt3R (or 24x faster considering accelerated versions like Speedy MASt3R), highlighting its efficiency for practical applications.
 - **Refinement Gains:** The UFM-Refine variant further improves accuracy, showing that the base UFM model can be effectively combined with refinement techniques.
 - **Pose Estimation:** UFM 560 (with refinement) achieves the best accuracy on ETH3D and TA-WB benchmarks for relative pose estimation and ranks second on ScanNet-1500 (zero-shot), confirming its suitability for 3D geometric tasks (Table 3).

- **State-of-the-Art Zero-Shot Optical Flow:**
 - **Performance:** UFM 980 achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on Sintel-Final and KITTI (Table 4) in terms of EPE and most pixel outlier metrics, while remaining competitive on Sintel-Clean. This underscores its strong generalization capabilities even to dynamic scenes.
 - **Comparison to Specialized Methods:** UFM demonstrates competitive or superior performance to highly specialized optical flow methods like SEA-RAFT, FlowFormer, and UniMatch, even though UFM is designed for a broader range of correspondence tasks.

- **Generalizable Matching on Out-of-Distribution Data:** UFM shows strong generalization and robust matching performance on images from the Ego-Exo4D dataset (Fig. 4), which features out-of-distribution environments, camera models, and challenging viewpoint shifts.

- **Architectural Scalability and Efficiency:**
 - **Generic Transformer Benefits:** Ablation studies (Fig. 5) reveal that UFM's simple, generic transformer architecture scales more effectively with large amounts of unified data compared to specialized architectures like SEA-RAFT, UniMatch, or RoMa. UFM consistently performs best on various validation sets at both data-bound and compute-bound scenarios.
 - **Direct Regression Superiority:** A specific ablation (Table S.2) shows that UFM's direct regression-based flow head is more accurate (22% lower EPE on average) than a patch-wise matching head inspired by UniMatch, even with the same backbone and training data. This supports the architectural choice of direct regression over coarse patch-level matching.

- **Importance of Encoder Finetuning:** Experiments (Fig. S.5, S.7, S.8) reveal that freezing the DINOv2 encoder, as done in some prior works, is suboptimal for UFM. Finetuning the encoder, even with low learning rates, significantly improves performance, especially when using features from intermediate layers, which often encode stronger correlating features than the last layer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UFM represents a significant advancement in the field of dense image correspondence, offering a paradigm shift that could have far-reaching implications:

1. **Unified Foundation Model for Correspondence:** By conclusively demonstrating that unified training can lead to mutual benefits and outperform specialized methods, UFM challenges the long-standing dichotomy between optical flow and wide-baseline matching. This paves the way for the development of general-purpose "foundation models" for dense correspondence, analogous to those in natural language processing.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The ability of UFM to handle diverse correspondence tasks—from subtle temporal motions to extreme viewpoint changes—with high accuracy and speed makes it exceptionally robust. This improved generalization is critical for real-world applications in uncontrolled environments where diverse motion patterns and scene conditions are common.
3. **Efficiency for Practical Applications:** UFM's high inference speed (e.g., 6.7x faster than RoMa, 60x faster than MASt3R for comparable precision) makes it suitable for deployment in compute-limited scenarios and real-time systems, such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, and augmented reality devices. This addresses a major bottleneck in integrating dense correspondence into practical systems.
4. **Advancements in Downstream Tasks:** A robust and general-purpose dense correspondence model like UFM can significantly improve performance in various downstream computer vision and robotics tasks, including:
 - **Visual Odometry and SLAM:** More accurate and robust motion estimation in dynamic or challenging environments.
 - **3D Reconstruction:** Higher quality and denser point clouds, especially from diverse image captures.
 - **Object Tracking and Re-identification:** Improved association of objects across frames or different viewpoints.
 - **Image Warping and Synthesis:** More precise manipulation and generation of images.
5. **New Research Directions:** UFM opens up exciting avenues for future research:
 - **Multi-modal Correspondence:** Extending the unified framework to other sensor modalities (e.g., depth, IR, event cameras).
 - **Long-range Correspondence:** Addressing even greater temporal or spatial disparities.
 - **Real-time Applications:** Further optimizing efficiency for extremely latency-sensitive tasks.
 - **Semantic Integration:** As acknowledged in limitations, integrating better semantic matching capabilities could further enhance robustness in extreme illumination or seasonal changes.
6. **New Benchmark for Challenging Scenarios:** The introduction of the TartanAir-Wide Baseline (TA-WB) benchmark will serve as a valuable resource for the research community, facilitating the development and rigorous evaluation of future dense wide-baseline correspondence methods.

While UFM demonstrates remarkable capabilities, the authors acknowledge limitations in handling extreme semantic changes (e.g., seasonal changes, visual-to-infrared matching) where methods like RoMa, benefiting from frozen DINOv2 features, might show more robustness. This indicates a future research direction: balancing the adaptability of finetuned encoders with the strong semantic priors of frozen foundation models, potentially through semantic matching data or preservation losses. Additionally, the current bias towards static objects in the training set suggests that incorporating more sceneflow datasets could improve performance on highly dynamic foreground objects. These identified limitations provide a clear roadmap for further enhancements, pointing towards an even more comprehensive and universally applicable correspondence system.
