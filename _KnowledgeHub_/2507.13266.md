# QuestA: Expanding Reasoning Capacity in LLMs via Question Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> The QUESTA framework from Tsinghua University and collaborators enhances large language models' reasoning capacity on complex math problems by augmenting training prompts with partial solution steps. This method significantly improved a 1.5B-parameter model's accuracy on benchmarks like AIME24 (+10.73%) and AIME25 (+12.79%), achieving performance comparable to models over 20 times its size while maintaining solution diversity and training stability.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) struggles to genuinely expand LLM reasoning capacity for highly difficult problems, often exploiting existing knowledge rather than acquiring new skills.
- RL training on challenging problems is inefficient due to sparse reward signals, leading to slow learning or stalled progress (null gradients) when successful trajectories are rare.
- Training on 'easy' prompts can degrade an LLM's intrinsic reasoning ability by promoting overfitting to shallow patterns, causing entropy collapse, and reducing the diversity of generated solutions (pass@k).

## Method

- QUESTA augments training prompts by prepending ==partial solution steps== (e.g., 25% or 50% of the solution) to the original question, effectively scaffolding the learning process for complex problems.
- It implements a two-stage filtering pipeline to specifically target high-difficulty problems where the base model fails, ensuring RL training focuses on the most impactful instances.
- An iterative two-stage curriculum, starting with 50% partial solutions and then reducing to 25%, gradually diminishes reliance on hints to promote the acquisition of intrinsic reasoning skills and improve generalization.

## Results

- QUESTA-Nemotron-**1.5B** achieved state-of-the-art results for its parameter size, boosting accuracy by +**10.73%** on AIME24 (**72.50%**) and +**12.79%** on AIME25 (**62.29%**), outperforming models over 20 times larger.
- The method maintained or modestly improved pass@k performance and preserved entropy stability during RL training, indicating enhanced solution diversity and robust exploration without overfitting.
- Performance improvements generalized to unhinted problems during evaluation, demonstrating that the model acquired expanded intrinsic reasoning capacity to solve a significantly larger fraction of previously inaccessible problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training LLMs on 'hard' problems is crucial for expanding intrinsic reasoning capabilities, whereas 'easy' problems can paradoxically lead to a degradation of reasoning capacity and solution diversity.
> - Augmenting questions with partial solutions provides denser and more informative reward signals for RL, making complex problems tractable and accelerating learning without causing entropy collapse or reducing pass@k.
> - A curriculum-based approach, dynamically adjusting the level of partial solution hints, is vital for sustained performance gains and effective generalization to unhinted evaluation scenarios.
