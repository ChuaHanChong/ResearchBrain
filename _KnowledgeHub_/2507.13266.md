# QuestA: Expanding Reasoning Capacity in LLMs via Question Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> The QUESTA framework from Tsinghua University and collaborators enhances large language models' reasoning capacity on complex math problems by augmenting training prompts with partial solution steps. This method significantly improved a 1.5B-parameter model's accuracy on benchmarks like AIME24 (+10.73%) and AIME25 (+12.79%), achieving performance comparable to models over 20 times its size while maintaining solution diversity and training stability.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) struggles to genuinely expand LLM reasoning capacity for highly difficult problems, often exploiting existing knowledge rather than acquiring new skills.
- RL training on challenging problems is inefficient due to sparse reward signals, leading to slow learning or stalled progress (null gradients) when successful trajectories are rare.
- Training on 'easy' prompts can degrade an LLM's intrinsic reasoning ability by promoting overfitting to shallow patterns, causing entropy collapse, and reducing the diversity of generated solutions (pass@k).

## Method

- QUESTA augments training prompts by prepending ==partial solution steps== (e.g., 25% or 50% of the solution) to the original question, effectively scaffolding the learning process for complex problems.
- It implements a two-stage filtering pipeline to specifically target high-difficulty problems where the base model fails, ensuring RL training focuses on the most impactful instances.
- An iterative two-stage curriculum, starting with 50% partial solutions and then reducing to 25%, gradually diminishes reliance on hints to promote the acquisition of intrinsic reasoning skills and improve generalization.

## Results

- QUESTA-Nemotron-**1.5B** achieved state-of-the-art results for its parameter size, boosting accuracy by +**10.73%** on AIME24 (**72.50%**) and +**12.79%** on AIME25 (**62.29%**), outperforming models over 20 times larger.
- The method maintained or modestly improved pass@k performance and preserved entropy stability during RL training, indicating enhanced solution diversity and robust exploration without overfitting.
- Performance improvements generalized to unhinted problems during evaluation, demonstrating that the model acquired expanded intrinsic reasoning capacity to solve a significantly larger fraction of previously inaccessible problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training LLMs on 'hard' problems is crucial for expanding intrinsic reasoning capabilities, whereas 'easy' problems can paradoxically lead to a degradation of reasoning capacity and solution diversity.
> - Augmenting questions with partial solutions provides denser and more informative reward signals for RL, making complex problems tractable and accelerating learning without causing entropy collapse or reducing pass@k.
> - A curriculum-based approach, dynamically adjusting the level of partial solution hints, is vital for sustained performance gains and effective generalization to unhinted evaluation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in complex reasoning tasks, including mathematics, programming, and formal logic. Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), has emerged as a prominent paradigm for enhancing these reasoning abilities by aligning model outputs with objective correctness through verifiable signals. This approach aims to address a limitation of traditional RL.

However, a fundamental debate exists within the research community regarding RLVR's true impact: whether it genuinely expands a model's intrinsic reasoning capacity or primarily exploits knowledge already present in the base model. Recent studies (Yue et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025) indicate that while state-of-the-art RL methods like GRPO and DAPO can improve pass@1 metrics by reinforcing successful completions, they exhibit limitations when applied to highly difficult tasks where the base model's performance is poor. This phenomenon contrasts with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), where varying problem difficulty can improve downstream task performance (Luo et al., 2025a). In the RLVR framework, training on "easy" prompts has been observed to potentially undermine inherent reasoning capabilities. Proposed explanations for this include model overfitting on correct solutions and subsequent entropy collapse, which restricts the model’s exploratory capacity (Cui et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a).

The present work, QUESTA, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenge of expanding LLM reasoning capacity for hard problems using RL, while mitigating the inefficiencies associated with training on such tasks. It identifies a tension: easy prompts can dilute reasoning capacity, but hard prompts lead to slow learning due to sparse reward signals. QUESTA seeks to overcome this by introducing a data-centric augmentation strategy that makes hard problems more tractable, aiming to achieve the benefits of training on difficult problems without the corresponding learning slowdowns or pass@k degradation observed in some prior RL applications. This approach is distinct from methods that modify reward functions or optimization algorithms, focusing instead on input-level transformations to provide richer learning signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core challenge motivating this research is to adapt RL strategies to more effectively solve complex reasoning problems, particularly those where standard RL methods show limitations in expanding intrinsic reasoning capacity beyond the base model. The authors identify two key observations that underpin their objectives:

1. **Impact of Dataset Difficulty on Reasoning Capacity:** Through controlled experiments, the authors observed that training with "easy" prompts (problems the base model can already solve) leads to a degradation in pass@k accuracy, suggesting an overfitting to shallow patterns and a reduction in the model's ability to explore diverse solution paths. This indicates that easy prompts may actually harm reasoning capacity. Conversely, training on "hard" prompts (problems where the base model performs poorly) is more beneficial for expanding reasoning ability, as it compels the model to explore and acquire novel solution strategies.
2. **Inefficiency of RL Training on Hard Problems:** While training on hard problems is crucial for capacity expansion, it is significantly slower and less sample-efficient for RL algorithms. This inefficiency stems from sparse reward signals, as successful trajectories for complex problems are rare, providing limited gradient information for policy improvement. The authors formalize this bottleneck by noting that if the model's capacity set (likely generated trajectories) does not intersect with the solution set, the RL process is unlikely to make progress due to a "null gradient from zero-reinforcement."

Given these insights, the key objectives of this work are:
- To underscore the critical importance of training on hard problems within the RLVR framework to truly expand an LLM's reasoning capabilities.
- To develop an efficient and parsimonious strategy that dynamically adjusts problem difficulty during RL training, thereby making high-difficulty tasks more tractable.
- To accelerate RL training on challenging problems by providing denser, more informative learning signals.
- To achieve these goals without compromising the model’s ability to generate diverse and high-quality solutions (i.e., maintaining or improving pass@k) and without causing entropy collapse.
- To provide theoretical justification for the observed empirical improvements in RL efficiency due to partial-solution augmentation.

The proposed method, QUESTA (Question Augmentation), is designed to meet these objectives by injecting partial solutions into training prompts, effectively "scaffolding" the learning process for hard problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QUESTA operates as a modular, data-centric framework designed to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of reinforcement learning for reasoning tasks by augmenting training prompts with partial solution sketches.

**Question Augmentation Mechanism:**
For a given problem `x` with a multi-step solution `y = (y1..., yn)`, QUESTA creates augmented prompts `x̃(p)` by prepending the first `p` steps of the solution as a prefix to the original question. The parameter `p` (e.g., 25%, 50%) quantifies the proportion of the solution revealed, offering a precise control over the problem's perceived difficulty during training.
The OpenR1-Math-220K dataset, which includes detailed chain-of-thought (CoT) sections and final solution blocks, served as the primary data source. For augmentation, only the final solution block was used to extract hints, avoiding speculative reasoning from the CoT. This extracted solution was then truncated at `p%` and prepended to the original question.

**Targeting High-Difficulty Problems:**
QUESTA focuses its augmentation efforts on problems where the base model typically fails. This is achieved through a two-stage filtering pipeline:
1. **Initial Filtering:** The full 220K problems from OpenR1-Math-220K were first reduced to 26K "hardest" candidates. This selection was based on the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B model's success rate, retaining problems where the model's pass rate was close to zero.
2. **Second Difficulty Screening (after augmentation):** Using the Nemotron-1.5B model, eight generations were sampled for each *augmented* prompt. Only instances with consistently low pass rates (0–4 correct predictions out of 8) were retained for RL training. This criterion targets "high-variance" cases, hypothesized to provide stronger learning signals, resulting in a final pool of no more than 10K problems.

**Integration with RL Pipelines:**
QUESTA is designed to be orthogonal to existing RL algorithms, such as GRPO and DAPO. Its integration involves replacing the original rollout dataset with the augmented dataset while preserving the original reward function and policy update mechanisms. The authors utilized AReaL as their RL training framework and the GRPO algorithm, specifically without the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence loss. A dynamic filtering mechanism was applied to discard prompts with either all correct or all incorrect rollouts during training, similar to DAPO.

**Iterative Curriculum RL Paradigm:**
To further optimize learning, QUESTA employs a two-stage curriculum:
1. **Stage 1 (Partial-50):** The dataset is augmented with `p = 50%` partial solutions. Difficulty filtering is applied, and RL training is conducted until performance saturation.
2. **Stage 2 (Partial-25):** The augmentation is reduced to `p = 25%` (fewer hints). Difficulty filtering is reapplied, and RL training continues until convergence. This curriculum aims to gradually reduce reliance on hints, aligning the learned policy more closely with the unhinted evaluation distribution.

**RL Training and Evaluation Setup:**
- **Training:** `n = 16` responses were sampled per prompt, with a maximum prompt length of 8192 tokens and generation length of 24000 tokens. A sampling temperature of 1.0 and clipping hyperparameters (ε_low = ε_high = 0.2) were used. The batch size was 128 (mini-batch size of 1), and optimization was performed with AdamW at a constant learning rate of 2 × 10⁻⁵. Eight NVIDIA H800 (80GB) nodes were utilized.
- **Reward Function:** An Outcome Reward Model, mirroring DeepScaleR, was used. It returned 1 if both the answer (passing LaTeX/Sympy checks) and format (e.g., `<think>` tags) were correct, and 0 otherwise.
- **Evaluation:** Models were evaluated on various math benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, HMMT FEB 25, Olympiad Bench, BRUMO25) by generating 32 samples per problem and reporting pass@1, using a sampling temperature of 0.7 and a top-p value of 0.95. A crucial aspect is that **no partial solutions were provided during evaluation**, testing the model's intrinsic reasoning capacity post-training. Pass@k estimation used the unbiased method from Chen et al. (2021).

**Theoretical Analysis:**
The paper provides a theoretical framework for why question augmentation improves RL efficiency. It introduces "Solution Set" (`S(q)`) and "Model Capacity Set" (`C(q, δp)`). Based on the assumption that RL algorithms do not update model weights without positive rewards (Assumption 4.3), Theorem 4.4 demonstrates that if the model's capacity set does not intersect with the solution set for any training question, the RL process will likely stall.
Definition 4.5 formalizes question augmentation by introducing a hint `h_q` that can be generated with non-negligible probability and guides the model towards a solution `s_q`. Theorem 4.6 (informal) suggests that such hints significantly reduce the required sampling budget, accelerating learnability. A formal proof in the appendix (Theorem C.4) for a simplified tabular RL setup corroborates that with a sampling budget proportional to `1/δ'p`, a high training success rate can be achieved.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The implementation of QUESTA yielded several key findings regarding LLM reasoning capacity and RL training efficiency:

**Enhanced Performance on Math Benchmarks:**
- QUESTA-Nemotron-1.5B achieved substantial improvements on challenging math benchmarks. For a 1.5B-parameter model, it demonstrated:
 - AIME24: 72.50% accuracy, a +10.73% increase over the baseline Nemotron-1.5B.
 - AIME25: 62.29% accuracy, a +12.79% increase.
 - HMMT25: 41.67% accuracy, a +10.11% increase.
- These improvements were consistent across all evaluated benchmarks, indicating enhanced problem-solving robustness.

**State-of-the-Art for 1.5B Models:**
- QUESTA-Nemotron-1.5B consistently outperformed other models of similar scale (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B and Qwen3-1.7B).
- Notably, it matched or exceeded the performance of the significantly larger DeepSeek-R1-Distill-32B model (over 20 times its parameter count) across several benchmarks, including a substantial +11% lead on AIME25. It also remained competitive with Qwen3-8B.

**Improved Training Dynamics and Entropy Stability:**
- Analysis of training dynamics (Figure 5) showed that average response length and average reward steadily increased during RL training with QUESTA.
- Crucially, the average entropy during RL training remained stable or increased, indicating that QUESTA did not suffer from entropy collapse, a common issue in RL that limits exploration. Instead, it appeared to encourage diverse and exploratory behavior.

**Preservation and Improvement of Pass@k:**
- Unlike observations in some prior RL-based training methods that reported a reduction in pass@k at larger `k` values, QUESTA preserved and, in many cases, modestly improved performance across a broad range of `k` (Figures 2, 7, 8). This suggests that QUESTA enhances both the quality and diversity of candidate solutions rather than merely overfitting to a single optimal trajectory.

**Generalization to Unhinted Problems at Test Time:**
- A key result was that the performance improvements persisted even when partial solutions were removed during evaluation (Figure 6). The pass rate distribution on the 26K training set shifted significantly towards higher success rates post-RL, indicating that the model learned to solve a larger fraction of problems independently.
- Specifically, the number of unsolved problems at Pass@32 dropped from 5 to 2 on AIME24 and from 6 to 3 on AIME25 (Table 2), demonstrating expanded coverage on hard problems that were initially inaccessible.

**Effectiveness of Curriculum Learning:**
- Ablation studies confirmed the benefit of the two-stage curriculum (Partial-50 followed by Partial-25). Training with this curriculum substantially outperformed training solely with Partial-50 data for the same budget (Table 3). The transition point was selected to prevent overconfidence and sustain training stability.

**Impact of Hints:**
- Comparing training with and without hints (QUESTA-Nemotron-1.5B w/ hint vs. w/o hint), the presence of hints yielded an additional +2.82% average gain across benchmarks and achieved comparable performance in nearly half the training steps (Table 6, Figure 7). This highlights the direct contribution of partial-solution guidance to learning efficiency and effectiveness.

**Generalizability Across Model Architectures and Domains:**
- Applying QUESTA to DeepScaleR-1.5B also resulted in consistent improvements across math benchmarks (average +6.50%), indicating that the benefits of QUESTA are not specific to a single model architecture (Table 7, Figure 8).
- Minor cross-domain generalization was observed on general knowledge, logic, and coding tasks (average +0.78%), suggesting that improved reasoning patterns might transfer modestly beyond the specific training domain (Table 8).

**Theoretical Support:**
- The theoretical analysis provided a framework for understanding how question augmentation increases the likelihood of sampling informative trajectories, thereby yielding denser reward signals and improving sample efficiency, which empirically correlates with the observed faster learning and higher accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The QUESTA framework introduces a method that carries several significant implications for the development and application of large language models, particularly in complex reasoning domains.

**Enhanced Reasoning Capacity and Efficiency:** QUESTA demonstrates that a data-centric approach, specifically question augmentation with partial solutions, can substantially expand the intrinsic reasoning capacity of LLMs. It directly addresses the challenge of making "hard" problems tractable for RL, which traditionally struggles with sparse rewards in such scenarios. This breakthrough is particularly impactful for problems requiring multi-step reasoning, as it provides a mechanism to scaffold learning effectively.

**Cost-Effective High Performance:** The achievement of new state-of-the-art results on challenging math benchmarks using 1.5B-parameter models is highly significant. This suggests that high-performance reasoning capabilities can be attained with considerably smaller models, potentially reducing the massive computational and financial resources typically required for training and deploying larger, frontier LLMs. This could democratize access to advanced reasoning AI and foster innovation in resource-constrained environments.

**Addressing RL Limitations:** QUESTA offers a practical solution to several known limitations of RL in language model training, such as the degradation of pass@k (a measure of solution diversity and quality) and entropy collapse. By providing a curriculum that gradually reduces hints and by yielding denser reward signals, the method encourages sustained exploration and prevents overfitting to narrow solution paths, leading to more robust and diverse problem-solving abilities.

**Orthogonal and Complementary Approach:** The framework's orthogonality to existing RL algorithms and reward designs means it can be seamlessly integrated into current training pipelines. This "plug-and-play" nature makes QUESTA a versatile tool that can complement ongoing advancements in RL algorithms and model architectures, allowing for synergistic improvements in LLM performance.

**Broader Generalizability:** The theoretical underpinnings, supported by empirical evidence across different model backbones, suggest that the principle of question augmentation—decomposing complex problems into more approachable pieces—is broadly applicable. The authors explicitly state its potential generalization to other domains such as competitive coding, software engineering, or other agentic tasks. This opens new avenues for research into designing appropriate augmentation pipelines for diverse complex domains.

**Advancing Curriculum Learning in RL:** The empirical success of the two-stage curriculum design (progressing from 50% to 25% partial hints) underscores the importance of structured learning paths in RL for complex tasks. This work contributes to the growing understanding of effective curriculum strategies for LLMs, moving beyond static datasets to dynamic adaptation of problem difficulty.

In summary, QUESTA represents a notable advancement in making LLMs more capable and efficient at complex reasoning. Its data-centric, curriculum-driven approach to leveraging partial solutions provides a powerful and adaptable method for expanding reasoning capacity, with implications for both the theoretical understanding of LLM learning and practical applications across various technical domains.
