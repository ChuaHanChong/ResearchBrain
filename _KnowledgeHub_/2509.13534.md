# Embracing Bulky Objects with Humanoid Robots: Whole-Body Manipulation with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology developed a reinforcement learning framework enabling humanoid robots to perform whole-body embracing of bulky objects. This approach integrates human motion priors with Neural Signed Distance Fields (NSDF) for robust, multi-contact manipulation, achieving adaptability to diverse object properties in simulation and successful deployment on a physical Unitree H1-2 robot.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation relies on end-effectors, which are insufficient for handling bulky, heavy, or irregularly shaped objects.
- Existing whole-body manipulation (WBM) methods struggle with the high dimensionality and complexity of multi-contact scenarios, requiring precise contact point specification and robust behavior generation.
- Long-horizon WBM tasks, such as embracing and transporting large objects, demand sustained coordination, precise perception, and stable contact, which are challenging for high-degree-of-freedom humanoids.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning framework that combines a pre-trained human motion prior, distilled into a VAE, with a ==Neural Signed Distance Field (NSDF)== representation for continuous geometric perception.
- A hierarchical control architecture uses a PPO-trained task policy that leverages the low-dimensional action space of the motion prior and integrates NSDF features for enhanced contact awareness.
- The training incorporates a comprehensive reward function and a multi-stage randomized environment initialization strategy to guide learning for complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- Policies trained with the NSDF module successfully established multi-point contact and transported objects, whereas policies without NSDF failed, confirming its critical role in robust manipulation.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization in simulation, achieving **100%** success for various shapes and high success rates (80-**93%**) for objects up to 7 kg, despite being trained on a single object type.
- The trained policy successfully transferred to a physical Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot, performing real-time whole-body embracing and transportation of a bulky cylinder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating human motion priors with task-specific geometric perception via NSDF is crucial for achieving anthropomorphic, stable, and robust whole-body manipulation of bulky objects.
> - The NSDF module provides precise, continuous geometric awareness, which is indispensable for establishing and maintaining stable multi-point contacts, significantly improving manipulation stability.
> - Utilizing a multi-stage randomized initialization strategy during training significantly enhances the convergence speed and stability of reinforcement learning for complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of objects is a fundamental capability for robots, but traditionally, this has focused on smaller objects handled by end-effectors (e.g., grippers). As humanoid robots become more sophisticated and are deployed in diverse environments (industrial, home services), they increasingly need to handle bulky, heavy, or irregularly shaped objects that cannot be managed by end-effectors alone. This demand has spurred research into **Whole-Body Manipulation (WBM)**, where robots leverage their entire body, including arms, torso, and even environmental contacts, to embrace and stabilize objects.

Existing WBM approaches face several critical challenges. Model-based methods, struggle with the high dimensionality and complexity of multi-contact scenarios, requiring precise contact point specification and expensive computation. Learning-based methods, while promising, often rely on meticulously engineered rewards and still contend with difficulties in generating robust, coherent, and stable behaviors in contact-rich, dynamically unstable situations. Furthermore, many current methods either simplify the robot model, leading to perception inaccuracies, or are limited to upper-body platforms. The integration of whole-body tactile sensing, is an active area, but often requires specialized hardware.

A significant challenge, highlighted by the authors, is the **long-horizon nature** of tasks like embracing and transporting bulky objects, which involves a sequence of coordinated actions (approaching, embracing, transporting) as depicted in Fig. 1. This demands sustained coordination, precise perception, and stable contact, which are hard to acquire due to high degrees of freedom and difficulty in constructing stable multi-contacts.

This paper positions itself directly within this landscape by addressing these limitations. It seeks to overcome the reliance on end-effector-only manipulation, enhance payload capacity, and ensure stability through distributed multi-contact interactions. Critically, the authors claim their work is **"the first RL framework that enables the humanoid robots to actively engage with bulky objects through whole-body manipulation,"** suggesting a novel comprehensive solution for this specific task.

The work leverages recent advancements in **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for humanoid control**, particularly the trend of using **behavior cloning (BC) from physics-based animation**, and **human motion priors**. These priors enable robots to imitate natural, human-like movements, which are kinematically natural and physically feasible, facilitating policy training convergence. However, these priors often lack crucial environmental interaction information. This paper integrates a **Neural Signed Distance Field (NSDF)** to provide accurate and continuous geometric perception, thereby addressing the lack of environmental awareness in motion priors and enhancing contact-rich interaction. By combining human motion priors with geometric perception, the research aims to achieve a more robust, adaptive, and anthropomorphic WBM solution than previously available.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and practical reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to perform **whole-body embracing tasks for bulky objects**. This involves not just grasping, but securely enveloping and transporting objects using coordinated movements of the arms and torso.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the inherent limitations of traditional robotic manipulation and the growing demands for versatile humanoid robots:
- **Overcoming End-Effector Limitations:** Traditional grasping mechanisms, reliant on end-effectors, are insufficient for bulky or heavy objects due to inherent stability and payload constraints. Humanoids, with their full bodies, offer a natural alternative.
- **Enhancing Stability and Load Capacity:** By distributing contact forces across multiple points on the robot's body (arms and torso), WBM can significantly enhance the stability of manipulation and increase the load capacity, allowing humanoids to handle objects that would otherwise be impossible.
- **Achieving Anthropomorphic and Natural Behaviors:** Humans naturally embrace large objects. The goal is to imbue humanoid robots with similarly natural and efficient whole-body motion patterns, which not only improves task performance but also makes robots more intuitive and acceptable in human environments.
- **Addressing Long-Horizon, Contact-Rich Tasks:** WBM tasks involving bulky objects are inherently long-horizon (requiring sequential stages like approaching, embracing, and transporting) and contact-intensive. These scenarios pose significant challenges for existing control and perception systems.
- **Improving Perception for Interaction:** Accurate modeling of spatial relationships and contact awareness is crucial for robust multi-contact manipulation. Current simplified models often lead to errors. Integrating continuous geometric perception can significantly improve performance.
- **Bridging Sim-to-Real Gap:** For practical deployment, policies trained in simulation must transfer effectively to real-world robots. The framework aims for successful sim-to-real transfer, a crucial step for real-world application.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to equip humanoid robots with a versatile and robust capability for handling bulky objects, a common requirement in both industrial and service applications, by drawing inspiration from human movement and leveraging advanced learning and perception techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is an **Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework** that integrates a pre-trained human motion prior with a Neural Signed Distance Field (NSDF) representation. It employs a hierarchical control architecture to coordinate arms and torso for robust whole-body embracing. The overall system architecture consists of three main stages: Data Processing, Motion Prior Distillation, and Embracing Policy Training.

**A. Data Processing:**
The process begins with converting human motion capture (mocap) data into kinematically feasible robot-specific trajectories.
- **Human Motion Data:** Utilizes the large-scale AMASS mocap dataset, which represents human motion using the SMPL model.
- **Filtering and Retargeting:** The MaskedMimic framework filters this raw data to remove kinematically invalid sequences (e.g., self-collisions, foot slips), creating a cleaned human motion dataset (D_h). This data is then retargeted to the target humanoid robot's morphology using the H2O framework, producing a robot motion dataset (D_r) of validated, physically plausible trajectories for the specific robot.

**B. Motion Prior Distillation:**
This module aims to distill human-like motion patterns into a compact latent representation, enabling efficient and anthropomorphic control.
- **Teacher-Student Learning Scheme:** A common approach, is adopted.
 - **Teacher Policy Training:** A teacher policy (π_teacher) is trained using the PHC framework on the retargeted robot motion dataset (D_r). This policy learns to precisely track reference motion trajectories, mapping the robot's proprioceptive state (velocities, joint positions, previous action) and a goal state (positional offsets and target configurations) to actions.
 - **Humanoid Motion Prior Distillation (Student VAE):** A Variational Autoencoder (VAE)-based student policy is then trained to distill the teacher's complex action space into a compact latent space.
 - An **Encoder (E)** infers a distribution over a latent motion variable (z_t) from the robot's proprioceptive and goal states.
 - A **Decoder (D)** reconstructs the robot's action from this latent code and a simplified robot state (excluding root linear velocity for sim-to-real feasibility).
 - A **learnable Prior Network (R)** approximates the latent code distribution, ensuring the learned prior aligns with the encoder's output.
- **Training Objective:** The VAE is trained using a loss function comprising:
 - **Action Reconstruction Loss (L_action):** Minimizes the difference between the student's reconstructed action and the teacher's action.
 - **Regularization Term (L_regu):** Penalizes abrupt changes in the latent trajectory (temporal consistency).
 - **KL Divergence Term (L_KL):** Aligns the encoder's latent distribution with the learned prior, ensuring the latent space effectively captures meaningful motion patterns.
- Once trained, the decoder (D) and prior (R) parameters are frozen, defining a new low-dimensional action space for subsequent task learning.

**C. Embracing Policy Training:**
A task policy (π_task) is trained using a tailored **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** algorithm to achieve the whole-body embracing task.

- **Observations and Actions:**
 - **Task Observation (s_task_t):** Combines the decoder input (s_d_t, proprioceptive state without root linear velocity) with task-specific features (s_s_t).
 - **Task-Specific Features (s_s_t):** Includes distance and direction angles between the robot torso and the object center, and between the robot torso and the target location.
 - **Neural Signed Distance Field (NSDF) Features (d_t):** This is a critical component. A pre-trained NSDF network evaluates the shortest distances between a selected target point (the object's geometric center) and a set of mesh segments representing the robot's upper body links (Fig. 3). These distances provide precise, continuous geometric perception of potential contact points, enhancing contact awareness.
 - **Actions:** The task policy (π_task) outputs a latent action (z_s_t) which is then combined with the motion prior (μ_p_t) generated by the learnable prior network (R). This combined latent token is then decoded by the frozen decoder (D) into the final robot actions (a_s_t). This approach leverages the human-like motion patterns while adapting them for the specific task.

- **Reward Design:** A comprehensive reward function guides the robot through the complex, multi-stage task:
 - **Smoothness Reward:** Penalizes high joint torques, accelerations, and rapid action changes (r_torque, r_acc, r_action) to promote natural and energy-efficient movements.
 - **Physical Limitation Reward:** Prevents joint limit violations (r_dof), reduces foot slippage (r_slippage), and limits foot contact force (r_feet) to ensure robot safety and stability.
 - **Task Reward (r_task):** Decomposed into stage-specific rewards for approaching, embracing, and transporting:
 - **Approaching (r_walk):** Encourages movement towards the object when outside a defined pick-up zone.
 - **Carrying (r_carry):** Encourages movement of the object towards the target location when inside the pick-up zone.
 - **Arm Position (r_arm):** Guides the end-effectors to hug the object.
 - **NSDF Reward (r_NSDF):** Implicitly encourages sustained contact by providing detailed contact awareness through NSDF features, aiding in robust embracing.

- **Random Initialization:** To effectively train for long-horizon tasks and improve exploration, the environment is randomly initialized at the start of each episode across three stages:
 1. Robot positioned far from object (focus on approaching).
 2. Robot initialized near object in a pre-hugging posture (focus on embracing).
 3. Robot initialized in a hugging posture with the object in its arms (focus on transporting).
 This ensures balanced learning across all phases of the task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research team thoroughly evaluated their proposed framework through extensive simulations and real-world experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in whole-body embracing tasks.

**A. Validation of Framework Components:**
- **Effectiveness of the NSDF Module:**
 - **With NSDF:** The policy trained with the NSDF module successfully established multi-point contact with the object using various upper-body joints (arms and torso). This significantly enhanced manipulation stability and increased the number of contact points, leading to successful object transportation (Fig. 4a).
 - **Without NSDF:** Without the NSDF observations and reward, the trained policy performed poorly. Only the torso moved close to the object, but the arms failed to embrace it properly, resulting in an inability to complete the transportation task (Fig. 4b). This conclusively demonstrates the critical role of NSDF in enabling robust, multi-contact whole-body manipulation by providing precise geometric and contact awareness.
- **Evaluation of Randomized Initialization:**
 - The proposed multi-stage randomized initialization strategy significantly improved the **convergence speed and stability** of policy training.
 - Compared to a baseline with fixed initial states, the method with randomized initialization (blue curve in Fig. 5) showed smoother reward growth and higher stability throughout training. The baseline (red curve) exhibited slower reward accumulation in early stages and greater instability, underscoring the importance of diverse initial conditions for effective learning in long-horizon tasks.

**B. Adaptability to Different Object Properties (Sim-to-Sim Transfer in MuJoCo):**
The policy's robustness and generalization capabilities were evaluated by testing it on objects with diverse sizes, masses, and geometries in the MuJoCo simulator, even though it was trained only on a fixed-size cylindrical object. Success was defined as the object remaining within 0.1m of the target without falling, over 30 independent trials for each condition (Table III, Fig. 6).
- **Impact of NSDF:** Policies trained *without* the NSDF module exhibited 0% success rates across all tested object shapes (cylinder, cuboid, sphere) and properties, further reinforcing NSDF's indispensable role.
- **High Generalization with NSDF:** When the NSDF module was incorporated, the policy demonstrated remarkable adaptability:
 - **Shape:** Achieved 100% success for various shapes including cylinders, cuboids, and spheres of standard mass (3 kg) and size.
 - **Mass:** Maintained high success rates even with significantly heavier objects (7 kg), achieving 93% for cylinders, 87% for spheres, and 80% for cuboids. The slight reduction for cuboids was attributed to their flat surface morphology affecting stable contact.
 - **Size:** Successfully handled larger objects (e.g., Φ 50 cm cylinder) and smaller ones (e.g., Φ 30 cm sphere) without performance degradation.
This comprehensive testing demonstrated strong sim-to-sim transfer and robustness to unseen object properties, a crucial aspect for practical deployment.

**C. Real-World Experiment (Sim-to-Real Transfer on Unitree H1-2):**
The trained policy was successfully deployed on a **Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot**, operating in real-time at 50 Hz.
- **Task:** The robot was tasked with embracing and transporting a bulky cylinder (Φ 40 cm × 60 cm), demonstrating its ability to handle realistic-sized objects.
- **Performance:** The robot successfully approached the object, entered the pick-up zone, established a stable whole-body embrace, and proceeded to transport the object, showcasing noteworthy manipulation capabilities (Fig. 7). This real-world validation confirms the successful sim-to-real transfer of the policy and the practical effectiveness of the proposed framework for complex, contact-rich WBM tasks on a physical humanoid platform.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of humanoid robotics and whole-body manipulation, with considerable potential impact:

- **Pioneering Whole-Body Embracing with RL:** The paper introduces what the authors claim to be the first reinforcement learning framework enabling humanoid robots to actively engage with bulky objects through *whole-body manipulation*, specifically focusing on embracing. This addresses a critical gap in robotic capabilities, moving beyond traditional end-effector-centric manipulation.
- **Enhanced Stability and Load Capacity:** By effectively coordinating the arms and torso to distribute contact forces across multiple points, the framework significantly enhances the robot's operational stability and load capacity. This is crucial for practical applications where robots need to handle heavy or unwieldy items.
- **Integration of Human Motion Priors for Anthropomorphic Skills:** The distillation of human motion priors into the policy training pipeline is a key innovation. This not only accelerates convergence in complex, long-horizon tasks but also imbues the humanoid robot with kinematically natural and anthropomorphic movement patterns, making its actions more intuitive and safer in human-centric environments.
- **Robust Contact Awareness via NSDF:** The novel integration of Neural Signed Distance Fields (NSDF) provides highly accurate and continuous geometric perception of robot-object interactions. This advanced contact awareness is critical for maintaining stable multi-contact grasps throughout dynamic and long-horizon tasks, a common pitfall in previous WBM approaches.
- **Demonstrated Adaptability and Generalization:** The extensive simulation results highlight the policy's remarkable adaptability to diverse object shapes, sizes, and masses, despite being trained on a single object type. This generalization capability is vital for real-world deployment where robots encounter varied items.
- **Successful Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful deployment and execution of the whole-body embracing task on a physical Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot validate the practical feasibility and robustness of the proposed framework. This bridge between simulation and reality is a major step towards widespread adoption of such advanced manipulation skills.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. It paves the way for humanoid robots to take on a wider array of tasks in logistics, manufacturing, domestic assistance, and disaster response, where handling bulky or irregularly shaped objects is common. By enabling robots to perform such tasks with human-like dexterity and stability, this research brings us closer to a future where humanoids can seamlessly integrate into and augment human capabilities in diverse real-world settings.
