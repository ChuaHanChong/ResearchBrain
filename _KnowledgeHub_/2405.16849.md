# Sync4D: Video Guided Controllable Dynamics for Physics-Based 4D Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for 4D generation, Sync4D, enables the creation of controllable and physically plausible dynamics on 3D Gaussian representations. It achieves this by extracting motion from casually captured reference videos and integrating it with physics-based simulation.

## Problem

- Current diffusion-based 4D generation methods often produce inaccurate motion representations, suffer from shape incoherence, and lack precise control from vague text prompts.
- Existing physics-integrated methods for 4D generation require labor-intensive hand-crafted input motions, significantly limiting their practical applicability and versatility.
- Directly transferring motion from a reference video to a target object for physical simulation can introduce cumulative errors due to inherent shape differences, leading to unrealistic or unstable dynamics.

## Method

- Employs the ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) for robust physics-based simulation, ensuring reliable and genuine dynamics on ==3D Gaussian== representations.
- Extracts high-fidelity shape and motion information from casually captured reference videos using a blend skinning-based non-parametric shape reconstruction method.
- Maps articulated parts from the reference video to the target static 3D Gaussian object through a feature-based shape correspondence, and then applies physics-integrated motion transfer using a differentiable MPM simulator with a learned delta velocity field and displacement loss.

## Results

- Sync4D consistently outperformed baseline methods (DMT+DreamGaussian4D and LLM+DreamGaussian4D) in generating 4D content with superior motion similarity and shape consistency.
- User studies confirmed a strong preference for Sync4D, with high scores (e.g., 82.4-100% for visual quality, 94.1-100% for motion similarity) across various target objects compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the delta velocity field optimization and using a minimal set of articulated parts were crucial for preventing cumulative errors and enhancing dynamic realism in the generated content.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precisely controlled, physically plausible 4D generation can be achieved by synergistically combining video-guided motion extraction with differentiable physics simulation.
> - A learned delta velocity field, optimized via a displacement loss and progressive training, is crucial for transferring reference motion accurately to target shapes while mitigating cumulative simulation errors.
> - Utilizing a minimal, motion-relevant skeletal representation of the reference object during motion extraction enhances the naturalness and quality of the transferred dynamics, avoiding stiffness from redundant parts.
