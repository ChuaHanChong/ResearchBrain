# Embodied AI: From LLMs to World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper outlines a joint Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) and World Model (WM) driven architecture to advance Embodied AI towards Artificial General Intelligence. This approach integrates MLLMs' semantic reasoning with WMs' physics-aware predictive capabilities, overcoming limitations in real-time adaptation and physical grounding to enable more robust and adaptable agents in dynamic environments.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) excel at semantic reasoning and task decomposition but lack grounding in physical laws, leading to impractical plans and poor real-time adaptation.
- World Models (WMs) are proficient at building internal representations and making physics-compliant predictions but struggle with open-ended semantic reasoning and generalizable task decomposition.
- Existing Embodied AI (EAI) research is often fragmented, with unimodal or isolated approaches failing to fully integrate high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical interaction for holistic intelligence.

## Method

- Proposed a joint ==MLLM-WM== driven ==embodied AI architecture== that synergistically combines the strengths of both model types.
- ==MLLMs== are utilized to enrich WMs with ==semantic knowledge==, interpret complex instructions, and guide ==high-level task decomposition== for embodied agents.
- ==WMs== are employed to ground MLLM-generated plans in physical reality, validating ==action plausibility== through ==physics-aware simulations== and enabling ==real-time adaptation== based on environmental feedback.

## Results

- The conceptual joint MLLM-WM architecture is qualitatively shown to offer superior performance across critical metrics including semantic understanding, task decomposition, physics compliance, future prediction, and real-time interaction compared to standalone MLLM or WM systems.
- The framework demonstrates how MLLMs can boost WM reasoning by providing semantic priors and task guidance, while WMs can significantly improve MLLMs by offering physical constraints, spatio-temporal relationships, and real-time interaction experiences.
- The paper provides a comprehensive overview of Embodied AI's history, foundational technologies, and future directions, establishing a clear roadmap for future research towards more robust, intelligent, and adaptable embodied agents.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) in embodied systems necessitates a harmonious integration of symbolic/semantic understanding with robust, physics-grounded interaction.
> - The evolution of Embodied AI from unimodal to multimodal approaches is critical for providing holistic environmental understanding and facilitating comprehensive information transfer across different components.
> - Neither MLLMs nor WMs alone are sufficient for advanced Embodied AI, as MLLMs lack physical grounding and WMs lack open-ended semantic reasoning, requiring their complementary integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper provides a timely and comprehensive survey of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI), positioning it as a cornerstone for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and driving the transition from cyberspace to physical systems. It strategically places recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and World Models (WMs) at the heart of EAI's current and future trajectory, effectively synthesizing their individual contributions and advocating for their synergistic integration.

Historically, the concept of Embodied AI traces back to Alan Turing's Embodied Turing Test in 1950, suggesting that intelligence is inherently linked to physical experience. Early cognitive science and philosophical insights, particularly from the 1980s (Lakoff and Johnson, Harnad), reinforced the idea that cognition arises from sensory-motor interaction, addressing the "symbol grounding problem." The late 1980s and 1990s saw these theories put into practice with advances in robotics, notably Brooks' subsumption architecture and the Cog project, which emphasized behavior-based control and developmental learning in humanoid robots. The "learning-driven paradigm" further accelerated EAI development, with deep learning enabling complex visuomotor policies and adaptive interactions in the 2010s.

The current research landscape is characterized by a rapid evolution of AI models. The paper highlights five foundational areas: Computer Vision (CV), Natural Language Processing (NLP), Reinforcement Learning (RL), LLMs/Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), and WMs. Each of these fields has seen breakthrough models (e.g., ResNet, Transformer, PPO, ChatGPT, Dreamer-v3, Sora) that have significantly enhanced perception, cognition, and interaction capabilities for embodied agents. The paper notes the critical shift from unimodal approaches—where individual components like perception, cognition, or interaction were driven by single sensory inputs (e.g., vision-only SLAM, language-only planning)—to multimodal systems. Unimodal approaches, while successful in their narrow domains, were limited by their narrow information scope and inherent gaps between modalities. The rise of multimodal EAI, integrating visual, auditory, and tactile feedback, is seen as crucial for creating more adaptive, flexible, and robust agents.

Within this landscape, LLMs/MLLMs have emerged as powerful tools for semantic reasoning and task decomposition, allowing agents to understand high-level natural language instructions and generate corresponding actions. However, these models often lack grounding in physical laws and struggle with real-time adaptation. Conversely, WMs excel at building internal representations and making future predictions aligned with physical laws, facilitating robust embodied interactions. Yet, WMs typically lack open-ended semantic reasoning and generalizable task decomposition abilities.

This paper's core contribution to the broader landscape is its comprehensive review of these separate streams and, more importantly, its explicit proposition and detailed outline of a **joint MLLM-WM driven embodied AI architecture**. This architecture is designed to bridge the limitations of each approach, integrating the high-level semantic understanding and planning capabilities of MLLMs with the physics-aware simulation and predictive power of WMs. By advocating for this synergy, the work charts a clear path forward for achieving more sophisticated, adaptable, and truly general-purpose embodied agents that can operate effectively in complex, dynamic physical worlds. It not only summarizes the current state but also proposes a new paradigm that will likely dominate next-generation embodied systems, making it a pivotal contribution to the ongoing pursuit of AGI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to provide a comprehensive and detailed exploration of the current literature in Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI), specifically focusing on the pivotal roles played by Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), and World Models (WMs). The authors aim to synthesize the advancements, challenges, and future directions of EAI, ultimately advocating for a synergistic integration of MLLMs and WMs to overcome existing limitations and propel the field towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

The motivation behind this comprehensive review and architectural proposal stems from several critical observations about the state of EAI research:

1. **Rapid Progress and Fragmentation:** The field of EAI is experiencing rapid advancements, particularly with breakthroughs in foundation models like LLMs and WMs. However, research often focuses on individual components or specific modalities, leading to a fragmented understanding of how these powerful models can be optimally leveraged for holistic embodied intelligence. The authors aim to consolidate this knowledge and provide a structured overview.
2. **Limitations of Unimodal and Isolated Approaches:**
 - **LLMs/MLLMs' Strengths and Weaknesses:** While LLMs and their multimodal extensions (MLLMs) have revolutionized semantic reasoning and task decomposition, enabling agents to understand complex natural language instructions and generate high-level plans, they inherently lack grounding in physical laws. This leads to predictions and plans that may be impractical or physically impossible in real-world scenarios. Their reliance on static, pre-trained knowledge also limits real-time adaptation to dynamic environmental feedback.
 - **WMs' Strengths and Weaknesses:** World Models excel at building internal representations of the external world and making future predictions, enabling physical law-compliant interactions and imagination-based planning. However, WMs often struggle with open-ended semantic reasoning and lack the ability for generalizable task decomposition without explicit priors, making them less adaptable to diverse, high-level, human-given goals.
3. **The Need for a Unified Architecture:** The authors identify a significant gap: neither MLLMs alone nor WMs alone can fully address the challenges of complex tasks in dynamic physical worlds. MLLMs offer high-level contextual reasoning but overlook physical constraints, while WMs provide physics-aware simulation but lack high-level semantics. This fundamental disjunction motivates the paper's core proposition: the necessity of a **joint MLLM-WM driven embodied AI architecture**.
4. **Guiding Future Research and Development:** Beyond summarizing current knowledge, the paper seeks to provide a roadmap for future research. By meticulously delineating the history, key technologies, components, and hardware aspects of EAI, and by discussing its evolution from unimodal to multimodal paradigms, the authors intend to offer a foundational understanding. Their identification of representative applications and crucial future directions (e.g., autonomous EAI, hardware, swarm EAI, trustworthiness) aims to steer the research community towards impactful areas.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the conviction that true AGI in embodied systems requires a harmonious integration of symbolic, semantic understanding with robust, physics-grounded interaction. Its key objective is to provide the intellectual framework and architectural blueprint for achieving this synergy, thereby bridging the divide between specialized AI agents and truly general physical intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey and invited paper, the methodology of "Embodied AI: From LLMs to World Models" is rooted in a structured and extensive literature review, synthesis, and architectural proposition, rather than empirical experimentation. The authors systematically dissect the domain of Embodied AI (EAI) by organizing existing knowledge, identifying critical trends, highlighting limitations, and proposing a novel framework.

The approach can be broken down into several key methodological steps:

1. **Historical Contextualization:** The paper begins by tracing the origins of Embodied AI from Alan Turing's early ideas to foundational concepts in cognitive science and advancements in robotics. This historical perspective grounds the discussion in philosophical and engineering roots, setting the stage for understanding current developments.
2. **Foundational Technology Review:** A significant part of the methodology involves reviewing the key technological models that underpin EAI's progress. This includes advancements in Computer Vision (CV), Natural Language Processing (NLP), Reinforcement Learning (RL), Large Language Models (LLMs)/Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), and World Models (WMs). The authors illustrate the interplay and contributions of these fields to EAI.
3. **Component-wise Analysis of Embodied AI:** The paper adopts a modular approach to analyze the core components of EAI:
 - **Active Perception:** Methods are categorized into Visual SLAM, 3D Scene Understanding, and Active Environment Exploration. For each category, representative methods are analyzed along dimensions like sensor type, feature type, and applicable scenarios (summarized in Table I).
 - **Embodied Cognition:** This crucial component is broken down into Task-driven Self-planning, Memory-driven Self-reflection, and Embodied Multimodal Foundation Models. Methods are compared based on input modalities, cognition type, reasoning mode, and output type (Table II).
 - **Dynamic Interaction:** The analysis covers Action Control, Behavioral Interaction, and Collaborative Decision-making, using input modalities, interaction type, learning paradigm, and task type for comparison (Table III).
4. **Hardware and Evaluation Review:** Recognizing the practical aspects of EAI, the authors review hardware optimization techniques (model compression, compiler optimization, domain-specific accelerators, hardware-software co-design) and critical benchmarks/evaluation metrics that are essential for real-world deployment.
5. **Trend Identification (Unimodal to Multimodal):** A dedicated section meticulously discusses the evolution of EAI from unimodal to multimodal approaches, emphasizing the limitations of the former and the advantages of the latter in achieving more adaptive and robust agents. This trend is visually supported by Figure 4.
6. **Detailed Scrutiny of LLM/MLLM and WM Contributions:** The core of the paper involves in-depth examinations of how LLMs/MLLMs empower EAI (semantic reasoning, task decomposition) and how WMs contribute (internal representations, future predictions). Each section details specific examples and existing research, outlining the strengths and inherent limitations of each paradigm in isolation. Roadmaps for the development of MLLMs and WMs for EAI are presented (Figures 5 and 6).
7. **Problem Formulation and Architectural Proposal:** The authors explicitly identify the critical shortcomings of MLLMs (lack of physical grounding, poor real-time adaptation) and WMs (lack of open-ended semantic reasoning, limited generalizability for task decomposition) when used in isolation. This clear problem statement motivates their central methodological contribution: the proposition of a **joint MLLM-WM driven embodied AI architecture**.
8. **Elaboration of the Joint Architecture:** The proposed architecture is not merely conceptual but includes a detailed workflow (Figure 7) illustrating the synergistic data exchange and functionalities between MLLMs, WMs, hardware, and the environment. This includes how MLLMs boost WM reasoning and how WMs boost MLLM interaction. A qualitative comparison (Table IV) further solidifies the advantages of this integrated approach over standalone MLLM-only or WM-only systems across various performance metrics.
9. **Application and Future Directions:** The paper concludes by reviewing representative applications of EAI (service robotics, rescue UAVs, industrial robots) to demonstrate real-world impact. Crucially, it extrapolates from the current state to identify key future research directions, including autonomous EAI, hardware advancements, swarm EAI, explainability and trustworthiness, lifelong learning, and human-in-the-loop systems. This forward-looking approach serves as a guiding framework for the community.

In essence, the methodology is a sophisticated blend of academic literature review, critical analysis, synthesis of complex information, and prescriptive architectural design, all aimed at advancing the understanding and practical implementation of Embodied AI. The comprehensive structure, supported by detailed comparisons and conceptual diagrams, ensures a thorough and insightful overview of the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's main findings and results are primarily derived from its comprehensive literature review and culminate in the proposition of a novel architectural paradigm for Embodied AI.

**Core Findings from the Literature Review:**

1. **Embodied AI as a Path to AGI:** The paper firmly establishes Embodied AI (EAI) as a crucial paradigm for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). It underscores the historical consensus that intelligence emerges from the dynamic coupling of perception, cognition, and interaction within a physical environment, and that EAI represents the evolution from abstract cyberspace problems to real-world physical systems.
2. **Evolution from Unimodal to Multimodal EAI:** A key finding is the significant shift in EAI development from unimodal approaches (e.g., vision-only perception, language-only cognition) to multimodal systems. The limitations of single modalities in providing holistic environmental understanding and facilitating cross-component information transfer have driven this transition, making multimodal integration essential for robust EAI.
3. **Strengths and Limitations of LLMs/MLLMs in EAI:**
 - **Strengths:** Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) are highly effective in empowering EAI through **semantic reasoning** (interpreting natural language instructions, understanding contextual relationships) and **task decomposition** (breaking down complex objectives into actionable sub-tasks). They bring high-level cognitive abilities to embodied agents.
 - **Limitations:** Critically, LLMs/MLLMs often **fail to ground predictions in physics-compliant dynamics**, leading to impractical plans. They also exhibit **poor real-time adaptation** to environmental feedback, as their knowledge is largely static and pre-trained, limiting responsiveness in dynamic physical worlds.
4. **Strengths and Limitations of WMs in EAI:**
 - **Strengths:** World Models (WMs) are powerful in enabling EAI by building accurate **internal representations** of the external world (capturing object dynamics, physics laws, spatial structures) and making reliable **future predictions** of environmental dynamics and action outcomes. This facilitates physical law-compliant embodied interactions and imagination-based planning.
 - **Limitations:** WMs typically **struggle with open-ended semantic reasoning** and **lack the ability for generalizable task decomposition** without explicit priors. Their focus on physical simulation means they may not inherently grasp the high-level semantic intent of complex human instructions.

**Main Result: The Joint MLLM-WM Driven Embodied AI Architecture:**

The most significant result of this paper is the proposition and detailed conceptualization of a **joint MLLM-WM driven embodied AI architecture**. This architecture is presented as the necessary and profound solution to bridge the semantic intelligence of MLLMs with the grounded physical interaction capabilities of WMs.

The key aspects of this proposed architecture and its synergistic benefits are:

- **Complementary Strengths:** The architecture is designed to leverage MLLMs for high-level semantic reasoning and task planning, while WMs provide physics-aware simulation and environmental modeling.
- **MLLMs Boosting WMs:** MLLMs can enrich WMs by injecting semantic knowledge (e.g., interpreting ambiguous visual cues, guiding attention to relevant objects) and improving task decomposition by breaking down high-level goals into sub-tasks that WMs can then simulate for feasibility. They can also facilitate human feedback to refine WM priors.
- **WMs Boosting MLLMs:** WMs can significantly improve MLLMs by providing explicit representations of physical laws, spatio-temporal relationships, and real-time interaction experiences. This helps constrain MLLM-generated actions to be physically plausible, stabilizes MLLM reasoning by maintaining environmental context, and enables iterative refinement of MLLM outputs through closed-loop interaction and memory of past failures.
- **Integrated Workflow:** The paper outlines a bidirectional workflow (Figure 7) that shows how robots' self-state inputs inform both MLLMs and WMs, how MLLM-generated task plans are validated and refined by WM predictions, and how active perception and dynamic interaction with environments are continuously informed and improved by the joint reasoning of MLLMs and WMs, with memory updating facilitating lifelong learning.
- **Qualitative Performance Gains:** A qualitative comparison (Table IV) demonstrates that the joint MLLM-WM architecture offers superior performance across critical metrics such as semantic understanding, task decomposition, physics compliance, future prediction, real-time interaction, memory structure, and scalability, compared to MLLM-only or WM-only systems.

In summary, the paper's core finding is that neither LLMs/MLLMs nor WMs alone are sufficient for advanced EAI. Its main result is the conceptualization and justification of a joint MLLM-WM architecture that harmonizes their respective strengths to enable more robust, intelligent, and adaptable embodied agents capable of navigating and performing complex tasks in the physical world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "Embodied AI: From LLMs to World Models" carries significant implications for the future of artificial intelligence, particularly in the realm of intelligent agents and robotics. Its impact stems from its comprehensive synthesis, critical analysis, and forward-looking architectural proposal.

**1. A Unified Roadmap for Embodied AI Research:**
The paper serves as an invaluable resource for researchers, providing a meticulously structured and comprehensive overview of Embodied AI. By detailing its history, key technologies, fundamental components (perception, cognition, interaction), hardware considerations, and evolutionary trends (unimodal to multimodal), it offers a holistic understanding of the field. This synthesis provides a much-needed roadmap, helping to organize fragmented research efforts and guide newcomers to the field.

**2. Bridging the Gap Towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):**
The ultimate goal of Embodied AI is AGI. The paper's core significance lies in its proposal of the joint MLLM-WM architecture as a crucial step towards this ambitious goal. By advocating for the synergy between the high-level semantic reasoning of MLLMs and the physics-aware predictive capabilities of WMs, the authors present a viable pathway to creating agents that can not only understand complex instructions but also execute them with physical plausibility and real-time adaptability in dynamic, unstructured environments. This integration addresses fundamental limitations of current AI systems and moves beyond specialized capabilities towards more general intelligence.

**3. Enhancing Robustness and Real-World Applicability:**
The proposed joint architecture has the potential to significantly enhance the robustness and real-world applicability of embodied agents. MLLMs, when grounded by WMs, can generate plans that are not just logically sound but also physically feasible, reducing the likelihood of errors or unsafe actions. Conversely, WMs, when informed by MLLMs, can reason about higher-level semantic contexts, making their simulations more relevant to human goals. This synergy is critical for deploying AI in safety-critical domains such as autonomous driving, complex robotic manipulation, and human-robot collaboration.

**4. Driving Innovation in Diverse Application Domains:**
The paper effectively demonstrates the wide applicability of Embodied AI across various sectors:
- **Service Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform complex domestic tasks or assist in healthcare with greater autonomy and adaptability.
- **Rescue UAVs:** Empowering drones to conduct search-and-rescue operations in hazardous and unknown environments more effectively, understanding human commands and navigating safely.
- **Industrial Robots:** Allowing factory robots to handle tasks with increased flexibility, adapt to changes in the workspace, and collaborate more intelligently with humans, moving beyond rigid, pre-programmed routines.
These examples highlight the immediate and future potential for EAI to revolutionize industries and improve human quality of life.

**5. Guiding Future Research Directions:**
Beyond the architectural proposal, the paper's discussion of future research directions is highly impactful. By identifying areas such as autonomous embodied AI, specialized EAI hardware, swarm embodied AI, and crucial aspects like explainability and trustworthiness, lifelong learning, and human-in-the-loop learning, the authors provide a clear agenda for the scientific community. This foresight is critical for channeling resources and efforts into developing robust, ethical, and increasingly capable embodied systems, fostering user confidence and regulatory acceptance. The emphasis on trustworthiness, in particular, acknowledges the growing societal demand for responsible AI development.

In conclusion, this invited paper is a seminal contribution that not only synthesizes the current state of Embodied AI but also offers a powerful vision for its future. By meticulously dissecting the field and championing a synergistic MLLM-WM architecture, it lays the groundwork for overcoming critical challenges, unlocking new capabilities, and ultimately accelerating the journey toward truly intelligent and physically interactive AI systems.
