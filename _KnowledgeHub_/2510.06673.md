# Heptapod: Language Modeling on Visual Signals

> [!summary] Summary
> Heptapod introduces "next 2D distribution prediction," a novel generative learning paradigm that enables causal Transformers to learn holistic visual semantics. It achieves state-of-the-art performance for causal autoregressive image generation on ImageNet-1K without relying on classifier-free guidance or semantic tokenizers, demonstrating intrinsic semantic learning from reconstruction-focused visual tokens.

## Problem

- Existing visual generative models often rely on external semantic crutches like Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) or pre-trained semantic tokenizers, masking intrinsic model limitations and compromising reconstruction fidelity.
- Applying 1D language modeling directly to 2D images is challenging due to the lack of intrinsic sequential order and the "curse of locality," which leads models to learn only local interpolations rather than global semantics.
- The research aimed to develop a visual generative learning paradigm that adheres to LLM "first principles": a tokenizer focused solely on faithful reconstruction and complex semantics emerging implicitly within the Transformer.

## Method

- Heptapod redefines "next token" for visual data by training a causal Transformer to predict the token distribution over the entire remaining ==2D spatial grid== in parallel at each timestep.
- The framework employs reconstruction-focused visual tokenizers (discrete ==VQ-VAE== or continuous ==VAE==) to decouple compression from semantic learning, aligning with LLM tokenizer principles.
- A standard 1D causal Transformer backbone is used in conjunction with specialized ==2D prediction heads== (Global or Chunk-based) to map its output to a full 2D spatial distribution prediction.

## Results

- Heptapod-H (**941M parameters**) achieved an FID of **2.70** and IS of **229.8** on ImageNet 256x256 class-conditional generation, dramatically outperforming prior causal autoregressive models like LlamaGen-3B (**3B parameters**, FID **9.38**) with fewer parameters.
- The model's strong performance is achieved without Classifier-Free Guidance or semantic tokenizers, demonstrating that visual semantics can emerge intrinsically from a well-posed generative objective within a causal Transformer.
- The "next 2D distribution prediction" objective demonstrated a substantial performance gain, achieving FID **3.97** and IS **185.3**, significantly outperforming traditional 1D next-token prediction methods (e.g., FID **13.07** for 1D-random scan order).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting the entire 2D spatial distribution compels the model to learn holistic global structures and semantics, effectively overcoming the "curse of locality" observed in 1D next-token prediction for images.
> - The approach implicitly unifies autoregressive modeling with Masked Autoencoding, combining the strengths of both paradigms by having the causal prefix act as context for predicting all future tokens.
> - Reconstruction-focused tokenizers are viable, with continuous VAE tokenizers ultimately yielding superior generative quality due to their better reconstruction fidelity, even with slower initial convergence than discrete VQ-VAE tokenizers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Heptapod" positions itself as a critical contribution to the burgeoning field of generative AI, specifically focusing on extending the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) to the visual domain. The broader research landscape is currently dominated by two parallel and sometimes intersecting trends:

**a. The LLM Paradigm Shift:** The success of LLMs, attributed to a simple recipe (scalable Transformer, causal attention, efficient subword tokenizer like BPE, and next-token prediction objective), has inspired a wave of research attempting to transplant these principles to other modalities like vision and audio. The allure is to achieve similar scalability, emergent capabilities, and generalizability in non-textual data.

**b. Challenges in Visual Generative Modeling:** Directly transferring the 1D language modeling paradigm to the 2D visual domain has proven challenging. This is largely due to images lacking an intrinsic sequential order, unlike text. The prevailing approaches to bridge this performance gap often involve incorporating what the authors term "external semantics":
 - **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** An inference-time technique that significantly boosts generation quality in diffusion models and some autoregressive models. However, the authors argue that heavy reliance on CFG obscures intrinsic model limitations, can lead to oversaturation, and reduces sample diversity. Many leading visual generative models show substantial performance degradation when CFG is disabled, highlighting this dependency.
 - **Semantic Tokenizers:** These approaches embed external semantic information directly into the visual tokenizer, often by distilling knowledge from pre-trained self-supervised learning (SSL) models like DINO or CLIP. While effective in improving generation quality, the authors contend this deviates from the LLM "first principles." In LLMs, the BPE tokenizer is semantically agnostic, serving only for efficient compression, while semantic understanding *emerges* within the Transformer. Semantic tokenizers, by pre-packaging semantics, limit a model's ability to learn beyond the SSL model and can compromise reconstruction fidelity (an "impossibility triangle" dilemma).

**Heptapod's Distinct Position:** Heptapod directly challenges these two prevailing dependencies. It advocates for a return to the foundational principles of LLMs: a tokenizer focused solely on faithful reconstruction, and complex semantics emerging *implicitly* within the causal Transformer through a self-supervised generative objective. It specifically seeks to address the "curse of locality" where visual autoregressive models, trained on standard next-token prediction, tend to focus on local interpolation due to high image redundancy, failing to capture long-range dependencies crucial for holistic understanding.

In the context of related work:
- **Visual Tokenization:** Heptapod firmly aligns with *reconstruction-focused tokenizers* (e.g., VQ-VAE, VAE, VQGAN), distinguishing itself from the *semantic tokenizers* that distill from SSL models.
- **Image Language Modeling:** While early works like iGPT operated directly on pixels with limited success, and VQGAN+Transformers established a dominant paradigm but often required CFG, Heptapod takes a unique path. It explicitly *retains the causal attention mechanism* (unlike some generalized autoregressive models like MAR or VAR that employ non-causal or partially non-causal attention) while fundamentally altering the learning objective to enable holistic spatial understanding. This makes it a principled approach to integrating visual generative training into the true language modeling paradigm, enhancing architectural coherence for potential multimodal LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central question driving the Heptapod research is articulated explicitly:
**"Can we devise a visual generative learning paradigm that returns to first principles, where the tokenizer is dedicated solely to faithful reconstruction, and complex semantics emerge implicitly within the Transformer through the next-token prediction objective?"**

This overarching objective is motivated by several key observations and challenges in the existing landscape of visual generative models:

- **Reliance on External Semantics:** The authors are motivated by the widespread use of external crutches like Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and semantic tokenizers. They argue that CFG, while effective, masks inherent limitations of the generative models and can negatively impact diversity. Semantic tokenizers, by embedding pre-computed semantics from external SSL models, violate the core LLM principle where semantic understanding arises organically from the Transformer's self-supervised training. This dependency compromises the model's intrinsic learning capabilities and can reduce reconstruction fidelity.
- **Ambiguity of "Next Token" in 2D:** The fundamental challenge of applying 1D language modeling to 2D images is the ill-defined nature of "next token." Text has a natural linear sequence, but in an image, any ungenerated patch could logically be "next." Existing 1D autoregressive models often rely on a fixed scan order or explicit positional encoding, which can limit their ability to capture truly global relationships.
- **The "Curse of Locality" in Visual Autoregressive Models:** Standard autoregressive models, when applied to visual tokens (especially those from reconstruction-focused tokenizers like VQ-VAE, which exhibit strong local correlations), tend to take a "shortcut." They excel at predicting highly correlated neighboring tokens, minimizing loss through local interpolation. This diverts the model's learning away from capturing the long-range, holistic dependencies crucial for understanding global image structure and semantics. As a result, semantics struggle to emerge within the Transformer itself.
- **Desire for First Principles:** The motivation is to create a visual generative model that is truly self-sufficient in learning semantics, mirroring how LLMs learn. This involves decoupling the tokenizer's role (pure compression/reconstruction) from the Transformer's role (semantic learning via generative objective).

In essence, Heptapod aims to design a learning paradigm that intrinsically compels a causal Transformer, using a simple reconstruction-focused tokenizer, to develop a comprehensive, holistic understanding of images by resolving the "next token" ambiguity and overcoming the "curse of locality" through a novel objective function.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Heptapod introduces a novel framework that generalizes the core concept of next-token prediction from a 1D sequence to a holistic 2D distribution prediction, while strictly adhering to the principles of causal attention.

**a. Core Innovation: Next 2D Distribution Prediction:**
The fundamental methodological shift in Heptapod is to redefine what "next token" means for visual data. Instead of predicting a single token at a specific subsequent position (as in traditional 1D autoregressive models), Heptapod's causal Transformer is trained to predict the *distribution of tokens over the entire remaining 2D spatial grid* of the image, in parallel, at each timestep. This is crucial for several reasons:
- **Resolving 2D Ambiguity:** It directly addresses the problem of an ill-defined "next token" in 2D space. The model is not told *where* to predict next; it must be prepared to predict *any* future position.
- **Forcing Holistic Understanding:** By requiring the model to predict distributions across the entire ungenerated portion of the image, it cannot rely on local interpolation shortcuts. To accurately predict spatially non-adjacent patches, the Transformer must learn and encode comprehensive global structure and semantics from the visible context.
- **Unifying AR and MAE:** This objective implicitly unifies autoregressive modeling with Masked Autoencoding (MAE). The causal prefix (visible tokens) acts as the unmasked context, and predicting the entire 2D grid in parallel is analogous to reconstructing masked patches. This blends the strengths of both paradigms.

**b. Visual Tokenization:**
Heptapod is designed to work with **reconstruction-focused tokenizers**, rejecting the trend of semantic tokenizers. It supports both:
- **Discrete Tokens:** Obtained from models like VQ-VAE (specifically, the VQGAN tokenizer from LlamaGen in experiments). The distribution is modeled as a categorical one via a linear classification head with **cross-entropy loss**.
- **Continuous Tokens:** Obtained from models like VAE (specifically, the VAE tokenizer from MAR in experiments). The distribution is modeled using a diffusion-style MLP head with **MSE loss**, which is equivalent to maximizing a lower bound on log-likelihood.
The authors argue that mathematically, both discrete and continuous token prediction aim to model the token distribution, though their optimization signals differ (cross-entropy is direct and sharp, diffusion loss is implicit and averaged).

**c. Causal Transformer Backbone:**
The core generative model remains a standard 1D causal Transformer, ensuring architectural coherence with LLMs. The complexity of 2D spatial modeling is offloaded to a specialized prediction head.

**d. 2D Prediction Head Architectures:**
To map the 1D causal Transformer's output to a full 2D spatial distribution, two architectural variants are explored for the prediction head:
- **Global Prediction Head:** This variant models global spatial dependencies across the *entire* image grid. At each timestep, the causal Transformer's hidden states (representing the visible context) are concatenated with learnable mask tokens, each augmented with a spatial positional embedding for a distinct masked position. This combined sequence is fed into a **bi-directional Transformer**. This forces the model to reason explicitly about global relationships, inferring masked content from the causal past. The loss is computed only on the masked positions.
- **Local Prediction Head (Chunk-based):** For efficiency, this variant partitions the image into non-overlapping chunks. At each step, the head focuses only on the chunk containing the next token. It uses learnable queries (for positions within the target chunk) that first attend to the causal Transformer's hidden states via **cross-attention**, followed by **bi-directional self-attention** layers operating *within* the chunk to model local spatial dependencies. This reduces computational cost but potentially limits global understanding.

**e. Training and Inference:**
- **Training:** The model is trained to predict the token distribution over all subsequent spatial positions given the causal prefix. The actual "next" token is then determined by the ground truth, and the loss is applied across all predicted future positions.
- **Inference:** At each step, the model predicts a distribution over the entire 2D grid for potential (position, token) pairs. A pair is then sampled and added to the sequence, serving as input for the next timestep until the grid is filled. The generation order (which future position to sample next) is randomized during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Heptapod's experiments, primarily on the ImageNet-1K 256x256 benchmark without Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), yield several significant findings:

**a. Convergence Efficiency with Different Tokenizers:**
- **Trade-off:** Models trained with discrete VQ tokenizers (using cross-entropy loss) exhibit significantly faster and smoother initial convergence (lower FID, higher IS in early epochs) compared to continuous VAE tokenizers (using diffusion-style MLP with MSE loss). This supports the hypothesis that cross-entropy provides a more efficient optimization signal.
- **Final Quality:** However, the VAE-based model, despite slower initial convergence, ultimately achieves superior generative performance after more epochs. This final performance aligns closely with the superior reconstruction fidelity of the VAE tokenizer itself.
- **Conclusion:** Reconstruction-focused tokenizers, regardless of discrete or continuous nature, are viable. The tokenizer's reconstruction quality sets a practical upper bound on the final generative performance. For maximum generative quality, VAE was chosen as the default.

**b. Properties of Next 2D Distribution Prediction:**
- **Impact of 2D Prediction:** Directly comparing 1D next-token prediction with Heptapod's 2D prediction objective demonstrated a substantial performance gain. "2D-random (Heptapod)" achieved FID 3.97 and IS 185.3, dramatically outperforming "1D-random" (FID 13.07, IS 91.4) and "1D-raster" (FID 19.23, IS 62.3). This strongly validates that explicitly predicting distributions over 2D images is far more impactful than 1D next-token prediction for visual signals.
- **Window Size vs. Supervision Density:** An ablation study on the prediction head architecture (global vs. chunk-based) revealed that the *spatial extent of the prediction target (window size)* is more critical for learning long-term semantics than simply increasing supervision density within a smaller window. The global prediction head (w256n1), which predicts the entire 256-token grid, achieved the best performance. Restricting prediction to smaller chunks (e.g., w16n16) yielded worse results, even with higher supervision density. This supports the core hypothesis that a holistic prediction task is key.

**c. Benchmarking with Previous Generative Models:**
- **Causal Autoregressive Dominance:** Heptapod-H (941M parameters) achieved an FID of 2.70 and IS of 229.8 on ImageNet 256x256 class-conditional generation (without CFG). This represents a dramatic improvement over previous causal autoregressive models like LlamaGen-3B (3B parameters, FID 9.38, IS 112.9), outperforming it by a wide margin with less than one-third of the parameters.
- **Competitive with Non-Causal and SSL-Dependent Models:** Heptapod's performance is competitive with, and in some cases surpasses, models that deviate from strict causal attention (e.g., MAR-B/L, VAR-d30) or rely on external pre-trained SSL models to inject semantics into their tokenizers (e.g., DiGIT, MAETok).
- **CFG-Free and Semantic Tokenizer-Free:** All reported results are obtained *without CFG* and *without semantic tokenizers*, using only reconstruction-focused tokenizers. This is a crucial finding, demonstrating that visual semantics *can* emerge intrinsically from a well-posed generative objective within a causal Transformer, without external "crutches."

**d. Scaled Supervision Density with Global Prediction:**
- **Effectiveness with Holistic View:** While increasing supervision density within small prediction windows showed limited gains, when combined with a *global prediction window* (w=256), increasing the number of tokens used for loss computation per sequence consistently improved both FID and IS metrics. This indicates that dense supervision becomes highly effective when the model is already compelled to learn long-range dependencies through a holistic prediction task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Heptapod's introduction of "next 2D distribution prediction" marks a significant conceptual and empirical advancement in visual generative modeling, with several key significances and potential impacts:

**a. Reaffirmation of First Principles:** The most profound significance lies in Heptapod's successful demonstration that the "first principles" underlying the success of LLMs — a semantically agnostic, reconstruction-focused tokenizer and semantics emerging from a scalable causal Transformer via a simple self-supervised objective — can be effectively transplanted to the visual domain. This challenges the prevailing trends of relying on external mechanisms like CFG or semantic tokenizers, advocating for intrinsically more robust and self-sufficient generative models.

**b. Novel Resolution of 2D Ambiguity:** Heptapod provides a principled solution to the long-standing challenge of applying 1D sequential language modeling to 2D non-sequential data. By generalizing "next-token" prediction to "next 2D distribution prediction," it resolves the inherent ambiguity of ordering in images and compels the model to learn holistic spatial relationships rather than merely local interpolation. This conceptual innovation could inspire similar reformulations for other non-sequential data modalities.

**c. Unification of Generative Paradigms:** The framework effectively unifies autoregressive modeling (sequential generation from a causal prefix) with Masked Autoencoding (MAE) (holistic reconstruction of masked parts). This synthesis combines the strengths of both, leading to models that learn rich, comprehensive visual semantics through generative training.

**d. State-of-the-Art Causal Autoregressive Performance:** Heptapod achieves significantly improved performance for *causal autoregressive* image generation, dramatically outperforming prior methods like LlamaGen while using fewer parameters, and doing so *without Classifier-Free Guidance* or *semantic tokenizers*. This elevates the viability and competitiveness of causal autoregressive models in the vision domain, proving they can generate high-fidelity images purely from their intrinsic learning.

**e. Paving the Way for Unified Multimodal LLMs:** By retaining the 1D causal attention mechanism and decoupling semantics from the tokenizer, Heptapod maintains architectural coherence with Large Language Models. This makes it highly suitable for seamless integration into future multimodal LLMs that can process and generate across text, image, and potentially other modalities in a unified, principled manner. This could lead to genuinely generalized AI agents.

**f. Inspiration for Broader Research:** The work "inspires a principled rethinking of language modeling on visual signals and beyond."
 - **Multi-token Prediction Extension:** Heptapod's approach can be seen as an extension of multi-token prediction (MTP) from LLMs to the 2D domain, highlighting the power of dense, long-range prediction objectives.
 - **Acoustic Signals:** The chunk-based prediction head variant suggests a promising direction for language modeling on long or streaming acoustic signals, where global prediction is infeasible.
 - **Tokenizer Agnosticism:** While currently using VQ-VAE/VAE, the core principle is agnostic to the tokenizer form, opening doors for integration with pixel-space generative models or other novel tokenization techniques.

In conclusion, Heptapod offers a compelling return to first principles for visual generative AI, demonstrating that a well-designed generative objective can empower causal Transformers to learn complex visual semantics internally, without external assistance. This could lead to more robust, efficient, and truly multimodal AI systems.
