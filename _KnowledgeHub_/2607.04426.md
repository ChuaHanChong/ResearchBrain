# ACE-Brain-0.5: A Unified Embodied Foundational Model for Physical Agentic AI

> [!summary] Summary
> ACE-Brain-0.5 integrates five core cognitive functionsSpatial Perception, Decision Making, Embodied Interaction, Self Monitoring, and Self Improvementinto a unified 8B embodied foundational model. It demonstrated improved spatial perception across 14 benchmarks, achieved a 98.2% average success rate on the LIBERO manipulation benchmark, and enhanced navigation success rates by 8.8% through a self-improvement framework.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI systems operate with fragmented capabilities, specializing in isolated functions like vision, language, or action, without a unified cognitive representation.
- Prior embodied models often exhibit limitations in sophisticated spatial reasoning, long-horizon planning, and comprehensive execution assessment within a single architecture.
- There is a lack of inherent mechanisms for self-monitoring (tracking progress, identifying failures) and self-improvement (learning from experience) essential for robust, long-term autonomous robot operation.

## Method

- Developed **ACE-Brain-0.5**, a unified 8B parameter embodied foundation model built upon a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, integrating five tightly coupled cognitive functions.
- Designed an all-in-one ==mixture-of-transformer== architecture with an ==Omni-Vision Encoder==, ==LLM Decoder==, ==Action Expert== (with Fast Vision Pathway), and task-specific decoding heads for spatial perception, decision-making, embodied interaction, and self-monitoring.
- Employed the "==Scaffold, Specialize, Reconcile, Reactivate (SSR+)==" training strategy to effectively manage and integrate heterogeneous supervision signals from diverse tasks into a single model.

## Results

- Improved spatial perception on **14 out of 18** benchmarks compared to ACE-Brain-0, including an **8.1%** gain on Scan2Cap and **16.5%** on Multi3DRef for language-grounded 3D perception.
- Achieved an average success rate of **98.2%** on the LIBERO manipulation benchmark and a **63.8%** success rate (SR) on RxR Val-Unseen for navigation, outperforming several strong VLA and VLN baselines.
- Demonstrated effective self-monitoring with **0.94** VOC on RBM-EVAL ID and a 'Navigation Evolving' self-improvement framework that boosted R2R Val-Unseen SR by **8.8%** (from **48.6%** to **57.4%**) by generating corrective supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is feasible to unify diverse cognitive functions (perception, decision-making, interaction, monitoring, and self-improvement) into a single large language model backbone, leading to synergistic performance across tasks.
> - Spatial intelligence, including 3D scene perception and egocentric reasoning, is a crucial enabler for grounding and unifying various embodied cognitive functions across heterogeneous robot platforms and tasks.
> - The SSR+ training strategy, particularly the optimization-based task vector merging in the 'Reconcile' stage, effectively integrates specialized learning while preserving task performance, mitigating cross-interface interference in multi-capability models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of Physical Agentic AI aims to create autonomous robotic agents capable of world understanding, goal-driven planning, physical action, progress monitoring, and continuous self-improvement. Historically, embodied AI has evolved through several paradigms. Classical robotics employed modular "Sense–Plan–Act" pipelines, which offered interpretability but suffered from fragmentation and limited generalization. The subsequent emergence of end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World-Action models, such as the π series, DreamZero, and GR00T, enabled direct policy learning from observations, enhancing manipulation capabilities through large-scale training. However, these models often exhibit limitations in spatial reasoning, long-horizon planning, and execution assessment due to their action-centric nature. More recently, robot-agent systems like QwenRobot and ABot integrated planning, tool use, and specialist models, but achieved autonomy through system-level orchestration rather than learning a single, shared cognitive representation.

This landscape indicates a fragmentation in existing embodied foundation models, where different systems specialize in isolated capabilities such as visual understanding, language-conditioned reasoning, navigation, manipulation, or execution assessment. While multimodal embodied models like RynnBrain demonstrated strong spatial understanding, they often lacked the ability to generate executable actions or monitor execution progress. Conversely, systems like Cosmos 3 unified action generation and perceptual understanding but without explicit self-evaluation mechanisms. The absence of a single system integrating spatial perception, decision-making, embodied interaction, and self-monitoring within a unified robot foundation model represents a significant gap. ACE-Brain-0.5 is positioned to address this gap by proposing a unified architecture that integrates these diverse cognitive functions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a Unified Embodied Foundation Model that integrates five tightly coupled cognitive functions necessary for general Physical Agentic AI: Spatial Perception, Decision Making, Embodied Interaction, Self Monitoring, and Self Improvement. This model aims to move beyond fragmented approaches by learning a single, shared robot representation that supports a closed-loop intelligence system.

The motivation stems from the observed limitations of prior embodied AI paradigms:
- **Fragmentation of Capabilities**: Existing systems typically address parts of the embodied AI loop in isolation. End-to-end policies excel at generating actions but often lack sophisticated spatial reasoning, long-horizon planning, and execution assessment. Robot-agent systems orchestrate multiple specialist models but do not learn a unified underlying representation. This fragmentation hinders the development of generalist physical agents.
- **Lack of Unified Representation**: There is a need for a single, shared cognitive representation across heterogeneous robot platforms and tasks. The authors identify spatial intelligence – the ability to localize objects, reason about 3D relationships, understand scenes from different viewpoints, and identify actionable regions – as the crucial enabler for unifying these functions.
- **Limited Self-Assessment and Improvement**: Many existing models lack inherent mechanisms for self-monitoring (tracking execution progress, identifying failures) and self-improvement (learning from accumulated experience and adapting over deployment time). These capabilities are considered essential for robust, long-term autonomous operation in the physical world.
- **Extension of Prior Work**: The predecessor, ACE-Brain-0, established a strong foundation in spatial understanding but was primarily an "understanding model" without a unified action interface or execution monitoring capability. ACE-Brain-0.5 seeks to extend this spatial foundation into a comprehensive, closed-loop embodied foundation model by incorporating decision-making, embodied interaction, and self-monitoring.

By integrating these functions into a single 8B backbone, the authors aim to achieve a system that can ground objects and affordances, reason over 3D and egocentric spatial relations, decompose instructions into subgoals, generate navigation and manipulation actions, and estimate execution progress for verification and recovery, ultimately enabling deployment-time adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ACE-Brain-0.5 is built upon a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model, extending its spatial understanding capabilities to a unified, closed-loop cognitive system.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The core architecture of ACE-Brain-0.5 is an all-in-one mixture-of-transformer design, which processes heterogeneous inputs and maintains a unified scene-and-task representation. Task-specific interfaces then decode this representation into various multimodal outputs.

- **Omni-Vision Encoder**: This shared component encodes single-view images, multi-view observations, and videos. Single-view images support object-level perception, multi-view observations provide 3D grounding and spatial relation reasoning, and videos preserve temporal context for progress estimation and sequential decision-making. Visual tokens are projected into the LLM token space.
- **LLM Decoder**: Language instructions are tokenized and fused with visual tokens in this decoder, which functions as the unified backbone. It takes language instructions, visual observations, and optional robot proprioceptive states to produce a shared embodied state. This state provides common context for all decoding heads and high-level semantic input for the Action Expert.
- **Action Expert and Fast Vision Pathway**: To address the real-time latency requirements of robot control, a dedicated Action Expert, implemented as a flow-matching policy head, generates executable action chunks. It is conditioned by the LLM Decoder's high-level embodied state and real-time visual features from a lightweight Fast Vision Pathway (using a DINOv3 encoder). This dual-timescale approach allows the LLM Decoder to operate at a lower frequency for high-level context, while the Fast Vision Pathway provides up-to-date perceptual features at the control frequency, maintaining action generation grounded in the shared backbone.
- **Task-Specific Decoding**: The shared embodied state is decoded into specific outputs for each cognitive function:
 - **Spatial Perception**: Autoregressive prediction of bounding boxes, segmentation masks, and spatial point coordinates for object grounding, regions, and affordances.
 - **Decision Making**: Autoregressive language generation to decompose high-level instructions into executable subgoals.
 - **Embodied Interaction**: Prediction of discrete actions for navigation and continuous action chunks (from the Action Expert) for manipulation.
 - **Self Monitoring**: Frame-wise sequence prediction of normalized progress scores and pairwise trajectory preference judgments.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy: Scaffold, Specialize, Reconcile, and Reactivate (SSR+)

To manage the heterogeneity of supervision signals across diverse tasks, ACE-Brain-0.5 employs an extended training strategy called SSR+:
- **Stage 1: Scaffold**: Initializes the model with ACE-Brain-0, providing a strong foundation in broad spatial and embodied understanding.
- **Stage 2: Specialize**: Independently trains four task-specialized checkpoints (θqa, θgrd, θnav, θprog) on their respective data distributions (spatial QA/planning, 2D/3D grounding, navigation, progress estimation). This isolation prevents cross-interface interference.
- **Stage 3: Reconcile**: Consolidates the specialized checkpoints into a single parameter space using optimization-based task vector merging. This involves calculating task vectors (weight-space displacements) and merging them to minimize output discrepancy across experts for each layer, resulting in θmerge.
- **Stage 4: Reactivate**: Fine-tunes the merged checkpoint (θmerge) on a small, mixed SFT dataset (Dmix) for a limited number of updates. This stage recalibrates output conventions across interfaces, leveraging the retained task-specific knowledge in the merged weights, and resolves output-format conflicts.

#### 3.3. Self-Improving Framework

ACE-Brain-0.5 is designed to operate within a closed-loop Perception–Planning–Action–Evaluation framework for real-world deployment. The model decodes four coordinated outputs: grounded scene state (Spatial Perception), executable subgoal plan (Decision Making), navigation/manipulation action (Embodied Interaction), and execution evaluation signal (Self Monitoring).

The self-improving component works by incrementally updating an external execution state (H), which includes task schemas, spatial memory, and failure-recovery cases, from accumulated rollout experience. Successful rollouts, corrected failures, and progress-labeled trajectories are used as feedback. This approach enables lightweight, deployment-time adaptation without requiring model retraining after every interaction. For navigation, a "Navigation Evolving" framework constructs corrective supervision by allowing the current policy to perform rollouts, identifying deviations with an oracle teacher, and generating oracle-recovered trajectories for retraining, thereby improving robustness to off-policy states.

#### 3.4. Training Data

The model is trained on a diverse set of large-scale datasets:
- **Spatial Perception Data**: Includes VSI-590K, VLM-3R, GPT4Scene, and MindCube for image, video, and multi-view 3D spatial understanding. Additional high-quality data is generated using a "Spatial Harness Annotation Pipeline" for complex embodied spatial understanding. Grounding data includes RefSpatial, PixMo-Points, and RoboPoint for 2D visual grounding, embodied spatial localization, and action-oriented affordance prediction.
- **Embodied Navigation**: Utilizes a mixture of public VLN trajectory datasets (R2R, RxR, EnvDrop, ScaleVLN) and SRDF-400K, unified into a VLN-CE action interface. It includes progress-aware Chain-of-Thought (CoT) navigation samples.
- **Embodied Manipulation**: A large-scale cross-embodiment trajectory corpus from public and in-house robot datasets. Trajectories are canonicalized into a shared schema for joint states, gripper states, and end-effector pose/rotation.
- **Embodied Task Progress**: Trained on RBM-1M for frame-wise progress prediction (scaled to) and pairwise trajectory preference samples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Spatial Perception

ACE-Brain-0.5 demonstrated improvements across numerous spatial perception benchmarks:
- **Egocentric and Multi-view Reasoning**: On VSI (egocentric video spatial reasoning), it achieved 62.2%. On MMSI (multi-image spatial reasoning), it reached 35.5%, an improvement over ACE-Brain-0 (32.2%). For MindCube (spatial mental modeling), it obtained 86.3%, surpassing ACE-Brain-0 (82.1%).
- **3D Scene Perception and Grounding**: It achieved 99.2 on ScanQA and 62.6% on SQA3D, both improvements over ACE-Brain-0. On language-grounded 3D perception, it reached 83.3 on Scan2Cap (8.1% improvement over ACE-Brain-0), 70.2% on ScanRefer (8.8% improvement), and 72.4% on Multi3DRef (16.5% improvement).
- **Planning-Oriented Spatial Reasoning**: On RoboSpatial (robot-centric spatial relations), it scored 60.1%, exceeding ACE-Brain-0 (55.6%). For RefSpatial (multi-step spatial reference resolution), it achieved 55.6%, a substantial improvement over ACE-Brain-0 (26.0%). On PointArena (language-guided pointing), it obtained 68.5%, outperforming ACE-Brain-0 (44.7%).
- **Affordance and Trajectory Prediction**: On RoboAfford, it reached 75.1%, an 18.6% improvement over ACE-Brain-0 (56.5%). For ShareRobot-Traj (trajectory prediction, lower error is better), it reduced the error to 0.32 from ACE-Brain-0's 0.46.
Overall, ACE-Brain-0.5 improved on 14 out of 18 spatial perception and grounding benchmarks compared to ACE-Brain-0, and showed competitive or superior performance against closed-source and open-source MLLMs.

#### 4.2. Decision Making

For autonomous driving benchmarks:
- **Scene Understanding**: On MME-RealWorld, it obtained 66.6%. On MAPLM (map-aware and traffic-structured reasoning), it achieved 71.3%, surpassing several strong baselines.
- **Action-level Decision Making**: It reached 78.2% on DriveAction, comparable to specialized driving models. On driving QA, it achieved 44.7% on NuScenesQA and 86.7% on NuPlanQA.
While showing moderate drops from ACE-Brain-0 on some driving-specific benchmarks, ACE-Brain-0.5 maintained strong decision-making capabilities within a broader unified framework.

#### 4.3. Embodied Interaction

- **Navigation (VLN-CE)**: On R2R Val-Unseen, the unified ACE-Brain-0.5 achieved 57.4% Success Rate (SR) and 4.8 Navigation Error (NE). A specialized variant, ACE-Brain-0.5-Specialist, further improved to 62.2% SR and 4.2 NE, achieving the best SR and NE among compared methods. On RxR Val-Unseen, the unified model achieved the best NE of 4.3 and the highest SR of 63.8%. The specialist variant achieved the best nDTW of 67.1%.
- **Manipulation**:
 - **LIBERO**: Using a frozen ACE-Brain-0.5 backbone with a trained FastVision and flow-matching action expert, the model achieved an average success rate of 98.2%, outperforming several strong VLA baselines. It reached 100.0% on both Spatial and Object suites and 97.0% on the Long suite.
 - **SimplerEnv-Bridge**: A fully fine-tuned VLA variant, ACE-Brain-0.5-VLA, achieved an average success rate of 82.3%, exceeding GTA-VLA (81.2%) and other baselines. It performed best on Cube (75.0%) and Eggplant (100.0%).

#### 4.4. Self Monitoring

ACE-Brain-0.5 demonstrated performance in robotic progress estimation:
- **RBM-EVAL Benchmarks**: It achieved 0.94 and 0.96 VOC on standard in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) splits, respectively. On the refined splits (RBM-EVAL-ID-Refined and RBM-EVAL-OOD-Refined), which include reversed successful trajectories to test temporal understanding, it obtained 0.80 and 0.88 VOC, respectively. These results indicate that ACE-Brain-0.5 captured temporal task evolution and action-direction better than baselines, which showed more significant performance drops on the refined tasks.

#### 4.5. Self Improvement

The "Navigation Evolving" framework, which converts closed-loop rollouts into corrective navigation supervision, demonstrated improvements:
- **Navigation Metrics**: Compared to static trajectory imitation (48.6% SR, 5.7 NE on R2R Val-Unseen), Navigation Evolving improved the unified ACE-Brain-0.5 to 57.4% SR and 4.8 NE. On RxR Val-Unseen, SR improved from 53.3% to 63.8% and NE from 6.0 to 4.3. This indicates increased robustness to off-policy states and reduced compounding errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ACE-Brain-0.5 represents an advancement toward general Physical Agentic AI by demonstrating the feasibility of unifying perception, decision-making, embodied interaction, self-monitoring, and self-improvement within a single foundation model. The key contributions and their potential impact include:

- **Unified Embodied Foundation Model Paradigm**: The proposed paradigm and its instantiation, ACE-Brain-0.5, offer a conceptual and practical framework for developing robots with integrated cognitive functions. This unification can streamline development and deployment by eliminating the need for disparate specialist models and complex system-level orchestration.
- **Enhanced Spatial Intelligence and Grounding**: Significant improvements across a wide range of spatial perception and grounding benchmarks indicate that the model possesses a robust understanding of the physical world. This is foundational for any physical agent, enabling it to accurately perceive its environment, localize objects, and interpret spatial relationships for complex tasks.
- **Integrated Action Generation and Monitoring**: The model's ability to generate both navigation and manipulation actions, coupled with its self-monitoring capabilities (progress estimation), provides a closed-loop system for autonomous task execution. This enables robots to assess their own performance, detect deviations, and potentially initiate recovery strategies, moving beyond mere execution to more intelligent and resilient behavior.
- **SSR+ Training Strategy**: The introduction of SSR+ effectively addresses the challenge of integrating heterogeneous supervision signals without incurring significant cross-task interference. This method facilitates the development of multi-capability foundation models by reconciling specialized learning while preserving individual task performance, making future expansion of capabilities more tractable.
- **Initial Steps towards Self-Improvement**: The companion framework for updating an external execution state and the navigation evolving mechanism demonstrate a pathway for continuous adaptation. This ability for robots to learn from their own experiences and correct failures is critical for long-term autonomy and deployment in dynamic, open-world environments, potentially reducing human intervention.

While ACE-Brain-0.5 marks an early step, its current self-improvement mechanisms are lightweight and domain-specific. Future work aims to generalize these mechanisms into model-level self-evolution and scale the model to broader embodiments and longer-horizon tasks. The findings suggest that jointly training these cognitive functions within a single robot foundation model can lead to synergistic improvements, laying a foundation for more general and self-improving robotic systems.
