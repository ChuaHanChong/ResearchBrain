# PACT: Self-Evolving Physical Safety Alignment for Diffusion Policies in Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PACT introduces a self-evolving post-training framework to align pretrained diffusion policies with physical safety constraints in embodied robotic manipulation. The framework effectively reduces safety violations by an average of 31.0% and improves task success rates by 30.7% in simulation, with comparable gains in real-world applications, without requiring new demonstration data or task rewards.

## Problem

- Diffusion policies, while adept at learning expressive action distributions for robotic manipulation, face challenges in real-world deployment due to strict physical safety requirements like collision avoidance.
- Existing safety enforcement methods (design-time or test-time) often demand extensive human expertise, task-specific engineering, or additional sensors, limiting policy expressivity or increasing inference overhead.
- Aligning diffusion models post-training is difficult because of their inherent probability dispersion, the typical absence of demonstration data or task utility functions in this phase, and the risk of catastrophic forgetting when aggressively enforcing constraints.

## Method

- **PACT** formulates safety alignment as a regularized projection problem within a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), seeking a safety-compliant policy that minimizes KL divergence to the original pretrained policy.
- It employs 'constraint distillation' to guide the ==diffusion policy== by directly computing and distilling the score function of an implicit safety teacher, derived from differentiable safety costs, providing dense supervision.
- A 'curriculum-based distillation strategy' is integrated to progressively increase the strength of safety constraints over iterations, ensuring monotonic improvement in safety while mitigating catastrophic forgetting and controlling policy shifts.

## Results

- PACT reduced safety violations by an average of **31.0%** and improved task success rates by **30.7%** across various simulated tasks, demonstrating consistent performance and safety gains across different base policies.
- In real-world evaluations, PACT substantially improved the physical Safe Rate by an average of **36.0%** and task progress by **36.8%** on safety-critical manipulation tasks such as Pour Water and GPU Assembly.
- The framework achieved superior success and safety rates compared to both on-policy (e.g., PPO, DIPO) and off-policy (e.g., Guided Rollouts, iDQL) baselines, exhibiting over **5x** faster convergence than RL-based methods due to stable and dense supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical safety constraints can be effectively integrated into diffusion policies post-training by distilling an 'implicit safety teacher' through a reverse-KL objective, leveraging dense, continuous gradients of safety costs.
> - A progressive curriculum for constraint enforcement is vital to maintain task competence and prevent catastrophic forgetting when aligning expressive policies without access to original task utility functions.
> - Efficient constraint injection can be achieved by approximating intermediate cost gradients and applying guidance primarily in the final few denoising steps of the diffusion process, reducing computational overhead.
