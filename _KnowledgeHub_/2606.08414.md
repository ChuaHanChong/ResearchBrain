# PACT: Self-Evolving Physical Safety Alignment for Diffusion Policies in Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PACT introduces a self-evolving post-training framework to align pretrained diffusion policies with physical safety constraints in embodied robotic manipulation. The framework effectively reduces safety violations by an average of 31.0% and improves task success rates by 30.7% in simulation, with comparable gains in real-world applications, without requiring new demonstration data or task rewards.

## Problem

- Diffusion policies, while adept at learning expressive action distributions for robotic manipulation, face challenges in real-world deployment due to strict physical safety requirements like collision avoidance.
- Existing safety enforcement methods (design-time or test-time) often demand extensive human expertise, task-specific engineering, or additional sensors, limiting policy expressivity or increasing inference overhead.
- Aligning diffusion models post-training is difficult because of their inherent probability dispersion, the typical absence of demonstration data or task utility functions in this phase, and the risk of catastrophic forgetting when aggressively enforcing constraints.

## Method

- **PACT** formulates safety alignment as a regularized projection problem within a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), seeking a safety-compliant policy that minimizes KL divergence to the original pretrained policy.
- It employs 'constraint distillation' to guide the ==diffusion policy== by directly computing and distilling the score function of an implicit safety teacher, derived from differentiable safety costs, providing dense supervision.
- A 'curriculum-based distillation strategy' is integrated to progressively increase the strength of safety constraints over iterations, ensuring monotonic improvement in safety while mitigating catastrophic forgetting and controlling policy shifts.

## Results

- PACT reduced safety violations by an average of **31.0%** and improved task success rates by **30.7%** across various simulated tasks, demonstrating consistent performance and safety gains across different base policies.
- In real-world evaluations, PACT substantially improved the physical Safe Rate by an average of **36.0%** and task progress by **36.8%** on safety-critical manipulation tasks such as Pour Water and GPU Assembly.
- The framework achieved superior success and safety rates compared to both on-policy (e.g., PPO, DIPO) and off-policy (e.g., Guided Rollouts, iDQL) baselines, exhibiting over **5x** faster convergence than RL-based methods due to stable and dense supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical safety constraints can be effectively integrated into diffusion policies post-training by distilling an 'implicit safety teacher' through a reverse-KL objective, leveraging dense, continuous gradients of safety costs.
> - A progressive curriculum for constraint enforcement is vital to maintain task competence and prevent catastrophic forgetting when aligning expressive policies without access to original task utility functions.
> - Efficient constraint injection can be achieved by approximating intermediate cost gradients and applying guidance primarily in the final few denoising steps of the diffusion process, reducing computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of diffusion policies has advanced robotic manipulation capabilities, enabling the learning of expressive and multimodal action distributions and facilitating generalization across diverse tasks and environments. However, the deployment of these policies in real-world settings is often constrained by the necessity of adhering to strict physical safety requirements. These constraints include collision avoidance, adherence to force limits, and kinematic feasibility. Violations of such constraints can result in task failure, damage to hardware, or injury, particularly in safety-critical applications like manufacturing and surgery.

Existing approaches to enforce safety in robotic systems generally fall into two categories:
1. **Design-time (or train-time) approaches:** These methods integrate safety constraints during data collection or model training. Examples include imitation learning from curated safe demonstrations or safe reinforcement learning (Safe RL) that uses auxiliary cost functions. While these approaches can enhance robustness, they typically demand extensive human expertise, task-specific engineering, and may limit policy expressivity due to restricted action support or reliance on sparse/delayed rewards.
2. **Test-time approaches:** These methods employ guardrails or backup policies to intercept unsafe actions during execution. While reactive, they often depend on predefined constraints, additional sensors for real-time monitoring, or auxiliary perception models, increasing inference-time overhead and potentially limiting applicability to novel scenarios.

Both categories of existing methods present limitations. Design-time methods may restrict the learning process prematurely, while test-time interventions can be reactive and less principled, compromising the expressivity and scalability of diffusion policies.

This work draws inspiration from post-training safety alignment techniques developed for large language models. It aims to bridge the identified gap by proposing a post-training framework specifically for embodied manipulation, addressing the unique challenges associated with continuous action spaces and physical constraints. These challenges include the inherent tendency of diffusion models to spread probability mass, the unavailability of demonstration data or task utility functions in a post-training phase, and the risk of catastrophic forgetting when aggressively enforcing safety constraints without considering task performance. The paper positions its contribution as the first post-training framework for aligning diffusion-based manipulation policies with physical safety constraints without requiring manual data collection or test-time guardrails.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable post-training framework that can align pretrained diffusion policies with physical safety constraints in embodied manipulation tasks. This alignment aims to project policy behaviors onto constraint-feasible regions without compromising the expressivity of the original policies or requiring access to the initial demonstration data or task rewards.

The motivation for PACT arises from several specific challenges inherent in deploying expressive diffusion policies in safety-critical robotic environments:

1. **Addressing Mode Concentration in Diffusion Models:** Diffusion models, by design, distribute probability mass across the action space, which can make it challenging to ensure strict satisfaction of physical constraints. This probabilistic nature means that even a well-trained policy might generate actions that fall outside safe regions due to noise or stochasticity. The motivation here is to develop a mechanism that can precisely steer the policy distribution to concentrate within feasible regions.

2. **Overcoming Data Accessibility Limitations in Post-Training:** Traditional fine-tuning methods for safety often rely on collecting additional safe demonstration data or optimizing with task-specific reward functions. However, in a post-training scenario, access to the original demonstration data might be restricted, and defining dense, reliable reward functions for safety can be difficult or costly. This necessitates an approach that can operate effectively using only self-generated trajectories, thereby eliminating dependencies on external demonstrations or complex reward engineering.

3. **Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting and Preserving Task Competence:** Aggressively enforcing safety constraints can lead to catastrophic forgetting, where the policy loses previously learned task-critical behaviors. This trade-off between safety and performance is particularly challenging in post-training alignment because the absence of task utility functions means there is no explicit signal to prevent or correct the loss of task competence. The motivation is to design a training mechanism that can progressively integrate safety constraints, control the magnitude of policy shifts, and ensure monotonic improvement in safety without sacrificing the policy's ability to complete its primary task.

To address these motivations, the authors formulate safety alignment as a regularized projection problem within a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). The objective is to find the closest safety-compliant policy to the original pretrained policy, measured by KL divergence, while satisfying a set of physical constraints. This framing seeks to reconcile the need for safety with the desire to preserve the acquired capabilities of the expressive diffusion policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Physical safety Alignment for Constrained Trajectories (PACT), is a self-evolving post-training method. It aligns diffusion policies with physical safety constraints without needing demonstration data or task rewards during the alignment phase.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The environment is modeled as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) with a set of safety cost functions $C = \{c_k(s, a)\}_{k=1}^m$. The objective is framed as finding a policy $\pi$ within the safe region $\Pi_{safe}$ that minimizes the KL divergence to the pretrained base policy $\mu_\phi$:
$\arg \min_{\pi \in \Pi_{safe}} E_{s \sim d_\pi} [D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot | s) \parallel \mu_\phi(\cdot | s))]$.
This problem is transformed into an unconstrained Lagrangian objective by introducing Lagrange multipliers $\lambda_k \ge 0$ for each constraint:
$E_{(s,a) \sim d_\pi} [D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot | s) \parallel \mu_\phi(\cdot | s)) + \sum_{k=1}^m \lambda_k (c_k(s, a) - d_k)]$.
For fixed Lagrange multipliers, the optimal policy $\pi^*(\cdot | s)$ has a closed-form structure, representing an exponential tilting of the base policy by the safety costs: $\pi^*(a | s) \propto \mu_\phi(a | s) \exp(-\sum_{k=1}^m \lambda_k c_k(s, a))$.

#### 3.2. Physical Safety Alignment for Diffusion Policies: Constraint Distillation

Directly sampling from $\pi^*(a | s)$ is challenging because its normalized density is intractable and requires evaluating cost functions that often depend on privileged environment states. To address this, PACT introduces **constraint distillation**.
The score function of the optimal policy, defined as the **implicit safety teacher** $\epsilon^*(a_t, s, t)$, is directly computable as:
$\epsilon^*(a_t, s, t) \triangleq \epsilon_\phi(a_t, s, t) - \sum_{k=1}^m \lambda_k \nabla_{a_t} c_{k,t}(s, a_t) / \sigma_t$.
Here, $\epsilon_\phi$ is the score of the base policy, and $c_{k,t}(\cdot)$ are intermediate costs derived from the original cost function.
PACT distills this implicit teacher into a student diffusion policy $\epsilon_\theta$ by minimizing a reverse-KL objective in the form of a squared error:
$\min_\theta E_{t,\epsilon,(s,a)\sim d_{\pi_\theta}} \parallel \epsilon_\theta(a_t, s, t) - \epsilon^*(a_t, s, t) \parallel^2$.
This objective provides dense, continuous supervision throughout the diffusion process, guiding the policy towards the feasible region without relying on sparse rewards. Theoretically, with unlimited model capacity and sufficient data, the optimal solution for this distillation objective recovers the same Boltzmann-tilted policy as the Lagrangian objective (Theorem 3.1).

#### 3.3. Curriculum Distillation to Mitigate Irreversible Out-of-Distribution Collapse

To prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain task competence, PACT incorporates a **curriculum-based distillation strategy**. Instead of immediately enforcing full safety constraints, the strength of the constraints is progressively increased over $N$ iterations using a monotonically increasing schedule $\eta_0, \ldots, \eta_N \in$, where $\eta_0=0$ and $\eta_N=1$.
At each iteration $i$, an intermediate objective is minimized, where the policy $\pi_{old}$ from the previous iteration serves as the reference:
$\min_\pi E_{(s,a)\sim d_\pi} [D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot | s) \parallel \pi_{old}(\cdot | s)) + \sum_{k=1}^m \Delta\eta_i \cdot \lambda_k (c_k(s, a) - d_k)]$.
The corresponding distillation objective involves a **Curriculum Implicit Safety Teacher** $\epsilon^+_i(a_t, s, t)$, which smoothly interpolates towards the final optimal teacher.
This curriculum strategy provides theoretical guarantees of **monotonic improvement** in the expected Lagrangian cost and a **controlled policy shift**, bounding the KL divergence between successive policies. This prevents abrupt distributional shifts and the policy from collapsing into unproductive regions.

#### 3.4. Practical Implementations

- **Training-free Approximation for Intermediate Cost Gradient:** The exact gradient of intermediate costs is complex to compute. PACT uses a training-free approximation for $\nabla_{a_t} c_{k,t}(s, a_t)$. For small $t$, it approximates the gradient at $a_t$ by evaluating it at the posterior mean of the clean action $a_{0|t}$. This approximation, related to Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS), is directly computable if cost functions are differentiable and restricts constraint injection to the final few denoising steps ($t_c = 0.03$ in experiments), significantly reducing computational overhead.
- **Self-Evolving Optimization:** The alignment process is self-evolving. The policy is iteratively refined by collecting self-generated rollouts, computing the implicit safety teacher's gradients, and distilling these gradients into the current policy. This cycle continues over multiple iterations, progressively tightening the constraints according to the curriculum schedule.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PACT was conducted across simulated and real-world embodied manipulation benchmarks, assessing its impact on both task success and physical safety.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness in Simulation

PACT consistently improved both task completion and manipulation safety across various pretrained diffusion-based policies, including Diffusion Policy (DP), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), RDT-1B, and $\pi_{0.5}$.
- On average, PACT reduced safety violations by 31.0% and improved task success rates by 30.7%.
- Improvements were particularly notable in precision-critical tasks (e.g., Pour Water and Stack Blocks), where minor errors often lead to safety violations and task failure.
- The aligned behaviors demonstrated increased effectiveness and physical compliance, reconciling the safety-performance trade-off.

#### 4.2. Comparison to On-Policy Baselines

PACT was compared against representative on-policy baselines, including imitation learning methods (Online Probe, Learn, Distill - PLD$_{online}$) and reinforcement learning methods (PPO, DIPO).
- PACT achieved the highest Success Rate and Safe Rate across the evaluated tasks, demonstrating superior constraint satisfaction without sacrificing task performance.
- PACT exhibited higher training stability and efficiency, converging over 5x faster than RL-based methods. This was attributed to its use of direct and dense supervision from differentiable safety cost gradients, contrasting with the high-variance, coarse trajectory-level signals and costly likelihood estimations of RL methods.
- Several other on-policy alternatives (AWR, RFT, QSM) experienced training instability and catastrophic forgetting, collapsing to near-zero success rates in the experimental setup.

#### 4.3. Comparison to Off-Policy Baselines

The framework was also benchmarked against off-policy alternatives, including imitation-based approaches (Guided Rollouts, PLD) and an RL-based method (iDQL).
- PACT generally attained higher success and safety rates than these off-policy methods, with substantial gains observed on tasks like Pick Dual Bottles and Pour Water.
- Offline distillation variants of PACT, while effective, underperformed the on-policy iterative update scheme, emphasizing the role of the reverse-KL objective in mitigating forgetting under utility-free conditions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Curriculum Distillation:** Direct distillation without the curriculum schedule resulted in significant performance drops, consistent with catastrophic forgetting, while curriculum-based enforcement yielded smoother, monotonically improving dynamics.
- **Lagrange Multipliers ($\lambda$):** A trade-off was observed; excessively large $\lambda$ led to policy collapse, similar to direct distillation, while overly small $\lambda$ resulted in limited improvements. Optimal performance was achieved within a practical multiplier range.
- **Maximum Diffusion Time for Cost Guidance ($t_c$):** Injecting constraint guidance only during a small number of late diffusion steps (e.g., $t_c = 0.03$) proved effective and efficient. Increasing $t_c$ often degraded performance and incurred additional computation, supporting the design choice of few-step constraint distillation.
- **Sensitivity to Rollouts and Update-to-Data (UTD) Ratios:** PACT demonstrated relatively stable performance across a wide range of rollout counts and UTD ratios, suggesting robustness to data regimes and efficient utilization of collected data, in contrast to interaction-intensive RL methods.
- **Robustness to Noisy Privileged State Information:** PACT maintained effectiveness under moderate levels of noise injected into the privileged state information used for safety supervision (up to 12% noise magnitude), indicating it does not require perfect perception.

#### 4.5. Real-World Evaluation

PACT was evaluated on safety-critical real-world manipulation tasks: Pour Water, Nail Insertion, Transfer Egg, and GPU Assembly, using a scaled-down RDT variant as the base policy.
- The framework substantially improved the physical Safe Rate by an average of 36.0% and the average task progress by 36.8% across these tasks.
- Qualitative observations confirmed that PACT induced fine-grained, constraint-driven corrections, enabling the robot to avoid hazardous interactions (e.g., preventing poking, correcting alignment). These results suggest the practicality of PACT for deploying diffusion-based policies in safety-critical robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces PACT, a post-training framework that addresses the critical challenge of ensuring physical safety for diffusion-based robotic manipulation policies.

**Methodological Contributions:**
- **Novel Post-Training Alignment:** PACT is presented as the first post-training framework specifically for aligning diffusion-based manipulation policies with physical safety constraints. This approach operates without requiring manual data collection or test-time guardrails, offering a distinct paradigm from existing train-time or test-time safety mechanisms.
- **Constraint Distillation Framework:** The core of PACT involves distilling constraint gradients into the diffusion model via a reverse-KL objective. This method provides dense and stable supervision signals directly from differentiable safety costs, which contrasts with the typically sparse and high-variance reward signals used in reinforcement learning.
- **Curriculum-Based Updates:** The integration of a curriculum that progressively tightens safety constraints is a key innovation. This mechanism, coupled with theoretical guarantees for bounded policy shift and monotonic improvement, effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, a common challenge when enforcing new constraints on pretrained models without access to task utility functions.

**Practical Significance:**
- **Enhanced Safety and Performance:** Experimental results, both in simulation and real-world scenarios, demonstrate that PACT consistently reduces safety violations while simultaneously improving task success rates. This dual improvement suggests that PACT can facilitate the deployment of expressive diffusion policies in safety-critical applications by making them more reliable and compliant with physical constraints.
- **Improved Training Efficiency:** Compared to traditional RL-based safety alignment methods, PACT exhibits higher training efficiency due to its direct and dense gradient supervision, requiring fewer interactions to achieve comparable or superior performance.
- **Scalability and Applicability:** By operating post-training and relying on self-generated rollouts and differentiable cost functions, PACT reduces the reliance on extensive human expertise, specialized data collection, or additional test-time hardware, making it a more scalable and practical solution for diverse embodied manipulation tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
The development of PACT could contribute to safer and more reliable robotic systems in various domains, including manufacturing, healthcare (e.g., assistive manipulation, surgery), and logistics, where human-robot interaction and precision are paramount. By reducing hazardous behaviors, PACT might enable wider adoption of advanced manipulation capabilities in environments requiring strict safety protocols.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors identify several areas for future development:
- **Perception Reliance:** The quality of safety alignment is intrinsically linked to the reliability of upstream perception and vision models. Inaccuracies in these models, especially under challenging visual conditions, could propagate errors into the safety supervision signals.
- **Computational Overhead:** Although PACT avoids deployment-time overhead, the self-evolving post-training process still requires iterative rollout collection and computation for policy updates.
- **Scope of Constraints:** The current formulation primarily focuses on geometric and kinematic safety constraints. Extending the framework to handle high-frequency dynamic interactions and force-sensitive contact behaviors represents an important direction for future research.

The paper acknowledges the potential dual-use risks associated with stronger manipulation policies and advocates for responsible use, emphasizing that alignment is a risk-reduction mechanism rather than a formal safety guarantee.
