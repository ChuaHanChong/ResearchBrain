# Embodied GPT-5.1: Evidence of a World Model?

> [!summary] Summary
> An investigation explored the ability of a disembodied large multimodal language model (GPT-5.1) to control a physical robot and generate behaviors consistent with a world model. The model demonstrated emergent spatial reasoning and understanding of physical interactions from visual input alone, though with some inefficiencies and perceptual limitations.

## Problem

- Cognitive science and robotics frameworks often assert that physical embodiment and sensorimotor experience are essential for developing intelligence and physical understanding.
- Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) integrated with robotics typically function as text-to-action translators, often lacking first-person vision or deep embodied grounding for direct physical control.
- Bridging the gap between high-level semantic reasoning in MLLMs and the physics-awareness of traditional World Models is a core challenge in Embodied AI.

## Method

- A ==closed-loop architecture== was established, integrating **GPT-5.1** as a high-level controller for a physical mobile robot.
- The MLLM received only real-time, low-resolution first-person visual input and could issue commands from a constrained ==discrete action set== (advance, reverse, rotate).
- An accumulated ==short-term memory==, comprising all previous images and the MLLM's reasoning traces, was provided to maintain situational awareness across decision cycles.

## Results

- GPT-5.1 consistently navigated towards targets and demonstrated spatial awareness by reversing when detecting obstacles, successfully completing **4 out of 5** 'touch' missions and **4 out of 5** exploration tasks.
- The model exhibited short-term memory and spatial inference, correctly inferring off-frame object locations after self-movement and commanding turns to re-center targets.
- In a 'knock-down' task, GPT-5.1 executed coherent sequences (collision, reverse for verification, repeat action) suggesting an emergent understanding of physical contact and its consequences, achieving **two full successes out of five trials**.
- Limitations included suboptimal alignment strategies, often resulting in spiral approaches, and occasional perceptual errors, such as misidentifying distant objects when the primary target was out of view.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs, even when trained exclusively on disembodied text and images, can exhibit emergent spatial reasoning and understanding of physical interactions when given control of a physical robot.
> - A substantial amount of 'world knowledge' or 'physical intuition' might be implicitly learned from vast multimodal pre-training datasets, potentially challenging existing theories on the necessity of direct embodied experience.
> - The observed behaviors suggest that general-purpose MLLMs can develop internal representations consistent with aspects of a world model, enabling predictions about action consequences without explicit embodiment-related training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the intersection of cognitive science, robotics, and artificial intelligence, particularly focusing on the capabilities and limitations of large multimodal language models (MLLMs) in embodied settings.

Historically, theoretical frameworks in cognitive science and robotics have largely posited that intelligence is fundamentally embodied. Concepts such as grounded cognition (cognitive processes depend on sensorimotor interaction), behavior-based robotics (the world serves as its own best model), and sensorimotor theories of perception all emphasize the crucial role of physical interaction and sensorimotor experience in developing intelligent behavior and understanding the real world. Harnad's symbol grounding problem further articulates the challenge of imbuing symbolic systems with intrinsic meaning without sensorimotor referents.

Recent advancements have seen significant efforts to integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotic systems. Previous approaches have explored hierarchical architectures like PaLM-SayCan for high-level planning, vision-language systems such as PaLM-E and EmbodiedGPT for generating goal-directed action sequences from visual input and instructions, and perception-to-text pipelines like LangNav and RobotGPT for generating policies. Some studies have utilized general-purpose LLMs (e.g., GPT-4-Turbo) for interpreting spoken commands and translating them into low-level robot motion primitives. However, the authors note that many of these previous efforts often operate with cloud-based LLMs that function primarily as text-to-action translators, lacking onboard first-person vision or deep embodied visual grounding. Furthermore, offline LLMs have exhibited unreliability for direct physical control, indicating a gap in grounding generic models in real-world execution.

A related concept in modern machine learning for robotics is the "World Model" (WM), which involves learning a compressed spatial and temporal representation of the environment to predict action consequences. While MLLMs like GPT-5.1 excel at semantic reasoning, they typically lack intrinsic understanding of physical constraints and environmental feedback. Conversely, traditional WMs provide physics-awareness and internal representations but struggle with semantic reasoning. The core challenge in Embodied AI (EAI) is to bridge this gap between high-level semantics and grounded physical interaction.

This research directly addresses a critical unanswered question within this landscape: Can an embodied MLLM, specifically GPT-5.1, which has not undergone explicit training on physical simulation or sensorimotor experience, develop the emergent capabilities required for a functional world model? By placing a disembodied, pre-trained MLLM into direct control of a physical robot with only visual input and a discrete action set, the study aims to probe whether an internal representation of the physical environment and the ability to predict action consequences can arise without prior embodied training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this exploratory study was to investigate whether large multimodal language models (MLLMs), specifically GPT-5.1, trained exclusively on text and images—without any prior physical embodiment, training in simulated environments, or exposure to sensorimotor experience—could effectively serve as a high-level controller for a physical mobile robot in the real world.

More specifically, the study aimed to evaluate several key capabilities:
1. **Spatial Orientation and Navigation:** Determine if the model could use first-person visual input to orient itself within an environment and execute navigation tasks, such as approaching a target object.
2. **Task-Directed Physical Behavior:** Assess the model's ability to perform goal-directed actions in the physical world, such as locating and physically contacting a specific target toy.
3. **Emergence of Proprioceptive Awareness:** Explore whether a disembodied model, when given control of a physical body for the first time, could develop an awareness akin to proprioception—an understanding of its own body's position and movement in space.
4. **Evidence of an Internal World Model:** Investigate whether the interactions between the MLLM and the physical robot would reveal the emergence of an internal world model. This implies an inner representation encoding the structure of the physical environment and enabling the model to generate accurate predictions about the consequences of its own actions.

The motivation for these objectives stems from a fundamental challenge to prevailing views in cognitive science and robotics. These fields often assert that a physical body and direct sensorimotor interaction are necessary prerequisites for developing sophisticated forms of intelligence, spatial reasoning, and physical understanding. If an MLLM, devoid of such embodied training, could demonstrate these capabilities, it would suggest that a substantial amount of "world knowledge" or "physical intuition" might be implicitly encoded within its vast pre-training data, potentially challenging established theoretical frameworks. The study seeks to provide empirical evidence to inform this debate and stimulate further research into the origins and nature of physical understanding in artificial intelligence systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study employed a closed-loop architecture integrating a physical mobile robot with the GPT-5.1 multimodal large language model, operating as the high-level controller.

**Robot Platform:**
The experimental platform was a differential-drive mobile robot, previously described in. Key features included:
- A two-wheel differential drive base for locomotion.
- An onboard battery supply for autonomous operation.
- A microcontroller for low-level motor actuation.
- A forward-facing camera module, providing real-time first-person visual input to the MLLM. Basic odometry data was also available. Figure 1 illustrates the robot used.

**System Architecture and Interaction Protocol:**
The interaction between the robot and GPT-5.1 followed an iterative closed-loop process (Figure 2):
1. **Initialization:** The LLM was configured with a specific role via a system prompt ("You are piloting a small mobile robot..."). A mission prompt then defined the specific task (e.g., "locating a specific object," such as a toy penguin).
2. **Perception:** At the beginning of each control cycle, the robot's onboard camera captured a low-resolution (960x576), compressed (JPEG 85), and potentially noisy first-person image of its environment.
3. **Transmission to LLM:** This captured image, along with the current mission context, was transmitted to GPT-5.1.
4. **LLM Processing and Command Generation:** GPT-5.1 analyzed the visual scene, generated a short textual reasoning trace to explain its decision-making, and then outputted one command selected from a constrained discrete action set:
 - `advance for t seconds`
 - `reverse for t seconds`
 - `rotate by θ degrees`
5. **Action Execution:** The robot executed the commanded motion.
6. **Memory and Context Update:** After the motion was completed, a new image was captured. This new image, along with *all* previous images and their corresponding reasoning traces, formed an accumulated short-term memory, which was then provided to the LLM for the subsequent decision cycle. This expanded context was crucial for maintaining situational awareness.
7. **Loop Termination:** The perception–action loop continued until GPT-5.1 explicitly declared "MISSION ACCOMPLISHED," at which point the trial terminated.

**Multimodal Model Utilized:**
The study exclusively used GPT-5.1, identified as one of the most advanced publicly available reasoning-capable multimodal language models at the time of experimentation. Crucially, this model was trained solely on text and images, without any specific training data related to physical embodiment, robotic control, or simulated environments.

**Experimental Tasks:**
The experiments involved a series of progressively challenging tasks:
- **Target Acquisition (Initial Test):** The robot was instructed to locate and physically touch a specific plush penguin. In initial scenarios, the penguin was within the robot's initial field of view.
- **Exploration and Navigation (Increased Difficulty):** The robot was placed facing a wall, requiring it to actively navigate and explore the environment before locating the target penguin.
- **Knock-Down Task (Physical Interaction Probe):** A more advanced mission was introduced: "find the penguin and knock it down with your head (camera)." This task aimed to evaluate the model's understanding of physical interaction and consequences beyond mere contact.

**Data Collection and Analysis:**
The evaluation was primarily qualitative, relying on observations of the robot's behavior, analysis of the LLM's reasoning traces, and the success or failure of mission completion. Specific runs (e.g., Run 3, Run 2, Run 6) were detailed with image sequences and corresponding LLM reasoning to illustrate emergent behaviors and limitations. Aggregate results were also reported for success rates across trials for the "touch" and "knock-down" missions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's exploratory experiments with GPT-5.1 in an embodied setting revealed emergent capabilities suggestive of spatial reasoning and physical understanding, alongside observable limitations.

**Emergent Capabilities and World-Model-Like Behavior:**

- **Navigation and Object-Directed Behavior:** When the target (a plush penguin) was initially visible, GPT-5.1 consistently identified it and issued movement commands that led to progress toward the object. Despite inefficiencies, the model typically managed to close distance and complete the "touch" mission (4 out of 5 successes).
- **Spatial Awareness and Obstacle Avoidance:** In tasks requiring exploration, GPT-5.1 generally succeeded in maneuvering (4 out of 5 instances when starting facing a wall). It demonstrated a form of spatial awareness by reversing when it detected proximity to obstacles, as confirmed by its reasoning traces. This suggests an understanding of its own body's position relative to environmental elements.
- **Short-Term Memory and Spatial Inference:** A significant finding was the model's ability to maintain short-term memory of object locations and perform spatial inference. In one instance (Run 2, Figure 7), after the robot advanced and the penguin left the camera's field of view, GPT-5.1 correctly inferred that the object was now off-frame to its left. It then commanded a turn to re-center the penguin, indicating an accurate understanding of how its own movement changed the relative spatial position of the target. This required tracking relative spatial changes and causal inference based on static images and accumulated context.
- **Understanding of Physical Interaction (Knock-Down Task):** In the "knock-down" mission, GPT-5.1 demonstrated a more complex understanding of physical interaction. After successfully colliding with the penguin (Figure 12), the model's reasoning indicated it inferred physical contact. Crucially, it then commanded the robot to reverse (Figure 13) to gain space and visually confirm if the penguin had toppled (Figure 14). When the penguin was still upright, it initiated another strike (Figure 15) and subsequently reversed again to reassess (Figure 16). This coherent action sequence—collision, visual verification, repeated action if needed, and re-verification—suggests an emergent grasp of physical presence, contact, and the consequences of its actions, despite lacking tactile sensors.

**Limitations and Inefficiencies:**

- **Suboptimal Alignment Strategy:** GPT-5.1 frequently exhibited an inefficient alignment strategy. Rather than aligning perfectly with the target before advancing, it often moved forward even when the penguin appeared off-center, then made small corrective rotations, and then advanced again, often resulting in a spiral approach pattern (Run 3, Figures 3-5). This suggests imprecise alignment control.
- **Perceptual Limitations and Misidentification:** The model occasionally displayed perceptual limitations. In Run 2, after the primary target penguin left the frame, the model mistakenly identified a distant, unrelated plush toy in the background as the target (Figure 10). This error raises questions about the model's ability to maintain object identity across frames, understand object permanence, or ignore distractors, especially when the primary target is no longer visible. The authors considered whether the model lacked the understanding that the original penguin could not have moved, or if it simply abandoned the earlier target for a new one.
- **Task Failures:** The robot experienced some failures:
 - In the "touch" mission, one failure occurred when the robot became stuck against a table leg, highlighting the absence of tactile feedback.
 - In the "knock-down" mission, out of five trials, there were two full successes, one partial outcome (located but failed to topple), and two failures (model did not find the penguin during navigation).

Overall, despite working with low-resolution, compressed, and sometimes noisy visual input, and operating from static frames one per action cycle, GPT-5.1 exhibited a degree of emergent spatial and physical understanding beyond its training regime. This behavior is consistent with elements of a world model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This exploratory study's findings carry significant implications for the fields of cognitive science, robotics, and artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the nature of intelligence and the development of embodied agents.

Firstly, the results directly challenge long-standing theoretical frameworks in cognitive science and robotics that emphasize the **necessity of a physical body and sensorimotor experience** for the development of spatial reasoning, physical understanding, and truly grounded intelligence. The demonstration that GPT-5.1, a model trained exclusively on disembodied text and images, could exhibit emergent capabilities like inferring object positions after self-movement, reasoning about physical contact, and executing coherent action sequences for manipulation (e.g., reversing to verify a knock-down) suggests that a substantial amount of "world knowledge" or "physical intuition" may be implicitly learned from vast datasets of human-generated multimodal information. This implies that such models may construct internal representations consistent with aspects of a world model without direct, explicit training in physical environments or simulations.

Secondly, the study provides **compelling evidence for the emergence of world-model-like behavior** in large multimodal language models, even in the absence of specialized embodiment-related training. The ability to predict the consequences of one's own actions (e.g., how moving forward changes an object's position relative to the camera, or how a collision might alter a target's state) is a core characteristic of a world model. The observed behaviors suggest that GPT-5.1 internally models aspects of its interaction with the physical environment, albeit imperfectly. This opens new avenues for understanding how complex cognitive capabilities might arise from generalized pre-training on diverse data rather than domain-specific instruction.

Thirdly, the work has **potential impact on the development of Embodied AI systems**. If MLLMs can acquire a rudimentary understanding of the physical world through their pre-training, they could serve as more robust and flexible high-level controllers for robots than previously thought. This could simplify the training process for embodied agents, reducing the need for extensive real-world interaction or complex simulated environments during the initial learning phases. Future embodied AI systems might leverage such pre-trained models to accelerate learning and adapt to novel tasks and environments more efficiently.

Finally, the study's identified limitations, such as inefficient navigation strategies and occasional perceptual errors, provide crucial directions for future research. Characterizing the **boundaries, robustness, and generality** of these emergent capabilities is essential. Investigating how to overcome these limitations, perhaps through fine-tuning with targeted embodied experiences, integrating more sophisticated perceptual modules, or enhancing internal representations for object permanence and spatial stability, represents a significant area for advancement. This research motivates a deeper investigation into how these models acquire and represent physical understanding and what this means for the design of truly intelligent, physically interactive AI.
