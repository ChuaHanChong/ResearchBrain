# DriveWorld: 4D Pre-trained Scene Understanding via World Models for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> DriveWorld is a world-model-based 4D pre-training framework for vision-centric autonomous driving that learns spatio-temporal BEV representations from multi-camera video via a Memory State-Space Model. Pre-trained on OpenScene, it lifts detection mAP +7.5%, tracking AMOTA +5.0%, mapping IoU +3.0%, and cuts planning L2 error by 0.34m over UniAD.

## Problem

- Vision-centric pre-training relies on 2D pre-text tasks like depth estimation, ignoring temporal dynamics needed for spatio-temporal driving scene understanding.
- 3D occupancy pre-training methods reconstruct only spatial structure per frame, overlooking the 4D nature of driving scenes across time.
- Prior world-model approaches for driving learn BEV segmentation or generative video without addressing aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty jointly for representation pre-training.

## Method

- **DriveWorld**'s ==Memory State-Space Model (MSSM)== splits spatio-temporal modelling into a ==Dynamic Memory Bank== for temporal-aware latent dynamics and a ==Static Scene Propagation== module for spatial-aware latent statics.
- Dynamic Memory Bank uses ==motion-aware layer normalization== with velocity and time-interval affine parameters plus ==cross-attention== over a growing memory bank of past histories.
- ==Task Prompt== conditions the shared encoder per downstream task using text-encoder embeddings (e.g. BERT/CLIP) injected via ==AdaptiveInstanceNorm== to decouple task-aware features.

## Results

- On OpenScene pre-training, DriveWorld raises 3D detection mAP by **7.5%** and NDS by **6.8%** over BEVFormer with ImageNet pre-training, beating OccNet, UniScene, BEVDistill baselines.
- Ablations show Dynamic Memory Bank plus motion-aware layer normalization and Task Prompt jointly push detection mAP from **0.416** to **0.436** versus RSSM baseline dropping to **0.381**.
- Fine-tuning with only **75%** of downstream data after full pre-training still matches the **100%** pre-train/**100%** fine-tune baseline, showing a **25%** annotation-cost reduction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating temporal-dynamic and static-spatial information into distinct pathways avoids the context loss that plagues single 1D-latent recurrent state-space models.
> - 4D (spatio-temporal) pre-training targets transfer more strongly to future-dependent tasks like tracking and forecasting than to purely current-frame tasks like detection.
> - Occupancy is a richer pre-training target than depth or BEV segmentation because it encodes full 3D structure and height, not just surfaces or ground-plane classes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Autonomous driving represents one of the most challenging applications of artificial intelligence, requiring vehicles to understand and navigate complex, dynamic environments in real-time. While traditional approaches have relied heavily on expensive LiDAR sensors, the industry is increasingly moving toward vision-centric systems that use multiple cameras as the primary sensor modality. However, a fundamental challenge remains: how to effectively learn representations from visual data that capture both the spatial structure of driving scenes and their temporal evolution.

Current pre-training methods for autonomous driving fall into two main categories: 2D approaches that treat driving as a series of independent image understanding tasks, and 3D approaches that incorporate spatial structure but largely ignore temporal dynamics. DriveWorld introduces a fundamentally different paradigm by treating autonomous driving as an inherently 4D problem - one that requires understanding not just where objects are in 3D space, but how they move and evolve over time.

### 2. The Challenge of 4D Scene Understanding

The core insight behind DriveWorld is that autonomous driving is fundamentally a spatio-temporal reasoning task. Consider a vehicle approaching an intersection: understanding the current positions of other vehicles, pedestrians, and traffic signals is important, but predicting their future states and planning accordingly is what enables safe navigation. This temporal dimension has been largely overlooked by existing pre-training methods.

Traditional approaches face several limitations:

**2D Pre-training methods** like ImageNet pre-training treat each camera frame independently, missing crucial temporal relationships and 3D spatial structure. While these methods work well for general computer vision tasks, they fail to capture the continuous, dynamic nature of driving scenarios.

**3D Pre-training methods** improve upon 2D approaches by incorporating spatial structure through techniques like occupancy prediction or knowledge distillation from LiDAR data. However, they typically treat each timestep as an independent 3D scene, missing the temporal correlations that are essential for understanding object motion and predicting future states.

**4D Pre-training**, as introduced by DriveWorld, explicitly models the temporal evolution of 3D scenes. This approach recognizes that autonomous driving requires understanding not just the current state of the environment, but also how that state is changing and what it will become in the future.

### 3. DriveWorld Architecture

DriveWorld implements a world model-based framework that learns to predict future states of the driving environment from multi-camera observations. The architecture consists of several key components working together to create a comprehensive 4D representation.

The framework takes as input a sequence of multi-view camera observations and corresponding actions, then learns to predict both current and future 3D occupancy structures as well as driving actions. The key innovation lies in the Memory State-Space Model (MSSM), which serves as the core component for spatio-temporal reasoning.

The overall pipeline begins with standard image encoding and 2D-to-3D view transformation to create Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) features. These features then pass through the MSSM, which maintains both deterministic history and stochastic state representations. The system learns to predict future states through a variational approach, optimizing the alignment between predicted and observed states.

### 4. Memory State-Space Model

The Memory State-Space Model represents the core innovation of DriveWorld, designed to handle the unique challenges of autonomous driving scenarios. It addresses two fundamental types of uncertainty that arise in real-world driving:

**Aleatoric uncertainty** stems from the inherently stochastic nature of the world - for example, a pedestrian might suddenly change direction, or a vehicle might brake unexpectedly. This type of uncertainty cannot be reduced by gathering more information; it's an inherent property of the environment.

**Epistemic uncertainty** arises from incomplete knowledge or information - objects might be occluded, sensors might have limited range, or certain parts of the scene might be temporarily invisible. This type of uncertainty can potentially be reduced by gathering more information or improving sensor capabilities.

The MSSM addresses these challenges through two key components:

**Dynamic Memory Bank (DMB)** handles temporal-aware latent dynamics and addresses aleatoric uncertainty. It maintains a memory of past states and incorporates motion parameters (velocity, time intervals) through Motion-aware Layer Normalization (MLN). This allows the model to learn how objects typically move and behave over time, accounting for the natural variability in dynamic scenarios.

**Static Scene Propagation (SSP)** focuses on spatial-aware latent statics and mitigates epistemic uncertainty. It recognizes that much of a driving scene remains static (roads, buildings, traffic signs) and maintains a separate representation for these stable elements. By separating static and dynamic components, the model can maintain comprehensive scene context even when parts of the environment are temporarily occluded or out of view.

The mathematical foundation of MSSM follows a probabilistic approach similar to Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSM). At each timestep t, the model maintains:

- A deterministic history state h_t that captures accumulated temporal information
- A stochastic state s_t that represents the current scene dynamics
- A static scene representation b̂ that provides stable spatial context

The training objective minimizes a variational lower bound combining KL divergence between posterior and prior state distributions with reconstruction losses for both 3D occupancy and action predictions.

### 5. Task Prompt Mechanism

One of the key challenges in developing foundation models for autonomous driving is adapting a single pre-trained representation to serve multiple downstream tasks effectively. DriveWorld addresses this through its Task Prompt mechanism, which allows the same pre-trained features to be adaptively specialized for different tasks.

The Task Prompt mechanism works by encoding natural language descriptions of downstream tasks using pre-trained language models like BERT or CLIP. For example, task descriptions might include "The task is for 3D object detection of the current scene" or "The task is for motion forecasting of dynamic objects." These descriptions are then converted into prompt encodings that are integrated with the learned spatio-temporal BEV features through adaptive normalization and convolutional layers.

This approach provides several advantages:

1. **Unified Representation**: A single pre-trained model can serve multiple downstream tasks without requiring separate specialized models.

2. **Task-Aware Feature Extraction**: The prompt mechanism allows the network to emphasize different aspects of the learned representation depending on the specific requirements of each task.

3. **Improved Performance**: Experimental results show consistent improvements across various tasks when using the Task Prompt mechanism compared to generic feature extraction.

### 6. Experimental Results

DriveWorld was evaluated across six critical autonomous driving tasks: 3D object detection, online mapping, multi-object tracking, motion forecasting, occupancy prediction, and planning. The experiments used nuScenes for fine-tuning and validation, with pre-training performed on both nuScenes and the larger OpenScene dataset.

The results demonstrate significant improvements across all evaluated tasks:

**3D Object Detection** showed a 7.5% increase in mAP and 6.8% increase in NDS compared to BEVFormer with ImageNet pre-training. This improvement is particularly notable because object detection is often considered a solved problem, yet DriveWorld's 4D understanding provides substantial gains.

**Multi-Object Tracking** exhibited the most dramatic improvements, with a 5.3% increase in AMOTA and the lowest ID switch scores among all compared methods. This result directly validates the benefit of temporal modeling - tracking inherently requires understanding how objects move over time.

**Motion Forecasting** achieved a 0.1m decrease in minimum Average Displacement Error (minADE), significantly outperforming other pre-training methods. This demonstrates DriveWorld's superior ability to predict future states, a core capability enabled by its world model approach.

**Planning** established new state-of-the-art results with an average 0.34m reduction in L2 error and 0.12 reduction in collision rate. This is particularly significant because planning represents the ultimate goal of autonomous driving systems - the improved perception and prediction capabilities directly translate to better decision-making.

The experiments also revealed important insights about data efficiency. DriveWorld achieved comparable performance when fine-tuned on only 75% of the available data, suggesting that the rich 4D representations learned during pre-training can significantly reduce annotation requirements for downstream tasks.

### 7. Significance and Impact

DriveWorld represents a significant advancement in autonomous driving research with implications extending beyond the immediate technical contributions:

**Paradigm Shift to 4D Understanding**: By explicitly modeling the temporal dimension, DriveWorld moves the field beyond static scene understanding toward dynamic, predictive models that better match the requirements of real-world autonomous driving.

**Foundation Model for Autonomous Driving**: The unified representation learned by DriveWorld can serve multiple downstream tasks effectively, moving toward the vision of foundation models in autonomous driving similar to those that have transformed natural language processing and computer vision.

**Practical Cost Reduction**: The vision-centric approach reduces reliance on expensive LiDAR sensors, while the demonstrated data efficiency can significantly reduce annotation costs - both critical factors for the commercial viability of autonomous vehicles.

**Uncertainty-Aware Design**: The explicit handling of both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties through the MSSM design makes the learned representations more robust to real-world complexities and edge cases.

The work also identifies important directions for future research. Current limitations include the continued reliance on LiDAR-based ground truth for 3D occupancy labels and validation primarily on a lightweight ResNet backbone. Future work could explore fully self-supervised approaches that eliminate the need for LiDAR data and scaling to larger, more powerful architectures.

DriveWorld's consistent improvements across the full spectrum of autonomous driving tasks - from low-level perception to high-level planning - validate the fundamental hypothesis that autonomous driving benefits from explicit 4D scene understanding. As the field continues to evolve toward more capable and reliable autonomous systems, the principles and techniques introduced in DriveWorld are likely to play an increasingly important role in enabling safe, efficient, and cost-effective self-driving vehicles.
