# BranchGRPO: Stable and Efficient GRPO with Structured Branching in Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> BranchGRPO introduces a tree-structured policy optimization framework for generative diffusion and flow models, which significantly enhances human preference alignment while boosting training efficiency. The method achieved up to a 4.7x speedup in training time and improved alignment scores on HPS-v2.1 by up to 16% over existing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) variants.

## Problem

- Existing GRPO methods suffer from computational inefficiency due to sequential, independent rollouts for each generated sample, leading to O(N*T) complexity.
- Sparse terminal rewards, uniformly assigned across all denoising steps, result in unreliable credit assignment and unstable optimization for diffusion models.
- Traditional GRPO struggles to attribute outcome rewards to specific intermediate denoising steps that critically shape final output quality.

## Method

- A ==tree-structured branching rollout algorithm== replaces sequential rollouts, allowing early denoising steps to be shared across multiple diverging trajectories for computational efficiency.
- A novel ==reward fusion and depth-wise advantage estimation== method transforms sparse terminal rewards into dense, step-level signals, improving credit assignment.
- ==Structured pruning strategies== (width and depth pruning) are applied during backpropagation to selectively reduce gradient computation and accelerate training.

## Results

- BranchGRPO achieved a **4.7x** speedup in training time over DanceGRPO, with its best variant reducing per-iteration time to **148s** while increasing HPS-v2.1 alignment scores from **0.360** to **0.369**.
- Qualitatively, BranchGRPO generated images with sharper details and better semantic alignment, and produced more temporally consistent and coherent videos than baseline models.
- The method maintained high sample diversity and demonstrated a favorable scaling law, with increased branching factors and steps consistently leading to higher reward growth and better final performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Restructuring the generative process into a tree enables efficient exploration and computational amortization by sharing early denoising prefixes.
> - Propagating and normalizing aggregated rewards backward through the tree provides finer-grained, more stable, and balanced advantage signals for policy optimization.
> - Strategic pruning of gradients during backpropagation can significantly speed up training without compromising the forward rollout's exploration breadth.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of advanced generative models and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), a frontier area in artificial intelligence.

The foundation of the work lies in **diffusion and flow-matching models**, which have revolutionized image and video generation by producing high-fidelity, diverse, and controllable content (Ho et al., 2020; Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022). While these models excel in raw generation capabilities, large-scale pretraining alone often fails to fully align outputs with nuanced human preferences, which can encompass aesthetic quality, semantic accuracy, and temporal consistency.

This alignment gap is precisely where **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)** emerges as a crucial solution. Pioneered in natural language processing (NLP) to align large language models (LLMs) with human instructions (Ouyang et al., 2022; Christiano et al., 2017), RLHF has inspired similar adaptations for visual generative models. In the visual domain, RLHF has evolved along two primary directions:
1. **Reward-model-based approaches** (e.g., ImageReward, Xu et al., 2023), which train a separate model to predict human preferences and then use its feedback to guide the generator.
2. **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its extensions like Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) and Videodpo (Liu et al., 2025c), which achieve alignment without explicit reward modeling by directly optimizing a policy against preference data. Policy-gradient methods like DDPO (Black et al., 2023) and DPOK (Fan et al., 2023) also explore online optimization but often face stability challenges.

More recently, **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** (Shao et al., 2024) has gained traction as a scalable and stable alternative to traditional PPO for preference optimization, particularly suited for high-dimensional tasks. Its application to visual generation has been spearheaded by works like DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025) and Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a), which unified diffusion and flow models under an SDE reformulation, demonstrating robust optimization for text-to-image, text-to-video, and image-to-video tasks. Subsequent efforts, such as TempFlow-GRPO (He et al., 2025), recognized and began to address the limitation of sparse terminal rewards in GRPO by introducing temporally-aware weighting. MixGRPO (Li et al., 2025a) further improved efficiency through a mixed ODE–SDE sliding-window scheme, though still grappling with trade-offs between overhead and performance.

BranchGRPO directly builds upon these advancements, particularly DanceGRPO, to tackle the remaining critical bottlenecks: **computational inefficiency** and **unreliable sparse reward attribution**. By introducing a novel tree-structured rollout and dense reward propagation, BranchGRPO positions itself as a significant evolutionary step within the GRPO-for-vision subfield. It draws conceptual parallels with tree-structured rollouts seen in LLM optimization (e.g., TreePO, Li et al., 2025b) but critically adapts these ideas to the distinct dynamics of diffusion processes. This work aims to push the boundaries of scalable and stable RLHF for generative visual models, making high-quality human-aligned content generation more feasible and efficient.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the human preference alignment of image and video generative models trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), by directly confronting and resolving two fundamental bottlenecks inherent in existing GRPO variants: inefficiency and sparse reward signals.

The authors identify these critical motivations:

1. **Addressing Computational Inefficiency:** Current GRPO variants, when applied to diffusion and flow-matching models, suffer from a sequential rollout design. This means each trajectory (i.e., each denoising process from noise to image/video) must be independently sampled under both the old and new policies. This leads to an undesirable computational complexity of O(N * T), where N is the group size and T is the number of denoising steps. Such redundancy significantly limits scalability, especially in large-scale image and video generation tasks that demand many denoising steps and diverse samples. The motivation is to reduce this computational burden while maintaining or improving exploration diversity.

2. **Overcoming Sparse and Unreliable Reward Assignment:** Existing GRPO methods typically assign a single terminal reward, obtained at the very end of the generation process, uniformly across all intermediate denoising steps. This approach fails to leverage potentially rich information from intermediate states and critically neglects the varying importance of decisions made at different stages of the denoising process. The consequence is unreliable credit assignment, where early critical decisions receive the same uniform reward signal as later, less impactful ones, leading to high-variance gradients and less stable optimization. The central motivating question is: "how can we attribute sparse outcome rewards to the specific denoising steps that truly shape final quality?"

To directly address these motivations, BranchGRPO proposes a multi-pronged approach:

- **Restructuring Rollouts with Branching:** Replace the inefficient independent sequential rollouts with a tree-structured branching process. This allows for computational amortization through shared prefixes, where early denoising steps are common across multiple diverging trajectories. This aims to improve sampling efficiency and reduce computational cost without sacrificing exploration diversity.
- **Transforming Sparse Rewards into Dense Signals:** Develop a novel reward fusion and depth-wise advantage estimation method. This mechanism is designed to aggregate leaf rewards and propagate them backward through the tree, generating finer-grained, step-level advantage signals instead of a single, uniformly propagated terminal reward. This aims for more stable optimization and accurate credit assignment.
- **Introducing Structured Pruning for Efficiency:** Implement pruning strategies that selectively reduce gradient computation during backpropagation. This aims to accelerate training by focusing computational resources on more promising or relevant parts of the trajectory space, without affecting the breadth of exploration or the forward rollout process.

By achieving these objectives, BranchGRPO seeks to establish a more stable, efficient, and scalable framework for aligning generative diffusion and flow models with human preferences, pushing the capabilities of RLHF in the visual domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BranchGRPO introduces a novel tree-structured policy optimization framework designed to address the inefficiencies and sparse reward problems in existing GRPO variants for diffusion and flow models. Its methodology is centered around three core contributions: a branching rollout scheme, an advanced reward fusion and depth-wise advantage estimation method, and complementary pruning strategies.

#### 3.1. Branch Rollout Algorithm

Unlike traditional GRPO variants that perform independent sequential rollouts, BranchGRPO reorganizes the denoising process into a tree structure.
- **Preliminaries:** The denoising process is reformulated using terminology from tree search: `depth T` (denoising steps), `width w` (completed trajectories/leaves), `branching steps B` (split timesteps), `branch correlation s` (diversity control), and `branching factor K` (children per split).
- **Branch Sampling:** For each prompt, a root node is initialized with a shared initial noise (z₀). The model then denoises step-by-step along the reverse SDE. At predefined `split steps B`, the current state stochastically expands into `K` children. These children share the early denoising prefixes but diverge afterward.
- **Stochastic Branching:** This divergence is achieved by injecting stochastic perturbations into the SDE transition (Eq. 2). The `branch correlation s` hyperparameter controls the diversity strength among child nodes. A smaller `s` leads to highly correlated (and thus stable) branches, while a larger `s` makes them nearly independent, fostering broader exploration. Crucially, each child generated this way maintains the same marginal distribution as a standard SDE step, ensuring diversity is preserved without altering the underlying generative distribution. The rollout continues until the maximum depth `T` is reached, at which point all leaf nodes (final generated samples) are collected for reward evaluation.

#### 3.2. Reward Fusion and Depth-Wise Advantage Estimation

This component transforms sparse terminal rewards into dense, step-level signals, improving credit assignment and stability.
- **Reward Fusion:** BranchGRPO employs a dynamically adjustable reward fusion scheme. For any internal node `n` in the tree, its value `¯r(n)` is computed by aggregating the rewards `r_ℓ` of its descendant leaves `L(n)`. This aggregation uses a soft weighting mechanism (Eq. 3), where weights `w_ℓ(n)` are based on the behavior policy's path log-probabilities `s_ℓ` (log p_beh(ℓ | n)) and a `β` parameter. A `β=0` defaults to uniform averaging (robust but high variance), while `β=1` weights by path probability (reduced variance, more stable).
- **Depth-wise Normalization:** To ensure balanced gradient contributions across different denoising steps, aggregated rewards are normalized within each depth `d`. The advantage `A_d(n)` for a node `n` at depth `d` is calculated by subtracting the mean `μ_d` and dividing by the standard deviation `σ_d` of all aggregated rewards at that depth (Eq. 4). This standardization prevents later denoising steps, which typically have smaller variance, from disproportionately influencing gradients. This scheme provides process-dense and balanced credit signals, which is a significant improvement over GRPO's prompt-level normalization that broadcasts a single terminal reward.
- **Policy Optimization:** The policy `θ` is updated using the standard clipped GRPO loss (Eq. 5), but applied over the tree edges `e`, which represent transitions at different depths. The advantage `A(e)` for an edge `e` is derived from its child node's depth-wise normalized reward.

#### 3.3. Pruning Strategies

To manage the potentially exponential growth of trajectories in the branching tree and accelerate training, BranchGRPO introduces two pruning strategies, applied *after* reward fusion and depth-wise normalization, and *only* during backpropagation (gradient computation), leaving forward rollouts and exploration unaffected.
- **Width Pruning:** This strategy reduces the number of leaves used for gradient updates.
 - **Parent-Top1:** Retains only the child with the highest reward from each parent at the last branching step. This roughly halves gradient computation, offering stable but slightly less diverse updates.
 - **Extreme Selection:** Preserves both the globally best and worst `b` leaves based on their reward scores. This aims to explicitly maintain strong positive and negative signals for learning, potentially enhancing exploration but with increased variance.
- **Depth Pruning:** This strategy skips gradient computation at selected timesteps. A set of `pruned depths D` is maintained, and gradients from nodes at these layers are ignored. To prevent permanently discarding signals from certain steps, a sliding window schedule is employed: the pruned depths gradually shift towards later timesteps as training progresses, ensuring all depths eventually contribute to gradients. This reallocates computation towards more promising regions of the trajectory space.

The entire BranchGRPO training process is summarized in Algorithm 1, detailing the iterative steps from sampling a batch, building a rollout tree, evaluating rewards, fusing and normalizing rewards, applying pruning, computing the GRPO loss, and updating the policy. This comprehensive methodology ensures both efficiency through shared computation and targeted learning through dense, normalized rewards and selective gradient updates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BranchGRPO demonstrates substantial improvements in efficiency, stability, and alignment quality across both image and video generation tasks compared to existing GRPO variants.

#### 4.1. Efficiency and Alignment Performance

- **Superior Alignment:** As shown in Table 1, BranchGRPO variants consistently achieve higher human preference scores across multiple metrics. BranchGRPO-DepthPruning achieved the best overall alignment, improving HPS-v2.1 from 0.360 (DanceGRPO) to **0.369**, and delivering the highest PickScore (**0.235**), ImageReward (**1.625**), and Unified Reward (**3.404**).
- **Significant Speedup:** BranchGRPO significantly reduces training time. Standard BranchGRPO reduces per-iteration time compared to DanceGRPO (tf=1.0) (493s vs. 698s). The hybrid variant, BranchGRPO-Mix, achieves the fastest training time at **148s**, representing a **4.7× speedup** over DanceGRPO, with only marginal drops in alignment quality. This makes large-scale training significantly more practical.
- **Faster and More Stable Convergence:** Figure 1 (Left) illustrates that BranchGRPO achieves faster early reward growth and smoother convergence, ultimately reaching higher final rewards compared to DanceGRPO. DanceGRPO (tf=0.6), a faster but less stable variant, suffers from pronounced instability.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4) demonstrate that BranchGRPO generates images with sharper details and better semantic alignment with the given prompts than both the base FLUX model and DanceGRPO.

#### 4.2. Preservation of Diversity

- A natural concern with shared prefixes in branching rollouts is reduced sample diversity. Section 3.1 and Figure 2 address this by showing that BranchGRPO largely preserves distributional and semantic diversity. Quantitative metrics like KID (KID=0.0057 for Inception, 0.00022 for CLIP) and MMD² (MMD²=0.0067 for Inception, 0.0149 for CLIP) indicate that the distributions of generated images from BranchGRPO and DanceGRPO are almost indistinguishable.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Figure 5, Table 3)

- **Branch Correlation (s):** A moderate branch correlation (`s=4.0`) was found to be optimal, balancing exploration with training stability, leading to the highest reward and stable convergence.
- **Branching Steps:** Early split steps (e.g., `(0, 3, 6, 9)`) were more effective for faster early reward growth, suggesting that early exploration is beneficial.
- **Branch Density:** Denser branching configurations (e.g., `(0, 3, 6, 9)`) accelerated early training without compromising stability.
- **Reward Fusion Strategies:** Path-probability weighting (β=1) consistently yielded higher and more stable rewards than uniform averaging (β=0), empirically supporting its role in improving credit assignment and convergence.
- **Pruning Strategies:** Depth pruning (with a sliding window schedule) achieved the best final reward, indicating that gradients from later denoising steps contain more redundancy that can be pruned. Width pruning (Parent-Top1) provided smoother, lower-variance updates, albeit with slightly lower final rewards.
- **Hybrid ODE–SDE:** Combining branching with a hybrid ODE–SDE schedule achieved the fastest speedups (148s) while maintaining stable and fast reward growth, significantly outperforming MixGRPO.

#### 4.4. Scaling Law with Branching

- BranchGRPO exhibits a favorable scaling law (Figure 6). Increasing the branch factor (`K`) and the number of branching steps consistently leads to higher reward growth and better final performance. This demonstrates that BranchGRPO can effectively leverage additional samples from expanded rollout trees, making large-scale scaling practical and beneficial without stability issues.

#### 4.5. Video Generation Results

- On the WanX video generation task, BranchGRPO significantly improved video quality. Compared to the base model (which suffered from temporal flickering and deformation) and DanceGRPO (which produced blurry details), BranchGRPO generated sharper, more coherent, and temporally consistent frames (Figure 7, Table 4). It achieved higher motion quality rewards and substantially improved efficiency (8 minutes per iteration vs. 20 minutes for DanceGRPO).

In summary, BranchGRPO successfully addresses the critical limitations of prior GRPO methods by providing a more efficient, stable, and high-performing framework for human preference alignment in visual generative models, validated across diverse tasks and modalities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BranchGRPO represents a significant advancement in the field of human preference alignment for generative AI, particularly for diffusion and flow-matching models. Its contributions have several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Efficiency-Quality Gap in RLHF:** The most immediate and profound impact of BranchGRPO is its ability to simultaneously improve both the efficiency and the quality of RLHF for visual generation. By reducing per-iteration training time by up to 55% and achieving up to 4.7x speedup over baselines while enhancing alignment scores by up to 16%, BranchGRPO makes large-scale preference optimization significantly more feasible. This is crucial for industries and researchers working with increasingly complex and resource-intensive generative models.
2. **Enhanced Stability and Reliability of Training:** The introduction of depth-wise reward fusion and normalization fundamentally addresses the "sparse reward" problem. By transforming a single terminal reward into dense, step-level signals, BranchGRPO ensures more accurate credit assignment, leading to more stable optimization and higher final alignment. This reliability is vital for the consistent development of high-performing AI models that meet nuanced human expectations.
3. **Scalability to Larger Models and Datasets:** The demonstrated scaling law, where larger branch factors and more branching steps consistently improve performance, indicates that BranchGRPO is well-suited for future growth. As generative models become even larger and datasets for RLHF expand, BranchGRPO's ability to efficiently handle increased computational demands through its tree-structured rollouts and pruning strategies will be invaluable.
4. **Generalizability Across Modalities:** The success of BranchGRPO on both text-to-image and image-to-video generation tasks highlights its broad applicability. This suggests that the core principles of structured branching and dense reward propagation can be effectively transferred across different visual modalities, potentially unifying RLHF approaches for a wider range of generative tasks.
5. **New Paradigm for Policy Optimization:** BranchGRPO establishes structured branching as a practical and scalable paradigm for RLHF in diffusion and flow models. This innovation moves beyond traditional sequential rollouts, opening new avenues for designing more efficient and robust policy optimization algorithms in generative AI. It draws inspiration from tree-search methods and adapts them effectively to the continuous and stochastic nature of diffusion processes.
6. **Paving the Way for Advanced Applications:**
 - **Dynamic and Adaptive Policies:** The framework lays the groundwork for future research into dynamic branching, where the branching factor, correlation, and pruning schedules could adapt in real-time based on intermediate rewards or sample difficulty, leading to even more efficient and targeted exploration.
 - **Extension to Other Generative Architectures:** The principles could potentially extend to other complex generative models, including diffusion-based Large Language Models (LLMs) and general multimodal foundation models, where efficient RLHF remains a challenge.
 - **Long-Horizon Video and Embodied AI:** Initial success in video generation (improving temporal consistency and sharpness) suggests a strong potential for high-resolution, long-duration video synthesis and, more broadly, applications in robotics and embodied AI. In robotics, where intermediate states provide verifiable rewards, tree-structured rollouts could lead to more effective action generation and task learning.
7. **Fostering Responsible AI Development:** The authors' commitment to releasing code and configuration files, coupled with an ethics statement acknowledging potential biases and avoiding safety-critical deployments, promotes transparency and responsible reuse within the research community. This allows for critical scrutiny and helps mitigate the risks associated with powerful generative AI.

In conclusion, BranchGRPO offers a powerful, efficient, and stable method for aligning advanced generative models with human preferences, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in visual content creation and setting the stage for future innovations in RLHF and embodied AI.
