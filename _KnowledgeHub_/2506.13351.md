# Direct Reasoning Optimization: Constrained RL with Token-Level Dense Reward and Rubric-Gated Constraints for Open-ended Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Microsoft and UCLA developed Direct Reasoning Optimization (DRO), an RL-based framework that allows large language models to self-refine their reasoning for open-ended tasks by using a novel internal Reasoning Reflection Reward (R3). This method leverages the model's self-certainty about a reference outcome given its Chain-of-Thought, leading to superior reasoning quality on tasks like paragraph revision and comparable performance to explicit verifiers on structured QA.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) struggles with open-ended, long-form reasoning tasks due to the lack of objective, scalable reward signals.
- Training separate reward models is computationally expensive and requires extensive human preference data, while "LLM-as-a-judge" introduces biases and prompt sensitivity.
- Traditional metrics like ROUGE fail to capture logical coherence or reasoning quality in open-ended generations.

## Method

- ==Direct Reasoning Optimization (DRO)==, an RL-based fine-tuning framework, is introduced to enable self-reward and self-refinement for LLMs on open-ended tasks.
- A novel internal reward signal, ==Reasoning Reflection Reward (R3)==, quantifies the consistency between a model's Chain-of-Thought and a reference outcome by emphasizing =="reasoning-reflective tokens."==
- R3 addresses log-probability aggregation issues by weighting crucial tokens and subtracting a ==masked-CoT baseline==, while a dynamic data filtering mechanism optimizes training efficiency.

## Results

- On the open-ended ParaRev dataset, DRO-R3 achieved higher win rates against GPT-4o than all baselines, including ROUGE-rewarded and aggregate-certainty models, as evaluated by GPT-4o and Claude 3.7 Sonnet.
- For the structured FinQA task, DRO-R3 demonstrated performance comparable to an ideal correctness-based reward, falling just **0.9%** short on Pass@1 and surpassing it for Pass@k (k 2).
- The dynamic data filtering mechanism reduced training time by approximately **45%** while maintaining similar final reward scores and ensuring smoother convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively reward and refine their own reasoning for open-ended tasks using internal self-certainty signals, eliminating reliance on external feedback.
> - Not all tokens contribute equally to reflecting reasoning quality; emphasizing "reasoning-reflective tokens" via variance weighting provides a more precise reward signal than naive log-probability aggregation.
> - Dynamically filtering training data based on model difficulty and reasoning variation significantly improves training efficiency and focuses learning on impactful samples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically for tasks where the quality of generated outputs cannot be programmatically verified. Traditional RL for LLMs, often termed Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), has achieved successes in structured domains like mathematics and programming, where outcomes can be definitively checked against a reference or unit tests.

However, a significant category of tasks, including scientific document revision, medical question answering, and creative writing, involves open-ended, long-form text generation. For these "unverifiable tasks," even when a high-quality reference answer exists, automatically assigning a stable and accurate reward signal for RL training remains difficult. Existing approaches in this area include:
- **Training separate reward models:** This introduces computational overhead and requires extensive human labeling to train the reward model itself.
- **Using internal model feedback (self-certainty):** This eliminates external verifiers but often struggles with long-form answers due to dilution of informative signals or susceptibility to reward hacking if not properly constrained. Some variants use uniform weighting of tokens or weight them by probability under a baseline, which the paper argues are suboptimal.
- **Leveraging rubrics:** Rubrics provide structured evaluation criteria. However, converting rubric judgments into dense rewards can be labor-intensive, inconsistent with LLM-generated rubrics, or prone to score saturation and over-regularization, leading to mode collapse.

This paper positions its proposed framework, Direct Reasoning Optimization (DRO), as an advancement that combines token-level dense rewards aligned with reasoning quality and principled feasibility constraints, aiming to overcome the limitations of prior methods in the context of unverifiable, long-form generation tasks. It leverages the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework as its underlying RL optimization backbone, which computes advantages by comparing sampled outputs within a group.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient RL training framework for LLMs on unverifiable tasks, particularly those requiring long-form answers. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current RL approaches for such tasks:

1. **Challenge of Reward Design for Unverifiable Tasks:** Unlike verifiable tasks where rewards can be objective (e.g., matching a reference answer exactly, passing a unit test), open-ended, long-form tasks lack such clear-cut verification mechanisms. Existing methods that rely on simple reference matching (e.g., ROUGE-L) or uniform self-certainty aggregation often fail to capture nuanced reasoning quality or are susceptible to reward hacking.
2. **Dilution of Informative Signals:** When using token-level self-certainty as a reward, the paper identifies that the most informative tokens—those whose certainty varies significantly with the quality of the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning—are often diluted by the large number of uninformative, low-variance tokens in long-form answers. This dilution masks critical differences between high- and low-quality reasoning traces, hindering effective policy optimization.
3. **Vulnerability to Reward Hacking:** Dense, token-level rewards, while providing fine-grained supervision, can be susceptible to reward hacking. An LLM might generate uniformly low-quality answers that still exhibit relative differences under a dense metric, leading to misguided gradient updates if not constrained by broader task principles. This can result in model collapse, where the model converges to unproductive strategies (e.g., making no revisions in a revision task).
4. **Instability of Training with Low Reward Variance:** Group-wise standardization, as used in GRPO, requires sufficient variance in reward signals within each rollout group for stable optimization. When reward variance is low, even minor absolute differences can be spuriously amplified, destabilizing gradient updates and making learning inefficient.
5. **Limitations of Rubric-based Rewards:** While rubrics offer structured criteria, directly converting them into dense rewards is problematic due to the effort involved in crafting fine-grained criteria, potential inconsistencies with LLM-generated rubrics, and noise from multi-criterion aggregation. However, rubrics reliably encode principled "guardrails" or feasibility constraints.

To address these motivations, the research proposes DRO, aiming to:
- Develop a token-level dense reward that specifically highlights informative "reasoning-reflective tokens."
- Integrate principled task criteria as hard "gating" constraints rather than scalar rewards, preventing reward hacking and ensuring adherence to fundamental task requirements.
- Introduce a mechanism to filter out training samples that offer insufficient signal for comparative learning, thereby stabilizing and accelerating training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Direct Reasoning Optimization (DRO) framework is structured as a constrained Reinforcement Learning setup built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). It integrates three main components: (i) the Reasoning Reflection Reward (R3), a token-level dense reward, (ii) rubric-gating as feasibility constraints, and (iii) variance-based query filtering for training stability.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework (Direct Reasoning Optimization - DRO)

DRO operates within the GRPO framework, where an actor model samples multiple (k=16) chain-of-thought (CoT) traces and subsequent answers for a given query. These "rollout groups" are then processed to compute rewards and evaluate constraints before updating the policy. Crucially, all reward computations and rubric judgments are derived from the same reference policy used during RL training, avoiding reliance on external judges or separate reward models.

#### 3.2. Reasoning Reflection Reward (R3)

R3 is a token-level dense reward designed for long-form answers where a reference outcome `y` is available.
- **Self-certainty and CoT:** The generation process is autoregressive, so a generated CoT `c` acts as a latent prefix conditioning the final answer. R3 measures the model's token-level self-certainty, $\pi(y_j | q, \hat{c}_i, y_{<j})$, which is the likelihood the model assigns to a reference token $y_j$ given the query `q`, the sampled CoT `c_i`, and previous reference tokens $y_{<j}$.
- **Reasoning-Reflective Tokens:** The core insight of R3 is that not all tokens contribute equally to discriminating reasoning quality. "Reasoning-reflective tokens" are those whose self-certainty varies substantially across different CoT traces for the same query, indicating a stronger influence from the preceding reasoning. Most other tokens exhibit low variance, diluting the signal if averaged uniformly.
- **Mitigating Dilution:** R3 addresses this dilution problem by weighting each reference token's self-certainty by its cross-rollout variability (standard deviation $\sigma_j$). The reward for rollout `i` is calculated as:
 $r^{R3}_i = \sum_{j=1}^{|y|} \frac{e^{\omega \sigma_j}}{\sum_{k=1}^{|y|} e^{\omega \sigma_k}} \cdot \text{clip}(\pi(y_j | q, \hat{c}_i, y_{<j}), \lambda_{low}, \lambda_{high})$
 where $\sigma_j$ is the standard deviation of token `j`'s self-certainty across rollouts, $\omega$ is a temperature-like hyperparameter controlling emphasis, and $\text{clip}(\cdot, \lambda_{low}, \lambda_{high})$ clips probabilities to a defined range. This emphasizes high-variance tokens and stabilizes the reward by bounding extreme probabilities. The softmax weighting ensures the contributions sum to one. Probabilities are used instead of log-probabilities to avoid noise from extremely low-probability tokens.

#### 3.3. Rubric-Gating

Rubric-gating acts as a feasibility constraint to prevent reward hacking and ensure final answers adhere to essential task principles.
- **Rubric Generation:** For each query, a compact set of query-specific yes/no rubrics is proposed by the reference policy LLM. These rubrics are validated by ensuring the reference answer satisfies each one.
- **Accept/Reject Gates:** Instead of converting rubrics into a scalar reward, they function as hard gates on entire rollout groups:
 - **Coverage gate:** Requires that every rubric item must be satisfied by at least $\mu$ rollouts within the group. This ensures a minimum level of compliance.
 - **Consistency gate:** Demands that the top $\rho$% of rollouts (ranked by R3 reward) must each satisfy at least $\nu$% of the rubrics. This ensures that the most promising rollouts also meet fundamental quality standards.
- If a rollout group fails either gate, it is rejected for policy updates, but the query is revisited in later epochs. This separation prevents low-quality groups from misleading the policy even if they show internal relative differences in R3.

#### 3.4. Variance-Based Filtering

This component enhances training stability and sample efficiency by selecting queries with sufficient comparative signal.
- **Motivation:** In GRPO, low reward variance within a rollout group can lead to spurious amplification of minor differences, destabilizing gradient updates.
- **Mechanism:** Each query in the corpus is scored once under the reference policy. For a given rollout group `G`, a "high-variance score" $\bar{\sigma}_G$ is computed by averaging the standard deviations of the top 10% most variable tokens ($\sigma_{G,j}$) across rollouts. Queries are then ranked by $\bar{\sigma}_G$, and only a top fraction (e.g., $q \approx 10\%$) is retained for training, along with a small additional fraction to include challenging cases. This ensures that training focuses on groups where reasoning differences are genuinely reflected in outcome certainty variance.

#### 3.5. Optimization Backbone

The framework uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as its underlying RL optimization method. GRPO is a PPO variant that computes advantages by comparing sampled outputs within a group for the same prompt, using `z-scoring` on the rewards. The paper also applies the "80/20 rule" (restricting policy gradient updates to high-entropy CoT tokens) on top of R3.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of DRO was conducted across four datasets: ParaRev (scientific paragraph revision), RaR-Medicine (medical QA), ContractNLI (legal contract review), and FinQA (financial numerical reasoning).

- **Overall Performance:** DRO consistently achieved the best performance across all four datasets compared to various RL baselines, including ROUGE-L F1, plain average probability (RLPR), average log-probability (VeriFree), and rubric-based rewards (RLER). For instance, on ParaRev, DRO achieved a 63.7% win rate against the base model, significantly higher than the 57.8% of R3 alone and 55.9% of rubric-based rewards.
- **Outperformance of Supervised and Distillation Baselines:** On ParaRev and RaR-Medicine, DRO substantially outperformed supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on reference answers and on-policy distillation from a larger teacher model (Qwen3-235B-A22B to Qwen3-14B student). SFT even degraded performance on ParaRev. This indicates that the RL formulation of DRO extracts more useful learning signals than imitation-based methods from the same reference data.
- **Synergy of R3 and Rubric-Gating:** Combining R3 with rubric-gating (Full DRO) yielded superior performance compared to using R3 or rubric-based rewards in isolation. Rubric-gating prevented policy updates from groups that violated fundamental task principles, addressing the "reward hacking" vulnerability of dense rewards.
- **Transferability to External Benchmarks:** Models trained with DRO on RaR-Medicine showed improved performance on the independent HealthBench medical QA benchmark (e.g., +2.1 for Qwen3-4B), suggesting that DRO fosters generalizable reasoning abilities.
- **Robustness to Reference Quality:** Even when trained with imperfect, model-generated reference answers (from Claude Sonnet 4.6), DRO continued to improve over the base model, though with a smaller magnitude than with human-written ground-truth references.
- **Effectiveness of R3 (Reasoning Reflection Reward):**
 - R3 consistently outperformed plain average probability and log-probability rewards, especially on long-form generation tasks where signal dilution is more severe.
 - R3 was found to be effective even without a strict CoT/answer boundary, indicating it measures the overall impact of the prefix on reference likelihood.
 - R3 led to faster learning, achieving comparable downstream performance in 2-3x fewer training steps than plain average probability rewards on several datasets, translating to wall-clock savings.
 - Ablations confirmed that both reward clipping and the reasoning reflectivity emphasis factor ($\omega$) contributed positively to performance, particularly on long-form tasks.
- **Effectiveness of Rubric Supervision (Gating):**
 - Rubric-gating stabilized dense-reward RL by rejecting rollout groups that uniformly failed task requirements, which prevented "no-revision mode collapse" observed in ParaRev when using dense rewards alone.
 - The constrained RL approach (rubric-as-gate) outperformed methods that merged rubric judgments into a scalar reward, validating the design choice of separating feasibility constraints from the reward signal.
 - Rubric satisfaction rates increased over training time on gated datasets, and on held-out test sets, indicating that the policy learned to progressively satisfy these constraints.
- **Effectiveness of Variance-Based Query Filtering:**
 - The filter improved overall performance and led to earlier and more stable convergence by focusing training on high-variance groups with strong comparative signals.
 - The paper found that quality (high-variance groups) outweighed quantity, as training on a filtered subset of queries often led to better performance than training on the full corpus.
 - DRO's token-selective variance filtering outperformed RLPR's all-token average variance filtering, better aligning the filter with the informative reasoning-reflective tokens.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Direct Reasoning Optimization (DRO) as a framework that addresses key challenges in applying Reinforcement Learning to LLMs for tasks lacking programmatic verification, particularly those requiring long-form reasoning.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Enhanced Reasoning Quality on Unverifiable Tasks:** By introducing the Reasoning Reflection Reward (R3), which prioritizes "reasoning-reflective tokens" via cross-rollout variance, DRO provides a more focused and informative reward signal for improving Chain-of-Thought reasoning. This is particularly impactful for open-ended, long-form tasks where traditional lexical metrics are insufficient and uniform self-certainty aggregation suffers from signal dilution.
- **Robustness and Stability in RL Training:** The integration of rubric-gating and variance-based query filtering directly tackles common problems in RL for LLMs, such as reward hacking and unstable gradients due to low reward variance. Rubric-gating ensures adherence to fundamental task criteria without the pitfalls of converting rubrics into scalar rewards, preventing detrimental policy shifts like mode collapse. Variance-based filtering enhances sample efficiency and stability by focusing learning on high-signal data.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The empirical results demonstrate that DRO can achieve comparable performance up to 2-3x faster than baselines, translating to reduced computational costs and training time. This makes RL-based training more practical for a broader range of LLM applications.
- **Generalizable Learning:** Evidence of cross-dataset knowledge transfer to independent benchmarks suggests that DRO encourages more generalizable reasoning capabilities rather than superficial memorization of training data patterns.
- **Practical Applicability:** By operating without external reward models or human judges during training (leveraging the reference policy for R3 and rubric generation/evaluation), DRO offers a more scalable and self-contained approach. Its robustness to imperfect reference answers further broadens its applicability in scenarios where high-quality human references might be limited.

The potential impact includes advancing the deployment of LLMs in complex, open-ended domains such as scientific research assistance, advanced medical diagnostics, and legal analysis, where nuanced reasoning and adherence to specific criteria are paramount but automated verification is challenging. While the framework relies on the availability of at least one reasonable-quality reference answer and shares a residual risk of reward hacking with other self-certainty methods, it represents a methodological step towards more effective and reliable RL for LLMs on tasks that closely mirror real-world human-level reasoning.
