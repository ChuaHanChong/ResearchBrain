# Human Motion Video Generation: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey decomposing human motion video generation into a five-phase pipeline (input, motion planning, motion video generation, refinement, output), organizing over two hundred papers across vision-, text-, and audio-driven modalities. It is the first survey to analyze LLMs as motion planners, runs its own nine-method benchmark on TikTok, and catalogs 64 public datasets.

## Problem

- Existing surveys focus on individual subtasks or methods, lacking a comprehensive pipeline view across the entire human motion video generation process.
- No prior survey examines the potential of large language models as motion planners for human motion video generation.
- Evaluation lacks a uniform system: metrics are fragmented across single-frame, video-quality, and characteristic measures, with no consolidated dataset catalog for the field.

## Method

- ==Five-phase pipeline taxonomy== (input, motion planning, motion video generation, refinement, output) organizing over two hundred papers into a single end-to-end framework for the first time.
- ==Modality classification scheme== (audio-driven, text-driven, vision-driven), ranking audio over text over vision when combined, applied to categorize and timeline over two hundred papers.
- Own ==quantitative benchmark== of nine open-source pose-driven dance video methods on the TikTok test set, plus a curated catalog of sixty-four public human-motion datasets with descriptions.

## Results

- In the paper's own TikTok benchmark, MagicAnimate leads SSIM at **0.7558** and PSNR at **18.1666**, while UniAnimate leads LPIPS at **0.2496** and FID at **73.8868**.
- Animate Anyone achieves the best L1 score at **0.0779** among nine compared methods, and UniAnimate also leads FID-VID at **12.6624**, showing no single method dominates all six metrics.
- The survey catalogs **64** public datasets, ranging up to **1800** hours of footage and over **1000** identities in several sets, exposing acute data scarcity for training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No architecture wins across all metrics; structural fidelity and perceptual/temporal quality trade off, so single-metric leaderboards mislead when comparing human motion video generation methods.
> - LLM-based motion planning remains confined to 3D skeletal motion and dialogue-reaction tasks, not yet generalized to full video-level planning, marking a genuinely open research direction.
> - High diffusion-model compute cost is the common blocker behind three separate open problems: real-time inference, long-duration coherence, and unified multi-signal (lip, head, gesture) control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human Motion Video Generation (HMVG) has emerged as a rapidly evolving and highly impactful sub-field within generative AI, driven by the increasing demand for advanced digital human technologies in diverse applications. This includes creating photorealistic singing heads, dynamic avatars for virtual reality, motion transfer for entertainment, and more intuitive human-computer interfaces.

The broader research landscape of HMVG has seen significant advancements, particularly with the rise of deep learning and generative models such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Diffusion Models (DMs). Early efforts were often limited to stylized characters or lacked realistic textures. However, the advent of general text-to-video generation models has allowed for the production of videos with increasingly realistic human appearances and movements. This has spurred a flourishing interest in human-centered video synthesis, with talking head, portrait animation, and dance video generation becoming prominent research hotspots.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in the existing literature: while numerous surveys have focused on individual methods or specific sub-tasks within HMVG (e.g., talking head synthesis, virtual try-on), none have provided a truly comprehensive, end-to-end overview of the entire generative process. This fragmentation makes it challenging for researchers to gain a holistic understanding of the field and identify interconnected challenges and opportunities across different stages of video generation.

This survey directly addresses this void by offering a unified and systematic framework. It positions itself as the first to decompose HMVG into five distinct, sequential phases: Input, Motion Planning, Motion Video Generation, Refinement, and Output. Furthermore, it pioneers the discussion of Large Language Models (LLMs) as a transformative component in enhancing human motion video generation, particularly in the motion planning phase – an aspect previously unexplored in dedicated HMVG surveys. By reviewing over 200 papers and categorizing them across three primary modalities (vision, text, audio), the survey consolidates milestone works and technological trends, providing an essential resource for navigating this complex and interdisciplinary field. It aims to serve as a foundational map, integrating disparate research efforts into a coherent narrative and guiding future advancements in the comprehensive applications of digital humans.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic and multi-faceted approach to survey the extensive landscape of human motion video generation. Their methodology is characterized by a hierarchical decomposition of the generation process, a multimodal categorization of inputs, and a detailed analysis of generative frameworks and human data representations.

**1. Hierarchical Decomposition into Five Key Phases:**
A core methodological innovation of this survey is the decomposition of human motion video generation into a novel five-phase pipeline, providing an end-to-end perspective:
 - **Phase 1: Input:** Identifying diverse driving sources, including vision cues (reference images, videos), text prompts, and audio signals. It notes that generating facial regions often requires distinct frameworks compared to holistic human bodies.
 - **Phase 2: Motion Planning:** This crucial phase determines the specific movements of the digital human. The survey distinctly highlights two main approaches: traditional implicit feature mapping and the novel application of Large Language Models (LLMs) for semantic-rich motion planning.
 - **Phase 3: Motion Video Generation:** Focusing on the core synthesis of videos, ensuring body consistency, precise movements, and high-quality output. This phase delves into various generative frameworks.
 - **Phase 4: Refinement:** Post-generation enhancement strategies, categorized into "part-specific" (e.g., hands, mouth, eyes, teeth) and "general refinement" (e.g., super-resolution, denoising) to optimize video quality and minimize artifacts.
 - **Phase 5: Output:** Addressing the practical deployment challenges, such as real-time performance and integration into streaming platforms, with a focus on acceleration techniques.

**2. Multi-Modal Categorization of Driving Inputs:**
The survey organizes the vast literature based on three primary input modalities, with a clear hierarchical classification to handle multimodal methods:
 - **Audio-Driven:** If audio is present, it takes precedence (e.g., Audio + Text + Vision, or Audio + Vision).
 - **Text-Driven:** If not audio-driven but text is present (e.g., Text + Vision).
 - **Vision-Driven:** If only vision is utilized.
This system effectively categorizes over 200 papers, providing a structured way to analyze advancements specific to each input type.

**3. In-Depth Analysis of Generative Frameworks:**
The authors systematically review the underlying generative models that power HMVG:
 - **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs):** Discussing their data representation capabilities and limitations (mode collapse, less sharp samples).
 - **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs):** Highlighting their ability to produce high-quality outputs by implicitly mapping feature relationships, while acknowledging issues like lack of diversity and mode collapse.
 - **Diffusion Models (DMs):** Emphasizing their widespread attention, high-quality sample generation, and the computational efficiency offered by Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs). The survey notes the shift towards DMs as the dominant paradigm.

**4. Comprehensive Review of Human Data Representations:**
To provide foundational knowledge, the survey details seven key types of human body posture representations (Mask, Mesh, Depth, Normal, Keypoint, Semantics, Optical Flow), explaining their respective strengths and applications in guiding video generation.

**5. Focus on Motion Planning and LLMs:**
A significant methodological contribution is the dedicated section on motion planning, particularly the pioneering discussion of LLMs. The authors identify two main LLM planning strategies:
 - Generating fine-grained motion descriptions for retrieval of relevant video clips.
 - Projecting descriptions into a latent space to directly guide generative models.
This highlights the survey's forward-looking perspective.

**6. Detailed Sub-Task Analysis and Comparative Insights:**
Within each modality and phase, the survey delves into specific sub-tasks like portrait animation, dance video generation (video-driven and pose-driven), virtual try-on, talking head synthesis, and audio-driven holistic human motion. For diffusion models, they meticulously classify five variants of attention fusion methods (SA-CA-TA, SA&SA-CA-TA, SA-CA-CA-TA, SA-CA&CA-TA, CFSA-CA&CA-TA), offering fine-grained technical insights.

**7. Evaluation and Benchmarking:**
The authors collect 64 human-centered datasets to support related tasks, offering detailed information. They also summarize commonly used evaluation metrics for single-frame image quality, video quality, and video characteristics, along with comprehensive video evaluators. Crucially, they perform a **comparative analysis** of nine open-source methods on the TikTok test dataset for pose-guided dance video generation, using six quantitative metrics (L1, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, FID-VID). This hands-on evaluation provides empirical insights into the performance and common artifacts of current state-of-the-art methods.

By combining a novel structural framework, multi-modal categorization, detailed technical analysis of models and representations, pioneering exploration of LLMs, and an empirical comparative study, the survey provides a robust and comprehensive overview of the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey by Xue et al. presents a comprehensive analysis of the human motion video generation landscape, yielding several key findings and results:

**1. Establishment of a Unified Five-Phase Pipeline:**
The most significant organizational finding is the successful decomposition and clear definition of human motion video generation into five key phases: Input, Motion Planning, Motion Video Generation, Refinement, and Output. This framework, the first of its kind, provides a structured and holistic understanding of the entire process, clarifying how different sub-tasks and technologies contribute to the final video output.

**2. Identification of Large Language Models (LLMs) as a Novel Motion Planning Paradigm:**
The survey is groundbreaking in its explicit discussion of LLMs' emerging role in motion planning. It identifies two primary strategies for LLM integration:
 - **Retrieval-based:** LLMs generate fine-grained motion descriptions used to retrieve relevant motion clips from a video database.
 - **Generative:** LLMs project motion descriptions into a latent space to directly guide generative models.
This highlights a nascent but highly promising direction, moving beyond implicit feature mapping towards semantically richer, more controllable motion generation. Despite the potential, the survey notes that LLMs remain underutilized and face challenges in effective intermediate representations and evaluation.

**3. Dominance of Diffusion Models and Advanced Generative Frameworks:**
The analysis reveals a clear trend towards Diffusion Models (DMs) as the leading generative framework for human motion video generation, surpassing traditional VAEs and GANs in achieving high-quality, diverse outputs. Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) are particularly favored for their computational efficiency. The survey details how these models are adapted for video generation, often incorporating temporal layers and various attention fusion methods (e.g., SA-CA-TA, SA&SA-CA-TA) to ensure spatio-temporal consistency.

**4. Comprehensive Categorization and Analysis of Modality-Driven Sub-Tasks:**
The survey provides a detailed review of over ten sub-tasks, categorized by driving modality:
 - **Vision-Driven:** Noted the significant research focus on portrait animation (e.g., OmniAvatar, LivePortrait), video-driven dance generation (e.g., Everybody Dance Now), and pose-driven dance generation (e.g., Animate Anyone, MagicAnimate). Challenges include maintaining identity consistency, precise control over detailed features (eye gaze, teeth, hands), and handling varying body proportions.
 - **Text-Driven:** Examined Text2Face (first-person scripts vs. third-person instructions) and Text2MotionVideo. While text can guide content and style, its effectiveness is constrained when combined with other explicit conditions, especially for complex motions. The trend is towards stable training, easier control, and finer granularity in identity preservation.
 - **Audio-Driven:** Covered lip synchronization (e.g., Wav2lip), head pose driving (e.g., StyleHEAT), audio-driven holistic human motion (e.g., Vlogger, ANGIE), and fine-grained style/emotion-driven animation (e.g., EMO, VASA-1). A major challenge is developing a unified framework for integrating lip sync, head motion, and rhythmic gestures while maintaining stable backgrounds and holistic body details. Real-time performance for DMs in this area remains a significant hurdle.

**5. Insights into Refinement and Output Challenges:**
The survey emphasizes the crucial role of refinement strategies, categorizing them into "part-specific" (e.g., hand/face enhancement) and "general" (e.g., super-resolution). It highlights that real-time generation and cost optimization are nascent but critical areas, particularly for diffusion models which have high computational demands.

**6. Quantitative Comparative Analysis and Identification of Artifacts:**
Through an empirical benchmark of nine open-source methods on the TikTok dataset, the survey provides quantitative performance metrics (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS, L1, FID, FID-VID). Key findings from this analysis include:
 - Different methods excel in different metrics (e.g., MagicAnimate in image quality, UniAnimate in perceptual quality and temporal consistency).
 - Common visual artifacts persist across methods, including subpar fidelity (hand blur, facial distortion), poor consistency (identity and background changes), unreal movements (limb dislocation), and low resolution. This confirms the ongoing challenges in achieving flawless human motion video generation.

**7. Compilation of Extensive Datasets:**
The authors compiled a valuable resource of 64 publicly available human-related video datasets, categorized by region (only head, half-body, full-body, general) and providing key attributes like duration, resolution, and support tasks. This addresses a practical need for researchers in a data-hungry field.

In summary, the survey not only consolidates existing knowledge into a coherent framework but also pioneers the discussion of LLM integration, provides empirical benchmarks, and clearly delineates the current state-of-the-art, its limitations, and future directions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey by Xue et al. carries significant implications for the field of human motion video generation and related domains, with potential impacts across research, industry, and application development.

**1. Centralized and Comprehensive Resource for Researchers:**
The most immediate impact is providing a much-needed, unified resource for the research community. By synthesizing over 200 papers and organizing them into a novel five-phase pipeline and multimodal taxonomy, the survey acts as a comprehensive map. This coherence helps new researchers quickly grasp the complexities of the field, understand its interdependencies, and identify relevant prior work. For experienced researchers, it offers a structured overview to track progress, pinpoint crucial gaps, and foster interdisciplinary collaboration.

**2. Catalyst for Next-Generation Motion Planning:**
The pioneering discussion of Large Language Models (LLMs) in motion planning is a crucial contribution. By explicitly highlighting LLMs' potential to enhance semantic understanding, contextual reasoning, and fine-grained control over human movements, the survey is likely to inspire a new wave of research at the intersection of generative AI and NLP. This could lead to more intelligent, natural, and controllable digital human behaviors, moving beyond purely data-driven, implicit motion generation to context-aware, intent-driven planning.

**3. Guiding Technological Development and Benchmarking:**
The detailed analysis of generative frameworks (especially the shift towards Diffusion Models), human data representations, and attention mechanisms provides critical insights into the underlying technologies. The quantitative comparative analysis of nine open-source methods on a real-world dataset (TikTok) offers valuable benchmarks and exposes common failure modes. This empirical evidence can guide researchers in developing more robust models, focusing on areas like consistency, fidelity (especially hands and faces), and realistic movement, ultimately accelerating technological advancements.

**4. Enabling Advanced Digital Human Applications:**
The advancements surveyed directly feed into the development of highly realistic and interactive digital humans. This has transformative potential in numerous sectors:
 - **Entertainment and Media:** Creating hyper-realistic virtual actors, animated characters, and personalized content (e.g., dance videos, singing avatars).
 - **Virtual and Augmented Reality:** Populating virtual worlds with believable, responsive avatars for enhanced immersive experiences.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** Developing more intuitive and empathetic AI assistants, customer service agents, and educational tools.
 - **Fashion and E-commerce:** Improving virtual try-on experiences for clothing, reducing returns, and enhancing online shopping.
 - **Communication:** Advancing tools for multilingual video dubbing and realistic video conferencing.

**5. Highlighting Key Challenges and Future Research Directions:**
By meticulously detailing challenges such as data availability, photorealism (e.g., hand blur, facial distortion), consistency (identity, background), realism of movement, expanding generation duration, refining control over specific body parts, and achieving real-time performance at reduced computational cost, the survey provides a clear roadmap for future research. This proactive identification of research frontiers ensures that efforts are directed towards the most impactful problems.

**6. Raising Ethical Awareness:**
The inclusion of ethical considerations, particularly regarding data privacy and the responsible development of digital humanoids, demonstrates a forward-thinking approach. This emphasizes that technological advancement must be accompanied by robust ethical frameworks, mitigating potential negative impacts and fostering trust in these powerful AI creations.

In essence, Xue et al.'s survey is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a strategic document that structures a complex field, spotlights groundbreaking intersections (like LLMs in motion planning), provides empirical guidance, and charts a responsible course for future innovation, profoundly impacting the trajectory of digital human technologies.
