# SECOND: Mitigating Perceptual Hallucination in Vision-Language Models via Selective and Contrastive Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University's AIDASLab developed SECOND (Selective and Contrastive Decoding), a training-free framework that addresses object hallucination in Vision-Language Models by mimicking human visual perception. The method uses selective multi-scale processing and multi-stage contrastive decoding, leading to consistent reductions in hallucination (e.g., POPE F1 scores improved from 86.5 to 89.2 for LLaVA-NeXT) while also improving general VLM task performance.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently generate confident but factually incorrect responses about objects not present in an image, a phenomenon known as object hallucination.
- Existing multi-scale VLM approaches integrate visual information uniformly, which can introduce irrelevant background noise and dilute object-specific context, worsening hallucination.
- The unreliability caused by hallucination limits the deployment of VLMs in sensitive, high-stakes applications requiring factual accuracy.

## Method

- SECOND employs a ==training-free, human-inspired multi-scale processing pipeline== that selectively integrates visual information based on ==dynamic patch selection==.
- Visual patches for higher-resolution processing are chosen based on ==attention scores and entropy calculations==, preserving object-relevant details while filtering out irrelevant background.
- A novel ==multi-stage contrastive decoding formulation== amplifies distinctions between sparse and dense visual information from different processing stages, leveraging a coarse-to-fine understanding to refine outputs.

## Results

- SECOND consistently reduced hallucination on the POPE benchmark, improving F1 scores (e.g., from **86.5** to **89.2** for LLaVA-NeXT on MSCOCO) across **11 out of 12** configurations.
- The framework also improved performance on general VLM tasks, increasing VQAv2 accuracy from **76.4** to **77.5**, MMStar from **37.3** to **38.6**, and MMBench from **75.8** to **80.0** for LLaVA-NeXT.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the dynamic patch selection based on visual attention entropy and the multi-stage contrastive decoding were crucial for the observed performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM object hallucination is inversely correlated with visual attention alignment, meaning models are more likely to hallucinate when their attention poorly aligns with ground truth objects.
> - Indiscriminate integration of visual information across multiple scales can dilute object-specific context and increase hallucination, indicating a need for selective processing.
> - A multi-stage contrastive decoding approach, leveraging hierarchical visual representations, can effectively amplify visual evidence and reduce hallucination without retraining.
