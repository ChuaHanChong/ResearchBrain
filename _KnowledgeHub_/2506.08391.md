# SECOND: Mitigating Perceptual Hallucination in Vision-Language Models via Selective and Contrastive Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University's AIDASLab developed SECOND (Selective and Contrastive Decoding), a training-free framework that addresses object hallucination in Vision-Language Models by mimicking human visual perception. The method uses selective multi-scale processing and multi-stage contrastive decoding, leading to consistent reductions in hallucination (e.g., POPE F1 scores improved from 86.5 to 89.2 for LLaVA-NeXT) while also improving general VLM task performance.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently generate confident but factually incorrect responses about objects not present in an image, a phenomenon known as object hallucination.
- Existing multi-scale VLM approaches integrate visual information uniformly, which can introduce irrelevant background noise and dilute object-specific context, worsening hallucination.
- The unreliability caused by hallucination limits the deployment of VLMs in sensitive, high-stakes applications requiring factual accuracy.

## Method

- SECOND employs a ==training-free, human-inspired multi-scale processing pipeline== that selectively integrates visual information based on ==dynamic patch selection==.
- Visual patches for higher-resolution processing are chosen based on ==attention scores and entropy calculations==, preserving object-relevant details while filtering out irrelevant background.
- A novel ==multi-stage contrastive decoding formulation== amplifies distinctions between sparse and dense visual information from different processing stages, leveraging a coarse-to-fine understanding to refine outputs.

## Results

- SECOND consistently reduced hallucination on the POPE benchmark, improving F1 scores (e.g., from **86.5** to **89.2** for LLaVA-NeXT on MSCOCO) across **11 out of 12** configurations.
- The framework also improved performance on general VLM tasks, increasing VQAv2 accuracy from **76.4** to **77.5**, MMStar from **37.3** to **38.6**, and MMBench from **75.8** to **80.0** for LLaVA-NeXT.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the dynamic patch selection based on visual attention entropy and the multi-stage contrastive decoding were crucial for the observed performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM object hallucination is inversely correlated with visual attention alignment, meaning models are more likely to hallucinate when their attention poorly aligns with ground truth objects.
> - Indiscriminate integration of visual information across multiple scales can dilute object-specific context and increase hallucination, indicating a need for selective processing.
> - A multi-stage contrastive decoding approach, leveraging hierarchical visual representations, can effectively amplify visual evidence and reduce hallucination without retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have achieved remarkable success in bridging visual and linguistic understanding, enabling applications from image captioning to visual question answering. However, these models suffer from a critical limitation: they frequently generate confident but factually incorrect responses about objects that are not present in the image—a phenomenon known as "object hallucination." This paper introduces SECOND (Selective and Contrastive Decoding), a training-free framework that addresses this fundamental challenge by mimicking human visual perception through selective multi-scale processing and contrastive reasoning.

The work emerges from Seoul National University's AIDASLab, where researchers identified that existing multi-scale VLM approaches integrate visual information indiscriminately, leading to information dilution and increased susceptibility to hallucination. SECOND addresses this by implementing a human-inspired visual processing pipeline that selectively focuses on relevant image regions while progressively refining understanding through multi-stage contrastive decoding.

### 2. The Hallucination Problem in Vision-Language Models

Object hallucination in VLMs represents a significant barrier to their deployment in high-stakes applications. The authors establish a crucial connection between visual attention alignment and hallucination probability through their analysis of the Attention Dice Coefficient—a metric measuring how well a model's visual attention aligns with ground truth object locations.

The analysis reveals that when VLMs fail to focus their attention on relevant objects, they are significantly more likely to hallucinate. This insight forms the theoretical foundation for SECOND's approach: by improving attention alignment through selective visual processing, hallucination rates can be substantially reduced.

Current multi-scale VLM implementations, while incorporating multiple resolutions, often process visual information uniformly across all scales. This approach can introduce irrelevant background information that dilutes object-specific context, particularly when scaling up to higher resolutions with more visual patches.

### 3. The SECOND Framework

SECOND introduces a two-component framework that addresses hallucination through selective visual processing and multi-stage reasoning. The approach is entirely training-free, making it immediately applicable to existing VLMs without requiring costly retraining or architectural modifications.

The framework operates on the principle that human visual perception is inherently selective and progressive. Rather than processing all visual information simultaneously, humans typically begin with a broad overview and progressively focus on relevant details. SECOND replicates this process through its staged architecture and selective attention mechanisms.

### 4. Selective Multi-Scale Feature Integration

The first component of SECOND implements a training-free multi-scale processing pipeline that generates visual representations at multiple resolutions while selectively focusing on relevant regions.

#### 4.1. Multi-Scale Generation

SECOND creates visual tokens at different scales by down-sampling input images and applying bilinear interpolation to positional embeddings. For models like LLaVA-NeXT, this extends the typical two-scale approach to four stages:

- **Stage 1**: Smallest scale (H/4×W/4 resolution)
- **Stage 2**: Medium scale (H/2×W/2 resolution)
- **Stage 3**: Base resolution (H×W resolution)
- **Stage 4**: Double-scale (2H×2W resolution)

#### 4.2. Dynamic Patch Selection

The key innovation lies in the dynamic patch selection mechanism. Rather than processing all patches at each scale, SECOND selectively chooses patches for higher-resolution processing based on visual attention from the previous stage. This selection process involves:

1. **Attention Calculation**: Combining vision encoder self-attention and language model cross-attention to generate a unified visual attention signal
2. **Entropy-Based Selection**: Using the entropy of visual attention H(V) to determine patch selection percentage:

$$p_{select} = \frac{\exp(\lambda \times H(V)) - 1}{\exp(\lambda) - 1}$$

where λ is a hyperparameter controlling selection sensitivity. Low entropy indicates clear object/background distinction requiring fewer patches, while high entropy suggests poor focus necessitating more fine-grained information.

3. **Selective Accumulation**: Choosing the top p_select% patches with highest attention scores to preserve object-relevant information while limiting irrelevant patch proliferation.

### 5. Multi-Stage Contrastive Decoding

The second component extends traditional contrastive decoding to leverage the hierarchical outputs from multiple processing stages. While conventional contrastive decoding contrasts a single "expert" model with an "amateur" model, SECOND utilizes the natural progression of visual understanding across stages.

#### 5.1. Multi-Stage Formulation

The final output logits are computed using a multi-stage contrastive formula:

$$logit_{SECOND} = logit_{expert} + \alpha(logit_{expert} - logit_{amateur3}) + \beta(logit_{amateur3} - logit_{amateur2}) + \gamma(logit_{amateur2} - logit_{amateur1})$$

where:
- $logit_{expert}$ represents the final stage output (Stage 4)
- $logit_{amateurX}$ represents outputs from earlier stages
- α, β, γ are contrasting hyperparameters (0 ≤ α, β, γ ≤ 1)

This formulation amplifies the distinctions between sparse and dense visual information, encouraging the model to rely more heavily on detailed visual evidence while maintaining the benefits of coarse-to-fine processing.

### 6. Experimental Results

SECOND was evaluated across multiple VLM architectures and benchmarks, demonstrating consistent improvements in both hallucination mitigation and general performance.

#### 6.1. Benchmarks and Models

The evaluation encompassed:
- **Models**: LLaVA-NeXT, LLaVA-OneVision, Yi-VL with various language model backends
- **Hallucination Evaluation**: POPE benchmark with yes/no questions about object presence
- **General Tasks**: VQAv2, MMStar, MMBench for comprehensive VLM capability assessment

#### 6.2. Performance Improvements

SECOND achieved significant improvements across all evaluated configurations:

- **POPE (Hallucination)**: Consistent outperformance in 11 out of 12 experimental configurations, with F1 scores improving from 86.5 to 89.2 for LLaVA-NeXT on MSCOCO
- **General Tasks**: Improvements in VQAv2 accuracy (76.4→77.5), MMStar (37.3→38.6), and MMBench (75.8→80.0) for LLaVA-NeXT

#### 6.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies revealed several key findings:

1. **Stage Configuration**: The 4-stage configuration provided optimal balance between performance and computational cost
2. **Patch Selection Strategy**: Dynamic selection based on visual attention entropy consistently outperformed fixed percentage or reversed selection approaches
3. **Multi-Stage vs. Single-Stage**: While single-stage contrastive decoding achieved comparable peak performance on POPE, multi-stage decoding demonstrated superior robustness across diverse benchmarks

#### 6.4. Hyperparameter Analysis

The framework demonstrated robustness across a wide range of hyperparameter settings, with consistent improvements over baselines across multiple parameter combinations. This stability indicates that SECOND's benefits are not dependent on precise hyperparameter tuning.

#### 6.5. Confidence Analysis

The confidence analysis reveals that SECOND not only reduces hallucination rates but also improves calibration, with later stages showing more appropriate confidence distributions for both correct and incorrect responses.

### 7. Significance and Impact

SECOND represents a significant advancement in addressing one of the most critical challenges facing modern VLMs. The framework's contributions extend beyond immediate performance improvements to fundamental insights about visual attention and multi-scale processing in AI systems.

#### 7.1. Technical Contributions

1. **Training-Free Framework**: SECOND's approach requires no additional training or architectural modifications, making it immediately applicable to existing VLMs
2. **Theoretical Foundation**: The connection between attention alignment and hallucination probability provides theoretical grounding for the approach
3. **Human-Inspired Design**: The selective, progressive processing pipeline mirrors human visual perception, suggesting broader applicability to other multimodal tasks

#### 7.2. Practical Impact

The framework addresses critical reliability concerns that have limited VLM deployment in high-stakes applications. By significantly reducing hallucination rates while maintaining general performance, SECOND enables more confident deployment of VLMs in domains requiring high accuracy, such as medical imaging analysis, autonomous systems, and content moderation.

#### 7.3. Future Directions

While SECOND demonstrates substantial improvements, the authors acknowledge that the framework's effectiveness depends on the quality of the underlying VLM's attention mechanisms. Future work should focus on developing more robust attention estimation techniques and exploring self-correction mechanisms for attention calibration.

The success of SECOND's multi-stage contrastive approach also suggests potential applications beyond hallucination mitigation, including enhanced reasoning capabilities and improved multimodal understanding in complex scenarios.

SECOND establishes a new paradigm for VLM reliability through selective visual processing and multi-stage reasoning, providing both immediate practical benefits and a foundation for future research in trustworthy AI systems.
