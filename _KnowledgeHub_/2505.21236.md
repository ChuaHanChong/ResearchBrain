# Breaking the Performance Ceiling in Reinforcement Learning requires Inference Strategies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at InstaDeep and Stellenbosch University demonstrated that explicitly incorporating inference-time search strategies can overcome performance limitations in complex reinforcement learning tasks. Their systematic evaluation across 17 multi-agent environments showed an average performance improvement of 45%, reaching up to 126%, using minimal additional execution time.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) policies often hit a 'performance ceiling' in complex, real-world tasks characterized by combinatorial action spaces, high dimensionality, and partial observability.
- The traditional focus on improving RL training phases neglects the potential of a dedicated 'inference phase' with available compute budgets in many practical applications, unlike instantaneous response systems.
- A systematic and broad evaluation of diverse inference strategies, particularly in Multi-Agent RL (MARL) and across varying compute and time budgets, has been largely absent from the literature.

## Method

- The study framed complex multi-agent scenarios as ==Decentralized Partially Observable== Markov Decision Processes (Dec-POMDPs), differentiating between training and inference phases.
- Four inference strategy paradigms—stochastic sampling, simulation-guided beam search (SGBS), online fine-tuning, and diversity-based COMPASS—were systematically adapted and evaluated for multi-agent settings.
- These strategies were tested on 17 challenging multi-agent RL tasks (from RWARE, SMACv2, and Connector benchmarks) using three state-of-the-art base policies: Independent PPO (IPPO), Multi-Agent PPO (MAPPO), and SABLE.

## Results

- Inference-time search strategies delivered an average performance improvement of **45%** (with a peak of **126%**) over zero-shot policies across the 17 multi-agent RL tasks, typically within 30 seconds of additional wall-clock time.
- The combination of the SABLE base policy with the COMPASS inference strategy achieved an aggregated normalized performance close to **100%**, establishing a new performance benchmark.
- COMPASS exhibited superior scaling with increasing compute capacity, showing near-linear performance gains under fixed time budgets, attributed to its efficient exploration of diverse policy latent spaces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inference-time search strategies are essential for surpassing the performance ceilings of even highly optimized, converged zero-shot RL policies in complex decision-making tasks.
> - The performance gains achieved by leveraging inference strategies are directly correlated with task complexity, yielding greater improvements in harder environments where base policies perform poorly.
> - The COMPASS diversity-based approach consistently outperformed other inference strategies, demonstrating robust performance and favorable scaling with increased parallel compute capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved remarkable successes in domains like game playing and robotic control. However, its widespread deployment in complex, real-world scenarios, such as energy-grid management, autonomous logistics, and molecular discovery, remains challenging. These problems are often characterized by their combinatorial nature, high-dimensional action and observation spaces, partial observability, and the need for intricate coordination among multiple agents. Even state-of-the-art RL policies, trained to convergence, frequently hit a "performance ceiling" where their "zero-shot" (single-attempt) performance is insufficient.

The prevailing research paradigm in RL has largely focused on improving the training phase to achieve better zero-shot performance. This paper argues that this narrow focus overlooks a critical opportunity: the "inference phase." Many practical applications, particularly those involving simulations or digital environments (e.g., train scheduling, PCB design), naturally allow for a dedicated inference phase with a specific time and compute budget (ranging from seconds to hours). During this phase, multiple attempts can be made to explore solutions, and the best one can be selected. This concept of leveraging inference-time compute has been successfully demonstrated in other rapidly advancing AI fields, most notably in Large Language Models (LLMs), where inference strategies are crucial for achieving state-of-the-art results.

This work specifically addresses several gaps in the existing RL literature:
- **Limited Scope of Inference Strategy Studies:** While some research in RL for Combinatorial Optimization (CO) has explored "active search" or "online adaptation" methods, these studies are often confined to a few problems, narrow budget settings, and lack insights into scaling properties. Moreover, they often evaluate on benchmarks where zero-shot optimality is already high, leaving little room for significant gains.
- **Neglect in Multi-Agent RL (MARL):** In MARL, research on inference strategies beyond "Ad Hoc Teamwork" (where agents must adapt to unknown partners) is scarce, despite the inherent complexity of collaborative multi-agent optimization tasks.
- **Lack of Systematic Evaluation:** The broader decision-making field has not systematically studied how different inference strategies perform and scale across a wide range of compute and time budgets.

By systematically investigating diverse inference strategies within complex multi-agent environments (formulated as Dec-POMDPs), the paper aims to bridge these gaps. It advocates for a paradigm shift, proposing that inference-time strategies should be considered core components of RL solutions, rather than minor post-processing steps, particularly in real-world applications where some compute budget at execution time is often available and highly valuable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **demonstrate that inference-time search and the deliberate choice of an inference strategy are instrumental in overcoming the performance ceiling observed in complex multi-agent Reinforcement Learning problems.**

The authors are primarily motivated by several key observations and challenges:
- **The "Performance Ceiling" of Zero-Shot RL:** Even highly optimized and converged RL policies struggle with the inherent complexity of many real-world tasks. The combinatorial explosion of solution spaces in multi-agent environments, coupled with partial observability and the need for tight coordination, means that a single, greedy execution of a trained policy often yields suboptimal results.
- **Underutilized Inference Budget:** Many real-world applications offer a valuable, yet often overlooked, opportunity for an "inference phase." Unlike instantaneous responses, these applications can tolerate a few seconds, minutes, or even hours of computation to refine a solution. The availability of efficient simulators and accurate scoring functions further amplifies this potential.
- **Inspiration from LLMs:** The recent success of inference strategies in Large Language Models (e.g., chain-of-thought prompting, sampling, ensembling) highlights the potential for similar gains in other AI domains, including RL. This suggests that "compute-optimal inference" is a critical, yet underexplored, aspect for RL.
- **Generalizability of Findings:** By formulating the problem in the highly complex Dec-POMDP framework, the authors aim to ensure that their findings are broadly applicable, as Dec-POMDPs subsume simpler problem formulations like MDPs.
- **Providing Practical Guidance:** The research seeks to offer concrete insights and tools for practitioners to better evaluate, deploy, and leverage RL systems, especially when faced with real-world constraints on time and compute during execution. The goal is to shift focus from solely improving training to also optimizing the crucial inference phase.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a comprehensive and systematic approach to evaluate the impact of inference strategies in complex multi-agent RL settings.

**Problem Formulation:** The problem is framed as solving a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP), which accurately captures the complexity of real-world scenarios involving multiple agents, partial information, and combinatorial action spaces. A clear distinction is drawn between the **training phase** (unconstrained in time/compute, focused on optimizing the policy for a distribution of instances) and the **inference phase** (constrained by a fixed time limit T_max and compute capacity B_max, focused on finding the best solution for a *single* new instance).

**Inference Strategies Investigated:** The paper provides a unifying view of four popular inference-strategy paradigms, adapted for the multi-agent setting:
1. **Stochastic Policy Sampling:** The simplest strategy, generating diverse solutions by sampling actions stochastically from the trained policy rather than deterministically choosing greedy actions.
2. **Tree Search (Simulation-Guided Beam Search - SGBS):** This method (Choo et al., 2022) explores partial solutions by expanding promising nodes (top-K actions from the policy) and evaluating them via simulated rollouts, then pruning to keep the best ones. The adaptation for MARL involves selecting top joint actions or sampling multiple times from a node for auto-regressive policies.
3. **Online Fine-tuning:** This approach (Bello et al., 2017) adapts the pre-trained policy parameters at inference time by performing policy gradient updates using rollouts on the specific problem instance.
4. **Diversity-Based Approaches (COMPASS):** A state-of-the-art method for combinatorial optimization (Chalumeau et al., 2023b). COMPASS pre-trains a policy conditioned on a continuous latent vector, creating a collection of diverse, specialized policies. At inference time, a search algorithm (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy - CMA-ES) explores this latent space to find the optimal latent vector (and thus the optimal specialized policy) for the given instance. For MARL, a single latent space is used for all agents to maintain tractability.

**Base Policies:** Three widely-used and state-of-the-art Multi-Agent RL algorithms are used to train the initial "base policies":
- **Independent PPO (IPPO):** A decentralized approach where each agent trains its own PPO policy.
- **Multi-Agent PPO (MAPPO):** A centralized training, decentralized execution approach.
- **SABLE:** A recent, performant sequence modeling approach for MARL (Mahjoub et al., 2025).

**Benchmark Tasks:** The study evaluates these strategies on 17 challenging multi-agent RL tasks specifically chosen for their difficulty and the demonstrated struggle of existing SOTA methods to achieve high zero-shot performance. These include tasks from:
- **Multi-Robot Warehouse (RWARE):** Requires coordination for package delivery with sparse rewards.
- **StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge (SMACv2):** Real-time combat scenarios with diverse, randomly generated teams.
- **Connector:** Models Printed Circuit Board (PCB) routing, requiring agents to connect targets without collision.
All environments exhibit combinatorial action spaces, partial observability, and demand tight coordination. JAX-based implementations (from Jumanji and JaxMARL) are used for efficiency.

**Training Protocol:** All base policies (IPPO, MAPPO, SABLE) and COMPASS checkpoints are trained until convergence to establish a clear performance ceiling for zero-shot execution, providing a strong baseline for measuring the impact of inference strategies. COMPASS training involves an additional phase to reincarnate base policies with latent conditioning and foster diversity.

**Evaluation Protocol:** The evaluation is extensive and designed to mimic real-world constraints:
- **Budgeting:** Performance is measured under varying wall-clock time budgets (30 to 300 seconds) and compute capacities (4 to 256 parallel attempts).
- **Hardware:** All experiments are conducted on a uniform hardware setup (NVIDIA-A100-SXM4-80GB GPU) to ensure fair comparison of time costs.
- **Statistical Robustness:** Each experiment involves 128 independent seeds.
- **Metrics:** Inter-Quartile Mean (IQM) and 95% stratified bootstrap confidence intervals are used for aggregation across tasks, along with normalized performance scores and win-rates where applicable. A total of over 60,000 episodes are evaluated, making it the largest study on inference strategies for complex RL to date.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several striking results that underscore the critical role of inference strategies:

- **Massive Performance Gains:** Inference-time search delivers a substantial performance boost over zero-shot state-of-the-art. Across 17 complex RL tasks, the best inference strategy achieved an **average improvement of 45%**, and in the most favorable case, a **126% improvement**, using only a few seconds of additional wall-clock time (e.g., 30 seconds) during execution.
- **Correlation with Task Complexity:** The performance improvement facilitated by inference strategies is directly correlated with task complexity, showing significant (almost exponential) gains in harder scenarios where zero-shot policies struggle the most. This suggests inference-time search becomes even more crucial as RL tackles increasingly realistic and difficult problems.
- **COMPASS as the Leading Strategy:** Among the evaluated inference strategies, COMPASS consistently emerges as the top performer across a wide range of tasks and base algorithms. When combined with the SABLE base policy, **SABLE + COMPASS** achieves aggregated performance close to 100% normalized score, setting a new state-of-the-art.
- **Nuanced Performance with Budget:**
 - **Stochastic sampling** provides a robust and effective baseline, often outperforming online fine-tuning under small time budgets.
 - **Online fine-tuning** shows high variance. While it can approach COMPASS's performance on some tasks with large budgets, it frequently struggles and can be detrimental by reducing the number of attempts within the time budget, or by getting stuck in local optima due to unstable policy gradients. This contradicts the naive assumption that merely "over-fitting" to the instance through fine-tuning is always optimal.
 - **Simulation-Guided Beam Search (SGBS)** performs competitively overall, even excelling in low-budget regimes (e.g., <100 seconds and <10 parallel attempts) for certain tasks, though it struggles with some Connector tasks.
- **Impressive Compute Scaling of COMPASS:** Under a fixed time budget (300 seconds), COMPASS exhibits the most favorable scaling properties with increasing compute capacity (number of parallel attempts). Its performance scales almost linearly before plateauing as it approaches the practical limits of optimal performance (over 95% win-rate). This is attributed to its ability to exploit the diversity encoded in its latent space through parallel exploration and the efficiency of the CMA-ES search mechanism in navigating this space.
- **Applicability Beyond Multi-Agent RL:** Initial supplementary experiments on the single-agent Craftax benchmark demonstrate similar benefits, with stochastic sampling yielding a 37% performance boost over zero-shot within a 30-second budget. This suggests the findings are broadly applicable across RL settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning and decision-making, with profound implications for both academic research and industrial applications.

- **Paradigm Shift in RL Evaluation and Deployment:** The most significant impact is the call for a fundamental shift in how RL systems are designed, evaluated, and deployed. The paper compellingly argues that inference-time strategies are not peripheral post-processing steps but rather core components essential for maximizing performance in complex, real-world scenarios. This encourages researchers to focus not just on training algorithms but also on robust, scalable inference mechanisms.
- **Unlocking Performance Ceilings:** The demonstrated ability to break through performance ceilings that even converged zero-shot policies cannot surpass is critical. This opens new avenues for solving previously intractable problems, making RL a more viable solution for high-stakes industrial optimization tasks such as PCB routing, bin packing, and train scheduling, where some execution-time budget is acceptable and highly valuable.
- **Practical Guidance for Practitioners:** The comprehensive evaluation across various time and compute budgets, visualized through contour plots, provides invaluable practical guidance for engineers and decision-makers. They can now make informed choices about inference strategies based on their specific resource constraints and desired performance levels. The release of code and experimental data further supports adoption and reproducibility.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research directions. It explicitly identifies the need to investigate combinations of existing inference paradigms to leverage their complementary strengths and to study how these strategies perform under out-of-distribution instances, which is crucial for real-world robustness.
- **Bridging Gaps in MARL and CO:** By systematically addressing inference in complex MARL problems and drawing parallels with successful inference strategies in LLMs, the paper helps unify disparate research threads and highlight under-explored areas within multi-agent RL and combinatorial optimization. The impressive scaling properties of SABLE + COMPASS demonstrate a robust solution for ever-increasing problem complexity.

In conclusion, "Breaking the Performance Ceiling in Reinforcement Learning requires Inference Strategies" redefines the scope of RL solutions. It firmly establishes inference-time search as an indispensable component for achieving high performance in complex decision-making tasks, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with current RL technology.
