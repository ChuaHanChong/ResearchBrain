# V$^{2}$-SAM: Marrying SAM2 with Multi-Prompt Experts for Cross-View Object Correspondence

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called V-SAM adapts the SAM2 foundation model for cross-view object correspondence, particularly in ego-exo settings, by integrating geometry-aware and appearance-guided multi-prompt experts. It achieved state-of-the-art results on Ego-Exo4D, with 46.3 IoU for Ego2Exo and 49.6 IoU for Exo2Ego, and showed strong generalization on DAVIS-2017 and HANDAL-X.

## Problem

- Identifying and associating the same object across different camera viewpoints is challenging due to drastic variations in viewpoint, appearance, scale, and shape, especially in ego-exo scenarios.
- Large single-view segmentation models like SAM2 rely on precise spatial prompts, which become problematic when target object positions shift significantly between views, hindering direct application in cross-view settings.
- Existing referring-based SAM variants often struggle with severe appearance changes and can reduce SAM2's inherent localization precision by removing spatial prompts.

## Method

- Developed V-SAM, a unified framework that adapts SAM2 for cross-view object correspondence by retaining its core components (Image Encoder, Prompt Encoder, Mask Decoder).
- Introduced a Cross-View Anchor Prompt Generator (V-Anchor) that uses DINOv3 features and stratified sampling to establish geometry-aware, coordinate-based prompts across views.
- Designed a Cross-View Visual Prompt Generator (V-Visual) with a learnable Visual Prompt Matcher (VPMatcher) to capture appearance-based cues, and integrated these into a multi-expert training scheme with a Post-hoc Cyclic Consistency Selector (PCCS).

## Results

- V-SAM achieved new state-of-the-art performance on Ego-Exo4D, scoring **46.3** IoU for Ego2Exo and **49.6** IoU for Exo2Ego, surpassing the previous best by 3.7 and **5.5** IoU points, respectively.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance across all metrics on DAVIS-2017 for video object tracking and strong zero-shot generalization on HANDAL-X with **77.2** IoU, outperforming prior methods by a large margin.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of both the V-Anchor module for geometric localization and the VPMatcher for appearance alignment, with the multi-expert combination and PCCS yielding the highest overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SAM2's spatial prompting capability can be effectively leveraged for cross-view scenarios by generating robust, geometry-aware anchor points through dense feature matching and spatial stratification.
> - Combining distinct experts for geometry-aware (V-Anchor) and appearance-guided (V-Visual) prompts effectively addresses the diverse challenges of cross-view variations, leading to enhanced robustness.
> - Dynamically selecting the most reliable expert's prediction using a lightweight point-level cyclic consistency check (PCCS) during inference improves overall performance and adaptability without significant computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Cross-view object correspondence is a fundamental task in computer vision that aims to identify and associate the same object across different camera viewpoints, segmenting it in a target view given its mask in a query view. This capability is relevant for various applications, including multi-view scene understanding, video perception, and embodied artificial intelligence (AI). A particularly challenging instance of this task is ego–exo object correspondence, where an object is simultaneously observed from a moving first-person (ego-centric) camera and a static third-person (exo-centric) camera. This scenario introduces difficulties due to significant variations in viewpoint, appearance, scale, and shape, as well as complex background clutter, dynamic motions, and occlusions.

Recent advancements in single-view segmentation have been driven by large foundation models, such as Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM2), which demonstrate robust mask generation given spatially grounded prompts like coordinates or bounding boxes. However, directly applying these models to cross-view scenarios is not straightforward. SAM2's reliance on spatially localized prompts becomes problematic when the target object's position changes drastically across views. While some extensions, like referring-based SAM variants, replace explicit positional prompts with visual cues, they often face limitations, including failures under severe appearance changes and a potential reduction in SAM2’s inherent localization precision by removing spatial prompts.

Existing approaches to cross-view object correspondence can be broadly categorized into matching-based methods and learnable model-based methods. Matching-based methods, such as O-MaMa and DOMR, often rely on post-hoc matching of mask proposals generated by external segmentation models, which can limit their learning and generalization capacity. Learnable model-based methods, exemplified by ObjectRelator, directly learn cross-view object segmentation. The present work contributes to the latter paradigm, seeking to improve robustness and generalization by integrating coordinate-based cues into a SAM2-based framework, an area not extensively explored previously for cross-view correspondence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework, termed V²-SAM, that adapts the SAM2 foundation model from single-view segmentation to the challenging task of cross-view object correspondence, particularly focusing on the ego–exo setting. The authors were motivated by two central questions arising from the limitations of existing methods when confronted with drastic viewpoint and appearance variations in cross-view scenarios:

1. **Can SAM2’s spatial prompting capability be unlocked for cross-view scenarios?** SAM2's strength lies in its ability to generate high-quality masks when provided with precise spatial prompts. However, direct application in cross-view settings is impeded because the target object’s coordinates shift significantly between views, making manual or direct position transfer infeasible. Referring-based segmentation models attempt to address this by using visual cues, but they tend to struggle with large appearance discrepancies and sacrifice SAM2's robust spatial localization power. The motivation here was to find a method that could re-establish reliable coordinate-based prompting across views despite geometric distortions and viewpoint changes.
2. **Can spatial and visual prompts complement each other to further enhance cross-view segmentation performance?** While spatial prompts are crucial for localization, visual prompts are essential for identifying objects based on their appearance, especially when geometric cues are ambiguous or absent. The authors observed that geometry-aware methods excel at determining "where an object is," while appearance-guided methods are more effective at identifying "what an object looks like." This led to the motivation to integrate both types of prompts within a single framework to leverage their respective strengths and address the diverse challenges posed by various cross-view conditions (e.g., cluttered backgrounds, dynamic motions, occlusions, and appearance changes).

By addressing these questions, the research aims to create a robust and adaptive cross-view object correspondence framework that can effectively mitigate the inherent difficulties introduced by viewpoint and appearance discrepancies, thereby improving segmentation accuracy and generalization across different datasets and scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The V²-SAM framework is built upon the core components of SAM2, specifically retaining its Image Encoder, Prompt Encoder, and Mask Decoder, while omitting memory-related modules to focus on frame-level correspondence. Given a query image ($I_q$), a target image ($I_t$), and a query object mask ($M_q$), the system aims to predict the corresponding object mask in the target view ($\hat{M_t}$). The methodology involves two distinct prompt generators, a multi-expert training scheme, and a post-hoc selection mechanism.

**Cross-View Anchor Prompt Generator (V²-Anchor)**
The V²-Anchor module is designed to restore SAM2's coordinate-based prompting capability in cross-view settings by establishing geometry-aware correspondences. It utilizes features from DINOv3, a vision transformer known for its fine-grained, spatially aware representations.
1. **Feature Matching:** DINOv3 patch-level features are extracted for both $I_q$ and $I_t$. A dense correspondence heatmap is computed using cosine similarity between query and target patches. For each patch within the foreground region defined by $M_q$, the most similar target patch is identified, yielding a set of point-level correspondences. This foreground-constrained matching helps reduce background noise.
2. **Point Stratification:** The initial set of matched target points may contain redundancies or clusters. A stratified sampling process is applied to enforce a minimum pairwise distance ($\tau$) among the points, resulting in a more compact and spatially diverse set of high-quality correspondences.
3. **Coordinate Transformation and Prompt Encoding:** The refined sparse correspondences are transformed into the canonical coordinate space compatible with SAM2. These transformed coordinates are then fed into SAM2's prompt encoder to generate geometry-based anchor prompts ($P_{q2t}^{anchor}$).

**Cross-View Visual Prompt Generator (V²-Visual)**
The V²-Visual module captures appearance-based cues to identify visually similar objects across views, complementing the geometric focus of V²-Anchor.
1. **Region-level Feature Extraction:** SAM2's encoder extracts visual feature maps from $I_q$ and $I_t$. Mask pooling is then applied using $M_q$ and the ground-truth target mask $M_t$ (during training) to obtain region-level features ($v_q, v_t$).
2. **Visual Prompt Matcher (VPMatcher):** This learnable module bridges the cross-view appearance gap and consists of two branches:
 - **Feature Mapping Branch:** Takes the query region feature $v_q$ and a mask embedding from $M_q$ to learn semantically consistent representations. It uses linear projections for query, key, and value spaces, followed by Transformer-based cross-attention layers and a residual multi-layer perceptron (Res-MLP) to predict a cross-view embedding ($\hat{v}_c$). Spatial gating suppresses background noise.
 - **Structural Mapping Branch:** Reconstructs a coarse target mask from the input query mask $M_q$, leveraging a lightweight CNN-based mask encoder and decoder. Semantic conditioning from $v_q$ is injected via feature-wise linear modulation. This branch produces a predicted cross-view mask ($\hat{M}_c$).
 - **Visual Prompt Generation:** New region features ($v_c'$) are obtained by mask pooling on SAM2 features using $\hat{M}_c$. The final appearance-guided prompt ($P_{q2t}^{visual}$) is formed by concatenating $\hat{v}_c$ and $v_c'$ and projecting them through a lightweight MLP.

**Multi-Expert Training**
Recognizing the complementary strengths of geometric and visual cues, a multi-expert framework is designed. Three experts are trained:
1. **Anchor Expert:** Uses the geometry-aware prompt ($P_{q2t}^{anchor}$) from V²-Anchor. The V²-Anchor module is non-learnable, and this expert reuses the pre-trained SAM2 mask decoder without further training.
2. **Visual Expert:** Uses the visual-guided prompt ($P_{q2t}^{visual}$) from V²-Visual. This expert trains the VPMatcher and the SAM2 mask decoder, keeping the SAM2 encoder frozen.
3. **Fusion Expert:** Combines both $P_{q2t}^{anchor}$ and $P_{q2t}^{visual}$ into a unified prompt embedding. This expert also trains the VPMatcher and the SAM2 mask decoder.
The training objective for the Visual and Fusion Experts comprises three loss components:
- **Visual Contrastive Loss ($L_v$):** Enforces cross-view region-level feature mapping by encouraging positive pairs to be close and negative pairs to be distant in the embedding space.
- **Mask Prediction Loss ($L_m$):** A combination of pixel-wise cross-entropy and Dice loss to ensure accurate spatial localization.
- **Structural Constraint Loss ($L_s$):** Applies a mask loss to the VPMatcher's reconstructed mask ($\hat{M}_c$) to encourage learning a consistent spatial transformation. The total loss $L = \lambda_1 L_v + \lambda_2 L_s + \lambda_3 L_m$.

**Post-hoc Cyclic Consistency Selector (PCCS)**
During inference, all three experts generate candidate target masks. The PCCS dynamically selects the most reliable prediction based on bidirectional consistency between the query and target masks. It back-projects each expert's predicted target mask ($\hat{M}_{t,k}$) to the query view using the V²-Anchor mechanism (reversing $I_q$ and $I_t$). Instead of reconstructing a full query-view mask, PCCS operates at the point level, computing the average distance between the back-projected points and reference points sampled from the original query mask $M_q$. The expert whose prediction yields the smallest cyclic distance is selected. This lightweight approach avoids an extra decoding pass while maintaining selection accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The V²-SAM framework was evaluated on three distinct benchmarks: Ego-Exo4D for ego–exo object correspondence, DAVIS-2017 for video object tracking, and HANDAL-X for robotic-ready cross-view correspondence. The SAM2-Hiera-Large model was used as the base segmentation backbone, and a DINOv3 ViT-L/16 Encoder served as the feature extractor.

**Results on Ego-Exo4D:**
- V²-SAM (Multi-Experts) achieved new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance, with 46.3 IoU for Ego2Exo and 49.6 IoU for Exo2Ego, surpassing the previous SOTA (O-MaMa) by 3.7 and 5.5 IoU points, respectively.
- Even the single-expert version of V²-SAM demonstrated competitive performance, exceeding all prior baselines with a total IoU of 45.9.
- V²-SAM achieved these results with fewer trainable parameters (15M) compared to models like ObjectRelator (1.6B parameters), indicating computational efficiency alongside performance gains.
- Ref-SAM*, a SAM2-adapted baseline, showed weaker performance, particularly in Ego2Exo, highlighting the challenges of direct visual-reference guidance for cross-view tasks and V²-SAM's ability to overcome these.

**Results on DAVIS-2017:**
- V²-SAM achieved the highest performance across all evaluation metrics (J&Fm, Jm, Fm) for video object tracking, outperforming existing self-supervised correspondence models by a notable margin on frame pairs separated by 20 frames. This demonstrated the framework's generality and robustness to large temporal and viewpoint shifts.

**Results on HANDAL-X:**
- In a zero-shot learning (ZSL) setting, where the model was trained on Ego-Exo4D and evaluated on HANDAL-X, V²-SAM demonstrated strong cross-view generalization. The multi-expert version achieved 77.2 IoU, substantially outperforming PSALM (39.9 IoU) and ObjectRelator (42.8 IoU). This indicates V²-SAM's capacity for practical human-to-robot transfer scenarios.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Individual Expert Contribution:** Removing the V²-Anchor module from the Anchor Expert resulted in near-zero performance (1.5 total IoU), underscoring its criticality for geometric localization. Similarly, removing the VPMatcher from the Visual Expert led to a significant degradation (3.0 total IoU), affirming the necessity of cross-view appearance alignment.
- **Expert Combinations:** Combining the Anchor and Visual Experts yielded a notable performance gain, confirming their complementary nature. The Fusion Expert, which jointly processes both prompt types, achieved balanced performance comparable to the two-expert combination. The highest overall performance was attained when integrating all three experts (Anchor, Visual, and Fusion) with the PCCS.
- **Expert Performance Analysis:** Qualitative analysis showed that the Anchor Expert performed well in structured, static environments (e.g., Cooking, Health), while the Visual Expert excelled in dynamic, human-centric scenarios (e.g., Basketball, Music) where appearance and deformation tolerance are crucial. The Fusion Expert showed broad coverage across scene categories. PCCS adaptively selected the optimal expert based on scene conditions.
- **V²-Anchor Point Count:** Experiments on varying the number of sparse anchor points in V²-Anchor revealed that a minimal set of high-confidence correspondences (e.g., 1 point) was more effective, with denser anchor distributions (e.g., 5, 10, 30 points) leading to performance degradation, likely due to increased noise.
- **PCCS Mechanism:** The proposed PCCS, which uses point-level cyclic consistency (Cycle-Points), achieved comparable or superior accuracy to a mask-based cyclic consistency selector (Cycle-Mask) while significantly reducing runtime and computational overhead.

**Qualitative Results:**
- Visual examples illustrated V²-SAM's ability to generate accurate and consistent predictions across challenging Ego2Exo and Exo2Ego scenarios, outperforming Ref-SAM.
- The visualizations showcased how the Anchor Expert, Visual Expert, and Fusion Expert responded differently to scene characteristics (e.g., cluttered backgrounds, dynamic motion, appearance changes), and how PCCS effectively selected the best prediction.
- Visualizations on HANDAL-X and DAVIS-17 confirmed the framework's robustness in segmenting small tools, handling articulated motion, occlusion, and maintaining temporal coherence across diverse scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The V²-SAM framework represents a contribution to the field of cross-view object correspondence by effectively adapting the SAM2 foundation model to address its inherent challenges. The primary significance lies in its architectural innovations and demonstrated performance:

- **Architectural Adaptation of SAM2:** V²-SAM is presented as the first unified framework that specifically adapts SAM2 for cross-view object correspondence, particularly for the challenging ego–exo setting. This extends the applicability of powerful single-view segmentation models to multi-view scenarios, which was previously non-trivial due to the reliance on spatially grounded prompts.
- **Novel Prompt Generation Mechanisms:** The introduction of the Cross-View Anchor Prompt Generator (V²-Anchor) enables coordinate-based prompting for SAM2 in cross-view settings, a capability previously lacking. This module leverages geometry-aware features to localize objects accurately across views. Complementing this, the Cross-View Visual Prompt Generator (V²-Visual) enhances appearance-guided cues through a novel visual prompt matcher, addressing issues of appearance variation.
- **Robust Multi-Expert Design:** The framework's multi-prompt expert design, combined with the Post-hoc Cyclic Consistency Selector (PCCS), offers an adaptive solution for diverse cross-view scenarios. By leveraging the complementary strengths of geometric and visual cues and dynamically selecting the most reliable expert, V²-SAM demonstrates enhanced robustness and generalization capacity.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** V²-SAM achieves new state-of-the-art performance across multiple established benchmarks, including Ego-Exo4D, DAVIS-2017, and HANDAL-X. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed components and the overall framework.

The potential impact of V²-SAM is broad, influencing several areas of AI and computer vision:

- **Multi-View Scene Understanding:** Improved cross-view correspondence can significantly enhance the ability of AI systems to build consistent 3D representations of environments from multiple camera feeds.
- **Video Perception:** The framework's performance on video object tracking tasks suggests its utility in applications requiring robust object tracking and segmentation across frames, especially under significant temporal and viewpoint changes.
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** The strong generalization demonstrated on the HANDAL-X dataset, which focuses on manipulable objects for human-to-robot transfer, indicates that V²-SAM could be instrumental in developing more capable robotic systems that can understand and interact with objects in complex, real-world settings from varying perspectives.
- **Foundation Model Extension:** The work provides a methodology for extending large foundation models like SAM2 to more complex, cross-domain tasks, offering insights for future research in adapting such models for broader applicability.
