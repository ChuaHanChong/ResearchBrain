# MASSV: Multimodal Adaptation and Self-Data Distillation for Speculative Decoding of Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Cerebras Systems researchers developed MASSV, a framework that accelerates Vision-Language Model (VLM) inference through speculative decoding by adapting existing smaller language models into multimodal drafters. This approach, leveraging a two-phase training with self-data distillation, achieves end-to-end speedups up to 1.46x and generalizes to larger VLM variants without additional training.

## Problem

- High computational cost and latency of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) during inference due to their autoregressive nature.
- Applying speculative decoding to VLMs is challenging due to architectural incompatibility (small drafters lack vision components) and distribution mismatch between unimodal drafters and VLM outputs.
- Prior methods either neglect crucial visual context or require computationally expensive training of multimodal drafters from scratch.

## Method

- MASSV employs a ==two-phase training strategy== to convert a small language model (SLM) into an efficient multimodal drafter for LLaVA-style VLMs.
- Phase 1: ==Multimodal Projector Pretraining== trains a lightweight projector to map visual features from a shared, frozen vision encoder into the SLM's embedding space.
- Phase 2: ==Self-Distilled Visual Instruction Tuning (SDViT)== fine-tunes the entire drafter (projector and SLM) using responses generated by the target VLM with diverse sampling, meticulously aligning the drafter's token prediction distribution to the target's visually-grounded outputs.

## Results

- MASSV achieved overall end-to-end inference speedups of up to **1.28x** for Qwen2.5-VL 7B Instruct and **1.18x** for Gemma3-12B IT, with peak speedups reaching **1.46x** on visually-intensive tasks like COCO Captioning.
- The Mean Accepted Length (MAL) increased by **30.1%** overall for Qwen2.5-VL 7B and by **47.5%** for visually-intensive tasks compared to text-only drafting baselines.
- The method demonstrated generalization to larger models, providing **1.17x** speedup for Qwen2.5-VL 32B and **1.14x** for Gemma3-27B, even without direct SDViT alignment on these larger variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective VLM speculative decoding requires both architectural adaptation (multimodal projector) and precise alignment of token prediction distributions via self-data distillation.
> - Self-Distilled Visual Instruction Tuning (SDViT) is crucial for maximizing token acceptance rates by ensuring the drafter's output closely matches the target VLM's distribution, especially for visually-conditioned generation.
> - Leveraging smaller language models from the same family as the target VLM facilitates compatibility and allows for generalization of learned acceleration benefits to larger models within that family.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have brought about unprecedented capabilities in various AI tasks, from reasoning and code generation to visual understanding. However, these powerful models come with substantial computational costs, particularly during inference, largely due to their autoregressive nature where tokens are generated sequentially.

**Speculative Decoding (SD)** has emerged as a crucial technique to accelerate LLM inference without compromising output quality. It achieves this by using a smaller, faster "draft model" to propose multiple candidate tokens, which are then verified in parallel by the larger "target model." This approach significantly reduces the number of sequential operations, amortizing the computational cost across multiple tokens. While SD has been well-explored for text-only LLMs (e.g., Chen et al., 2023; Leviathan et al., 2023), its application to VLMs introduces unique and complex challenges.

VLMs integrate visual information alongside text, mapping both modalities into a joint embedding space. This multimodal conditioning poses two fundamental hurdles for speculative decoding:
1. **Architectural Incompatibility:** Small language models suitable for drafting typically lack the architectural components (like a vision encoder or multimodal projector) required to process visual inputs.
2. **Distribution Mismatch:** Even if a small model could process visual input, a unimodal draft model's token predictions would inevitably fail to align with the visually-grounded outputs of a VLM target model, leading to low acceptance rates and diminished speedups.

Prior attempts to extend SD to VLMs have either involved excluding image tokens entirely (treating the VLM as a text-only model during drafting), which neglects crucial visual context and limits acceleration gains for visually-grounded tasks, or training small multimodal models from scratch (Gagrani et al., 2024). Training from scratch is computationally expensive and doesn't guarantee distribution alignment with existing large VLMs. Ensemble-based methods (Lee et al., 2024) combine multiple drafting strategies but act as a post-hoc aggregation, not fundamentally addressing the core distribution mismatch between individual drafters and the target.

MASSV positions itself within this landscape by offering a novel, principled approach that directly tackles these architectural and distributional challenges. It proposes to adapt *existing* smaller language models from the *same family* as the target VLM, leveraging their inherent compatibility in tokenization and vocabulary. Furthermore, it introduces a sophisticated self-data distillation strategy specifically designed to align the draft model's multimodal output distribution with that of the target VLM. By doing so, MASSV aims to achieve superior token acceptance rates and end-to-end speedups, outperforming conventional text-only drafting baselines and offering a more scalable and efficient solution than training new multimodal drafters from scratch. The work also builds upon recent advancements in knowledge distillation, particularly self-data distillation (Thangarasa et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2024), extending these concepts to the complex domain of multimodal speculative decoding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the MASSV research is to **significantly accelerate the inference speed of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using speculative decoding, without compromising the quality of the generated outputs.** This goal is motivated by the high computational costs associated with VLM inference, which currently limit their real-world deployability and responsiveness.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving the MASSV framework are:

1. **Overcome Architectural Incompatibility for Multimodal Drafting:** Existing small language models (SLMs), which are ideal candidates for draft models due to their efficiency, inherently lack the architectural components to process visual inputs. The first objective is to seamlessly integrate visual processing capabilities into these SLMs, enabling them to serve as *multimodal* drafters. This involves connecting the target VLM's vision encoder to the SLM via a lightweight, trainable projector, ensuring architectural compatibility and shared visual feature extraction.
2. **Address Distribution Mismatch between Draft and Target VLMs:** A critical challenge is that unimodal (text-only) draft models fail to generate token predictions that align with the visually-grounded outputs of VLM target models. This misalignment leads to low token acceptance rates in speculative decoding, negating potential speedups. The second, and perhaps most crucial, objective is to develop a training methodology that explicitly aligns the draft model's token prediction distribution with that of the target VLM, especially for visually-conditioned outputs.
3. **Enhance Token Acceptance Rates for Practical Speedups:** The efficacy of speculative decoding directly correlates with the "mean accepted length" (MAL) – the average number of tokens accepted per target model forward pass. A core objective is to maximize this MAL by ensuring the draft model's predictions are as close as possible to the target VLM's, thereby translating to higher end-to-end inference speedups.
4. **Develop a Scalable and Generalizable Solution:** The authors aim for a method that is not only effective for specific VLM architectures but also scalable across different model families (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma3) and capable of generalizing to larger model variants within those families without requiring extensive re-training. This would provide a computationally efficient adaptation strategy.
5. **Leverage Existing Model Families:** Instead of building multimodal draft models from scratch, the motivation is to adapt readily available smaller language models from the same family as the target VLM. This choice leverages inherent compatibilities in tokenization and vocabulary, simplifying the drafting process.

In essence, MASSV seeks to transform general-purpose SLMs into highly effective VLM drafters by imparting multimodal understanding and meticulously aligning their output distributions with complex VLM targets, thereby unlocking the full potential of speculative decoding for multimodal AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MASSV (Multimodal Adaptation and Self-Data Distillation for Speculative Decoding of Vision-Language Models) employs a two-phase training approach to adapt a small language model (SLM) into an efficient multimodal drafter for a target Vision-Language Model (VLM). This methodology is specifically designed for LLaVA-style VLMs, which feature a modular architecture with a separate vision encoder and a language model connected by a projection layer.

**Core Components of a VLM and Drafter:**
- **Target VLM ($M^p_{VLM}$):** Comprises a vision encoder ($\phi^p_I$), a multimodal projector ($g^p_\theta$), and a language model ($M_p$).
- **VLM Drafter ($M^q_{VLM}$):** Constructed by sharing the target's frozen vision encoder ($\phi^p_I$), adding a randomly initialized *trainable* multimodal projector ($g^q_\psi$), and a small language model ($M_q$) from the same family as $M_p$. The key is that $M_q$ is initially a text-only SLM. The output dimension of $g^q_\psi$ is configured to match the embedding dimension of $M_q$. Sharing the vision encoder is crucial; it ensures the drafter processes the exact same visual features as the target, avoiding redundant vision encoding during inference and reducing computational costs.

The training process consists of two sequential phases:

#### 3.1. Phase 1: Multimodal Projector Pretraining (Architectural Adaptation)

This phase focuses on establishing initial visual grounding and architectural compatibility for the drafter.
1. **Objective:** To train the newly introduced multimodal projector ($g^q_\psi$) to effectively map image features extracted by the shared vision encoder into the embedding space of the small language model ($M_q$).
2. **Procedure:** The vision encoder ($\phi^p_I$) and the small language model ($M_q$) are *frozen*. Only the projector ($g^q_\psi$) is trainable during this phase.
3. **Data:** A large dataset of paired image-caption data, such as LLaVA-Pretrain-LCS-558K, is used.
4. **Loss Function:** A standard cross-entropy loss is minimized, predicting the next token of the caption given the previous tokens and the projected visual features.
 - $L_{pre}(\psi) = -\sum_{j=1}^{N} \sum_{i=1}^{|C_j|} \log q_\psi (c^j_i | c^j_{1:i-1}, V_j)$, where $V_j = g^q_\psi(\phi^p_I(I_j))$.
5. **Outcome:** The projector learns to transform visual information into a representation understandable by the SLM, thus equipping the SLM with basic multimodal capabilities.

#### 3.2. Phase 2: Self-Distilled Visual Instruction Tuning (SDViT - Multimodal Alignment)

This phase is critical for aligning the drafter's token-level prediction distribution with that of the target VLM, which is essential for maximizing token acceptance rates in speculative decoding.
1. **Objective:** To fine-tune the entire drafter (projector $g^q_\psi$ and SLM $M_q$) such that its output token distribution closely matches the target VLM's distribution on visually-grounded tasks.
2. **Procedure (Self-Data Distillation):**
 - **Data Generation:** Unlike traditional instruction tuning that uses fixed human-annotated labels, SDViT employs self-data distillation. The *target VLM itself* ($M^p_{VLM}$) is used to generate responses ($y'_i$) for a visual instruction dataset (LLaVA-mix-665K), conditioned on both images ($I_i$) and text instructions ($X_i$).
 - **Diverse Sampling:** To prevent "teacher hacking" (where the smaller model overfits to imperfections of a fixed teacher distribution), the target VLM generates these responses using diverse sampling strategies, specifically across different temperature values with top-p sampling. This ensures the generated dataset is varied and representative of the target's full response distribution.
 - **Fine-tuning:** With the target VLM's vision encoder ($\phi^p_I$) still frozen, the drafter's projector ($g^q_\psi$) and SLM ($M_q$) are fine-tuned using the self-generated data ($y'_i$).
3. **Loss Function:** Cross-entropy loss is minimized with respect to the target VLM's generated responses.
 - $L_{SDViT}(\theta) = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{k=1}^{|y'_i|} \log q_\theta (y'^k_i | y'_{1:k-1,i}, X_i, V_i)$, where $\theta = \{\psi, \theta_q\}$ (projector and SLM parameters).
4. **Outcome:** By training on the target's actual outputs, the drafter's distribution ($q$) is directly optimized to overlap maximally with the target's distribution ($p$), leading to significantly higher token acceptance rates during speculative decoding. This process implicitly guides the drafter to learn the nuances of visual reasoning and token generation characteristic of the larger VLM.

**Inference Configuration:** During speculative decoding inference, the adapted VLM drafter ($M^q_{VLM}$) generates draft tokens autoregressively. The shared vision encoder ($\phi^p_I$) is invoked only once per image, providing features to both the drafter's projector and the target VLM, ensuring efficiency.

The theoretical underpinning for SDViT is supported by research showing that data diversity is critical in preventing "teacher hacking" during distillation, especially in complex domains like multimodal data where superficial shortcuts can easily be learned. MASSV's diverse sampling strategy directly addresses this, ensuring genuine alignment rather than brittle exploitation of teacher patterns.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate that MASSV significantly enhances the efficiency of VLM inference through speculative decoding, consistently outperforming conventional text-only drafting baselines across various models and tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and End-to-end Speedups

- **Mean Accepted Length (MAL) Improvements:** MASSV consistently achieves a higher MAL, indicating that more draft tokens are accepted per target VLM forward pass.
 - For **Qwen2.5-VL 7B Instruct** at temperature T=0, MASSV increased the overall MAL by **30.1%** (from 2.46 to 3.20 tokens). At T=1, MAL improved by 23.3% (from 2.58 to 3.18).
 - The largest relative gains were observed in **visually-intensive tasks** like COCO Captioning, where MAL increased by **47.5%** (from 2.21 to 3.26) at T=0, highlighting the critical role of multimodal drafting for such tasks.
 - Similar improvements were seen for the **Gemma3-12B IT** model, with MAL increasing by 15.6% (from 2.76 to 3.19) at T=0, demonstrating MASSV's effectiveness across different VLM architectures despite their unique characteristics (e.g., dynamic visual token count in Qwen vs. interleaved attention in Gemma).
- **End-to-end Inference Speedups:** These MAL improvements directly translate into substantial wall-clock speedups.
 - MASSV achieved an overall end-to-end speedup of **1.28x** for Qwen2.5-VL 7B Instruct at T=0.
 - On specific tasks, such as COCO captioning, speedups reached **1.46x**.
 - For Gemma3-12B IT, an acceleration of 1.18x was observed.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Larger Model Variants

- A significant finding is MASSV's ability to generalize to larger models within the same family, even without direct SDViT alignment on those larger targets.
 - MASSV, when trained with smaller target models (7B and 12B), provided meaningful gains for their larger counterparts: **1.17x speedup for Qwen2.5-VL 32B** and **1.14x for Gemma3-27B**.
- This suggests that the knowledge transfer and distribution alignment learned from smaller target VLMs can effectively "transfer" to larger, architecturally similar models, offering substantial computational and time savings.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Self-Distilled Visual Instruction Tuning (SDViT):**
 - SDViT was found to be **crucial** for effective multimodal drafting.
 - For Qwen2.5-VL 7B Instruct, MASSV without SDViT (MASSV w/o SDViT) yielded only a modest MAL of 2.56, barely above the baseline (2.46). With SDViT, it dramatically improved to 3.20.
 - For Gemma3-12B IT, skipping SDViT led to a **performance regression**, with MAL dropping to 2.33, a 13% decrease compared to the baseline's 2.74. This highlights that naive architectural adaptation without proper distribution alignment can be detrimental. With SDViT, MAL increased to 3.14 (14.6% improvement over baseline).
 - **Distribution Analysis (Total Variation Distance - TVD):** A histogram of TVD showed that drafters trained with SDViT produced a highly skewed distribution concentrated at low TVD values, indicating a much tighter alignment with the target's token distribution. In contrast, drafters without SDViT had a broader, heavier-tailed TVD distribution, signifying reduced alignment. This quantitatively confirms that SDViT effectively narrows the distributional gap.

- **Text-Only vs. Multimodal Drafting:**
 - Multimodal drafting consistently **outperformed text-only drafting** by the adapted VLM drafters.
 - For Qwen2.5-VL 7B Instruct, multimodal drafting achieved an MAL of 3.20, compared to 2.84 for text-only drafting (+12.7%).
 - For Gemma3-12B IT, multimodal drafting achieved an MAL of 3.19, versus 2.99 for text-only (+6.7%).
 - This confirms that while distribution alignment is primary, incorporating visual information provides additional, meaningful benefits for predicting the target VLM’s outputs, especially for visually-grounded content.

In summary, MASSV achieves substantial and consistent speedups by effectively addressing the unique challenges of multimodal speculative decoding. The two-phase training, particularly the self-distilled visual instruction tuning, is shown to be indispensable for achieving strong distribution alignment and, consequently, high token acceptance rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MASSV represents a significant advancement in the field of efficient AI inference, particularly for Vision-Language Models, and holds substantial potential impact across several dimensions:

1. **Accelerated VLM Inference and Cost Reduction:** The primary significance of MASSV lies in its demonstrated ability to deliver substantial end-to-end inference speedups (up to 1.46x) for VLMs. This directly translates to lower computational costs and reduced latency, making VLM deployment more economically viable and responsive. For industries relying on real-time visual AI (e.g., autonomous systems, content moderation, interactive AI assistants), these speedups are critical for practical adoption.

2. **Bridging the Modality Gap in Speculative Decoding:** MASSV is one of the first principled frameworks that effectively tackles the fundamental architectural incompatibility and distribution mismatch challenges when applying speculative decoding to multimodal models. By intelligently adapting existing SLMs and aligning their distributions through self-data distillation, it provides a robust solution where prior text-only or naive multimodal drafting approaches have fallen short.

3. **Scalability and Generalizability:** The method's success across different VLM families (Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma3) and its observed generalization to larger models within the same family without additional training are highly impactful. This generalization property implies that MASSV can be adapted to smaller, more manageable target models, and its benefits will scale to larger, more powerful variants, saving significant computational resources and development time. This makes MASSV a more versatile and future-proof solution compared to model-specific optimizations.

4. **Enhanced Visual Reasoning in Drafters:** The ablation studies highlight that self-distilled visual instruction tuning (SDViT) is crucial for performance and that multimodal drafting consistently outperforms text-only approaches. This indicates that MASSV empowers the smaller draft models with genuine visual reasoning capabilities, allowing them to better anticipate the target VLM's visually-grounded outputs, rather than just relying on superficial linguistic patterns. This leads to higher-quality draft proposals and superior acceptance rates.

5. **Democratization of VLM Deployment:** By making VLM inference faster and cheaper, MASSV can help democratize access to sophisticated multimodal AI capabilities. Researchers and developers with more constrained computational resources can deploy and experiment with powerful VLMs more readily, fostering further innovation.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several promising avenues for future research, including exploring its effectiveness with heterogeneous vocabularies in multimodal SD and conducting a deeper exploration of the generalization mechanism to larger models. It also reinforces the importance of knowledge distillation techniques, particularly self-data distillation, for aligning complex model distributions in novel multimodal contexts.

In conclusion, MASSV offers a "readily deployable solution" that addresses a critical bottleneck in VLM deployment. Its blend of architectural adaptation and sophisticated self-distillation provides a robust and scalable method for accelerating VLM inference, paving the way for more efficient, responsive, and widespread multimodal AI applications across diverse domains.
