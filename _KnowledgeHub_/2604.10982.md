# Ψ-Map: Panoptic Surface Integrated Mapping Enables Real2Sim Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> Zhejiang University researchers developed Ψ-Map, a panoptic surface integrated mapping system designed to create high-fidelity digital twins of large-scale environments. It achieves robust panoptic segmentation (PQ 74.12 on ScanNet-V2) and real-time rendering at 45 FPS by integrating geometric reinforcement and an end-to-end panoptic learning framework, enhancing Real2Sim transfer for robotic applications.

## Problem

- The Sim2Real Gap hinders deploying robotic policies learned in simulation to physical environments, requiring high-fidelity Real2Sim mapping for digital twin creation.
- Existing 3D reconstruction methods often lack geometric integrity (high-quality surface meshes) for physics simulation and struggle with consistent object-level panoptic understanding in large-scale scenes.
- Integrating high-dimensional semantic/instance features into 3D representations often creates computational bottlenecks, preventing real-time rendering necessary for robotic control loops.

## Method

- Ψ-Map uses ==2D Gaussian Surfels== as a foundational map, reinforced with absolute scale and normal constraints from a ==Self-Organizing Gaussian Mixture Model (SOGMM)== (SOGMM) for geometric fidelity.
- An end-to-end ==query-guided architecture== with multi-modal surfel features and distance-aware, frustum-constrained cross-attention provides panoptic segmentation.
- Rendering efficiency is improved via precise tile intersection using Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABBs) and a Top-K hard selection strategy for high-dimensional feature accumulation.

## Results

- Ψ-Map achieved an F1 score of **98.07** for geometric reconstruction on self-collected datasets, outperforming **13** baselines, and a C-L1 metric of **4.51** (lower is better) compared to **14.23****-25.30** for others.
- The system demonstrated state-of-the-art panoptic segmentation with a Panoptic Quality (PQ) of **74.12** and Recognition Quality (RQ) of **100.0** on the ScanNet-V2 benchmark.
- The full Ψ-Map framework achieved a rendering latency of **22 ms** (**45 FPS**), meeting real-time requirements for robotic control loops while maintaining high-dimensional feature output.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating SOGMM priors into 2D Gaussian Surfels effectively suppresses geometric artifacts like 'floaters' and ensures higher surface integrity for physical simulations.
> - An end-to-end query-guided panoptic segmentation pipeline, with dynamic query adjustment and geometric-semantic fusion, provides more globally consistent instance identities than multi-stage methods.
> - Optimizing rendering with precise tile intersection and Top-K feature selection drastically reduces computational load for high-dimensional features, enabling real-time performance at robotic control frequencies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a critical challenge in contemporary robotics: the Sim2Real Gap. Learning from simulation is a prevalent paradigm for developing robotic policies; however, the successful deployment of these policies in physical environments necessitates a high-fidelity Real2Sim mapping. This mapping facilitates the creation of a digital twin, enabling the migration of real-world environmental characteristics into simulation for policy adaptation.

Current Real2Sim research primarily concentrates on small-scale indoor environments, such as tabletop manipulation, often relying on monocular techniques, single-view semantics, and generative priors. These methods typically exhibit limitations in scalability and validation within expansive, complex scenes. Conversely, existing large-scale reconstruction frameworks frequently prioritize visual fidelity over metric accuracy, utilizing vision-only pipelines that may lack the geometric precision required for physical simulation and often overlook semantic integration.

Specifically, the paper identifies three core challenges in developing effective Real2Sim transfer:
1. **Geometric Integrity:** The need for high-quality surface meshes, rather than sparse point clouds, to support collision detection and contact dynamics in physics engines. Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods frequently encounter geometric degradation, such as collapse, holes, or "floaters," in large-scale scenes, largely due to reliance on sparse Structure-from-Motion (SfM) point clouds for initialization and a lack of strong structural regularization.
2. **Panoptic Understanding:** The requirement for object-level reasoning, moving beyond holistic maps to identify discrete, manipulatable entities for complex interactions. Earlier efforts embedding open-vocabulary features into radiance fields demonstrated potential but struggled with distinguishing multiple instances of the same semantic class. More recent multi-stage panoptic segmentation pipelines, which lift 2D instance labels into 3D, often suffer from error accumulation and the complexities of cross-frame association due to reliance on noisy 2D pseudo-labels.
3. **High-efficiency Rendering:** The necessity for photorealistic textures aligned with geometry, delivered at high frame rates to meet the demands of robotic simulation loops. Integrating high-dimensional semantic or instance features into 3DGS often results in significant performance bottlenecks, with inference frequencies below 10 FPS, which is insufficient for real-time robotic control.

The Ψ-Map framework positions itself as a comprehensive solution designed to concurrently address these limitations by integrating geometric reinforcement, end-to-end panoptic learning, and efficient rendering within a unified system. It aims to achieve geometric accuracy, coherent panoptic understanding, and real-time performance in large-scale environments, thereby providing a foundation for deploying robotic learning in real-world applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive panoptic reconstruction system, Ψ-Map, that enables seamless Real2Sim transfer. This system is designed to create high-fidelity digital twins of physical environments, facilitating the adaptation and deployment of robotic policies.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical gaps and requirements in current robotics and simulation research:

- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** The fundamental discrepancy between synthetic training data and real-world dynamics, known as the Sim2Real Gap, significantly hinders the direct deployment of policies learned in simulation to physical robots. A high-fidelity Real2Sim mapping is essential to mitigate this gap, allowing physical environment characteristics to be accurately represented in simulation for environment-specific policy adaptation.
- **Achieving Geometric Integrity for Physics Simulation:** Robotic applications require geometric representations that are precise enough to support realistic collision detection and contact dynamics within physics engines. Traditional visual-only reconstruction methods and existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) approaches often produce representations with geometric inaccuracies, such as sparse point clouds, holes, or "floaters," which are inadequate for physical realism. A key objective is to generate high-quality surface meshes with metric accuracy.
- **Enabling Object-Level Reasoning (Panoptic Understanding):** For robots to engage in complex interactions and manipulation tasks, they require an understanding of individual objects in their environment, including their semantic categories and distinct identities. Existing 3D reconstruction methods frequently provide holistic scene maps but lack the capability for consistent, object-level panoptic segmentation across different viewpoints, or rely on error-prone multi-stage pipelines. The objective is to achieve globally consistent panoptic understanding that identifies discrete, manipulatable entities.
- **Ensuring Real-Time Performance for Robotic Control Loops:** Robotic simulation and control demand high-frequency feedback and low-latency rendering. Incorporating high-dimensional semantic and instance features into 3D representations often introduces substantial computational bottlenecks, leading to inference rates that are too slow for real-time robotic operations. A core objective is to develop an efficient rendering pipeline that maintains photorealistic textures and high-dimensional semantic features at frame rates exceeding the requirements of typical robotic control loops (e.g., >50 FPS).
- **Unified System Solution:** The authors identify a critical need for a unified system that integrates geometric, semantic, and rendering components, rather than relying on separate modules that can accumulate errors. The motivation is to create a low-coupling yet highly efficient framework that addresses all Real2Sim requirements in an end-to-end manner, minimizing cumulative errors and supporting downstream policy verification and deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Ψ-Map framework is structured around three integrated modules: geometric reinforcement, end-to-end panoptic learning, and efficient rendering, all designed to enable Real2Sim transfer in large-scale environments.

#### 3.1. Geometric Representation and Reinforcement

The system adopts 2D Gaussian Surfels as its foundational map representation, an approach derived from LI-GS and 2DGS. Each surfel is explicitly defined within its local tangent plane, constraining its energy to a 2D manifold. This formulation is intended to improve geometric accuracy and suppress artifacts such as "floaters" compared to traditional 3DGS. The appearance at each pixel is rendered via $\alpha$-blending of overlapping surfels.

To enhance geometric fidelity, Ψ-Map integrates absolute scale information from 3D point clouds using a Self-Organizing Gaussian Mixture Model (SOGMM).
- **SOGMM Modeling:** Scene point clouds are fitted into a set of anisotropic Gaussian components through a recursive splitting strategy. This forms a continuous 4D probability density function that augments 2D local coordinates with grayscale intensity, capturing both geometric structure and surface texture.
- **Normal Constraint:** Eigenvalue decomposition of the SOGMM's covariance matrix provides eigenvectors that define local planar priors and a reference normal for each Gaussian node. This mechanism supplies explicit directional constraints for the 2D Gaussian surfels.
- **Geometric Anchoring:** SOGMM initializes the central positions and rotations of 2D Gaussians and serves as a continuous geometric reference. A distance function measures the deviation of a surfel's center from the local tangent plane of the SOGMM, helping to rectify surface drifts caused by visual matching errors. Anisotropy regularization is also applied to penalize excessive stretching of surfels, promoting structural integrity.

#### 3.2. End-to-End Panoptic Reconstruction

To overcome limitations of multi-stage pipelines, an end-to-end query-guided architecture is employed for panoptic segmentation.
- **Multi-modal Surfel Representation:** Each 2D Gaussian surfel is augmented with learnable high-dimensional semantic feature vectors ($f^{sem}_i$) and instance feature vectors ($f^{ins}_i$), creating a continuous neural feature field.
- **Query-guided Instance Segmentation:** Instance queries ($\mathcal{Q}_k$) are represented as dual-component entities comprising high-dimensional query features and spatial 3D Gaussian distributions.
 - **Distance-aware Cross-Attention:** The affinity between an instance query and a scene surfel is determined by fusing their feature similarity with their geometric correlation, specifically the ratio of their spatial probability densities. This combined attention map ($A(q, g)$) is used to assign discrete instance labels to surfels via a softmax operation. Pixel-level instance identity is then rendered by $\alpha$-blending these surfel labels.
 - **Frustum-constrained Cross-Attention:** To manage computational complexity in large scenes, cross-attention is restricted to a local set of Gaussians whose 99% confidence intervals intersect the current camera's view frustum. This mechanism is implemented using a custom CUDA kernel to parallelize computation and reduce memory usage.
 - **Dynamic Query Adjustment Strategy:** The system adaptively prunes redundant instance tokens. "Useless tokens," defined as those never or infrequently assigned, are removed. "Duplicate tokens," representing different portions of the same physical object, are identified and merged using a 3D Intersection over Mask (IoM) metric, retaining the query with the largest mask coverage.

#### 3.3. End-to-End Joint Optimization

A multi-task joint optimization framework unifies geometric prior constraints and panoptic perceptual supervision.
- **Geometric and Photometric Supervision:** The system is supervised with a composite photometric loss ($\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$) for visual realism and a geometric loss ($\mathcal{L}_{geo}$) that anchors surfel centers and aligns surfel normals with SOGMM priors. Anisotropy regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{iso}$) promotes surfel compactness.
- **Panoptic Feature Supervision:** Training of semantic and instance fields uses 2D pseudo-labels. The Hungarian Algorithm performs per-frame linear assignment of instance queries to pseudo-ground truth labels. The instance branch is supervised with Dice and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) losses ($\mathcal{L}_{ins}$), while the semantic field uses Cross-Entropy (CE) loss ($\mathcal{L}_{sem}$).
- **Total Optimization Objective:** The final objective function is a weighted sum of these component losses, facilitating a complementary gradient flow between geometry and perception.

#### 3.4. Efficient Rendering for High-Dimensional Features

To achieve real-time inference for high-dimensional semantic and instance features, the rendering pipeline is optimized.
- **Precise Tile Intersection:** Isotropic bounding spheres, traditionally used for Gaussian-tile intersection, are replaced with compact Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABBs) derived from the 2D projected covariance matrix. This precisely filters out surfels that do not overlap with the current tile, reducing computational redundancy.
- **Top-K Hard Selection Strategy:** For high-dimensional feature accumulation, which is a computational bottleneck, the system restricts feature accumulation to a fixed set ($K$) of Gaussians with the highest opacity weights per pixel. This changes the accumulation complexity from $O(N)$ to $O(K)$, streamlining operations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate Ψ-Map focused on geometric reconstruction accuracy, panoptic segmentation quality, and real-time rendering performance across indoor and outdoor large-scale environments.

#### 4.1. Geometric Reconstruction Evaluation

- On self-collected datasets, Ψ-Map achieved an F1 score of 98.07, significantly outperforming all 4 LiDAR-only and 9 Gaussian-based baselines. For instance, the next best performing method, SuGaR, achieved an F1 score of 78.23.
- The system demonstrated superior Accuracy (4.37), Completeness (4.63), and F-score (98.07). The C-L1 metric, a combined measure of accuracy and completeness, was 4.51 for Ψ-Map, while other methods ranged from 14.23 to 25.30.
- Qualitative results indicated that Ψ-Map reconstructs denser surfaces and effectively mitigates geometric degradation, artifacts, and "floaters" compared to traditional Gaussian methods, which was attributed to the SOGMM-guided normalization and local planar priors.

#### 4.2. Panoptic Segmentation Performance

- Ψ-Map demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on the ScanNet-V2 and ScanNet++ benchmarks.
- On ScanNet-V2, Ψ-Map achieved a Panoptic Quality (PQ) of 74.12, significantly higher than other methods like PanopticRecon (63.70) and OpenGaussian (48.73). It also achieved a Recognition Quality (RQ) of 100.0, indicating robust instance identity persistence.
- On ScanNet++, Ψ-Map similarly achieved the highest PQ of 77.09 and an RQ of 93.60.
- The performance gains were attributed to the end-to-end joint optimization framework, query-based temporal consistency, and distance-aware spatial reasoning, which collectively provide a more globally consistent and noise-robust representation.

#### 4.3. Rendering Efficiency and Real-time Inference

- An ablation study on rendering efficiency demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed optimizations.
 - **Precise Tile Intersection:** Reduced the total rendered surfel count (RN-Total) from 6.1 million to 3.9 million, indicating a significant reduction in redundant computations and memory bandwidth pressure.
 - **Top-K Hard Selection Strategy:** Further reduced RN-Total to 1.2 million and lowered inference latency to 27 ms (approximately 37 FPS), demonstrating substantial acceleration for high-dimensional feature rendering without a significant loss in Panoptic Quality.
 - **Combined Optimizations (Full Method):** Achieved a low latency of 22 ms, corresponding to 45 FPS. This rate meets the real-time requirements for robotic control loops in high-fidelity simulation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Geometric Representation

- The study confirmed that the proposed geometric representation, which integrates SOGMM-guided constraints with 2DGS, yielded the best panoptic understanding performance (74.75 PQ on ScanNet-V2). This configuration outperformed standard 3DGS (73.83 PQ) and 2DGS (73.30 PQ) models, highlighting the importance of the high-fidelity surface reconstruction and floater suppression for consistent instance association.

#### 4.5. Case Studies

- **3D Object Completion and Generation:** On the Scan2CAD dataset, Ψ-Map significantly outperformed baseline methods. It achieved a CD-S (Chamfer Distance – Shape) of 0.016 (lower is better) and an F-Score-S (Shape) of 0.903 (higher is better), indicating high-fidelity shape completion. The method also demonstrated improved performance in object completion (CD-O 0.036, F-Score-O 0.710) and collision avoidance (Col-O 0.203, Col-S 0.015), ensuring generated objects are spatially consistent with the scene.
- **Object Goal Navigation:** In a robot navigation task, fine-tuning a VLFM-based perception module with 3D-consistent labels generated by Ψ-Map led to significant improvements in navigation success rates (SR). For example, in a "Laboratory Room," the SR increased from 20% to 100%, and in a "Private House," it rose from 25% to 50%. This demonstrates the utility of Ψ-Map in bolstering the robustness of robotic perception for downstream tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Ψ-Map framework offers advancements in 3D scene reconstruction and understanding with direct implications for robotics and simulation.

**Real2Sim Transfer and Digital Twins:** A primary significance is its contribution to bridging the Sim2Real Gap. By enabling the creation of high-fidelity digital twins with accurate geometry and consistent panoptic understanding, Ψ-Map facilitates the transfer of learned policies from simulation to real-world robotic systems. This capability is critical for developing and testing complex robotic behaviors in virtual environments that faithfully replicate physical characteristics.

**Enhanced Robotic Perception and Interaction:** The system provides robots with a more robust and semantically aware understanding of their surroundings. The ability to perform real-time, 3D-consistent open-vocabulary panoptic reconstruction allows robots to identify, localize, and reason about individual objects and their categories. This is fundamental for advanced robotic tasks such as precise manipulation, intelligent navigation, and human-robot interaction in unstructured environments.

**Improved Simulation Fidelity and Efficiency:** Ψ-Map addresses limitations in current 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods, particularly regarding geometric accuracy and panoptic consistency in large-scale scenes. The integration of LiDAR data for geometric reinforcement and the end-to-end panoptic learning pipeline lead to more reliable and usable scene representations for simulation. Furthermore, the optimized rendering pipeline ensures that these high-fidelity representations can be rendered at speeds compatible with real-time robotic control loops, reducing computational bottlenecks associated with high-dimensional feature processing.

**Robustness for Downstream Robotic Applications:** The demonstrated improvements in case studies highlight the practical impact of Ψ-Map.
- **3D Object Completion:** The system's capacity for generating complete 3D object models from partial observations, while maintaining pose consistency and avoiding collisions, is valuable for populating simulations, creating virtual assets, and enhancing scene editing capabilities.
- **Object Goal Navigation:** The significant increase in navigation success rates after fine-tuning perception modules with Ψ-Map-generated 3D-consistent labels indicates its utility as a powerful data engine for improving the robustness and accuracy of robotic perception in navigation tasks. This suggests broader applicability for enhancing autonomous capabilities in complex deployments.

**Foundation for Future Research:** Ψ-Map provides a low-coupling and efficient system solution that serves as a robust foundation for future research in several areas. These include active robotic perception (where robots might actively explore to refine their environmental understanding), the development of more sophisticated high-fidelity simulations, and advancements in generalizable embodied AI, where robust scene understanding is a prerequisite for intelligent behavior. The modular design may allow for incremental improvements and extensions to different sensing modalities or application domains.
