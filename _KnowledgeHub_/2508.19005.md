# Building Self-Evolving Agents via Experience-Driven Lifelong Learning: A Framework and Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces the Experience-driven Lifelong Learning (ELL) framework and the StuLife benchmark to advance self-evolving AI agents. Evaluation of state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) on StuLife reveals a considerable performance difference compared to human baselines, particularly in long-term memory and proactive behavior.

## Problem

- Developing AI agents capable of continuous, autonomous learning, adaptation, and evolution through direct experience remains a critical gap for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
- Existing continual learning methods primarily focus on mitigating catastrophic forgetting rather than proactive knowledge acquisition, limiting their utility for truly autonomous agents in dynamic, real-world environments.
- Current AI benchmarks lack the interactivity, dynamic environments, and long-term cumulative knowledge acquisition necessary to evaluate self-evolving agents effectively.

## Method

- Introduced the **ELL** (Experience-driven Lifelong Learning) framework, a formal model for self-evolving agents based on continuous interaction, long-term memory, skill learning, and knowledge internalization.
- Developed **StuLife**, a comprehensive, narrative-driven benchmark simulating a student's college journey to assess agents' continuous learning, autonomous decision-making, and long-term memory in complex, dynamic environments.
- Defined a multi-dimensional evaluation framework including ==StuGPA== (holistic performance), ==Long-Term Retention Rate (LTRR)==, and ==Proactive Initiative Score (PIS)== to measure various aspects of agent intelligence.

## Results

- State-of-the-art LLMs, including GPT-5, achieved very low StuGPA scores (e.g., GPT-5 at **17.90** out of 100 versus a human baseline of **85.24**), coupled with critically low PIS and LTRR.
- Existing self-evolving mechanisms like Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning and Agent Workflow Memory provided measurable performance gains (e.g., StuGPA improved by **2.12 points** with RFT on Qwen3-8B), but these gains remained bounded and did not fundamentally address the gap.
- An "All-in-One Prompt" strategy, unifying proactive planning, skill execution, and MemGPT-based memory, achieved the highest StuGPA (**21.07**), surpassing individual LLM baselines and demonstrating improvements in PIS and LTRR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A profound gap exists between current state-of-the-art LLMs and human-level competence in long-horizon, experience-driven tasks, especially concerning long-term memory and proactive initiative.
> - Effective "context engineering," which integrates proactive planning, skill augmentation, and structured memory management (e.g., MemGPT), is crucial for enhancing agent performance beyond raw model capabilities.
> - True autonomous agency emerges from the integrated synergy of proactivity, skill, and structured memory, rather than from isolated cognitive components.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical gap in the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), specifically the need for agents that can learn continuously, adapt autonomously, and evolve over extended periods through direct experience. The authors position their work at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields:

- **Continual Learning (CL) / Lifelong Learning:** Traditional CL methods primarily focus on mitigating catastrophic forgetting, where models lose previously acquired knowledge when learning new tasks. While strides have been made with techniques like regularization, architectural modifications, and replay-based strategies, these largely operate under constrained assumptions (static datasets, predefined task boundaries, supervised signals). The paper argues that these approaches emphasize "performance retention rather than proactive knowledge acquisition," limiting their ability to support truly autonomous agents in dynamic, real-world environments. This work aims to move beyond performance retention to genuine knowledge acquisition and skill transfer from continuous interaction.
- **Self-Evolving Agents:** The concept of self-evolving agents capable of open-ended growth and adaptation is a core pillar of AGI. Prior research in this area often focuses on theoretical frameworks or narrow implementations, frequently lacking comprehensive memory mechanisms, experience-driven skill abstraction, or long-term goal-directed behavior. This paper seeks to provide a unified framework that integrates robust memory, autonomous skill acquisition, and long-term goal-directed behavior into a concrete, evaluable system.
- **Benchmarks for AI Agents:** Evaluating self-evolving agents requires benchmarks that transcend static datasets and isolated task performance. Existing benchmarks have limitations:
 - **Continual Learning Benchmarks** (e.g., Lifelong-CIFAR10, CGLB) are valuable for catastrophic forgetting but lack interactivity and dynamic environments crucial for agentic learning.
 - **Embodied AI Benchmarks** (e.g., EgoThink, EmbodiedBench) offer interactive environments but are not designed for long-term, cumulative knowledge acquisition or skill abstraction over an agent's lifetime.
 - **LLM Agent Benchmarks** (e.g., AgentBench, LoCoMo, StoryBench) assess reasoning and tool-use in LLM-based agents but often focus on static, one-off task performance rather than continuous growth or self-evolution.
 - **LifelongAgentBench** was the first to target self-evolving agents with interdependent tasks, but its focus on technical domains (databases, operating systems) leaves a void for benchmarks modeling the narrative-driven, intrinsically motivated aspects of human-like learning.

The presented work, with its Experience-driven Lifelong Learning (ELL) framework and the StuLife benchmark, aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a system where agents learn from a first-person perspective, acquire first-hand experience through autonomous exploration, and continually evolve in complex, dynamic environments. It fundamentally shifts the paradigm from systems optimized for static tasks to open-ended agents that learn and adapt autonomously.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the development of self-evolving AI agents capable of continuous, autonomous learning and adaptation in dynamic, unpredictable environments. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging the Gap Towards AGI:** The authors explicitly state that as AI progresses towards general intelligence, the focus must shift from systems excelling at static, isolated tasks to those capable of continuous growth, skill transfer, and strategic planning driven by intrinsic curiosity. They identify a "critical gap in developing AI agents that not only retain knowledge across time but also autonomously evolve by learning from experience and transferring skills."
- **Developing Experience-Driven Learning:** Inspired by Alan Turing's vision of "a machine that can learn from experience," the paper advocates for a paradigm shift where machines accumulate and learn from first-person experiences rather than merely mimicking human knowledge outputs from static datasets. This involves active engagement with the world through perception, action, and experimentation to build a unique experiential system.
- **Formalizing Lifelong Learning:** A core objective is to introduce a "formal and mathematically grounded framework for ELL," moving from conceptual vision to a concrete foundation for building agents that evolve through real-world interaction.
- **Creating a Comprehensive Evaluation Platform:** To facilitate progress in ELL, the authors aim to construct a robust and realistic benchmark. This benchmark, StuLife, is designed to simulate a holistic, long-term learning journey (a student's college experience) to assess agents' continuous learning, autonomous decision-making, long-term memory, and self-motivation in complex, dynamic, and interconnected environments.
- **Understanding Current LLM Limitations:** A crucial motivation is to thoroughly evaluate the capabilities and deficiencies of state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) when applied to lifelong learning scenarios, particularly concerning their ability to manage long-term memory, initiate proactive behavior, and evolve through experience. This includes exploring the role of "context engineering" as a potential avenue for improvement.

In essence, the research is motivated by the aspiration to create truly intelligent, autonomous agents that, like humans, learn from life itself, continuously adapting and becoming "wiser with every experience."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a two-pronged methodology: the Experience-driven Lifelong Learning (ELL) framework and the StuLife benchmark, designed to embody and evaluate this framework.

#### 3.1. The Experience-driven Lifelong Learning (ELL) Framework

The ELL framework (Figure 1 in the paper) conceptualizes a continuous learning cycle for self-evolving agents interacting with dynamic environments. It is built upon four core principles:

1. **Experience Exploration:** Agents learn through self-motivated interaction with dynamic environments, navigating interdependent tasks and generating rich experiential trajectories. This involves decomposing complex, long-horizon tasks and iteratively refining strategies based on dynamic feedback.
2. **Long-term Memory:** Agents preserve and structure historical knowledge, including personal experiences (trajectory memory), domain expertise (declarative knowledge), and commonsense reasoning (structural knowledge), into a persistent, active memory system. This memory supports retrieval over long time spans and context-aware reasoning.
3. **Skill Learning:** Agents autonomously improve by abstracting recurring patterns from experience into reusable skills (procedural knowledge, meta-knowledge, heuristic knowledge). These skills are actively refined, validated, and managed (adding, updating, deleting, combining) for application in new tasks.
4. **Knowledge Internalization:** Beyond storing memories and reusing skills, agents undergo a process of transforming explicit, discrete experiences into implicit, intuitive capabilities, akin to making learned behavior "second nature" for improved efficiency and adaptability.

The framework defines an agent (Definition 5) operating within an environment (Definition 1) to accomplish a sequence of tasks (Definition 2), generating trajectories (Definition 3) that are distilled into dynamic knowledge (Definition 4). The lifelong learning process (Definition 6) involves a sequential loop of interaction, knowledge abstraction/refinement, and validation, with the objective of maximizing expected performance over a lifetime of tasks (Definition 7), promoting forward transfer and guarding against catastrophic forgetting.

#### 3.2. The StuLife Benchmark

StuLife (Figure 2 in the paper) is a comprehensive evaluation dataset designed to simulate a student's entire college journey, providing a realistic, longitudinal environment for assessing ELL agents. It features three core phases and ten detailed sub-scenarios:

- **In-Class Tasks:** Focus on structured academic learning (e.g., Regulations Learning, Core Course Instruction).
- **Daily Campus Tasks:** Capture self-directed activities emphasizing planning, resource management, and social integration (e.g., Campus Exploration, Initial Course Selection, Library Resource Management, Student Club Engagement).
- **Examination Tasks:** Evaluate knowledge retention and performance under pressure (Midterm Exams, Final Exams).

StuLife is designed around three key paradigm shifts:

- **From Passive to Proactive:** Agents must develop intrinsic motivation, set their own goals, and initiate actions without external prompting, managing agendas and adapting to changing conditions.
- **From Context to Memory:** Tasks are interconnected temporally and logically, requiring robust long-term memory to retain and retrieve critical experiences from evolving state variables (e.g., resource availability, time).
- **From Imitation to Learning:** Agents must abstract generalizable skills (e.g., course registration, campus navigation) from interactions and transfer them across evolving challenges, rather than merely retrieving past experiences.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Construction

The construction of StuLife employs a systematic, multi-stage process (Figure 3 in the paper):

1. **Background and Status Generation:** Uses the Deepseek-R1 model to generate a rich campus background (maps, institutional documents, databases) and deterministic algorithms/scripts to create logically and causally dependent task statuses, ensuring a coherent timeline.
2. **Instruction Generation:** Transforms structured information from Step 1 into natural language instructions and prompts for the agent using the Deepseek-R1 model.
3. **Solution Generation and Verification:** Adapts a generate-and-verify protocol. An LLM first generates correct answers and distractors. Then, an "optimal long-term memory" simulation (providing only essential knowledge points) verifies task solvability. Manual sampling and inspection ensure quality.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

To assess ELL agents comprehensively, the paper defines a multi-dimensional evaluation framework:

- **StuGPA (General Performance Assessment):** A holistic score (out of 100) combining Exam Performance (50 pts), Class Performance (30 pts), and Campus Daily Life (20 pts, including advisor tasks, club activities, and personal responsibility).
- **Long-Term Retention Rate (LTRR):** Measures success on tasks requiring knowledge recall over extended temporal distances.
- **Proactive Initiative Score (PIS):** Assesses prospective memory and self-motivation – the ability to remember and execute planned intentions at appropriate times without immediate prompts.
- **Success Rate:** Overall task completion rate and accuracy for each core module (In-Class, Daily Campus, Examination).
- **Average Turns (AvgTurn):** Measures interaction efficiency by counting average turns for successful task completion.

The paper also outlines standard continual learning metrics like Average Performance (AP), Average Incremental Performance (AIP), Forgetting Measure (FGT), Backward Transfer (BWT), and Forward Transfer (FWT) to assess stability and plasticity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis on StuLife reveals several critical insights into the capabilities and limitations of current Large Language Models (LLMs) in lifelong learning scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance of State-of-the-Art LLMs

The evaluation of ten prominent LLMs, including Llama-3.1, various Qwen3 models, DeepSeek variants, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok4, and GPT-5, under a default stateless setting showed **exceptionally poor performance**. Even the most advanced model, **GPT-5, achieved a StuGPA of only 17.90 out of 100**, drastically contrasting with the human baseline of 85.24. This highlights a "vast gap" towards AGI.
The results also showed a **positive correlation with model scale**, where larger parameter counts generally yielded higher scores. More importantly, models designed for step-by-step reasoning (e.g., Qwen3-32B-Thinking, StuGPA 12.67) significantly outperformed their standard instruction-following counterparts (e.g., Qwen3-32B-Instruct, StuGPA 7.36), indicating that internal architectures supporting deliberative reasoning are more critical for complex agency tasks.

#### 4.2. Limitations in Long-Term Memory and Proactive Behavior

Current stateless LLMs demonstrated a fundamental inability to manage long-term memory and proactive behavior.
- **Proactive Initiative Score (PIS)** was critically low across all models. Even GPT-5 scored only 4.68%, and many models were below 1%. This indicates a profound lack of self-motivation, where agents fail to initiate scheduled actions even when the time is appropriate, suggesting they lack the intrinsic drive to connect external stimuli with internal long-term goals.
- **Long-Term Retention Rate (LTRR)** was also very low, even for top models (Grok4 at 10.65%). The authors clarify that for stateless LLMs, LTRR measures a "fragile, multi-step, tool-dependent process" (recognizing information, using a tool to externalize it, then retrieving it). The low scores confirm that reliable knowledge accumulation is currently unattainable without a dedicated memory system, contrasting sharply with human LTRR of 84.91%. Case studies (e.g., Long-Term Memory Failure, Proactive Initiative Failure) qualitatively support these quantitative findings.

#### 4.3. Impact of Self-Evolving Mechanisms

The paper explored the effectiveness of existing self-evolving mechanisms:
- **Training-based evolution (Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning - RFT)** on Qwen3-8B showed tangible benefits, boosting StuGPA from 13.31 to 15.43 and Total Success from 6.71% to 8.63%. This indicates learnable patterns within the benchmark.
- **Inference-time evolution (Agent Workflow Memory - AWM and Reflexion)** on Qwen3-235B-A22B also yielded gains. AWM improved StuGPA to 17.81 and Total Success to 10.12%, while also increasing efficiency by reducing AvgTurn. Reflexion showed marginal gains.
While these mechanisms provided "tangible benefits in both learnability and efficiency," the authors conclude that "these gains remain bounded," as they have yet to bridge the gap for proactive initiative and sustained long-term memory.

#### 4.4. Role of Context Engineering

A significant finding is the impact of context engineering on agent performance. The authors designed five prompting strategies: Vanilla, Proactive, Skill-Augmented, Memory-Augmented (Vanilla RAG, Graph RAG, MemGPT, MemoryBank), and All-in-One.
- **Targeted Prompts:** Proactive prompts improved time-sensitive performance (In-Class success doubled, PIS rose), while Skill-Augmented prompts boosted complex Daily Campus tasks. This suggests context engineering can effectively guide models to address specific bottlenecks.
- **Memory-Augmented Approaches:** Naive RAG methods (Vanilla RAG, Graph RAG) often *harmed* performance by introducing unfiltered noise and worsening long-context degradation. In contrast, **MemGPT**, a sophisticated structured memory system, achieved the highest StuGPA among memory-only methods (19.99) and improved Examination success, demonstrating the importance of intelligent memory management.
- **All-in-One Approach:** The most compelling result was the **All-in-One Prompt**, which unified proactive planning, skill execution, and MemGPT-based memory augmentation. This approach achieved the highest StuGPA (21.07), surpassing GPT-5's baseline, and significantly improved PIS (3.76%) and LTRR (9.39%). This finding suggests that "true autonomous agency emerges not from isolated components, but from the integrated synergy of proactivity, skill, and structured memory."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper carries significant implications for the future direction of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and the development of more capable AI systems.

#### 5.1. Reaffirming the AGI Gap

The most striking finding is the profound gap between state-of-the-art LLMs (even GPT-5) and human-level competence in long-horizon, experience-driven tasks. The extremely low StuGPA scores (17.90 for GPT-5 vs. 85.24 for humans), coupled with dismal LTRR and PIS, underscore that current LLMs, despite their impressive language capabilities, fundamentally lack the architectural components for persistent memory, intrinsic motivation, and self-directed, continuous learning that characterize true intelligence. This serves as a strong call to action for the research community, redirecting focus from mere scaling of parameters to foundational architectural changes for sustained cognitive growth.

#### 5.2. The Critical Role of Context Engineering

The paper demonstrates that while raw model capabilities are important, "context engineering" – the strategic organization of memory, prompts, and experience – can significantly enhance agent performance. The "All-in-One" prompt, which integrates proactive planning, skill augmentation, and structured memory (MemGPT), achieved the highest performance, even surpassing individual LLM baselines. This suggests that the path to AGI may depend as much on *how we guide* and *structure the cognitive framework* of agents as on improving the underlying models themselves. This insight can immediately impact the design of agentic systems by advocating for more sophisticated prompting and memory management strategies.

#### 5.3. A New Paradigm for Lifelong Learning

The Experience-driven Lifelong Learning (ELL) framework provides a formal and mathematically grounded vision for building self-evolving agents. By emphasizing continuous interaction, long-term memory, skill learning, and knowledge internalization, ELL offers a compelling alternative to static, task-specific AI systems. This paradigm shift encourages researchers to develop agents that actively engage with and learn from their environment, mirroring human cognitive development.

#### 5.4. A Foundational Benchmark for Future Research

The StuLife benchmark is a crucial contribution. By simulating a complex, narrative-driven, and temporally evolving "college journey," it moves beyond the limitations of existing benchmarks. Its focus on interconnected tasks, dynamic environments, sparse rewards, and the need for self-initiated behavior provides a realistic and challenging platform to evaluate key aspects of self-evolving intelligence, including memory retention, proactive behavior, and skill transfer. StuLife will be instrumental in catalyzing research, allowing for standardized comparisons and fostering innovation in memory architectures, meta-learning, and intrinsic motivation mechanisms.

#### 5.5. Identifying Specific Capability Gaps

Through detailed analysis and case studies, the paper clearly identifies specific failure modes in current LLMs: catastrophic forgetting, lack of proactive initiative, tool-use inconsistencies, goal decomposition failures, and poor information prioritization. These granular insights provide concrete targets for future research and development efforts, guiding the design of more robust and human-like AI agents.

In conclusion, this research not only presents a conceptual framework and a rigorous benchmark but also delivers stark empirical evidence of current AI limitations while pointing towards promising avenues for progress. By shifting the focus from isolated task performance to sustained, experience-driven growth, the paper lays a principled foundation for developing intelligent systems that truly evolve and learn from "life."
