# UniTac2Pose: A Unified Approach Learned in Simulation for Category-level Visuotactile In-hand Pose Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> UniTac2Pose is an energy-based diffusion model trained entirely in simulation that unifies visuotactile in-hand pose estimation, tracking, and uncertainty quantification for objects. The model achieves category-level generalization to unseen objects and records an average ADD/ADD-S error of 2.1 mm in real-world settings, outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing methods for in-hand object pose estimation using tactile sensors struggle with precision and generalizing to unseen objects within a category.
- Traditional regression, registration, and feature-matching techniques are often susceptible to localized tactile ambiguity, sensitive initialization, and the sim-to-real gap.
- Practical manipulation tasks require a unified solution for static pose estimation, pose tracking, and uncertainty quantification, which are typically addressed by separate, cascaded systems.

## Method

- **UniTac2Pose** develops a unified ==energy-based diffusion model== that integrates pose estimation, tracking, and uncertainty quantification into a single framework.
- The model is trained exclusively on synthetic data generated by the XENSIM tactile simulator and incorporates a '==render-compare==' architecture to bridge the sim-to-real gap.
- Inference employs a three-stage process: pre-filtering of pose candidates, gradient-based refinement using a ==probability flow ODE==, and post-ranking with the learned energy function.

## Results

- UniTac2Pose achieved an average ADD/ADD-S error of **2.1 mm** on 10 distinct real-world objects, outperforming FilterReg (**13.3 mm**), Regression (**13.0 mm**), and Matching (**16.3 mm**) baselines.
- The system demonstrated category-level generalization, with errors on unseen objects (e.g., **1.9 mm** for unseen Pipes) remaining comparable to seen objects (**1.6 mm** for seen Pipes).
- The framework enables robust pose tracking at **10 Hz** and provides effective uncertainty estimates that identify grasps with lower pose errors, facilitating re-grasping strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified energy-based diffusion model can effectively learn the conditional log-likelihood of object poses, enabling coherent refinement, ranking, and uncertainty estimation.
> - The 'render-compare' architecture, which compares real tactile inputs with rendered tactile images based on hypothesized poses, is crucial for robust sim-to-real transfer in visuotactile contexts.
> - Training solely on simulated data, coupled with appropriate architectural designs and data augmentation, can achieve strong category-level generalization for in-hand pose estimation without real-world training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the critical problem of accurate in-hand pose estimation of objects using tactile sensors, a fundamental capability for advanced robotic manipulation in both industrial and domestic settings. This problem is particularly challenging due to factors like the need to handle unseen objects, manage regrasp decisions under uncertainty, and track pose changes.

Historically, tactile perception for pose estimation has evolved from low-resolution sensors providing binary contact signals to modern high-resolution visuotactile sensors like GelSlim. Early methods often combined vision and tactile sensing, with tactile data primarily refining visual estimates. More recent efforts, to which this paper contributes, focus on leveraging high-resolution tactile data as a primary modality, enabling finer contact shape recovery and improved localization.

The existing landscape of tactile in-hand pose estimation methods can broadly be categorized into:
- **Regression-based methods:** These approaches train a direct mapping from tactile images and object CAD models to pose estimates. However, they often suffer from ambiguity due to the localized nature of tactile sensing, where different poses can produce similar imprints.
- **Registration-based methods:** Techniques like ICP (Iterative Closest Point) and FilterReg attempt to align tactile point clouds with the object's global model. These methods are notoriously sensitive to initialization and prone to local minima, especially with noisy tactile depth estimates.
- **Feature-matching strategies:** Methods like Tac2Pose convert tactile data into contact maps and match them against rendered maps from pose grids. While promising, these can be limited by noisy contact maps, object specificity, and an inability to capture detailed in-plane surface features, particularly under significant object deformation.

UniTac2Pose distinguishes itself significantly from these prior works by introducing several innovations:
- **Unified Energy-based Diffusion Model:** Unlike chained or separate modules for different tasks, UniTac2Pose proposes a single, end-to-end trained energy-based diffusion model that unifies pose estimation, tracking, and uncertainty quantification. This avoids compounding errors inherent in multi-stage pipelines and offers a coherent probabilistic framework.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer with Render-Compare:** A major challenge in robotics is bridging the "sim-to-real" gap, where models trained in simulation fail to perform well in the real world. UniTac2Pose addresses this by training *solely on simulated data* and incorporating a "render-compare" architecture (borrowed from visual pose estimation like FoundationPose). This mechanism compares real tactile inputs with rendered tactile images based on hypothesized poses, enhancing the model's robustness to real-world variations.
- **Category-level Generalization:** While some methods achieve instance-level pose estimation, generalization to *unseen CAD models within a category* is a significant step forward. UniTac2Pose achieves this by conditioning on object CAD models and leveraging simulated training, learning shared features within categories. This is presented as a novel capability in visuotactile pose estimation.
- **Robustness to Arbitrary Contacts:** The framework is designed to handle diverse contact scenarios and arbitrary subsets of tactile observations, enhancing its applicability in varied manipulation tasks.

By integrating state-of-the-art generative modeling (diffusion models) with a render-compare architecture and a comprehensive, unified framework, UniTac2Pose pushes the boundaries of tactile in-hand pose estimation, offering a robust, generalizable, and sim-to-real capable solution. It stands out by demonstrating category-level generalization and unifying several critical aspects of manipulation under a single model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to achieve **accurate and robust in-hand pose estimation of an object based on its CAD model**, a capability deemed "crucial" for a wide range of robotic applications, from industrial assembly to everyday tasks like inserting USB connectors.

The authors are motivated by several persistent challenges in this domain:
1. **High Precision Requirement:** Many manipulation tasks demand very precise object localization, which existing methods often struggle to provide consistently.
2. **Generalizability to Unseen Objects:** Robots often encounter novel object instances within known categories. Current methods frequently lack the ability to generalize effectively to these unseen CAD models without extensive real-world training data.
3. **Ambiguity in Tactile Sensing:** The localized nature of tactile sensing can lead to significant ambiguity, as multiple object poses might produce similar tactile imprints, making accurate regression difficult.
4. **Sensitivity of Traditional Methods:** Classical registration methods (e.g., ICP, FilterReg) are sensitive to initialization and prone to local minima, while feature-matching methods (e.g., Tac2Pose) can be limited by noisy contact maps and failure to capture intricate surface features.
5. **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Training models purely in simulation offers significant advantages by avoiding laborious and expensive real-world data collection. However, successfully transferring these simulated learnings to real-world performance remains a major hurdle.
6. **Unified Solution for Multiple Tasks:** Beyond static pose estimation, practical manipulation requires additional capabilities such as **pose tracking** (to follow object movements) and **uncertainty estimation** (to inform regrasp decisions when pose certainty is low). Existing solutions often address these problems separately, leading to complex, cascaded systems.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of UniTac2Pose are:
- To develop a novel framework that outperforms conventional regression, feature matching, and registration-based baselines in real-world tactile in-hand pose estimation.
- To create a unified model that integrates pose estimation, pose tracking, and uncertainty quantification within a single framework.
- To achieve strong intra-category generalization, enabling the model to accurately estimate poses for previously unseen CAD models belonging to trained categories.
- To train the entire system exclusively on simulated data, thereby alleviating the need for costly real-world data collection, while still demonstrating excellent sim-to-real transfer performance.
- To incorporate architectural designs, like render-compare, that are specifically tailored to bridge the sim-to-real gap effectively for visuotactile sensing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniTac2Pose proposes a novel three-stage framework, unified by an energy-based diffusion model, designed for robust visuotactile in-hand pose estimation, tracking, and uncertainty quantification. The entire system is trained solely on simulated data.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Pose Representation

The goal is to estimate the 6D pose of an in-hand object, `p`, within the Tool Center Point (TCP) frame, given its CAD model `O` and tactile imprints `T = (T_1, ..., T_k)`. The pose `p` is represented as a 9-dimensional variable `[R|T]`, where `R ∈ R^6` (using a continuous 6-D rotation representation to avoid discontinuities of quaternions or Euler angles) and `T ∈ R^3` for translation.

#### 3.2. Synthetic Data Generation

The model is trained on a synthetic dataset `D = {(p_i, O_i, T_i)}` generated using the FEM-based tactile simulator XENSIM. This process involves:
- Randomly sampling diverse in-hand poses by varying position (within object bounding box) and rotation (around grasp axis).
- Simulating RGB tactile images from GelSlim 3.0 sensors for these poses, including varied indentation depths.
- Filtering out data points with minimal contact to ensure data quality.
- Utilizing data augmentation techniques like ColorJitter to enhance sim-to-real transfer.

#### 3.3. Energy-based Diffusion Model Training

At the core of UniTac2Pose is an energy-based diffusion model, `E_θ`, which learns the (unnormalized) log-likelihood of an object pose.
- **Energy Network:** `E_θ` takes as input an intermediate pose `p`, the canonical object point cloud `O`, concatenated tactile images `T`, and a continuous time variable `t ∈`, outputting a scalar energy value `E_θ(p, O, T, t)`.
- **Training Objective:** The network is trained end-to-end using a Denoising Score Matching (DSM) objective (Equation 1). This objective aims to match the gradient of the energy network's output (`∇_p log E_θ`) with the score function of the perturbed conditional data distribution (`∇_p log p_t(p|O, T)`). This ensures that the energy function provides accurate gradients for pose refinement and a meaningful scalar for quality assessment.
- **Architectural Design - Render-Compare:** A key innovation for sim-to-real performance is the "render-compare" architecture within `E_θ`. Instead of direct encoding of all inputs, the hypothesized pose `p(t)` is used to render RGB images (`I`) of the object from multiple pre-calibrated camera views (mimicking sensor cameras) using Nvdiffrast. Separate encoders (CNNs) process the real tactile images and the rendered images. The extracted features are then concatenated and fed into Convolutional Residual Blocks and ViT Blocks. This design allows the network to implicitly compare observed tactile imprints with what *should be seen* from a given pose, leveraging the geometry of the CAD model.
- **Parameterization Trick:** The energy network uses a parameterization `E_θ(p, O, T, t) = ⟨Φ_θ(p, O, T, t), p⟩`, where `Φ_θ` is the network backbone outputting a 9D vector matching the pose dimension.

#### 3.4. Three-Stage Inference Framework

During inference, given an object CAD model `O*` and real tactile imprints `T*`, UniTac2Pose performs the following three stages:
1. **Pre-filtering (Pre-ranking):**
 - A large set of initial pose candidates (`N_s` candidates) is sampled from a prior distribution (`π_est`), for global pose estimation, this is based on viewpoints around the object.
 - The energy network `E_θ` (at a small noise level `ϵ`) is used to rank these candidates.
 - The bottom `M - K` candidates (low-likelihood) are discarded, keeping the top `K` candidates (`{˜p_i}^K_{i=1}`).
2. **Pose Refinement:**
 - The top `K` candidates from the pre-filtering stage are iteratively refined using gradients derived from the trained energy network.
 - This refinement follows a modified Probability Flow (PF) ODE (Equation 6), which integrates from an initial time `t_0` down to `ϵ`. The score function `∇_p log p_t(p|O)` is approximated by `∇_p E_θ(p, O, T, t)`.
 - The RK45 solver is used for ODE integration. Refinement starts from a smaller `t` because initial candidates are already relatively close to ground truth.
3. **Post-Ranking:**
 - After refinement, the `K` refined pose candidates (`{ˆp_i}^K_{i=1}`) are again ranked by their energy values using `E_θ(ˆp_i, O, T, ϵ)`.
 - The single candidate with the highest energy (lowest energy value if `E_θ` represents negative log-likelihood, or highest if `E_θ` is `log p_t`) is selected as the final pose estimate `ˆp_est`.

#### 3.5. Extensions for Pose Tracking and Uncertainty Estimation

- **Pose Tracking:** This is achieved by modifying the prior distribution (`π_track`) in the pre-filtering stage to incorporate temporal continuity. The prior is centered around the previously estimated pose (`N(˜p_{t+1}; ˆp_t, σ^2_track I)`). This enables faster inference (10 Hz) due to a more informed starting point for refinement and fewer steps.
- **Uncertainty Estimation:** The variance (`S^2`) of the `K` refined pose candidates is computed (Equation 10). This `S^2` serves as a metric for the uncertainty of a grasp, allowing the system to identify and select grasps with lower uncertainty (e.g., for re-grasping actions) by comparing `S^2` values across different grasp candidates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniTac2Pose demonstrates significant improvements over existing baselines and offers robust performance across various evaluation scenarios.

#### 4.1. Generalizable Tactile Pose Estimation within a Single Instance (Sim-to-Real)

- **Superior Performance:** UniTac2Pose consistently outperforms all conventional baselines (FilterReg, Vanilla Regression, and Matching/Tac2Pose) in instance-level sim-to-real tactile pose estimation across 10 distinct McMaster objects (Table 1). The average ADD/ADD-S error for UniTac2Pose is 2.1 mm, significantly lower than FilterReg (Global) (13.3 mm), Regression (13.0 mm), and Matching (16.3 mm). FilterReg (Partial), an oracle baseline, performs comparably (3.2 mm), highlighting the effectiveness of UniTac2Pose in approximating oracle-level performance without ground truth partial point clouds.
- **Baseline Weaknesses:** Regression struggles with symmetric or larger objects. FilterReg (Global) fails due to global point cloud registration issues. Matching underperforms due to sim-to-real discrepancies in contact shapes. UniTac2Pose's score-matching training and render-compare architecture effectively handle these challenges.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Render-Compare (R-C) Mechanism:** Removing R-C (`Ours w/o R-C`) leads to the most significant performance drop (average 8.3 mm), underscoring its critical role in bridging the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Unified Network:** Training a separate score-based diffusion network (`Ours w/o Unify`) performs similarly but with slightly higher errors (average 3.8 mm), validating the benefit of a unified energy network for aligning refinement, ranking, and evaluation.
 - **Data Augmentation:** Disabling augmentation (`Ours w/o Aug`) results in performance decline (average 12.5 mm), especially for objects like Cotter and Hook, confirming its importance.
 - **Modality Robustness:** Both RGB and depth-based rendered inputs yield comparable performance, but RGB is found to be slightly more stable for certain objects (e.g., Stud, Hook).
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** The method exhibits a relatively small sim-to-real gap (Table 7), with an average difference of 0.8 mm (ranging from -0.5 mm to 1.9 mm). This is attributed to extensive data augmentation and randomization during training.

#### 4.2. Generalizable Tactile Pose Estimation to Unseen Intra-category Objects

- **Category-level Generalization:** UniTac2Pose successfully generalizes to unseen instances within object categories (Pipe and Connector). Performance on unseen objects did not degrade significantly compared to seen objects (Figure 4). For example, in the Pipe category, the average error for seen objects was 1.6 mm, while for unseen objects it was 1.9 mm. This suggests that the energy network learns generalizable representations by leveraging shared local features within categories. This is a novel contribution, described as the first to demonstrate intra-category generalization in visuotactile pose estimation.
- **Multi-object Training:** A single model trained on multiple diverse objects (Round Nut, Hose, Rail) achieves performance comparable to individual models trained separately for each object (Table 6), indicating its capability to learn across different object categories within a single model.

#### 4.3. Validation of Uncertainty Estimation, Pose Tracking, and Arbitrary Contacts

- **Pose Tracking:** The framework demonstrates robust in-hand pose tracking, maintaining an error within 2mm across various objects (Table 2) with a stable frame rate of 10 Hz. This is significantly faster than initial pose estimation, leveraging temporal continuity.
- **Uncertainty Estimation:** The uncertainty estimation method effectively identifies grasps with lower pose uncertainty. Grasps selected based on higher confidence (lower variance in refined poses) consistently yield lower average pose errors compared to random selection (Table 3), confirming its utility for guiding re-grasping strategies.
- **Arbitrary Contacts:** UniTac2Pose can effectively handle scenarios with arbitrary numbers of tactile contacts (e.g., single sensor input vs. double). While using both sensors provides the best performance, the "Arbitrary" variant (randomly masking sensors during training/testing) still performs comparably to the "Single" variant (using only one sensor), demonstrating robust generalization to partial or varied tactile inputs (Table 4).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniTac2Pose represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic tactile manipulation, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Core Contributions

- **Unified Framework:** The development of a single, energy-based diffusion model that unifies in-hand pose estimation, tracking, and uncertainty quantification is a major architectural contribution. This simplifies the system design, avoids cascading errors, and provides a coherent probabilistic approach to tactile perception in manipulation.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** UniTac2Pose consistently outperforms existing baselines (regression, feature matching, registration) in real-world tactile pose estimation tasks, setting a new benchmark for accuracy.
- **Category-Level Generalization:** The ability to generalize to *unseen CAD models within a category* is a critical step towards more adaptable and versatile robotic systems. This is a novel achievement in visuotactile pose estimation, reducing the need for extensive training data for every new object instance.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Training the entire system *solely on simulated data* while achieving excellent real-world performance is a substantial practical advantage. The render-compare architecture is identified as a crucial component for bridging this sim-to-real gap, significantly reducing the labor and cost associated with real-world data collection.
- **Robustness and Practicality:** The framework's ability to handle arbitrary contacts, perform pose tracking at practical frame rates, and provide meaningful uncertainty estimates enhances the robustness and practicality of tactile perception in dynamic manipulation scenarios.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Industrial Automation:** High-precision assembly, quality control, and pick-and-place operations in manufacturing environments can greatly benefit from UniTac2Pose's accuracy and generalizability. Robots can handle a wider variety of components, even new designs, with minimal re-training.
- **Domestic Robotics:** For household robots, accurate in-hand pose estimation can enable more dexterous manipulation of everyday objects, improving tasks like object sorting, fetching, and assistive functions.
- **Advanced Robotic Research:** The unified energy-based diffusion model paradigm could inspire new approaches for other perception and control problems in robotics, especially those dealing with uncertainty and continuous state spaces. The sim-to-real strategy with render-compare offers a blueprint for future research aiming to reduce reliance on real-world data.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By enabling training exclusively in simulation, UniTac2Pose significantly lowers the development cost and time for deploying new manipulation skills, democratizing advanced robotic capabilities.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge two main limitations:
1. **Inference Speed for Initial Estimation:** While pose tracking operates at 10 FPS, initial pose estimation takes 1-2 seconds per estimate (less than 1 FPS). This is slower than some previous methods. Future work proposes leveraging faster diffusion models, such as Flow-Matching, which could accelerate the pipeline to over 25 FPS by requiring fewer denoising steps.
2. **Scope of Generalization:** The category-level generalization was demonstrated on a relatively small set of objects within specific categories. Future work will focus on validating the approach on a larger, more diverse set of objects and assessing its generalization to novel objects from *unseen categories*, which is a more challenging form of out-of-distribution generalization.

By open-sourcing their training and inference pipeline, datasets, and evaluation data, the authors aim to facilitate further research and accelerate progress in visuotactile in-hand pose estimation. Overall, UniTac2Pose represents a strong step forward, offering a comprehensive, data-efficient, and highly performant solution for a critical problem in robotics.
