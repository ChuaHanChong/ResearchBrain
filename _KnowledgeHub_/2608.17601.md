# Physics-Informed Sliding-Window Particle Filtering for Tactile-Only In-Hand 6-DoF Object Pose Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> A physics-informed sliding-window particle filter on SE(3) refines grasped-object 6-DoF pose from whole-hand tactile taxels alone. Likelihood fuses contact SDF, force-normal alignment, friction-cone feasibility, and zero-force evidence over a temporal window, with potential-field-guided proposals and symmetry-aware resampling. Achieves lowest normalized ADD-S (0.361) across five objects versus tactile-only baselines.

## Problem

- Vision-based pose estimators become unreliable once the hand occludes the object or lighting/motion degrades RGB(-D) sensing during grasping.
- Prior tactile-only methods rely on sparse contact/proprioception or first-touch local geometry, not distributed whole-hand taxels with measured 3D forces.
- Existing contact particle filters localize objects via touch or robot-body pose but do not jointly encode geometric, frictional, and non-penetration feasibility over time.

## Method

- ==Physics-informed likelihood== combining signed-distance consistency, force-normal alignment, friction-cone feasibility, zero-force negative evidence, and front-side/equilibrium guards as robust Huber penalties.
- ==Sliding-window particle filter== on SE(3) that fuses log-likelihoods across a short FIFO buffer of recent tactile frames to reduce single-frame ambiguity.
- ==Potential-field-guided proposal== using finite-difference gradients of a hand-object penetration potential to steer particles away from implausible penetrations, plus ==symmetry-aware mode-preserving resampling==.

## Results

- Full model achieves best average normalized ADD-S of **0.361/0.124** (median/IQR) across five objects, beating ICP-force (**0.706**), Covariance T2G (**0.739**), and visuo-tactile-derived baseline (**0.758**).
- Ablations show physics terms cut median ADD-S from 0.461 to 0.375 (**18.7%**), and removing the sliding window worsens it from 0.375/0.176 to 0.568/0.224.
- Disabling mode preservation drops success rate from **92%** to **71%**; relocalization reduces failure rate from **28.7%** to **8.2%** under perturbed initialization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining multiple physics-feasibility cues (geometry, normal, friction, non-penetration) into one likelihood suppresses implausible poses better than any single geometric residual alone.
> - Short temporal fusion over a handful of frames meaningfully resolves single-frame tactile ambiguity without excessive staleness, a cheap robustness lever for sparse-contact estimation.
> - Symmetric or near-symmetric objects need explicit multi-modal belief preservation; naive resampling collapses prematurely to one arbitrary symmetric equivalent, hurting success substantially.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reliable 6-DoF object pose estimation is a foundational capability for robotic manipulation tasks such as regrasping, reorientation, and contact-rich assembly. Traditional vision-based methods for pose estimation often face significant challenges when an object is grasped by a robotic hand. The hand itself can cause severe occlusion, limiting viewpoints, and common RGB(-D) sensing can degrade due to specularities, motion blur, or poor lighting conditions. These factors can render vision-only estimators unreliable once an object is firmly grasped.

Tactile sensing offers an alternative or complementary modality, as it remains effective even under heavy hand-object occlusion. Consequently, tactile sensing has been widely employed for contact-rich manipulation and in-hand state estimation. However, tactile-only 6-DoF pose estimation presents its own set of difficulties, primarily stemming from "tactile partial observability." Whole-hand taxel contacts are typically sparse, intermittent, and inherently local. Furthermore, object symmetries can introduce ambiguity, as multiple poses might explain the observed tactile patterns equally well.

Existing tactile and visuo-tactile methods address related problems but often operate under different sensing assumptions or update mechanisms. Some approaches rely on local, high-resolution tactile images (e.g., from GelSight or DIGIT sensors) or infer pose from a "first touch" contact geometry. Others utilize sparse contact information or proprioceptive cues (joint angles, torques). Visuo-tactile systems, while leveraging tactile data, typically assume the availability of additional visual or RGB-D observations to resolve ambiguities.

This research distinguishes itself by focusing specifically on **tactile-only quasi-static 6-DoF pose refinement and belief maintenance for grasped objects** using distributed whole-hand taxel arrays that provide 3D contact-force measurements. Unlike first-touch or local rendering methods, and distinct from visuo-tactile approaches, this work is dedicated to maintaining a belief over the object's pose in scenarios where only whole-hand tactile feedback is available and the object is held in static or slowly changing ("quasi-static") configurations. The primary contribution lies in integrating physics-informed likelihood factors, a sliding-window temporal fusion mechanism, and a potential-field-guided particle propagation strategy within a particle filter framework on SE(3) to address the challenges of sparse, intermittent, and ambiguous tactile data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust method for tactile-only 6-DoF pose refinement and belief maintenance for objects grasped by a robotic hand. This capability is critical in situations where visual feedback is compromised or entirely unavailable due to occlusion by the hand, limited viewpoints, or environmental factors.

The motivation for this research stems from several key challenges inherent to tactile-only pose estimation:
- **Partial Observability:** Tactile data is inherently local, providing information only at contact points. This sparsity means that the entire object surface is not observed, leading to ambiguity in pose estimation, especially for objects with symmetries.
- **Sparsity and Intermittency:** Contacts can be sparse across the entire hand and may change intermittently as the hand slightly adjusts its grip or the object shifts minimally. A single frame of tactile data might offer insufficient information to disambiguate the object's pose.
- **Ambiguity:** Local tactile measurements can be ambiguous. For instance, a flat contact surface might provide similar tactile readings for various object orientations, particularly in the presence of rotational symmetries.
- **Noise and Compliance:** Tactile measurements are subject to sensor noise, and the compliant nature of both the tactile sensors and the object can lead to discrepancies between actual contact geometry and expected CAD models.

To address these difficulties, the paper proposes a physics-informed sliding-window particle filter designed for low-rate refinement and belief maintenance rather than high-speed global localization or dynamic tracking. The specific objectives and core contributions are:

1. **Physics-Informed Bayesian Filtering Formulation:** To integrate various physical constraints and geometric consistencies into a comprehensive likelihood model. This involves jointly encoding geometric consistency (signed distance), force direction (force-normal alignment), contact stability (friction-cone feasibility), and the absence of contact (zero-force negative evidence), along with auxiliary guards.
2. **Sliding-Window Particle Filtering on SE(3):** To enhance robustness against intermittent contacts and noisy observations by fusing information from a short temporal history of tactile frames. This temporal integration helps reduce single-frame ambiguity and provides a more stable pose estimate.
3. **Potential-Field-Guided Particle Propagation:** To improve the efficiency and stability of the particle filter by guiding particles away from physically implausible states, specifically hand–object penetrations. This mechanism helps to concentrate particles in physically plausible regions of the pose space, thereby improving sample efficiency and accelerating convergence.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a more reliable and robust framework for in-hand object pose estimation using only tactile feedback, thereby enabling more autonomous and dexterous manipulation in visually challenging environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method integrates a physics-informed tactile likelihood with a sliding-window particle filter operating on the SE(3) manifold, augmented by a potential-field-guided particle propagation strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The system estimates the 6-DoF object pose `x_t = (t_t, q_t)` (translation and quaternion rotation) in the world frame. Tactile observations `Z_t` at each time `t` consist of a set of taxel positions `p^W_k`, 3D contact forces `f^W_k`, and outward taxel front directions `z^W_k`. Taxels are categorized into active-contact (`C_t`) and zero-force (`Z^0_t`) sets based on force magnitude thresholds. The object is represented by a mesh `M`, providing a Signed Distance Field (SDF) `d(p_O)` and a nearest-point operator `NN_M(p_O)` for surface queries.

#### 3.2. Sliding-Window Contact Particle Filter

A particle filter with `N` weighted particles `{(x^(i)_t, w^(i)_t)}` is used to estimate the posterior `p(x_t | Z_{1:t})`.
- **Particle Propagation:** Particles are propagated using a random-walk proposal on SE(3). The proposal variance is annealed, decreasing from an initial value to a final value over `N_a` iterations. For improved local geometry matching, anisotropic perturbations are optionally used, aligned with a selected object axis (e.g., dominant principal axis), with different variances for translation and rotation along/about this axis versus perpendicular directions. An adaptive multiplier adjusts the proposal scale based on the effective sample size (ESS) ratio and changes in the best particle's log-likelihood, ensuring a balance between exploration and exploitation.
- **Sliding-Window Update:** Instead of relying solely on the current tactile frame, the filter maintains a FIFO buffer of the `W` most recent observations. Each particle's pose hypothesis `x` is evaluated using a sliding-window log-likelihood `ℓ_SW(x)`, which is a weighted sum of per-frame log-likelihoods `ℓ(x; Z_{t-k})` with an exponential forgetting factor `α`. This temporal fusion enhances robustness against intermittent contact changes and brief sensing dropouts.
- **Weight Update and Resampling:** Particle weights `w^(i)_t` are updated using a tempered likelihood `exp(β ℓ^(i)_SW)`, where `β` controls the sharpness of the distribution. The ESS ratio `r_{ESS,t}` is monitored, and if it falls below a threshold `τ_ess`, low-variance resampling is performed to prevent weight degeneracy.

#### 3.3. Potential-Guided Proposal

Before stochastic diffusion, a hand–object potential drift is applied to the top-weighted particles.
- **Potential Field:** A potential `U(X)` is defined based on the hand's signed distance field `d_H(·)`, penalizing object surface points that penetrate the hand. This potential is computed over sampled object surface points, optionally restricted to the current contact region.
- **Gradient-Based Drift:** Finite-difference gradients of `U(X)` with respect to translation `∇_t U` and rotation `∇_ω U` are estimated. A damped drift is then applied to the particle's translation and rotation, steering it away from regions of hand–object penetration, which improves sample efficiency and stabilizes convergence. This drift is applied periodically to a subset of high-weight particles.

#### 3.4. Likelihood Factors as Robust Soft Constraints

The per-frame log-likelihood `ℓ(x; Z_t)` is a sum of robust soft-constraint penalties. Each factor is constructed using a Huber loss function applied to a normalized residual, scaled by a weight `λ` and, for active contacts, by a force-dependent weight `ω_k`. The combined likelihood `ℓ(x; Z_t) = ℓ_sd + ℓ_ang + ℓ_fric + ℓ_0 + ℓ_front + ℓ_eq + ℓ_req` incorporates the following terms:
- **Signed-Distance Residual (ℓ_sd):** Ensures consistency between active taxel positions and the object surface. It accounts for compliant indentation with a force-dependent offset. An optional `ℓ_req` penalizes forceful taxels far outside the object.
- **Force-Normal Residual (ℓ_ang):** Measures the angular alignment between the measured 3D contact force and the object's nearest surface normal at the contact point.
- **Friction-Cone Residual (ℓ_fric):** Enforces friction feasibility by penalizing contact forces where the tangential component exceeds the product of the normal force and a nominal friction coefficient `μ`.
- **Front-Side Feasibility Residual (ℓ_front):** Uses the taxel's outward front direction to guard against physically implausible backside contacts.
- **Zero-Force Residual (ℓ_0):** Provides negative evidence from zero-force taxels, penalizing pose hypotheses where these taxels would be inside or too close to the object.
- **Static-Equilibrium Residual (ℓ_eq):** As a weak auxiliary check for quasi-static interaction, it gates measured forces by their SDF consistency and checks global force and torque balance, considering gravity and the object's center of mass.

#### 3.5. Multi-modality Preservation for Symmetric Objects

To prevent premature collapse of particles into an arbitrary symmetric equivalent for objects with rotational symmetries, the filter employs a symmetry-aware mode preservation strategy.
- **Mode Identification:** Particles are clustered based on a mode label derived from a principal-axis flip (aligned or anti-aligned with a reference axis) and a discretized yaw bin around that axis.
- **Grouped Resampling:** During resampling, particles are grouped by their mode label. Low-variance resampling is then performed independently within each mode, ensuring that each plausible symmetry-related hypothesis retains a minimum particle budget.
- **Final Pose Estimate:** The final pose estimate is derived from the dominant mode (the mode with the largest total weight mass). Translation is a weighted mean, and rotation is the principal eigenvector of a weighted sum of quaternions.

#### 3.6. Degeneracy Detection and Relocalization

An auxiliary relocalization mechanism is included for recovery from severe particle collapse or poor initialization.
- **Triggering Criteria:** Relocalization is triggered when specific conditions indicative of severe degeneracy are met, such as high hand–object penetration, persistent low ESS ratio, or very poor best-particle log-likelihood, especially when a minimum number of active taxels are available.
- **Coarse Reinitialization:** Upon triggering, the particle set is reset around a contact-driven coarse estimate. This estimate is computed from a weighted contact centroid and the average force direction, with an offset along the negative force direction to avoid initializing the object inside the hand. Optional local SDF refinement can be applied to this initial seed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Experiments were conducted using an Allegro Hand V5 equipped with 16 PaXini tactile modules across its fingers and palm. Five rigid objects (screwdriver, electrical connector, cuboid, flat bottle, banana) with varying geometries were evaluated using 200 trials per object, each consisting of 240 frames of short quasi-static teleoperated sequences. Object meshes were used for geometry queries, and AprilTags provided ground truth for evaluation. The primary metric was normalized ADD-S (Average Distance of points on model surface) against object scale, reported as median and interquartile range (IQR).

#### 4.1. Main Comparisons with Baselines

The proposed method was compared against several tactile-only baselines:
- **Geometric Baselines:** ICP (point-to-point alignment), ICP-force (ICP with force-displaced taxels), and Covariance T2G (covariance-based tactile-to-geometry matching).
- **Particle Filter Ablations:** Contact-SDF PF (particle filter with only signed-distance likelihood) and Single-frame Physics PF (full physics-informed likelihood, but `W=1` for no temporal fusion).
- **Learning Baseline:** Dikhale-TactileOnly\* (a modified version of a visuo-tactile method using only tactile point clouds).

The results (Table II) show:
- **Superior Accuracy:** The proposed method (with a window length `W=5`) achieved the lowest average normalized ADD-S of 0.361 (median) / 0.124 (IQR) across all five objects. This represents a substantial improvement over all baselines.
 - It outperformed ICP (0.737/0.306), ICP-force (0.706/0.299), and Covariance T2G (0.739/0.635), indicating that sparse active-contact geometry alone is insufficient for robust pose refinement.
 - It also surpassed Contact-SDF PF (0.502/0.209) and Single-frame Physics PF (0.568/0.214), demonstrating the benefits of integrating the full physics-informed likelihood and temporal fusion, respectively.
 - The learning-based Dikhale-TactileOnly\* also yielded higher errors (0.758/0.381).
- **Runtime:** The proposed method had a higher average runtime per update (0.480 s on GPU) compared to baselines (0.018-0.156 s). This positions the current implementation for low-rate quasi-static refinement rather than high-speed dynamic tracking. The internal ablations (Contact-SDF PF and Single-frame Physics PF) provided lower-cost operating points (0.118-0.156 s).

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A detailed ablation study on the screwdriver and connector sequences confirmed the individual contributions of the proposed components (Table IV):
- **Physics-Informed Feasibility:** Comparing the full model to Contact-SDF PF (which lacks the force-normal, friction, zero-force, front-side, and static equilibrium terms) showed a reduction in median ADD-S from 0.461 to 0.375 (18.7% improvement) and IQR from 0.235 to 0.176 (25.1% improvement). This indicates that the additional physics constraints effectively suppress physically implausible poses.
- **Sliding-Window Fusion:** Disabling temporal fusion (`W=1`, Single-frame Physics PF) increased median/IQR normalized ADD-S from 0.375/0.176 to 0.568/0.224. Further analysis (Fig. 2) indicated that a window length of `W=5` offered a practical balance, leading to smoother and lower-error trajectories compared to single-frame updates.
- **Potential-Field Guidance:** Removing the potential-field-guided propagation increased the average convergence length from 12.4 to 45.1 filter updates. This suggests that the guidance mechanism significantly improves sample efficiency by steering particles away from hand–object penetration, contributing to faster convergence.
- **Symmetry-Aware Mode Preservation:** For near-axisymmetric objects, disabling mode preservation worsened ADD-S from 0.375/0.176 to 0.426/0.218 and reduced the success rate from 92% to 71%. This confirms that preserving multiple plausible yaw modes prevents premature collapse under sparse local contacts, which is crucial for symmetric objects.
- **Proposal Design and Scale:** Testing isotropic proposals and varying the anisotropic proposal scales (0.5x, 2.0x nominal) showed stable performance near the nominal settings. This indicates that the proposal design primarily affects convergence speed and dispersion, while the likelihood model remains robust.
- **Relocalization:** Relocalization (Table V) was infrequently triggered in nominal sequences (2.4%), but it proved effective as a recovery mechanism. It reduced the failure rate from 6.1% to 2.6% in nominal sequences and significantly from 28.7% to 8.2% under perturbed initialization (random 30mm translation and 30-degree rotation offsets).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for tactile-only 6-DoF object pose refinement that offers enhanced accuracy and robustness in challenging manipulation scenarios where visual information is limited or absent. Its significance and potential impact lie in several areas:

- **Enabling Robust In-Hand Manipulation:** The capability to accurately maintain an object's pose belief using only tactile feedback is fundamental for advanced in-hand manipulation tasks, such as precise regrasping, reorientation for tool use, or contact-rich assembly operations, especially in environments with visual occlusions (e.g., tight spaces, cluttered environments, or during complex grasping maneuvers).
- **Integrated Physics-Informed Approach:** The core contribution is the principled integration of multiple physics-informed constraints (geometric, force-normal, friction, non-penetration) into a single, robust Bayesian filtering framework. This demonstrates that leveraging a comprehensive physical model of contact interactions significantly improves pose estimation accuracy compared to purely geometric or less informed approaches.
- **Improved Robustness through Temporal Fusion and Guidance:** The sliding-window temporal fusion mechanism addresses the inherent sparsity and intermittency of tactile data, leading to more stable and lower-error pose trajectories. The potential-field-guided particle propagation mechanism enhances the efficiency and stability of the particle filter by actively steering particles away from physically implausible configurations (penetration), which is critical for real-world robustness.
- **Handling Object Symmetries:** The development of a symmetry-aware mode preservation strategy is important for practical application, as many common objects exhibit symmetries that can lead to ambiguous tactile readings. By maintaining multiple plausible pose hypotheses, the method avoids premature collapse and provides more reliable estimates for such objects.
- **Foundational for Future Work:** The research identifies critical components for robust tactile sensing and poses it as a solid foundation for future advancements. Acknowledged limitations, such as reliance on rigid watertight meshes, perfectly calibrated taxels, quasi-static assumptions, and computational bottlenecks, provide clear directions for future research in areas like deformable objects, sensor calibration, dynamic tracking, and real-time performance optimization.
- **Complementary to Vision:** While tactile-only, this work can serve as a crucial component in hybrid visuo-tactile systems. In situations where vision fails, the tactile system can take over, providing resilience to the overall perception pipeline.

The proposed method advances the state of the art in tactile perception for robotics, demonstrating a practical approach to object pose refinement that could contribute to more autonomous and dexterous robotic systems in diverse applications.
