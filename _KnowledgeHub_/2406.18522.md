# ChronoMagic-Bench: A Benchmark for Metamorphic Evaluation of Text-to-Time-lapse Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced ChronoMagic-Bench and ChronoMagic-Pro, a specialized benchmark and large-scale dataset, respectively, to evaluate text-to-time-lapse video generation. This work also developed new metrics, MTScore and CHScore, demonstrating that existing T2V models generally struggle with generating videos depicting significant, long-duration physical changes but can improve with fine-tuning on the new dataset.

## Problem

- Existing Text-to-Video (T2V) evaluation primarily focuses on general video quality and text relevance, neglecting the unique challenges of 'time-lapse' or 'metamorphic' video generation.
- Current benchmarks and metrics are not designed to accurately quantify metamorphic amplitude (degree of change over time) or temporal coherence (logical progression) crucial for time-lapse videos.
- Large-scale T2V datasets predominantly contain general videos with limited metamorphic content and short durations, hindering models from learning and reproducing complex physical transformations.

## Method

- Proposed **ChronoMagic-Bench**, a new benchmark featuring 1,649 diverse prompts and reference videos categorized across physical, biological, human creation, and meteorological phenomena.
- Developed two novel automatic metrics: MTScore for metamorphic amplitude (including coarse-grained retrieval and fine-grained GPT-4o variants) and CHScore for temporal coherence, based on a video tracking model.
- Created ChronoMagic-Pro, the first large-scale dataset of 460K high-quality 720p time-lapse video-text pairs with detailed, time-aware captions, specifically curated for high physical content and metamorphic amplitude.

## Results

- Evaluation of **14 open-source** and **4 closed-source** T2V models on **ChronoMagic-Bench** revealed that models trained on general videos often produce limited metamorphic amplitude, generating general actions instead of significant state changes.
- Fine-tuning **OpenSoraPlan v1.1** on **ChronoMagic-Pro** using a 'Magic Training Strategy' resulted in improved performance across visual quality (UMT-FVD), text relevance (UMTScore), and metamorphic amplitude (MTScore, GPT4o-MTScore), validating the dataset's efficacy.
- Human preference studies confirmed the strong alignment of MTScore, CHScore, and GPT4o-MTScore with human judgments, while the UMTScore showed low correlation for text relevance in time-lapse videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Most contemporary T2V models, both open-source and closed-source, generally lack the ability to generate time-lapse videos with substantial and physically accurate changes over extended durations.
> - Models specifically trained with time-lapse videos (e.g., MagicTime, CogVideoX-2B) exhibit notably superior metamorphic amplitude compared to those trained solely on general video datasets.
> - The newly developed MTScore and CHScore metrics demonstrate strong consistency with human perception, providing reliable automated evaluation for metamorphic amplitude and temporal coherence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Text-to-Video (T2V) generation has experienced rapid advancements with the proliferation of generative models. However, the evaluation methodologies for these models have primarily focused on general video content, assessing visual quality and text relevance using metrics such as Frechet Inception Distance (FID), Frechet Video Distance (FVD), and CLIPScore, or their enhanced versions like UMT-FVD and UMTScore.

This existing landscape exhibits a notable gap: the evaluation of T2V models’ ability to generate "time-lapse" or "metamorphic" videos has been largely overlooked. Time-lapse videos, characterized by significant changes over extended durations, inherently contain more "physical priors" (e.g., processes like plant growth, melting, construction, celestial movements) compared to typical short, action-focused videos. Current benchmarks, such as UCF-101, Make-a-Video-Eval, MSR-VTT, FETV, and VBench, do not specifically account for the metamorphic amplitude (the degree of change over time) or the temporal coherence (logical progression and continuity) required for accurate time-lapse generation.

Furthermore, existing large-scale T2V datasets (e.g., WebVid-10M, Panda-70M, HD-VG-130M, InternVid) predominantly comprise general videos with limited metamorphic content and short persistence of change. This limitation restricts the capacity of models trained on these datasets to learn and reproduce complex physical transformations, leading to a struggle in generating videos with rich physical knowledge. While some smaller time-lapse specific datasets exist (e.g., Time-Lapse-D, Sky Time-Lapse, ChronoMagic), they either lack corresponding text descriptions or are not of sufficient scale to drive significant model development.

This research addresses these deficiencies by proposing a specialized benchmark and dataset designed to evaluate and foster the generation of time-lapse videos that exhibit pronounced metamorphic characteristics and temporal consistency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a comprehensive evaluation framework for Text-to-Video (T2V) models, specifically targeting their capabilities in generating time-lapse videos. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in the current T2V research landscape:

1. **Inadequate Evaluation of Metamorphic Capabilities:** Existing T2V benchmarks predominantly focus on assessing visual quality and text relevance in general video generation. They largely overlook the models' ability to synthesize videos depicting significant temporal transformations, referred to as "metamorphic amplitude," and maintain logical "temporal coherence" throughout these extended processes. This oversight hinders the development of models capable of generating videos with rich physical content, such as biological growth, construction processes, or natural phenomena.

2. **Lack of Specialized Metrics:** The standard evaluation metrics (e.g., FID, FVD, CLIPScore, UMT-FVD, UMTScore) are not designed to quantify metamorphic amplitude or temporal coherence effectively. While some benchmarks attempt to assess coherence through human evaluation or feature space calculations, these methods are often expensive, time-consuming, or lack intuitiveness. A need exists for automated metrics that can objectively measure these specific attributes crucial for time-lapse video quality.

3. **Absence of Large-Scale Time-Lapse Datasets:** Current large-scale T2V datasets primarily consist of general videos that typically exhibit minimal metamorphic changes and short durations. This data composition limits the exposure of generative models to diverse physical priors, thereby impeding their capacity to learn and produce compelling time-lapse sequences. A dedicated, large-scale dataset of high-quality time-lapse videos with detailed captions is necessary to train and improve T2V models in this specialized domain.

4. **Need for Comprehensive Model Assessment:** With the rapid proliferation of T2V models, both open-source and closed-source, there is an urgent requirement for a standardized and thorough evaluation system. Such a system would enable researchers and practitioners to understand the strengths and weaknesses of different architectures across various categories of time-lapse phenomena, facilitating informed model selection and targeted improvement efforts.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:
- To propose **ChronoMagic-Bench**, a new benchmark specifically designed for the metamorphic evaluation of text-to-time-lapse video generation, covering physics, biology, and chemistry capabilities through free-form text prompts.
- To introduce two novel automatic metrics, **MTScore** (Metamorphic Score) and **CHScore** (Temporal Coherence Score), engineered to accurately quantify metamorphic amplitude and temporal coherence, respectively, and to align with human perception.
- To conduct a comprehensive evaluation of representative T2V models using ChronoMagic-Bench to identify their performance characteristics, strengths, and weaknesses across different time-lapse categories.
- To create **ChronoMagic-Pro**, a large-scale dataset comprising high-quality time-lapse video-text pairs with detailed captions, specifically curated to possess high physical content and substantial metamorphic amplitude, thereby serving as a resource for advancing T2V research in this specialized domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces ChronoMagic-Bench as a benchmark for evaluating text-to-time-lapse video generation and ChronoMagic-Pro as a large-scale dataset. The methodology encompasses benchmark construction, development of new automatic metrics, and dataset curation.

#### 3.1. ChronoMagic-Bench Construction

1. **Prompt Construction:** To ensure comprehensive evaluation of time-lapse video generation capabilities, the authors initiated the creation of a diverse search term database. This database was manually curated to include terms suitable for broadly applicable time-lapse videos. After filtering based on the frequency of obtainable videos, 75 distinct subcategories were identified. These subcategories were further grouped into four major categories:
 - **Biological:** Encompassing living organisms (e.g., plant growth, animal activities).
 - **Human Creation:** Including objects or processes influenced by human activities (e.g., building construction, urban traffic).
 - **Meteorological:** Covering atmospheric phenomena (e.g., cloud movement, storm formation).
 - **Physical:** Relating to non-biological physical phenomena (e.g., water flow, volcanic eruptions).
 For each of these 75 subcategories, at least 20 high-quality videos were crawled from video platforms, accumulating a total of 1,649 videos. GPT-4o was subsequently employed to generate accurate captions for these videos, which then served as the text prompts for the benchmark. The length of these prompts was limited to 77 tokens to ensure compatibility with CLIP-based text encoders commonly used in T2V models.

2. **Benchmark Statistics:** ChronoMagic-Bench comprises 1,649 prompts, each paired with a reference video and categorized by sub-category and major category. The distribution across 75 subcategories is relatively balanced. Analysis of the word cloud and word count range of the prompts indicates a focus on terms reflecting change, progression, and duration (e.g., "transitioning," "progressing," "increasing," "gradually"), along with specific terms from the four major categories.

#### 3.2. New Automatic Metrics

Recognizing the limitations of existing metrics that primarily assess visual quality and text relevance, the authors developed two new automatic metrics to quantify metamorphic amplitude and temporal coherence:

1. **Metamorphic Score (MTScore):**
 - **Coarse-grained MTScore:** This score is calculated using a video retrieval model. Ten retrieval sentences were designed: five describing general videos and five describing time-lapse videos. An input video is processed by the retrieval model, generating probabilities for each sentence. The coarse-grained MTScore (S_c) is derived as the ratio of the sum of probabilities for metamorphic sentences to the sum of probabilities for both metamorphic and general sentences.
 - **Fine-grained GPT4o-MTScore:** Leveraging GPT-4o's understanding capabilities, this score uses GPT-4o as an evaluator. A 5-point rating scale is employed, with detailed guidelines provided for each score level (e.g., 1 for minimal change, 5 for dramatic change). Uniformly sampled frames from the video are input to GPT-4o for scoring.

2. **Temporal Coherence Score (CHScore):** This metric is based on a video tracking model (specifically, CoTracker) and designed to assess the smoothness and logical progression of video content.
 - The input video is processed by a pre-trained tracking model to obtain `p_vis[i, j]`, representing the visibility of tracking point `j` in frame `i`.
 - The number of missing tracking points `m[i]` is counted for each frame, accounting for `temporally coherent disappearance` (i.e., points disappearing due to camera/object movement are excluded from the `m[i]` calculation).
 - The change in missed points between consecutive frames, `Δm[i]`, is calculated.
 - Several sub-metrics are derived:
 - `R_missed`: Average proportion of missed points per frame.
 - `V_missed`: Variation in missed points between consecutive frames.
 - `R_cut`: Ratio of frames requiring editing due to significant point tracking issues.
 - `C_missed`: Cumulative change in missed points exceeding a threshold.
 - `M_missed`: Maximum continuous change in missed points.
 - These normalized sub-metrics are combined into a final CHScore using a weighted sum, with specific weights (`λ_1` to `λ_5`) assigned based on their significance to coherence.

#### 3.3. ChronoMagic-Pro Dataset Creation

To address the scarcity of large-scale time-lapse datasets for training T2V models, ChronoMagic-Pro was developed:

1. **Multi-Aspect Data Curation:** The dataset was constructed by collecting time-lapse videos based on the search terms used for ChronoMagic-Bench. Initially, 66,226 original videos were acquired. These were filtered for quality based on metadata (views, comments, likes).

2. **Data Preprocessing:** To obtain semantically consistent single-scene video clips, a two-stage process was applied:
 - **Automatic Video Clips Cutting:** OpenCV was used to split videos by detecting significant pixel differences between adjacent frames (`D_t`).
 - **Video Clips Recovery:** ImageBind was then used to recombine erroneously split clips by analyzing feature space differences between adjacent clips (`F_i`). This resulted in 460K single-scene video clips.

3. **Time-Aware Annotation:** For captioning, an annotation strategy similar to MagicTime was employed, but with the open-source ShareGPT4Video instead of GPT-4V to reduce computational cost. This involved uniformly sampling multiple frames from each video clip, using the multimodal model to describe each frame, and then summarizing these descriptions, along with their temporal positions, into a comprehensive video caption. This process aimed to ensure captions captured high physical content and metamorphic amplitude.

4. **Dataset Statistics:** ChronoMagic-Pro contains 460K high-quality 720p video-text pairs across 75 categories. The average video length is 234 seconds, with 27% exceeding 60 seconds. 97% of videos are in 720p resolution, and 95% of captions are over 100 words, indicating detailed descriptions. Aesthetic scores were predominantly high, with 73% of videos scoring between 4 and 6, and 14% scoring above 6. The word distribution in captions emphasizes terms related to changes and processes over time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research involved a comprehensive evaluation of various Text-to-Video (T2V) models using the newly introduced ChronoMagic-Bench and ChronoMagic-Pro dataset.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Setup

- **Models Evaluated:** Fourteen open-source T2V models (including U-Net-based like ModelScopeT2V, ZeroScope, T2V-zero, LaVie, AnimateDiff V3, VideoCrafter2, MCM-MSLION, MagicTime; and DiT-based like Latte, OpenSora 1.1, OpenSora 1.2, OpenSoraPlan v1.1, EasyAnimate V3, CogVideoX-2B) were selected. Four closed-source models (Gen-2, Pika-2.0, Dream Machine, KeLing) were also evaluated on a subset of the benchmark.
- **Evaluation Criteria:** Four aspects were assessed: Visual Quality (using UMT-FVD), Text Relevance (using UMTScore), Metamorphic Amplitude (using MTScore and GPT4o-MTScore), and Temporal Coherence (using CHScore). Human evaluations were conducted to cross-verify the automatic metrics.
- **Implementation:** Each open-source model generated three videos for each of the 1,649 prompts in ChronoMagic-Bench, totaling 4,947 videos per model, which were then evaluated using the automated metrics.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation (Open-Source Models)

- **Metamorphic Amplitude:** MagicTime and CogVideoX-2B, which were trained with time-lapse videos (ChronoMagic and refined Panda70M/COCO/WebVid respectively), demonstrated the highest MTScore and GPT4o-MTScore. Other models, primarily trained on general videos, produced videos with limited metamorphic amplitude. This indicates a general inability of models trained solely on general video datasets to generate videos with significant state changes.
- **Temporal Coherence:** ZeroScope exhibited a high CHScore, suggesting good coherence, despite limited metamorphic amplitude. In contrast, T2V-zero, a zero-shot model, recorded the lowest CHScore, indicating poor temporal consistency. Overall, U-Net-based and DiT-based models showed comparable CHScores, but U-Net-based models generally displayed superior average metamorphic amplitude.
- **Visual Quality and Text Relevance:** CogVideoX-2B and EasyAnimate V3 performed strongly in visual quality and text relevance. OpenSora 1.1/1.2 and OpenSoraPlan v1.1 showed visual quality comparable to U-Net methods but slightly inferior text relevance. The UMTScore metric for text relevance was observed to be inconsistent with human perception, particularly for time-lapse videos.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Evaluation (Open-Source Models)

- Visual inspection reinforced the quantitative findings: most T2V models, both U-Net and DiT-based, struggled to generate time-lapse videos that followed prompts involving significant motion and temporal spans (e.g., radish seedlings germinating, house construction in Minecraft). Instead, they often produced videos resembling general actions with minimal or incorrect transformations. MagicTime and CogVideoX-2B were exceptions that could generate more compelling metamorphic content.
- Flickering was identified as a common issue, particularly in models like T2V-zero and OpenSora 1.2, even when single-frame quality was high, signaling poor temporal coherence.

#### 4.4. Human Preference Alignment

- A human study involving 212 participants evaluating generated videos from 16 prompts confirmed the effectiveness of the new metrics. MTScore, CHScore, and GPT4o-MTScore demonstrated strong consistency with human perception, as indicated by high Kendall and Spearman coefficients.
- The UMTScore, however, showed a low correlation with human judgments for text relevance in time-lapse videos, suggesting its limitations for this specific domain.

#### 4.5. Extended Analysis (Closed-Source Models on ChronoMagic-Bench-150)

- A subset of 150 prompts (ChronoMagic-Bench-150) was used to evaluate closed-source models.
- **Metamorphic Amplitude:** Dream Machine exhibited relatively higher metamorphic amplitude compared to other closed-source models, capable of generating moderate night-to-day transitions, though still falling short of complex transformations like plant growth.
- **Text Relevance:** Pika-2.0 showed the weakest text relevance.
- **Visual Quality:** DiT-based closed-source models (Dream Machine, KeLing) generally outperformed U-Net-based ones (Gen-2, Pika-2.0) in visual quality, producing more realistic scenes.
- Overall, closed-source models demonstrated superior visual quality and text relevance in qualitative assessments compared to open-source counterparts, despite the automatic UMT-FVD and UMTScore metrics not fully capturing these nuanced differences. Most still struggled with complex metamorphic changes.

#### 4.6. Exploratory Experiment on ChronoMagic-Pro

- To validate ChronoMagic-Pro, OpenSoraPlan v1.1 was fine-tuned on a randomly selected 10,000 video-text pairs from the dataset, using a "Magic Training Strategy."
- The fine-tuned model showed improvements across all evaluated metrics: UMT-FVD (visual quality), UMTScore (text relevance), MTScore, and GPT4o-MTScore (metamorphic amplitude).
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated that the fine-tuned model could generate videos with actual object state changes (e.g., sunflower seed germination) rather than just lighting or camera movements, while maintaining visual quality and coherence. This suggests ChronoMagic-Pro's efficacy in endowing models with the ability to generate physically meaningful time-lapse videos. Simple full-parameter fine-tuning was found less effective than the Magic Training Strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of Text-to-Video (T2V) generation by addressing critical limitations in evaluation and data availability for time-lapse video synthesis.

1. **Advancement in Evaluation Frameworks:** The introduction of **ChronoMagic-Bench** provides a specialized and comprehensive benchmark for metamorphic evaluation of T2V models. By focusing on metamorphic amplitude and temporal coherence—aspects largely overlooked by existing general video benchmarks—it establishes a more rigorous standard for assessing model capabilities. This benchmark allows for a nuanced understanding of how T2V models handle complex physical, biological, and human-created transformations over time, moving beyond mere visual fidelity and text-matching.

2. **Novel Automated Metrics:** The development of **MTScore** and **CHScore** fills a crucial gap in automated evaluation. These metrics offer objective and quantifiable measures for metamorphic amplitude and temporal coherence, respectively, and have been shown to align with human perception. This enables more efficient and consistent assessment of T2V models, reducing reliance on expensive and subjective human evaluations. Their integration with existing metrics allows for a holistic, four-dimensional evaluation of T2V generation.

3. **New Insights for Model Development and Selection:** The comprehensive evaluation of various open-source and closed-source T2V models using ChronoMagic-Bench yields valuable insights into their current strengths and weaknesses. The findings highlight that most contemporary T2V models struggle with generating videos depicting substantial, long-duration physical changes. This points to a significant area for future research, particularly in training models to encode richer physical priors. The provided guidelines for model selection offer practical advice for researchers and users, informing decisions based on specific generation requirements (e.g., for metamorphic content, visual quality, or temporal stability).

4. **Enabling Resource for Future Research:** The creation of **ChronoMagic-Pro**, the first large-scale, high-quality time-lapse video-text dataset, is a substantial contribution. This dataset, rich in physical content and metamorphic amplitude, directly addresses the data scarcity that has limited the development of time-lapse T2V models. Its availability is expected to facilitate the training of new models capable of generating more realistic, dynamic, and meaningful time-lapse videos, thereby promoting advancements in T2V research. The exploratory experiment demonstrating improved metamorphic generation capabilities of a model fine-tuned on ChronoMagic-Pro validates its potential as a training resource.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced T2V Capabilities:** The benchmark and dataset could accelerate the development of T2V models that can generate more sophisticated and physically accurate simulations of real-world processes, from scientific visualizations (e.g., cellular growth, chemical reactions) to creative content (e.g., building construction, artistic processes).
- **Scientific Discovery and Education:** Improved time-lapse generation could provide novel tools for scientific research, allowing for synthetic generation of complex phenomena for analysis, and for educational purposes, creating compelling visual explanations of processes that unfold over long periods.
- **Creative Industries:** The ability to generate high-quality time-lapse videos opens new avenues for content creation in filmmaking, advertising, and animation, enabling the rapid prototyping of complex temporal sequences.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
1. **Data Quality and Licensing:** The reliance on YouTube for ChronoMagic-Pro data means varying video quality and a predominant CC BY 4.0 license, which, while permitting academic research, has broader usage limitations. Extensive filtering was required, and future efforts could focus on acquiring data with more permissive licenses or from more controlled environments.
2. **Metric Alignment:** Despite the careful design, a gap still exists between the proposed MTScore and CHScore and perfect human judgment. Future work aims to refine these automated metrics for even closer alignment with human preferences.
3. **Societal Impact:** The paper's ethics statement highlights concerns about the environmental impact of large model training, the potential for generating misleading content, and dataset biases (e.g., English-only captions). Mitigation strategies, such as focusing on algorithm optimization for reduced energy consumption, content authenticity verification mechanisms, and future multilingual support in dataset construction, are suggested.

Overall, ChronoMagic-Bench and ChronoMagic-Pro represent a foundational step towards robustly evaluating and significantly advancing the capabilities of T2V models in generating dynamic, physically coherent time-lapse videos.
