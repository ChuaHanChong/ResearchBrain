# Video-MSR: Benchmarking Multi-hop Spatial Reasoning Capabilities of MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Baidu Inc., in collaboration with Nanjing University, The University of Queensland, and Peking University, introduced Video-MSR, the first benchmark to evaluate multi-hop spatial reasoning (MSR) in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) using dynamic video. The study revealed that state-of-the-art MLLMs struggle significantly with complex spatial logic, but performance can be substantially improved through specialized instruction tuning with the MSR-9K dataset.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily focus on "single-step perception-to-judgment" tasks, which only evaluate basic object recognition or simple spatial relationships.
- The multi-hop spatial reasoning (MSR) capabilities of MLLMs, involving complex 2D/3D spatial relationships and logical chains, remained largely unbenchmarked.
- There was a critical gap between MLLMs' superficial visual perception and their deep logical reasoning abilities, limiting their application in advanced embodied AI tasks.

## Method

- Developed ==Video-MSR==, a novel benchmark featuring four distinct multi-hop spatial reasoning tasks: Constrained Localization, Chain-based Reference Retrieval, Route Planning, and Counterfactual Physical Deduction.
- Created a scalable data construction pipeline that combines automated video-QA generation with a ==dual-phase verification process== (automated blind test and manual expert review) to produce **4,993** high-quality video-QA pairs.
- Curated ==MSR-9K==, a specialized instruction-tuning dataset of **8,369** MSR QA pairs, designed to enhance MLLMs' capacity for deep spatial reasoning.

## Results

- State-of-the-art MLLMs achieved overall accuracies between **20%** and **44%** on Video-MSR, with Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (**43.92%**) and GPT-4o (**41.87%**) being top performers, yet indicating significant MSR limitations.
- Performance varied across tasks: Gemini-2.5-Flash excelled at Chain-based Reference Retrieval (**66.33%**) and Route Planning (**61.21%**) but performed poorly on Counterfactual Physical Deduction (**24.77%**) and Constrained Localization.
- Fine-tuning models on the MSR-9K instruction-tuning dataset yielded substantial improvements, with Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct showing a **+7.82%** absolute gain in overall accuracy and a remarkable **+48.62%** improvement on the Route Planning task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit significant performance drops, spatial disorientation, and hallucination when faced with multi-hop spatial reasoning tasks.
> - MSR proficiency is not solely an emergent property of model scale; generic enhancement strategies like Chain-of-Thought prompting often prove detrimental, and atomic capability training does not translate to long-horizon spatial reasoning.
> - Specialized, logically-grounded instruction-tuning data is crucial for fostering deep spatial reasoning, demonstrating substantial performance gains in complex MSR tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, demonstrating remarkable proficiency in tasks requiring visual perception and semantic understanding. These models are increasingly capable of recognizing objects, comprehending scenes, and generating human-like responses based on visual inputs. However, the existing benchmarks designed to evaluate MLLMs primarily focus on "single-step perception-to-judgment" tasks. These tasks typically involve direct recognition of objects, simple comparisons, or basic spatial relationships, where a single inference step is sufficient to derive an answer from explicit visual cues. Examples include object counting, perspective transformations, relative positional awareness, and depth estimation, as noted by the authors referencing works like VSR, CV-Bench, VSI-Bench, and MM-Perspective.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the "Multi-hop Spatial Reasoning (MSR)" capabilities of MLLMs remain significantly underexplored. True visual intelligence, as the authors emphasize, extends beyond superficial recognition to encompass the ability to understand and manipulate complex 2D and 3D spatial relationships, synthesizing visual cues into coherent mental models and adhering to spatial constraints across long logical chains. This higher-order cognition is essential for human-like interaction with the physical world, underpinning abilities like inferring 3D structures from 2D views or planning multi-step routes.

The work directly addresses this gap by introducing **Video-MSR**, the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate MSR in dynamic video scenarios. By moving beyond static images and single-step inferences, Video-MSR pushes the boundaries of MLLM evaluation into more complex, real-world-like challenges. It acknowledges that spatial reasoning is a prerequisite for advanced downstream tasks such as embodied planning, robotics, and complex human-AI interaction, where systems must not only perceive but also understand and act within a spatio-temporal environment. Therefore, this benchmark is a crucial step towards developing MLLMs with deeper visual intelligence and more robust reasoning capabilities, moving from merely "seeing" to truly "understanding" space and action within it.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **systematically evaluate and benchmark the Multi-hop Spatial Reasoning (MSR) capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in dynamic video environments.** The authors are motivated by the recognition that while MLLMs have excelled in basic visual perception and semantic understanding, their ability to perform complex, multi-step spatial logic remains largely untested and poorly understood.

Specifically, the paper aims to address two critical questions:
1. **To what extent do MLLMs possess the capacity for MSR?** This involves assessing the current state-of-the-art models' inherent abilities to process and reason over intricate spatial relationships that unfold over time within video contexts.
2. **What are the primary bottlenecks limiting their performance in MSR?** By identifying the specific failure modes and deficiencies of MLLMs when faced with multi-hop spatial reasoning, the research seeks to provide actionable insights for future model development.

The motivation for this work is deeply rooted in the foundational role of spatial thinking in human intelligence. As Barbara Tversky states, spatial thinking is "the foundation for all thought," enabling navigation, abstract concept structuring, and complex problem-solving. For AI, developing strong spatial reasoning is not merely an academic pursuit but a critical prerequisite for achieving high-level reasoning and interaction in real-world applications, such as autonomous navigation, robotic manipulation, and advanced embodied AI. By rigorously defining and benchmarking MSR, the authors intend to highlight the "critical gap between superficial visual perception and deep logical reasoning," thereby guiding the trajectory of future multimodal intelligence research. Furthermore, a secondary objective is to provide a solution to identified shortcomings by curating a specialized instruction-tuning dataset, MSR-9K, to empower models with enhanced MSR capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is the introduction of **Video-MSR**, the first benchmark designed for Multi-hop Spatial Reasoning (MSR) in dynamic video scenarios. The methodology is comprehensive, encompassing a formal problem definition, a novel task taxonomy, a scalable data construction pipeline, and a rigorous evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The authors formally define MSR as a process that necessitates the construction of an explicit spatial reasoning chain, rather than direct perception-to-judgment. This process is modeled as sequential transitions of spatial states ($S = \{s_0, s_1, \ldots, s_T\}$), where $T$ is the number of reasoning "hops." Each state $s_t$ depends on the preceding state $s_{t-1}$, specific spatial constraints $c_t$ from the query, and visual evidence in the video $V$. The objective is to maximize the joint probability of the reasoning chain and the final answer, requiring recursive extraction of spatial features to form a coherent chain.

#### 3.2. Task Taxonomy

Video-MSR comprises four distinct task categories, each designed to probe a specific dimension of spatial reasoning:
- **Constrained Localization (CL):** Evaluates exclusionary spatial reasoning. Models must identify a broad set of objects, then filter them based on spatial constraints (e.g., "ignore objects in a room with a bed"), and finally pinpoint the target among the remainder. This tests logical negation and set-based spatial operations.
- **Chain-based Reference Retrieval (CRR):** Assesses sequential spatial dependencies. Models must trace a path from an anchor object through intermediate spatial nodes using directional cues (e.g., "move left to find the first washbasin"). This task strictly adheres to the Markovian property, making it sensitive to error propagation.
- **Route Planning (RP):** Mimics embodied navigation, challenging models to generate a feasible trajectory from a start to a goal point while respecting physical or semantic constraints (e.g., "avoid touching the soccer ball"). This requires constructing a 3D mental map and planning in a potential action space.
- **Counterfactual Physical Deduction (CPD):** Probes predictive capabilities for dynamic spatial interactions. Models are given a hypothetical scenario (e.g., "acoustic guitar is tipped over sideways") and must deduce the physical consequence based on visual estimates of dimensions and distances. This tests internalized physical common sense and spatial kinematics.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Construction

The construction pipeline employs a sophisticated, visually-grounded approach combining automated generation with multi-stage verification:
- **Data Collection:** 3,052 diverse video instances from six high-fidelity public datasets (ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, ADT, S3DIS, ProcTHOR) are curated. These datasets provide a rich spectrum of real-world and synthetic indoor environments, ensuring object diversity and complexity.
- **Question-Answer Generation:** Manually designed templates for each of the four tasks guide the generation process. Gemini-3.0-Pro serves as the core generation engine, prompted with video clips and templates to synthesize questions strictly grounded in visual details and necessitating complex spatial reasoning chains.
- **Quality Control (Dual-Phase Verification):**
 - **Automated Blind Test:** Text-only queries (without video) are fed to Gemini-3.0-Pro (with multi-sampling) to discard questions solvable without visual context, ensuring visual grounding.
 - **Manual Expert Verification:** 10 human experts conduct a final examination to eliminate hallucinations, verify logical correctness, and ensure ground truth accuracy.
- **Data Statistics:** The final Video-MSR comprises 4,993 high-quality QA pairs, stratified into "easy" and "hard" categories based on reasoning steps and video duration.

#### 3.4. MSR-9K Instruction-Tuning Dataset

To enhance MLLM capabilities, a specialized instruction-tuning dataset, MSR-9K, containing 8,369 MSR QA pairs, is also curated using a similar pipeline.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol

- **Models Evaluated:** A comprehensive set of 20 state-of-the-art MLLMs, including lightweight open-source models (e.g., Qwen3-VL, InternVL, LLaVA-NeXT-Video) and proprietary systems (Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-4o).
- **Evaluation Environment:** Open-source models use 8x NVIDIA A800 (80GB) GPUs; proprietary models are accessed via official APIs. Greedy decoding strategy is adopted for reproducibility.
- **Metrics:**
 - For multiple-choice tasks (CL, CPD), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct acts as a parser, extracting predictions for direct string comparison against ground truth.
 - For open-ended tasks (CRR, RP), Gemini-3.0-Pro is used as an "expert evaluator" (LLM-as-a-Judge) to assess correctness and logical coherence, comparing model responses against ground truth and video context.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation of 20 state-of-the-art MLLMs on the Video-MSR benchmark revealed significant insights into the current limitations and capabilities of these models in multi-hop spatial reasoning.

#### 4.1. Significant Performance Gaps and Failure Modes

The most critical finding is that while MLLMs demonstrate considerable proficiency in surface-level visual perception tasks, they exhibit **distinct and significant performance drops** when confronted with MSR tasks. Models frequently suffer from **spatial disorientation and hallucination** during multi-step deductions, indicating a fundamental lack of deep internalization of combined spatial perception and logical reasoning within their architectures.

#### 4.2. Top-Performing Models

- Among open-source models, the **Qwen3-VL series** generally emerged as the most capable, with Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct achieving the highest overall accuracy of 43.92%.
- For proprietary models, **GPT-4o** demonstrated the strongest overall performance at 41.87%.

#### 4.3. Task-Specific Performance Observations

- **Constrained Localization (CL):** Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (38.44%) and GPT-4o (38.07%) performed best. Surprisingly, Gemini-2.5-Flash significantly underperformed, lagging behind even smaller open-source models, suggesting a weakness in handling multi-constraint spatial filtering.
- **Chain-based Reference Retrieval (CRR):** Gemini-2.5-Flash secured the top spot with 66.33%, followed by Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (57.33%). This indicates Gemini-2.5-Flash excels at sequential, single-threaded spatial localization, but its lower CL performance suggests difficulty with simultaneous processing of multiple spatial conditions.
- **Route Planning (RP):** Gemini-2.5-Flash (61.21%) and Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (57.93%) again led this task, which shares similar requirements with CRR. In contrast, the InternVL series and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B showed notably poor performance.
- **Counterfactual Physical Deduction (CPD):** Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (38.36%) and Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (36.53%) achieved the highest scores. Intriguingly, both Gemini-2.5-Flash (24.77%) and GPT-4o (25.57%) performed poorly, ranking among the lowest, highlighting a significant gap in their capacity for implicit spatial reasoning and mental simulation of physical interactions.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis Insights

- **Non-monotonic Scaling Law:** The study found a non-monotonic relationship between model size and MSR proficiency. While some families (e.g., InternVL3) generally improved with scale, others (e.g., InternVL3.5, Qwen3-VL) showed volatility, with smaller models sometimes outperforming larger ones on specific tasks or subsets. This suggests MSR is not solely an emergent property of scale but requires specific architectural biases or high-quality data fostering logical spatial connectivity.
- **Ineffectiveness of Existing Enhancement Strategies:**
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Contrary to its success in other domains, CoT often proved detrimental for MSR tasks (performance drops of 1.80% to 4.54%). This was attributed to models fabricating non-existent spatial details, leading to error propagation.
 - **Atomic Capability Training:** Models fine-tuned on basic spatial concepts (e.g., Cambrian-S-7B on VSI-590K) unexpectedly suffered performance drops (over 5%), indicating that strong fundamental perception alone does not translate to constructing long-horizon spatial reasoning chains.
- **Efficacy of MSR-9K Instruction Tuning:** Fine-tuning models on the specialized MSR-9K dataset yielded substantial improvements. Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct saw a remarkable +7.82% absolute gain in overall accuracy, with the RP task improving by +48.62%. Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct also gained +3.10%. These gains, particularly on "hard" subsets, validate the necessity of logically grounded spatial data for enhancing deep reasoning capabilities.
- **Benchmark Robustness:** An ablation study demonstrated that the choice of LLM-as-a-Judge (Gemini-3.0-Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B) for open-ended tasks yielded highly consistent performance scores with negligible variance, confirming the objectivity and rigor of the Video-MSR benchmark.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Video-MSR marks a significant advancement in the evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with profound implications for future AI research and development.

**Addressing a Critical Research Gap:** Video-MSR is the first benchmark specifically designed to assess multi-hop spatial reasoning in dynamic video environments. This directly addresses a crucial limitation in existing MLLM evaluations, which predominantly focus on single-step, superficial visual perception tasks. By doing so, it provides a much-needed tool to probe deeper cognitive capabilities essential for true visual intelligence.

**Revealing Fundamental Bottlenecks:** The comprehensive evaluation demonstrates unequivocally that current state-of-the-art MLLMs, despite their advancements in perception, struggle significantly with complex spatial logic. Their susceptibility to spatial disorientation and hallucination during multi-step deductions reveals a critical architectural and data-related deficiency. This finding is pivotal, as it challenges the assumption that scaling up models or relying on basic visual training data will automatically confer advanced reasoning abilities. It underscores the need for more targeted research into architectures and training paradigms that can internalize and apply complex spatial constraints.

**Guiding Future MLLM Development:** The benchmark provides clear, actionable insights for the research community. It highlights that MSR proficiency is not strictly correlated with model scale, nor can it be trivially improved by generic strategies like Chain-of-Thought prompting or atomic visual training. This will redirect research efforts towards:
- **Architectural Innovations:** Developing MLLM architectures specifically designed to maintain intermediate reasoning states, integrate spatial constraints, and mitigate error propagation in logical chains.
- **Data-Centric Approaches:** Emphasizing the creation and utilization of high-quality, logically-grounded instruction-tuning datasets that teach multi-hop spatial dependencies, similar to the MSR-9K curated in this work.

**Empowering Embodied AI and Robotics:** Multi-hop spatial reasoning is a foundational capability for advanced embodied AI agents and robots. Tasks like route planning, object manipulation, and understanding physical interactions in dynamic environments directly rely on the ability to perceive, reason about, and predict spatial changes over time. By pushing MLLMs towards stronger MSR, Video-MSR contributes directly to the development of more intelligent, autonomous, and safely interactive AI systems in the physical world.

**Establishing a New Baseline for Progress:** The MSR-9K dataset and the demonstrated substantial performance gains achieved by fine-tuning models on it establish a strong baseline for future research. This provides a valuable resource and a clear path for researchers aiming to improve MLLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities.

In conclusion, "Video-MSR" is a landmark contribution that moves the field beyond basic visual recognition towards a deeper understanding of MLLMs' spatial cognitive abilities. It serves as a vital foundation, urging the AI community to innovate in architectural design and data curation to equip MLLMs with the robust spatial intelligence required for truly advanced, human-like reasoning and interaction.
