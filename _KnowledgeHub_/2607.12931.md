# ExToken: Structured Exploration for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Fine-tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> ExToken is a framework designed to enhance the sample efficiency of Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning (VLA-RL) by promoting structured exploration. It uses exploration tokens derived from offline demonstrations to guide the policy, resulting in improved task performance, faster convergence, and better robustness in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning (VLA-RL) faces significant sample inefficiency, making fine-tuning large models prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- Conventional stochastic exploration in VLA-RL often leads to 'action mode collapse,' where policies generate redundant trajectories and fail to explore diverse behaviors, thereby hindering learning progress.
- Existing VLA-RL methods primarily focus on optimizing policy updates or maximizing the utility of already collected data, rather than actively improving the quality and diversity of the interaction data itself.

## Method

- **ExToken** introduces a structured exploration framework that leverages discrete behavioral priors from offline demonstrations as ==exploration tokens== by performing ==K-means clustering== on ==trajectory embeddings==.
- During online RL, the VLA policy's input is conditioned on a uniformly sampled exploration token, explicitly guiding the policy toward diverse behavioral modes to enhance rollout diversity.
- A state-conditioned ==Token Selector==, optimized via ==REINFORCE==, is integrated to adaptively predict the most relevant token for a given initial state during inference while supporting diverse sampling during training.

## Results

- ExToken achieved an average success rate of **98.2%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming RLinf-GRPO (**96.8%**) and demonstrating faster convergence across task suites under constrained interaction budgets.
- In real-world robotic manipulation, ExToken improved average task performance by **6.25%** compared to the Evo-RL baseline across four complex tasks with limited interaction data.
- The framework exhibited stronger robustness to environmental variations, with performance degradation typically limited to **5%-15%**, and demonstrated robustness to different numbers of token clusters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Trajectory diversity is more critical for VLA-RL sample efficiency and algorithmic performance than the sheer quantity of raw interaction data collected.
> - Standard stochastic exploration often causes 'action mode collapse' in VLA-RL, leading policies to repeatedly exploit similar action patterns and plateau at suboptimal performance.
> - Discrete behavioral priors extracted from offline demonstrations can effectively provide structured guidance for exploration, alleviating the observed exploration stagnation in VLA-RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a class of AI systems capable of interpreting visual observations and language instructions to generate actions, typically for robotic manipulation. These models have shown capabilities in performing complex tasks. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a method for refining pre-trained VLA models through environmental interaction and feedback. This "reinforcement fine-tuning" (VLA-RL) allows policies to improve beyond initial imitation learning by experiencing diverse scenarios and learning from trial and error.

However, a significant challenge in scaling VLA-RL to practical applications is sample inefficiency. Training large VLA architectures with extensive environmental interactions, whether in simulation or real-world robotics, incurs substantial computational costs, time, and potential safety risks. Prior research in VLA-RL has largely focused on improving sample efficiency implicitly through strategies such as offline-to-online learning paradigms, refining reward formulations, enhancing value functions, or leveraging model-based approaches and latent planning. These methods primarily concentrate on optimizing policy updates or maximizing the utility of *already collected* data.

This paper positions itself by identifying a gap in the existing literature: the fundamental role of *active exploration* in VLA-RL sample efficiency has been less explored. Conventional stochastic exploration, often relied upon, is observed to lead to "action mode collapse," where the policy generates redundant trajectories, hindering further learning. ExToken directly addresses this limitation by proposing a structured exploration framework, shifting the focus from merely collecting more data to actively generating more *diverse* trajectories. This work complements existing VLA-RL efforts by providing a mechanism to improve the quality and diversity of the interaction data itself, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of subsequent policy optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to:
1. Investigate the exploration dynamics in current VLA-RL frameworks and identify bottlenecks to sample efficiency.
2. Empirically determine the relative importance of trajectory diversity versus the sheer quantity of collected rollouts for algorithmic performance and sample efficiency.
3. Propose and develop a novel, general framework for structured exploration in VLA-RL fine-tuning that actively promotes diverse behavioral modes.
4. Demonstrate the effectiveness of this framework in improving sample efficiency, accelerating convergence, and enhancing task performance and robustness across simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, particularly under constrained interaction budgets.

The motivation for this work stemmed from several observations and insights:
- **Prohibitive Sample Inefficiency in VLA-RL:** Despite the potential of RL for VLA models, the high cost of environmental interactions remains a severe practical limitation.
- **Exploration Stagnation and Action Mode Collapse:** The authors observed that standard stochastic exploration in VLA-RL tends to lead to "action mode collapse." As training progresses, policies generate increasingly homogeneous trajectories, repeatedly exploiting similar action patterns and causing task performance to plateau prematurely at suboptimal levels (as illustrated in Figure 1, Left). This indicated that unstructured exploration was insufficient for the high-dimensional action spaces of VLA models.
- **The Criticality of Trajectory Diversity:** To understand the implications of action mode collapse, an empirical study was conducted. It compared training with a large number of standard rollouts (1024), a reduced number of standard rollouts (512), and a reduced number of *diversified* rollouts (512, selected from 1024 collected by discarding the most homogeneous half). The results showed that training with 512 diversified rollouts achieved comparable performance to 1024 standard rollouts, while significantly outperforming 512 standard rollouts (Figure 1, Right). This compelling insight revealed that the *diversity* of trajectories is more critical for sample efficiency and algorithmic performance than the *quantity* of raw interactions.
- **Need for Active Diversity Enhancement:** This finding motivated the central question: how can exploration diversity be actively enhanced during VLA-RL fine-tuning, beyond passive stochastic sampling or brute-force oversampling? The research aimed to develop a mechanism to explicitly guide VLA policies to explore structured and diverse behavioral modes, thereby addressing the exploration stagnation bottleneck and improving learning efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ExToken is introduced as a unified framework for structured exploration in VLA-RL fine-tuning. It consists of two main components: a token conditioning mechanism for guiding exploration and a state-conditioned Token Selector for adaptive token prediction and deterministic inference.

#### 3.1. Promoting Structured Diversity via Exploration Token

The framework leverages discrete behavioral priors derived from offline demonstrations to formulate "exploration tokens."
- **Token Formulation:** Given a dataset of offline demonstrations ($\mathcal{D}$), a pre-trained video embedding model ($\mathcal{E}$) is used to extract latent spatio-temporal features for each trajectory. K-means clustering is then performed on these embeddings. Each cluster centroid $c_k$ is defined as a discrete exploration token $k$, representing a distinct behavioral mode present in the demonstration data. The total number of tokens is denoted by $K$.
- **Token-Conditioned Warm-Up:** To enable the VLA policy to utilize these tokens, a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) warm-up phase is conducted. Each demonstration trajectory is associated with the token corresponding to its assigned cluster. This token is appended to the VLA policy's input sequence within the token embedding layer. This phase trains the policy to associate different tokens with their respective behavioral modes, allowing it to generate token-conditioned action patterns.
- **Structured Exploration during RL:** During the online RL interaction phase, exploration diversity is promoted by conditioning each rollout on a uniformly sampled exploration token. For an initial state $s_0$, a token $k \sim \mathcal{U}(1, K)$ is sampled and prepended to the policy input. This explicit guidance directs the policy toward diverse behavioral modes, enhancing rollout diversity and state-action coverage during training, which aims to alleviate trajectory homogenization and facilitate accelerated convergence.

#### 3.2. Reinforce Token Selection through ExToken

To reconcile the randomized token conditioning used for exploration during training with the need for deterministic behavior during deployment, ExToken integrates a state-conditioned Token Selector.
- **State-Conditioned Token Selection:** Given an initial observation $s_0$ (image $o_0$ and language instruction $l$), the Token Selector ($\varphi$) predicts a categorical distribution over the token space. It uses a pre-trained SigLIP encoder followed by an MLP head to output logits for each token. Initially, the Token Selector is warmed up on the clustered offline dataset, using the assigned cluster labels as supervision, to establish an initial state-to-token mapping. During RL training, tokens are sampled from this temperature-scaled distribution to maintain exploration diversity while progressively biasing selection towards contextually relevant behavioral modes. For inference at deployment, the selector deterministically outputs the most likely token via `argmax`.
- **REINFORCE Bi-level Optimization:** The Token Selector ($\varphi$) and the VLA policy ($\pi_\theta$) are jointly optimized during RL training. While the VLA policy is updated using standard RL objectives (specifically, Group Relative Policy Optimization - GRPO in this work), the Token Selector is optimized at the episode level using the REINFORCE algorithm. The objective for the selector is to maximize the expected return, where the reward for a chosen token is the trajectory reward $R(\tau)$ obtained when that token was used to condition the policy. A baseline $b$ is used for variance reduction. This bi-level optimization allows the selector to learn adaptively which behavioral modes are effective for different initial states as the policy itself learns.

ExToken is designed as a lightweight and compatible framework, allowing its integration into various VLA architectures and RL paradigms by simply conditioning the input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted across simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks to evaluate ExToken's performance.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiment (LIBERO Benchmark)

- **Performance and Convergence:** ExToken demonstrated superior performance compared to both supervised (SFT) and RL-based VLA baselines on the LIBERO benchmark, achieving an average success rate of 98.2%, outperforming RLinf-GRPO (96.8%) under identical warm-up protocols and constrained interaction budgets. The improvement was particularly pronounced on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite, where ExToken increased the success rate from 95.2% to 97.8%.
- **Sample Efficiency:** The learning curves showed that ExToken consistently converged faster than RLinf-GRPO across all four LIBERO task suites, indicating that structured, token-conditioned exploration helps alleviate learning stagnation and improves sample efficiency.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiment (Robotic Manipulation Tasks)

- **Task Performance:** ExToken outperformed the Evo-RL baseline on four complex real-world manipulation tasks (Fold clothes, Wipe table with towel, Pour water, Insert pen into pen holder), achieving an average improvement of 6.25% under constrained interaction budgets (20 rollouts per task per iteration). This suggests that ExToken's directional guidance allows policies to reach higher performance ceilings with limited interaction.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** ExToken exhibited stronger robustness to environmental variations (unseen objects, background variations, lighting conditions) compared to $\pi_{0.5}$ and Evo-RL. Its performance degradation was typically limited to 5%–15%, whereas baselines often experienced substantially larger drops. This resilience was attributed to the structured diversity induced by token-conditioned exploration, which encourages the policy to experience more varied interaction patterns during training, leading to better generalization.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

- **Distinct Behavioral Modes of ExToken Clusters:** Analysis of the token clusters from the Fold clothes task revealed that they captured semantically distinct behavioral modes (e.g., corrective straightening for misaligned garments, post-folding smoothing). This indicates that the learned tokens encode meaningful structural information from demonstrations, which can be leveraged for diverse exploration.
- **Promoting Diverse Exploration Trajectories:** Visualization of 3D end-effector (EE) positions showed that conditioning the policy on different sampled tokens generated distinct trajectory patterns, forming clearly separated spatial regions. Quantitatively, token-conditioned exploration covered a substantially larger spatial region (measured by convex hulls) compared to standard stochastic exploration, confirming that ExToken encourages a broader range of behaviors and alleviates exploration stagnation.
- **Alleviating Extreme Interaction Constraints:** Under reduced rollout budgets (from 512 to 128 trajectories per optimization step), ExToken demonstrated greater robustness and graceful degradation than RLinf-GRPO. For instance, ExToken with 256 rollouts (93.4% success rate) rivaled the RLinf baseline using double the data (512 rollouts). While performance became less stable at the extreme limit of 128 rollouts due to increased gradient variance, ExToken still outperformed the baseline in highly constrained settings.
- **Robustness to Token Granularity:** ExToken showed relative stability across different numbers of token clusters ($K \in \{3, 6, 10\}$). Performance remained highly comparable between $K=3$ and $K=6$, with only a slight drop at $K=10$. This suggests that ExToken is generally robust to the choice of token granularity, reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning of $K$.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of VLA-RL by fundamentally re-evaluating the role of exploration. The key significance and potential impact are:

- **Addressing a Core Bottleneck:** ExToken directly tackles the critical problem of sample inefficiency in VLA-RL, which is a major barrier to the widespread deployment of VLA models in robotics. By demonstrating that trajectory diversity is more important than sheer data quantity, the work shifts focus towards generating *qualitative* exploration data.
- **Enhanced Performance and Convergence:** The framework consistently improves task performance and accelerates policy convergence across diverse simulated and real-world tasks. This means VLA policies can achieve higher success rates and learn more efficiently with fewer environmental interactions, making RL fine-tuning more feasible.
- **Increased Robustness and Generalization:** The structured exploration induced by ExToken leads to policies that are more robust to environmental variations and generalize better to unseen scenarios. This is crucial for real-world robotic systems, where variability is inherent.
- **Lightweight and General Framework:** ExToken's design as an input conditioning mechanism makes it broadly compatible with different VLA architectures and existing RL paradigms (online or iterative offline RL), facilitating its integration and adoption by the wider research community.
- **Foundation for Future Exploration Strategies:** The work lays a foundation for more sophisticated exploration strategies in VLA-RL. The identified areas for future refinement—such as joint optimization of token representations, probabilistic token dropout, more structured latent models for token derivation, and temporally-conditioned tokens for long-horizon tasks—represent promising directions for further advancements in sample-efficient robot learning.

In summary, ExToken offers a practical and effective method for improving VLA-RL, making the process of fine-tuning large robotic policies more efficient, robust, and scalable for complex manipulation tasks.
