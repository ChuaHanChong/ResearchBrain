# House of Dextra: Cross-embodied Co-design for Dexterous Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> The "House of Dextra" framework introduces a co-design methodology for dexterous robot hands, simultaneously optimizing morphology and control policies. It achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex manipulation tasks, enabling the design, fabrication, and deployment of a new robot hand within 24 hours while identifying task-specific optimal geometries.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic design separates mechanical hardware from control policies, often leading to fundamental limitations in a robot's overall dexterity.
- Co-design for dexterous manipulation is challenging due to the immense search spaces for both complex hardware designs and fine motor control strategies.
- Many existing co-design approaches for manipulation face difficulties with scalability, sparse rewards, and a significant sim-to-real gap, hindering real-world application.

## Method

- Researchers developed **House of Dextra**, a ==cross-embodied co-design== framework that jointly optimizes hand morphology and control using a ==bi-level optimization== approach.
- The framework incorporates a ==procedural, constraint-based hand generation== system to construct diverse, physically valid, and manufacturable morphologies.
- It utilizes a ==morphology-conditioned cross-embodiment control policy== and a ==graph-based design encoder== to enable scalable evaluation and generalization of control across varied hand designs.

## Results

- The framework achieved an in-hand object rotation velocity of **3.3 rad/s** with fine-tuning (**1.85 rad/s** without), significantly surpassing baseline methods (e.g., **0.26 rad/s** for RoboGrammar).
- It demonstrated robust **zero-shot sim-to-real transfer**, with the optimal 3-fingered co-design successfully manipulating **15 out of 17** unseen real-world objects using a blind policy.
- The entire end-to-end pipeline, from design generation to real-world deployment of a new robot hand, was completed within **24 hours**, including 3D printing and algorithmic runtime.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, when integrated with cross-embodiment policies, provides an effective method for handling the complex contact dynamics and sparse rewards inherent in dexterous manipulation co-design tasks.
> - Task-specific optimal hand morphologies, which may not be anthropomorphic, demonstrate superior performance compared to general-purpose designs for specialized manipulation tasks.
> - Morphological parameters such as finger body length scale and palm width scale have a more pronounced impact on dexterous manipulation performance than material properties or basic actuator characteristics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic manipulation capabilities traditionally involves a decoupled approach, where hardware mechanical design is established prior to the development of control strategies. This separation can limit the achievable dexterity, as the morphology of a robot hand, including its degrees of freedom and sensing capabilities, can fundamentally constrain the performance of even optimized control policies. The research community has recognized this limitation, leading to the emergence of co-design, an approach that aims to overcome these constraints by simultaneously optimizing both the mechanical design (morphology) and the control policy.

However, co-design for dexterous manipulation presents significant challenges. The combined search space for complex hardware designs and fine motor control policies is extensive, leading to what the authors refer to as a "two-fold curse of dimensionality." Prior co-design efforts have often faced limitations in scaling, either by restricting the complexity of the hardware search space or by employing simpler control methods, such as sampling-based controllers, which can struggle with sparse rewards and long-horizon tasks common in dexterous manipulation. Furthermore, a substantial portion of co-design research has been confined to simulation environments, with challenges in transferring these designs and control policies to the real world due to simplifications in hardware manufacturing constraints, contact dynamics, and physical property modeling.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing these scalability and sim-to-real transfer issues for dexterous manipulation. It proposes a framework designed to efficiently iterate on complex hardware designs and quickly develop sophisticated control, leveraging morphology-conditioned cross-embodied policies and physically realistic hardware grammars. By doing so, "House of Dextra" aims to expand the design complexity and control fidelity in co-design, facilitating practical real-world deployment of optimized robot hands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a co-design framework that jointly optimizes robot hand morphology and complementary dexterous control policies to maximize task-specific performance. The underlying motivation stems from the observation that current limitations in dexterous manipulation arise from both control strategies and hardware design, without a clear understanding of what constitutes an optimal manipulator for various dexterous tasks. The traditional separation of design and control is identified as a bottleneck, as hardware choices can inherently limit the effectiveness of control policies.

The specific key objectives and motivations articulated by the authors include:

- **Addressing the Co-design Challenge:** The fundamental motivation is to tackle the "how to design and control robot manipulators that are optimized for dexterity" question. This involves moving beyond decoupled design and control paradigms.
- **Expansive Morphology Search Space:** A key objective is to enable exploration of a broad range of hand designs, encompassing joint configurations, finger structures, and palm geometries. This seeks to move beyond limited design parameters often seen in prior co-design work.
- **Scalable Evaluation:** To counter the high dimensionality of the joint design and control space, the framework aims to provide a method for efficient and scalable evaluation of numerous candidate designs. This is proposed through morphology-conditioned cross-embodied control policies that can generalize across different hand embodiments.
- **Real-world Fabricability and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A significant objective is to ensure that the co-designed hands are manufacturable using accessible components and can transfer effectively from simulation to physical deployment. The ambition is to achieve an end-to-end pipeline capable of designing, training, fabricating, and deploying a new robotic hand within a 24-hour timeframe. This directly addresses the sim-to-real gap that has constrained much of the prior co-design research.
- **Modular Hardware Platform:** The research aims to create a flexible robot hand platform with modular mechanical and electrical structures. This modularity is intended to facilitate the exploration of variable kinematics, degrees of freedom, and diverse morphologies, enabling practical deployment of multiple distinct hand embodiments.
- **Accurate Robot Hand Generation:** An objective is to develop modular and physically realistic grammars and rule structures for hand generation. These grammars are designed to ensure that generated robot hands can be successfully built and deployed in the real world, thus bridging the gap between simulated designs and physical prototypes.
- **Design Analysis and Impact Identification:** The framework seeks to expand co-design analysis beyond basic parameters to identify more impactful design parameters for future research in dexterity. This involves systematically evaluating the influence of various morphological and control characteristics on task performance.

In essence, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of traditional robotic hand development by integrating design and control into a unified, efficient, and physically grounded framework that can generate and deploy dexterous manipulators for complex tasks in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "House of Dextra" framework is structured around a cross-embodied co-design approach that jointly optimizes robot hand morphology and control policies. This is formulated as a bi-level optimization problem where an inner loop optimizes control for a fixed morphology, and an outer loop searches for the morphology that yields the best achievable task performance.

**Morphology Representation and Encoding:** Each robot hand is represented as a fixed-topology attributed graph. This graph consists of a palm node and up to five finger-slot nodes, connected in a star configuration. Each finger node possesses attributes such as servo count, grammar codes, fingertip type, and finger index. To process this graph structure, a Graph Neural Network (GNN) `f_ϕ` is employed. This GNN performs message passing to produce a fixed-dimensional embedding `y(G)`, which is subsequently utilized by a design value network to evaluate and rank candidate morphologies during the search process.

**Morphology-Conditioned Control Policy:** Dexterous manipulation for a given morphology `G` is modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The core of the control approach is a policy `π` that is conditioned on the hand's morphology. This conditioning is achieved by providing a one-hot morphology vector `m(G)` as part of the state input to the policy. An action mask `M(G)` is applied to the policy's raw output, ensuring that only valid actions corresponding to the available actuators in a specific morphology are executed. This mechanism allows a single, shared policy to operate across diverse embodiments without requiring separate training for each design. The policy objective is to maximize a task-specific reward `R(s, a)` over trajectories, which considers object state, joint effort, and object proximity.

**Co-Design Process:** The framework utilizes a two-stage approach. First, a cross-embodiment base policy `˜π_θ` is pre-trained across a diverse set of sampled hand designs `G`. This pre-training enhances the efficiency of subsequent design evaluation. The co-design process then iteratively generates candidate morphologies, evaluates them using the pre-trained cross-embodiment policy, and selects designs for manufacturing.

**Key Components:** The methodology integrates four main components:
1. **Generation `G ∼ p_grammar(r, c)`:** A procedural constraint-based hand generator creates diverse and physically valid morphologies. This generator samples palm layouts (symmetric, asymmetric, anthropomorphic) and independently samples each finger's internal morphology, including actuated joint count (2-3 servos), segment scale grammar codes (`c1, c2`), and fingertip geometry (standard, wedged, rounded, thin). The generation system incorporates real hardware constraints, such as pre-computed collision geometries for modular components and realistic joint limits, ensuring manufacturability and sim-to-real transfer.
2. **Design Encoder `y = f_ϕ(G)`:** A GNN encodes the generated morphology graph `G` into a fixed-dimensional embedding `y(G)`, which captures the structural properties of the hand.
3. **Design Value Network `V_design(y(G))`:** This network learns to predict the expected task performance of a morphology based on its GNN embedding. It is trained on observed design-performance pairs, where performance is evaluated by the cross-embodiment policy.
4. **Cross-Embodiment Policy `π_θ(a | s, m(G))`:** A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent is used for policy learning, enhanced with morphology-conditioning via the one-hot vector `m(G)`. Policies are pre-trained by grouping generated hands into kinematic families (e.g., symmetric radial, anthropomorphic, variable finger counts) to enable generalization within these groups.

**Graph Heuristic Search Algorithm:** Following cross-embodiment pre-training, Graph Heuristic Search is employed for morphology optimization. In each iteration, `K` candidate designs are generated through sequential grammar expansion. For each partial design, the design value network, augmented with Gumbel noise for exploration, scores potential next rule applications. The highest-scoring expansion is chosen until a complete design is formed. These candidates are then evaluated in parallel simulation using the pre-trained cross-embodiment policy. A lookup table `T` stores the best observed scores for designs, which provides supervision for training the design value network. An annealing epsilon schedule shifts the search from exploration to exploitation, and path-level tabu tracking maintains diversity.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The highest-ranked design identified through the search process undergoes fine-tuning with domain randomization. This involves varying actuator characteristics, contact and friction parameters, and object poses in simulation to improve robustness. For real-world deployment, the controller is trained as a "blind policy," meaning it removes object-state inputs and relies solely on proprioception (joint encoder information and morphology encoding) to reduce sim-to-real mismatch. The selected design graph is directly translated into modular hardware specifications for 3D printing and assembly using Dynamixel actuators. After PID tuning on the hardware, the blind policy is deployed on the physical system without requiring additional fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the efficacy of the cross-embodied co-design framework, particularly in enabling efficient design, training, fabrication, and deployment of dexterous robot hands.

**Overall Framework Performance:**
The framework demonstrated the ability to co-design and deploy a new robotic hand in under 24 hours (12 hours for 3D printing, 6.48 hours for algorithm execution, 0.8 hours for assembly, and 2.0 hours for sim-to-real setup). The cross-embodiment learning, combined with modular hardware grammars, allowed for efficient and generalizable co-design without the typical loss of runtime or dexterity associated with traditional co-design methods.

**Comparative Performance in In-Hand Rotation:**
For the continuous in-hand rotation task, the proposed method significantly outperformed established baselines. The co-designed hands, with fine-tuning, achieved a continuous angular velocity of 3.3 rad/s in simulation. Without fine-tuning, the best designs achieved 1.85 rad/s and maintained stable rotation for the entire three-minute evaluation period, avoiding falls. In contrast, RoboGrammar achieved 0.26 rad/s with a time to fall of 4.63 seconds, while Monte Carlo methods performed worse with a fall time of 2.71 seconds. MPPI control also exhibited limited performance (0.62 rad/s) and often resorted to "jitter-heavy, throwing-like motions" that failed to maintain stable, long-horizon manipulation. This indicates that reinforcement learning, as implemented in this framework, is better suited for the sparse rewards, long horizons, and complex finger gaits required for dexterous manipulation co-design.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer and Real-World Deployment:**
The framework demonstrated zero-shot blind deployment from simulation to reality using proprioception-only policies. Three co-designed embodiments and one anthropomorphic baseline were manufactured and tested across 17 unseen objects with varying properties (friction, compliance, shape, mass).
- **Three-fingered optimal design:** This hand exhibited superior generalization, successfully rotating 15 out of 17 objects for durations up to 18 seconds. Its "three altering diagonals" gait enabled effective manipulation of irregular objects like pinecones and pentagons, and soft objects. For instance, it rotated a tennis ball for over 10 minutes without failure, a feat not matched by other designs.
- **Other designs:** The anthropomorphic and four-fingered co-designed hands, predicted to be lower performing in simulation (0.62 rad/s and 0.78 rad/s respectively, compared to 1.85 rad/s for the 3-finger hand), were only able to blindly rotate a small subset of the objects (3 out of 17 tested). This validated the algorithm's ability to accurately predict real-world task success rankings from simulation.

**Simulation Task-Specific Design Results:**
- **Grasping:** The optimal designs for grasping frequently featured thinner fingertips (54% of best designs), full degrees of freedom (4 servo motors per finger), and five-finger layouts (symmetric and anthropomorphic). These hands achieved stable grasps in under half a second.
- **In-hand Rotation:** Beyond the overall performance metrics, the best rotation designs predominantly utilized three fingers, confirming its efficacy. Standard fingertips were found to be most effective (64% of designs), as thinner fingertips led to unstable rotation.
- **Object Flipping:** This task, requiring rotation around the z-axis with a fixed wrist, favored designs with a combination of wedged or thin fingertips on one side (for lifting) and standard fingertips on the other (for controlled reset). Four and five-finger symmetric hands with full degrees of freedom consistently performed well, completing flips in under 6 seconds.

**Parameter Analysis (Why Morphology?):**
A systematic parameter analysis using Bayesian sampling on a LEAP hand revealed that morphological parameters had the most significant impact on manipulation performance. Specifically, finger body length scale showed a strong positive correlation (r=0.748) with rotation improvement, while palm width scale exhibited a strong negative correlation (r=-0.729). Actuator damping (r=-0.476) and dynamic friction (r=-0.459) also showed moderate negative correlations. This analysis supports the argument for co-design by demonstrating that morphology and control parameters have a more pronounced impact on task success than material or contact properties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "House of Dextra" framework presents several contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotics and dexterous manipulation:

**Scalability and Efficiency in Co-Design:** The introduction of morphology-conditioned cross-embodied control significantly enhances the scalability of co-design for complex manipulation tasks. By allowing a single policy to generalize across diverse hand morphologies, the framework addresses the "curse of dimensionality" inherent in joint hardware-control optimization. This translates into substantial computational savings, reducing the evaluation time for thousands of designs from potentially hundreds of days (with individual policy training) to a matter of hours, making co-design optimization more tractable for real-world deployment.

**Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The use of physically realistic grammars for hardware generation, coupled with domain randomization and a "blind policy" approach for deployment, enables effective zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real world. This capability to directly manufacture and deploy optimized designs without extensive real-world fine-tuning is a notable advancement, addressing a persistent challenge in robotics research. The modular hardware platform further facilitates this by ensuring consistency between simulated models and physical components.

**Challenging Anthropomorphic Design Paradigms:** The finding that task-specific optimized morphologies, such as the three-fingered radial design, can significantly outperform anthropomorphic baseline hands for specific dexterous tasks (e.g., in-hand rotation) suggests that human-inspired designs may not always be optimal for robotic manipulation. This encourages further exploration of non-anthropomorphic designs tailored for specific functionalities, potentially leading to novel and more capable robotic manipulators.

**Guiding Future Research and Design:** The parameter analysis, which identified morphology (finger length, palm width) and control characteristics (damping, dynamic friction) as having the most substantial impact on task performance, provides valuable insights for future research. This suggests that efforts in robot design should prioritize these parameters over material or contact properties, informing the focus of both academic and industrial development.

**Increased Accessibility for Research:** By open-sourcing the full framework, including generated robot hands and build guides, the authors aim to lower the barrier to entry for co-design research. The use of accessible, 3D-printable components and readily available Dynamixel motors allows more research groups to replicate, extend, and build upon this work, fostering broader participation and innovation in the field.

**Environmental and Computational Efficiency:** The significant reduction in computational resources required for co-design, converting days of training to hours, also translates into reduced energy consumption and CO2 emissions. This demonstrates a path towards more environmentally conscious robotic research and development practices.

In summary, this work advances the state of the art in robot co-design by providing a robust, efficient, and practical framework for developing dexterous hands. Its impact lies in accelerating the discovery of novel robot morphologies, enabling direct real-world deployment, and shifting the design paradigm towards task-optimized solutions, ultimately contributing to more capable and versatile robotic systems.
