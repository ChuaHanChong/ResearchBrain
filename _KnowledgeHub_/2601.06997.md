# ObjSplat: Geometry-Aware Gaussian Surfels for Active Object Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> ObjSplat is an active object reconstruction system that uses 2D Gaussian surfels as a single representation for both mapping and view planning. A geometry-aware uncertainty map combining surfel confidence, back-face detection and occlusion-aware covisibility guides a next-best-path planner that does multi-step lookahead on a k-NN viewpoint graph. It reaches 32.35 dB novel-view PSNR and 91.4% coverage with 3.96 m of motion, about 4x shorter than greedy NBV.

## Problem

- Radiance-field reconstruction relies on dense passive capture, SfM initialization and heavy offline optimization, requiring manual intervention that blocks scalable autonomous digitization of unknown objects.
- Existing uncertainty cues based on opacity, depth residuals or frustum covisibility ignore self-occlusion and back-face visibility, so they misjudge completeness on hollow, thin, non-closed surfaces.
- Greedy single-step NBV planners ignore movement cost, giving long erratic trajectories, while one-shot and RL planners lack adaptivity, need training, or suffer diffusion-prior latency.

## Method

- **ObjSplat** ==Gaussian surfel== reconstruction: normal-guided geometry-texture joint optimization (photometric, depth, normal, consistency, opacity, mask losses) with a four-criterion update mask, low opacity, photometric, geometric, back-facing, seeding new surfels.
- ==Geometry-aware uncertainty map==: weighted sum of surfel-wise confidence (distance, front-facing, angular-diversity weights), back-face score, and opacity-based visibility completeness, rendered in about 2 ms via ==CUDA rasterization==.
- ==Next-best-path planner==: ==prize-collecting TSP== variant over a ==k-NN viewpoint graph==, scoring top-M shortest paths by gain minus distance, with dynamic uncertainty weighting and an auto-stop threshold.

## Results

- On **16** GSO objects at **30** views, novel-view PSNR **32.35 dB**, Chamfer **0.611 mm**, **91.42%** coverage, beating GauSS-MI (**30.83 dB**, **2.191 mm**, **72.66%**) and FisherRF.
- Path length drops to **3.96 m** versus **18.02 m** for the greedy variant (**4x**) and **24.78 m** for GauSS-MI; online time **121.96 s**, **2.3x** faster.
- Ablations: dropping occlusion-awareness, back-face, or dynamic weighting cuts PSNR to **32.22**/**31.97**/**30.54 dB**; swapping baselines onto the surfel backend still leaves ObjSplat ahead by **0.81 dB**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sharing one representation between mapper and planner is the lever: rendering-based uncertainty costs milliseconds, so foresighted planning becomes affordable inside a closed perception loop.
> - Greedy information maximization is not free, it buys early gains with erratic motion; a conservative path-level planner catches up and ends smoother and more accurate.
> - Object-centric sensing needs explicit back-face and occlusion reasoning; generic opacity or density cues are biased by empty background and stop scanning prematurely.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the challenge of high-quality digitization of physical objects, a capability that finds application in several fields, including extended reality (XR) experiences, digital cultural heritage preservation, and robotics (particularly for bridging the simulation-to-reality gap).

Traditional high-quality reconstruction methods often rely on dense, passive data acquisition and computationally intensive offline processing, such as Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS), or explicit mesh fusion techniques. More recently, implicit radiance field representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have demonstrated high-fidelity novel view synthesis but typically require extensive manual intervention for data collection and optimization.

To overcome these limitations, Active Object Reconstruction (AOR) has emerged. AOR aims to automate the data acquisition process using robotic systems by intelligently identifying under-reconstructed regions and planning efficient viewpoints. Existing AOR strategies generally fall into two categories:
- **Iterative Next-Best-View (NBV) Planning:** These methods sequentially select a single next viewpoint based on maximizing information gain metrics (e.g., frontier information, color/density, visibility fields). However, they often suffer from short-sighted, greedy optimization, neglecting robot kinematic constraints and movement costs, leading to inefficient and lengthy scanning trajectories.
- **Global One-Shot Planning:** These approaches aim to determine an entire scanning trajectory in a single step, often by formulating the problem as a set covering optimization or Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) variant. Recent advancements leverage 3D diffusion priors for geometry inference. While addressing path efficiency, these methods can struggle with unknown object scales, severe occlusions, diffusion model inference latency, and lack the adaptability for progressive refinement of complex, unseen objects.

ObjSplat positions itself within this landscape by seeking to combine the adaptability of progressive, iterative reconstruction with the foresight of global planning. It aims to bridge the gap between the short-sighted nature of traditional NBV and the static, non-adaptive character of one-shot methods, particularly for object-level reconstruction tasks using a unified Gaussian surfel representation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ObjSplat is to develop an autonomous, high-fidelity object reconstruction framework that produces physically consistent digital models with both accurate geometry and photorealistic appearance. This capability is motivated by several applications:
1. **Digital Asset Creation:** Generating fine-grained digital models for immersive XR experiences and long-term preservation of cultural heritage.
2. **Robotics and Simulation:** Bridging the simulation-to-reality (sim-to-real) gap by providing accurate 3D models for developing and validating robotic perception, planning, and control algorithms in physics-based simulated environments.

The authors identify several limitations in existing active reconstruction systems that ObjSplat seeks to address:
- **Inadequate Reconstruction Completeness and Quality Assessment:** Conventional methods often rely on simplistic cues (e.g., opacity or depth residuals) to identify under-reconstructed regions. These methods frequently fail to reliably detect unobserved back-face regions, accurately quantify true multi-view covisibility, or handle geometrically complex objects with hollow, self-occluded, or thin structures. This leads to incomplete models or unreliable guidance for exploration.
- **Inefficient Planning Strategies:** Traditional greedy Next-Best-View (NBV) planners optimize for only the immediate next best view. This single-step optimization typically ignores the robot's kinematic constraints and cumulative movement costs, resulting in long, suboptimal, and redundant scanning trajectories, which increases overall scanning time.
- **Lack of Unified Representation and Optimization:** Many pipelines disconnect the data acquisition, model representation, and view planning stages. There is a need for a unified framework that tightly couples high-quality reconstruction with efficient exploration, allowing for incremental model updates and joint geometry-texture optimization.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Existing radiance field methods (like NeRF) are often computationally intensive for real-time applications, and recent one-shot methods relying on diffusion models introduce significant inference latency.

To address these motivations, ObjSplat sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- Develop a unified active reconstruction system leveraging 2D Gaussian surfels for both model optimization and exploration guidance.
- Introduce a geometry-aware viewpoint evaluation pipeline that explicitly models back-face visibility and occlusion-aware multi-view covisibility to reliably identify under-reconstructed regions on complex objects.
- Implement a Next-Best-Path (NBP) planning strategy that performs multi-step lookahead on a dynamically constructed spatial graph, jointly optimizing information gain and movement cost to generate globally efficient trajectories.
- Ensure the framework produces physically consistent, watertight models suitable for direct use in physics simulations within a practical timeframe.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ObjSplat is presented as a closed-loop framework comprising three main components: Gaussian Surfel-based Reconstruction, Geometry-Aware Viewpoint Evaluation, and Next-Best-Path Planning. The system processes RGB-D inputs, progressively updates an object representation, assesses reconstruction quality, and plans subsequent viewpoints.

#### 3.1. Gaussian Surfel-based Reconstruction

1. **Gaussian Surfels for Object Representation:** The system represents the target object as an unstructured set of 2D Gaussian surfels (GSurfels). Each surfel is parameterized by its 3D center, a covariance matrix (decomposed into a rotation and a 2D scaling matrix, with zero z-scale to enforce planar structure), an opacity value, and view-dependent color information encoded using spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients. The planar constraint allows direct derivation of surface normals from the surfel's orientation. Differentiable splatting is used to render color, depth, and normals from the GSurfel model.

2. **Geometry–Texture Joint Optimization:** To achieve accurate and consistent reconstruction, a comprehensive loss function is employed, combining:
 - **Photometric Loss (L_p):** An L1 term and a differentiable SSIM term to supervise color appearance against the observed RGB image.
 - **Depth Loss (L_d):** An L1 term against the observed depth map.
 - **Normal Supervision (L_n + L_c):** An L1 term against observed surface normals and a geometric consistency term to align rendered normals with those derived from the rendered depth map.
 - **Opacity Regularization (L_o):** A term that encourages surfel opacities to be either fully opaque or fully transparent, promoting sharp surface boundaries.
 - **Mask Loss (L_m):** A binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss to supervise the object mask, extracted using a pre-trained visual foundation model.
 These terms are weighted to balance their contributions during optimization.

3. **Progressive Update of Gaussian Surfels:** New observations are integrated efficiently by identifying and refining insufficiently reconstructed or uncovered regions. New GSurfels are selectively initialized in areas exhibiting:
 - **Low Opacity:** Accumulated rendered opacity below a threshold.
 - **Photometric Discrepancy:** Rendered color deviating significantly from observed color.
 - **Geometric Deviation:** Rendered depth significantly behind observed depth.
 - **Back-facing Surfaces:** Rendered normal forming an acute angle with the camera's optical axis, indicating observation of the "back" of an existing surface while valid geometry is detected.
 For these identified pixels, new GSurfels are initialized by back-projecting the observed depth, assigning color from the RGB image, aligning orientation with the sensor-derived normal, and adaptively scaling based on local point density.

#### 3.2. Geometry-Aware Viewpoint Evaluation

1. **Occlusion-Aware Co-visibility Analysis:** This module addresses limitations of traditional depth or opacity-based covisibility metrics, which can overestimate shared visibility due to self-occlusions or non-closed surfaces in object-level scenarios. It samples points from a source view, back-projects them to 3D, and reprojects them into a target view. A correspondence is deemed invalid if the reprojected point is occluded by the rendered surface or falls on a back-facing region, as determined by comparing reprojected depth with rendered depth and checking rendered normal orientation. This yields a robust covisibility ratio.

2. **Global–Local View Selection:** During online reconstruction, a hybrid strategy balances local adaptability and global consistency. A local window of top-k covisible views is used for incremental optimization of newly observed regions. A sparse set of global views, sampled from earlier keyframes with a reweighting scheme, maintains long-range consistency and prevents overfitting.

3. **Geometry-Aware Uncertainty Qualification:** This core component generates a dense, view-dependent uncertainty map for candidate viewpoints, guiding path planning. It combines three elements:
 - **Surfel-wise Confidence Modeling:** Each GSurfel is assigned a confidence κ, aggregated from its valid historical observations. Weights prioritize near-orthogonal and non-distant observations, and an angular diversity factor encourages multi-view coverage.
 - **Back-Face Observation:** A score B(u) is calculated for pixels where the rendered normal indicates a back-facing surface, signaling a need for exploration from an opposing direction.
 - **Visibility-based Completeness:** A score V(u) derived from 1 minus the rendered opacity indicates areas with insufficient surfel density or unobserved space.
 The overall uncertainty map Û(u) is a dynamic weighted sum of these three components, allowing for real-time evaluation due to CUDA-accelerated rasterization.

#### 3.3. Next-Best-Path Planning

1. **Candidate Viewpoint Generation:** Candidate viewpoints are uniformly generated on a hemispherical shell surrounding the object's current bounding box using a Vogel spiral pattern. Previously visited viewpoints are pruned, and candidates are oriented towards the object center.

2. **Next-Best-Path Strategy:** This strategy moves beyond greedy NBV by formulating the planning problem as a variant of the Prize-Collecting Traveling Salesman Problem (PC-TSP). A long-term goal (viewpoint with highest uncertainty) is identified. A spatial topology graph is constructed connecting candidate viewpoints to their k-nearest neighbors. Edge weights are assigned to balance Euclidean distance and potential information gain (from the uncertainty map). The algorithm then finds a set of top-M shortest simple paths from the current pose to the goal on this weighted graph. The optimal path is selected by maximizing a utility function that explicitly trades off accumulated information gain and path length. This multi-step lookahead aims to generate smoother, globally efficient trajectories, with iterative replanning as the robot moves. The system includes an auto-stop mode, terminating when average reconstruction quality uncertainty falls below a threshold.

3. **Dynamic Uncertainty Weighting:** The weights for the components of the uncertainty map (confidence, back-face, visibility) are dynamically adjusted. In early stages, all components are equally weighted to prioritize rapid exploration. As reconstruction progresses and visibility stabilizes, the visibility term is de-prioritized, shifting focus to refining subtle defects (back-facing surfaces, low-confidence areas).

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The experimental platform consists of a robotic arm with an eye-in-hand RGB-D camera and a motorized turntable. Object-centric pose tracking is implemented using pre-trained segmentation models for object mask extraction and point-cloud registration (ICP) to compensate for robot odometry inaccuracies. The reconstruction process is divided into an online mapping stage (L_D=0 for efficiency, 10 optimization iterations per frame, local window optimization) and an offline refinement stage (L_D=3 for photorealism, 7,000 optimization iterations, joint optimization of poses and GSurfels, exposure compensation, and mesh extraction via TSDF or Poisson reconstruction).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive experiments in both simulation (using 16 diverse models from the Google Scanned Objects dataset) and real-world scenarios (four cultural heritage artifacts) to evaluate reconstruction quality, exploration efficiency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Analysis of Reconstruction Quality

- ObjSplat consistently produced complete, photorealistic models with sharp details, accurately capturing fine-grained patterns.
- Baseline methods (GauSS-MI, FisherRF), relying on vanilla 3DGS, exhibited noticeable degradation with sparse observations, leading to blurry textures and significant floating artifacts due to overfitting and lack of explicit geometric constraints.
- ObjSplat’s performance is attributed to its geometry-aware uncertainty module, which guides the robot to acquire high-information observations, and the robust Gaussian surfel representation with geometric consistency constraints, which suppresses artifacts.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation of Reconstruction Process (Fixed Views)

- **Robust Initialization (2 views):** ObjSplat variants significantly outperformed baselines in completeness (Completion Ratio ~40% vs. ~18%) and geometric quality (F-Score ~0.65 vs. ~0.32), indicating that the Gaussian surfel representation provides strong geometric priors for sparse data.
- **Efficiency-Completeness Trade-off (10 views):** ObjSplat-NBV showed aggressive exploration and high completeness (>90%) and rendering quality (>30dB) by pursuing distant, high-information viewpoints. ObjSplat-NBP adopted a more conservative strategy, prioritizing path efficiency (1.17 m movement) while achieving moderate coverage (74.41%), avoiding erratic movements.
- **Global Optimality at Convergence (30 views):**
 - Both ObjSplat variants achieved high final coverage (>91%) and geometric accuracy, outperforming other GS-based baselines in completion ratio.
 - ObjSplat-NBP achieved comparable final quality to ObjSplat-NBV but with a significantly reduced total path length (3.96 m vs. 18.02 m), representing a 4.5x improvement in motion efficiency. Compared to the point-cloud-based PB-NBV (18.84 m), ObjSplat-NBP was approximately 4.7x more efficient.
 - ObjSplat-NBP exhibited superior visual quality compared to greedy NBV, suggesting that smoother, continuous trajectories promote more consistent surfel optimization.
 - NeRF-PRV (one-shot, appearance-based) showed competitive training fidelity but limited novel-view and depth performance due to slower NeRF convergence and reliance on initial appearance priors.
- **System Efficiency:** ObjSplat-NBP demonstrated superior online efficiency, completing the online process in 121.96 s (2.3x faster than GauSS-MI, 6x faster than FisherRF), with a total mission time (online + offline refinement) of approximately 5 minutes.

#### 4.3. Performance under Auto-Stop Mode

- Under native auto-stop criteria, ObjSplat-NBP achieved the highest PSNR (31.96 dB) and the lowest movement cost (3.55 m), balancing quality and efficiency.
- The greedy NBV variant terminated earlier, leading to slightly lower completeness, while NBP's multi-step lookahead ensured more thorough coverage.

#### 4.4. Reconstruction Process Analysis by Views vs. Path Length

- **Convergence over Views:** ObjSplat-NBV showed rapid initial growth in PSNR and Completion Ratio, while ObjSplat-NBP demonstrated steady and robust convergence, eventually matching NBV in all metrics. Both variants showed rapid geometric convergence (CD < 1.0 mm, F1-Score > 0.85 within 10 views), significantly outperforming baselines.
- **Dominance in Path Efficiency:** ObjSplat-NBP achieved high quality levels (PSNR > 32dB, Completion > 90%) within a minimal movement budget (~5 m). Greedy methods and baselines required much longer trajectories (>15 m) for comparable performance, highlighting NBP’s ability to maximize information acquisition per unit of movement cost.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis of Reconstruction Process

- Baselines (GauSS-MI, FisherRF) exhibited chaotic trajectories and suffered from floating artifacts and noise in their models due to overfitting. Their uncertainty maps were coarse.
- ObjSplat-NBP generated smooth, enveloping trajectories, achieving the highest coverage (96.14%) with a drastically reduced path length (4.05 m). Its geometry-aware metric accurately identified fine-grained uncertainty, leading to targeted refinement and superior geometric fidelity (CD 0.648 mm, F-Score 0.982).

#### 4.6. Ablation Study and System Performance

- **Viewpoint Evaluation:** The full geometry-aware uncertainty module (surfel confidence, back-face observation, visibility-based completeness, and dynamic weighting) was crucial. Removing components, particularly occlusion-awareness and back-face checks, degraded completeness and quality. Dynamic scheduling improved performance by adapting exploration-exploitation balance.
- **View Planning Strategy:** ObjSplat-NBP (executing the entire planned path) achieved the best trade-off, matching the high fidelity of greedy NBV while maintaining low movement cost and the lowest online processing time among active methods. Random and fixed strategies were less effective, and a receding horizon approach (NBP-R) performed worse than full path execution.
- **Unified Reconstruction Backend:** While using Gaussian Surfels for baselines improved their performance, ObjSplat’s planning strategy still yielded superior fidelity and efficiency, confirming the primary gains were from the geometry-aware evaluation and NBP planning.
- **Parameter Sensitivity:** The system demonstrated robustness to variations in key hyperparameters, indicating its reliability without extensive tuning.
- **Geometry-Texture Optimization:** Normal consistency loss was found to be important for reducing geometric fluctuations and improving overall fidelity.
- **Robustness to Initial Pose:** The system showed consistent performance with low standard deviations across different initial object orientations, confirming its adaptability.
- **Runtime Analysis:** A full perception-planning-action loop completed in approximately 4 seconds, with view evaluation and path planning constituting a small fraction of the total processing load.

#### 4.7. Real-world Experiments

- Deployed on a robotic platform, ObjSplat successfully reconstructed four complex cultural artifacts.
- The system actively identified high-uncertainty regions and generated collision-free paths. Average trajectory length was less than 4.0 m, confirming NBP’s efficiency.
- The final models demonstrated accurate geometry and photorealistic texture fidelity, faithfully recovering fine-grained details. Extracted meshes exhibited smooth surfaces with sharp features.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ObjSplat represents an advancement in active object reconstruction by integrating high-fidelity model representation with an intelligent, multi-step exploration strategy.

**Significance:**
- **Unified Framework:** The use of 2D Gaussian surfels as a unified representation for both model optimization and exploration guidance effectively couples reconstruction quality with planning efficiency.
- **Enhanced Perception for Planning:** The introduction of a geometry-aware viewpoint evaluation pipeline, which explicitly considers back-face visibility and occlusion-aware multi-view covisibility, provides more reliable and fine-grained guidance for identifying under-reconstructed regions, especially for complex object geometries. This addresses a critical limitation of previous opacity or depth-based metrics.
- **Globally Efficient Planning:** The Next-Best-Path (NBP) planning strategy moves beyond greedy, single-step optimization by performing multi-step lookahead on a dynamically constructed spatial graph. By jointly optimizing information gain and movement cost, it generates globally efficient, smooth trajectories, significantly reducing redundant motion and scanning time.
- **High-Fidelity Output:** The system consistently produces photorealistic and geometrically accurate, watertight digital assets, suitable for direct use in various downstream applications.
- **Real-world Validation:** Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world scenarios on cultural artifacts demonstrate the robustness and practical applicability of the framework.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Autonomous Digital Asset Creation:** ObjSplat could significantly automate and accelerate the process of creating high-quality 3D models for industries such as digital cultural heritage preservation, e-commerce, and extended reality (XR) content development, reducing manual labor and specialized expertise.
- **Robotics Development:** The ability to generate accurate and physically consistent 3D models rapidly and autonomously is crucial for bridging the "sim-to-real" gap in robotics. These models can be directly integrated into physics-based simulation environments, facilitating the training and validation of perception, planning, and control algorithms for robotic manipulation and interaction.
- **Manufacturing and Inspection:** Potential applications include automated quality control, reverse engineering, and geometric inspection of complex parts, where high-fidelity 3D models are required.
- **Accessibility:** By making high-fidelity 3D scanning more autonomous and efficient, ObjSplat could broaden access to advanced 3D reconstruction capabilities for a wider range of users and applications.

Despite its advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as focusing on single, static, rigid objects and challenges with complex optical properties. Future work aiming to address these aspects could further expand the impact and applicability of such autonomous digitization systems.
