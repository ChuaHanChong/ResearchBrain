# GraspGen: A Diffusion-based Framework for 6-DOF Grasping with On-Generator Training

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspGen is an object-centric 6-DOF grasping framework. It pairs a Diffusion-Transformer generator (DDPM over SE(3) with a PointTransformerV3 object encoder) with a lightweight discriminator trained on the generator's own simulated samples. It is backed by a 53-million-grasp Objaverse dataset spanning three grippers. It reaches state-of-the-art FetchBench clutter grasping and 81.3% real-robot success despite sim-only training.

## Problem

- Learned grasping systems remain unreliable as subroutines: prior methods score below 20% on FetchBench, and pipelines like OK-Robot lose notable trials to grasp-model failures alone.
- Contact-point architectures such as Contact-GraspNet and M2T2 assume symmetric parallel-jaw grippers, generalize poorly to adaptive or suction embodiments, and score their predicted grasps comparatively badly.
- Grasp discriminators are trained only on offline sampled datasets, which miss the generator's own failure modes like slightly colliding or outlier grasps, so false positives survive filtering.

## Method

- **GraspGen** diffusion grasp generator: separate ==DDPM schedulers== for translation and ==SO(3) rotation==, a dataset-derived ==translation normalization multiplier==, a ==PointTransformerV3== object tokenizer, and only 10 denoising steps.
- ==On-Generator Training== recipe: sample about 2K grasps per training object from the trained diffusion model, label them in simulation, then train the discriminator on these generated positives and negatives.
- Efficient ==discriminator== reusing the frozen generator object encoder plus an MLP over concatenated object embedding and grasp pose, trained on a new 53M-grasp ==Objaverse== dataset for Franka, Robotiq-2F-140 and suction grippers.

## Results

- On ACRONYM Franka object-centric evaluation, GraspGen reaches **0.947** precision-coverage AUC versus **0.636** for M2T2, **0.344** for DexDiffuser and **0.200** for SE3-Diff.
- On FetchBench clutter, GraspGen beats Contact-GraspNet and M2T2 by **16.9%** and **7.8%**; On-Generator discriminator training raises AUC from **0.886** with offline-only data to **0.947**.
- Trained only in simulation, it reaches **81.3%** real-robot grasp success on a UR10 versus **52.6%** for M2T2 and **63.7%** for AnyGrasp, with a discriminator using **21x** less memory.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Train a verifier on the generator's own output distribution, not offline data; generator-specific failure modes stay invisible to discriminators trained on independently sampled negatives.
> - Generative sampling without a learned scorer underperforms badly; final grasp quality depends on discriminator calibration as much as on generator expressiveness.
> - With strong foundation segmentation, object-centric models trained without scene simulation generalize to baskets and shelves better than scene-centric models trained on tabletop clutter.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic grasping is a foundational skill for autonomous manipulation, yet despite extensive research, it remains a complex challenge, particularly for achieving robust generalization across diverse objects, environments, and robot embodiments. GraspGen directly addresses critical gaps in the current state-of-the-art for 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DOF) grasping, positioning itself as a significant advancement towards more generalized and "turnkey" robotic systems.

Historically, 6-DOF grasping has evolved from classical model-based approaches, which required precise object pose information and struggled with unknown or cluttered environments, to learning-based methods. While learning-based approaches have shown promise in data generation, generalization, and integration with various sensing modalities, the authors highlight significant shortcomings:
- **Lack of Generalization:** Current state-of-the-art (SOTA) grasping systems still exhibit low success rates on benchmarks like FetchBench (sub-20% accuracy) and in real-world deployments (e.g., OK-Robot reporting 8% grasp model failures). This indicates a struggle to generalize across different robot embodiments (grippers) and in-the-wild settings.
- **Practical Limitations:** Many existing methods are not turnkey. Some necessitate multi-view scans, making them impractical for cluttered scenes. Contact point-based architectures, while effective for symmetric parallel-jaw grippers, often fail to generalize to different gripper morphologies (e.g., adaptive grippers).
- **Data Generation for Clutter:** Simulating or manually collecting data for cluttered scenes at scale is challenging, leading to reliance on instance segmentation for target-driven grasping.

GraspGen builds upon several recent successful paradigms while seeking to overcome their limitations. It leverages the rising popularity of **diffusion models** as powerful generative tools for continuous and high-dimensional distributions, a trend increasingly adopted in robot manipulation for tasks like visuomotor policy learning and motion planning. Specifically, it expands on prior work that formulates 6-DOF grasping as a diffusion process (e.g., Urain et al., 2023) and dexterous grasping (Weng et al., 2024), but critically improves upon their efficiency, generalization, and the effectiveness of grasp evaluation.

Furthermore, the paper capitalizes on the maturation of **instance segmentation models**, such as SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2024). This allows GraspGen to re-emphasize **object-centric models** for grasp generation, simplifying both training and inference by focusing on individual objects rather than entire complex scenes, while still enabling target-driven grasping in clutter. The release of a new, large-scale **multi-gripper simulated dataset** (53 million grasps from Objaverse) is also a crucial contribution to the broader landscape, addressing the persistent need for diverse and high-quality data in robotics research. By demonstrating superior performance across various grippers, observational complexities (partial vs. complete point clouds), and sim-to-real transfer, GraspGen significantly advances the field towards truly general-purpose robotic grasping.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of GraspGen is to develop a **robust, general-purpose, and performant 6-DOF grasping system** that overcomes the limitations of current state-of-the-art approaches. The authors are motivated by the persistent "critical gaps" in robotic grasping, which prevent it from being a truly "turnkey" capability, crucial for its reliability as a subroutine in higher-level reasoning and manipulation systems.

The key specific objectives and motivations driving the GraspGen framework are:

1. **Enhance Grasp Generation and Quality:** To move beyond existing generative models that may produce "false positives" or struggle with efficiency. This involves leveraging a Diffusion-Transformer architecture for high-quality and diverse grasp sampling.
2. **Improve Grasp Evaluation and Filtering:** To ensure that generated grasps are reliably successful in practice. This necessitates an efficient and accurate discriminator that can score and rank sampled grasps effectively, filtering out erroneous predictions.
3. **Address Distribution Shift in Discriminator Training:** A critical motivation is the observed mismatch between offline datasets (typically used to train discriminators) and the distribution of grasps generated by generative models. The authors aim to introduce a novel "On-Generator Training" recipe to specifically train the discriminator on grasps *produced by the generator*, making it aware of the generator's failure modes and improving its ability to filter them.
4. **Achieve Generalization Across Diverse Settings:** To build a flexible system capable of scaling across:
 - **Embodiments:** Compatibility with multiple distinct gripper types (e.g., Franka Panda, Robotiq-2F-140, vacuum grippers).
 - **Observability:** Robustness to different levels of sensory input (partial vs. complete point clouds).
 - **Complexity:** Performance in both singulated object scenarios and complex cluttered scenes.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Ensuring that models trained purely in simulation generalize effectively to real-world robot deployment.
5. **Scale Data for Research:** To support future research in the community, a major objective is the creation and release of a new, large-scale simulated dataset comprising over 53 million grasps across various objects and grippers.
6. **Improve Computational Efficiency:** To enhance both inference time and memory usage compared to prior methods, making the system more practical for real-time robotic applications.

By focusing on these objectives, GraspGen aims to deliver a significant leap forward in robot grasping, enabling more reliable and adaptable manipulation capabilities for a wide range of robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GraspGen proposes a novel framework for 6-DOF robotic grasping that integrates a powerful diffusion-based generative model with an efficient, specially trained discriminator. Its core technical novelties lie in its **on-generator training recipe** for the discriminator and its architectural design for flexibility and efficiency.

#### 3.1. Grasp Generation with Diffusion (Generator)

The framework formulates 6-DOF grasp generation as a **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)**. Unlike some prior works that use computationally slow Energy-Based Models (EBMs), DDPMs are empirically faster and simpler. The diffusion process occurs in the SE(3) Lie group, which is factorized into Euclidean R3 (translation) and SO(3) Lie algebra (rotation) spaces to be compatible with DDPMs.

- **Conditional Diffusion:** The noise prediction network is conditioned on an object's point cloud encoding, allowing for shape-aware grasp generation.
- **Translation Normalization:** To handle the unbounded nature of translation and dependence on object scale, grasp translations are normalized by a multiplier ($\kappa$) computed directly from dataset statistics, ensuring optimal neural network performance without arbitrary scaling.
- **Object Tokenization:** For robust object encoding, GraspGen employs the state-of-the-art **PointTransformerV3 (PTv3)** backbone (Wu et al., 2024). This choice is significant as it surpasses traditional PointNet++ architectures by converting unstructured point clouds to a structured format, enabling efficient transformer processing and avoiding common bottlenecks like nearest neighbor ball queries.
- **Diffusion Network Architecture:** The network iteratively denoises a sampled noise vector to generate grasp poses. It uses 10 denoising steps, a number empirically found sufficient for the lower dimensionality of grasp data compared to image diffusion. Separate denoising processes with dedicated schedulers are used for translation and rotation components, and the training loss is an L2 difference between predicted and actual noise values.

#### 3.2. Grasp Evaluation with On-Generator Training (Discriminator)

Recognizing that a generative model trained solely on successful grasps can still produce false positives, GraspGen introduces a discriminator for scoring, ranking, and filtering generated grasps. This component features two key improvements:

- **On-Generator Training (Novelty):** This is a critical innovation. Instead of relying solely on offline datasets, the discriminator is trained on a synthetic "On-Generator Dataset." This dataset is created by running inference with the *trained diffusion model* on the training objects and then simulating/annotating these *generated* grasps as successful or unsuccessful. This ensures the discriminator learns to identify and filter out the specific failure modes (e.g., slight collisions, outliers) characteristic of the diffusion model's outputs, which may differ from pre-existing offline datasets.
- **Efficient Evaluation:** The discriminator's architecture is designed for efficiency. It reuses the *frozen object encoder* (PTv3) from the generator, significantly reducing memory footprint and training time for the discriminator. Unlike prior methods that might convert grasp poses to point clouds, GraspGen simply concatenates the object embedding with the SE(3) representation of the grasp pose and passes it through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict a sigmoid success score. The discriminator is trained separately from the generator using a binary cross-entropy loss.

#### 3.3. Dataset

A cornerstone of GraspGen's methodology is its new, large-scale simulated dataset, comprising over **53 million grasps**.
- **Source:** It uses meshes from the **Objaverse dataset** (Deitke et al., 2023), which is larger and more diverse than previous datasets like ACRONYM's reliance on ShapeNetSem. A subset of 8,515 objects (to match ACRONYM size) from 36,366 meshes (LVIS categories) are used.
- **Grasp Labeling:** Following the ACRONYM pipeline, 2K grasp transformations are uniformly sampled per object and simulated in the **Isaac simulator** (Makoviychuk et al., 2021). A grasp is labeled successful if it maintains stable contact after a simulated shaking motion.
- **Multi-Gripper Support:** Datasets are constructed for three distinct gripper types: Franka Panda, Robotiq-2f-140, and a vacuum gripper (with analytical success model). Each gripper comprises approximately 17 million grasps.

#### 3.4. Real Robot Evaluation Setup

To demonstrate real-world generalization, GraspGen is evaluated on a **UR10 robot arm** equipped with a **RealSense D435 RGB-D camera**.
- **Perception Pipeline:** **SAM2** (Ravi et al., 2024) is used for instance segmentation, and **FoundationStereo** (Wen et al., 2025) for depth estimation, demonstrating integration with modern foundation models for perception.
- **Motion Planning & Collision Avoidance:** **cuRobo** (Sundaralingam et al., 2023) handles collision-free minimum-jerk motion planning, while **NVBlox** (Millane et al., 2024) provides occupancy mapping for collision checking.
- **Adaptations for Baselines:** For comparison with M2T2 and AnyGrasp on the real robot, specific modifications (e.g., point cloud rotation, box cropping, translation offsets) were required to align their input distributions with the real-world setup, highlighting the robustness of GraspGen's approach.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating state-of-the-art generative models, novel training paradigms, efficient architectures, and large-scale data generation, positions GraspGen as a significant advancement in robotic grasping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GraspGen's experimental evaluation reveals significant performance improvements across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios, validating its design choices and contributions.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (Single Objects)

- **Superior Performance:** On the Franka-ACRONYM dataset for single, singulated objects, GraspGen outperforms prior methods like M2T2, DexDiffuser, and SE3-Diff by over 48% in Area Under Curve (AUC) on the Precision-Coverage metric. This metric effectively captures both grasp success rate (precision) and spatial diversity (coverage).
- **Importance of Discriminators:** The results clearly show that methods employing discriminative reasoning (GraspGen, DexDiffuser, M2T2) consistently outperform purely generative methods (SE3-Diff), emphasizing the critical role of a robust discriminator in filtering out sub-optimal grasps.
- **Discriminator Quality Matters:** GraspGen's discriminator, with its novel On-Generator training, demonstrated superior performance compared to DexDiffuser's discriminator, highlighting the effectiveness of training specifically on the distribution of generated grasps.

#### 4.2. Task-level Evaluation in Clutter (FetchBench)

- **State-of-the-Art in Clutter:** GraspGen achieves state-of-the-art results on the challenging FetchBench benchmark, designed for complex grasping in cluttered environments. It surpasses Contact-GraspNet by 16.9% and M2T2 by 7.8% in task success rates.
- **Challenges of Clutter:** The benchmark itself proved difficult, with an oracle planner achieving only 65% grasp success and 49.2% task success, indicating inherent complexities due to collision-free path limitations and motion planning challenges. Despite this, GraspGen's relative improvement is substantial.

#### 4.3. Sensitivity to Occlusions

- **Generalization to Observation Types:** GraspGen demonstrates its ability to generalize across different observation settings (complete vs. single-view partial point clouds). By training on a 50-50 mix of both, the model maintains high performance (AUCs of ~0.94 for complete and ~0.80 for partial point clouds), significantly outperforming models trained exclusively on one type of input when tested on the other. This highlights GraspGen's flexibility for downstream applications.

#### 4.4. Analysis of On-Generator Training

- **Distribution Shift Confirmed:** The authors quantitatively demonstrate a measurable distribution shift between traditionally collected "offline" datasets and grasps generated by the diffusion model using Earth Mover's Distance (EMD). This shift is particularly pronounced for negative grasps.
- **Performance Impact:** The discriminator trained exclusively on the On-Generator dataset achieved the highest performance, outperforming a discriminator trained only on offline data by 6.5% AUC. This empirical evidence strongly supports the hypothesis that On-Generator training effectively captures and corrects the diffusion model's specific failure modes (e.g., slight collisions, outliers).

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Discriminator Efficiency & Accuracy:** GraspGen's discriminator is not only more accurate (6.7% higher AUC, 5.87% higher mAP) than previous SOTA architectures (e.g., 6-DOF GraspNet) but also remarkably more memory-efficient (21x less memory). This is attributed to reusing the object encoder and directly processing SE(3) grasp representations.
- **Translation Normalization:** The proposed adaptive translation normalization method, derived from dataset statistics, was found to reliably lead to optimal performance (minimal error, maximal recall).
- **Rotation Representation:** All tested rotation representations (6D, Euler angles, Lie Algebra) performed comparably, suggesting that proper normalization is more critical than the specific representation choice for diffusion models on grasp datasets.
- **Point Cloud Encoder:** Using PointTransformerV3 (PTv3) for the object encoder yielded substantial gains over PointNet++ (5.3mm reduction in translation error, 4% increase in recall), confirming the benefits of SOTA transformer architectures.

#### 4.6. Performance on Multiple Grippers

- **Cross-Gripper Proficiency:** GraspGen proved to be the most proficient method across all three tested gripper types (Franka Panda, Robotiq-2F-140, and suction grippers), consistently outperforming baselines.
- **Adaptive Gripper Advantage:** The performance margin over M2T2 was significantly larger for the Robotiq-2F-140 (44% AUC higher in sim, 57% higher in real) due to M2T2's contact-point representation struggling with adaptive grippers.

#### 4.7. Real Robot Evaluation

- **Strong Sim-to-Real Transfer:** GraspGen generalized well to the real world, achieving an impressive overall success rate of 81.3% (Robotiq-2F-140 gripper), significantly outperforming M2T2 (52.6%) and AnyGrasp (63.7%).
- **Generalization to Diverse Environments:** GraspGen performed well across various real-world settings, including isolated objects, cluttered tables, baskets, and shelves. Its object-centric design proved superior to scene-centric models for generalizing to more complex and varied environments where baselines struggled.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate GraspGen's superior performance, robustness, and flexibility, making a strong case for its approach as a foundation for future manipulation systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GraspGen represents a substantial step forward in the field of 6-DOF robotic grasping, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts on both research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Advancement of State-of-the-Art in Grasping

The paper conclusively demonstrates that GraspGen outperforms prior methods across multiple grippers, object types, and environmental complexities (single objects vs. clutter), achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like FetchBench and in real-world deployments. This directly addresses the critical need for more reliable and generalized grasping systems highlighted by the authors.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Robustness and Generalization

One of the most impactful aspects of GraspGen is its demonstrated ability to generalize across diverse settings. This includes:
- **Multi-Embodiment Grasping:** Proving proficiency across different gripper types (parallel-jaw, adaptive, suction) is crucial for universal robotic applicability.
- **Observational Robustness:** The capacity to handle both complete and partial point cloud inputs makes GraspGen adaptable to various sensing setups, from multi-view systems to single-camera observations common in constrained environments.
- **Clutter Handling:** Achieving high success rates in cluttered environments on FetchBench and a real robot moves closer to practical manipulation in unstructured settings.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The impressive real-robot performance, despite being trained solely in simulation, validates its potential for rapid deployment and scalability without extensive real-world data collection.

#### 5.3. Novel Methodological Contributions

- **On-Generator Training:** This is a key conceptual and practical innovation. By training the discriminator on grasps generated by the diffusion model itself, GraspGen effectively adapts to the specific failure modes of its generator. This principle could be widely applicable to other generative models in robotics and beyond, leading to more robust filtering and improved sample quality.
- **Efficient Discriminator Architecture:** The design choice to reuse the generator's frozen object encoder and directly process SE(3) grasp representations significantly reduces the discriminator's memory footprint and improves its accuracy. This provides a practical blueprint for building computationally efficient and effective grasp evaluation modules.
- **Leveraging SOTA Architectures:** The successful integration of PointTransformerV3 (PTv3) as the point cloud encoder highlights the benefits of adopting advanced deep learning architectures from broader computer vision research into robotics, pushing performance boundaries.

#### 5.4. Data Contribution to the Community

The release of a massive, multi-gripper simulated dataset comprising over 53 million grasps is a substantial open-science contribution. Large-scale, diverse datasets are critical for training robust deep learning models in robotics, and this resource will undoubtedly accelerate future research in grasp generation, multi-gripper learning, and sim-to-real transfer.

#### 5.5. Facilitating Higher-Level Robotic Autonomy

By making 6-DOF grasping a more reliable and "turnkey" subroutine, GraspGen directly supports the development of more complex and intelligent robotic systems. Tasks involving long-horizon planning, language-guided manipulation, or knowledge-based reasoning increasingly rely on robust low-level skills like grasping. GraspGen's improved performance means fewer grasp model failures, enabling downstream systems to operate more consistently and effectively in real-world scenarios.

#### 5.6. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions

The authors transparently discuss limitations such as dependence on depth sensing and instance segmentation quality, occasional struggles with cuboidal objects (suggesting a need for more diverse training data), and the high computational cost for training. These acknowledgements provide valuable insights for future research, including investigating methods for improved data diversity, mitigating sensor noise, and exploring more efficient training paradigms.

In conclusion, GraspGen represents a significant milestone in robotic grasping, providing a highly performant, flexible, and robust framework. Its methodological innovations and substantial data contribution are poised to have a lasting impact on both fundamental research in robot learning and the practical deployment of autonomous manipulation systems.
