# Next Token Prediction Towards Multimodal Intelligence: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey systematically charts the rapidly evolving field of multimodal artificial intelligence by unifying understanding and generation tasks through the lens of Next Token Prediction (NTP). It provides a detailed taxonomy for Multimodal NTP, categorizing advancements in tokenization, model architectures, training strategies, and evaluation, while also identifying critical open challenges.

## Problem

- The rapidly expanding field of multimodal AI, particularly with Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), lacks a unified conceptual framework that integrates both understanding and generation capabilities.
- Previous surveys on LMMs often focus on specific aspects like understanding, architecture, or applications, missing a comprehensive review from the unifying perspective of Next Token Prediction (NTP).
- There is a need to consolidate current knowledge, trends, and challenges within multimodal learning to effectively guide researchers toward developing more general and human-like AI systems.

## Method

- The survey proposes a comprehensive taxonomy for ==Multimodal Next Token Prediction (MMNTP)==, structuring the field into five core components: multimodal tokenization, MMNTP model architectures, unified task representation, datasets & evaluation, and open challenges.
- It systematically reviews and categorizes existing research on techniques for transforming raw multimodal data into tokens, various model architectures (==compositional vs. unified==), training objectives (discrete vs. continuous token prediction), and strategies for pretraining and finetuning.
- The work details current datasets and evaluation benchmarks while also outlining critical unsolved problems and providing an associated GitHub repository with relevant resources for the research community.

## Results

- The survey establishes a comprehensive taxonomy that categorizes advancements in multimodal tokenization, MMNTP model architectures (compositional vs. unified), training objectives, pretraining/finetuning strategies, and both holistic and emerging evaluation benchmarks.
- It highlights the historical development and current state of NTP-based LMMs, demonstrating their increasing capabilities in unifying understanding and generation tasks across modalities.
- The research articulates four critical open challenges for future work: scaling MMNTP with unlabeled data, overcoming modality interference, increasing efficiency in training and inference, and expanding MMNTP as universal interfaces for new tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Next Token Prediction (NTP) has emerged as a versatile and universal training objective, effectively unifying both understanding and generation tasks across diverse modalities (text, image, audio, video).
> - The field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) is experiencing rapid evolution, moving towards increasingly sophisticated architectures that integrate multiple modalities and tasks.
> - Effective multimodal tokenization (both discrete and continuous), strategic multi-stage training (pretraining for alignment, finetuning for instruction/preference), and comprehensive evaluation benchmarks are foundational for advancing MMNTP.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research landscape in Artificial Intelligence (AI) is undergoing a significant transformation, marked by a paradigm shift from specialized unimodal models designed for single tasks to versatile multimodal models capable of handling a diverse array of tasks across various information types. This evolution is largely catalyzed by the remarkable advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) within Natural Language Processing (NLP). LLMs, powered by the Next Token Prediction (NTP) objective, have effectively unified various natural language understanding and generation tasks within the textual modality.

Recent research has demonstrated that this powerful NTP paradigm can be extended beyond text to encapsulate tasks from different modalities, such as vision and audio. The core idea is to transform diverse multimodal information into sequences of tokens and then predict the next token given the context, mirroring the success seen in LLMs. This extension has led to the emergence of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), which are at the forefront of the quest for artificial general intelligence (AGI). Figure 1 in the paper, illustrating the historical development of NTP-based LMMs, vividly captures the rapid pace and increasing complexity of models incorporating vision, audio, and other modalities.

This survey differentiates itself from prior work by adopting a unique and systematic perspective. As articulated in Section 1.2, numerous recent surveys have reviewed LMMs, often focusing on their understanding capabilities (e.g., Yin et al., Awais et al., Bordes et al.), specific architectural aspects, training strategies, datasets, or evaluation metrics (e.g., Ghosh et al., Caffagni et al., Zhang et al.). Others have investigated specialized applications like multimodal agents or autonomous driving, or specific challenges such as hallucination and efficiency. However, "Next Token Prediction Towards Multimodal Intelligence: A Comprehensive Survey" stands out as the *first* survey to comprehensively review LMMs from the *specific perspective of Next Token Prediction (NTP)*. It uniquely integrates *both understanding and generation* in multimodal learning under this unifying paradigm. This holistic approach provides a novel framework for understanding the underlying principles and future directions of multimodal AI, positioning the work as a crucial guide for researchers exploring the frontier of multimodal intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind this comprehensive survey stems from the inherently multimodal nature of human interaction with the world and the aspiration to imbue artificial intelligence systems with similar capabilities. Humans seamlessly engage with the universe through various modalities—seeing and sketching, reading and writing, listening and composing, touching and sculpting. This multifaceted interaction underscores the interwoven nature of perception and response in human experience. Within AI research, multimodal learning endeavors to create systems that can both comprehend and generate diverse multimodal information.

A significant paradigm shift has occurred in AI, moving from specialized unimodal models to more versatile multimodal ones. This transition is largely driven by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs), like GPT-3 and LLaMA, which have demonstrated the scalability and efficacy of the Next Token Prediction (NTP) objective in unifying numerous natural language understanding and generation tasks. Recent research has further revealed that this scalable NTP framework can be effectively extended to tasks across different modalities by converting multimodal information into tokens and predicting the next one in sequence. This unification offers a promising path towards developing more general and human-like AI systems.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this survey are:
1. **Introduce a Comprehensive Taxonomy:** The primary goal is to present a systematic and comprehensive taxonomy that unifies *both understanding and generation* within multimodal learning, all through the cohesive lens of the NTP paradigm.
2. **Cover Key Aspects:** This taxonomy is structured around five critical areas: Multimodal tokenization, MMNTP model architectures, unified task representation, datasets & evaluation, and open challenges. This structured approach aims to provide a clear and organized overview of the field.
3. **Aid Researchers:** By consolidating existing knowledge and outlining current trends and future directions, the survey aims to effectively guide and support researchers in their exploration and advancement of multimodal intelligence.
4. **Provide Resources:** To facilitate ongoing research, the authors have made an associated GitHub repository available, which collects the latest papers and resources related to Multimodal Next Token Prediction.

In essence, the survey is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of AI towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) by leveraging the proven scalability and versatility of the NTP paradigm to create models capable of both comprehending and producing information across multiple modalities, akin to human cognitive abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology of this work revolves around a systematic review, categorization, and synthesis of existing research within the domain of Multimodal Next Token Prediction (MMNTP). The authors propose a comprehensive taxonomy that structures the field into five core components.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Tokenization (Section 2)

This section details the critical first step in MMNTP: transforming raw multimodal data into discrete or continuous tokens.
- **Types of Tokenization:** The survey distinguishes between **discrete tokenization** (quantizing continuous data into a fixed, finite codebook, akin to language vocabularies, e.g., VQVAE, VQGAN) and **continuous tokenization** (representing data directly in a continuous feature space without quantization, preserving more information, e.g., CLIP, raw pixel projections).
- **Tokenizer Features:** It highlights the importance of **representation ability** (encoding semantically relevant information) and **reconstruction ability** (recovering original input from tokens), noting the trade-offs.
- **Training Methods:** Four main categories are identified: Auto-Encoding (for reconstruction), Denoising Auto-Encoding (for robust features), Supervised Pretraining (for task-specific representations), and Contrastive Learning (for distinguishing similar/dissimilar pairs).
- **Modality-Specific Tokenization:** The survey delves into specialized techniques for images (addressing granularity, density, and 2D structure), audio (handling long sequences, using neural codecs, and semantic distillation), video (managing the temporal dimension with 3D tokenizers or frame-by-frame methods, and features like joint image-video tokenization), and extends to other modalities like robotics and AI-for-Science.

#### 3.2. MMNTP Model Architectures (Section 3)

This section categorizes the overarching structures used for MMNTP, outlining the general pipeline and specific applications for vision and audio tasks.
- **General Pipeline:** The typical workflow involves encoding inputs into shared token sequences, using a multimodal transformer for autoregressive next token prediction, and decoding the predicted tokens back into their respective modalities. Different attention masks (causal, non-causal) are employed based on task requirements.
- **Unified Structures:** The survey illustrates how various vision tasks (visual question answering, text-to-image generation, image editing) and audio tasks (understanding, generation, full-duplex dialogue) are framed within the NTP paradigm.
- **Model Types:**
 - **Compositional Model:** Leverages powerful, pre-trained external encoders and decoders (e.g., CLIP for vision encoding, diffusion models for image generation), with a large language model (LLM) handling the textual reasoning and coordination (e.g., Flamingo, MiniGPT4, Emu).
 - **Unified Model:** Integrates lightweight modality-specific encoders and decoders directly into a single, large transformer decoder backbone, which performs most of the understanding and generation tasks (e.g., Unified-IO, Chameleon, Moshi).
- **Comparison:** A comparative analysis discusses their strengths and weaknesses in terms of general multimodal intelligence, training efficiency, and deployment efficiency. Unified models are seen as closer to foundational models, potentially offering better scaling, while compositional models benefit from specialized, powerful components.

#### 3.3. Unified Task Representation (Section 4)

This section describes the training objectives and strategies for MMNTP models.
- **Training Objectives:**
 - **Discrete Token Prediction (DTP):** Predicts the next discrete token (text or quantized multimodal codes) using cross-entropy loss. It supports both understanding (predicting text from multimodal inputs) and generation (predicting discrete multimodal tokens).
 - **Continuous Token Prediction (CTP):** Predicts continuous vectors as conditions for external generative models (e.g., diffusion models) or directly generates continuous data using MSE loss.
- **Pretraining: Modality Alignment:** Focuses on aligning the representation spaces of different modalities with the language space using large-scale, often noisy, multimodal datasets (e.g., image-caption pairs).
- **Finetuning: Instruction and Preference:**
 - **Instruction Tuning:** Improves models' ability to follow complex user commands (e.g., VQA, text-to-image) on meticulously curated datasets.
 - **Preference Alignment:** Refines model behavior to align with human preferences (e.g., using RLHF or DPO) to mitigate issues like hallucination and bias.
- **Inference: Prompt Engineering:** Discusses the adaptation of LLM-proven techniques like In-Context Learning (ICL) for few-shot demonstrations and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting for step-by-step reasoning in multimodal contexts.

#### 3.4. Datasets and Evaluation (Section 5)

This part covers the empirical foundations and assessment methods for MMNTP.
- **Training Datasets:** Categorized into pre-training data (text-only, image-based, video-based, audio-based, including interleaved and grounded formats) and fine-tuning data (for multimodal understanding and generation, such as image editing). Tables 5 and 6 provide statistics of commonly used datasets.
- **Evaluation:**
 - **Holistic Evaluation:** Benchmarks that comprehensively assess general multimodal abilities (e.g., MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench, MMMU, MVBench, VBench).
 - **Emerging Benchmarks:** Specialized evaluations targeting advanced capabilities and specific challenges, such as conversational competence (SparklesEval), mathematical reasoning (MathVista), hallucination detection (HallusionBench, Bingo), chart understanding (MMC-Benchmark), and robustness to visual styles (BenchLMM). Table 7 provides statistics on these benchmarks.

#### 3.5. Open Challenges (Section 6)

The survey concludes by outlining critical unsolved problems that serve as a roadmap for future research, including scaling with unlabeled data, mitigating modality interference, improving efficiency, and developing MMNTP as universal interfaces for new tasks.

By organizing existing knowledge into this detailed taxonomy, the authors provide a structured and comprehensive framework for understanding the advancements, methodologies, and persistent challenges in Next Token Prediction Towards Multimodal Intelligence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, this paper's "main findings and results" are not experimental outcomes but rather a structured synthesis of the current state, trends, and challenges in the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Next Token Prediction (MMNTP). The authors effectively consolidate disparate research into a coherent framework, offering a clear picture of progress and future directions.

**1. NTP as a Universal Training Objective:** The central finding is the successful and expanding application of Next Token Prediction (NTP) beyond its origins in language modeling to become a versatile training objective across various modalities. It has proven effective in unifying both understanding and generation tasks within multimodal learning. This paradigm shift has enabled models to process diverse information (text, image, audio, video) by transforming it into a token sequence and predicting the subsequent token.

**2. Rapid Evolution of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs):** The survey highlights the exponential growth and increasing sophistication of NTP-based LMMs, as illustrated in Figure 1. Initially focusing on unimodal understanding or generation tasks (e.g., ImageGPT for image understanding, DALL-E for text-to-image generation), the field has quickly progressed towards unified models capable of both understanding and generating information across multiple modalities (e.g., GPT-4o, Chameleon, Emu3). This historical development underscores the increasing ambition to build truly generalist multimodal AI.

**3. Categorization of Core MMNTP Components:** The survey's primary "result" is the comprehensive taxonomy, which systematically breaks down the MMNTP landscape into manageable, interconnected components:
 - **Multimodal Tokenization:** The research emphasizes that effective tokenization is foundational. Both discrete (Vector Quantization based, for reconstruction and structured output) and continuous (direct feature projection, for rich representation) tokenization methods are prevalent, each with distinct advantages and use cases (e.g., VQVAE for generation, CLIP for understanding). Modality-specific optimizations are crucial due to the unique characteristics of images, audio, and video.
 - **MMNTP Model Architectures:** The field is broadly divided into "Compositional Models" (leveraging powerful, pre-trained external encoders/decoders alongside an LLM for reasoning) and "Unified Models" (integrating lightweight modality components directly into a single large transformer backbone). While compositional models benefit from specialized expertise, unified models aim for greater end-to-end coherence and scaling potential. Figure 9 and 10 illustrate the general structure and flexible attention mechanisms.
 - **Unified Task Representation:** Training strategies commonly involve a multi-stage approach: initial pretraining for modality alignment (often on vast web-scale datasets) followed by finetuning for instruction-following and preference alignment (on curated, high-quality datasets). Both Discrete Token Prediction (for symbolic output) and Continuous Token Prediction (for generative models like diffusion) objectives are employed, with active research on how to best integrate them.
 - **Datasets and Evaluation:** The survey meticulously details the extensive range of training datasets, from massive text-only corpora to multimodal datasets (image-text, video-text, audio-text, and interleaved formats), crucial for both pre-training and fine-tuning. For evaluation, it presents a landscape of "Holistic Evaluation" benchmarks (e.g., VQAv2, MMBench, MMMU, MVBench) that assess general capabilities, alongside "Emerging Benchmarks" (e.g., MathVista, HallusionBench, SparklesEval) that probe specialized or frontier abilities like mathematical reasoning, hallucination detection, and conversational competence. Figures 17 and 18 visually demonstrate the performance trends of various models on key benchmarks, indicating the competitive and often superior performance of NTP-based models.

**4. Identification of Key Challenges:** Despite significant progress, the survey clearly articulates four major open challenges, which constitute critical areas for future research:
 - **Scaling Up MMNTP with Unlabeled Data:** How to effectively leverage the vast amounts of unlabeled multimodal data, given that simple scaling laws observed in LLMs may not directly apply across modalities.
 - **Overcoming Modality Interference and Boosting Synergy:** Addressing optimization conflicts when jointly training diverse multimodal tasks and ensuring mutual assistance rather than negative interference between modalities.
 - **Increasing Efficiency:** Tackling the computational demands of training and inference, particularly concerning heterogeneous data types, variable sequence lengths, and redundancy in tokenization.
 - **MMNTP as Universal Interfaces:** Expanding the NTP paradigm to formulate and efficiently solve tasks in novel domains like robotics, molecular biology, and beyond, and comparing its efficacy against alternative generative frameworks like diffusion models.

In summary, the survey's main contributions lie in its comprehensive mapping of the MMNTP landscape, its detailed categorization of methodologies, its analysis of data and evaluation trends, and its precise articulation of the scientific and engineering hurdles that remain. It serves as an authoritative snapshot of a dynamic field, grounded in the unifying power of next-token prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey, "Next Token Prediction Towards Multimodal Intelligence," carries substantial significance and holds the potential to profoundly impact the trajectory of multimodal AI research and development. Its primary importance lies in being the *first* to systematically and comprehensively integrate both understanding and generation within multimodal learning through the lens of the Next Token Prediction (NTP) paradigm.

**1. Providing a Unified Conceptual Framework:** The most immediate impact is the provision of a much-needed unified conceptual framework for the rapidly expanding and often fragmented field of multimodal AI. By demonstrating how NTP serves as a versatile, unifying objective across diverse modalities and tasks, the survey helps to consolidate understanding, allowing researchers to see connections and commonalities that might otherwise be overlooked. This coherence is crucial for a field characterized by rapid innovations and a proliferation of models and approaches.

**2. Guiding Future Research Directions:** The detailed taxonomy, covering tokenization, model architectures, training objectives, datasets, and evaluation, offers a clear roadmap for researchers. By meticulously dissecting each component, the survey identifies strengths, weaknesses, and areas ripe for innovation. The explicit outlining of "Open Challenges" in Section 6—such as scaling with unlabeled multimodal data, overcoming modality interference, enhancing efficiency, and establishing MMNTP as a universal interface—provides concrete, high-impact problems for the community to tackle. This structured identification of challenges will undoubtedly guide future grant proposals, Ph.D. theses, and research initiatives.

**3. Fostering Benchmarking and Evaluation Standards:** The extensive overview of training datasets and, particularly, the discussion of "Holistic" and "Emerging Evaluation Benchmarks," is critical. In a field where comparing models can be complex due to diverse tasks and metrics, consolidating evaluation practices helps to standardize performance assessment. By highlighting gaps in current benchmarks (e.g., for specific reasoning capabilities or real-world robustness), the survey encourages the development of more sophisticated and comprehensive evaluation tools, which are essential for objectively measuring progress towards multimodal intelligence.

**4. Accelerating Model Development and Deployment:** The in-depth analysis of model architectures (compositional vs. unified) and training efficiency challenges directly addresses practical concerns in building and deploying large-scale multimodal models. Insights into system efficiency, multimodal tokenization efficiency, and modeling efficiency can inform better engineering practices, leading to faster training times and more efficient inference for MMNTP models. This is particularly relevant as models grow in size and complexity, demanding optimized resource utilization.

**5. Advancing Towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The underlying motivation of enabling AI systems to comprehend and generate information across multiple modalities, akin to human abilities, is a cornerstone of AGI. By focusing on NTP as a scalable and unified approach for both understanding and generation, the survey implicitly endorses a promising pathway towards more generalist AI systems. The success of NTP in language, and its burgeoning success in multimodal contexts, suggests it could be a foundational mechanism for more encompassing AI.

**6. Empowering the Research Community:** The inclusion of an associated GitHub repository is a highly significant and practical contribution. It serves as a living, open-source resource for researchers, providing updated lists of papers and repositories. This initiative fosters collaboration, reduces redundancy, and ensures that the research community has immediate access to the latest advancements, thereby accelerating collective progress.

In conclusion, "Next Token Prediction Towards Multimodal Intelligence: A Comprehensive Survey" is more than just a literature review; it is a foundational document that frames the current state of a vital field, provides intellectual scaffolding for its future, and offers practical resources to empower the next generation of multimodal AI researchers. Its impact will be felt in guiding theoretical explorations, informing practical implementations, and ultimately, pushing the boundaries of what AI can perceive, understand, and create across the rich tapestry of human experience.
