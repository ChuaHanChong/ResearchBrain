# dVLA: Diffusion Vision-Language-Action Model with Multimodal Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Developed by researchers from Midea Group, Peking University, and Shanghai Jiaotong University, dVLA unifies visual perception, language reasoning, and action generation within a single diffusion framework for robotic control. The model achieves state-of-the-art average success rates of 96.4% in simulation (LIBERO benchmark) and 65% in real-world robotic tasks, while also accelerating inference speed by approximately 2x.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face gradient conflicts and architectural disparities when co-training distinct modalities, hindering effective cross-modal learning.
- Current VLA approaches struggle with open-world instructions and long-horizon tasks due to a lack of explicit intermediate reasoning and insufficient understanding of underlying physical laws.
- Complex multimodal generation in VLA models leads to high inference latency, posing a significant barrier to their practical, real-time deployment in robotics.

## Method

- Introduces **dVLA**, a unified discrete ==diffusion== model that converts vision, language, and continuous actions into discrete tokens and optimizes them under a single ==diffusion== objective.
- Implements a multimodal ==Chain-of-Thought== (==CoT==) mechanism that jointly generates visual subgoals, textual reasoning, and action sequences by masking tokens across all modalities during training.
- Integrates acceleration strategies, including a prefix attention mask and KV caching (dLLM-Cache), to significantly improve inference efficiency without substantial performance degradation.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art average success rates of **96.4%** on the LIBERO simulation benchmark and **65%** in real-world tasks (e.g., Bin Picking), outperforming all continuous and discrete action baselines.
- Multimodal Chain-of-Thought reasoning enhanced dVLA's average success rate by 6.6 percentage points in simulation and 12.5 percentage points in real-world tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in complex scenarios.
- Demonstrated ~**2x** inference speedup (from 1.3-1.5 Hz to 2.9-3 Hz) with minimal performance degradation (<**1%** drop in success rates) across tasks, making dVLA viable for real-time robotic deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single discrete diffusion objective effectively resolves gradient conflicts and architectural gaps across vision, language, and action modalities, leading to more coherent cross-modal learning in VLA systems.
> - Jointly generating multimodal Chain-of-Thought (visual subgoals and textual reasoning) alongside actions deepens the model's understanding of task decomposition and implicit physical laws, allowing it to predict consequences, including potential failures.
> - Practical inference efficiency for complex multimodal VLA models can be achieved through techniques like prefix attention masks and KV caching, enabling real-time robotic control with only marginal performance trade-offs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as the "next-generation paradigm for robotics," aiming to integrate visual perception, language reasoning, and robotic control into coherent systems. The authors delineate the evolution of VLA research into two stages:
1. **First Stage:** Utilized pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) purely as feature extractors, mapping these features directly to robot actions. This approach faced limitations in open-world instruction following and executing long-horizon tasks due to its inability to generate explicit intermediate reasoning.
2. **Second Stage:** Introduced co-training on image-text data alongside action trajectories. This paradigm sought to preserve knowledge from pre-trained VLMs while also predicting sub-step reasoning (often called Chain-of-Thought or CoT) and robotic actions. More recent advancements in this stage have even incorporated image generation capabilities, treating subsequent images as a "visual Chain-of-Thought" to provide more detailed guidance for action prediction.

Despite these advancements, the authors identify several critical limitations in existing VLA models that dVLA aims to address:
- **Gradient Conflicts:** Co-training visual-text data and robotic action data, each with distinct objectives, often leads to interfering gradients. Gradients optimized for knowledge preservation or scene understanding can negatively impact the learning of effective robot actions.
- **Integration Challenges for Image Generation:** Harmonizing multimodal generation (like image generation) and understanding within autoregressive VLMs is difficult due to fundamental differences in their training objectives and architectural designs. This limits the full exploitation of cross-modal knowledge.
- **Limited Physical Law Understanding:** Existing VLAs struggle to fully capture the underlying physical laws that connect actions and generated images, even with explicit Chain-of-Thought mechanisms.

dVLA directly tackles these limitations by introducing a novel approach that unifies these disparate components. It leverages the growing trend of **discrete diffusion language models (DLMs)**, which have shown promise as alternatives to autoregressive models in language modeling and multimodal understanding (e.g., MMaDA, LaViDa). While some prior work has explored discrete diffusion for action prediction in VLAs (e.g., Discrete Diffusion VLA, LLaDA-VLA), dVLA distinguishes itself by:
- **Unifying all modalities under a single diffusion objective:** This aims to resolve the gradient conflicts and architectural gaps by optimizing perception, language understanding, image generation (visual CoT), textual reasoning, and action prediction simultaneously within a shared framework.
- **Introducing multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) with joint generation:** Instead of separately generating reasoning and then actions, dVLA is trained to *simultaneously* generate visual subgoals, textual reasoning, and action sequences by masking tokens across all these modalities. This forces a tighter coupling and consistency.
- **Addressing inference latency for practical deployment:** Recognizing that complex multimodal generation can be slow, dVLA incorporates acceleration strategies like prefix attention masks and KV caching, a crucial step for real-world robotic applications.

In essence, dVLA pushes the frontier of VLA models by moving towards a truly *unified generative model* that not only predicts actions but also explicitly reasons and visualizes future states, all while being optimized for practical efficiency. It represents a significant step towards more robust, generalizable, and physically grounded robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the dVLA research is to develop a unified and highly capable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that seamlessly integrates visual perception, language reasoning, and robotic control. The core motivation stems from the perceived limitations of existing VLA architectures.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Overcome Gradient Conflicts and Architectural Gaps:** Current VLA models often employ separate training objectives or distinct modules for different modalities (e.g., visual-text understanding vs. action prediction, or image generation vs. action control). This "decoupled optimization" leads to gradient conflicts, where improving one aspect can degrade another, and architectural disparities that hinder effective cross-modal learning. dVLA aims to resolve this by jointly optimizing all components under a single, consistent diffusion-based objective.
- **Enhance Cross-Modal Reasoning and Generalization:** By enforcing a unified learning paradigm, dVLA seeks to enable stronger cross-modal reasoning capabilities. This, in turn, is expected to lead to better generalization to novel instructions, unseen objects, and diverse environments, crucial for open-world robotic deployment. The multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is specifically designed to provide explicit, interpretable step-by-step guidance across visual and textual domains, improving the model's ability to translate high-level commands into precise low-level actions.
- **Learn Implicit Physical Laws:** A significant motivation is to move beyond mere action prediction to an understanding of the underlying physical dynamics. The authors motivate this by noting that current models struggle to capture the physical laws connecting actions and observed outcomes. By requiring dVLA to simultaneously generate subgoal images that align with actual execution outcomes (including failures), the model is incentivized to learn a deeper, physically consistent representation of how actions affect the environment.
- **Improve Practical Deployability:** Advanced VLA models, especially those involving multimodal generation, can be computationally intensive, leading to high inference latency. This is a major barrier to real-time robotic control. A key objective is therefore to mitigate this latency through dedicated acceleration strategies (prefix attention mask and KV caching) to make dVLA viable for practical, real-world robotic deployment.
- **Establish a Robust Foundation for VLA Robotics:** Ultimately, the work is motivated by the vision of a "next-generation paradigm for robotics" that can handle complex, long-horizon tasks in diverse settings. By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in both simulation and real-world tasks, dVLA aims to lay a solid foundation for future research and development in unified diffusion-based VLA systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The dVLA framework is built upon a principled approach that unifies vision, language, and action modalities under a single discrete diffusion objective, enhanced by a multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanism and optimized for inference efficiency.

#### 3.1. Unified Probabilistic Formulation for Training

The core innovation lies in a unified discrete strategy and discrete diffusion modeling.
- **Unified Discrete Strategy:** dVLA processes data from all three modalities (vision, text, action) by converting them into discrete tokens.
 - **Vision:** Raw images are encoded into discrete semantic tokens using MAGVIT-v2, known for its high compression ratio and codebook size (e.g., 256 or 1024 tokens for 256x256 or 512x512 images).
 - **Text:** Language instructions and robot states are mapped to discrete tokens using the LLaDA text tokenizer.
 - **Action:** Continuous robot actions are discretized into compact tokens using the FAST tokenizer, which employs Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and Byte Pair Encoding (BPE).
 The vocabulary size is expanded to accommodate tokens from all modalities.
- **Discrete Diffusion Modeling:** The model is trained to reconstruct randomly masked tokens from an input sequence `x = {o, l, s, o_goal, r, a_chunk}` (observations, language, state, visual reasoning/subgoal image, textual reasoning, action chunk). The training objective, `L_unify(θ)`, is defined as a denoising objective, where the model predicts the original ground truth tokens (`x_0`) given a noisy version (`x_t`) by focusing the loss solely on the masked tokens. This single objective ensures consistent optimization across all modalities.

#### 3.2. The dVLA Architecture

The architecture of dVLA initializes from MMaDA, a unified diffusion model recognized for multimodal understanding and generation. It features:
- **Modality-Specific Tokenizers:** As described above, MAGViT-v2 for images, LLaDA's tokenizer for text, and FAST for actions. These tokenizers convert raw inputs into a common discrete token space.
- **Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) Backbone:** The core model is a discrete diffusion MLLM, which takes the concatenated token sequence from all modalities as input. It then iteratively denoises and reconstructs the masked tokens.
- **Decoding:** Dedicated image, text, and action de-tokenizers convert the generated discrete tokens back into their original continuous forms.

#### 3.3. Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning

This is a critical component for task decomposition and robust action generation.
- **CoT Data Structure:** The input sequence to dVLA is structured to include not just observations, instructions, and robot state, but also explicit multimodal CoT tokens (subgoal image and reasoning text), followed by action tokens. The sequence is `[BOS] {images} {text} {state} [BOI]{Subgoal}[EOI]{Reasoning} [BOA]{action}...[EOA] [EOS]`.
- **Joint Generation:** During training, dVLA is mandated to simultaneously generate *subgoal images*, *textual reasoning*, and *action sequences*. This is achieved by randomly masking tokens not only from actions but also from subgoal images and textual reasoning. The model must reconstruct all these masked elements, fostering a shared parameter space and consistency between high-level reasoning (visual and textual) and low-level action execution.
- **Physical Understanding:** This joint generation, particularly the visual CoT, encourages dVLA to learn the implicit physical laws governing actions, as it must predict future visual states that align with actual (even failed) execution outcomes.

#### 3.4. Acceleration Strategies

To ensure dVLA's practical utility, two strategies mitigate inference latency:
- **Prefix Attention Mask:** A block-wise causal attention mask is applied during training. The input tokens (observations, language, state) form one block, and the generated tokens (subgoal image, reasoning text, action) form another. Full bidirectional attention operates within each block, but tokens in the second block are restricted from attending to subsequent tokens. This partial causality allows for partial KV caching.
- **KV Caching:** dVLA incorporates dLLM-Cache, a training-free technique that caches intermediate Key-Value features during iterative denoising steps. By refreshing these cached results at a lower frequency, computational overhead is reduced without significant performance degradation, leading to faster inference.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Robot Setup:** Evaluated in both simulation (LIBERO benchmark, covering spatial, object, goal, and long-horizon tasks) and real-world scenarios using a 7-DoF Franka robot arm equipped with multiple cameras. Real-world tasks included Bin Picking (challenging multi-step planning and cluttered grasping), Open Box, Hang Cups (requiring precise alignment), and Pick & Place Object (language-conditioned).
- **Baselines:** Comprehensive comparison against state-of-the-art continuous action policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, GR00T-N1, Octo, π0, OpenVLA-OFT (Continuous)) and discrete action policies (e.g., OpenVLA, CoTVLA, WorldVLA, Discrete Diffusion VLA). An ablation "vanilla dVLA" (without explicit multimodal CoT) was also used.
- **Datasets:** LIBERO simulation dataset (regenerated at 256x256 resolution) and 1100 human-teleoperated real-world trajectories collected across the four tasks.
- **Training Details:** Finetuning utilized the MMaDA pipeline. Images were resized to 256x256. Visual CoT predicted subgoal images at future timesteps (0.9-1.1 times action chunk length) using classifier-free guidance. Textual CoT for long-horizon tasks leveraged SEED-1.5VL annotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of dVLA demonstrates its superior performance across a range of tasks in both simulation and the real world, validating the effectiveness of its unified diffusion architecture and multimodal Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance in Simulation (LIBERO Benchmark)

- dVLA achieved an outstanding **96.4% average success rate** across the four LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), setting a new state-of-the-art.
- It consistently outperformed all continuous action policies, including OpenVLA (Cont-Diffusion) by 1.0%, GR00T-N1 by 2.5%, and π0 by 2.2%.
- Among discrete action policies, dVLA surpassed WorldVLA by 14.6%, CoTVLA by 27.4%, and marginally exceeded Discrete Diffusion VLA by 0.1%. These results underscore the benefits derived from dVLA's unified training objective and model architecture.

#### 4.2. Robust Performance on Real-World Tasks

- In the real-world evaluation on a Franka robot across four diverse tasks (Bin Picking, Open Box, Hang Cups, Pick&Place Object), dVLA achieved the highest **average success rate of 65%**.
- This significantly outscored continuous baselines like GR00T (47.5% average) and Diffusion Policy (35% average), as well as discrete baselines like OpenVLA (35% average).
- dVLA demonstrated particular strength in the challenging **Bin Picking task**, achieving a 7/10 success rate, where cluttered scenarios typically pose significant challenges for precise grasping. This highlights its ability to handle complex, multi-step planning in real-world environments.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning

- An ablation study comparing dVLA with and without explicit multimodal CoT revealed the significant impact of the CoT mechanism.
- "Vanilla dVLA" (without explicit CoT) already performed commendably, achieving an 89.8% average success rate in LIBERO and 52.5% in real-world tasks, outperforming many baselines.
- However, integrating multimodal CoT reasoning further **improved dVLA's average success rate by 6.6 percentage points in LIBERO (to 96.4%) and by 12.5 percentage points in real-world tasks (to 65%)**.
- Empirical observations showed that CoT was particularly beneficial in tasks like bin-picking, where it enabled more precise grasping poses by generating explicit visual subgoals and textual reasoning, effectively mitigating multi-object interference.

#### 4.4. Prediction of Unsafe Actions via Multimodal CoT

- A profound finding was dVLA's ability to generate visual CoT that **accurately predicted future images of *failed* executions and unsafe behaviors**. For example, it could foresee an object getting stuck or the robot moving in the wrong direction.
- This suggests that dVLA, through its unified discrete diffusion training strategy, learns not just to generate successful subgoals but also to implicitly capture the underlying physical laws and consequences of actions, including negative outcomes. This indicates a deeper, more consistent understanding of the action-perception loop.

#### 4.5. Impact of Acceleration Strategies

- The implemented acceleration strategies—prefix attention mask and KV caching—were highly effective in improving inference efficiency.
- Inference speed was significantly boosted from approximately **1.3-1.5 Hz to 2.9-3 Hz (nearly 2x speedup)** on both LIBERO and real-world bin-picking tasks.
- Crucially, this substantial speedup came with **only marginal performance degradation** (e.g., a drop of 0.5% in LIBERO Spatial SR and 0.6% in Object SR, and a slight drop in one real-world task while maintaining performance in others). This demonstrates that dVLA can operate efficiently in real-time robotic settings without sacrificing overall accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The dVLA framework represents a significant advancement in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics, with several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

- **Unified and Principled Architecture:** dVLA offers a conceptually elegant and technically robust solution to long-standing challenges in VLA research, particularly the "gradient conflicts" and "architectural gaps" inherent in multi-objective or decoupled training paradigms. By unifying visual perception, language reasoning, and robotic control under a single discrete diffusion objective, it establishes a more coherent and consistent learning framework. This unification is a crucial step towards building truly general-purpose robotic agents.

- **Enhanced Reasoning and Generalizability:** The introduction of a truly multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT), where visual subgoals, textual reasoning, and actions are jointly generated and optimized, significantly boosts the model's ability to perform complex, step-by-step reasoning. This leads to:
 - **Improved Task Decomposition:** High-level instructions are more effectively broken down into manageable subtasks.
 - **Better Generalization:** The model can adapt more effectively to novel objects, environments, and instructions, moving beyond rote memorization of demonstrations.
 - **Greater Interpretability:** Explicit visual and textual reasoning steps make the model's decision-making process more transparent, which is vital for debugging, trust, and human-robot collaboration.

- **Deeper Physical Understanding and Safety Implications:** The surprising finding that dVLA can accurately predict future images of *failed* or *unsafe* executions is profoundly significant. This indicates that the model learns an implicit, physically grounded understanding of how actions interact with the environment. Such a capability has immense potential for:
 - **Proactive Safety:** Robots could foresee and avoid dangerous situations before executing unsafe actions.
 - **Error Recovery:** An understanding of failure modes could enable more intelligent error detection and recovery strategies.
 - **Robustness:** A model that understands the consequences of its actions, both good and bad, is inherently more robust and reliable.

- **Practical Real-World Deployability:** Addressing the critical issue of inference latency with effective acceleration strategies (prefix attention mask and KV caching) is a major contribution. Achieving a ~2x speedup with minimal performance degradation transforms dVLA from a theoretical concept into a practical tool for real-time robotic control. This makes it viable for deployment in industrial settings, service robotics, and other applications where immediate response is crucial.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** dVLA lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
 - **Scaling Unified DLMs:** The success of dVLA encourages further exploration into scaling diffusion-based multimodal models for even more complex and diverse robotic tasks.
 - **Leveraging Physical Understanding:** Future work can delve deeper into how this implicit physical understanding can be explicitly leveraged for planning, exploration, and learning in novel environments.
 - **Advanced Multimodal CoT:** Investigating richer forms of multimodal reasoning, perhaps incorporating other sensory modalities or more complex hierarchical planning, could further enhance capabilities.
 - **Transfer Learning across Embodiments:** A truly unified VLA model has the potential for more effective knowledge transfer across different robot platforms.

Given the industrial affiliation of the lead authors (Midea Group), the practical implications for automated manufacturing, smart home devices, and general service robotics are clear. dVLA offers a compelling paradigm shift that could accelerate the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and safe robotic systems for the real world.
