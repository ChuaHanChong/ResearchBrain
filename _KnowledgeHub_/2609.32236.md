# RoboFFT: Finetuning generative robot policy via online reinforcement learning with forward process

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboFFT is an online RL framework that finetunes diffusion, flow, and VLA robot policies through the forward noising process. It re-noises sampled actions and uses the weighted score/flow matching loss as a surrogate likelihood ratio for PPO-style clipped updates, with SDE sampling for exploration. It matches or beats reverse-process baselines on Robomimic, trains 38x faster than DPPO, scales to π0 on LIBERO, and raises real-robot success.

## Problem

- Imitation-trained diffusion and flow policies are capped by demonstration quality and coverage, suffering from imperfect demos and covariate shift unless more expert data is collected.
- Diffusion and flow policies lack tractable exact likelihoods, so policy-gradient objectives like PPO, which need likelihood ratios on rollout actions, cannot be applied directly.
- Reverse-process methods like DPPO and ReinFlow treat denoising as an inner MDP, adding compute and memory cost and gradient instability over long chains; forward-process FPO targets mainly locomotion.

## Method

- ==Forward-process surrogate ratio==: rollout actions are re-noised along the pretraining path, and the exponentiated difference of old and new ==weighted matching losses== replaces the ==PPO likelihood ratio==, justified via ==ELBO==.
- ==Euler-Maruyama SDE sampling== injects fixed-strength noise into flow-policy ODE sampling, used only for exploratory rollout collection, not for likelihood computation as in reverse-process methods.
- Stabilizers: ==Monte Carlo loss estimation== sharing noise levels and perturbations across old and new policies, plus a ==ratio temperature== recalibrating loss scale; an offline-to-online ==IQL== variant enables real-robot deployment.

## Results

- On Robomimic Lift, Can, Square and Transport with state and pixel inputs, RoboFFT matches or beats DPPO, ReinFlow, FPO and DRWR, with largest gains on pixel Square and Transport.
- Per-iteration training is **1.53x** faster than ReinFlow for flow policies and **38.0x** faster than DPPO for diffusion policies on state tasks, despite using **16** Monte Carlo samples.
- Real robot: pick eggplant **12/20** to **20/20**, cabinet retrieval **5/20** to **17/20**, while BC aggregation reaches only **14/20**; finetuning **3.3B** π0 improves LIBERO-Object and Spatial, competitive with πRL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reusing the pretraining matching loss as a surrogate likelihood aligns RL with imitation objectives and avoids backpropagating through sampling chains, a cheap recipe transferable to any diffusion or flow policy.
> - The surrogate need not rank actions like true likelihood: Spearman correlation stayed near 0.2 yet updates worked, suggesting PPO clipping tolerates coarse ratio estimates when relative errors stay small.
> - Exploration noise is a sharp knob: too little collapses the policy, too much degrades it; RL mainly concentrates action distributions at decision-critical states rather than broadly exploring.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

RoboFFT is a method for improving diffusion- and flow-based robot policies with reward feedback. A robot can learn a useful action policy from demonstrations, but policies trained purely by imitation can be limited by imperfect demonstrations and by distribution shifts in the states met during deployment. Learning from interaction offers another way to improve. Diffusion and flow policies are expressive, but their sampled action distributions make the likelihood ratios used by standard PPO difficult to compute.

RoboFFT gets around this by reusing the task these policies already train on: add noise to an action, then learn to predict how to undo it. Comparing how well the old and updated policies perform that task gives a stand-in for the missing ratio. With this approach, RoboFFT is competitive on easier manipulation tasks. It shows stronger, more sustained improvement on the harder Square and Transport tasks, especially with pixel observations.

### 2. Reusing the training signal

The pipeline runs in a fixed order:

1. A policy is pretrained on demonstrations by adding noise to actions and learning a score or velocity prediction.
1. The policy samples actions, and the environment returns rewards.
1. Each sampled action is forward-noised.
1. The same weighted matching loss is evaluated under both the current policy and the rollout policy.
1. The old-minus-current loss difference serves as a surrogate policy ratio in a PPO-style clipped update, weighted by estimated advantage.

The method thus avoids optimizing likelihoods through the full reverse sampling chain. Reverse-process methods, by contrast, optimize along the internal denoising chain. For flow policies, Gaussian noise is added to the sampling path during rollout collection to provide controllable exploration.

Three measures support stability. The matching loss is estimated with Monte Carlo samples. The same noise samples are reused when comparing the old and current policies. A temperature calibrates the scale of the ratio. PPO clipping limits the update, but it does not make the surrogate an exact likelihood.

The idea has a close predecessor. [[2507.21053|Flow Matching Policy Gradients (FPO)]] already builds a PPO-style ratio from differences in flow-matching losses. The paper positions RoboFFT as an extension to difficult robot manipulation, diffusion as well as flow policies, VLA finetuning, and real-robot deployment.

### 3. What the evidence shows

The Robomimic benchmarks measure success rate, defined as the fraction of parallel evaluation rollouts that complete the task. On the easier Lift and Can tasks, RoboFFT reaches final performance comparable to reverse-process methods. Its advantage appears on the harder tasks. Square is precision-sensitive nut-on-peg assembly, and Transport is long-horizon, coordinated bimanual object movement.

Against FPO, RoboFFT-F performs better on the harder tasks overall. The implementations differ, however. RoboFFT adds stochastic SDE rollout sampling and ratio-temperature calibration, while the adapted FPO baseline omits both and uses eight Monte Carlo samples. The comparison therefore does not isolate which addition caused the difference.

The surrogate also has a measured limit. For RoboFFT-F on Square-state, the surrogate was compared with a ReinFlow-style closed-form reference. Spearman rank correlation stayed around 0.2, even as relative errors fell below 5% for at least 90% of samples near the end of training. The lower numerical error does not establish strong ranking agreement or exact likelihood equivalence.

### 4. On a real robot

Real deployment uses offline-to-online adaptation. Humans evaluate sparse binary rewards, and IQL-based advantages drive the offline updates. Further robot rollouts are then added, the imitation training is refreshed with demonstrations, and online PPO-style updates follow.

On Pick Eggplant, the strategies were compared at the same data budget, according to the authors:

StrategySuccesses out of 20Pretrained policy12Behavior-cloning aggregation14DRWR12Offline RoboFFT18Online RoboFFT20 (matching RL100)

This comparison supports a gain beyond simple behavior-cloning data aggregation in this setting. It does not prove that every difference between methods is due to RL alone.

RoboFFT's contribution is a practical route to reward-driven updates for demonstration-trained diffusion and flow policies. It reuses the forward-noising matching objective instead of computing likelihoods through the full reverse chain. The main boundary is that the matching loss remains a surrogate, and its scale can vary by schedule, action dimension and architecture. In addition, on-policy PPO cannot fully reuse historical data.
