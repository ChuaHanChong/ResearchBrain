# Perception-R1: Advancing Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities of MLLMs via Visual Perception Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> Perception-R1 enhances the multimodal reasoning abilities of Multimodal Large Language Models by explicitly integrating a novel visual perception reward into the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards framework. This approach yields new state-of-the-art performance on various multimodal benchmarks while achieving remarkable data efficiency, using only 1,442 training samples.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for MLLMs primarily focus on logical reasoning and neglect the foundational aspect of multimodal perception.
- MLLMs trained with accuracy-only RLVR can "guess" correct answers without genuinely understanding the visual context, leading to brittle reasoning.
- Reward sparsity in accuracy-only RLVR training makes it difficult for MLLMs to learn and correct underlying visual perception flaws.

## Method

- Perception-R1 introduces a ==visual perception reward== to explicitly guide MLLMs towards improved multimodal perception during RLVR training.
- High-quality =="visual annotations"== are curated from powerful proprietary MLLM Chain-of-Thought trajectories, serving as ground-truth references for critical visual elements.
- A =="judging LLM"== assesses the consistency between the MLLM's generated response and these visual annotations, contributing to an enhanced reward function that includes format, accuracy, visual perception, and repetition penalty components.

## Results

- Achieves new state-of-the-art performance across numerous multimodal reasoning benchmarks, including MathVista, MathVerse, MMMU, and MMStar.
- Demonstrates statistically significant improvements in multimodal perception capabilities, particularly on vision-only subsets of MathVerse and MMMU-Pro, validating the effectiveness of the visual perception reward.
- Obtains SOTA results using an exceptionally small dataset of only **1,442** training samples, showcasing remarkable data efficiency compared to previous methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal perception is a critical bottleneck for robust MLLM reasoning, often overlooked by existing accuracy-only RLVR methods.
> - Explicitly rewarding accurate visual perception, grounded in verifiable annotations, significantly improves MLLM reasoning capabilities and robustness.
> - Intelligent reward design, by incorporating richer, perception-focused signals, can dramatically enhance data efficiency in MLLM development.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and the enhancement of their reasoning capabilities represent a significant area of research aimed at advancing Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Initial efforts in MLLM reasoning focused on techniques such as prompt engineering and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using high-quality reasoning datasets. More recently, the success of large-scale reinforcement learning (RL), particularly Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), has drawn considerable attention in both Large Language Models (LLMs) and MLLMs. Models like OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, and Kimi-k1.5 have demonstrated the efficacy of RLVR in improving advanced reasoning.

In the multimodal domain, several studies have adapted RLVR to MLLMs, aiming to boost their reasoning performance. Works such as MM-Eureka, R1-VL, R1-OneVision, and Vision-R1 have successfully applied RLVR, often incorporating specialized training mechanisms or large-scale dataset construction, to achieve substantial improvements in multimodal reasoning capabilities. These approaches commonly optimize MLLMs based primarily on the correctness of the final answer, which serves as the verifiable reward signal.

However, the current work identifies a gap in this landscape: while existing RLVR methods have shown improvements in logical reasoning, they largely overlook the enhancement of multimodal perception capabilities in MLLMs. Multimodal perception, which involves accurately interpreting visual content to derive essential information, is posited as a fundamental prerequisite for complex multimodal reasoning. The paper argues that this oversight constitutes a bottleneck, limiting further advancements in MLLM reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the multimodal reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. The authors identify a specific limitation in current RLVR-based approaches: these methods, which typically assign rewards solely based on the correctness of the final answer (referred to as "accuracy-only RLVR"), fail to effectively improve the multimodal perception capabilities of MLLMs. This deficiency in perception is identified as a major bottleneck that restricts the overall advancement in multimodal reasoning.

The motivation for this work stems from two key observations:
1. **Empirical Evidence of Perception Errors:** Through detailed analysis of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories from existing MLLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B-IT and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-IT), the authors found that a substantial proportion of incorrect problem-solving cases (72%-78% on MathVista and MathVerse) were attributable to multimodal perception errors. These errors often involve misinterpreting or misidentifying visual elements crucial for problem-solving.
2. **Lack of Perception Improvement via Existing RLVR:** A statistical analysis using McNemar’s test was conducted to compare the multimodal perception capabilities of base MLLMs against their accuracy-only RLVR-trained counterparts. For both Qwen2-VL-7B-IT and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-IT, the p-values (0.22 and 0.69, respectively) were found to be significantly above the 0.05 significance level. This indicated that existing RLVR methods did not lead to a statistically significant improvement in the multimodal perception abilities of MLLMs. Critically, some models even managed to guess correct answers despite exhibiting severe perception errors, suggesting that answer-accuracy alone does not necessarily incentivize accurate visual understanding.

The authors attribute this limitation to "reward sparsity for multimodal perception" in accuracy-only RLVR. Since a correct final answer does not guarantee accurate intermediate visual perception, the reward signal does not sufficiently guide the MLLM to correct perception errors. To address this, the paper proposes to introduce a novel visual perception reward that explicitly encourages MLLMs to accurately perceive visual content, thereby alleviating reward sparsity and strengthening the perceptual foundation for multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Perception-R1 is built upon the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework but introduces a novel visual perception reward to overcome the identified limitations of accuracy-only RLVR.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Baseline RLVR

The task is defined as multimodal reasoning, where an MLLM receives a visual input (V), a textual query (Q), and aims to generate a textual token sequence (y) that matches the ground-truth answer (a).
The standard RLVR, as adopted by prior works, utilizes a reward function comprising:
- **Format Reward ($r_f$):** Encourages generation in a structured "think-then-answer" format.
- **Accuracy Reward ($r_a$):** Evaluates the correctness of the final answer, driving reasoning optimization.
These rewards are typically combined as $r(y_i, a) = \alpha \cdot r_f(y_i) + \beta \cdot r_a(y_i, a)$. The policy model is optimized using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, a variant of PPO that estimates baseline rewards from groups of rollouts without requiring a separate critic.

#### 3.2. Addressing Multimodal Perception Limitations with Perception-R1

Perception-R1 extends the RLVR framework by introducing a visual perception reward ($r_v$) and a repetition penalty ($r_p$) into the reward function. The core idea is to provide an additional reward signal that explicitly guides MLLMs to perceive visual content accurately.

##### 3.2.1. Curation of Visual Annotations

To assign a visual perception reward, a reference for accurate visual understanding is required. Instead of focusing on generating comprehensive image captions, the approach prioritizes visual information relevant to problem-solving.
- **CoT Trajectory Collection:** State-of-the-art proprietary MLLMs (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro) are used to generate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories on a multimodal reasoning dataset. Only trajectories with correct final answers are retained.
- **Visual Annotation Extraction:** A strong text-only LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B-IT) is then prompted to extract natural language visual annotations from these CoT trajectories. These annotations (V = ($v_1, v_2..., v_m$)) represent atomic, critical visual facts (e.g., "GE = 10", "GE ⊥ DF"). These serve as ground-truth references for evaluating visual perception. Manual examination indicated a 96% accuracy rate for these extracted annotations. The Geometry3K dataset was specifically chosen for training due to its high proportion of extractable visual information (82%).

##### 3.2.2. Visual Perception Reward ($r_v$)

During RLVR training, the MLLM generates a response ($y_i$). A judging LLM ($\Phi$, e.g., Qwen2.5-32B-IT) is employed to assess the consistency between the MLLM's response and each atomic visual annotation in the reference set V.
- **Judgment Process:** For each annotation $v_j \in V$, the judging LLM determines if $v_j$ is accurately reflected in $y_i$, outputting a binary judgment $o_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\}$.
- **Reward Calculation:** The visual perception reward $r_v(y_i, V)$ is calculated as the sum of positive judgments divided by the total number of visual annotations in V.

##### 3.2.3. Visual-Enhanced Reward Function

The final reward function for Perception-R1 aggregates all components:
$r(y_i, a, V) = \alpha \cdot r_f(y_i) + \beta \cdot r_a(y_i, a) + \gamma \cdot r_v(y_i, V) + r_p(y_i)$
- $\gamma$ is a coefficient controlling the impact of the visual perception reward.
- $r_p(y_i)$ is a repetition penalty, implemented using an N-gram repetition penalty, introduced to mitigate increased repetitiveness observed when $r_v$ is directly incorporated.

The MLLM is then trained using GRPO to maximize this visual-enhanced reward function, with the KL penalty term often removed (δ=0) as in prior works for better performance.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Training Data:** Geometry3K dataset, comprising 1,442 samples with associated visual annotations.
- **Base Models:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-IT (primary) and Qwen2-VL-7B-IT (for generalization tests).
- **Judging LLM:** Qwen2.5-32B-IT.
- **Hyperparameters:** Learning rate 1e-6, warmup ratio 0.05, 25 epochs, global batch size 128. Reward coefficients: $\alpha=0.1$, $\beta=0.9$, $\gamma=0.7$.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** A diverse set of 8 multimodal benchmarks: MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, WeMath (math domains); MMMU, MMMU-Pro, MMStar, EMMA (general domains).
- **Inference:** vLLM with greedy decoding (temperature 0.0).
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set including proprietary models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, OpenAI-o1), open-source general MLLMs (Qwen2/2.5-VL-7B/72B-IT, InternVL2.5-8B), and open-source reasoning MLLMs (URSA-7B, R1-VL-7B, R1-OneVision-7B, OpenVLThinker-7B, VLAA-Thinker-7B, SophiaVL-R1-7B, MM-Eureka-7B, Vision-R1-7B).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated the effectiveness and robustness of Perception-R1 across multiple benchmarks, particularly highlighting improvements in multimodal perception and data efficiency.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

Perception-R1-7B consistently achieved superior performance compared to a range of powerful baselines, including both general and reasoning-focused MLLMs, across most of the 8 multimodal benchmarks.
- **Math Benchmarks:** Perception-R1-7B achieved 74.2% on MathVista, 54.3% on MathVerse, 28.6% on MathVision, and 72.0% on WeMath. These scores generally surpassed the best open-source reasoning MLLMs, such as Vision-R1-7B (e.g., 73.1% on MathVista, 52.4% on MathVerse).
- **General Benchmarks:** On MMMU (60.8%), MMMU-Pro (42.4%), and MMStar (64.5%), Perception-R1-7B also achieved leading performance among open-source models. It trailed slightly on EMMA (27.5%).
- **Statistical Significance:** A one-sample t-test indicated that the average performance improvement of Perception-R1 was statistically significant (p < 0.01) when compared to Vision-R1-7B and MM-Eureka-7B.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Perception Capabilities

Perception-R1 demonstrated tangible improvements in multimodal perception:
- **Vision-Only Subsets:** On the "Vision-Only" subsets of MathVerse and MMMU-Pro, which rigorously challenge perception, Perception-R1-7B achieved 50.1% and 40.3% respectively. These results significantly outperformed standard GRPO and prior state-of-the-art methods (e.g., Vision-R1-7B achieved 47.0% on MathVerse vision-only).
- **McNemar's Test:** A McNemar’s test on 50 MathVista problems revealed that the multimodal perception capabilities of Perception-R1 were statistically significantly improved (p = 0.04) compared to the original MLLM, contrasting with the non-significant improvement observed for accuracy-only RLVR.

#### 4.3. Data Efficiency

Perception-R1 demonstrated high data efficiency, achieving its performance using only 1,442 training samples. This contrasts with methods like Vision-R1, which used 200K data samples, and MM-Eureka, which used 15K. This finding suggests that incorporating richer reward signals beyond the final answer can substantially improve data efficiency in MLLM training.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Results

- **Component Effectiveness:** Removing either the visual perception reward or the repetition penalty led to declines in performance across benchmarks, indicating the necessity of both components.
- **Comparison to Alternatives:**
 - Directly using a powerful MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-32B-IT) as a reward model did not yield better performance than Perception-R1, attributed to reward hacking issues.
 - Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the same 1,442 CoT trajectories resulted in inferior performance, even falling below that of the base model on some benchmarks, highlighting the superior generalization and data efficiency of Perception-R1's RL approach.
- **Visual Perception Reward Impact:** Ablations consistently showed that methods incorporating the visual perception reward outperformed accuracy-only RLVR on "Vision Only" subsets, further validating its role in enhancing multimodal perception.

#### 4.5. Further Analysis of Visual Perception Reward

- **Coefficient $\gamma$:** Models trained with varying $\gamma$ values (0.1 to 0.9) achieved comparable performance, all significantly outperforming the model trained without the visual perception reward ($\gamma=0.0$). This suggests that even a small visual optimization signal is effective, and GRPO's normalization process may balance the impact of different reward magnitudes.
- **Judging LLM Capability:** The capability of the judging LLM significantly influenced performance. Weaker judging LLMs led to performance degradation and quicker saturation of the visual perception reward, indicating reward hacking. Stronger judging LLMs were crucial for effective training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PERCEPTION-R1 framework presents a significant advancement in enhancing the multimodal reasoning capabilities of MLLMs by explicitly addressing a previously overlooked bottleneck: multimodal perception.

**Key Significance:**
- **Addressing Multimodal Perception Deficiencies:** The research empirically demonstrates that existing accuracy-only RLVR methods fail to substantially improve MLLM perception. Perception-R1 provides a targeted solution by incorporating a visual perception reward, leading to statistically significant improvements in accurate visual understanding.
- **Richer Reward Signals for RLVR:** This work highlights the importance of incorporating richer, more granular reward signals beyond just final answer correctness in RLVR training for MLLMs. By breaking down reasoning into perception and logic, it allows for more precise guidance during learning.
- **Exceptional Data Efficiency:** Perception-R1 achieves leading performance with a remarkably small training dataset (1,442 samples), orders of magnitude smaller than many competitive approaches. This suggests a paradigm shift toward more data-efficient RL for complex multimodal tasks, potentially reducing computational costs and data annotation burdens.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The method's effectiveness across diverse math and general benchmarks, and its successful application to a different base model (Qwen2-VL-7B-IT) and dataset (Mulberry), indicate its robustness and broad applicability.
- **Guidance for Future MLLM Development:** The findings underscore the critical role of robust multimodal perception as a foundational component for advanced reasoning. Future research on MLLMs and their reasoning capabilities may benefit from adopting perception-oriented enhancements and moving beyond solely outcome-based evaluations.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved MLLM Performance in Real-World Tasks:** Enhanced perception and reasoning could lead to more reliable and capable MLLMs for applications requiring complex visual understanding, such as scientific discovery, medical diagnosis assistance, advanced robotics, and educational tools involving diagrams and visual problem-solving.
- **Reduced Training Costs:** The high data efficiency of Perception-R1 could democratize access to advanced MLLM development by lowering the data and computational resources required for training, enabling more researchers and organizations to contribute.
- **New Directions for Multimodal RL:** The work opens avenues for exploring other fine-grained reward signals related to intermediate reasoning steps or specific multimodal understanding aspects, moving towards more interpretable and controllable MLLM learning.
- **Ethical Considerations:** As MLLMs become more capable in understanding and reasoning with multimodal information, their deployment necessitates careful consideration of ethical implications, including potential biases in visual interpretation, misuse in critical applications, and the need for explainability and trustworthiness. The explicit focus on accurate perception may contribute to more transparent and explainable reasoning processes.
