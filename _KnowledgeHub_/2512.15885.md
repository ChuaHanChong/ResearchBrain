# Seeing Beyond Words: Self-Supervised Visual Learning for Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> JARVIS integrates a JEPA-inspired self-supervised visual learning objective into Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), enabling them to acquire fine-grained visual perception and reasoning beyond textual descriptions. The framework achieves consistent improvements on vision-centric benchmarks across various LLM families, including a 0.8 to 1.6 average point gain on visual tasks, while maintaining performance on general multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs often rely too heavily on subjective and incomplete textual descriptions for visual understanding, leading to an inability to grasp nuanced visual details.
- MLLMs frequently exhibit poor performance in fundamental visual reasoning tasks such as object counting, precise spatial relationship understanding, and distance estimation.
- Models tend to overfit strong language priors from their pre-training, which can result in visual 'hallucinations' or interpretations based on linguistic association rather than actual visual evidence.

## Method

- JARVIS introduces a ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)==-inspired masked predictive objective during the first (alignment) stage of ==LLaVA-style MLLM== training.
- It leverages early layers of the LLM as a ==predictor network== to reconstruct masked visual embeddings, derived from an unbiased target visual encoder (e.g., DINOv2), from visible context embeddings.
- The approach combines this self-supervised visual loss (
L
JEPA
) with a modified next-token prediction objective for image captioning, ensuring a concurrent focus on both intrinsic visual understanding and text-image alignment.

## Results

- JARVIS consistently improved vision-centric task performance across different LLM backbones, achieving an average gain of **0.8 points** for Vicuna-7B and **0.7 points** for Qwen2-7B compared to the LLaVA baseline.
- The method maintained or improved performance on general, knowledge, and OCR tasks, demonstrating no trade-off in broader multimodal reasoning abilities.
- Qualitative examples illustrated JARVIS's superior performance in detailed visual tasks like object counting, spatial reasoning, and depth estimation over LLaVA and other related methods such as VIRAL, which did not improve vision-centric tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a purely visual, self-supervised learning signal, like the JEPA objective, is crucial for MLLMs to develop robust visual grounding independent of linguistic supervision.
> - The early-to-middle layers of the Large Language Model are more effective for processing and predicting fine-grained visual information, making them ideal for the JEPA task.
> - Using negative cosine similarity as the distance function in the JEPA loss and periodically training on unmasked images (with a dropout rate
λ) are critical for balancing effective visual learning with general multimodal capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which have emerged as a natural extension of Large Language Models (LLMs) to process and reason across various modalities, particularly images and text.

The broader landscape of MLLMs began with pioneering works like Flamingo, BLIP-2, and LLaVA. **LLaVA (Large Language and Vision Assistant)**, in particular, established a widely adopted two-stage training recipe: first, a lightweight projector aligns image representations from a frozen vision encoder with the textual embedding space of a frozen LLM using image-caption pairs; second, visual instruction tuning fine-tunes the entire model (projector and LLM) on multimodal dialogues. This approach, while highly effective for general multimodal tasks, serves as the foundational baseline against which "Seeing Beyond Words" positions itself.

Despite the impressive progress, the authors identify a significant limitation: **current MLLMs often exhibit poor performance in fundamental visual reasoning tasks**, such as counting objects, understanding precise spatial relationships, or estimating distances. This deficiency arises because MLLMs primarily learn visual understanding through textual descriptions, which are inherently subjective, incomplete, and can lead to models overfitting language priors rather than focusing on visual details. Essentially, MLLMs "see" images through the lens of human language, which often overlooks nuanced visual information. Recent studies cited in the paper (e.g.,) corroborate this observation, showing that MLLMs can over-rely on textual cues, leading to visual "blind spots" or hallucinations.

To address this, the paper advocates for **self-supervised visual learning (SSL)**, a paradigm that allows models to learn rich, intrinsic visual representations directly from image data without explicit human annotations. This places the work within a growing trend of integrating SSL techniques, initially successful in computer vision, into the multimodal domain to enhance MLLM capabilities.

The novelty of JARVIS lies in its specific integration of the **Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)** paradigm, particularly **I-JEPA** (Image-JEPA), into the LLaVA-style MLLM training. JEPAs, championed by Yann LeCun, are self-supervised methods that learn by predicting latent representations of compatible parts of an input signal. While previous works like ROSS explored image reconstruction for MLLMs, and VIRAL aligned intermediate LLM activations with external vision models, JARVIS is presented as the **first to integrate an I-JEPA-like objective directly into the LLaVA pre-training stage using a masked predictive loss**. This is a crucial distinction: instead of merely aligning representations or reconstructing pixels, JARVIS trains the LLM to *predict masked portions of visual embeddings* from visible context, forcing it to learn structural and semantic regularities within images themselves, independent of linguistic supervision. This deep integration aims to fundamentally change *how* the LLM perceives images at an early stage of its multimodal learning.

By doing so, this work contributes to the broader goal of building more robust, visually grounded MLLMs that can reason effectively about the physical world, moving beyond purely language-driven interpretations. It suggests a pathway to mitigate the "blind faith in text" often observed in current MLLMs and fosters a deeper, more intrinsic visual understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **significantly enhance the fundamental visual perception and reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**. The authors aim to enable MLLMs to "see beyond words," acquiring a more robust and fine-grained understanding of visual inputs directly from images rather than relying solely on textual descriptions.

This primary objective is motivated by several critical limitations identified in the current state-of-the-art MLLMs:

1. **Reliance on Subjective and Incomplete Textual Descriptions**: MLLMs primarily learn to understand images by generating or aligning with textual captions. However, image captions are inherently subjective and can never fully capture the exhaustive wealth of information present in an image. Annotators often omit details considered irrelevant, or it is simply not feasible to provide every possible description. Consequently, MLLMs trained this way may overlook crucial visual details that are not explicitly mentioned in captions.
2. **Overfitting to Language Priors**: Due to the massive scale of unsupervised pre-training on text corpora, LLMs develop strong language priors. When multimodal training is introduced, which is typically of a more modest scale, MLLMs tend to over-rely on these language priors. This can lead to situations where the model "hallucinates" or infers visual properties based on common linguistic associations rather than actual visual evidence, thus overlooking visual details present in the image.
3. **Limited Proficiency in Basic Visual Reasoning Tasks**: Despite their impressive multimodal capabilities, current MLLMs often struggle with seemingly simple yet crucial visual reasoning tasks. These include accurate object counting, precise understanding of spatial relationships between objects, and estimating relative distances. Such limitations highlight a severe deficit in their core visual perception skills.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes **JARVIS (JEPA-inspired framework for self-supervised visual enhancement in MLLMs)**. The core idea is to introduce a purely visual, self-supervised learning signal during the early alignment stage of MLLM training, inspired by the principles of Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), specifically I-JEPA. By integrating this objective, the authors aim to teach the MLLM to discover and understand visual inputs intrinsically, beyond the constraints and biases of textual descriptions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The JARVIS framework is built upon and integrated into the widely adopted two-stage training paradigm of LLaVA, aiming to enhance the visual perception of MLLMs without disrupting their core architecture or general multimodal reasoning abilities.

#### 3.1. Background: The LLaVA Framework

The baseline MLLM architecture, following LLaVA, consists of:
- A pre-trained, frozen **visual encoder** ($F_v$) (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14@336) to process images into a sequence of d-dimensional visual embeddings.
- A trainable **projector** (`proj`) (e.g., a two-layer MLP) that aligns the visual embeddings from $F_v$ with the input embedding space of the LLM.
- A pre-trained **Large Language Model (LLM)** ($G$) that serves as the reasoning backbone.

LLaVA's training proceeds in two stages:
1. **Alignment Stage**: The LLM ($G$) is kept frozen, and only the `proj` is trained. The objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood ($L_{NTP}$) of generating an image caption ($x$) given the image ($I$), i.e., $P(x_i | I, x_{1...,i-1}; G, proj)$. This teaches the projector to map visual features into a language-interpretable space.
2. **Visual Instruction Tuning Stage**: Both the LLM ($G$) and the `proj` are unfrozen and trained to follow multi-turn user instructions involving images, again using an NTP objective.

#### 3.2. JARVIS: Integrating JEPA into MLLMs

JARVIS intervenes specifically in the **first (alignment) stage** of LLaVA training by introducing an additional, purely visual self-supervised learning objective inspired by I-JEPA.

- **Revisiting I-JEPA for Images**:
 - The goal of I-JEPA is to learn visual representations by predicting the latent representation of masked-out "target" parts of an image from "context" parts of the same image.
 - Unlike pixel-level masking, JARVIS performs masking at the **visual embedding level**.
 - A **context block ($M_{ctx}$)** of image patches is sampled, covering 85-100% of the image. Its embeddings ($I_{ctx}$) are extracted by a frozen visual encoder ($F_{ctx_v}$, which is the standard $F_v$ from LLaVA).
 - **Multiple target blocks ($M_{tgt}$) (k=4)** are sampled, covering smaller regions. The key is that these are *also* extracted by a separate, additional frozen **target visual encoder ($F_{tgt_v}$)** (e.g., DINOv2-L/14), which ensures an unbiased, language-independent visual signal. These target embeddings are denoted as $I_{tgt}$.
 - To prevent trivial predictions, $M_{ctx}$ and $M_{tgt}$ must not intersect, and target blocks cannot overlap with each other in terms of their *visible* context (though the *latent* representation prediction allows for target block overlap).

- **Layer-wise Target Prediction (The Predictor)**:
 - In JARVIS, the **LLM ($G$) itself acts as the predictor network**. Specifically, the early layers of the LLM are leveraged for this task. The intuition is that early-to-middle layers of MLLMs are more sensitive to visual tokens. For instance, the first quarter of the LLM's layers (j-th layer) is chosen.
 - A **latent variable ($z_{tgt}$)** is introduced for each masked target patch. It is a learnable embedding combined with the positional encoding of the masked patch, signaling its location.
 - The LLM is fed the concatenation of the visible context embeddings and these latent variables: $[I_{ctx}, z_{tgt}]$.
 - A dedicated **target projector ($proj_{tgt}$)** (a two-layer MLP) transforms the output of the LLM's j-th layer at the target positions to match the dimensionality of $I_{tgt}$.
 - The **JEPA loss ($L_{JEPA}$)** is computed as the negative cosine similarity (distance function $d(\cdot)$) between the predicted embeddings from $proj_{tgt}(G_j([I_{ctx}, z_{tgt}]))$ and the ground-truth target embeddings $I_{tgt}$. This loss guides the LLM to learn to recover the masked visual information.

- **Combined Training Objective**:
 - Concurrently with $L_{JEPA}$, the MLLM is still trained with a modified next-token prediction objective ($\tilde{L}_{NTP}$). This objective asks the LLM to generate the image caption ($x$) while being conditioned on the masked input $[I_{ctx}, z_{tgt}]$. Importantly, the $z_{tgt}$ tokens, through $L_{JEPA}$, implicitly encode information about the missing image parts, making the captioning task more robust.
 - The overall training objective during the alignment stage is $\tilde{L}_{NTP} + L_{JEPA}$.

- **Efficient Implementation via Attention Mask**:
 - To achieve a single forward pass for both losses, JARVIS cleverly modifies the LLM's attention mask.
 - Bidirectional attention is allowed among context and target visual embeddings.
 - **Crucial Constraints**:
 - Context embeddings ($I_{ctx}$) cannot attend to target embeddings ($I_{tgt}$).
 - Target embeddings from *different blocks* cannot attend to each other, but each can attend to $I_{ctx}$.
 - Textual tokens can attend to all visual tokens ($I_{ctx}$ and $z_{tgt}$), but visual tokens (specifically for $L_{JEPA}$ prediction) are not influenced by the caption, ensuring no language conditioning for predicting $I_{tgt}$.

- **Balancing Visual and Textual Supervision**:
 - To prevent the model from only learning from masked images (which could degrade general image-text alignment), $L_{JEPA}$ is skipped with a probability $\lambda$ (e.g., 0.2). In these cases, the original $L_{NTP}$ is computed on the *unmasked* image, effectively extending $I_{ctx}$ to the entire image.

- **Post-Alignment**: After the alignment stage, $F_{tgt_v}$ and $proj_{tgt}$ are discarded, and the model proceeds to the standard visual instruction tuning stage with unmasked images.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Visual Encoders**: $F_{ctx_v}$ is CLIP ViT-L/14@336. $F_{tgt_v}$ is DINOv2-L/14 (for most experiments).
- **Projectors**: `proj` and `proj_{tgt}` are two-layer MLPs with GELU activation.
- **LLM Predictor Layer**: $j$ is set to the layer at one-fourth of the LLM's depth.
- **Loss Function**: Negative cosine similarity for $L_{JEPA}$.
- **Masking**: I-JEPA's block-wise masking strategy. $M_{ctx}$ is a single block (85-100% of image), $M_{tgt}$ consists of 4 overlapping blocks (15-20% of image each).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that JARVIS consistently improves the visual perception capabilities of MLLMs across various benchmarks and LLM families without degrading performance on general multimodal reasoning tasks.

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies (using Vicuna-7B)

- **Varying the LLM Layer for $L_{JEPA}$**:
 - Computing $L_{JEPA}$ on intermediate LLM layers (e.g., layer 8 for Vicuna-7B, which is ¼-depth) significantly boosts visual task performance compared to using the final layer. The ¼-depth layer showed the best visual performance, with notable gains on MMVP (+3.4 points) and CVBench2D (+4.9 points) compared to the final layer.
 - This supports the hypothesis that early-to-middle LLM layers are more relevant for processing fine-grained visual information.
 - General, Knowledge, and OCR tasks remained stable across different layers.

- **Scaling the Target Projector (`proj_{tgt}`)**:
 - An MLP projector for `proj_{tgt}` is crucial. A simple linear projection degraded visual performance (e.g., -1.5 points on Vision-Centric avg) compared to the LLaVA baseline.
 - The MLP projector resulted in a +1.7 points gain on Vision-Centric tasks, indicating that a non-linear transformation is necessary to effectively map intermediate LLM embeddings to the visual encoder's output space.

- **Changing the Distance Function in $L_{JEPA}$**:
 - Using negative cosine similarity (cosine distance) as the distance function for $L_{JEPA}$ yielded the best visual performance (+2.6 avg points on visual tasks) compared to the smooth L1 distance (used in original I-JEPA).
 - Smooth L1, which requires matching both direction and magnitude, severely degraded General and Vision-Centric performance, suggesting that learning the direction of visual embeddings is more effective for this task.

- **Validating the Masking Strategy**:
 - Allowing overlap among target regions during training (as in original I-JEPA) improved visual performance (+1.6 points on Vision-Centric avg) compared to enforcing non-overlapping targets, suggesting a mild regularization effect.

- **Effect of Attention Patterns**:
 - Preventing attention between different target blocks while allowing text tokens to attend to predicted targets (`attn_{tgt\text{_}\neg\text{_}tgt, tgt\rightarrow txt}`) yielded the best overall performance, confirming the design choice of isolating target predictions while integrating them for language generation.

- **Effect of $L_{JEPA}$ Dropout Rate ($\lambda$)**:
 - A non-zero $\lambda$ is essential. $\lambda=0.2$ achieved the best overall performance. Setting $\lambda=0.0$ (always active $L_{JEPA}$) led to degradation, indicating that periodically training on unmasked images is necessary to maintain high-level image-text understanding.

#### 4.2. Main Experimental Results and Comparison with Related Methods

JARVIS was compared against LLaVA, VIRAL, and an "Ours w/o Masking" baseline (which aligns LLM intermediate activations with a target encoder without the masked predictive objective).

- **Performance on Vicuna-7B**:
 - JARVIS surpassed LLaVA on most vision-centric tasks, achieving an average gain of +0.8 points.
 - Crucially, neither VIRAL nor "Ours w/o Masking" improved over LLaVA on vision-centric benchmarks, highlighting that the **masked predictive objective (I-JEPA) is the key contributor** to JARVIS's success.
 - JARVIS maintained competitive performance on General, Knowledge, and OCR tasks, showing no trade-off.

- **Performance on Qwen2-7B**:
 - JARVIS further improved upon LLaVA with Qwen2-7B, with an average gain of +0.7 points on vision-centric tasks and a significant +6.2 points improvement on the challenging CVBench3D benchmark.
 - Again, VIRAL and "Ours w/o Masking" did not improve over LLaVA.
 - JARVIS also showed competitive or better scores across other task categories.
 - Qualitative examples demonstrated JARVIS's superior performance in counting, spatial reasoning, depth estimation, and localization compared to LLaVA and VIRAL.

#### 4.3. Generalization Across Different LLMs

JARVIS demonstrated consistent improvements across a range of LLM families and sizes:
- **Smaller LLMs**: Gemma2-2B (+0.8 avg Vision-Centric) and LLaMA-3.2-3B (+1.4 avg Vision-Centric).
- **Mid-size LLMs**: Vicuna-7B (+0.8 avg Vision-Centric), Ministral-8B (+1.6 avg Vision-Centric). Ministral-8B also saw improvements in General (+1.7) and OCR (+1.0) tasks.
- **Stronger Visual Encoders**: When paired with Qwen2-7B and a more advanced visual encoder (SigLIP2-So400M L/14@384), JARVIS outperformed LLaVA across *all four categories*, including +1.8 on Vision-Centric, +1.6 on General, +0.8 on Knowledge, and +4.8 on OCR. This indicates JARVIS benefits more from stronger visual encoders than the original LLaVA recipe.

#### 4.4. Scaling the Target Encoder

- DINOv2/DINOv3 (unsupervised vision models) consistently delivered better performance as target visual encoders than CLIP or SigLIP2 (language-supervised vision models).
- Scaling up DINO variants (from Base to Giant/Huge) generally led to higher performance, demonstrating the benefits of using a strong target encoder for the JEPA objective.

In summary, JARVIS successfully integrates self-supervised visual learning, based on I-JEPA, into MLLM training. It significantly boosts visual reasoning capabilities across diverse LLMs, particularly for fine-grained tasks, without compromising general multimodal performance. The core mechanism is the masked predictive objective, implemented efficiently, which forces the LLM to learn intrinsic visual regularities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Seeing Beyond Words: Self-Supervised Visual Learning for Multimodal Large Language Models" offers a significant contribution to the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with far-reaching implications and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Core Significance and Novelty

The most significant contribution of JARVIS is its demonstration that **self-supervised visual learning, specifically through a JEPA-inspired masked predictive objective, is a critical missing piece for robust visual understanding in MLLMs.** Unlike previous efforts that primarily rely on textual supervision or simpler representation alignment, JARVIS forces the MLLM to learn internal visual regularities by predicting missing parts of an image in a latent space, entirely devoid of linguistic conditioning during the visual prediction task. This approach fundamentally alters how the LLM "sees" and processes visual information from an early stage.

The novelty lies in:
- **First-time integration of I-JEPA into MLLM pre-training**: No prior work has incorporated the masked predictive objective of I-JEPA directly into the vision-language alignment stage of MLLMs.
- **Leveraging LLM as the Predictor**: Utilizing the early layers of the LLM itself as the predictor network for the JEPA objective is an innovative architectural choice, ensuring that the visual learning directly influences the core reasoning engine.
- **Efficient Implementation**: The method integrates seamlessly into the LLaVA framework with a single forward pass and attention mask manipulations, minimizing computational overhead.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

1. **Enhanced Visual Grounding and Reasoning**: By training MLLMs to inherently understand visual structures and semantics beyond verbal descriptions, JARVIS leads to models that are less prone to "visual hallucinations" or over-reliance on language priors. This directly impacts their ability to perform fine-grained visual reasoning tasks, such as accurate counting, precise spatial relationship understanding, and depth estimation, as evidenced by the consistent improvements on vision-centric benchmarks like Cambrian and CVBench.
2. **More Robust and Reliable MLLMs**: MLLMs that "see beyond words" are inherently more robust. Their visual understanding is grounded in the actual pixels and their intrinsic relationships, rather than being biased by the subjectivity and incompleteness of human annotations. This increased reliability is crucial for deployment in real-world applications.
3. **Broader Applicability**: Improved visual reasoning capabilities have broad implications across various domains:
 - **Robotics**: Better understanding of object positions, distances, and interactions in complex environments.
 - **Medical Imaging**: More accurate interpretation of visual details, potentially aiding diagnosis and analysis.
 - **Autonomous Driving**: Enhanced perception of road conditions, obstacles, and spatial relationships.
 - **Visual Question Answering (VQA)**: More precise answers to questions requiring detailed visual analysis.
 - **Creative AI**: Models capable of generating more visually coherent and contextually accurate images or visual narratives.
4. **Advancing Self-Supervised Learning in Multimodal AI**: This work reinforces the growing importance of self-supervised learning for multimodal models. It suggests that while massive datasets are crucial, the quality and nature of the learning signal—especially for modalities beyond text—are equally vital. It paves the way for exploring other sophisticated SSL techniques to further bridge the gap between human-like perception and current MLLM capabilities.
5. **Efficient Model Development**: The method's ability to integrate into existing MLLM training pipelines with minimal additional computational cost (frozen encoders, single forward pass) makes it an attractive and practical enhancement for developers.
6. **Future Research Directions**: JARVIS opens up several avenues for future research, including:
 - Investigating principled methods for selecting the optimal LLM layer for visual prediction tasks, rather than empirical choices.
 - Exploring different JEPA variants or other self-supervised objectives tailored for multimodal scenarios.
 - Analyzing the learned representations within the LLM layers to gain deeper insights into how visual understanding evolves.
 - Applying JARVIS to other multimodal tasks beyond image-text (e.g., video-text, 3D-text).

#### 5.3. Limitations Acknowledged by Authors

Despite its strengths, the authors candidly acknowledge that MLLMs still fall short of human-level spatial reasoning. Examples provided in the supplementary material (e.g., miscounting spider legs, incorrect assessment of surface features) highlight areas where even JARVIS struggles. This indicates that while significant progress has been made, the pursuit of truly robust and nuanced visual intelligence in MLLMs remains an active area of research.

In conclusion, JARVIS represents a crucial step toward building MLLMs that are not just excellent language processors but also sophisticated visual reasoners. By integrating a potent self-supervised visual learning paradigm, it allows MLLMs to truly "see beyond words," fostering a deeper, more intrinsic understanding of the visual world.
