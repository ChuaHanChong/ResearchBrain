# Curiosity-driven Exploration by Self-supervised Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley developed an Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM) to generate intrinsic motivation signals for reinforcement learning agents, enabling them to learn effectively in environments with sparse or absent external rewards. The approach defines curiosity as the error in predicting the consequences of the agent's actions within a learned feature space, allowing agents to acquire complex behaviors autonomously in environments like Super Mario Bros. and VizDoom.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning algorithms struggle in real-world environments that offer sparse or delayed reward signals, making it difficult for agents to learn optimal behaviors.
- Random exploration is inefficient in large state spaces, and agents receive little to no learning signal for most of their interactions when external rewards are rare.
- Assigning credit for actions becomes challenging when rewards are significantly delayed, hindering effective policy updates.

## Method

- Proposes the ==Intrinsic Curiosity Module== (**ICM**), a neural network architecture that generates ==intrinsic rewards== based on an agent's ability to predict the consequences of its actions.
- **ICM** consists of an ==Inverse Dynamics Model==, which learns a feature space by predicting the action taken between two states, and a ==Forward Dynamics Model==, which predicts the next state's feature representation given the current state's features and the action.
- The ==prediction error== of the forward dynamics model serves as the intrinsic reward signal, which is then combined with any available extrinsic reward to train a standard reinforcement learning algorithm (e.g., **A3C**).

## Results

- The Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM) consistently outperformed standard A3C and pixel-prediction baselines in environments with sparse rewards, with its advantage increasing as reward sparsity grew (e.g., VizDoom navigation).
- The curiosity-driven agent learned to progress through Super Mario Bros. levels (e.g., avoid enemies, collect coins) without any external rewards, demonstrating autonomous skill acquisition driven solely by intrinsic motivation.
- Knowledge gained through curiosity-driven exploration generalized, enabling agents pre-trained with ICM to adapt more quickly and effectively to new maze configurations and scenarios compared to agents trained from scratch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Curiosity can be effectively formulated as the prediction error of an agent's ability to model the consequences of its own actions in a learned feature space.
> - Learning a feature space that encodes information relevant to predicting the agent's actions allows curiosity to focus on controllable aspects of the environment, ignoring irrelevant or uncontrollable noise.
> - Intrinsic motivation signals derived from prediction error provide continuous learning signals, addressing the fundamental challenge of sparse or absent external rewards in reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL) has proven effective in scenarios where clear feedback signals guide the learning process. However, many real-world environments offer sparse or delayed rewards, creating a fundamental challenge: how can agents learn to perform tasks when actionable feedback is rare or nonexistent? This question brings us to one of the most fascinating aspects of human learning—curiosity.

Pathak, Agrawal, Efros, and Darrell from UC Berkeley addressed this challenge in their paper "Curiosity-driven Exploration by Self-supervised Prediction," introducing a novel approach for generating intrinsic motivation signals that encourage exploration in environments with sparse external rewards.

The authors' key insight was to formulate curiosity as the error in an agent's ability to predict the consequences of its own actions. This approach draws inspiration from human curiosity, where we are naturally driven to explore scenarios that challenge our understanding of how the world works. By implementing this concept in reinforcement learning agents, the researchers created systems that can learn and explore effectively even without explicit external rewards.

### 2. The Challenge of Sparse Rewards in Reinforcement Learning

Traditional reinforcement learning relies heavily on reward signals from the environment to guide the learning process. An agent takes actions, observes the resulting state changes, and receives rewards that indicate the desirability of those state transitions. By maximizing these rewards over time, the agent learns optimal behavior.

However, many practical scenarios present sparse reward landscapes:
- A robot learning to navigate a building might only receive a reward upon reaching a specific destination
- A game-playing agent might only get feedback upon completing a level
- An autonomous vehicle might only receive clear signals when reaching a destination or avoiding accidents

In these sparse reward settings, standard reinforcement learning algorithms struggle because:

1. Random exploration is inefficient in large state spaces
2. The agent receives little to no learning signal for most of its interactions
3. Credit assignment becomes extremely difficult when rewards are delayed

These challenges necessitate alternative approaches that can provide useful learning signals even when external rewards are absent. This is where intrinsic motivation and curiosity-driven exploration become crucial.

### 3. Intrinsic Curiosity Module

The authors propose the Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM), a neural network architecture that generates intrinsic rewards based on an agent's ability to predict the consequences of its actions in a learned feature space.

The ICM consists of two main components:

1. **Inverse Dynamics Model**: This component learns a feature space that encodes information relevant to predicting the agent's actions. Given the current state $s_t$ and next state $s_{t+1}$, it learns to predict the action $a_t$ that caused this transition. Mathematically, it models $P(a_t | s_t, s_{t+1})$.

2. **Forward Dynamics Model**: Using the feature representation learned by the inverse model, this component predicts the feature representation of the next state given the current state's features and the action taken. In formal terms, it models $P(\phi(s_{t+1}) | \phi(s_t), a_t)$, where $\phi$ represents the learned feature mapping.

The prediction error of the forward model then serves as the intrinsic reward:

$$r_t^i = \frac{\eta}{2} \| \hat{\phi}(s_{t+1}) - \phi(s_{t+1}) \|_2^2$$

Where $\hat{\phi}(s_{t+1})$ is the predicted feature representation, $\phi(s_{t+1})$ is the actual feature representation, and $\eta$ is a scaling factor.

### 4. Methodology

The authors' approach combines the ICM with a standard reinforcement learning algorithm (Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic or A3C) to create a curiosity-driven agent. The total reward signal becomes a combination of extrinsic and intrinsic rewards:

$$r_t = r_t^e + r_t^i$$

Where $r_t^e$ is the external reward provided by the environment (which could be sparse or even zero), and $r_t^i$ is the intrinsic curiosity reward.

The training process involves:

1. Processing raw sensory inputs (like images) through the feature encoding network
2. Training the inverse dynamics model to predict actions from state transitions
3. Training the forward dynamics model to predict future states in feature space
4. Computing prediction errors to generate intrinsic rewards
5. Using these combined rewards to train the policy network via A3C

This approach offers several advantages:

- It focuses curiosity on aspects of the environment that the agent can control
- It ignores uncontrollable or irrelevant aspects of the environment
- It scales to high-dimensional observation spaces like images
- It provides a consistent learning signal even when external rewards are absent

### 5. Experimental Evaluation

The authors evaluated their approach in two main environments:

1. **VizDoom**: A 3D navigation environment based on the first-person shooter Doom, where the agent receives visual input and must navigate through mazes.

2. **Super Mario Bros**: A side-scrolling platform game where the agent must navigate through levels with various obstacles.

The experiments were designed to test several key aspects:

- **Performance in sparse reward settings**: How well does the curiosity-driven agent perform when external rewards are rare?
- **Learning without rewards**: Can the agent learn useful behaviors even in the complete absence of external rewards?
- **Robustness to noise**: How does the approach handle stochastic or uncontrollable elements in the environment?
- **Generalization**: Can knowledge gained through curiosity transfer to new scenarios?

### 8. Significance and Implications

The research presented in this paper has several important implications for the field of reinforcement learning and artificial intelligence:

1. **Solution to Sparse Reward Problems**: By providing a continuous intrinsic reward signal, curiosity-driven learning addresses one of the fundamental challenges in reinforcement learning—the sparse reward problem.

2. **Autonomous Skill Acquisition**: The ability to learn meaningful behaviors without external rewards brings us closer to systems that can autonomously acquire skills in open-ended environments.

3. **Feature Learning**: The self-supervised approach to learning relevant features demonstrates how agents can focus on aspects of the environment that matter for their interaction while ignoring irrelevant details.

4. **Robustness to Stochasticity**: By using feature predictions rather than raw sensory predictions, the approach handles stochastic elements of the environment more effectively than previous methods.

The mathematical formulation of curiosity as prediction error in a learned feature space offers a principled way to implement intrinsic motivation in artificial agents. This connects to our understanding of human curiosity, where we are often driven to explore situations that challenge our predictive models of the world.

### 9. Conclusion

The paper "Curiosity-driven Exploration by Self-supervised Prediction" presents a significant advance in reinforcement learning by providing a solution to the challenge of learning in environments with sparse or absent rewards. The Intrinsic Curiosity Module offers a principled approach to generating intrinsic motivation signals that encourage systematic exploration and learning.

Key takeaways from this research include:

- Formulating curiosity as prediction error in a learned feature space provides an effective intrinsic reward signal
- Self-supervised learning can identify relevant features for curiosity without manual engineering
- Curiosity-driven agents can learn useful behaviors even without external rewards
- Knowledge gained through curiosity-driven exploration can transfer to new scenarios

This work opens up promising directions for developing more autonomous learning systems that can explore and adapt to complex, real-world environments without requiring extensive human supervision or reward engineering.
