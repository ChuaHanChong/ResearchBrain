# Sensorless Four-Channel Control Architecture Using Inverse Dynamics Modeling for Human-Scale Bilateral Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Alberta developed a sensorless four-channel control architecture for human-scale bilateral teleoperation that estimates external forces via inverse dynamics modeling. This system improves transparency metrics like position tracking and maximum transmittable impedance without needing force/torque sensors, enabling detection of whole-body interactions and reducing system complexity.

## Problem

- Human-scale teleoperation systems suffer from low transparency due to high inertia and damping, which distorts operator perception and increases effort.
- Existing transparency-enhancing control schemes often rely on expensive, noisy, and complex force/torque (F/T) sensors.
- F/T sensors are typically limited to end-effector measurements, failing to detect interactions along the robot's body, which is crucial for large manipulators.

## Method

- Developed a ==sensorless four-channel control architecture== (**4c-DC**) that employs ==inverse dynamics modeling== to estimate real-time external joint torques without physical F/T sensors.
- Implemented ==feedforward dynamic compensation== and ==estimated force feedback== on both leader and follower manipulators.
- Formulated ==control laws== to utilize these estimated interaction torques for ==haptic feedback== and dynamic compensation to enhance transparency.

## Results

- The identified inverse dynamics model accurately predicted robot torques, yielding low **Normalized Root Mean Square Error** (e.g., leader Joint 1 at **5.25%**).
- The sensorless 4c-DC architecture consistently improved transparency metrics, including free-motion position tracking, reduced leader impedance, and significantly increased maximum transmittable impedance, outperforming baseline controllers (**p < 0.05**).
- Successfully demonstrated robust performance in a door-opening task involving whole-body contact, showing improved position and force tracking compared to other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity transparency in human-scale teleoperation can be achieved by accurately estimating external forces via inverse dynamics modeling, enabling detection of whole-body interactions.
> - A four-channel control architecture, combined with dynamic compensation and estimated force feedback, is crucial for optimizing both free-motion and hard-contact transparency.
> - Maintaining system passivity and stability with sensorless force estimation requires careful dynamic parameter identification and passivity-preserving gain tuning, such as selectively modeling friction and scaling acceleration signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bilateral teleoperation systems are designed to allow human operators to remotely control robotic manipulators, often in hazardous or inaccessible environments, providing the operator with haptic feedback to enhance interaction realism and precision. A central objective in this field is to achieve high transparency, meaning the operator perceives the remote environment as if directly interacting with it through a physically ideal, massless, and infinitely stiff link.

Previous research on teleoperation transparency has explored various control architectures, including the four-channel framework, which is recognized for its potential to optimize transparency by exchanging both position and force information and compensating for robot dynamics. However, achieving high transparency in *human-scale* teleoperation systems presents significant challenges. These systems, characterized by large workspaces and high force capabilities, typically involve manipulators with high inertia and damping, which increase operator effort and distort the perception of the remote environment.

Furthermore, many existing transparent control schemes rely on force/torque (F/T) sensors. While these sensors provide direct force measurements, they are often costly, susceptible to noise, difficult to integrate, and primarily limited to end-effector measurements. This limitation prevents the detection of interaction forces along the manipulator's body, which is a significant drawback for large robots that frequently interact with the environment through their body links during complex tasks.

The current work addresses these limitations by introducing a sensorless four-channel control architecture. It builds upon established principles of transparency-optimized teleoperation but deviates from conventional approaches by estimating external forces through inverse dynamics modeling, thereby eliminating the reliance on physical F/T sensors. This approach positions the research within the broader landscape of model-based control and learning-based dynamics estimation, aiming to make high-transparency teleoperation more robust, cost-effective, and capable of detecting whole-body interactions, particularly for human-scale robotic systems. The paper also compares its approach against other transparency-enhancement methods like force feedforward and local force feedback, establishing its performance relative to existing techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a sensorless four-channel control architecture for human-scale bilateral teleoperation systems that enhances transparency without requiring force/torque sensors.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in achieving high-fidelity teleoperation, particularly with human-scale manipulators:
- **Limitations of Traditional Teleoperation:** While bilateral teleoperation aims for transparency, human-scale manipulators typically possess high inertia and significant damping. These physical characteristics distort the operator's perception of the remote environment, leading to increased operator effort and reduced fidelity of haptic feedback.
- **Issues with Force/Torque Sensors:** Many existing transparency-enhancing control schemes depend on external force/torque sensors. These sensors introduce practical disadvantages, including:
 - **Cost and Complexity:** They are often expensive and add complexity to system integration.
 - **Noise Sensitivity:** F/T sensor readings can be noisy, potentially affecting control stability and accuracy.
 - **Limited Measurement Scope:** Most F/T sensors are installed at the end-effector, meaning they cannot detect contact or interaction forces occurring along the body of the manipulator. For large robots, tasks often involve whole-body contact, making these unobservable interactions a safety concern and a barrier to full transparency.
- **Need for Robust and Cost-Effective Solutions:** The desire to overcome the aforementioned limitations motivates the search for alternative, sensorless methods that can provide accurate force estimation and dynamic compensation. Such methods could reduce system cost and complexity while expanding the range of detectable interactions.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper include:
- Developing a four-channel teleoperation architecture that uses inverse dynamics modeling to estimate external joint torques without relying on physical force/torque sensors.
- Implementing feedforward dynamic compensation on both leader and follower manipulators using these estimated dynamics.
- Utilizing the estimated interaction torques to provide haptic feedback to the operator.
- Experimentally validating the proposed sensorless architecture on a customized WAM bilateral teleoperation system.
- Quantitatively evaluating the transparency performance of the proposed method against conventional two-channel and four-channel schemes, as well as other transparency-enhancement techniques, across various metrics including position tracking, leader impedance, hard-contact force tracking, and maximum transmittable impedance.
- Demonstrating the practical effectiveness of the method in a realistic human-scale manipulation task involving sustained whole-body contact, such as a door-opening scenario.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to contribute a more practical and capable teleoperation solution for human-scale robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a sensorless four-channel teleoperation architecture that integrates inverse dynamics modeling for real-time external torque estimation and dynamic compensation. The methodology involves theoretical analysis, dynamic parameter identification, control law formulation, and experimental validation.

#### 3.1. A. General Transparency Analysis Framework

The transparency of a teleoperation system is analyzed using a two-port network model. For a 1-DOF linearized system, transparency is quantified by the hybrid matrix H, where perfect transparency corresponds to a specific ideal hybrid matrix. The paper uses experimentally measurable parameters to characterize transparency:
- **h₁₁:** Leader impedance (should ideally be zero).
- **h₂₁:** Free-motion position tracking (should ideally be one).
- **F₁₂:** Hard-contact force tracking (should ideally be one).
- **Z₁₁:** Maximum transmittable impedance (should ideally be infinite).

#### 3.2. B. Four-Channel Teleoperation System Architecture

The control architecture is based on the principle that optimal transparency is achieved by exchanging both position and force information and compensating for robot dynamics. The control efforts for the leader ($F_l$) and follower ($F_f$) robots are defined to include:
- **Dynamic Compensation:** Terms $Z_l X_f$ and $Z_f X_l$ compensate for the respective robot's impedance/dynamics.
- **PID Position Control:** Terms $C_p (X_f - X_l)$ and $C_p (X_l - X_f)$ act on the position error.
- **Force Feedback:** Terms $-C_f F_e$ and $C_f F_h$ provide feedback from the estimated environment and human forces.
The architecture uses four communication channels: two for position ($C_1$, $C_4$) and two for force ($C_2$, $C_3$). It is established that dynamics compensation improves free-motion transparency, but force feedback ($C_f = 1$) is necessary to achieve optimal maximum transmittable impedance in hard contact.

#### 3.3. C. Inverse Dynamics Modeling and External Torque Estimation

The core of the sensorless approach lies in inverse dynamics modeling.
1. **Robot Dynamics Model:** A robot manipulator's dynamics are described by the equation:
 $M(q)\ddot{q} + b(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + g(q) + \tau_d = \tau$
 where $M(q)$ is the inertia matrix, $b(q, \dot{q})\dot{q}$ is Coriolis and centrifugal torque, $g(q)$ is gravity torque, $\tau$ is actuation torque, and $\tau_d$ is dissipative torque. Crucially, the model retains only viscous friction ($\tau_d = F_v \dot{q}$) as including static and Coulomb friction was found to degrade passivity experimentally.
2. **Parameter Identification:** The dynamic model is linearized with respect to a set of inertial parameters ($\pi_i$) for each link. These include inertia tensor components, mass, and first moments of mass, along with viscous friction coefficients. Only identifiable *base* parameters ($\pi_b$) are considered. Using an excitation trajectory (modeled as finite Fourier series to ensure parameter excitation) and a least-squares estimation method, the base parameters ($\hat{\pi}_b$) are identified from measured joint positions, velocities, accelerations, and actuation torques.
3. **External Torque Estimation:** When the manipulator interacts with the environment, an external torque ($\tau_{ext}$) is present. By rearranging the dynamics equation and substituting the identified parameters, the external torque can be estimated as:
 $\hat{\tau}_{ext} = \tau - Y_b(q, \dot{q}, \ddot{q})\hat{\pi}_b$
 This method allows for the estimation of external joint torques without direct force/torque sensors, enabling the detection of contacts anywhere along the manipulator body.

#### 3.4. D. Sensorless Four-Channel Control Implementation

The derived control efforts in joint space for the leader ($\tau_l$) and follower ($\tau_f$) are:
- $\tau_l = Y_{b_l}(q_f, \dot{q}_f, \ddot{q}_f)\hat{\pi}_{b_l} + C_p(q_f - q_l) - C_f \hat{\tau}_e$
- $\tau_f = Y_{b_f}(q_l, \dot{q}_l, \ddot{q}_l)\hat{\pi}_{b_f} + C_p(q_l - q_f) + C_f \hat{\tau}_h$
Here, $\hat{\tau}_e$ and $\hat{\tau}_h$ are the estimated environmental and human-applied torques, respectively, derived using the external torque estimation method for both the follower and leader. The controller uses estimated dynamics as feedforward terms and estimated external torques for force feedback.

#### 3.5. E. Experimental Setup and Validation

- **Hardware:** A 7-DOF WAM Barrett arm serves as the follower, and a 4-DOF WAM Barrett arm with a 3-DOF custom haptic wrist acts as the leader. The WAM arms were chosen for their back-drivability and low friction.
- **Software and Communication:** Custom C++ control software with the libbarrett library was used. Communication between leader and follower occurred via UDP on the same computer at 500 Hz to minimize delay.
- **Inverse Dynamics Estimation:** The first four DOFs of each WAM arm were considered, with wrist dynamics neglected. Base dynamic parameters were identified independently for leader and follower using excitation trajectories. Model accuracy was validated using Normalized Root Mean Square Error (NRMSE) between measured and predicted torques, which showed low NRMSE values (e.g., Joint 1: 5.25% leader, 5.34% follower).
- **Gain Tuning for Passivity:** Experimental evaluation revealed critical factors for stability and passivity:
 - Only viscous friction was retained in the model.
 - Raw joint accelerations were scaled by 25% to mitigate noise-induced instability.
 - Force feedback gain ($C_f$) was reduced to 0.5 from the ideal 1.0 to preserve passivity, reflecting a stability-transparency trade-off.
- **Evaluation Procedure:** Transparency was quantitatively assessed in joint space using metrics:
 - **Free motion:** Position tracking (NRMSE between leader/follower positions) and leader impedance ($\hat{\tau}_{h,RMS}/\delta q_{l,RMS}$).
 - **Hard contact:** Force tracking (NRMSE between estimated external joint torques) and maximum transmittable impedance ($\hat{\tau}_{h,RMS}/RMSE(q_l, q_f)$).
 - External torque estimation error was also quantified.
- **Comparison:** The proposed 4c-DC (four-channel with dynamics compensation) was compared against:
 - 2c-GC (two-channel P-P with gravity compensation)
 - 2c-DC (two-channel P-P with dynamics compensation)
 - 4c-GC (four-channel with gravity compensation)
 - Transparency-enhancement methods: 2c-GC-FF (force feedforward) and 2c-GC-LFB (local force feedback).
- **Case Study:** A door-opening task with a human operator was used to demonstrate the system's performance in a realistic scenario involving whole-body contact.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation on the WAM bilateral teleoperation system yielded several findings regarding the performance of the proposed sensorless four-channel architecture (4c-DC) compared to baseline and transparency-enhancement controllers.

#### 4.1. A. Inverse Dynamics Estimation

- The identified base dynamic parameters accurately captured the robot dynamics for both the leader and follower WAM arms. Validation with five random reference trajectories showed low Normalized Root Mean Square Error (NRMSE) between measured and predicted torques. For example, Joint 1 on the leader had an NRMSE of 5.25% (STD 0.34%), and Joint 2 on the follower had 2.79% (STD 0.55%). This indicates the effectiveness of the parameter estimation approach.

#### 4.2. B. Transparency Performance Comparison

The results of the objective transparency evaluation across various control architectures are summarized:
- **Effect of Dynamics Compensation:** Comparing 2c-GC (gravity compensation) with 2c-DC (dynamics compensation) showed that dynamics compensation significantly improved free-motion position tracking and reduced leader impedance (p < 0.05). This confirms the benefit of actively compensating for the robot's inherent dynamics. However, hard-contact force tracking and maximum transmittable impedance remained largely unchanged.
- **Effect of Force Feedback:** Comparing 4c-GC (four-channel with gravity compensation) with 2c-GC (two-channel with gravity compensation) demonstrated that the inclusion of force feedback (four channels) significantly increased the maximum transmittable impedance (p < 0.05). This supports the theoretical premise that force feedback is crucial for optimal transparency during hard contact. 4c-GC also improved free-motion tracking and leader impedance.
- **Proposed 4c-DC Performance:** The proposed sensorless 4c-DC architecture consistently outperformed all baseline controllers (2c-GC, 2c-DC, 4c-GC) across multiple key transparency metrics (p < 0.05):
 - **Free-motion position tracking:** 4c-DC achieved better tracking, indicating superior remote position reproduction.
 - **Leader impedance:** 4c-DC resulted in lower leader impedance, reducing operator effort and making free motion feel lighter.
 - **Maximum transmittable impedance:** 4c-DC significantly increased this metric, implying better perception of environmental stiffness and contact forces during hard contact.
- **Force Tracking:** No statistically significant difference in hard-contact force tracking was observed across the controllers. This was attributed to the near-identical nature of the leader and follower robots and the use of identical control gains.
- **Comparison with Transparency-Enhancement Methods:**
 - **2c-GC-FF (Force Feedforward):** While it improved leader impedance, enhancing free-motion transparency, it degraded maximum transmittable impedance and hard-contact force tracking compared to 2c-GC.
 - **2c-GC-LFB (Local Force Feedback):** It also improved leader impedance but showed no significant improvement in hard-contact metrics.
 - In both free motion and hard contact, 4c-DC demonstrated superior transparency compared to these transparency-enhancement methods.
- **External Torque Estimation Error:** The NRMSE values for external torque estimation were consistently small across all controllers and joints (Table III). For 4c-DC, leader joint 2 showed 3.23% (STD 2.18%) and follower joint 2 showed 11.72% (STD 8.62%), indicating that the inverse dynamics model effectively estimated external interactions.

#### 4.3. C. Case Study: Door-Opening Task

- The door-opening task involved both end-effector and whole-body interactions along the follower arm.
- The proposed 4c-DC system outperformed all other tested methods in both free motion and hard contact phases of this realistic manipulation task.
- During the contact period (gray regions in Fig. 7), 4c-DC exhibited improved position tracking, reflecting a higher maximum transmittable impedance. This suggests better perception of door constraints for the operator.
- During free motion, 4c-DC showed improved force tracking, indicating lower leader impedance and reduced operator effort.
- These qualitative observations align with the quantitative metrics, demonstrating that 4c-DC achieved the highest transparency across both operational regimes (free motion and hard contact).

In summary, the results indicate that the sensorless four-channel architecture using inverse dynamics modeling effectively enhances transparency in human-scale teleoperation, specifically improving position and force tracking, reducing operator effort, and increasing the maximum transmittable impedance without the need for external force/torque sensors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a sensorless four-channel teleoperation architecture that contributes to the field of human-robot interaction by addressing key limitations in human-scale teleoperation. The significance and potential impact of this work are as follows:

- **Enhanced Transparency without Force Sensors:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating that high levels of transparency, crucial for natural and effective teleoperation, can be achieved on human-scale manipulators without relying on costly, noisy, and complex force/torque sensors. This challenges the conventional wisdom that explicit force sensing is indispensable for advanced haptic feedback.
- **Detection of Whole-Body Interactions:** By employing inverse dynamics modeling to estimate external joint torques, the proposed method enables the detection of interaction forces along the entire manipulator body, not just at the end-effector. This is particularly impactful for human-scale robots, which often engage in tasks involving sustained contact with the environment via their links, improving both safety and the operator's comprehensive perception of the remote scene.
- **Reduced System Complexity and Cost:** Eliminating the need for external F/T sensors directly translates to a reduction in hardware complexity and overall system cost. This makes high-performance teleoperation more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications and research environments.
- **Improved Operator Experience and Task Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate improvements in position and force tracking, reduced operator effort in free motion, and increased maximum transmittable impedance in hard contact. These factors collectively contribute to a more intuitive and immersive teleoperation experience, which can lead to increased precision, reduced fatigue, and better performance of complex manipulation tasks in remote or hazardous environments.
- **Robustness in Realistic Scenarios:** The successful validation in a door-opening case study, which involves sustained whole-body contact and kinematic constraints, suggests that the architecture is robust and effective in realistic, unstructured human-scale manipulation tasks. This moves beyond controlled laboratory conditions to demonstrate applicability in more challenging operational contexts.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a foundation for future advancements in sensorless control. The identified areas for future work, such as improving the modeling of unmodeled dynamics (e.g., stiction, Coulomb friction), incorporating online adaptation (e.g., disturbance observers), extending to heterogeneous leader-follower systems, and evaluating under communication delays, indicate a path toward even more robust and versatile teleoperation systems.

In summary, this sensorless approach offers a practical, cost-effective, and comprehensive solution for achieving high transparency in human-scale teleoperation. Its ability to sense whole-body interactions and improve fundamental transparency metrics holds the potential to significantly enhance human-robot collaboration in various applications, from industrial assembly and maintenance to space exploration and disaster response.
