# Beyond the 80/20 Rule: High-Entropy Minority Tokens Drive Effective Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Research from Alibaba Inc.'s Qwen Team and Tsinghua University's LeapLab reveals that in large language models, only a small fraction of high-entropy tokens are critical for reasoning improvements during Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). By focusing policy gradient updates exclusively on these minority tokens, models achieve superior performance, including setting new state-of-the-art scores on mathematical benchmarks while reducing computational overhead and improving generalization.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods improve LLM reasoning empirically, but lack a mechanistic understanding of how or why.
- Current RLVR training applies uniform policy gradient updates across all generated tokens, without distinguishing their individual contributions to reasoning.
- The internal dynamics of how RLVR enhances Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning at a token-level remained largely unexplored.

## Method

- The authors analyzed ==token-level generation entropy== within LLM Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to identify distinct functional roles for tokens.
- They adapted the ==Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization (DAPO)== algorithm to restrict policy gradient updates to only a predefined proportion (e.g., 20%) of the ==highest-entropy tokens==.
- Evaluations were conducted across Qwen and Llama models on multiple mathematical reasoning and out-of-distribution benchmarks to assess the impact of this targeted training.

## Results

- Focusing policy gradient updates on the top **20%** highest-entropy tokens improved reasoning performance, achieving gains of **+7.71** on AIME'24 and **+11.04** on AIME'25 for Qwen3-32B, setting new state-of-the-art scores.
- Conversely, training on the **80%** lowest-entropy tokens resulted in a notable decline in reasoning performance, indicating these tokens contribute little or negatively to effective learning.
- The optimized approach also demonstrated enhanced generalization, with models trained on high-entropy tokens significantly outperforming vanilla RLVR on the out-of-distribution LiveCodeBench benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM Chain-of-Thought reasoning is driven by a sparse minority of high-entropy "forking tokens" that represent critical decision points, while most tokens are low-entropy "following tokens" completing linguistic structures.
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) primarily increases the entropy of these existing high-entropy tokens, preserving the base model's inherent entropy patterns and focusing on refining existing uncertainty.
> - Uniform policy gradient updates are suboptimal; focusing training on high-entropy minority tokens leads to better performance and generalization, suggesting these tokens are key to adaptable reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically focusing on **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. LLMs have demonstrated remarkable progress in complex reasoning tasks such as mathematics and programming, driven by scaling methodologies and advanced fine-tuning techniques. RLVR has emerged as a particularly powerful paradigm in this context, where LLMs optimize their outputs by learning from automated correctness verification (e.g., checking mathematical answers programmatically).

Prior research in RLVR, including recent advancements by OpenAI (o1), Anthropic (Claude 3.7), DeepSeek-AI (DeepSeek R1), Kimi (K1.5), and Qwen (Qwen3), has primarily focused on algorithmic innovations (e.g., DAPO, VAPO), cross-domain applications, or empirical insights. However, the authors argue that these existing implementations, while effective, often "directly train over all tokens with limited understanding of which tokens actually facilitate reasoning." This implies a knowledge gap regarding the internal mechanisms of how RLVR improves reasoning and which parts of an LLM's generated "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) are most critical for learning.

This paper addresses this gap by undertaking a "pioneering exploration of RLVR through the novel perspective of token entropy patterns." By analyzing token-level entropy, the authors aim to identify the heterogeneous functional roles of different tokens within a reasoning trajectory. This new perspective provides a mechanistic understanding of RLVR, moving beyond purely empirical observations to shed light on *why* and *how* RLVR enhances reasoning. It aligns with a growing interest in interpreting and optimizing LLM behavior at a more granular level, as seen in related work on "critical tokens" that influence outcomes. By proposing that only a minority of high-entropy tokens are truly instrumental, the paper challenges the conventional approach of uniform policy gradient updates across all tokens, offering a more targeted and potentially more efficient training strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives and motivations of this research can be summarized as follows:

- **Understanding RLVR Mechanisms:** The primary motivation is to gain a deeper, mechanistic understanding of how RLVR enhances the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Despite its empirical success, the internal dynamics of RLVR, particularly at the token level, remained largely unexplored.
- **Investigating Token Entropy Patterns:** The authors aim to pioneer an exploration of RLVR through the novel lens of token entropy patterns. They hypothesize that not all tokens contribute equally to reasoning and that entropy—a measure of uncertainty in token generation—might reveal these differing roles.
- **Identifying Critical Tokens for Reasoning:** A key objective is to identify which tokens, based on their entropy, play critical roles in shaping reasoning pathways within Chain-of-Thought (CoT) responses. They seek to distinguish between tokens that merely complete linguistic structures and those that represent pivotal decision points.
- **Analyzing Entropy Evolution During RLVR:** The research aims to observe how token entropy patterns change during the RLVR training process, specifically focusing on whether RLVR modifies all tokens uniformly or prioritizes certain types of tokens.
- **Optimizing RLVR Efficiency and Performance:** Based on the insights gained from token entropy analysis, the ultimate objective is to develop a more efficient and effective RLVR algorithm. If specific tokens are more critical, then focusing gradient updates on these tokens could reduce computational cost while potentially improving, or at least maintaining, performance.
- **Challenging the "80/20 Rule":** The paper aims to demonstrate that reasoning gains might be driven by an even smaller minority of tokens than generally assumed by rules of thumb, potentially leading to a "beyond the 80/20 rule" discovery in LLM training.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond treating LLM outputs as monolithic sequences, instead dissecting them at the token level to uncover the underlying dynamics of reasoning and, in turn, leverage this understanding to build more powerful and efficient LLM reasoning systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology combining observational analysis, controlled experiments, and an adapted reinforcement learning approach.

#### 3.1. Token Entropy Calculation

The foundation of the analysis is the **token-level generation entropy**, defined as $H_t := -\sum_{j=1}^{V} p_{t,j} \log p_{t,j}$, where $p_{t,j}$ is the probability of token $j$ at time step $t$ according to the LLM's policy $\pi_\theta$ and $V$ is the vocabulary size. The decoding temperature $T$ is also a factor. The authors clarify that $H_t$ refers to the entropy of the probability distribution for generating the token at index $t$, not the entropy of a specific token *value*.

#### 3.2. Analyzing Token Entropy in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

- **Data Collection:** Qwen3-8B was used to generate over $10^6$ response tokens for queries from AIME’24 and AIME’25 benchmarks, operating in "thinking mode" with a decoding temperature $T=1.0$.
- **Entropy Pattern Identification:** The entropy for each generated token was computed. The distribution of these entropies was statistically analyzed (Figure 2a).
- **Qualitative Analysis of Token Roles:** Word clouds were generated for the top 100 highest-average-entropy and lowest-average-entropy tokens (considering tokens with frequency > 100 to mitigate noise). This qualitative analysis aimed to understand the semantic and functional roles of these distinct groups of tokens. High-entropy tokens were posited as "forking tokens" that bridge logical connections and determine reasoning directions, while low-entropy tokens were seen as "following tokens" completing linguistic structures.
- **Quantitative Confirmation of "Forking" Role:** Controlled experiments were conducted by modulating decoding temperatures ($T_{high}$, $T_{low}$) for high-entropy (forking) and low-entropy (other) tokens, respectively. An entropy threshold $h_{threshold}=0.672$ (80th percentile) was used to distinguish these groups. The impact on AIME’24 and AIME’25 scores was measured to confirm the importance of maintaining high entropy for forking tokens.

#### 3.3. Analyzing Entropy Evolution during RLVR Training

- **RLVR Training Setup:** The Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization (DAPO) algorithm was applied to the Qwen3-14B base model. DAPO is a state-of-the-art RLVR algorithm that uses a clipped objective, group-based advantage estimation, a clip-higher mechanism, dynamic sampling, token-level policy gradient loss, and overlong reward shaping.
- **Overlap Ratio Calculation:** The authors tracked the positions of the top 20% high-entropy tokens across different stages of RLVR training. The "overlap ratio" (fraction of shared high-entropy positions) was calculated between intermediate models and both the base model and the final RLVR model to assess how much the initial entropy patterns are preserved.
- **Average Entropy Change Analysis:** The average entropy change after RLVR was computed for each 5% entropy percentile range of the base model to observe which entropy levels are most affected by training.
- **Entropy Percentile Evolution:** The evolution of entropy percentiles throughout RLVR training was visualized to show how different segments of the entropy distribution change.

#### 3.4. Optimizing RLVR with High-Entropy Minority Tokens

- **Modified DAPO Objective:** The core methodological innovation involved adapting DAPO's objective (Equation 4) to (Equation 6). This modified objective restricts policy gradient updates to only a predefined proportion (ρ) of tokens with the highest entropy within each batch. An indicator function $I[H_{i,t} \geq \tau_B^\rho]$ was introduced to mask gradients of low-entropy tokens, and the normalization term was adjusted accordingly. The default proportion $\rho$ was set to 20%.
- **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Base Models:** Experiments were conducted using Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and Qwen3-32B base models. An additional experiment used Llama-3.1-8B (after a cold-start SFT phase using Qwen3-32B generated data) to test generalization to other model families.
 - **Training Data:** DAPO-Math-17K, a mathematics-focused dataset.
 - **Hyperparameters:** Standard DAPO hyperparameters were adopted for fair comparison, including `ϵ_high=0.28`, `ϵ_low=0.2` for clip-higher, and a maximum response length of 20480 (with an extended 29696 length for further analysis). No KL divergence loss or entropy loss was used.
 - **Evaluation:** Models were evaluated on 6 standard mathematical reasoning benchmarks: AIME’24, AIME’25, AMC’23, MATH500, Minerva, and OlympiadBench, in a zero-shot setting with 16 independent responses per question. LiveCodeBench (an out-of-distribution programming benchmark) was used to test generalization.
- **Ablation Studies:** The proportion $\rho$ of high-entropy tokens used for gradient updates was varied (10%, 20%, 50%, 100% and bottom 80%) to study its impact on performance and overall entropy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings across its analytical and experimental phases:

#### 4.1. Token Entropy Patterns in CoT Reasoning

- **Sparse High-Entropy Distribution:** In LLM Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, only a small minority of tokens exhibit high entropy, while the vast majority are generated with very low entropy (over 50% below $10^{-2}$).
- **Functional Distinction:** High-entropy tokens (referred to as "forking tokens") typically serve as critical decision points, bridging logical connections or introducing assumptions ("wait," "however," "thus," "suppose"). Low-entropy tokens (referred to as "following tokens") tend to complete ongoing linguistic structures, such as word suffixes or code fragments.
- **Beneficial High Entropy:** Quantitatively, artificially lowering the temperature ($T_{high}$) for forking tokens degrades reasoning performance, while increasing it improves performance. This confirms that maintaining high entropy at these forking points is beneficial for branching into diverse reasoning directions.

#### 4.2. RLVR Preserves and Reinforces Base Model Entropy Patterns

- **Pattern Preservation:** RLVR training largely preserves the base model's inherent token entropy patterns. Even at convergence, over 86% of the top 20% high-entropy token positions from the base model remain high-entropy.
- **Targeted Entropy Adjustment:** RLVR predominantly increases the entropy of tokens that already exhibit high entropy in the base model. Low-entropy tokens show minimal variation during training, suggesting RLVR's focus is on refining existing uncertainty rather than creating new uncertainty in deterministic parts of the output.

#### 4.3. High-Entropy Minority Tokens Drive Effective RLVR

- **"Beyond the 80/20 Rule" Performance:** Restricting policy gradient updates to only the top 20% highest-entropy tokens (forking tokens) yielded remarkable results:
 - On Qwen3-8B, performance remained comparable to full-gradient updates.
 - On Qwen3-14B, it significantly *surpassed* full-gradient updates, with gains of +5.21 on AIME’24 and +4.79 on AIME’25.
 - On Qwen3-32B, the improvements were even more substantial: +7.71 on AIME’24 and +11.04 on AIME’25, setting new State-of-the-Art (SoTA) scores (63.5 on AIME’24 and 56.7 on AIME’25) for base models under 600B parameters.
- **Ineffectiveness of Low-Entropy Tokens:** Conversely, training exclusively on the 80% lowest-entropy tokens led to a marked decline in reasoning performance, demonstrating their minimal or even detrimental contribution.
- **Optimal Exploration:** Ablation studies showed that retaining approximately 20% of high-entropy tokens optimally balances exploration and performance. Deviating from this (e.g., 10%, 50%, or 100%) generally led to persistently lower overall entropy and diminished performance, suggesting that only a critical subset of high-entropy tokens contributes meaningfully to exploration.
- **Scaling Trend:** The benefits of focusing on forking tokens scaled positively with model size, yielding greater performance gains for larger models (32B > 14B > 8B).
- **Enhanced Generalization:** Training on only the top 10% or 20% highest-entropy tokens using DAPO on Qwen3-32B significantly outperformed vanilla DAPO on the out-of-distribution LiveCodeBench benchmark, suggesting a link between high-entropy tokens and generalization ability.
- **Unlocking Further Potential:** Extending the maximum response length from 20k to 29k further boosted the Qwen3-32B model's AIME’24 score from 63.54 to 68.12, indicating that the full potential of this approach might not yet be reached.
- **Llama-3.1-8B Observations:** Similar trends were observed on Llama-3.1-8B (after a cold-start SFT), where training on forking tokens slightly surpassed vanilla DAPO, though overall performance remained lower than Qwen models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers profound implications for understanding and optimizing Large Language Model training, particularly within the RLVR paradigm.

#### 5.1. Deeper Understanding of LLM Reasoning and RLVR Mechanisms

The core significance lies in providing a novel, mechanistic understanding of LLM reasoning through the lens of token entropy. By categorizing tokens into "forking" (high-entropy, decision-making) and "following" (low-entropy, linguistic completion) roles, the paper reveals that the efficacy of RLVR stems predominantly from optimizing these critical decision points. This moves beyond treating LLM outputs as a black box, offering insights into the internal workings of reasoning trajectories.

#### 5.2. More Efficient and Effective RLVR Training

The practical impact is substantial. Demonstrating that significant performance gains can be achieved (or at least maintained) by updating only a small fraction (e.g., 20%) of tokens is a breakthrough. This "beyond the 80/20 rule" finding suggests:
- **Reduced Computational Cost:** Training with fewer gradients can drastically cut down computational resources and time required for RLVR, making it more accessible and scalable.
- **Improved Performance:** Counterintuitively, selectively training only the most critical tokens *improves* performance for larger models, potentially by focusing optimization efforts on relevant aspects of reasoning and avoiding noise from less impactful tokens.
- **Enhanced Scaling:** The observed positive scaling trend with model size indicates that this focused training approach becomes even more beneficial for larger, more capable LLMs.

#### 5.3. Insights into Generalization vs. Memorization

The paper's discussion on how high-entropy minority tokens could explain RL's generalization capabilities compared to SFT's tendency to memorize is a critical theoretical contribution. By showing that RL preserves or increases entropy in forking tokens (maintaining flexibility), while SFT might reduce it (leading to less adaptable, one-hot like distributions), the work opens new avenues for designing models that generalize better to unseen tasks. The improved performance on out-of-distribution benchmarks further supports this hypothesis.

#### 5.4. Rethinking RL Algorithms for LLMs

The findings challenge traditional RL assumptions, particularly regarding entropy bonuses. Unlike classical RL where uniform action entropy is often assumed, LLM CoTs exhibit heterogeneous entropy patterns. The research suggests that uniformly applied entropy bonuses might be suboptimal for LLMs by disrupting low-entropy majority tokens. Instead, mechanisms like DAPO's "clip-higher," which implicitly target and increase the entropy of high-importance (and often high-entropy) tokens, appear more aligned with the unique nature of LLM reasoning. This could inspire the development of more tailored RL algorithms for language models.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The authors propose several promising future directions:
- **Broader Model and Domain Applicability:** Extending experiments to a wider range of LLM architectures beyond Qwen (and addressing cold-start challenges for models like Llama). Expanding the dataset scope to other complex domains like programming (e.g., LiveCodeBench), or multimodal tasks like ARC-AGI, would further validate the findings.
- **Novel RLVR Algorithms:** Developing new RLVR algorithms specifically designed to leverage high-entropy minority tokens.
- **Impact on Other LLM Paradigms:** Exploring how these insights can enhance other LLM training and inference techniques, such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT), distillation, more efficient inference strategies, and multimodal training.

**Limitations** acknowledged by the authors include the focus on Qwen models (with Llama results being less conclusive), the mathematics-centric dataset, and the possibility that the optimal 20% proportion might vary across different RLVR settings. These limitations highlight areas for future investigation, but do not detract from the significance of the current findings.

In conclusion, this paper presents a highly impactful piece of research that not only deepens our fundamental understanding of LLM reasoning under RLVR but also provides immediately actionable strategies for significantly improving the efficiency and effectiveness of training large language models. The concept of "high-entropy minority tokens" stands to reshape how we approach LLM optimization for complex tasks.
