# GroundingPI: A Grounding Foundation Model towards Physical Intelligence with Visual Primitives

> [!summary] Summary
> GroundingPI is a 4B grounding foundation model, a MoonViT-V2 encoder plus a Qwen3-4B decoder, that outputs points and boxes as quantized coordinate tokens in a shared vocabulary. It is trained with multimodal pretraining, SFT and GRPO localization rewards. It averages 73.68% across 34 grounding benchmarks, above GPT-6 Astra. As the backbone for a fixed action head, it leads every out-of-distribution manipulation setting tested.

## Problem

- VLA and world-action models inherit perception from general-purpose vision-language or video-generation backbones that fail at precise grounding in clutter, dense scenes and tiny objects.
- Weak backbone perception forces scarce robot demonstrations to carry both perceptual and action learning, which hurts data efficiency and out-of-distribution generalization.
- Existing perception boosts for policies, such as bounding-box prediction in pi0.5 or auxiliary objectives, are task-specific proxies needing extra annotations and policy-specific components that scale poorly.

## Method

- ==Unified visual-primitive output protocol==: labels, protocol markers and ==1,000 quantized coordinate tokens== share one vocabulary, casting grounding, pointing, OCR, GUI and layout as ==autoregressive structured generation==.
- ==Multi-teacher data engine== fuses annotations field by field with task-specific validation, training a grounding expert that iteratively relabels data while complementary teachers resolve uncertain cases.
- ==Staged training==: multimodal pretraining, SFT for coordinate alignment, then ==GRPO== with eight rollouts blending set-completeness and strict-localization rewards 0.7/0.3, plus ==Hungarian-matched OCR rewards==.

## Results

- Averages **73.68%** across 34 benchmarks versus GPT-6 Astra's **71.54%**; Dense200 detection reaches **74.53 F1mIoU** against Astra's **65.04** and Qwen3-VL-4B's **14.02**.
- With a fixed layer-wise Action DiT it ranks first in all four OOD manipulation settings, e.g. **17.60%** versus Rex-Omni's **14.10%** on RoboTwin2.0 Clean2Random, and **66.20%** on RoboTwin Full.
- With **50%** of RoboCasa-GR1 demonstrations it reaches **28.75%** success, beating every baseline trained on 75%; nuScenes open-loop L2 is **0.296 m** versus Qwen3-VL-4B's **0.301 m**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Perception may be an underrated bottleneck for VLAs: swapping only the pretrained backbone under a fixed action head shifts OOD robustness more than embodied or video pretraining.
> - Grounding-data composition matters as much as scale: dense multi-object supervision forms the transferable spatial core, while OCR seems to amplify it yet transfers poorly alone.
> - Base-model architecture, such as gated linear attention or multi-level visual injection, may hinder perception-to-action transfer, so judge backbones by downstream training behavior, not grounding benchmarks alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

The authors illustrate the problem with a cluttered desk. Before a robot decides how to grasp or move a mug, it must first identify the target and where it is. This is an illustration, not a reported experiment. General-purpose vision and video backbones can understand scenes broadly. However, they are not necessarily trained to identify a particular object and its precise location amid clutter, which is the information an action learner needs before it can control a robot.

GroundingPI's approach is to teach a model to point out and locate visual targets, then use it as an action-learning backbone. On RoboTwin 2.0 Clean2Random, GroundingPI's policy achieves 17.60% task success, versus 14.10% for the strongest other evaluated backbone, Rex-Omni. In this comparison, the downstream action learner and its training are shared.

### 2. Teach the model where

Here, grounding means linking a language instruction to the target object and its image location. GroundingPI is a 4B-parameter model that pairs a MoonViT-V2 visual encoder with a Qwen3-4B language decoder. Labels, protocol markers, and 1,000 quantized coordinate tokens share one vocabulary. A response can express boxes or points, and it can say `None` when the queried category is absent.

Training proceeds in stages:

1. Multimodal and spatial pretraining.
1. Supervised fine-tuning to learn the structured output.
1. GRPO reinforcement post-training with grounding and OCR rewards.

The rewards address coverage, localization quality, and, for OCR, text–geometry consistency.

The authors evaluate GroundingPI across 34 grounding benchmarks spanning 11 perceptual capabilities. Its reported 34-benchmark average is 73.68%, versus 71.54% for the larger GPT-6 Astra. Astra is evaluated at High thinking effort. This average is an aggregate over a varied suite, not a universal accuracy measure. The paper reports F1mIoU for box grounding and OCR, F1@Point for object pointing, and task-specific accuracy for spatial, robot, and GUI grounding.

### 3. Does perception transfer?

The transfer tests use matched backbones. Within each benchmark, the authors hold the Action DiT, action data, training budget, action representation, and inference procedure fixed, and vary only the pretrained foundation.

Clean2Random trains on clean demonstrations and tests on randomized scenes, with task success rate as the outcome. The randomized conditions vary scene factors such as clutter, lighting, textures, table height, and instructions. The 17.60% versus 14.10% result comes from this setting.

GroundingPI leads all four reported out-of-distribution (OOD) settings, but it does not lead all six manipulation evaluations. On RoboCasa-GR1 in-distribution (ID), RynnBrain scores 39.00% against GroundingPI's 37.75%.

This supports the authors' interpretation that a perception-native foundation transfers usefully to action learning. It does not establish that grounding is the only difference or cause separating foundation models.

### 4. Which supervision matters?

A training-mixture ablation tests the proposed explanation by removing one task group at a time. The full mixture has six groups:

- A: basic grounding
- B: dense grounding
- C: referring
- D: basic pointing
- E: robot pointing
- F: OCR, layout, and GUI

Relative to all six groups, removing dense grounding has three effects:

- RoboCasa-GR1 ID success rate falls by 4.67 percentage points.
- The OOD average falls by 3.47 points.
- nuScenes average open-loop L2 trajectory error rises by 0.015 m.

The authors read this as dense grounding supplying a spatial core. However, the ablation removes the dense-grounding group as a whole. It does not disentangle object size, scene crowding, or annotation coverage.

Table 17 task-group ablation. Rows list the six-group mixture, reduced mixtures, and F-only. Columns report RoboCasa-GR1 ID and OOD average success rate (percent, higher is better) and nuScenes average open-loop L2 error (meters, lower is better). The all-six row reads 37.75, 33.38, 0.296, and F-only reads 24.42, 7.28, 0.391. F-only is not an OCR-only test.

### 5. Where to act first

The evidence connects explicit localization before action training with better manipulation robustness under tested distribution shifts. Precise perception may be a useful foundation for action learning. The main boundary is that driving was evaluated offline on nuScenes and manipulation was evaluated in simulation, so these experiments do not demonstrate real-world autonomous deployment.
