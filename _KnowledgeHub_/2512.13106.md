# TraPO: A Semi-Supervised Reinforcement Learning Framework for Boosting LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> TRAPO is a semi-supervised reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance large language model reasoning by efficiently leveraging a small labeled dataset to guide learning on a larger unlabeled dataset. The framework achieves strong performance, often surpassing fully supervised models, by selectively incorporating unlabeled samples whose learning dynamics align with those of verified examples.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs incur prohibitively high annotation costs for ground-truth labels.
- Purely unsupervised RLVR methods, while alleviating annotation burdens, are prone to model collapse, where they reinforce internally consistent but factually incorrect reasoning paths.
- Simple integration of supervised and unsupervised losses in semi-supervised RLVR yields only marginal benefits, lacking an effective mechanism to bridge labeled and unlabeled data.

## Method

- TRAPO introduces a ==semi-supervised reward== function that combines supervised rewards for labeled data with unsupervised proxy rewards for unlabeled data.
- It uses 'pass rate trajectories' to track the learning dynamics of individual questions across training epochs and a 'Trajectory-based Cosine Similarity' (TCS) to identify reliable unlabeled samples.
- Only unlabeled samples with TCS scores above a threshold or within a top percentile, indicating learning dynamics similar to labeled data, are included in the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) training objective.

## Results

- With only **1K** labeled and **3K** unlabeled samples, TRAPO achieved **42.6%** accuracy on ID benchmarks, outperforming the best unsupervised method (**38.3%** with **45K** unlabeled) by **4.3%**.
- Using **4K** labeled and **12K** unlabeled samples (**10%** of the full labeled dataset), TRAPO attained **45.6%** ID and **59.7%** OOD accuracy, surpassing a fully supervised model trained on the entire **45K** labeled samples (**45.5%** ID, **57.3%** OOD).
- TRAPO consistently demonstrated strong generalization to out-of-distribution tasks, is robust across different LLM architectures (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill, LLaMA-3.1-**8B**-Instruct), and its trajectory-based selection improves various semi-supervised baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimal external supervision is critical to stabilize and anchor consistency-based training on unlabeled data, preventing the reinforcement of incorrect reasoning patterns.
> - Leveraging the 'learning dynamics' of the model through pass rate trajectories provides a robust method to connect labeled and unlabeled data, outperforming simple loss function combinations.
> - Reliable unlabeled samples exhibit learning dynamics that align with those of validated labeled samples, and quantifying this similarity allows for intelligent selection of data for training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, specifically focusing on the paradigm of **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. RLVR, exemplified by methods like DeepSeek-R1, has proven highly effective in training Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by using answer-verifiable signals (e.g., matching a ground-truth label for a final answer) to optimize policy. This approach guides LLMs to explore reasoning paths that lead to correct outcomes.

However, a critical limitation of traditional supervised RLVR is its **prohibitively high annotation cost**. Scaling these methods to large datasets or specialized domains (like medicine or finance) becomes impractical due to the scarcity and expense of obtaining gold-standard labels.

To address this, recent research has explored **unsupervised RLVR methods**. These methods aim to eliminate reliance on external supervision by deriving rewards solely from the model's internal consistency, using signals such as self-certainty, entropy, or majority voting. While seemingly promising for reducing annotation burdens, the paper highlights a significant drawback: these unsupervised methods often suffer from **model collapse** in later stages of training. This collapse occurs because, without external ground truth, the reward signal can become self-reinforcing, inadvertently strengthening incorrect reasoning patterns and leading to a degenerate feedback loop. The model confidently produces internally consistent but factually incorrect outputs.

TRAPO enters this landscape by proposing a novel **semi-supervised RLVR paradigm**. It aims to strike a balance by leveraging the stability and correctness guidance of a *small* labeled set while still benefiting from a large pool of *unlabeled* data. The core insight is that external supervision, even if minimal, is crucial to anchor and stabilize consistency-based training on unlabeled samples, preventing the "vicious cycle" of reinforcing incorrect patterns. This approach bridges the gap between costly fully supervised methods and unstable unsupervised ones, offering a more practical and robust solution for LRM training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind TRAPO are multifaceted:

1. **Alleviate High Annotation Costs:** The most pressing motivation is to overcome the prohibitive cost of obtaining large-scale ground-truth labels required for fully supervised RLVR. TRAPO seeks to achieve comparable or superior performance with significantly less labeled data.
2. **Prevent Model Collapse in Unsupervised RLVR:** Recognizing the inherent instability and propensity for reinforcing incorrect patterns in purely unsupervised methods, TRAPO aims to introduce a mechanism that grounds the learning process with external correctness signals, thereby ensuring stable and effective policy optimization.
3. **Achieve Data Efficiency and Strong Generalization:** The goal is to develop a framework that can effectively utilize a large volume of readily available unlabeled data, guided by a small, carefully curated labeled set, to boost LLM reasoning capabilities and enable robust generalization to both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks.
4. **Establish a Robust Semi-supervised Learning Paradigm for RLVR:** The authors hypothesize that LLMs, similar to human learners, can generalize correct reasoning patterns from a few verified examples to a larger body of unlabeled problems. TRAPO seeks to formalize and operationalize this hypothesis within the RLVR framework.
5. **Address the "How to Learn" Challenge in Semi-supervised RLVR:** Simple combination of supervised and unsupervised losses yields marginal benefits. The paper aims to develop a sophisticated mechanism that identifies the *internal links* between labeled and unlabeled data, ensuring that only reasoning patterns verified on labeled instances are incorporated into RL training for unlabeled samples.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TRAPO (Trajectory-based Policy Optimization) introduces a novel mechanism to bridge labeled and unlabeled data by focusing on the **learning dynamics** of the model rather than direct semantic alignment of answers.

The core approach involves:

1. **Hybrid Reward Function:** TRAPO utilizes a semi-supervised reward function (`R_semi`) that applies traditional supervised RLVR rewards (based on ground-truth labels `y_i`) for labeled data (`D_l`) and unsupervised proxy rewards (`R_u`, e.g., majority voting consensus) for unlabeled data (`D_u`).
2. **Limitations of Naive Integration:** The authors empirically show that simply combining supervised and unsupervised RLVR provides only marginal benefits. This motivates the need for a deeper connection between the labeled and unlabeled sets.
3. **Trajectory-based Policy Optimization (TRAPO):** The key insight is that *reliable unlabeled samples should exhibit learning dynamics similar to those of labeled samples*. TRAPO leverages the concept of a **"pass rate trajectory"** to measure this dynamic similarity.
- **Pass Rate Calculation:** For each question `q` at epoch `t`, a (pseudo) pass rate (`P_q^(t)`) is calculated. For labeled questions, this is the fraction of generated responses that match the ground-truth answer. For unlabeled questions, it's the fraction of responses matching the majority-voted pseudo-answer (`y~_i^(t)`).
- **Trajectory Definition:** A question's trajectory (`T_q^(t)`) is the sequence of its pass rates across training epochs, `(P_q^(1), P_q^(2)..., P_q^(t))`.
- **Reliable Database and Reference Trajectory:** A `D_reliable` database is maintained, initialized with all labeled sample trajectories. The **average trajectory (`T_reliable^(t)`)** from this database serves as a trusted reference for what "good" learning dynamics look like.
- **Trajectory-based Cosine Similarity (TCS):** To identify reliable unlabeled samples, TRAPO computes the `TCS(T_u^(t), T_reliable^(t))` between an unlabeled sample's trajectory and the average reliable trajectory. Higher TCS indicates stronger alignment in learning dynamics.
- **Unlabeled Sample Selection:** A mask `M(u)` is used to select reliable unlabeled samples for training. This selection is based on two criteria: samples in the **top-p** percentile of TCS scores and any sample whose TCS score exceeds a predefined **threshold Γ**. The trajectories of selected unlabeled samples are then added to `D_reliable` for future averaging.
- **Loss Function Integration:** The overall loss function (`L(θ)`) combines the GRPO objective (Group Relative Policy Optimization, a common RLVR algorithm) for all labeled data and only for the `U_reliable` set of selected unlabeled data, `L(θ) = J_labeled_GRPO(θ) + M ⊙ J_unlabeled_GRPO(θ)`.
- **Warm-up Phase:** To ensure stability, TRAPO includes a warm-up phase where only labeled data is used for updates, while unlabeled trajectories are accumulated. Unlabeled data are only included in updates after this warm-up.
4. **Theoretical Foundation:** The paper provides a theoretical analysis, including a **Trajectory-Consistent Generalization Bound**. This theorem informally states that the generalization error is bounded by the empirical risk on labeled data, an irreducible domain shift, and a term directly related to `(1 - TCS)` (encouraging unlabeled samples to follow labeled learning dynamics) and `(1 - C_avg)` (where `C_avg` is average voting confidence). This provides theoretical grounding for why trajectory consistency and pseudo-label confidence are important for generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TRAPO demonstrates significant advancements in data efficiency and reasoning performance across various benchmarks:

1. **Superior Performance with Limited Labeled Data:**
- With **only 1K labeled and 3K unlabeled samples**, TRAPO achieves an average accuracy of **42.6%** on six in-distribution (ID) mathematical reasoning benchmarks, significantly surpassing the best unsupervised method (38.3%) which was trained on 45K unlabeled samples. This represents a 4.3% ID gain.
- It also outperforms naive semi-supervised baselines (combining 1K labeled and 3K unsupervised) by 2.6% (ID) and the supervised baseline (1K labeled) by 3.2% (ID) and 4.0% (OOD).
2. **Outperforming Fully Supervised Models with 10% Labeled Data:**
- When trained with **4K labeled and 12K unlabeled samples**, TRAPO achieves **45.6% ID and 59.7% OOD accuracy**. This not only surpasses all other semi-supervised and unsupervised baselines but also **outperforms the fully supervised model trained on the *full 45K labeled samples*** (45.5% ID and 57.3% OOD). This highlights an exceptional balance between data efficiency and learning effectiveness, using only 10% of the total labeled data.
3. **Strong Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Tasks:**
- TRAPO consistently shows strong OOD generalization, outperforming baselines on ARC-c, GPQA-diamond, and MMLU-pro. Its ability to guide learning even with OOD unlabeled data (using 1K labeled ID and 1K unlabeled OOD samples) is remarkable, achieving 41.0% ID and 56.9% OOD average accuracy, closely matching the fully supervised model trained on 2K labels.
4. **Effectiveness of Trajectory Matching:**
- Empirical analysis shows a strong positive correlation between trajectory similarity (TCS) and final performance of unlabeled samples. Samples with learning dynamics more aligned with labeled data achieve substantially higher performance (e.g., top 10% samples outperform bottom 10% by over 40%). This validates the core hypothesis of TRAPO.
5. **Robustness and Universal Applicability:**
- **Sensitivity Analysis:** TRAPO's performance is sensitive to `top-p` and `Γ` parameters (optimal at 0.1 for top-p and 0.5 for Γ, demonstrating intelligent denoising) and warm-up length (stabilizing with longer warm-up). It consistently outperforms random selection of unlabeled samples.
- **Model Agnostic:** TRAPO's effectiveness generalizes to other LLMs, including smaller models like DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and instruction-tuned models like LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, preventing performance collapse observed in unsupervised training.
- **Plug-and-Play Component:** The trajectory-based selection strategy can be integrated into various semi-supervised baselines (e.g., Sentence-level Entropy, Token-level Entropy, TTRL), consistently yielding performance gains, demonstrating its modularity.
- **DeepMath Dataset Validation:** TRAPO also performed strongly on the DeepMath dataset, demonstrating its applicability to new mathematical reasoning datasets, even surpassing fully supervised training on OOD metrics.
6. **Training Cost:** TRAPO incurs no substantial additional training cost compared to supervised RLVR, as its time complexity is dominated by rollout generation, just like standard RLVR.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TRAPO marks a significant advancement in the field of LLM reasoning, particularly for practical applications of RLVR. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key areas:

1. **Overcoming the Annotation Bottleneck:** By demonstrating that high-performance LRM reasoning can be achieved with a fraction (as low as 10%) of labeled data compared to fully supervised methods, TRAPO offers a powerful solution to the prohibitively high annotation costs. This opens doors for deploying advanced reasoning LLMs in specialized domains (e.g., medical diagnosis, financial analysis, scientific discovery) where ground-truth labels are scarce, expensive, or require expert knowledge.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Stability:** TRAPO directly addresses the critical issue of model collapse in unsupervised RLVR. By using labeled data as an "anchor" and guiding unlabeled learning through validated reasoning patterns, it ensures a stable and robust training process, preventing the model from reinforcing incorrect but internally consistent outputs. This stability is crucial for building trustworthy AI systems.
3. **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The framework's ability to generalize effectively to out-of-distribution tasks, even when trained on primarily in-domain labeled data and mixed unlabeled data, is a strong indicator of its capacity to teach fundamental reasoning principles rather than just memorizing task-specific patterns. This broadens the applicability of trained models.
4. **Novel Approach to Semi-Supervised Learning:** TRAPO's innovative use of "pass rate trajectories" to measure learning dynamics and identify reliable unlabeled samples represents a paradigm shift from traditional semi-supervised methods that rely on feature similarity or direct label propagation. This "how the model learns" approach is particularly well-suited for complex generative tasks like LLM reasoning, where output spaces are vast and highly diverse.
5. **Practical Applicability for Iterative Refinement:** The data efficiency and high quality of TRAPO-trained models suggest its potential integration into iterative human-in-the-loop or active learning pipelines. Models trained with TRAPO could generate high-quality pseudo-labels or reasoning paths, which humans can then efficiently review and correct, further enhancing data quality and model performance over time.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** TRAPO opens new avenues for research into understanding and leveraging the learning dynamics of LLMs. Future work could explore what types of labeled examples are most effective in guiding unlabeled data training (akin to active learning), the applicability to even larger models (beyond 7B/8B parameters), and further theoretical grounding of trajectory-based learning.

In conclusion, TRAPO presents a sophisticated and highly effective semi-supervised framework that skillfully navigates the challenges of data scarcity and model instability in RLVR, offering a compelling path toward more practical, efficient, and robust large reasoning models.
