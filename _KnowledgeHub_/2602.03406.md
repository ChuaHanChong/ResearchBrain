# Deep-Learning-Based Control of a Decoupled Two-Segment Continuum Robot for Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection

> [!summary] Summary
> DESectBot, a novel two-segment continuum robot developed by a multi-institutional team, integrates a deep learning-based Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) controller for precise endoscopic submucosal dissection. The system achieves sub-millimeter positional accuracy and sub-degree orientational accuracy in complex trajectory tracking, and successfully completes ex vivo tissue manipulation.

## Problem

- Manual Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection (ESD) is technically demanding, requires extensive training, and carries a high risk of complications.
- Traditional endoscopes and existing single-segment robotic tools offer limited maneuverability, precision, and range of motion for intricate surgical tasks in complex GI anatomies.
- Multi-segment continuum robots, while offering higher dexterity, introduce significant control complexity due to increased degrees of freedom and inherent nonlinear dynamics, and deep learning methods had not been systematically applied for their simultaneous position and orientation control in endoscopy.

## Method

- Developed DESectBot, a novel dual-segment continuum robot featuring a decoupled design, 6 DoFs tip dexterity, and an integrated surgical forceps end effector.
- Implemented a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network as a data-driven controller to learn the inverse kinematics for simultaneous position and orientation control of DESectBot.
- Generated a large, diverse dataset using a Monte Carlo method and trained the GRU controller, comparing its performance against other deep learning models (FNN, LSTM) and model-based methods (Jacobian, ==MPC==).

## Results

- The GRU controller achieved the lowest Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for position (**1.11** mm) and orientation (**4.62**°) on a nested rectangle path, and **0.81** mm (position) and **2.59**° (orientation) on a Lissajous curve, outperforming all other tested control methods.
- In orientation control at fixed points, the GRU controller demonstrated superior performance with a mean RMSE of **0.14** mm in position and **0.72**° in orientation, reducing errors by up to **51%** compared to alternatives.
- DESectBot, guided by the GRU controller, achieved a **100%** success rate in a unilateral peg-transfer task with an average transfer time of **11.8** seconds, positioning it competitively as a platform for ESD skill training and demonstrating successful ex vivo tissue manipulation on porcine gastric mucosa.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GRU networks effectively model the complex, nonlinear inverse kinematics of multi-segment continuum robots, outperforming LSTMs, FNNs, and model-based methods (Jacobian, MPC) in precision and computational efficiency.
> - The decoupled design of DESectBot's two segments, combined with the GRU controller, enables high-fidelity simultaneous position and orientation control, critical for delicate endoscopic procedures.
> - Deep learning-based control offers a robust approach to mitigate inherent challenges like hysteresis, friction, and inter-segment coupling in continuum robots, significantly enhancing their accuracy and reliability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Gastrointestinal (GI) cancers represent a significant global health burden, with high mortality rates emphasizing the need for advancements in early diagnosis and effective treatments. Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection (ESD) is a minimally invasive procedure offering advantages over conventional methods, such as lower recurrence rates and the ability to excise larger lesions en bloc. However, manual ESD is technically demanding, requiring extensive training and posing risks of complications. Traditional endoscopes further limit maneuverability and precision in complex GI anatomies, and their manual control can lead to physician fatigue and inconsistent outcomes.

To address these limitations, robotic technology has been integrated into endoscopy. Continuum robots, known for their flexibility and ability to navigate natural orifices, have gained traction. Early robotic endoscopic tools, often single-segment, demonstrated limited flexibility, range of motion, and stable visualization, particularly in intricate regions like the esophago-gastric junction. The development of multi-segment robotic endoscopes has emerged as a promising direction, offering increased degrees of freedom (DoFs) and higher distal dexterity for precise surgical maneuvers. While multi-segment robots provide enhanced capabilities, they introduce challenges such as increased nonlinearities and greater control complexity due to more actuators.

Deep learning (DL) methods have been increasingly applied in continuum robot control to model complex, nonlinear dynamics and improve precision. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks have been explored for tasks like predicting distal-end forces and compensating for hysteresis. However, prior to this work, DL methods had not been systematically applied to the control of two-segment endoscopic robots for the challenging task of simultaneous position and orientation control, a critical requirement for advanced ESD procedures. This research endeavors to fill this gap by developing and validating a DL-based control approach for a novel dual-segment continuum robot.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the technical demands and limitations associated with current ESD procedures and robotic tools:
- **Technical Difficulty of Manual ESD:** Manual ESD requires advanced technical skills and extensive training, carrying a high risk of complications.
- **Limitations of Traditional Endoscopes:** Conventional endoscopes offer limited maneuverability and precision, hindering accurate targeting and resection of lesions, especially in anatomically complex GI regions. This reliance on manual dexterity contributes to variability in outcomes and places significant physical strain on clinicians during prolonged procedures.
- **Dexterity of Existing Robotic Tools:** Current single-segment robotic tools provide limited dexterity and range of motion, struggling to maintain stable visualization and adequately access lesions in tight spaces.
- **Control Complexity of Multi-segment Robots:** While multi-segment robots offer superior dexterity, their increased degrees of freedom and inherent nonlinearities present significant control challenges.
- **Gap in Deep Learning Application:** Despite the proven capability of deep learning in modeling complex dynamics, its application to two-segment endoscopic robots for simultaneous position and orientation control, essential for ESD, remained largely unexplored.

Based on these motivations, the study set forth several key objectives:
1. **Develop DESectBot:** To establish a novel dual-segment continuum robot, named DESectBot, with a decoupled structure and an integrated surgical forceps end effector, designed to provide 6 DoFs tip dexterity for ESD.
2. **Implement and Validate DL-based Control:** To propose and implement a data-driven control method, specifically a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network, for modeling the inverse kinematics of DESectBot. This objective includes validating the GRU controller's precision in simultaneous position and orientation control through benchtop experiments, comparing its performance against both model-based (Jacobian, MPC) and other data-driven (FNN, LSTM) methods.
3. **Benchmark for Surgical Training:** To carry out a benchmark peg-transfer task using the GRU-driven DESectBot and compare its performance with established surgical robots, thereby assessing its suitability as a standardized platform for ESD skill training.
4. **Ex Vivo Evaluation:** To conduct an ex vivo ESD demonstration to confirm DESectBot's manipulation capabilities, including grasping, elevating, and resecting tissue, and to evaluate its stiffness and operative workspace for handling gastric mucosa and lesions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involved the design and construction of the DESectBot system, the development and comparative evaluation of various control strategies, and extensive experimental validation.

**System Design (DESectBot):**
DESectBot is a novel continuum robot designed to offer 6 DoFs through four integrated modules:
- **Actuation Module:** Comprises seven DC motors (Maxon ECXSP16L with GPX16HP gearboxes) controlled by a multi-axis motion controller (Elmo P-MASON0N). Five motors actuate the NiTi tendons (0.5 mm diameter), while one controls rotation and another translation of the entire module.
- **Passive Bending Module:** A 1.2-meter ebonite sheath through which NiTi tendons are routed, providing support for the continuum structure.
- **Double Active Bending Module (DABM):** Consists of proximal and distal bending segments mechanically decoupled by a rigid mid-transition segment. Each segment employs a dual continuum manipulator mechanism based on Spatial Cross-Curved Disk (SCD) joints (SCD-1 for proximal, SCD-2 for distal). Tendon guide holes are arranged to enable full 3D bending with minimal inter-segment coupling.
- **Surgical Forceps End Effector:** A 5mm diameter, 20mm length surgical forceps is integrated at the distal tip of the DABM via a custom sleeve. It features a compact antagonistic NiTi cable-driven mechanism for tissue manipulation.
- **Sensing:** A 6 DoFs electromagnetic (EM) tracker (Northern Digital Inc. Aurora V3.1) is embedded in the tool segment to provide real-time tip pose feedback.
- **Hysteresis Mitigation:** The design incorporates tendon pre-tensioning and low-friction sheath routing to mitigate inherent hysteresis, friction, and load-dependent drift in the tendon–sheath mechanism. The components are biocompatible and compatible with sterilization procedures.

**Robot Control Methods:**
Five control methods were developed and compared:
- **Jacobian Kinematic Control:** A model-based approach building on a constant-curvature assumption for each continuum segment, extended to include the surgical forceps. It provides closed-form expressions for forward and differential kinematics and computes instantaneous kinematics using a Jacobian matrix.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** A nonlinear model-based closed-loop controller that utilizes predictions of future outputs to determine optimal system inputs. It solves an optimization function at each control moment to minimize pose error, considering future states and system constraints.
- **Data-Driven Inverse-Kinematics Networks:**
 - **Feed-forward Neural Network (FNN):** A static, memoryless model that learns a direct mapping from a fixed window of past tip error vectors to current actuator commands.
 - **Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Network:** A memory-based recurrent neural network designed to capture long-range, nonlinear interactions. It takes a sequence of past robot states (pose and motor commands) as input to predict current motor rotation counts.
 - **Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) Controller:** Another memory-based recurrent neural network, conceptually similar to LSTM but with reduced complexity. It uses update and reset gates to capture temporal dynamics. The GRU receives a 12-dimensional input vector (desired tip pose and previous motor rotation counts) and targets the current motor-rotation vector.

**Data Acquisition and Training:**
- **Setup:** The DESectBot was installed horizontally using four support brackets for the passive bending module, with the NDI EM tracker tip affixed to the DABM's tool connection segment.
- **Data Generation:** Training data were generated using a Monte Carlo method, randomly generating trajectories over 4080 seconds to ensure broad coverage of the robot's workspace. The NDI sensor sampled position and orientation at 5 Hz, yielding 20400 samples. The dataset was split into 14280 (training), 4080 (validation), and 2040 (test) samples.
- **Hardware:** All data-driven models were trained on an 8 GB NVIDIA RTX 4060Ti GPU.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** A systematic ablation study compared GRU and LSTM architectures, varying hidden units (64, 128, 256) and layers (3, 4, 5). The optimal GRU configuration was found to be four layers with 128 hidden units, demonstrating lower validation loss and better computational efficiency.
- **Workspace for Training/Evaluation:** Training data were confined to a dome-shaped workspace of ϕ 60 × 45 mm. Evaluation trajectories (nested-rectangle and Lissajous) were specifically defined within a smaller, distinct sub-region (40×30 mm for the rectangle, 15 mm amplitude for Lissajous) and entirely excluded from the training dataset to ensure independent performance assessment.

**Experimental Setup and Calibration:**
- **Control Implementation:** Model-based controllers were implemented in C++ using Maxon's CAN-based API for low latency. Learning-based controllers were developed in Python (PyTorch) and interfaced with the C++ motor drivers via a Flask REST API over an isolated TCP/IP network.
- **Control Frequency:** All controllers operated at 5 Hz, with mean inference times ranging from 5.02 ms (FNN) to 17.45 ms (MPC).
- **Calibration:** The NDI planar field generator established the global frame. An auto-reset script zeroed motor counts after each run. Manual adjustments guided by real-time NDI ToolBox feedback calibrated the end effector to within ±0.5 mm in position and ±0.3° in Euler orientation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation yielded several key findings regarding the performance of DESectBot and its various control methods:

**Ablation Study (GRU vs. LSTM):**
- The GRU architecture consistently achieved lower validation loss and faster convergence rates compared to LSTM across all tested configurations (varying hidden units and layers).
- The optimal GRU model (4 layers, 128 hidden units) had approximately 75% fewer trainable parameters (350208) than the best-performing LSTM model (466944), indicating higher computational efficiency.

**Position and Orientation Control (Trajectory Tracking):**
- **Nested Rectangle Path:**
 - The GRU controller demonstrated superior precision and trial-to-trial consistency, achieving the lowest overall Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for both position (1.11 mm) and orientation (4.62°).
 - This performance surpassed LSTM (1.13 mm / 4.99°), MPC (1.89 mm / 4.99°), FNN (1.50 mm / 6.80°), and Jacobian (2.38 mm / 9.70°).
 - GRU exhibited lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in position (X: 0.57 mm, Y: 0.45 mm, Z: 0.62 mm) and orientation (X: 1.59°, Y: 2.99°, Z: 2.12°) compared to most alternatives, along with lower standard deviations (STD), indicating enhanced robustness.
- **Lissajous Curve Path:**
 - For this complex curved trajectory, the GRU controller again achieved the lowest positional RMSE of 0.81 mm and orientational RMSE of 2.59°.
 - It outperformed LSTM (1.07 mm / 2.65°), MPC (1.19 mm / 3.80°), FNN (1.58 mm / 4.52°), and Jacobian (1.61 mm / 6.86°).
 - These results suggest that the GRU effectively compensates for nonlinear coupling in the dual-segment continuum, maintaining high tracking fidelity for curved motions.

**Orientation Control with Fixed Tip Position:**
- Experiments involved rotating the DABM tip's orientation at four fixed spatial points (P1-P4).
- Across all four positions, the GRU controller demonstrated superior performance, achieving a mean RMSE of 0.14 mm in position and 0.72° in orientation.
- This was a significant improvement compared to LSTM (0.17 mm / 0.96°), FNN (0.435 mm / 1.24°), MPC (0.24 mm / 1.46°), and the Jacobian method (0.49 mm / 3.14°).
- The GRU reduced positional RMSE by approximately 18% relative to LSTM and 36% relative to MPC, while orientation RMSE was lowered by 25% and 51%, respectively.
- The GRU also consistently achieved minimal STD values for most pose cases, indicating high robustness in orientation control.

**Peg Transfer Benchmark and Ex Vivo Evaluation:**
- **Unilateral Peg-Transfer Task:**
 - Using a 6 DoFs master haptic interface, six volunteer participants performed a unilateral peg-transfer task involving 120 sequential transfers.
 - The GRU-driven DESectBot achieved a 100% success rate (120/120 attempts) with an average transfer time of 11.8 seconds.
 - The standard deviation for per-transfer time was 3.93 seconds.
 - **Benchmarking:** This performance was competitive, being faster than novice-controlled dVRK systems (12.3 s mean, 95.9% success) and 21.3% faster than volunteers using the IDCM platform (15 s mean, 100% success), with a significantly lower STD for transfer time than novice dVRK users.
- **Ex Vivo ESD Demonstration:**
 - A simplified ESD procedure was conducted on porcine gastric mucosa, where the DESectBot performed grasping, elevating, and resecting tissue.
 - The successful completion of these key steps confirmed that the robot possesses sufficient stiffness to manipulate thick gastric mucosa and offers an operative workspace adequate for addressing lesions. The total cutting duration was 403 seconds, comparable to existing robotic ESD systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DESectBot, a novel dual-segment continuum robot, and demonstrates the effectiveness of a GRU-based deep learning control strategy for enhancing its precision and reliability in endoscopic submucosal dissection. The findings have several implications:

- **Enhanced Surgical Precision and Reliability:** The GRU-based controller consistently outperformed both model-based and other data-driven methods in complex trajectory tracking and orientation control tasks, achieving sub-millimetric positional accuracy and precise orientation. This level of control fidelity is crucial for safely and effectively performing intricate ESD procedures.
- **Addressing Control Challenges in Continuum Robotics:** The successful application of GRU networks highlights their capability to effectively model and compensate for the nonlinear, time-varying coupling between continuum segments, which is a fundamental challenge in controlling such complex robotic systems.
- **Suitability for ESD Skill Training:** The robust performance in the peg-transfer task, characterized by a 100% success rate and competitive transfer times, positions DESectBot as a promising platform for standardized ESD skill training. This could potentially shorten the learning curve for surgeons and improve the accessibility of advanced endoscopic techniques.
- **Advancement Towards Clinical Translation:** The ex vivo demonstration on porcine gastric mucosa provided initial evidence of DESectBot's practical manipulation capabilities, including sufficient stiffness for tissue division and an adequate operative workspace. These results contribute to advancing the robot's readiness for preclinical and ultimately clinical integration.
- **Foundation for Future Robotic Surgery:** This work establishes a foundation for integrating sophisticated deep learning control with advanced continuum robot designs, paving the way for more autonomous and intelligent robotic assistance in minimally invasive surgery.

**Future Work:**
The authors outline plans to improve the control update rate through faster pose-sensing modalities and optimization of the control and communication pipeline. Further developments include integrating haptic and shape sensing for enhanced feedback, implementing real-time self-calibration to counteract tendon hysteresis and encoder drift, and recruiting professional surgeons for comprehensive comparative assessments. Investigations into workspace coordination, collision avoidance, intelligent assistance features (e.g., image-based lesion detection and motion scaling), and comprehensive preclinical animal trials are also foreseen to facilitate robust clinical translation and broaden the robot's applicability.
