# REVISOR: Beyond Textual Reflection, Towards Multimodal Introspective Reasoning in Long-Form Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xiaomi Inc. and Renmin University of China developed REVISOR, a framework that enhances long-form video understanding by integrating tool-augmented multimodal reflection into MLLMs. This approach, leveraging a two-stage reasoning process and a Dual Attribution Decoupled Reward (DADR) mechanism, achieved an average accuracy improvement of approximately 2% on video understanding benchmarks and set new state-of-the-art results for temporal video grounding with 51.4% mIoU on Charades-STA.

## Problem

- Existing text-based self-reflection mechanisms in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are inadequate for long-form video understanding due to the complex, dynamic visual content.
- Purely text-centric reflection struggles to detect and correct reasoning errors originating from misinterpretations within extensive visual streams.
- Current multimodal reflection lacks genuine cross-modal interaction, preventing MLLMs from fully integrating and re-evaluating visual information during reasoning.

## Method

- Introduced **REVISOR**, a two-stage ==tool-augmented== multimodal reflection framework for MLLMs.
- The framework proposes relevant video segments in an initial inference stage, then uses a "Visual Toolbox" to re-sample these segments at a higher density for reflective reasoning.
- Developed the Dual Attribution Decoupled Reward (DADR) mechanism, which incorporates a Causal Segment Sufficiency Reward (CSSR) to guide reinforcement learning in identifying truly relevant visual evidence.

## Results

- Achieved an average accuracy improvement of approximately **2%** on long-form video understanding benchmarks, including a **2.8%** gain on longer video subsets of VideoMME.
- Established state-of-the-art performance in temporal video grounding, reaching **51.4%** mIoU on Charades-STA and outperforming previous methods like iMOVE by **4.1%** mIoU.
- Demonstrated through ablation studies that the Causal Segment Sufficiency Reward (CSSR) within DADR is crucial for learning to correctly locate review segments, with its removal leading to a performance degradation below the base model on VideoMME.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Purely text-based reflection is insufficient for long-form video understanding; true multimodal introspection, including specific visual re-evaluation, is necessary.
> - Explicitly rewarding the model for identifying causally sufficient video segments through the DADR mechanism is critical for learning to locate relevant visual evidence in sparse reward environments.
> - The model can learn to precisely identify minimal, sufficient video segments, demonstrating that forcing longer textual reflection can degrade performance in video tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen substantial advancements, enabling models to process and reason across various data types, including text and images. A significant area of research within MLLMs is the development of advanced reasoning strategies, particularly those involving self-reflection. Traditionally, self-reflection mechanisms, inspired by cognitive processes, have been applied to Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance their ability to handle complex reasoning by reviewing, evaluating, and revising their thought processes. These mechanisms aim to mitigate issues like repetitive or incorrect reasoning steps, which often arise from token-level Markov assumptions that rely on local contextual dependencies.

When these self-reflection mechanisms were extended to multimodal settings, they often retained a purely text-based re-evaluation approach. While effective for many general multimodal tasks, such as image understanding (e.g., interpreting scientific figures or performing geometric reasoning), their efficacy diminishes when applied to long-form video understanding. Long-form videos present unique challenges due to their rich, dynamic, and extensive visual content, which far exceeds the complexity of static images. Existing text-only reflection processes struggle to integrate and re-evaluate this dense visual information adequately.

Previous efforts in long-form video understanding have largely fallen into three categories: external-model augmentation, agentic methods, and model-internalized selection. External-model augmentation often relies on separate vision-text similarity models for static keyframe selection. Agentic methods employ iterative frameworks, sometimes using LLMs as central agents, to dynamically refine frame selection. Model-internalized selection methods embed frame selection directly into the model’s reasoning but often maintain a decoupled relationship between selection and question-answering stages.

This work positions itself by diagnosing the limitations of conventional text-based self-reflection in the context of long-form video understanding and proposing a novel framework that moves beyond this paradigm. It integrates a tool-augmented multimodal reflection process directly within the MLLM’s reasoning, allowing for a collaborative introspection across both textual and visual modalities. By enabling the model to autonomously identify and re-examine critical video segments, REVISOR aims to bridge the gap between advanced reasoning capabilities in text-centric MLLMs and the specific demands of dynamic, long-duration visual data. It specifically differentiates from prior work by integrating the visual content review more deeply and causally into the reflection process, rather than relying on static or indirectly guided selection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from observed limitations of existing text-based self-reflection mechanisms when applied to long-form video understanding. The authors identify two fundamental reasons for this diminished performance:

1. **Insufficient Visual Rethinking for Dynamic Content:** Long-form videos contain significantly richer and more dynamic visual input compared to static images or short video clips. Text-only reflection processes are inherently limited in their ability to detect and correct reasoning errors that originate from misinterpretations or oversights within the visual stream. The sheer volume and temporal nature of video data mean that rethinking only the textual representation derived from a sparsely sampled video is inadequate; a specific process for re-evaluating visual information is necessary.
2. **Lack of Cross-Modal Interaction in Reflection:** Existing purely text-based reflection mechanisms operate primarily within the textual modality, even in multimodal contexts. They lack the capability to genuinely integrate and interact with visual information during the reflection process. This absence of cross-modal interaction prevents MLLMs from fully leveraging the visual cues to refine their reasoning, thereby restricting their potential for improvement in video understanding tasks.

Motivated by these insights, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

- **Develop a Novel Multimodal Reflection Framework:** To propose REVISOR (REflective VIsual Segment Oriented Reasoning), a two-stage reasoning framework that transforms conventional text-based self-reflection into a tool-augmented, multimodal process. This framework is designed to enable MLLMs to collaboratively construct introspective reflection processes that span both textual and visual modalities.
- **Enhance Long-Form Video Reasoning Capability:** To significantly improve the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs for long-form video understanding tasks by integrating this multimodal reflection. This includes addressing the challenges posed by extended video durations and complex visual dynamics.
- **Ensure Accurate Visual Segment Identification:** To design and integrate a mechanism that allows REVISOR to learn to accurately review video segments highly relevant to a given question during reinforcement learning. This involves enforcing causal alignment between the model's reasoning and the selected visual evidence.
- **Achieve Performance Gains Without Extensive External Resources:** To enhance MLLM performance in long-form video understanding without requiring supplementary supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or the integration of external models, thereby promoting efficiency and ease of deployment.

These objectives aim to overcome the inherent limitations of text-centric reasoning in visual-heavy tasks, pushing MLLMs towards a more comprehensive and introspective understanding of dynamic video content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The REVISOR framework is a novel two-stage reasoning approach designed to enhance long-form video understanding through tool-augmented multimodal reflection. It is structured to allow MLLMs to collaboratively construct introspective reflection processes across textual and visual modalities.

#### 3.1. Tool-Augmented Multimodal Reflection

The REVISOR framework operates in two distinct stages:

- **Stage 1: Initial Inference and Visual Review Proposal:**
 1. **Initial Frame Sampling:** Given a video $V$ and a user question $Q$, the process begins by performing a sparse, uniform sampling of frames from the video to create an initial frame set $F_{init}$. This step helps manage the computational cost and context-length limitations of the MLLM for the initial pass.
 2. **Preliminary Reasoning:** An MLLM, denoted as $M_{infer}$, takes $Q$ and $F_{init}$ as input. It then generates a preliminary chain-of-thought reasoning trace ($T_{init}$).
 3. **Segment Proposal:** Crucially, alongside its preliminary reasoning, the MLLM is prompted to identify and output a specific temporal segment $S = [t_{start}, t_{end}]$ from the video that it deems most relevant or uncertain for its conclusion. This segment proposal emerges naturally from the model’s reasoning process.

- **Visual Toolbox Call: Retrieve Review Segment Frames:**
 1. **Targeted Re-sampling:** Upon receiving the proposed segment $S$ from the MLLM, the REVISOR framework activates a "Visual Toolbox," denoted as $T$. This toolbox's function is to perform a targeted, high-density re-sampling of frames from the original video $V$, specifically within the temporal window defined by $S$. This results in a denser frame sequence, $F_{review}$.
 2. **Offloading Computational Burden:** This tool-assisted step allows the MLLM to focus its detailed examination on critical moments without needing to process the entire long-form video at full resolution, thereby managing computational overhead and context window limitations.

- **Stage 2: Reflective Reasoning and Answer Refinement:**
 1. **Enriched Context:** The MLLM is re-invoked in a "reflection mode" ($M_{reflect}$). It receives the original question $Q$, its own initial reasoning trace $T_{init}$, and the newly acquired, densely sampled visual evidence $F_{review}$.
 2. **Iterative Refinement:** With this enriched context, the MLLM reassesses its preliminary conclusions. It validates earlier hypotheses, resolves ambiguities identified in Stage 1, or corrects prior misinterpretations.
 3. **Final Output:** This process leads to the generation of a refined reasoning trace ($T_{refine}$) and a final, more accurate answer ($A_{final}$). This iterative refinement mirrors human expert analysis, moving from an overview to a focused examination of critical evidence.

#### 3.2. Dual Attribution Decoupled Reward (DADR) Mechanism

To effectively train the REVISOR framework using reinforcement learning (specifically, the GRPO algorithm), the authors introduce the Dual Attribution Decoupled Reward (DADR) mechanism. This addresses the limitation that a single, sparse reward based solely on the final answer’s correctness is insufficient to guide the model in learning to accurately identify relevant video review segments.

- **Problem with Standard Reward:** Training with only final-answer verification rewards (as in traditional GRPO) led to degraded performance. The complex, multi-component reasoning process of REVISOR (initial reasoning, segment proposal, reflective reasoning) meant that correct segment predictions received insufficient positive feedback, and incorrect ones lacked adequate penalties, hindering effective learning of key segment identification.
- **DADR Solution:** The total reward $R(\tau)$ for a reasoning trajectory $\tau$ is decoupled into two components:
 $R(\tau) = \lambda_1 R_{final} + \lambda_2 R_{causal}$
 where $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ are hyperparameters controlling the balance.
 1. **Final Answer Verification Reward ($R_{final}$):** This is a standard task-specific reward that verifies the correctness of the final refined answer $A_{final}$ against the ground truth answer $A^*$. It directly targets the accuracy objective of the reflective stage.
 2. **Causal Segment Sufficiency Reward (CSSR, $R_{causal}$):** This component is specifically designed to enforce the quality and relevance of the proposed review segment $S$ (from Stage 1).
 - **Sufficiency Test:** The MLLM (in a sufficiency prediction mode $M_{suff}$) is prompted to derive an attribution-based answer ($\hat{A}$) *solely* based on the original question $Q$ and the fine-grained visual evidence $F_{review}$ extracted *only* from the proposed segment $S$.
 - **Reward Definition:** $R_{causal} = I(\hat{A} = A^*)$, meaning a positive reward is given only if this attribution-based prediction is correct.
 - **Purpose:** This implicitly encourages the MLLM to select segments that are truly causal and sufficient for answering the question, discouraging the selection of irrelevant or overly long segments.
- **Training Integration:** By incorporating $R_{causal}$ into the GRPO optimization loop, DADR guides the REVISOR framework toward policies that not only yield accurate final answers but also robustly identify the critical temporal evidence in the initial inference stage.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The base model used is Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Training is conducted with a single reinforcement learning phase, extending the verl framework. Key hyperparameters include $\lambda_1 = 0.6$, $\lambda_2 = 0.3$, a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-6}$, batch size of 32, and 8 rollouts. Input video tokens are capped at 8192, sampled at 1 FPS for initial inference and 2 FPS for the review segment. The training dataset comprises 25K samples from various sources, including short video QA (LLaVA-Video-178K, CLEVRER, NExT-QA, PerceptionTest, STAR), temporal grounding (TimeRFT), and grounded video QA (CG-Bench, ReXTime).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the REVISOR framework encompassed several benchmarks and detailed analyses, demonstrating its capabilities in long-form video understanding and temporal video grounding.

#### 4.1. Long-Form Video Understanding Task

REVISOR was evaluated on four benchmarks: VideoMME, LongVideoBench, MLVU, and LVBench.
- **Overall Performance:** REVISOR, utilizing 8K video tokens, achieved an average accuracy improvement of approximately 2% over the base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Specifically, it reached 65.7% on VideoMME, outperforming the base model by 1.4%.
- **Performance on Longer Videos:** The framework demonstrated more substantial improvements on longer video subsets, such as a 2.8% gain on the long subset of VideoMME and 2.5% on MLVU (which includes videos up to 120 minutes). This suggests that the ability to accurately review relevant video content becomes increasingly critical as video duration increases.
- **Comparison to Text-Only Reflection:** REVISOR showed clear advantages over purely text-based reasoning approaches. It outperformed Video-R1 (a text-only reasoning method) by 4.3% on VideoMME. Compared to VL-Rethinker and a custom-trained, video-data-equipped text-only reflection model, REVISOR achieved gains of 3.6% and 2.3% respectively, underscoring the necessity of visual rethinking.

#### 4.2. Temporal Video Grounding Task

REVISOR's capability in localizing specific video segments was assessed on Charades-STA and NExT-GQA.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** REVISOR achieved 51.4% mIoU on Charades-STA, surpassing the previous SFT-based state-of-the-art method iMOVE by 4.1% mIoU.
- **Reinforcement Learning Comparison:** It also outperformed TVG-R1, an RL-based temporal grounding model, by 4.7% mIoU on Charades-STA and 3.9% mIoU on NExT-GQA, indicating superior temporal localization abilities.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An in-depth investigation into the DADR mechanism and training data composition provided insights into REVISOR's components.
- **Dual Attribution Decoupled Reward (DADR):**
 - Setting $\lambda_2$ to 0 (i.e., relying only on the Final Answer Verification Reward, $R_{final}$) resulted in a significant performance drop on VideoMME from 65.7% to 62.2%, falling below the base model's 64.3%. This confirms that the Causal Segment Sufficiency Reward (CSSR) is essential for the model to learn to locate correct review segments from sparse reward signals.
 - When $\lambda_2$ exceeded $\lambda_1$, temporal video grounding improved, but long-form video understanding performance declined (e.g., MLVU dropped from 69.8% to 68.7%). This indicated that an overemphasis on segment localization can lead to neglect of effectively utilizing the segment for answer derivation. The optimal balance found was $\lambda_1 = 0.6$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.3$.
- **Data Composition:** REVISOR's long-form video understanding capabilities improved with the inclusion of more diverse datasets, demonstrating a rise in MLVU performance from 66.7% to 69.8%. Even training exclusively on short video QA datasets, when combined with DADR, led to an increase in NExT-GQA mIoU from 20.9% to 25%, indicating effective translation of understanding into accurate segment localization.

#### 4.4. Extended Analysis

- **Importance of Visual Rethinking:** During training, the length of textual reasoning steadily decreased, while the length of reviewed video segments initially increased and then decreased. This suggests that the model learns to recognize the minor role of textual reflection in long-form video understanding and to precisely locate minimal, sufficient video segments. Furthermore, experiments showed that forcing the model to perform longer textual reflection paradoxically led to a degradation in performance on video understanding tasks.
- **Accuracy of Visual Information Retrieval:** The DADR mechanism was shown to improve the accuracy of retrospective video segment extraction. Models trained with DADR identified more relevant video segments, as evidenced by higher answer accuracy when these segments were provided to a base MLLM. DADR also outperformed four temporal grounding methods in recalling relevant video segments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, REVISOR, that addresses a critical limitation in the application of current self-reflection mechanisms to long-form video understanding. By diagnosing the inadequacy of purely text-based reflection for rich, dynamic visual content, the work highlights a fundamental gap in multimodal reasoning capabilities.

The significance of REVISOR lies in several key aspects:
- **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning:** It introduces a truly multimodal introspective reasoning process, moving beyond text-centric reflection. This framework enables MLLMs to actively engage with and re-evaluate visual information, which is crucial for complex tasks involving long-duration dynamic content.
- **Improved Long-Form Video Understanding:** REVISOR consistently demonstrates significant performance improvements across multiple challenging benchmarks for long-form video understanding. The greater gains observed on longer videos underscore its effectiveness in scenarios where managing extensive visual context is paramount.
- **Efficient Knowledge Acquisition:** The framework achieves these enhancements without requiring supplementary supervised fine-tuning or the integration of external models. This design choice contributes to the efficiency and scalability of the approach, making it more practical for real-world applications.
- **Causal Evidence Learning:** The Dual Attribution Decoupled Reward (DADR) mechanism is a notable contribution. By explicitly incentivizing the model to identify and utilize causally sufficient video segments, DADR not only improves temporal grounding abilities but also provides a more robust method for training MLLMs to derive answers from verifiable visual evidence. This mechanism has broader implications for teaching models to attribute their conclusions to specific, relevant multimodal inputs.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** REVISOR lays a foundation for future research in more sophisticated, human-like iterative reasoning within complex multimodal environments. Its findings regarding the superior importance of visual reflection over textual reflection in video understanding challenge conventional wisdom and suggest new directions for designing MLLMs capable of deeper visual comprehension and introspection.

The potential impact of this work extends to various applications requiring accurate and robust understanding of extended video content, such as surveillance, video summarization, autonomous driving, content moderation, and educational tools. By enabling MLLMs to "think slowly" and "look again" at critical visual evidence, REVISOR contributes to the development of more reliable and interpretable AI systems for multimodal tasks.
