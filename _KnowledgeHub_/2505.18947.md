# OpenHOI: Open-World Hand-Object Interaction Synthesis with Multimodal Large Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenHOI introduces a framework for generating physically plausible 3D hand-object interaction sequences for unseen objects and open-vocabulary instructions. It integrates a 3D Multimodal Large Language Model for instruction understanding and affordance reasoning with an affordance-driven diffusion model and a training-free physical refinement stage, achieving state-of-the-art performance across various metrics.

## Problem

- Existing 3D hand-object interaction (HOI) synthesis methods struggle with generalization to 'unseen' objects and are limited to closed datasets.
- Prior approaches often fail to respond to free-form, open-vocabulary language commands and generate long-horizon, multi-stage manipulation sequences coherently.
- Many generated HOI sequences lack physical plausibility, exhibiting interpenetration or unnatural motion, and existing 3D MLLMs mainly focus on perception, not dynamic interaction synthesis.

## Method

- A two-stage framework: (1) Instruction Decomposition and ==Affordance Reasoning== using a fine-tuned 3D MLLM (ShapeLLM backbone) to generate spatial affordance maps and temporal sub-task sequences.
- (2) Affordance-driven HOI Diffusion for motion synthesis, conditioned on MLLM outputs and object point clouds, employing a transformer-based denoising diffusion model.
- A training-free physical refinement strategy applied during diffusion sampling, utilizing spherical Gaussian constraints to minimize hand-object penetration, ensure precise affordance contact, and maintain smooth motion transitions for long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- OpenHOI achieved superior quantitative performance over state-of-the-art methods across GRAB and ARCTIC datasets, showing lower MPJPE, FOL, and FID, particularly on unseen object splits (e.g., MPJPE of **51.34** vs. Text2HOI's **60.67** on unseen GRAB).
- The framework demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, synthesizing realistic and coherent long-horizon HOI sequences for completely unseen objects and complex, open-vocabulary instructions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of each core component, including affordance awareness, classifier-free guidance, and physical/motion in-between refinement, for achieving robust, physically plausible, and temporally coherent interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining 3D MLLMs for semantic understanding and affordance reasoning with diffusion models for motion synthesis effectively bridges 3D perception and dynamic interaction generation in an open-world setting.
> - Explicit affordance grounding from an MLLM, refined through a coarse-to-fine tuning strategy, is critical for achieving semantically meaningful and physically plausible hand-object interactions with novel objects.
> - Training-free physical refinement, guided by task-specific losses during diffusion sampling, provides a robust mechanism to enhance physical realism and temporal coherence without introducing distribution shifts or requiring re-training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Understanding and synthesizing realistic 3D hand-object interactions (HOI) is a cornerstone of advancing artificial intelligence capabilities for a wide array of human-centric applications, from immersive augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR) experiences to sophisticated, dexterous robotics. The broader research landscape for HOI synthesis has seen significant advancements, yet it is characterized by persistent challenges in generalization.

Historically, HOI synthesis has progressed from methods relying on handcrafted motion priors to more recent data-driven and diffusion-based approaches. Datasets like GigaHands and ARCTIC have provided rich resources for training, while techniques for affordance learning and physics-aware synthesis have aimed at improving realism. Notable prior works include Text2HOI and HOIGPT. Text2HOI pioneered text-guided 3D HOI generation using diffusion models for contact prediction and motion generation. However, it often struggled with fine-grained control from low-level text and produced relatively short interaction sequences. HOIGPT, leveraging LLMs, aimed for long-horizon sequences but lacked explicit 3D grounding and affordance-guided mechanisms, often failing to ensure smooth transitions between sub-sequences or generate physically plausible interactions. Other methods like SemGrasp and Grasp as You Say focused on semantic-aware grasping but were limited to static grasps or required handcrafted templates.

The critical limitation across many existing approaches is their "closed-set" nature. These models perform well on objects and tasks they were specifically trained on but fail to generalize to "unseen" objects or open-vocabulary instructions. This deficiency severely restricts their applicability in real-world, unstructured environments where novelty is a constant.

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has reshaped the landscape of AI. LLMs have excelled in natural language understanding, while MLLMs, particularly 3D MLLMs like ShapeLLM and AffordanceLLM, have begun to bridge language with 3D perception, enabling tasks like 3D object generation, visual question answering, and affordance understanding. However, these MLLMs have largely been confined to perception tasks, lacking the ability to synthesize continuous 3D motion dynamics. LLM-based approaches, conversely, generate motion from text but lack grounding in actual 3D environments or reasoning about spatial affordances.

OpenHOI is positioned precisely at this intersection, aiming to bridge the critical gap between 3D perception (MLLMs) and 3D interaction generation (diffusion models). It introduces the concept of "open-world HOI synthesis," which is a significant paradigm shift from previous closed-set methods. By combining the strong 3D reasoning capabilities of MLLMs with robust diffusion-based motion synthesis and physics-aware refinement, OpenHOI addresses the long-standing generalization problem. It allows for the generation of long-horizon, physically plausible HOI sequences for *novel objects* guided by *free-form language commands*, thereby pushing the boundaries of what is possible in human-robot interaction and immersive computing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of OpenHOI is to introduce the **first framework for open-world hand-object interaction (HOI) synthesis**. This overarching goal is driven by a critical need to overcome the generalization limitations inherent in existing HOI generation methods.

The specific key objectives and motivations are:

- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** Current HOI models are typically trained and evaluated on closed datasets of predefined objects. This dramatically limits their utility in real-world scenarios where users might interact with novel items. OpenHOI aims to synthesize realistic HOI sequences for objects never encountered during training, a crucial step for practical applications like AR/VR and robotics.
- **Response to Open-Vocabulary Instructions:** Beyond just novel objects, the framework seeks to interpret and execute free-form, open-vocabulary language commands. This means moving beyond simple, low-level instructions to complex, high-level user intents (e.g., "I'm feeling thirsty, could you find a water bottle and take a sip?"). The motivation here is to enable natural and intuitive human-machine communication, mirroring how humans express their needs.
- **Synthesis of Long-Horizon Manipulation Sequences:** Many real-world tasks involve a series of sequential interactions rather than isolated grasps. Previous methods often struggle with generating coherent, multi-stage manipulation sequences. OpenHOI is motivated to generate "long-horizon" sequences, breaking down complex instructions into executable sub-tasks and ensuring smooth transitions between them.
- **Generation of Physically Plausible Interactions:** Realistic HOI sequences must adhere to physical laws, avoiding interpenetration between hands and objects, and ensuring stable contact and natural motion dynamics. Prior models often generate physically unrealistic motions. OpenHOI aims to ensure physical plausibility through dedicated refinement mechanisms.
- **Bridging 3D Perception and Interaction Generation:** The motivation stems from the observation that while 3D MLLMs are powerful for 3D understanding and affordance grounding, they typically lack dynamic interaction synthesis. Conversely, existing motion generation models lack deep 3D semantic understanding. OpenHOI's goal is to effectively combine these two capabilities, leveraging the reasoning power of MLLMs to inform and guide robust motion generation.

In essence, OpenHOI is motivated by the vision of enabling machines to understand and execute human manipulation intentions in diverse, unstructured environments, moving beyond constrained laboratory settings to truly dynamic and open-ended applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OpenHOI proposes a novel, two-stage framework to achieve open-world HOI synthesis: (1) **Instruction Decomposition and Affordance Reasoning via 3D MLLM**, and (2) **Affordance-driven HOI Diffusion with Physical Refinement**.

#### 3.1. Instruction Decomposition and Affordance Reasoning via 3D MLLM

This initial stage is responsible for understanding the user's high-level intent and the object's functional properties. Given a 3D object point cloud and a free-form natural language instruction, the 3D Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) performs two crucial tasks:
- **Spatial Affordance Map Generation:** It precisely localizes interaction regions on the object (e.g., handles, buttons, caps), identifying geometrically plausible areas for interaction.
- **Temporal Sub-task Sequence Decomposition:** It breaks down the complex, open-vocabulary instruction into a sequence of executable, atomic sub-tasks (e.g., "grasp bottle cap," "twist counterclockwise," "lift bottle to mouth").

**Network Structure and Training:**
- **Backbone:** The authors adopt **ShapeLLM** as their MLLM backbone, recognizing its strong pre-trained capabilities in embodied interaction and 3D understanding. ShapeLLM uses a ReCon++ point-cloud encoder and a LLaMa-initialized language component.
- **Affordance Grounding:** To enable dense 3D prediction, the MLLM's vocabulary is augmented with a dedicated segmentation token `<AFF>`, similar to Lisa. This allows the model to represent and reason about segmentation outputs within its language-based framework. For each `<AFF>` token in the generated sub-task sequence, an affordance decoder processes the corresponding MLLM embedding and object point cloud to generate a specific affordance mask.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Affordance Tuning:** The MLLM undergoes a two-stage fine-tuning process:
 1. **Coarse-grained tuning:** Initial training on large-scale, object-centric static affordance datasets (e.g., Affordance dataset) to instill robust affordance priors and enhance open-vocabulary generalization.
 2. **Fine-grained tuning:** Subsequent training on smaller, dynamic hand-object interaction datasets (GRAB, ARCTIC) to refine affordance maps and task decomposition for precise interaction scenarios.
- **Loss Functions:** Training involves an auto-regressive cross-entropy loss ($L_{task}$) for supervising sub-task generation and a combination of Dice loss and Binary Cross-Entropy loss ($L_{aff}$) for guiding affordance prediction.

#### 3.2. Affordance-driven HOI Diffusion with Physical Refinement

This stage takes the outputs from the MLLM (affordance map, sub-task sequence) along with the object point cloud as conditions to synthesize physically plausible, long-horizon HOI sequences.

**Training HOI Diffusion with Affordance Prior:**
- **Input:** The conditioning signal $C$ comprises the predicted affordance map ($\tilde{A}_{obj}$), the CLIP embedding of the decomposed sub-tasks ($f_{clip}(\tilde{T}_{sub\_tasks})$), and the object point cloud features ($F_{obj}$).
- **Diffusion Process:** A transformer-based neural network ($\hat{X}_\theta$) is trained to predict the original HOI sequence from a noisy version, following a standard denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) framework.
- **Loss Functions:** The training objective includes:
 - **Denoising Loss ($L_{hoi\_diff}$):** Standard diffusion loss for HOI sequence reconstruction.
 - **HOI Distance Map Loss ($L_{hoi\_distance}$):** Encourages precise surface contact between hand and object.
 - **HOI Relative Orientation Loss ($L_{hoi\_orient}$):** Ensures accurate hand-object rotational poses, critical for grasping and manipulation.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** During training, 10% of conditional inputs are randomly masked to train an unconditional model. This enables CFG during inference, improving alignment between generated HOI sequences and the conditioning input by scaling the difference between conditional and unconditional predictions.

**Training-free HOI Diffusion with Physical Refinement:**
A key innovation is a training-free refinement strategy applied *during the diffusion sampling process* to address physical implausibility (penetration) and temporal incoherence (discontinuous motion) without introducing distribution shifts. Inspired by DSG, this involves:
- **Affordance Refinement ($l_{aff}$):** Minimizes the Euclidean distance between hand joints and the closest affordance regions, ensuring precise hand-object contact.
- **Penetration Refinement ($l_{penetration}$):** Penalizes interpenetration between hand vertices and object points, promoting physical realism.
- **Motion In-between Refinement ($l_{transition}$):** For long-horizon tasks, it ensures smooth transitions between sub-sequences by penalizing discrepancies between the end pose of a preceding sequence and the start pose of a subsequent one.
- **Guided Sampling:** Instead of conventional gradient descent, which can cause distribution shifts, the refinement uses a spherical Gaussian constraint during sampling. It combines a deterministic steepest gradient descent direction (derived from the refinement losses) with a random sampling direction, controlled by a guidance rate $w$, to enhance alignment with constraints while preserving the learned data distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OpenHOI demonstrates significant advancements in open-world HOI synthesis, establishing new state-of-the-art performance across diverse scenarios.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Superiority over State-of-the-Art Methods

The model was rigorously evaluated on two prominent hand-object interaction datasets: GRAB (single-hand interactions) and ARCTIC (bimanual interactions), using both seen and unseen object splits.
- **Overall Performance:** OpenHOI consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, including MDM, TM2T, MotionGPT, and Text2HOI, across all evaluated metrics.
- **Motion Accuracy (MPJPE↓, FOL↓):** OpenHOI achieves the lowest Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Final Object Location Error (FOL), indicating higher geometric precision and more accurate object placement. For instance, on unseen GRAB objects, OpenHOI achieved an MPJPE of 51.34 compared to Text2HOI's 60.67 and an FOL of 0.27 compared to Text2HOI's 0.41. Similar improvements are observed on ARCTIC.
- **Generation Realism (FID↓):** The model produces more realistic motions, as evidenced by the lowest Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). On unseen GRAB, OpenHOI obtained an FID of 28.29, significantly lower than Text2HOI's 36.96.
- **Diversity & Multi-modality (Diversity→, MModality↑):** OpenHOI generally achieves competitive or superior diversity and multi-modality scores, indicating its ability to generate varied outputs for the same prompt and capture within-prompt variability.

#### 4.2. Strong Generalization to Unseen Objects and Open-Vocabulary Instructions

The superior performance on the "Unseen" splits of both GRAB and ARCTIC datasets is a critical finding, confirming OpenHOI's core claim of open-world generalization. This indicates that the MLLM's affordance reasoning and the diffusion model's conditioning effectively generalize to novel object geometries and diverse linguistic commands without requiring re-training for each new object.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Visualizations provided in the paper and project page showcase the framework's ability to generate physically realistic and coherent long-horizon HOI sequences. This includes interactions with seen objects, completely unseen objects (e.g., "water bottle," "milk container," "duck," "airplane"), and multi-object scenarios, all guided by complex, open-vocabulary instructions. The generated sequences demonstrate natural hand articulations, appropriate object manipulation, and smooth transitions between sub-tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Validation

Ablation studies systematically validated the necessity and effectiveness of each core component:
- **Affordance Awareness (w/o Affordance):** Removing the MLLM's affordance map prediction capabilities resulted in a significant degradation across all metrics (e.g., higher MPJPE, FID). This confirms that explicit, accurate affordance grounding is fundamental for generating semantically meaningful and natural interactions, preventing unnatural hand placements and increased penetrations.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (w/o CFG):** The absence of CFG led to poorer alignment with input conditions and semantic misalignment, demonstrating its critical role in steering the diffusion process effectively.
- **Loss-Guided Physical Refinement (w/o $l_{penetration}$, w/o $l_{aff}$):** Removing the penetration and affordance refinement losses severely impacted physical plausibility, leading to frequent object penetrations and unnatural contact patterns. This highlights the importance of the training-free refinement stage for generating physically valid and temporally coherent long-horizon sequences, especially for complex bimanual interactions.
- **Motion In-between Refinement (w/o Motion In-between):** The "Smooth Rate" metric (introduced for this work) significantly worsened without this component, proving its effectiveness in ensuring seamless transitions between different sub-sequences of a long-horizon task.
- **Multi `<AFF>` Token:** The use of multiple `<AFF>` tokens for affordance prediction was shown to improve performance over a single token, indicating enhanced capability to capture nuanced semantic details.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Affordance Tuning:** The two-stage MLLM tuning strategy significantly improved the AUC score for affordance prediction compared to no fine-tuning or only coarse-grained tuning, validating its effectiveness in acquiring strong and fine-grained affordance priors.

#### 4.5. Extreme-Case Generalization (H2O Dataset)

Further testing on the entirely novel H2O dataset, which contained unseen objects and instructions relative to GRAB and ARCTIC training, demonstrated OpenHOI's robust performance, maintaining state-of-the-art results even under extreme distribution shifts. This underscores the exceptional generalizability of the proposed MLLM-guided framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OpenHOI represents a significant leap forward in the field of 3D hand-object interaction synthesis, with profound significance and potential impact across multiple domains.

#### 5.1. Pioneering Open-World HOI Synthesis

The most critical contribution of OpenHOI is its introduction as the *first framework for open-world HOI synthesis*. This paradigm shift moves beyond the limitations of closed-set methods, which are inherently unsuited for real-world unpredictability. By explicitly addressing generalization to novel objects and open-vocabulary instructions, OpenHOI tackles a fundamental challenge that has long hampered practical deployment of HOI technologies.

#### 5.2. Bridging 3D Reasoning and Dynamic Generation

OpenHOI effectively bridges two previously disparate areas: the 3D perception and reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with the continuous motion synthesis power of diffusion models. This unification is crucial because it allows the system to not only *understand* what an object is and how it can be interacted with (affordances, task decomposition) but also to *generate* the corresponding dynamic, physically plausible interaction sequences. This synergistic approach harnesses the strengths of both foundational model types.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Realism and Robustness

The methodology's integration of an MLLM for detailed affordance grounding and instruction decomposition, coupled with an affordance-driven diffusion model and a training-free physics-aware refinement stage, leads to significantly more realistic and robust HOI sequences. The ability to minimize penetration, ensure precise hand-object contact, and maintain temporal coherence throughout long-horizon tasks addresses key limitations of prior work, making the generated interactions far more believable and usable.

#### 5.4. Transformative Impact on AR/VR

In Augmented and Virtual Reality, the ability to synthesize natural and physically plausible hand-object interactions is paramount for creating immersive experiences. OpenHOI's framework can allow users to manipulate virtual objects with unprecedented realism, responding to nuanced gestures and complex verbal commands. This would enhance user engagement, training simulations (e.g., surgical training, complex machinery operation), and virtual prototyping, making digital interactions feel as natural as real-world ones.

#### 5.5. Advancing Dexterous Robotics

For robotics, OpenHOI offers a powerful foundation for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and dexterous robots. By enabling robots to:
- **Understand High-Level Commands:** Translate human free-form language (e.g., "Pour some milk") into a sequence of executable physical actions.
- **Interact with Novel Objects:** Generalize manipulation skills to unseen objects based on their perceived affordances (e.g., identifying a bottle's cap or handle).
- **Perform Long-Horizon Tasks:** Execute multi-stage manipulation sequences seamlessly.
- **Ensure Physical Plausibility:** Generate motions that respect physical constraints, avoiding damage or instability.
This capability is crucial for robots operating in unstructured environments (e.g., homes, hospitals, disaster zones), moving closer to human-like versatility in manipulation. The project has clear implications for real-world robotic applications, as highlighted in the discussion section regarding "Robotics Manipulation."

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The paper acknowledges several promising avenues for future work, indicating that OpenHOI opens doors for further innovation:
- **Fine-Grained Dynamics:** Incorporating more sophisticated physics models (e.g., fluid simulation for pouring) to enhance realism in specific tasks.
- **Hierarchical Task Decomposition:** Developing advanced chain-of-thought reasoning to handle extremely complex, multi-level instructions (e.g., "cook a meal").
- **Improved Logical Reasoning:** Enhancing the MLLM's capabilities to handle spatial and logical disambiguation in instructions (e.g., "Open the second cabinet").
- **Real-time Inference for Robotics:** Optimizing the diffusion inference process (e.g., via DPM-Solver) for rapid execution in real-world robotic systems.

In conclusion, OpenHOI represents a significant conceptual and technical breakthrough by enabling machines to understand and generate human-like interactions in an open, unpredictable world. Its robust methodology and impressive empirical results position it as a foundational work for future advancements in embodied AI, AR/VR, and robotics.
