# Agentic-R1: Distilled Dual-Strategy Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University's LTI developed Agentic-R1, a language model trained with the DualDistill framework to adaptively combine long chain-of-thought and tool-augmented reasoning. The model achieved 65.3% accuracy on DeepMath-L and 52.0% on Combinatorics300, demonstrating improved performance over specialized baselines on complex mathematical tasks.

## Problem

- Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) models struggle with numerical accuracy and efficiency for computations, often "overthinking" when exact results are needed.
- Tool-augmented agentic models excel at precise computation but falter on abstract, complex logical reasoning problems without clear tool interfaces.
- Combining these complementary strengths often relies on computationally expensive and unstable reinforcement learning or heuristic methods.

## Method

- The ==DualDistill framework== distills complementary reasoning strategies from two specialized teacher models (an ==Agentic Teacher== and a ==Reasoning Teacher==) into a unified student model.
- A ==Trajectory Composition== process generates solutions for each problem from both teachers sequentially and composes informative training trajectories, including explicit strategy transition segments.
- A ==Self-Distillation== phase refines the student's strategy selection by generating its own candidate trajectories, evaluating them, and learning from both successful and corrected unsuccessful attempts with teacher feedback.

## Results

- Agentic-R1-SD achieved **65.3%** accuracy on DeepMath-L and **52.0%** on Combinatorics300, outperforming specialized long-CoT and tool-augmented baselines by significant margins (e.g., **9.0%** and **7.5%** respectively over DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B).
- The model demonstrated adaptive tool usage, invoking code tools in **79.2%** of Combinatorics300 problems (computation-heavy) but only **52.0%** in simpler AMC problems, reflecting an implicit understanding of task demands.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the proposed trajectory composition strategy is crucial for teaching strategy adaptation, consistently outperforming non-compositional training across benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A distillation framework can effectively unify heterogeneous reasoning strategies, such as text-based reasoning and tool-augmented execution, into a single student model without relying on reinforcement learning.
> - The model learns adaptive strategy selection, dynamically choosing between complex textual reasoning and precise tool-assisted computation based on problem characteristics.
> - The approach enables learning robust agentic behaviors even from imperfect teacher models, with the student model surpassing the agentic teacher's performance on certain tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in complex reasoning tasks, largely driven by the development of "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) prompting. This paper specifically addresses a critical challenge within this landscape: the dichotomy between purely text-based, long-form reasoning and tool-augmented, agentic problem-solving.

Historically, two dominant paradigms have emerged for enhancing LLM reasoning:
1. **Long Chain-of-Thought (long-CoT) Reasoning:** Models like DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) and GPT-o1 (Jaech et al., 2024) leverage extensive, iterative natural language reasoning trajectories, often incorporating self-verification and refinement. These approaches excel at abstract logical tasks and understanding nuanced problem descriptions. However, they are inherently slow, computationally intensive, and prone to errors when exact numerical calculations or rigorous external verification are required. The reliance on natural language for arithmetic, for instance, can lead to hallucinations or inaccuracies. This paradigm also suffers from "overthinking," where excessive reasoning steps might not always lead to better or more efficient solutions, especially when a direct computational tool would be more appropriate (Cuadron et al., 2025).

2. **Tool-Augmented Agentic Reasoning:** This paradigm integrates external tools, such as code interpreters or search engines, into the LLM's reasoning process. Frameworks like PaL (Gao et al., 2023), Toolformer (Schick et al., 2023), ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), and OpenHands (Wang et al., 2024) allow LLMs to perform reliable numerical computations, access up-to-date information, or execute complex algorithms. These methods offer greater efficiency and accuracy for tasks that require precise calculation or external data validation. However, tool-augmented agents often struggle with more abstract, conceptually complex reasoning problems that do not have a clear tool-use interface or require deep logical inference that cannot be offloaded to an external tool. They might also default to tool use even when simpler textual reasoning would suffice, potentially introducing unnecessary overhead or errors if the tool-use ability is not perfectly robust.

The current work, "Agentic-R1," emerges precisely at the intersection of these two paradigms. Prior efforts to combine these strengths have often relied on reinforcement learning (RL) (e.g., ReTool (Feng et al., 2025), R1-Searcher (Song et al., 2025)), which, while powerful, can be computationally expensive, data-intensive, and sometimes less stable to train. Other approaches might focus on different domains or use more heuristic-based prompting for tool invocation.

Agentic-R1 distinguishes itself by introducing **DualDistill**, a novel *distillation framework* that aims to unify these heterogeneous reasoning strategies into a *single student model* without resorting to reinforcement learning. It seeks to create a model that can *adaptively* select the optimal strategy—be it pure textual reasoning or tool-augmented execution—based on the specific demands of each problem. This represents a significant step towards building more versatile, robust, and efficient language agents that can dynamically leverage the best available problem-solving approaches, addressing a core limitation in current LLM reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient language model that can dynamically and intelligently combine the complementary strengths of long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning and tool-augmented agentic reasoning. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Existing Paradigms:**
 - **Long-CoT models:** While excellent at abstract and iterative reasoning, they are often computationally expensive, slow, and prone to numerical or factual errors without explicit verification. They can also "overthink" simple problems, generating unnecessarily long and inefficient reasoning traces, particularly when precise computation is needed.
 - **Tool-augmented agents:** Excelling in tasks requiring precise calculations or external verification, these models frequently falter on complex logical or abstract reasoning problems that do not neatly map to tool functions. Their strategy selection might also be less nuanced, leading to sub-optimal choices.

2. **Enable Adaptive Strategy Selection:** The authors aim to train a unified student model that, for any given problem, can intelligently determine whether to engage in text-based reasoning (for abstract concepts) or invoke external tools (for arithmetic, algorithms, or rigorous verification). This dynamic adaptation is crucial for achieving high performance across a diverse range of challenging tasks.

3. **Enhance Robustness and Efficiency:** By combining the strengths of both paradigms, the goal is to create a model that is more robust to various problem types and more efficient in its resource allocation. For instance, delegating arithmetic to a code interpreter can significantly speed up computation and reduce errors compared to purely textual calculation, while retaining the ability to perform complex logical inferences in natural language.

4. **Develop a Stable and Data-Efficient Training Method:** Unlike many previous attempts that rely on reinforcement learning (which can be unstable and data-hungry), the paper seeks a stable distillation-based approach. This aims to transfer knowledge from multiple specialized teachers to a student model efficiently, minimizing the amount of novel training data required.

5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance on Complex Mathematical Tasks:** The chosen domain of mathematical reasoning provides an excellent testbed for these hybrid capabilities, as it often requires both rigorous calculation and deep logical understanding. The ultimate objective is to demonstrate superior accuracy on benchmarks that demand a blend of these skills.

In essence, the motivation is to build a "generalist" reasoning agent by teaching it when and how to switch between different "specialist" problem-solving modes, thereby leveraging the best of both worlds—the nuanced understanding of long-CoT and the precision of tool-use—within a single, coherent framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed solution is **DualDistill**, a novel distillation framework that fuses complementary reasoning strategies from multiple teachers into a unified student model. This framework comprises three main stages: Trajectory Composition, Training Instance Selection, and Self-Distillation.

#### 3.1. Trajectory Composition

DualDistill begins by leveraging two distinct teacher models, each specializing in a different reasoning paradigm:
- **Agentic Teacher (π_A):** An instance of OpenHands (Wang et al., 2024), built upon Claude-3.5-Sonnet, which excels at tool-assisted reasoning and code execution.
- **Reasoning Teacher (π_R):** DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), a model highly proficient in pure text-based long Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

For each problem in the training set, a two-step trajectory generation process occurs:
1. **Initial Solution (y_1):** One teacher is randomly selected to generate an initial solution. A token budget is imposed on this step to encourage efficiency.
2. **Second Solution (y_2):** The *other* teacher then generates a second solution, conditioned on the original problem and the first solution (y_1). This step has no token budget constraint.
Both solutions (y_1 and y_2) are then evaluated for correctness against a reference answer using a rule-based grader (g_1, g_2 ∈ {0, 1}). Based on these correctness scores, informative training trajectories are composed for the student model:
- **Correction Trajectory (g_1=0, g_2=1):** If the first teacher fails and the second succeeds, the composed trajectory is `y_1 ⊕ t^(-+) ⊕ y_2`. This teaches the student how to recover from an initial incorrect strategy.
- **Complementary/Verification Trajectory (g_1=1, g_2=1):** If both teachers provide correct solutions, the trajectory is `y_1 ⊕ t^(++) ⊕ y_2`. This teaches the student that multiple correct paths exist or how to verify a solution using an alternative strategy.
- **Successful Single Strategy Trajectory (g_1=1, g_2=0):** If only the initial teacher is correct, the trajectory is simply `y_1`.
- **Discarded (g_1=0, g_2=0):** If both teachers fail, the problem is discarded.
Pre-defined "transition segments" (e.g., "Wait, use text reasoning is too tedious, let’s try code reasoning.") are inserted between `y_1` and `y_2` to explicitly signal strategy shifts and their rationale to the student. Loss masking is applied during training to prevent the student from learning from incorrect segments or executor errors.

#### 3.2. Training Instance Selection

To ensure the student learns effective strategy switching, the training dataset is carefully curated. Instead of generic datasets, problems are drawn from DeepMath (He et al., 2025) and filtered into two contrasting subsets:
- **Agentic-Favored Subset:** Problems with large numerical answers (>10^5) or those difficult for a pure text-reasoning model under limited context. These problems highlight the benefits of tool-assisted computation.
- **Pure Reasoning-Favored Subset:** Problems where the tool-assisted strategy is more prone to error, emphasizing the value of textual reasoning.
This ensures a balanced dataset (2.6k distilled trajectories) where the advantages of each strategy are apparent, enabling the student to learn when and how to deploy them.

#### 3.3. Student Model

The student model is DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B. This choice is strategic because it has already been fine-tuned on text-based reasoning trajectories and pre-trained with exposure to code-related data. This pre-existing familiarity with both modalities minimizes the amount of new training data required to teach strategy composition, allowing the focus to be on learning *when* to switch strategies rather than learning the strategies themselves from scratch.

#### 3.4. Self-Distillation

After initial fine-tuning on the composed trajectories from teachers (yielding π_S1), the student model undergoes a self-distillation phase to refine its strategy selection. This addresses potential ability mismatches (e.g., a smaller student model might misapply tools where simple reasoning is better).
The process involves:
1. **Student Generation:** The student model (π_S1) generates K candidate trajectories for problems in the training set.
2. **Evaluation:** Each trajectory is graded for correctness (g^(i,j)), and an average score (g^(i)) is calculated for each problem.
3. **Buffer Population:**
 - If `g^(i) > β_1` (e.g., 0, indicating at least one correct student solution), a correct student trajectory, followed by a teacher verification, is added to a self-distillation buffer.
 - If `g^(i) < β_2` (e.g., 0.9, indicating low average correctness), an incorrect student trajectory, along with a corrected solution provided by a teacher (specifically text-based reasoning due to observed coding ability gaps), is added to the buffer.
This iterative process allows the student to reinforce effective strategies and learn from its own suboptimal choices, guided by teacher feedback, leading to the final Agentic-R1-SD model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on Agentic-R1 and Agentic-R1-SD demonstrate significant advancements in mathematical reasoning performance, particularly for tasks requiring a blend of computational accuracy and complex logical inference.

#### 4.1. Performance on Challenging Benchmarks

The core strength of Agentic-R1 is highlighted on two specialized benchmarks:
- **DeepMath-L:** A subset of DeepMath problems featuring large numerical answers (absolute value > 10^5), heavily favoring tool-aided computation.
- **Combinatorics300:** A collection of combinatorics problems with answers larger than 10^4, requiring both complex enumeration logic and precise calculations.

On these benchmarks, which are designed to stress-test combined reasoning and tool-use capabilities, **Agentic-R1 (ours)** and especially **Agentic-R1-SD (ours)** demonstrate substantial performance improvements compared to strong baselines:
- **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (w/o tool):** A pure short-CoT model.
- **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (w/ tool):** A tool-augmented short-CoT model.
- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B:** A pure long-CoT text-reasoning model.

For example, at the large token budget (L=32768):
- On **DeepMath-L**, Agentic-R1-SD achieved **65.3% accuracy**, significantly outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B (56.3%) and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (w/ tool) (34.7%).
- On **Combinatorics300**, Agentic-R1-SD reached **52.0% accuracy**, surpassing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B (44.5%) and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (w/ tool) (28.9%).

These results clearly indicate that the DualDistill framework effectively enables the student model to leverage tool-use for computation-intensive problems while maintaining or improving reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.2. Performance on Standard Mathematical Benchmarks

To assess generalizability, the models were also evaluated on widely used benchmarks like MATH500, AIME, and AMC.
- Agentic-R1 maintained comparable performance to the pure text-reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B) and often surpassed tool-augmented baselines.
- Agentic-R1-SD further enhanced performance, achieving the highest or second-highest scores on these benchmarks. For instance, on MATH500 at a large budget, Agentic-R1-SD achieved **93.3% accuracy**, outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B (89.2%). On AMC, Agentic-R1-SD (85.8%) also slightly edged out DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B (84.8%).
- A slight performance decrease on simpler benchmarks (like MATH500 for Agentic-R1 without self-distillation compared to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B) was noted, which is a recognized limitation, but generally, the self-distilled model (Agentic-R1-SD) resolves this.

#### 4.3. Adaptive Strategy Selection

A key finding is that Agentic-R1 learns to dynamically select the appropriate strategy:
- It automatically invokes code tools when beneficial, as evidenced by 79.2% tool usage in Combinatorics300 (where large computations are common) but only 52.0% usage in the simpler AMC dataset. This demonstrates an implicit understanding of problem characteristics.
- Qualitative examples showcase the model's ability to switch strategies: from tool-aided to textual reasoning to correct an error (Fig. 6) or from textual reasoning to tool-aided to bypass tedious calculations (Fig. 7).

#### 4.4. Learning from Imperfect Teachers

Surprisingly, the student model (Agentic-R1) was able to learn valuable agentic strategies even from an "imperfect" teacher. For example, the OpenHands-based agentic teacher achieved only 48.4% accuracy on Combinatorics300, yet the student's performance significantly improved from 44.7% (initial R1-Distill-7B) to 50.9% (Agentic-R1-SD), surpassing the teacher. This highlights the effectiveness of distillation in transferring useful behaviors even if the teacher isn't universally superior.

#### 4.5. Efficacy of Trajectory Composition

An ablation study confirmed that the proposed trajectory composition strategy is crucial. Training with composition consistently outperformed a non-compositional approach (where student trajectories were purely from one teacher) across DeepMath-L, AIME, and AMC benchmarks, validating its role in teaching strategy adaptation.

In summary, Agentic-R1, particularly with self-distillation, successfully integrates dual reasoning strategies, leading to a more capable, adaptive, and robust mathematical reasoning agent that outperforms specialized baselines on complex tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Agentic-R1: Distilled Dual-Strategy Reasoning" holds significant implications for the development of advanced language models and their application in complex problem-solving domains.

#### 5.1. Towards Truly Versatile AI Agents

The most profound significance of DualDistill is its contribution to building more versatile and adaptive AI agents. By effectively unifying text-based reasoning for abstract logic with tool-augmented execution for precise computation, Agentic-R1 moves beyond the limitations of single-paradigm LLMs. This unification is crucial for AI systems that need to operate in real-world scenarios, which rarely present problems solvable by only one type of intelligence. The ability to dynamically select the optimal strategy based on problem characteristics is a critical step towards creating truly intelligent and autonomous agents.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Performance and Efficiency

The demonstrated performance gains, particularly on challenging mathematical benchmarks requiring both deep reasoning and exact computation, signify a new benchmark for LLM capabilities. By leveraging the strengths of each strategy, Agentic-R1-SD achieves superior accuracy and potentially greater efficiency. For tasks that involve extensive numerical calculations, offloading these to a code interpreter is far more reliable and faster than attempting them through natural language generation, which is prone to errors and token bloat. This optimized use of computational resources can lead to more cost-effective inference in practical applications.

#### 5.3. Stable and Data-Efficient Training Paradigm

DualDistill offers a compelling alternative to reinforcement learning (RL) for teaching complex, multi-modal strategies. RL, while powerful, is often characterized by instability, high computational costs, and significant data requirements. Distillation, as implemented here, provides a more stable, data-efficient, and practical training setup. This makes the development of sophisticated multi-strategy agents more accessible, potentially accelerating research and deployment in this area. The ability to distill knowledge from proprietary, high-performing teacher models (like Claude-3.5-Sonnet) into smaller, open-source student models is also a major practical advantage, democratizing access to advanced reasoning capabilities.

#### 5.4. Addressing the "Reasoning-Action Dilemma"

The paper directly addresses the "reasoning-action dilemma," where LLMs might "overthink" or misapply reasoning in scenarios better suited for direct tool use. By teaching the student model *when* to switch strategies, DualDistill helps mitigate issues like redundant reasoning, unnecessary token generation, and errors from attempting computations in natural language. This leads to more calibrated and appropriate problem-solving behaviors.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The limitations highlighted by the authors also open several promising avenues for future research:
- **Automatic Transition Design:** Automating the creation of natural and fluid transition phrases between strategies would enhance the readability and user experience of the model's output.
- **Expanded Training Data:** Scaling the dataset to incorporate a wider variety of problem types and strategy interactions could enable the student model to learn entirely new reasoning strategies from scratch, rather than relying on pre-existing familiarity with modalities.
- **Generalization to Other Domains:** The DualDistill framework, while demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, could be applied to other complex domains requiring a mix of logical inference, data retrieval, and specialized computation (e.g., scientific discovery, engineering problem-solving, medical diagnosis).
- **Mitigating Repetitive Behavior:** Addressing the self-distillation induced repetitive behavior is crucial for further refining the student model's autonomy and expressiveness.

In conclusion, Agentic-R1 and the DualDistill framework represent a significant step forward in the quest for intelligent, versatile, and robust AI systems. By providing a stable and effective method for integrating diverse reasoning paradigms, this work paves the way for a new generation of language models capable of tackling real-world problems with greater sophistication and efficiency.
