# MAMMA: Markerless & Automatic Multi-Person Motion Action Capture

> [!summary] Summary
> A markerless motion capture pipeline, MAMMA, accurately reconstructs SMPL-X human body parameters from multi-view video, demonstrating 3D reconstruction quality comparable to commercial marker-based systems with a 0.862mm difference in held-out marker error. The system also reduces processing time from approximately 72 hours to 26 hours for a typical capture, even for complex multi-person interactions.

## Problem

- Traditional marker-based motion capture is accurate but costly, labor-intensive, and time-consuming, particularly in scenarios with complex poses or close multi-person interactions.
- Existing markerless motion capture solutions often lack the accuracy of marker-based systems, are expensive, closed-source, or struggle with occlusions and physical interactions.
- The research community lacked open-source, high-accuracy markerless systems and comprehensive datasets for challenging multi-person human interactions.

## Method

- A two-stage markerless motion capture pipeline directly recovers ==SMPL-X== parameters from multi-view video, beginning with a novel dense 2D landmark estimator called MammaNet.
- MammaNet utilizes a Transformer decoder with learnable landmark queries and optional segmentation mask conditioning to predict 2D coordinates, uncertainty, visibility, and contact probabilities for 512 surface landmarks.
- An L-BFGS optimization process fits SMPL-X parameters to the multi-view 2D landmarks, incorporating robust reprojection, temporal smoothness, and a dedicated contact optimization stage.

## Results

- MAMMA achieved 3D reconstruction accuracy competitive with commercial marker-based systems, exhibiting only a **0.862mm** difference in held-out marker error compared to a Vicon+MoSh++ pipeline.
- The full pipeline reduced processing time for SMPL-X parameter recovery by nearly threefold, from approximately 72 hours for traditional marker-based methods to 26 hours on consumer-grade hardware.
- The system consistently outperformed academic baselines in 3D fitting errors (e.g., **17.73mm** MPJPE on MammaEval-D) and generated perceptually more realistic reconstructions with significantly reduced body interpenetration in multi-person interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense 2D surface landmarks, augmented with uncertainty and contact probabilities, enable robust 3D SMPL-X parameter recovery without requiring strong pose or shape priors.
> - Synthetic datasets specifically designed for multi-view coverage, extreme poses, and two-person interactions are highly effective for training robust markerless motion capture networks.
> - Integrating person-specific segmentation masks and a dedicated contact optimization stage are crucial for achieving high accuracy and physical plausibility in challenging multi-person interaction scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional marker-based motion capture (mocap) systems are widely recognized for their high accuracy in capturing 3D human movement. However, their reliance on specialized hardware, labor-intensive manual marker placement, and extensive post-processing requirements make them costly and time-consuming. These limitations are particularly pronounced in scenarios involving complex poses or close multi-person interactions, where marker occlusions or detachment necessitate significant data cleanup.

In response, markerless motion capture methods have emerged as an alternative. Existing commercial markerless systems, such as Captury, Move AI, Moverse, and Theia Markerless, generally offer less accuracy compared to marker-based solutions, are expensive, and often lack transparent performance reporting or open-source availability. Academic vision-based markerless approaches range from monocular methods detecting sparse 2D keypoints or regressing parametric body model (e.g., SMPL, SMPL-X) parameters, which often struggle with depth ambiguity and occlusion, to multi-view systems that triangulate 2D detections. Many multi-view methods typically output skeleton-level data and require additional model-fitting stages with strong priors to reconstruct full surface parameters. Current learning-based methods frequently cater to single-person capture, utilize sparse keypoints, or encounter difficulties with occlusions and physical interactions. Furthermore, datasets featuring complex, close human-human interactions remain underrepresented.

MAMMA contributes to this landscape by addressing these limitations. It aims to develop an open-source markerless pipeline that achieves accuracy comparable to commercial marker-based systems, specifically focusing on the challenging domain of multi-person close interactions. By predicting dense 2D surface landmarks and directly recovering SMPL-X parameters, MAMMA bypasses the need for sparse keypoint triangulation and subsequent model-fitting with strong priors. The work also introduces a novel synthetic dataset rich in varied interactions and new real-world benchmarks, addressing gaps in existing evaluation resources for dense landmark prediction and markerless motion capture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop MAMMA (Markerless Accurate Multi-person Motion Acquisition), a markerless motion-capture pipeline capable of accurately recovering SMPL-X parameters from multi-view video. The system aims to achieve accuracy comparable to commercial marker-based solutions while significantly reducing the manual effort and time typically associated with traditional mocap workflows.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current motion capture methodologies:

- **Cost and Labor of Traditional Mocap:** Traditional marker-based systems are capital-intensive due to specialized hardware and operational costs associated with trained personnel. The process of applying markers, calibrating the environment and subjects, and particularly the laborious post-processing to correct noisy, missing, or swapped markers (especially in complex poses or close interactions), introduces substantial overhead and can take many hours.
- **Limitations of Existing Markerless Solutions:** While attempts have been made to develop markerless systems, many current learning-based methods are designed for single-person capture, rely on sparse keypoints, or struggle with robust performance under occlusions and physical interactions common in multi-person scenarios. Commercial markerless systems exist but are often expensive, closed-source, and do not provide detailed accuracy benchmarks against established standards.
- **Democratization of Motion Capture:** The researchers aim to develop a freely available, high-accuracy markerless system for academic research. This would reduce the barrier to entry for motion capture, allowing for broader access and increasing the scale at which complex human motion, including nuanced interactions, can be studied.
- **Addressing Challenging Interactions:** Close human-human interactions (e.g., hugging, dancing, martial arts) are common in daily life but are under-represented in existing motion-capture datasets and pose significant challenges for both marker-based (due to marker occlusion) and markerless systems (due to ambiguity). MAMMA explicitly targets these two-person interaction scenarios.
- **Direct SMPL-X Parameter Recovery:** The objective is to directly estimate SMPL-X body parameters, providing a comprehensive representation of human pose, shape, and hand/face articulation, rather than just sparse skeleton joints. This reduces the need for subsequent, potentially error-prone, model-fitting stages.
- **Leveraging Pixel-Level Information:** The work is motivated by the potential of vision-based systems to incorporate richer pixel-based supervisory signals, such as segmentation masks, to resolve ambiguities that marker-based systems cannot.
- **Creating Comprehensive Training Data:** To train a robust network capable of handling diverse and challenging motions, the researchers identified a need for a large, synthetic multi-view dataset that includes extreme poses, high-fidelity hand motions, and dense multi-person interactions, as existing datasets lack this coverage and detailed annotations (like contact and visibility).
- **Establishing Benchmarks:** A further motivation is to address the absence of common benchmarks for evaluating dense-landmark prediction and markerless motion capture, by introducing new evaluation settings derived from real multi-view sequences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MAMMA operates as a two-stage markerless motion capture pipeline designed to reconstruct SMPL-X parameters from multi-view video.

#### 3.1. Dense Landmark Estimation (MammaNet)

The core of MAMMA is MammaNet, a novel dense 2D landmark estimator.
- **Architecture:** MammaNet employs a Transformer decoder with a ViT-Base backbone for extracting image features and an additional Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for processing segmentation masks.
- **Learnable Landmark Queries:** A key innovation is the use of N=512 distinct learnable embeddings, termed "landmark queries," each corresponding to a specific surface landmark. This design enables cross-attention layers to learn to match each landmark query to relevant image patches and self-attention layers within the decoder to learn pairwise correlations between landmarks.
- **Mask Conditioning:** The network can be optionally conditioned on person-specific segmentation masks, typically obtained using SAM2. Image and mask features are encoded into the same space and combined via element-wise summation to integrate mask information, which is particularly beneficial for disambiguating individuals in multi-person scenarios. The SAM2 tracking labels are also assigned to each landmark prediction to aid cross-view matching.
- **Predicted Outputs:** For each landmark, MammaNet predicts:
 - 2D pixel coordinates (μ).
 - Associated uncertainty (σ).
 - Visibility probability (p).
 - Person-person contact probability (pc).
 - Floor contact probability (fl).
- **Loss Functions:** Training involves a Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss for landmark locations, binary cross-entropy for visibility probabilities, and focal loss for contact probabilities (to handle class imbalance). All losses are weighted per-landmark.

#### 3.2. Synthetic Training Data (MammaSyn)

To train MammaNet, a large synthetic multi-view dataset named MammaSyn was constructed, extending the BEDLAM pipeline.
- **Coverage Expansion:** MammaSyn specifically targets multi-view coverage, extreme poses, high-fidelity hands, and two-person interaction scenarios.
- **Motion Sources:** It integrates SMPL-X motion sequences from various sources:
 - BEDLAM and MOYO datasets (for diverse single-person motions and extreme poses).
 - Newly captured marker-based Vicon datasets (Latin-Dance, Interacting Couples) for high-quality interaction sequences.
 - Existing interaction datasets like Hi4D, Harmony4D, and Inter-X.
 - Hand motion datasets such as SignAvatars and Interhand2.6M (merged with BEDLAM body parameters).
- **Rich Annotations:** The dataset provides per-subject segmentation masks, depth maps, per-vertex visibility, and per-vertex contact labels (for floor and inter-subject interactions, computed using the Signed Distance Function). Dense landmark ground truth is generated by sampling 512 SMPL-X vertices using Farthest Point Sampling, with increased weighting on hands, feet, and head regions to provide finer detail.

#### 3.3. Model Fitting

After 2D landmark estimation, SMPL-X body parameters M(β, θ, t) are fit to the multi-view sequence using L-BFGS optimization. Camera calibration parameters are assumed to be known.
- **Initialization:** SMPL-X bodies are initialized at the 3D point that minimizes the distance between rays projected from camera centers. Unlike some previous methods, MAMMA does not require pose or shape priors/initialization, relying on the rich information provided by the dense surface landmarks.
- **Optimization Stages:** The process unfolds in multiple stages:
 1. **Global Translation and Rotation:** Minimize a reprojection error (E_ldmks) between the predicted 2D landmarks and the projected SMPL-X vertices, weighted by predicted uncertainty and visibility. A robust Geman-McClure function is used.
 2. **Pose, Shape, and Translation:** Optimize pose, shape (16 coefficients), and translation parameters using E_ldmks combined with an L2 regularizer on body priors (E_shape).
 3. **Uncertainty Refinement & Temporal Smoothing:** Uncertainty values (σ) are updated based on the reprojection error from the second stage, re-weighting landmarks. Large joint accelerations are penalized with an L2 regularizer (E_temp) for temporal smoothness.
 4. **Contact Optimization:** This final stage minimizes interpenetration using two terms (E_cont = E_p + E_c). E_p is a repulsion term penalizing vertices inside another person's body (allowing for limited interpenetration for soft-tissue deformation). E_c is a contact term that attracts points above the surface, weighted by predicted contact probabilities. A custom CUDA kernel accelerates the Signed Distance Function (SDF) computation.
- **Final Energy Function:** The overall energy function for optimization is E(Φ; Q, L) = E_ldmks + E_shape + E_temp + E_cont, where Φ represents SMPL-X parameters, Q are camera parameters, and L are the predicted landmarks, uncertainty, visibility, and contact probabilities.

#### 3.4. Multiview Correspondence

Accurate subject identification and tracking across multiple views are critical.
- **Within-View Tracking:** SAM2 is used to obtain and propagate segmentation masks, tracking each person within individual camera views.
- **Cross-View Matching:** Pairwise geometric affinity is established between detected people across different views using symmetric epipolar distance (D_g), calculated only for visible landmarks. A Hungarian assignment algorithm is then used to find optimal one-to-one matches based on a cost matrix derived from these affinities.
- **Consistency Graph:** High-affinity matches are linked across all view pairs to form a cycle-consistent correspondence graph, grouping detections belonging to the same person across all views.

#### 3.5. Real Evaluation Data (MammaEval)

Three real-world datasets (MammaEval-Singles, MammaEval-Dance, MammaEval-Extra) were captured using a synchronized Vicon marker-based system and multi-view RGB cameras. Marker data was manually cleaned and processed with MoSh++ to provide SMPL-X ground truth. MammaEval-Extra includes additional markers not used for MoSh++ fitting, serving as independent ground truth for quantitative comparison against marker-based systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance of MAMMA and its components:

- **2D Landmark Prediction Accuracy:**
 - MammaNet consistently demonstrated higher accuracy in 2D dense landmark prediction (lower mean 2D Euclidean distance error in pixels) compared to prior methods like CameraHMR and Look-Ma\* across single-person (RICH, MOYO, MammaEval-S) and two-person (Harmony4D, CHI3D, MammaEval-D) datasets. For instance, on MammaEval-D, MammaNet achieved an error of 9.87 pixels, outperforming CameraHMR (10.21) and Look-Ma\* (15.01) without masks.
 - The network exhibited generalization capabilities to complex and unseen poses, such as those found in the MOYO yoga dataset.
 - Mask conditioning using SAM2 significantly improved 2D landmark accuracy for two-person interactions (e.g., reducing error on Harmony4D from 31.96 to 18.33 pixels and CHI3D from 6.22 to 4.36 pixels when IoU > 0.5), indicating its effectiveness in resolving inter-person ambiguities.

- **Contact Prediction Performance:**
 - MAMMA's dedicated branches for person-person and floor contact probability prediction achieved high accuracy, with an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.91 for person-person contact (on Hi4D) and 0.96 for floor contact (on MOYO). This indicates the model's ability to identify contact points reliably.

- **Multiview Correspondence Robustness:**
 - The multiview correspondence algorithm achieved 100% accuracy in matching identities across views on the evaluation datasets, even when using potentially noisy SAM2 masks. This suggests that the dense landmarks provide sufficient geometric and temporal information for robust identity association. The method proved robust even with a reduced number of cameras, maintaining 100% accuracy down to 2 cameras on MammaEval-D.

- **3D Markerless Motion Capture Quality:**
 - MAMMA, both with and without contact optimization (MAMMA-C), consistently outperformed academic baselines like SMPLify-X, Look-Ma\*, and CameraHMR in 3D fitting errors (Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Per-Vertex Error (PVE)) across all tested datasets (Table 4). For example, on MammaEval-D, MAMMA-C achieved 17.73mm MPJPE and 19.78mm PVE, significantly lower than CameraHMR's 20.41mm MPJPE and 21.06mm PVE.
 - On the challenging Hi4D dataset, MAMMA-C yielded an MPJPE of 12.44mm for 19 SMPL joints, considerably outperforming the next best method, AvatarPose (32.10mm).
 - MAMMA-C's contact optimization stage resulted in reduced body interpenetration (lower Mean Penetration Depth and Mean Penetrating Vertices) compared to other methods and even the ground truth in interaction datasets (Table 3), indicating physically more plausible reconstructions.
 - A perceptual study revealed that MAMMA-C reconstructions were rated as significantly more realistic than ground truth on several datasets, further supporting its visual quality.

- **Comparison with Vicon Marker-Based Systems:**
 - A unique quantitative evaluation against a Vicon marker-based pipeline (MoSh++) on the MammaEval-Extra dataset, using 37 held-out markers, showed a minimal difference in held-out marker error. Vicon+MoSh++ had an average error of 21.619mm, while MAMMA achieved 22.481mm, a difference of 0.862mm. This suggests MAMMA's accuracy is competitive with traditional marker-based approaches for SMPL-X capture.

- **Efficiency and Usability:**
 - The MAMMA pipeline significantly reduced the total processing time from raw capture to SMPL-X parameters to approximately 26 hours on a consumer GPU (GTX-4090), compared to roughly 72 hours for a manual marker-based workflow. This represents a nearly threefold acceleration.
 - Performance analysis indicated strong accuracy with as few as 4 cameras, with optimal accuracy reached around 12 cameras, suggesting applicability to lower-cost capture setups.
 - The system was demonstrated to work with consumer-grade hardware, specifically four synchronized iPhone 17 Pro Max devices, for both indoor and outdoor captures.

- **Dataset Contributions:**
 - The introduction of the MammaSyn synthetic dataset and the MammaEval real-world evaluation datasets (Singles, Dance, Extra), along with a new MAMMA Dance release, provides valuable resources for training and benchmarking markerless motion capture methods, particularly for interaction scenarios.
 - MammaSyn demonstrated its effectiveness as training data, improving network performance especially on challenging interaction and extreme pose sequences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MAMMA represents a notable advancement in the field of markerless human motion capture, with several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Accuracy Parity with Traditional Mocap:** The demonstration that MAMMA achieves 3D reconstruction quality competitive with commercial marker-based systems, evidenced by the sub-millimeter difference in held-out marker error against Vicon+MoSh++, is a key finding. This suggests that markerless systems can potentially replace traditional pipelines as a "ground truth" source for SMPL-X capture.
- **Democratization of High-Quality Motion Capture:** By making the MAMMA system freely available for academic research and showcasing its capability with fewer cameras and consumer-grade hardware (e.g., iPhones), the work has the potential to democratize access to high-accuracy motion capture. This can enable researchers and practitioners with limited resources to conduct studies and create content that previously required expensive, specialized equipment and expertise.
- **Efficient Workflow for Complex Motion:** The substantial reduction in processing time from capture to SMPL-X parameters (from ~72 hours for marker-based to ~26 hours for MAMMA) addresses a major bottleneck in motion capture. This efficiency can facilitate larger-scale data collection and faster iteration in research and production pipelines, particularly for complex, close human interactions.
- **Robust Handling of Multi-Person Interactions:** The explicit design choices, including landmark queries, mask conditioning, and contact optimization, enable MAMMA to accurately reconstruct motion even under challenging conditions of close multi-person interaction and occlusions. This addresses a critical limitation of many existing markerless and even marker-based systems.
- **Rich Data for Parametric Body Models:** By directly recovering SMPL-X parameters, MAMMA provides a comprehensive representation of body pose, shape, and hand/face articulations. This rich output is valuable for applications in biomechanics, animation, virtual reality, robotics, and human-computer interaction.
- **Novel Dataset and Benchmarks:** The creation and release of MammaSyn, a synthetic dataset with diverse multi-view interactions, extreme poses, and detailed annotations, alongside new real-world evaluation datasets (MammaEval), fill critical gaps in the research community's resources. These datasets can serve as future benchmarks and training grounds for developing and evaluating advanced markerless motion capture techniques.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The novel architectural elements, such as learnable landmark queries and the multi-stage optimization incorporating uncertainty and contact, provide a strong foundation for future research. Potential directions include joint multi-view landmark prediction, enhanced temporal modeling, and the integration of diffusion-based human motion priors, all aimed at further improving accuracy and robustness.

In summary, MAMMA offers a practical, accurate, and efficient solution for markerless human motion acquisition, particularly for complex multi-person scenarios, with implications for broadening the accessibility and applicability of high-fidelity human motion data.
