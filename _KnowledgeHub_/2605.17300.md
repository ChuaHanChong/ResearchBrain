# HCLM: A Hierarchical Framework for Cooperative Loco-Manipulation with Dual Quadrupeds

> [!summary] Summary
> HCLM, a hierarchical framework for cooperative loco-manipulation with dual quadrupeds, enables robust and precise collaboration by decoupling high-level spatial coordination from low-level dynamic execution. It introduces an SE(3)-invariant policy, achieving a 62.5% success rate under arbitrary initial orientations, and a task-centric whole-body controller with force regulation, maintaining end-effector stability within 2 cm under 100 N base perturbations.

## Problem

- Single-agent mobile manipulation has limited kinematic reach and operational capacity for spatially distributed or multi-point coordinated tasks.
- Existing multi-robot systems for cooperative tasks are often task-specific, lack scalability for complex scenarios, and struggle with fine-grained 6D pose control.
- Challenges in multi-robot control include poor generalization to novel configurations due to state estimation drift, and effectively managing conflicting demands of spatial coordination, robust locomotion, and closed-chain physical interactions.

## Method

- A hierarchical **HCLM** framework is developed, decoupling a high-level Canonicalized Joint ==Diffusion Policy== for collaborative reasoning from a low-level Task-Centric Cooperative Whole-Body Controller (WBC) for robust motion execution.
- The high-level policy employs an SE(3)-invariant task-space representation, relying solely on relative poses between robots to ensure configuration agnosticism and robustness to global state estimation drift.
- The low-level WBC integrates a proactive Kinematic Model Predictive Control (MPC) for collision-aware velocity planning and a reactive layer (QP for manipulation, RL for locomotion) that includes a cooperative admittance scheme for active internal force regulation during closed-chain interactions.

## Results

- The SE(3)-invariant policy achieved a **62.5%** success rate in simulations with randomized global yaws, while a world-frame baseline failed completely (**0%**), demonstrating robust configuration agnosticism.
- The framework maintained end-effector deviation below 2 cm despite an approximate 8 cm base displacement caused by a 100 N impact on the base, exhibiting resilience to physical perturbations.
- The cooperative admittance scheme successfully dissipated destructive internal forces (exceeding 50-80 N without it) during closed-chain interactions, preserving stability and preventing task failure.
- Successful real-world deployment of a dynamic Move-Handover task validated sim-to-real transferability and the policy's ability to manage relative closing speeds with two untethered quadrupeds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level spatial coordination from low-level dynamic execution in a hierarchical framework effectively addresses the complex, conflicting demands of multi-robot loco-manipulation.
> - An SE(3)-invariant task-space representation enables learning and execution of coordinate-agnostic collaborative skills, making policies robust to initial configuration variability and state estimation drift without retraining.
> - Integrating proactive kinematic Model Predictive Control with a reactive whole-body execution layer, combined with a cooperative admittance scheme for internal force regulation, ensures robust locomotion, precise task-space tracking, and safe closed-chain physical interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of mobile manipulation endeavors to integrate stable locomotion with precise object interaction. While single-agent manipulation has demonstrated substantial progress, its inherent kinematic reach and operational capacity are limited when faced with spatially distributed interactions or multi-point coordination requirements. Collaborative quadrupedal manipulators present a solution by offering dynamically reconfigurable workspaces and expanded operational ranges, thereby enabling complex, spatially coordinated tasks.

Existing research in multi-robot systems often focuses on isolated sub-fields. Some works address cooperative locomotion without incorporating manipulation capabilities, while others explore non-prehensile interactions, such as pushing, utilizing structural parts of the robots. When mobile manipulators are involved in cooperative tasks, current frameworks are often task-specific, primarily designed for pre-defined cooperative transportation scenarios. These approaches frequently lack the scalability required for handling increased task complexity or achieving fine-grained 6D pose control. Additionally, current learning-based methods for mobile manipulation typically confine end-effector control to the local body frame, which limits global task-space coordination for complex manipulation.

Imitation Learning (IL) has been effective in single-agent manipulation, but its application to multi-robot cooperative manipulation, particularly with mobile platforms, remains an area of ongoing development. Conventional IL frameworks often rely on fixed cameras or global point clouds, which poses challenges for mobile platforms due to issues such as state estimation drift and generalization to novel configurations. While hybrid architectures integrating high-level IL with low-level whole-body controllers exist for single-agent execution, they generally lack the coordination mechanisms necessary for multi-robot systems.

This work addresses these limitations by developing a hierarchical framework that aims to provide a general-purpose solution for precise, safe, and collaborative manipulation across multiple floating bases. It seeks to bridge the gap between high-level collaborative reasoning and low-level robust motion execution, enabling dual quadrupedal manipulators to learn and execute diverse manipulation skills in a coordinate-agnostic manner while ensuring stable locomotion and regulated internal forces during closed-chain interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a hierarchical framework, named HCLM, for general-purpose cooperative loco-manipulation using dual quadrupedal systems. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific sub-objectives:

- **Addressing Challenges of Multi-Robot Loco-Manipulation:** Coordinating multi-robot collaborative manipulation across floating bases is inherently complex. This complexity arises from conflicting demands for spatial coordination, robust locomotion, and the management of closed-chain physical interactions. The work aims to systematically decouple these demands to enable effective collaboration.
- **Enabling General-Purpose Collaborative Skills:** Current multi-robot systems are often confined to specific, pre-defined tasks, lacking the scalability to extend to new scenarios or achieve fine-grained 6D pose control. A key motivation is to empower quadrupedal teams to learn diverse collaborative skills that are generalizable across various manipulation tasks, rather than being limited to pre-programmed actions.
- **Achieving Configuration Agnosticism and Robustness to State Estimation Drift:** Policies parameterized in absolute global coordinates often suffer from poor generalization to novel configurations and high susceptibility to state estimation drift. The objective is to learn coordinate-free policies that enable spatially separated agents to execute diverse manipulation skills across varied initial configurations, independent of absolute reference frames.
- **Ensuring Stable Whole-Body Control with Precise Task-Space Tracking:** At the low level, the system must guarantee stable whole-body control, achieve precise task-space tracking for end-effectors, effectively reject disturbances originating from floating bases, and strictly regulate internal forces during closed-chain interactions, which are crucial for safe and stable physical collaboration.
- **Integrating High-Level Learning with Low-Level Dynamic Control:** There is a gap between complex spatial coordination learned through high-level policies and the high-frequency dynamic control required for physical execution. The research aims to bridge this gap by synergizing high-level imitation learning with a robust low-level whole-body controller.
- **Guaranteeing Collision-Free Motion and Active Force Regulation:** The system needs to proactively plan for collision avoidance between robots and with the environment, and reactively manage physical interactions. Specifically, during closed-chain interactions, the framework must safely resolve kinematic conflicts and strictly regulate internal stresses through active force regulation to prevent destructive forces.

By addressing these motivations and objectives, the HCLM framework intends to provide a highly robust pathway for multi-robot embodied coordination in complex real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HCLM framework is a hierarchical system designed for dual quadruped-manipulator systems, systematically decoupling high-level collaborative reasoning from low-level robust motion execution.

**A. System Design Overview**
The architecture consists of a high-level **Canonicalized Joint Diffusion Policy** and a low-level **Task-Centric Cooperative Whole-Body Controller (WBC)**. The Diffusion Policy operates asynchronously on a remote server, while the hybrid WBC runs synchronously onboard at a higher frequency (100 Hz).

**B. Canonicalized Joint Diffusion Policy (High-Level)**
This module formulates dual-agent collaboration as a centralized imitation learning (IL) problem.
- **Input and Output:** The policy processes an observation history, which includes eye-in-hand fisheye images and end-effector poses from both robots, to predict a future action sequence. The action `a_t` is parameterized as absolute target poses (3D position, 4D quaternion, and binary gripper state) defined within a canonical task reference frame.
- **SE(3)-Invariant Task-Space Representation:** To ensure generalization across diverse initial configurations and mitigate state estimation drift, the policy leverages an SE(3)-invariant task-space. This representation relies exclusively on the relative poses between the two robots, decoupling the joint policy from any absolute reference frame. A canonical task reference frame `T_ref` is anchored to the base pose of the primary robot (Robot 1) at the initial time step `t=0`. All input end-effector poses are transformed into this relative frame as `T_ref⁻¹ T_we,i(t)`. This formulation mathematically ensures spatial invariance.
- **Training:** The policy is trained on an expert dataset comprising synchronized camera streams, end-effector poses, and gripper states. This base-centric formulation facilitates data extensibility, including the potential for integrating human demonstrations.

**C. Task-Centric Cooperative Whole-Body Control (Low-Level)**
This module translates the synchronized task-space end-effector trajectories generated by the high-level policy into physical motion. It is designed to reconcile slow base dynamics with high-bandwidth manipulation and strictly regulate closed-chain internal forces. The WBC is further decoupled into proactive and reactive layers.

1. **Proactive Layer: Kinematic Model Predictive Control (MPC)**
 - **Purpose:** This layer optimizes base velocities and arm postures over a receding horizon to provide collision-free velocity distributions and resolve long-horizon end-effector tracking.
 - **Model:** The robot is modeled as a floating-base manipulator with a state vector `x` (base position, Euler orientation, manipulator joint angles) and a control input `u` (base linear/angular velocities, arm joint velocities).
 - **Formulation:** The MPC minimizes an objective function that includes an augmented error vector (end-effector tracking, manipulator posture) and a control penalty. It is subject to kinematic constraints, joint limits, and a Discrete Control Barrier Function (DCBF) to enforce proactive collision avoidance between agents and with environmental obstacles.
 - **Computational Aspects:** The MPC operates at the kinematic level due to the computational demands of solving full nonlinear dynamics for an 18-DoF system on an edge device. It primarily generates collision-aware velocity references, delegating dynamic stabilization to the reactive layer. The NLP is solved in real-time using the CasADi framework.

2. **Reactive Layer: Hybrid Whole-Body Execution**
 - **Purpose:** This high-frequency layer compensates for unmodeled multi-body dynamics, payload variations, and external impacts. It consists of a Quadratic Programming (QP) solver and a Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy.
 - **Quadratic Programming (QP) for Manipulator Control:**
 - **Objective:** The QP framework computes instantaneous joint velocities (`ν`) to balance competing whole-body tasks, including end-effector tracking in Cartesian space and nominal posture regulation in joint space.
 - **Cooperative Admittance Scheme for Force Regulation:** To address destructive internal stresses from rigid tracking during multi-robot manipulation, a cooperative admittance scheme is integrated. Measured multi-arm contact wrenches (`f_c`) are orthogonally decomposed using a grasp matrix (`G`) to isolate internal forces (`f_int`) in the null space. This internal force error is mapped to a corrective yield velocity (`Δx_adm`) via a virtual damping matrix (`K_d`). This `Δx_adm` is superimposed onto the task-space references in the QP objective, leading to a soft-constrained formulation that relaxes rigid positional tracking and actively dissipates antagonistic forces while preserving closed-chain stability.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Policy for Base Locomotion:**
 - **Purpose:** The RL policy ensures robust execution of the target base velocities commanded by the MPC and refined by the QP.
 - **Training and Operation:** To handle dynamic coupling and varying interaction forces, the policy is trained using domain randomization (injecting randomized payloads and external wrenches). It infers optimal base joint commands directly from proprioceptive observations, acting as a reactive stabilizer to maintain high-fidelity locomotion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HCLM framework was evaluated through extensive experiments across progressively challenging cooperative loco-manipulation tasks in simulation and validated with real-world hardware deployments.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
The simulation environment utilized Isaac Sim with a physics engine at 1000 Hz and the control framework at 100 Hz. The multi-agent system comprised two quadrupedal manipulators, each equipped with a 12-DoF Unitree Go2 base and a 6-DoF ARX5 arm, along with an eye-in-hand fisheye camera.

Three tasks were used for evaluation:
- **Dynamic Handover:** Robot A transfers a bottle to Robot B mid-air while both are in motion. Requires dynamic spatial targeting and temporal synchronization.
- **Cooperative Carrying:** Robots grasp a rigid rod and transport it collaboratively, assessing maintenance of closed-chain kinematic constraints during locomotion.
- **Cooperative Packing:** Robot A stabilizes a container mid-air while Robot B inserts a bottle into it, evaluating high-precision spatial coordination.

**1. Task Performance and Configuration Agnosticism:**
A quantitative comparison was made between policies parameterized in the absolute world frame (W) and the proposed base-centric canonical frame (BW). Both were evaluated under nominal initial setups and setups with randomized global yaws (arbitrary orientations).
- **Nominal Configurations:** Under nominal conditions, the BW formulation achieved a 66.25% overall success rate across 80 trials (53/80), comparable to the W baseline's 67.5% (54/80). This indicated that the canonicalized relative representation provided effective kinematic guidance.
- **Arbitrary Orientations:** When subjected to randomized initial global yaws, the W baseline experienced complete failure (0/80), demonstrating its overfitting to absolute coordinates. In contrast, the SE(3)-invariant BW formulation maintained a robust 62.5% success rate (50/80), showing minimal degradation from its nominal performance. This result empirically validated the framework's configuration agnosticism.

**2. Robustness and Capability Analysis:**
The framework's resilience against common real-world challenges was investigated.

- **Robustness to Physical Perturbations on the Base:** Lateral wrenches were applied to the floating base. Kinematic trajectories demonstrated spatial decoupling, where the base yielded to absorb the impulse while the end-effector (EE) maintained stable target tracking. For a 100 N impact lasting 0.2 s, EE deviation remained below 2 cm despite an approximate 8 cm base displacement. The system exhibited a "robust region," sustaining reliable performance (10-25% success rate drop) under perturbations up to 100 N.

- **Dynamic Collision Avoidance:** Upon encountering an obstacle, the mobile base autonomously deviated to bypass the threat, while the end-effectors strictly maintained their coordinated collaborative tracking and closed-chain grasp on the payload. This demonstrated the low-level WBC's ability to ensure operational safety without requiring high-level replanning.

- **Closed-Chain Compliance:** The cooperative admittance scheme was evaluated by injecting uncoordinated spatial drift to simulate vision jitter or odometry errors. Without the admittance module (rigid tracking), these deviations induced destructive internal wrenches (exceeding 50 N in Handover, 80 N in Carrying), leading to kinematic conflicts and task failure. With the active admittance scheme, internal force buildups were mapped to corrective yield velocities, dissipating antagonistic forces and preserving closed-chain stability.

**B. Real-World Setups and Experiments**
A fully untethered hardware setup comprising two Unitree Go2 quadrupeds with ARX5 manipulators and eye-in-hand fisheye cameras was used. The low-level WBC ran onboard at 100 Hz, while the high-level Diffusion Policy inferred asynchronously at 20 Hz on a remote desktop.
- **Sensing:** Base poses were provided by OptiTrack, and end-effector poses were computed via forward kinematics. The SE(3)-invariant policy design allowed independence from absolute frames, suggesting future integration with onboard SLAM without policy retraining.
- **Dynamic Move-Handover Task:** The framework was deployed on a real-world dynamic Move-Handover task, focusing on kinematic coordination. Robot A successfully retrieved and transferred an object to Robot B while both platforms were in continuous motion, starting from randomized initial positions. This validated the sim-to-real transferability and the policy's ability to manage relative closing speeds through synchronized waypoint generation in a physical distributed system.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HCLM framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multi-robot systems and embodied artificial intelligence, specifically addressing the complex challenges of cooperative loco-manipulation with dual quadrupedal platforms.

The introduction of a hierarchical architecture that systematically decouples high-level spatial coordination from low-level dynamic execution offers a principled approach to managing the conflicting demands of multi-robot systems. This decoupling enables dual quadrupedal manipulators to integrate stable locomotion with precise object interaction, facilitating complex, spatially distributed tasks that are beyond the capabilities of single-agent systems or existing limited multi-robot frameworks.

A key contribution is the development of an SE(3)-invariant task-space representation for the high-level Joint Diffusion Policy. This innovation allows policies to learn coordinate-agnostic spatial coordination patterns, making them robust to arbitrary initial configurations and inherently resilient to global pose estimation drift. The empirical demonstration of maintaining a high success rate (62.5%) under arbitrarily rotated global configurations, while baseline methods failed, highlights the practical utility of this approach for real-world deployments where initial conditions are often varied and uncertain.

The task-centric hybrid Whole-Body Controller further enhances the framework's capabilities by synergizing proactive kinematic Model Predictive Control with a reactive execution layer. This combination ensures precise end-effector tracking, dynamic disturbance rejection (maintaining task integrity even under significant external base perturbations up to 100 N), and safe management of closed-chain physical interactions through a cooperative admittance scheme. The ability to actively dissipate internal forces, which commonly arise from kinematic inconsistencies in multi-robot grasps, is critical for robust and safe collaboration.

The successful validation across progressively challenging simulated tasks (Dynamic Handover, Cooperative Carrying, Cooperative Packing) and the real-world deployment of the Dynamic Handover task underscore the framework's reliability and practical viability. This work establishes a robust pathway for multi-robot embodied coordination, demonstrating successful sim-to-real transfer for kinematic coordination patterns.

The potential impact of HCLM extends to various applications requiring complex, mobile, and collaborative manipulation in unstructured environments, such as logistics, construction, disaster response, and assistance in hazardous settings. By enabling mobile robots to collaboratively interact with and manipulate objects with precision and robustness, this research contributes to advancing the autonomy and utility of robotic systems in diverse real-world scenarios.
