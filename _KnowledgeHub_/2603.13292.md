# Pragma-VL: Towards a Pragmatic Arbitration of Safety and Helpfulness in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Pragma-VL, an end-to-end framework from Fudan University and Ant Group, enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to dynamically arbitrate between safety and helpfulness based on context. It enhances visual risk perception and uses a prompt-regulated parallel reward model, leading to improved multimodal safety rates, such as increasing SIUO safety from 38.78% to 63.47% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, while maintaining or improving general abilities.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM safety alignment techniques enforce a static, 'one-size-fits-all' balance between helpfulness and safety, often leading to excessive caution or dangerous compliance.
- MLLMs exhibit a foundational deficiency in perceiving contextual visual risks, as visual encoders are typically optimized for semantic description rather than implicit danger signals.
- Current alignment frameworks lack context-aware preference signals, relying on single subjective scores or uniformly weighted multi-head reward models, preventing dynamic prioritization of safety or helpfulness.

## Method

- A novel data augmentation method, ==PragmaSafe==, enriches datasets with dynamic, context-aware labels, including helpfulness/harmlessness scores and variance-aware safety-utility weight vectors.
- A two-stage MLLM Cold Start process rectifies visual risk blindness by restructuring the visual latent space via risk-aware contrastive learning and integrating this enhanced perception using specialized Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- A policy alignment stage utilizes a parallel, multi-head reward model, theoretically proven superior, to dynamically arbitrate between helpfulness and safety via prompt-regulated rewards and the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

## Results

- Pragma-VL significantly improved multimodal safety on benchmarks, reducing Attack Success Rate (ASR) to **31.66%** for Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** and increasing Safe Inputs but Unsafe Output (SIUO) safety from **38.78%** to **63.47%** for Qwen.
- The framework maintained or slightly enhanced the model's general capabilities across a majority of benchmarks (e.g., GQA, ScienceQA, VQAv2), avoiding the common trade-off where safety alignment degrades performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic contribution of both the risk-aware cold-start phase and the policy alignment stage, with their combined effect achieving peak SIUO scores and superior robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic, context-aware arbitration is crucial for MLLMs to pragmatically balance safety and helpfulness, moving beyond static policies that often result in either over-refusal or dangerous compliance.
> - Rectifying an MLLM's internal visual risk perception through a 'cold-start' phase is fundamental for addressing latent cross-modality safety issues that traditional alignment methods often miss.
> - A parallel multi-head reward model architecture is empirically and theoretically superior for multi-objective alignment, leveraging inter-task correlations to yield lower asymptotic Mean Squared Error and Expected Pairwise Preference Error compared to single or sequential designs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which integrate visual and linguistic information, have demonstrated advanced capabilities across various tasks. However, this progress introduces significant safety challenges. MLLMs are susceptible to generating harmful content, whether inadvertently for benign users or through adversarial attacks like jailbreaking. Current research efforts primarily focus on internal safety alignment strategies, such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), to mitigate these risks.

A fundamental limitation of existing alignment techniques is their tendency to enforce a fixed, static balance between helpfulness (providing useful responses) and safety (avoiding harmful content). This "one-size-fits-all" approach often leads to a "dual failure pattern." Models can become overly cautious, refusing benign queries and reducing their utility, or they can exhibit "dangerous compliance," generating harmful content in response to seemingly innocuous prompts, especially when risky visual information is involved. This indicates a lack of context-aware arbitration and a deficiency in perceiving implicit visual dangers, as visual encoders are typically optimized for semantic description rather than risk signals. Existing alignment frameworks also often rely on single subjective quality scores or uniformly weighted multi-head reward models, failing to dynamically prioritize safety or helpfulness based on context. This work addresses these issues by proposing a framework for dynamic, context-aware arbitration in MLLMs, aiming to overcome the limitations of static safety policies and enhance visual risk perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to empower MLLMs to dynamically arbitrate the helpfulness-safety trade-off, moving beyond fixed, context-agnostic safety policies. The motivation for this objective stems from two critical shortcomings observed in current MLLMs and their alignment methodologies:

1. **Internal Perception Deficiency:** MLLMs often exhibit a flawed perception of contextual risk, particularly from visual inputs. Their visual encoders, primarily trained on datasets rich in helpful information but sparse in explicit risk signals (e.g., image captions), struggle to identify implicit visual dangers. This creates a "modality imbalance" where visual risks are often overlooked or misinterpreted, leading to unsafe responses despite potentially benign textual prompts. The researchers identified a need to rectify this foundational perceptual gap within the model itself, making it intrinsically aware of potential visual hazards.
2. **External Framework Inadequacy:** Existing alignment frameworks lack the necessary context-aware preference signals to guide MLLMs in making nuanced judgments. Reward models typically collapse safety and helpfulness into a single, often subjective, quality score or apply uniform weighting across multiple objectives. This prevents the model from understanding when to prioritize safety over helpfulness, or vice-versa, based on the specific query and its multimodal context. Consequently, current models cannot dynamically adjust their behavior to optimally balance these competing objectives, leading to either excessive caution or dangerous compliance.

To address these challenges, the authors propose Pragma-VL, an end-to-end alignment framework designed to:
- Rectify the model's perceptual deficiencies by enhancing visual risk perception.
- Equip the model with a dynamic decision-making policy that can contextually arbitrate between helpfulness and safety.

This approach aims to enable MLLMs to make "pragmatic" judgments that are both safe and helpful, adapting their responses to the specific context of each multimodal interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Pragma-VL is an end-to-end, three-stage pipeline designed to instill context-aware safety-helpfulness judgment in MLLMs.

#### 3.1. Contextual Data Augmentation (PragmaSafe)

The foundation of Pragma-VL is a novel data augmentation method called PragmaSafe, which enriches existing datasets (e.g., BeaverTails-V) with dynamic, context-aware labels.
- **Response Generation and Annotation:** Diverse responses are generated for image-question pairs using six MLLMs. A GPT-4o annotator assigns three labels to each response: a Helpfulness score (scale of -2 to 2), a Harmlessness score (scale of -2 to 2), and a Safety-Utility weight vector (chosen from five predefined options, e.g., [1.0, 0.0] for helpfulness-focused, [0.5, 0.5] for neutral). This annotation process is repeated five times per response to gather diverse feedback.
- **Variance-Aware Weight Adjustment:** To enhance label robustness and mitigate reward model overfitting to fixed weights, a variance-aware weight adjustment mechanism is introduced. While helpfulness and harmlessness scores are determined by majority voting, the weight vector undergoes a refinement process. The final weight, $W_{final}$, is derived from an initial base weight, $W_{base}$, through stochastic interpolation. The direction of adjustment is guided by a target function $T$, which shifts emphasis towards the dimension (helpfulness or harmlessness) with higher rater agreement (lower variance). The magnitude of this adjustment is proportional to the absolute difference between the variances of helpfulness ($\sigma^2_h$) and harmlessness ($\sigma^2_s$) scores, controlled by a dynamically scaled standard deviation. This acts as a soft regularization, preventing the model from collapsing into discrete weight patterns. The PragmaSafe dataset consists of 122,961 data items and 22,636 unique image-question pairs, balancing general capabilities and safety-critical content.

#### 3.2. MLLM Cold Start: Establishing the Risk-Aware Foundation

This pre-alignment phase addresses the inherent visual risk blindness in MLLMs. It consists of two stages:
- **Stage 1: Restructuring the Visual Latent Space via Risk-Aware Contrastive Learning:** This stage calibrates the visual encoder's latent space using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) to encourage representations to cluster by risk severity, complementing existing semantic arrangements. A Risk-Aware Contrastive Loss ($L_{Risk-Aware}$) is applied, using image severity tags from the BeaverTails-V dataset as class labels. The objective is to group representations of images with the same risk level while separating them from images with different risk levels. A "zero-risk" class is established by augmenting training data with diverse safe images. This trains the visual encoder to be sensitive to risk-related features.
- **Stage 2: Integrating Perception and Cognition with Risk-Aware SFT:** The LoRA-tuned visual encoder is integrated back into the MLLM. A specialized Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) process is performed with the visual encoder unfrozen, allowing language-driven objectives to further refine its representations. The model is trained on a curated, interleaved dataset combining standard safety Q&A pairs with targeted risk-identification tasks (e.g., "What is the potential harm in this image?"). GPT-4o generates high-quality responses for these risk-identification prompts. This stage teaches the model to identify risks arising from visual input or cross-modal interactions.

#### 3.3. Policy Alignment via Prompt-Regulated Rewards

This final stage leverages a parallel, multi-head reward model to dynamically arbitrate between helpfulness and safety.
- **Why Parallel Rewards?** The researchers compare three reward model architectures:
 - **Single-Objective:** A single MLP head predicts a scalar score.
 - **Sequential-Objective:** Multi-score heads (e.g., helpfulness, harmlessness) are trained via MSE, then frozen, and their outputs feed into a "meta-voter" MLP for the final scalar score.
 - **Parallel-Objective (Ours):** The backbone connects to parallel heads that are jointly trained to simultaneously output multi-objective scores (for interpretability) and a weighted scalar score (for policy optimization). All components are optimized in a single stage via a joint loss.
 Empirical evaluation (Table 1) shows the parallel model consistently outperforms sequential and single-head models in preference accuracy. This empirical advantage is supported by theoretical analysis (Theorem 1), which demonstrates that the parallel architecture provably yields lower asymptotic Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Expected Pairwise Preference Error (Err$_{pref}$) by leveraging inter-task correlations and capturing more Fisher information.
- **Reward Modeling and RL Alignment:** The reward model is optimized using a joint loss function ($L_{RM}$) combining Bradley-Terry (BT) and Mean Squared Error (MSE). The BT loss optimizes the scalar output of the weighted head ($r^w_\theta(x, y)$) on high-fidelity preference pairs from the $D_{BT}$ dataset, serving as the primary reward for policy updates. The MSE loss aligns the model’s full vector output ($r_\theta(x, y) = [r_{help}, r_{harm}, r^w_\theta]$) with ground truth scores from the $D_{MSE}$ dataset. Hard-negative mining is employed, replacing 10% of rejected responses with formulaic reward hacking outputs to improve robustness. Finally, the context-aware reward signal $r^w_\theta$ guides the MLLM during the reinforcement learning phase using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, steering its behavior toward dynamic, context-dependent judgments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experiments demonstrating Pragma-VL's effectiveness in balancing safety and helpfulness while preserving general capabilities.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Safety

Pragma-VL consistently outperformed baseline models on multimodal safety benchmarks, including Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Llava-1.5-7B (Table 2).
- **Balanced Performance:** Pragma-VL achieved the highest win rates on BeaverTails-V (Qwen: 62.65% Help, 67.91% Harm; Llava: 86.93% Help, 88.96% Harm) and SPA-VL (Qwen: 87.17% Help, 87.92% Harm; Llava: 97.93% Help, 92.05% Harm).
- **Robustness to Attacks:** On MM-SafetyBench, Pragma-VL significantly reduced the Attack Success Rate (ASR) to 31.66% for Qwen, a reduction of over 17 percentage points from the base model, and to 31.67% for Llava.
- **Cross-Modal Risk Mitigation:** Pragma-VL demonstrated a notable ability to address latent cross-modality risks, improving the SIUO (Safe Inputs but Unsafe Output) safety rate from 38.78% to 63.47% for Qwen and from 14.37% to 55.42% for Llava. This improvement is attributed to the enhanced visual risk perception from the cold-start phase and the contextual arbitration provided by the reward model.
- **Situational Safety:** The framework also excelled on MSSbench, which evaluates situational safety, achieving the highest Safety scores (55.89% on Qwen and 55.05% on Llava), indicating the model learned to recognize subtle visual contexts requiring safety-oriented responses without excessive refusals.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** Pragma-VL achieved balanced gains across helpfulness, harmlessness, and robustness, outperforming simpler alignment methods (like DPO) that often necessitate trade-offs, and more rigid approaches like Safe-RLHF-V.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on General Ability

Pragma-VL demonstrated the ability to maintain and often slightly enhance the model's core general capabilities, avoiding the common trade-off where safety alignment degrades performance (Table 3).
- **Preservation and Enhancement:** Pragma-VL achieved top scores on a majority of general benchmarks (e.g., GQA, ScienceQA, VQAv2) for both Qwen and Llava models.
- **Contrast with Other Methods:** Methods aligned using specialized safety datasets (e.g., BeaverTails-V, SPA-VL) often showed a noticeable drop in performance across general tasks, highlighting Pragma-VL's effectiveness in integrating safety alignment as part of general capabilities rather than a separate constraint.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic contributions of the MLLM Cold-Start and Policy Alignment stages (Table 4, Figure 4).
- **Cold-Start Contribution:** Incorporating the risk-aware encoder (EC+SFT) yielded an 8.67% gain in SIUO Safety compared to standard SFT alone (40.12% to 48.79%) for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. For Llava-1.5-7B, EC+SFT improved SIUO Safety by 5.8% and BeaverTails-V Helpfulness by 5.6% compared to standard SFT alone (Table 7).
- **Policy Alignment Contribution:** The full Pragma-VL framework demonstrated superior robustness (lowest ASR of 31.66%) and utility (highest SPA-VL Helpfulness of 87.17%) compared to the SFT+GRPO baseline.
- **Synergistic Effect:** The integration of both stages achieved the peak SIUO score (63.47%), surpassing the individual contributions of Cold-Start alone (48.79%) or Policy Alignment alone (59.88%), underscoring that both foundational perception and delicate policy arbitration are essential.

#### 4.4. Reward Model Architecture Comparison

The empirical comparison of reward model architectures (Table 1, Table 8) and theoretical proof (Theorem 1) validated the superiority of the parallel, multi-head design.
- **Empirical Superiority:** The parallel model consistently outperformed sequential and single-head models in preference accuracy across all metrics, especially for pairs with large score differences.
- **Theoretical Justification:** The proof, grounded in Fisher information theory, demonstrated that the parallel framework leverages inter-task correlations to capture more information, resulting in lower parameter covariance, reduced MSE, and lower expected preference error compared to single-objective or sequential approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Pragma-VL represents an advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Model safety alignment. By proposing an end-to-end framework that enables pragmatic, context-aware arbitration between safety and helpfulness, this work addresses a critical limitation of existing static, context-agnostic safety policies.

The primary significance lies in its ability to overcome the perennial safety-utility trade-off, a challenge that frequently forces model developers to compromise between providing helpful responses and ensuring harmlessness. Pragma-VL's innovations—namely, the risk-aware "cold-start" phase and the theoretically-grounded parallel reward model with dynamic, prompt-regulated signals—enable MLLMs to perceive subtle visual risks and adjust their arbitration policies contextually. This results in models that exhibit enhanced safety and helpfulness simultaneously, rather than at the expense of one another.

The consistent outperformance on specialized safety benchmarks, coupled with the preservation and even slight enhancement of general capabilities, indicates that Pragma-VL offers a more integrated and robust approach to MLLM development. This work suggests a shift from rigid safety protocols to a more nuanced, dynamic decision-making process, potentially leading to the deployment of MLLMs that are more trustworthy, reliable, and adaptable to a wider range of real-world scenarios. The findings could influence future research directions in MLLM alignment, prompting the development of more sophisticated, context-sensitive models that better align with human values and operational requirements.
