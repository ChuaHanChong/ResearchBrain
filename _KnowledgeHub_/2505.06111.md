# UniVLA: Learning to Act Anywhere with Task-centric Latent Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> A generalist robot policy framework learns task-centric latent actions from internet-scale, action-label free videos. This approach achieves average success rates of 95.2% on LIBERO and 47.1% on Room2Room, demonstrating efficient, cross-embodiment generalization and robust real-world performance.

## Problem

- Robot policies heavily rely on expensive, ground-truth action labels, limiting scalability to new tasks, robots, or environments.
- Heterogeneous action and observation spaces across different robot embodiments hinder knowledge transfer and the development of unified generalist policies.
- Existing latent action learning methods often capture task-irrelevant dynamics (e.g., camera shakes, background motion), resulting in noisy and ineffective action representations.

## Method

- A two-stage ==task-centric latent action== learning framework that uses ==DINOv2== features and language conditioning to explicitly decouple task-relevant actions from environmental noise.
- Pretraining a generalist policy on a **Prismatic-7B** Vision-Language Model (VLM), extending its vocabulary with special tokens for learned latent actions to leverage vast, unannotated video data.
- A lightweight action decoder and ==Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning== (PEFT) for efficient deployment, incorporating historical latent actions into prompts to enhance sequential decision-making.

## Results

- Achieved **95.2%** average success on the LIBERO manipulation benchmark (**18.7%** improvement over OpenVLA) and **47.1%** oracle success on the Room2Room navigation benchmark (**29.6%** improvement over OpenVLA).
- Demonstrated robust real-world robot deployment with an **81.7%** average success rate and an average score of 2.63 on an AgileX Piper arm, outperforming LAPA by **36.7%** in success rate.
- Showcased superior data efficiency, achieving higher success rates on LIBERO-Goal (**86.3%**) with only **10%** of demonstration data compared to OpenVLA trained on the full dataset (**79.2%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling task-centric dynamics from task-irrelevant visual changes is crucial for learning robust and effective latent action representations from noisy, large-scale video datasets.
> - Utilizing semantically rich visual features like DINOv2 as reconstruction targets for latent action learning allows models to focus on meaningful task-related movements over pixel-level noise.
> - A unified, embodiment-agnostic latent action space enables significant knowledge transfer across diverse data sources, including human videos, fostering generalist robot policies with reduced computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies that can perform a wide array of tasks across diverse environments and embodiments remains a grand challenge in robotics and artificial intelligence. This paper directly addresses several critical limitations of current approaches, positioning UniVLA at the forefront of scalable and efficient robot policy learning.

Traditionally, robot policies, especially those based on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like RT-1, Octo, RT-2, and OpenVLA, have made significant strides by leveraging large-scale robotic datasets. However, a major bottleneck for these models is their heavy reliance on **ground-truth action labels**. This dependence severely restricts their scalability, as manually annotating action data for every new robot, task, or environment is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. Moreover, the **heterogeneity of action and observation spaces** across different robot embodiments (e.g., a Franka arm vs. a WidowX arm, or even human hands) and tasks (e.g., precise manipulation vs. broad navigation) poses a substantial challenge for knowledge transfer, preventing the development of truly unified generalist policies.

Recognizing these limitations, a burgeoning area of research has explored **latent action learning**—methods that attempt to extract meaningful action representations directly from visual data, often videos, without explicit action labels. Prior works like VQ-BeT, Quest, Genie, LAPO, LAPA, and IGOR have investigated this direction. However, as highlighted by the authors, many of these approaches suffer from a critical flaw: their reconstruction-based objectives often lead to the capture of **task-irrelevant dynamics**. This means that latent actions might encode noise from camera shakes, movements of non-ego agents, or unpredictable environmental changes, rather than focusing on the actual task-centric actions of the robot or human. Such noisy representations inevitably hinder effective policy pretraining and downstream performance.

UniVLA distinguishes itself by directly tackling this "task-irrelevant dynamics" problem through a novel **task-centric latent action learning** framework. By explicitly decoupling task-relevant actions from environmental noise and leveraging semantically rich visual features (DINOv2) instead of raw pixels, UniVLA aims to learn a cleaner, more informative, and **unified action representation**. This allows it to exploit vast quantities of unannotated internet-scale video data, including human demonstrations, which were previously difficult to leverage effectively due to the "action-label free" and "cross-embodiment" nature of the data. By doing so, UniVLA seeks to enable a generalist policy that can plan effectively and efficiently, transcending the boundaries of specific robot hardware and task domains, much like large language models learn transferable knowledge across diverse languages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **generalist robot policy** capable of performing effectively across a wide array of environments, robotic embodiments, and tasks. To achieve this overarching goal, the authors are motivated by and aim to address several specific challenges inherent in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Overcome Data Scalability Limitations:** Current state-of-the-art robot policies heavily depend on large, action-annotated datasets. This dependency severely limits their ability to scale, as the cost and effort of collecting and labeling such data for every new scenario are enormous. UniVLA aims to break this reliance by enabling policy learning from **internet-scale, action-label free video data**, including diverse robot demonstrations and egocentric human videos.

2. **Facilitate Cross-Embodiment Knowledge Transfer:** The diverse physical specifications and control interfaces of different robots (e.g., various manipulators, mobile robots) create heterogeneous action and observation spaces. This heterogeneity makes it difficult to transfer learned knowledge across different embodiments. A core motivation is to learn a **unified, embodiment-agnostic action representation** that allows a single policy to generalize across different robots and even human actions.

3. **Mitigate Task-Irrelevant Dynamics:** Existing latent action learning approaches often struggle with "noisy" representations. When trained to reconstruct visual changes from raw pixels, these models frequently capture dynamics irrelevant to the task, such as camera movements, lighting changes, or background motion. This noise degrades policy performance. A key objective is to derive **task-centric latent actions** that explicitly filter out such irrelevant dynamics, focusing only on the meaningful movements necessary for task completion.

4. **Achieve Efficient Policy Deployment:** After pretraining, the generalist policy needs to be adapted for specific tasks and robots with minimal additional data and computational cost. The authors aim for an **efficient latent action decoding mechanism** that can translate the unified latent plans into concrete, executable control signals for various robotic systems.

In essence, UniVLA is motivated by the vision of "learning to act anywhere"—developing a versatile robot brain that can understand and execute tasks regardless of the specific body it inhabits, by distilling actionable knowledge from the vast ocean of available video data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniVLA's methodology is structured into three interlinked stages designed to achieve scalable, efficient, and generalizable robot policy learning:

#### 3.1. Task-centric Latent Action Learning

This foundational stage is crucial for deriving the "pseudo action labels" (latent action tokens) that will train the generalist policy.

- **Latent Action Quantization:** The process begins with pairs of consecutive video frames, `{o_t, o_{t+k}}`, separated by a calibrated time interval (approximately 1 second across datasets). An Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) based encoder, `I(a_t | o_t, o_{t+k})`, infers a latent action, and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) based decoder, `F(o_{t+k} | o_t, a_t)`, predicts future observations. The encoder is a spatial-temporal transformer, while the decoder is a spatial transformer.
 - Crucially, unlike prior work that predicts raw pixels, UniVLA uses **DINOv2 spatial patch features** as both inputs and prediction targets. DINOv2 features are semantically rich, object-centric, and spatially aware, which helps the model focus on task-relevant visual changes rather than noisy, task-irrelevant details like textures or lighting variations common in web-scale videos. The objective is to minimize the embedding reconstruction error `||O_{t+k} - O_{t+k}||^2`.
 - To further compress information and align with an auto-regressive policy, the latent actions are quantized using a **VQ-VAE** (Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder) objective with a predefined codebook of `|C|` vocabulary size.

- **Latent Action Decoupling:** This is a novel two-stage training framework to disentangle task-centric dynamics from irrelevant visual changes.
 - **Stage 1 (Task-Irrelevant Learning):** Language instructions `ℓ` (encoded by a pretrained T5 text encoder) are fed as conditioning signals to both the encoder and decoder. In this setup, the language provides high-level semantic guidance for the task. Consequently, the quantized latent actions are optimized to encode **environmental changes and visual details** (`a_TI`), effectively becoming "task-irrelevant" representations, as the higher-level task information is handled by the language input due to the codebook's constrained capacity.
 - **Stage 2 (Task-Centric Learning):** The codebook and parameters from Stage 1 (for `a_TI`) are repurposed and frozen. A *new* set of latent actions, `a_TC`, is introduced. In this stage, `a_TC` replaces the role of language, and the model focuses on extracting **task-centric dynamics** from the DINOv2 features. This explicit decoupling ensures that `a_TC` models task-related dynamics (e.g., object manipulation, goal-directed motion) while `a_TI` handles background changes. This approach ensures that the learned `a_TC` representations are more informative and lead to faster convergence for policy learning.

#### 3.2. Pretraining of Generalist Policy

Once the latent action model is trained, it's used to annotate video frames with `a_TC` latent actions. A generalist policy is then trained using these labels.

- **Architecture:** The generalist policy is built upon the **Prismatic-7B Vision-Language Model (VLM)**. This VLM integrates a fused visual encoder (SigLip and DINOv2), a projection layer, and the LLaMA-2 large language model (LLM).
- **Action Representation:** Unlike VLA models that map continuous actions to language tokenizer vocabulary (e.g., OpenVLA), UniVLA extends the LLaMA vocabulary with `|C|` special tokens (`{ACT_1..., ACT_C}`) representing the indices of the task-centric action codebook. This preserves the original VLM architecture and training objectives, allowing full leverage of pretrained VLM knowledge.
- **Training Objective:** The policy `π_φ` is an auto-regressive model that takes observation `o_t`, task instruction `l`, and prefixes of latent action tokens `a_{z,<i}` to predict the next latent action `a_{z,i}`. It's optimized to minimize the negative log-probability of the next latent action.
- **Efficiency:** The compressed latent action space (e.g., `16^4` vs. `256^7` in OpenVLA) significantly accelerates model convergence and reduces pretraining compute (960 A100-hours vs. 21,500 A100-hours for OpenVLA).

#### 3.3. Post-training for Deployment

This stage focuses on efficiently adapting the pretrained generalist policy to specific robotic systems.

- **Latent Action Decoding:** A lightweight action decoder (only 12.6M parameters) translates predicted latent actions into executable control signals. It uses multi-head attention pooling to aggregate visual embeddings into a single token, which then queries the latent action embeddings. The resulting action embedding is linearly projected into the desired action space of the target robot. Since latent actions represent dynamics over a one-second interval, they are naturally decoded into **action chunks**, allowing for smoother and more precise control customized for different embodiments.
- **Learn from History Outputs (Chain-of-Thought for Robotics):** To enhance sequential decision-making, historical latent action outputs are incorporated into the input prompt at each timestep during rollouts. The latent action model annotates actions from historical frames, which are then mapped to LLaMA tokens and appended to task instructions. This creates a feedback loop, allowing the policy to learn from its past decisions and adapt to dynamic environments without incurring the high computational cost of processing multiple historical visual frames.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT):** LoRA is employed during downstream adaptation, making the process efficient with a small number of trainable parameters (approx. 123M total with the action head). The entire model is trained end-to-end, optimizing both next-latent action prediction and L1 loss for low-level actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniVLA demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across a comprehensive suite of benchmarks and real-world deployments, showcasing its efficiency, scalability, and generalizability.

- **Superior Manipulation Performance (LIBERO, CALVIN, SimplerEnv):**
 - On the challenging LIBERO benchmark, UniVLA achieves an average success rate of **95.2%** when pretrained on the full dataset, significantly outperforming OpenVLA (76.5%) by **18.7%** and LAPA (65.7%) by **29.5%**.
 - Remarkably, even when pretrained *solely* on the Bridge-V2 dataset, UniVLA reaches **92.5%** average performance, surpassing methods like MaIL and MDT that use additional camera inputs and larger datasets.
 - Pretraining *only* on human videos (Ego4D) still allows UniVLA to outperform OpenVLA, highlighting the power of leveraging diverse, unlabeled data.
 - On CALVIN, UniVLA achieves a **56.5%** success rate for completing five tasks in sequence, exceeding CLOVER (45.4%) by **11.1%** and OpenVLA by **13%**, particularly excelling in long-horizon tasks.
 - On SimplerEnv-Bridge, UniVLA achieves a **47.9%** task success rate, outperforming OpenVLA (1.0%) by a massive **46.9%**.

- **Strong Navigation Capabilities (Room2Room):**
 - UniVLA achieves a **47.1%** oracle success rate on the R2R benchmark, significantly surpassing OpenVLA (17.5%) by **29.6%** and LLaVA-Nav by **33.1%**.
 - Its performance is comparable to NaVid, a navigation model that processes all historical observations, despite UniVLA relying only on the current observation and historical latent actions.

- **Robust Real-world Robot Deployment:**
 - In real-world tests with an AgileX Piper arm, UniVLA achieves an average success rate of **81.7%** and an average score of **2.63** across four diverse tasks, outperforming LAPA (45% success, 1.95 score) by **36.7%** in success rate and **0.68** in average score.
 - UniVLA maintains a real-time, closed-loop inference frequency of **10Hz** by planning in its compact latent action space and using efficient action chunk prediction, addressing the execution stuttering issues faced by OpenVLA due to its inference latency.
 - The model demonstrates exceptional generalizability to unseen real-world scenarios, including lightning variations, visual distractors, and novel objects, achieving an average success rate of **68.9%** and an average score of **2.49** across these challenging settings.

- **Effective Latent Action Learning:**
 - **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations show that latent actions learned by UniVLA are semantically consistent across different data sources and embodiments (e.g., "Pick up things," "Move Down"). They successfully generalize to unseen domains and bridge different modalities (e.g., wrist-view manipulation with ego-centric navigation).
 - **Quantitative Analysis (Ablation):** Training policies with labels derived from the proposed *task-centric* latent actions significantly outperforms those using naive latent actions (Genie) or *task-irrelevant* ones. Task-centric latent actions yield an **88.7%** average success rate on LIBERO (human video pretraining) compared to 82.3% for Genie and 56.5% for task-irrelevant actions, particularly crucial for long-horizon tasks.

- **Scalability and Data Efficiency:**
 - **Data Scalability:** UniVLA's performance consistently improves as more heterogeneous pretraining data (OpenX, Ego4D human videos) is incorporated, demonstrating its ability to leverage diverse data sources despite embodiment gaps.
 - **Data Efficiency:** UniVLA shows superior data efficiency during downstream adaptation. With only **10%** of demonstration data, it achieves a higher success rate on LIBERO-Goal (86.3%) than OpenVLA trained on the *full* dataset (79.2%).

- **Ablations on Design Choices:**
 - The proposed action decoder design, leveraging visual embeddings as query inputs, significantly outperforms auto-regressive decoding (95.2% vs. 73.6% average success on LIBERO).
 - Incorporating historical latent actions as prompt inputs, even with minimal tokens, enhances performance, especially in long-horizon tasks (e.g., **16.5%** improvement on R2R, **3.9%** on LIBERO-Long).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniVLA represents a significant leap forward in the quest for generalist robot policies, offering solutions to several long-standing challenges in robot learning. Its potential impact spans both research and practical applications:

1. **Unlocking Internet-Scale Data for Robotics:** The most profound impact of UniVLA is its ability to learn from vast, **action-label free video data**, including diverse robot demonstrations and human videos. This dramatically expands the training corpus for robot learning, moving beyond the bottleneck of expensive, manually annotated datasets. This paves the way for robots to acquire skills from the wealth of unstructured visual information available online, accelerating the development of more capable and versatile robotic systems.

2. **Facilitating Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** By creating a unified, embodiment-agnostic latent action space, UniVLA enables true knowledge transfer across different robotic platforms and even human demonstrations. This means that a policy trained on one type of robot or on human videos can be readily adapted to another, greatly reducing the development cycle for new robotic systems and allowing for faster deployment in diverse real-world scenarios.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Accessibility:** UniVLA achieves state-of-the-art results with significantly reduced pretraining compute (1/20th of OpenVLA) and less downstream data (1/10th). This computational efficiency makes advanced robot learning more accessible to researchers and developers with fewer resources, democratizing the field and fostering more rapid innovation.

4. **Robustness and Real-world Applicability:** The explicit decoupling of task-centric from task-irrelevant dynamics results in cleaner, more robust action representations. This directly translates to improved performance in complex, dynamic, and often noisy real-world environments, as demonstrated by UniVLA's superior generalizability to lighting variations, visual distractors, and novel objects, as well as its strong real-robot deployment success. The efficient inference latency also addresses a critical practical hurdle for real-time robot control.

5. **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The two-stage task-centric latent action learning framework, utilizing DINOv2 features and language conditioning for dynamic decoupling, is a novel and impactful methodological contribution. This approach provides a blueprint for future work on learning meaningful and robust action representations from raw perceptual data.

6. **Paving the Way for Advanced Robotic Capabilities:** The ability to learn a unified planning space and leverage historical latent actions for decision-making sets the stage for more sophisticated robotic intelligence. This includes potential for advanced in-context learning, where robots could acquire new skills zero-shot by merely observing human demonstrations encoded into latent actions, or more complex long-horizon planning by integrating with world models and planning trees.

In conclusion, UniVLA not only achieves superior empirical performance but also introduces a robust and scalable framework that addresses fundamental limitations in generalist robot policy learning. Its implications are profound, moving the field closer to robots that can truly "act anywhere" and learn autonomously from the world around them.
