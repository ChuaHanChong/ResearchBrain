# Can Vision-Language Models Assess Proxemic Risk from Egocentric Robot Images?

> [!summary] Summary
> A benchmark study asking whether open-weight VLMs can judge proxemic danger from egocentric robot images. The authors relabel 1,243 JRDB frames into four Hall-derived danger zones, then evaluate InternVL3.5-4B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B and SmolVLM2-2.2B across three prompt strategies and two QLoRA rounds, scoring danger class plus bounding-box IoU. All configurations sit near a random baseline; only Qwen reaches useful high-danger recall, and grounding stays decoupled from classification.

## Problem

- Safe robot navigation among humans mostly relies on explicit geometric reasoning or depth sensors; whether general visual understanding can replace or complement them is untested.
- Prior VLM spatial-intelligence benchmarks never evaluated the egocentric viewpoint of a robot operating in shared human environments under proxemic constraints.
- Existing egocentric robot datasets label detection and tracking, not graded proxemic risk zones, so no benchmark scores fine-grained danger classification.

## Method

- A new proxemic-danger dataset: 1,243 egocentric JRDB frames hand-labeled into high/moderate/low/minimum ==Hall zones==, split indoor good-light, indoor poor-light, and outdoor for lighting realism.
- Three structurally distinct prompts (simple, moderate, advanced ==chain-of-reasoning==) sharing one zone definition and a ==JSON schema== returning zone, distance estimate, bounding box, and danger level.
- ==Two-stage QLoRA fine-tuning== (r=8, alpha=16, dropout 0.05) on 200 class-balanced images: stage one adapts q_proj/v_proj, stage two extends to all projections plus lm_head.

## Results

- All models sit near the random baseline: weighted F1 spans **0.211-0.238** versus **0.25** random, and fine-tuning shifts weighted F1 by less than **0.02** in either direction.
- Qwen reaches **0.395-0.440** high-danger recall against near-zero for InternVL (**0.026-0.033**) and SmolVLM (**0.016-0.069**); its best advanced-prompt configuration hits **0.790** recall at **0.182** precision.
- Grounding is decoupled: mean IoU on correct versus incorrect frames is **0.204** versus **0.215**, and Qwen's high-danger true positives (**0.488**) barely beat its false negatives (**0.464**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A correct safety label does not prove the model attended to the responsible person, so classification accuracy alone is an unsafe deployment criterion for embodied risk triggers.
> - Prompt complexity trades consistency for sensitivity: chain-of-reasoning prompts raise rare-class recall while lowering overall F1 and mean IoU, a measurable format tax.
> - Parameter-efficient tuning on a few hundred images cannot install spatial reasoning absent from pretraining, and actively degrades sub-3B models through catastrophic forgetting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Mobile robots operating in human-populated environments are required to perform continuous risk assessment for safe and socially acceptable navigation. This necessitates the ability to understand and adhere to human personal space, a concept known as proxemics. Traditional approaches to safety assessment in human-robot interaction often rely on explicit geometric reasoning or specialized depth sensors to gauge distances and potential collision risks.

This research contributes to the evolving field of embodied artificial intelligence by investigating the potential of universal visual understanding systems, specifically Vision-Language Models (VLMs), as an alternative or complementary method for proxemic risk assessment. VLMs have demonstrated proficiency in various visual perception tasks, including zero-shot and few-shot learning, due to their capacity for cross-modal reasoning between visual and linguistic representations. Their adaptability through prompt engineering and lightweight fine-tuning makes them attractive candidates for complex, real-world robotic applications.

However, a recognized limitation in the broader VLM landscape is their spatial intelligence, particularly in fine-grained reasoning from an egocentric perspective. Previous studies have explored proxemics in robotics through established frameworks like Edward T. Hall's proxemic zones, often employing methods that directly calculate distances or analyze crowd density. This paper positions itself to address the question of whether modern VLMs can interpret these proxemic zones solely from raw egocentric images, without explicit geometric computations, and if their understanding is spatially grounded to the relevant elements in the scene (e.g., people). By using egocentric images, the work directly ties perception to the robot's immediate operational context. The creation and open-sourcing of a new dataset based on proxemic danger further contributes to the community by providing a standardized benchmark for future research in this specific area of human-robot interaction safety.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research was to evaluate the capability of open-source Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to assess proxemic danger from images captured from a robot's egocentric perspective. This overarching goal was supported by several specific objectives and driven by core motivations related to robot safety and autonomy.

**Specific Objectives:**

1. **Proxemic Danger Classification**: To classify egocentric robot images into four predefined levels of proxemic danger—High, Moderate, Low, and Minimum—based on Edward T. Hall's formalized concept of personal space.
2. **VLM Performance Comparison**: To compare the performance of three open-source VLMs (InternVL, Qwen-VL, and SmolVLM) in this multi-class classification task.
3. **Prompting Strategy Evaluation**: To investigate the impact of different prompt engineering strategies (Simple, Moderate, and Advanced) on the models' ability to classify danger and extract relevant information.
4. **Fine-tuning Impact Assessment**: To analyze the effect of lightweight fine-tuning using the QLoRA method, applied in two distinct stages, on VLM performance, including overall accuracy and specific class recall.
5. **Spatial Grounding Analysis**: To evaluate whether the models' predictions regarding danger levels are spatially grounded, meaning if they accurately identify and localize the person or persons responsible for the perceived danger within the image using bounding boxes.

**Motivation:**

1. **Ensuring Safe and Socially Acceptable Robot Navigation**: Mobile robots operating in environments shared with humans must continuously assess potential risks to avoid collisions, prevent discomfort, and maintain social acceptability. A robot unable to differentiate a high-danger crowded area from an empty space may invade personal space, fail to yield, or execute unsafe maneuvers.
2. **Exploring Alternatives to Traditional Sensor-Based Systems**: Most existing robot safety assessment systems rely on explicit geometric reasoning derived from specialized depth sensors. The research is motivated by the open question of whether "universal visual understanding" capabilities, inherent in advanced VLMs, could complement or potentially replace such dedicated hardware and algorithms, offering a more flexible and potentially cost-effective solution.
3. **Leveraging Egocentric Vision**: Egocentric images captured by a robot's onboard camera provide a direct and cost-effective data stream reflecting the robot's immediate field of view. Converting this raw visual input into a structured risk assessment is crucial for embodied agents, as it reflects their spatial understanding of the scene relevant to their actions.
4. **Assessing VLM Readiness for Embodied AI**: VLMs have demonstrated strong performance in various visual perception tasks. The flexibility offered by prompt engineering and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., QLoRA) suggests they could be adapted for specific robotics tasks. This study aims to determine if this flexibility extends to reliable, fine-grained proxemic reasoning, an area that demands both visual and contextual understanding.
5. **Addressing Gaps in Spatial Reasoning for VLMs**: Prior research has identified limitations in the spatial intelligence of VLMs. This work specifically investigates these limitations within the context of egocentric robot perception, where spatial grounding of risk factors (e.g., the proximity of people) is critical for practical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The research employed a systematic approach to evaluate Vision-Language Models for proxemic risk assessment using a newly developed dataset, comparing various models, prompting strategies, and fine-tuning techniques.

#### 3.1. Dataset Creation and Annotation

A dataset comprising 1,243 egocentric images was derived from the JRDB dataset, collected from a mobile robot operating in realistic environments. This dataset was balanced to represent real-world conditions by including images from both indoor (n=713, with good lighting n=505 and poor lighting n=208) and outdoor (n=530) settings.

Each image was manually annotated according to four proxemic danger levels, inspired by Edward T. Hall's proxemic zones:
- **High Danger** (n=222, 17.9%): A person is within close personal space, indicating an unavoidable collision or discomfort without immediate intervention.
- **Moderate Danger** (n=407, 32.7%): A person is within personal or close social space, requiring trajectory adjustment.
- **Low Danger** (n=299, 24.1%): A person is present but at a safe social distance, requiring monitoring.
- **Minimum Danger** (n=315, 25.3%): No proxemic risk detected.
The manual annotation focused on semantic interpretation rather than strict distance thresholds. The class distribution reflects the infrequent occurrence of high-danger situations, while emphasizing that false negatives in this class carry significant consequences, thus motivating a focus on recall for this specific class alongside overall weighted F1 score. A subset of 200 images, evenly distributed across danger levels and sourced from different video segments than the test set, was designated for fine-tuning to prevent data leakage. The modified dataset is planned for open-source publication.

#### 3.2. Models Evaluated

Three open-weight VLMs, selected for their suitability for local deployment, were evaluated:
- **Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct**: A 3-billion-parameter model from Alibaba, featuring a dynamic-resolution visual encoder and Qwen2.5 language foundation, designed for structured output and spatial understanding.
- **InternVL3.5-4B**: A 4-billion-parameter model by OpenGVLab, utilizing a high-resolution visual encoder trained with contrastive and generative objectives, known for spatial reasoning and visual question-answering.
- **SmolVLM2-2.2B-Instruct**: A 2.2-billion-parameter model from HuggingFace, chosen as the smallest model for efficient inference and to establish a performance lower bound.

#### 3.3. Prompt Strategies

Three distinct prompt strategies, varying in complexity, were developed. All prompts incorporated a consistent set of proxemic-zone definitions and a uniform JSON output format.
- **Simple Prompt**: A concise task description designed to assess the models' inherent capabilities.
- **Moderate Prompt**: Expanded on the simple prompt by adding explicit instructions and output constraints.
- **Advanced Prompt**: Incorporated an internal chain-of-reasoning, guiding the model through visual cues, similar to instruction-based reasoning chains.
The JSON output format for all prompts required the model to predict a distance zone, a distance estimate, a bounding box around the relevant person, and an overall danger level. For classification, only the danger level was scored, while the bounding box was assessed separately for spatial grounding.

#### 3.4. Fine-Tuning Procedure

Parameter-efficient QLoRA fine-tuning was performed in two stages, generating three checkpoints: 'before' (no fine-tuning), 'after' (Stage 1), and 'after_2' (Stage 2). A balanced training set of 200 images (50 per danger level) was used.
- **Stage 1 ('after')**: Adapted only the attention projections (q_proj, v_proj) of the models.
- **Stage 2 ('after_2')**: Extended the adaptation to all main projections (q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj, gate_proj, up_proj, down_proj, lm_head).
Consistent QLoRA parameters were used: r=8, α=16, dropout=0.05, and no offset adaptation. This two-stage approach allowed for assessing the impact of increased adaptation scope and training duration on performance.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

Performance was assessed using a suite of metrics across all model, fine-tuning, and prompt configurations:
- **Accuracy**: The proportion of correctly classified images.
- **Weighted F1 Score**: The F1 score, averaged across all classes and weighted by their frequency of occurrence, to provide a comprehensive measure of classification quality, especially given class imbalance.
- **Recall of High Danger Class**: A critical safety metric, defined as the proportion of actual high-danger scenes correctly identified by the model. A false negative in this class carries a higher cost than other errors.
- **Intersection over Union (IoU)**: To quantify spatial grounding, measuring the overlap between predicted and ground truth bounding boxes for persons. Reported as mean IoU, the ratio of predictions with IoU > 0.5, and the ratio of predictions with IoU = 0 (no overlap). This metric aimed to determine if correct danger classification correlated with accurate localization of the source of danger.

### 4. Main findings and results

The evaluation explored the performance of three Vision-Language Models (VLMs) under various prompting strategies and fine-tuning conditions, focusing on proxemic risk assessment and spatial grounding.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

The overall performance, measured by accuracy and weighted F1 score, indicated that all evaluated models performed near a stratified random baseline (weighted F1 of 0.25). Without fine-tuning, models achieved weighted F1 scores between 0.21 and 0.23. Fine-tuning with QLoRA provided only modest overall improvements, with changes in weighted F1 typically less than 0.02.

However, a notable difference emerged in the recall rate for the high-danger class. Qwen-VL consistently achieved substantially higher recall for high-danger cases (approximately 0.39 to 0.44 across stages) compared to InternVL and SmolVLM, which exhibited near-zero recall for this critical class (0.016 to 0.069). This indicated Qwen-VL's relative capability in identifying the most critical scenarios.

#### 4.2. Impact of Prompt Strategies

Prompt structure influenced performance. The simple prompt strategy yielded the highest overall weighted F1 score (0.303) and mean IoU (0.232). In contrast, the advanced prompt, which incorporated a chain-of-reasoning, resulted in the highest recall for the high-danger class (0.316). This observed trade-off, where a more complex prompt improved detection of outliers (high danger) at the expense of overall classification consistency, was interpreted as a potential "format tax." The moderate prompt did not demonstrate a clear advantage for either overall F1 or high-danger recall, but was associated with the lowest total misalignment score (0.416 IoU = 0, compared to 0.434 for simple and 0.451 for advanced).

#### 4.3. Spatial Grounding

Analysis of spatial grounding through Intersection over Union (IoU) revealed that only Qwen-VL consistently provided useful localization information. Before fine-tuning, Qwen-VL achieved an average IoU of 0.459, with IoU > 0.5 for 54.5% of predictions. InternVL and SmolVLM generally failed to provide meaningful localization, exhibiting very low mean IoU values (e.g., SmolVLM mean IoU 0.061) and a high proportion of predictions with IoU = 0 (over 60% for SmolVLM). Consistent with classification performance, fine-tuning did not improve spatial grounding for any model; in fact, it slightly deteriorated for Qwen-VL.

#### 4.4. Per-Class Analysis

A detailed per-class analysis for Qwen-VL (the best-performing model) under its best overall and best high-recall configurations provided further insights:
- **Best Overall Configuration (Qwen / after_2 / simple prompt)**: This configuration achieved a weighted F1 of 0.290. While balanced across classes, individual class F1 scores remained relatively low (e.g., high danger F1 = 0.278, minimum danger F1 = 0.440). The mean IoU for correctly classified instances in the high danger class was 0.505.
- **Best High-Recall Configuration (Qwen / after / advanced prompt)**: This configuration achieved a high recall of 0.790 for the high-danger class. However, this came at the cost of very low precision (0.182) for high danger and poor F1 scores for other classes (e.g., low danger F1 = 0.038). The mean IoU for correctly classified high-danger instances was 0.442.

A key finding was the observed disconnect between classification success and spatial grounding. The IoU values showed no substantial difference between correctly and incorrectly classified frames (overall mean IoU for correct classifications was 0.204 compared to 0.215 for incorrect ones). Even Qwen-VL's best performance on true positives in the high-danger class (mean IoU = 0.488) barely exceeded its performance on false negatives in the same class (mean IoU = 0.464). This indicated that a model might assign a correct danger label without reliably identifying or localizing the specific person or region causing that danger.

#### 4.5. Limited Gains from Fine-Tuning

The limited improvements from QLoRA fine-tuning were attributed to several factors: the relatively small size of the training dataset (200 images), the possibility that spatial reasoning is largely acquired during VLM pre-training rather than through adaptation of QLoRA layers, and the phenomenon of "catastrophic forgetting" observed in smaller models like SmolVLM, where additional fine-tuning could degrade performance.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research contributes to the understanding of current Vision-Language Models' capabilities and limitations in a safety-critical embodied AI application: proxemic risk assessment from a robot's egocentric perspective.

**Limitations of Current VLMs for Fine-Grained Proxemic Reasoning:** The study demonstrates that, despite advancements in multimodal understanding, the evaluated open-source VLMs generally exhibit limitations in performing fine-grained proxemic reasoning. Their overall classification performance, close to a random baseline, suggests they are not yet robust enough to serve as standalone, comprehensive proxemic risk assessment systems in practical robotic deployments. This finding indicates that "universal visual understanding" does not yet fully translate into the precise spatial and contextual reasoning required for safe human-robot interaction in complex environments.

**Partial Success in High-Danger Detection:** While overall performance was limited, Qwen-VL demonstrated a notable ability to detect high-danger situations, particularly with an advanced prompt. This suggests a potential role for VLMs as an early warning or "danger trigger" mechanism within a robot's control system. However, the associated low precision and poor performance on other danger levels imply that such a VLM output would need to be integrated carefully, potentially alongside other, more precise safety modules, to mitigate false positives and ensure comprehensive safety.

**Discrepancy Between Classification and Spatial Grounding:** A critical finding is the observed disconnect between a model's ability to classify danger and its spatial grounding. The models could label a scene as high danger without reliably localizing the source of that danger (e.g., the specific person). This poses a significant challenge for embodied robots, as control systems often require precise spatial information to react appropriately (e.g., which person to avoid, where to adjust trajectory). A robot cannot meaningfully respond to a "high danger" label if it does not know *where* the danger originates. This highlights a fundamental gap in the spatial intelligence of current VLMs for tasks demanding actionable geometric understanding.

**Implications for Embodied AI and Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The research underscores the need for VLMs to evolve beyond semantic understanding to incorporate robust spatial and geometric reasoning for safe and effective HRI. Future VLM development for embodied agents should explicitly address the joint improvement of classification accuracy and the precise spatial grounding of relevant objects or entities within the scene. The findings serve as a benchmark, illustrating that current models require further development to meet the stringent requirements of safety-critical applications in human-robot collaboration.

**Guidance for Future Research and Development:** The study provides clear directions for subsequent work. It recommends exploring larger and more diverse training datasets for fine-tuning to overcome potential data limitations. Comparative analyses with larger, API-scale models could help differentiate whether observed performance limitations are inherent architectural constraints or simply a matter of model scale. Crucially, future research should focus on methodologies that explicitly aim to jointly improve both classification and spatial grounding, potentially through multi-task learning frameworks or models designed with stronger inherent spatial reasoning capabilities. The provision of the open-source modified dataset is intended to facilitate this future comparative testing and development within the research community.
