# Multi-critic Learning for Whole-body End-effector Twist Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich and ANYbotics AG developed an RL framework for whole-body end-effector twist tracking on quadrupedal robots, achieving smooth, velocity-aware manipulation during locomotion. This framework introduces a multi-critic architecture and a twist-based command formulation, demonstrating improved trajectory tracking and robustness to reward tuning.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body control methods for legged mobile manipulators struggle with accurately tracking continuous manipulation trajectories due to pose-based task formulations leading to jerky motions.
- Traditional RL approaches face difficulty in resolving conflicting objectives between stable locomotion (e.g., horizontal base) and effective manipulation (e.g., base tilting for reach).
- Designing and tuning a unified reward function for multi-task loco-manipulation RL policies is notoriously complex and sensitive, hindering robust learning.

## Method

- Introduces a multi-critic actor architecture where distinct critics handle decoupled rewards for locomotion, manipulation, and contact schedule, summing normalized advantages for policy optimization.
- Employs a twist-based end-effector command that explicitly specifies desired linear and angular velocities, enabling smooth tracking of continuous trajectories.
- Utilizes a robot-centric task frame for end-effector trajectories, allowing the policy to learn coordinated whole-body movements where the base assists the arm.

## Results

- Demonstrated smooth and precise tracking of various end-effector trajectories (straight lines, circles, semicircles) on a real ANYmal D robot, with consistent low position errors.
- The multi-critic policy exhibited generalization to unseen gaits, successfully performing a trotting gait after training only on static walking by modifying foot swing trajectories.
- Outperformed existing RL methods and a whole-body MPC controller in simulation, achieving significantly lower position and velocity tracking errors for loco-manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The multi-critic architecture effectively resolves conflicting locomotion and manipulation goals, leading to emergent whole-body coordination where the robot's base assists the arm.
> - Decoupling rewards into dedicated critics significantly simplifies the reward tuning process, making multi-task RL training more robust and less sensitive to individual reward weights.
> - A twist-based end-effector command formulation directly enables smooth, velocity-aware control for continuous trajectories, overcoming the jerky motions of pose-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in robotics: **whole-body control for loco-manipulation**. This involves enabling a robot, specifically a quadruped equipped with a manipulator arm, to simultaneously move (locomote) and perform manipulation tasks. Historically, whole-body control approaches have been broadly categorized into model-based control and reinforcement learning (RL) based methods.

**Model-based control** (e.g., Model Predictive Control – MPC) offers high accuracy and predictability but struggles with robustness in the face of unmodeled disturbances, complex terrain, and accurate interaction modeling. Its reliance on precise system models can be a significant limitation in dynamic, unstructured environments.

In contrast, **Reinforcement Learning** has emerged as a powerful paradigm, particularly for developing robust and versatile locomotion controllers capable of handling challenging terrains and unmodeled dynamics. Recent successes in training agile quadrupedal robots using RL have demonstrated its potential for complex motor skills. However, extending RL to **whole-body loco-manipulation** introduces specific, often conflicting, challenges:

1. **Conflicting Task Goals:** Locomotion policies typically favor a horizontal base orientation for energy efficiency and stability. Manipulation tasks, however, often require the robot's base to tilt or reposition to extend the arm's reach or improve its dexterity. Balancing these conflicting objectives within a single policy is difficult, often leading to compromises in one or both tasks. Previous RL approaches attempting unified policies have frequently suffered from accuracy issues, with tracking errors in the range of tens of centimeters.
2. **Task Specification for Manipulation:** Most existing RL methods for end-effector control rely on "pose-based tracking" (i.e., tracking a desired position and orientation). While effective for reaching discrete goal poses, this formulation often leads to jerky, unsmooth movements when attempting to follow continuous trajectories, as the controller rigidly attempts to reach each intermediate waypoint without explicit velocity control.
3. **Reward Tuning Complexity:** Training a single RL policy for multiple, potentially conflicting tasks requires careful design and tuning of a unified reward function. Weighting different reward components (e.g., locomotion speed, arm accuracy, balance) can be notoriously difficult and sensitive, making the learning process brittle.

This paper positions itself as a significant advancement by directly tackling these core limitations. It seeks to develop an RL-based framework that offers dynamic, velocity-aware whole-body end-effector control, overcoming the deficiencies of prior art through novel architectural and task formulation designs. By demonstrating superior performance on complex trajectories and robust generalization, the work contributes to making mobile manipulators more capable and practical for real-world applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations driving this research are clearly articulated by the limitations observed in existing whole-body control methods for quadrupedal robots:

1. **To enable dynamic and velocity-aware whole-body end-effector control:** A core motivation stems from the inability of current pose-based RL approaches to smoothly execute continuous manipulation trajectories. The authors aim to design a task formulation that directly allows for the control of end-effector velocity, ensuring smooth and precise movements, which is crucial for many real-world manipulation tasks.
2. **To effectively resolve conflicting goals between locomotion and manipulation:** The inherent trade-off between efficient, stable locomotion (often favoring a horizontal base) and extended arm reach/dexterity (which might require base tilting) presents a major challenge. The authors aim to develop an architecture that allows a single policy to handle these diverging objectives more effectively and learn coordinated whole-body behaviors without explicit programming.
3. **To simplify the reward tuning process in multi-task Reinforcement Learning:** The difficulty of designing and tuning a single, unified reward function for complex loco-manipulation tasks often leads to extended training times, sub-optimal policies, or sensitivity to hyperparameters. A key objective is to reduce this complexity and make the RL training process more robust and manageable.
4. **To validate the proposed framework on both simulation and real hardware:** To ensure practical applicability and robustness, the authors aim to thoroughly test their approach on a physical quadruped robot (ANYmal D with a Dynaarm manipulator) in addition to extensive simulations.

In essence, the research aims to push the boundaries of whole-body control for legged mobile manipulators, making them more versatile, precise, and easier to train for complex tasks requiring simultaneous movement and manipulation in dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates several innovative components within a Reinforcement Learning paradigm, specifically utilizing Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in a teacher-student architecture.

#### 3.1. Task Definition and Control Loop

The task is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). A teacher policy, $\pi_T_\theta$, receives a comprehensive observation vector $s_t$ (187 dimensions, including joint history, proprioception, privileged information from the simulator, previous actions, and command signals) and generates an action $a_t$. Unlike standard approaches that apply actions to default joint positions, this method applies joint position residuals directly to the current joint positions, providing relative offsets for precise control.

The command vector $c_t$ is crucial, defining desired base linear and angular velocities, end-effector linear and angular velocities (twists), a final end-effector goal pose for the trajectory, and desired swing heights for each foot.

#### 3.2. Command Formulation

A novel aspect of the methodology lies in its command formulation for the end-effector:

- **End-effector Trajectory Sampling:** During training, manipulation trajectories are generated by sampling start and goal poses. Goal positions are sampled within a sphere centered at the robot's shoulder, rejecting samples within the torso region for physical feasibility. Orientations are constructed relative to gravity and the torso-to-end-effector vector, with random perturbations. Intermediate waypoints are generated via linear interpolation for position and spherical linear interpolation (SLERP) for orientation.
- **Twist-based End-effector Command:** This is a major contribution. Instead of just tracking a goal pose, the command explicitly includes end-effector linear velocity ($v_{EE}$) and angular velocity ($\omega_{EE}$) derived from the difference between the current end-effector pose and the intermediate trajectory waypoint over the control time step ($\Delta t$). This direct velocity specification addresses the "jerky motion" problem of pose-based controllers by enabling smooth transitions along trajectories. The end-effector command provided to the policy is a stacked vector of these twists and the final goal pose.
- **Local Trajectories:** To accelerate learning by providing denser positive tracking examples, a subset of trajectories is defined locally, re-initializing the start pose at every control step.
- **Robot-centric Task Frame:** End-effector trajectories are represented in a robot-centric task frame, agnostic to torso height, pitch, and roll. This decouples the manipulation task from specific torso motions, allowing the policy to learn coordinated whole-body movements where the base assists the arm.
- **Foot Trajectory Generation:** Foot swing heights for locomotion are generated sinusoidally based on a gait phase and fixed foot phase offsets, enabling various gait patterns.
- **Base Command:** Base linear and angular velocities are periodically resampled during training, allowing the policy to learn to follow diverse locomotion commands.

#### 3.3. Multi-critic Actor Learning

To address the challenges of conflicting task goals and reward tuning, the paper introduces a **multi-critic actor architecture**:

- **Decoupled Rewards:** Rewards are categorized into three distinct groups:
 1. **Locomotion:** Base velocity tracking and regularization terms.
 2. **Manipulation:** End-effector pose (position and orientation) tracking and regularization terms.
 3. **Contact Schedule:** Rewards for tracking desired foot contact states, recognized as a sparser reward signal.
- **Dedicated Critics:** Each reward group has its own dedicated critic network ($\phi_i$) that estimates a corresponding value function $V_{\phi_i}(.)$.
- **Total Advantage Calculation:** The overall advantage ($\hat{A}$) for policy optimization is calculated as the sum of the *normalized* advantage estimates ($\hat{A}_i$) from each critic. This normalized summation is crucial as it avoids the need for explicit weighting of advantage functions, simplifying reward tuning.
- **PPO Framework:** Policy optimization is performed using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), minimizing the policy surrogate loss.

This multi-critic setup allows the policy to resolve task conflicts more effectively by considering separate value functions for each objective, promoting the emergence of coordinated behaviors. It also seamlessly combines dense tracking rewards with sparse contact schedule rewards without complex weighting schemes.

#### 3.4. Training Details and Sim-to-Real Transfer

The policy is trained using 4096 parallel agents in IsaacLab, a GPU-accelerated simulation framework. A curriculum learning approach is used, starting on flat terrain and progressing to rougher terrains. Extensive **domain randomization** (e.g., foot friction, torso/end-effector mass, external forces/torques, torso push velocities) is applied to physical parameters to enhance robustness and facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

A **teacher-student distillation** approach is employed for hardware deployment. A teacher policy with access to privileged information is first trained. Then, a student policy with an identical network structure, but without privileged information, is trained using supervised learning to implicitly estimate the privileged information by mimicking the teacher's actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings validated through extensive simulation and hardware experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness and advantages of the proposed multi-critic, twist-based approach.

#### 4.1. Hardware Validation of End-Effector Tracking Performance

- **Smooth and Precise Trajectory Tracking:** The controller successfully tracks various trajectory types (straight lines, circles, semicircles) on the ANYmal D robot. Figure 3 visually confirms smooth and accurate tracking of both desired position and velocity, a key achievement of the twist-based formulation.
- **Varying Velocity Tracking:** The system demonstrates the ability to execute trajectories at different commanded velocities (e.g., 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 m/s). Table 1 shows that while accuracy might be slightly lower at slower speeds for linear trajectories, the policy consistently maintains low position errors and good velocity tracking across various speeds and trajectory types.
- **Emergent Whole-Body Behaviors:** During these experiments, the robot's base is observed to assist the arm by tilting and repositioning to extend reachability and maintain accuracy, even though this coordinated behavior was not explicitly programmed into the reward function. This highlights the effectiveness of the multi-critic architecture in resolving task conflicts and enabling emergent synergy.

#### 4.2. Multi-Critic Architecture for Gait Generalization

- **Adaptation to Unseen Gaits:** A significant finding regarding the multi-critic architecture is its ability to generalize to unseen gait patterns. Although trained with a static walking gait, the policy successfully performs a trotting gait at runtime simply by modifying the commanded foot swing trajectories (Figure 4). This suggests that separating the contact schedule into its own critic allows the policy to learn a more general representation of gait, enabling adaptation without retraining. This generalization capability is noted as not previously reported in the literature.

#### 4.3. Comparative Evaluation with Existing Methods (Simulation)

- **Superior Loco-Manipulation Performance:** The proposed multi-critic approach consistently outperforms various existing locomotion policies (Ma, Taka 3-DoF, Taka 6-DoF) when integrated with the arm control. Table 2 and Table 3 show that the proposed whole-body policy achieves the lowest position and orientation tracking errors in both standing and walking scenarios for linear, circular, and workspace sweep trajectories.
- **Advantage over Pose-Based Controllers:** Compared to the pose-based whole-body policy by Portela et al., the proposed twist-based approach achieves significantly better task space position tracking while maintaining comparable orientation accuracy, even though Portela's policy focuses only on stationary manipulation. The "workspace sweep" trajectory comparison (Figure 5 and Table 3) further emphasizes this, showing improved end-effector tracking across an extended workspace.
- **Outperformance of Whole-Body MPC:** The multi-critic approach also demonstrates superior performance over a whole-body MPC controller in simulation across all trajectory types and both standing and walking conditions, particularly in velocity tracking errors (Table 3).

#### 4.4. Reward Sensitivity Analysis

- **Robustness to Reward Tuning:** An important ablation study compares the multi-critic and single-critic frameworks' robustness to reward scaling. By multiplying locomotion or manipulation rewards by factors of 5 or 10, the study (Figure 6) shows that the multi-critic approach remains largely insensitive to these weighting factors, maintaining good performance across both tasks.
- **Single-Critic Limitations:** In contrast, the single-critic approach exhibits significant sensitivity: scaling one reward category (e.g., locomotion) improves performance for that task but severely degrades the other (manipulation), or vice-versa. This highlights the practical benefit of multi-critic learning in simplifying reward design and making training more stable for multi-objective problems.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Appendix)

- **Effect of Curriculum Learning on Command Frame:** The appendix details that using a curriculum approach for the end-effector twist command frame (starting in the base frame and switching to the control frame after initial learning) leads to faster and better end-effector position tracking compared to fixed frames or mixtures of frames (Figure 7).
- **Single-Critic vs. Multi-Critic Trade-off:** Further analysis confirms that a single-critic policy, while potentially achieving marginally lower end-effector errors, completely fails to learn locomotion behaviors, effectively adopting a stationary posture to prioritize arm control (Figure 8). The multi-critic, however, successfully learns to balance and satisfy both locomotion and manipulation objectives.
- **Chicken-Head Control:** The policy demonstrates excellent whole-body coordination by achieving very low end-effector position tracking errors (mean 0.0156m) while the robot is actively walking (Figure 9), effectively performing "chicken-head" stabilization of the end-effector in the world frame despite base motion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Multi-critic Learning for Whole-body End-effector Twist Tracking" paper presents a significant step forward in the field of mobile manipulation, particularly for legged robots. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Loco-Manipulation Capabilities:** By enabling smooth, velocity-aware end-effector control during both stationary operation and dynamic locomotion, the framework allows quadrupedal robots to perform more complex and delicate manipulation tasks while moving. This vastly expands the operational envelope of mobile manipulators, making them suitable for applications requiring precise interaction in unstructured or dynamic environments, such as construction, inspection, service robotics, or disaster response.
2. **Simplified Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Task Problems:** The introduction of the multi-critic actor architecture is a major methodological contribution. By decoupling reward signals for different task categories (locomotion, manipulation, contact schedule) and calculating total advantage from normalized individual advantages, the approach significantly simplifies the notoriously difficult process of reward tuning. This robustness to reward weighting drastically reduces the engineering effort required to develop multi-task RL policies, making advanced robot behaviors more accessible to a wider research and development community.
3. **Emergent Coordinated Whole-Body Behaviors:** The observation that the robot's base spontaneously assists the arm in extending its workspace, despite a lack of explicit formulation in the reward function, is a powerful demonstration of the multi-critic architecture's ability to foster emergent, synergistic behaviors. This implies a more intelligent and adaptable robot that can leverage its full kinematic capabilities without explicit hand-crafted rules.
4. **Improved Trajectory Tracking and Smoothness:** The twist-based end-effector task formulation directly addresses a fundamental limitation of previous pose-based methods. By enabling explicit control over end-effector velocity, it ensures smoother execution of continuous trajectories, which is vital for tasks requiring fluid motions, delicate handling, or interaction with dynamic objects.
5. **Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive validation on real hardware (ANYmal D) and the impressive generalization capabilities (e.g., adapting to unseen trot gaits from static walk training) underscore the robustness of the learned policies. This indicates that the methods are well-suited for real-world deployment, reducing the sim-to-real gap.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge two main limitations:
1. **Restricted Locomotion Performance on Rough Terrains:** The current controller is limited to moderately rough terrains, as robust rough-terrain locomotion was not the primary focus. Future work plans to integrate perception mechanisms and reward formulations from existing rough-terrain frameworks. The authors suggest their multi-critic formulation should simplify the tuning of these new, complex reward terms.
2. **Joint Limit Issues during Whole-Body Maneuvers:** The robot occasionally reaches shoulder joint limits, triggering safety mechanisms. This can be addressed by incorporating joint limits (e.g., as penalties, terminations, or constrained PPO objectives) during policy training.

Despite these limitations, the paper's contributions lay a strong foundation for future research in general-purpose, agile, and robust mobile manipulation, paving the way for legged robots to operate autonomously and effectively in increasingly complex human environments. The demonstrated practical efficacy and architectural innovations promise to influence how multi-task robot control policies are designed and trained in the coming years.
