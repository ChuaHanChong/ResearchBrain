# Sample-Efficient Diffusion-based Reinforcement Learning with Critic Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ShanghaiTech University developed Critic-Guided Diffusion Policy Optimization (CGPO), a framework for diffusion-based reinforcement learning that integrates training-free critic guidance into the denoising process to generate high-value action targets. The method demonstrates superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance across MuJoCo locomotion tasks and successfully applies diffusion policies to real-world robotic manipulation through direct reinforcement learning, bypassing the need for human demonstrations.

## Problem

- Balancing exploration and exploitation in existing diffusion-based RL is challenging, with sampling-based methods facing a 'refinement bottleneck' and gradient-based methods often leading to unimodal policy collapse.
- Applying diffusion policies to real-world robotic control is predominantly limited to imitation learning, constrained by the cost and availability of expert demonstrations and unable to surpass expert performance.
- Current diffusion RL approaches struggle to consistently provide strong, stable policy update signals, which can hinder efficient convergence and lead to suboptimal solutions.

## Method

- Introduces Critic-Guided Action Generation (**CGPO**) that integrates training-free critic guidance, specifically ==Diffusion== Guidance with Spherical Gaussian Constraint (DSG), into the diffusion denoising process to synthesize a single, refined high-value target action.
- Employs robust Q-signal calibration using conservative value estimates via truncated quantiles from an ensemble of distributional critics and Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) targets to mitigate overestimation bias and stabilize guidance.
- Utilizes a lightweight Value-Calibrated Network (Vϕ) to anchor the scale of critic-derived weights to unguided actor samples, providing stable state reweighting for actor updates.

## Results

- CGPO achieved the highest episodic returns across all five MuJoCo v3 locomotion tasks (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Humanoid, Walker2d), demonstrating superior performance and learning stability compared to various RL baselines.
- Successfully enabled the first direct real-world reinforcement learning with diffusion policies on a Franka Panda robot, achieving an **80%** success rate on the cylindrical peg-in-hole task after 15,000 steps without human demonstrations and producing smoother trajectories than a SAC baseline.
- Ablation studies validated the importance of each component of CGPO, including critic guidance, DSG, Q-signal calibration, and the value-calibrated network, confirming their positive contributions to learning performance and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training-free critic guidance, particularly when applied in later denoising stages, effectively overcomes the 'refinement bottleneck' in diffusion policies by providing a consistent and informative update signal for high-value action synthesis.
> - The Diffusion Guidance with Spherical Gaussian Constraint (DSG) mechanism is crucial for stably integrating critic gradients by ensuring guidance perturbations align with the inherent manifold structure of the diffusion process.
> - Reliable and unbiased Q-signal calibration through truncated quantiles and DDQN, combined with a value-calibrated network for state reweighting, is essential for robust and sample-efficient policy optimization in diffusion RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has seen advancements through the integration of diffusion models, which offer capabilities in modeling complex and multimodal action distributions, thereby encouraging diverse exploration. The existing landscape of diffusion-based RL methods can be broadly categorized into two lines of work: sampling-based optimization and gradient-based optimization.

Sampling-based methods, such as those that reweight actions sampled from a diffusion policy based on estimated values, tend to exhibit strong exploration capabilities, particularly at the beginning of training. However, these methods can suffer from slow exploitation of Q-value information, which in turn leads to a slower policy convergence. As training progresses and the policy distribution concentrates, the diversity of sampled candidates can diminish, making critic signals less discriminative and weakening the policy update signal.

Conversely, gradient-based policy optimization methods leverage the gradients of a Q-function to guide the diffusion policy toward high-quality actions. While these methods effectively exploit Q-value information and can be sample-efficient, they often lead to a collapse into a unimodal policy, thereby reducing action diversity. This presents an inherent tension in the exploration-exploitation trade-off within current diffusion-based RL approaches.

In parallel, diffusion policies have also been applied to real-world robotic control, predominantly in imitation learning (IL). These IL-based approaches, by learning from extensive human demonstrations, have demonstrated performance in manipulation tasks. However, imitation learning is fundamentally constrained by the quality and availability of demonstrations, which are often expensive and labor-intensive to collect. Moreover, IL cannot inherently surpass the performance level of the expert demonstrations. The application of diffusion-based RL methods directly in real-world settings, enabling learning from interaction without reliance on demonstrations and potentially surpassing expert performance, remains an area with limited exploration.

This work, by proposing CGPO (Critic-Guided Diffusion Policy Optimization), aims to bridge these gaps. It positions itself by introducing a mechanism that balances exploration and exploitation more effectively than existing diffusion RL methods. Furthermore, it explicitly addresses the challenge of applying diffusion policies to real-world robotic control through direct RL interaction, moving beyond the limitations of imitation learning. By integrating training-free guidance into the denoising process, CGPO seeks to overcome the refinement bottleneck of sampling-based methods and promote diverse yet high-value action generation, aiming for sample-efficient and robust learning in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were derived from identified limitations within existing diffusion-based reinforcement learning methodologies and their application in real-world robotic systems.

### 3. Addressing the Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off in Diffusion RL

The core motivation stems from the inherent difficulties in balancing exploration and exploitation in current diffusion-based RL frameworks.
 - **Sampling-based methods:** While these approaches promote diversity and exploration, they rely on sampling multiple candidate actions and reweighting them based on critic values. As training progresses and the policy becomes more concentrated, the sampled actions become similar. This reduces the discriminative power of the critic, leading to a "refinement bottleneck" where the policy improvement signal becomes weak and convergence slows down. The objective is to find a more direct and efficient way to identify and generate high-value actions without extensive candidate sampling.
 - **Gradient-based methods:** These methods efficiently exploit Q-value gradients but often lead to policies that are overly concentrated and lack diversity, potentially hindering further exploration and leading to suboptimal solutions. The objective is to leverage critic information for exploitation while maintaining sufficient action diversity.

### 4. Enabling Real-World Diffusion-based Reinforcement Learning from Scratch

A significant motivation is to extend the capabilities of diffusion policies to real-world robotic control where learning occurs directly from interaction, rather than through imitation learning.
 - **Limitations of Imitation Learning:** Current diffusion policies in robotics primarily rely on imitation learning from human demonstrations. This approach is constrained by the high cost and labor intensity of collecting quality demonstrations, and it inherently limits the policy's performance to that of the human expert. The objective is to develop a diffusion-based RL method that can learn directly from real-world environmental interaction, eliminating the dependency on demonstrations and enabling the agent to potentially surpass human-level performance.
 - **Practical Deployment Challenges:** The integration of complex generative models like diffusion policies into real-world control loops requires efficiency. Any proposed method must generate actions within acceptable latency constraints during deployment, even if training involves more complex computations.

### 5. Developing a Principled Policy Optimization Framework

The overarching objective is to propose CGPO, a novel diffusion-based RL framework that systematically integrates critic guidance into the diffusion process to address the aforementioned limitations. This includes:
 - **Training-free guidance:** To steer action generation towards high-value regions without requiring an additional time-conditioned guidance model, thus reducing training overhead.
 - **Stable and reliable critic signals:** To ensure that the critic's feedback is accurate and robust throughout training, even in early stages when it might be noisy, and to prevent issues like overestimation bias from distorting policy updates.
 - **Maintained action diversity:** To ensure that while the policy exploits high-value regions, it does not collapse into a unimodal distribution, thereby preserving exploration capabilities.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to create a diffusion-based RL method that achieves a principled balance between exploration and exploitation, leading to sample-efficient learning and making diffusion policies viable for direct application in real-world robotic systems.

### 6. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Critic-Guided Diffusion Policy Optimization (CGPO), introduces a framework for diffusion-based reinforcement learning that integrates training-free critic guidance into the denoising process of a diffusion policy. This approach aims to balance exploration and exploitation by generating high-quality action targets for policy improvement.

#### 6.1. Critic-Guided Action Generation

The core of CGPO's approach to policy improvement is the generation of a single, refined target action per state, denoted as $a_g(s)$, through a critic-guided reverse diffusion process. This contrasts with sampling-based methods that rely on reweighting a finite set of candidate actions, which the authors identify as becoming less effective as the policy concentrates.

- **Motivation for Guidance:** The paper argues that as sampling-based policies become more concentrated, candidate actions become less diverse, and the critic's ability to discriminate between "good" and "bad" actions within a limited sample set diminishes (quantified by $\Delta_Q(s)$). This leads to a weaker policy improvement signal. To overcome this, CGPO draws inspiration from classifier guidance in generative models to directly synthesize higher-value actions.
- **Training-Free Guidance:** Instead of training an additional time-conditioned critic for guidance, CGPO employs training-free guidance. This involves backpropagating the gradient of a standard action-value critic, $\hat{Q}(s, \hat{x}_0(x_t, s, t))$, with respect to the noisy latent variable $x_t$ (Equation 10). The objective $L_{guide}$ is defined as the negative Q-value, steering generation towards higher Q-values.
- **DSG (Diffusion Guidance with Spherical Gaussian Constraint):** To integrate guidance while maintaining the manifold structure of the diffusion process and ensuring stability, CGPO adopts the DSG mechanism.
 - The guidance signal $g_t$ (gradient of $L_{guide}$) is mapped to a constrained descent direction $d^\star$ that lies on the spherical shell typical of Gaussian perturbations (Equation 11). This ensures that the guidance perturbation aligns with the inherent noise magnitude of the diffusion step.
 - This deterministic direction $d^\star$ is then mixed with standard Gaussian noise $\epsilon_t$ using a guidance rate $\rho$ (Equation 12) to preserve the stochastic nature of diffusion.
 - The mixed direction $d_m$ is finally projected back onto the typical-radius shell (Equation 13), and the reverse step updates $x_{t-1}$ by shifting the mean along this direction.
- **Stabilizing Guidance with Partial Application and Truncated Quantiles:**
 - CGPO applies DSG guidance only in the *last G denoising steps*. Earlier steps follow the unconditional reverse transition. This design choice is motivated by the observation that in early denoising steps, $\hat{x}_0$ is highly noisy, making critic signals less reliable. Concentrating guidance in later steps focuses refinement when action-level changes are more semantically meaningful.
 - To further improve the reliability of the Q-signal for guidance, CGPO uses a conservative value estimate based on *truncated quantiles*. For a distributional critic, the top $k$ quantiles are discarded, and the remaining quantiles are aggregated across an ensemble of critics (Equation 14). This mitigates overestimation bias and provides a more stable guidance signal.

#### 6.2. State Reweighting via Calibrated Value Signals

The generated guided actions $a_g(s)$ serve as supervision for the diffusion actor. CGPO employs a refined weighting scheme for the actor's training objectives, coupled with robust critic signal calibration.

- **Value-Calibrated Network ($V_\phi(s)$):** To prevent the scale of critic-derived weights from drifting during training, CGPO introduces a lightweight value-calibrated network $V_\phi(s)$. This network is trained to track the critic value of *unguided* policy samples (Equations 16-17). The weights $w(s)$ for actor updates are then computed as a rectified advantage: $w(s) = \max(\hat{Q}(s, a_g(s)) - V_\phi(s), 0)$ (Equation 15). This approach anchors the weight scale to the current policy's performance, providing stable state reweighting.
- **Weighted Denoising and Diversity Regularization:** The diffusion actor is trained using two weighted objectives:
 - **Weighted Denoising Regression ($L_{diff}$):** The primary objective is a noise-prediction loss (similar to standard DDPM training) where the target action is the critic-guided $a_g(s)$, and the loss is weighted by $w(s)$ (Equation 18).
 - **Diffusion Entropy Regularization ($L_{ent}$):** To preserve action diversity, an additional entropy regularization term is included. This loss also uses a matched weighting $w_{ent}(s) = \lambda_{ent}w(s)$ but targets actions sampled from a uniform distribution (Equation 19).
- **Q-Signal Calibration:** The reliability of the Q-signal is critical for both generating guided targets and assigning weights. CGPO employs:
 - **DDQN Targets:** Critic targets for learning $\hat{Q}$ follow a Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) update, decoupling action selection from target evaluation to mitigate overestimation (Van Hasselt et al., 2016).
 - **Truncated-Quantile Aggregation:** As mentioned earlier, for distributional critics, scalar $\hat{Q}$ values are formed by discarding the highest quantiles before target computation (Kuznetsov et al., 2020), further enhancing robustness against overestimation.

#### 6.3. Overall Training Procedure

The overall training procedure follows an off-policy actor-critic loop. States are sampled from a replay buffer. For each state, a refined target action $a_g(s)$ is synthesized using the critic-guided refinement process (Algorithm 2). The diffusion actor is then updated using the weighted denoising and entropy regularization losses. Finally, the critic is updated using a conservative Bellman regression objective incorporating DDQN targets and truncated-quantile aggregation. A key aspect is that the critic-guided sampler is used *only during training* to produce supervised targets. For environment interaction and deployment, the standard unguided sampler of the diffusion policy is used, preserving runtime efficiency.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted comprehensive evaluations of CGPO in both simulated MuJoCo locomotion tasks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, alongside an ablation study and parameter analysis.

#### 7.1. Comparative Evaluation (MuJoCo v3 Locomotion Tasks)

CGPO was benchmarked against a range of model-free RL baselines and recent diffusion-policy methods on five MuJoCo v3 continuous control tasks: Ant-v3, HalfCheetah-v3, Hopper-v3, Humanoid-v3, and Walker2d-v3. All methods were trained for $10^6$ environment steps under identical interaction and evaluation protocols across five random seeds.

- **Performance:** CGPO consistently achieved the highest episodic returns across all five tasks, demonstrating superior overall performance compared to traditional off-policy (TD3, SAC), on-policy (PPO, SPO), and other diffusion-based RL methods (DIPO, QSM, DACER, QVPO, SDAC).
- **Learning Stability:** The learning curves indicated that CGPO maintained stable optimization progress even in later training regimes where sampling-based diffusion policy improvements typically slow down or plateau. This observation supports the hypothesis that the critic-guided target generation mechanism provides a more consistent and informative update signal.
- **Deployment Efficiency:** The study highlighted that critic guidance is exclusively used during training for target generation. Environment interaction and deployment utilize the unguided diffusion sampler, ensuring that test-time inference costs and control-loop latencies remain comparable to vanilla diffusion policies.

#### 7.2. Ablation Study

A detailed ablation study was performed on Ant-v3 and Walker2d-v3 to assess the contribution of each component of CGPO.

- **Guidance Design:**
 - Removing critic guidance entirely resulted in substantial performance degradation.
 - Replacing DSG (Diffusion Guidance with Spherical Gaussian Constraint) with "naive guidance" improved performance over the unguided variant but still fell short of full CGPO. This indicates that while critic gradients are beneficial, the structured, manifold-preserving nature of DSG is crucial for stable and effective guidance.
- **Q-signal Calibration:**
 - Removing DDQN (Double Deep Q-Network) target construction or truncated-quantile aggregation individually led to degraded learning performance. This finding emphasizes the importance of a reliable and unbiased Q-signal for both guiding action generation and weighting actor updates.
- **Base Value Network ($V_\phi$):**
 - Ablating the base value network $V_\phi$ resulted in lower and less stable performance. This confirms its role in anchoring the scale of critic-derived weights to unguided actor samples, which is essential for stable state reweighting.

#### 7.3. Real-World Experiments

CGPO was evaluated on a Franka Emika Panda robotic arm for two challenging manipulation tasks: Cube Stacking and Cylindrical Peg-in-Hole, utilizing a human-in-the-loop (HIL-SERL) framework.

- **Success Rate:** In the cylindrical peg-in-hole task, CGPO achieved an 80% success rate (16/20 trials) after 15,000 training steps, outperforming a SAC baseline which achieved 65%.
- **Qualitative Observations:** CGPO policies generated smoother exploration trajectories during the insertion phase, whereas the SAC baseline exhibited more pronounced jittering.
- **Direct RL without Demonstrations:** This demonstration marked the first successful incorporation of diffusion policies into real-world RL directly, without relying on large-scale human demonstrations for initial policy learning.

#### 7.4. Parameter Analysis

An analysis of key hyperparameters on Ant-v3 provided insights into the method's behavior.

- **Guidance Rate ($\rho$):** Higher guidance rates (e.g., $\rho=0.95$) generally led to better asymptotic performance, suggesting that stronger emphasis on the critic-induced refinement direction is beneficial, provided guidance is applied in the late denoising regime.
- **Guidance Step (G):** A moderate number of guided denoising steps (G=10) performed optimally. Too few steps (G=5) yielded weaker gains, while applying guidance too early (G=15, across more initial noisy steps) slightly degraded performance, reinforcing the strategy of concentrating refinement in later denoising stages.

#### 7.5. Quantitative Analysis of Critic Contrast ($\Delta_Q(s)$)

The study quantitatively supported the motivation for CGPO by analyzing the critic contrast $\Delta_Q(s)$ (the value gap between the best sampled candidate and the average of the candidate set).

- **Sampling-Based Methods:** These methods showed a large $\Delta_Q(s)$ early in training, indicating initial effectiveness, but the gap decreased and became more fluctuating as training progressed, consistent with the "refinement bottleneck."
- **CGPO:** CGPO maintained a more stable $\Delta_Q(s)$ in the later stages of training, suggesting that critic-guided target synthesis provides a more persistent and effective improvement signal even when the policy becomes concentrated.

Overall, the findings indicate that CGPO effectively balances exploration and exploitation, leading to robust and sample-efficient learning in complex environments, and successfully demonstrates the feasibility of direct reinforcement learning with diffusion policies in real-world robotic systems.

### 8. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents CGPO, a diffusion-based reinforcement learning framework that makes several contributions to the field and carries significant potential impact.

### 9. Advancement in Diffusion-based Reinforcement Learning

CGPO addresses a central challenge in existing diffusion-based RL methods: the trade-off between exploration and exploitation. By integrating training-free critic guidance, it provides a mechanism to efficiently generate high-quality action targets. This approach overcomes the limitations of sampling-based optimization, which can suffer from diminishing returns as policies converge, and mitigates the risk of unimodal policy collapse seen in some gradient-based methods. The ability to maintain an informative policy update signal throughout training leads to improved sample efficiency and higher asymptotic performance in complex tasks.

### 10. Enabling Real-World RL with Diffusion Policies

A critical impact of this work is its demonstration of successfully applying diffusion policies to real-world robotic systems through direct reinforcement learning, without requiring extensive human demonstrations. This capability fundamentally bridges a gap between advanced generative models and practical, interactive robotic learning.
 - **Reduced Reliance on Demonstrations:** By enabling learning from scratch via interaction, CGPO reduces the significant cost and labor associated with collecting large-scale, high-quality human demonstrations, which are a bottleneck for imitation learning approaches.
 - **Potential for Super-human Performance:** Unlike imitation learning, which is inherently bounded by expert performance, direct RL allows the agent to explore and optimize beyond the demonstrated behaviors, potentially leading to novel and superior control strategies.
 - **Practical Deployment:** The design choice to use guided sampling only during training, while deploying the unguided diffusion sampler, ensures that CGPO maintains runtime efficiency comparable to vanilla diffusion policies, making it suitable for real-world control-loop latency constraints.

### 11. Integration of Generative Models and Critic-Driven Optimization

CGPO demonstrates a principled way to combine the expressive power of diffusion models, known for their ability to model multimodal distributions and capture complex action spaces, with the refinement capabilities of critic-driven optimization. This integration could inspire future research in embodied intelligence that leverages the strengths of both generative modeling for diverse exploration and value-based methods for efficient exploitation.

### 12. Foundation for Future Research

The framework proposed by CGPO offers a general and extensible foundation. Future work could build upon this by exploring different guidance mechanisms, investigating how to further enhance sample efficiency for even faster real-world deployment, or extending it to more complex multi-modal or long-horizon tasks. The insights into stabilizing critic signals and integrating guidance within the diffusion process are broadly applicable to other domains where generative models are used for action generation.

In summary, CGPO represents a significant step towards practical and scalable diffusion-based reinforcement learning. Its ability to achieve robust performance in both simulation and real-world robotics by effectively balancing exploration and exploitation, without relying on extensive demonstrations, could accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous intelligent agents in diverse applications.
