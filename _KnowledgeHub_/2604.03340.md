# Learning Additively Compositional Latent Actions for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Wuhan University, Tsinghua University, and Microsoft Research developed AC-LAM, a model that imposes an additive composition prior on continuous latent actions, yielding more structured and displacement-calibrated representations. This approach enhances downstream robotic policy learning, achieving improved success rates on both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing latent action learning methods often entangle irrelevant scene details or future observations with actual state changes.
- Current latent actions frequently miscalibrate the magnitude of physical motion, hindering precise control and interpretability.
- The absence of explicit structural priors, such as compositionality, limits the physical grounding and transferability of learned latent action representations.

## Method

- Introduces the Additively Compositional Latent Action Model (**AC-LAM**) that enforces a scene-wise ==additive composition prior== (z_ik ≈ z_ij + z_jk) within the continuous latent action space.
- Implements the additive composition using an ==L_AC-FDM loss==, which leverages the ==forward dynamics model== to reconstruct observations from summed latent actions, ensuring training stability.
- The framework is theoretically shown to encourage algebraic properties like identity (zero action for no change) and inverse (negative action for reverse change), while suppressing non-compositional information.

## Results

- AC-LAM consistently demonstrated lower normalized residuals for additive composition (e.g., **0.086** on Fractal) compared to baselines (up to **0.960**), indicating stronger adherence to the additive prior.
- Latent actions learned by AC-LAM showed higher correlation with physical displacement magnitude (e.g., Pearson r of **0.489** on Fractal vs. **0.289** for Villa-X), indicating better calibration.
- Downstream policies trained with AC-LAM supervision achieved higher success rates on both simulated (e.g., **55%** success on Emoji Table-Top ID split vs. **42%** for Villa-X LAM) and real-world robot tasks (e.g., **60%** success on ID tasks vs. **40%** for Villa-X LAM).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Imposing an additive composition prior effectively regularizes continuous latent action spaces to reflect fundamental physical properties of motion.
> - This prior leads to the emergence of desirable algebraic properties, such as identity, inverse, and cycle consistency, and significantly improves motion magnitude calibration in latent actions.
> - Enforcing additive composition helps disentangle pure motion semantics by suppressing static scene information and future observation leakage, making latents more focused on actionable state changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on latent action learning. Latent action learning is an emerging paradigm that aims to derive pseudo-action labels from visual transitions observed in large-scale video datasets, such as internet videos, to pretrain models for robot control. This approach offers a scalable alternative to relying solely on costly, explicitly labeled robotic action data.

Existing methods in latent action learning, as highlighted by the authors, typically infer these pseudo-actions without incorporating explicit structural priors that reflect the physical nature of motion. Consequently, these learned latent representations often exhibit several limitations. They tend to entangle irrelevant scene details or information related to future observations with the actual state changes. Additionally, they frequently miscalibrate the magnitude of motion, meaning the latent action's representation does not accurately reflect the physical displacement or intensity of the action. Prior work, such as LAPA, UniVLA, and Villa-X, has advanced latent action learning through techniques like VQ-VAE for discretization, language conditioning, or integration of proprioceptive states. Other efforts have explored continuous latent actions with VAE regularization, optical-flow-based methods to emphasize motion, and label-supervised designs to mitigate distractors. Some research has also focused on temporally extended or viewpoint-invariant latent actions.

The current work, AC-LAM, addresses these specific limitations by introducing an additive compositional prior into the latent action space. While the concept of compositionality has been explored in other deep learning domains (e.g., word embeddings and vision-language models), and some prior work like Adaworld has touched upon latent action composition in discrete settings, AC-LAM is presented as the first approach to explicitly endow the *continuous latent action space* for embodied AI with an additive compositional prior. This distinguishes it from existing methods by aiming to enforce a fundamental physical property of motion directly within the latent representation, thereby seeking to yield more structured and robust latent actions for continuous control tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified shortcomings of current latent action learning methodologies. The authors observe that while latent action learning offers a scalable pathway to pretrain embodied AI agents from vast amounts of video data, the resulting latent actions often lack the structured properties inherent in physical motion.

Specifically, the key motivations include:
- **Entanglement of Irrelevant Information:** Existing latent action models frequently encode extraneous details, such as static scene characteristics or cues about future observations, into the latent action representations. This entanglement complicates the disentanglement of pure motion semantics, hindering the model's ability to focus solely on actionable state changes.
- **Miscalibration of Motion Magnitude:** A recurring issue is the failure of current latent actions to accurately reflect the magnitude or intensity of the physical motion they represent. For instance, the norm of a latent action might not correlate proportionally with the actual displacement or degree of movement, as illustrated by baseline models exhibiting under-calibrated motion magnitudes (Figure 1). This lack of calibration impairs the interpretability and utility of latent actions for tasks requiring precise control.
- **Absence of Structural Priors:** Physical motion, particularly over short durations, often exhibits an additive compositional nature. For example, combining a "move right" action with a "move up" action results in a "move diagonally right and up" action, where the net effect is a sum of individual movements. Current latent action models typically do not incorporate such structural priors, missing an opportunity to regularize the latent space towards more physically grounded representations. This absence limits the transferability, planning capabilities, and generalization performance of downstream policies.

Based on these motivations, the authors established the following key objectives for their research:
- **Introduce a Structural Prior:** To develop and integrate a scene-wise additive composition prior into the latent action space, enforcing that for any three observations `o_i, o_j, o_k` within the same scene, the latent action from `o_i` to `o_k` can be approximated as the sum of latent actions from `o_i` to `o_j` and from `o_j` to `o_k` (i.e., `z_ik ≈ z_ij + z_jk`).
- **Promote Algebraic Structure:** To demonstrate that this additive composition prior inherently encourages desirable algebraic properties within the latent action space, such as the emergence of an identity element (zero action for no change), an inverse element (negative action for reverse change), and cycle consistency.
- **Suppress Non-Compositional Information:** To show that enforcing additive composition helps in suppressing irrelevant, non-additive components, such as static environment features or information about future target observations, which can otherwise act as shortcuts during training and detract from learning pure motion semantics.
- **Improve Latent Action Quality:** To learn latent actions that are more structured, motion-specific, and accurately calibrated with respect to physical displacement, thereby enhancing their interpretability and utility.
- **Enhance Downstream Policy Learning:** To provide stronger, more effective supervision signals for training downstream robotic policies, leading to improved performance, generalization, and robustness in both simulated and real-world embodied AI tasks, compared to policies trained with unstructured latent actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around the introduction of the Additively Compositional Latent Action Model (AC-LAM), which imposes a scene-wise additive composition structure on the latent action space.

**Problem Formulation:**
The work begins by defining a standard Latent Action Model (LAM) framework, comprising an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) `f(o_i, o_j)` that maps an observation pair `(o_i, o_j)` to a latent action `z_ij`, and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) `F(o_i, z_ij)` that predicts a future observation `o_j` given a current observation `o_i` and a latent action `z_ij`. Standard LAMs are typically trained using a reconstruction loss `L_rec` to ensure the FDM can reconstruct `o_j` from `o_i` and `z_ij`.

**Additively Compositional Latent Actions (ACLA):**
The key innovation is the additive composition constraint: `z_ik = z_ij + z_jk` for any three observations `o_i, o_j, o_k` belonging to the same scene. This constraint implies that the FDM should also satisfy `F_{z_jk}(F_{z_ij}(o_i)) = F_{z_ij+z_jk}(o_i)`. The authors justify this additive approximation by referencing the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff (BCH) approximation, which suggests that for small inter-frame rigid-body motions (common in LAMs), both translational and rotational components can be approximated as additive in a Euclidean space. This additive structure is also highlighted for its practical benefits in forming differentiable objectives and potentially improving interpretability through displacement calibration of the latent vector norm.

**Theoretical Analysis:**
The paper formally analyzes the implications of enforcing this additive structure:
- **Identity:** It is proven that `z_ii = 0`, meaning the latent action representing no change from `o_i` to `o_i` is the zero vector.
- **Inverse Consistency:** It is demonstrated that `z_ji = -z_ij`, implying that the latent action for reversing a transition is the negative of the forward action.
- **Cycle Consistency:** For any cycle of observations within a scene `i_0 → i_1 → ... → i_m = i_0`, the sum of latent actions along the cycle equals zero.
- **Suppression of Non-Additive Components:** The analysis suggests that additive composition discourages the latent actions from encoding non-additive information. This includes static scene identifiers (`b_s`) and future leakage (`g_j`), as these components would violate the additive property across observation triples, pushing their values towards zero. This theoretical framework suggests that the additive prior acts as a regularizer, guiding the latent space to represent motion-centric information more cleanly.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Scene Definition:** For practical implementation, each video trajectory is considered a distinct "scene" as observations within a trajectory are reachable from each other.
- **Loss Formulation:** Two forms of additive composition loss are considered:
 - `L_AC-IDM`: Applies the additive constraint directly to the IDM outputs: `||f(o_i, o_k) - f(o_i, o_j) - f(o_j, o_k)||^2_2`. Empirically, this form was found to be unstable, often collapsing latent actions to zero.
 - `L_AC-FDM`: Enforces the constraint through the FDM by reconstructing `o_k` using the summed latent actions: `||o_k - F(o_i, f(o_i, o_j) + f(o_j, o_k))||^2_2`. This form was chosen as the default due to its empirical stability.
- **Overall Objective:** The final training objective for AC-LAM is a weighted sum of the reconstruction loss (`L_rec`), regularization losses (`L_reg`, including VQ-VAE codebook and commitment losses), and the proposed additive composition FDM loss (`λ_AC L_AC-FDM`).
- **Latent Space:** AC constraints are applied to post-VQ (Vector Quantization) continuous embeddings, which serve as the continuous latent actions, aligning with common practices in modern LAMs.
- **Architecture and Training:** AC-LAM builds upon the `villa-X` LAM architecture, incorporating a proprioceptive FDM. Pretraining utilizes a diverse dataset combining robot trajectories (e.g., OpenX, AgiBot) and human egocentric videos (e.g., Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS). Triples `(i, j, k)` for `L_AC-FDM` are sampled from the same trajectory with bounded temporal offsets (e.g., [0.1, 3] seconds for robot data), and large rotations in robot data are filtered to ensure the additive approximation holds. Training is extensive, utilizing 32 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
- **Policy Evaluation:** The quality of learned latent actions is assessed by training downstream policies (based on the `villa-X` architecture) using these latent actions as supervision targets, alongside actual robot actions. Policy performance in closed-loop settings serves as a proxy for latent action quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to validate whether AC-LAM learns structured latent actions, improves downstream policy performance, and to understand the impact of design choices.

#### 4.1. Structured Latent Actions

AC-LAM was evaluated for its ability to learn well-structured latent actions along four axes:
- **Adherence to Additive Composition:** Quantified by `L_Norm-AC`, a normalized residual for additive composition. AC-LAM consistently demonstrated lower `L_Norm-AC` values across Fractal, Bridge, and LIBERO datasets compared to baselines (e.g., 0.086 on Fractal for AC-LAM vs. 0.374 for Villa-X, 0.960 for UniVLA, 0.845 for LAPA). This indicates stronger adherence to the additive composition prior.
- **Alignment with Motion Magnitude:** Assessed by the Pearson correlation `r(||z||, ||Δs||)` between the latent action norm and the norm of proprioceptive state changes. AC-LAM showed generally higher correlation (e.g., 0.489 on Fractal vs. 0.289 for Villa-X, 0.325 for UniVLA, 0.024 for LAPA). Qualitative visualizations (Figure 3, 6) further illustrate that AC-LAM's latent action norm tracks physical displacement magnitude more faithfully than baselines, suggesting better displacement calibration.
- **Emergence of Identity and Inverse Elements:** Quantified by `L_Norm-Identity` (norm of `z_ii`) and `Δ_inv` (norm of `z_ij + z_ji`). AC-LAM exhibited values closer to zero for both metrics across datasets, indicating stronger emergence of identity (`z_ii ≈ 0`) and inverse (`z_ji ≈ -z_ij`) properties, thus implying a more structured latent space.
- **Suppression of Non-Compositional Leakage:**
 - **Environment-specific Leakage:** Measured by the accuracy of an XGBoost classifier (`Acc_mlp_env`) predicting the robot dataset ID from latent actions. AC-LAM yielded a lower `Acc_mlp_env` (50.2%) compared to Villa-X (63.0%), UniVLA (80.2%), and LAPA (52.0%). This suggests better suppression of environment-specific information within the latents, promoting environment-agnostic representations.
 - **Future Information Leakage:** Indirectly assessed through the composition-adherence metric (`L_Norm-AC`), which implies suppression of non-additive goal-only terms.

#### 4.2. Downstream Policy Learning Performance

AC-LAM's effectiveness in providing supervision for downstream policy learning was evaluated on two benchmarks:
- **Emoji Table-Top (Simulation):** Policies trained with AC-LAM supervision achieved higher success rates across in-distribution (ID), novel combinations (Train), and held-out validation (Val) splits. Notably, AC-LAM improved the Recognition Ratio (R), which reflects the policy's ability to identify the correct target card. For instance, on the ID split, AC-LAM achieved a Success rate of 55% and an R of 0.90, compared to Villa-X LAM's 42% Success and 0.79 R.
- **Real-World Tabletop Manipulation:** On a physical robot setup, AC-LAM consistently outperformed all baselines (LAPA, UniVLA, Villa-X, and a no-LAM baseline) in average task success under ID scenes, out-of-distribution distractors (OOD-D), and out-of-distribution backgrounds (OOD-B). For example, AC-LAM achieved 60% success on ID tasks, 53.3% on OOD-D, and 33.3% on OOD-B, surpassing Villa-X LAM's 40%, 20%, and 26.7% respectively. These results indicate AC-LAM's robust generalization and more effective supervision under real-world distribution shifts.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Design Choices

Ablations on AC-LAM's design factors (loss form, VQ-VAE placement, stop-gradient) revealed:
- **AC Loss Form:** The `L_AC-FDM` (decoder-side) was more stable than `L_AC-IDM` (encoder-side), as it regularizes through the observation space. `L_AC-IDM` was prone to collapse without stop-gradients, or latent norm explosion with specific stop-gradient placements.
- **VQ-VAE Placement:** Applying AC pre-VQ (on continuous encoder latents) improved displacement calibration (`r` = 0.476) but weakened additive consistency (`L_Norm-AC` = 0.456). Applying AC post-VQ (on codebook embeddings, the default) strengthened additive consistency (`L_Norm-AC` = 0.102) with stable training, while maintaining moderate displacement calibration (`r` = 0.256). The post-VQ approach was adopted to align with established LAM/policy architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the Additively Compositional Latent Action Model (AC-LAM) represents a notable advancement in the field of embodied AI, particularly in learning actionable representations from large-scale video data.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that imposing a scene-wise additive composition prior, reflecting the fundamental structure of physical motion over short horizons, leads to qualitative improvements in latent action representations. This structured approach moves beyond merely learning abstract latent codes and imbues them with interpretable, physically grounded properties.

The potential impacts of this work include:
- **Enhanced Latent Action Quality:** By learning latent actions that are more motion-specific, displacement-calibrated, and adhere to algebraic properties like identity and inverse, AC-LAM provides a more robust and interpretable foundation for robot control. The quantitative and qualitative evidence of improved displacement calibration, in particular, offers a clearer "amount of motion" signal, which can be critical for precise robotic manipulation.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The effective suppression of non-compositional information, such as static environment details and future leakage, contributes to latent actions that are more focused on the core dynamics of an action. This disentanglement reduces spurious correlations, enhancing the generalization capabilities and robustness of downstream policies to out-of-distribution scenarios and visual distractions, as demonstrated in both simulated and real-world experiments.
- **Stronger Supervision for Policy Learning:** The learned structured latents provide higher-quality supervision signals for training robotic policies. The significant improvements observed in downstream task success rates across diverse benchmarks highlight the practical utility of AC-LAM in building more capable and reliable embodied AI agents. This can reduce the dependency on explicit action labels, accelerating the development of generalist robot policies.
- **Principled Framework for Embodied AI:** AC-LAM establishes a principled framework for incorporating physical priors into latent action learning, offering a foundation for future research in designing more expressive and physically consistent latent spaces.

Future work suggested by the authors, such as exploring stronger scene identification methods (e.g., cross-trajectory triples or unsupervised scene clustering), indicates avenues for further aligning structured latents with more complex physical motion priors and overcoming the current limitation of defining scenes strictly by individual trajectories. Overall, this research contributes to building more effective and interpretable embodied AI systems capable of learning and generalizing from diverse visual experiences.
