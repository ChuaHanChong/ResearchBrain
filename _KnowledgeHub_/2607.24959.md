# Amortising Trajectory Optimisation for Residual MPC via Implicit Contact Differentiation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Edinburgh developed an Implicit Function Theorem-based method for differentiating contact dynamics in Google DeepMind's MuJoCo MJX, significantly reducing memory consumption. This enabled an optimizer distillation framework that notably improved the success rates of short-horizon residual Model Predictive Control (MPC) for contact-rich robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Standard automatic differentiation (AD) for iterative contact solvers in physics engines leads to excessive memory consumption, scaling linearly with solver iterations and limiting batch processing.
- Full-horizon trajectory optimization, while effective for complex contact behaviors, is often too computationally intensive for real-time online Model Predictive Control (MPC) in contact-rich tasks.
- Short-horizon MPC lacks the lookahead to plan multi-stage contact sequences, and purely learned policies may struggle with precise contact interactions required for robust control.

## Method

- Developed an **Implicit Function Theorem (IFT)-based method** for computing derivatives of ==regularized smooth contacts== in MuJoCo MJX, which differentiates a ==stationarity residual== of the converged solution, avoiding storage of intermediate solver steps.
- Implemented this **IFT** approach as an ==AD-assisted, modular backward pass== using `jax.lax.custom_root` to robustly solve a transposed linear system for adjoint computation.
- Introduced an **optimizer distillation framework** where ==full-horizon iLQR== (leveraging the IFT-enabled dynamics) generates 'teacher' trajectories offline to train a policy, which then provides a long-horizon nominal action for a ==short-horizon residual iLQR== in online MPC.

## Results

- The IFT-based method reduced temporary memory usage by up to **20x** for increasing active contacts and **9x** for increasing model dimensions compared to unrolled automatic differentiation, while maintaining gradient accuracy.
- Full-horizon iLQR consistently achieved lower mean running costs and tighter variance compared to Adam, confirming its suitability for generating high-quality teacher trajectories.
- The hybrid residual MPC controller demonstrated significantly higher success rates (**28 to 98 percentage points** increase for **H=6** horizon) across various robotic tasks compared to standard short-horizon iLQR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit differentiation can provide accurate gradients for converged contact dynamics solutions while decoupling memory requirements from the number of internal solver iterations, addressing a critical bottleneck in differentiable physics.
> - Amortizing the computational cost of expensive full-horizon trajectory optimization into a learned policy allows for practical, high-performance hybrid control strategies suitable for real-time deployment.
> - Combining a learned policy for long-horizon guidance with a local, contact-aware model-based controller (residual iLQR) effectively addresses the limitations of both purely learned and purely short-horizon model-based approaches in complex contact tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research operates at the intersection of differentiable simulation, trajectory optimization, and model predictive control (MPC) for contact-rich robotic systems. It addresses a recognized challenge in modern physics engines, specifically the computational and memory demands of differentiating contact dynamics.

In the domain of **differentiable simulation**, existing methods for handling contacts typically employ finite differences, which are computationally expensive and sensitive to step size; unroll automatic differentiation (AD) through iterative contact solvers, leading to a growing computation trace and memory footprint; or rely on complex, solver-specific derivations of Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) sensitivities. This paper introduces an alternative based on implicit differentiation, positioning itself as a modular and memory-efficient approach within this landscape. It specifically integrates with Google DeepMind's MuJoCo MJX simulator, a high-performance differentiable physics engine.

Within **trajectory optimization**, methods such as iLQR and DDP exploit local dynamics structure for control, often benefiting from accurate and efficient derivatives. The ability to perform batched, GPU-parallel trajectory optimization is crucial for scaling these methods to complex tasks and for generating large datasets for learning. This work aims to enhance the scalability of such optimizers by providing a more efficient contact derivative, which directly impacts the capacity for batch processing.

The second core contribution relates to **model predictive control (MPC) and policy learning**. Traditional MPC can be computationally intensive for long horizons in contact-rich tasks, while purely learned policies may lack precision or robustness. Hybrid control strategies, combining learned policies with model-based controllers, have gained traction. This paper introduces a specific hybrid approach termed "optimiser distillation for residual MPC," where a learned policy provides a nominal long-horizon action, and a short-horizon, model-based MPC (residual iLQR) provides local, contact-aware corrections. This builds upon prior work in optimiser distillation, policy-guided planning, and residual policy learning, offering a mechanism to amortize the cost of expensive full-horizon trajectory optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research is driven by two primary objectives, each motivated by limitations in current methods for simulating and controlling contact-rich systems:

The first key objective is to **develop a memory-efficient and scalable method for computing derivatives of contact-rich simulation steps**. The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties of differentiating iterative contact solvers. In many physics engines, contact resolution involves an iterative numerical solve rather than an explicit transition map. When standard reverse-mode automatic differentiation (AD) is applied, every iteration of the contact solver becomes part of the backward trace. This leads to a significant accumulation of intermediate states, causing memory consumption to grow linearly with the number of solver iterations (K). This "convergence–parallelism trade-off" restricts the number of parallel trajectories that can be processed (batch size) or necessitates truncating the solver, resulting in gradients based on an under-solved contact response. The authors aim to overcome this memory bottleneck to enable larger-scale differentiable simulations, which are critical for tasks requiring extensive trajectory optimization or for generating data for machine learning.

The second key objective is to **introduce and evaluate an optimiser distillation framework for residual Model Predictive Control (MPC)** that leverages these scalable contact derivatives. The motivation here is to improve the efficiency and success rate of controlling complex, contact-rich robotic tasks, such as robotic manipulation or legged locomotion, under real-time constraints. Full-horizon trajectory optimization, while capable of discovering complex contact behaviors, is often too slow for online MPC due to its computational cost. Conversely, short-horizon MPC may lack the necessary lookahead to plan complex, multi-stage contact sequences, and purely learned policies might struggle with the precise contact interactions required. The authors seek to combine the strengths of both approaches: using expensive full-horizon optimization offline or in the background to train a policy, and then deploying a faster hybrid controller that uses the learned policy for long-horizon guidance and a local, contact-aware MPC for short-horizon corrections. This amortization strategy aims to achieve high performance with a reduced online computational burden for MPC.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper presents two main methodological contributions: an Implicit Function Theorem (IFT)-based approach for contact differentiation in MuJoCo MJX and an optimiser distillation framework for residual MPC.

#### 3.1. Implicit Differentiation for Regularized Smooth Contacts in MJX

The core of the first contribution is to compute derivatives of the converged MJX contact solution without storing the intermediate steps of the iterative solver. This is achieved by differentiating a stationarity residual that vanishes at the solution, leveraging the Implicit Function Theorem (IFT).

- **Contact Model:** The approach uses MuJoCo's regularized convex problem for frictional contact. This formulation ensures a unique, locally smooth solution for acceleration (a) within a fixed contact set and smooth frictional regime. The problem is defined by minimizing a reduced objective function $L(a; \theta)$, which combines inertial terms and a term related to a weighted projection onto the dual friction cone. Here, $\theta$ represents the simulation parameters (configuration, velocity, actuation, friction coefficients, regularization).
- **Stationarity Residual:** The method defines a stationarity residual $F(a, \theta) = \nabla_a L(a; \theta)$. The exact minimizer $a^\star$ is the unique root where $F(a^\star, \theta) = 0$. This residual can be evaluated through the existing MJX computation of generalized forces.
- **Implicit Function Theorem (IFT) Application:** Since the Hessian $F_a = \partial F / \partial a$ (which is $M + J^T \nabla^2_u s J$) is positive definite and thus nonsingular for the regularized problem, the Implicit Function Theorem can be applied. It provides the derivative of the solution $a^\star$ with respect to parameters $\theta$ as $da^\star / d\theta = -F_a^{-1} F_\theta$.
- **Backward Pass Implementation:** For a scalar loss $\ell(a^\star(\theta), \theta)$, the chain rule requires computing $d\ell / d\theta$. Instead of forming the full sensitivity matrix $da^\star / d\theta$, the method defines an adjoint $\eta$ by solving the transposed linear system $F_a^T \eta = \nabla_a \ell$. The total derivative is then computed as $d\ell / d\theta = \nabla_\theta \ell - \eta^T F_\theta$.
- **AD-Assisted and Modular:** This implementation uses `jax.lax.custom_root`, a JAX primitive for implicit differentiation. Automatic differentiation is used to compute the partial derivatives $F_a$ and $F_\theta$ of the stationarity residual, thus avoiding the need for hand-assembled KKT systems. The approach leaves the MJX forward solver unchanged, operating as a modular backward pass. A dense QR solve is used for the linear system, ensuring robustness without relying on exact numerical symmetry of the AD-constructed Jacobian.
- **Locality:** The resulting derivative is local, valid within a fixed contact set and smooth projection region, and may change across contact creation/removal or friction-mode changes.

#### 3.2. Optimiser Distillation for Short-Horizon Contact MPC

The second contribution is a hybrid control strategy that amortizes the computational cost of full-horizon trajectory optimization into a policy for online residual MPC.

- **Teacher Trajectory Generation:** Batched full-horizon iLQR, utilizing the IFT-enabled MJX dynamics, is employed to generate "teacher" trajectories. This offline or background process solves `min_U J(U)` subject to `x_{t+1} = f(x_t, u_t)`, providing optimized action sequences $u^\star_t$. The accuracy and memory efficiency of the IFT derivatives allow for effective batching of these iLQR solves.
- **Policy Distillation:** The optimized actions $u^\star_t$ from successful teacher trajectories are used as supervised targets to train a policy $\pi_\theta(o_t)$, where $o_t$ is the observation (state and task information). The training objective is a mean squared error loss: $L_{sup}(\theta) = \sum_{(o,u^\star) \in M} \| \pi_\theta(o) - u^\star \|^2_2$. A target policy $\bar{\theta}$ (exponential moving average of $\theta$) is used for stability in online distillation.
- **Hybrid Residual MPC at Deployment:** For online control, a hybrid controller is deployed. The control action $u_t$ is defined as $u_t = \text{sg}[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(o_t)] + r_t$, where $\text{sg}[\cdot]$ denotes a stop-gradient operation (meaning the policy action is treated as a constant by the iLQR) and $r_t$ is a short-horizon iLQR-optimized residual.
- **Residual iLQR:** The short-horizon iLQR solves `min_{r_{0:H-1}} \sum_{t=0}^{H-1} \ell_{hyb}(x_t, r_t; x^\star) + \ell_T(x_H; x^\star)` subject to `x_{t+1} = f(x_t, \text{sg}[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(o_t)] + r_t)`. The hybrid running cost $\ell_{hyb}$ includes the task cost and a penalty term $\|r_t\|^2_R$ to regularize the residual, encouraging the controller to stay close to the policy's suggestion while making local, contact-aware corrections.
- **Evaluation:** The hybrid controller's performance is evaluated against standard short-horizon iLQR on tasks involving a Finger (rotating a spinner), Franka (pushing a box), and Unitree A1 quadruped (accelerating to velocity), using metrics like cumulative cost and success rate across varying planning horizons.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several findings across gradient accuracy, memory scaling, trajectory optimization performance, and closed-loop control efficacy.

#### 4.1. IFT-based Derivative Validation and Memory Scaling

- **Gradient Accuracy:** The IFT-based implicit derivative for MJX contact was validated against whole-rollout central finite differences (FD) and unrolled reverse-mode AD on a ball bounce-to-target task. At a solver iteration count of K=5, the IFT and unrolled AD gradients showed agreement with FD across the entire horizon, including during active contact, demonstrating that IFT recovers the same local sensitivity as the converged solver trace. In contrast, shorter unrolls (K=1, K=2) exhibited errors concentrated around contact events. This indicates that IFT provides accurate derivatives corresponding to the converged solution.
- **Temporary Memory Scaling:** A key finding concerns the memory efficiency of the IFT method.
 - **Solver Effort (K):** When scaling the number of solver iterations (K) at fixed model dimension and contact count, unrolled AD's temporary memory grew linearly with K (a 10.6x increase from one to ten iterations). In contrast, IFT's temporary memory remained nearly constant, varying by less than 4% over the same range. This validates IFT's ability to remove the solver-iteration-dependent memory bottleneck.
 - **Active Contacts:** As the number of active contacts increased while generalized velocity dimension ($n_V$) remained fixed, IFT demonstrated substantially smaller memory usage, requiring 20x less memory at 256 active contacts compared to unrolled AD.
 - **Model Dimension (DoF):** When both contacts and model dimension increased (e.g., N free particles each with one contact), IFT used 6x less memory at 16 contacts and 96 DoF. Although dense QR solves for IFT can lead to approximately quadratic memory growth with $n_V$, IFT successfully completed the largest tested problems, whereas unrolled AD and parallel FD encountered out-of-memory failures earlier. This indicates IFT's practical advantage for complex, high-DoF, contact-rich scenarios.

#### 4.2. Trajectory Optimization Performance

- **iLQR vs. Adam:** The paper compared the performance of Adam (a first-order optimizer) and iLQR (a structured shooting method) for full-horizon trajectory optimization on four contact-rich tasks (Finger, Hand, Franka, Unitree), after 20 optimization iterations. iLQR consistently achieved lower mean running costs and tighter 10th-90th percentile ranges across all tasks. This result supported the decision to use full-horizon iLQR as the teacher for policy distillation, given its effectiveness in generating high-quality trajectories.

#### 4.3. Optimiser Distillation for Short-Horizon Contact MPC

- **Improved Success Rates with Residual MPC:** The hybrid controller, combining a distilled policy with short-horizon residual iLQR, was evaluated against standard short-horizon iLQR and policy-only execution across varying planning horizons (H) on Finger, Franka, and Unitree tasks.
 - For a short planning horizon of H=6, the hybrid controller significantly increased success rates by 28 to 98 percentage points across the three tasks compared to standard iLQR.
 - **Franka Arm Task:** The most pronounced difference was observed on the Franka task, where standard short-horizon iLQR maintained near-zero success rates across all evaluated horizons. In contrast, the hybrid controller achieved substantially higher success, indicating that the policy effectively provided the necessary long-horizon pushing strategy, while iLQR refined the local box contact interactions.
 - **Finger Task:** For very short horizons (e.g., H=0, policy-only), the policy could sometimes outperform short residual iLQR, suggesting that myopic residual optimization might occasionally perturb an already effective policy. However, iLQR became beneficial again with sufficient lookahead.
 - **Unitree A1 Task:** On the Unitree task, short-horizon control could achieve low cumulative cost by remaining stationary, failing to meet the forward motion criterion. The hybrid controller became effective once the horizon was long enough to initiate and stabilize forward motion, with success rate being the more informative metric.

These results collectively demonstrate that the policy guidance, enabled by the IFT-accelerated teacher generation, allows the hybrid controller to achieve higher success rates with significantly shorter online planning horizons than a purely model-based short-horizon iLQR.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers contributions with implications for the development and application of robot control and learning in contact-rich environments.

The introduction of an **AD-assisted implicit derivative for regularized smooth contacts in MuJoCo MJX** represents an advancement in differentiable physics simulation. By leveraging the Implicit Function Theorem and differentiating the stationarity residual, the method addresses a primary memory bottleneck associated with unrolling automatic differentiation through iterative contact solvers. This allows for nearly constant memory usage with increasing solver iterations and significantly reduces memory consumption compared to unrolled AD and finite differences when scaling active contacts and model dimensions. This improved memory efficiency is crucial for **scaling differentiable simulations to complex, high-degree-of-freedom robotic systems and for enabling large-batch processing on accelerators**, which is essential for efficient training of deep learning models and for large-scale trajectory optimization.

The subsequent application of these scalable derivatives to **optimiser distillation for residual MPC** provides a practical framework for controlling contact-rich tasks. By amortizing the computational cost of full-horizon trajectory optimization into a learned policy, and then using this policy to guide a short-horizon model-based MPC, the approach combines the benefits of long-horizon planning with local, contact-aware feedback. The demonstrated increase in success rates (28-98 percentage points) on various robotic tasks at short planning horizons indicates that this hybrid control strategy can improve the robustness and effectiveness of MPC in real-time scenarios. This work suggests a viable pathway for **deploying sophisticated, contact-aware control algorithms on hardware with constrained computational resources**, by reducing the online computational burden while maintaining high performance.

The open-source release of the implementation further contributes to the research community by enabling reproducibility and facilitating broader adoption and extension of these methods. Overall, this research contributes to bridging the gap between high-fidelity physics simulation, advanced control techniques, and machine learning, potentially enabling more capable and efficient autonomous robots in complex physical interactions.
