# Tac2Real: Reliable and GPU Visuotactile Simulation for Online Reinforcement Learning and Zero-Shot Real-World Deployment

> [!summary] Summary
> Tac2Real is a visuotactile simulation framework for online reinforcement learning with GelSight-style sensors. It runs a PNCG-IPC contact solver across multi-node multi-GPU workers, emits marker displacement fields as a lightweight plugin to Isaac Lab or MuJoCo, and adds TacAlign, a four-stage calibration pipeline. Policies trained purely in simulation insert an 8 mm peg zero-shot with 91.7% real-world success.

## Problem

- Penalty-based tactile simulators are non-physical and cannot model soft elastomer deformation or multi-phase contact dynamics, so their marker fields diverge sharply from real sensors.
- Material point method simulators capture deformation but handle shear poorly and become numerically unstable under large deformation, producing particle splashing during rotation and slip.
- Few tactile pipelines support multi-GPU parallelism, making large-scale online RL infeasible, and zero-shot tactile sim-to-real has few successful demonstrations on real hardware.

## Method

- **PNCG-IPC** solver replaces Newton steps with ==nonlinear conjugate gradient== and a ==collision-detection-free line search==, trading per-iteration accuracy for GPU-parallel throughput; implemented in ==Taichi== as a plugin.
- A ==Ray==-based multi-node multi-GPU architecture runs tactile simulation outside the physics engine, consuming only relative sensor-object pose and velocity, so it drops into ==Isaac Lab== or ==MuJoCo==.
- **TacAlign** calibrates in four stages: alternating sim-real controller gain optimization on trajectories, ==CMA-ES== fitting of Young's modulus, Poisson ratio, density and friction, task-based contact tuning, then ==domain randomization==.

## Results

- At **4,096** environments on **16** RTX 4090 GPUs it reaches **4,465** FPS with pseudo-structured meshes and **1,665** FPS unstructured, outperforming Tacchi's parallel throughput.
- Zero-shot real peg insertion succeeds in **55** of **60** trials (**91.7%**), versus **15%** for TacSL, **8.3%** for Tacchi, and **6.7%** without tactile input.
- Removing task-based calibration drops real success to **25%** and removing controller alignment to **53.3%**; trajectory alignment cut translation error from **11.11 mm** to **2.521 mm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simulation success rates can look identical across tactile simulators while real transfer differs sixfold; only hardware deployment exposes which simulator has physical fidelity.
> - Low-dimensional marker displacement fields discriminate contact modes far better than RGB tactile images, and the smaller observation space eases large-scale RL training.
> - Matching controller gains across sim and real does not monotonically shrink trajectory error; calibrate at trajectory level and tune both domains jointly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation tasks often involve complex contact interactions, necessitating tactile feedback for perceiving contact forces, surface textures, and local geometries. Vision-based tactile sensors (VBTSs), such as the GelSight family, provide high-resolution perceptual signals by capturing tactile images or marker displacement fields. However, integrating these sensors into data-driven policy training, particularly for online reinforcement learning (RL), presents a challenge due to the computational demands and fidelity requirements of simulating tactile signals.

Existing approaches to visuotactile simulation can be categorized into several types, each with limitations:
- **Penalty-based methods** (e.g., TacSL, Tacto) offer scalability for data generation but often lack the fidelity required to model soft deformation and multi-phase contact dynamics accurately.
- **High-fidelity physics-based methods**, such as Material Point Methods (MPM) and Finite Element Methods (FEM), can capture deformations with greater accuracy. However, they frequently encounter issues with shear response and numerical stability under large deformations. Furthermore, many existing high-fidelity simulation pipelines are not designed for multi-GPU acceleration, which restricts their use in large-scale parallelization necessary for online RL.
- **Incremental Potential Contact (IPC) methods**, like TacIPC, offer robustness in contact handling. However, traditional IPC implementations using Newton's method with continuous collision detection (CCD) are computationally expensive and difficult to parallelize on GPUs.

The field has evolved through stages: initial standalone sensor-object modeling (e.g., Tacchi, TacIPC), then closed-loop policy generation and sim-to-real trials (e.g., Difftactile, Chen et al.), and finally holistic system integration for real-world deployment (e.g., TacSL, TacFlex, Taccel). This paper contributes to the third stage by introducing a framework that aims to simultaneously achieve high-fidelity tactile simulation and computational efficiency, specifically for online RL, while also providing a systematic approach to bridge the sim-to-real gap, a challenge that remains largely unresolved for tactile-based RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work is to enable efficient online reinforcement learning with tactile feedback for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks, followed by reliable zero-shot real-world deployment. This objective stems from several key challenges:

1. **Balancing Physics Fidelity and Computational Efficiency:** Visuotactile sensors are essential for intricate manipulation tasks. However, training policies with tactile feedback in simulation, particularly for online RL, demands a delicate balance. High-fidelity simulations that accurately model soft deformation and multi-phase contact dynamics are computationally intensive and often unsuitable for the high-throughput, parallel environments required by online RL. Conversely, computationally efficient simulations frequently compromise physical realism, leading to a significant domain gap between simulation and reality. The paper aims to develop a simulation framework that addresses this trade-off.

2. **Enabling Large-Scale Online RL Training:** Traditional high-fidelity physics simulators struggle with multi-GPU acceleration and scalability. Online RL requires hundreds or thousands of parallel simulation environments to gather sufficient data for effective policy learning. The lack of a simulation pipeline capable of generating realistic tactile signals at interactive rates across multiple GPUs impedes the adoption of tactile sensing in large-scale RL paradigms. The research seeks to overcome this bottleneck by developing a highly parallelizable simulation architecture.

3. **Mitigating the Sim-to-Real Gap for Tactile Sensing:** Transferring policies trained in simulation directly to the real world (zero-shot transfer) for tactile-based tasks is challenging. This "reality gap" encompasses both structured discrepancies, such as mismatches in robot dynamics, material parameters, and contact models, and stochastic variations arising from unmodeled noise and environmental uncertainties. Without a systematic strategy to narrow this gap, policies trained in simulation may perform poorly or fail in real-world scenarios. The paper aims to provide a comprehensive methodology to ensure reliable sim-to-real transfer.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this work are:
- To present Tac2Real, a lightweight visuotactile simulation framework designed for efficient online RL training by integrating a robust physics solver with a multi-node, multi-GPU architecture.
- To propose TacAlign, a systematic approach for reducing both structured and stochastic sources of the domain gap, thereby ensuring reliable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- To evaluate the effectiveness and robustness of Tac2Real and TacAlign on a contact-rich task, specifically the peg insertion task, demonstrating successful real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Tac2Real framework addresses the aforementioned challenges through two main components: a high-performance visuotactile simulation pipeline and a systematic sim-to-real gap mitigation strategy.

**A. Tac2Real Visuotactile Simulation Framework**
1. **PNCG-IPC Method:** The core of Tac2Real’s tactile simulation is built upon the Preconditioned Nonlinear Conjugate Gradient Incremental Potential Contact (PNCG-IPC) solver.
 - **IPC Formulation:** IPC models elastodynamic contact simulation as a variational optimization problem. Using implicit Euler integration, it minimizes the sum of inertial and hyperelastic potential energies, incorporating a smooth log-barrier contact potential and a frictional potential to handle contact constraints and Coulomb friction.
 - **PNCG Enhancement:** Unlike standard IPC which uses Newton's method (computationally expensive and difficult to parallelize), PNCG-IPC employs the Dai-Kou (DK) nonlinear conjugate gradient algorithm. This algorithm directly solves the nonlinear optimization without explicitly assembling or factorizing the Hessian matrix, relying instead on highly GPU-parallelizable operations like gradient evaluations, diagonal Hessian entries, and vector-vector dot products.
 - **Efficiency and Stability:** This design prioritizes faster iteration throughput over per-iteration precision. While each conjugate gradient step is less precise than a Newton step, its low computational cost on GPUs enables convergence to sufficient accuracy within tens of iterations, which is suitable for tactile simulation where visually plausible and physically consistent deformation is paramount. A CCD-free line search strategy further enhances efficiency by deriving an analytical step-size upper bound, avoiding expensive continuous collision detection queries.
 - **Implementation:** PNCG-IPC is implemented using the Taichi programming language, which compiles concise Python code into high-performance GPU kernels, facilitating its integration.

2. **Tactile Representation:** Tac2Real focuses on simulating marker displacement fields (e.g., 9x7 for GelSight Mini sensors) as the tactile representation.
 - **Rationale:** An evaluation showed that marker displacement fields exhibit greater sensitivity and variation across different contact modes (stationary, press-down, move-forward, move-backward) compared to RGB tactile images, which is critical for contact-rich manipulation. Markers also provide a lower-dimensional representation, potentially improving regularity and efficiency for large-scale RL training.
 - **Generation:** A mapping between physical markers and initial IPC mesh nodes is established using the k-nearest neighbor method, and marker displacements are then interpolated from the deformed mesh node positions.

3. **Integration with Physics Engines:** Tac2Real is designed as a lightweight, plugin-based module that seamlessly integrates with existing mainstream robot simulators like Isaac Lab and MuJoCo.
 - **Architecture:** The tactile simulation functions as an external interface. After the physics engine computes one step based on the RL agent's action, Tac2Real extracts relative physical quantities (position, orientation, velocities) between the grasped object and the tactile sensor. These quantities are fed to Tac2Real, which then performs the tactile simulation and returns marker displacement fields. These fields, combined with other observations from the physics engine, are sent back to the RL agent.
 - **Parallelization:** Leveraging the parallelizability of PNCG-IPC and Taichi, Tac2Real employs a multi-node, multi-GPU architecture. A Ray cluster distributes tactile simulation tasks across multiple GPUs, with each GPU managing tactile simulations for a set of environments. Ray handles inter-node communication for result aggregation.

**B. TacAlign to Narrow Sim-to-Real Gap**
TacAlign is a systematic methodology designed to mitigate both structured and stochastic discrepancies between simulation and reality.
1. **Robot Control Alignment:**
 - **Method:** Acknowledging that simply matching controller parameters (e.g., proportional and damping gains for Cartesian impedance control) is insufficient due to unmodeled dynamics and non-linearities, TacAlign formulates control alignment as minimizing trajectory discrepancy.
 - **Procedure:** An alternating minimization approach is used to jointly calibrate simulation (k_p_sim) and real-world (k_p_real) controller gains. This iterative procedure minimizes the mean squared error (MSE) of end-effector trajectories across six canonical motions (single-axis translation and rotation).

2. **Baseline IPC Calibration:**
 - **Method:** To ensure the simulated elastomer's mechanical response matches reality, material parameters for PNCG-IPC (Young’s modulus E, Poisson’s ratio ν, density ρ, and friction coefficient μ) are calibrated.
 - **Procedure:** An experimental setup with a 6-DOF positioning stage, GelSight Mini sensor, and various 3D-printed indenters is used to record real-world marker displacement field sequences under controlled pressing, sliding, and rotating deformations. These deformations are replicated in the IPC simulator, and the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) is employed to optimize the material parameters by minimizing the MSE between simulated and real displacement fields.

3. **Task-based Calibration:**
 - **Method:** Recognizing that baseline calibration may not fully capture task-specific interactions, this stage fine-tunes simulation parameters within the primary physics engine (e.g., Isaac Lab's friction coefficient μ_isaac and contact stiffness s_isaac).
 - **Procedure:** Marker displacement field sequences are recorded for four representative contact states (stationary grasping, press-down, forward, and backward collisions) in real-world peg insertion. These real measurements serve as ground truth, and μ_isaac and s_isaac are adjusted until the MSE of displacement fields between corresponding frames in simulation and reality falls below a predefined threshold. Compliant contact and friction are identified as critical parameters during this stage.

4. **Randomization:**
 - **Method:** To address unmodeled stochastic uncertainties and encourage policy robustness, domain randomization is applied during RL training.
 - **Procedure:** This includes randomizing physical parameters (controller gains, friction), geometric configurations (object and socket poses, initial hand pose), and sensing channels (end-effector pose noise, IPC perturbations via small random movements on the holding object). This stochastic regularization aims to make the policy invariant to low-level dynamics mismatches and contact variations that are difficult to resolve through deterministic calibration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Tac2Real was evaluated through simulation performance, the impact of TacAlign, online RL learning outcomes, and zero-shot real-world deployment on contact-rich manipulation tasks.

**A. PNCG-IPC Simulation Results**
- **Reliability:** In a cube indenter rotation task, Tac2Real generated realistic deformations consistent with real-world data, similar to Tacchi. TacSL, using a non-physical penalty-based approach, exhibited significant discrepancies. Under large rotational deformations, Tac2Real demonstrated robustness, handling slips and recovering its original state post-contact reliably, in contrast to Tacchi, which suffered from particle splashing and numerical instability due to adhesion deficiencies.
- **Parallel Performance:** Benchmarked on a node with 16 RTX 4090 GPUs, Tac2Real achieved 4,465 Frames Per Second (FPS) with 4,096 pseudo-structured mesh environments and 1,665 FPS with unstructured mesh environments. This performance surpassed Tacchi. While TacSL achieved higher raw FPS, it did so by sacrificing physical fidelity. Tacchi's lower parallel efficiency was attributed to overhead from constructing large background grids for multiple environments on a single GPU. Tac2Real demonstrated a balance of high-throughput parallelism and physical accuracy.

**B. TacAlign Results**
- **Control Alignment:** Initial trajectory discrepancy between simulation and reality with default controller gains was 11.11 mm (translational mismatch), which was deemed too large for the peg insertion task (8 mm socket diameter). Through alternating optimization, the discrepancy was reduced to 2.521 mm (translation) and 0.454° (rotation). The optimized controller gains were k_p_sim = (600, 50) and k_p_real = (400, 20).
- **Baseline IPC Calibration:** Using four distinct indenters (cube, cylinder, moon, triangle) and three deformation modes (pressing, sliding, rotating), CMA-ES optimization of material parameters (E, ν, ρ, μ) resulted in the simulated elastomer's mechanical behavior closely approximating that of the real sensor gel.
- **Task-based Calibration:** Fine-tuning Isaac Lab's friction coefficient (μ_isaac) and contact stiffness (s_isaac) using marker displacement fields from real-world peg insertion contact events (static, press-down, forward, backward collisions) resulted in strong alignment between simulated and real marker displacement fields. Compliant contact and μ_isaac were identified as critical parameters.

**C. Online RL Learning Results in Simulation**
Policies were trained for two contact-rich tasks: random orientation peg insertion and random orientation nut threading. The policy observed only end-effector pose, tactile marker displacement from a single finger, and the previous action, without object pose or visual data.
- **Peg Insertion (simulation):** Tac2Real achieved a success rate of 0.776, comparable to TacSL (0.789). Both significantly outperformed Tacchi (0.173) and the tactile-free case (0.168).
- **Nut Threading (simulation):** Tac2Real achieved a success rate of 0.702, again comparable to TacSL (0.708). Both substantially outperformed Tacchi (0.152) and the tactile-free case (0.313).
These results indicate the effectiveness of both Tac2Real's simulation framework and the critical role of tactile sensing in these tasks.

**D. Real-world Deployment**
A policy trained entirely in Tac2Real simulation was deployed zero-shot on a real Franka robot for the peg insertion task with random initial orientations (0°, ±15°).
- **Zero-Shot Transfer Success:** Tac2Real achieved a real-world success rate of 91.7% (55 out of 60 trials).
- **Comparison to Baselines:** In contrast, TacSL achieved 15.0%, Tacchi 8.3%, and the tactile-free policy 6.7% success rates. TacSL's lower real-world performance was attributed to the discrepancy between its simulated and real marker displacement fields, while Tacchi's low success was linked to numerical instability in MPM.
- **TacAlign Ablation Study:**
 - Full TacAlign (control alignment, baseline IPC, task-based calibration, randomization) yielded 91.7%.
 - Removing control alignment (levels 2,3,4) reduced success to 53.3%.
 - Removing baseline IPC calibration (levels 1,2,4) reduced success to 25.0%.
 - Removing task-based calibration (levels 1,2,3) reduced success to 76.7%.
 The ablation study indicated that task-based calibration plays a significant role in narrowing the sim-to-real gap, with its absence leading to a notable decrease in success rate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Tac2Real, a visuotactile simulation framework designed to facilitate online reinforcement learning and zero-shot real-world deployment for contact-rich robotic manipulation. The work makes several contributions that address long-standing challenges in the field.

The development of Tac2Real, based on the PNCG-IPC method, provides a high-fidelity, computationally efficient, and robust solution for tactile simulation. Its multi-node and multi-GPU parallel architecture enables the generation of marker displacement fields at interactive rates, a crucial capability for large-scale online RL training that was previously limited by the computational expense and stability issues of existing high-fidelity simulators. This advancement means that RL policies can be trained with physically consistent tactile feedback without the common trade-off between fidelity and training speed.

Complementing the simulation framework, TacAlign offers a systematic methodology for mitigating the sim-to-real gap. By addressing structured discrepancies through robot control alignment, baseline IPC material parameter calibration, and task-based fine-tuning of physics engine parameters, along with stochastic variations via domain randomization, TacAlign provides a comprehensive pipeline to ensure reliable transfer of policies from simulation to reality. The ablation studies demonstrated the individual importance of these alignment stages, particularly the task-based calibration, in achieving high real-world success rates.

The experimental validation on the challenging peg insertion task, including online RL learning in simulation and zero-shot deployment on a real robot, substantiates the effectiveness and robustness of the Tac2Real framework. The achieved high success rate in real-world scenarios, significantly outperforming comparison methods, indicates that the framework provides a robust foundation for scaling up tactile-based policy learning.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Advancing Tactile Robotics:** Tac2Real contributes to the long-standing goal of reliable, real-world dexterous manipulation by offering a tool that bridges the gap between simulation and reality for tactile-based control. This could accelerate research and development in areas requiring fine manipulation and contact-rich interactions.
- **Enabling Scalable RL:** By providing a high-performance simulation environment, Tac2Real makes online RL with tactile feedback more feasible and scalable, potentially leading to the development of more sophisticated and adaptable robotic policies.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework can be extended to more complex manipulation scenarios, such as dexterous hand manipulations or interactions with deformable objects. It also has the potential to generate large-scale, high-quality data for training advanced models, including tactile-based vision-language-action (VLA) systems.
- **Broader Applicability:** The architectural principles of Tac2Real, particularly the integration of high-performance physics solvers with parallel computing architectures and systematic sim-to-real mitigation, could be generalized to other physical sensor simulations, such as acoustic or olfactory sensing, suggesting a wider impact on multi-modal robotic perception.
