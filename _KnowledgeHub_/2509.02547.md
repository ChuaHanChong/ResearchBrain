# The Landscape of Agentic Reinforcement Learning for LLMs: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey formally defines Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Large Language Models (LLMs) using Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes, distinguishing it from conventional LLM RL. It offers a detailed taxonomy of RL-enhanced agentic capabilities and application domains, providing a structured overview of the field and identifying future research directions.

## Problem

- The rapidly evolving field of Agentic Reinforcement Learning for LLMs lacked a unified conceptual framework, consistent terminology, and systematic categorization.
- Prior Reinforcement Learning approaches for LLMs often treated them as static generators for single-turn text outputs, not as adaptive agents in dynamic, multi-step sequential decision processes.
- LLM agents, while showing capabilities like tool use and planning, frequently relied on heuristic pipelines or supervised fine-tuning, missing a unified, adaptive learning mechanism.

## Method

- Formally defined Agentic RL using temporally extended ==Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs)==, contrasting it with conventional LLM RL (PBRFT) modeled as degenerate MDPs.
- Developed a comprehensive ==two-fold taxonomy==: one based on RL-optimizable agentic capabilities (e.g., planning, tool use, memory) and another on diverse application domains (e.g., code, math, embodied agents).
- Consolidated over **500** recent works, identifying key trends, methodologies, and advancements, alongside a compendium of open-source environments, benchmarks, and frameworks.

## Results

- Presented a formal distinction between conventional LLM RL (PBRFT, a degenerate MDP) and Agentic RL (a POMDP), clarifying the theoretical underpinnings of treating LLMs as interactive agents.
- Provided a systematic taxonomy categorizing existing research across **six** core agentic capabilities (e.g., Planning, Tool Use, Self-Improvement) and **seven** application domains (e.g., Code Agents, Embodied Agents).
- Compiled a comprehensive list of open-source environments and frameworks, offering practical resources for researchers, and outlined a roadmap for future research by identifying key challenges and opportunities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agentic RL fundamentally shifts LLMs from static conditional generators to active, autonomous decision-makers capable of sustained interaction and adaptation within complex, dynamic environments.
> - Reinforcement learning acts as a critical mechanism for transforming heuristic agentic modules into adaptive, robust, and learnable behaviors, optimizing capabilities through environmental interaction.
> - Significant challenges for Agentic RL include ensuring trustworthiness (e.g., security, hallucination), efficiently scaling training and environments, and developing a mechanistic understanding of how RL installs or amplifies capabilities in LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a significant convergence point in artificial intelligence: the integration of large language models (LLMs) with reinforcement learning (RL) to create autonomous, decision-making agents. This field, termed "Agentic Reinforcement Learning (Agentic RL)" by the authors, represents a shift from conventional applications where LLMs primarily function as static sequence generators.

Prior to this work, research in this area broadly fell into two complementary categories:
1. **Synergy between RL and LLMs:** This thread explores how RL algorithms are utilized to improve or align LLMs, particularly for instruction-following, ethical alignment, and code generation. Notable approaches include Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its extensions like Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Conversely, it also includes efforts to deploy LLMs to enhance traditional RL tasks, such as planning, reward design, or goal generation in control environments.
2. **LLM Agents:** This line focuses on developing LLMs as autonomous or semi-autonomous decision-making entities capable of reasoning, planning, and executing actions. Existing surveys in this area have primarily explored methodology-centered taxonomies (e.g., architectural foundations, collaboration mechanisms), core capabilities (reasoning, acting, interacting), and specific functionalities like tool use or planning strategies.

The identified research gap is the absence of a unified treatment of Agentic RL that conceptualizes LLMs as policy-optimized agents embedded within sequential decision processes. Current studies often examine isolated capabilities, domains, or custom environments with inconsistent terminology, making systematic comparison and generalization challenging. This survey aims to bridge this gap by providing a coherent synthesis that connects theoretical foundations with algorithmic approaches and practical systems, specifically focusing on how RL empowers LLM-based agents in dynamic environments. It explicitly excludes traditional RL not based on LLMs, RL for human value alignment (e.g., refusal of harmful queries), and RL for boosting pure LLM performance on static benchmarks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to formalize and comprehensively map the emerging paradigm of Agentic Reinforcement Learning (Agentic RL). This involves a systematic conceptual shift from viewing large language models (LLMs) as passive text generators to autonomous, decision-making agents operating within complex, dynamic environments.

The key motivations and detailed objectives are:

1. **Formalizing the Conceptual Shift:** The authors aim to formally delineate Agentic RL from conventional LLM RL by contrasting their underlying theoretical frameworks. Conventional LLM RL, particularly Preference-Based Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (PBRFT), is conceptualized as optimizing LLMs within degenerate single-step Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) where the task horizon is fixed (T=1) and observations are full. In this traditional view, LLMs are primarily static conditional generators optimized for single-turn output alignment or benchmark performance. In contrast, Agentic RL is formalized as optimizing LLMs within temporally extended Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs). This framework captures the inherent dynamism, partial observability, and long-horizon decision-making characteristic of agents interacting with complex worlds. The motivation here is to provide a clear, symbolically grounded distinction that underpins the architectural and behavioral differences.
2. **Proposing a Comprehensive Taxonomy:** To address the fragmentation and inconsistent terminology in the rapidly evolving field, the survey proposes a twofold taxonomy:
 - **Capability-centered Taxonomy:** This categorizes Agentic RL based on fundamental agentic capabilities that RL can optimize, including planning, tool use, memory, reasoning, self-improvement (reflection), and perception. The motivation is to illustrate how RL transforms these traditionally static or heuristic modules into adaptive, robust, and jointly optimizable components of an autonomous agent.
 - **Application-centered Taxonomy:** This organizes Agentic RL based on its deployment across diverse task domains, such as search and research agents, code agents, mathematical agents, GUI agents, vision agents, embodied agents, and multi-agent systems. The motivation is to showcase the practical utility and challenges of Agentic RL in solving real-world, interactive problems.
3. **Highlighting Reinforcement Learning as the Critical Mechanism:** A central thesis of the survey is that RL is indispensable for transforming agentic capabilities from static, pre-programmed modules into adaptive behaviors that learn from environmental feedback. This is motivated by the limitations of purely imitation-based or prompt-engineered approaches, which lack the strategic flexibility and robustness required for novel, dynamic, and partially observable environments. RL enables agents to refine strategies, adapt to unforeseen errors, and achieve long-horizon goals through outcome-driven optimization.
4. **Consolidating Research Resources:** To support and accelerate future research, a practical compendium of open-source environments, benchmarks, and frameworks relevant to Agentic RL is provided. This objective is motivated by the need for standardized tools and evaluation protocols to facilitate systematic comparison, reproducibility, and cross-domain generalization, which are currently hindered by disparate approaches.
5. **Charting the Field's Contours and Future Directions:** By synthesizing over five hundred recent works, the survey aims to provide a clear overview of the current state of Agentic RL, identify key opportunities, and articulate the challenges that will shape the development of scalable, general-purpose AI agents. This includes discussing critical issues such as trustworthiness (security, hallucination, sycophancy), scalability of training (computation, model/data size, efficiency), and the development of more complex and adaptive environments.

In essence, the authors are motivated by the need for a structured understanding of Agentic RL to guide its development towards more capable, robust, and general-purpose autonomous AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this survey is a comprehensive synthesis of over five hundred recent works, structured around a formal conceptual framework and a two-fold taxonomy. The approach systematically delineates Agentic RL, analyzes its core components, and maps its applications and resources.

#### 3.1. Formal Definition and Distinction of Agentic RL

The survey initiates its methodology by formally distinguishing Agentic RL from conventional LLM RL (termed Preference-Based Reinforcement Fine-Tuning or PBRFT). This distinction is grounded in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs):

- **PBRFT:** Characterized as a degenerate MDP `⟨S_trad, A_trad, P_trad, R_trad, T = 1, γ = 1⟩`.
 - **State space (S_trad):** A single prompt `{s_0}`. Episodes terminate after one response.
 - **Action space (A_trad):** Pure text sequences.
 - **Transition dynamics (P_trad):** Deterministic transition to a terminal state after one action.
 - **Reward function (R_trad):** A single scalar reward `r(a)` for the entire output sequence, typically from a human- or AI-preference model.
 - **Learning Objective:** Maximize `E_a~π_θ[r(a)]` without a discount factor.
- **Agentic RL:** Modeled as a POMDP `⟨S_agent, A_agent, P_agent, R_agent, γ, O⟩`.
 - **State space (S_agent):** Dynamic world state `s_t` where the agent receives observations `o_t = O(s_t)`. Interactions span multiple time-steps (T > 1).
 - **Action space (A_agent):** A composite space `A_text ∪ A_action`. `A_text` includes free-form natural language, while `A_action` includes abstract, non-linguistic actions (e.g., tool calls, environment interactions) often delimited by special tokens. `A_action` can be recursively constructed for primitive or composite actions.
 - **Transition dynamics (P_agent):** Dynamic and uncertain, `s_(t+1) ~ P(s_(t+1) | s_t, a_t)`.
 - **Reward function (R_agent):** Step-wise rewards `R(s_t, a_t)`, combining sparse task completion rewards `r_task` and dense sub-rewards `r_sub(s_t, a_t)`.
 - **Learning Objective:** Maximize discounted cumulative reward `E_τ~π_θ[Σ_(t=0)^(T-1) γ^t R_agent(s_t, a_t)]` with a discount factor `0 < γ < 1`.

#### 3.2. Overview of RL Algorithms

The survey discusses four foundational RL algorithms central to both PBRFT and Agentic RL frameworks:

- **REINFORCE:** A foundational policy gradient method, emphasizing increasing probabilities of high-reward actions, with a simple objective function `∇_θ J(θ) = E_s_0 [1/N Σ_i (R(s_0, a^(i)) - b(s_0)) ∇_θ log π_θ(a^(i)|s_0)]`. Noted for high variance and sample inefficiency.
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** Dominant for LLM alignment due to stability. It limits policy updates to prevent large changes, using a clipped objective function and an advantage function `A(s_t, a_t) = R(s_t, a_t) - V(s_t)` derived from a critic network.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Bypasses explicit reward models by reframing reward maximization as a likelihood-based objective on human preference data `L_DPO(π_θ; π_ref) = -E_(x,y_w,y_l)~D [log σ(β log (π_θ(y_w|x) / π_ref(y_w|x)) - β log (π_θ(y_l|x) / π_ref(y_l|x))) ]`. Its performance depends on preference data quality.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Addresses PPO's critic overhead by using relative rewards within groups of responses to compute advantages. Its objective function is similar to PPO's clipped objective, but the advantage function `A^(s_t, a_t)` is estimated based on the mean and standard deviation of rewards within a group.

The survey also briefly mentions popular variants for each family of algorithms, highlighting their specific mechanisms (e.g., adaptive KL penalty, stable advantage updates, dynamic KL coefficient, online stabilization, etc.).

#### 3.3. Two-fold Taxonomy

The core of the survey's analytical approach is its two-fold taxonomy:

1. **Capability-Centered Taxonomy:** This part details how RL empowers specific agentic capabilities. Each section (e.g., Planning, Tool Using, Memory, Self-Improving, Reasoning, Perception, Others) discusses the evolution of the capability, how RL is integrated (e.g., as an external guide or internal driver for planning), and prospective research directions. For instance, in "Tool Using," the evolution from "ReAct-style Tool Calling" (prompt engineering/SFT) to "Tool-integrated RL" is traced, followed by the "Prospective: Long-horizon TIR."
2. **Application-Centered Taxonomy:** This section surveys the deployment of Agentic RL across various task domains (e.g., Search & Research Agent, Code Agent, Math Agent, GUI Agent, Vision Agent, Embodied Agent, Multi-Agent Systems, Other Tasks). Within each domain, discussions often categorize methods by the type of reward signal used (outcome-only vs. process-based) and illustrate key contributions, including both open-source and closed-source systems where applicable. Case studies (e.g., OpenAI Deep Research, Voyager) are provided for illustrative purposes.

#### 3.4. Resource Consolidation and Scope Delineation

The survey further contributes by consolidating existing open-source environments, benchmarks, and RL frameworks into practical compendiums. This includes:

- **Environment Simulators:** Categorized by agent capability, task domain, and modality (e.g., Web, GUI, Coding & Software Engineering, Domain-specific, Simulated & Game, General-Purpose Environments).
- **RL Frameworks:** Summarized into Agentic RL frameworks, RLHF and LLM fine-tuning frameworks, and general-purpose RL frameworks.

Crucially, the scope of the survey is explicitly defined: it focuses on how RL empowers LLM-based agents in dynamic environments, while excluding RL for human value alignment, traditional non-LLM RL algorithms, and RL for boosting pure LLM performance on static benchmarks. This clear boundary ensures a focused and coherent analysis of Agentic RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, the "findings" are not experimental results but rather a comprehensive mapping, classification, and synthesis of the current landscape of Agentic Reinforcement Learning (Agentic RL). The main findings and results are encapsulated in the formal distinctions, taxonomies, and identified trends.

#### 4.1. Formalization of Agentic RL

The survey formalizes Agentic RL as a paradigm shift from traditional LLM RL. It establishes that conventional LLM RL (like PBRFT) can be characterized as optimizing LLMs within degenerate, single-step Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with full observability, focusing on passive sequence generation. In contrast, Agentic RL is framed as operating within temporally extended Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs), where LLMs act as learnable policies engaging in autonomous, multi-turn decision-making in dynamic, partially observable environments, equipped with agentic capabilities. This formal distinction provides a theoretical foundation for understanding the behavioral and architectural complexities of Agentic RL.

#### 4.2. Comprehensive Two-fold Taxonomy

The paper presents a detailed two-fold taxonomy, organizing the field along core agentic capabilities and diverse application domains:

- **Capability-Centered Taxonomy:** RL is identified as a critical mechanism for transforming and optimizing six core agentic capabilities:
 - **Planning:** RL enables agents to refine multi-step decision trajectories, moving from external guidance of search algorithms to internally driving the LLM's intrinsic planning policy. The future lies in synthesizing intuitive plan generation with deliberative reasoning.
 - **Tool Use:** RL evolves tool-enabled agents from static, imitation-based (ReAct-style) pipelines to adaptive, outcome-driven Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) systems, allowing agents to strategically decide *when*, *how*, and *which* tools to deploy. Long-horizon temporal credit assignment remains a key challenge.
 - **Memory:** RL transforms memory from passive data stores into dynamic, control-optimized subsystems. This includes RL for RAG-style retrieval, token-level memory management (explicit and latent tokens), and points towards future work in structured memory representations.
 - **Self-Improvement:** RL provides mechanisms for ongoing reflection and learning from mistakes. This progresses from verbal self-correction heuristics to internalizing self-correction through gradient-based updates and iterative self-training via self-play, execution-guided curriculum generation, and collective bootstrapping. The meta-evolution of reflection ability is identified as a future frontier.
 - **Reasoning:** The survey differentiates between fast (intuitive, efficient) and slow (deliberate, structured) reasoning. RL is highlighted for enhancing slow reasoning by enabling deeper reflection and logical consistency, with an emphasis on integrating these mechanisms into agentic reasoning to balance efficiency and accuracy.
 - **Perception:** RL facilitates the shift from passive perception to active visual cognition in multimodal LLMs. This is achieved through grounding-driven (linking text to visual regions), tool-driven (using external visual tools), and generation-driven (imagining sketches) active perception, also extending to audio and 3D modalities.
 - **Others (Long-horizon interaction):** RL addresses temporal credit assignment in extended interactions through process-based supervision and segment-level preference optimization.

- **Application-Centered Taxonomy:** Agentic RL has demonstrated potential across various task domains:
 - **Search & Research:** Evolution from simple RAG to autonomous deep research agents, optimizing query generation and search-reasoning coordination through RL, often categorized into open-source vs. closed-source methods and external (web) vs. internal (LLM knowledge) search.
 - **Code Agents:** RL is crucial for code generation (outcome vs. process rewards), iterative code refinement (debugging), and automated software engineering (SWE), with the emergence of Code World Models (CWMs) for symbolic simulation.
 - **Mathematical Agents:** RL enhances both informal (natural language, programming language tools) and formal (proof assistants like Lean) mathematical reasoning, leveraging outcome, process, and hybrid reward signals (e.g., expert iteration).
 - **GUI Agents:** Progression from RL-free (VLM/SFT) methods to RL-based agents in static and interactive GUI environments, optimizing navigation and control with sparse or shaped rewards.
 - **Vision Agents:** Broad application of RL across image, video, and 3D tasks to enhance perception, reasoning, structured prediction, and generation.
 - **Embodied Agents:** RL is a post-training strategy for VLA (Vision-Language-Action) models in navigation and manipulation tasks, enhancing planning and trajectory prediction. Challenges include the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Multi-Agent Systems (MAS):** RL-driven optimization ranges from non-parametric coordination modules to selected agent policies and end-to-end multi-agent reinforcement learning, addressing complex collaboration and credit assignment.
 - **Other Tasks:** Includes text games, table understanding (Text-to-SQL), time series analysis, general QA, and social intelligence tasks.

#### 4.3. Resource Compendium

The survey successfully consolidates a comprehensive list of open-source environments (e.g., WebShop, Mind2Web, SWE-bench, ALFWorld, Crafter, Agentbench) and RL frameworks (e.g., SkyRL, AREAL, EasyR1, OpenRLHF, TRL) that support experimentation and benchmarking in Agentic RL. This compilation serves as a practical resource for researchers.

#### 4.4. Identification of Open Challenges and Future Directions

The paper identifies critical challenges hindering the development of scalable, general-purpose AI agents:
- **Trustworthiness:** Addresses security (expanded attack surface, reward hacking), hallucination (ungrounded outputs, hallucination tax), and sycophancy (conforming to user biases).
- **Scaling Up Agentic Training:** Discusses computational demands, the impact of model and data size on RL effectiveness (synergy vs. conflict), and the need for greater training efficiency (e.g., post-training recipes, dynamic fine-tuning).
- **Scaling Up Agentic Environments:** Highlights the need for dynamic, optimizable environments that can automate reward function design and curriculum generation.
- **Mechanistic Debate:** Explores the debate on whether RL primarily "amplifies" pre-existing capabilities or "creates" new knowledge and reasoning algorithms in LLMs. The evidence suggests RL can do both, with new capabilities emerging under specific conditions (high-fidelity rewards, compositional tasks, intermediate base models, explicit exploration).
- **Architectural Patterns for Real-World Deployment:** Identifies practical design principles such as safety guardrails, human-in-the-loop (HITL) supervision, hierarchical orchestration, and inter-agent communication protocols as crucial for robust deployment.
- **Broader Social Impact:** Discusses dual-use risks, environmental sustainability, labor market implications, bias amplification, and evaluation contamination as significant societal considerations shaping research priorities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this survey are multi-faceted, addressing conceptual clarity, research acceleration, and responsible development within the rapidly expanding field of Agentic Reinforcement Learning.

#### 5.1. Conceptual Clarity and Field Unification

The survey's most immediate impact lies in its rigorous formalization of Agentic RL, distinctly separating it from conventional LLM RL. By framing Agentic RL within the context of temporally extended Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs), the authors provide a unified theoretical lens through which disparate research efforts can be understood. This clarification of terminology and underlying assumptions is crucial for coherent discourse and avoids ambiguity in a field characterized by rapid innovation and evolving definitions. The two-fold taxonomy (capability-centered and application-centered) offers a structured and comprehensive map, enabling researchers to quickly position their work, identify related efforts, and understand the interdependencies between different aspects of agentic intelligence.

#### 5.2. Acceleration of Research and Development

By synthesizing over five hundred recent works and consolidating open-source environments, benchmarks, and frameworks, the survey acts as a practical compendium for the research community. This consolidation reduces the overhead for new entrants to the field and provides established researchers with a centralized reference point for tools and evaluation protocols. Such a resource is vital for fostering systematic comparison, reproducibility, and enabling cross-domain generalization, thereby accelerating the pace of innovation in agentic systems. It highlights mature techniques and critical gaps, guiding future research toward promising directions.

#### 5.3. Guiding the Development of Robust AI Agents

The paper's detailed analysis of how RL empowers core agentic capabilities (planning, tool use, memory, self-improvement, reasoning, perception, and long-horizon interaction) directly informs the design and training of more robust and adaptive AI agents. By demonstrating RL's role in transforming static modules into dynamic, adaptive behaviors, the survey underscores the necessity of outcome-driven optimization for agents operating in complex, dynamic environments. This is particularly impactful for tasks requiring multi-step reasoning, adaptive tool invocation, and continuous learning from environmental feedback, such as deep research, automated software engineering, formal mathematics, and embodied robotics.

#### 5.4. Addressing Critical Challenges and Fostering Responsible AI

The explicit identification and detailed discussion of open challenges, including trustworthiness (security, hallucination, sycophancy), scalability of training (computation, model/data size, efficiency), and environment scaling, are crucial. This proactive approach highlights areas requiring immediate attention and encourages the development of solutions that integrate safety, alignment, and efficiency from the outset. By examining the "mechanistic debate" regarding RL's role (amplifier vs. new-knowledge creator), the survey encourages deeper theoretical understanding of how RL genuinely enhances LLM capabilities, which is fundamental for predictable and controllable AI development. Furthermore, the discussion on architectural patterns for real-world deployment (e.g., safety guardrails, human-in-the-loop supervision) provides practical guidance for building robust and reliable agentic systems.

#### 5.5. Broader Societal Awareness and Impact

The inclusion of a section on broader social impact significantly elevates the significance of this survey. By addressing dual-use risks, environmental sustainability, labor market implications, bias amplification, and evaluation contamination, the authors contextualize the technical advancements within a wider societal framework. This encourages researchers to consider the ethical, economic, and environmental consequences of agentic AI, promoting a more responsible and holistic approach to its development and deployment. This awareness is vital as agentic LLMs move closer to widespread real-world applications, potentially transforming various industries and aspects of daily life.

In summary, "The Landscape of Agentic Reinforcement Learning for LLMs: A Survey" is a foundational work that clarifies, organizes, and projects the future of a pivotal area in AI. Its formal rigor, comprehensive scope, and forward-looking analysis make it an influential resource for academic researchers, industrial practitioners, and policymakers alike, guiding the field towards the development of capable, trustworthy, and beneficial general-purpose AI agents.
