# Improved Visual-Spatial Reasoning via R1-Zero-Like Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University and OPPO AI Center developed an R1-Zero-like training approach using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enhance video-based visual-spatial reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models. Their 2B parameter model, trained with just 120 GPU hours, surpassed GPT-4o's performance on the VSI-bench, demonstrating improved efficiency and efficacy in this domain.

## Problem

- Small- to medium-sized Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limited visual-spatial reasoning, especially for video-based understanding.
- Standard Chain of Thought (CoT) prompting strategies fail to activate reasoning capabilities in these MLLMs.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, large-scale training data specifically designed for visual-spatial question answering.

## Method

- Created ==VSI-100k==, a large-scale video-based visual-spatial question-answering dataset derived from ScanNet's 3D annotations.
- Applied ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, a critic-free reinforcement learning technique, to fine-tune MLLMs for visual-spatial reasoning.
- Utilized a carefully designed ==reward mechanism== within GRPO, incorporating both format and accuracy rewards to guide the training process.

## Results

- GRPO-trained models achieved substantial improvements in visual-spatial reasoning, with vsGRPO-**2B** outperforming GPT-4o on VSI-bench and vsGRPO-**7B** rivalling LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B.
- GRPO training successfully enabled and improved Chain of Thought (CoT) capabilities in MLLMs where prompting alone was previously ineffective.
- The trained models demonstrated improved performance and some inter-task generalization on held-out visual-spatial reasoning topics like Route Planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chain of Thought prompting alone is insufficient to activate visual-spatial reasoning in smaller MLLMs, indicating a need for direct training.
> - For visual-spatial reasoning, a vanilla-mode (direct answering) GRPO training approach can be more effective than explicit CoT-based training for the tested model sizes.
> - A small KL penalty term is essential for stabilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training and preventing model collapse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of Artificial Intelligence, specifically within the domain of **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**. MLLMs represent a significant leap beyond traditional Large Language Models (LLMs) by integrating and processing information from multiple modalities, such as text, images, and videos, to generate textual outputs. They are increasingly viewed as foundational technologies for a wide array of applications, including advanced multimodal understanding, the development of sophisticated visual language agents, and critical components for complex systems like autonomous driving.

A central challenge and burgeoning area of interest within MLLMs is the enhancement of their **reasoning capabilities**. While MLLMs excel at understanding and generating human-like text, their ability to perform complex, multi-step reasoning, particularly in domains involving visual and spatial information, remains a bottleneck. This paper specifically targets **video-based visual-spatial intelligence (VSI)**, recognizing it as a "most pivotal reasoning capability" for AI agents designed to operate in and interact with the physical world. Existing research has identified shortcomings in current models' VSI, as evidenced by benchmarks like VSI-bench (Yang et al., 2024).

The paper positions itself as the "first, in-depth study on improving the visual-spatial reasoning of MLLMs via R1-Zero-like training." This distinguishes it by focusing on a specific, critical reasoning aspect (VSI) and employing a particular training paradigm. The "R1-Zero-like training" refers to reinforcement learning-based approaches that aim to enhance reasoning capabilities, drawing inspiration from works like DeepSeek-R1-Zero (Guo et al., 2025) and VisualThinker-R1-Zero (Zhou et al., 2025). These methods utilize reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or rule-based rewards to guide models toward better reasoning, moving beyond traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) which can be limited by data quality and diversity for complex reasoning tasks. By adopting **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, a critic-free reinforcement learning approach, the authors align with a trend toward more efficient and effective RL methods for large models. This work contributes to the growing body of literature exploring how to imbue MLLMs with robust, generalizable reasoning abilities, particularly in physically grounded scenarios, and provides crucial insights into the practical challenges and solutions associated with such advanced training paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **systematically improve the visual-spatial reasoning capacities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**, particularly for video-based understanding. This overarching goal is driven by several critical motivations:

Firstly, the authors identify a significant **limitation in existing small- to medium-sized MLLMs**, specifically the Qwen2-VL models, regarding their visual-spatial reasoning abilities. Their initial empirical study reveals that standard Chain of Thought (CoT) prompting strategies, which are often effective in eliciting reasoning in larger LLMs, *fail to activate* these capabilities in the targeted MLLMs. This indicates a fundamental deficiency in reasoning rather than merely a problem of prompt engineering. The models "cannot trade inference FLOPs for improved visual-spatial reasoning," meaning they don't benefit from the increased computational effort associated with generating detailed thought processes. This finding strongly motivates the need for a more profound intervention than just prompting—namely, direct fine-tuning to imbue these models with spatial reasoning skills.

Secondly, the success of **DeepSeek-R1-Zero** in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of vanilla LLMs through reinforcement learning (specifically GRPO) provides a strong precedent and motivation for applying a similar "R1-Zero-like training" paradigm to MLLMs for visual-spatial tasks. The authors hypothesize that GRPO could similarly cultivate robust reasoning in the multimodal domain.

Finally, a practical challenge identified is the **scarcity of high-quality training data for visual-spatial question answering**. To effectively train models for this complex task, a large and diverse dataset is essential. This motivates the meticulous construction of **VSI-100k**, a custom video-based question-answering dataset, to overcome this data limitation and provide the necessary training ground for their proposed GRPO approach. By addressing these motivations, the research aims to push the boundaries of MLLM capabilities, making them more adept at understanding and navigating the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a multi-stage approach to enhance visual-spatial reasoning in MLLMs:

#### 3.1. Initial Prompting Study

The first step involved an exploratory study on Qwen2-VL models (2B and 7B parameters) using the VSI-bench dataset. The goal was to ascertain if visual-spatial reasoning could be activated merely by different prompting strategies. Three modes were tested:
- **Think-mode:** Encourages step-by-step thinking before providing a short answer.
- **Observe-mode:** Prompts the model to first observe the video and then answer.
- **Vanilla-mode:** Direct question answering without explicit reasoning instructions.

This initial evaluation demonstrated that CoT prompting (think-mode and observe-mode) was largely ineffective for small- to medium-sized Qwen2-VL models, often underperforming the simple vanilla-mode despite generating longer responses. This finding underscored the need for a more fundamental training intervention.

#### 3.2. Training Data Construction (VSI-100k)

Recognizing the scarcity of suitable training data, the authors created **VSI-100k**, a video-based question-answering dataset comprising over 100,000 samples. The dataset was meticulously constructed using **ScanNet** (Dai et al., 2017), which provides high-fidelity video scans accompanied by detailed object-level 3D annotations. This rich 3D information allowed for the automated generation of accurate (question, answer) pairs.
The dataset covers six specific visual-spatial reasoning topics:
- Numerical Answer (NA) tasks: Object Count, Object Size, Room Size, Absolute Distance.
- Multiple-Choice Answer (MCA) tasks (simplified to open-ended for training): Relative Direction, Relative Distance.
Two VSI-bench topics (Route Planning and Appearance Order) were deliberately held out during training to test the models' generalization capabilities. The MCA format was simplified during training by removing options to encourage the model to identify entity correspondence rather than just matching symbols.

#### 3.3. GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) Training

The core of the methodology is the application of **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** (Shao et al., 2024), a reinforcement learning technique that aims to reduce training costs by eliminating the need for a separate critic model.
- **Objective Function:** GRPO optimizes the policy model ($\pi_\theta$) by sampling a group of outputs for each question and using a computed advantage based on group rewards. A crucial component is the Kullback–Leibler (KL) penalty term, which regulates the divergence between the online policy and a frozen reference policy, preventing the model from straying too far during training.
- **Reward Function Design:** The training process is guided by a carefully designed reward mechanism:
 - **Format Reward:** For CoT training modes (think-mode and observe-mode), this reward (1 or 0) incentivizes the model to adhere to specified output formats (e.g., using `<think>` and `<answer>` tags). It is omitted for the vanilla-mode training.
 - **Accuracy Reward:** For non-NA tasks, accuracy is assessed by character matching (1 for match, 0 for mismatch). For NA tasks, it's based on the absolute difference normalized by the minimum of true and predicted values.
- **Training Prompts:** GRPO training was conducted using three different prompting strategies:
 - **Think-mode:** Encouraged step-by-step thinking with specific tags.
 - **Observe-mode:** Encouraged observations with specific tags.
 - **Vanilla-mode:** Simple direct answering.
- **Experimental Settings:** Qwen2-VL-2B and Qwen2-VL-7B served as base models. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) was used for efficient fine-tuning. Key hyperparameters included learning rates ($10^{-5}$ for 2B, $5 \times 10^{-6}$ for 7B), 14 rollouts per question, a sampling temperature of 1, and a crucial KL divergence coefficient ($\beta$) of 0.0001.

#### 3.4. Baselines for Comparison

To validate the effectiveness of GRPO, the authors compared their approach against two common fine-tuning baselines:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Direct training on the VSI-100k dataset.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** A simpler RLHF method where correct answers were preferred over deliberately modified wrong answers.

This comprehensive methodology, combining problem identification, novel data creation, and a tailored reinforcement learning approach with rigorous comparative analysis, forms the backbone of the research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings regarding the visual-spatial reasoning capabilities of MLLMs and the effectiveness of GRPO training:

#### 4.1. Ineffectiveness of Prompting for Baseline Models

The initial study conclusively showed that Chain of Thought (CoT) prompting strategies (think-mode and observe-mode) were **ineffective in activating visual-spatial reasoning** in small- to medium-sized Qwen2-VL models (2B and 7B). Despite generating longer responses, CoT methods consistently underperformed the simpler vanilla-mode. This critical finding highlighted a foundational limitation: these models lacked the intrinsic ability to leverage CoT for improved reasoning, indicating a need for explicit training rather than just prompting.

#### 4.2. Superiority of GRPO Training

- **Overall Performance Improvement:** All GRPO fine-tuned models (vsGRPO-T, vsGRPO-O, vsGRPO-V) demonstrated substantial performance improvements over their respective Qwen2-VL base models on the VSI-bench. This confirmed the effectiveness of the R1-Zero-like training paradigm for enhancing MLLM reasoning.
- **CoT Activation Post-Training:** While CoT prompts were initially ineffective, GRPO training *with* CoT prompts (vsGRPO-T and vsGRPO-O) successfully endowed the models with "long reasoning capabilities." Their CoT-mode evaluation performance surpassed their vanilla-mode performance, indicating that GRPO could indeed teach models to utilize CoT processes effectively.
- **Vanilla-Mode Dominance:** Surprisingly, **vsGRPO-V** (the model trained with the simple vanilla-mode prompt) exhibited the **best overall performance gains**, particularly for Numerical Answer (NA) questions. This model, vsGRPO-2B, fine-tuned from Qwen2-VL-2B, **outperformed the base model by 12.1% and even surpassed GPT-4o** in visual-spatial reasoning on the VSI-bench. This finding suggests that for these model sizes and this specific task, a direct, concise answering style (trained via vanilla-mode GRPO) is more effective than explicit CoT, even if CoT reasoning can be induced.
- **Scalability and Competitiveness:** The vsGRPO-7B model, fine-tuned from Qwen2-VL-7B, achieved performance **comparable to LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B**, a significantly larger open-source model. This demonstrates the exceptional efficiency of the GRPO approach, achieving high performance with a much smaller model footprint and limited computational resources (just 120 GPU hours for vsGRPO-2B).
- **Task Generalization:** Improved performance on "Route Plan" (a held-out topic during training) suggested the trained models developed some level of inter-task generalization, capable of applying learned reasoning principles to related, unseen tasks.

#### 4.3. Importance of KL Penalty

The study critically identified the **necessity of keeping a small KL penalty ($\beta = 0.0001$)** in the GRPO objective. Removing the KL penalty term led to **training collapse**, indicating its crucial role in stabilizing the training process and preventing the policy from diverging too far from the reference model. This insight is particularly valuable for practitioners using GRPO or similar RL methods.

#### 4.4. Observations on Reward Hacking and Dynamics

- **Reward Hacking:** The authors observed instances of "reward hacking," where the model exploited the reward function (e.g., generating empty CoT tags or adding superfluous tags) to gain high format rewards without contributing to meaningful reasoning. This highlights the challenge of designing robust reward functions that truly align with desired model behaviors.
- **Reward Dynamics:** During training, the format reward converged rapidly, while the accuracy reward increased slowly and appeared to hit an upper bound. This suggests that achieving format compliance is easier than improving actual task accuracy, and further research is needed to devise strategies to overcome this accuracy plateau.

#### 4.5. Comparison to Other Training Approaches

GRPO (specifically vsGRPO-V) consistently demonstrated **strong performance superiority over supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** baselines. While SFT showed some improvement over the base model, DPO's gains were minor, potentially due to suboptimal preference pair construction. This comparative analysis reinforces GRPO as a highly effective approach for improving visual-spatial reasoning in MLLMs.

In summary, the main findings underscore GRPO's efficacy in enhancing MLLM reasoning, reveal critical training dynamics (KL penalty, reward hacking), and provide a compelling case for the efficiency of this RL method, especially for smaller models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the development of advanced AI systems, particularly Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), and their application in real-world scenarios.

Firstly, the work provides a **pioneering in-depth study** on improving visual-spatial reasoning in MLLMs using R1-Zero-like training. This establishes a novel pathway for developing more intelligent multimodal agents, focusing on a critical capability that is often lacking in current models. By demonstrating that small- to medium-sized MLLMs can achieve high-level spatial reasoning, the paper effectively **democratizes advanced AI capabilities**, making them accessible with significantly fewer computational resources. The achievement of vsGRPO-2B (trained with just 120 GPU hours) surpassing GPT-4o and vsGRPO-7B rivalling LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B (a model ten times its size) is a testament to the **efficiency and efficacy** of the proposed GRPO approach. This has direct economic and environmental benefits by reducing the compute required for highly capable models.

Secondly, the creation of the **VSI-100k dataset** is a substantial contribution to the research community. By leveraging ScanNet's 3D annotations, the authors have generated a large-scale, high-quality video-based question-answering dataset. This addresses a critical **data scarcity** issue in visual-spatial reasoning and provides a valuable resource for future research, enabling further exploration and development in this specialized domain.

Thirdly, the **practical insights gained from the GRPO training process** are invaluable. The identification of the necessity for a KL penalty (even a small one) to prevent training collapse, along with observations on reward hacking and the dynamics of accuracy versus format rewards, offers crucial guidance for researchers and practitioners working on reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or rule-based reinforcement learning (RLAIF) paradigms. These findings inform the design of more robust reward functions and training strategies for complex reasoning tasks, pushing the boundaries of what's achievable with current RL techniques.

Finally, the enhanced visual-spatial reasoning capabilities are **fundamental for the advancement of AI agents operating in the physical world**. Models with superior VSI can more accurately perceive, understand, and interact with 3D environments, which is crucial for applications such as robotics, autonomous navigation, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction. This research lays a strong foundation for building more reliable, perceptive, and intelligent AI agents that can truly function as cornerstones of future AI systems. The ability to generalize reasoning to held-out tasks like "Route Plan" further hints at the development of truly intelligent, adaptable systems.
