# More with LESS -- Local Scene Representations for Tactile Imaging

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Technion introduce the Local Encoder for Spatial Sensing (LESS) architecture, enabling hand-held, real-time 3D tactile imaging of soft objects with zero-shot generalization to complex structures and localized uncertainty estimation. The approach improved 2D reconstruction F1 scores to 85.2% and accurately imaged multiple unseen inclusions with an F1 score of 71.1%, demonstrating operational feasibility for clinical applications.

## Problem

- Prior self-supervised learning approaches for tactile imaging relied on global representations, struggling to generalize to complex or novel object configurations.
- Existing tactile imaging systems were dependent on robot-controlled sensing, limiting their practical deployment as hand-held diagnostic tools for human interaction.
- Learning-based tactile imaging typically produced 2D slices, providing incomplete information about the full 3D internal structures of soft objects.
- The lack of localized uncertainty feedback meant operators couldn't effectively identify ambiguous regions that required additional tactile measurements.

## Method

- A novel neural architecture, **LESS** (Local Encoder for Spatial Sensing), was developed to represent tactile scenes as a collection of local representations, each with a spatially defined ==receptive field==.
- External ==fiducial-based pose tracking== and new datasets mimicking human-like palpation were incorporated to transition from robot-controlled to real-time hand-held tactile imaging.
- The tactile imaging framework was extended to reconstruct full 3D volumes of internal structures, utilizing 3D MRI ground truth and a combined ==Focal and Dice loss== for training.

## Results

- 3D training improved 2D F1 scores to **85.2%** for 3D-trained models, compared to **79.9%** for 2D-trained models, and provided a richer 3D visualization of internal structures.
- LESS achieved an F1 score of **71.1%** on unseen phantoms with multiple inclusions, substantially outperforming a global baseline (**44.6%**), and generalized effectively to larger objects.
- A proof-of-concept real-time (approximately **4Hz**) hand-held tactile imaging system was demonstrated with minimal performance degradation (F1 score of **67.3%** with estimated pose versus **67.6%** with true pose).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling tactile scenes with local, spatially receptive fields enables zero-shot compositional generalization, allowing accurate imaging of complex, unseen objects (e.g., multiple inclusions) from simpler training data.
> - Training the reconstruction model with full 3D ground truth data, rather than just 2D slices, enhances overall accuracy and captures richer spatial regularity in internal structures.
> - The local nature of the proposed representation allows for generating localized uncertainty estimates, which can guide operators to collect additional samples in ambiguous regions to improve diagnostic reliability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of tactile imaging (TI), which aims to reconstruct the internal structure of soft objects using touch sensing. This capability holds relevance for medical diagnosis, such as breast cancer screening, and for advanced robotic manipulation in various domains.

Previous research, including that by Rimon et al., introduced self-supervised learning (SSL) approaches for TI. These methods demonstrated promising results by training neural networks to learn tactile representations from robot-palpated soft phantoms with known internal structures (e.g., via MRI). However, these earlier approaches typically relied on global, unstructured representations of the tactile scene and required robot-controlled sensing during both training and inference. These characteristics limited the models' generalization capabilities to unseen object configurations and their practical applicability in real-world scenarios, particularly as hand-held diagnostic tools.

Within the broader landscape of tactile sensing and representation learning, existing methods for image-based tactile sensors (e.g., Sparsh, AnyTouch) and tactile sensor arrays (e.g., T-DEX) primarily focused on representing individual tactile measurements rather than entire tactile scenes. For tactile scene representations, much prior work has concentrated on rigid objects (e.g., Qi et al., NeuralFeels, Tactile-Informed 3DGS, VTacO), which is not directly applicable to imaging the internal structure of soft, deformable objects crucial for medical applications.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a novel object-centric tactile representation. It introduces a compositional architecture designed to improve generalization across varying object structures and enables the transition from robot-controlled to hand-held tactile imaging. Furthermore, it extends tactile imaging beyond 2D slices to full 3D reconstruction, aiming to provide a more comprehensive understanding of soft object internals. The work therefore contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in learning-based tactile imaging for soft materials, enhancing its robustness, versatility, and clinical feasibility.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were driven by several limitations identified in prior work on tactile imaging, particularly those employing self-supervised learning:

1. **Develop an Object-Centric Tactile Representation for Enhanced Generalization**:
 - **Motivation**: Existing SSL approaches often use a single, unstructured vector to represent an entire tactile scene. This global representation struggles to generalize when encountering complex objects composed of multiple elements or varying material properties, as it would need to learn representations for an exponentially growing number of possible combinations. The researchers hypothesized that touch is inherently local, meaning the tactile response at a given position is largely independent of structures far away.
 - **Objective**: To design a novel architecture, Local Encoder for Spatial Sensing (LESS), that models the tactile scene as a collection of local representations, each with a defined spatial receptive field. This compositional design aims to facilitate generalization by allowing the model to learn local tactile responses for individual material properties, rather than requiring training on all possible object configurations. This approach is intended to enable zero-shot compositional generalization, where models trained on simple objects (e.g., single inclusions) can accurately image more complex, unseen objects (e.g., multiple inclusions or different sizes).

2. **Enable Hand-Held Tactile Imaging for Practical Application**:
 - **Motivation**: Previous SSL methods for tactile imaging were typically reliant on robot-operated sensing, which involved precise robot kinematics for sensor pose tracking and often used limited, robot-specific motion patterns (e.g., vertical pokes). This constrained practical application, especially in medical settings where a hand-held device would offer physicians greater maneuverability and maintain patient contact, similar to ultrasound examinations.
 - **Objective**: To overcome these dependencies by incorporating external pose tracking for the tactile sensor and collecting new datasets that better resemble natural human palpation motions. This aims to demonstrate a proof-of-concept for a real-time, hand-held tactile imaging system, making the technology more accessible and clinically relevant.

3. **Extend Tactile Imaging to Full 3D Reconstruction**:
 - **Motivation**: Prior work on learning-based tactile imaging was limited to reconstructing 2D slices of internal structures. While informative, 2D images do not fully capture the complete size, shape, and spatial position of internal features within a soft object, potentially limiting diagnostic richness.
 - **Objective**: To adapt the tactile imaging framework to reconstruct full 3D volumes of internal structures. This aims to provide a richer and more precise visualization of objects, and also to investigate if the additional geometric information from 3D training could enhance the accuracy of 2D image slices derived from the 3D volume.

4. **Provide Localized Uncertainty Estimates**:
 - **Motivation**: Hand-held diagnostic devices, such as ultrasound probes, can be operator-dependent, leading to variability in results. Providing feedback to the operator could help mitigate this issue.
 - **Objective**: To leverage the local nature of the proposed representation to generate localized uncertainty estimates for the reconstructed image. This feedback could guide the operator to collect additional tactile measurements in regions of high uncertainty, potentially improving the overall accuracy and reliability of the imaging process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development of the Local Encoder for Spatial Sensing (LESS) architecture, accompanied by advancements in data collection and training strategies to facilitate compositional generalization and hand-held operation.

**A. LESS Architecture – Local Encoder for Spatial Sensing**

The LESS architecture is designed to exploit the local nature of touch by representing a tactile scene not as a single global vector, but as a collection of localized representations. It consists of two main modules: a tactile sequence encoder-decoder and an image reconstruction module.

1. **LESS Sequence Encoder**:
 - **Localized Representations**: The core idea is a grid of *N* "particles," where each particle ¯x_i has a spatial receptive field X_i. Each local representation ¯z_¯x_i_t is generated by a recurrent neural network that processes only the tactile measurements {x_j, f_j} falling within its specific receptive field.
 - **Receptive Field and Centering**: For each ¯x_i, inputs outside its receptive field (defined by a Euclidean distance D(x, ¯x_i) < β) are filtered out. The pose inputs x_j are centered around ¯x_i by subtraction.
 - **Force-Location Encoder (FLE)**: Each centered pose x_j is encoded using sinusoidal positional encoding (PE), and the force f_j is processed by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The outputs are summed.
 - **Recurrent Neural Network**: The sequence of FLE-encoded outputs is fed into a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), and the GRU's hidden state serves as the local representation ¯z_¯x_i_t. All *N* GRUs share weights, assuming similar patterns in localized sensory readings. The ¯x_i positions are arranged on a uniform 2D grid.
 - **Force Decoder**: To predict a force ˆf(x, ¯z_¯x_i) at a given position x, the position is centered around ¯x_i, processed by PE, then combined with an MLP output of ¯z_¯x_i, and finally fed into another MLP to predict the force.

2. **LESS Image Reconstruction**:
 - **Local Image Patches**: Each local representation ¯z_¯x_i is mapped to a local image patch (N_p × N_p pixels, with κ possible class values) using a transposed convolution neural network. The transposed convolutions for all patches share weights.
 - **Image Composition**: All individual patch logits p(¯x) are summed, and a softmax function is applied to obtain class probabilities for every pixel in the complete image.
 - **3D Extension**: For 3D reconstruction, each patch is extended to a square column of N_p × N_p × N_z voxels.

**B. SSL Training and Data Collection**

1. **Self-Supervised Loss**: The training of the LESS encoder-decoder is driven by a force prediction task. The loss function is an average of the Mean-Squared Error (MSE) in predicting forces for individual particles. It uses subsampling of time steps to manage memory complexity, similar to, but adapted for localized predictions.

2. **Phantom Fabrication**: A modular fabrication technique was developed using 3D-printed molds for soft silicone and hydrogel phantoms. This enabled precise control over skin thickness and the creation of complex inclusion shapes. The phantoms consist of an outer shell and an insert with an inclusion that can be rotated to 8 different angles. The dataset includes 40 inserts (with inclusions varying in size and shape) and 8 shells.

3. **Tactile Sensor**: An off-the-shelf Xela uSCu ALHA sensor, capturing data at 100Hz, was used.

4. **Dataset Generation**:
 - **`data-v1`**: The previously published dataset featuring vertical poke motions with fixed sensor orientation.
 - **`data-poke`**: A new dataset collected using poke trajectories, but with *various sensor orientations*.
 - **`data-primitive`**: A new dataset generated by recording human-like motion primitives via teleoperation and then replaying them robotically using position control.
 - **`data-handheld`**: A smaller test dataset collected via manual human palpation, with poses recorded using a fiducial-based tracking system.
 - **Automation**: An automated data collection system featuring a motorized scissor lift and a Franka Panda manipulator was designed to reorient inserts and enable continuous data acquisition. This resulted in approximately 800 hours of tactile data, significantly larger than `data-v1`.

**C. Tactile Imaging Training and Data**

1. **Ground Truth**: Unlike previous work that used 2D MRI slices, this research utilizes full 3D MRI volumes of the phantom inserts as ground truth. An MRI processing pipeline was developed to classify each voxel into four categories: background, insert, pillar, or inclusion.

2. **Loss Function**: To address severe class imbalance in 3D (where inclusion voxels are a small proportion), a combined loss function of Focal loss and Dice loss was employed. A dynamic scaling factor was introduced to balance the magnitudes of these two loss components during optimization.

**D. Hand-Held Tactile Imaging**

1. **Pose Estimation**: For hand-held operation, an external fiducial-based pose tracking system was implemented using two AprilTags (on the sensor and as a global reference) and a calibrated Intel RealSense 415 camera. The system's accuracy (positional error ≤0.45mm, angular error ≤0.012 radians) was designed to be within the stability thresholds of the LESS model.

2. **Generalization to Different Object Dimensions**: The system allows for imaging objects of different sizes than those used in training. The set of particles in the image decoder can be extended to cover a larger volume at test time, leveraging the shared weights among particles.

3. **Uncertainty Estimation Guidance**: Shannon entropy of the class probabilities for each pixel (or voxel) is used to quantify uncertainty. This local uncertainty measure is intended to guide the operator to collect additional samples in ambiguous regions, potentially improving diagnostic quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments focused on evaluating 3D tactile imaging, compositional generalization, localized uncertainty estimation, and the feasibility of a hand-held system.

**A. 3D Tactile Imaging**
- **Improved 2D Metrics with 3D Training**: Training models with 3D MRI labels consistently improved performance on 2D metrics (F1 score, Center-of-Mass (CoM) error, diameter error) compared to models trained solely on 2D labels (e.g., 2D F1 Score of 85.2% for 3D trained model vs. 79.9% for 2D trained model). This suggests that the additional geometric prior captured by 3D representations enhances spatial regularity and leads to more accurate reconstructions, even when evaluating 2D slices.
- **Richer Visualization**: 3D reconstruction provides a more complete visualization of internal structures, including elements like supporting pillars that are not directly touched by the sensor, demonstrating learned inference capabilities.

**B. Zero-Shot Compositional Generalization with LESS**
- **Performance on In-Distribution Data**: On phantoms with single inclusions (in-distribution), LESS achieved a slightly lower F1 score (83.5%) compared to the global baseline (GLOBAL, 85.2%), and comparable CoM and diameter errors. This was attributed to the localized nature of information processing in LESS.
- **Superior Generalization on Out-of-Distribution Data**:
 - **Multiple Inclusions**: On phantoms with multiple inclusions (out-of-distribution), LESS significantly outperformed GLOBAL. GLOBAL often failed to reconstruct more than one inclusion, resulting in a low F1 score (44.6%) and high area error (78.4 mm²). In contrast, LESS accurately identified and imaged multiple inclusions, achieving an F1 score of 71.1% and a much lower area error (46.1 mm²).
 - **Large Phantoms**: For a `large` phantom with 4x the base area (completely out-of-distribution for GLOBAL), GLOBAL exhibited extremely poor performance (F1 score 1.1%, high CoM and diameter errors). LESS, due to its centering process and local representations, generalized effectively to the larger spatial extent, achieving a high F1 score of 71.3% and substantially lower CoM and diameter errors. These results demonstrate LESS's ability for zero-shot compositional generalization.

**C. Receptive Field Tuning**
- **Trade-off in Receptive Field Size (β)**: Increasing the receptive field size (β) generally improved accuracy on single-inclusion phantoms by allowing more information per particle. However, beyond an optimal value (approximately 10mm), further increases in β led to degraded compositional generalization on the multiple-inclusion set, indicating a reduction in local invariance. A β of approximately 10mm was identified as optimal for balancing these factors.

**D. Local Uncertainty Estimation**
- **Informative Uncertainty Maps**: LESS produced more informative and localized uncertainty estimates compared to GLOBAL. LESS showed low confidence in unobserved areas, and confidence increased as more tactile data was collected in the vicinity. GLOBAL, in contrast, tended to exhibit high confidence even in untouched areas early in the process, suggesting it struggled to localize uncertainty effectively.

**E. Hand-Held Tactile Imaging**
- **Robustness to Pose Noise**: LESS demonstrated robustness to simulated i.i.d. Gaussian noise in sensor pose, maintaining stability for errors up to approximately 1mm in position and 0.1 radian in orientation. The custom fiducial-based pose estimation system achieved real-world accuracy within these thresholds (positional error ≤0.45mm, angular error ≤0.012 radians), resulting in negligible performance degradation (F1 score of 67.3% with estimated pose vs. 67.6% with true pose).
- **Impact of Diverse Training Data**: Training LESS on a combination of `data-poke` and `data-primitive` (human-like motion primitives) significantly improved performance when tested on `data-primitive` compared to training only on `data-poke` (F1 score of 80.9% vs. 45.8%). This indicates that diverse training motions reduce the distribution shift when moving towards human-like palpation.
- **Performance on Hand-Held Data**: When tested on `data-handheld` (manual human motion), training with `data-primitive` improved performance (F1 score of 41.3% vs. 34.7% without). While robot-held sensing still offered superior performance, the results suggest a positive trend, indicating potential for further improvement with more diverse robotic training data.
- **Real-Time System**: A proof-of-concept hand-held tactile imaging system was demonstrated, capable of real-time prediction (approximately 4Hz) and visualization of internal structures and uncertainty maps, showing its operational feasibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces several advancements that carry significant implications for the field of tactile imaging and broader robotics:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Data Efficiency**: The core contribution of LESS is its object-centric, local representation. This compositional design significantly improves the model's ability to generalize to complex, previously unseen object configurations (e.g., multiple inclusions, varying sizes) from training data consisting of simpler objects. This generalization capability can substantially reduce the need for extensive, exhaustive data collection covering all possible object variations, thereby decreasing the cost and complexity of developing tactile imaging systems.

2. **Feasibility of Hand-Held Tactile Imaging**: By integrating external pose tracking and leveraging palpation data that mimics human motion, the work demonstrates a proof-of-concept for a real-time, hand-held tactile imaging system. This development is crucial for transitioning tactile imaging from controlled robotic laboratory settings to practical applications, particularly in clinical environments. A hand-held device would enable physicians to maintain direct patient contact and maneuver the sensor akin to ultrasound, potentially improving diagnostic workflows and patient comfort.

3. **Richer Diagnostics through 3D Reconstruction**: Extending tactile imaging from 2D slices to full 3D volumes provides a more comprehensive and precise visualization of internal structures. This richer information on the size, shape, and spatial relationships of anomalies could lead to more accurate diagnoses and treatment planning, for instance, by better informing the decision for multiple biopsies in breast examination.

4. **Mitigation of Operator Dependence with Localized Uncertainty**: The ability of LESS to provide localized uncertainty estimates offers a mechanism to guide operators to regions requiring more data. This feedback loop could reduce the operator-dependence often observed in manual diagnostic modalities like ultrasound, thereby improving the consistency and reliability of tactile imaging results, which is valuable for both clinical and home-use devices.

5. **Broadening Applications of Learning-Based Tactile Sensing**: Beyond medical diagnostics, the proposed neural architecture and data collection techniques may be applicable to other robotic domains that involve interpreting complex spatio-temporal tactile signals. This includes tasks such as learning object affordances from visual-tactile data or enabling more nuanced manipulation of soft objects.

While the clinical relevance of a method trained on synthetic data remains a question for future evaluation, the technical contributions, particularly the hand-held system and the publicly available diverse datasets, provide a foundation for subsequent human-subject studies. This research represents a step toward robust, generalizable, and practical learning-based tactile imaging for soft objects, with potential to impact healthcare and advanced robotics.
