# Accelerating and Scaling MPC-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Locomotion and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for Model Predictive Control (MPC)-guided Reinforcement Learning (RL) introduces πnMPC, an efficient batched GPU MPC solver, and a centroidal-dynamics MPC reward, enabling practical and scalable training for humanoid locomotion and forceful loco-manipulation. Policies learned with this method were deployed on a humanoid robot, demonstrating robust locomotion and the ability to push objects weighing up to 290 kg in hardware.

## Problem

- Integrating long-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) guidance into massively parallel Reinforcement Learning (RL) for humanoids is computationally expensive due to repeated batched MPC solves.
- Existing MPC solvers struggle with efficient parallelization across the prediction horizon, often bottlenecked by horizon-wise recursions and high memory demands.
- Prior MPC-RL hybrid research predominantly addresses locomotion, with complex loco-manipulation tasks remaining an underexplored challenge for these combined approaches.

## Method

- Developed πnMPC, a parallel-in-horizon, construction-free batched GPU ==MPC== solver, to reduce computational overhead for training-time MPC guidance.
- Formulated a centroidal-dynamics MPC (CD-MPC) reward with "prediction-confidence weighting" to provide long-horizon, dynamically consistent guidance to RL policies.
- Extended the MPC-guided RL architecture to humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, such as forceful object pushing, leveraging interactive models.

## Results

- The πnMPC solver demonstrated superior computational efficiency and scalability on GPU compared to state-of-the-art batched QP solvers, avoiding high memory usage and compilation overhead.
- MPC-RL policies consistently improved translational velocity tracking, push-recovery, and contact constraint satisfaction in humanoid locomotion compared to Pure RL baselines.
- Policies successfully deployed on a humanoid robot for both locomotion (robust to disturbances and payloads up to 10 kg) and loco-manipulation, pushing a 290 kg cart (**829%** of body mass) in real-world hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient, parallel-in-horizon MPC solvers like πnMPC are essential to make long-horizon MPC guidance practical for large-scale RL training.
> - Structured MPC-derived rewards, particularly with prediction-confidence weighting and landmark-based extraction, provide superior long-horizon, contact-aware guidance compared to simpler reward designs.
> - Optimizing MPC prediction horizon and update rate is crucial for balancing performance gains and training efficiency, with intermediate values often yielding the best results.
