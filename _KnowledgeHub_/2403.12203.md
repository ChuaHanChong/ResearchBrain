# Bootstrapping Reinforcement Learning with Imitation for Vision-Based Agile Flight

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Zurich developed a three-phase learning framework that enables a quadrotor to perform agile flight and racing using only visual inputs (gate corners or RGB images), bypassing explicit state estimation. This approach achieves higher success rates and faster lap times in simulation and real-world compared to imitation learning baselines, demonstrating robustness to disturbances and reaching near champion-level performance.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policy learning for agile quadrotors faces challenges due to inefficient exploration with high-dimensional visual inputs and the platform's inherent instability.
- Current autonomous drone racing policies depend on explicit state estimates, preventing purely vision-based, end-to-end control from raw visual data.
- While Reinforcement Learning (RL) is sample-inefficient for vision-based tasks, Imitation Learning (IL) suffers from covariate shift and cannot exceed the performance of expert demonstrations.

## Method

- A three-phase learning framework integrates state-based teacher policy training, imitation learning (IL) with visual inputs, and performance-adaptive online ==reinforcement learning== (RL) fine-tuning.
- A student policy learns from a privileged state-based teacher using DAgger, processing either raw RGB images or detected gate corner coordinates as visual input.
- Online RL fine-tuning incorporates a performance-constrained adaptive mechanism for learning rates and PPO clip range, along with an asymmetric critic for enhanced efficiency.

## Results

- The method consistently outperformed DAgger and pure RL baselines, achieving higher success rates, lower gate-passing errors, and faster lap times across various simulated tracks.
- Pure RL (from scratch) consistently failed with **0%** success rates, while the adaptive RL fine-tuning prevented catastrophic forgetting, maintaining and improving policy performance.
- Real-world validation showed faster lap times and reduced gate errors compared to DAgger, with an optimal training balance of **60%** IL pre-training and **40%** RL fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An integrated three-phase approach, leveraging IL for initialization and RL for performance-adaptive fine-tuning, overcomes the sample inefficiency of pure vision-based RL.
> - Adaptive learning rates and PPO clip range, adjusted based on real-time policy performance, effectively prevent catastrophic forgetting during online RL fine-tuning.
> - Using an asymmetric actor-critic, where the critic accesses privileged state information while the actor operates solely on visual input, enhances learning efficiency and robustness in partially observable environments.
