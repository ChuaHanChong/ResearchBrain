# Bootstrapping Reinforcement Learning with Imitation for Vision-Based Agile Flight

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Zurich developed a three-phase learning framework that enables a quadrotor to perform agile flight and racing using only visual inputs (gate corners or RGB images), bypassing explicit state estimation. This approach achieves higher success rates and faster lap times in simulation and real-world compared to imitation learning baselines, demonstrating robustness to disturbances and reaching near champion-level performance.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policy learning for agile quadrotors faces challenges due to inefficient exploration with high-dimensional visual inputs and the platform's inherent instability.
- Current autonomous drone racing policies depend on explicit state estimates, preventing purely vision-based, end-to-end control from raw visual data.
- While Reinforcement Learning (RL) is sample-inefficient for vision-based tasks, Imitation Learning (IL) suffers from covariate shift and cannot exceed the performance of expert demonstrations.

## Method

- A three-phase learning framework integrates state-based teacher policy training, imitation learning (IL) with visual inputs, and performance-adaptive online ==reinforcement learning== (RL) fine-tuning.
- A student policy learns from a privileged state-based teacher using DAgger, processing either raw RGB images or detected gate corner coordinates as visual input.
- Online RL fine-tuning incorporates a performance-constrained adaptive mechanism for learning rates and PPO clip range, along with an asymmetric critic for enhanced efficiency.

## Results

- The method consistently outperformed DAgger and pure RL baselines, achieving higher success rates, lower gate-passing errors, and faster lap times across various simulated tracks.
- Pure RL (from scratch) consistently failed with **0%** success rates, while the adaptive RL fine-tuning prevented catastrophic forgetting, maintaining and improving policy performance.
- Real-world validation showed faster lap times and reduced gate errors compared to DAgger, with an optimal training balance of **60%** IL pre-training and **40%** RL fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An integrated three-phase approach, leveraging IL for initialization and RL for performance-adaptive fine-tuning, overcomes the sample inefficiency of pure vision-based RL.
> - Adaptive learning rates and PPO clip range, adjusted based on real-time policy performance, effectively prevent catastrophic forgetting during online RL fine-tuning.
> - Using an asymmetric actor-critic, where the critic accesses privileged state information while the actor operates solely on visual input, enhances learning efficiency and robustness in partially observable environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visuomotor policy learning, which involves directly mapping visual information to robotic actions, has enabled complex behaviors across various robotic systems, including visual navigation, dexterous manipulation, and agile maneuvers. However, learning directly from high-dimensional visual data introduces significant challenges, primarily due to inefficient policy exploration during the learning process.

In the context of agile quadrotor flight, these challenges are exacerbated by the platform's inherent instability, high agility requirements, and reliance on precise, low-latency control commands (e.g., collective thrust and body rates). While previous work has demonstrated high-speed quadrotor flight using visual inputs for acrobatics and obstacle avoidance, these systems typically rely on high-frequency proprioceptive data from Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) for accurate state estimation. A notable gap exists in training and deploying autonomous drones directly using only RGB images, particularly in scenarios such as First-Person-View (FPV) drone racing, where human pilots achieve high-performance flight using solely monocular camera feeds.

Two primary machine learning paradigms address these challenges: Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL). RL offers a framework for learning high-performance controllers through trial and error, a capability difficult to achieve with classical methods. However, RL is known for its sample inefficiency and substantial computational demands, especially with high-dimensional visual inputs, making efficient exploration a critical challenge. Conversely, IL efficiently learns policies from expert demonstrations in a supervised manner, requiring fewer samples and exhibiting applicability in real-world robotic systems. Despite its sample efficiency, IL faces limitations such as covariate shift, where discrepancies between training data and real-world observations degrade performance. Additionally, IL policies cannot surpass the performance level of the expert demonstrations they are trained on, and they may struggle with out-of-distribution observations.

Previous state-of-the-art autonomous drone racing policies, which have outperformed human world champions, still depend on explicit state estimates derived from images and inertial sensors. The current research focuses on developing visuomotor policies that map visual information directly to control commands without relying on such explicit state estimation, aiming to achieve human-like navigation. This ambition has been hindered in drone racing by the fundamental challenge of sample inefficiency in vision-based learning. The paper aims to bridge this gap by integrating the complementary advantages of IL and RL. While many contemporary visuomotor policy learning approaches utilize a teacher-student IL framework, this work extends it by incorporating adaptive RL fine-tuning to enhance the performance and robustness of the learned policy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and high-performing visuomotor policy for agile quadrotor flight, specifically autonomous drone racing, that operates solely on visual inputs. The work aims to achieve this by overcoming the individual limitations of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL).

The key motivations and more detailed objectives include:

- **Addressing Sample Inefficiency in Vision-Based RL:** High-dimensional visual inputs lead to inefficient policy exploration in RL from scratch. The authors aim to leverage IL to provide a robust initial policy, thereby bootstrapping the RL process and improving sample efficiency, enabling learning in complex vision-based environments where pure RL might fail.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Imitation Learning:** While IL is sample-efficient, its performance is inherently capped by the expert's performance, and it suffers from covariate shift, leading to degradation when encountering out-of-distribution observations. The research seeks to overcome these issues by using RL fine-tuning, allowing the policy to adapt and potentially surpass the expert's performance while improving generalization.
- **Enabling Purely Vision-Based Agile Flight:** A central goal is to train a quadrotor to navigate through a race course using only visual information (either raw RGB images or detected gate corners), without relying on metric state estimation (position, velocity, orientation) or IMU data. This aligns with how human FPV pilots operate and represents a significant step towards more autonomous and human-like robotic navigation.
- **Developing a Robust and High-Performance Framework:** The authors aim to create a learning framework that combines the strengths of both RL (for performance and robustness through trial-and-error) and IL (for sample efficiency and initialization). This involves a phased approach to policy training that progressively builds capabilities.
- **Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting in RL Fine-tuning:** A common challenge when fine-tuning pre-trained policies with RL is catastrophic forgetting, where new learning erases previously acquired skills. A specific objective is to develop an adaptive fine-tuning mechanism that conditions exploration and network updates on the policy's performance, thereby preserving learned behaviors while allowing for continuous improvement.
- **Generalizability of the Approach:** While validated in drone racing, a motivation is to develop an approach that is not overly task-specific, suggesting potential applicability to other robotic platforms and visuomotor control tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL) within a three-phase policy learning framework designed for vision-based agile quadrotor flight. The task is formulated as minimizing the time to navigate a predefined sequence of gates using only egocentric visual input to output collective thrust and body rate commands.

**Phase I: State-based Teacher Policy Training**
The initial phase involves training a teacher policy, denoted as $\pi_{teacher}$, using privileged state information. The state observation $s$ comprises the drone's 3D position, a 6-dimensional representation of its rotation, linear and angular velocities, and the relative position of the next gate center.
- **Method:** A model-free reinforcement learning approach, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), is employed.
- **Architecture:** The policy network is a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
- **Reward Function:** The reward $r_t$ is a composite sum of several components:
 - $r_{prog}$: Encourages progress towards the next gate.
 - $r_{perc}$: Promotes perception awareness by orienting the camera towards the next gate's center.
 - $r_{act}$: Penalizes large changes in actions to encourage smoothness.
 - $r_{br}$: Penalizes high body rates to reduce motion blur.
 - $r_{pass}$: A binary reward for successfully passing a gate.
 - $r_{crash}$: A binary penalty for collisions, terminating the episode.
The hyperparameters for these reward components are determined through iterative tuning for optimal state-based policy performance.

**Phase II: Imitation Learning using Visual Input**
The objective of this phase is to distill the knowledge from the state-based $\pi_{teacher}$ into a student policy, $\pi_{student}$, which operates solely on visual input.
- **Input:** The vision-based student policy receives a sequence of perceptual observations $[o_{t-H+1}, \dots, o_t]$, where $H$ is the history length. Two types of visual inputs are evaluated:
 - **Implicitly learned representations:** Embeddings of RGB images generated by a pre-trained ResNet50 network.
 - **Task-specific representations:** Normalized pixel coordinates of detected gate corners, with domain randomization (noise, missing probability) to simulate real-world conditions.
- **Architecture:** A Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) encodes the series of visual embeddings or corner coordinates. The output features from the TCN are then fed into a two-layer MLP to generate actions.
- **Loss Function:** The supervision loss, $L_A$, is the Mean Square Error between the actions output by the student policy and the teacher policy: $L_A(D, \theta_{student}) = E_D[\|\pi_{student}([o_{t-N+1}, \dots, o_t]; \theta_{student}) - \pi_{teacher}(s_t)\|]$.
- **Training Method:** DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) is utilized, which iteratively trains the student policy by executing it and collecting new demonstrations, gradually increasing the student's probability of execution. This on-policy training helps generate a broader distribution of demonstrations compared to Behavior Cloning (BC), leading to improved performance.

**Phase III: Performance-Adaptive Online Fine-Tuning**
This phase aims to enhance the performance and robustness of the pre-trained student policy through online RL fine-tuning. A primary challenge in this stage is "catastrophic forgetting," where exploration generates out-of-distribution data, leading to the unlearning of previously acquired skills.
- **Initialization:** The pre-trained student policy from Phase II serves as the initial actor for RL.
- **Warm-up Period:** The critic function is exclusively trained for a fixed number of iterations to adapt to the pre-trained actor before joint actor-critic updates.
- **Adaptive Learning Rates and Clip Range (Algorithm 1):** The algorithm dynamically adjusts the learning rates for both the policy (actor) and the value function (critic), as well as the PPO clip range, based on the agent's performance (rollout reward).
 - If policy improvement is small, the critic's update rate increases (by decreasing its learning rate).
 - If high-reward action sequences are achieved, the policy's update rate and clip range increase.
 - This adaptive mechanism eliminates the need for heuristic tuning of learning rates to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and links learning progression directly to performance.
- **Asymmetric Critic:** An asymmetric actor-critic setup is employed. While the actor takes only visual inputs (partially observable setting), the critic's inputs are augmented with privileged state information ($s_t$). This allows the critic to learn a more precise value function, which, in turn, enhances the efficiency and performance of actor training.

**Training Configurations and Evaluation Metrics:**
The approach is validated on three simulated race tracks: "SplitS," "Figure 8," and "Kidney," varying in complexity. Performance is evaluated using Success Rate (SR), Mean-Gate-passing-Error (MGE), and Lap Time (LT). A realistic simulation based on the BEM model for aerodynamic effects is used. Policies are tested over 100 trials, starting from varied initial positions, and each method is repeated 5 times with different random seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and real-world scenarios indicate that the proposed three-phase approach effectively addresses the challenges of vision-based agile quadrotor flight.

**Performance Comparison to Baseline Approaches:**
- The approach was benchmarked against Behavior Cloning (BC), Reinforcement Learning (RL) from scratch, DAgger, and PIRLNav (a BC policy fine-tuned with non-adaptive RL) using a fixed sample budget of 10 million data samples.
- Across all three racing tracks ("Figure 8", "SplitS", "Kidney") and both visual input types (RGB pixel embeddings and gate corner coordinates), the proposed method consistently exhibited superior performance. It achieved higher success rates (SR), lower mean-gate-passing-errors (MGE), and faster lap times (LT) compared to all baselines (Table 1).
- RL from scratch, using either pixel or corner inputs, consistently failed to achieve any success (0% success rate) on all tracks, underscoring the difficulty of exploration in high-dimensional visual environments without effective bootstrapping.
- DAgger performed moderately, but our approach achieved a significant improvement in SR (e.g., 63% to 85% for pixel input on Figure 8 track) and reduced LT (e.g., 5.19s to 4.57s). The asymmetric critic in our RL fine-tuning phase, which uses privileged state information, contributed to this enhanced understanding and performance.

**Training Effectiveness with Different RL Configurations:**
- A comparative analysis of training performance showed that RL from scratch (even with an asymmetric critic) with RGB images resulted in a 0% success rate.
- A "Vanilla fine-tuning" baseline, initializing with a DAgger policy but without the adaptive RL components, initially showed learning improvement but subsequently deteriorated over time. This highlights the importance of the adaptive mechanism in Phase III for maintaining and improving policy performance.
- The proposed adaptive RL fine-tuning was the only configuration able to learn and sustain high-performance for the task (Figure 5, left).

**Sample Efficiency Analysis:**
- By varying the proportion of IL pre-training within a fixed total sample budget (10M samples), the research demonstrated that an optimal balance exists. A ratio of approximately 60% IL pre-training followed by 40% RL fine-tuning yielded the peak deployment reward (Figure 5, right), indicating that appropriate bootstrapping significantly enhances performance beyond what pure IL can achieve.

**Robustness to Unknown Disturbances:**
- To evaluate robustness, policies were tested under simulated real-world uncertainties: random frame blackouts (perceptual disturbance) and random positional jumps (positional disturbance).
- The proposed approach demonstrated superior robustness compared to the DAgger baseline, maintaining higher success rates and lower gate errors under both 1% and 5% disturbance probabilities (Table 2). This suggests that the adaptive RL fine-tuning contributes to a more generalized and resilient policy.

**Towards Champion-Level Performance:**
- The paper investigated whether the end-to-end vision-based policy could approach the "super-human" performance previously demonstrated by state-based RL policies (which outperformed human world champions).
- On the SplitS track, using corner observations and increased thrust limits, the policy achieved an average lap time of 5.82 seconds (best 5.54 seconds). This performance is comparable to state-based policies achieving champion-level times (e.g., within 0.05s of the policy in), indicating that the proposed method enables end-to-end policies to reach extreme performance levels.

**Real-world Experiments:**
- Real-world validation was conducted using Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) simulations with a VICON motion capture system for perceptual inputs.
- Compared to a DAgger policy trained with an equivalent number of samples, the proposed approach consistently achieved faster lap times and smaller gate errors in the real-world setting for both corner and image inputs (Table 3).
- Trajectory analysis (Figure 1, Figure 6) visually confirmed that the RL-fine-tuned policy executed tighter turns and longer straights, indicative of optimized speed and enhanced flight maneuvers beyond what imitation learning alone could discover.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of visuomotor control for agile robotics, particularly in the context of autonomous drone racing.

**Core Contributions and Novelty:**
- The work demonstrates, for the first time, a visuomotor policy capable of navigating a quadrotor through a sequence of gates using solely gate corners or raw RGB images, without relying on explicit metric state estimation (position, velocity, orientation) or IMU data. This represents a critical step towards more autonomous systems that perceive and act similarly to human pilots.
- The proposed three-phase framework effectively combines the strengths of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL), addressing their individual limitations. It leverages IL for sample-efficient policy initialization and RL for performance enhancement and robustness through adaptive fine-tuning.

**Technical Advancements:**
- The introduction of a performance-constrained adaptive RL fine-tuning mechanism is a key technical advancement. This mechanism dynamically adjusts learning rates and the PPO clip range based on the policy's real-time performance, which effectively mitigates the problem of catastrophic forgetting during online exploration. This allows the policy to continuously improve without losing previously learned skills.
- The utilization of an asymmetric actor-critic setup, where the critic has access to privileged state information while the actor only uses visual input, significantly improves learning efficiency and robustness in partially observable environments.

**Demonstrated Performance and Robustness:**
- The empirical results show that the approach consistently outperforms existing IL methods (e.g., DAgger) in terms of success rate, gate-passing accuracy, and lap times in both simulated and real-world environments.
- Crucially, the method enables learning in scenarios where RL from scratch fails, highlighting the effectiveness of the bootstrapping strategy.
- The demonstrated robustness to unknown disturbances (perceptual blackouts and positional jumps) indicates that the learned policies are more resilient and generalizable than baseline IL approaches.
- The achieved lap times, which approach the performance of state-based policies that have outperformed human world champions, underscore the potential for purely vision-based systems to reach high levels of agile flight performance.

**Broader Impact and Generalizability:**
- The authors state that the approach does not rely on task-specific adaptations for drone racing, suggesting its potential applicability to a wider range of robotic platforms and visuomotor control tasks. This could include other forms of agile navigation, manipulation, or interaction in complex, dynamic environments where only visual information is available.
- By enabling end-to-end vision-to-control for high-speed, agile robots, this research contributes to the development of autonomous systems that are more akin to biological agents, capable of interpreting complex visual scenes and translating them directly into precise motor commands. This could reduce system complexity by minimizing the need for explicit state estimation pipelines, leading to more robust and deployable autonomous robots in various domains.
- Future work, as noted in the limitations, involves improving the perception module for "in-the-wild" scenarios and integrating customized vision encoders, which could further broaden the impact of this framework by making it applicable to less controlled, real-world conditions.
