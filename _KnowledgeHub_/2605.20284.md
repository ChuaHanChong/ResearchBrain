# JUDO: A Juxtaposed Domain-Oriented Multimodal Reasoner for Industrial Anomaly QA

> [!summary] Summary
> The JUDO framework introduces a three-stage learning approach to systematically internalize industrial domain knowledge into Large Multimodal Models for anomaly understanding. This framework achieved an overall accuracy of 81.20% on the MMAD benchmark, outperforming existing general-purpose LMMs and GRPO-based baselines, particularly excelling in defect-related subtasks.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often lack specialized domain knowledge for industrial anomaly detection, hindering accurate reasoning in complex manufacturing scenarios.
- Existing LMMs and GRPO-based models primarily optimize instruction-response matching, neglecting explicit integration of domain knowledge essential for generalizable defect reasoning.
- Providing external knowledge or normal samples as context during inference proves insufficient without deep internalization, potentially leading to plausible but inaccurate responses from LMMs.

## Method

- **JUDO** employs a three-stage ==progressive learning== framework that systematically internalizes industrial domain knowledge and context into an LMM, based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- The first stage focuses on learning anomaly segmentation-based juxtaposed reasoning, enabling fine-grained visual comparison between defective and normal images.
- The second stage injects domain-specific knowledge through supervised fine-tuning on a constructed industrial domain Question-Answering dataset, grounded with visual context.
- The third stage uses Domain-Oriented Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with multi-faceted reward functions to unify visual grounding and domain semantics for aligned reasoning.

## Results

- JUDO achieved an overall averaged accuracy of **81.20%** on the MMAD benchmark, surpassing leading LMMs like Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** (**72.56%**), GPT-4o (**74.92%**), Gemini-2.5-pro (**81.19%**), and AnomalyR1-**7B** (**77.29%**).
- The framework demonstrated particular strength in defect-related subtasks, achieving **73.01%** for localization, **84.56%** for description, and **89.41%** for analysis, indicating effective domain knowledge integration.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each progressive stage of JUDO, including domain knowledge injection and juxtaposed segmentation, incrementally contributes to performance improvement, validating the framework's design.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically internalizing domain-specific knowledge directly into LMM parameters, rather than relying on external retrieval, significantly enhances performance in specialized industrial anomaly tasks.
> - Explicitly training LMMs with juxtaposed anomalous and normal images for segmentation enables fine-grained visual comparative reasoning crucial for identifying and localizing defects.
> - A multi-faceted reinforcement learning approach with specific rewards for domain reasoning, segmentation, and structural alignment effectively guides LMMs to generate accurate, domain-oriented, and explainable insights.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual anomaly detection within industrial environments has undergone significant development, evolving from traditional unsupervised methods to advanced applications of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). Initially, approaches like reconstruction-based autoencoders or Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), and embedding-based methods, focused on identifying deviations from normal data patterns to detect and localize anomalies. While effective for these tasks on benchmarks such as MVTec AD, these earlier methods typically did not provide extensive defect analysis.

The emergence of LMMs has transformed this landscape by enabling more comprehensive defect analysis. LMMs can now respond to diverse analytical queries regarding defect characteristics, including identifying defect types, describing their visual appearance, and analyzing potential consequences. The MMAD benchmark was introduced to facilitate the evaluation of LMMs' reasoning capabilities in industrial anomaly problems. In this context, models such as AnomalyGPT leveraged LMMs for zero-shot anomaly detection and report generation. More recently, reinforcement learning techniques, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have been incorporated to refine reasoning quality, as seen in AnomalyR1 and OmniAD. AnomalyR1 was among the first to apply GRPO to anomaly detection, while OmniAD further integrated anomaly segmentation for visually grounded reasoning.

Despite these advancements, a significant limitation persists: current LMMs, including those utilizing GRPO, often lack the specialized domain knowledge essential for robust and generalizable defect reasoning in complex industrial scenarios. Their pretraining is typically optimized for general question-answering tasks and common knowledge, resulting in noticeable performance degradation in specialized application domains where domain-specific expertise is critical. Although external knowledge or normal samples can be provided as context during inference, this approach is limited when LMMs do not possess sufficient internalized domain knowledge, potentially leading to plausible but inaccurate responses. This gap—the insufficient internalization of domain knowledge and contextual understanding—is the central challenge JUDO aims to address, distinguishing itself from prior models that primarily focus on optimizing instruction-response matching without explicit domain alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named JUDO (Juxtaposed Domain-Oriented Multimodal Reasoner), that systematically internalizes domain-specific knowledge and context into both the visual and textual reasoning processes of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for industrial anomaly understanding. This endeavor is motivated by several identified limitations and challenges within the current state of LMM applications in industrial anomaly detection.

**Motivation and Problem Statement:**
1. **LMMs' Deficiency in Domain-Specific Knowledge:** While LMMs have significantly advanced general multimodal reasoning, they inherently lack specialized knowledge pertaining to industrial anomaly detection. This deficiency hinders their ability to generate accurate and reliable responses in complex industrial scenarios, where domain-specific characteristics of normal and defective samples (definitions, causes, consequences) are crucial for reliable analysis.
2. **Limitations of Existing GRPO-Based Models:** Current GRPO-based models, such as AnomalyR1 and OmniAD, primarily focus on optimizing instruction-response matching. This approach often overlooks the critical role of integrating domain knowledge, which is essential for generalizable defect reasoning that goes beyond mere pattern recognition or direct response generation.
3. **Ineffectiveness of External Context Provision:** Prior studies have explored providing external knowledge or normal samples as context during inference (e.g., through Retrieval-Augmented Generation, RAG). However, this method has proven insufficient. Without internalizing this knowledge, LMMs may become overly dependent on external context, potentially leading to responses that are plausible within the context but inaccurate regarding the actual anomaly, due to a lack of deep understanding.
4. **Underutilization of Normal Samples:** Normal images are often only used as optional context during inference. Their potential for enabling explicit comparative reasoning during training to establish a fundamental notion of normality as a baseline for anomaly understanding remains largely underexplored.

**Key Objectives of JUDO:**
1. **Systematic Internalization of Domain Knowledge:** To introduce a novel approach that systematically injects and internalizes industrial domain knowledge and context directly into the model's parameters, rather than relying solely on external retrieval during inference.
2. **Fine-Grained Visual Comparative Inspection:** To enable the model to perform precise visual comparative inspection by juxtaposing query images with corresponding normal images. This aims to facilitate fine-grained defect segmentation and visually grounded reasoning.
3. **Enhanced Context Understanding:** To improve the LMM's understanding of industrial contexts by integrating domain knowledge through supervised fine-tuning, thereby building a foundational understanding for anomaly reasoning.
4. **Domain-Oriented Reasoning Guidance:** To guide the model's textual reasoning process to be domain-oriented through reinforcement learning, utilizing tailored reward functions that promote accuracy in domain-specific insights, segmentation, and structural coherence.
5. **Improved Performance, Reliability, and Explainability:** To achieve superior performance on industrial anomaly detection benchmarks (e.g., MMAD) while simultaneously enhancing the reliability and explainability of the model's predictions by localizing defects and aligning reasoning with specialized knowledge.

These objectives collectively aim to bridge the gap between general LMM capabilities and the specific demands of industrial anomaly understanding, fostering more robust and interpretable AI solutions in this critical domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JUDO (Juxtaposed Domain-Oriented Multimodal Reasoner) employs a three-stage progressive learning framework designed to systematically internalize domain knowledge and context into a Large Multimodal Model (LMM). The framework aims to fuse comparative visual reasoning and domain semantics into a unified, domain-aligned reasoning process. The base model utilized for this framework is Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Training is conducted on a single node with 4 NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

**Stage 1: Learning Anomaly Segmentation-Based Juxtaposed Reasoning**
The first stage focuses on establishing fine-grained visual reasoning by enabling the model to pinpoint defect regions through segmentation, explicitly comparing anomalous images against their normal counterparts.
- **Paradigm:** Juxtaposed reasoning, where a defective image is directly compared with a normal image from the same object category during training. This shifts the learning objective from simple pattern memorization to a fine-grained recognition of deviations from normality.
- **Mechanism:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is used, inspired by text-based segmentation methods like Text4Seg. The model is trained to output two components:
 - **Anomaly Patches:** A textual sequence of coordinates (e.g., `(11,12)-(11,14), (12,11)`) within `<seg>` tags, representing 16x16 grid cells that contain the anomaly.
 - **Comparative Explanation:** A corresponding textual explanation within `<think>` tags, describing the defect by contrasting the query image with the normal sample, given the defect type and bounding box.
- **Dataset Construction:** A training dataset for juxtaposed fine-grained anomaly segmentation is constructed. It includes one image from each defect category in the MMAD dataset and 10 instances from each category in the REAL-IAD dataset. Each anomalous query image is paired with a randomly sampled normal template. GPT-4o is used to synthetically generate comparative explanations after annotating the anomalous region with a red outline.

**Stage 2: Domain-Knowledge Injection**
This stage addresses the LMM's lack of sufficient domain-specific knowledge for industrial anomaly problems by internalizing this knowledge directly into the model's parameters.
- **Mechanism:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is employed on a specially constructed domain Question-Answering (QA) dataset. Unlike conventional supervised learning that targets direct answers to MMAD queries, this SFT focuses on providing foundational domain knowledge.
- **Dataset Construction:** QA pairs are generated from the raw, unstructured domain snippets provided in the MMAD benchmark using GPT-4o. These snippets describe characteristics of object categories and their associated defect types. GPT-4o is prompted to reflect industrial inspection knowledge (e.g., defect criteria, functional implications) into structured QA pairs. Each QA is paraphrased into multiple variants to enhance robustness. Crucially, every QA instance is paired with a normal image from the corresponding object category to ground the textual domain knowledge in its visual context.

**Stage 3: Domain-Oriented Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
The final stage unifies the visual grounding (from Stage 1) and domain semantics (from Stage 2) through reinforcement learning, aligning the model's behavior for accurate, domain-oriented reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is utilized, a method that optimizes policies by comparing relative performance within groups of trajectories to improve generalization. This stage employs multi-faceted reward functions to provide comprehensive feedback.
- **Reward Functions:**
 - **Domain Reasoning Reward (R_domain):** Guides the reasoning generated within `<think>` tags to align with a domain-oriented reasoning process, particularly for MMAD benchmark queries. Since ground-truth rationales are absent, GPT-4o generates pseudo-domain rationales for 1-shot train split, incorporating full contexts (query, answer, images, juxtaposed reasoning, domain knowledge). The reward is calculated as the cosine similarity between the embeddings of the model's generated reasoning and the pseudo-domain rationale, using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 SentenceTransformer embeddings. A coefficient `λ = 0.1` is applied to avoid over-emphasizing semantic similarity.
 - **Segmentation Reward (R_seg):** Evaluates the spatial precision of anomaly patch coordinates within `<seg>` tags using the F1 score. Improperly formatted coordinate strings result in a 0 reward. For valid outputs, the reward is `0.2 + 0.8 * F1(P, P_G)`, where `P` are predicted cells and `P_G` are ground-truth cells.
 - **Choice and Structural Alignment Reward:** This composite reward ensures correctness, structure, and logical soundness:
 - **Choice Reward:** Rewards correct multiple-choice selections within `<answer>` tags.
 - **Format Reward:** Ensures adherence to the required `<seg>...<think>...<answer>` output structure.
 - **Reasoning Structure Reward:** Encourages the conclusive answer within the reasoning and the final answer to be correct, while penalizing premature commitment by mentioning answer choices in the first half of the reasoning text.
- **Dataset:** For GRPO, a sparse sampling strategy is used, employing only one training instance per category from MMAD (identical to Stage 1 samples), resulting in 1.4k QA pairs for reinforcement alignment.

The training process for Stage 1 and Stage 2 runs for 8 and 2 epochs, respectively, with specific learning rates. GRPO in Stage 3 runs for 14 epochs with 16 generations per prompt and a batch size of 8.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of JUDO was conducted on the MMAD benchmark, which integrates four datasets (MVTec AD, MVTec LOCO, VisA, GoodsAD) and covers seven subtasks for multiple-choice QA. The reported metric is averaged accuracy.

**Overall Performance:**
- JUDO achieved an overall averaged accuracy of 81.20% on the MMAD benchmark. This performance surpassed several leading general-purpose LMMs, including Qwen2.5-VL-7B (72.56%), InternVL3.5-8B (66.30%), GPT-4o (74.92%), Gemini-2.5-pro (81.19%), and commercial models like Claude-3.5-sonnet (68.36%).
- JUDO also outperformed AnomalyR1-7B, a GRPO-based anomaly-focused baseline, which achieved 77.29%.

**Performance Across Subtasks:**
- **Superiority in Defect-Related Subtasks:** JUDO demonstrated particular strength across the four defect-related subtasks: classification, localization, description, and analysis. These tasks are highly dependent on domain-specific knowledge and contextual understanding, highlighting the effectiveness of JUDO's progressive domain knowledge integration.
 - Defect Localization: JUDO achieved 73.01%, a notable increase from the base Qwen2.5-VL's 61.17%.
 - Defect Description: JUDO reached 84.56%.
 - Defect Analysis: JUDO achieved 89.41%.
- **Anomaly Discrimination Trade-off:** JUDO’s performance in anomaly discrimination tasks was 65.04%. While a modest improvement over AnomalyR1 (60.93%), it remained below some general LMMs such as Kimi-VL-A3B (72.93%) and the base Qwen2.5-VL (71.39%). This observation suggests a trade-off where explicit verbal reasoning (Chain-of-Thought, CoT) can sometimes impair performance on perception-heavy tasks like binary anomaly discrimination, a phenomenon also observed in prior research. The paper noted a sharp drop in anomaly discrimination accuracy (from 71.39% to 61.90%) when the base Qwen2.5-VL switched from direct answering to CoT reasoning mode, indicating that this degradation is linked to the reasoning paradigm itself rather than solely the JUDO framework.

**Ablation Study:**
An ablation study systematically evaluated the contribution of each component within the JUDO framework:
- **Base Qwen2.5-VL-7B:** Achieved 72.56% average accuracy (without CoT reasoning).
- **+ GRPO (AnomalyR1 equivalent):** Standard GRPO training improved performance to 77.29%, indicating the general benefit of instruction-following.
- **+ GRPO + RAG:** Dynamically providing external information via RAG to the GRPO-trained model resulted in a decrease in accuracy to 76.29%. This indicates that external context without internalization can be detrimental, possibly due to contextual distraction or misalignment.
- **+ GRPO + DomInj (Stage 2):** Injecting domain knowledge through supervised fine-tuning significantly improved accuracy to 79.82%, demonstrating the effectiveness of learning-based knowledge integration over RAG.
- **+ GRPO + SegJux + DomInj (Stage 1 + Stage 2):** Adding juxtaposed segmentation training further increased accuracy to 80.35%, confirming the value of fine-grained visual analysis.
- **+ GRPO_dom + SegJux + DomInj (Full JUDO - Stage 1 + Stage 2 + Stage 3 with Domain Reasoning Reward):** The complete JUDO framework achieved the peak performance of 81.20%, affirming that each stage provides meaningful, incremental benefits.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
Qualitative examples illustrated JUDO's ability to correctly identify and locate defects, provide visually grounded explanations (with segmentation maps), and deliver domain-aligned reasoning. In contrast, the Base GRPO model sometimes failed to detect defects or gave incorrect answers, while the Base GRPO + RAG model was observed to be misled by retrieved domain snippets, leading to mislocalization and incorrect defect descriptions by prioritizing plausible but visually ungrounded information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The JUDO framework presents a notable advancement in the application of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for industrial anomaly detection and understanding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Systematic Internalization of Domain Knowledge:** JUDO addresses a critical limitation of general-purpose LMMs by demonstrating a systematic and effective method for internalizing domain-specific knowledge and context directly into the model's parameters. This approach significantly outperforms dynamic external context provision (e.g., RAG-based methods), highlighting that integrated learning is more beneficial for specialized tasks. This finding has broader implications for fine-tuning LMMs in various domain-specific applications beyond industrial anomaly detection.

2. **Enhanced Reliability and Explainability:** By integrating anomaly segmentation and domain-aligned textual reasoning, JUDO improves the reliability and explainability of anomaly analysis. The model's ability to precisely localize defects and provide coherent, domain-specific explanations for its judgments fosters greater trust and facilitates human interpretation of AI-driven decisions in industrial quality control. This leads to more interpretable and visually verifiable decision-making processes.

3. **Unified Multimodal Reasoning Framework:** The proposed three-stage learning framework, which progressively builds comparative visual reasoning, injects domain knowledge, and then unifies these capabilities through reinforced alignment, offers a robust paradigm for developing highly specialized multimodal AI systems. This structured approach could serve as a blueprint for similar challenges where deep domain understanding is paramount.

4. **Advancement in Industrial Quality Control:** The superior performance of JUDO in defect-reasoning subtasks directly contributes to advancing industrial quality control and automation. By enabling LMMs to perform more accurate classification, localization, description, and analysis of anomalies, JUDO can lead to more efficient identification of manufacturing defects, potentially reducing waste, improving product quality, and streamlining inspection processes in various industrial sectors.

5. **Understanding LMM Behavior in Specialized Domains:** The research provides insights into the trade-offs between general perception-heavy tasks (like anomaly discrimination) and reasoning-intensive domain-specific tasks in LMMs. The observed phenomenon where explicit verbal reasoning can impact simple detection tasks contributes to the ongoing discussion about the optimal design and application of CoT-like processes for diverse tasks. This understanding can guide future research in balancing broad generalization with specialized precision.

In conclusion, JUDO represents a significant step towards creating more intelligent, reliable, and explainable AI systems for complex industrial applications by effectively bridging the gap between general LMM capabilities and the stringent requirements of domain-specific industrial understanding.
