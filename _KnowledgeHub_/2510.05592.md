# In-the-Flow Agentic System Optimization for Effective Planning and Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> AGENTFLOW introduces a trainable agentic framework for large language models that integrates specialized modules with in-the-flow reinforcement learning for effective planning and tool use. Utilizing a 7B-scale backbone, this system surpassed GPT-4o's performance across diverse complex reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Monolithic LLMs for tool-integrated reasoning face challenges in scaling, maintaining stability over long reasoning horizons, and generalizing to diverse tasks and toolsets.
- Existing agentic systems often rely on training-free, handcrafted logic or prompting, hindering robust coordination, adaptation to dynamic tool outputs, and recovery from early mistakes.
- Long-horizon, multi-turn tasks with sparse, final-outcome rewards create a credit assignment problem for reinforcement learning, making it difficult to attribute success or failure to intermediate actions.

## Method

- AGENTFLOW proposes a modular agentic architecture with four specialized components: an ==Action Planner== (the trainable policy), Tool Executor, Execution Verifier, and Solution Generator, which interact iteratively via an evolving memory.
- It implements Flow-based Group Refined Policy Optimization (Flow-GRPO), an algorithm for *in-the-flow* on-policy training that allows the planner to adapt dynamically within the multi-turn interaction loop.
- Flow-GRPO addresses sparse rewards by assigning a single, verifiable final-outcome reward to the entire trajectory, effectively converting the multi-turn RL problem into independent single-turn updates with a global success signal.

## Results

- AGENTFLOW, using a **7B**-scale backbone, consistently outperformed all baseline models, including specialized tool-integrated systems and GPT-4o, across 10 diverse reasoning benchmarks, achieving up to an **18.0%** gain in mathematical reasoning over GPT-4o.
- The Flow-GRPO fine-tuned planner demonstrated optimized and adaptive tool usage, such as increasing Google Search usage by **42.0%** for broad knowledge tasks and shifting to targeted searches for domain-specific retrieval.
- Training with Flow-GRPO substantially reduced tool-calling error rates by up to **28.4%** on tasks like GAIA, indicating improved reliability, and exhibited enhanced self-correction capabilities in complex problem-solving scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a modular agentic design with adaptive reinforcement learning provides a more robust and efficient approach to complex, multi-step tasks than monolithic LLM agents or static agentic systems.
> - Broadcasting a global, trajectory-level reward, coupled with group-normalized advantages, simplifies the credit assignment problem in long-horizon, sparse-reward environments, enabling stable on-policy learning.
> - In-the-flow optimization allows the agent to dynamically refine planning and tool use based on real-time feedback, leading to improved reliability, task-appropriate tool selection, and autonomous solution discovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of LLMs has spurred significant advancements in AI, particularly in complex reasoning tasks. The paper positions AGENTFLOW at the intersection of two critical, yet often disparate, lines of research: **outcome-driven reinforcement learning (RL) for LLMs** and **agentic systems with tool use**.

Firstly, prior work in outcome-driven RL has focused on fine-tuning monolithic LLMs to maximize verifiable rewards, enabling self-correction and multi-step deduction. A prominent sub-area involves **tool-integrated reasoning (TIR)**, where LLMs learn to interleave thoughts and tool calls (e.g., web search, code execution) to augment their capabilities. However, the authors identify key limitations with these monolithic TIR approaches:
1. **Scaling Challenges:** Training a single policy becomes unstable with longer reasoning horizons, diverse toolsets, and dynamic environments where tool feedback shifts the state distribution.
2. **Weak Generalization:** Monolithic models struggle to generalize effectively to unseen tasks or new tools, often exhibiting brittle performance.

Secondly, **agentic systems** have emerged as a promising alternative. These systems decompose complex problems across specialized modules (e.g., planner, critic, executor), often comprising distinct LLMs or dedicated components, which coordinate via shared memory. While agentic systems excel at tasks requiring diverse tools and multi-stage reasoning, most existing solutions are **training-free**, relying on handcrafted logic or prompting heuristics. The authors point out that this static nature prevents robust coordination, adaptation to evolving tool outputs, or recovery from early mistakes. Even systems employing offline training methods like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or preference optimization remain decoupled from the live dynamics of multi-turn interaction, leading to poor learning from downstream successes or failures and issues with sparse rewards.

AGENTFLOW directly addresses this gap by proposing a **trainable, in-the-flow agentic framework**. It aims to combine the modularity and flexibility of agentic systems with the adaptive learning capabilities of RL, specifically addressing the long-horizon, sparse-reward credit assignment problem inherent in dynamic, tool-augmented environments. By enabling on-policy optimization *within* the multi-turn interaction loop, AGENTFLOW seeks to overcome the limitations of both monolithic RL agents and static agentic systems, pushing the boundaries towards more adaptive, robust, and efficient AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a **trainable agentic system that can effectively plan and use tools over long reasoning horizons with sparse rewards**. The authors' motivation stems from the recognition that current LLM-based agents, despite their impressive capabilities, fall short in dynamic, multi-turn interactions, especially when tasked with complex problems requiring diverse tool use.

Specifically, the key objectives are:

1. **To design a modular agentic framework:** This involves decomposing the reasoning process into specialized, interacting modules (planner, executor, verifier, generator) that coordinate through an evolving memory. This modularity is intended to improve robustness and interpretability.
2. **To enable *in-the-flow* optimization:** The core objective is to move beyond offline or training-free approaches by directly optimizing the agent's decision-making (specifically, the planner) *within* the live multi-turn interaction loop. This allows the agent to dynamically adapt to tool feedback and evolving reasoning trajectories.
3. **To solve the long-horizon, sparse-reward credit assignment problem:** In multi-turn tasks where the final outcome is the only clear reward, attributing success or failure to specific intermediate actions is notoriously difficult. The research aims to devise a robust RL algorithm that can effectively assign credit across long reasoning chains.
4. **To achieve superior performance and generalization:** The ultimate goal is to demonstrate that such an optimized agentic system can outperform existing monolithic LLM agents, specialized tool-integrated reasoning models, and even larger, proprietary LLMs across a wide array of diverse reasoning tasks.
5. **To ensure training efficiency and stability:** The proposed RL method must be practical and stable, overcoming the instability often associated with long-horizon RL and complex action spaces.

The motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful but often brittle monolithic LLM agents and flexible but static agentic systems. By creating a framework that *learns* to orchestrate its components and adapt to dynamic environments, the authors aim to build more intelligent, reliable, and versatile AI systems capable of tackling real-world problems that demand sophisticated planning and tool-use capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AGENTFLOW introduces a novel **trainable, in-the-flow agentic system** characterized by four specialized modules and a unique reinforcement learning optimization algorithm.

#### 3.1. AGENTFLOW Architecture: An In-the-Flow Agentic System

AGENTFLOW is designed as a multi-turn architecture composed of four distinct modules that interact iteratively via a shared, evolving memory (M) and a toolset (K), given an initial query (q):

1. **Action Planner (P):** This is the **trainable policy** (πθ) of the system. At each turn `t`, it observes the current state (query, toolset, and memory `Mt`), then formulates a sub-goal, selects an appropriate tool from `K`, and retrieves relevant context from `Mt` to produce an action `at`.
2. **Tool Executor (E):** Upon receiving `at` from the planner, the executor invokes the chosen tool with the specified context. It returns an execution observation `et` (e.g., search results, code output).
3. **Execution Verifier (V):** This module evaluates `et` to determine its validity and whether the accumulated memory `Mt` is sufficient to resolve the original query. It produces a binary verification signal `vt` (0 for continue, 1 for terminate).
4. **Solution Generator (G):** If `vt = 1` (termination condition met), the generator synthesizes the final solution `o` based on the query `q` and the complete accumulated memory `MT`.

The core concept is "in-the-flow": each turn cycles through planning, execution, and verification. The memory `Mt` is an *explicit and deterministic* structured record of the reasoning process, which is crucial for transparency, controllability, and bounded context growth. This iterative process continues until the verifier signals termination or a maximum turn budget is reached.

#### 3.2. In-the-Flow Reinforcement Learning Optimization (Flow-GRPO)

To train the Action Planner on-policy within this dynamic agentic system, the authors propose **Flow-based Group Refined Policy Optimization (Flow-GRPO)**, an algorithm specifically designed to tackle long-horizon, sparse-reward credit assignment.

1. **In-the-Flow Rollouts:** Flow-GRPO operates on actual trajectories generated by the full AGENTFLOW system under the current policy. These "in-the-flow rollouts" capture the entire sequence of states, actions, and tool events, exposing the planner to the exact dynamics it will face during inference.
2. **Final-Outcome Reward:** Instead of relying on brittle intermediate heuristics, Flow-GRPO assigns a *single, verifiable final-outcome reward* `R(τ)` to the *entire trajectory* `τ`. This reward (binary, {0, 1}) is based on the correctness of the final solution `o` against the ground truth `y*`, as judged by an LLM-as-judge. This global success signal is then broadcast to *every action* `at` within that trajectory.
3. **Multi-turn to Single-turn Conversion:** By broadcasting the trajectory-level reward to every turn, Flow-GRPO effectively transforms the complex multi-turn RL problem into a sequence of simpler, independent single-turn policy updates. At each turn, the planner receives a consistent reward signal aligned with global success, simplifying credit assignment.
4. **Policy Optimization Objective:** Flow-GRPO optimizes the planner policy `πθ` to maximize the expected return over a group of parallel rollouts. The objective function includes:
 - **Clipped Surrogate Objective:** A PPO-style clipping mechanism (`Lclip`) applied to token-level importance ratios (`ρt,i,j`) to ensure stable updates and prevent large policy changes.
 - **Group-Normalized Advantages:** To further stabilize training and sharpen credit assignment across the group of rollouts, the advantage `At,i` for each action `at,i` is calculated as the `i`-th trajectory's reward minus the mean of all group rewards, normalized by their standard deviation. This contrasts with traditional RL, where advantages might be specific to states or actions within a single trajectory.
 - **KL-Divergence Penalty:** A KL-divergence term (`βDKL(πθ || πref)`) is added to keep the updated policy `πθ` close to a reference policy `πref`, preventing policy collapse.

In summary, Flow-GRPO enables stable, on-policy learning in complex agentic systems by simplifying credit assignment with a global outcome reward and stabilizing optimization through group-level advantage normalization and PPO-style clipping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AGENTFLOW's comprehensive evaluation across ten diverse benchmarks yielded significant findings, demonstrating its superior performance and validating the efficacy of in-the-flow optimization:

#### 4.1. Superior Performance Across Diverse Reasoning Domains

- **Outperformance Against Baselines:** AGENTFLOW, utilizing a 7B-scale backbone (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), consistently outperformed all baseline models, including open-source LLMs, specialized tool-integrated reasoning models, and even larger proprietary models.
 - **Search-intensive tasks (Bamboogle, 2Wiki, HotpotQA, Musique):** Achieved an average accuracy gain of 14.9% over the best-performing 7B tool-integrated system (AutoGen).
 - **Agentic tasks (GAIA):** Showed a 14.0% gain over Search-R1.
 - **Mathematical reasoning (AIME24, AMC23, GameOf24):** Demonstrated a 14.5% gain over ToRL.
 - **Scientific reasoning (GPQA, MedQA):** Achieved a 4.1% gain over TIR.
- **Surpassing GPT-4o:** Notably, the 7B-backbone AGENTFLOW surpassed the ~200B-parameter GPT-4o across all evaluated domains, with gains ranging from 8.2% (search) to 18.0% (mathematical reasoning). This highlights that a well-designed, trainable agentic architecture can be more efficient and effective than monolithic, even significantly larger, models for complex, multi-step tasks.

#### 4.2. In-Depth Analysis of Optimized Planning

- **Adaptive Tool Usage:** Flow-GRPO fine-tuning optimized the planner's tool usage distribution. For instance, on 2Wiki (broad factual knowledge), Google Search usage increased by 42.0%. On MedQA (domain-specific retrieval), Google Search calls decreased by 66.2% in favor of targeted Web Search (+19.5%) and Wikipedia Search (+59.8%), demonstrating that the planner learned to select task-appropriate tools.
- **Enhanced Tool-Calling Efficacy:** The training process significantly reduced tool-calling error rates across tasks (up to 28.4% on GAIA), indicating improved reliability in invoking tools with correct arguments and formats.
- **Autonomous Discovery of Solutions:** Qualitative analyses and case studies showed that the trained planner exhibited enhanced capabilities for task planning, stronger self-correction, adaptive efficiency, and spontaneous integration of tools. It learned to autonomously discover effective solution pathways, even recovering from initial failures (e.g., switching from Python Coder error loops to a correct, systematic approach).

#### 4.3. Training Strategies on the Planner

- **In-the-flow RL is Crucial:** An ablation study confirmed the critical role of Flow-GRPO. Replacing the 7B planner with a frozen GPT-4o yielded only modest gains (5.8%), indicating that static planners struggle with live dynamics. Offline Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on GPT-4o trajectories led to a catastrophic performance collapse (19.0% average drop), underscoring the limitations of token-level imitation and the misalignment with trajectory-level task success. Flow-GRPO, by optimizing for final outcomes in the live loop, achieved a substantial 17.2% average gain over the frozen baseline.

#### 4.4. Training Efficiency Analysis

- **Increased Rewards and Condensed Responses:** Flow-GRPO training led to a steady increase in training rewards, indicating effective policy improvement. Concurrently, response lengths, after an initial exploratory phase, progressively shortened and stabilized, suggesting the planner learned to be more concise and informative.
- **Superior Efficiency over Monolithic RL:** Compared to a monolithic tool-integrated reasoning baseline (ToRL), AGENTFLOW with Flow-GRPO showed sustained performance gains, while ToRL's performance quickly stagnated or declined. This validated the efficiency of the agentic training approach due to its decomposition and stable credit assignment.

#### 4.5. Scaling Trends in AGENTFLOW

- **Backbone Size Scaling:** Flow-GRPO fine-tuning provided consistent performance gains when scaling the backbone LLM from Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct to Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, demonstrating its effectiveness across different model capacities.
- **Inference Turn Budgets:** Increasing the maximum allowed inference turns (`Tmax`) from 3 to 10 consistently improved outcomes across all tasks, accompanied by an increase in average turns consumed. This confirmed that a longer reasoning horizon, managed by the adaptive planner, allows the system to achieve better solutions through iterative refinement without leading to degenerate loops.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of AGENTFLOW and Flow-GRPO marks a significant advancement in the field of large language model agents, with profound implications for the development of more intelligent, robust, and adaptable AI systems.

**1. Bridging the Gap in Agentic AI:** AGENTFLOW successfully unifies the benefits of modular agentic architectures (flexibility, task decomposition) with the adaptive power of reinforcement learning. This addresses a critical limitation of previous approaches, which were either flexible but untrainable (training-free agentic systems) or trainable but brittle for complex, long-horizon tasks (monolithic RL agents).

**2. Solving the Long-Horizon Credit Assignment Problem:** Flow-GRPO's innovative approach of broadcasting a single, verifiable trajectory-level outcome reward to every turn, coupled with group-normalized advantages, provides a robust solution to the notoriously difficult problem of sparse rewards in multi-turn RL. This breakthrough enables stable on-policy learning in complex, dynamic environments where intermediate actions' values are only apparent at the end of a long reasoning chain.

**3. Enhanced Performance and Efficiency for Complex Tasks:** By significantly outperforming leading specialized baselines and even much larger proprietary models like GPT-4o with a smaller backbone, AGENTFLOW demonstrates that architectural design and intelligent training strategies can yield superior performance more efficiently. This suggests a pathway for developing highly capable AI agents without solely relying on ever-increasing model sizes, which has substantial implications for resource efficiency and accessibility.

**4. Increased Reliability and Adaptability:** The observed improvements in tool-calling reliability (reduced error rates) and adaptive tool usage (learning to switch tools or refine queries based on task context) indicate that AGENTFLOW agents are more trustworthy and capable of dynamic self-correction. This makes them more suitable for real-world applications where unexpected outcomes and errors are common.

**5. Foundation for Future Agentic Systems:** AGENTFLOW provides a solid trainable framework for building future generations of AI agents. The modular design simplifies debugging and future extensions, such as incorporating more sophisticated modules or integrating advanced reward signals. The focus on "in-the-flow" learning suggests a paradigm shift towards agents that continuously learn and adapt from their live interactions, paving the way for truly autonomous and self-improving AI.

The potential impact spans various domains, from advanced scientific discovery and complex problem-solving to highly capable personal assistants and autonomous decision-making systems. AGENTFLOW contributes a crucial piece to the puzzle of building more capable and general-purpose AI, offering a blueprint for systems that can reason, plan, and utilize tools with human-like adaptability and efficiency.
