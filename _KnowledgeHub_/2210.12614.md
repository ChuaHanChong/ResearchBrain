# A Solution to Slosh-free Robot Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A slosh-free trajectory optimization method that models the robot end effector as a point mass on a spherical pendulum, so no lateral reaction force acts on the carried liquid. Linearizing the pendulum dynamics about equilibrium turns motion generation into a quadratic program that couples container tilt with translation, avoiding fluid simulation. Validated in simulation and on a 7 DoF Franka Emika Panda arm.

## Problem

- Computational fluid dynamics slosh models take hours to converge, making them infeasible for online robot planning and control of liquid transport.
- Planar and frequency-domain slosh-suppression models are case-specific to container shape or robot, and do not scale to high-DoF manipulators.
- Prior methods separate trajectory smoothing from container tilt compensation, giving suboptimal or computationally heavy inverse-motion solutions unfit for kilohertz control loops.

## Method

- ==Spherical-pendulum point-mass model==: end effector is the pivot, liquid a suspended mass; Euler-Lagrange equations of motion inherently satisfy the zero-lateral-force slosh-free condition.
- ==Small-angle linearization== about the stable equilibrium, using two orthogonal planar-pendulum angles (singularity-free near equilibrium), discretized by zero-order hold into a linear state-space model.
- ==Quadratic program== minimizing squared deviation from a nominal trajectory, with pendulum dynamics as equality constraints plus state, input, jerk, and boundary-condition inequality constraints; joint trajectories via **dual-quaternion differential inverse kinematics**.

## Results

- Point-mass validity analysis gives rod-to-container-height ratio r = l/h = **3** as a rule of thumb, yielding an inertia approximation error below **1.8%**.
- On a 0.3 m step, force-alignment error falls from **2.51e-02** at r=3 to **0.75e-02** at r=9, kinematic error from **7.66e-04** to **3.15e-04**.
- Panda experiments: a demonstrated square path tracked at **0.48 m/s** and **1.22 m/s²** with near-zero lateral forces; wine glass step at **0.63 m/s** spilled under a proportional controller but not under the optimized motion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A physically grounded surrogate, the suspended point mass, can replace expensive fluid simulation when the invariant to enforce is a force-alignment condition, not the fluid field.
> - Linearizing about the operating equilibrium can be nearly lossless in practice: aggressive trajectories rarely violated the small-angle assumption here.
> - Caution: vertical acceleration effects vanish in the first-order approximation, yaw is held fixed, and accuracy depends on knowing the object's center of mass.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of transporting dynamic fluids and fragile materials with robots in a slosh-free and time-optimal manner. This area of robotics control is relevant to various applications, from consumer tasks like carrying beverages to industrial applications involving hazardous liquids.

Existing research in this domain employs several strategies. One common approach involves minimizing higher derivative terms such as jerk and snap in trajectories through optimal control, which aims to reduce liquid sloshing. However, the authors note that such methods often provide suboptimal results in completely preventing spills. Other approaches involve computationally intensive methods, such as Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations, which are generally not suitable for real-time robotic control due to their processing demands. Simplified numerical approaches for slosh modeling, often decoupled from direct robot control, also exist but may not scale effectively to high Degrees of Freedom (DoF) systems.

Some prior work has utilized fluid analysis and optimization for trajectory smoothing and container tilt-coupling, but these solutions tend to be computationally heavy and case-specific. Learning from Demonstration (LfD) techniques have also been explored to teach robots fluid manipulation skills, and optimization-based pouring strategies exist that rely on simplified fluid dynamics. In the context of slosh control, strategies include applying smoothing methods to desired trajectories and actively compensating for liquid oscillations by varying the container’s orientation. The concept of using pendulum-like dynamics to model liquid behavior and design control strategies, inspired by human grasping, has also been explored in the literature, for example, in the context of active acceleration compensation in transport vehicles or analyzing human manipulation of non-rigid objects.

The authors identify a gap in the existing landscape: a need for real-time, optimal, and safe solutions that leverage collaborative robot capabilities without being computationally demanding or restricted to specific robot or container types. Current frameworks are often suboptimal or computationally intensive when solving the inverse motion problem required for rapid, reactive control. This work aims to bridge this gap by proposing a fundamentally different approach. It formulates the slosh-free transportation problem as a linearized point mass system, which is inspired by the Spillnot mechanism, a passive device that allows a cup to swing like a pendulum to prevent spills. The authors state their approach is the first to propose a slosh-free manipulation solution based on a closed-form linearized yet accurate dynamics that accounts for translation and tilt-coupled dynamics, specifically designed for real-time quadratic programming.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for fast, slosh-free fluid transportation using robotic manipulators. The motivation stems from the challenges associated with safely and efficiently manipulating dynamic fluids and fragile materials, particularly in scenarios demanding aggressive, high-speed maneuvers that can exceed human capabilities.

The authors detail several key motivations:
- **Safety and Performance in Fluid Transportation:** Tasks such as transporting a full cup of coffee, a glass of wine, or hazardous industrial liquids require precise control to prevent spills. While humans can innately adjust container frequency to match fluid dynamics, achieving this consistently and aggressively with robots, especially under time constraints, is challenging. The priority in such tasks is safety, which translates to avoiding spillage.
- **Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Current methods for slosh avoidance are often computationally expensive (e.g., relying on complex fluid dynamics simulations or intensive system analysis) or narrowly tailored to specific robot designs and container geometries. This renders them unsuitable for real-time applications on modern collaborative robots, which often operate with fast inner control loops (e.g., 1 KHz). The computational overhead or specificity of these methods hinders their practical deployment.
- **Inspiration from Passive Mechanisms:** The "Spillnot" mechanism, a market solution that prevents spills by allowing a container to freely swing, provides a conceptual inspiration. This mechanism suggests that a simplified, pendular-like dynamic model could be exploited to achieve similar slosh-free properties in robot motion.
- **Enabling Above-Human Performance:** The goal is not just to match human performance but to exceed it by leveraging robotic capabilities for extreme time-optimal solutions. This requires a control strategy that can predict and compensate for reaction forces in real-time.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the work are:
- To model the robot's end-effector and the contained fluid as a point mass suspended by a spherical pendulum, thereby studying the conditions required for slosh-free motion and evaluating the validity of this simplified model.
- To develop an optimization-based motion generation solution that can achieve real-time, above-human performance in fluid transfer without spillage, even for aggressive motion profiles.
- To formulate the trajectory optimization problem as a quadratic program (QP), which allows for computationally efficient generation of valid control inputs within the robot's fast control loops.
- To integrate the tilt of the container with the system's maximum task-space acceleration and jerk constraints, based on a closed-form linearized dynamics model that accounts for both translation and tilt-coupling.
- To validate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed approach through both simulations and physical experiments on a robotic manipulator.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology for generating slosh-free robot trajectories is based on modeling the fluid and container as a point mass governed by spherical pendulum dynamics, coupled with a Quadratic Programming (QP) optimization framework.

**Conditions for Slosh-free Motion:** The underlying principle for slosh-free motion is derived from the requirement that no lateral reaction forces or external torques act on the container. This condition is met when the total reaction force is aligned with the object's vertical direction and passes through its center of mass. Mathematically, this relationship is expressed as (a_z + g) tan θ = a_x, where a_x and a_z are lateral and vertical accelerations, g is gravitational acceleration, and θ is the tilt angle.

**Spherical Pendulum Parametrization and Dynamics:**
- The system models the robot's end-effector (carrying the container) as the pivot of a virtual spherical pendulum, with the fluid's effective mass concentrated at the pendulum's bob.
- For parametrization, the authors use a linear model of the pendulum, which is based on the small angle approximation and linearization around the stable equilibrium. This approach is chosen to avoid singularities common in spherical coordinates near the equilibrium point and to simplify the dynamics. The motion is described by two orthogonal planar pendulums.
- The equations of motion for the spherical pendulum are derived using the Euler-Lagrange equations. These nonlinear equations relate the angular accelerations (¨θ, ¨φ) to the pivot's accelerations (¨x_p, ¨y_p, ¨z_p), gravity, and the current state of the pendulum. The paper states that these derived nonlinear equations inherently satisfy the slosh-free condition.

**Validity of a Point Mass Model:**
- To justify approximating the fluid as a point mass, the authors investigate the conditions under which this approximation is valid. A point mass model is considered appropriate if the object's mass moment of inertia with respect to its center of mass (J_c) is negligible compared to its mass moment of inertia with respect to the rotation axis (l²m, where l is pendulum length, m is mass).
- An approximation error 'p' is introduced to quantify this validity, with pl²m = J_c. By modeling J_c as that of a cube (mh²/6, where h is object height), a relationship is established between 'l', 'h', and 'p'.
- A key finding for this approximation is that a ratio 'r' = l/h = 3 (pendulum length three times the object height) results in an approximation error 'p' less than 1.8%, supporting the use of the point mass model for practical applications.

**Spillnot Motion-Generator Controller (Quadratic Programming Formulation):**
- **Linearization for Optimization:** The nonlinear spherical pendulum equations of motion are linearized using a first-order Taylor expansion around the stable equilibrium point (where all state variables are zero). This simplification transforms the equations into a linear form: l¨θ = -gθ + u1 and l¨φ = -gφ - u2, where u represents the pivot accelerations.
- **Discrete State-Space Model:** The linearized dynamics are then converted into a continuous linear state-space model and subsequently discretized using a zero-order hold assumption for constant inputs between time steps. This yields discrete state-space matrices (A, B, C, D) that define the system dynamics for the optimization problem.
- **Quadratic Program (QP) Formulation:** The trajectory optimization problem is formulated as a Quadratic Program (QP) to find the optimal pivot trajectory.
 - **Objective Function:** The optimization aims to minimize the deviation from a desired nominal trajectory. This is quantified as half the sum of squared distances between the desired and optimized positions across all nodes of the discretized trajectory.
 - **Decision Variables:** The QP's decision variables include the pendulum's state (position, velocity, tilt angles, angular velocities) and the pivot's input (accelerations) at each discrete time step.
 - **Constraints:**
 - **Equality Constraints:** The linearized discrete-time pendulum dynamics (Ax_k + Bu_k - x_{k+1} = 0) are enforced as equality constraints for each segment of the trajectory. Additional equality constraints can be defined for initial and final conditions (e.g., desired start/end positions, zero velocity, zero acceleration).
 - **Inequality Constraints:** Bounds are imposed on the state variables (e.g., pendulum angles, velocities) and control inputs (pivot accelerations). Furthermore, constraints on the rate of change of control inputs (pivot jerk) are included, using finite differences, to limit the aggressiveness of the trajectory.

**Joint Space Trajectory Generation:**
- Once the optimal workspace trajectory (pivot position, tilt angles) is obtained from the QP, it needs to be converted into robot-specific joint commands.
- The poses of the mass (end-effector) are reconstructed from the pendulum states using dual-quaternions.
- Differential inverse kinematics, specifically the pseudo-inverse of the manipulator's zero-Jacobian, is employed to calculate the corresponding joint velocities.
- These joint velocities are then integrated to obtain joint configurations. Finite differences are applied to derive joint accelerations and jerks.
- Finally, the joint torques are calculated by simulating a dynamic model of the manipulator, and these are checked against the robot's dynamic limits to ensure executability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed slosh-free trajectory optimization framework was evaluated through both simulations and physical experiments using a 7 DoF Franka Emika Panda Robot.

**Simulation Results:**
- **Reference Trajectory:** A 0.3m position step in the x-direction of the robot's base frame was used as the desired trajectory. The container was approximated as a 0.1m side cube with a 10g load.
- **Influence of Pendulum Length Ratio (r):** The parameter 'r', representing the ratio of pendulum length (l) to container height (h), was varied (r = 3, 6, 9) to observe its effect on the optimized trajectories.
 - **Motion (Figure 4):** Lower 'r' values allowed for higher velocities and resulted in larger tilt angles, leading to more aggressive trajectories. Conversely, higher 'r' values resulted in smaller tilt angles and less aggressive motions.
 - **External Forces (Figure 5):** The simulated external forces (lateral F_x and vertical F_z) exerted on the object by the manipulator showed that higher 'r' values led to lower magnitudes of lateral forces. This indicates improved alignment of the total force with the object's vertical axis, which is crucial for slosh-free movement.
- **Slosh-free Constraint Evaluation (Table I):** The quantitative evaluation of slosh-free conditions involved two metrics:
 - **Force Alignment Error:** The maximum proportion of lateral forces to the total external force during the trajectory.
 - **Kinematic Error:** The maximum mismatch in the kinematic slosh condition (Equation 1).
 Both error measures consistently decreased with an increase in 'r'. This reduction in error was attributed to two factors: the increased accuracy of the point mass model for larger 'r' (as l >> h, the approximation error 'p' approaches zero), and the decrease in maximum tilt angles, which improves the accuracy of the linearized pendulum model.

**Experimental Results:**
- **Experiment 1 (Square-like path):** A square-like path was recorded via demonstration and used as the desired trajectory for optimization. The rod length was set to 0.6m, and a time step of 33ms was used. The robot executed the resulting joint space trajectory using a joint velocity controller.
 - The optimized motion accurately matched the desired path (Figure 7, top). The visualization of the virtual pendulum's rod throughout the trajectory demonstrated the end-effector's tilt angle.
 - Measurements of external forces from the robot's torque sensors (Figure 7, bottom) showed that lateral forces remained close to zero during the trajectory execution, indicating successful avoidance of spillage. The robot achieved a maximum velocity of 0.48m/s and a maximum acceleration of 1.22m/s².
- **Experiment 2 (Position step with wine glass):** The robot was commanded to follow a position step trajectory while grasping a glass filled with wine. For comparison, a simple proportional controller was also used.
 - With the proposed optimized motion, the orientation of the wine's surface remained constant relative to the glass throughout the movement, and spillage was successfully avoided (Figure 6, top panel). A maximum velocity of 0.63m/s was achieved.
 - In contrast, the simple proportional controller resulted in spillage (Figure 6, bottom panel), demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed method in a direct comparison.

**Validation of Linearization:** The authors report that the small angle approximation, which underpins the linearization of the pendulum dynamics, was "rarely violated" even during aggressive trajectories. This observation supports the applicability and accuracy of the linearized model for slosh-free trajectory generation in practical scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a novel strategy for slosh-free liquid manipulation, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts on robotics and automation.

**Efficiency and Real-time Capabilities:**
- A key significance is the development of a method that avoids computationally intensive fluid simulations (such as CFD), which are typically prohibitive for real-time robotic applications. Instead, the approach relies on a simplified, closed-form pendular-like dynamics.
- By formulating the trajectory optimization problem as a Quadratic Program (QP), the method achieves fast computation times. This allows for real-time motion generation, enabling its deployment within the fast inner control loops of modern collaborative robots (e.g., running at 1 KHz). This real-time capability is crucial for reactive and dynamic tasks.
- The method allows for aggressive fluid maneuvers at high speeds, surpassing the capabilities of human operators in maintaining slosh-free conditions.

**Generality and Applicability:**
- The approach is not tied to specific robot architectures or container shapes, making it broadly applicable across various robotic manipulators. It primarily requires knowledge of the desired reference trajectory and the robot's kinematic limits. This generality is an improvement over many existing case-specific solutions.
- The closed-form linearized dynamics, coupled with a validated point mass approximation, provides a robust model for predicting and compensating reaction forces.

**Enhanced Safety and Performance:**
- The ability to reliably transport fluids and fragile materials without spillage under aggressive motion profiles significantly enhances safety in industrial settings (e.g., handling hazardous chemicals) and improves the quality and efficiency of service robotics (e.g., serving beverages).
- By preventing spills, the method reduces cleanup time, material waste, and potential hazards, thereby increasing operational efficiency and safety.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**
- **Point Mass Approximation Dependence:** The accuracy of the point mass approximation relies on precise knowledge of the fluid's center of mass, which can be dynamic for complex fluids or varying fill levels.
- **Vertical Acceleration Compensation:** The first-order approximation used for linearization means that trajectories involving high vertical accelerations may not be fully compensated for sloshing effects.
- **Yaw Limitation:** The current spherical pendulum model holds the object's yaw constant. While extending the model to include yaw is considered "trivial," its validity would then depend directly on the mass moment of inertia around the object's vertical axis, potentially limiting the range of viable joint space trajectories.

**Future Work:**
- The authors plan to extend this work by designing a slosh-free Cartesian PD-controller, potentially integrated within an admittance control framework, to handle interactions with the environment.
- Another future direction involves leveraging the fast computation speed of the QP-based model within a receding horizon control strategy. This would enable real-time reactive behavior, allowing the robot to adapt to unforeseen disturbances or dynamic changes in its environment while maintaining slosh-free transportation.

In conclusion, this research offers a practical and computationally efficient solution to a challenging problem in robotics, demonstrating a potential impact on the safe and efficient automation of fluid handling tasks across diverse applications.
