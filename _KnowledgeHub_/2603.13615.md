# Egocentric World Model for Photorealistic Hand-Object Interaction Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoHOI is an egocentric world model that synthesizes photorealistic hand-object interactions purely from action signals, bypassing the need for future object state information by integrating physics-informed embeddings derived from 3D geometry and kinematics. The model demonstrates enhanced photorealism, improved alignment with user actions, and more realistic hand-object interaction dynamics compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric Human-Object Interaction (HOI) world models struggle with challenges like rapid head motions, severe occlusions, and high hand articulation degrees of freedom.
- Many HOI video generation methods operate from static, exocentric views or rely on privileged future object information, hindering true first-person, action-driven simulation for embodied AI applications.
- A reliable egocentric HOI world model capable of predicting future hand-object interactions solely from current action signals, without future-state shortcuts, was lacking.

## Method

- EgoHOI formulates HOI synthesis as an action-driven ==world model==ing problem within a compact latent state space, utilizing a Diffusion Transformer backbone for state evolution.
- The model avoids relying on privileged future object states by distilling 3D geometric and kinematic estimates into three physics-informed embeddings: Hand Kinematic Embeddings (HKE), Ego-Motion Embeddings (EME), and Object Entity Embeddings (OEE).
- These embeddings are integrated into a frozen Wan-DiT backbone via lightweight adapters, providing explicit metric and kinematic structure to guide physically plausible, egocentric rollouts.

## Results

- EgoHOI achieved superior performance across frame prediction, ego-motion consistency, and kinematic fidelity metrics, outperforming baselines like Cosmos 14B and Uni3C, with a PSNR of **21.05** (vs. Cosmos 14B's **15.89**) and an Object-CLIP score of **0.92** (vs. **0.88**), indicating enhanced visual quality and object preservation.
- The model significantly reduced ego-motion drift (Absolute Trajectory Error of **0.084** vs. Cosmos 14B's **0.112**) and improved kinematic fidelity (Missing Ratio of **5.84%** vs. Cosmos 14B's **14.61%**), confirming the effectiveness of physics-informed embeddings.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that Hand Kinematic Embeddings, Ego-Motion Embeddings, and Object Entity Embeddings each contribute to specific improvements in hand articulation, viewpoint stability, and object integrity, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit 3D geometric and kinematic priors, extracted from unstructured videos, can effectively regularize egocentric HOI rollouts, leading to physically plausible and consistent interaction dynamics.
> - Causal simulation of hand-object interactions from action signals, without future-state shortcuts, is achievable by augmenting generative models with physics-informed embeddings.
> - Distilling complex 3D information into compact, interpretable embeddings for hand kinematics, ego-motion, and object integrity provides a robust inductive bias for world models in egocentric contexts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of egocentric Human-Object Interaction (HOI) world models, which aim to simulate physically grounded interaction dynamics from a first-person perspective, driven by user controls. These predictive rollouts are increasingly relevant for generating scalable training and evaluation data across various downstream applications, including dexterous manipulation, Vision-Language-Actions (VLA) models, spatiotemporal reasoning, physical intelligence, and data augmentation.

Despite the recognized potential, developing robust egocentric HOI world models presents significant challenges. These include rapid head motions, severe occlusions, and the high degrees of freedom (DoF) associated with hand articulations, which can lead to abrupt changes in contact topologies. Existing research in HOI video generation often addresses these complexities in static, exocentric (third-person) views, which do not directly support the dynamic, first-person rollouts required for embodied AI and robotics. Furthermore, many current approaches for HOI generation condition their outputs on privileged future object information, such as ground-truth trajectories or waypoints. This reliance on future states transforms the task into conditional video generation rather than a true simulation of interaction dynamics inferred solely from current observations and actions.

This paper identifies a gap: the absence of a reliable egocentric HOI world model that can predict future hand-object interaction purely from action signals without relying on such future-state shortcuts. It argues that simply scaling RGB data is insufficient for achieving physical accuracy in such models. The authors propose that models need to be augmented with explicit metric and kinematic structure, an opportunity made increasingly feasible by recent advancements in 3D foundation models for geometry and hand pose estimation. This work positions itself as a development that integrates these 3D priors to enable causal simulation of physically plausible hand-object interactions, retaining generative diversity and reconstruction quality, without relying on future state information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce EgoHOI, an egocentric HOI world model designed for photorealistic synthesis of hand-object interactions. The model's distinguishing feature is its capacity to simulate these interactions strictly from action signals, without requiring privileged access to future object states.

The motivation for this approach stems from two limitations observed in existing HOI video generation pipelines:
1. **Perspective Limitation:** Much of the progress in HOI video generation has occurred in static, exocentric (third-person) views. These views are not directly applicable to first-person rollouts, which are crucial for embodied AI and robotic applications that require dynamic interactions from the agent's perspective.
2. **Reliance on Privileged Information:** Many contemporary methods heavily condition their outputs on known future object trajectories or waypoints. This effectively bypasses the fundamental challenge of reasoning about contact-driven dynamics, turning the task into a form of conditional video generation. This contradicts the definition of a true world model, which should infer future states and interaction dynamics based solely on current observations and specified actions. The authors contend that such "future-state shortcuts" prevent models from learning the underlying physical dynamics.

To overcome these limitations and build a world model that genuinely infers interaction dynamics from user actions, the authors are motivated to augment the model with physics-informed embeddings. These embeddings are intended to inject explicit metric and kinematic structure into the generative process. The rationale is that recent advancements in 3D foundation models for geometry and hand pose estimation make it practical to extract these crucial 3D priors directly from unstructured videos. By distilling these priors into interpretable estimates of scene, hand, and geometry, the embeddings can provide complementary physical structure (hand kinematics, metric-scale ego motion, object integrity) to regularize egocentric rollouts toward physically valid dynamics. This allows EgoHOI to achieve causal simulation of physically plausible hand-object interactions while maintaining the generative diversity and reconstruction quality typically associated with models trained on large datasets.

The specific contributions of EgoHOI are outlined as:
- Introducing an egocentric HOI world model that simulates first-person hand–object interaction dynamics under user-specified actions and controls, without privileged future object states.
- Developing physics-informed embeddings derived from 3D geometry and kinematic priors to regularize egocentric HOI rollouts toward physically consistent interaction dynamics.
- Demonstrating that EgoHOI improves alignment of rollouts with user actions and enhances hand–object interaction realism, supported by comprehensive metrics and ablation studies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoHOI formulates egocentric hand-object interaction (HOI) synthesis as an action-driven world modeling problem operating within a compact latent state space.

#### 3.1. World Model Formulation

The world model `f_θ` captures internal state dynamics driven by actions, enabling rollouts by iteratively predicting state evolution. An internal state `x_t` transitions to `x_t+1` based on `x_t` and action `a_t`. An observation function `g_ϕ` maps the internal state to observable signals `o_t`. Given an initial observation `o_1`, an encoder `e_ϕ` produces `x_1`. The goal is to predict future frames by recursively applying `f_θ` under an action sequence and mapping the resulting latent states via `g_ϕ`.

In EgoHOI, the world model instantiates `f_θ` using a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, which handles the action-driven evolution of the model’s internal state in a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) latent space. The VAE encoder (`e_ϕ`) and decoder (`g_ϕ`) manage the mapping between video frames and their latent representations. The action signals at each step `t` consist of hand kinematics `H_t` and metric head motion `C_t`.

#### 3.2. Diffusion Process

The generative process relies on a diffusion model, which iteratively denoises random noise to generate samples. A latent representation `z_0` of a clean data sample is obtained from a VAE. A forward process gradually adds Gaussian noise to `z_0` over `T` steps, creating `z_t`. The reverse process learns to invert this by predicting and removing the noise, `ε_θ(z_t, t)`, from `z_t` to reconstruct `z_0`.

#### 3.3. Physics-Informed Embeddings

To guide HOI rollouts without privileged future object states, EgoHOI distills 3D geometric and kinematic estimates into compact physics-informed embeddings. Three types of embeddings are derived:
- **Hand Kinematic Embeddings (HKE):** These capture dense hand motion structure from reconstructed 3D hand estimates. Instead of sparse coordinate vectors, explicit per-frame hand renders from reconstructed meshes serve as a dense 3D representation. These renders are processed by a temporal 3D convolutional stack with SiLU activations, which maps the hand stream to the shared latent space. A reference-hand branch, encoding the first-frame hand pose via a 2D convolutional pyramid, is used to stabilize hand identity and reduce temporal drift.
- **Ego-Motion Embeddings (EME):** EME encodes metric head trajectory estimates to enable geometry-consistent viewpoint evolution. Head pose sequences (intrinsics `K` and extrinsics `E`) are converted into Plücker embeddings, which represent a per-pixel ray field invariant to scene origin and preserving metric structure. This ray-based representation avoids collapsing geometry into a low-dimensional pose vector. The Plücker tensors are stacked into a trajectory, processed by causal 3D convolutions and hybrid spatiotemporal blocks (2D residual convolutions with temporal self-attention), and then mapped into the latent grid via a projection head and patchifier.
- **Object Entity Embeddings (OEE):** OEE anchors each object to the first frame to stabilize its appearance, color, texture, and fine details under rapid ego motion and partial occlusions. The first-frame object segmentation image (obtained from an off-the-shelf segmentation model) isolates object-centric evidence. This segmentation is encoded by the frozen video VAE encoder into a latent grid, and a 3D convolutional patchifier converts it into tokens aligned with the main latent stream. Shifted RoPE (Rotary Position Embeddings) are assigned to spatially anchor these tokens as an independent reference stream.

#### 3.4. Latent Space Integration

Physics-informed embeddings are incorporated into a frozen Wan-DiT backbone through lightweight adapters in a single token-space forward pass. Each embedding stream is aligned to the main token layout and shared token dimension.
- **Action-Driven Token (HKE):** Hand tokens (`T_HKE`) are fused into the main token stream via a gated residual update. The reference-hand feature is added to the first-frame latent and concatenated with the noise latent before patchification.
- **Ego-Motion Token (EME):** Geometry tokens (`T_EME`) are zero-padded for sequence length compatibility. Zero-initialized linear adapters apply residuals to the input of the first `D` DiT blocks, regularizing viewpoint evolution.
- **Object-Entity Token (OEE):** Object entity features from the first frame (`T_OEE`) are patchified and assigned shifted RoPE. These tokens are appended to form an extended sequence and participate as KV-only context during self-attention, while only the main tokens are decoded. This provides a persistent entity reference.
- **First-Frame Anchor:** The first frame is encoded by the Wan VAE encoder, and the reference-hand feature is added. It is then concatenated with the noise latent along the channel dimension. Global image embeddings from a CLIP image encoder are provided as fixed keys and values to the cross-attention layers, preserving overall scene structure and appearance.

#### 3.5. Experiment Setup

- **Backbone:** Wan 2.1 14B, fine-tuned from an official checkpoint with LoRA.
- **Dataset:** HOT3D, using a benchmark of 100 clips (excluded from training) containing 150 frames each, providing hand mesh renders and camera trajectories. 81-frame sub-clips are extracted using a sliding-window scheme.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Visual Prediction:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, Object-CLIP, and VBench scores (Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Motion Smoothness, Dynamic Degree, Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality).
 - **Ego-Motion Consistency:** Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE), Relative Pose Error (RPE), and Relative Rotation Error (RRE) between estimated and ground-truth camera trajectories (using MapAnything).
 - **Kinematic Fidelity:** Missing Ratio (MR), Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE), and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) on hand segmentation images (using HaMeR and Segment Anything for masks).
 - **Object Integrity (additional ablation metric):** Object Position Error (OPE) and 2D Orientation Error (OOE) computed from object masks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EgoHOI included quantitative comparisons with baselines and an ablation study of its physics-informed components.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Baselines

EgoHOI was compared against a Wan backbone, Cosmos world models (2B and 14B), and Uni3C. All baselines were fine-tuned using the same data and protocol.
- **Overall Performance:** EgoHOI achieved the best performance across all evaluated metric groups: frame prediction, ego-motion consistency, and kinematic fidelity.
- **Frame Prediction:** EgoHOI demonstrated a PSNR of 21.05, an SSIM of 0.65, an LPIPS of 0.27, and an Object-CLIP score of 0.92. These values represent improvements compared to baselines, where Cosmos 14B, for example, achieved PSNR 15.89, SSIM 0.59, LPIPS 0.38, and Object-CLIP 0.88.
- **Ego-Motion Consistency:** EgoHOI reported ATE of 0.084, RRE of 5.192, and RPE of 0.021, indicating closer alignment with ground-truth viewpoint changes and reduced accumulated drift. This significantly outperformed baselines; Cosmos 14B had ATE 0.112, RRE 9.046, RPE 0.022, while Uni3C, designed for motion control, exhibited larger errors (ATE 0.168, RRE 14.209, RPE 0.026).
- **Kinematic Fidelity:** EgoHOI achieved a Missing Ratio (MR) of 5.84%, MPJPE of 0.014, and RMSE of 0.044. These results suggest more reliable hand localization and improved joint accuracy compared to Cosmos 14B (MR 14.61%, MPJPE 0.041, RMSE 0.074) and Uni3C (MR 20.50%, MPJPE 0.073, RMSE 0.089).
- **VBench Evaluation:** EgoHOI achieved the highest scores in Subject Consistency (95.51%), Background Consistency (94.91%), Aesthetic Quality (52.03%), and Imaging Quality (64.48%). Its Dynamic Degree (53.29%) was lower than Cosmos models (e.g., Cosmos 14B at 89.50%), which the authors interpreted as prioritizing stable hand-object interactions over highly dynamic camera motion.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 1, 3) indicated that EgoHOI better preserved hand and object geometry, maintained object identity, and produced more stable interaction dynamics over time, with clearer contact details and reduced drift, compared to baselines which showed deformation, contact-region errors, and small-object inconsistencies.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study examined the individual contributions of Hand Kinematic Embeddings (HKE), Ego-Motion Embeddings (EME), and Object Entity Embeddings (OEE).
- **Kinematic Fidelity (HKE):**
 - Compared to the base model (Wan 2.1 DiT without embeddings), HKE injection reduced MR from 28.77% to 6.47% and MPJPE from 0.576 to 0.015. RMSE also improved from 0.089 to 0.044.
 - The full EgoHOI model (with all components) further improved these to MR 5.84%, MPJPE 0.014, and RMSE 0.044, demonstrating HKE's role in stabilizing hand articulation and reducing local shape distortion.
- **Ego-Motion Consistency (EME):**
 - EME injection reduced ATE from 0.133 (base) to 0.096, RRE from 15.249 to 6.525, and RPE from 0.039 to 0.023.
 - The full EgoHOI model achieved the best ego-motion metrics: ATE 0.084, RRE 5.192, RPE 0.021, indicating that EME is a key factor in mitigating long-horizon ego-motion drift and improving viewpoint stability.
- **Object Integrity (OEE):**
 - OEE injection improved Object-CLIP from 0.81 (base) to 0.83, VBench Subject Consistency from 85.26% to 87.90%, and Imaging Quality from 52.55% to 54.75%.
 - Additionally, OEE reduced Object Position Error (OPE) from 0.141 to 0.131 and Object Orientation Error (OOE) from 27.739 to 24.124.
 - The full EgoHOI model yielded further improvements: Object-CLIP 0.92, Subject Consistency 95.51%, Imaging Quality 64.48%, OPE 0.015, and OOE 9.412. This indicates OEE's effectiveness in preserving object identity, appearance, and pose stability.

The ablation studies collectively validate that each physics-informed embedding contributes to improving different aspects of egocentric HOI rollout quality, and their joint integration in EgoHOI leads to superior performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of EgoHOI represents a notable advancement in the development of egocentric world models for embodied AI. Its primary significance lies in its departure from methods that rely on privileged future object states, moving closer to a true simulator that infers interaction dynamics strictly from user actions. This causal simulation capability is crucial for generating data that can serve as a robust and scalable resource for various downstream tasks.

The potential impact of EgoHOI extends to several areas:
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** By providing physically grounded first-person rollouts, EgoHOI can generate synthetic data for training and evaluating agents in tasks requiring dexterous manipulation and navigation. This could reduce the reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world data collection.
- **Vision-Language-Actions (VLA) Models:** The model's ability to simulate complex hand-object interactions can support the development of VLAs that require a deeper understanding of physical interactions and action consequences in egocentric contexts.
- **Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** EgoHOI's improved ego-motion consistency and kinematic fidelity can aid in advancing spatiotemporal reasoning models, which often struggle with the ambiguities of egocentric HOI videos (as noted in the paper with VLM4D).
- **Physical Intelligence:** The explicit incorporation of physics-informed embeddings contributes to building models with a more robust understanding of physical laws and dynamics, which is fundamental for developing physically intelligent systems.
- **Data Augmentation:** The generation of photorealistic, contact-consistent interactions from action signals can serve as a powerful data augmentation tool, especially for scenarios where real-world data is scarce or difficult to obtain.

The methodological innovation of distilling 3D geometric and kinematic priors into physics-informed embeddings and integrating them into a large video diffusion backbone provides an effective inductive bias for egocentric HOI simulation. This approach demonstrates that explicit structural information can lead to more plausible and consistent generative outputs compared to relying solely on visual data scaling or general motion controls. While the current benchmark captures multiple complementary aspects of rollout quality, the authors acknowledge that fully evaluating physical plausibility remains an open problem, suggesting future work should focus on developing more direct contact-aware and dynamics-aware evaluations for first-person hand-object interaction rollouts. This indicates a recognition of ongoing research challenges beyond the scope of the current work, while highlighting the foundational steps taken by EgoHOI.
