# OralGPT-Plus: Learning to Use Visual Tools via Reinforcement Learning for Panoramic X-ray Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> OralGPT-Plus introduces an agentic vision-language model for panoramic dental X-ray analysis, leveraging reinforcement learning to operate visual tools including a novel "Mirror-In" for symmetry-aware comparisons. The system surpassed both state-of-the-art and proprietary large vision models on the new MMOral-X benchmark, achieving higher accuracy across various diagnostic complexities through iterative, clinician-like reasoning.

## Problem

- Interpreting panoramic dental X-rays demands detailed spatial reasoning, understanding bilateral symmetry, and multi-step verification, which static AI models cannot fully replicate.
- Current single-pass Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for medical imaging lack the ability to iteratively refine understanding or re-examine ambiguous regions, limiting diagnostic depth.
- Existing general-purpose agentic VLMs are not tailored for medical diagnostics, lacking domain-specific priors and exhibiting uncontrolled exploration behavior.

## Method

- Introduces **OralGPT-Plus**, an agentic VLM that uses visual tools like "==Zoom-In==" and a novel "Mirror-In" for symmetry-aware comparison in panoramic X-ray analysis.
- Curates DentalProbe, a dataset with over 8,000 expert-guided diagnostic trajectories, to teach multi-step reasoning and tool-use behaviors.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy involving supervised fine-tuning and reinspection-driven reinforcement learning with rubric-based continuous rewards to stabilize long-horizon optimization.

## Results

- OralGPT-Plus-**7B** achieved the highest accuracy on the MMOral-X benchmark, surpassing powerful proprietary and medical-specific VLMs across all diagnostic difficulty levels.
- The model secured the best overall score and excelled in specific diagnostic areas (Teeth, Pathology, Jaw) on the MMOral-OPG benchmark.
- Ablation studies validated the necessity of dentist-like instruction tuning, sufficient model capacity for robust RL, the role of conditioned rewards in preventing reward hacking, and the "Mirror-In" tool's contribution to diagnostic reliability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expert-guided instruction tuning is crucial for teaching agentic models to perform tool-based diagnostic reasoning, as it doesn't emerge from RL alone.
> - Adequate model capacity, as seen with the 7B model, is necessary for reinforcement learning to achieve stable, multi-round tool-based reinspection behaviors.
> - A conditioned diagnostic-driven reward mechanism is vital to prevent "reward hacking" during RL, ensuring tool usage is meaningful and clinically justified.
> - The novel "Mirror-In" tool, leveraging bilateral symmetry, significantly improves diagnostic reliability by aiding verification of subtle, low-contrast findings.
