# ReefMapGS: Enabling Large-Scale Underwater Reconstruction by Closing the Loop Between Multimodal SLAM and Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution developed ReefMapGS, a framework that integrates multimodal SLAM with incremental 3D Gaussian Splatting for large-scale underwater 3D reconstruction. The system achieved the lowest Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE of 0.135m on the Tektite scene and reduced processing time by 70-80% compared to traditional SfM pipelines.

## Problem

- Degraded underwater visual conditions (light attenuation, turbidity, low texture) complicate accurate camera pose estimation and visual feature extraction for Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs).
- Traditional offline Structure-from-Motion (SfM) methods are computationally expensive and impractical for rapid, real-time underwater deployment required for scientific analysis.
- Existing visual SLAM systems are unreliable underwater, while multimodal SLAM generates sparse maps insufficient for detailed analysis, and terrestrial 3DGS-based SLAM systems are not robust to underwater sensor noise and distortion.

## Method

- A closed-loop framework integrates ==multimodal pose-graph SLAM== with ==incremental 3D Gaussian Splatting== (==3DGS==) for mutual refinement of AUV pose and dense scene reconstruction.
- **ReefMapGS** bypasses SfM by initializing Gaussians using a ==fine-tuned monocular depth estimator== and incrementally builds the 3DGS scene from low-uncertainty regions around a known landmark.
- Local camera poses are refined through ==differentiable rendering== against the current 3DGS model, and these refinements are propagated globally by integrating 3DGS-derived pose factors into the factor graph for re-optimization.

## Results

- Achieved an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE of **0.135 m** on the Tektite scene, improving multimodal SLAM accuracy by **58.8%** on Tektite and **19.0%** on Yawzi, outperforming other visual SLAM methods.
- Produced high-fidelity 3DGS reconstructions (e.g., PSNR **22.219** on Tektite) that were competitive with, and in some cases surpassed, results obtained using 'oracle' (SfM-derived) data.
- Reduced processing time to approximately 2.5-3.5 hours per scene, representing a **70-80%** reduction compared to COLMAP (10-17 hours) and Metashape (7-8 hours) for camera pose estimation alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tightly coupling robust multimodal SLAM (for global consistency) with high-fidelity 3DGS (for dense representation and local refinement) effectively addresses the challenges of underwater environments.
> - Incremental 3DGS scene building, starting from regions of high certainty and incorporating uncertainty-aware optimization, enables stable and high-quality reconstruction without relying on traditional SfM.
> - A fine-tuned monocular depth estimator can serve as a viable and efficient substitute for SfM-derived point clouds for 3DGS initialization, reducing hardware and computational requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in ReefMapGS addresses challenges in autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) mapping and 3D reconstruction, particularly within complex underwater environments such as coral reefs. This domain is characterized by degraded visual conditions, including light attenuation, turbidity, color distortion, and low-texture scenes, which significantly complicate visual perception and pose estimation. Furthermore, AUVs often operate without global positioning system (GPS) signals, necessitating robust simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) solutions.

Historically, dense 3D reconstruction in underwater settings has relied on offline Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) pipelines, such as COLMAP or commercial software like Metashape. While these methods can produce high-quality models, they are computationally intensive, making them unsuitable for rapid, real-time field deployments. Visual SLAM systems, designed for incremental pose estimation and mapping, often prove unreliable underwater due to the aforementioned visual degradation, leading to drift or failure.

To enhance robustness in underwater navigation over long trajectories, state-of-the-art underwater SLAM systems commonly integrate multimodal sensors, combining inertial measurement units (IMUs), Doppler Velocity Logs (DVLs), and pressure sensors with visual or acoustic cues within pose-graph optimization frameworks. These systems are effective for trajectory estimation but typically generate sparse maps optimized for navigation rather than the dense, high-fidelity scene representations required for detailed scientific analysis, such as coral reef monitoring.

Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as an efficient and differentiable representation for high-fidelity dense 3D scene reconstruction, also enabling pose refinement through gradient-based optimization. This development has led to 3DGS-based SLAM systems designed for terrestrial environments. However, these systems often assume reliable visual characteristics, consistent illumination, high-quality inputs, significant visual overlap, and sometimes RGB-D sensing capabilities—assumptions that generally do not hold in the challenging underwater domain with noisy and distorted sensor measurements.

ReefMapGS positions itself by bridging the gap between robust multimodal underwater SLAM and high-fidelity 3DGS-based dense reconstruction. It seeks to develop a system that leverages the complementary strengths of these approaches to provide both accurate AUV trajectory estimation and dense scene reconstruction in challenging underwater contexts, without the computational burden of traditional SfM pipelines. The paper addresses the mismatch between existing sparse mapping systems and the need for scalable, dense reconstruction for scientific applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing ReefMapGS stems from the limitations of existing underwater mapping and reconstruction techniques in meeting the demands of modern scientific and ecological tasks. For instance, tasks like coral reef monitoring and large-scale spatiotemporal environmental surveys require rapid acquisition of dense, high-quality 3D scene representations. However, several critical challenges impede this objective:

- **Degraded Underwater Visual Conditions:** The underwater environment severely compromises visual data quality. Light attenuation, turbidity, color distortion, and prevalence of low-texture scenes make accurate camera pose estimation and reliable visual feature extraction difficult.
- **Computational Burden of Traditional Methods:** Offline Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) methods, while capable of high-quality reconstruction, are computationally expensive. This makes them impractical for rapid analysis and deployment in remote field robotics applications where immediate feedback and efficiency are valuable.
- **Limitations of Existing SLAM Approaches:**
 - **Visual SLAM:** Vision-only and visual-inertial SLAM systems are prone to failure or significant drift underwater due to poor visual conditions.
 - **Multimodal SLAM:** While robust for long-horizon trajectory estimation by fusing inertial, velocity, and depth sensors with visual/acoustic cues, these systems typically produce sparse maps. These sparse representations are optimized for navigation and are insufficient for applications requiring detailed environmental information.
- **Inapplicability of Terrestrial 3DGS SLAM:** Recent 3DGS-based SLAM systems, which offer efficient and high-fidelity dense reconstruction, are primarily designed for terrestrial environments. They rely on assumptions such as well-calibrated, illumination-consistent, high-quality inputs with substantial visual overlap, and often RGB-D data. These conditions are rarely met in underwater settings, particularly with low-cost AUV platforms featuring monocular cameras and dome ports, leading to noisier and less precise data.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of the ReefMapGS project are:

1. **Develop a Novel Framework:** Design and implement a framework that tightly integrates multimodal pose-graph SLAM with incremental 3D Gaussian Splatting.
2. **Achieve Large-Scale Reconstruction:** Enable the dense 3D reconstruction of large-scale underwater environments, such as coral reefs, which spans hundreds of meters.
3. **Improve Reconstruction Quality:** Produce high-quality, dense 3DGS reconstructions that surpass or are competitive with methods relying on oracle (SfM-derived) data, even under challenging underwater conditions.
4. **Enhance Pose Estimation Accuracy:** Improve the global accuracy of AUV trajectory estimation compared to multimodal SLAM systems that do not incorporate dense visual information.
5. **Eliminate SfM Dependence:** Create a bundle-adjustment-free pipeline that does not rely on computationally intensive SfM for initial pose estimation or point cloud generation.
6. **Leverage Multimodal Data and Landmarks:** Utilize the complementary strengths of multimodal sensor data (IMU, DVL, pressure, visual) and prior knowledge of a fixed landmark to initiate and constrain the mapping process.
7. **Enable Incremental Operation:** Implement an incremental reconstruction approach that starts from regions of high certainty and progressively expands, suitable for real-time or near real-time field deployment.
8. **Provide a Real-World Dataset:** Contribute a dataset of multimodal robot sensor data from challenging coral reef surveys to facilitate further research in this domain.

The overarching motivation is to provide a practical and efficient solution for reliable, scalable, and high-fidelity underwater mapping, thus overcoming a significant bottleneck for a wide range of scientific and ecological applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReefMapGS proposes a closed-loop framework that synergistically combines multimodal pose-graph SLAM with incremental 3D Gaussian Splatting to achieve accurate robot trajectory estimation and dense scene reconstruction in underwater environments. The system is designed for AUVs performing surveys around a fixed, known landmark, typically in a rosette-shaped trajectory.

**A. Problem Formulation and Sensor Data:**
The system aims to estimate the robot's camera poses (X) and the landmark's position (L) by fusing asynchronous multimodal sensor data: monocular RGB images, linear velocities from a DVL, angular velocities and linear accelerations from an IMU, and depth measurements from a pressure sensor.

**B. Pose-Graph Optimization for Trajectory Estimation:**
The robot's trajectory is estimated as a factor graph optimization problem, maximizing the posterior probability of poses and landmark positions given sensor measurements.
1. **Odometry Estimation:** Raw IMU data is pre-processed with a complementary filter for stable orientation. An Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) fuses the filtered IMU data, depth, and DVL velocity measurements to provide odometry estimates.
2. **Factor Graph Construction:** Odometry factors and landmark measurement factors (from visual detection of a known landmark) are incorporated into the graph. Robust noise models (e.g., Huber) are used to manage outliers.
3. **Optimization:** The factor graph is solved using Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) optimization to yield an initial estimate of the robot's trajectory.

**C. Incremental 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):**
ReefMapGS employs a 3DGS representation, where the scene is parameterized by isotropic 3D Gaussians with zero-order spherical harmonics for color (mean, scale, opacity, color). Unlike conventional 3DGS methods that rely on SfM for initial poses and point clouds, ReefMapGS adopts a novel, SfM-free incremental approach.

1. **Gaussian Initialization (COLMAP-free):**
 - To bypass SfM, a pre-trained DepthAnythingV2 model, fine-tuned to output metric depth for underwater benthic imagery, is used. This model is fine-tuned by fitting dense depth from a relative depth estimator with sparse point clouds derived from Metashape.
 - The metric depth estimates from this monocular depth estimator are used to initialize new Gaussians in the 3DGS scene.

2. **Incremental Scene Building:**
 - Leveraging the known landmark, the 3DGS scene is built incrementally, starting from regions of lowest pose uncertainty (near the landmark).
 - The robot trajectory is discretized into "rings" of paired poses and image observations based on their distance from the central landmark. These rings are progressively integrated into the 3DGS scene.

3. **Local Camera Pose Refinement:**
 - Before incorporating new observations from a frontier ring, their pose estimates are locally refined. Given the current 3DGS model of the partial scene, a new image observation, and its initial pose estimate from the factor graph, the camera pose is refined by minimizing the reconstruction error through differentiable rendering. Gradients are propagated through the 3DGS model back to the pose parameters. This process requires visual overlap between the observed image and the rendered image from the current 3DGS model.

4. **Global Robot Trajectory Refinement (Closing the Loop):**
 - Improvements from local pose refinement are propagated globally by integrating the refined camera poses as 3DGS-derived factors into the original factor graph.
 - A cost term is added to the factor graph objective, penalizing deviations between the refined 3DGS poses and the pose variables in the graph, with predefined uncertainty.
 - The entire factor graph is then re-optimized, combining the original sensor factors with these new 3DGS camera factors. This global optimization primarily occurs when the average uncertainty of camera poses at the frontier region has not significantly increased from the central, low-uncertainty seed region, allowing the 3DGS model to correct biases in the factor graph. This step reconciles redundant visual structures and corrects accumulated errors over the extended trajectory.

5. **3DGS Model Optimization:**
 - The 3DGS map (G) is optimized using a modified uncertainty-aware reconstruction loss. This loss includes an L1 term, modulated by an uncertainty map (β) predicted by a shallow MLP from DINOv2-based 3D-aware features, and a Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) loss term.
 - An additional edge-aware total variation loss on rendered depth (L_Z_TV) promotes smoothness while preserving details along object edges.
 - The uncertainty MLP (P) is jointly optimized with the 3DGS map using a modified SSIM loss and regularization terms to prevent degenerate uncertainty predictions (minimizing variance for similar features, preventing uniformly high uncertainty).

**D. Dataset Collection:**
The framework was evaluated on data collected from two coral reef sites (Tektite, Yawzi) in the US Virgin Islands using CUREE, a low-cost AUV. The vehicle was equipped with a 4K downward-facing monocular camera, DVL, IMU, and pressure sensor. Fixed rosette trajectories were followed around an AprilTag landmark. Images were white-balanced, converted to sRGB, rectified, and center-cropped to mitigate distortion and handle low overlap.

**E. Evaluation Metrics:**
Performance was assessed using:
- **Visual Fidelity:** Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) to evaluate reconstruction quality.
- **Pose Accuracy:** Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE RMSE) in meters, comparing estimated trajectories against Metashape-derived ground truth. Trajectory length was also reported to contextualize RMSE.

**F. Comparative Baselines and Ablations:**
The framework was compared against various visual SLAM methods (ORB-SLAM3, DROID-SLAM, MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM, MonoGS, WildGS-SLAM) and a multimodal landmark SLAM baseline (GTSAM). Ablation studies investigated the impact of different input data permutations (Metashape vs. GTSAM poses, SfM vs. monodepth initialization) and 3DGS optimization strategies (batch vs. incremental, with/without global re-optimization).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ReefMapGS demonstrated its capabilities in both pose estimation accuracy and 3D reconstruction quality in challenging underwater environments.

**A. Pose Estimation Accuracy:**
- **Superior ATE RMSE:** ReefMapGS achieved an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE of 0.135 m on the Tektite scene, marking the lowest among all tested methods. On the Yawzi scene, it achieved an RMSE of 0.229 m, which was the second lowest (behind ORB-SLAM3's very short trajectory). Importantly, ReefMapGS consistently estimated trajectory lengths close to the reference, unlike some methods that achieved low RMSE over only a fraction of the path.
- **Improvement over Multimodal SLAM:** ReefMapGS improved upon the ATE RMSE of the GTSAM multimodal SLAM baseline (which does not integrate dense visual information) by 58.8% on Tektite and 19.0% on Yawzi. This indicates that incorporating a dense 3DGS-based map representation and its derived pose factors provides a significant signal for more accurate global trajectory estimation.
- **Challenges for Learning-Based and Other Visual SLAM:** Learning-based methods (DROID-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM, MASt3R-SLAM, WildGS-SLAM) and some 3DGS-based systems (MonoGS) encountered substantial difficulties. Their RMSE values were typically an order of magnitude higher (in the 4-5 m range), and they often failed to track the entire trajectory or struggled with sharp turns in the rosette pattern. This performance degradation was attributed to the out-of-distribution characteristics of the underwater data, including a top-down viewpoint, narrow field of view, low overlap, and unique semantic content, which differ significantly from typical training datasets.
- **Metric Scale Trajectories:** GTSAM and ReefMapGS provided close to metric-scale trajectories due to their fusion of inertial, velocity, and depth measurements, a capability often lacking in purely visual methods.

**B. 3D Reconstruction Quality:**
- **High Fidelity Reconstruction:** ReefMapGS (configured as GTSAM+md+inc+reopt) demonstrated high reconstruction quality, achieving PSNR values of 22.219 (Tektite) and 21.226 (Yawzi), SSIM values of 0.567 (Tektite) and 0.551 (Yawzi), and LPIPS values of 0.721 (Tektite) and 0.777 (Yawzi). These metrics were competitive with, and in some cases surpassed, results obtained using "oracle" data configurations (e.g., Metashape+sfm+batch), which rely on perfectly accurate poses and SfM-derived point clouds.
- **Effectiveness of Incremental Optimization:** The incremental 3DGS optimization strategy (+inc) consistently led to a significant increase in reconstruction quality across all input data scenarios, including when initialized with SfM data. This highlights the method's effectiveness in maintaining scene coherence by optimizing from high-certainty regions and robustly aligning new observations.
- **Monocular Depth for Initialization:** Initializing Gaussians using data from the monocular depth estimator (+md) resulted in only a slight, rather than catastrophic, decrease in quality compared to using SfM point clouds. This outcome validates the utility of monocular depth estimators as a viable alternative for 3DGS initialization when stereo vision or dedicated depth sensors are unavailable or ineffective in low-texture underwater conditions.
- **Impact of Global Re-optimization:** The global re-optimization of the factor graph using 3DGS camera pose factors (+reopt) further increased reconstruction quality. This suggests that propagating local pose refinements globally helps reconcile redundant visual structures from different parts of the trajectory and corrects accumulated pose errors that could otherwise lead to inconsistencies in the dense reconstruction.

**C. Running Time:**
- **Reduced Processing Time:** ReefMapGS significantly reduced processing time compared to traditional SfM pipelines. It required approximately 2 hours and 30 minutes for the Tektite scene and 3 hours and 22 minutes for the Yawzi scene. In contrast, COLMAP required 10-17 hours, and commercial Metashape required 7-8 hours for camera pose estimation alone.
- **Efficiency Compared to Other 3DGS Systems:** ReefMapGS was 2-3 times faster than other 3DGS-based SLAM systems like WildGS-SLAM.
- **Trade-off with Learning-Based Methods:** While learning-based and classical methods processed scenes in mere minutes, they failed to produce coherent or accurate outputs for the datasets used. The local pose refinement component of ReefMapGS was identified as the slowest part of its pipeline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ReefMapGS represents a notable advancement in the field of underwater robotics and environmental mapping, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enabling Large-Scale, High-Quality Underwater Mapping:** ReefMapGS provides a practical framework for generating dense, high-quality 3D reconstructions of challenging underwater environments over large scales (hundreds of meters). This capability is crucial for scientific and ecological tasks such as detailed coral reef monitoring, habitat mapping, and geomorphological surveys, where fine-grained spatial information is essential for analysis.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between SLAM and Dense Reconstruction:** The framework effectively closes the loop between robust multimodal pose-graph SLAM and the high-fidelity representation offered by 3D Gaussian Splatting. By allowing pose estimation and dense scene reconstruction to mutually refine each other, ReefMapGS addresses a fundamental mismatch in previous systems, which typically treated dense mapping as a separate, often post-processing, step or used sparse maps unsuitable for detailed analysis.

3. **Enhanced Accuracy in Challenging Environments:** The ability of ReefMapGS to achieve lower Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE and higher reconstruction quality than several baselines, particularly in visually degraded underwater conditions, demonstrates a substantial improvement in handling real-world field challenges. This is especially impactful for AUV operations where visual inputs are unreliable and GPS is unavailable.

4. **Efficiency and Field Deployment Suitability:** By eliminating the reliance on computationally intensive Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines, ReefMapGS significantly reduces processing time. This makes the system more suitable for rapid deployment and analysis in remote field contexts where computational resources are often limited, enabling quicker data turnaround for scientific expeditions.

5. **Resource-Efficient Initialization:** The integration of a fine-tuned monocular depth estimator for initializing Gaussians offers a cost-effective alternative to stereo vision or dedicated depth sensors. This reduces hardware requirements and broadens the applicability of dense 3D mapping to a wider range of low-cost AUV platforms.

6. **Robustness to Data Limitations:** The incremental, uncertainty-aware approach, combined with global re-optimization of the pose graph, enhances the system's robustness to noisy initial pose estimates, input image distortions, and limited visual overlap—common characteristics of underwater datasets that cause other visual SLAM methods to fail.

7. **Foundation for Future Autonomy:** The framework lays groundwork for future developments in autonomous underwater systems. Potential extensions include enabling mapping in environments without fixed landmarks, integrating more sophisticated global re-optimization strategies, and ultimately, real-time deployment onboard AUVs for adaptive sampling or inspection tasks.

In summary, ReefMapGS offers a pathway toward more reliable, accurate, and efficient large-scale underwater mapping, directly supporting critical scientific research and environmental monitoring efforts by providing a detailed understanding of complex subsea ecosystems.
