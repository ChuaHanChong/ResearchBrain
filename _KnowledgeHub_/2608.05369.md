# World-to-Wrist: Task-Conditioned Future Wrist Modeling for Fine-Grained Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The W²-VLA framework introduces a method for Vision-Language-Action models to anticipate future wrist-local dynamics by conditioning on global task context, leading to superior performance in fine-grained and contact-sensitive robot manipulation across various simulated and real-world tasks. It integrates a dedicated wrist predictor and a novel Chain-of-Thought synthesis pipeline for auxiliary supervision, enabling real-time action generation.

## Problem

- Multi-view VLA models often treat main-view and wrist-view observations as equally important, neglecting the distinct, action-proximal role of wrist observations for fine-grained tasks.
- Existing future prediction in visuomotor models typically targets global visual subgoals or unified latent representations, without isolating and modeling the temporal evolution of local interactions around the end effector.
- Purely wrist-centered predictions can be ambiguous without global task context to disambiguate and identify the task-relevant future states.

## Method

- **W²-VLA** develops an architecture that explicitly models future wrist dynamics, conditioned on global task context through a ==compact, fixed-length latent interface== generated by the VLM.
- A dedicated ==wrist predictor==, utilizing a ==V-JEPA== encoder and a bidirectional Transformer, forecasts future wrist latents, focusing on action-relevant changes without requiring pixel-level reconstruction.
- A structured ==Chain-of-Thought (W²-CoT)== annotation synthesis pipeline provides auxiliary supervision to ground the task-conditioned latent interface with manipulation progress, physical transition cues, and wrist-local evidence during training, without runtime inference overhead.

## Results

- W²-VLA achieved an average **98.5%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, a **1.3 percentage point** improvement over the strongest baseline, and a **60.71%** success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 (clean setting), surpassing the baseline by **7.79 percentage points**.
- In real-world evaluations, W²-VLA attained an average **70.00%** success rate under standard conditions and **52.22%** under out-of-distribution conditions, outperforming VLA-JEPA by **15.56** and **14.44 percentage points** respectively across three complex tasks.
- The framework demonstrates high inference efficiency, generating a 16-step action chunk in **183 ms** (**87.43 Hz**), and ablation studies confirmed the synergistic benefits of the wrist predictor and W²-CoT auxiliary supervision for enhanced performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly connecting global task context with anticipatory local wrist dynamics via a “World-to-Wrist” pathway significantly enhances robot performance in fine-grained and contact-sensitive manipulation.
> - A compact, fixed-length latent interface effectively facilitates task-conditioned future wrist prediction and maintains real-time action generation, outperforming approaches that decode explicit Chain-of-Thought at inference.
> - Targeted prediction of only wrist-view future latents proves more effective for action-relevant supervision compared to main-view or joint view prediction, emphasizing the importance of end-effector-centric dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of robot manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLA models aim to enable robots to understand language instructions and visual observations to perform complex tasks. Recent advancements in this area include large-scale pretraining, cross-embodiment transfer, and continuous action generation, leading to improved generality and control capabilities.

The paper identifies a limitation in many existing multi-view VLA models, which often treat different camera views (e.g., main-view and wrist-view) as equally parallel visual inputs. This approach may overlook the distinct and action-proximal role of wrist observations, particularly for fine-grained manipulation. Previous research in multi-view modeling has explored cross-view attention or adaptive weighting, and future-aware prediction methods have forecasted visual subgoals, future observations, or latent representations. However, these often generalize over the global scene or unified policy representations without isolating temporal changes specific to the end effector.

W²-VLA differentiates itself by explicitly modeling the "World-to-Wrist" pathway, connecting global task context with future wrist-local dynamics. It proposes task-conditioned future wrist modeling as a dedicated prediction target, distinct from global scene prediction. Furthermore, while intermediate supervision and structured grounding have been shown to enhance VLA models (e.g., through visual goals, textual reasoning, or spatial representations), W²-VLA introduces a novel Chain-of-Thought (CoT) synthesis pipeline (W²-CoT) that provides auxiliary supervision for manipulation progress, physical transition cues, and wrist-local evidence. This supervision aims to shape a compact, fixed-length task-conditioned interface without requiring CoT generation during inference, which contrasts with approaches that explicitly decode intermediate reasoning steps at runtime.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for fine-grained robot manipulation by enabling them to anticipate future wrist-local interactions within a global task context. The motivation stems from several identified limitations in current VLA approaches:

1. **Undifferentiated Treatment of Multi-View Observations:** Existing multi-view VLA models commonly process main-view and wrist-view observations as equivalent visual inputs. The main view provides global context (scene layout, object identity, task progress), but wrist observations are crucial for revealing rapidly changing gripper-object interactions around the end effector. This distinction is particularly critical for tasks requiring precise contact or delicate object handling, such as plug insertion. Treating all views equally can hinder the model's ability to focus on the action-proximal dynamics essential for fine-grained control.

2. **Lack of Dedicated Future Wrist Modeling:** While some visuomotor models incorporate future prediction, these predictions often target global visual subgoals, entire future observations, or unified latent representations. They typically do not isolate and model the temporal evolution of local interactions specifically around the end effector. A dedicated, wrist-centered latent prediction can capture how local interactions may evolve beyond the current state, focusing on end-effector-relevant changes without requiring pixel-level reconstruction.

3. **Task-Ambiguity in Wrist-Centered Prediction:** Purely wrist-centered prediction can be ambiguous. Similar wrist histories might correspond to multiple plausible future states (e.g., an approach could lead to grasping or pushing). Global task context is necessary to disambiguate and identify the task-relevant future. This necessitates a mechanism to bridge the global task understanding (from the main VLM) with the local wrist prediction.

4. **Need for Efficient and Grounded Interface:** To address the task ambiguity, a compact and task-conditioned interface is required to connect the VLM's global context to future wrist prediction. This interface should be effectively shaped by structured semantic supervision that grounds the model's understanding in manipulation progress, physical transition cues, and wrist-local evidence, without incurring high inference latency.

To address these points, the research proposes W²-VLA, which aims to:
- Develop a VLA architecture that explicitly models future wrist dynamics conditioned on global task context.
- Introduce a compact, fixed-length latent interface that facilitates communication between the VLM and a wrist predictor.
- Design a structured annotation synthesis pipeline (W²-CoT) to provide auxiliary supervision that grounds the task-conditioned latent interface, without impacting real-time inference.
- Demonstrate improved performance in fine-grained and contact-sensitive manipulation tasks across various robotic setups, including real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

W²-VLA integrates task-conditioned future wrist modeling into a VLA framework, comprising a Vision-Language Model (VLM), a wrist predictor, and a flow-matching action head. The methodology is structured around four main components:

#### 3.1. Task-Conditioned Latent Modeling Interface

The interface is instantiated by a set of `K` dedicated latent modeling tokens. At each control step `t`, given current multi-view observations `O_t` (main-view `I_m_t` and wrist-view `I_w_t`) and a language instruction `ℓ`, these tokens are appended to the instruction prompt. The VLM, `F_VLM_θ`, processes this combined input to contextualize the latent modeling tokens. Their final-layer hidden states, denoted `S_t ∈ R^(K×d)`, form a fixed-length task-conditioned interface, where `d` is the VLM hidden dimension. This interface serves as a compact representation connecting global task context to the wrist predictor and is influenced by auxiliary annotation supervision, future wrist prediction, and action generation objectives during training.

#### 3.2. Task-Conditioned Future Wrist Prediction

The wrist branch focuses on predicting task-relevant future wrist dynamics.
1. **Wrist Encoding:** A frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encoder, `E_ϕ`, maps a historical wrist-view clip `I_w_t,hist` (ending at step `t`) to historical wrist tokens `Z_w_t,hist`. Similarly, during training, a future target clip `I_w_t,fut` is encoded to future target tokens `Z_w_t,fut`. For multiple wrist views, per-view tokens are concatenated.
2. **Latent Prediction:** The task-conditioned interface states `S_t` are repeated across the V-JEPA latent time steps and processed alongside the historical wrist tokens by a bidirectional Transformer, `G_ψ`. This predictor forecasts the future wrist latents, denoted `b_Z_w_t,fut`. The training objective, `L_wrist`, minimizes the L1 loss between `b_Z_w_t,fut` and the detached ground-truth `Z_w_t,fut`, ensuring that the predictor learns wrist-view state transitions without reconstructing pixel-level observations. The `sg(·)` stop-gradient operator prevents the `L_wrist` objective from directly updating the V-JEPA encoder.
3. **Future-Aware Wrist Context Adapter:** To provide a compact input to the action head, a Q-Former-style adapter `A_ω` with `M` learnable queries `Q_w` processes the predicted future wrist latents `sg(b_Z_w_t,fut)`. This adapter extracts a fixed number of future-aware wrist context tokens `C_w_t` and projects them to the VLM hidden dimension. A stop-gradient operator on `b_Z_w_t,fut` prevents the action objective from updating the wrist predictor through this adapter path.

#### 3.3. Structured W²-CoT Annotation Synthesis and Auxiliary Supervision

To shape the VLM's task-conditioned representations, W²-VLA employs a pipeline to synthesize structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations. Each annotation consists of three fields:
1. **Subtask:** Describes the current manipulation stage and its progress.
2. **Reasoning:** Summarizes the robot-centric physical transition based on state-action and visual evidence (e.g., approach-to-contact, grasp stabilization).
3. **Wrist:** Records wrist-local evidence (e.g., target proximity, fingertip contact, alignment). For bimanual tasks, it specifies evidence for each gripper.

The synthesis pipeline involves:
- **Segment Identification:** Candidate manipulation segments are identified based on gripper openness, end-effector motion, and action changes. Visual keyframes are sampled around these boundaries.
- **VLM Annotation:** An offline VLM annotator generates a structured proposal for each segment, conditioned on the task instruction, synchronized multi-view frames, and state-action evidence.
- **Verification and Normalization:** Proposals undergo deterministic checks for gripper-state consistency, temporal ordering, release preconditions, and wrist locality. Spurious stages are suppressed, and annotations are normalized into a consistent language style.
- **Frame-Level Propagation:** Verified segment-level annotations are propagated to individual frames.

During training, an auxiliary next-token prediction objective, `L_cot`, is optimized over the structured annotation sequence `y*_t`. This objective supervises the VLM and encourages the latent modeling interface `S_t` to capture manipulation progress, physical transition cues, and wrist-local evidence. Importantly, W²-CoT decoding is not required during inference.

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Inference

1. **Action Head Conditioning:** The VLM's action-conditioning context `H_act_t` is formed by selecting visual, instruction, and latent modeling states. This context is concatenated with the future-aware wrist context `C_w_t` to form `C_t`.
2. **Flow-Matching Action Generation:** A DiT-based flow-matching action head, `Π_η`, generates an action chunk `b_A_t` by integrating a conditional velocity field `D_η`. The action objective `L_act` uses a flow-matching loss to train the action head.
3. **Joint Objective:** The overall training loss `L` is a weighted sum of the action generation loss `L_act`, the CoT prediction loss `L_cot`, and the future wrist latent prediction loss `L_wrist`.
4. **Inference Procedure:** At inference, the model receives current observations, wrist history, and instruction. The VLM generates `H_act_t` and `S_t` in a single pass. `S_t` conditions the wrist predictor to forecast future wrist latents, which are converted to `C_w_t` by the adapter. The fused context `C_t` then conditions `Π_η` to generate the action chunk. This process does not involve future wrist observations or autoregressive W²-CoT decoding, enabling real-time action generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of W²-VLA encompassed extensive experiments across simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world manipulation tasks, assessing performance, component contributions, inference efficiency, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** W²-VLA achieved an average success rate of 98.5% across the four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), surpassing the strongest reported baseline by 1.3 percentage points. It demonstrated the highest success rates on Spatial (99.6%), Object (99.8%), and Goal (99.2%) suites. On the Long suite, which involves temporally extended manipulation, W²-VLA achieved 95.2%, maintaining competitive performance. These results suggest that integrating task-conditioned future-wrist modeling can enhance performance without compromising broad task generalization.

- **RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark:** For bimanual manipulation, W²-VLA obtained an average success rate of 60.71% under the clean (Easy) setting, outperforming the strongest baseline by 7.79 percentage points. Under the domain-randomized (Hard) setting, W²-VLA achieved 18.21% success, exceeding the strongest reported baseline by 1.87 percentage points. These gains in both clean and domain-randomized environments indicate the benefit of conditioning wrist-local prediction on the current task context, especially for complex bimanual tasks and robustness to variations.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation

Experiments were conducted on the CoBoT Magic platform for three tasks: Table Cleaning (long-horizon), Occluded Placement (global-to-local coordination), and Bimanual Plug Insertion (fine-grained bimanual manipulation).

- **Standard Conditions:** W²-VLA achieved the highest success rate on all three tasks, averaging 70.00%. This outperformed VLA-JEPA and π₀ by 15.56 and 28.89 percentage points, respectively.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Conditions:** Under OOD scenarios (table clutter, random lighting, background variations), W²-VLA maintained the highest success rate across all tasks, averaging 52.22%. It exceeded VLA-JEPA by 14.44 percentage points, with a notable margin in Bimanual Plug Insertion (33.33% vs. 10.00% for VLA-JEPA).
- **Progress Score and Failure Analysis:** W²-VLA consistently achieved higher progress scores on every task and setting, indicating more frequent partial completion even when full task success was not achieved. For Bimanual Plug Insertion, W²-VLA improved progress scores from 1.86 to 2.60 (standard) and 1.53 to 2.27 (OOD) compared to VLA-JEPA, suggesting it reaches contact-sensitive stages more reliably.
- **Inference Efficiency:** W²-VLA generated a 16-step action chunk in 183 ms, resulting in an action-generation rate of 87.43 Hz. This throughput supports real-time deployment, aligning with or exceeding requirements for robotic control.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Component Contributions:**
 - Removing the Wrist Predictor reduced the average success rate on LIBERO from 98.5% to 97.5%, with the largest drop on the Long suite (95.2% to 93.6%). This indicates the utility of future wrist prediction, especially for extended tasks.
 - Removing W²-CoT supervision reduced the average success rate to 98.0%. This suggests that structured auxiliary supervision helps shape the task-conditioned interface. The combination of both components yielded the strongest performance.
- **Interface Design and Inference Efficiency:** Explicit CoT decoding at inference time led to significantly higher latency (over 1.5 seconds per action chunk). In contrast, the fixed latent modeling token interface reduced latency by an order of magnitude while achieving comparable or higher success rates. The 16-token configuration offered the best balance of performance (98.5%) and latency (110.58 ms).
- **Future-View Prediction Targets:** Predicting only wrist-view future latents yielded the strongest results (98.5% average success). Predicting main-view or joint main/wrist-view future latents resulted in slightly lower performance and/or increased computational cost, suggesting that focusing the predictive objective on wrist-local dynamics is more effective for action-relevant supervision.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

- **Attention Visualization:** Visualizations showed that the latent modeling tokens' attention dynamically shifts to task-relevant regions in main and wrist views, such as target objects, active grippers, and contact areas. This suggests that the task-conditioned interface effectively draws on both global and wrist-local evidence to guide future wrist prediction.
- **Wrist Predictor Effectiveness:** The V-JEPA wrist predictor demonstrated improved latent-token mean squared error (0.749 vs. 2.183 for a copy-current baseline) and cosine similarity (0.888 vs. 0.699), indicating its ability to predict future wrist latents that closely align with ground-truth future observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The W²-VLA framework contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing specific limitations in how VLA models process multi-view observations for fine-grained control. Its significance and potential impact include:

1. **Enhanced Fine-Grained Manipulation:** By explicitly modeling the "World-to-Wrist" pathway, W²-VLA improves the robot's ability to perform tasks requiring precise local interactions, contact sensitivity, and close coordination with global task understanding. This is crucial for applications such as assembly, delicate object handling, and precise insertion tasks.

2. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world tasks, including challenging out-of-distribution conditions, suggest that W²-VLA offers a more robust and generalizable approach to robot manipulation. The enhanced performance in bimanual tasks indicates its applicability to complex multi-arm scenarios.

3. **Efficient Task-Conditioned Foresight:** The introduction of a compact, fixed-length task-conditioned latent interface for future wrist modeling provides a mechanism for foresight without incurring high computational costs during inference. This design allows for real-time action generation (over 80 Hz), making it suitable for practical robotic deployments.

4. **Novel Auxiliary Supervision Mechanism:** The W²-CoT synthesis pipeline provides structured, evidence-grounded auxiliary supervision for manipulation progress, physical transitions, and wrist-local evidence. This method shapes the VLM's latent representations during training without requiring explicit CoT decoding at inference, avoiding the latency issues often associated with language-based reasoning at runtime.

5. **Targeted Predictive Learning:** By demonstrating that dedicated wrist-only future latent prediction is more effective than main-view or joint view prediction, the research highlights the importance of focusing predictive objectives on action-relevant, end-effector dynamics. This insight could guide future research in designing more efficient and effective predictive control policies.

In summary, W²-VLA offers a method for VLA models to integrate global task context with anticipatory local wrist dynamics effectively. This integration enhances performance in fine-grained and contact-sensitive manipulation, contributes to the development of more generalist and robust robot policies, and does so within practical latency constraints for real-world deployment.
