# Emu3.5: Native Multimodal Models are World Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> BAAI's Emu3.5 introduces a native multimodal model trained extensively on internet videos, capable of understanding and generating interleaved vision-language sequences across long horizons. The model achieves strong performance in various multimodal tasks, including complex image generation with accurate text rendering and embodied AI scenarios, while accelerating image inference by approximately 20x using a novel Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) technique.

## Problem

- Large language models are inherently limited by their text-only input, offering a restricted view of the world and hindering comprehensive understanding.
- Many existing vision-language models employ separate encoders or adapter-based approaches, lacking deep, native integration of vision and language for unified prediction.
- Autoregressive models suffer from slow, token-by-token inference, creating a practical bottleneck for generating high-resolution images in real-time applications.

## Method

- Develop ==Emu3.5==, a 34.1 billion parameter decoder-only transformer for native multimodal prediction of the 'next state across vision and language' via a ==unified next-token prediction objective==, pre-trained on over 13 trillion tokens from ~63 million internet videos.
- Introduce ==Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA)==, a technique that converts autoregressive image generation into bidirectional parallel prediction, significantly accelerating inference.
- Apply extensive post-training through two-stage supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) guided by ==comprehensive multimodal rewards== to enhance reasoning and generation.

## Results

- Emu3.5 achieves strong performance in Any-to-Image (X2I) generation, including open-world image editing, precise control, and complex text-rich image rendering, outperforming various state-of-the-art T2I models on benchmarks like LeX-Bench and CVTG-2K.
- The model demonstrates generalizable world-modeling abilities, achieving a **65.5%** win rate against Gemini 2.5 Flash Image for world exploration and a **67.1%** win rate for embodied manipulation in automated preference evaluations.
- Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) accelerates per-image inference by approximately **20x** (reducing generation time for a 1024x1024 image from **120s** to **10s**) without sacrificing performance on T2I and X2I tasks, making it competitive with fast-sampling diffusion models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Native multimodal architectures trained on vast, high-quality, filtered internet video and long-horizon interleaved vision-language data move closer to a 'world model' capturing temporal dynamics and rich context, with compute scaling yielding progressively stronger generalization.
> - The Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) method effectively transforms sequential autoregressive image generation into an efficient parallel process, addressing a major practical limitation for high-resolution output.
> - A unified post-training strategy, incorporating large-scale SFT and RL with a comprehensive multimodal reward system, is crucial for aligning model outputs with human preferences and improving generalization across diverse tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Emu3.5 emerges within a rapidly evolving landscape of multimodal AI, where the goal is to create models that can understand and generate content across different modalities, primarily vision and language, in a unified and coherent manner. This work directly addresses several key trends and challenges:

- **Beyond Text-Centric LLMs:** While large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in linguistic reasoning, they are inherently limited by their text-only input, offering a restricted view of the world. Emu3.5, as a native multimodal model, seeks to overcome this by integrating vision and language at a foundational level, acknowledging that humans acquire knowledge from rich, spatiotemporally extended multimodal experiences, especially videos.
- **Native Multimodal Integration vs. Adapters:** Many existing vision-language models (VLMs) operate by using separate encoders for different modalities or by adapting pre-trained LLMs with visual components. Emu3.5, however, emphasizes "native" multimodal prediction of the "next state across vision and language" using a unified next-token prediction objective. This suggests a more deeply integrated architecture designed for joint understanding and generation of interleaved sequences, rather than a modular or adapter-based approach.
- **World Models and Embodied AI:** The paper explicitly frames Emu3.5 as a "multimodal world model." This aligns with a growing research direction aiming to build AI systems that can learn comprehensive representations of the world, including its dynamics, causality, and physics, to enable more generalizable reasoning and interaction. The focus on "world exploration" and "embodied manipulation" directly contributes to the vision of embodied AI agents that can perceive, reason, and act in complex environments.
- **Long-Horizon Multimodal Understanding and Generation:** A significant challenge in multimodal AI is processing and generating long, temporally consistent sequences of interleaved vision and language, such as entire internet videos. Emu3.5 directly tackles this by pre-training on a corpus primarily derived from sequential frames and transcripts of internet videos, a scale significantly beyond many existing models that focus on short clips or static image-text pairs. This pushes the frontier of learning temporal dynamics and contextual coherence.
- **Inference Efficiency for Generative Models:** Autoregressive models, while powerful, suffer from slow, token-by-token inference, especially for high-resolution image generation. Emu3.5's introduction of Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) addresses this practical bottleneck, making the model competitive with diffusion models in terms of inference speed, which is crucial for real-world applications and interactive systems.
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art:** The paper benchmarks Emu3.5 against leading models like Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (Nano Banana), OpenAI's GPT-Image-1, DALL-E 3, and various diffusion models (FLUX.1, SD3, Seedream 3.0), as well as open-source multimodal models like Qwen-Image. This places Emu3.5 at the forefront of multimodal generative AI capabilities, particularly for complex interleaved tasks and image generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind Emu3.5 are multifaceted, aiming to advance the state-of-the-art in multimodal AI:

- **Develop a Large-Scale Multimodal World Model:** The core objective is to build a foundation model that can "natively predict the next state across vision and language." This goes beyond merely processing different modalities to genuinely understanding their interdependencies and generating coherent, multimodal sequences.
- **Learn from Long-Horizon Multimodal Data:** Motivated by the observation that humans learn extensively from rich, spatiotemporally extended experiences like videos, Emu3.5 aims to effectively learn from and generalize over long-horizon vision-language data. This involves scaling pre-training to a massive corpus of internet videos, overcoming the limitations of previous efforts that focused on short-form or smaller-scale data.
- **Enable General-Purpose Multimodal Interaction:** A key motivation is to facilitate diverse and flexible multimodal interaction. This includes capabilities like long-horizon vision-language generation, any-to-image (X2I) generation (including complex editing and text-rich image generation), visual narrative creation, visual guidance for procedural tasks, and generalizable world modeling through exploration and embodied manipulation.
- **Enhance Multimodal Reasoning and Generation through Post-training:** Recognizing the importance of aligning model outputs with human preferences and complex task requirements, Emu3.5 integrates large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) guided by multimodal rewards. This aims to refine the model's ability to reason over and generate sophisticated interleaved content.
- **Achieve Efficient Multimodal Inference:** A practical limitation of autoregressive generative models is their sequential decoding process, which is computationally expensive for high-resolution visual outputs. A significant objective is to address this by developing novel inference acceleration techniques, specifically Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA), to enable fast, parallel image generation without sacrificing quality.
- **Open-Source a Foundational Multimodal Model:** The team is motivated to open-source Emu3.5, providing a robust foundation and interactive interface to support broader community research and development in world models and multimodal intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Emu3.5 employs a comprehensive, multi-stage methodology involving a unified architecture, extensive pre-training, sophisticated post-training, and an innovative inference acceleration technique.

#### 3.1. Unified Architecture

Emu3.5 is built upon a standard **decoder-only transformer architecture**, similar to modern large language models, but with modifications for multimodal adaptability. It comprises **64 transformer layers** with a hidden size of 5,120, employing Grouped Query Attention (GQA), RMSNorm with pre-normalization, QK-Norm, SwiGLU activation, and Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE). The model contains **34.1 billion parameters** (31.2B in transformer, 2.9B in embeddings) and supports a context length of up to 32,768 tokens. Its total vocabulary size is 282,926, integrating 151,854 text tokens (reusing QwenTokenizer) and 131,072 vision tokens.

#### 3.2. Tokenizer

For visual tokenization, Emu3.5 primarily uses the **IBQ (Index Backpropagation Quantization) framework** with a downsampling factor of *f*=16. The codebook size is expanded to 131,072, and the tokenizer model itself has 455 million parameters. Feature distillation from SigLIP is integrated to enrich semantic information.
- **Image Decoder:** A vanilla image decoder and an optional **diffusion-based image decoder** are used. The diffusion decoder generates images at twice the resolution, improving local details and fine-grained aspects (e.g., text, faces). LoRA-based distillation accelerates this decoding by ~10x.
- **Video Decoder:** Emu3.5 extends to continuous video generation using a **diffusion-based video decoder** conditioned on keyframe tokens. It is built on the DiT architecture, using quantized embeddings and supporting arbitrary intermediate frames via a 4-channel mask.

#### 3.3. Pre-training

Emu3.5 undergoes a two-stage pre-training on **over 13 trillion multimodal tokens** with a unified next-token prediction objective using cross-entropy loss (visual token loss weighted at 0.5).
- **Training Data:** The corpus integrates four major components:
 - **Video Interleaved Data (Primary):** Sourced from ~63 million internet videos (~790 years of footage) across diverse domains. This data preserves spatiotemporal continuity and cross-modal alignment. Frames are extracted using PySceneDetect, and audio transcripts are generated via Whisper-large-v2. Data undergoes two stages of filtering (basic and advanced, including DeQA for quality, DINO/FG-CLIP for redundancy, LLM for text quality) and annotation (semantic segmentation, visual captioning, multimodal summarization using LLMs like Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3).
 - **Vision-Text Paired Data:** ~500M image-text pairs and 30M video-text pairs, largely from Emu3, re-labeled and enriched with Qwen2.5-VL. Includes synthetic pairs from T2I models and open-source datasets (InfinityMM, LLaVA-OV).
 - **Any-to-Image Data:** ~27.35M samples compiled from open-source (SEED-Data-Edit, OmniGen-X2I, etc.) and in-house data, carefully curated for diversity, quality, and scale.
 - **Text-only Data:** ~3 trillion tokens, building on Emu3's text data with additional high-quality open-source corpora (English and Chinese) for robust language modeling.
- **Training Details:** Stage 1 (10T tokens) focuses on general multimodal alignment with 512x512 max resolution. Stage 2 (3T tokens) improves visual resolution diversity (512x512 to 1024x1024), data quality, and incorporates more annotations. Both stages utilize FlagScale for distributed training (TP=8, CP=2) and AdamW optimizer.

#### 3.4. Post-training

- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** A two-stage SFT is applied to 150 billion samples across a broad spectrum of tasks:
 - **General Tasks:** T2I, Language Understanding, Vision-Language QA.
 - **Any-to-Image (X2I):** General-purpose image editing with arbitrary interleaved image-text inputs.
 - **Visual Narrative:** Generating coherent storylines with interleaved text and images, spanning diverse themes (real, fictional, educational).
 - **Visual Guidance:** Step-by-step procedural action generation with interleaved vision and language, leveraging dual-level Chain-of-Thought (CoT) with Qwen3 and Qwen2.5-VL.
 - **World Exploration:** Immersive navigation in user-defined virtual worlds, generating visual observations and textual narrations, supporting User-Interactive and Free-Exploration modes.
 - **Embodied Manipulation:** Decomposing long-horizon robotic tasks into interleaved subtask instructions and keyframe states, trained on OXE, Agi-world Alpha, and Songling Aloha datasets.
 The SFT uses a two-stage resolution strategy, starting with standard resolutions and progressing to higher resolutions (1024px for X2I, 720px for interleaved tasks) to enhance quality.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Emu3.5 undergoes large-scale joint RL training across multiple multimodal tasks to enhance reasoning and generation.
 - **Reward System:** A comprehensive system is designed with general (aesthetic, image-text alignment), task-specific (OCR for text rendering, face detection for identity), and unified components. Rewards are normalized and combined to guide optimization.
 - **Training Data:** Curated subsets from SFT data (~10K prompts/task), 1K human feedback samples, plus additional X2I (58K) and T2I (50K) data.
 - **Training Details:** Optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with vLLM-based sampling for rollouts, encouraging cross-task synergy.

#### 3.5. Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) for Efficient Inference

To address the inefficiency of sequential token-by-token decoding for image generation, DiDA converts autoregressive image generation into **bidirectional parallel prediction**.
- **Approach:** It extends discrete diffusion to visual tokens, allowing an entire image token sequence to be initialized and progressively refined through discrete denoising steps. Attention masks are modified: noisy image tokens attend causally to preceding clean tokens and bidirectionally to other noisy tokens within the same image, while clean tokens follow the original causal pattern.
- **Infrastructure:** Built on FlagScale, it incorporates PyTorch FlexAttention for flexible cross-modal attention and a Hybrid Inference Framework with an FSM-based scheduler for dynamic modality-switching, achieving significant speedups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Emu3.5 demonstrates powerful and generalizable multimodal capabilities, achieving state-of-the-art or comparable performance across a wide range of tasks:

- **Native Multimodal Capabilities:** Emu3.5 natively accepts and generates interleaved vision-language inputs/outputs, supporting:
 - **Long-Horizon Vision-Language Generation:** Capable of producing interleaved sequences of visual frames and text that maintain temporal consistency and semantic coherence across long chains of frames (e.g., visual narratives, visual guidance).
 - **Any-to-Image (X2I) Generation:** Achieves strong performance in open-world image editing, precise control, and free spatiotemporal manipulation. Quantitative evaluations on benchmarks like ImgEdit, GEdit-Bench, OmniContext, and ICE-Bench show Emu3.5 surpassing or being comparable to leading models including Gemini 2.5 Flash Image and Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, especially with strong instruction following and consistency.
 - **Complex Text-Rich Image Generation:** Excels at generating accurate and controllable text rendering in images, including dense English and Chinese text and mathematical formulas. It achieves superior results on LeX-Bench, LongText-Bench, and CVTG-2K, outperforming state-of-the-art T2I models.
- **Generalizable World-Modeling Abilities:**
 - **World Exploration:** Enables spatiotemporally consistent world exploration through controllable interaction and free-form navigation in diverse scenarios (User-Interactive and Free-Exploration Modes). Automated preference evaluations against Gemini 2.5 Flash Image show Emu3.5 achieving a 65.5% win rate.
 - **Embodied Manipulation:** Facilitates open-world embodied manipulation across various scenarios and tasks by generating interleaved subtask instructions and keyframes. Emu3.5 achieved a 67.1% win rate against Gemini 2.5 Flash Image in automated preference evaluations.
- **Performance Benchmarking:**
 - **Text-to-Image (T2I):** Emu3.5 achieves top performance on TIIF-Bench (best avg score) and OneIG-Bench (superior in English, second in Chinese), demonstrating robust instruction following and general generation capabilities. It generates higher-resolution images (1024x1024) with fewer visual tokens than Emu3 while significantly improving semantic alignment and aesthetic quality (Table 16).
 - **Comparison to Gemini 2.5 Flash Image:** Emu3.5 achieves performance comparable to Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (Nano Banana) on image generation and editing tasks and demonstrates superior results on a suite of interleaved generation tasks (Visual Narrative, Visual Guidance, World Exploration, Embodied Manipulation), with win rates ranging from 49.2% to 67.1% against Gemini (Figure 1b, Table 14).
- **Tokenizer Reconstruction:** The tokenizer shows superior representations on textual and facial features (e.g., 51.11 T-ACC, 70.52 T-NED) and competitive general reconstruction ability (Table 15). The diffusion-based decoder further improves visual fidelity and detail perception (Figure 11).
- **Inference Acceleration with DiDA:** The Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) technique significantly accelerates per-image inference by approximately **20x** (from 120s to 10s for a 4096-token 1024x1024 image) without sacrificing performance on T2I and X2I tasks, making it competitive with fast-sampling diffusion models (Table 16).
- **Scaling Behavior:** Validation loss on out-of-distribution multimodal tasks continues to decrease as pre-training compute scales, suggesting progressively stronger generalization. Unified post-training through RL, guided by a comprehensive reward system, leads to stable and continuous improvement in average reward scores, demonstrating effective balancing of heterogeneous objectives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Emu3.5 represents a significant step forward in the pursuit of general multimodal intelligence, with profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Towards True World Models:** By natively predicting the next state across vision and language, trained on vast quantities of interleaved video data, Emu3.5 pushes the boundaries beyond mere multimodal understanding and generation. It moves closer to learning a "world model" that captures temporal dynamics, causality, and rich context, essential for more sophisticated AI systems.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI Agents:** The demonstrated capabilities in "world exploration" and "embodied manipulation" are crucial building blocks for future embodied AI. Emu3.5 can serve as a powerful foundation for developing agents that can perceive, reason, and interact with the physical world in dynamic and intelligent ways, including robots that can plan and execute complex tasks.
- **Enhanced Interactive AI Systems:** The model's ability to generate coherent long-horizon visual narratives and provide step-by-step visual guidance opens up new possibilities for interactive AI assistants. These systems could generate educational content, assist with complex procedures, or create rich multimedia stories, making AI more engaging and intuitive for human users.
- **State-of-the-Art Multimodal Generation:** Emu3.5's strong performance in text-to-image and any-to-image generation, particularly its accurate text rendering and sophisticated editing capabilities, sets a new benchmark for multimodal generative models. This has direct applications in creative industries, content creation, and design.
- **Overcoming Inference Bottlenecks:** The Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) method is a critical technical contribution. By making autoregressive generation significantly more efficient, Emu3.5 addresses a major practical limitation, making high-quality, high-resolution multimodal generation feasible for real-time or near real-time applications. This bridges the gap in inference speed between autoregressive and diffusion models.
- **Open-Source Catalyst for Research:** The open-sourcing of Emu3.5, including its data pipeline, tokenizer, training methodology, and DiDA, is a substantial contribution to the research community. It provides a robust and powerful foundation for other researchers to build upon, accelerating progress in multimodal AI, world models, and various downstream applications. This collaborative approach can foster innovation and collectively advance the field.
- **Insights into Scaling and Training:** The paper's observations on the benefits of scaling pre-training, the mutual advantages of unified post-training, and the flexibility of transforming predictive models for efficient inference offer valuable insights for future large-scale AI model development.
