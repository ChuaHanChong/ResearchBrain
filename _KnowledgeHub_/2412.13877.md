# RoboMIND: Benchmark on Multi-embodiment Intelligence Normative Data for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboMIND introduces a large-scale, multi-embodiment dataset for robot manipulation, collected under unified standards across four distinct robot types, including a humanoid. This dataset significantly enhances the performance and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action models, enabling higher success rates on complex real-world tasks and facilitating cross-embodiment policy transfer.

## Problem

- Existing robotic datasets lack unified data collection standards, leading to inconsistencies and hindering the training of generalizable models across different labs and hardware.
- Current datasets offer insufficient diversity in robot embodiments, primarily focusing on single-arm setups, which limits the development of policies for complex multi-arm or dexterous humanoid manipulation.
- There is a scarcity of real-world failure data and fine-grained linguistic annotations, which are crucial for developing robust policies that can learn from errors and understand tasks in detail.

## Method

- Introduced **RoboMIND**, a large-scale, high-quality, multi-embodiment dataset collected under a unified data collection standard using ==human teleoperation== across single-arm, dual-arm, and humanoid robotic platforms.
- Provided comprehensive data types for each trajectory, including ==multi-view RGB-D==, ==proprioceptive states==, end-effector information, and linguistic task descriptions, stored in a standardized ==H5 format==.
- Included unique data subsets: 5,000 real-world failure demonstrations with detailed causes and 10,000 trajectories with fine-grained, frame-level linguistic annotations, alongside a closely replicated ==digital twin== environment in Nvidia Isaac Sim.

## Results

- RoboMIND comprises **107,000** demonstration trajectories (**305.5 hours**) from four robot embodiments (Franka, UR5e, AgileX, Tien Kung humanoid), covering **479** tasks, **96** object classes, **5,000** real-world failure demonstrations, and **10,000** trajectories with frame-level linguistic annotations.
- Evaluation showed VLA models like RDT-1B and CrossFormer achieved significantly enhanced performance, with CrossFormer demonstrating near-perfect scores on some dual-arm and humanoid tasks after pre-training on the full RoboMIND dataset, where it previously struggled.
- A strong positive correlation (Pearson **0.83–0.91**) between real-world and simulation results validated the utility of the digital twin for policy evaluation, though real-world data remains crucial due to persistent sim-to-real gaps, especially for contact-rich interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RoboMIND effectively pre-trains and fine-tunes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling high success rates and cross-embodiment transfer across diverse robot types and tasks.
> - The inclusion of multi-embodiment data, particularly from humanoid robots, contributes to better overall policy learning and generalization even for other robot types.
> - While models show good generalization to unseen objects, generalizing to unseen backgrounds remains a significant challenge, and simulation data, though useful for pre-training, still exhibits sim-to-real gaps for complex contact tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robotic manipulation policies is a grand challenge in robotics, akin to the progress seen in computer vision (CV) and natural language processing (NLP) through large-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet, LAION, Common Crawl). However, the paper points out a critical bottleneck: robotic data is far more challenging and costly to acquire than internet-sourced vision or language data, as it requires precise observations of physical interactions and actions in controlled environments.

Existing robotic datasets, while valuable, suffer from several limitations that RoboMIND seeks to address:
- **Lack of Unified Data Collection Standards**: Many large-scale datasets are aggregations from various laboratories, each with differing hardware, environments, and data collection protocols. This inconsistency introduces variability and noise, hindering the training of generalizable models. Open X-Embodiment, while massive (1.4M trajectories), is explicitly mentioned as an aggregation from 21 institutions with differing standards, making it "difficult to learn efficient manipulation policies across the entire dataset." RoboMIND, in contrast, emphasizes its collection "within the same standardized setting, adhering to a standardized data collection protocol to ensure consistency and reliability."
- **Insufficient Diversity in Robot Embodiments**: Most existing datasets tend to focus on single-arm robots with two-finger grippers, limiting the variety of tasks and skills that can be learned. Datasets like RoboSet, BridgeData V2, RH20T, and DROID, while large, primarily feature single-arm setups. RoboMIND explicitly tackles this by including data from four distinct embodiments: single-arm robots (Franka Emika Panda, UR5e), a dual-arm robot (AgileX Cobot Magic V2.0), and a humanoid robot with dexterous hands (X-Humannoid Tien Kung). This multi-embodiment approach is crucial for advancing cross-embodiment transfer learning and truly general-purpose policies.
- **Limited Task and Object Diversity**: While some datasets offer task variety, RoboMIND expands significantly, featuring 479 diverse tasks involving 96 distinct object classes across various real-world scenarios (domestic, industrial, kitchen, office, retail). This comprehensive coverage allows for more robust skill learning.
- **Lack of Failure Data and Fine-grained Annotations**: Previous datasets largely focus on successful demonstrations. RoboMIND uniquely includes 5,000 real-world failure demonstrations with detailed causes, enabling research into failure reflection, recovery, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). Additionally, it provides 10,000 trajectories with frame-level fine-grained linguistic annotations, which is valuable for training advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and detailed task planning.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap**: While simulators reduce data collection costs, the "sim-to-real gap" remains a challenge. RoboMIND addresses this by providing a digital twin environment in Nvidia Isaac Sim that closely replicates real-world tasks and assets, facilitating low-cost data collection and evaluation while also providing data to study the sim-to-real transfer.

In summary, RoboMIND positions itself as a benchmark dataset that pushes the boundaries of robotic data collection by offering an unprecedented combination of scale, multi-embodiment diversity, standardized collection, comprehensive annotations (including failures), and a closely matched digital twin, thereby directly addressing the critical data needs for training the next generation of generalizable and robust robotic manipulation policies. It aims to accelerate research in imitation learning, VLA models, cross-embodiment transfer, and real-world robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for the RoboMIND project stems from the recognized need for comprehensive and diverse datasets to train robust and general-purpose manipulation policies in robotics. Drawing parallels with the success of large models in computer vision and natural language processing, the authors identify that a similar paradigm is essential for robotics to achieve effective generalization across varying conditions, such as new robots, unfamiliar environments, or different objects.

The specific key objectives and motivations are:

- **To address the lack of a unified data collection standard and high-quality data:** Existing robotic datasets often suffer from inconsistencies due to varying collection protocols and hardware setups across different labs. RoboMIND aims to rectify this by establishing and adhering to a unified data collection standard on a single platform.
- **To provide diverse, large-scale, and high-quality data:** The paper aims to curate a dataset that encompasses a broad spectrum of robotic interactions and experiences. This includes a large number of demonstration trajectories (107k), a wide variety of tasks (479), numerous object classes (96), and different robot embodiments (single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid with dexterous hands). The emphasis is on "high-quality" data, ensured through rigorous quality assurance criteria.
- **To enable the training of generalizable robotic models:** By offering diverse data in terms of robots, tasks, objects, and scenarios, the dataset is designed to help models learn to perform reliably under varied conditions, thus fostering generalizable manipulation policies.
- **To incorporate comprehensive robotic-related information:** Beyond basic actions, RoboMIND seeks to provide rich contextual data, including multi-view observations (RGB-D), proprioceptive robot state information, end-effector details, and linguistic task descriptions.
- **To facilitate learning from failures:** A novel objective is to include real-world failure demonstrations (5k trajectories) with detailed causes. This is motivated by the desire to enable policies to reflect on and correct errors, mimicking the human learning process, and advancing research in areas like failure recovery and RLHF.
- **To provide a low-cost, scalable simulation environment:** To complement real-world data and address the significant costs of physical data collection, RoboMIND aims to provide a digital twin environment in Nvidia Isaac Sim, allowing for the generation of additional training data and efficient evaluation.
- **To serve as a comprehensive benchmark:** The dataset is intended to be a benchmark for evaluating various robot learning algorithms, from single-task imitation learning methods to state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, providing insights into their performance and limitations in diverse, real-world scenarios.

In essence, RoboMIND is motivated by the aspiration to accelerate the development of versatile, general-purpose robotic models by providing an unprecedented, meticulously curated, and diverse dataset that addresses critical limitations of prior work in robotic data collection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of RoboMIND's methodology revolves around a meticulously designed, unified data collection and processing pipeline, aimed at producing a large-scale, high-quality, and diverse dataset.

#### 3.1. Data Collection Philosophy: Human Teleoperation

Unlike scripted or purely autonomous data collection, RoboMIND primarily utilizes human teleoperation. This approach captures natural human motion patterns and expert demonstrations, which are then mapped onto the robots. This ensures the collected trajectories are kinematically feasible and demonstrative of successful task completion, mirroring how humans perform complex manipulation.

#### 3.2. Multi-Embodiment Robotic Platforms

A key aspect of the methodology is the collection of data across four distinct robotic embodiments, all under the unified collection standard:
- **Single-arm robots**: Franka Emika Panda and UR5e, typically equipped with grippers.
- **Dual-arm robot**: AgileX Cobot Magic V2.0, also with grippers.
- **Humanoid robot**: X-Humannoid Tien Kung, notably equipped with dexterous hands and a high degree of freedom (42 DoF whole body).

Each robot type utilizes specific teleoperation devices tailored to its kinematics:
- **Single-arm**: 3D-printed components and servo motors matching the robot's Degrees of Freedom (DoF) for mapping human arm movements.
- **Dual-arm**: Bilateral teleoperation devices, similar to Mobile ALOHA, where an auxiliary robotic arm controls the main robot.
- **Humanoid**: Advanced motion capture suits (Xsens) to capture full-body human joint movements, which are then mapped to the humanoid robot's corresponding joints. This is a more direct and accurate method than relying on VR headsets or camera-based pose recognition.

#### 3.3. Comprehensive Data Types

For each collected trajectory, a rich set of information is recorded and stored in a standardized H5 format:
- **Multi-view RGB-D data**: From various camera viewpoints (e.g., top, left, right for Franka; head, chest, waist, back for Tien Kung; hand-eye and front-facing for AgileX; top for UR5e). This provides diverse visual observations.
- **Proprioceptive robot state information**: Detailed joint positions, velocities, and other internal states of the robot body.
- **End-effector state information**: Specific data about the gripper or dexterous hand, such as opening/closing states or force readings.
- **Teleoperation body state information**: Data from the human operator's movements.
- **Linguistic task descriptions**: Semantic information about the task being performed.

#### 3.4. Unified Data Collection Platform and Protocol

To ensure consistency and reliability, RoboMIND employs an "intelligent data platform" with a cloud-native architecture and distributed computing. This platform provides five core functionalities:
1. **Data Collection**: Automated transmission of data from teleoperation systems to the platform.
2. **Data Storage**: Packaging and storing data in the standardized H5 format.
3. **Data Preprocessing**: Crucial for quality control. It involves filtering based on predefined standards.
4. **Data Classification**: Categorizing datasets by robot type and specific task names.
5. **Data Annotation**: Detailed linguistic descriptions for both overall tasks and fine-grained sub-actions.

#### 3.5. Rigorous Data Preprocessing and Quality Assurance

Given that human teleoperation can introduce errors (fatigue, distractions, physical limitations), extensive quality checks are performed:
- **Quality Assurance Criteria**: Eight specific criteria are defined to identify problematic trajectories, including "Movement not Smooth," "Secondary Grabbing," "Collision before Grabbing," "Failed Placement," "Image Distortion," and "Gripper out of the Camera."
- **Three-step Inspection**: Initial inspection for obvious technical issues, detailed frame-by-frame inspection for subtle errors, and systematic logging of non-compliant data. This filtering process ensures the high quality of the released successful trajectories.

#### 3.6. Task-centric Classification and Fine-grained Annotation

- **Task Definition**: Each task is a fundamental unit, comprehensively defined by robot embodiment, manipulation skill, involved objects, and scene descriptions. Tasks are broadly categorized into six types: Articulated Manipulations, Coordination Manipulations, Basic Manipulations, Multiple Object Interactions, Precision Manipulations, and Scene Understanding.
- **Fine-grained Language Annotation**: Recognizing that long-horizon tasks require more than a single description, 10,000 trajectories are annotated with frame-level linguistic descriptions. This process involves:
 - **AI-assisted segmentation**: Using Gemini (a large multimodal model) to segment videos into operational sequences.
 - **Manual refinement**: Human reviewers verify and correct Gemini's annotations to ensure accuracy, identify key objects/actions, and maintain consistent temporal logic. This hybrid approach leverages AI for initial drafts and human expertise for precision.

#### 3.7. Inclusion of Failure Demonstrations

A unique aspect is the documentation of 5,000 real-world failure cases. These are trajectories where tasks were not completed successfully, either due to human operator error or robot execution issues. Each failure is accompanied by a detailed cause, providing valuable negative examples for robust learning.

#### 3.8. Digital Twin Environment

A digital twin of the real-world setup is created in Nvidia Isaac Sim. This replica facilitates:
- Low-cost collection of additional training data (e.g., 30,035 simulation trajectories for Franka).
- Efficient evaluation of policies in a simulated environment, allowing for rapid iteration and testing without physical robot wear-and-tear.
- Studies on sim-to-real transfer and physics disparities.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that RoboMIND is not merely a collection of videos, but a structured, high-quality, and richly annotated dataset designed to push the state-of-the-art in robot learning towards general-purpose, multi-embodiment manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive experiments to validate the quality and utility of RoboMIND as a benchmark for robot learning, evaluating both single-task imitation learning (IL) models and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) large models.

#### 4.1. Dataset Scale and Diversity

- **Overall**: RoboMIND comprises 107,000 demonstration trajectories (305.5 hours of interaction data), covering 479 diverse tasks, 96 distinct object classes, and 38 operational skills.
- **Embodiment Distribution**: Includes 26,856 trajectories from Franka, 15,187 from Tien Kung (humanoid), 10,269 from AgileX (dual-arm), 25,170 from UR5e, and 30,035 from simulation (primarily Franka).
- **Task Horizon**: Franka and UR tasks often have shorter trajectories (good for primitive skills), while Tien Kung and AgileX tasks are longer (over 500 timesteps), ideal for long-horizon task training and skill composition (e.g., AgileX tasks often involve 2+ skills, Tien Kung up to 5).
- **Object Diversity**: Over 96 object categories from five usage scenarios (domestic, industrial, kitchen, office, retail), including rigid and deformable objects, and articulated mechanisms.
- **Unique Data**: 5,000 real-world failure demonstrations with detailed causes, and 10,000 trajectories with frame-level fine-grained language descriptions.

#### 4.2. Performance of Single-task Imitation Learning Models

- **Models Evaluated**: ACT, Diffusion Policy, and BAKU.
- **Results (45 tasks across 4 robots)**:
 - **ACT**: Achieved an average success rate of 55.3% on AgileX dual-arm tasks, outperforming Franka (30.7%), UR5e (38.0%), and Tien Kung (34.0%). It showed particularly strong performance on complex dexterous hand manipulation tasks by the humanoid robot (e.g., 60% on HR-CloseDrawerLowerCabinet).
 - **Diffusion Policy**: Also demonstrated capacity for complex tasks, outperforming ACT on some Franka and Tien Kung tasks.
 - **BAKU**: Generally exhibited lower success rates, possibly due to hyperparameters optimized for simulation rather than real-world conditions.
- **Conclusion**: RoboMIND serves as a high-quality training set for improving single-task imitation learning performance across various robot types, including complex dual-arm and dexterous hand manipulations.

#### 4.3. Performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Large Models

- **Models Evaluated**: OpenVLA, RDT-1B, and CrossFormer, fine-tuned on aggregated multitask demonstrations from RoboMIND.
- **Results (15 tasks across Franka, Tien Kung, AgileX)**:
 - **RDT-1B**: Consistently demonstrated significantly enhanced performance, especially excelling in dual-arm manipulation tasks (e.g., 10/10 on AX-AppleYellowPlate, 9/10 on AX-PutPepper).
 - **OpenVLA**: Showed comparable success rates for simpler single-arm tasks (e.g., FR-PlaceBreadPlate, FR-SlideCloseDrawer) but had limitations as it only supported single-arm manipulations.
 - **CrossFormer**: Improved performance on single-arm and humanoid tasks after fine-tuning with RoboMIND, showing some success where it had none without fine-tuning.
- **Conclusion**: RoboMIND effectively fine-tunes VLA models, enabling high success rates in complex real-world multi-task scenarios across different robot embodiments.

#### 4.4. Leveraging RoboMIND to Enhance VLA Large Models (Pre-training)

- **Experiment**: Pre-training RDT-1B and CrossFormer on the *entire* RoboMIND dataset, then fine-tuning on a small expert multitask subset.
- **Results**: Pre-training with the full RoboMIND dataset led to significant improvements in task success rates across all robot types. Notably, CrossFormer, which initially struggled with dual-arm tasks (0/10 success), achieved near-perfect scores (10/10) on tasks like AX-TakePotato and AX-PutPepper after RoboMIND pre-training. It also achieved 100% success on HR-PressDownToaster.
- **Ablation Study (Humanoid Data)**: Pre-training RDT-1B with RoboMIND *excluding* humanoid data resulted in lower success rates for certain unimanual tasks (e.g., FR-PickStrawberryInBowl, FR-OpenTrashCan, FR-SideOpenDrawer), indicating that the humanoid data contributes to better overall policy learning, even for other robot types (13.3% relative improvement with full dataset).
- **Conclusion**: The breadth and diversity of RoboMIND, particularly its multi-embodiment data, make it a powerful dataset for pre-training VLA models, significantly enhancing their robustness and generalization capabilities, even for cross-embodiment transfer.

#### 4.5. Generalization of VLA Large Models

- **Experiment**: Tested VLA models (OpenVLA, RDT-1B, CrossFormer) on FR-PlaceBreadPlate-related tasks with unseen objects (apple, banana, corn) and unseen backgrounds.
- **Results**:
 - **Object Generalization**: RDT-1B and CrossFormer showed good generalization for manipulating unseen objects, especially those similar in shape to training data (e.g., bananas).
 - **Background Generalization**: All models performed relatively poorly when encountering unseen backgrounds, indicating a current limitation in generalizing to visual distractors or environmental changes.
- **Conclusion**: While RoboMIND helps models generalize to new objects, generalization to diverse backgrounds remains a significant challenge for current VLA models.

#### 4.6. Failure Case Analysis and Insights

- **Method**: Categorized failure reasons during real-world tests (e.g., Inaccurate Positioning, Cannot Close Gripper, Object Drop).
- **Key Finding**: "Inaccurate Positioning" was the most common failure reason across all robots for ACT (e.g., 48% for humanoid tasks). "Improper gripper actions" (Cannot Close Gripper, Object Drop) were also significant contributors.
- **Implications**: These insights highlight critical areas for algorithm improvement (3D positioning accuracy, robust gripper control) and data collection refinement (e.g., ensuring diverse object placements, slower gripper actions during demonstration capture).

#### 4.7. Real and Simulation Data Co-training and Correlation

- **Co-training**: Trained ACT on FR-UprightBlueCup using varying ratios of real-world (100) and simulation (up to 500) trajectories from the digital twin.
- **Results**: Increasing simulation data improved success rates in *both* real-world and simulation environments, but simulation data alone was insufficient for robust real-world performance (10% success in real vs. 90% in sim for sim-only training). This suggests persistent sim-to-real physics gaps, particularly for contact-rich tasks like gripping.
- **Correlation**: Evaluation of ACT and Diffusion Policy on five tasks in both real and simulation environments showed strong positive Pearson Correlation Coefficients (0.83 for ACT, 0.91 for Diffusion Policy) between sim and real results, validating the utility of the digital twin for policy evaluation.
- **Conclusion**: RoboMIND's digital twin is valuable for supplementing real-world data and evaluating policies, but real-world data remains crucial, and simulation fidelity for contact physics needs further improvement.

In summary, RoboMIND has proven to be a robust and highly effective dataset for training and benchmarking various robot learning algorithms. It significantly improves the performance and generalization capabilities of VLA models and provides critical insights into current challenges in robotic manipulation, particularly for diverse embodiments and complex tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboMIND represents a substantial leap forward in robotic learning datasets, with significant implications and potential impact on the field:

#### 5.1. Enabling Generalizable and Robust Robotic Manipulation

- **Largest Unified Multi-embodiment Dataset**: To the best of the authors' knowledge, RoboMIND is the largest multi-embodiment teleoperation dataset collected on a *unified platform*. This standardization is crucial for reducing data variability and noise, allowing models to learn more robust and generalizable policies across different robot types, tasks, and environments. This directly addresses a critical bottleneck identified in current robot learning.
- **Accelerating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models**: The comprehensive nature of RoboMIND, including multi-view observations, proprioceptive data, and fine-grained linguistic descriptions, makes it an ideal resource for training and pre-training VLA models. The experimental results clearly demonstrate that leveraging RoboMIND significantly enhances VLA models' task success rates and generalization capabilities, pushing towards more capable embodied AI.
- **Fostering Cross-Embodiment Transfer Learning**: By providing high-quality data from diverse robotic platforms (single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid with dexterous hands) collected under a unified standard, RoboMIND uniquely facilitates research into cross-embodiment policy transfer. The ablation study showing the positive impact of humanoid data on single-arm task performance exemplifies this potential.

#### 5.2. Advancing Research in Key Areas of Robot Learning

- **Failure Analysis and Recovery**: The unique inclusion of 5,000 real-world failure demonstrations, complete with detailed causes, provides an invaluable resource for developing algorithms for failure detection, diagnosis, and recovery. This can lead to more resilient and autonomous robotic systems that can learn from their mistakes, much like humans do. This aligns with approaches like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for robotics.
- **Fine-grained Task Planning and Understanding**: The 10,000 trajectories with frame-level linguistic annotations enable models to learn more nuanced and detailed understandings of tasks, supporting research in fine-grained task planning, visual question answering, and human-robot communication.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer and Fidelity**: The meticulously constructed digital twin environment allows for low-cost data augmentation and iterative evaluation. While the paper highlights existing sim-to-real gaps (especially for contact-rich tasks), the dataset provides a robust platform for future research focused on improving simulation fidelity and developing effective sim2real transfer techniques without reliance on external datasets.
- **Benchmarking and Algorithm Development**: RoboMIND serves as a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating various imitation learning and VLA algorithms. The detailed performance analyses provide insights into the strengths and weaknesses of current methods, guiding future algorithm development (e.g., the emphasis on improving accurate 3D positioning and robust gripper control).

#### 5.3. Practical Utility and Accessibility

- **Ready-to-Use Dataset**: The standardized collection and quality assurance procedures mean that RoboMIND is "ready-to-use" for other roboticists, reducing the significant overhead typically associated with preparing robotic datasets for model training. The provision of code scripts for adaptation with the LeRobot framework further enhances accessibility.
- **Community Resource**: As a large-scale, high-quality, and publicly available dataset (via the project website), RoboMIND is poised to become a foundational resource for the global robot learning community, fostering collaborative research and accelerating innovation.

In conclusion, RoboMIND is a seminal contribution that addresses a critical data bottleneck in embodied AI. By providing an unprecedentedly diverse, high-quality, and richly annotated dataset collected under unified standards, it has the potential to significantly advance the development of general-purpose, robust, and intelligent robotic manipulation capabilities across a wide range of real-world applications. Its focus on multi-embodiment data, failure cases, and fine-grained annotations sets a new standard for robotic datasets and promises to accelerate progress in imitation learning, VLA models, and autonomous robot learning from experience.
