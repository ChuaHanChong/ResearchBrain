# RoboMIND: Benchmark on Multi-embodiment Intelligence Normative Data for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboMIND introduces a large-scale, multi-embodiment dataset for robot manipulation, collected under unified standards across four distinct robot types, including a humanoid. This dataset significantly enhances the performance and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action models, enabling higher success rates on complex real-world tasks and facilitating cross-embodiment policy transfer.

## Problem

- Existing robotic datasets lack unified data collection standards, leading to inconsistencies and hindering the training of generalizable models across different labs and hardware.
- Current datasets offer insufficient diversity in robot embodiments, primarily focusing on single-arm setups, which limits the development of policies for complex multi-arm or dexterous humanoid manipulation.
- There is a scarcity of real-world failure data and fine-grained linguistic annotations, which are crucial for developing robust policies that can learn from errors and understand tasks in detail.

## Method

- Introduced **RoboMIND**, a large-scale, high-quality, multi-embodiment dataset collected under a unified data collection standard using ==human teleoperation== across single-arm, dual-arm, and humanoid robotic platforms.
- Provided comprehensive data types for each trajectory, including ==multi-view RGB-D==, ==proprioceptive states==, end-effector information, and linguistic task descriptions, stored in a standardized ==H5 format==.
- Included unique data subsets: 5,000 real-world failure demonstrations with detailed causes and 10,000 trajectories with fine-grained, frame-level linguistic annotations, alongside a closely replicated ==digital twin== environment in Nvidia Isaac Sim.

## Results

- RoboMIND comprises **107,000** demonstration trajectories (**305.5 hours**) from four robot embodiments (Franka, UR5e, AgileX, Tien Kung humanoid), covering **479** tasks, **96** object classes, **5,000** real-world failure demonstrations, and **10,000** trajectories with frame-level linguistic annotations.
- Evaluation showed VLA models like RDT-1B and CrossFormer achieved significantly enhanced performance, with CrossFormer demonstrating near-perfect scores on some dual-arm and humanoid tasks after pre-training on the full RoboMIND dataset, where it previously struggled.
- A strong positive correlation (Pearson **0.83–0.91**) between real-world and simulation results validated the utility of the digital twin for policy evaluation, though real-world data remains crucial due to persistent sim-to-real gaps, especially for contact-rich interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RoboMIND effectively pre-trains and fine-tunes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling high success rates and cross-embodiment transfer across diverse robot types and tasks.
> - The inclusion of multi-embodiment data, particularly from humanoid robots, contributes to better overall policy learning and generalization even for other robot types.
> - While models show good generalization to unseen objects, generalizing to unseen backgrounds remains a significant challenge, and simulation data, though useful for pre-training, still exhibits sim-to-real gaps for complex contact tasks.
