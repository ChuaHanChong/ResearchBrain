# WorldMem: Long-term Consistent World Simulation with Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from S-Lab, Nanyang Technological University, along with collaborators from Peking University and Shanghai AI Laboratory, developed WORLDMEM, a framework for long-term consistent world simulation using an autoregressive conditional diffusion transformer. It integrates a token-level memory bank with state-aware attention to maintain spatiotemporal coherence, demonstrating superior performance in retaining object states and environmental dynamics across hundreds of frames on both Minecraft and RealEstate10K benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing video generative models suffer from limited temporal context windows, leading to spatial and temporal inconsistencies in long-term world simulations.
- Current methods struggle with accurately reconstructing previously observed scenes after significant viewpoint changes or large temporal gaps, often 'forgetting' past states.
- Geometric-based solutions are inflexible and prone to detail loss in dynamic, unbounded scenes, while implicit methods often lack visual fidelity or scalability.

## Method

- **WorldMem** integrates a ==token-level memory bank==, storing latent representations of memory frames along with their 5D poses and timestamps, into a ==conditional diffusion transformer==.
- It employs a ==state-aware memory attention== mechanism where both query and key features are enriched with explicit, relative pose (==Plücker embedding==) and ==timestamp embeddings== for robust spatiotemporal reasoning.
- A ==confidence-based memory retrieval== algorithm, utilizing FOV overlap and temporal proximity, is used to select the most relevant historical information, coupled with ==progressive sampling== during training.

## Results

- **WorldMem** significantly outperformed baseline methods on the Minecraft benchmark, maintaining high quality (**PSNR 23.98**, **LPIPS 0.1429**, **rFID 15.37**) when generating hundreds of frames beyond the context window, where Diffusion Forcing severely degraded.
- It demonstrated robust capabilities for modeling dynamic environmental changes and persisting user-placed objects over hundreds of frames and extensive camera movements.
- Achieved superior consistency across all metrics (**PSNR 23.34**, **LPIPS 0.1672**, **rFID 43.14**) on the RealEstate10K dataset, outperforming methods like Viewcrafter in real-world loop closure scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining token-level memory with explicit state cues (pose and timestamp) and state-aware attention enables flexible, high-fidelity long-term consistency in dynamic world simulations.
> - Relative state encoding simplifies the learning objective and improves stability for reasoning about spatiotemporal relationships across significant gaps.
> - Progressive memory sampling during training and confidence-based, similarity-filtered retrieval are critical for effectively managing and leveraging historical context in memory-augmented generative systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"WORLDMEM" is positioned within the rapidly evolving landscape of **world simulation** and **generative AI**, specifically focusing on **long-term video generation** and **interactive environments**. Recent advancements in video diffusion models have significantly propelled the field, enabling the creation of high-fidelity visual rollouts for predicting outcomes of actions and modeling virtual environments. These capabilities are crucial for applications ranging from autonomous navigation to next-generation game engines.

However, a fundamental limitation in existing video generative models, and by extension, interactive world simulators, is their **limited temporal context window**. Due to computational and memory constraints, these models typically operate with a fixed number of past frames for conditioning and often discard earlier generated content. This leads to a critical problem of **world inconsistency**, where the simulated environment fails to maintain 3D spatial coherence over long periods. For example, if a virtual camera moves away from an object and then returns, the regenerated scene might show the object in a different state or location, or even entirely missing, violating the expectation of a consistent world.

The paper categorizes existing approaches to consistent world simulation into two main types:

- **Geometric-based methods:** These approaches explicitly reconstruct the generated world into a 3D or 4D representation (e.g., using neural radiance fields or meshes). While they can reliably maintain consistency, they suffer from inflexibility in dynamic environments and are prone to loss of detail, especially for large, unbounded scenes. Modifying or interacting with these reconstructed worlds can also be challenging. Examples include works by Liu et al. (2024) and Gao et al. (2024).
- **Geometric-free (implicit) methods:** These methods focus on implicit learning without explicit 3D representations. Some approaches ensure consistency by overfitting to predefined scenarios (e.g., specific game maps), which limits their scalability and generalizability. Others store abstract features (e.g., LoRA modules), which are compact but often lose visual fidelity and spatial specificity required for accurate scene reconstruction. Discrete token-based representations also face limitations with fixed token counts, struggling to capture diverse and evolving environments. Examples include StreamingT2V (Henschel et al., 2024) and SlowFastGen (Hong et al., 2024).

"WORLDMEM" distinguishes itself by proposing a **geometry-free solution that utilizes a token-level memory bank augmented with explicit state cues (poses and timestamps)**. This approach aims to achieve both the flexibility of implicit methods and the high-fidelity reconstruction capabilities often associated with geometric methods, without their inherent limitations. By building on Conditional Diffusion Transformers (CDiT) (Peebles and Xie, 2023) and Diffusion Forcing (DF) (Chen et al., 2025), WORLDMEM leverages state-of-the-art autoregressive video generation techniques to extend consistent world simulation theoretically indefinitely. It directly tackles the problem of reasoning over large spatiotemporal gaps to accurately reproduce detailed scenes, a crucial capability that previous methods often struggled with.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching **primary objective** of "WORLDMEM" is to develop a framework that enables **long-term consistent world simulation with memory**. This directly addresses the significant challenge posed by the limited temporal context windows in existing video generative models, which frequently lead to spatial and temporal inconsistencies in simulated environments.

The **specific motivations and secondary objectives** driving this research are:

1. **Overcoming Inconsistency Due to Limited Context:** Current video generative models typically discard previously generated content, resulting in a "forgetting" phenomenon where returning to a previously seen location yields a different, inconsistent scene. The core motivation is to counteract this by providing a mechanism to retain and retrieve historical information.
2. **Accurate Reconstruction Across Gaps:** To facilitate truly consistent world simulation, the model must be capable of accurately reconstructing previously observed scenes even when there are significant viewpoint changes or large temporal gaps between observations. This requires sophisticated spatiotemporal reasoning.
3. **Modeling Dynamic World Evolution:** Beyond static scene consistency, the framework aims to capture and reflect dynamic changes in the environment over time. This includes evolving events (like plant growth) or user interactions (like placing objects), allowing the simulated world to respond realistically to the passage of time.
4. **Enabling Perception and Interaction:** The goal is to create a simulated world where an agent can not only perceive its surroundings accurately but also interact with them (e.g., placing objects), with these interactions having long-term, consistent effects on the environment.
5. **Developing a Flexible and Scalable Solution:** The authors seek a solution that avoids the inflexibility and detail loss associated with explicit 3D reconstruction methods, as well as the limitations of overfitting to specific scenarios or abstract feature representations. The proposed memory mechanism should be flexible enough to handle diverse and evolving environments and scalable using modern hardware and attention architectures.
6. **Providing General-Purpose Memory for Generative Models:** The design of WORLDMEM's memory mechanism is intended to be general enough to inspire future research on memory-based solutions for various generative tasks, especially those requiring long-term coherence.

In essence, "WORLDMEM" aims to build a simulated world that behaves as expected: objects and environmental features persist, changes propagate logically, and past observations can be reliably recalled and reproduced, even after extensive exploration or significant time lapses.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WORLDMEM's methodology is centered on integrating an external memory mechanism into an autoregressive conditional diffusion transformer, designed to achieve long-term consistency in world simulation.

#### 3.1. Foundational Architecture: Interactive World Simulator

The framework builds upon a strong baseline for interactive world simulation, leveraging:
- **Conditional Diffusion Transformer (CDiT)** (Peebles and Xie, 2023): This Transformer-based architecture is used for high-fidelity video generation.
- **Diffusion Forcing (DF)** (Chen et al., 2025): This paradigm enables stable and flexible autoregressive video generation by employing per-frame noise levels, extending generation beyond typical context windows.
The model consists of multiple DiT blocks with spatial and temporal modules. Interaction is facilitated by embedding external control signals (actions) into the model via MLPs and Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN), which condition the temporal blocks.

#### 3.2. Memory Representation and Retrieval

To overcome the limited context window, WORLDMEM introduces a memory mechanism with a **memory bank**:
- **Memory Units:** The memory bank stores tuples `{(x_m_i, p_i, t_i)}`, where `x_m_i` is a **token-level latent representation** of a memory frame (compressed by a visual encoder), `p_i ∈ R^5` is its 5D pose (x, y, z, pitch, yaw), and `t_i` is its timestamp. Storing at the token level retains detail while being more flexible than abstract features.
- **Memory Retrieval Algorithm:** Since the model cannot condition on all past memory, an efficient selection strategy is crucial. WORLDMEM uses a greedy matching algorithm (Algorithm 1) based on a **confidence score** that combines:
 - **Field-of-View (FOV) overlap ratio:** Computed via Monte Carlo sampling, this measures how much of the current scene's view overlaps with a memory frame's view.
 - **Temporal proximity:** The time difference between the current frame and the memory frame.
 This strategy ensures that the most relevant historical information (spatially and temporally) is retrieved. A **similarity filtering** step is also applied to remove redundant memory units.

#### 3.3. State-aware Memory Condition

The core innovation for robust reconstruction across spatiotemporal gaps is the **state-aware attention mechanism**:
- **State Embedding:** To provide explicit spatial and temporal context, each memory unit's state (`p_i`, `t_i`) is converted into an embedding:
 - **Pose Embedding:** Plücker embedding (Sitzmann et al., 2021) is used to transform the 5D pose into dense positional features `PE(p) ∈ R^(h×w×6)`. This rich geometric representation helps in spatial reasoning.
 - **Timestamp Embedding:** A lightweight MLP processes sinusoidal-embedded timestamps `SE(t)`.
 - These are combined: `E = G_p(PE(p)) + G_t(SE(t))`, where `G_p` and `G_t` are MLPs.
- **State-aware Memory Attention (Cross-Attention):** Standard cross-attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) is used, but both the query features (from the current input frames) and key features (from retrieved memory frames) are **enriched with their corresponding state embeddings**. This `˜X_q = X_q + E_q` and `˜X_k = X_k + E_k` allows the attention mechanism to reason about spatial and temporal relationships alongside visual features.
- **Relative State Formulation:** To simplify learning and improve robustness to viewpoint changes, a relative state formulation is adopted. The current query frame's state is treated as a zero reference, and the states of key frames are normalized to relative values.

#### 3.4. Incorporating Memory into the Pipeline

- **Noise Level Assignment:** During training, memory frames are assigned the lowest noise level (`k_min`, treated as "clean" ground truth), while context window frames receive independently sampled noise. During inference, both memory and context frames are treated as clean inputs (`k_min`), while the frames being generated are assigned the highest noise level (`k_max`).
- **Temporal Attention Mask:** A specific attention mask `A_mask` is applied to ensure causal attention (each frame only attends to preceding frames) and prevent memory units from interacting with each other, focusing their influence solely on the generation of new frames.

This comprehensive approach allows WORLDMEM to flexibly retrieve relevant historical context, spatially and temporally align it with the current generation task using state-aware attention, and integrate it into a diffusion-based video generation pipeline for long-term consistent world simulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in "WORLDMEM" validate its effectiveness across both virtual (Minecraft) and real-world (RealEstate10K) scenarios, demonstrating superior performance in long-term consistency and quality.

#### 4.1. Results on Minecraft Benchmark

The Minecraft benchmark, featuring diverse terrains, actions, and interactions, served as a robust environment for evaluation. Comparisons were made against a standard full-sequence (Full Seq.) training method and Diffusion Forcing (DF).

- **Within Context Window Evaluation:**
 - WORLDMEM significantly outperformed both Full Seq. and DF in PSNR (25.98 vs. 20.14 and 24.11), LPIPS (0.0072 vs. 0.0691 and 0.0094), and rFID (13.73 vs. 13.87 and 13.88).
 - This indicates that even for short-term consistency, the integrated memory mechanism provides a benefit by allowing greater information exchange and more robust self-contained consistency.
- **Beyond Context Window Evaluation:**
 - This is where WORLDMEM showed its most dramatic advantage. Full-sequence methods could not perform long rollouts. DF exhibited poor performance (PSNR 17.32, LPIPS 0.4376, rFID 51.28), suffering from severe inconsistency and quality degradation after generating 100 frames beyond its 8-frame context, even with a 600-frame memory bank initialized.
 - WORLDMEM, in contrast, maintained significantly higher quality and consistency (PSNR 23.98, LPIPS 0.1429, rFID 15.37), effectively recalling and reconstructing past scenes after extensive exploration.
- **Qualitative Results:**
 - Visualizations (Figure 3) demonstrated WORLDMEM's ability to model diverse dynamics (e.g., rain) and, critically, persist user-placed objects (like hay or pumpkin lights) or environmental changes (like growing wheat) over hundreds of frames and significant camera movements. This highlights its capability for both perception and interaction within the dynamic world.

#### 4.2. Results on RealEstate10K (Real Scenarios)

Evaluation on RealEstate10K, a dataset of real-world video clips, focused on loop closure consistency over trajectories longer than typical training lengths.

- WORLDMEM achieved superior performance across all metrics (PSNR 23.34, LPIPS 0.1672, rFID 43.14) compared to CameraCtrl, TrajAttn, Viewcrafter, and DFoT.
- The results showcased WORLDMEM's robustness in real-world scenes, even though the dataset's short, non-interactive nature limits the full display of its dynamic interaction capabilities. Viewcrafter, using explicit 3D reconstruction, performed well but was ultimately surpassed by WORLDMEM's flexible memory approach.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies provided critical insights into the design choices:

- **Embedding Designs:**
 - Dense Plücker embeddings for poses significantly outperformed sparse embeddings, emphasizing the importance of rich pose representations.
 - Switching from absolute to **relative state encoding** further improved performance (especially LPIPS and rFID), demonstrating its effectiveness in facilitating relationship reasoning and maintaining stability over long sequences.
- **Sampling Strategy for Training:**
 - **Progressive sampling** (gradually expanding the memory range during training) was found to be crucial, significantly outperforming fixed small-range or large-range sampling strategies. This highlights the benefit of a curriculum learning approach for effective memory reasoning.
- **Time Condition:**
 - Incorporating **timestamps** into both embedding and retrieval mechanisms significantly improved PSNR and LPIPS, enabling the model to faithfully reproduce event changes and differentiate between states at the same location but different times.
- **Memory Retrieval Strategy:**
 - Random sampling from the memory bank led to poor performance.
 - A **confidence-based filtering** strategy dramatically enhanced consistency and quality.
 - Further refinement by **similarity filtering** (removing redundant units) provided additional improvements across all metrics.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that WORLDMEM's novel memory-augmented framework, combined with state-aware attention and carefully designed retrieval and embedding strategies, successfully addresses the challenge of long-term consistency in world simulation, outperforming existing methods and enabling dynamic, interactive experiences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of WORLDMEM marks a significant step forward in the field of generative AI and interactive world simulation, with profound implications and potential impacts across various domains.

1. **Resolving a Fundamental Challenge in Generative Models:** WORLDMEM directly addresses the "forgetting" problem inherent in many autoregressive generative models due to their limited context windows. By providing a robust mechanism for long-term memory, it ensures spatial and temporal consistency over extended simulated periods, which is crucial for any realistic or functional virtual environment. This opens avenues for more reliable and immersive generative experiences.

2. **Advancing World Simulation Capabilities:** The ability to simulate diverse, dynamic, and consistently evolving worlds without explicit 3D reconstruction offers a flexible and scalable alternative to traditional methods. This is particularly impactful for:
 - **Autonomous Navigation and Robotics:** Training agents in simulated environments that remain consistent over long exploration paths can lead to more robust and generalizable policies for real-world deployment. Agents can learn to remember previously visited locations and objects, improving their navigation and interaction strategies.
 - **Next-Generation Game Engines and Virtual Reality:** WORLDMEM’s approach could lay the groundwork for generative game engines capable of creating vast, dynamic, and coherent open worlds on the fly, reducing manual asset creation and enabling truly emergent gameplay. This could revolutionize content generation in virtual spaces.
 - **Agent Learning and Reinforcement Learning:** Providing agents with consistent world models is fundamental for complex tasks and long-term planning, enabling them to build more sophisticated understandings of their environment and the consequences of their actions.

3. **Methodological Innovations:** The paper introduces several key innovations that can inspire future research:
 - **Token-level Memory Bank with State Cues:** This approach balances detail retention with flexibility, offering a powerful alternative to abstract features or rigid 3D representations.
 - **State-aware Memory Attention:** Explicitly conditioning attention mechanisms on detailed pose and timestamp embeddings allows for highly accurate spatiotemporal reasoning over large gaps, a novel and effective strategy.
 - **Relative State Encoding:** The demonstration that relative state formulations simplify the learning objective and improve long-term stability is a valuable contribution for any task involving pose-dependent generative processes.
 - **Progressive Training and Smart Retrieval:** The effectiveness of progressive sampling and confidence-based, similarity-filtered memory retrieval highlights the importance of curriculum learning and intelligent information management in memory-augmented generative systems.

4. **Enabling Richer Interaction and Dynamic Environments:** WORLDMEM's capacity to model dynamic environmental changes (e.g., plant growth, weather) and the persistence of user-placed objects underscores its potential for highly interactive and responsive simulations. This moves beyond merely generating video sequences to creating living, evolving worlds that react to actions and the passage of time.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as potential retrieval failures in occluded views, limited interaction diversity in current real-world scenarios, and linear memory usage for extremely long sequences, WORLDMEM provides a strong foundation. Its promising results and scalable designs open up exciting avenues for future research in memory-based world simulators, paving the way for more immersive, intelligent, and truly consistent virtual worlds.
