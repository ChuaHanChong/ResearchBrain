# Explicit Kinematic Guidance from Analytic Concepts for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Westlake University introduce SAGE, a framework that integrates explicit 3D structural and kinematic knowledge into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. The framework leverages "Analytic Concepts" to provide dense rewards and precise action-space constraints, leading to improved success rates and accelerated learning efficiency across various manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily process 2D visual data, leading to limited 3D spatial awareness and understanding of object structures and kinematic constraints.
- These models struggle with generalization to novel objects and environments, requiring extensive data for fine-tuning due to their implicit re-learning of fundamental physical principles.
- Reinforcement learning for VLA models faces challenges with sparse reward functions for complex, constrained manipulation tasks, hindering learning efficiency.

## Method

- The **SAGE** (Spatial Analytic-concept Guided Enhancement) framework introduces a =="Concept Expert"== module that uses =="Analytic Concepts"== to represent objects as programmatic blueprints of their topological structures and affordances.
- A two-phase guidance mechanism initializes concept parameters from 3D vision models and dynamically tracks these parameters during interaction by aligning ==VLA model features==.
- **SAGE** provides high-quality guidance for VLA fine-tuning through ==kinematic constraint supervision==, mapping physical constraints into the action space, and ==concept-derived rewards== (kinematic progress and affordance alignment) to overcome reward sparsity in reinforcement learning.

## Results

- SAGE-SFT improved OpenVLA-OFT's average success rate from **54.3%** to **69.0%** in offline learning (SimplerEnv), and SAGE-CQL achieved **71.7%** in offline reinforcement learning, surpassing all compared methods, particularly on kinematically complex tasks like 'Open/Close Drawer'.
- SAGE-enhanced online RL (PPO(SAGE) and GRPO(SAGE)) demonstrated faster convergence and consistently higher final success rates across diverse articulated object manipulation tasks in complex simulation environments.
- Real-world validation showed SAGE improved baseline success rates, for example, increasing 'Place the stapler in the lower drawer' from **60%** to **85%** on an AGILE PiPER Dual-Arm robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly embedding 3D structural and kinematic knowledge via "Analytic Concepts" effectively addresses the representational gap in 2D-centric VLA models, enhancing spatial awareness and physical reasoning.
> - Dynamically tracked analytic concept parameters can serve as a potent external supervision signal, providing dense rewards and precise action-space constraints that significantly improve learning efficiency and task success.
> - VLA models can learn physically grounded interaction behaviors more robustly and efficiently by integrating structured knowledge without altering their fundamental end-to-end learning paradigm.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is advancing through the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to enable robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute actions based on sensory inputs. Current VLA models, exemplified by architectures such as RT-1, Octo, and OpenVLA, primarily process 2D visual data. This reliance often results in a limited understanding of the 3D physical world, particularly concerning object structures, kinematic constraints, and commonsense physical knowledge. Consequently, these models exhibit constrained spatial awareness, reduced adaptability for complex, high-precision manipulation tasks, and difficulties in generalizing to novel environments or objects. Fine-tuning existing VLA models for new tasks often requires extensive, task-specific data, as the policies must implicitly re-learn fundamental physical principles (e.g., how a door rotates around a hinge or a drawer slides along a rail) with each new scene configuration.

This work positions itself as a methodology to address these limitations by integrating explicit 3D structural and kinematic knowledge into VLA models. It draws inspiration from cognitive science, specifically the Recognition-by-Components theory, which suggests that humans process objects by decomposing them into elementary volumetric primitives and their spatial relationships. By leveraging "Analytic Concepts," a pre-existing annotation paradigm for structural knowledge (Sun et al., 2024, 2025a), the research aims to move beyond implicit 2D perception toward an object-centric spatial understanding. This approach seeks to provide VLA models with explicit geometric and physical priors, thereby enhancing their ability to reason about physics and structure. This integration is intended to improve generalization capabilities and increase the efficiency of fine-tuning processes for robotic manipulation tasks. The proposed framework, SAGE, complements existing VLA architectures by acting as a post-training enhancement, providing structured guidance without altering their fundamental end-to-end learning paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to enable robots to robustly follow human instructions and manipulate unfamiliar objects within unstructured 3D environments, mitigating the challenges faced by contemporary VLA models. The motivation stems from several identified limitations:

- **Representational Gap:** Existing VLA models predominantly rely on 2D visual inputs, which often neglect rich 3D object structural information and commonsense physical knowledge inherent in the physical world. This deficiency restricts their spatial awareness and adaptability for intricate, high-precision manipulation tasks.
- **Limited Generalization:** Due to the absence of explicit geometric and physical priors, VLA models often struggle to generalize. Minor changes in environmental conditions, such as object appearance or illumination, can lead to significant performance degradation. This necessitates extensive data collection for fine-tuning to new visual and action domains.
- **Inefficient Learning:** Without structured 3D knowledge, VLA policies must repeatedly infer or rediscover fundamental physical constraints and affordances (e.g., the pivot point of a door, the sliding direction of a drawer) whenever encountering a new scene layout. This leads to inefficient use of data and computational resources during both pre-training and fine-tuning.
- **Reward Sparsity in RL:** For reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning of VLA models, the design of dense and informative reward functions for complex, constrained manipulation tasks remains a challenge. Sparse rewards hinder learning efficiency and often lead to unstable training.

To address these motivations, the research establishes the following key objectives:

- **Integrate Explicit 3D Knowledge:** To bridge the representational gap by constructing a "Concept Expert" module that builds and leverages executable "Analytic Concepts." These concepts represent objects as explicit, programmatic blueprints that encapsulate topological structures and affordance knowledge.
- **Develop a Two-Phase Guidance Mechanism:** To enable robust, object-centric learning through a synergistic two-phase process:
 1. **Initialization:** Prior to VLA inference, the Concept Expert will utilize 3D information from Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) to estimate initial kinematic and structural parameters of Analytic Concepts.
 2. **Dynamic Tracking:** Throughout the manipulation process, the VLA model's internal capabilities will assist in dynamically tracking these concept parameters, continuously aligning them with observational changes to maintain accuracy.
- **Provide High-Quality Guidance for VLA Fine-Tuning:** Once established, the Analytic Concepts will offer explicit, high-quality guidance for VLA model fine-tuning through two primary mechanisms:
 1. **Dense, Programmatic Manipulation Rewards:** For reinforcement learning, to overcome reward sparsity.
 2. **Precise Spatial Guidance:** For action space constraint regularization, to enable high-precision physical interactions.
- **Enhance Learning Efficiency and Robustness:** To demonstrate that this structured, concept-based guidance consistently improves success rates and learning efficiency across both supervised and reinforcement learning settings, allowing VLA models to learn physically grounded interaction behaviors while retaining end-to-end learning flexibility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, SAGE (Spatial Analytic-concept Guided Enhancement), integrates explicit structural knowledge into the VLA fine-tuning pipeline. SAGE introduces a Concept Expert module that wraps around any existing VLA model to instantiate and dynamically track Analytic Concepts, which represent objects as programmatic blueprints.

The methodology operates in two main synergistic phases:

#### 3.1. Analytic Concept Initialization (t = 0)

At the beginning of each episode, the Concept Expert establishes a structural understanding of the scene:

1. **Object Identification and Segmentation:** Given an input image (I0) and a natural language task instruction (l_task), a Vision-Language Model (VLM) identifies the task-relevant object. The object's visual observation is then segmented using models like SAM (Ravi et al., 2025).
2. **Concept Instantiation:** Within the Concept System, a VLM-driven Concept Constructor (Sun et al., 2025b) organizes geometric primitives to instantiate a corresponding "Manipulation Blueprint." This blueprint, composed of "Structural Blueprints" (mathematical procedures defining common spatial structures like layout and relationships) and "Manipulation Blueprints" (procedures defining interaction methods such as grasp poses and force directions), is tailored to the recognized object category.
3. **Parameter Estimation:** Geometry-aware features extracted by VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) are encoded into 3D-aware representations. These representations are fed into a Pre-trained Head, acting as the Structural Parameter Head (Wei et al., 2026). This module estimates the initial **Structural Parameters (P_s)**, which are time-invariant properties defining the object's geometry and kinematic structure (e.g., axis of rotation for a hinge), and the initial **Kinematic Parameters (P_0k)**, which describe the initial time-varying state of movable components (e.g., initial drawer displacement). This process fits the instantiated blueprint to the observed object.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Parameter Tracking (t > 0)

As the robot interacts with the environment, the kinematic parameters (P_k) evolve. SAGE dynamically tracks these changes by leveraging the VLA model's internal representations:

1. **VLA Feature Alignment:** Intermediate features (F_VLA) are extracted from a specific VLA Layer of the main VLA model. These features are processed by an Adapter (consisting of batch normalization and an MLP) to ensure dimensional compatibility with spatial representations (F_VGGT) derived from the 3D foundation model VGGT. An Alignment Strategy minimizes the cosine similarity loss (L_align) between the adapted F_VLA and F_VGGT, implicitly compelling the VLA model to learn features that are predictive of the object's explicit structural state.
2. **Dynamic Parameter Head:** The aligned features from the Adapter are fed into the Dynamic Parameter Head. This head continuously computes the updated **Dynamic Kinematic Parameters (P_tk)**, such as a drawer's current position. A cross-attention mechanism is utilized to embed object-centric 3D structure into the spatial representation, enhancing the head's sensitivity to object boundaries and kinematic changes. The VLA's intermediate features or action predictions serve as contextual priors to guide the tracker's attention.

#### 3.3. VLA Fine-Tuning

The dynamically tracked Analytic Concepts (P_t) provide external supervision through two mechanisms:

1. **Kinematic Constraint Supervision (L_kcs):**
 - The Concept Expert calculates an **Optimal Interaction Reference Direction (v*)**, a 3D unit vector representing the ideal motion direction (e.g., linear sliding for a drawer) required for precise manipulation according to the object's kinematic constraints.
 - A supervised loss term (L_kcs) minimizes the discrepancy between the VLA policy's predicted action direction (v_at) and v*. This directly maps physical constraints into the VLA's action space, supervising precise interaction dynamics.

2. **Concept Derived Rewards (R_AC):** For reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning, R_AC addresses reward sparsity and consists of two components:
 - **Kinematic Progress Reward (ϕ_prog):** Quantifies progress based on the continuously tracked P_tk. It measures the difference between the current cumulative kinematic change (ΔP_tk = P_tk - P_0k) and the total required change for task success (ΔP_goal_k). It is formulated as an exponential decay, providing dense, proximity-based feedback.
 - **Affordance Alignment Reward (ϕ_afford):** Utilizes structural parameters (P_s) and pre-defined Affordance Annotations (e.g., target grasp poses, G_set). It quantifies the geometric alignment between the robot's end-effector pose (G_tcp) and the nearest target affordance pose. This reward uses an exponential decay based on a combined 6D pose distance (positional L2 norm and angular error) to guide the agent towards correct initial grasping or pre-grasping configurations.
 - The total R_AC is a linear combination of ϕ_prog and ϕ_afford, serving as the auxiliary part of the overall reward function (r_t = α ⋅ I_success + (1 - α) ⋅ R_AC(st)).

#### 3.4. Total Optimization Objective

The complete objective for VLA fine-tuning in SAGE is given by:
L_total = L_task + λ_k * L_kcs + λ_a * L_align
where L_task is the task-specific loss (e.g., RL objective or cross-entropy for SFT), and λ_k and λ_a are scalar hyperparameters. The weight λ_a for the alignment loss is annealed over time to prioritize spatial consistency early in training.

Upon deployment, the learned VLA policy operates directly on raw camera observations, with only a lightweight forward pass through the Concept Expert required.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the SAGE framework was conducted across offline, online, and real-world settings, using various VLA models and manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Offline Learning Evaluation

Experiments in the SimplerEnv, focusing on tasks with canonical kinematic constraints, demonstrated the efficacy of SAGE variants:

- **SAGE-SFT:** This variant, incorporating Analytic Concept and Kinematic Constraint Supervision, improved the average success rate of the OpenVLA-OFT baseline from 54.3% to 69.0%. For the π0 baseline, it increased the average success rate from 56.8% to 66.3%.
- **SAGE-CQL:** This variant, which integrated Concept-Derived Rewards into the CQL objective for offline RL, achieved an average success rate of 71.7%. This outcome surpassed all other compared methods, including SpatialVLA(FT) (70.6%), which is specifically designed for 3D inputs. The impact was particularly pronounced in kinematically complex tasks such as "Open/Close Drawer."

#### 4.2. Online Reinforcement Learning Performance

The framework's performance during active interaction was assessed using established online RL algorithms (PPO and GRPO) in both SimplerEnv and the more complex Sapien simulation environment, featuring articulated objects from the ShapeNet-Mobility dataset.

- **Open Drawer Task (SimplerEnv):** Learning curves indicated that SAGE-enhanced variants (PPO(SAGE) and GRPO(SAGE)) exhibited faster convergence and achieved higher final success rates compared to their non-SAGE counterparts. This suggests that the explicit knowledge injection from SAGE stabilizes exploration and accelerates the learning process.
- **Articulated Object Manipulation (ShapeNet Mobility):** Across six challenging tasks (Faucet, Microwave, Laptop, StorageFurniture, KitchenPot, Bucket), both PPO(SAGE) and GRPO(SAGE) consistently outperformed their baselines. PPO(SAGE) achieved the highest overall success rates, reaching near-perfect performance on tasks such as "Microwave," "StorageFurniture," and "KitchenPot." These results indicate the effectiveness of SAGE's explicit knowledge mechanisms for online RL in diverse, contact-rich interactions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the "Open Drawer" task quantified the contribution of different SAGE components:

- **Removal of L_align:** Excluding the feature alignment loss (L_align) consistently degraded performance, suggesting its role in learning stable representations aligned with analytic concepts.
- **Using Ground-Truth Kinematic Parameters (P*k):** Providing ground-truth kinematic parameters resulted in a slight performance gain, indicating that accurate kinematic parameter estimation is beneficial, but also implying that SAGE's inferred parameters are sufficiently accurate for effective policy learning.
- **Using Ground-Truth Kinematic and Structural Parameters (P*k + P*s):** Supplying both ground-truth kinematic and structural parameters yielded comparable or only marginally improved performance. This result suggests that the structural parameters estimated by the Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) are precise enough, and any residual estimation error does not substantially impact RL outcomes.

#### 4.4. Real-World Validation

The SAGE-SFT framework was applied to the π0.5 baseline policy on an AGILE PiPER Dual-Arm robot for real-world validation across five manipulation tasks.

- SAGE consistently improved the baseline's success rates for tasks involving precise, multi-step manipulation. For example, "Place the stapler in the lower drawer" saw an improvement from 60% to 85% success, and "Stack blue bowl on middle then stack the pink bowl" improved from 60% to 90%. This demonstrated SAGE's capability to enhance policies in physical environments.

#### 4.5. Analytic Concept Instantiation Reliability

Quantitative evaluation on PartNet-Mobility (Appendix A.1) demonstrated the reliability of the Analytic Concept System:

- **Concept Identification Accuracy:** The VLM-based Concept Constructor achieved high accuracy (94.2% on training categories, 95.6% on testing categories).
- **Parameter Estimation Accuracy:** The average distance between the point cloud of detected parts and the mesh rendered from estimated analytic concept parameters was low (5.18mm for training categories, 6.55mm for testing categories). This suggests accurate parameter estimation and structural fitting.
- The system also exhibited robustness, capable of tolerating some VLM recognition errors due to the adaptability of parameterized Analytic Concepts.
- **General Coverage:** An analysis showed that Analytic Concepts are reusable across categories, with a diminishing marginal increase in concepts needed as object categories grow (e.g., 39 concepts covered all 46 PartNet-Mobility categories), supporting scalability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by explicitly incorporating structural and kinematic knowledge, addressing several limitations inherent in existing 2D-centric approaches. The proposed SAGE framework provides a mechanism to ground VLA models in the physical realities of 3D objects and their affordances, fostering more robust and efficient robotic manipulation.

The work's significance lies in its demonstrated ability to consistently improve VLA model performance. The observed gains in success rate and learning efficiency across both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) settings, in both simulated and real-world environments, indicate that explicit kinematic guidance from Analytic Concepts is beneficial. By translating abstract object knowledge into dense rewards and precise action-space constraints, SAGE alleviates common challenges like reward sparsity in RL and the need for extensive task-specific data. This approach allows VLA models to acquire physically grounded interaction behaviors without sacrificing the flexibility of end-to-end learning.

The potential impact of this research includes:

- **Enhanced Generalization:** By providing explicit structural and physical priors, SAGE can enable VLA models to generalize more effectively to novel object instances and adapt efficiently to new manipulation tasks, reducing the dependence on large, domain-specific datasets.
- **Improved Precision and Robustness:** The kinematic constraint supervision and affordance alignment mechanisms facilitate high-precision physical interactions, particularly for complex tasks involving articulated objects, where fine-grained control and understanding of object mechanics are critical.
- **Accelerated Learning:** The dense, concept-derived rewards are shown to accelerate convergence in RL, making the training process more data-efficient and practical for real-world robotic applications.
- **Bridging Perception-Action Gap:** The framework offers a structured way to connect high-level visual and language understanding from foundation models with low-level robot actions, explicitly grounding abstract instructions in physical reality.

Future research directions suggested by this work include extending Analytic Concepts to handle deformable objects, which currently lie outside the scope of the system's focus on structured regularities. Further optimization of the computational cost associated with concept initialization, while not affecting real-time inference, could also be considered. Additionally, evaluating SAGE on a broader range of VLA architectures would further establish its generality and practical applicability across the evolving landscape of robotic learning models. This work contributes to the development of more capable and intelligent robotic systems that can explicitly reason about and interact with the physical world.
