# DriveDreamer-2: LLM-Enhanced World Models for Diverse Driving Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> DriveDreamer-2 presents an approach to generate diverse, user-customized multi-view driving videos by integrating a Large Language Model for scenario control via text prompts. It achieves improved spatio-temporal consistency across views and demonstrates that its synthetic data enhances 3D object detection and tracking performance for autonomous driving.

## Problem

- Existing world models for autonomous driving heavily rely on structured inputs (e.g., 3D bounding boxes, HDMaps) for video generation, limiting user interactivity and the diversity of generated scenarios.
- Collecting sufficient real-world data for 'long-tail scenarios' (rare, challenging events) for autonomous driving system training is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- Maintaining spatio-temporal consistency across multiple camera views is a significant technical challenge in multi-view video generation, often leading to inconsistencies or inefficiencies in prior methods.

## Method

- The system employs a three-stage pipeline: a finetuned LLM generates precise ==BEV== agent trajectories from natural language queries.
- A diffusion model-based HDMap Generator, conditioned on the BEV trajectory map, produces high-definition maps that align with traffic regulations.
- A Unified Multi-View Video Model (**UniMVM**), built upon ==Stable Video Diffusion==, synthesizes multi-view driving videos by unifying views into a single patch and incorporating conditional inputs like HDMaps and 3D bounding boxes.

## Results

- DriveDreamer-2 achieves superior video generation quality, obtaining an **FID of 25.0** and **FVD of 105.1** without image conditioning, and an **FID of 11.2** and **FVD of 55.7** with first-frame multi-view image conditioning, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods.
- Synthetic videos generated by DriveDreamer-2 enhance downstream 3D object detection, improving mAP by **3.8%** and NDS by **4.4%** when used to augment training data for StreamPETR without image condition.
- The method also improves multi-object tracking, boosting AMOTA by **8.3%** and AMOTP by **2.3%** without image condition, demonstrating the practical utility of its diverse synthetic data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can be finetuned to translate natural language prompts into precise agent trajectories, enabling user-friendly customization of complex driving scenarios.
> - A Unified Multi-View Model (UniMVM) significantly improves spatio-temporal consistency across multiple camera views by treating them as a single concatenated image, enhancing realism and coherence.
> - Synthetically generated diverse driving videos, particularly those created without direct image conditioning, effectively augment real-world datasets, leading to improved performance in downstream 3D object detection and tracking tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" for autonomous driving has emerged as a crucial area of research, aiming to create dynamic environmental models that allow intelligent agents to predict future scenarios and plan actions. These models are particularly valuable for generating diverse driving videos, especially for "long-tail scenarios" (rare, challenging events) that are difficult to collect in real-world data. The generated videos can then be used to augment training data for various driving perception and planning algorithms, enhancing robustness and safety.

Historically, early world models often operated in simplified environments like Bird’s Eye View (BEV) semantic segmentation space or relied on Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. More recently, the field has seen a significant leap forward with the integration of **diffusion models** (e.g., Stable Diffusion, SVD) and **large language models (LLMs)**. Notable works like DriveDreamer, Drive-WM, MagicDrive, Panacea, GAIA-1, and ADriver-I have pushed the boundaries.

However, a significant limitation of many existing methods is their heavy reliance on **structured information** (e.g., 3D bounding boxes, High-Definition Maps (HDMaps), optical flow) or real-world image frames as conditions for video generation. This dependency restricts interactivity, making it difficult for users to directly influence the generated content, and consequently, limits the diversity of the generated scenarios. For instance, creating a video of a specific, rare traffic maneuver might require meticulously annotating 3D boxes or providing a precise initial image.

"DriveDreamer-2" positions itself directly to address these limitations. It bridges the gap between user-friendly natural language inputs and complex driving scene generation. By incorporating an LLM, it moves beyond rigid structured inputs, allowing users to define scenarios with simple text prompts. This represents a significant advancement in the field of controllable video generation, particularly for autonomous driving, where the ability to simulate diverse and challenging scenarios on demand is highly valuable. The work also contributes to the general domain of video generation by proposing the Unified Multi-View Model (UniMVM) to improve spatio-temporal consistency across multiple camera views, a persistent challenge in multi-view synthesis. Furthermore, by demonstrating the utility of synthetic data for improving downstream perception and tracking tasks, DriveDreamer-2 reinforces the practical value of high-quality world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DriveDreamer-2 is to enable the **generation of diverse, user-customized multi-view driving videos** in a user-friendly manner. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enhance Interactivity and Diversity:** Current world models for autonomous driving often require complex structured inputs (e.g., 3D bounding boxes, precise HDMaps, or initial image frames) to condition video generation. This limits user interaction and the ability to easily generate novel or "uncommon" scenarios. DriveDreamer-2 aims to overcome this by allowing users to input simple text descriptions.
2. **Generate Long-Tail Scenarios:** Autonomous driving systems need to be robust to rare and challenging situations (e.g., abrupt cut-ins, pedestrians crossing unexpectedly). Collecting sufficient real-world data for such long-tail scenarios is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. DriveDreamer-2 seeks to provide a controllable mechanism to synthesize these scenarios on demand.
3. **Improve Data Augmentation for Downstream Tasks:** The ultimate utility of synthetic driving videos lies in their ability to augment real datasets, thereby improving the training of perception and planning modules for autonomous vehicles. A key motivation is to demonstrate that videos generated by DriveDreamer-2, particularly those with increased diversity, can effectively boost the performance of 3D object detection and tracking methods.
4. **Advance Spatio-Temporal Coherence in Multi-View Video Generation:** Generating consistent video across multiple camera views (e.g., front, front-left, back-right) is a significant technical challenge. Existing methods often struggle with maintaining coherence or achieve it at the expense of efficiency or diversity. DriveDreamer-2 aims to develop a novel architecture, UniMVM, to specifically address and improve this aspect.
5. **Build upon Prior Work (DriveDreamer):** As an extension of the original DriveDreamer, the motivation also includes pushing the boundaries of the established framework by integrating state-of-the-art LLMs and refining the video generation pipeline for superior performance and user control.

In summary, DriveDreamer-2 is motivated by the need for more flexible, diverse, and high-quality synthetic data for autonomous driving, achievable through intuitive user control via natural language, with a strong focus on enhancing the realism and utility of generated multi-view videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DriveDreamer-2 proposes a novel three-stage pipeline to achieve user-customized multi-view driving video generation: 1) Customized Traffic Simulation via LLM, 2) HDMap Generation, and 3) Unified Multi-View Video Model (UniMVM) for video generation. The entire process begins with a user text query and culminates in a multi-view driving video.

**Overall Framework (Fig. 2):**
The pipeline starts with a user-provided text query (e.g., "On a rainy day, there is a car cutting in."). This query is processed by a finetuned Large Language Model (LLM) to generate BEV (Bird's Eye View) agent trajectories. These trajectories, representing the motion of the ego-car and other agents, along with the text prompt, condition an HDMap Generator to produce a BEV HDMap (High-Definition Map) that defines the road structure. Finally, the generated agent trajectories (as 3D boxes) and the HDMap (as structural information) are fed into the Unified Multi-View Video Model (UniMVM), built upon the DriveDreamer framework, to synthesize multi-view driving videos.

#### 3.1. Customized Traffic Simulation

This stage disentangles the traffic simulation into foreground (agent trajectories) and background (HDMaps) conditions.

- **Finetuning LLM for Trajectory Generation:**
 - **Problem:** Traditional traffic simulation requires intricate parameter specification (speed, position, acceleration).
 - **Solution:** DriveDreamer-2 finetunes a Large Language Model (specifically, GPT-3.5) to convert user-friendly text inputs into precise agent trajectories.
 - **Mechanism:** A "trajectory-generation function library" is constructed. This library contains 18 functions covering various agent maneuvers (steering, constant speed, acceleration, braking, cut-ins, U-turns), pedestrian actions (crossing), and utility functions (e.g., setting random seeds, saving trajectories).
 - **Data Curation:** Manually curated "Text-to-Python-Script pairs" are used to finetune the LLM. For instance, the prompt "a vehicle cuts in" corresponds to a Python script using `agent.cut_in` and `agent.forward` functions.
 - **Output:** The finetuned LLM directly outputs agent trajectories as array data.

- **HDMap Generation:**
 - **Motivation:** A complete traffic simulation needs consistent background elements (lanes, crosswalks) that adhere to traffic regulations and align with the foreground trajectories.
 - **Approach:** HDMap generation is formulated as a conditional image generation problem using a diffusion model.
 - **Input/Output:** The conditional input is a BEV trajectory map (T_b), where different agent categories are represented by distinct colors. The target is a BEV HDMap (H_b) with channels for lane boundaries, lane dividers, and pedestrian crossings.
 - **Architecture:** Built upon Stable Diffusion 2.1 (SD2.1), with ControlNet parameters made trainable to incorporate the trajectory map condition. Stacks of 2D convolution layers integrate the trajectory map features into the diffusion model.
 - **Training:** Optimized using denoising score matching (Eq. 1), ensuring that generated HDMaps are diverse but also adhere to traffic constraints and seamlessly integrate with the provided trajectories (e.g., lanes on either side of dividers).

#### 3.2. Unified Multi-View Model (UniMVM)

This component is designed to enhance temporal and spatial coherence in the generated multi-view driving videos.

- **Challenge:** Previous methods struggled with maintaining consistency across multiple camera views (e.g., front, front-left, back). View-wise attention or sequential generation of views (e.g., p(x2|x1)...) can be inefficient or compromise consistency.
- **UniMVM Formulation:**
 - Instead of generating views independently, UniMVM unifies multiple views (e.g., {FL, F, FR, BR, B, BL}) into a single, spatially unified image patch `x'` (concatenated horizontally).
 - Video generation is then treated as generating this single large patch `p(x')`.
 - A key innovation is the use of a **mask `m`** (Eq. 3). By adjusting this mask, UniMVM can perform various video generation tasks:
 - **Future video prediction:** Masking future `T-1` frames (conditioned on the first frame).
 - **Multi-view outpainting:** Masking specific views (e.g., {FL, FR, BR, B, BL}) while conditioning on a front-view video input.
 - **Generation without image condition:** Masking all video frames. This allows for maximum diversity.
 - This unified approach eliminates the need for separate cross-view parameters, simplifying the architecture while improving coherence and potentially efficiency.
- **Video Generation Process:**
 - The structured conditions (HDMaps and 3D bounding boxes derived from agent trajectories) are also unified into sequences aligned with the concatenated multi-view images.
 - 3D boxes are directly projected onto the image plane to serve as control conditions, avoiding additional control parameters.
 - Three encoders embed HDMaps (`y_H`), 3D boxes (`y_B`), and image frames (`y_I`) into a latent space.
 - The core video generator is built upon **Stable Video Diffusion (SVD)**, which is finetuned for this task. It utilizes denoising score matching (Eq. 5) to learn the generative process, incorporating the conditional signals (`y_H`, `y_B`, and text prompt embeddings `c_p`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DriveDreamer-2 demonstrates significant advancements in controllable and diverse multi-view driving video generation, backed by both qualitative and quantitative evidence.

#### 4.1. User-Customized Driving Video Generation

- **Diversity and User-Friendliness:** DriveDreamer-2 successfully generates multi-view driving videos based on simple text prompts (e.g., "on a rainy day, there is a car cut in").
- **Uncommon Scenarios:** It can synthesize complex and uncommon scenarios like vehicles abruptly cutting in, pedestrians crossing unexpectedly at night, or ego cars changing lanes.
- **Realism:** Qualitative examples (Fig. 1a, Fig. 6) show high visual realism, including details like reflections of high beams on pedestrians during a rainy night scene. The ability to generate long, temporally consistent videos (e.g., a pedestrian crossing, a car cutting in over multiple frames, and even dynamic elements like windshield wipers moving on a truck in a rainy scene) further highlights its realism and scene understanding (Fig. 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 in supplementary materials).

#### 4.2. Quality Evaluation of Generated Videos

- **Superior Generation Quality:** DriveDreamer-2 consistently outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods across various experimental settings on the nuScenes validation set, using FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) and FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) as metrics (lower is better).
 - **Without Image Condition:** DriveDreamer-2 achieves FID of 25.0 and FVD of 105.1, significantly improving over DriveDreamer (26.8 FID, 353.2 FVD).
 - **With 1-View Video Condition:** Despite being limited to a single-view input, DriveDreamer-2 (18.4 FID, 74.9 FVD) shows a 39% relative improvement in FVD compared to Drive-WM, which uses a three-view video condition (15.8 FID, 122.7 FVD).
 - **With 1st-Frame Multi-View Image Condition (Optimal Setting):** DriveDreamer-2 achieves the best performance with an FID of 11.2 and FVD of 55.7. This represents relative improvements of approximately 30% in FID and 50% in FVD compared to previous best-performing methods like Panacea (16.9 FID, 139.0 FVD).

#### 4.3. Downstream Task Enhancement (Data Augmentation)

- **Improved 3D Object Detection:** When synthetic videos generated by DriveDreamer-2 are used to augment real training data for StreamPETR (a 3D object detection method), performance metrics show improvement.
 - With initial frame condition: mAP improves by 2.8% and NDS by 3.9% relative to baseline.
 - **Without image condition (higher diversity):** mAP improves by 3.8% and NDS by 4.4% relative to baseline. This highlights the value of generating diverse scenarios, even if they don't perfectly match a specific initial frame style.
- **Improved Multi-Object Tracking:** Similar enhancements are observed for multi-object tracking.
 - With initial frame condition: AMOTA improves by 8.0% and AMOTP by 1.6%.
 - **Without image condition:** AMOTA improves by 8.3% and AMOTP by 2.3% relative to baseline. These significant relative improvements underscore the practical utility of DriveDreamer-2's synthetic data for robust autonomous driving perception.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Diffusion Backbone (SVD vs. SD1.4):** Using SVD as the backbone for the video generator significantly improves FVD (from 340.8 to 94.6) compared to the SD1.4 used in the original DriveDreamer, demonstrating the benefit of a stronger video-specific prior.
- **Effectiveness of UniMVM:** The UniMVM module is crucial for multi-view consistency. Without UniMVM, generated videos show inconsistencies between views (e.g., foreground vehicles and background structures). With UniMVM, both foreground and background elements are consistent across views, leading to significant improvements in FID (from 17.2 to 11.2) and FVD (from 94.6 to 55.7).
- **Influence of Conditions on Diversity:**
 - Using the 1st-frame multi-view image condition leads to high fidelity and similarity to ground truth, with constrained diversity.
 - Using a 1-view video condition introduces more diversity while retaining some GT aspects.
 - Generating without any image condition yields the highest diversity, with generated appearances (e.g., car colors, street backgrounds) significantly differing from ground truth, aligning with the goal of generating diverse scenarios for data augmentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DriveDreamer-2 represents a significant step forward in the field of autonomous driving world models and controllable video generation, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging Natural Language and Driving Simulation:** This work pioneers the integration of Large Language Models directly into the pipeline of generating complex, realistic multi-view driving videos. By enabling user-customized scenario generation via simple text prompts, DriveDreamer-2 significantly lowers the barrier for simulating diverse traffic conditions. This marks a shift from data-intensive, manual scenario creation to a more intuitive, AI-driven process.

2. **Enhanced Data Augmentation for Autonomous Driving:** The demonstrated improvements in downstream 3D object detection and multi-object tracking tasks, particularly when using synthetic data generated *without* explicit image conditions (which fosters greater diversity), highlight the practical utility of DriveDreamer-2. This capability is critical for training robust autonomous driving systems, especially for handling rare "long-tail" events and corner cases that are underrepresented in real-world datasets. The ability to create these challenging scenarios on demand can accelerate development and validation cycles, leading to safer and more reliable self-driving cars.

3. **Advancements in Multi-View Video Consistency:** The proposed Unified Multi-View Model (UniMVM) is a notable technical contribution to the broader field of generative AI. By unifying multiple camera views into a single patch and intelligently using masking strategies, UniMVM effectively addresses the complex challenge of maintaining spatio-temporal coherence across different perspectives. This approach can inspire future research in multi-camera synthesis beyond autonomous driving, such as virtual reality, surveillance, or content creation.

4. **Foundation for Interactive Driving Simulators and Testing:** DriveDreamer-2 lays a strong foundation for building highly interactive and customizable driving simulators. Engineers and researchers could use such a system to quickly prototype and test specific driving behaviors or perception algorithms under precisely defined, yet synthetically generated, conditions. This could revolutionize the way autonomous vehicles are developed, tested, and validated, making the process more efficient and comprehensive.

5. **New Possibilities for AI Research:** The success of combining LLMs with generative world models opens new avenues for research in understanding and generating complex, dynamic environments. It suggests that LLMs can go beyond text generation to serve as powerful interfaces for controlling and orchestrating complex visual simulations, potentially leading to more advanced AI agents capable of planning and reasoning in diverse environments.

In conclusion, DriveDreamer-2 not only pushes the boundaries of autonomous driving simulation by making it more accessible and diverse but also contributes fundamentally to the capabilities of generative AI and its intersection with natural language understanding. Its practical implications for safety and development in the autonomous vehicle industry are substantial, while its methodological innovations offer valuable insights for future AI research.
