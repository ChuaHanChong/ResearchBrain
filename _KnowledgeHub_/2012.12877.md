# Training data-efficient image transformers & distillation through attention

> [!summary] Summary
> A new training methodology and distillation strategy enable Vision Transformers (ViT) to achieve competitive performance on ImageNet-1k, reaching 85.2% top-1 accuracy without requiring massive proprietary datasets. This work introduces a "distillation token" and optimized training to make pure transformer models practical and accessible for image classification.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViT) required pre-training on exceptionally large, private datasets (e.g., JFT-300M) and extensive computational resources, limiting their widespread adoption.
- Initial findings suggested transformers do not generalize well when trained on standard, publicly available datasets like ImageNet-1k, making them impractical for many researchers.
- A lack of specific, effective knowledge distillation methods for transferring beneficial inductive biases, particularly from CNNs, to data-hungry transformer architectures existed.

## Method

- Developed a data-efficient training regimen for Vision Transformers (**DeiT**) by extensively leveraging state-of-the-art ==data augmentation== techniques like ==Rand-Augment==, ==Mixup==, ==Cutmix==, and ==Random Erasing==, along with optimized hyperparameters.
- Introduced a novel ==distillation token== that is appended to the input sequence and interacts through ==self-attention==, enabling the student transformer to learn from a teacher model's predictions.
- Leveraged a ==hard-label distillation== strategy, using the teacher's argmax prediction as a pseudo-label, and empirically found that using a powerful ==Convolutional Neural Network (ConvNet)== as the teacher provided superior performance.

## Results

- DeiT models achieved competitive top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k (e.g., DeiT-B at **83.1%**), trained on a single **8-GPU** node in less than **3 days**, making them computationally accessible.
- Distilled DeiT-B models (DeiT-B V 384) reached **85.2%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k, outperforming the ViT-B model pre-trained on the **300M** image JFT dataset (**84.15%**).
- DeiT models demonstrate a superior accuracy-throughput trade-off compared to many state-of-the-art CNNs, and exhibit strong transfer learning capabilities on various downstream computer vision tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Transformers can achieve high performance with standard datasets like ImageNet-1k when trained with optimized strategies, disproving the initial data-hunger perception.
> - Hard-label distillation with a dedicated "distillation token" is highly effective for transformers, allowing them to learn from strong teacher models.
> - Using a ConvNet as a teacher model is more beneficial for distilling knowledge into a transformer, effectively transferring CNN's inductive biases and improving generalization on smaller datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

#### 1.1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

For nearly a decade, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have been the dominant paradigm for image understanding tasks, propelled by their exceptional performance on large-scale datasets like ImageNet. Their success has been attributed to their inductive biases, such as locality and translation equivariance, which align well with the structure of image data.

However, the field of Natural Language Processing (NLP) witnessed a paradigm shift with the introduction of the Transformer architecture in 2017 ("Attention Is All You Need"). Transformers, relying solely on self-attention mechanisms, rapidly became the state-of-the-art for sequence modeling tasks due to their ability to capture long-range dependencies and inherent parallelism. This success spurred interest in adapting Transformers for computer vision. Initial attempts involved hybrid architectures combining CNNs with attention mechanisms.

The landmark paper "An Image Is Worth 16x16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale" (ViT) by Dosovitskiy et al. (2020) demonstrated for the first time that a pure Transformer architecture, without any convolutions, could achieve competitive results on image classification tasks by treating image patches as tokens. This was a significant breakthrough, challenging the long-held belief that convolutions were indispensable for visual recognition.

Despite ViT's impressive performance, it came with a major caveat: it required pre-training on exceptionally large, private datasets (e.g., JFT-300M with 300 million images) and demanded extensive computational resources. The ViT paper itself concluded that "transformers do not generalize well when trained on insufficient amounts of data," implying that their benefits were confined to those with access to massive datasets and infrastructure. This limitation severely restricted the widespread adoption and research of pure vision transformers.

This paper directly addresses this critical challenge. It positions itself as a response to ViT's data and computational hunger, aiming to make vision transformers practical and accessible for researchers and practitioners who operate with standard datasets like ImageNet-1k (1.2 million images) and more modest hardware (a single GPU node). It seeks to show that the performance of vision transformers is not solely dependent on gargantuan datasets but can be achieved through optimized training strategies and novel architectural modifications like their distillation token. By doing so, this work aims to democratize the use of vision transformers and accelerate their integration into mainstream computer vision applications. It also critically explores the role of knowledge distillation in transferring beneficial inductive biases, particularly from well-established CNNs, to data-hungry transformers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and train pure Transformer models for image classification that are **data-efficient** and **computationally accessible**, thereby overcoming the significant limitations identified in prior Vision Transformer (ViT) work.

The specific motivations and objectives can be broken down as follows:

1. **Reduce Data Dependency:** The core motivation is to demonstrate that Vision Transformers can achieve competitive performance on standard, publicly available datasets like ImageNet-1k (approximately 1.2 million images), without requiring pre-training on hundreds of millions of proprietary images (e.g., JFT-300M). This directly challenges the initial conclusion of the seminal ViT paper regarding transformers' generalization capabilities on "insufficient amounts of data."

2. **Enhance Computational Accessibility:** To enable broader adoption, the authors aim to train these high-performing vision transformers using significantly less computational infrastructure. Specifically, they target training on a single GPU node (4-8 GPUs) within a few days, making the technology feasible for typical academic labs and smaller industry teams.

3. **Introduce a Transformer-Specific Distillation Strategy:** Recognizing that transformers might benefit from the inductive biases inherent in CNNs, the authors propose a novel teacher-student learning approach. Their objective is to design a unique distillation mechanism, featuring a "distillation token," that is specifically tailored to the Transformer architecture and can effectively transfer knowledge from a strong teacher model (especially a CNN) to a student Transformer. This aims to boost the student's performance beyond what is achievable through direct training on ImageNet-1k alone.

4. **Achieve Competitive Performance:** Ultimately, the goal is for their "Data-efficient image Transformers" (DeiT) to be competitive with, or even surpass, state-of-the-art CNNs (like EfficientNets and RegNets) and even large-scale pre-trained ViT models, in terms of accuracy, throughput, and parameter efficiency, all while operating under the aforementioned data and computational constraints.

5. **Demonstrate Generalization via Transfer Learning:** Beyond ImageNet, the authors aim to show that DeiT models learn robust and generalizable representations, performing well when transferred and fine-tuned on various downstream computer vision tasks (e.g., fine-grained classification, CIFAR datasets).

In essence, the research is motivated by a desire to democratize the advanced capabilities of Vision Transformers, making them a practical and accessible tool for the broader machine learning community by tackling their Achilles' heel: extreme data and compute requirements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces "Data-efficient image Transformers" (DeiT) by building upon the standard Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture but significantly modifying its training methodology and introducing a novel distillation technique. The core approach revolves around two main pillars: rigorous data-efficient training strategies and a transformer-specific knowledge distillation method.

#### 3.1. Data-Efficient Training (DeiT)

The foundational architecture of DeiT models (DeiT-Ti, DeiT-S, DeiT-B) is identical to the original ViT models, meaning they are pure attention-based networks without any convolutional layers. The innovation lies entirely in the training procedures applied to these architectures to make them perform well with less data.

- **Optimized Training Regimen:** The authors meticulously tuned hyperparameters and adopted a robust training schedule. They utilized the AdamW optimizer and scaled the learning rate according to batch size. Notably, they found that a much smaller weight decay than typically used for ViT was beneficial for convergence in their setting.
- **Extensive Data Augmentation:** Recognizing that transformers, lacking inherent inductive biases like locality, are more data-hungry than CNNs, the authors heavily rely on state-of-the-art data augmentation techniques. Key augmentations employed include:
 - **Rand-Augment:** A technique that applies a random set of image transformations from a predefined pool, with magnitudes sampled from a fixed range.
 - **Mixup and Cutmix:** These techniques regularize the model by creating synthetic training samples via linear interpolation of images and their labels (Mixup) or by cutting and pasting patches between images (Cutmix).
 - **Random Erasing:** Randomly erases a rectangular patch of an image, forcing the model to learn more robust features from occluded inputs.
 - **Stochastic Depth:** A regularization technique that randomly drops entire layers during training, improving optimization and reducing overfitting in very deep networks.
- **Repeated Augmentation:** This is identified as one of the key ingredients for their data-efficient training. It involves feeding the same image multiple times in an epoch, but each time with a different random data augmentation. This effectively increases the diversity of input samples without requiring a larger dataset, helping to stabilize training and improve performance.
- **Exclusion of Dropout:** Interestingly, their ablation study found dropout to be detrimental in their training setup and was thus excluded.
- **Initialization:** Weights are initialized with a truncated normal distribution, a method found to be more stable for transformer training.
- **Fine-tuning at Higher Resolution:** Similar to prior work (e.g., FixEfficientNet), models are initially trained at a lower resolution (224x224) and then fine-tuned at a higher resolution (e.g., 384x384). Positional embeddings are interpolated using bicubic interpolation (which approximately preserves vector norms) before fine-tuning, adapting them to the new input size.

#### 3.2. Distillation Through Attention (DeiT⚗)

This is the core novel contribution of the paper, addressing how to effectively transfer knowledge from a powerful teacher model to a student transformer.

- **Distillation Token:** The most significant innovation is the introduction of a new, trainable "distillation token." This token is appended to the input sequence (alongside the standard patch tokens and the class token) and interacts with all other tokens through the self-attention layers within the transformer blocks. After processing through the network, the output representation of this distillation token is fed into its own linear classifier.
 - **Objective:** The primary objective for this distillation token's output classifier is to reproduce the predictions (labels) of a pre-trained, strong teacher model. This is distinct from the class token, which aims to predict the ground truth label.
 - **Interaction:** By interacting through self-attention, the distillation token allows the student transformer to learn from the teacher's "soft" or "hard" targets in a more integrated and flexible manner compared to traditional knowledge distillation methods that only apply a loss on the final output logits. The authors observed that the class and distillation tokens converge to distinct embeddings during training but become more similar at the last layer, suggesting they capture complementary information.
- **Hard-Label Distillation Preference:** The authors experimented with both "soft distillation" (minimizing KL divergence between teacher and student softmax outputs) and "hard-label distillation" (using the teacher'sargmax prediction as an additional pseudo-label for the student). They found that hard-label distillation significantly outperformed soft distillation for transformers, being simpler and parameter-free. Their final distillation loss combines the cross-entropy loss with the ground truth label and another cross-entropy loss with the teacher's hard pseudo-label.
- **Convnet as Teacher:** A crucial empirical finding was that using a **Convolutional Neural Network (ConvNet)** as a teacher (specifically, a RegNetY-16GF) yielded superior performance compared to using another transformer as a teacher. This is hypothesized to be due to the effective transfer of CNN's strong inductive biases (e.g., locality, hierarchy) into the transformer student, allowing it to generalize better on ImageNet-1k, which is a relatively smaller dataset compared to JFT-300M.
- **Joint Classification:** At inference time, the final prediction is made by combining the outputs of both the class token's classifier and the distillation token's classifier, typically through late fusion (summing softmax outputs).

In summary, DeiT combines a highly optimized and extensively augmented training procedure with a novel distillation token strategy that leverages the strengths of CNN inductive biases, all within a pure transformer architecture to achieve data-efficient and computationally accessible high performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several significant findings that collectively establish the viability and competitiveness of data-efficient Vision Transformers (DeiT) trained on ImageNet-1k.

#### 4.1. ImageNet Performance and Efficiency

- **Competitive Performance on ImageNet-1k:** The DeiT models, trained solely on ImageNet-1k without any external large-scale datasets, achieve competitive top-1 accuracy. For instance, DeiT-B (86M parameters), which has the same architecture as ViT-B, reaches 83.1% top-1 accuracy (single-crop) after fine-tuning at 384x384 resolution. This is a substantial improvement (+6.3% top-1) over previous ViT models trained only on ImageNet-1k, directly challenging the notion that ViT models require immense datasets.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A key achievement is the drastically reduced training time and resources. DeiT-B can be trained on a single 8-GPU node in less than 3 days (53 hours for pre-training), significantly more accessible than the hundreds of GPUs and weeks of training required for ViT on JFT-300M. The smaller DeiT-S and DeiT-Ti models can be trained in even less time on a 4-GPU setup.
- **Outperforming Large-Scale ViT with Distillation:** When employing the novel distillation strategy (DeiT⚗), the models achieve even higher accuracies. The best performing model, DeiT-B⚗↑384, reaches an impressive 85.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k. Crucially, this *outperforms* the ViT-B model pre-trained on the massive JFT-300M dataset (84.15% at 384 resolution), while being significantly faster and more resource-efficient to train.
- **Accuracy-Throughput Trade-off:** DeiT models, especially with distillation, demonstrate a superior trade-off between accuracy and inference throughput (images processed per second on a V100 GPU) compared to highly optimized CNNs like EfficientNets and RegNets. Figure 1 and Table 5 clearly show that DeiT⚗ models achieve higher accuracy at comparable or better throughputs than many strong CNN baselines, and significantly better than the original ViT models.

#### 4.2. Distillation Effectiveness and Inductive Bias

- **Hard Distillation Superiority:** The paper demonstrates that "hard-label distillation" (where the teacher's argmax prediction acts as a pseudo-label) significantly outperforms traditional "soft distillation" (KL divergence between teacher and student softmax outputs) for transformers. This simplified approach provides better results and is parameter-free.
- **Efficacy of the Distillation Token:** The introduction of the "distillation token" is a crucial innovation. Models trained with this token (DeiT⚗) consistently outperform those trained with vanilla distillation methods. The class and distillation tokens, while initially distinct, become highly similar by the last layer, suggesting they learn complementary representations. The joint classifier combining outputs from both tokens achieves the highest accuracy.
- **Convnet Teachers are Better:** A surprising and insightful finding is that using a **ConvNet** (specifically, a RegNetY) as a teacher model results in better student transformer performance than using another transformer as a teacher, even if the transformer teacher has comparable standalone performance. This strongly suggests that the distillation process effectively transfers the beneficial **inductive biases** (e.g., locality, hierarchical feature extraction) inherent to CNNs into the Transformer architecture, which otherwise lacks these biases. This transfer helps the transformer generalize better on ImageNet-sized datasets.
- **Longer Training Benefits Distillation:** Unlike non-distilled training which plateaus, the performance of distilled networks (DeiT⚗) continues to improve with longer training schedules (e.g., up to 1000 epochs), indicating that distillation enables the model to effectively leverage more training time.

#### 4.3. Transfer Learning and Data Regimes

- **Strong Transfer Learning Performance:** DeiT models, pre-trained on ImageNet-1k, show competitive transfer learning capabilities across various downstream tasks, including fine-grained classification datasets (Flowers-102, Stanford Cars) and general classification datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, iNaturalist). Their performance is on par with, or in some cases superior to, strong CNN baselines like EfficientNet-B7 and other ViT variants, reinforcing the generalizability of the learned representations.
- **Feasibility of Training from Scratch:** While ImageNet pre-training remains beneficial, the paper shows that it is possible to train a reasonable transformer model (DeiT-B) from scratch on smaller datasets like CIFAR-10, achieving 97.5% accuracy (98.5% with distillation). This indicates that the architecture itself, combined with the optimized training, can adapt to diverse data regimes, though larger datasets provide richer diversity for pre-training.

In essence, the main findings highlight that the limitations of Vision Transformers regarding data and compute are not fundamental to the architecture but rather to their initial training methodology. By meticulously optimizing training and introducing an effective distillation strategy, DeiT makes Transformers truly data-efficient and sets a new standard for their accessibility and performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Training data-efficient image transformers & distillation through attention" carries significant implications and potential impacts across several dimensions of deep learning and computer vision.

1. **Democratization of Vision Transformers:** The most profound impact of this work is the **democratization of Vision Transformers**. By demonstrating that high-performing pure transformers can be trained effectively on standard datasets like ImageNet-1k using accessible computational resources (a single GPU node in days), the authors dismantle the previous perception that Transformers are exclusive to research groups with access to massive proprietary datasets and supercomputing clusters. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, enabling wider exploration, development, and application of this powerful architecture.

2. **Challenging the Data-Hunger Narrative of ViTs:** The paper directly refutes the early conclusion that "transformers do not generalize well when trained on insufficient amounts of data." This recalibration of understanding is crucial, showing that the perceived data hunger was largely a consequence of suboptimal training strategies rather than an inherent architectural limitation. This shift in perspective opens up new avenues for research into making Transformers even more data-efficient.

3. **Advancing Knowledge Distillation for Transformers:** The introduction of the **distillation token** is a novel methodological contribution that could have a lasting impact on knowledge distillation research. It provides a transformer-specific mechanism for knowledge transfer, allowing the student to learn from the teacher's internal representations via attention, rather than just its final logits. This more integrated approach to distillation could inspire similar token-based or attention-based distillation strategies in other sequence models.

4. **Harnessing Inductive Biases through Distillation:** The finding that CNNs serve as better teachers for transformers is particularly insightful. It empirically validates the concept of **transferring inductive biases** through knowledge distillation. For tasks where CNNs inherently excel due to their architectural priors (e.g., handling local pixel correlations), this method allows transformers to "inherit" these strengths, making them more robust and efficient. This suggests a powerful paradigm for combining the best of both worlds: the global receptive field and sequence modeling capabilities of transformers with the robust local feature extraction of CNNs.

5. **Boosting Performance and Efficiency in Computer Vision:** DeiT models, especially with distillation, achieve accuracies on ImageNet-1k that are competitive with, and in some cases surpass, highly optimized CNNs (like EfficientNets) and even larger-scale pre-trained ViT models, while maintaining excellent throughput. This makes DeiT a strong candidate for practical applications where both accuracy and computational efficiency (at inference) are crucial. The ability to achieve 85.2% on ImageNet-1k without external data is a new state-of-the-art for this specific setting at the time of publication.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The work explicitly states that their achievements are built upon existing data augmentation and regularization strategies optimized for CNNs. This implicitly highlights a significant area for future research: developing **transformer-specific data augmentation and regularization techniques** or even **learned augmentation policies** tailored to the unique characteristics of attention-based models. Such innovations could unlock even higher performance from vision transformers.

7. **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to **sharing code and models** (via GitHub) is critical for accelerating research. It provides a robust and reproducible baseline for others to build upon, fostering further innovation and widespread adoption of data-efficient vision transformers.

In conclusion, this paper marks a pivotal moment in the evolution of vision transformers, transforming them from a conceptually powerful but practically demanding architecture into a truly viable and competitive solution for a wide range of computer vision tasks. By making transformers accessible and efficient, it significantly broadens their potential impact on both academic research and industrial applications.
