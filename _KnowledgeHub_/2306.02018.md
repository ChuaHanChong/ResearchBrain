# VideoComposer: Compositional Video Synthesis with Motion Controllability

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoComposer extends compositional generation from images to videos, enabling fine-grained control over spatial and temporal elements through diverse conditions like text, sketches, and direct motion vectors. The model generates coherent videos with enhanced temporal consistency and precise motion control across various synthesis tasks.

## Problem

- Existing video generative systems offer limited controllability, lacking fine-grained command over spatial layouts, styles, and specific motions beyond basic text-to-video generation.
- Maintaining cross-frame consistency and coherence in synthesized videos becomes challenging when integrating multiple and diverse input conditions.
- There is a need for explicit and efficient mechanisms for direct motion control in video diffusion models, as current approaches often rely on implicit learning or limited structural guidance.

## Method

- The model develops a latent ==diffusion model== for video synthesis, extending the compositional generation paradigm from images to videos by decomposing videos into controllable conditions.
- It introduces a Spatio-Temporal Condition Encoder (STC-encoder) to process and integrate diverse sequential spatial and temporal conditions like depth maps, sketch sequences, and motion vectors.
- Motion vectors extracted from compressed videos are incorporated as an explicit, direct, and efficient control signal to guide fine-grained temporal dynamics within generated videos.

## Results

- VideoComposer successfully performs a wide array of compositional video generation tasks, including image-to-video animation, video inpainting, and sketch-to-video generation, guided by various combined conditions.
- It demonstrates enhanced motion controllability (reduced End-Point-Error via motion vectors) and improved temporal consistency, evidenced by higher average CLIP cosine similarity between consecutive frames and more coherent, detailed videos than baselines.
- VideoComposer maintains competitive performance in standard text-to-video generation tasks on the MSR-VTT dataset, indicating that the compositional framework enhances overall video synthesis capabilities without degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The compositional generation paradigm, successful in image synthesis, can be effectively adapted to video by thoughtfully decomposing video content into independently controllable spatio-temporal conditions.
> - Explicit motion vectors from compressed video provide a direct and efficient mechanism for precise temporal control over object movements and dynamic patterns.
> - A unified Spatio-Temporal Condition Encoder is crucial for integrating diverse and sequential input conditions, ensuring temporal consistency and structural coherence in the synthesized video outputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual generative models has experienced rapid advancements, particularly with the advent of diffusion-based models, which have achieved remarkable success in synthesizing high-fidelity and diverse images and videos from various inputs, primarily text. Early efforts focused on generating images (e.g., DALL-E 2, GLIDE, Imagen, Latent Diffusion Models) and then extended to video synthesis (e.g., ImagenVideo, Make-A-Video, MagicVideo, Video LDM).

However, a persistent challenge in this landscape has been the limited controllability of these generative systems. While initial methods allowed basic text-to-image or text-to-video generation, the demand for more fine-grained control—enabling users to precisely dictate spatial layouts, styles, or specific motions—has grown significantly. In image synthesis, models like ControlNet and T2I-Adapter introduced spatial conditions (e.g., segmentation maps, sketches), and the "Composer" framework specifically proposed a paradigm of *compositional generation*, allowing for the combination of various input conditions to create images.

Video synthesis presents unique challenges compared to image synthesis. The inherent complexity of videos involves not only spatial content but also dynamic temporal evolution and the critical need for cross-frame consistency. Existing video diffusion models have explored various approaches: some operate in pixel space (computationally intensive, e.g., ImagenVideo), others in latent space (more efficient, e.g., MagicVideo). Some works have introduced limited forms of structural or motion guidance, such as Gen-1 (depth maps) or motion-guided generation with keypoints (MCDiff) or learnable motion latents (LaMD). However, the application of a comprehensive *compositional generation paradigm* – one that flexibly integrates diverse spatial and *temporal* conditions, especially explicit motion controls – to the video domain has been largely unexplored.

VideoComposer positions itself directly in this gap. It aims to extend the successful compositional generation framework from images to videos, specifically addressing the critical need for improved temporal controllability and consistency. By introducing motion vectors as an explicit temporal control signal and developing a unified encoder for spatio-temporal conditions, VideoComposer seeks to elevate the state-of-the-art in controllable video synthesis beyond text-only or limited-condition approaches. It draws inspiration from the efficiency of latent diffusion models and the power of compositional image synthesis, adapting these concepts to the more complex temporal domain of video.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of VideoComposer are multifaceted:

1. **Enhance Controllability in Video Synthesis:** The overarching goal is to move beyond mere text-to-video generation towards a system where users can exert flexible and fine-grained control over both the spatial and temporal aspects of synthesized videos. This includes controlling visual content, structure, style, and, crucially, motion patterns.
2. **Extend Compositional Generation to Videos:** Inspired by the success of compositional generation in image synthesis (e.g., Composer), the authors aim to apply this powerful paradigm to the more challenging domain of videos. This involves decomposing videos into various representative factors that can be independently controlled and then recomposed.
3. **Introduce Explicit Motion Control:** A significant motivation is to overcome the limitations of implicit motion control in existing models. The authors propose integrating *motion vectors* from compressed videos as an explicit, direct, and efficient control signal to guide temporal dynamics. This is motivated by the understanding that motion vectors directly encode inter-frame movements and have been proven useful in video understanding tasks.
4. **Improve Temporal Consistency with Diverse Conditions:** When combining multiple input conditions, especially sequential ones (like depth or sketch sequences), maintaining high inter-frame consistency and coherence is critical for realistic video generation. The authors are motivated to design a mechanism that effectively captures spatio-temporal relations from these diverse sequential inputs to ensure a unified and consistent temporal evolution in the generated video.
5. **Develop a Unified Interface for Multi-Modal Conditions:** To support compositional generation, the model needs to efficiently incorporate and interpret a wide variety of input conditions (text, image, sketch, depth, motion). A unified architecture that can handle these diverse modalities, particularly sequential conditions, is a key design objective.

In essence, VideoComposer is motivated by the desire to build a more user-friendly and powerful video creation tool by offering unprecedented control over video content and motion, addressing the complexities of temporal dynamics and consistency inherent in video data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoComposer is designed as a latent diffusion model (LDM) built upon the Video Latent Diffusion Models (VLDMs) paradigm, which processes videos in a compressed latent space to ensure computational efficiency. The core methodology involves decomposing videos into a rich set of composable conditions and introducing a specialized encoder to integrate these conditions effectively.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Video Latent Diffusion Models (VLDMs)

The model leverages a pre-trained autoencoder to compress high-resolution video frames into a lower-dimensional latent representation, significantly reducing computational overhead. A 3D UNet architecture, augmented with temporal convolutions and cross-attention mechanisms, serves as the denoising function ($\epsilon_{\theta}$) to learn the reverse diffusion process in this latent space, recovering realistic video content from noise. This 3D UNet extends a 2D UNet from pre-trained image LDMs (like Stable Diffusion) by introducing temporal processing blocks.

#### 3.2. Videos as Composable Conditions

VideoComposer decomposes video generation into three distinct types of conditions:

- **Textual Condition:** Uses the pre-trained text encoder from OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 to derive semantic embeddings from text descriptions, guiding the coarse-grained content and motion.
- **Spatial Conditions:**
 - **Single Image:** The first frame of a video provides content and structural guidance for image-to-video generation.
 - **Single Sketch:** A sketch extracted from the first frame (using PiDiNet) guides the structure and texture.
 - **Style:** An image embedding (from OpenCLIP ViT-H/14) provides stylistic guidance.
 - *Note:* Single spatial conditions are repeated along the temporal dimension to align with sequential inputs.
- **Temporal Conditions:** These are the critical new additions for fine-grained temporal control:
 - **Motion Vector (MV):** A key innovation. These are 2D vectors (horizontal and vertical) explicitly encoding pixel-wise movements between adjacent frames, extracted from compressed MPEG-4 videos. They serve as a direct control signal for internal motions.
 - **Depth Sequence:** Extracted using a pre-trained depth estimation model, providing depth information for each frame.
 - **Mask Sequence:** Manually added tube masks for regional editing and inpainting tasks.
 - **Sketch Sequence:** A sequence of sketches providing detailed structural control over time, extending beyond a single initial sketch.

#### 3.3. Spatio-Temporal Condition Encoder (STC-encoder)

To effectively incorporate the complex spatio-temporal dependencies within sequential conditions, the authors design the STC-encoder. This unified interface consists of:
1. A light-weight spatial architecture: Two 2D convolutions and an average pooling layer to extract local spatial information from each frame in the sequence.
2. A temporal Transformer layer: Applied to the spatially processed sequence to model temporal relations and enhance temporal awareness.
This design ensures explicit embedding of temporal cues and facilitates a unified input format for diverse sequential conditions, crucial for maintaining inter-frame consistency.

#### 3.4. Condition Fusion

After processing by the STC-encoder (or CLIP for textual/stylistic conditions), all condition sequences are brought to an identical spatial shape as the latent representations. They are then fused with the noisy latent `z_t` through element-wise addition and concatenation along the channel dimension. Textual and stylistic embeddings are injected via cross-attention mechanisms within the UNet.

#### 3.5. Two-Stage Training Strategy

To manage the complexity of simultaneously learning temporal dynamics and multi-compositional generation, VideoComposer employs a two-stage training strategy:
1. **Stage 1 (Pre-training for Temporal Modeling):** The model is initially pre-trained for text-to-video generation, specializing in learning fundamental temporal dynamics.
2. **Stage 2 (Compositional Training):** VideoComposer is further optimized to handle diverse compositional conditions, learning to synthesize videos that adhere to combined spatial and temporal guidance.

#### 3.6. Inference

During inference, DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) is used for efficient and high-quality sampling. Classifier-Free Guidance is applied to ensure the generated videos strongly adhere to the specified conditions, allowing for extrapolation between conditional and unconditional generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VideoComposer demonstrates significant advancements in controllable video synthesis, validated through extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments across various tasks.

#### 4.1. Versatile Compositional Video Generation

The model successfully performs a wide array of compositional generation tasks:
- **Compositional Image-to-Video Generation:** Animates a static image based on text and the initial frame, with additional control from temporal conditions for evolving structure (Fig. 4).
- **Compositional Video Inpainting:** Fills masked regions in videos according to text descriptions, with enhanced structural control through additional temporal conditions like depth maps and sketches (Fig. 5).
- **Compositional Sketch-to-Video Generation:** Animates static sketches guided by text, further refined by mask sequences for temporal patterns or style guidance from an image (Fig. 6).
- **Compositional Sketch/Depth Sequence-to-Video Generation:** (Appendix B) Generates videos strictly adhering to sequences of sketches or depth maps, with additional text and style control.
- **Motion Transfer:** (Appendix B) Effectively transfers motion patterns from a source video or even hand-crafted motions to a generated video based on a single image.

#### 4.2. Enhanced Motion Controllability with Motion Vectors

- **Quantitative Evidence:** Table 1 shows that incorporating motion vectors (MV) as a condition significantly reduces the motion control error (End-Point-Error, EPE), indicating improved control over object movements. The STC-encoder further reduces this error, highlighting its effectiveness in processing temporal signals.
- **Qualitative Evidence (Static-Background Removal):** Figure 7 demonstrates that motion vectors prioritize moving cues, effectively excluding static backgrounds during video-to-video translation. This capability is superior to depth maps or sketches, which capture static elements.
- **Qualitative Evidence (Versatile Hand-Crafted Motion Control):** Figure 8 illustrates that motion vectors, easily derived from simple hand-crafted strokes (e.g., an arrow for trajectory), enable flexible and precise motion control, a feat challenging for text-only methods like CogVideo.

#### 4.3. Improved Temporal Consistency via STC-encoder

- **Quantitative Evidence:** Table 2 demonstrates that the STC-encoder consistently augments frame consistency (measured by average CLIP cosine similarity between consecutive frames) across different temporal conditions (sketch, depth, motion vectors). This confirms its role in capturing temporal dependencies.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Figure 9 shows that models without the STC-encoder often produce structurally inconsistent videos, miss fine details, or exhibit noticeable defects in human faces and poses. VideoComposer with STC-encoder, in contrast, generates more coherent, detailed, and structurally consistent outputs.

#### 4.4. Competitive Text-to-Video Generation

While its primary focus is compositional generation, VideoComposer also demonstrates competitive performance in traditional text-to-video generation tasks. Table A3 (Appendix) shows that VideoComposer achieves strong Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and CLIP Similarity (CLIPSIM) scores on the MSR-VTT dataset, even outperforming its own first-stage text-to-video pre-training, indicating that the compositional framework does not detract from its basic text-to-video capabilities.

In summary, the main findings highlight VideoComposer's ability to simultaneously control spatial and temporal patterns in synthesized videos through a diverse set of conditions, with motion vectors and the STC-encoder being key innovations for precise motion control and enhanced temporal consistency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoComposer represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI for visual content, offering several layers of significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Pioneering Compositional Video Synthesis:** This work successfully extends the powerful "compositional generation" paradigm, previously demonstrated for images, to the more complex domain of video synthesis. This opens new avenues for integrating diverse control signals into temporal sequences.
- **Explicit Motion Control with Motion Vectors:** The introduction and effective utilization of motion vectors as an *explicit* and *efficient* control signal for temporal dynamics is a novel contribution. Unlike implicit learning or more complex keypoint tracking, motion vectors provide a direct and intuitive way to guide object movement and camera motion, addressing a critical need in controllable video generation.
- **Unified Spatio-Temporal Condition Encoding:** The design of the STC-encoder is crucial for handling the heterogeneity and sequential nature of various spatial and temporal conditions. It provides a robust and unified interface for embedding complex spatio-temporal relationships, which is vital for maintaining inter-frame consistency and structural coherence in the synthesized videos.

#### 5.2. Practical and Commercial Impact

- **Revolutionizing Content Creation:** VideoComposer has the potential to transform the content creation industry. By offering unprecedented flexibility and control, it can drastically reduce the labor and cost associated with traditional video production. Designers, artists, and creators can manipulate subjects, styles, scenes, and motions using intuitive inputs like text, sketches, reference images/videos, or even simple hand-crafted strokes. This empowers faster prototyping, personalized content generation, and more accessible video editing for a broader audience.
- **Enhanced Customization and Personalization:** The ability to combine diverse conditions allows for highly customized video outputs, catering to specific user needs and preferences. This could be invaluable for advertising, social media content, animated storytelling, and personalized media experiences.
- **Applications in Education and Training:** VideoComposer could facilitate the creation of unique and customized video scenarios for educational purposes, helping to visualize complex concepts, simulate real-world situations, or create interactive learning materials.
- **Accessibility in Video Editing:** Simplifying complex video editing tasks, such as inpainting or style transfer, through intuitive prompts and controls could make advanced video manipulation accessible to users without extensive technical expertise.

#### 5.3. Dual-Use Technology and Ethical Considerations

The authors responsibly acknowledge that VideoComposer, like other powerful generative AI models (e.g., Imagen Video, Make-A-Video), is a dual-use technology with inherent risks:
- **Propagation of Biases:** As the model is pre-trained on large-scale datasets (WebVid10M, LAION-400M) and built upon foundational models (Stable Diffusion), it inherits the implicit biases present in these data. This could lead to the propagation of social biases (e.g., gender, racial stereotypes) in generated content.
- **Creation of Offensive or Misleading Content:** The ability to generate highly realistic and customizable videos poses a risk for creating offensive, harmful, or misleading content, including deepfakes.
The authors emphasize that VideoComposer is primarily a research project. For any deployment beyond research, they recommend rigorous evaluation, oversight of deployment context, and implementing necessary filtering mechanisms for prompts and generated content to ensure responsible and ethical use.

In conclusion, VideoComposer marks a significant step towards truly controllable and flexible video synthesis. Its innovative approach to combining compositional conditions with explicit motion control paves the way for a new era of highly customizable and efficient video content generation, while also highlighting the importance of addressing the ethical implications of such powerful AI technologies.
