# A Survey on Physics Informed Reinforcement Learning: Review and Open Problems

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey proposes a novel taxonomy to categorize Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning (PIRL) methods, detailing how physics knowledge is integrated into RL to enhance physical plausibility and data efficiency. The review highlights dominant integration strategies and prevalent application domains, noting an exponential growth in the field.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) struggles with sample inefficiency and the "sim-to-real" gap when applied to complex real-world systems.
- High-dimensional continuous state and action spaces pose challenges for traditional RL algorithms, often leading to the "curse of dimensionality."
- Ensuring safe exploration and designing effective reward functions remain difficult in safety-critical RL applications.

## Method

- The authors developed a novel, multi-faceted ==taxonomy== to classify existing ==Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning (PIRL)== approaches.
- The taxonomy categorizes works by the ==form of physics information==, its specific contribution to RL architectures, and its connection to the RL pipeline stages.
- The paper provides an algorithmic review, analyzing how ==physics priors== are incorporated into RL components like ==state design==, ==reward functions==, ==action regulation==, and ==policy/value network augmentation==.

## Results

- The proposed taxonomy successfully provided a unified framework for understanding and classifying diverse PIRL research, linking physics types to RL integration methods and pipeline stages.
- Physics simulators/models and barrier certificates/physical constraints are the most frequently utilized physics priors, with controller/policy design dominating application areas (approximately **85%**).
- PIRL approaches demonstrate improvements in mitigating challenges such as sample inefficiency, safety exploration, and the curse of dimensionality by leveraging physics-informed representations and constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics information is primarily integrated into RL through reward design, state design, action regulation, and the augmentation of simulators/models or policy/value networks.
> - PIRL methods effectively employ various biases, including learning bias for soft constraints (e.g., safety filters) and inductive bias for hard constraints within network architectures (e.g., physics-embedded networks).
> - The field of PIRL is experiencing exponential growth, driven by its ability to address key RL limitations like sample efficiency, safety, and the curse of dimensionality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in solving complex decision-making and optimization problems across various domains, including autonomous driving, locomotion control, and robotics. Despite these successes, primarily achieved in simulated environments, RL faces challenges when applied to real-world systems. These challenges include sample inefficiency, difficulty with high-dimensional continuous state and action spaces, the necessity for safe exploration, the design of well-defined reward functions, discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics (the "sim-to-real" gap), and effective policy transfer.

Concurrently, the field of Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) has emerged as an approach to integrate mathematical physics models and principles into machine learning frameworks. PIML aims to enhance learning by incorporating physical constraints, adhering to physical laws, and guiding models towards physically consistent solutions, even with incomplete data. This approach has shown benefits in improving data efficiency, accelerating training, enhancing generalization, and increasing the interpretability and physical soundness of models.

Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning (PIRL) represents a specific intersection of these two fields. Given that many RL applications address real-world problems with inherent physical structures, RL is a suitable candidate for the integration of physics information. Recent research indicates that incorporating physics priors into RL training pipelines can mitigate common RL challenges, such as reducing high-dimensional state spaces, improving world models for safer and more efficient training, and designing reward functions that incorporate physical constraints for safer exploration and more effective policy learning. The authors note a growing trend in PIRL publications, signifying increasing research interest in this area.

This survey paper distinguishes itself from other reviews by offering a focused and comprehensive examination specifically of RL approaches that leverage the structure, properties, or constraints derived from the underlying physics of a process or system. While other surveys have discussed PIML generally or the use of prior knowledge in robotics-specific RL, this work covers a broader range of application domains, including motion control, molecular structure optimization, safe exploration, and robot manipulation, all through the lens of physics-informed integration. It aims to provide a cohesive perspective on PIRL implementation, identifying application areas, gaps, opportunities, and unresolved issues to guide future research in this developing field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the inherent limitations of purely data-driven Reinforcement Learning (RL) when transitioning from controlled simulations to complex, unpredictable real-world environments. While RL has achieved notable successes, it frequently encounters significant hurdles such as:
- **Sample Inefficiency:** Traditional RL algorithms often require vast amounts of interaction data, making real-world deployment costly or impractical.
- **High-Dimensional Spaces:** Effectively handling continuous and high-dimensional state and action spaces remains a challenge, leading to the "curse of dimensionality."
- **Safe Exploration:** In safety-critical applications, unrestricted trial-and-error exploration can lead to undesirable or dangerous outcomes.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Policies trained in simulators often fail to transfer effectively to physical systems due due to disparities between simulated and real dynamics.
- **Reward Function Design:** Manually designing effective and non-ambiguous reward functions that accurately guide complex behaviors is often difficult and prone to sub-optimality.

The inclusion of physical information, or physics priors, into machine learning models, known as physics-informed machine learning (PIML), has demonstrated its capacity to address some of these issues by ensuring physical consistency, enhancing data efficiency, accelerating training, and improving model generalization and interpretability. Given that many real-world RL problems possess an explainable physical structure, integrating these physics priors into RL (PIRL) is posited as a natural and promising pathway to overcome the aforementioned challenges. This integration aims to produce RL algorithms that are more physically plausible, precise, data-efficient, and directly applicable in real-world scenarios.

Against this motivation, the key objectives of this survey paper are to:
1. **Develop a Unified Taxonomy:** Propose a novel framework to classify existing PIRL works based on the representation and form of governing physics modeled, their specific contribution to typical RL architectures, and their connection to various stages of the underlying RL pipeline. This aims to provide a structured way to understand how physics knowledge is incorporated.
2. **Conduct an Algorithmic Review:** Present an overview of state-of-the-art physics-informed RL methods, utilizing consistent notations and functional diagrams to facilitate understanding and comparison of the literature.
3. **Analyze Training and Evaluation Benchmarks:** Review the benchmarks and platforms used in the surveyed literature to identify popular trends and provide a reference for future research.
4. **Provide Detailed Analysis:** Delve into how physics information is integrated into specific RL approaches, identifying the physical processes modeled, the network architectures or augmentations utilized, and the impact across various model-based and model-free RL applications.
5. **Identify Open Problems:** Summarize current challenges, unresolved research questions, and outline potential future research directions to guide the development of the nascent PIRL field.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to offer a comprehensive and cohesive perspective on PIRL, highlighting its current state, identifying gaps in research, and stimulating further innovation in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors conducted a comprehensive literature review of Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning (PIRL) approaches. Their methodology is structured around introducing a novel taxonomy and using it as a backbone to analyze existing works.

### 4. Taxonomy Development

The core of the methodology is a multi-faceted taxonomy designed to classify and understand PIRL research. This taxonomy organizes works based on three main components:
- **Physics Information (Types):** This categorizes the form in which physics knowledge is represented:
 - **Differential and Algebraic Equations (DAE):** Includes PDEs/ODEs and boundary conditions, often integrated via PINNs.
 - **Barrier Certificate and Physical Constraints (BPC):** Uses control-theoretic concepts like Control Lyapunov Functions (CLF) and Control Barrier Functions (CBF) to ensure safety, sometimes learned as Neural Barrier Certificates (NBC).
 - **Physics Parameters, Primitives, and Physical Variables (PPV):** Direct use of physics values, movement primitives, or physical state/target information by RL agents.
 - **Offline Data and Representation (ODR):** Employs non-task-specific data, such as real-robot data for sim-to-real transfer, or learned low-dimensional physically relevant representations.
 - **Physics Simulator and Model (PS):** Utilizes simulators as test-beds or alongside RL algorithms to impart physical correctness, including fully or partially known system models.
 - **Physical Properties (PPR):** Fundamental knowledge about system structure or properties, such as morphology or symmetry.
- **PIRL Methods (Physics Prior Augmentations to RL):** This classifies how physics information is incorporated into specific components of the RL paradigm:
 - **State Design:** Modifying or expanding the observed state representation to be more informative (e.g., state fusion, extracted features, state constraints).
 - **Action Regulation:** Imposing constraints on action values, often for safety protocols (e.g., using safety filters or barrier functions).
 - **Reward Design:** Inducing physics information through effective reward function design or augmentation with physics-based bonuses/penalties.
 - **Augment Policy or Value Network (N/W):** Incorporating physics principles by adjusting update rules, losses, or making direct structural changes to policy/value networks (e.g., physics-based losses, constraints, specialized network architectures).
 - **Augment Simulator or Model:** Developing improved simulators or learned models by incorporating underlying physics knowledge for more accurate system modeling and sim-to-real transfer.
- **RL Pipeline Stages:** The authors map the PIRL methods to the conventional RL pipeline, which consists of five stages: Problem Representation, Learning Strategy, Network Design, Training, and Trained Policy Deployment.

### 5. Additional Categorization

Beyond the primary taxonomy, two supplementary categories are introduced to provide further perspectives on PIRL implementation:
- **Bias:** Relates PIRL approaches to existing PIML bias categories:
 - **Observational Bias:** Uses multi-modal data reflecting physical principles.
 - **Learning Bias:** Reinforces knowledge through soft penalty constraints in the loss function.
 - **Inductive Bias:** Incorporates prior knowledge via custom neural network-induced "hard" constraints.
- **Learning Architecture:** Identifies common architectural modifications introduced by PIRL algorithms:
 - Safety filter, PI reward, Residual learning, Physics embedded network, Differentiable simulator, Sim-to-Real, Physics variable, Hierarchical RL, Data augmentation, PI model identification.

### 6. Algorithmic Review and Analysis

The paper reviews state-of-the-art PIRL literature, structuring the discussion primarily according to the "PIRL methods" component of their taxonomy. For each method, specific examples from the literature are provided, detailing how physics information is integrated and the impact on performance. The analysis also includes:
- **Statistical Analysis:** Visual representations (bar charts) are used to illustrate trends in:
 - The most frequently used RL algorithms.
 - The types of physics priors employed across different PIRL methods.
 - The relationship between PIRL learning architectures and the associated biases.
 - The distribution of PIRL applications across various domains.
- **Challenge-Addressing Analysis:** A detailed discussion on how PIRL approaches address specific open problems in RL, such as sample efficiency, the curse of dimensionality, safety exploration, partial observability, and under-defined reward functions.

### 7. Benchmark Review

The authors compile a summary of the simulation and evaluation benchmarks utilized in the reviewed literature, highlighting popular platforms like OpenAI Gym, MuJoCo, and CARLA, as well as domain-specific and customized environments.

### 8. Identification of Open Problems

Based on the comprehensive review and analysis, the paper concludes by outlining open challenges and suggesting future research directions for the PIRL field.

This systematic approach, combining a novel taxonomy with detailed analysis and statistical insights, allows for a structured and comprehensive understanding of the current landscape of Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The analysis of the surveyed literature yielded several findings regarding the landscape and characteristics of Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning (PIRL):

### 10. Taxonomy and Categorization Efficacy

The proposed taxonomy successfully provided a unified framework for categorizing existing PIRL works. It effectively connected different types of physics information, the methods used to incorporate them into RL, and their impact on various stages of the RL pipeline. The additional categorizations by "bias" (observational, learning, inductive) and "learning architecture" further elucidated the implementation strategies.

### 11. Dominant Physics Information Types and PIRL Methods

- **Key Physics Priors:** Physics simulators and models, along with barrier certificates and physical constraints, are the most frequently utilized forms of physics information in PIRL. Other common types include physics parameters, primitives, physical variables, and learned low-dimensional representations from offline data.
- **Integration Points:** Physics information is predominantly incorporated into RL through:
 - **Reward Design:** Augmenting or creating reward functions based on physical objectives, constraints, or costs.
 - **State Design:** Enriching or modifying the agent's observation space with physics-derived features or representations.
 - **Action Regulation:** Implementing safety filters or control-theoretic constraints (e.g., CBFs) to modify or bound agent actions.
 - **Augment Simulator or Model:** Enhancing the accuracy or physical consistency of environment models, particularly in model-based RL or sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Augment Policy or Value Networks:** Embedding physics directly into the structure or loss functions of the neural networks comprising the agent's policy or value function.

### 12. Relationship between Learning Architectures and Biases

- **Learning Bias:** Architectures such as "PI reward" and "safety filter" primarily incorporate physics via "learning bias." This involves adding soft penalty constraints or physics-based terms to the loss function, guiding the learning process without imposing strict structural changes.
- **Inductive Bias:** The "physics embedded network" architecture frequently leverages "inductive bias." This entails designing specialized network structures (e.g., Hamiltonian Neural Networks, Lagrangian Neural Networks) that inherently respect physical laws or symmetries, acting as "hard" constraints.
- **Observational Bias:** Approaches related to "Sim-to-Real" and "Data Augmentation" often fall under "observational bias," where multi-modal or physics-generated data is used to train the model, reflecting underlying physical principles.

### 13. Application Domains

A substantial majority (approximately 85%) of PIRL applications reviewed focus on controller or policy design. Specifically:
- **Miscellaneous Control:** This category, encompassing optimal policy learning for diverse sectors like energy management and data center cooling, constitutes the largest segment.
- **Safe Control and Exploration:** About 25% of works address safety-critical systems, aiming to ensure safe exploration and policy learning.
- **Dynamic Control:** Control of dynamic systems, including robotic systems, represents approximately 23% of the applications.
Other applications include optimization and prediction (e.g., molecular structure), motion capture/simulation, and general policy optimization improvement.

### 14. Addressing Core RL Challenges

The survey highlights how PIRL approaches contribute to mitigating recognized challenges in RL:
- **Sample Efficiency:** Physics-augmented simulators and physics-informed model learning reduce the need for extensive real-world interactions and data, thereby improving sample efficiency.
- **Curse of Dimensionality:** PIRL methods extract physically relevant low-dimensional representations from high-dimensional state or observation spaces, simplifying the learning task.
- **Safety Exploration:** Control-theoretic constructs like Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) and Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs), often learned or informed by physics, are used to define and enforce safe exploration boundaries and ensure system stability.
- **Partial Observability:** Physics-informed state design and state fusion approaches help in creating more informative state representations, even when direct observations are incomplete.
- **Under-defined Reward Functions:** Physics-informed reward design provides structured and physically meaningful objectives or penalties, guiding the agent more effectively than purely heuristic reward formulations.

### 15. Growth of the Field

The paper presents statistical evidence demonstrating an exponential growth in the number of PIRL papers published over the last six years, indicating increasing academic and research interest in this interdisciplinary field.

### 16. Benchmark Diversity

While standard RL benchmarks like OpenAI Gym and MuJoCo are utilized, a notable finding is the frequent reliance on customized or adapted environments for training and evaluation. This diversity, while specific to problem domains, can complicate direct comparisons between different PIRL algorithms.

### 17. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey on Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning (PIRL) holds significant implications for both the academic research community and the practical application of AI in engineering and scientific domains.

**1. Structured Understanding of a Nascent Field:** The primary significance lies in providing a detailed and cohesive framework for a rapidly evolving and interdisciplinary field. By introducing a novel taxonomy that categorizes PIRL works based on the form of physics information, the methods of its incorporation, and its alignment with the RL pipeline, the paper brings order and clarity to a previously fragmented area of research. This structured understanding facilitates easier navigation, comprehension, and comparison of diverse approaches, which is crucial for accelerating progress.

**2. Guidance for Future Research:** The identification of existing gaps, opportunities, and unresolved issues serves as a roadmap for future investigations. Specifically, the paper highlights critical challenges such as handling high-dimensional spaces with physics-informed representations, ensuring safety in complex and uncertain environments, the nuanced selection of appropriate physics priors, and the need for standardized evaluation and benchmarking platforms. This directive guidance can help researchers focus their efforts on high-impact areas, preventing redundant work and promoting novel solutions.

**3. Enhancement of RL Performance and Applicability:** The core value proposition of PIRL is its potential to address the inherent limitations of purely data-driven RL. By systematically reviewing how physics integration enhances properties like physical plausibility, precision, and data efficiency, the survey underscores PIRL's capacity to make RL algorithms more robust, reliable, and applicable in real-world, safety-critical scenarios. This directly impacts fields such as autonomous systems, robotics, energy management, and material science, where physical consistency is paramount.

**4. Bridging Disciplinary Gaps:** The work promotes a deeper integration between physics/engineering principles and advanced machine learning techniques. It showcases how domain-specific scientific knowledge can be effectively encoded and leveraged within AI decision-making systems, moving beyond black-box models towards more interpretable and scientifically consistent solutions. This interdisciplinary approach can foster collaboration and innovation across traditionally separate fields.

**5. Informing System Design and Deployment:** For practitioners, the detailed algorithmic review, coupled with the analysis of learning architectures and biases, offers insights into designing and deploying PIRL systems. Understanding how physics is integrated at different stages of the RL pipeline—from problem representation and reward design to model augmentation and policy learning—can inform decisions on system architecture, data utilization, and safety mechanisms for real-world applications.

In summary, this survey not only consolidates the current state of PIRL but also strategically outlines its trajectory. It serves as an essential reference for both newcomers and seasoned researchers, stimulating innovation and paving the way for the broader adoption of physically consistent and robust AI solutions across scientific and engineering disciplines.
