# RoboPARA: Dual-Arm Robot Planning with Parallel Allocation and Recomposition Across Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboPARA is an LLM-driven two-stage planner for dual-arm robots. It retrieves task knowledge via RAG, has an LLM emit a dependency DAG over meta-operations, validates and corrects that DAG, then re-traverses it with a deadlock-aware scheduler that assigns start times and arms to minimize makespan. It cuts execution time 30-50% while raising success rates, and ships the X-DAPT parallelism benchmark.

## Problem

- LLM planners for dual-arm robots optimize success rate and completion time only, so their plans collapse into effectively single-arm sequential execution.
- No prior dataset evaluates dual-arm task parallelism across scenes and difficulty levels, leaving parallelism-aware planning unmeasured and underexplored.
- Existing methods treat each task package as an isolated unit, missing inter-package redundancy and cross-task tool reuse when multiple instructions arrive concurrently.

## Method

- ==Dual-Arm Cooperative Scheduling Problem==: a formal ==makespan-minimizing objective== over a global ==DAG== with arm-assignment and scheduling functions under dependency, exclusivity, and locking constraints.
- Stage 1, ==dependency-graph candidate generation==: ==RAG== over short-term environment state plus long-term execution history, ==structured prompting== to a DAG, then a validation routine driving up to two LLM correction rounds.
- Stage 2, ==graph re-traversal scheduler==: a ==ready-queue with arm-lock compatibility== for pick-use-place chains and a ==rollback rule== that undoes the later conflicting pick subtree to break deadlocks.

## Results

- On X-DAPT with GPT-4o, RoboPARA reaches **1.407** task efficiency in the kitchen versus **0.912** for the best baseline, and **0.869** on hard packages where baselines peak at **0.481**.
- Parallel steps exceed all baselines by over **2.8x** on easy packages and over **10x** on medium and hard ones; baseline planning failure rates run **2.7x** higher on hard packages.
- Ablations: removing environment info raises failure **28%** and cuts parallelism **25%**; dropping instructional constraints adds **51%** failure; removing the deadlock check costs **22%** efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Making parallelism an explicit scheduling objective, not a hoped-for byproduct of LLM prompting, is what unlocks genuine dual-arm collaboration.
> - Splitting symbolic structure generation from constraint-respecting scheduling lets a classical algorithm supply the guarantees an LLM cannot, notably deadlock freedom.
> - Benefits come at a cost: iterative DAG correction consumes 1.3x more tokens, and the skill library stays tied to predefined scenarios and templates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dual-arm robot manipulation is a key area within embodied artificial intelligence (AI), providing capabilities for complex and collaborative tasks. Effective task planning is essential for translating high-level objectives into executable actions for these systems. Recent advancements, particularly the integration of large language models (LLMs), have contributed to improving coordination and adaptability in dual-arm task planning.

Existing research has explored LLMs for robotic task planning, including methods for iterative plan refinement and integrating task decomposition with memory management. However, a recurrent limitation in many of these approaches is a tendency toward single-arm sequential execution. These methods primarily prioritize task success rate and completion time, often without fully optimizing for parallelism between the two robot arms. Consequently, the collaborative potential inherent in dual-arm systems remains largely unexploited.

This paper addresses a specific gap in the current research landscape by focusing on parallelism-aware dual-arm planning. It recognizes that in many real-world scenarios, multiple tasks are issued concurrently, requiring efficient parallel planning. The work draws inspiration from human multitasking behaviors, where various actions are interleaved to optimize efficiency. Within the System-1 + System-2 architectural framework for embodied AI, where System-2 handles high-level symbolic reasoning and scheduling, this research specifically enhances System-2 planning to improve efficiency, parallelism, and adaptability for long-horizon, parallel dual-arm collaboration, an area previously underexplored. The work builds upon the compositional skill learning paradigm, which emphasizes developing reusable meta-skills, rather than end-to-end methods that often require extensive retraining for new tasks or environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an LLM-driven framework that enables dual-arm robots to plan and execute tasks with optimized parallelism, thereby maximizing the utilization of both arms and improving overall efficiency in complex multitasking scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and challenges:

- **Underutilization of Dual-Arm Potential:** While dual-arm robots offer advantages in efficiency and flexibility for complex tasks, existing task planning methods often fail to fully optimize for task parallelism. This leads to sequential execution by individual arms, limiting the inherent collaborative potential of dual-arm systems.
- **Real-world Multitasking Demands:** In practical applications, robots are frequently required to handle multiple tasks concurrently. The challenge lies in effectively planning under these multitasking demands to achieve optimal execution.
- **Inspiration from Human Efficiency:** Human behavior often involves interleaving tasks (e.g., boiling water while brushing teeth) to enhance efficiency. This ability to reason about temporal overlap and parallel execution is a key inspiration for developing more efficient robotic planning strategies.
- **Dynamic Arm Synchronization and Decoupling:** In a robotic environment, some tasks inherently require both arms to work together (e.g., cutting an object), while others can be performed by a single arm, leaving the other arm free for a different task. A crucial challenge is to decide when to synchronize both arms for collaborative actions and when to decouple them for independent parallel operations to optimize overall execution time.
- **Limitations of Current LLM-based Approaches:** While LLMs have advanced task planning, many current methods are designed primarily to optimize task success rates and completion times. They often neglect the critical dimension of parallelism, leading to plans that are effectively single-arm sequential executions.

To address these issues, the paper proposes the "Dual-Arm Cooperative Scheduling Problem." This problem seeks to find a valid schedule (σ) and arm assignment (μ) for a collection of task packages, consolidated into a global Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) G=(V, E), that minimizes the makespan (Cmax, the total completion time of all operations). This minimization must adhere to all task dependencies, arm availability constraints, and consistency requirements throughout execution. RoboPARA is designed as a novel framework to specifically tackle this problem by ensuring both task correctness and optimal arm utilization through parallelism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboPARA framework addresses the Dual-Arm Cooperative Scheduling Problem through a two-stage architecture: (1) Dependency Graph-based Planning Candidates Generation and (2) Graph Re-Traversal-based Dual-Arm Parallel Planning.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Dependency Graph-based Planning Candidates Generation

This initial stage focuses on constructing a task-level Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), G = (V, E), which serves as the structural foundation for dual-arm scheduling.

- **Memory Retrieval and Prompt Formulation:** Given a user instruction, RoboPARA utilizes Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) to fetch relevant task knowledge from a hybrid memory system. This system integrates short-term observations (e.g., current object states) and long-term execution history (e.g., a package knowledge base). The retrieved task steps are then augmented with contextual information, including environmental constraints, dependency rules, parallelism guidelines, and structured formatting examples, to formulate a comprehensive prompt for the LLM.
- **DAG Generation:** The LLM processes this prompt to produce an initial instance of the DAG. Each node v ∈ V represents a meta-operation, characterized by its action label (name), semantic type (type), the required number of arms (δv ∈ {1, 2}), and estimated execution duration (tv). Directed edges (v_i → v_j) ∈ E denote precedence constraints, meaning v_j cannot start before v_i completes. For example, a 'use' action must follow a 'pick' of the same tool, and a 'place' must not precede its associated 'use'. Tool-related action sequences maintain intra-object dependency consistency. A 'complete' node serves as the unique sink, aggregating all terminal dependencies.
- **DAG Validation and Correction:** The initial DAG generated by the LLM may contain errors in dependency relationships. RoboPARA employs a structural validation routine (Algorithm 2 in Appendix) to detect and localize these errors. Three primary categories of invalid dependencies are identified: (1) an operation (e.g., 'use' or 'place') incorrectly depending on another object's 'place' action; (2) a 'place' node in a 'pick-use-place' sequence directly depending on its 'pick' node instead of the preceding 'use' node; and (3) any node depending on the 'use' node of an unrelated object. Once errors are identified, relevant nodes and dependency rules are integrated into a new prompt. This prompt is then re-submitted to the LLM for iterative correction until a valid DAG is generated.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Graph Re-Traversal-based Dual-Arm Parallel Planning

Upon receiving a validated task graph G, this stage constructs a feasible and efficient dual-arm execution plan (σ, μ), where each node v ∈ V is assigned a start time σ(v) and a responsible arm set μ(v) ⊆ A, with the objective of minimizing the makespan Cmax while respecting all structural and physical constraints.

- **Scheduling Framework:** The system maintains a dynamic scheduling queue (Q) containing all nodes whose predecessors have been scheduled ('Ready' nodes). Nodes in Q are assigned based on arm availability, task type (single-arm or dual-arm), and object-holding consistency. Single-arm tasks are assigned to an idle arm, with the left arm favored in case of a tie. Dual-arm tasks require both arms to be free and not subject to object-locking constraints.
- **Formal Constraints:** The planning process adheres to four categories of constraints:
 1. **Task Dependency:** Each task must be scheduled after all its predecessors are completed.
 2. **Unlocked Arm Availability:** Each arm can perform at most one operation at a time. Operations are scheduled only if the assigned arm(s) are free for the duration.
 3. **Arm Lock Compatibility:** A 'pick-use-place' sequence for a specific object must be executed by the same arm(s) throughout its duration.
 4. **Deadlock Prevention:** If a dual-arm task is ready but cannot execute because each arm is holding a different object, a potential deadlock is detected. To resolve this, the 'pick' action that occurred later among the conflicting chains is rolled back, along with its dependent subtree. These rolled-back operations are re-inserted into the scheduling queue, freeing the arm and allowing the earlier 'pick' chain to proceed. The dual-arm task is then rescheduled once both arms are synchronized and one arm holds the object chain.
- **Execution Procedure:** Scheduling is an iterative process. Feasible task pairs are selected from Q and assigned to arms based on priority and constraints. Dual-arm tasks require both arms to synchronize their start times. After each 'pick' action, the arm's object lock status is updated, binding related tasks to that arm. If no feasible task can be scheduled due to conflicts or readiness issues, the deadlock resolution mechanism is triggered.

#### 3.3. Cross-Scenario Dual-Arm Parallel Task Dataset (X-DAPT)

To benchmark the new task and method, the X-DAPT dataset was introduced. This dataset is designed to evaluate dual-arm task planning parallelism across 10 diverse scenes (e.g., kitchen, factory, hospital) and three difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard). It comprises over 1,000 task packages, emphasizing efficient parallel operations and dual-arm coordination in multitasking scenarios. Task complexity increases with difficulty, involving more steps, diverse skills, and complex dependencies.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

Experiments utilized the m3e-base model for memory retrieval and OpenAI GPT-4o and DeepSeek V3 APIs as foundation LLMs. For the System-1 phase (low-level action execution), ACT, DP, and π0 algorithms were employed. Evaluations were conducted across four scenes with hardware verification using Franka Research 3 and UR5e robotic arms, as well as a humanoid robot.

#### 3.5. Baselines and Metrics

Seven LLM-based agents (LLM3, ChatGPT-Prompts, VOYAGER, Embodied TaPA, LLM-Planner, FLTRNN, RoCo) were used as baselines, with their prompts adapted to fit the experimental scenarios while retaining core methodologies. Performance was assessed using four quantitative metrics:
- **TEI (Task Efficiency Index):** Measures successful steps executed per unit time, reflecting overall efficiency.
- **TFR (Task Failure Rate):** Quantifies the proportion of failed steps, indicating system robustness.
- **PPR (Package Parallelism Rate):** Reflects efficiency gained by merging redundant steps across multiple task packages.
- **APR (Arm Parallelism Rate):** Captures the degree of parallel utilization of both robotic arms, indicating concurrency and scheduling efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate RoboPARA's performance against existing planning methods across various scenes, task difficulties, and LLM backbones.

- **Enhanced Efficiency:** RoboPARA consistently achieved a higher Task Efficiency Index (TEI) compared to baseline methods. In tests across kitchen, office, agricultural greenhouse, and factory scenes, RoboPARA showed at least a 1.3x to 1.5x increase in the number of successful steps executed per unit time. This resulted in an observed reduction of 30% to 50% in overall execution time.
- **Increased Robustness and Reliability:** RoboPARA exhibited a lower Task Failure Rate (TFR), especially in complex scenarios. In "hard" task packages, baseline methods showed failure rates over 2.7 times higher than RoboPARA. The DAG output verification algorithm (Stage 1) was identified as a critical component for detecting node dependency and resource usage conflicts, which contributed to the robustness of task planning. VOYAGER, as a baseline, failed to generate concrete execution steps, and RoCo struggled to solve tasks even after numerous prompting iterations.
- **Effective Integrated Execution and Resource Reuse:** RoboPARA demonstrated a notable ability to reason over both intra-package logic and inter-package dependencies. This allowed for the successful merging of redundant operations across tasks (e.g., reusing a knife for multiple cutting operations), leading to a 7% reduction in the total number of executed steps while ensuring task completion. This contrasts with baselines that often treated each package as an isolated unit.
- **Superior Dual-Arm Parallelism:** RoboPARA consistently achieved higher Arm Parallelism Rates (APR) across all evaluated scenes and difficulty levels. In simple task packages, RoboPARA's parallel steps were over 2.8 times higher than baselines, escalating to over 10 times in medium and complex packages with the GPT-4o backbone. While some baselines attempted to increase parallelism, they often incurred elevated failure rates due to infeasible plan generation. Even with the DeepSeek V3 backbone, where baseline parallelism improved, RoboPARA still outperformed all of them by over 1.3x.
- **Robustness Across Diverse Conditions:** The framework demonstrated consistent superior performance across both GPT-4o and DeepSeek V3 LLM backbones, various scenes, and different difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard task packages). This indicated strong generalizability and resilience to variations in underlying models and task complexity.
- **Real-world Validation:** Deployment on Franka Research 3 robotic arms, UR5e robotic arms, and a humanoid robot in various scenarios illustrated that RoboPARA generated execution plans closely aligned with efficient human-like behaviors, effectively managing dual-arm collaboration and reducing execution time in physical environments.
- **Ablation Study Insights:** The ablation studies highlighted the importance of several design choices:
 - **Environment information:** Excluding this from prompts increased failure rates by 28% and decreased parallel execution by 25%.
 - **Instructional constraints and DAG correction:** Removing these mechanisms individually led to a significant average increase of 51% and 18% in failure rates, respectively, especially for hard packages. This indicates their criticality for generating correct DAG nodes and preventing deadlocks or infinite loops.
 - **Arm selector deadlock check:** Without timely deadlock detection and case-specific reasoning, execution efficiency dropped by an average of 22%, and the failure rate increased by an average of 18%.
- **Closed-Loop Error Handling:** RoboPARA demonstrated a capability for closed-loop error handling and replanning. When simulated execution errors occurred (e.g., an object falling), the system could update its task description with the new environment state and dynamically generate a revised plan, enabling task resumption and completion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several contributions to the field of dual-arm robot manipulation and LLM-driven planning:

- **Novel Problem Formulation:** The introduction of the "Dual-Arm Cooperative Scheduling Problem" explicitly emphasizes the optimization of parallelism and multitasking efficiency, advancing the scope of dual-arm robotic manipulation research.
- **New Benchmark Dataset:** The Cross-Scenario Dual-Arm Parallel Task (X-DAPT) dataset is the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating dual-arm task parallelism. Its diverse scenarios, varied difficulty levels, and focus on long-horizon, complex tasks provide a valuable resource for future research.
- **Innovative Planning Framework:** RoboPARA presents a novel LLM-driven framework that addresses a significant gap in existing methods by moving beyond sequential execution to enable highly parallel and efficient dual-arm collaboration. Its two-stage architecture, integrating LLM-driven DAG generation with a graph re-traversal scheduler, provides a structured approach to complex planning.
- **Demonstrated Performance Improvements:** The experimental results indicate substantial improvements in task efficiency, reliability, and dual-arm utilization compared to existing LLM-based planning baselines. These quantitative gains, particularly in complex task combinations, suggest a more effective utilization of dual-arm robot capabilities.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** By enabling robots to reason about and execute tasks with human-like multitasking and parallelization strategies, RoboPARA contributes to advancing robot autonomy in complex, dynamic environments.
- **Practical Applicability:** The framework has potential implications for real-world applications requiring efficient dual-arm coordination, such as robotic kitchens, manufacturing facilities, agricultural greenhouses, and disaster rescue operations, where maximizing throughput and reliability is critical.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations that represent avenues for future work. These include the computational cost associated with iterative LLM interactions (e.g., GPT-4o, DeepSeek V3), persistent inaccuracies in self-correction for highly intricate dependencies within limited correction rounds, and the current coupling of the skill library to predefined scenarios, which limits generalization to unseen tasks or environments. Future research could explore more cost-effective LLMs, adaptive DAG generation, and robust verification mechanisms to enhance RoboPARA's scalability and broad applicability.
