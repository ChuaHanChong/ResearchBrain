# PaperBanana: Automating Academic Illustration for AI Scientists

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and Google Cloud AI Research developed PaperBanana, an agentic framework that automates the generation of academic illustrations, including methodology diagrams and statistical plots. It significantly outperformed existing baselines on a new benchmark, achieving an overall score of 60.2 and demonstrating human-competitive results for statistical plots.

## Problem

- The manual creation of high-quality academic illustrations, such as methodology diagrams and statistical plots, is time-consuming and a bottleneck in the research workflow.
- Existing automated diagram generation methods, whether code-based or general-purpose image generation, struggle to meet the specific stylistic rigor and factual accuracy required for scientific publications.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks has hindered rigorous evaluation and comparison of systems designed for automated academic illustration.

## Method

- A ==reference-driven agentic framework called PaperBanana== was developed, orchestrating five specialized AI agents (Retriever, Planner, Stylist, Visualizer, Critic) powered by state-of-the-art VLMs and image generation models.
- The framework employs an ==iterative Visualizer-Critic loop== for refinement, ensuring content fidelity and adherence to academic aesthetic standards.
- A new benchmark, ==PaperBananaBench==, was introduced, featuring 292 methodology diagrams from NeurIPS 2025, alongside a VLM-as-a-Judge evaluation protocol, to standardize assessment.

## Results

- PaperBanana achieved an overall score of **60.2** on the PaperBananaBench, outperforming vanilla (**43.2**) and few-shot baselines by **17.0%** and demonstrating specific gains in faithfulness (**+2.8%**), conciseness (**+37.2%**), readability (**+12.9%**), and aesthetics (**+6.6%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the Critic agent in improving faithfulness and overall quality through iterative refinement, and highlighted that any relevant structural/stylistic reference is more important than content-specific retrieval.
- The framework successfully generated statistical plots by converting descriptions to Python Matplotlib code, achieving human-competitive results in conciseness, readability, and aesthetics while maintaining strong faithfulness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agentic framework mimicking human workflow, with specialized roles for content planning, style optimization, and iterative self-critique, is effective for complex generative tasks like academic illustration.
> - While style-optimizing agents can enhance aesthetics and conciseness, a critical feedback loop (Critic agent) is essential to maintain factual faithfulness.
> - For statistical plots, a hybrid visualization strategy leveraging code generation for numerical precision and image generation for visual presentation offers the most balanced approach.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous AI scientists, often powered by advanced large language models (LLMs), represents a significant direction in artificial general intelligence. These systems have shown capabilities in automating various aspects of the research lifecycle, including literature review, idea generation, and experimental iteration. However, the effective communication of scientific discoveries is a critical bottleneck in the overall research process. Specifically, the generation of publication-ready visual aids, such as methodology diagrams and statistical plots, remains a labor-intensive task that current autonomous AI scientists struggle to perform to academic standards.

Prior efforts in automated diagram generation have largely relied on code-based paradigms, utilizing tools like TikZ, Python-PPTX, or SVG to programmatically synthesize diagrams. While effective for structured content, these methods can exhibit limitations in expressiveness when dealing with the intricate visual elements, specialized icons, and custom shapes common in contemporary AI publications. Concurrently, recent advancements in image generation models have demonstrated sophisticated instruction-following capabilities and high-quality visual outputs. However, these models have not consistently produced academic illustrations that meet the rigorous fidelity and aesthetic requirements of scholarly publishing. Researchers are often required to invest substantial manual effort and specialized expertise in professional illustration tools to visually convey complex ideas.

This work introduces PaperBanana to address this gap, positioning itself as a framework that leverages state-of-the-art visual language models (VLMs) and image generation models to automate the creation of high-quality academic illustrations. By focusing on both methodology diagrams and statistical plots, it aims to integrate visual communication into the broader workflow of autonomous AI scientists, thereby reducing manual burden and accelerating the dissemination of scientific discoveries. The paper also notes concurrent research such as AutoFigure and AutoFigure-Edit, which focus on transforming scientific content into symbolic representations for image rendering, and differentiates PaperBanana by its adaptive retrieval, academic-style transfer, and unified pipeline supporting diverse illustration types.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to automate the generation of publication-ready academic illustrations. This encompasses both complex methodology diagrams and precise statistical plots, with the overarching goal of integrating high-quality visual communication into the workflow of autonomous AI scientists.

The motivation for this work is multifaceted:
- **Addressing a Workflow Bottleneck:** Despite rapid progress in autonomous AI scientists capable of textual analysis and code execution, the generation of academic illustrations that adhere to scholarly standards remains a labor-intensive and manual process. This creates a significant bottleneck in the effective visual communication of scientific discoveries, hindering the efficiency of the research lifecycle.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Methods:**
 - **Code-based Diagram Generation:** Current code-based approaches (e.g., TikZ, Python-PPTX) are effective for structured content but often lack the expressiveness required for the intricate visual elements, specialized icons, and custom shapes increasingly common in modern AI publications.
 - **General Image Generation Models:** While advanced, general image generation models struggle to consistently produce academic illustrations that meet specific scholarly standards, such as content fidelity and visual aesthetics.
- **Reducing Manual Effort and Specialized Expertise:** Researchers frequently invest substantial time and effort in crafting figures using professional illustration tools, which often require specialized design expertise. Automating this process aims to lift this burden and democratize access to high-quality visual communication.
- **Enabling Autonomous AI Scientists:** To achieve truly autonomous scientific discovery, AI systems need to be proficient not only in generating and analyzing research content but also in effectively communicating their findings visually. PaperBanana aims to equip AI scientists with this capability.
- **Establishing Evaluation Standards:** The absence of dedicated benchmarks for automated academic illustration generation makes rigorous evaluation challenging. A key objective is to introduce PaperBananaBench, a comprehensive benchmark for methodology diagram generation, to facilitate systematic assessment and advancement in the field.

In summary, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between AI-driven scientific discovery and its effective visual communication by developing an automated, agentic framework that produces high-quality academic illustrations, supported by a dedicated evaluation benchmark.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PaperBanana framework is designed as a reference-driven agentic system composed of five specialized agents: Retriever, Planner, Stylist, Visualizer, and Critic. It formulates the task of automated academic illustration generation as learning a mapping function `I = f(S, C, E)`, where `I` is the generated image, `S` is the source context (e.g., methodology description), `C` is the communicative intent (e.g., figure caption), and `E` is an optional set of reference examples. The system primarily focuses on methodology diagrams, where `S` is the textual description of the method and `C` is the figure caption.

#### 3.1. PaperBanana Framework Architecture

- **Retriever Agent:** Given the source context `S` and communicative intent `C`, this agent identifies the `N` most relevant examples `E` from a fixed reference set `R`. It employs a generative retrieval approach using a VLM (Gemini-3-Pro) to rank candidates by matching both research domain (e.g., Agent & Reasoning) and diagram type (e.g., pipeline, architecture), prioritizing visual structure over topic similarity. The retrieved examples provide structural logic and visual style guidance.
- **Planner Agent:** This agent acts as the cognitive core. It receives `S`, `C`, and the retrieved examples `E`. Through in-context learning from `E`, a VLM (Gemini-3-Pro) translates the input `S` into a detailed textual description `P` of the target illustration.
- **Stylist Agent:** To ensure adherence to academic aesthetic standards, this agent synthesizes a comprehensive Aesthetic Guideline `G` by traversing the entire reference collection `R`. Using this guideline, a VLM (Gemini-3-Pro) refines the initial description `P` into a stylistically optimized version `P*`. This ensures visual professionalism in terms of color palette, shapes, lines, layout, typography, and icons.
- **Visualizer Agent:** This agent collaborates with the Critic Agent to render and refine illustrations.
 - **For Methodology Diagrams:** It uses an image generation model (Nano-Banana-Pro or GPT-Image-1.5) to transform the textual description `P_t` (initially `P*`) into a visual output `I_t`.
 - **For Statistical Plots:** It converts `P_t` into executable Python Matplotlib code using a VLM (Gemini-3-Pro), which then renders the plot `I_t`.
- **Critic Agent:** This agent forms a closed-loop refinement mechanism. It inspects the generated image `I_t` against the original `S` and `C` to identify factual misalignments, visual glitches, or areas for improvement. A VLM (Gemini-3-Pro) then provides targeted feedback and produces a refined description `P_t+1`, which is fed back to the Visualizer. This iterative Visualizer-Critic loop runs for `T=3` rounds to achieve high standards.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction: PaperBananaBench

To rigorously evaluate the framework, the authors constructed PaperBananaBench, a benchmark specifically for methodology diagram generation, addressing a previous lack of such resources.

- **Data Curation:**
 - **Collection & Parsing:** 2,000 papers from NeurIPS 2025 were sampled. The MinerU toolkit extracted methodology sections, diagrams, and captions.
 - **Filtering:** Papers without methodology diagrams, or those with extreme aspect ratios (outside [1.5, 2.5] to fit common layouts and image generation model capabilities), were filtered, yielding 610 candidates.
 - **Categorization:** Gemini-3-Pro categorized diagrams into four classes based on visual topology and content: Agent & Reasoning, Vision & Perception, Generative & Learning, and Science & Applications.
 - **Human Curation:** Annotators verified and corrected extracted texts, validated categorizations, and filtered out diagrams of insufficient visual quality. This resulted in 584 valid samples, which were partitioned into a test set (292 cases) and a reference set (292 cases) for retrieval-augmented learning.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** A VLM-as-a-Judge approach (Gemini-3-Pro) was used for reference-based scoring, comparing model-generated diagrams against human-drawn references.
 - **Evaluation Dimensions:** Diagrams were assessed on four criteria:
 - **Content:** Faithfulness (alignment with source context and communicative intent) and Conciseness (focus on core information, absence of clutter).
 - **Presentation:** Readability (intelligible layouts, legible text, minimal crossing lines) and Aesthetics (adherence to academic stylistic norms).
 - **Referenced Scoring:** The VLM judge determined whether the model-generated diagram "Model wins," "Human wins," or resulted in a "Tie" for each dimension, mapped to scores of 100, 0, and 50 respectively. A hierarchical aggregation strategy prioritized Faithfulness and Readability as primary dimensions, followed by Conciseness and Aesthetics for overall scoring.
 - **Reliability Validation:** The VLM judge's reliability was verified through inter-model agreement (Kendall's tau correlations of 0.43-0.60 with Gemini-3-Flash and GPT-5) and human alignment (Kendall's tau correlations of 0.41-0.57 with human annotators).

#### 3.3. Extension to Statistical Plots

The framework adapts for statistical plots by modifying the Visualizer and Critic agents. The Visualizer converts the description `P_t` into executable Python Matplotlib code. The Critic evaluates the rendered plot for accuracy and imperfections, generating a refined description `P_t+1`. The same 3-round iterative refinement process is applied. A specialized test set of 240 cases and 240 reference examples, repurposed from ChartMimic (Yang et al., 2025b), was curated, stratified across seven plot categories and two complexity levels, to evaluate this extension.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the performance of PaperBanana across methodology diagram generation and its extension to statistical plots.

#### 4.1. Methodology Diagram Generation

- **Overall Performance:** PaperBanana consistently outperformed leading baselines on the PaperBananaBench across all four evaluation dimensions when using Nano-Banana-Pro as the image generation model. Compared to the Vanilla Nano-Banana-Pro baseline, PaperBanana achieved improvements in:
 - Faithfulness: +2.8%
 - Conciseness: +37.2%
 - Readability: +12.9%
 - Aesthetics: +6.6%
 - Aggregated Overall Score: +17.0%
- **Baseline Comparison:**
 - **GPT-Image-1.5:** Exhibited poor performance in both vanilla and agentic settings, attributed to weaker instruction following and text rendering capabilities.
 - **Paper2Any:** Underperformed due to an objective mismatch; it prioritizes presenting high-level ideas rather than the faithful depiction of specific methodological flows required for methodology diagrams.
- **Performance by Category:** The framework achieved the highest overall score in the "Agent & Reasoning" category (69.9%), followed by "Scientific & Application" (58.8%), "Generative & Learning" (57.0%), and "Vision & Perception" (52.1%).
- **Human Evaluation:** A blind human evaluation on a subset of 50 cases compared PaperBanana against vanilla Nano-Banana-Pro. PaperBanana achieved an average win/tie/loss rate of 72.7% / 20.7% / 6.6% from three human judges, providing further validation of the agentic workflow's improvements.
- **Limitations in Faithfulness:** Despite overall improvements, PaperBanana still showed some underperformance compared to human references in terms of faithfulness, with prevalent errors identified in fine-grained connectivity (e.g., misaligned start/end points, incorrect arrow directions).

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of each agent component:
- **Retriever Agent:** When compared to random or no-retriever baselines, the Retriever Agent demonstrated significant impact. Without reference examples, the system underperformed in Conciseness, Readability, and Aesthetics, producing verbose and less visually refined outputs. The random retriever performed comparably to the semantic approach, suggesting that providing general structural and stylistic patterns from references is more critical than precise content matching alone.
- **Stylist and Critic Agents:** The Stylist Agent improved Conciseness (+17.5%) and Aesthetics (+4.7%) but initially lowered Faithfulness (-8.5%) as visual polishing sometimes omitted technical details. The Critic Agent effectively recovered Faithfulness and further enhanced all metrics through iterative refinement, indicating its role in balancing aesthetics with technical accuracy.

#### 4.3. Statistical Plots Generation

PaperBanana's extension to statistical plots, which utilizes code-based visualization for numerical precision, demonstrated effectiveness:
- **Performance Gains:** Compared to vanilla Gemini-3-Pro, PaperBanana outperformed the baseline across all dimensions:
 - Faithfulness: +1.4%
 - Conciseness: +5.0%
 - Readability: +3.1%
 - Aesthetics: +4.0%
 - Overall Improvement: +4.1%
- **Competitive with Human Performance:** PaperBanana slightly surpassed human performance in Conciseness, Readability, and Aesthetics for statistical plots, while remaining competitive in Faithfulness.

#### 4.4. Discussion Points

- **Enhancing Human-Drawn Diagrams:** The summarized aesthetic guidelines generated by the Stylist Agent were shown to elevate the aesthetic quality of existing human-drawn diagrams. Refined diagrams achieved a 56.2% win rate in aesthetics against their original counterparts across 292 test cases.
- **Coding vs. Image Generation for Statistical Plots:** A comparison revealed trade-offs: image generation (Nano-Banana-Pro) excelled in presentation (Readability and Aesthetics) but underperformed in content fidelity (Faithfulness and Conciseness). Image models struggled with dense or complex data, exhibiting numerical hallucinations or element repetition, while code-based approaches (Gemini-3-Pro) ensured precision. This suggests a potential for a hybrid approach, using image generation for sparse visualizations and code for dense plots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PaperBanana framework represents a step towards automating a critical, labor-intensive component of the scientific research workflow: the generation of publication-ready academic illustrations. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Acceleration of Scientific Discovery:** By automating the creation of high-quality methodology diagrams and statistical plots, PaperBanana aims to reduce the manual effort currently required by researchers. This could significantly accelerate the process of scientific communication and the overall research lifecycle, allowing AI scientists to autonomously communicate their findings more effectively.
- **Democratization of Visual Communication:** The framework provides tools to generate professional-grade visualizations, potentially benefiting researchers who may lack specialized design resources or expertise. This could contribute to a more equitable dissemination of scientific knowledge by lowering the barrier to producing high-quality visual aids.
- **Establishment of a Standardized Benchmark:** The introduction of PaperBananaBench addresses a notable gap in the research landscape by providing a comprehensive, rigorously curated benchmark for evaluating automated academic illustration generation. This benchmark is crucial for driving future research and enabling systematic comparisons of new methods.
- **Generalizable Agentic Paradigm:** The framework's core paradigm—leveraging retrieval for content instruction and automatic style summarization for aesthetic guidance—offers a generalizable approach. This decoupling of structural planning from aesthetic rendering suggests potential applicability to other specialized domains requiring strict adherence to community standards, such as UI/UX design, patent drafting, or industrial schematics.
- **Advancement in VLM and Image Generation Capabilities:** The work highlights both the current capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art VLMs and image generation models in understanding complex scientific context, adhering to specific design constraints, and performing iterative refinement. The identified failure modes, particularly in fine-grained faithfulness, point to crucial directions for future advancements in foundation models.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations and propose future research directions:
- **Raster Output:** The current output is primarily raster images, which are difficult to edit. Future work may explore generating editable vector graphics, potentially through image editing models, reconstruction pipelines (OCR, segmentation), or GUI agents operating professional design software.
- **Style Standardization vs. Diversity:** The unified style guide, while ensuring compliance with academic standards, can reduce stylistic diversity. Future research could investigate dynamic style adaptation mechanisms for broader artistic expression.
- **Fine-Grained Faithfulness:** A performance gap in faithfulness, especially regarding connection errors (e.g., misaligned nodes, incorrect arrow directions), remains. Addressing this requires advancements in the fine-grained visual perception capabilities of foundation VLMs.
- **Evaluation Paradigms:** The VLM-as-a-Judge setup, while effective, could benefit from more precise structure-based or rubric-based metrics, and customized reward models for better alignment with human aesthetic preferences.
- **Test-Time Scaling for Diverse Preferences:** Generating a spectrum of candidates with varying styles could allow users to select the most suitable illustration, shifting towards a "generate-and-select" workflow.

The research emphasizes the necessity of human oversight to mitigate ethical risks associated with generative models, such as "visual hallucination" or unfaithful representation of technical details, ensuring the scientific integrity of published illustrations.
