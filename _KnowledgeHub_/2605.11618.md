# Sampling-Based Follow-the-Leader Motion Planning for Manipulator-Mounted Continuum Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A sampling-based motion planning framework addresses Follow-the-Leader (FTL) motion for continuum robots mounted on robotic manipulators with full 6-DOF base control. The method achieves 0% tip deviation in simulation and substantially reduces online computation time compared to optimization-based approaches, facilitating FTL for complex real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing FTL methods for continuum robots primarily assume fixed or limited 1-DOF linear insertion mechanisms, which restricts their use with manipulator-mounted CRs that have full 6-DOF base control.
- The high-dimensional, non-linear configuration space of manipulator-mounted CRs poses computational challenges for traditional iterative optimization methods, often leading to local minima and inefficiency.
- Many prior FTL approaches rely on simplified kinematic models (e.g., PCC), which may not accurately represent the behavior of physical tendon-driven CRs and limit generalizability.

## Method

- A **sampling-based framework** that decouples global shape search from 6-DOF base pose determination, by pre-computing a library of possible CR shapes offline.
- ==Closed-form geometric construction== determines the manipulator's 6-DOF base pose for any candidate CR shape and waypoint, guaranteeing exact tip tracking without iterative optimization.
- An optional ==clustering mechanism== and a ==two-pass search strategy== are used to efficiently find the best-fitting shape online, alongside interpolation methods that exploit radial symmetry for continuous trajectories.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently achieved **0%** tip deviation in simulation across **120** diverse paths using both PCC and more complex pseudo-rigid-body (PRB) models in MuJoCo.
- The clustered variant of the method reduced computation time by over an order of magnitude (e.g., **1.81s** vs. **36.4s** for C Curves with PCC model) compared to an optimization baseline, with only a minimal increase in shape deviation.
- Hardware demonstrations on a physical manipulator-mounted tendon-driven CR qualitatively showed successful FTL motion, with mean tip tracking errors ranging from **5.5%** to **9.1%** (normalized by CR length), primarily attributed to model-reality discrepancies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the global shape search from local base pose determination through a pre-computed library and geometric construction avoids local minima and offers predictable, efficient online planning.
> - The closed-form geometric approach to determine the 6-DOF base pose inherently guarantees exact tip tracking, simplifying a critical aspect of FTL planning in high-dimensional spaces.
> - The framework's model-agnostic nature allows the use of high-fidelity kinetostatic models by offloading computation to an offline shape library generation phase.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Continuum robots (CRs) are highly flexible, high-degree-of-freedom robotic systems inspired by biological structures, offering potential for navigation in confined and tortuous environments where rigid-link robots may not be suitable. The "follow-the-leader" (FTL) motion primitive is a fundamental capability for CRs, allowing their bodies to retrace the path of their tip, thereby minimizing interaction forces with the environment and reducing collision risks.

Existing research on FTL motion planning for CRs has explored various robot architectures. Early work established foundational concepts for snake-like robots. Steerable needles and vine robots are designed to inherently extend and follow their tip path. Concentric tube robots (CTRs) can achieve FTL through specific tube parameter selection and coordinated extension.

For tendon-driven continuum robots (TDCRs), two main lines of research have emerged. The first focuses on hardware-based solutions, adding degrees of freedom to the TDCR design itself through extensible segments, active stiffness modulation, or magnetic actuation. While effective, these solutions often consume valuable space that could be used for payloads and may not be universally generalizable. The second approach, more relevant to this work, integrates additional degrees of freedom at the TDCR base, coordinating them with the CR's configuration. These methods typically employ differential, heuristic-based, or optimization-based inverse kinematics. However, these approaches frequently rely on simplified kinematic models, such as the piece-wise constant curvature (PCC) model, which may not accurately represent physical TDCR behavior. Furthermore, local solvers or iterative optimization methods used in these approaches can converge to suboptimal solutions in the high-dimensional, nonlinear configuration space of CRs and are sensitive to initialization.

A critical limitation shared by nearly all prior FTL methods is the assumption of a fixed robot base or a single degree-of-freedom (1-DOF) linear insertion mechanism. This assumption significantly restricts their applicability to practical systems where CRs are mounted on robotic manipulators, which provide full 6-DOF base pose control. Such manipulator-mounted CRs are increasingly deployed in various fields, including minimally invasive surgery, industrial inspection, and search-and-rescue. The current work addresses this gap by developing an FTL motion planning framework specifically for CRs with a fully actuated SE(3) base, thereby expanding the applicability of FTL motion to a broader range of real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a motion planning framework for Follow-the-Leader (FTL) motion of continuum robots (CRs) that are mounted on robotic manipulators with fully actuated SE(3) base pose control. Given a spatial path defined by a sequence of waypoints, the framework aims to generate a continuous motion plan that satisfies two main properties throughout the insertion process:
1. **Tip Tracking:** The robot tip must accurately track the specified path, passing through each waypoint precisely and following a smooth interpolation between them.
2. **Shape Following:** The inserted portion of the robot body must minimize deviation from the corresponding segment of the target path.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations of existing FTL planning methods:
- **Fixed or Limited Base Actuation:** Traditional FTL methods predominantly assume a fixed robot base or a 1-DOF linear insertion mechanism. This assumption renders them unsuitable for practical applications involving CRs mounted on serial manipulators, which offer full 6-DOF base pose control and are increasingly common. The additional 6 degrees of freedom at the base introduce a significantly expanded and more complex planning space.
- **Computational Challenges in High-Dimensional Spaces:** The combined configuration space of a manipulator-mounted CR (SE(3) for the base and the CR's internal configuration space) is high-dimensional and non-linear. Iterative optimization methods, commonly used in prior work, are susceptible to local minima and are computationally expensive, making them less predictable and efficient for safety-critical or real-time applications.
- **Reliance on Simplified Kinematic Models:** Many existing methods rely on simplified kinematics like the Piece-wise Constant Curvature (PCC) model, which often does not accurately capture the behavior of physical TDCRs, especially when considering complex interactions like segment coupling. A generalizable solution should support various, potentially more accurate, forward models.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a sampling-based framework that decouples the global search for appropriate robot shapes from the local determination of the base pose. This decoupling is intended to enable efficient online planning, provide formal theoretical guarantees, and support a wide range of forward models beyond simplified kinematic assumptions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method is a sampling-based motion planning framework designed to decouple global shape search from base pose determination, thereby avoiding iterative optimization during online planning. It comprises three main stages: offline library generation, online base pose alignment and shape search at each waypoint, and interpolation between waypoints.

#### 3.1. A. Shape Library

1. **Library Generation:** A library of possible CR shapes is generated offline. This involves uniformly sampling `N_lib` configurations from the robot's configuration space `Q`. For each sampled configuration, the forward model (FM) is used to compute a discretized backbone shape representation. Each shape `S^(i)` is stored as a tuple `(q^(i), P^(i))`, where `q^(i)` is the configuration and `P^(i)` is a set of `D+1` points along the backbone. This shifts the majority of the computational burden offline, allowing the use of complex kinetostatic models.
2. **Threshold Clustering (Optional):** To accelerate online planning, an optional clustering mechanism can be employed. Shapes in the library are grouped based on pairwise similarity, quantified by a modified Chamfer distance (Equation 1), which sums point-wise Euclidean distances. Clusters are formed by iterating through ungrouped shapes, designating each as a cluster center, and adding all shapes within a similarity threshold `γ` to its cluster. This creates local "balls" in shape space. During online search, only cluster centers are initially evaluated, and then an exhaustive search is performed within the best-scoring cluster. This two-pass strategy reduces computational complexity from `O(N_lib)` to approximately `O(sqrt(N_lib))` but can potentially miss the globally optimal shape.

#### 3.2. B. Base Pose Alignment

For each waypoint `w_i` and each candidate shape `S` from the library, a closed-form geometric construction determines the 6-DOF base pose `T_b` that aligns the shape to the active path.
1. **Active Shape Subset:** The portion of the CR backbone whose arc length best matches the inserted path length up to `w_i` is identified. This is done by finding the index `m*` such that the arc length from `p_m*` to the tip `p_D` (of the candidate shape) is closest to the path length from `w_1` to `w_i`.
2. **Base Pose Alignment Steps:** The base pose `T_b` is determined through three sequential geometric transformations:
 - **Transformation 1 (Translation):** The entire shape is translated such that its tip (`p_D`) coincides exactly with the current waypoint (`w_i`). This guarantees exact tip tracking.
 - **Transformation 2 (Rotation about tip):** The shape is rotated around `w_i` to align the direction from `w_i` to `p_m*` (along the translated shape) with the direction from `w_i` to `w_1` (along the path). This rotation preserves the tip's position at `w_i`.
 - **Transformation 3 (Axial rotation):** A remaining rotational degree of freedom about the path axis (from `w_1` to `w_i`) is resolved. A third correspondence point (`w_k` from the active path, `p_k` from the active shape subset) is used, chosen to maximize distance from the line defined by `w_1` and `w_i`. The angle `ϕ` for axial rotation is computed to align the projections of `(w_k - w_1)` and `(p_k - w_1)` onto the plane orthogonal to the path axis. This rotation also preserves tip placement.
These three transformations uniquely determine the full 6-DOF base pose `T_b` for any given shape, ensuring exact tip tracking by construction without iterative optimization.

#### 3.3. C. Shape Evaluation

After aligning each candidate shape with the active path, the deviation between the transformed shape and the path is quantified using the symmetric Chamfer distance (Equation 3). This metric is robust to differences in point density and penalizes deviations bidirectionally. The shape minimizing this deviation is selected, along with its associated `T_b`, forming a complete configuration `(q, T_b)`. This process can be viewed as a nearest-neighbor search in a task-induced metric space.

#### 3.4. D. Interpolation

After selecting configurations at each waypoint, a sparse plan `{(q_j, T^b_j)}` is generated. To create a smooth, continuous trajectory for hardware deployment, interpolation is performed between waypoints.
1. **Exploiting Radial Symmetry:** To minimize orientation discontinuities during interpolation, radial symmetry of CRs is exploited. Each configuration `(q_j, T^b_j)` is rotated about its backbone axis to pre-align it with a common reference direction (e.g., the base x-axis of the first configuration). This can be applied continuously (for PCC-like robots), discretely (for TDCRs with `k` tendons per segment), or empirically through data-driven clustering for unknown symmetries.
2. **Local Interpolation Between Waypoints:** For each interval between `w_j` and `w_j+1`, `h` intermediate configurations are generated. The tip position is linearly interpolated, and the tip orientation is interpolated using SLERP (Spherical Linear Interpolation). Simultaneously, the CR joint state (after pre-alignment) is linearly interpolated. The base pose `T_b` for each intermediate step is then constructed using Equation 8, which guarantees exact tip placement at the interpolated desired tip pose. This process requires one forward model evaluation per interpolation step, providing a fixed and predictable computational cost.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The method was evaluated through a series of experiments across increasing levels of fidelity: a comparison with an optimization-based baseline using a PCC kinematic model, simulation with a system-identified pseudo-rigid-body (PRB) model in MuJoCo, and a hardware demonstration on a physical tendon-driven CR mounted on a 7-DOF manipulator. Performance was assessed using tip deviation, shape deviation, and compute time.

#### 4.1. A. PCC Model: Comparison with Optimization Baseline

- **Setup:** The proposed method (linear search and clustered variants) was compared against an optimization-based FTL baseline using a PCC model, which employs SciPy’s L-BFGS-B solver. A shape library of 20,000 configurations was used.
- **Results:** Across 120 simulated paths (C Curves, S Curves, Robot Curves), the proposed method achieved **0% tip deviation**, confirming the theoretical guarantee of exact tip tracking. In terms of shape deviation, the linear search variant of the proposed method **outperformed the optimization baseline** (e.g., 1.25% vs. 1.74% for C Curves), demonstrating its ability to avoid local minima. The clustered variant significantly **reduced computation time by over an order of magnitude** (e.g., 1.81s vs. 36.4s for C Curves) with only a minimal increase in shape deviation (e.g., 1.61% vs. 1.25% for C Curves).

#### 4.2. B. MuJoCo Simulation: Pseudo-Rigid-Body Model

- **Setup:** The method was tested using a custom PRB model in MuJoCo, with parameters identified from a physical TDCR, including segment coupling effects. The simulated CR was mounted on a 7-DOF Franka Robotics manipulator. The clustered variant was used with a library size of 20,000.
- **Results:** Consistent with the PCC results, the method achieved **0% tip deviation** across all 120 paths. Shape deviation was slightly higher compared to the PCC experiments (e.g., 2.46% for C Curves), attributed to the increased complexity and fidelity of the PRB model and the use of discrete 3-fold radial symmetry for pre-alignment. These results indicate the method's scalability and robustness to more realistic CR models.

#### 4.3. C. Effect of Shape Library Size

- **Findings:** An analysis of library size (`N_lib`) revealed that shape deviation decreased with diminishing returns as `N_lib` increased, eventually saturating as the library became sufficiently dense. This aligns with the resolution completeness theory. Computation time for the linear search variant grew linearly with `N_lib`, while the clustered variant maintained nearly constant computation time across varying `N_lib` with a small accuracy trade-off, validating the efficiency of the two-pass search strategy.

#### 4.4. D. Hardware Demonstration

- **Setup:** The method was demonstrated on a physical setup comprising a 3-segment tendon-driven CR mounted on a 7-DOF Franka Robotics manipulator. Motion plans were generated using the MuJoCo PRB model and post-processed for time-optimal path parameterization.
- **Results:** The robot successfully tracked C, S, and robot curve paths qualitatively, with coordinated manipulator and CR motion. Tip tracking error, measured using an optical tracker, showed mean errors of 5.5% (C-shape), 9.1% (S-shape), and 8.3% (robot-curve shape), normalized by CR length. These errors are attributed to the discrepancies between the simplified simulation model and the physical system (e.g., unmodelled tendon stretch, friction, hysteresis) and the open-loop execution, rather than a limitation of the planner itself, which achieved zero tip error in simulation.

#### 4.5. E. Ablation Studies (Appendix)

- **Effect of Clustering:** Confirmed that clustering provides a smooth trade-off between speed and accuracy. Smaller clusters (closer to linear search) yielded lowest deviation and highest time, while larger clusters reduced computation significantly but increased deviation.
- **Effect of Library Size on Sparse Plan:** Re-confirmed diminishing returns for shape deviation with increasing `N_lib` (without interpolation noise), linear time growth for linear search, and `O(sqrt(N_lib))` for clustered search.
- **Effect of Radial Symmetry Pre-Alignment:** Demonstrated that pre-alignment consistently improved overall shape deviation during interpolation. Full continuous symmetry offered marginal additional improvement over 3-fold discrete symmetry, suggesting that even coarse symmetry exploitation yields substantial benefits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a novel sampling-based motion planning framework that addresses a critical gap in continuum robotics: Follow-the-Leader (FTL) motion for manipulator-mounted continuum robots with full 6-DOF base pose control. The significance of this research stems from several key aspects:

1. **Enabling Advanced FTL Applications:** By accounting for a fully actuated SE(3) base, the framework significantly expands the applicability of FTL motion. This is particularly relevant for real-world scenarios in minimally invasive surgery, industrial inspection, and search-and-rescue, where CRs are increasingly integrated with robotic manipulators to enhance reach and dexterity.
2. **Theoretical Guarantees for Planning:** The proposed method establishes formal guarantees that are often lacking in optimization-based FTL approaches. These include resolution completeness for the shape search (ensuring that a valid plan will be found given a sufficiently large library), exact tip tracking by geometric construction, and asymptotic tip-tracking convergence during interpolation. These guarantees contribute to the predictability and reliability of the planner, which are crucial for safety-critical applications.
3. **Model Agnosticism and Efficiency:** The decoupling of global shape search from base pose determination allows the framework to support any forward model, including complex kinetostatic models that would be computationally intractable for online iterative optimization. By pre-computing shapes offline, the online planning process becomes highly efficient, involving primarily library lookups and closed-form geometric constructions, with a predictable number of forward model evaluations during interpolation. The user-adjustable clustering further enables a principled trade-off between computation time and shape accuracy, making it adaptable to various application requirements.
4. **Decoupled Planning Paradigm:** The core idea of decoupling shape search from base pose determination by constructing the base pose through closed-form geometry represents a distinct approach compared to traditional coupled optimization methods. This paradigm offers a foundation for scalable FTL planning in increasingly complex and hybrid rigid-continuum robotic systems.
5. **Reproducibility and Open-Source Contribution:** The open-sourcing of the implementation supports reproducibility and facilitates further research and development in the field, allowing other researchers to build upon this framework.

The hardware validation, while showing some deviations attributable to real-world model-reality gaps, demonstrates that the method produces smooth, executable trajectories that achieve qualitatively correct FTL motion on a physical manipulator-mounted CR system. Future work can build upon this foundation by improving model fidelity, incorporating closed-loop shape control, or leveraging hardware-recorded shapes to enhance practical execution accuracy. Overall, this research advances the state of the art in continuum robot motion planning, providing a robust, efficient, and theoretically grounded approach for complex FTL tasks.
