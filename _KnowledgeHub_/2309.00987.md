# Sequential Dexterity: Chaining Dexterous Policies for Long-Horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed a system for chaining dexterous manipulation policies to achieve long-horizon tasks, leveraging a novel bi-directional optimization process centered on a Transition Feasibility Function. The approach demonstrated a 46% success rate in simulation for multi-stage tasks and achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, successfully completing complex block assembly tasks with a real robotic hand.

## Problem

- Enabling high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) dexterous robot hands to perform complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks that require chaining multiple skills.
- Addressing the challenge of seamless transitions between individual learned dexterous policies, particularly when initial states generated by a preceding policy are out-of-distribution for the successor.
- Overcoming the limitations of uni-directional policy chaining methods that often neglect the influence of long-term goals on earlier, non-adjacent policies.

## Method

- Decomposing complex tasks into sequences of sub-tasks within the **Sequential Dexterity** framework, with individual dexterous sub-policies trained using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== and forward initialization.
- Introducing a novel ==bi-directional optimization== framework that learns a ==Transition Feasibility Function (TFF)== for each sub-policy, used to fine-tune preceding policies by propagating long-term goal information backward.
- Utilizing the learned TFFs during deployment as ==policy-switching identifiers== to autonomously and robustly manage transitions between sub-policies, including dynamic recovery and bypassing of stages.

## Results

- Achieves a **46%** success rate for the 4-stage 'Building Blocks' task in simulation, significantly outperforming uni-directional baselines (e.g., T-STAR's **19%**).
- Demonstrates successful zero-shot transfer from simulation to a real Franka Emika robot with an Allegro Hand, achieving a **33%** success rate for a double-block structure, while baselines achieved **0%**.
- Autonomous policy switching, guided by the Transition Feasibility Function, improves long-horizon task success by over **30%**, enabling dynamic recovery from failures and bypassing redundant stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bi-directional optimization is crucial for effectively chaining dexterous policies, enabling earlier sub-policies to anticipate and prepare for future task requirements.
> - A dedicated Transition Feasibility Function, trained to predict the success of subsequent policies, significantly outperforms general value functions for robust policy chaining.
> - Incorporating temporal information (e.g., a sequence of states) when assessing transition feasibility improves the system's ability to handle dynamic dexterous motions and complex state spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a critical challenge in robotics: enabling robots, especially those with high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) dexterous hands, to perform **long-horizon manipulation tasks**. These tasks are characterized by a sequence of distinct sub-goals, requiring seamless transitions between different skills. The paper explicitly positions itself within several overlapping research areas:

- **Dexterous Manipulation:** Prior work in dexterous manipulation has made significant strides in single-stage skills such as grasping, object re-orientation, or isolated in-hand manipulation tasks (e.g., Rubik's Cube solving). However, these studies often stop short of integrating these individual skills into a cohesive sequence for multi-stage tasks. The challenge lies in the high-dimensional action space of dexterous hands and the complex, contact-rich dynamics involved. This paper aims to bridge this gap by enabling the *chaining* of such versatile, single-stage dexterous policies.
- **Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation:** The general problem of long-horizon tasks in robotics is often tackled by decomposing them into simpler, reusable subtasks. Established methods include hierarchical reinforcement learning, learning from demonstrations, or pre-defined behavioral primitives. While these approaches help manage complexity, they often do not specifically account for the unique challenges posed by dexterous hands, such as the expansive state space and the precise coordination required between subtasks. Even with high-level plans, smooth transitions between dexterous policies remain a significant hurdle.
- **Skill-Chaining/Policy Composition:** This sub-field focuses on how to link multiple learned policies or skills. Traditional skill-chaining methods often involve updating a subsequent policy based on the terminal states of a preceding one (forward initiation). Some approaches introduce discriminators to regularize the state space between skills (e.g., T-STAR). However, a key limitation of many existing methods is their *uni-directional* nature, where optimization primarily focuses on adjacent skills. This paper identifies a crucial gap: the influence of long-term goals on *earlier, non-adjacent* policies is often neglected. The "complex coordination between non-adjacent tasks" (e.g., orienting a block for *later* insertion, not just immediate grasping) is a central problem this work aims to solve.

"Sequential Dexterity" distinguishes itself by introducing a **bi-directional optimization framework** for policy chaining. This framework moves beyond uni-directional approaches by allowing the "backpropagation" of long-term goals to influence earlier policies. By focusing on dexterous hands and enabling robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, the work aims to advance the state-of-the-art in practical, real-world complex robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **general system for chaining multiple dexterous manipulation policies to achieve long-horizon task goals.** The authors seek to overcome the inherent challenges of high-dimensional action spaces and complex compositional dynamics that arise when combining individual dexterous skills.

The motivation stems from the inherent capabilities and untapped potential of **dexterous hands**. Unlike simpler grippers, dexterous hands possess remarkable adaptability and versatility, theoretically capable of seamlessly transitioning between different modes of functionality (e.g., searching, orienting, grasping, inserting) without the need for re-grasping or external tools. This ability mirrors human-level dexterity and is crucial for many real-world tasks. However, fully leveraging this potential for complex, sequential tasks remains an "outstanding challenge."

Specifically, the paper aims to:
- Enable dexterous robots to perform complex, multi-stage tasks (e.g., building a block structure from a pile of blocks, precise tool positioning).
- Address the "out-of-distribution" issue where a subtask policy might fail if its starting state, generated by a preceding policy, deviates significantly from its training distribution.
- Introduce a mechanism for "comprehensive optimization of the entire skill chain," ensuring that earlier sub-policies strategically set up the conditions for success in later stages, rather than just optimizing for immediate success.
- Develop a system that can autonomously manage transitions between policies, including recovery from failures and bypassing redundant stages, thereby increasing robustness.
- Demonstrate generalization to novel objects and robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real world, a critical step towards practical robotic deployment.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of dexterous hands for truly complex, human-like manipulation tasks by developing a systematic way to compose and coordinate individual learned skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

"Sequential Dexterity" proposes a novel bi-directional optimization process named **Sequential Dexterity** to chain dexterous policies for long-horizon tasks. The core components and their interactions are as follows:

#### 3.1. Task Decomposition and Learning Dexterous Sub-policies

The first step involves decomposing a complex long-horizon task into a K-step sequence of simpler sub-tasks, G = (g1, g2..., gK). Each sub-task is then learned individually using a standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithm, specifically **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**.

- **Forward Initialization:** A crucial observation is that the successful end state of a preceding sub-task (π_i-1) naturally provides plausible initial states (ρ_i) for training the succeeding policy (π_i). This avoids the difficulty of randomly sampling valid initial states for high-dimensional dexterous manipulation. The framework sequentially trains sub-policies in chronological order. After training each π_i, successful terminal states {s_i^T} are collected and used to define the initial state distribution ρ_i+1 for training π_i+1.

#### 3.2. Policy Chaining with Transition Feasibility Function (Novelty)

Forward initialization alone is insufficient because a preceding policy (π_i-1) might reach a termination state that its successor (π_i) cannot successfully handle. To address this, the system introduces a **backward policy fine-tuning mechanism** using a **Transition Feasibility Function (F)**.

- **Learning Transition Feasibility Function:** For each sub-policy π_i, a function F_i: S → R is learned. This function maps a "transition state" (specifically, a sequence of observation states s_[T-10:T] leading up to the end of the previous policy) to the *expected sum of rewards* that policy π_i would achieve if starting from that state. This effectively quantifies the "feasibility" of the state for the subsequent policy. A multi-head attention network is used to process the sequence of states and extract temporal information, which is deemed crucial for dynamic dexterous motions. The learning objective minimizes the squared error between the predicted feasibility and the actual accumulated reward observed from policy rollouts.
- **Backward Policy Fine-tuning:** Once F_i is trained, it serves as an **auxiliary reward component** to fine-tune the *preceding* policy π_i-1. This fine-tuning process proceeds backward through the policy chain, from π_K-1 to π_1. The reward for fine-tuning π_i-1 becomes a weighted sum of its original sub-task reward R_i-1 and the feasibility score F_i from the subsequent policy: R'_i-1 = λ1 * R_i-1 + λ2 * F_i(s_[T-10:T]). This mechanism allows the long-term goals (represented by the feasibility function of later stages) to "backpropagate" and influence the optimization of earlier policies. This addresses the "comprehensive optimization" objective by ensuring earlier policies produce states that are not just locally successful but also globally conducive to the entire task chain.

#### 3.3. Policy Switching with Transition Feasibility Function (Novelty)

During deployment, the trained Transition Feasibility Functions are repurposed as **policy-switching identifiers** to determine the appropriate timing and target policy for transitions.

- Instead of predetermined execution horizons, the system continuously evaluates the feasibility score (c_i+1_t = F_i+1(s_[t-10:t])/h_i+1) for the next potential sub-policy. A switch is triggered when c_i+1_t > 1 (where h_i+1 is a threshold based on successful task executions).
- For enhanced robustness, the system considers the entire skill chain. At each policy-switching step, it calculates feasibility scores backward from the final task (F_K, F_K-1...). The first sub-policy (starting from the final one) for which its feasibility score c_i_t > 1 is selected as the *next policy to execute*. This allows for dynamic recovery from unexpected failures (by switching back to an earlier, more feasible policy) and bypassing redundant stages (by jumping forward if a sub-task is implicitly completed).

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **RL Reward:** The backward fine-tuning mechanism alleviates the burden of complex reward shaping for individual sub-policies, as the long-term goal information is implicitly transmitted via the feasibility function. Simple sub-task rewards (e.g., lifted object for grasping) are sufficient.
- **State-Action Space:** The state space incorporates proprioception (joint positions/velocities), motor tactile information, and 6D object poses relative to a wrist-mounted camera. For real-world deployment, policies are distilled using a teacher-student framework to abstract away difficult-to-estimate observations. The action space includes 16-dimensional hand joints and 3D wrist translation. A motion-planning-based operational space controller (OSC) handles large movements, transferring control to the learned policy for fine manipulation near the target.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive domain randomization is applied during simulation training (robot mass/friction, joint limits/stiffness/damping, object mass/friction/scale, observation/action noise, gravity) to improve sim-to-real transfer.
- **Real-world System:** A Franka Emika robot arm with an Allegro Hand is used. Object pose and segmentation are obtained via top-down and wrist-mounted RGB-D cameras using standard computer vision techniques (Xmem, Densefusion). A scripted pressing motion is added for the final insertion step in the real world due to simulation challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the superior performance of Sequential Dexterity across two challenging long-horizon dexterous manipulation tasks: "Building Blocks" (a 4-stage task) and "Tool Positioning" (a 2-stage task).

#### 4.1. Bi-directional Optimization is Key for Chaining Dexterous Policies

- In both simulation tasks (Building Blocks and Tool Positioning), "Ours" (with bi-directional optimization) significantly outperforms prior uni-directional skill-chaining methods (V-Chain, Policy-Seq, T-STAR). For Building Blocks, "Ours" achieves a 46% success rate, compared to T-STAR's 19% (a 27% absolute improvement). Similar trends are observed in Tool Positioning (60% for "Ours" vs. 37% for T-STAR).
- A visualization of the Transition Feasibility Function's output for the Grasp sub-policy in Building Blocks (Fig. 5b) reveals that "Ours" encourages grasping the block with its studs facing up, which is optimal for the subsequent insertion task. In contrast, T-STAR, a uni-directional method, suggests many states where studs face horizontally, making insertion challenging. This visually confirms the effectiveness of backward fine-tuning in achieving global task objectives.

#### 4.2. Transition Feasibility Function Significantly Improves Performance

- Models utilizing the Transition Feasibility Function (Ours and Ours w/o temporal) show a substantial improvement (over 30% in task success rate) compared to using the PPO-trained value function (V-chain). This highlights that a dedicated feasibility function, explicitly trained to predict the success of subsequent policies, is more effective for policy chaining than a general value function.

#### 4.3. Temporal Inputs Facilitate Handling High-Dimensional State Spaces

- In the Building Blocks task, "Ours" (which incorporates temporal information by feeding a sequence of 10 history states to the feasibility function) outperforms "Ours w/o temporal" by 8% (46% vs 38%). This indicates that extracting velocity and temporal information is crucial for accurately assessing the feasibility of dynamic dexterous motions and improving chaining results. An ablation study (Appendix Tab. 9) confirms that 10-15 history steps are ideal.

#### 4.4. Ability to Switch Sub-policy Autonomously is Essential

- The system's autonomous policy-switching capability, driven by the Transition Feasibility Functions, drastically improves long-horizon task success. Allowing even a few (e.g., three) policy-switching times can increase task success rate by over 30% (Fig. 5a). This confirms the importance of dynamic decision-making for recovery from failures or bypassing redundant stages, leading to more robust execution.

#### 4.5. Real-World Results and Generalization

- "Sequential Dexterity" demonstrates impressive zero-shot transfer from simulation to a real-world Franka Emika robot with an Allegro Hand. In the challenging "Building Blocks" task, "Ours" achieves a 33% success rate for building a double-block structure, while all other baselines achieve 0%. This significant gap underscores the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed framework in physical deployment.
- The system also shows generalization capabilities to novel object shapes, successfully manipulating unseen blocks and tools in both simulation and real-world tests, validating the effectiveness of domain randomization and the learned skills.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Sequential Dexterity" represents a significant advance in robotic manipulation, particularly for complex tasks involving dexterous hands. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Pioneering Long-Horizon Dexterous Manipulation:** This work is highlighted as the "first to explore policy chaining for long-horizon dexterous manipulation." This establishes a new benchmark and research direction, moving beyond isolated dexterous skills towards comprehensive, multi-stage tasks that are closer to real-world applications.
- **General Bi-directional Optimization Framework:** The proposed bi-directional optimization scheme, centered around the Transition Feasibility Function, is a core conceptual contribution. It provides a robust and effective method for composing sequential policies by allowing global objectives to influence local skill learning. The authors suggest its potential applicability "beyond dexterous manipulation," including tasks for bimanual robots, which could open new avenues for complex cooperative manipulation.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Autonomy:** The intelligent policy-switching mechanism, leveraging the learned feasibility functions, significantly improves task robustness by enabling autonomous recovery from failures and efficient bypassing of redundant steps. This adaptive control paradigm is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, unpredictable real-world environments.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Achieving state-of-the-art results in multi-stage dexterous manipulation tasks, coupled with successful zero-shot transfer to a real-world robot, validates the practical utility and scalability of the approach. This pushes the frontier of sim-to-real research, a critical bottleneck for robot learning.
- **Reduced Reward Shaping Burden:** By allowing the backward fine-tuning mechanism to propagate long-term goals, the framework alleviates the need for meticulously hand-crafting sub-task rewards, simplifying the overall learning process. This is a practical benefit for robot learning engineers.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges a few limitations:
- Difficulty in simulating contact-rich insertion processes, necessitating a manually designed pressing motion in the real world. This points to ongoing challenges in high-fidelity physics simulation for complex contacts.
- Limited observed improvement from motor tactile sensors. This suggests future research could explore more advanced sensor-based tactile signals for richer contact information.

Despite these limitations, "Sequential Dexterity" lays a strong foundation for future research in generalizable, robust, and truly dexterous robotic manipulation. Its bi-directional chaining approach and the use of the Transition Feasibility Function are powerful concepts that could inspire similar advancements in various domains of sequential robot skill acquisition and deployment.
