# Real-Time Iteration Scheme for Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> A new Real-time Iteration Scheme for Diffusion Policy (RTI-DP) significantly reduces inference latency for diffusion-based robotic policies by orders of magnitude without requiring model retraining. The method enables real-time performance and often surpasses full-step denoising policies in task success across various manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Diffusion policies require hundreds of iterative denoising steps, leading to high inference latency unsuitable for real-time robotic tasks like contact-rich or high-speed manipulations.
- Existing acceleration methods for diffusion policies, such as distillation or architectural redesign, typically involve high computational costs for retraining or can compromise policy quality and stability.
- The slow inference time limits the responsiveness of diffusion-based control, resulting in sluggish robot movements and hindering practical deployment in real-world scenarios.

## Method

- The **Real-Time** ==Iteration Scheme== for Diffusion Policy (RTI-DP) warm-starts the denoising process for subsequent time steps using action sequences predicted in the previous step, applying only a reduced number of denoising steps.
- It leverages the spatiotemporal consistency inherent in physical systems, where actions and states evolve smoothly, making prior predictions effective initial guesses for current ones.
- A scaling-based mechanism is introduced to robustly handle discrete actions within the continuous denoising framework, either by clipping for pre-trained policies (`RTI-DP-clip`) or preprocessing training data (`RTI-DP-scale`).

## Results

- RTI-DP achieved order-of-magnitude speedups, reducing prediction times from hundreds/thousands of milliseconds to tens of milliseconds (e.g., 25 ms for state-based Push-T, 36 ms for image-based Push-T).
- The method consistently matched or exceeded the performance of standard Diffusion Policy with full denoising, for instance, `RTI-DP-scale` achieved a **1.00**/**0.93** (Max/Avg) score for state-based Push-T compared to DP's **0.92**/**0.91**.
- RTI-DP demonstrated superior performance over other state-of-the-art acceleration methods like Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP) and Consistency Policy (CP) in balancing inference speed with task success rates across diverse benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical systems exhibit strong spatiotemporal consistency, allowing previous action predictions to serve as highly informative warm starts for subsequent real-time control iterations.
> - The reverse diffusion process demonstrates local contractivity, theoretically ensuring that errors from warm-started initial guesses diminish exponentially, leading to high-quality action predictions even with truncated denoising steps.
> - Optimal control principles, specifically the Real-Time Iteration scheme, can be effectively adapted to accelerate generative models like diffusion policies, bridging the gap between powerful planning capabilities and real-time execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of robotic manipulation, generative models (specifically diffusion models), and real-time optimal control. The broader landscape of robotic control has seen significant advancements with the advent of deep learning, and more recently, diffusion models have emerged as a powerful tool for learning complex visuomotor policies.

**Background:** Diffusion models, such as the widely influential Diffusion Policy (DP), have demonstrated remarkable success in robotic manipulation tasks due to their ability to model intricate multimodal action distributions and their robust training stability. They excel in improving control, adaptability, and generalization across diverse and complex scenarios.

**Existing Challenges:** Despite their impressive performance, a major drawback of standard diffusion models in robotics is their slow inference time. This slowness stems from the iterative denoising process, which typically requires hundreds of steps to refine an action prediction from a pure Gaussian noise distribution. This prolonged inference latency severely restricts their applicability in real-world, latency-critical robotic tasks, such as contact-rich manipulations or high-speed operations that demand continuous and timely corrections. The inability to react quickly results in sluggish robot movements, reduces productivity, and limits the responsiveness of diffusion-based policies compared to human performance, hindering their practical deployment.

**Prior Attempts at Acceleration and Their Limitations:**
- **Distillation Techniques:** Methods like Consistency Policy (CP) aim to condense the iterative denoising into fewer steps, often a single step, for faster inference. While effective in speeding up, these approaches typically involve high computational costs during training, potential reductions in policy quality or diversity, and can suffer from unstable training.
- **Alternative Policy Structures:** Other research, such as Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP), focuses on redesigning the policy architecture itself to facilitate faster decision-making. While potentially effective, these methods often necessitate complete retraining of the model, which is computationally expensive and resource-intensive, especially for large, pre-trained robotic models.
- **Reduced Denoising Steps (DDIM, EDM):** Techniques like Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) and Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models (EDM) allow models to be evaluated with fewer denoising steps during inference. However, reducing steps in these methods often compromises sample quality, affecting the robustness and precision of the generated actions.

**The Gap This Work Fills:** The current paper addresses the critical need for a solution that accelerates diffusion policy inference without incurring the costs of additional training, distillation, or policy redesign. By focusing on leveraging the spatiotemporal consistency inherent in physical systems and drawing inspiration from optimal control, this work proposes a method that can be seamlessly integrated into *pre-trained* diffusion models, thereby making high-performance robotic policies practical for real-time applications without substantial re-engineering efforts. This significantly broadens the real-world applicability of diffusion-based control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly reduce the inference time of diffusion-based policies** in robotic manipulation tasks, thereby overcoming a major bottleneck that limits their real-world applicability. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Enabling Real-Time Performance:** The hundreds of denoising steps traditionally required by diffusion models result in high latency, making them unsuitable for real-time, latency-critical tasks such such as contact-rich or high-speed manipulations. The authors aim to achieve a drastic reduction in prediction time to enable high-frequency control, preventing errors and ensuring smooth, responsive robot movements.
2. **Minimizing Computational Overhead:** A key motivation is to achieve this acceleration *without* requiring additional training, computationally expensive distillation processes, or a complete redesign of the policy architecture. This is particularly crucial for large, resource-demanding robotic models, where retraining is often prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. The goal is to provide a "plug-and-play" solution for existing pre-trained models.
3. **Maintaining Performance and Quality:** While accelerating inference, the authors are motivated to ensure that the policy's overall performance, including task success rates, precision, and the ability to handle multimodal action distributions, remains comparable to or even surpasses that of standard diffusion policies with full denoising. This balance between speed and quality is paramount for practical deployment.
4. **Leveraging Spatiotemporal Consistency:** The work is motivated by the insight that physical systems exhibit inherent spatiotemporal consistency—actions and states evolve smoothly and with bounded changes over time. This property suggests that previous predictions can serve as highly informative initial guesses for subsequent steps, an idea central to optimal control methods.
5. **Addressing Discrete Actions:** Robotic tasks often involve discrete actions (e.g., grasping). The authors aim to develop a robust mechanism to handle these abrupt transitions within their continuous-action-focused framework, ensuring broad applicability.
6. **Providing Theoretical Foundations:** A further objective is to provide theoretical conditions, specifically regarding local contractivity, to explain why the proposed scheme works effectively with fewer denoising steps and to offer guidance for optimally selecting the number of initial denoising steps.
7. **Achieving Fully Closed-Loop Control:** Traditional diffusion policies often operate in a partially closed-loop manner, predicting a chunk of actions from a single observation. By accelerating inference, the authors aim to enable a truly fully closed-loop system where a single action is predicted and executed per time step, allowing for continuous feedback and adaptation.

In essence, the research is motivated by the practical need to bridge the gap between the powerful capabilities of diffusion models and the stringent real-time demands of real-world robotic applications, doing so in a computationally efficient and non-invasive manner for existing models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, named Real-Time Iteration for Diffusion Policy (RTI-DP), is inspired by the Real-Time Iteration (RTI) scheme from optimal control. This scheme significantly accelerates iterative optimization by leveraging solutions from previous time steps as initial guesses for subsequent iterations.

**Core Approach (Real-Time Iteration Scheme):**
1. **Initial Prediction (First Action Chunk):** At the very beginning of a task (t=0), the first action sequence (chunk) is computed using the standard Diffusion Policy with its *full number of denoising steps*. This initial computation can be performed offline or at the start of execution, as its latency is not critical for the subsequent real-time loop.
2. **Iterative Refinement (Subsequent Action Chunks):** For all subsequent time steps (t > 0), instead of starting the denoising process from pure Gaussian noise, RTI-DP utilizes the action sequence predicted in the *previous time step* as an informed initial guess. On top of this warm-started guess, only a *reduced number of denoising steps* are applied to refine the prediction for the current time step.
3. **Action Sequence Update:** After executing the first action of the predicted chunk, the action sequence is updated by removing the executed action and appending a newly predicted action at the end. This 'sliding window' approach, combined with warm-starting, facilitates efficient and continuous policy adaptation, as illustrated in Figure 1 and detailed in Algorithm 1.

**Underlying Principle: Spatiotemporal Consistency:**
The effectiveness of RTI-DP hinges on the fundamental property of **spatiotemporal consistency** in physical systems. Robotic actions and states typically exhibit continuity and bounded changes over time. This means that an action performed at time `t` will be closely related to the action at `t+1`, ensuring that the previous prediction serves as an excellent "warm start" or initial guess for the current one. This consistency allows the reverse diffusion process to quickly converge to a high-quality prediction with significantly fewer denoising steps.

**Handling Discrete Action Space:**
A challenge arises when dealing with discrete actions (e.g., a binary grasp command), as these can violate the assumption of smooth, bounded changes. To address this, RTI-DP introduces a scaling-based method:
- **`RTI-DP-clip` (for pre-trained policies):** For policies that are already trained, discrete actions within the initial guess are directly scaled down by a fixed factor (e.g., dividing by 10). This transforms the discrete values into a continuous, manageable range, allowing the diffusion model to interpret and denoise them more smoothly, even if the policy was not originally trained with scaled discrete actions.
- **`RTI-DP-scale` (for retrainable tasks):** For tasks where some retraining or data preprocessing is feasible, a more robust approach is to preprocess the discrete actions in the *training dataset* itself using a scaling factor. This ensures that the diffusion model learns to associate the scaled continuous representation with the original discrete commands from the outset, leading to more robust performance in handling discrete actions. It should be noted that while the paper's core premise is "no retraining," this variant implies a specific data preprocessing and subsequent training.

**Theoretical Justification: Local Contractivity:**
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the scheme's effectiveness, deriving conditions for local contractivity in the denoising process.
- **Proof Sketch:** Assuming the learned denoising function is L-Lipschitz continuous and using well-behaved noise schedules, the mean of the reverse diffusion process is also Lipschitz continuous. This leads to a contractive property, meaning that if the initial estimate `˜A_K'` at an intermediate step `K'` is close to the true noisy sample `A_K'`, then the error `||˜A_k - A_k||` will shrink exponentially as the reverse process progresses from `K'` down to 0.
- **Implications:** This contractivity guarantees stability, ensuring that small initialization errors do not accumulate but rather diminish, and consistency, meaning the process converges to the true underlying action `A_0`. This theoretical foundation provides insights for estimating the optimal initial denoising step `K'`, balancing speed and prediction quality.

By combining an informed warm-starting strategy, a mechanism for discrete actions, and a theoretical understanding of its convergence properties, RTI-DP provides a robust and efficient approach to accelerating diffusion-based robotic policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RTI-DP across a diverse set of simulation benchmarks demonstrates its significant effectiveness in accelerating inference while maintaining comparable, and in some cases superior, performance to traditional diffusion policies. The experiments address both image-based and state-based control in tasks of varying complexity and horizons.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Substantial Reduction in Inference Time:** The most prominent finding is the drastic reduction in prediction time achieved by RTI-DP.
 - For state-based tasks (Table I), RTI-DP-clip achieved prediction times as low as 25 ms (Push-T), a massive improvement compared to Diffusion Policy (DP) which took 816 ms. RTI-DP-scale also showed similar impressive speeds (e.g., 25 ms for Square).
 - For image-based tasks (Table II), RTI-DP-clip similarly reduced inference time to 36 ms (Push-T) compared to DP's 1008 ms. Consistency Policy (CP) was faster in some cases (e.g., 18 ms for Push-T), but often at the cost of performance.
 - The results for Multimodal Block Pushing (Table III) also showed a significant speedup, with RTI-DP achieving 25 ms compared to DP-DDPM's 818 ms.
 - Overall, RTI-DP achieved order-of-magnitude speedups, consistently reducing inference time to tens of milliseconds from hundreds or even over a thousand milliseconds.

2. **Comparable or Superior Performance:** Despite the dramatic speedup, RTI-DP consistently maintained performance comparable to, and in several cases, *surpassed* the Diffusion Policy with full-step denoising.
 - In state-based Push-T, RTI-DP-clip achieved a score of 0.95/0.91 (Max/Avg), outperforming DP's 0.92/0.91. RTI-DP-scale further improved this to 1.00/0.93.
 - In the Multimodal Block Pushing task, RTI-DP (0.54/0.45 for p1 and 0.24/0.19 for p2) outperformed DP-DDPM (0.44/0.37 for p1 and 0.18/0.17 for p2), highlighting the advantage of fully closed-loop control enabled by faster inference.
 - For several Robomimic tasks (Can, Lift), RTI-DP matched DP's perfect or near-perfect scores (e.g., 1.00/0.99 for Can and Lift with RTI-DP-clip, matching DP).
 - This indicates that the warm-starting mechanism effectively guides the denoising process to high-quality action sequences even with truncated steps.

3. **Superiority Over Other Acceleration Methods:** RTI-DP demonstrated advantages over other state-of-the-art inference acceleration methods like Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP) and Consistency Policy (CP).
 - While SDP offered speedups, its performance often degraded, notably in image-based Tool-Hang where SDP scored a mere 0.02/0.01 compared to DP's 0.86/0.82 and RTI-DP-clip's 0.66/0.62.
 - CP, while fast, also showed lower scores in several tasks (e.g., 0.69/0.65 for image-based Push-T vs. RTI-DP-clip's 0.88/0.86).
 - RTI-DP thus achieves a more favorable balance between speed and performance.

4. **Effectiveness in Continuous and Discrete Action Spaces:**
 - The approach demonstrated a clear advantage in tasks with continuous action spaces (Push-T, BlockPush), validating the method's reliance on the continuity of physical systems.
 - For tasks involving discrete actions, the `RTI-DP-scale` variant (which preprocesses the dataset with scaling) consistently outperformed `RTI-DP-clip` (which scales directly on initial guesses for pre-trained policies). `RTI-DP-scale` often achieved effective denoising within just three steps, indicating that integrating the scaling at the dataset level leads to more robust performance. While `RTI-DP-clip` showed slightly lower performance, it still provided significant speedups for pre-trained policies without requiring retraining.

In summary, the experimental results unequivocally support the claims that RTI-DP offers a powerful solution for real-time robotic control, delivering unprecedented inference speeds for diffusion policies without sacrificing accuracy, and often enhancing overall control performance due to its ability to enable truly closed-loop feedback.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Real-Time Iteration Scheme for Diffusion Policy (RTI-DP) presents a highly significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, with broad potential impacts across research and practical applications.

1. **Bridging the Gap to Real-World Robotics:** The most significant contribution of RTI-DP is its ability to overcome the major obstacle of slow inference in diffusion-based policies. By drastically reducing prediction latency, it transforms powerful but computationally intensive diffusion models into practical tools for real-time, latency-critical robotic applications such as contact-rich tasks, high-speed manipulations, and continuous feedback control. This directly addresses a bottleneck that has limited the widespread adoption of diffusion policies in industrial and service robotics.
2. **Compatibility with Pre-trained Models:** A critical advantage of RTI-DP is that it can be seamlessly integrated with *existing pre-trained diffusion-based models* without requiring additional training, expensive distillation, or architectural redesign. This "plug-and-play" capability is invaluable, especially for large-scale, resource-demanding models, as it avoids the prohibitive computational costs and time associated with retraining. This makes the benefits of accelerated inference immediately accessible to a vast ecosystem of existing diffusion policy models.
3. **Enhanced Control Performance:** In several tasks, RTI-DP not only matched but surpassed the performance of full-step denoising diffusion policies. This suggests that enabling faster, truly closed-loop control (one action per prediction) can lead to more responsive and effective robot behavior, avoiding the potential for mode oscillation or commitment to suboptimal long action chunks generated by slower, partially closed-loop methods.
4. **Theoretical Foundation:** The provided theoretical conditions for local contractivity offer valuable insights into why the method works and provide a principled basis for potentially automating the selection of optimal denoising steps, moving beyond empirical tuning.
5. **Handling Discrete Actions:** The scaling-based approach for discrete actions makes RTI-DP applicable to a wider range of manipulation tasks, including those involving grasping or other binary commands, ensuring its versatility.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Wider Adoption of Diffusion Models:** By making diffusion policies real-time capable, RTI-DP will accelerate their adoption in various robotic domains, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics and exploration, where responsiveness and efficiency are paramount.
2. **Reduced Hardware Requirements:** Faster inference means that high-performance diffusion policies could potentially be deployed on less powerful, more cost-effective GPUs or edge devices. This could democratize access to advanced robotic capabilities and lower the barrier to entry for many applications, particularly in resource-constrained environments.
3. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for further research into adaptive step selection strategies for denoising, integrating optimized initialization with other generative models (e.g., flow-based models), and exploring one-step denoising without architectural changes.
4. **Influence on Optimal Control:** By drawing a parallel between diffusion models and Real-Time Iteration schemes from optimal control, the paper highlights potential synergies between these fields, fostering interdisciplinary research that could yield further innovations.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

- **Empirical Tuning of Denoising Steps:** While theoretical guidelines are provided, the choice of the number of denoising steps (K') still requires some empirical tuning, particularly in environments with significant or unpredictable noise. Future work could focus on automating this selection process dynamically based on environmental feedback or uncertainty.
- **Sensitivity to Action Changes:** The method's reliance on spatiotemporal consistency implies that it performs best when demonstrated motions exhibit low accelerations and relatively constant velocities. Tasks requiring sudden, abrupt changes in actions (e.g., highly dynamic maneuvers) might require more denoising steps or specialized handling. The quality of teleoperated data, specifically its smoothness, can be a limiting factor.
- **`RTI-DP-scale` vs. "No Retraining":** While the paper emphasizes "no retraining," the `RTI-DP-scale` variant involves preprocessing the dataset and subsequently training the diffusion model on this modified data. This is a subtle nuance; while it doesn't involve "policy redesign" in terms of architecture, it's not strictly a "plug-and-play" for *any* pre-trained model unless the original training incorporated similar data scaling. The `RTI-DP-clip` variant, however, fully aligns with the "no retraining" objective for off-the-shelf policies.
- **Beyond Diffusion Models:** The authors suggest that similar improvements could extend to flow-based models with denoising components, pointing towards a broader impact on generative models in control.

In conclusion, RTI-DP is a timely and impactful contribution that significantly enhances the practical viability of diffusion policies in robotics. By offering a clever and effective solution to the inference speed problem, it moves these powerful AI models closer to widespread adoption in real-world robotic systems.
