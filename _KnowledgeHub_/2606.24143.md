# AsyncOPD: How Stale Can On-Policy Distillation Be?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced AsyncOPD, an asynchronous pipeline for on-policy distillation (OPD) that efficiently trains large language models (LLMs) for reasoning tasks, addressing the challenge of stale data and finite teacher-score caches. This system delivers up to a 3.8x throughput speedup over synchronous methods while maintaining model accuracy.

## Problem

- Synchronous on-policy distillation (OPD) for LLMs suffers from an 'on-policy systems bottleneck,' where learners are idle while waiting for computationally intensive rollout generation.
- Asynchronous training, while improving efficiency, introduces 'stale-policy data,' meaning rollouts are generated by older policies, potentially degrading model performance.
- OPD faces a 'teacher-cache constraint' where finite caches for teacher scores lead to missing feedback for relevant actions due to the prohibitive cost of full-vocabulary logits.

## Method

- A systematic study was conducted on how staleness impacts OPD, including an analysis of KL divergence directions and various policy-gradient surrogates.
- Optimized OPD estimator designs were developed to handle ==finite teacher-score caches==, with a multi-sample Monte Carlo approach proposed to reduce variance.
- **AsyncOPD**, a fully asynchronous training pipeline, was implemented to decouple and overlap rollout generation, teacher scoring, and learner updates, maximizing resource utilization.

## Results

- AsyncOPD achieved substantial training throughput speedups, ranging from **1.6x** to **3.8x**, compared to conventional synchronous OPD methods.
- The asynchronous pipeline successfully maintained or even improved final model accuracy on reasoning benchmarks while significantly reducing wall-clock training time.
- Multi-sample Monte Carlo was shown to be superior to stale sparse top-k for cached supports, effectively reducing variance and improving performance under increased staleness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Forward KL divergence exhibits greater robustness to stale data than reverse KL because its teacher-weighted nature is less sensitive to outdated student action choices.
> - For reverse KL, recomputing the advantage term using the current student policy at learner time, without PPO-style clipping, proves to be the most effective strategy against staleness.
> - Multi-sample Monte Carlo with importance sampling effectively manages finite teacher-cache constraints and high variance in asynchronous OPD by leveraging and averaging multiple teacher-scored action samples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of large language model (LLM) post-training, specifically focusing on on-policy distillation (OPD) and its relationship with reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. OPD and RL are increasingly employed to enhance LLM capabilities, particularly in reasoning tasks such as mathematics and coding.

A recognized challenge in on-policy training methods, including both OPD and RL, is the "on-policy systems bottleneck." This bottleneck arises because each learner update must await fresh rollouts from the currently evolving model. For computationally intensive reasoning workloads, rollout generation often consumes a significant portion of training time, leading to underutilization of learner resources.

Asynchronous training pipelines have emerged in RL as a strategy to mitigate this bottleneck by decoupling rollout generation from learner updates. This decoupling allows rollout workers to continuously generate data while the learner processes earlier rollouts, thereby improving training efficiency and hardware utilization. However, this asynchronous execution introduces the problem of "stale-policy data," where the data used for learning is generated by an older version of the policy. While the implications of stale data have been investigated in asynchronous RL, their effects within the context of OPD have remained largely unexplored.

This paper addresses this gap by presenting a systematic study of staleness in asynchronous OPD. It specifically considers a practical setting where teacher feedback relies on local KL losses, and the high cost of storing or transferring full-vocabulary teacher logits necessitates the use of finite teacher-score caches. The work aims to understand how existing asynchronous RL solutions translate to OPD and how OPD-specific constraints (like limited teacher caches) influence estimator design. By developing and open-sourcing AsyncOPD, the research provides a concrete framework that extends asynchronous principles to OPD, aiming to improve training throughput while maintaining model accuracy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized inefficiency inherent in synchronous on-policy distillation (OPD) for large language models (LLMs). Training LLMs for reasoning tasks requires generating lengthy and complex rollouts, which is a computationally intensive process. In a synchronous setup, the learner must idly wait for these rollouts to complete before proceeding with model updates, leading to underutilization of computational resources and prolonged training times.

To address this "on-policy systems bottleneck," asynchronous training pipelines, analogous to those in reinforcement learning (RL), are proposed. These pipelines decouple the data generation (rollout) phase from the model update (learner) phase, allowing them to operate in parallel. While this approach enhances training efficiency and hardware utilization, it introduces a new challenge: "stale-policy data." The rollouts processed by the learner are generated by an older version of the student policy, and learning from such outdated data can potentially degrade the quality of the trained model.

A critical distinction in OPD, compared to traditional RL, is the nature of teacher feedback. OPD often employs local Kullback-Leibler (KL) losses, which necessitate teacher scores over actions for student-visited prefixes. Storing or transferring full-vocabulary teacher logits in an asynchronous, distributed pipeline is prohibitively expensive. Consequently, teacher scores are typically cached only for a finite, sparse set of actions. This "teacher-cache constraint" poses unique challenges for OPD, as the learner might need teacher scores for actions not present in the cached data, which cannot be recovered without additional teacher access.

Given these motivations and constraints, the research sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

- **Systematically Study Staleness in Asynchronous OPD:** To conduct the first comprehensive investigation into how stale-policy data affects the training dynamics and performance of OPD in an asynchronous setting, with a particular focus on the practical teacher-cache constraint.
- **Analyze the Impact of KL Direction:** To determine how the choice between forward-KL and reverse-KL objectives influences the robustness of OPD to stale rollouts, considering their respective teacher-weighted and student-weighted characteristics.
- **Evaluate Asynchronous RL Solutions for OPD:** To assess whether policy-gradient surrogates and stale-data mitigation techniques developed for asynchronous RL (e.g., PPO-style clipping, Decoupled PPO, M2PO) are effective in addressing staleness in OPD, or if OPD-specific solutions are required.
- **Optimize OPD Estimator Design for Finite Caches:** To understand how the finite teacher-score cache constraint affects the bias-variance trade-off for sparse (e.g., top-k) and sampled (e.g., Monte Carlo) OPD estimators, and to propose improved estimator designs that maintain correctness while reducing variance.
- **Develop and Validate an Asynchronous OPD Training Pipeline:** To instantiate the findings into a fully asynchronous OPD training system, AsyncOPD, and empirically demonstrate its ability to improve training throughput and hardware utilization while maintaining comparable model accuracy.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to provide a robust and efficient framework for post-training LLMs using OPD, overcoming the existing systems bottleneck and making the approach more scalable and practical for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a systematic experimental approach to investigate staleness in asynchronous OPD, focusing on LLM post-training for reasoning tasks.

#### 3.1. Experimental Setup

- **Student Models:** Experiments primarily used Qwen3-4B-Base models for staleness and estimator studies. Additional AsyncOPD experiments also included Qwen3-1.7B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base, as well as their "thinking-disabled" variants (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B).
- **Teacher Model:** A larger Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 model was used as the teacher.
- **Training Data:** The DeepMath dataset, filtered to 57,630 mathematical problems with a difficulty level of at least 6, served as the training corpus. Prompt and response lengths were set to 2,048 and 16,384 tokens, respectively.
- **Training Configuration:** Training was conducted for 200 iterations, with each iteration comprising four mini-batch optimizer updates. The optimization batch size was 256, and the mini-batch size was 64. An AdamW-style optimizer with a learning rate of 3 × 10⁻⁶ (constant schedule) and weight decay of 0.01 was utilized. Rollout generation used a temperature of 1.0, top-p=1.0, and no top-k truncation.
- **Evaluation:** Model accuracy was assessed by generating 32 samples per problem (temperature 1.0) and calculating Avg@32 on AIME24, AIME25, and AMC datasets.
- **Hardware and Implementation:** All experiments were performed on a single 8×B200 node. vLLM was used for efficient rollout generation and teacher scoring, while PyTorch FSDP managed learner training.
- **Staleness Control:** "Staleness" was introduced as an experimental variable, defined as the number of train-batch steps (k) by which the consumed rollout cache lagged the current learner. Runs initiated by generating *k* rollout batches with the initial student snapshot. Subsequently, the oldest available batch was consumed, and a new one generated with the latest student snapshot to restore the target cache depth. This allowed for controlled prefix- and action-level staleness. The sweep covered k ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128}.

#### 3.2. Key Methodological Investigations

1. **Forward KL vs. Reverse KL Under Staleness (Section 4):**
 - The study compared the performance degradation of sparse top-k forward KL and PPO-style reverse-KL surrogates as staleness increased. This comparison aimed to understand how the teacher-weighted nature of forward KL and the student-weighted nature of reverse KL interacted with stale-policy data and finite teacher-score caches.

2. **Reverse-KL Policy-Gradient Surrogates Under Staleness (Section 5):**
 - **Ablation Study:** A 2×2 ablation investigated the impact of two design choices for the reverse-KL policy-gradient surrogate:
 - **Advantage Term:** Using the rollout-time advantage (A_old) versus recomputing the advantage under the current student (A_θ) at learner time.
 - **Clipping:** Applying PPO-style clipping (ϵ=0.2) to the importance ratio versus no clipping. This resulted in four configurations: L_clip^old, L_noclip^old, L_clip^θ, and L_noclip^θ.
 - **Comparison with Asynchronous RL Surrogates:** Decoupled PPO and M2PO, which are designed to stabilize asynchronous RL, were also evaluated to determine their applicability and effectiveness in the OPD context.

3. **Reverse-KL Cached Supports Under Staleness (Section 6):**
 - This section addressed how finite teacher-score caches influence OPD estimator design.
 - **Sparse Top-k:** Evaluated the performance of sparse top-k, both with and without reweighting on the stale top-k support, noting its potential for support mismatch.
 - **One-Sample Monte Carlo (MC):** Investigated using a single action sampled from the behavior policy (p_old), combined with old-to-current importance sampling (IS), as an unbiased estimator for the current reverse-KL gradient.
 - **Multi-Sample Monte Carlo (Proposed Solution):** Introduced and evaluated a novel approach where, at each decoding step, multiple local next-token samples (a_t,1..., a_t,m) are drawn from the behavior policy. These samples are cached, teacher-scored, and their IS-corrected gradients are averaged to reduce variance. Different values of `m` (1, 4, 16, 64) were tested to quantify variance reduction.

#### 3.3. AsyncOPD System Implementation (Section 7)

- **Scheduler Design:** AsyncOPD was developed as a fully asynchronous pipeline, overlapping rollout, teacher scoring, and learner updates. It was contrasted with:
 - **Strict Synchronous:** A conventional barrier-based approach.
 - **Two-Step-Off:** A scheduler that fixes rollout lag to two learner updates but still waits for complete rollout batches (similar to VeRL).
 - **AsyncOPD (Proposed):** A streaming approach where workers pause only for weight synchronization, preserve in-flight prefixes, and process data continuously. A queue depth parameter (τ=4) was used as a capacity bound.
- **Resource Allocation:** For two-step-off and AsyncOPD, the 8-GPU node was split: one GPU for teacher scoring, four GPUs for rollout workers, and three GPUs for the FSDP learner. Strict sync time-shared seven GPUs between rollout and training.
- **Performance Metrics:** Training throughput (tokens per second), pipeline overlap (average concurrent OPD-stage activity), and final AIME24 Avg@32 accuracy were measured.

This comprehensive methodology allowed for a systematic exploration of the challenges posed by staleness in asynchronous OPD and the development of effective mitigation strategies, culminating in the AsyncOPD system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding staleness in asynchronous OPD, its interaction with different KL objectives, and the effectiveness of various estimation and architectural choices.

#### 4.1. Forward- and Reverse-KL OPD Under Staleness

- **KL Direction Impact:** The direction of the KL divergence significantly influenced robustness to staleness. While reverse KL started with higher accuracy at zero staleness, its performance degraded more rapidly as staleness increased, eventually being surpassed by forward KL.
- **Support-Ownership Difference:** This phenomenon was attributed to the "support-ownership" difference: forward KL (teacher-weighted) is inherently less exposed to stale student action choices, as it primarily relies on the teacher's distribution. Reverse KL (student-weighted) is vulnerable because stale teacher-scored caches may not cover actions necessary for the current student policy. Consequently, the remaining analysis focused on making reverse-KL OPD more robust.

#### 4.2. Reverse-KL: Policy-Gradient Surrogates Under Staleness

- **Advantage Recomputation and Clipping:** An ablation study comparing combinations of advantage calculation (A_old vs. A_θ) and clipping found that the most effective strategy for reverse-KL OPD was to recompute the advantage term (A_θ) using the current student policy at learner time, *without* applying PPO-style clipping.
 - Clipping, while sometimes beneficial for the frozen A_old baseline by mitigating large updates from stale data, was found to be detrimental when A_θ was recomputed. This suggested that recomputing A_θ already addresses issues that clipping is designed to resolve (e.g., by reducing high-percentile importance ratio tails).
- **Advanced Asynchronous RL Surrogates:** Methods like Decoupled PPO and M2PO, designed to stabilize asynchronous RL, did not consistently outperform the simpler strategy of recomputing A_θ without clipping in the OPD context. This indicates that direct transfer of RL-specific solutions may not always be optimal for OPD.

#### 4.3. Reverse-KL: Cached Supports Under Staleness

- **Sparse Top-k Limitations:** Sparse top-k implementations, which rely on a fixed top-k support from the rollout policy, were found to be support-mismatched under staleness. When the rollout policy is old, its top-k actions may not encompass the actions relevant to the current student's top-k objective. Reweighting within this stale, fixed support could not recover the missing teacher scores, leading to limited performance improvement.
- **One-Sample Monte Carlo (MC) Effectiveness:** One-sample MC, where actions are drawn from the behavior policy (p_old) and corrected via old-to-current importance sampling (IS), demonstrated superior robustness compared to stale sparse top-k. This approach is "action-level correctable" in expectation, meaning IS can theoretically correct for stale sampling. However, it can suffer from high variance.
- **Multi-Sample MC for Variance Reduction:** To address the high variance of one-sample MC, the paper proposed and evaluated multi-sample MC. This method involves caching and teacher-scoring multiple local next-token samples from the behavior policy at each decoding step, and then averaging their IS-corrected gradients. This approach successfully preserved MC correctability while significantly reducing variance. Experimental results showed that increasing the number of samples (e.g., from m=1 to m=4, 16, or 64) led to improved performance, particularly at higher staleness levels.

#### 4.4. AsyncOPD System Performance

- **Throughput and Overlap Improvement:** AsyncOPD, a fully asynchronous pipeline built upon these estimator choices (current-policy A_θ, no clipping, old-to-current IS, and multi-sample MC), consistently achieved the highest training throughput and pipeline overlap across all tested Qwen3-Base student models and MC settings, compared to strict synchronous and two-step-off schedulers. Throughput speedups ranged from 1.6x to 3.8x over synchronous training.
- **Accuracy Maintenance:** Despite significant throughput gains, AsyncOPD maintained comparable, and sometimes even superior, final accuracy to the synchronous baseline.
- **Wall-Clock Efficiency:** The improved efficiency meant that AsyncOPD reached equivalent or higher accuracy levels earlier in wall-clock time, demonstrating practical benefits for LLM post-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of large language model (LLM) post-training, with substantial potential impact on the efficiency and scalability of advanced training techniques.

#### 5.1. Foundational Understanding of Asynchronous OPD

The paper presents the first systematic study of staleness in asynchronous on-policy distillation (OPD), addressing a previously underexplored area. By meticulously analyzing how policy lag and finite teacher-score caches affect OPD, it provides foundational insights into the unique challenges of asynchronous OPD compared to traditional asynchronous reinforcement learning (RL). This work establishes a baseline and a framework for future research in this domain.

#### 5.2. Practical Guidance for OPD Implementers

The findings offer concrete, empirically-backed recommendations for designing robust and efficient asynchronous OPD pipelines:
- **KL Direction Choice:** Demonstrating that forward KL is more robust to staleness than reverse KL provides a crucial design heuristic, especially when deep asynchronous pipelines are desired.
- **Reverse-KL Surrogate Selection:** The identification that recomputing the current advantage (A_θ) at learner time without clipping is the most effective policy-gradient surrogate for reverse-KL OPD simplifies implementation and improves performance over more complex asynchronous RL methods.
- **Estimator for Sparse Teacher Feedback:** The finding that multi-sample Monte Carlo (MC) is superior to stale sparse top-k for handling finite teacher-score caches under staleness offers a practical solution to a common constraint in LLM distillation. This specifically shows how to leverage multiple local samples to reduce variance while maintaining theoretical correctness via importance sampling.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Training Efficiency and Resource Utilization

The development and open-sourcing of AsyncOPD demonstrate a fully asynchronous training pipeline that significantly improves training throughput (up to 3.8x) and pipeline overlap compared to synchronous methods, without compromising model accuracy. This efficiency gain translates into:
- **Reduced Computational Costs:** Faster training means fewer GPU hours, making advanced LLM post-training more accessible and cost-effective.
- **Accelerated Research and Development:** Developers and researchers can iterate more quickly on new models and techniques, speeding up the pace of innovation in LLM capabilities.
- **Improved Scalability:** The asynchronous architecture is inherently more scalable, enabling the effective utilization of larger and more distributed computational resources for training increasingly complex LLMs.

#### 5.4. Contribution to LLM Development for Reasoning Tasks

By improving the efficiency of OPD, this work directly supports the development of LLMs with enhanced reasoning abilities. OPD is a key technique for distilling knowledge from powerful teachers into smaller student models, or for self-distillation, particularly for tasks like mathematics and coding where dense token-level feedback is valuable. Making this process more efficient will enable the creation of more specialized and capable LLMs for these critical applications.

#### 5.5. Open-Source Contribution

The release of AsyncOPD as open-source fosters community engagement and allows other researchers and practitioners to build upon the presented findings and system. This promotes reproducibility and accelerates collective progress in the field.

In summary, this research not only provides a deep understanding of the intricacies of staleness in asynchronous OPD but also delivers a practical, high-performance solution. Its impact is expected to be felt across the lifecycle of LLM development, from fundamental research to the deployment of more efficient and capable AI systems.
