# d1: Scaling Reasoning in Diffusion Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents `d1`, a two-stage post-training framework that applies reinforcement learning to discrete diffusion large language models (dLLMs) to enhance their reasoning abilities. The work introduces `diffu-GRPO`, a novel policy gradient algorithm for masked dLLMs, which demonstrates consistent performance improvements on mathematical, planning, and coding benchmarks, such as a +26.2% gain on Countdown for the LLaDA-8B-Instruct model.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) based on the autoregressive (AR) paradigm have substantially improved their reasoning abilities through reinforcement learning (RL) post-training, but this approach has not been explored for discrete diffusion LLMs (dLLMs).
- Adapting existing RL algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to dLLMs is challenging because their non-sequential generation process makes efficient computation of token-level and sequence-level log-probabilities difficult.
- It was unclear if dLLMs, with their iterative denoising generation, could effectively integrate and benefit from RL signals to enhance their reasoning capabilities beyond basic language modeling.

## Method

- The paper introduces ==d1==, a two-stage post-training framework for masked dLLMs that combines masked Supervised Finetuning (SFT) with a novel reinforcement learning algorithm.
- It proposes ==diffu-GRPO==, a policy gradient method specifically tailored for masked dLLMs, which utilizes an efficient ==one-step, prompt-masked log-probability estimation== to enable online RL training.
- ==Random prompt masking== is incorporated during `diffu-GRPO`'s inner gradient updates, serving as a regularization technique that allows for more frequent updates per batch, reducing costly online generations and accelerating convergence.

## Results

- `diffu-GRPO` consistently outperforms the base LLaDA-8B-Instruct model across all evaluated math and planning benchmarks, showing improvements in all **12** tested setups.
- The combined `d1` framework (Supervised Finetuning + `diffu-GRPO`) consistently yields the highest gains, outperforming the `diffu-GRPO`-only approach in **11** out of **12** setups, demonstrating synergistic benefits.
- `d1` achieves improvements of **+26.2%** on Countdown and **+10.0%** on Sudoku, and the approach generalizes effectively to multi-task settings and coding benchmarks like HumanEval and MBPP.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy gradient-based reinforcement learning, specifically `diffu-GRPO`, can be effectively applied to large discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs) for complex reasoning tasks, challenging prior assumptions about autoregressive models' unique suitability for RL-based reasoning.
> - A novel one-step, prompt-masked log-probability estimation technique enables efficient online RL training for dLLMs by circumventing the computational challenges of their non-sequential generation process.
> - Randomized prompt masking during gradient updates acts as a strong regularization, allowing for significantly more inner optimization steps per batch and reducing the need for costly online generations, thereby improving training efficiency and stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen unprecedented advancements, with models demonstrating remarkable capabilities across diverse tasks, from chatbot interactions to complex coding and reasoning. These successes have largely been driven by the "autoregressive" (AR) paradigm, where models generate text sequentially, token by token, from left to right. This paradigm, when combined with online reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, has notably enhanced reasoning abilities in leading AR LLMs such as DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi K1.5. These RL-based post-training methods allow models to explore and learn from reward signals, moving beyond static datasets and significantly improving performance on complex tasks.

In parallel, "discrete diffusion large language models" (dLLMs) have emerged as a promising alternative. Unlike AR models, dLLMs operate non-autoregressively, generating text through an iterative denoising process that refines sequences over multiple steps. This approach inherently leverages bidirectional attention, considering both past and future context simultaneously. Recent open-source dLLMs like LLaDA and closed-source models such as Mercury have achieved competitive language modeling performance, even demonstrating superior inference efficiency in some cases. However, despite their promise, leading open-source dLLMs have not yet fully explored the benefits of RL post-training, leaving a significant gap in the research landscape.

The primary challenge in adapting RL algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to dLLMs stems from their fundamental architectural differences. AR models naturally lend themselves to policy optimization because their sequential generation allows for straightforward computation of token-level and sequence-level log-probabilities via the chain rule. Denoising dLLMs, conversely, lack this sequential factorization, making direct application of standard policy gradient methods computationally prohibitive or even conceptually incompatible due to the iterative nature of their decoding process.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. It aims to demonstrate whether dLLMs can leverage the same advanced RL techniques that have propelled AR LLMs to new reasoning heights. By proposing a novel framework and adapting existing RL algorithms, the authors contribute to expanding the capabilities of dLLMs beyond basic language modeling, pushing them towards more complex reasoning tasks and potentially diversifying the architectural approaches to building powerful AI systems. This work is positioned at the intersection of generative models, reinforcement learning, and advanced reasoning, exploring how the strengths of diffusion models can be harnessed for high-level cognitive tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate and enable the application of modern reinforcement learning (RL) techniques to discrete diffusion large language models (dLLMs) to enhance their reasoning capabilities. The authors aim to demonstrate that dLLMs, despite their non-autoregressive generation paradigm, can similarly benefit from the post-training advancements that have significantly improved reasoning in traditional autoregressive (AR) LLMs.

The primary motivations driving this research are rooted in two key observations:
1. **Unexplored Potential of RL in dLLMs:** While AR LLMs have shown remarkable reasoning improvements through RL-based post-training (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Kimi K1.5), this avenue remains largely unexplored for dLLMs. It is unclear if dLLMs, with their iterative, coarse-to-fine generation process, can effectively integrate and benefit from RL signals. Bridging this gap could unlock new performance ceilings for dLLMs and foster architectural diversity in the LLM landscape.
2. **Challenges in Adapting Existing RL Methods:** Current successful RL algorithms for LLMs, such as PPO and GRPO, inherently rely on the ability to efficiently compute log-probabilities of generated sequences and individual tokens. This computation is straightforward in AR models due to their sequential factorization. However, dLLMs lack this natural decomposition, as their decoding process involves multiple, non-sequential unmasking steps, making the direct application of these RL methods challenging and computationally inefficient.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes `d1`, a two-stage post-training framework. The key objectives are:
- To develop and adapt existing techniques to improve reasoning in pre-trained masked dLLMs.
- Specifically, to utilize a masked Supervised Finetuning (SFT) technique for knowledge distillation and instilling self-improvement behaviors from high-quality reasoning datasets.
- To introduce `diffu-GRPO`, a novel critic-free, policy-gradient based RL algorithm that represents the first integration of such methods into masked dLLMs. This includes addressing the challenge of efficient log-probability estimation in the non-autoregressive setting.
- To empirically evaluate the effectiveness of these post-training recipes—SFT, `diffu-GRPO`, and their combination (`d1`)—on multiple mathematical and planning benchmarks, demonstrating performance improvements over state-of-the-art base dLLMs.

Ultimately, the research seeks to validate that dLLMs are not inherently limited in their reasoning capabilities by their generation paradigm and can, in fact, leverage advanced post-training techniques to achieve competitive or even superior performance on complex reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, `d1`, is a two-stage post-training pipeline designed to enhance the reasoning capabilities of pre-trained masked dLLMs. It combines Supervised Finetuning (SFT) with a novel reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm. The base model for their empirical studies is LLaDA-8B-Instruct, a state-of-the-art open-source dLLM.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Supervised Finetuning (SFT)

The first stage involves fine-tuning the base LLaDA model on high-quality reasoning traces. The authors use the `s1K` dataset, which contains 1000 detailed step-by-step problem-solving processes, including intermediate result verification and backtracking.
- **Masked SFT:** The SFT process for dLLMs involves randomly masking tokens during training according to a time-varying schedule. The model is optimized to predict the original tokens given their context, aligning with the masked language modeling objective of dLLMs.
- **Practical Considerations:** The authors highlight several design choices crucial for effective SFT, such as using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter efficiency, careful handling of sequence length truncation, and incorporating loss on PAD tokens to ensure proper generation termination. They also note the importance of balancing dataset difficulty for optimal learning.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning with `diffu-GRPO`

The core innovation of `d1` lies in its RL stage, which introduces `diffu-GRPO`, a novel policy gradient method specifically tailored for masked dLLMs. This addresses the key challenge of applying RL to non-autoregressive models, which traditionally struggle with log-probability estimation.

#### 3.2.1. Efficient Log Probability Estimation for Masked dLLMs

Standard policy gradient methods like GRPO require computing the log-probabilities of generated sequences and individual tokens to calculate likelihood ratios and KL divergence. In dLLMs, where generation is an iterative denoising process rather than sequential, this is non-trivial. The authors propose an efficient estimation method:
- **Mean-Field Approximation:** For sequence log-probability (`log π_θ(o|q)`), they use a mean-field decomposition, approximating it as a product of independent per-token log-probabilities (`Σ log π_θ(o_k|q)`).
- **One-Step Per-Token Log Probability Estimation with Prompt Masking:** To estimate the per-token log-probability (`log π_θ(o_k|q)`), the method involves:
 1. Perturbing the input prompt `q` by randomly masking some of its tokens with a probability `p_mask`, resulting in a new prompt `q'`.
 2. Performing a *single-step unmasking* operation using the dLLM's unmasking predictor `f_θ` to obtain `log f_θ(o_k | q' ⊕ mask...mask)`. This is used as the estimate for `log π_θ(o_k|q)`.
 This approach contrasts with LLaDA's computationally expensive Monte Carlo approximation (e.g., 128 samples per estimation), making online RL feasible.

#### 3.2.2. `diffu-GRPO` Algorithm

Building upon these log-probability estimators, `diffu-GRPO` extends the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective to masked dLLMs. The objective function (Equation 4 in the paper) incorporates:
- A clipped likelihood ratio term for policy updates, similar to PPO, but using the proposed log-probability estimations.
- A reverse KL penalty (`D_KL[φ_π_θ(·|q') || φ_π_ref(·|q')]`) to regularize the policy toward a reference model, also using the new estimators.
- **Random Prompt Masking as Regularization:** A crucial aspect of `diffu-GRPO` is the use of randomized prompt masking (`q'` generation) *for each inner gradient update iteration*. This means that for a given batch of (prompt, completion) pairs, multiple gradient updates are performed, but each update uses a different random masking pattern on the prompt. This stochasticity acts as a powerful form of regularization and data augmentation, creating "perturbed views" of the same data. It allows the algorithm to perform many more gradient updates per batch (higher `μ`) without overfitting, significantly reducing the number of costly online generations required for RL training and thus accelerating convergence and lowering computational cost.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Tasks:** The effectiveness of `d1` is evaluated across diverse reasoning tasks:
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** GSM8K (grade school math) and MATH500 (high-school competition math).
 - **Planning:** 4x4 Sudoku and Countdown with 3 numbers.
 - **Coding:** HumanEval and MBPP.
- **Reward Functions:** Task-specific composite reward functions combine formatting, correctness, and in some cases, safety checks (for coding).
- **Evaluation:** Zero-shot prompting with greedy decoding, evaluating at different generation sequence lengths (128, 256, 512) to assess generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical studies demonstrate that `d1` significantly enhances the reasoning capabilities of masked dLLMs, particularly when combining supervised finetuning with the novel `diffu-GRPO` algorithm.

#### 4.1. Performance of Individual Post-Training Recipes

- **`diffu-GRPO` vs. Baselines:** `diffu-GRPO` consistently outperforms the base LLaDA-8B-Instruct model across all evaluated math and planning benchmarks and generation sequence lengths. This includes 12 setups (4 tasks x 3 sequence lengths), where `diffu-GRPO` showed improvements in all of them.
- **SFT vs. Baselines:** Supervised finetuning (SFT) also yields improvements over the base LLaDA model, but less consistently than `diffu-GRPO` (outperforming in 7 out of 12 setups).
- **`diffu-GRPO` vs. SFT:** Crucially, `diffu-GRPO` alone demonstrates stronger overall performance than SFT alone, suggesting that policy gradient methods provide more robust gains for reasoning tasks in dLLMs. This highlights the power of online RL in guiding the model towards better reasoning strategies.

#### 4.2. Superiority of the `d1` Framework

- **Synergistic Gains:** The `d1` recipe, which applies `diffu-GRPO` on top of the SFT-adapted checkpoint (`LLaDA+SFT+diffu-GRPO`), consistently yields the highest gains. It outperforms the `diffu-GRPO`-only approach in 11 out of 12 setups, indicating a synergistic effect where SFT provides a strong foundation of reasoning traces, and `diffu-GRPO` further refines and optimizes these behaviors through online interaction.
- **Task-Specific Magnitudes:** While `d1-LLaDA` shows consistent improvements across all benchmarks, the magnitude varies. Modest improvements are observed on mathematical tasks like GSM8K (+3.9%) and MATH500 (+4.0%), while significantly larger gains are achieved on planning tasks such as Countdown (+26.2%) and Sudoku (+10.0%). The authors hypothesize that this might be due to the base model's relative saturation on mathematical tasks, leaving more room for improvement on planning benchmarks that require complex constraint satisfaction.

#### 4.3. Generalization and Scaling

- **Unified Model Performance:** Training a single `diffu-GRPO` (and `d1`) model on a combined dataset of all reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MATH500, Countdown, Sudoku) demonstrates that the approach scales well to multi-task settings without sacrificing accuracy compared to task-specific training.
- **Coding Domain Application:** `diffu-GRPO` consistently improves performance on coding benchmarks (HumanEval and MBPP), irrespective of whether it starts from the base LLaDA model or an SFT-adapted checkpoint. This suggests the broad applicability of the method beyond mathematical and planning reasoning.
- **Reasoning Beyond Training Length:** Despite `diffu-GRPO` training being conducted at a fixed sequence length of 256, performance gains are observed at other evaluation lengths (128 and 512). This indicates that the model learns more general reasoning strategies rather than overfitting to a specific output length.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Insights and Design Choice Ablations

- **"Aha Moments" in SFT and `d1-LLaDA`:** Qualitative analysis reveals that SFT and `d1-LLaDA` models, particularly at longer generation lengths (512 tokens), exhibit "aha moments"—instances of self-correction and backtracking. This behavior is attributed to the distillation of such reasoning traces from the `s1K` dataset during the SFT stage.
- **Sequential Scaling Trends:** Mathematical tasks (GSM8K, MATH500) show improved performance with increasing generation sequence lengths, consistent with observations in AR models. However, planning tasks (Countdown, Sudoku) show mixed trends, indicating that more robust base dLLMs and efficient inference algorithms are needed to fully exploit longer sequences for complex search-based problems.
- **Benefits of Random Masking:** The ablation studies confirm the significant advantage of random prompt masking for likelihood estimation in `diffu-GRPO`. Random masking consistently outperforms fixed masking, enabling higher values of inner policy optimization updates (`μ` = 12 or 24 vs. conventional 2) without leading to overfitting. This translates to substantial computational efficiency gains by reducing the number of expensive online generations.
- **Optimal Masking Rate:** Lower prompt masking rates (`p_mask` = 0.1, 0.3) for log-probability estimation yield more stable training and better performance compared to no masking (`p_mask` = 0.0), validating the regularization effect. Higher rates (`p_mask` = 0.5, 0.7) introduce instability in later training stages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the capabilities of discrete diffusion large language models (dLLMs) and their role in the broader landscape of artificial intelligence. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering RL for Large dLLMs:** This work is, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first to successfully demonstrate the efficacy of policy gradient-based reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms on *large* masked dLLMs for complex reasoning tasks. Prior RL work in discrete diffusion was limited to smaller models or different objectives. This breakthrough opens up a new, highly promising avenue for enhancing dLLMs beyond traditional language modeling.

2. **Validation of dLLM Reasoning Potential:** By showing that dLLMs can achieve substantial reasoning improvements through `d1` (SFT + `diffu-GRPO`), the paper validates that their non-autoregressive nature is not an inherent limitation to developing sophisticated reasoning abilities. This challenges the implicit assumption that AR models are uniquely suited for RL-based reasoning, paving the way for architectural diversity in advanced AI.

3. **Introduction of `diffu-GRPO` and Efficient Log-Probability Estimation:** The proposed `diffu-GRPO` algorithm, coupled with its novel one-step, prompt-masked log-probability estimation technique, is a key methodological contribution. It directly addresses the long-standing challenge of applying policy gradients to dLLMs, making online RL training feasible and efficient. This innovation could be generalized to other policy gradient methods (like PPO) for dLLMs.

4. **Enhanced Training Efficiency via Random Masking:** The discovery that random prompt masking during gradient updates acts as a potent regularization, allowing for more inner optimization steps (`μ`) and reducing the need for costly online generations, is a significant practical insight. This efficiency gain is crucial for scaling RL training to larger dLLMs and more complex reasoning problems.

5. **Blueprint for Future dLLM Development:** The `d1` framework provides a concrete, effective, and validated recipe for enhancing dLLMs. The combination of SFT for knowledge distillation and `diffu-GRPO` for behavior refinement demonstrates a synergistic effect. This recipe can serve as a blueprint for researchers and practitioners looking to build more capable dLLMs for reasoning, planning, and other complex AI tasks.

6. **Insights into Reasoning Mechanisms:** The qualitative observation of "aha moments" and self-correction in SFT and `d1-LLaDA` models suggests that even without explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting during inference, the models can internalize and exhibit sophisticated reasoning behaviors learned from high-quality demonstrations. This provides valuable insights into how reasoning capabilities are instilled and improved in different LLM architectures.

Looking forward, this work highlights the need for continued research into efficient inference algorithms for dLLMs to facilitate longer generation lengths during RL training, which could further unlock their reasoning potential. Ultimately, this research strengthens the case for dLLMs as competitive and potentially advantageous alternatives to AR models, especially for tasks where their unique properties, like bidirectional context and coarse-to-fine generation, might offer inherent benefits.
