# AWARE: Adaptive Whole-body Active Rotating Control for Enhanced LiDAR-Inertial Odometry under Human-in-the-Loop Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> AWARE, developed by researchers from Wuhan University and Nanyang Technological University, introduces an adaptive whole-body active rotating control framework for UAVs that enhances LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO) by leveraging the UAV's yaw agility as a sensing mechanism. It integrates a hybrid Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) approach to dynamically optimize yaw for improved observability, achieving an average APE reduction of 27.8% in simulation and an average real-world drift rate of 0.2124% across challenging environments, while seamlessly incorporating human operator input.

## Problem

- Lightweight UAVs with narrow-field-of-view LiDAR sensors experience weakened LIO observability in geometrically degenerate or feature-sparse scenes, leading to drift and unstable state estimation.
- Current active sensing solutions either add payload-heavy mechanical gimbals or rely on software methods (MPC with fixed weights, end-to-end RL) that lack environmental adaptability or interpretability.
- Existing approaches often lack a principled mechanism for safely integrating human operator translational intent with autonomous yaw planning for perception optimization in human-in-the-loop (HITL) scenarios.

## Method

- **AWARE** utilizes the UAV's intrinsic whole-body yaw agility as a 'virtual gimbal' to dynamically expand the effective sensor horizon and enhance LIO observability without requiring additional mechanical hardware.
- A hybrid control architecture combines ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) for real-time trajectory tracking and a Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent to adaptively tune the MPC's observability-maximizing cost weights.
- The framework decouples human translational commands from autonomous yaw optimization, integrating a Safe Flight Corridor (SFC) mechanism to ensure safe operation during human-in-the-loop interaction.

## Results

- In diverse simulated environments, AWARE achieved an average Absolute Pose Error (APE) of **3.41** m, representing a **27.8%** reduction compared to the best static MPC baseline and an **84.9%** improvement over passive scanning methods.
- Real-world deployment on a quadrotor UAV demonstrated robust performance, with an average APE RMSE of **0.3618** m and an average drift rate of **0.2124%** across four challenging environments, indicating effective Sim2Real transfer.
- The RL agent adaptively modulated observability weights based on environmental geometry, prioritizing observability more in difficult scenes, and the control loop maintained real-time performance with an average processing time of **46.90** ms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent yaw agility of a UAV can be effectively exploited as a software-driven active sensing mechanism to overcome narrow LiDAR FoV limitations, reducing the need for mechanical gimbals and their associated costs and complexities.
> - A hybrid approach combining the stability and interpretability of MPC with the adaptability of RL allows for dynamic, context-aware tuning of perception-driven control parameters, surpassing the limitations of static tuning.
> - Quantifying LIO observability using the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) and integrating it into MPC's cost function enables the system to directly optimize state estimation accuracy through active, geometry-aware yaw maneuvers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in state estimation for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) operating in complex and safety-critical environments, such as underground mines, disaster areas, and dense forests. These missions often necessitate human-in-the-loop (HITL) control due to the unpredictability and partial observability of the environments. A primary limitation for UAVs in these scenarios is their reliance on resource-constrained platforms, which typically employ lightweight LiDAR sensors with narrow fields of view (FoV). This restricted FoV can lead to weakened observability for LiDAR Inertial Odometry (LIO) systems in geometrically degenerate or feature-sparse scenes, resulting in drift accumulation, degraded geometric accuracy, and unstable state estimation. This directly compromises safe HITL operation and the reliability of acquired data.

Previous research to mitigate FoV limitations has explored hardware augmentation (e.g., motorized gimbals) and algorithmic compensation. While hardware-centric approaches can effectively sweep sensors, they increase payload, power consumption, and mechanical vulnerability, making them less suitable for lightweight UAVs. Software-based control strategies, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), have emerged to optimize UAV motion for active sensing. However, pure MPC often suffers from fixed cost weights that are difficult to tune and adapt across diverse environments, while end-to-end RL policies can lack interpretability and require significant computational resources. Critically, many existing approaches are designed for fully autonomous agents and do not provide a principled mechanism for safely integrating human operator intent with autonomous yaw planning.

AWARE positions itself within this landscape by proposing a purely software-driven approach that addresses the dual gap of (i) adaptively maintaining accurate and robust LIO in changing environments without additional mechanical complexity, and (ii) seamlessly incorporating human operator commands into a safety-guaranteed, observability-aware control loop. It shifts the paradigm from mechanical complexity to algorithmic sophistication, exploiting the UAV's own yaw agility as a "whole-body gimbal" to expand the effective sensor horizon. This aligns with the trend toward coupled flight-sensing paradigms that optimize vehicle dynamics for sensing objectives under strict Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP) constraints of micro-UAVs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the necessity of reliable and accurate state estimation for UAVs in challenging environments under human-in-the-loop (HITL) operation. The key challenges are the narrow FoV of lightweight LiDAR sensors on resource-constrained UAVs and the need for seamless integration of human control with autonomous perception optimization.

The key objectives of the AWARE framework are:

- **Enhance LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO) Observability:** To overcome the limitations imposed by narrow-field-of-view LiDAR sensors in geometrically degenerate or feature-sparse scenes. The goal is to reduce drift accumulation, improve geometric accuracy, and stabilize state estimation.
- **Develop a Bio-inspired Whole-body Active Yawing Framework:** To exploit the UAV’s inherent rotational agility to extend the effective sensor horizon and improve LIO's observability. This approach aims to achieve active sensing capabilities without introducing additional mechanical actuation (e.g., gimbals), thereby adhering to strict payload and power constraints.
- **Integrate Adaptive Control with Observability Maximization:** To dynamically balance the trade-off between estimation accuracy (information gain) and flight stability. This involves developing a mechanism that can adjust control behavior online based on the current environmental context.
- **Support Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Interaction Safely:** To enable human operators to provide navigational intent while the onboard autonomy simultaneously optimizes yaw for perceptual objectives. This requires decoupling operator commands from autonomous yaw optimization and ensuring operational safety through a robust mechanism.
- **Develop a Robust and Transferable Control Policy:** To create a system that performs consistently across diverse and previously unseen environments, including structured, degraded, and unstructured scenes, through training in a high-fidelity simulation environment that supports real-to-sim-to-real transfer.

These objectives are driven by the need for reliable aerial surveying, inspection, and disaster response missions where current autonomous systems struggle due to perception limitations and the practical requirement for human supervision in complex, safety-critical scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AWARE framework integrates a bio-inspired active sensing strategy, a hybrid control architecture, and a safety mechanism for human-in-the-loop operation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and System Overview

The system is designed for HITL active sensing, maximizing localization accuracy via adaptive yaw planning while preserving operator translational intent.
- **UAV State and Dynamics:** The UAV state $\mathbf{x}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$ includes position, velocity, acceleration, and yaw angle. Control input $\mathbf{u}_k \in \mathbb{R}^4$ consists of translational jerk and yaw rate. A discrete-time linear time-invariant system models the dynamics, leveraging the differential flatness of a quadrotor with a triple-integrator for translation and a first-order integrator for yaw.
- **Unified Panoramic Representation:** To efficiently process perception data, a Panoramic Depth Map $\mathcal{D}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ is constructed. This involves:
 - **ScanBlock Aggregation:** A sliding window of recent LiDAR observations is aggregated and downsampled to form $\mathcal{S}_t$, densifying the geometric representation for robustness and managing data size.
 - **Spherical Projection:** Points in $\mathcal{S}_t$ are projected onto a discretized spherical coordinate system to generate the 2D panoramic map, where each pixel stores the shortest range observation.
- **Observability Analysis:** LIO localization quality is quantified using the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM), which approximates the inverse of the estimation covariance. The core of LIO minimizes point-to-plane distance residuals.
 - **FIM Computation:** For a candidate yaw angle $\psi$, a virtual point cloud $P_t$ is simulated, and the FIM $\mathbf{\Phi}(\psi)$ is computed from the Jacobians of point-to-plane residuals.
 - **Observability Metric:** The A-optimality criterion, $\mathcal{J}_{obs}(\psi) = \text{Tr}(\mathbf{\Phi}(\psi)^{-1})$, is used. Minimizing this metric guides the UAV toward viewing directions that enhance state estimation.

#### 3.2. Hybrid MPC Formulation with Human-in-the-Loop

The control problem is formulated as a constrained Quadratic Programming (QP) optimization over a prediction horizon.
- **Task Decoupling:** The human operator controls translational motion ($x, y, z$) via remote controller commands, which generate the reference trajectory $\mathbf{x}_{ref,k}$. The yaw dimension is autonomously optimized by the MPC to maximize observability, leveraging LiDAR's sensitivity to heading direction.
- **MPC Objective Function:** The objective minimizes a composite cost function:
 - Tracking fidelity to $\mathbf{x}_{ref,k}$ (position, velocity, acceleration).
 - Regularization of control energy and smoothness.
 - An observability cost term $\lambda_{obs} \cdot \mathcal{J}_{obs}(\psi_k)$ that incentivizes yaw behaviors maximizing state observability.
- **Constraints:** System dynamics, physical actuation limits, and a Safe Flight Corridor (SFC) (a collision-free convex region from the local map) are incorporated as linear position constraints.
- **Differentiable Observability Integration:** Since $\mathcal{J}_{obs}(\psi)$ lacks an analytical derivative, a quadratic approximation $\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{obs}(\psi) \approx \frac{1}{2} h_{obs}\psi^2 + g_{obs}\psi + c_{obs}$ is used. The approximate Hessian $h_{obs}$ and gradient $g_{obs}$ are directly incorporated into the MPC’s QP matrices, allowing gradient-based solvers to optimize yaw.

#### 3.3. RL-based Adaptive Parameter Tuning

To enable dynamic adaptation of MPC cost weights across environments, a Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent acts as a meta-controller within a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
- **State Space:** The RL agent's state $s_t$ comprises:
 - **Internal State ($s_{int}$):** UAV kinematics (relative position to goal, velocity, acceleration, yaw, yaw rate) and sampled observability costs around the current yaw.
 - **External State ($s_{ext}$):** A low-resolution panoramic depth map $\mathcal{D}_t$, flattened into a vector and processed by a shallow MLP encoder $E_\phi$ into a compact 128-dimensional latent vector.
- **Network Architecture:** A lightweight Actor-Critic network (MLP with 256, 256, and 128 hidden units) is used, optimized via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The combined internal and external state vector feeds into both Actor and Critic.
- **Action Space:** A continuous 5-dimensional vector $\mathbf{ac}_t \in [-1, 1]^5$ modulates the diagonal weights of the MPC cost matrices. These correspond to position, velocity, acceleration, yaw rate, and the observability cost $\lambda_{obs}$. Actions are linearly scaled to predefined weight bounds.
- **Reward Design:** The reward function $r_t$ is a weighted sum of three objectives:
 - **SLAM Accuracy Reward ($r_{acc}$):** Exponentially penalizes Relative Trajectory Error (RTE) between estimated and ground-truth trajectories within a local sliding window, encouraging precise local state estimation.
 - **Exploration Reward ($r_{exp}$):** Rewards the discovery of newly observed voxels, promoting coverage.
 - **Smoothness Reward ($r_{smooth}$):** Exponentially penalizes variance in yaw rate over a sliding window, promoting stable rotation for scan-to-map registration.

#### 3.4. Point Cloud-Based Simulation and Control Policy Training

A high-fidelity, closed-loop simulation environment is developed following a Real-to-Sim-to-Real methodology.
- **Simulation Environment:** It synthesizes sensor data directly from dense point cloud maps of real-world environments.
 - **Dynamics Simulation:** Incorporates realistic aerodynamic damping and control delays.
 - **LiDAR Render Node:** Performs ray-casting based on UAV pose to generate simulated point clouds.
 - **LIO Node:** Processes simulated LiDAR and IMU data for state estimation.
 - **Observability Calculate Node:** Computes observability metrics.
 - **RL Policy Node & Planning and Control Node:** Outputs and executes control commands, closing the loop.
- **Training Datasets and Scenarios:** A diverse dataset is used, combining public benchmarks and self-collected data, spanning structured (corridors, buildings), degraded (featureless tunnels), and unstructured (lava tube, forest) environments. Expert-demonstrated reference trajectories serve as navigation baselines and behavioral priors for fast policy warm-up.
- **Training Implementation:** PPO is used for $3 \times 10^6$ timesteps with dynamic environment sampling. Only the first 50% of trajectories are used for training, reserving the rest for evaluation. Online reward normalization and standard PPO hyper-parameters (e.g., decaying learning rate, GAE, clipping) are applied.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AWARE was conducted through extensive simulations and real-world deployments, benchmarked against passive scanning and static MPC baselines.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics and Baselines

- **Metric:** Absolute Pose Error (APE) was the primary metric, quantifying the Euclidean deviation between estimated and ground-truth trajectories. Lower APE indicates better LIO performance.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Passive Fixed-Rate Scanning:** Two variants were tested: slow (1.0 rad/s) for high point density and fast (8.0 rad/s) for maximum FoV coverage. These lack environmental awareness.
 - **Static Optimization-based MPC:** Standard MPC with fixed observability weights ($\lambda_{obs} \in \{100, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000\}$). These cannot adapt to changing scene geometry.

#### 4.2. Evaluation in Simulation

AWARE was evaluated across eight diverse simulated scenes (Spine, Wuhan Subway, Building, Istanbul Tunnel, Wuhan Tunnel, Lava Tube, Forest, Nebra Karst Cave).
- **Trajectory Errors:** AWARE consistently achieved low per-axis trajectory errors, with sub-meter APE in geometrically rich environments and bounded errors in degenerate scenes.
- **Statistical Distribution:** Box plots of APE (log scale) showed AWARE achieving sub-meter median APE in feature-rich scenes and maintaining bounded error elsewhere. The Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of per-frame errors indicated smaller localization errors for most frames, signifying higher reliability.
- **Quantitative Performance (Table 2):**
 - AWARE yielded an average APE of **3.41 m** across all simulated scenes.
 - This represented a **27.8% reduction** compared to the best static MPC baseline ($\lambda_{obs}=500$, 4.72 m).
 - It showed an **84.9% improvement** over passive fixed-rate scanning (average 22.53 m for fast, 20.72 m for slow).
 - Fixed-rate strategies failed significantly in degenerate environments (e.g., Istanbul Tunnel: >100 m APE), while static MPC baselines were sensitive to manual $\lambda_{obs}$ tuning and lacked consistent performance across diverse scenes.
 - AWARE's RL agent dynamically adjusted $\lambda_{obs}$ based on real-time context, automatically finding an optimal trade-off and demonstrating strong generalization without manual tuning.

#### 4.3. Real-World Deployment Validation

AWARE was deployed on a custom-built quadrotor UAV in four challenging real-world environments: an underground tunnel, abandoned athletic infrastructure, a derelict bunker, and a dense forest.
- **Ground Truth:** Survey-grade Riegl VZ-400 terrestrial laser scanner data, registered using BSC, provided high-resolution prior maps for accurate ground truth.
- **Overall Performance (Table 4):** AWARE maintained bounded localization error across all four sites with an average APE RMSE of **0.3618 m** and an average drift rate of **0.2124%**. This indicated effective Sim2Real transfer without fine-tuning.
- **Scene-wise Results (Fig. 12):**
 - **Abandoned Athletic Infrastructure & Dense Forest:** Achieved low RMSE (0.0510 m and 0.0355 m) and drift rates (0.0545% and 0.0778%) due to spatially distributed vertical structures providing persistent lateral constraints.
 - **Abandoned Bunker:** Showed the lowest error (APE mean 0.0081 m, RMSE 0.0091 m, max 0.0281 m) despite dust, occlusions, and clutter, indicating robustness to measurement degradation.
 - **Underground Tunnel:** The most challenging environment due to axial degeneracy and repetitive structures, resulting in higher errors (APE mean 1.2008 m, RMSE 1.3516 m, drift rate 0.6598%). However, the error remained bounded over a 407.17 m trajectory, and the estimated path qualitatively followed the ground truth, suggesting learned geometry-aware adaptation rather than scene-specific heuristics.

#### 4.4. Time Performance Analysis

Latency analysis was performed on an edge-grade x86 Intel-N305 processor.
- **Efficiency:** The AWARE decision loop achieved an average processing time of **46.90 ms**.
- **Real-Time Capability:** 95% of control iterations completed within **81.02 ms**, well within the 100 ms window required for a 10 Hz LiDAR scanning frequency, ensuring real-time synchronization with sensory updates.

#### 4.5. Interpreting Yaw Exploration and Scene-Driven Observability Adapting

Analysis of commanded yaw rate and RL-modulated observability weights provided insights into AWARE's behavior.
- **Yaw Rate Adaptation (Fig. 14):** The UAV exhibited faster yaw rates in corridor-like (feature-poor) regions and slower rates in landmark-rich (feature-rich) regions. This indicates targeted viewpoint reallocation in degenerate areas to acquire stronger constraints (e.g., wall intersections) and reduced effort when sufficient constraints are present.
- **Observability Weight Modulation (Table 5):** The RL policy assigned higher average observability weights ($\lambda_{obs}$) in geometrically difficult environments (Underground Tunnel: 1585.54, Abandoned Bunker: 1544.85) and lower weights in feature-rich scenes (Dense Forest: 268.30). This empirically validated that AWARE prioritizes observability more strongly when needed, aligning with the observed error trends.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AWARE framework presents a significant advancement in robust state estimation for UAVs, particularly in complex and safety-critical environments where human supervision is crucial. Its primary significance lies in enabling lightweight UAVs equipped with narrow-FoV LiDARs to achieve accurate and robust LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO) without requiring additional, often heavy and fragile, mechanical gimbals. By exploiting the UAV's inherent whole-body rotational agility as a sensing mechanism, it addresses a fundamental trade-off between payload constraints and perceptual coverage.

The integration of a hybrid Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) architecture is impactful because it combines the interpretability and stability guarantees of MPC with the adaptability of RL. This allows the system to dynamically adjust sensing priorities based on real-time environmental context, moving beyond static parameter tuning. The explicit coupling of environmental feature analysis (via Fisher Information Matrix-based observability) with flight control decisions is a key contribution, as it directly mitigates localization drift in perceptually challenging scenes.

Furthermore, the principled incorporation of Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) operation through task decoupling and Safe Flight Corridor (SFC) constraints enhances the practicality and safety of UAV deployment in real-world missions. This co-design ensures that human operators can maintain navigational intent while the system autonomously optimizes perception, fostering efficient and safe human-robot collaboration.

The development of a high-fidelity, point cloud-based simulation platform facilitates rigorous training and validation, demonstrating strong Sim2Real transfer capabilities. This enables policies to be learned in diverse simulated environments and deployed on physical hardware without extensive real-world re-training, which is critical for scalability and cost-effectiveness.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced UAV Autonomy:** Provides a foundation for more reliable and robust autonomous navigation in GPS-denied, underground, cluttered, or unstructured environments, which are common in mining, infrastructure inspection, and disaster response.
- **Improved Data Quality for Surveying:** By reducing LIO drift and improving geometric accuracy, AWARE can lead to higher-fidelity 3D maps and more reliable surveying products, which are crucial for decision-making in critical applications.
- **Reduced Hardware Costs and Complexity:** Eliminating the need for active mechanical gimbals lowers the cost, weight, and power consumption of UAV platforms, making advanced sensing capabilities more accessible for a wider range of applications and smaller platforms.
- **Safer Human-Robot Collaboration:** The HITL design, coupled with SFC, allows human operators to guide missions in dangerous or unknown areas while offloading the cognitive load of perception optimization to the autonomous system, enhancing operational safety and efficiency.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework's adaptability and interpretability open avenues for future work, such as multi-UAV coordination for cooperative active perception and integration with immersive VR interfaces for more intuitive human interaction.
