# Force-Aware Residual DAgger via Trajectory Editing for Precision Insertion with Impedance Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University developed TER-DAgger, a human-in-the-loop imitation learning framework for robotic precision insertion. This framework mitigates covariate shift through force-aware error detection and an optimization-based trajectory editing method, achieving an average 77.2% success rate across various insertion tasks while ensuring compliant interaction.

## Problem

- Imitation learning policies perform poorly in contact-rich precision insertion tasks due to covariate shift (out-of-distribution states), causing failures or damage.
- Human-in-the-loop (HIL) methods for addressing covariate shift require continuous human supervision, limiting scalability for widespread robotic deployment.
- Ensuring compliant and safe robot interaction during contact-rich tasks remains challenging, with existing methods often leading to excessive forces or damage.

## Method

- Proposed Trajectory Editing Residual DAgger (TER-DAgger), a framework combining a force-aware base policy, an error detector, a residual policy, and Cartesian impedance control.
- Implemented force-aware error detection by comparing predicted and measured end-effector forces, triggering human intervention only for out-of-distribution states.
- Developed an optimization-based trajectory editing method to smoothly integrate human corrections with the base policy's trajectory, generating consistent residual training data.

## Results

- TER-DAgger achieved a **77.2%** average success rate across simulation and real-world precision insertion tasks (e.g., **96%** for two-pin plug in real-world), surpassing existing baselines by over **37%**.
- The force prediction error detector demonstrated an average precision of **98.8%** for identifying out-of-distribution states, maintaining **100%** recall of failure cases.
- Ablation studies confirmed that including end-effector force in the base policy and the multi-component trajectory editing for residual data generation were essential for TER-DAgger's performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating end-effector force as input into imitation learning policies enhances performance and interaction awareness for contact-rich tasks.
> - Using force prediction error as an error detection mechanism offers high precision for identifying out-of-distribution states in contact-rich scenarios, reducing the need for continuous human supervision.
> - An optimization-based approach to smoothly merge human corrective demonstrations with existing policy trajectories effectively generates high-quality residual training data, improving policy adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in robotic precision insertion tasks, which are critical in industrial automation but are sensitive to misalignment and environmental uncertainty, often leading to excessive forces or damage. Imitation Learning (IL) has demonstrated potential for complex manipulation, including contact-rich tasks. However, many existing IL approaches primarily rely on visual and proprioceptive data and often utilize position-based control, which can limit compliance and safety during physical contact. While some research has begun to integrate force, torque, or tactile sensing and combine IL policies with impedance control to improve safety, they frequently do not explicitly address the problem of covariate shift during real-world deployment.

Covariate shift occurs when a learned policy encounters states that are out-of-distribution (OOD) compared to its training data, due to factors like perception noise, modeling errors, or contact uncertainties. In contact-rich manipulation, this can manifest as abnormal interaction forces and lead to unsafe behavior. Human-in-the-loop (HIL) approaches, such as DAgger and its variants, aim to mitigate covariate shift by incorporating expert feedback during execution. However, these methods typically require continuous human monitoring, which limits their scalability. Subsequent HIL research has sought to reduce expert burden by predicting when intervention is needed, but often at the cost of increased model complexity. Furthermore, abrupt transitions between autonomous execution and human control can themselves introduce distribution shifts. While some recent work explores learning residual corrections to smooth adaptation, continuous human monitoring often remains a requirement.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing a scalable and force-aware human-in-the-loop imitation learning framework. It aims to overcome the limitations of continuous expert supervision and abrupt control switching in contact-rich tasks by:
1. Integrating force awareness and compliant control within the IL framework.
2. Developing a mechanism to automatically detect out-of-distribution states that warrant human intervention, thereby reducing continuous monitoring.
3. Employing an optimization-based method to smoothly incorporate human corrections, mitigating distribution shift.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to develop an imitation learning framework that can:
1. **Mitigate Covariate Shift in Contact-Rich Precision Insertion:** The core motivation is to address the performance degradation of IL policies when deployed in real-world contact-rich tasks due to encountering states not seen during training. This often leads to failures like jamming or component damage.
2. **Enable Scalable Human-in-the-Loop Supervision:** Traditional HIL methods demand continuous human monitoring, which is impractical for deploying robots at scale. The objective is to significantly reduce the burden on human experts by only triggering intervention when necessary, allowing one expert to potentially supervise multiple robots.
3. **Ensure Compliant and Safe Interaction during Contact:** Precision insertion inherently involves physical contact. The objective is to guarantee that the robot interacts safely and compliantly with its environment, preventing excessive forces and potential damage to the robot or manipulated objects.
4. **Provide Consistent and Stable Supervision for Residual Policy Learning:** Abrupt transitions between autonomous policy execution and human control can introduce inconsistencies in the training data, leading to further distribution shifts. A key objective is to generate smooth and consistent residual supervision data that effectively blends policy rollouts with human corrections.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the recognized gap between the potential of imitation learning in manipulation and its practical, robust deployment in industrial settings. Current IL methods often fail when faced with real-world uncertainties and contact dynamics, and existing HIL solutions are often too costly in terms of human resources or introduce new complexities. By combining force awareness, intelligent error detection, and sophisticated trajectory editing, the authors aim to create a more robust, safe, and deployable IL system for precision assembly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Trajectory Editing Residual Dataset Aggregation (TER-DAgger), integrates several components: a force-aware base policy, an error detector, a residual policy, and a Cartesian impedance controller.

**A. Force-Aware Base Policy:**
- **Architecture:** A Transformer-based architecture, similar to Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), is used. Both the encoder and decoder are implemented using CVAE Transformers.
- **Input:** The encoder takes the current Cartesian end-effector pose (position and quaternion orientation), a sequence of future actions, and a sequence of future end-effector forces. The decoder conditions on a latent style variable (output of the encoder), the current pose, current end-effector force, and two RGB images from front and wrist cameras.
- **Force Inclusion:** Explicitly incorporates the external end-effector wrench (force and torque) as input. This wrench is estimated from measured joint torques using the pseudoinverse of the Jacobian transpose. This aims to improve performance and interaction awareness in contact-rich scenarios.
- **Output:** The decoder jointly predicts a future action sequence (end-effector poses) and a future end-effector force sequence. This joint prediction is intended to enable the policy to learn a force-consistent latent representation.
- **Execution Strategy:** The base policy operates at 1 Hz, predicting a horizon of 100 steps (2 seconds). However, only the first 50 steps are executed before replanning (receding-horizon execution), which is designed to enhance robustness.

**B. Error Detection:**
- **Mechanism:** Detects Out-of-Distribution (OOD) states by comparing the predicted end-effector force from the base policy, $\hat{F}_t$, with the actual measured force, $F_t$, at each timestep. This comparison is performed at 50 Hz.
- **Error Metric:** The $L_1$ norm of the difference between predicted and measured forces, $e_t = ||\hat{F}_t - F_t||_1$, quantifies the force prediction error.
- **Trigger Condition:** An error is triggered when $e_t$ exceeds a predefined threshold $c$. This implicitly serves as an uncertainty estimator; large discrepancies suggest unexpected contact dynamics or potential failure.

**C. Trajectory Editing Residual DAgger:**
This component manages human intervention and generates residual training data.
1. **Residual Policy:**
 - **Architecture:** A lightweight two-layer Transformer encoder.
 - **Input:** Current Cartesian end-effector pose, the next action predicted by the base policy, and two RGB images.
 - **Output:** A corrective action (pose residual) $\Delta A_{t+1}$. The final executed action becomes the sum of the base policy's prediction and this residual correction: $A^{exec}_{t+1} = \hat{A}_{t+1} + \Delta A_{t+1}$.
2. **Corrected Trajectory Construction:**
 - **Trigger:** When an OOD state is detected by the error detector, execution is paused, and a human provides a short corrective demonstration.
 - **Nearest-Point Alignment:** The start of the human demonstration, $x_{h_0}$, is aligned to the closest point, $x^b_{k^*}$, on the base policy's trajectory $X_b$. A weighted sum of position and orientation distances is used for alignment.
 - **Local Trajectory Optimization:** A segment of the base trajectory of length $N$ preceding the alignment point, $X^b_{seg}$, is optimized to smoothly transition from the base trajectory to the start of the human demonstration, $x_{h_0}$. This optimization minimizes a cost function comprising:
 - A fidelity term to preserve similarity to the original base trajectory.
 - A smoothness term to ensure local motion continuity.
 - An endpoint consistency term to align with the human corrective pose.
 - **Final Corrected Trajectory:** The resulting optimized segment $\tilde{X}$ is fused with the preceding part of the base trajectory and the human demonstration to form a complete corrected trajectory $X^{correct}$.
3. **Residual Training Data Generation:**
 Supervised tuples $(x_t, I^f_t, I^w_t, \hat{A}_{t+1}, \Delta A_{t+1})$ are generated from $X^{correct}$, divided into four regions:
 - **Pre-editing samples:** For segments before the optimized transition, $\Delta A_{t+1} = 0$, teaching the residual policy to be inactive in nominal states.
 - **Transition samples:** For the optimized segment, $\Delta A_{t+1} = \tilde{x}_{t+1} - \hat{A}_{t+1}$, training the residual policy to deform the base trajectory smoothly.
 - **Human demonstration samples:** For the human-provided segment, $\Delta A_{t+1} = x_{h_{t+1}} - \hat{A}^{base}_{t+1}$, training the residual policy to reproduce the human correction relative to the base prediction.
 - **Post-editing samples:** For segments of the base trajectory following the human demonstration, $\Delta A_{t+1}$ is computed relative to the nearest pose in the human demonstration, encouraging consistency with the corrective intent.

**D. Cartesian Impedance Controller:**
- **Function:** A low-level controller operating at 1 kHz that tracks the combined action from the base and residual policies while ensuring compliant and safe interaction.
- **Mechanism:** Shapes the end-effector's apparent mechanical behavior as a virtual mass-spring-damper system. It computes desired joint torques based on the error between desired and measured Cartesian poses and velocities, using stiffness ($K$) and damping ($D$) matrices, along with gravity compensation. This allows the manipulator to absorb unexpected contact forces while following the high-level policy trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments in both simulation (MuJoCo with Franka Research 3) and real-world scenarios (Franka robot with Intel RealSense D415) to evaluate TER-DAgger.

**A. TER-DAgger Performance:**
- **Tasks:** Evaluated on USB insertion and two-pin plug insertion in simulation, and USB, two-pin plug, and three-pin plug insertion in the real world. Insertion tolerance was 0.1 mm.
- **Baselines:** Compared against:
 - **ACT:** A foundational action chunk transformer.
 - **FILIC:** A multimodal IL framework incorporating visual, pose, and force data.
 - **HG-DAgger:** A human-guided DAgger variant with direct expert intervention.
 - **Retrain:** Base policy retrained with original and corrected data.
 - **Finetune:** Pre-trained base policy finetuned with corrected data.
- **Results (Table I):** TER-DAgger consistently outperformed all baselines.
 - Achieved an average success rate of 77.2% across all tasks.
 - Exceeded the strongest baseline (Finetune, 40.0%) by over 37%.
 - In simulation, TER-DAgger achieved 90% (USB) and 96% (Two-pin Plug) success rates.
 - In real-world tasks, it achieved 96% (Two-pin Plug) and 82% (Three-pin Plug), with 22% for USB insertion.
 - Standard IL methods (ACT, FILIC) showed poor performance (27.6%, 25.6% average success), indicating covariate shift issues.
 - HG-DAgger showed limited real-world generalization (31.6% average).
 - Retraining and finetuning provided inconsistent gains and were inferior to TER-DAgger (32.4%, 40.0% average).

**B. Error Detection Performance:**
- **Baselines:** Compared against:
 - **KL Loss:** KL divergence of CVAE encoder output to a standard normal distribution.
 - **Reconstruction Loss:** Reconstruction error of the base policy.
 - **Position Prediction Error:** Euclidean error between predicted and current end-effector pose.
 - **Force Prediction Error (Ours):** The proposed method.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Precision, while maintaining 100% recall of failure cases. Thresholds were task-specific and selected to ensure full recall (Table III).
- **Results (Table II):**
 - The proposed Force Prediction Error consistently achieved the highest precision across all tasks, averaging 98.8%.
 - KL Loss (75.6%), Reconstruction Loss (75.2%), and Position Prediction Error (73.2%) exhibited substantially lower average precision, indicating a higher rate of false positives despite achieving 100% recall.
 - The discrepancy was more pronounced in real-world tasks for uncertainty-based metrics.

**C. Ablation Studies (Two-pin Plug Insertion in Simulation):**
- **Adding End-effector Force to Base Policy (Table IV):** Incorporating end-effector force as input to the ACT architecture (Base Policy (Ours)) improved the average success rate from 27.6% (ACT) to 32.4%. This suggests force information provides valuable contact cues.
- **Training Samples for Residual Policy (Table V):**
 - Using individual components (Transition, Demonstration, Post-editing) yielded limited improvements over the base policy (28% baseline). Post-editing samples provided the most significant single contribution (66%).
 - Combining components progressively improved performance. The best result (96% success rate) was achieved when all components (Transition + Demonstration + Post-editing, along with Pre-editing) were used jointly. This indicates the complementary nature of the different residual data types.
- **Number of Points for Optimization (Table VI):** Performance was robust within a moderate range of $N$ (number of points for local trajectory editing), peaking at $N=20$ (96% success rate). Too few points limited smoothness, while overly long segments degraded performance (80% at $N=40$). This suggests a balanced optimization horizon is crucial.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces TER-DAgger, a framework that addresses key limitations in applying imitation learning to contact-rich robotic tasks. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** By effectively mitigating covariate shift through the trajectory editing residual DAgger mechanism, the framework enables learned policies to perform more robustly in real-world environments, where deviations from training data are inevitable. This is critical for dependable automation of precision assembly tasks.
2. **Reduced Human Supervision Burden:** The force-aware error detection mechanism represents a step towards more scalable human-in-the-loop systems. By triggering intervention only when significant discrepancies in predicted and measured forces indicate an OOD state or imminent failure, it minimizes the requirement for continuous human monitoring. This could allow a single human expert to supervise multiple robots, significantly lowering operational costs and increasing deployment feasibility.
3. **Improved Safety and Compliance:** The integration of Cartesian impedance control ensures compliant interaction during physical contact. This is crucial for tasks like precision insertion, where excessive forces can damage delicate components or the robot itself. The policy's ability to predict future interaction forces further enhances its awareness and ability to manage contact.
4. **Novel Data Generation for Residual Learning:** The optimization-based trajectory editing method for generating residual training data is a novel contribution. It provides a structured approach to integrate human corrections smoothly, avoiding the distribution discontinuities often introduced by abrupt control switching in traditional DAgger-like methods. This consistent and stable supervision facilitates more effective learning of corrective behaviors.
5. **Generalizability:** While validated on precision insertion, the core principles of force-aware error detection, residual learning, and smooth trajectory blending could potentially be extended to other contact-rich manipulation and assembly tasks, thereby broadening the applicability of imitation learning in industrial automation.

The findings indicate that TER-DAgger offers a practical solution for deploying imitation learning in complex, contact-rich robotic applications, demonstrating an avenue for future development in human-robot collaboration and robust autonomous systems.
