# Visual Prompting in Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper by Junda Wu and colleagues systematically categorizes and examines visual prompting techniques for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It outlines how visual cues, such as bounding boxes and pixel-level masks, enhance MLLMs' abilities in visual grounding, object referring, and compositional reasoning by providing direct control over model attention.

## Problem

- Text-only instructions often struggle to precisely describe visual elements, leading to ambiguity and a lack of fine-grained control over Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
- MLLMs can suffer from visual hallucinations and language bias when solely relying on textual prompts for interpreting visual content.
- There was a lack of a comprehensive overview of visual prompting techniques specifically for MLLMs, distinguishing it from textual prompting for LLMs or visual prompting for pure vision models.

## Method

- The survey categorizes visual prompts into four main types: ==bounding boxes==, ==marker prompts==, ==pixel-level prompts== (detailed outlines), and ==soft prompts== operating in the model's feature space.
- Visual prompt generation methods include manual engineering, automatic generation via segmentation models like SAM, or object detection models to create precise cues.
- MLLMs are trained to interpret visual prompts through pre-training with prompt-augmented datasets, fine-tuning on smaller prompt-specific data, or via in-context learning through demonstrations.

## Results

- Visual prompts demonstrably improve MLLMs' visual grounding by connecting language expressions to specific visual elements, reducing misidentification.
- The use of visual prompts enables more precise object referring, clarifying ambiguous references in complex scenes with multiple similar objects.
- Models show enhanced compositional reasoning by processing relationships between highlighted elements, and improved navigation/planning in embodied AI by marking spatial cues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual prompts enhance MLLM capabilities in visual grounding, object referring, compositional reasoning, and navigation/planning by providing direct spatial and semantic guidance.
> - Effective visual prompts can be generated through engineering, segmentation-based, or detection-based approaches, with hybrid methods offering increased robustness.
> - Successfully integrating visual prompts requires specific learning strategies, including pre-training, fine-tuning, or in-context learning, along with careful alignment to human intent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have emerged as powerful tools that combine language understanding with visual perception capabilities. While much attention has been given to textual prompting methods, visual prompting—the use of visual cues to guide MLLMs—remains a relatively unexplored frontier with significant potential. The survey paper "Visual Prompting in Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey" by Junda Wu and colleagues offers a comprehensive examination of visual prompting techniques, their applications, and the challenges they address.

Visual prompting addresses fundamental limitations of text-only instructions when working with visual content. Traditional textual prompts often struggle to precisely describe visual elements, leading to issues like visual hallucinations and language bias. Visual prompts provide a more direct and nuanced way to instruct MLLMs about specific visual elements requiring attention or interpretation.

This review fills a significant gap in the research landscape. While existing surveys cover textual prompting for LLMs or visual prompting for pure vision models, none provide a comprehensive overview of visual prompting specifically for multimodal systems that integrate both language and vision capabilities.

### 2. Types of Visual Prompts

Visual prompts can be categorized into four primary types, each with distinct characteristics and applications:

1. **Bounding Box Prompts**: These are rectangular outlines that highlight specific regions of an image. Bounding boxes are particularly effective for tasks requiring attention to specific objects or areas, such as visual question answering about particular elements. They provide spatial localization but lack fine-grained object delineation.

2. **Marker Prompts**: These include points, arrows, circles, or numbered annotations that direct attention to specific locations or features. Markers are simpler than bounding boxes but can be combined to create complex attention patterns. They excel at highlighting small details or creating sequences of attention points.

3. **Pixel-level Prompts**: These involve modifications to the image pixels themselves, such as segmentation masks that precisely outline objects, or color overlays that highlight regions of interest. Pixel-level prompts offer the most detailed spatial information but may require sophisticated generation techniques.

4. **Soft Prompts**: Unlike the previous "hard" prompts that are directly visible in the image, soft prompts operate in the model's feature space. These might include learnable embeddings or attention maps that guide the model's focus without visible modifications to the input image. Soft prompts are particularly useful for end-to-end training and can be optimized for specific tasks.

Each type offers a trade-off between precision, ease of generation, and interpretability. The choice depends on the specific application requirements and the capabilities of the MLLM being used.

### 3. Visual Prompt Generation

Creating effective visual prompts is a crucial challenge. The survey identifies three main approaches to visual prompt generation:

1. **Engineering Approaches**: These involve manual creation or rule-based systems for generating visual prompts. While labor-intensive, they offer high precision and control. Engineering approaches are often used for creating demonstration datasets or for specialized applications requiring human expertise.

2. **Segmentation-based Generation**: This approach leverages segmentation models like Segment Anything Model (SAM) to automatically generate pixel-level prompts that outline objects or regions. Segmentation-based methods are effective for creating detailed masks but may struggle with abstract concepts or complex scenes.

3. **Detection-based Generation**: Object detection models can automatically generate bounding boxes or markers for identified objects. These methods are particularly useful for large-scale applications where manual annotation would be prohibitive. They excel at localizing common objects but may miss novel or uncommon entities.

Increasingly, researchers are exploring hybrid approaches that combine multiple generation techniques. For instance, a system might use object detection to identify regions of interest and then apply segmentation for fine-grained delineation. These hybrid methods can offer more robust and versatile prompt generation capabilities.

The quality of generated visual prompts significantly impacts MLLM performance. Poor quality prompts—those that are imprecise, misleading, or irrelevant—can degrade model performance worse than having no prompts at all. This highlights the importance of developing reliable and accurate prompt generation techniques.

### 4. Impact on Perception and Reasoning

Visual prompts substantially enhance MLLMs' abilities in several key areas:

1. **Visual Grounding**: Visual prompts improve the model's ability to connect language expressions with specific visual elements. For example, when asked to describe "the red object on the table," a visual prompt highlighting that object ensures the model focuses on the correct item rather than hallucinating or misidentifying it.

2. **Object Referring**: Visual prompts enable more precise object identification and description. Rather than ambiguous references like "the dog on the left," visual prompts can explicitly indicate which specific dog is being referenced, reducing ambiguity in complex scenes with multiple similar objects.

3. **Compositional Reasoning**: By highlighting multiple elements and their relationships, visual prompts enhance the model's ability to reason about how different parts of an image relate to each other. This is particularly valuable for tasks requiring understanding of spatial relationships or complex visual scenes.

4. **Navigation and Planning**: In embodied AI applications, visual prompts can mark waypoints, obstacles, or goals, helping models understand navigation instructions more effectively. This capability is especially valuable for robotics and virtual agents that need to interpret and execute complex spatial instructions.

The effectiveness of visual prompting varies across different MLLMs and tasks. Models specifically trained with visual prompting capabilities generally perform better than those that must interpret prompts without explicit training. The alignment between the model's training data and the style of visual prompts used at inference time also significantly impacts performance.

### 5. Learning and Alignment

For MLLMs to effectively utilize visual prompts, they must be properly trained to understand these cues. The survey identifies several learning approaches:

1. **Pre-training with Visual Prompts**: Incorporating visual prompts during the pre-training phase helps models develop a foundational understanding of what these cues represent. This approach requires large-scale datasets with visual prompts but creates models with robust prompt interpretation capabilities.

2. **Fine-tuning on Prompt-augmented Data**: Models pre-trained without visual prompts can be fine-tuned on smaller datasets that include these cues. This approach is more resource-efficient but may result in less generalizable prompt interpretation abilities.

3. **In-context Learning**: Some models can learn to interpret visual prompts through demonstration examples provided at inference time, without explicit training. This flexibility allows models to adapt to new prompt styles, but performance is typically lower than with explicit training.

A critical challenge in visual prompt learning is alignment—ensuring the model interprets visual prompts in the way humans intend. Misalignment can occur when models fail to attend to prompted regions or when they attend to them but misinterpret what they represent. Addressing this alignment problem requires careful dataset curation and explicit training objectives that encourage proper prompt interpretation.

The mathematical formulation for visual prompt learning can be represented as:

$$L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,p,y) \sim D} [L_{task}(f_\theta(x,p), y) + \lambda L_{align}(f_\theta, x, p)]$$

Where $x$ is the input image, $p$ is the visual prompt, $y$ is the target output, $f_\theta$ is the model with parameters $\theta$, $L_{task}$ is the task-specific loss, and $L_{align}$ is an alignment loss that encourages proper attention to prompted regions.

### 6. Challenges and Limitations

Despite their potential, visual prompting methods face several significant challenges:

1. **Prompt Ambiguity**: Visual prompts can be ambiguous, particularly when they overlap multiple objects or indicate regions with multiple potential interpretations. Resolving this ambiguity often requires additional contextual information or more precise prompt generation.

2. **Hallucination Problems**: MLLMs may still generate hallucinated content even with visual prompts, particularly when prompts are partial or imprecise. The model might fill in missing information with plausible but incorrect details.

3. **Domain and Distribution Shifts**: Models trained on one visual prompt style often struggle to generalize to different prompt styles or to visual domains not represented in their training data. This limits their practical applicability in diverse real-world scenarios.

4. **Data Scarcity**: High-quality datasets with visual prompts are scarce, making it difficult to train robust models. Creating such datasets is labor-intensive and requires domain expertise to ensure prompt quality and relevance.

5. **Evaluation Challenges**: There is no standardized evaluation framework for visual prompting methods, making it difficult to compare different approaches. Current evaluations often use task-specific metrics that may not capture the full range of prompt interpretation abilities.

These challenges highlight the need for continued research in visual prompting methods, particularly in creating more standardized benchmarks, improving prompt generation techniques, and developing more robust learning approaches.

### 7. Future Directions

The survey identifies several promising directions for future research:

1. **Unified Prompt Representation**: Developing frameworks that can handle multiple types of visual prompts within a single model would enhance flexibility and reduce the need for specialized models for different prompt types.

2. **Cross-modal Prompt Interaction**: Exploring how visual and textual prompts can complement each other could lead to more powerful multimodal systems. This includes understanding when each modality is most effective and how they can be combined optimally.

3. **Dynamic and Interactive Prompting**: Moving beyond static prompts to interactive systems where prompts are dynamically generated based on model responses could enable more natural and effective human-AI collaboration.

4. **Long-term Prompt Memory**: Developing methods for models to maintain contextual understanding of prompts across extended interactions or complex tasks could improve performance on multi-step reasoning tasks.

5. **Safety and Ethical Considerations**: As visual prompting becomes more powerful, ensuring these systems cannot be misused for deception or manipulation becomes increasingly important. Research into detecting manipulated prompts and ensuring robust performance is essential.

Researchers also emphasize the need for standardized benchmarks specifically designed to evaluate visual prompting capabilities. Current evaluations often repurpose existing datasets, which may not adequately test the full range of prompt interpretation abilities.

### 8. Conclusion

Visual prompting represents a significant advancement in multimodal AI, offering more precise control over MLLMs' attention and reasoning processes than text-only instructions. The survey by Wu et al. provides a comprehensive overview of this emerging field, categorizing existing approaches, identifying key challenges, and suggesting promising future directions.

By enabling more direct guidance of model attention, visual prompts help address fundamental limitations of textual instructions when working with visual content. They enhance models' abilities in visual grounding, object referring, and compositional reasoning, leading to more accurate and reliable multimodal understanding.

As research in this area continues to evolve, we can expect to see more sophisticated visual prompting methods that further bridge the gap between human visual communication and machine understanding. These advances will likely enable more natural and effective human-AI collaboration across a wide range of applications, from assistive technologies to creative tools and embodied AI systems.

The field of visual prompting in MLLMs remains relatively young but is rapidly developing. With continued research addressing the challenges identified in this survey, visual prompting has the potential to significantly enhance the capabilities and reliability of multimodal AI systems.
