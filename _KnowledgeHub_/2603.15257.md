# HapticVLA: Contact-Rich Manipulation via Vision-Language-Action Model without Inference-Time Tactile Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> HapticVLA enables tactile-aware, contact-rich manipulation for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models without needing physical tactile sensors during inference. It achieved an 86.7% average success rate on challenging pick-and-place tasks involving fragile objects, surpassing sensor-dependent baselines.

## Problem

- Conventional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack explicit force or tactile awareness, restricting their ability to perform safe and dexterous contact-rich manipulation, especially with fragile objects.
- Integrating physical tactile or force sensors into VLA systems, while improving performance, leads to increased hardware cost, reduced reproducibility, and compatibility issues across diverse robotic platforms.
- Existing tactile-enhanced VLA solutions typically require specialized tactile hardware to be present and active during inference, which limits their widespread adoption and scalability.

## Method

- Develops Safety-Aware Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (SA-RWFM) to train an offline VLA action expert, incorporating safety-aware tactile rewards that penalize excessive forces, high pressures, and slip during interaction.
- Implements a Tactile Distillation (TD) framework to transfer the learned tactile-aware capabilities from the SA-RWFM teacher VLA (which uses tactile sensors during training) into a conventional VLA student.
- The student VLA predicts a compact tactile token from its vision and state inputs at inference time, enabling tactile-aware action generation without requiring physical tactile sensors during deployment.

## Results

- Achieved a mean success rate of 86.7% across three contact-rich pick-and-place tasks (jar, waffles, egg), surpassing all benchmarked baseline VLA models.
- Demonstrated a 45% absolute improvement in success rate for egg manipulation compared to the foundational SmolVLA model, highlighting enhanced precision for fragile objects.
- The Safety-Aware Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (SA-RWFM) component, even when requiring tactile sensors at inference, led to a substantial performance improvement with a 75% mean success rate, validating the safety reward mechanism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The ability to perform tactile-aware, contact-rich manipulation can be learned effectively during an offline training phase by integrating safety-aware tactile rewards into a VLA action expert.
> - Knowledge distillation can successfully transfer complex tactile-aware capabilities from a sensor-dependent teacher policy to a sensor-agnostic student policy, enabling inference without physical tactile sensors.
> - Blending ground-truth actions with teacher-generated actions during distillation provides a robust training signal, leveraging both demonstrated behavior and the teacher's refined, reward-weighted insights.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an emerging paradigm in robotics, aiming to integrate visual perception and language understanding to generate robot control commands for complex manipulation tasks. These models typically rely on visual and proprioceptive inputs to produce motor outputs, enabling scalable manipulation in everyday scenarios. However, a significant limitation of conventional VLAs is their lack of explicit force or tactile awareness. This deficiency restricts their ability to perform safe and dexterous manipulation in contact-rich tasks, particularly when handling soft or fragile objects, as they cannot perceive material properties like hardness or solidity directly.

To address this, several recent VLA models have begun incorporating tactile or force modalities. Approaches typically involve integrating visual-force sensors on gripper fingers, which use internal cameras to capture surface deformations and infer contact forces, textures, and material properties. Examples include Tactile-VLA, VTLA, OmniVTLA, VLA-Touch, and MLA. However, these methods often encode raw tactile sensor signals as a visual modality, potentially overlooking the distinct nature of local mechanical interactions captured by tactile sensing compared to remote photometric properties captured by scene vision. A separate line of research incorporates force feedback directly from manipulator joint motors, as seen in ForceVLA and TaF-VLA, to achieve force-aware manipulation. A common challenge across these tactile- and force-enhanced VLAs is their reliance on specialized and often expensive tactile hardware during inference, which can increase system cost and reduce reproducibility and compatibility across diverse robotic platforms.

Another research direction explores contact-rich manipulation without requiring force sensors at inference time. Works by Gano et al. and Jones et al. demonstrate that pre-training policies with tactile data can improve vision-only manipulation. More closely related to the current work is FD-VLA, which uses force distillation to learn force awareness without requiring force sensors during deployment, mapping a learnable query token to a predicted force token aligned with actual force signals. This approach showed promise in performing force-aware manipulation by injecting a distilled force token into a pre-trained VLM.

This paper positions HapticVLA within this landscape by addressing the hardware dependency of tactile-aware VLAs. It builds upon the concept of distilling sensor information to enable inference-time sensor-agnostic operation, similar to FD-VLA's force distillation. However, HapticVLA focuses on tactile feedback and integrates it through a safety-aware reward mechanism within a reinforcement learning context for flow matching policies. The work leverages advancements in reinforcement learning for flow matching VLA policies, such as Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (RWFM), and cross-modal knowledge distillation techniques, which are used to transfer complex capabilities from large teacher models to more compact and efficient student policies. By proposing a method that learns tactile-aware manipulation offline and then distills this capability, HapticVLA aims to remove the inference-time need for physical tactile sensors, thereby offering a solution that is both effective and more deployable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of conventional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in contact-rich manipulation scenarios. Current VLAs predominantly rely on visual perception and proprioceptive feedback, which are insufficient for understanding nuanced physical interactions such as object deformability, surface textures, or precise contact forces. This lack of explicit force or tactile awareness severely restricts their ability to safely and dexterously manipulate fragile, soft, or complex objects. Consequently, tasks requiring careful grasping, varying pressure, or intricate insertion are challenging for standard VLA architectures.

While the integration of dedicated tactile or force sensors into VLA systems has demonstrated improved performance in contact-rich tasks, this approach introduces practical drawbacks. The necessity for specialized hardware, such as visual-force sensors on grippers or force sensors in manipulator joints, significantly increases the overall cost of robotic systems. Furthermore, the variability and complexity of integrating such sensors often lead to reduced reproducibility across different robotic platforms and can pose compatibility issues with commonly used grippers and manipulators. This hardware dependency limits the widespread adoption and scalability of tactile-aware manipulation capabilities.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Develop a methodology for learning tactile-aware manipulation offline:** The research aims to demonstrate that the ability to perform contact-rich manipulation with tactile awareness can be acquired during an offline training phase, rather than solely relying on direct, real-time haptic feedback. This involves incorporating safety-aware tactile rewards into the learning process.
2. **Integrate safety-aware tactile rewards into a VLA action expert:** A specific objective is to train a flow-matching action expert that can effectively utilize precomputed, safety-aware tactile rewards. These rewards are designed to penalize excessive grasping force, suboptimal grasping trajectories, and other unsafe contact patterns that could lead to object damage or task failure. This component, termed Safety-Aware Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (SA-RWFM), is intended to imbue the VLA with an understanding of safe interaction forces.
3. **Enable tactile-aware action generation without inference-time tactile sensing:** The central objective is to decouple tactile knowledge from the requirement for physical tactile sensors during deployment. This is achieved through a Tactile Distillation (TD) framework. The goal is to transfer the tactile-aware capabilities learned by the SA-RWFM trained teacher VLA into a conventional VLA student. The student VLA should then be able to predict a compact tactile token from its vision and state modalities, enabling tactile-aware action generation at inference time without any on-board tactile sensors.
4. **Enhance reproducibility and reduce cost for contact-rich VLA deployment:** By eliminating the need for real-time tactile sensor input during inference, the project aims to decrease the hardware complexity and cost associated with deploying VLAs for contact-rich tasks. This would facilitate the deployment of dexterous manipulation capabilities on a wider range of robotic setups that may lack specialized tactile sensing hardware.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the necessity of tactile awareness for safe, dexterous manipulation and the practical constraints imposed by dedicated tactile hardware, by learning and then distilling this critical capability into a more deployable VLA architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HapticVLA framework operates through two main, interconnected stages: Safety-Aware Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (SA-RWFM) and Tactile Distillation (TD). This design allows for the learning of tactile-aware manipulation offline and its deployment without direct haptic feedback during inference.

**A. Safety-Aware Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (SA-RWFM)**

This stage focuses on training a VLA action expert that incorporates safety-aware tactile rewards. It comprises several sub-components:

1. **Offline Tactile Reward Calculation:**
 - **Data Input:** At each timestep `t`, tactile maps `ML_t`, `MR_t` (H x W dimensions) from left and right tactile sensors are received.
 - **Normalization, Activation, and Calibration:** Raw tactile readings are normalized using dataset-level statistics. Reward thresholds (e.g., safe force band `[f_min, f_max]`, peak pressure `p_max`, concentration `c_max`, inter-pad force asymmetry `δ`) are calibrated from contact statistics in the dataset using quantiles.
 - **Contact Statistics:** For each active tactile side, mean force proxy `f_s_t`, peak pressure `p_s_t`, and pressure concentration `c_s_t` are computed from the tactile maps. The center of pressure (CoP) `(cop_s_x,t, cop_s_y,t)` is also calculated on a normalized grid.
 - **Holding and Slip Detection:** A binary holding state `h_t` indicates stable contact. Slip `slip_t` is detected during holding based on a CoP jump or a sudden force drop.
 - **Per-step Reward (`r_t`):** This reward is designed to penalize unsafe tactile patterns. It is a sum of weighted squared ReLU functions that activate for over-force, under-force (while holding), excessive peak pressure, high pressure concentration, significant inter-pad asymmetry, and slip.
 - **Episode Risk:** To capture near-failure behavior over a full trajectory, an episode-level risk score is computed. This involves the maximum normalized threshold exceedance across force, peak pressure, and concentration during holding, and the average slip over the episode. The risk score is clipped to ``.
 - **Final Episode Reward (`R_episode`):** The overall episode reward combines the averaged per-step reward, task outcome (success, drop, damage), and the computed safety risk penalty.

2. **SA-RWFM Fine-Tuning:**
 - **Base Model:** The SmolVLA architecture, optimized for edge deployment, serves as the foundational VLA and is fine-tuned.
 - **Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (RWFM) Objective:** The core idea is to weight each training sample in an offline dataset `D` based on its computed `R_episode`.
 - A masked per-sample flow matching loss `L_i` is calculated, considering task-dependent degrees of freedom (DoF) masks.
 - **Mixed Local/Global Returns:** For each action chunk, a discounted chunk return `R_chunk_t` (local reward) and the episode reward `R_episode` (global outcome) are combined.
 - **Robust Group-wise Normalization:** Scores are normalized within groups to address scale bias across datasets.
 - **Advantage-like Score (`A_i`):** A clipped combination of normalized episode and chunk scores determines an "advantage" for each sample.
 - **Exponentiated, Clipped, Renormalized Weights (`w_i`):** Raw weights `exp(αA_i)` are computed, clipped to `[w_min, w_max]`, and then renormalized within each batch. These weights dictate the influence of each sample on the learning process, giving higher-reward samples more prominence.
 - The `L_rwfm` is the weighted average of per-sample losses.
 - **Anchor Regularization:** To prevent drift and mode collapse often associated with exponentiated weighting in generative policies, an `L_anchor` term is added. This term constrains the current policy parameters to remain close to the initial imitation solution (`θ_0`). The total loss is `L_total = L_rwfm + λ_anchor L_anchor`. A warm-up schedule adjusts `α` and `λ_anchor` during training.
 - **Output:** A teacher VLA policy (`π_T`) that is capable of tactile-aware action generation, but still requires tactile sensor input (`s_T`) at inference time.

**B. Tactile Distillation (TD)**

This stage aims to transfer the tactile-aware capability of the SA-RWFM trained teacher (`π_T`) into a conventional VLA student, making it independent of tactile sensors during deployment.

1. **Problem Formulation:** The teacher policy `π_T(o, s_T)` takes visual observation `I`, language instruction `ℓ`, proprioceptive joint state `q`, and a tactile embedding `f` as input. The goal is for the student to achieve similar performance without `f`.
2. **Stage 1: Offline Teacher Target Generation:** The teacher policy (`π_T`) is run on the entire training dataset to generate "soft targets." For each training sample, the teacher predicts an action chunk `ˆa_T_i`. These predictions implicitly encode the teacher's tactile-conditioned force awareness and reward-weighted quality bias.
3. **Stage 2: Teacher Backbone Initialization:** The student VLA is initialized from the teacher's weights, selectively copying all parameters except those of the tactile encoder. The state augmentation projection is specifically adapted: the teacher's projection matrix `W_T` (mapping proprio-tactile state) is truncated to only include columns corresponding to proprioception, preserving the learned mapping from joint positions to the latent space while discarding tactile components.
4. **Stage 3: Blended Target Training:** The student VLA is then trained using a distillation loss. The target actions `˜a_i` for this training are a blend of ground-truth demonstration actions (`a_GT_i`) and the teacher's predicted actions (`ˆa_T_i`), using a blending coefficient `α=0.5`. This blending strategy anchors the student to ground-truth behavior while injecting the teacher's tactile-aware shaping. During validation, `α` is set to `0` for unbiased evaluation.
 - **Output:** The final HapticVLA model, which is a distilled VLA capable of tactile-aware, contact-rich manipulation without requiring direct tactile sensor measurements during inference.

**C. Dataset Collection**

- **Real-world Dataset:** An in-house dataset was collected with 310 episodes across three tasks: 70 successful and 20 faulty episodes for each single-arm task, and 100 successful and 30 faulty episodes for bimanual tasks. Faulty episodes involved excessive grasping force leading to object damage, providing critical negative examples for the SA-RWFM expert.
- **Digital Twin Dataset:** To augment the dataset size and improve robustness to pose variations, 1,000 additional pick-and-place episodes were generated in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, a high-fidelity digital twin of the robotic setup.

**D. Experimental Setup**

- **Robotic Platform:** A bimanual setup with two LeRobot SO-101 manipulators. The right arm featured a parallel gripper equipped with tactile sensors.
- **Perception:** Three RGB camera streams: an Intel RealSense D435 for an external view and two wrist-mounted IMX335 cameras, all at 640x480 resolution.
- **Tactile Sensors:** High-density tactile sensor arrays (10x10 taxels per fingertip, 200 total) were embedded in the gripper fingertips, offering a force detection range of 1–9 N per point at 120 Hz.
- **Compute:** An NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX 16 GB edge computer.

**E. Experimental Tasks**

Evaluation was performed on three contact-rich pick-and-place tasks involving objects with varying fragility:
- **Jar pick-and-place:** Manipulating a deformable plastic jar. Minor surface deformations were allowed if the jar retained its shape.
- **Waffles pick-and-place:** Manipulating fragile packaged waffles, requiring precise force and position. Waffles must remain fully intact.
- **Egg pick-and-place:** Manipulating an egg, requiring high precision in grasp force and width. The egg must remain completely intact.

Each model underwent 20 test trials per task, with success defined by the object remaining intact and correctly placed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of HapticVLA was conducted across three contact-rich manipulation tasks and benchmarked against several VLA baselines, with a particular focus on the impact of Safety-Aware Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (SA-RWFM) and Tactile Distillation (TD).

**Overall Performance:**
HapticVLA achieved a mean success rate of 86.7% across the "Jar pick-and-place," "Waffles pick-and-place," and "Egg pick-and-place" tasks. This performance consistently surpassed all benchmarked baseline models. For instance, in egg manipulation, HapticVLA demonstrated a 45% absolute improvement over the foundational SmolVLA model.

**Baseline Performance Comparison:**
- **SmolVLA (Base Model):** This foundational VLA model struggled with the contact-rich nature of the tasks, particularly when manipulating highly fragile objects like waffles and eggs. Its performance was significantly lower than HapticVLA.
- **SmolVLA with SA-RWFM Action Expert:** Integrating the SA-RWFM action expert into SmolVLA, which means the model *requires* tactile sensors during inference, resulted in a substantial performance improvement. This configuration achieved a mean success rate of 75%, demonstrating the effectiveness of incorporating safety-aware tactile rewards. It reached an 85% success rate in two out of the three tasks.
- **X-VLA and VLA-0:** These larger, pre-trained VLA models exhibited a zero-percent success rate across all contact-rich tasks. This outcome suggests that these models failed to generalize to the specific high-precision and contact-rich requirements of the experimental setup and tasks, highlighting a limitation in their adaptability to scenarios where explicit tactile or force awareness is crucial.

These results indicate that while SmolVLA with SA-RWFM effectively learns tactile-aware manipulation, HapticVLA (which does not require tactile sensors at inference) further refines and stabilizes this capability.

**Ablation Study of Tactile Distillation (TD):**
An ablation study was conducted to quantify the contribution of the Tactile Distillation (TD) component. Four configurations were evaluated:
- **"w/o TD, async":** SmolVLA with SA-RWFM and asynchronous inference (requires tactile sensors at inference). Mean success rate: 81.7%.
- **"w/o TD":** SmolVLA with SA-RWFM and synchronous inference (requires tactile sensors at inference). Mean success rate: 75%.
- **"w/ TD, async":** HapticVLA (with SA-RWFM and TD) with asynchronous inference. Mean success rate: 80%.
- **"w/ TD":** HapticVLA (with SA-RWFM and TD) with synchronous inference. Mean success rate: 86.7%.

The ablation study demonstrated that Tactile Distillation substantially improved contact-rich manipulation performance. The configuration combining TD with SA-RWFM achieved the highest mean success rate (86.7%), confirming the efficacy of the complete HapticVLA pipeline. A notable observation from the ablation study was that asynchronous inference, in some cases, led to a considerable performance degradation compared to synchronous counterparts. The authors attributed this to potential temporal misalignment and increased effective latency between tactile observations (or their distilled representations) and control actions. Crucially, HapticVLA ("w/ TD") performed better than the teacher model even when the teacher was provided with direct tactile feedback during inference (e.g., "w/o TD, async" at 81.7%), suggesting that the distillation process not only removed hardware dependency but also potentially stabilized or refined the learned behavior.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that HapticVLA successfully enables tactile-aware, contact-rich manipulation without requiring inference-time tactile sensing. The SA-RWFM component effectively imbues the VLA with safety awareness, and the subsequent Tactile Distillation process successfully transfers this capability, leading to robust performance that exceeds baselines, including those equipped with physical tactile sensors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HapticVLA framework introduces a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models operating in contact-rich environments. Its significance and potential impact can be considered from several perspectives:

1. **Increased Accessibility and Reproducibility of Tactile-Aware Manipulation:** The primary contribution of HapticVLA is its ability to enable tactile-aware manipulation without requiring physical tactile sensors during inference. This innovation directly addresses a key limitation of existing approaches, which often rely on expensive, complex, and platform-specific tactile hardware. By eliminating this hardware dependency, HapticVLA reduces the overall cost and complexity of robotic systems capable of dexterous, contact-rich tasks. This can significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, fostering greater reproducibility of results and enabling wider deployment of advanced manipulation capabilities across diverse robotic platforms that may lack specialized tactile sensing.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Precision in Handling Fragile Objects:** The integration of Safety-Aware Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (SA-RWFM) allows HapticVLA to learn from precomputed tactile rewards that penalize excessive forces and suboptimal grasping trajectories. This explicit focus on safety during the offline training phase contributes to more robust and precise manipulation. The experimental results, particularly the substantial improvements in handling fragile objects like waffles and eggs, demonstrate that HapticVLA can perform tasks requiring fine force control while minimizing damage. This has implications for industries dealing with delicate items, such as food processing, electronics manufacturing, and healthcare.

3. **Advancement in Knowledge Distillation for Robotics:** HapticVLA provides a novel application of knowledge distillation, where a specific sensory modality's "awareness" (tactile feedback) is transferred from a sensor-equipped teacher to a sensor-agnostic student. Unlike previous distillation methods that might focus on architectural efficiency or general skill transfer, HapticVLA specifically distills a compact tactile token from vision and state inputs. This approach expands the scope of knowledge distillation, demonstrating its utility in decoupling critical sensory capabilities from their physical sensing requirements, potentially inspiring similar methods for other specialized modalities.

4. **Broader Practical Applicability of VLAs:** By overcoming the tactile sensor bottleneck, HapticVLA expands the practical applicability of VLA models. Robots equipped with HapticVLA could be deployed in a wider range of real-world scenarios, from logistics and manufacturing to domestic assistance, where contact-rich interactions with diverse objects are common. The ability to perform safe manipulation without expensive sensor upgrades makes advanced VLA capabilities more feasible for mainstream robotic applications.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of HapticVLA in distilling tactile awareness opens avenues for future research. This includes exploring the distillation of other complex sensory information (e.g., haptic texture, thermal cues) or intricate interaction behaviors into simpler, vision- and proprioception-based policies. Furthermore, the framework's use of offline reinforcement learning with safety rewards could inspire new methods for injecting nuanced physical constraints into general-purpose robotic learning.

In conclusion, HapticVLA represents a significant step towards more adaptable, cost-effective, and robust robotic systems. By enabling tactile-aware contact-rich manipulation without the need for inference-time tactile sensing, it removes a practical barrier to the widespread adoption of advanced VLA capabilities, offering a pathway for safer and more dexterous robots in real-world environments.
