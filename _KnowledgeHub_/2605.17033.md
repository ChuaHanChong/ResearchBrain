# Generalizable and Actionable Parts Pose Estimation with Symmetry Annotation-Free Learning Strategy

> [!summary] Summary
> SAFAG, a new framework for Generalizable and Actionable Parts (GAParts) pose estimation, addresses the multi-solution problem for symmetric parts and eliminates the need for explicit symmetry annotations. Combining a two-stage quaternion regression with a self-supervised symmetry learning strategy, SAFAG achieved superior pose estimation on the GAPartNet dataset, with average rotation errors of 3.23° for seen categories and 10.83° for unseen categories, outperforming existing methods.

## Problem

- Generalizable and Actionable Parts (GAParts) often exhibit high degrees of symmetry, leading to a "multi-solution problem" where multiple 6D poses can be functionally equivalent, making ground truth pose ambiguous.
- Many existing symmetry-aware pose estimation methods rely heavily on costly and difficult-to-obtain explicit symmetry annotations (e.g., planes or axes), limiting their practical applicability and generalizability in data-scarce scenarios.

## Method

- Proposed SAFAG, a symmetry annotation-free framework that uses a two-stage quaternion regression to generate and refine multiple pose candidates, effectively addressing the multi-solution problem for symmetric parts.
- Developed a self-adaptive, implicit, and self-supervised network that estimates symmetry axes or planes by modeling probability distributions, removing the dependency on explicit symmetry annotations.
- Incorporated a novel HyperS3 convolution layer within a 3D Graph Convolution Network backbone to extract rotation-aware representations from partial point clouds, enhancing feature learning.

## Results

- SAFAG achieved superior pose estimation on the GAPartNet dataset, with average rotation errors of **3.23**° for seen categories (a **39.1%** reduction) and **10.83**° for unseen categories (an **8.68%** reduction), surpassing existing baselines.
- Demonstrated approximately **20%** and **19%** reductions in translation errors for seen and unseen categories, respectively, compared to the second-best methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical impact of the self-adaptive symmetry learning strategy, reducing rotation error by **79.9%** for seen categories and **67.3%** for unseen categories when enabled.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Symmetry reasoning can be effectively formulated as a distribution estimation problem, enabling implicit learning of symmetry properties without requiring explicit, manual annotations.
> - A two-stage quaternion regression framework, by generating and refining multiple candidates, robustly handles the multi-solution problem of symmetric parts, providing a more accurate representation of equivalent poses.
> - The use of rotation-aware HyperS3 convolution and refinement on the quaternion hypersphere significantly improves the precision and robustness of pose estimation, especially for parts with complex symmetries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI, specifically addressing challenges in robot object interaction and manipulation through high-quality cross-category object perception. A fundamental task in this area is model-based generalizable manipulation and interaction, which involves 6D pose estimation. Traditional 6D pose estimation has been studied at both instance-level and category-level. Recent efforts have focused on cross-category generalization, enabling pose estimation for objects unseen during training.

A specific sub-area gaining attention is the understanding of Generalizable and Actionable Parts (GAParts), introduced by Geng et al. (2023). This approach focuses on estimating the pose of object parts rather than entire objects, based on the rationale that robots often interact with parts, and part classes are more fundamental. However, GAParts frequently exhibit higher degrees of symmetry compared to whole objects, which introduces two primary challenges for existing pose estimation methods:
1. **Multi-solution Problem:** For symmetric objects or parts, the ground truth pose is not unique; an entire set of equivalent poses may offer similar affordances. Current methods, such as GAPartNet, sometimes simplify this by enforcing a unique pose solution (e.g., through Normalized Part Coordinate Space - NPCS), which can compromise accuracy. Generative approaches like GenPose (Zhang et al., 2023) and RFMPose (Ouyang et al., 2026) have been introduced to model inherent one-to-many mappings by stochastically sampling pose hypotheses.
2. **Annotation Dependency:** Many symmetry-aware pose estimation methods (e.g., GASEM, DFGAP, SARNet, SGPA, NOCS) rely on extensive and precise symmetry annotations, such as symmetry planes or axes, to design symmetry-aware loss functions or augment ground truth poses. Obtaining these annotations is often costly and difficult, limiting the generalizability and practicality of these methods in data-scarce environments.

This work, SAFAG (Symmetry Annotation-Free framework for Generalizable and Actionable Parts Pose Estimation), positions itself by directly addressing these two challenges. It aims to achieve precise GAPart pose estimation by tackling the multi-solution problem implicitly and enabling symmetry learning without requiring explicit symmetry annotations. By eliminating the dependency on costly symmetry annotations, SAFAG seeks to enhance the generalizability and practical applicability of GAPart pose estimation in real-world scenarios, particularly for robot manipulation tasks where cross-category generalization and robustness to symmetry are critical.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, SAFAG, for Generalizable and Actionable Parts (GAParts) pose estimation that overcomes limitations of existing methods, particularly concerning symmetry.

The motivation for this objective stems from two critical problems identified in the current landscape of GAPart understanding:

1. **Addressing the Multi-Solution Problem in Symmetric GAParts:** GAParts, by their nature, often exhibit rotational or mirror symmetries. For such parts, multiple 6D poses can be functionally equivalent or visually indistinguishable. For instance, rotating a pot lid by 180° around its symmetry axis might yield the same manipulation affordance. Current methods often struggle with this "multi-solution" problem. Some approaches enforce a single, unique pose solution, which can lead to reduced accuracy because the network is trained to predict one specific ground truth when several are valid. The objective is to design a system that can effectively handle this inherent ambiguity without sacrificing precision.

2. **Eliminating Dependency on Symmetry Annotations:** Many advanced pose estimation techniques that attempt to address symmetry rely heavily on explicit symmetry annotations (e.g., precise definitions of symmetry planes or axes). Such annotations are resource-intensive to acquire and difficult to maintain across diverse object categories and data-lacking scenarios. This dependency severely constrains the practicality and generalizability of these methods in real-world applications. The motivation is to develop a learning strategy that can implicitly understand and leverage symmetry properties from the data itself, thereby circumventing the need for manual symmetry annotation.

Specifically, the key objectives articulated in the paper are:
- To propose SAFAG, a symmetry annotation-free framework for generalizable and actionable parts pose estimation.
- To design a stepwise refinement two-stage framework for quaternion regression that generates candidate poses and then aggregates them into a final pose.
- To implement an implicit and self-supervised learning strategy for robustly predicting symmetry axes or planes without ground truth annotations for these features.
- To demonstrate superior performance and robustness of SAFAG on the GAPartNet dataset and through real-world experiments, thereby showcasing its potential for embodied AI systems, especially in generalizable robot manipulation.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to advance cross-category object perception for robots, making manipulation and interaction more precise, generalizable, and practical in diverse environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, SAFAG (Symmetry Annotation-Free framework for Generalizable and Actionable Parts Pose Estimation), consists of a two-stage quaternion regression approach with a self-supervised symmetry learning strategy. The input is a partial point cloud of an observed object.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Framework

SAFAG is structured into four main components:
1. **HyperS3 Convolution for Quaternion Regression:** A modified 3D Graph Convolution Network (3D-GCN) backbone with a tailored HyperS3 convolution layer designed for learning rotation-aware representations consistent with the S3 hyperspherical manifold.
2. **Quaternion Regression with Candidates to Final:** A two-stage process where initial quaternion candidates are generated, refined on the hyperspherical manifold, and then aggregated to produce a final pose.
3. **Self-Adaptive Symmetry-Aware Design:** An implicit and self-supervised network that estimates symmetry axes or planes by modeling probability distributions, without requiring explicit symmetry annotations. This allows for the construction of symmetric equivalent pose sets for supervision.
4. **Training Strategy and Loss Function:** A two-stage training scheme that uses symmetry-aware loss functions, including explicit equivalent solution losses for rotational and mirror symmetries, and a mirror-consistency loss.

#### 3.2. HyperS3 Convolution for Quaternion Regression

The backbone feature extractor is a 3D-GCN enhanced with a novel HyperS3 convolution layer. This layer aims to produce rotation-aware representations.
- **Local Coordinate Frame:** For each point in the input point cloud, a local neighborhood is identified using KNN. A rotation-invariant local coordinate frame (E_i ∈ SO(3)) is derived from the local covariance matrix (S_i) of these neighbors. This involves computing principal directions (e_1,i) and orthogonal secondary (e_2,i) and tertiary (e_3,i) directions.
- **Dual Branch Feature Extraction:** Directional vectors (R_ij) are transformed into this local frame (R_(local)_ij). Convolution is applied in two parallel branches: one on R_(local)_ij (producing F_align_i, rotation-sensitive features) and another on R_ij in the Euclidean frame (producing F_euclid_i, preserving original geometric structure).
- **Adaptive Fusion:** A confidence weight (α_i) is generated by a small CNN based on the trace and anisotropy of the covariance matrix S_i. This weight adaptively fuses the two feature branches: F_i = α_i * F_align_i + (1 − α_i) * F_euclid_i.
- **Performance Enhancement:** The features also incorporate modules from HS-Pose (STE for translation preservation and ORL for outlier robustness), resulting in the final point cloud feature F_pc.

#### 3.3. Quaternion Regression with Candidates to Final

This component addresses the multi-solution problem by predicting multiple candidates and refining them.
- **Motivation for Quaternion:** Quaternions are chosen over rotation matrices due to their compact, singularity-free, and coupled representation of 6DoF rotation, simplifying candidate generation and refinement.
- **Candidate Generation:** K (set to 64) initial quaternion candidates (Q = {q1..., qk}) are generated. This involves concatenating the point cloud feature F_pc with randomly sampled noise vectors (z_i) to form perturbed input features (F_cand_i). These are processed in parallel by a shared CNN.
- **Candidates Refinement:** The candidates are then refined in the tangent space of the unit quaternion hypersphere.
 - **Candidates Encoder:** To provide context for offset prediction, a specialized encoder learns implicit distribution information from the candidates. This involves:
 - Calculating a mean quaternion (q_mean) after aligning the signs of individual candidates.
 - Computing residual quaternions (r_i) between each candidate and q_mean.
 - Converting r_i into axis-angle representations (δ_i ∈ R3) to represent deviation in tangent space.
 - Computing mean offset (¯δ), averaged squared cosine similarity (¯μ), and eigenvalues/eigenvectors ({λ_j}, {v_j}) of the second moment matrix of offsets (M) to capture cluster tightness and orientation.
 - **Feature Embedding:** These descriptive quantities are concatenated into F_embedding, which is then fused with F_pc and fed into a linear layer to obtain F_fused.
 - **Offset Prediction and Aggregation:** A small CNN uses F_fused to predict offsets (∆Q) in the tangent space for each candidate. The refined quaternions (∆Q_correct) are then aggregated by a linear layer to produce the final quaternion (q_final).

#### 3.4. Self-Adaptive Symmetry-Aware Design

This component learns symmetry implicitly, without annotations.
- **Symmetry as Distribution Estimation:** Symmetry reasoning is formulated as a distribution estimation problem over symmetry axes or planes.
- **Rotational Symmetry:** The final quaternion (q_final) is converted to its axis-angle representation (F_rot). F_rot and F_pc are processed by CNNs, concatenated, and used to predict a probability distribution (π_x, π_y, π_z) for symmetry along x, y, and z axes. The symmetry axis (n) is obtained via a weighted combination of canonical axes.
- **Mirror Symmetry:** This involves predicting plane normals and their positions. The framework predicts a principal normal (n) and a secondary orthogonal normal (n'). A third orthogonal direction (n'') is derived.
 - **Mirror-Consistency Metric:** For each candidate normal (u_j), a reflected point cloud (P'_(j)) is generated. The Chamfer distance (L_geom(P, u_j)) between the original and reflected point clouds quantifies geometric symmetry.
 - **Geometry-Aware Scores:** These scores (s_j) are calculated by normalizing the inverse of L_geom, which are used to suppress outlier plane predictions. The entire process of symmetry learning is self-supervised.

#### 3.5. Training Strategy and Loss Function

Training is divided into two stages:
- **Warm-up Stage (Rotational Symmetry):** Only candidate quaternions and their distributions are constrained using symmetry-aware loss, encouraging candidates to cluster around equivalent solutions. Equivalent solutions (Q_eq_k) are generated by sampling rotation angles around the x, y, z axes from the ground truth. A soft-relaxed minimum angular distance (L_rot_warm) is calculated between candidates and these equivalent sets.
- **Main Stage (Rotational Symmetry):** The hypothetical symmetry axes are replaced by the predicted symmetry axis (n) from the self-adaptive network to generate the equivalent solutions (Q_eq). Separate losses (L_rot_cand and L_rot_final) are computed for candidates and the final quaternion against this predicted equivalent set.
- **Mirror Symmetry:** The loss (L_mirror) comprises an angular deviation term (L_mirror_angle) similar to L_rot, but using reflection-based equivalent solutions, and a mirror-consistency loss (L_mirror_geom). In the warm-up, L_mirror_geom is evaluated over canonical x, y, z plane normals. In the main stage, it uses the predicted mirror-plane normals.
- **Asymmetric Objects:** For asymmetric objects, the loss is a direct measure of angular deviation between prediction and ground truth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SAFAG was evaluated on the GAPartNet dataset, derived from PartNet-Mobility and AKB-48, encompassing 9 GAPart classes across 27 object categories. Performance was assessed using rotation error (degrees) and translation error (cm), along with Average Precision (AP) under various thresholds. Additional experiments included real-world evaluations and cross-dataset generalization tests.

#### 4.1. Pose Estimation Results

SAFAG was compared against GAPartNet, GASEM, DFGAP (designed for GAParts), and GenPose++, RFMPose (category-level object pose estimation methods).
- **Overall Performance:** SAFAG consistently demonstrated superior performance across both "seen" and "unseen" categories of GAParts.
 - **Rotation (Seen Categories):** SAFAG achieved an average rotation error of 3.23°, which is a 39.1% reduction compared to the second-best method. All categories exhibited rotation errors within 10°.
 - **Rotation (Unseen Categories):** The average rotation error was 10.83°, an 8.68% reduction over the second-best method.
 - **Translation (Seen and Unseen Categories):** SAFAG achieved the lowest translation errors, with approximately 20% and 19% reductions respectively, compared to the second-best methods.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - SAFAG outperformed GAPartNet and GASEM, suggesting that its approach, which moves beyond reliance on precise NPCS prediction, yields better results.
 - It showed highly competitive performance against DFGAP, despite DFGAP's specific design for depth-free conditions and symmetry-aware loss, indicating the strength of SAFAG's multi-modality approach.
 - SAFAG significantly surpassed category-level methods like GenPose++ and RFMPose, especially on highly symmetric parts (e.g., Slider Button, Hinge Knob, Hinge Lid). This highlights the importance of specific designs for the richer symmetry prevalent in GAParts, which these category-level methods lack.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Symmetry Analysis for Rotation Estimation (Table 4):** Removing the symmetry-aware network led to a substantial increase in rotation error. For seen categories, the average error decreased by 79.9% with the symmetry-aware design, and for unseen categories, it decreased by 67.3%. This demonstrates the critical role of the self-adaptive symmetry learning strategy.
- **Backbone Analysis (Table 2):** The proposed HyperS3 layer within the 3D-GCN backbone contributed to performance improvements. Compared to a baseline HS-Layer, HyperS3 reduced average rotation error by 28.2% (seen) and 7.8% (unseen). It also improved threshold-based metrics (10°10cm, 5°5cm, 5°2cm) by 3-9% for seen categories and 3-7% for unseen categories.
- **Explicit Equivalent Solution Analysis (Table 3):** Using an explicit equivalent solution loss for rotational symmetry objects resulted in significantly lower rotation errors compared to directly supervising the angular difference around the symmetry axis. Errors were reduced by approximately 60-80% for seen categories and 20-60% for unseen categories, indicating that modeling the distribution of equivalent rotations is more effective.
- **Parameter Sensitivity (Table 5):**
 - **Number of Candidates (K):** The model showed minor sensitivity to K (32, 64, 128), with K=128 yielding slightly better results. K had negligible impact on inference speed.
 - **Number of Sampled Equivalent Poses (N_eq):** Performance improved with larger N_eq (18, 36, 72), as it provided denser supervision. However, a larger N_eq increased training computational cost. N_eq=36 was selected as a balance.
- **Impact of Symmetry Misclassification (Table 6):**
 - When rotationally symmetric objects were incorrectly labeled as mirror-symmetric, the model’s performance degraded but still captured some underlying symmetry (e.g., 10.06° seen error vs. 3.35° correct).
 - When mislabeled as asymmetric, performance degraded noticeably (e.g., 25.01° seen error), indicating the model failed to recognize valid equivalent solutions. This highlights the robustness of the symmetry-aware design even with partial mislabeling, but also the importance of general symmetry class identification.

#### 4.3. Real-world Evaluation (Qualitative)

Qualitative results from real-world experiments on four GAPart classes (Hinge Door, Hinge Lid, Slider Drawer, Hinge Handle) demonstrated the robustness and generalizability of SAFAG, accurately estimating poses in unconstrained environments.

#### 4.4. First-stage Candidate Quality Analysis (Appendix B.2)

The analysis revealed that the initial candidates generated in the first stage formed a reasonable pose neighborhood around the ground truth. The errors between candidates and ground truth (Cand vs. GT) were consistent with errors between candidates and the final prediction (Cand vs. Final). The final prediction (GT vs. Final) showed further reduction in error, confirming the effectiveness of the second-stage aggregation.

#### 4.5. Cross-Dataset Generalization (Appendix B.3)

Experiments on the ClearPose dataset, a non-part-centric dataset, demonstrated SAFAG's generalization ability. For objects with clear symmetry (plate, water cup, wine cup, bowl), SAFAG maintained low rotation errors (1.52° to 3.07°) and translation errors (0.007 to 0.008 cm), suggesting its applicability beyond GAPartNet.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SAFAG represents a contribution to the field of 6D pose estimation, particularly for Generalizable and Actionable Parts (GAParts). The core significance of this work lies in its ability to address the multi-hypothesis problem induced by object symmetries and, crucially, to do so without requiring explicit symmetry annotations. This annotation-free learning strategy offers several potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Generalizability and Practicality:** By removing the dependency on costly and difficult-to-obtain symmetry annotations, SAFAG increases the generalizability of GAPart pose estimation methods to diverse object categories and data-scarce scenarios. This improves the practicality of deploying such systems in real-world environments where extensive manual annotation is not feasible.
- **Improved Precision in Symmetric Object Pose Estimation:** The proposed stepwise refinement two-stage framework, combined with the self-supervised symmetry learning strategy, allows the model to implicitly understand and account for rotational and mirror symmetries. This approach leads to more precise and robust pose estimations for parts exhibiting such symmetries, as evidenced by the superior performance on the GAPartNet dataset compared to methods that either ignore symmetry or rely on explicit annotations.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI and Robotics:** High-quality GAPart perception is a foundational requirement for generalizable robot object interaction and manipulation. SAFAG's ability to provide accurate and robust GAPart pose estimations has the potential to significantly advance downstream embodied AI tasks such as robot grasping, assembly, and complex manipulation. The qualitative results from real-world robot manipulation tasks illustrate this potential, enabling robots to interact with objects more effectively across categories.
- **Methodological Contributions:** The introduction of the HyperS3 convolution layer, designed for rotation-aware representations on the S3 hyperspherical manifold, and the quaternion regression framework with candidate generation and refinement, provide novel architectural components that could be adapted to other pose estimation tasks. The formulation of symmetry reasoning as a distribution estimation problem, coupled with a self-supervised mirror-consistency metric, offers a new paradigm for handling symmetry without explicit labels.
- **Broader Applications:** Beyond robotics, the framework's capabilities in robust and generalizable 6D pose estimation without specific symmetry annotations could find applications in other domains requiring 3D object understanding, such as augmented reality, virtual reality, industrial automation, and 3D scene reconstruction.

Overall, SAFAG's approach to achieving accurate and generalizable GAPart pose estimation by effectively addressing symmetry without manual annotations moves the field closer to developing more autonomous and adaptable embodied AI systems.
