# PlaM: Training-Free Plateau-Guided Model Merging for Better Visual Grounding in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Northeastern University, China, and LMU Munich developed PlaM, a training-free model merging framework that addresses linguistic reasoning degradation in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It consistently improved visual grounding and overall performance across various MLLMs and benchmarks, for instance, boosting MMStar by +1.52 and MME-C by +15.00 for LLaVA-v1.5-7B.

## Problem

- Multimodal instruction fine-tuning in MLLMs often paradoxically degrades the base language model's inherent linguistic reasoning capabilities.
- This degradation leads to poor visual grounding, where MLLMs exhibit scattered attention and make incorrect inferences despite visual input.
- Existing solutions to mitigate linguistic reasoning degradation are training-based, requiring substantial computational resources and complex data curation.

## Method

- PlaM employs a ==layer-wise vision token masking strategy== to diagnose and identify a consistent three-stage pattern of visual information utilization within MLLM decoders.
- A training-free, ==plateau-guided model merging approach== selectively injects parameters from the original base language model into the fine-tuned MLLM.
- Only the self-attention projection weights of decoder layers from an identified =="plateau onset layer"== to the final layer are merged via linear interpolation, leaving early layers and the vision encoder/projector fixed.

## Results

- PlaM consistently improved performance across **five** MLLM architectures (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5, Qwen2.5/3-VL) and **nine** diverse vision-language benchmarks, with notable gains on MMStar (**+1.52**), MME-C (**+15.00**), and RealWorldQA (**+0.26**) for LLaVA-v1.5-7B.
- The method significantly outperformed alternative merging strategies (early-layer, mid-layer, full-layer merging), confirming the importance of plateau-guided, selective intervention.
- Attention analysis revealed that PlaM merging substantially increased attention mass to vision tokens and led to more focused, task-relevant visual grounding in case studies, improving decision-making.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit a consistent three-stage pattern in their decoder's visual information processing: early feature separation, mid-layer alignment/fusion, and late-layer feature degradation or plateau.
> - The performance plateau in late decoder layers is primarily linked to weakened language capabilities rather than insufficient visual information, making these layers ideal targets for restoring linguistic reasoning.
> - Selective, late-layer parameter merging from the base LLM effectively recovers linguistic abstraction and improves visual grounding without disrupting critical early-to-mid multimodal alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like GPT-4V and Gemini has marked a significant paradigm shift in AI, enabling machines to understand and generate content across text and visual modalities. However, this paper addresses a critical and increasingly recognized challenge within this landscape: the "multimodal instruction fine-tuning paradox." While instruction fine-tuning is crucial for MLLMs to develop impressive multimodal reasoning capabilities, it often comes at the cost of degrading the base language model's original linguistic reasoning ability. This degradation is problematic because strong textual reasoning is the backbone that enables MLLMs to compose coherent responses, interpret complex instructions, and effectively leverage visual cues. When this backbone weakens, models can suffer from poor visual grounding, leading to scattered attention and incorrect inferences, as illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper.

Prior research has identified this problem, with mounting evidence suggesting MLLMs often remain text-dominant, under-utilizing visual information and resorting to textual knowledge even for visual questions (e.g., Wu et al., 2025b; Liu et al., 2025). Attempts to mitigate this language reasoning degradation have largely focused on *training-based* solutions. These include introducing auxiliary architectural components (Zhang et al., 2024), replaying or interleaving text-only data during multimodal training (McKinzie et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024d), or using preference alignment objectives (Li et al., 2024c). While effective, these methods demand substantial additional computational resources, carefully curated datasets, or significant architectural modifications, making them costly and complex to implement.

This paper distinguishes itself by exploring a comparatively *underexplored* area: *training-free* solutions. Previous training-free efforts have touched upon the influence of base LLM choice (Ratzlaff et al., 2025) or steering modality preference through latent representation engineering (Zhang et al., 2025b). However, PlaM is novel in proposing a general, efficient, and training-free method that explicitly aims to restore language model functionality in specific, underutilized late layers of MLLMs. By identifying a consistent three-stage pattern of visual information utilization within MLLM decoders, PlaM offers a targeted intervention, moving beyond broad architectural or training regime changes to a precise, layer-wise parameter manipulation. This work contributes significantly by offering a practical, low-cost solution to a pervasive problem, potentially democratizing access to improved MLLM performance without the prohibitive costs of retraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the PlaM research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Address Linguistic Reasoning Degradation:** The core motivation stems from the observed phenomenon where multimodal instruction fine-tuning, while enhancing multimodal capabilities, paradoxically degrades the base language model's inherent text reasoning abilities. This degradation weakens the model's capacity for coherent thought, logical inference, and general language understanding, which are essential for robust multimodal performance.
2. **Improve Visual Grounding:** A direct consequence of degraded linguistic reasoning and potentially sub-optimal multimodal alignment is poor visual grounding. MLLMs often exhibit diffuse or semantically irrelevant attention patterns over images, failing to focus on task-relevant visual regions to derive correct answers. The research aims to enhance the model's ability to precisely localize and leverage visual evidence.
3. **Develop a Training-Free Solution:** Recognizing the high computational and data annotation costs associated with existing training-based methods for mitigating degradation, a key objective was to develop an efficient and general *training-free* framework. This would make improvements more accessible and less resource-intensive.
4. **Gain Mechanistic Understanding of MLLM Behavior:** A deeper motivation was to understand *how* MLLMs process and integrate visual information across their layers. By identifying a consistent "three-stage pattern" (early-modal separation, mid-modal alignment, late-modal degradation/plateau), the authors sought to pinpoint where interventions would be most effective, moving beyond black-box optimization.
5. **Restore Base LLM Capabilities Selectively:** Based on the mechanistic insights, the goal was to selectively re-inject the strong linguistic capabilities of the original base language model into specific MLLM layers where visual information processing reaches a plateau or where language degradation is most impactful, without disrupting earlier, critical multimodal alignment.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to reconcile the trade-off between multimodal instruction following and strong linguistic reasoning, offering a cost-effective, principled approach to make MLLMs both more intelligent and more reliably grounded in visual reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PlaM (Plateau-guided model Merging) methodology is a training-free framework designed to mitigate linguistic reasoning degradation and improve visual grounding in MLLMs. It consists of two main components: a diagnostic tool for understanding MLLM layer-wise behavior and a targeted parameter merging strategy.

#### 3.1. Layer-wise Vision Token Masking (Diagnostic Tool)

To understand how MLLMs process and utilize visual information across different layers, the authors adopted a depth-controlled masking strategy on visual tokens (Shi et al., 2025). The process involves:

- **Input Representation:** An MLLM takes an image `I` and a text prompt `T`. The image is processed by a vision encoder `E_vis` and then projected into the language model's embedding space via a projector `P`, yielding visual tokens `X_vis`. The text prompt is embedded to produce textual tokens `X_txt`. These are concatenated to form the language model's input `X`.
- **Masking Intervention:** For a selected layer `k`, visual tokens are processed normally in all layers `l < k`. However, for layers `l >= k`, access to visual tokens is blocked by removing their positions from the attention keys and values (`Attn(l)(Q, K, V) => Attn(l)(Q, K_¬V, V_¬V)`). This ensures that subsequent layers cannot attend to visual information while preserving the textual information pathway.
- **Layer-wise Profiling:** By sweeping `k` from shallow to deep layers (from 1 to `L+1`, where `L+1` means no masking), a performance profile is generated. This profile characterizes where and to what extent visual information contributes to the decoder's predictions.

This analysis revealed a consistent "three-stage pattern" in MLLMs:
1. **Early Layers (Feature Separation):** Models struggle to effectively use visual evidence, exhibiting insufficient visual grounding due to weak cross-modal interactions. Performance gains from visual information are limited.
2. **Middle Layers (Feature Alignment and Fusion):** A rapid and pronounced performance increase is observed. In this stage, visual and language representations are effectively aligned and fused, allowing the model to attend to semantically critical visual features and perform image-grounded inference.
3. **Late Layers (Feature Degradation and Absorption / Plateau):** Model performance plateaus or even slightly declines. Visual features are largely absorbed by textual information, and additional visual access yields diminishing returns. This plateau is identified as being linked to weakened language capabilities and a bottleneck in guiding attention to correct visual features.

#### 3.2. Plateau-Guided Model Merging (PlaM)

Motivated by the insights from the layer-wise masking analysis, particularly the observation of performance plateaus in late layers, PlaM proposes a targeted parameter merging strategy:

- **Identifying Merge Layers:** The "plateau onset layer" (`k*`) is identified from the performance–`k` curves (Figure 2 in the paper) as the "elbow point" where the rapid-gain regime transitions into a stable plateau. A lightweight hyperparameter `k0` (merge start layer) is selected in the vicinity of `k*`. All decoder layers from `k0` to the final layer (`L`) constitute the target layer set `L(k0) = {k0..., L}`.
- **Selective Parameter Injection:** PlaM aims to restore the compromised text reasoning ability by selectively injecting parameters from the original base language model (`W_lm`) into the fine-tuned MLLM (`W_vlm`) within the identified target layer set `L`.
- **Linear Interpolation:** For each layer `l` in the target set `L`, merged parameters `W_merged^(l)` are constructed via linear interpolation:
 `W_merged^(l) = λ_1 * W_lm^(l) + λ_2 * W_vlm^(l)`
 where `λ_1` and `λ_2` are mixing coefficients ranging from `[0, 1.5]`. The coefficients are determined through a grid search with a step size of 0.1, typically ranging from 0.0 to 1.0 for each.
- **Fixed Components:** Crucially, the vision encoder and projector components of the MLLM are kept fixed and *do not* undergo merging. Only the self-attention projections (Query, Key, Value, Output weights – Q/K/V/O) of the Transformer decoder layers within the specified range are merged, as the attention mechanism is fundamental to cross-modal interaction.
- **Preserving Early Layers:** For layers outside the selected range `L`, the original MLLM parameters (`W_merged^(l) = W_vlm^(l)`) are preserved, ensuring that the critical early-to-mid stage multimodal alignment is not disturbed.

This selective, training-free merging approach aims to recover high-quality linguistic abstractions and stable reasoning patterns from the base LLM in the late layers, where visual signals are already sufficiently integrated, thereby compensating for any degradation introduced during multimodal fine-tuning and improving visual grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across five representative MLLMs (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct, Qwen3-VL-4B/8B-Instruct) and nine diverse vision-language benchmarks (MMStar, MMMU, MME, MMBench-EN/CN, GQA, RealWorldQA, SEED-Bench-2-Plus, POPE) yielded several key findings:

1. **Consistent Three-Stage Pattern of Visual Information Absorption:** The layer-wise vision token masking consistently revealed a three-stage pattern of visual information utilization across all MLLMs and tasks (as depicted in Figure 2):
 - **Early Stage (Feature Separation):** Low `k` values resulted in poor performance, indicating that early layers struggle to effectively integrate visual information.
 - **Middle Stage (Feature Alignment and Fusion):** As `k` increased in the middle layers, a rapid and pronounced performance increase was observed, signifying effective cross-modal alignment and feature fusion.
 - **Late Stage (Feature Degradation and Absorption / Plateau):** In later layers (high `k`), performance stabilized or even slightly declined, forming a plateau. This suggested that further visual access provided diminishing returns, and bottlenecks stemmed more from linguistic degradation than visual modeling limitations.

2. **PlaM Outperforms Baseline and Alternative Merging Strategies:** PlaM consistently improved performance across all five evaluated MLLM architectures and nine benchmarks compared to the original fine-tuned MLLMs (Base Model).
 - For example, on LLaVA-v1.5-7B, PlaM boosted MMStar by +1.52, MME-C by +15.00, MME-P by +6.41, MMMU by +0.56, and RealWorldQA by +0.26. Similar positive trends were observed across the Qwen model families.
 - Crucially, PlaM significantly outperformed alternative merging strategies (early-layer, mid-layer, and full-layer merging). Early-layer and mid-layer merging often led to performance degradation compared to the base model, likely because they interfered with crucial initial cross-modal alignment. Full-layer merging also generally underperformed PlaM, highlighting the importance of *selective*, plateau-guided intervention. This finding strongly validates the hypothesis that the effectiveness of merging critically depends on the specific layers targeted.

3. **Task-Dependent Performance Gains:** The benefits of PlaM varied across benchmarks, indicating a task-dependent pattern:
 - **Pronounced Improvements:** PlaM delivered the most significant gains on benchmarks like MMStar, MME (MME_C and MME_P), and MMMU. These tasks typically require late-stage semantic decision-making and robust integration of visual evidence, which directly benefits from PlaM's repair of degraded semantic decision structures in late layers.
 - **Moderate Gains:** Benchmarks such as MMBench-EN/CN and SEED-Bench-2-Plus saw moderate but consistent improvements.
 - **Minimal Gains:** GQA and POPE showed minimal gains. This is attributed to GQA's reliance on early-to-mid compositional grounding and spatial/relational modeling, and POPE being closer to evidence-verification with already high baseline accuracy, where late-layer semantic decision improvements have less impact.

4. **Mechanistic Insights from Attention Analysis:**
 - **Increased Attention Mass to Vision Tokens:** Layer-wise attention mass analysis demonstrated that PlaM merging substantially increased the attention mass allocated to vision tokens in the merged (late) layers. This suggests that the merged models rely more heavily on visual information during both prompt encoding (`Instruction -> Vision`) and output generation (`Response -> Vision`).
 - **Enhanced Visual Grounding (Case Studies):** Visualizations of attention heatmaps revealed that the increased attention mass corresponded to more effective visual grounding. The original MLLMs often exhibited diffuse and scattered attention patterns, leading to incorrect predictions. After PlaM merging, attention became more concentrated and task-relevant, focusing precisely on decision-critical visual evidence (e.g., clock hands for time questions, or specific parts of interlocked rings for size comparison), leading to correct answers.

In summary, PlaM successfully identified a critical behavioral pattern in MLLMs and leveraged it to develop an effective, training-free merging strategy that consistently improves multimodal performance, particularly visual grounding, by selectively restoring linguistic reasoning capabilities in the late layers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PlaM research presents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal large language models, with several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Efficient and Accessible MLLM Improvement:** The most immediate and impactful aspect of PlaM is its **training-free** nature. This bypasses the enormous computational resources, time, and data annotation efforts typically required for fine-tuning or re-training MLLMs. This makes advanced MLLM performance enhancements more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited resources, potentially accelerating development and deployment in diverse settings.
2. **Addressing a Core MLLM Challenge:** PlaM directly addresses the "multimodal instruction fine-tuning paradox," a known issue where multimodal training can inadvertently degrade the foundational linguistic reasoning capabilities inherited from base LLMs. By providing a principled way to restore this lost ability while preserving multimodal alignment, PlaM helps MLLMs become more robust and reliable.
3. **Enhanced Visual Grounding and Reduced Hallucinations:** The mechanistic insights from attention analysis clearly show that PlaM transforms diffuse, scattered attention into focused localization on task-relevant visual regions. This direct improvement in visual grounding is crucial for reducing object hallucinations and ensuring that MLLMs produce responses that are genuinely anchored in the visual input, rather than relying solely on textual priors or generic knowledge.
4. **Deeper Understanding of MLLM Mechanics:** The identification of the "three-stage pattern" (early-modal separation, mid-modal alignment, late-modal degradation/plateau) provides valuable mechanistic insight into how MLLMs process multimodal information across their layers. This layer-wise understanding can guide future architectural designs, training strategies, and debugging efforts, allowing for more targeted and effective interventions.
5. **Foundation for Future Training-Free Interventions:** PlaM opens new avenues for research into other training-free interventions for MLLMs. The success of selective parameter merging suggests that other forms of lightweight, post-hoc modifications could be explored to optimize different aspects of MLLM behavior, such as factual correctness, safety, or style, without the need for costly retraining cycles.
6. **Broader Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across five diverse MLLM architectures (LLaVA-based and Qwen-based) and nine varied benchmarks demonstrates its generality and robustness, suggesting it could be widely applicable to other MLLMs built upon similar Transformer decoder architectures.

**Limitations noted by the authors:**
- The optimal `k0` (merge start layer) for PlaM can vary across different datasets and models, requiring additional experimentation and cost for hyperparameter tuning. This suggests that while training is avoided, some empirical tuning might still be necessary for peak performance on specific tasks.
- The current model merging strategy is a simple linear interpolation. More complex or sophisticated merging methods could potentially yield even better results. However, the simplicity of the current approach also highlights its efficiency and serves as a strong baseline for future exploration.

Despite these limitations, PlaM's ability to significantly enhance MLLM performance in a training-free manner offers a compelling and practical solution to a critical problem, paving the way for more robust, visually grounded, and accessible multimodal AI systems.
