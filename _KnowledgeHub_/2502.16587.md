# Human2Robot: Learning Robot Actions from Paired Human-Robot Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Fudan University developed Human2Robot, a framework and corresponding H&R dataset, enabling robots to acquire complex manipulation skills from precisely synchronized human-robot video demonstrations. The approach leverages conditional video generation to learn fine-grained temporal dynamics, achieving 95% success on basic tasks and robust one-shot generalization to various unseen scenarios, including entirely new tasks like writing.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with a "generalization gap" in imitation learning from human demonstrations, failing to perform tasks on unseen objects, positions, or environments.
- Existing methods for learning from human videos mainly capture coarse, global features, neglecting the fine-grained temporal dynamics essential for complex manipulation.
- The field faces a "vicious circle" where the lack of finely-grained human-robot datasets impedes advanced model development, and current models do not fully exploit such detailed supervision.

## Method

- The H&R dataset was created, featuring 2,600 episodes of precisely synchronized human and robot motions, collected via a VR teleoperation system to ensure high-fidelity, frame-level alignment.
- ==The HUMAN2ROBOT== framework, an end-to-end generative model, was introduced, framing fine-grained human-robot alignment as a conditional video generation problem using a two-stage training process (Video Prediction Model and Action Decoding).
- A K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) based approach was integrated for seen tasks, allowing the robot to operate without real-time human demonstrations by retrieving relevant human videos based on environment context.

## Results

- Achieved a **95%** average success rate on basic tasks (e.g., Push & Pull, Pick & Place), outperforming baseline methods like XSkill (**53%**) and Diffusion Policy (**28%**) by a large margin.
- Demonstrated strong one-shot generalization across various unseen scenarios, including **100%** success on appearance variations, **80%** on positional shifts, **70%** on new object instances, and **80%** on novel backgrounds.
- Successfully generalized to brand-new, complex tasks like writing characters ('H' or 'R') after being trained only on random movements, showcasing an exceptional implicit understanding of human intent and robot control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precisely synchronized, fine-grained human-robot video data is critical for models to learn intricate manipulation dynamics and achieve robust one-shot generalization.
> - Treating human-robot alignment as a conditional video generation task, rather than simple feature matching, compels the model to internalize the precise execution (how) of a task, leading to a richer dynamic understanding.
> - A two-stage generative model, first pre-trained for video prediction and then used to extract motion-rich features for policy learning, effectively distills fine-grained action correspondence from human demonstrations.
