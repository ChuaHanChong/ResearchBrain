# $π_\texttt{RL}$: Online RL Fine-tuning for Flow-based Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed πRL, an online reinforcement learning framework designed to fine-tune flow-based Vision-Language-Action models. This framework resolves issues with log-likelihood estimation and action stochasticity, achieving up to 31.0% performance gains and enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Large-scale expert demonstration datasets for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of VLA models are costly and time-consuming to collect.
- SFT-trained models tend to overfit expert demonstrations, limiting generalization and robustness in real-world scenarios.
- Flow-based VLA models, such as the π-series, face challenges in online RL due to their inherently deterministic action generation and intractable log-likelihood estimation for standard policy gradient algorithms.

## Method

- **πRL**, an ==online reinforcement learning== framework, was developed for fine-tuning ==flow-based Vision-Language-Action models== (**π0**, **π0.5**, **GR00T**).
- ==Flow-Noise==: Integrates a ==learnable noise network== into the flow matching denoising process, modeling it as a ==discrete-time Markov Decision Process (MDP)== for exact ==log-likelihood computation==.
- ==Flow-SDE==: Converts the deterministic ==ODE== denoising process into a ==Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE)== formulation to inject stochasticity and enable a ==two-layer MDP== for coupling denoising with environmental interaction.

## Results

- πRL yielded performance improvements up to **29.2%** for π0 and **31.0%** for π0.5 over SFT baselines across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks (LIBERO, ManiSkill, MetaWorld, CALVIN).
- Few-shot SFT followed by πRL optimization with the π0.5 model achieved a **98.3%** success rate on LIBERO, surpassing the **96.9%** success rate of the full-dataset SFT baseline.
- A zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was successfully demonstrated, with an RL-tuned policy achieving a **40%** success rate in the real world compared to the SFT baseline's failure, utilizing a 3D Gaussian Splatting simulator to reduce the visual domain gap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Flow-based VLA models can be effectively fine-tuned with online reinforcement learning by addressing the challenges of intractable log-likelihoods and deterministic action generation through methods like learnable noise injection or ODE-to-SDE conversion.
> - RL training enhances performance beyond supervised fine-tuning, demonstrating improved robustness and generalized action-level refinement that transfers to out-of-distribution scenarios without catastrophic forgetting.
> - Careful design of the critic architecture, optimal noise levels, and action chunk sizes are critical for stable and effective online RL training of flow-based VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, reinforcement learning (RL), and generative flow models, specifically addressing a critical limitation in the application of RL to flow-based VLAs.

**VLA Models for Robotics:** VLA models have emerged as a leading paradigm for general-purpose robots, enabling them to interpret multimodal inputs (vision, language) and translate abstract commands into low-level physical actions. Architectures like Octo, RT, OpenVLA, and the π-series models represent significant advancements in unified perception, reasoning, and control.

**Standard Training Paradigm and its Limitations:** Traditionally, VLAs are trained using a two-stage process: pre-training on large-scale datasets, followed by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on specific task demonstrations. However, SFT faces two major hurdles:
1. **Data Scarcity and Cost:** Collecting large-scale, high-quality human demonstration datasets for SFT is notoriously laborious and expensive.
2. **Overfitting to Demonstrations:** SFT-trained models tend to overfit the expert demonstrations, limiting their ability to generalize beyond seen trajectories and recover from minor errors, thus constraining overall performance.

**The Rise of RL in VLA Training:** To overcome SFT's limitations, recent research has started incorporating RL into the VLA training paradigm (Pre-training → SFT → RL). This allows VLAs to interact directly with the environment, learn from rewards, and improve performance beyond initial expert demonstrations, leading to more generalizable and robust policies. Examples include SimpleVLA-RL, RL4VLA, and RLinf-VLA.

**The Unaddressed Gap: RL for Flow-Based VLAs:** While RL has shown promise with *autoregressive* VLAs (e.g., OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT) that use discrete action decoders, its application to *flow-based* VLAs (like the π-series models: π0, π0.5) has remained challenging. Flow-based models generate actions through iterative refinement using flow matching, offering advantages in generating high-frequency action chunks for dexterous tasks. However, their continuous, deterministic nature and the difficulty of characterizing an exact logarithmic likelihood for actions generated via flow matching pose significant obstacles for standard policy gradient RL algorithms.

**πRL's Contribution to the Landscape:** This paper directly addresses this gap by introducing πRL, a framework specifically designed for online RL fine-tuning of flow-based VLAs. It provides two novel technical solutions (Flow-Noise and Flow-SDE) to enable tractable log-likelihood estimation and introduce stochasticity for exploration, thereby making flow-based VLAs compatible with powerful online RL algorithms like PPO. By successfully integrating RL into flow-based VLA training, πRL extends the benefits of RL (performance gains, enhanced generalization, reduced reliance on demonstrations) to a new class of highly capable robotic models, pushing the boundaries of general-purpose robot control. It builds upon prior work in RL for general flow models (e.g., ReinFlow, Flow-GRPO, FPO) but specifically adapts and integrates these concepts within the complex VLA framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop and validate an online reinforcement learning (RL) framework, named πRL, for fine-tuning large-scale flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.**

This core objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming SFT Limitations for Flow-Based VLAs:** While supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is a standard training paradigm for VLAs, it inherently suffers from the high cost and laboriousness of collecting expert demonstration data. More critically, SFT-aligned models often overfit to the demonstrations, limiting their robustness and generalization capabilities in real-world, uncertain environments. RL offers a path to improve performance beyond expert data and develop more generalizable policies through direct environmental interaction.
2. **Addressing Intractable Log-Likelihoods in Flow Matching:** Flow-based VLAs, such as the π-series models, generate actions through an iterative denoising process based on flow matching. A fundamental challenge for applying standard policy gradient RL algorithms (like PPO) to these models is the inability to compute an exact and tractable action log-likelihood for the executed actions. Without this, computing policy gradients for optimization becomes infeasible or inaccurate.
3. **Introducing Stochasticity for Exploration:** The action generation process in flow-based models, being based on Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs), is inherently deterministic during inference. RL, however, relies on exploration to discover optimal policies. The deterministic nature of flow-based action generation precludes effective exploration, making direct application of RL non-trivial.
4. **Extending RL Benefits to High-Performance VLA Architectures:** Flow-based VLAs are known for their ability to generate action chunks at high frequencies and perform highly dexterous tasks, making them promising for advanced robot control. Enabling RL for these models would unlock their full potential for higher performance, better adaptation, and improved generalization across a wider range of complex tasks.
5. **Facilitating Open-World Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By allowing flow-based VLAs to refine policies through interaction, the framework aims to achieve genuine policy enhancement, enabling better generalization in out-of-distribution (OOD) settings and facilitating seamless zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, especially when coupled with advanced simulation technologies like 3D Gaussian Splatting for reduced visual domain gap.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of flow-based VLAs by integrating them with powerful online RL techniques, thereby pushing the boundaries of general robot control and embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of πRL's methodology lies in addressing the intractable log-likelihood estimation and deterministic nature of flow-based VLA action generation, which are critical for effective online RL. The paper proposes two primary technical approaches: Flow-Noise and Flow-SDE, both enabling policy optimization via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

#### 3.1. Flow-Noise: Discrete-Time MDP with Learnable Noise

Inspired by ReinFlow, this approach models the denoising process as a discrete-time Markov Decision Process (MDP) to enable exact log-likelihood computation.
- **Stochasticity Injection:** A *learnable noise network* is integrated into the flow matching denoising process. This network parameterizes the noise schedule, allowing the magnitude of injected Gaussian noise to be learned dynamically during training. The step transition during denoising is modeled as a Gaussian distribution where the mean is from the forward Euler ODE update and the variance is controlled by the learnable noise network. This network is used only during training and discarded for deterministic inference.
- **Log-Likelihood Estimation:** By modeling the denoising stage as a discrete MDP, the exact and tractable log probability of the entire denoising sequence (A0..., AK) can be computed. This joint log-likelihood is then used in the policy optimization objective, making flow-based policies amenable to standard policy gradient methods within a one-layer MDP framework.

#### 3.2. Flow-SDE: ODE-to-SDE Conversion for Exploration

Inspired by Flow-GRPO, this method enhances stochastic exploration by converting the deterministic ODE denoising process into a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) formulation.
- **Stochasticity Injection:** The deterministic ODE of flow matching is transformed into an equivalent SDE by introducing a drift term (correcting the original velocity) and a diffusion term (introducing noise). This SDE preserves the marginal probability density of the generated actions. By linking the score function of the marginal distribution to the velocity field and setting a specific noise schedule, a discretized SDE transition probability (Gaussian) is derived, allowing for an isotropic Gaussian distribution for p(Aτ+δ|Aτ).
- **MDP Formulation (Two-Layer MDP):** To couple the denoising process with environmental interaction, a two-layer MDP is constructed.
 - **Inner MDP (Denoising):** Defined at denoising time (τ), with state ¯sτt = (ot, Aτt) and action ¯aτt as the next sampled denoised action. Transitions occur between denoised action states while the observation ot remains fixed.
 - **Outer MDP (Environment Interaction):** Defined at environment time (t). The final action (A1t) from the inner MDP interacts with the environment, yielding a new observation (ot+1) and an environment reward.
 - **Reward:** Rewards are sparse, granted only upon completion of the denoising process and interaction with the environment (τ=1).
- **Hybrid ODE-SDE Sampling:** To mitigate the computational cost and extended horizon of the two-layer MDP, a mixed ODE-SDE rollout strategy is adopted. At each environment step, one denoising step is randomly sampled for stochastic SDE exploration, while all remaining denoising steps are treated as deterministic ODE updates. This effectively shortens the MDP horizon while maintaining theoretical consistency.

#### 3.3. Policy Optimization

- **Algorithm:** Both Flow-Noise and Flow-SDE frameworks are optimized using the widely adopted Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm.
- **Chunk-Based Actions:** Since π-series models output a sequence of H future actions (an "action chunk"), this entire sequence is treated as a single macro-step for RL, and its reward is the sum of per-step rewards within that chunk.
- **Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE):** GAE is used to compute low-variance advantage estimates for policy updates.
- **Clipped Objective:** PPO's clipped objective function constrains policy updates within a trust region to ensure training stability.
- **Critic Design (Shared Actor-Critic):** A shared actor-critic architecture is employed for memory efficiency.
 - **π0 Variant:** The critic is placed *after the action expert model*, requiring approximation by averaging value estimates across the denoising trajectory due to the coupled input structure of state and noisy action.
 - **π0.5 Variant:** The critic is attached directly *to the VLM output*, as π0.5 merges proprioceptive state with prompt embeddings within the VLM itself.

The overall approach intelligently modifies the action generation and likelihood estimation for flow-based models to integrate them seamlessly into established online RL frameworks, aiming for improved performance and generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents compelling results demonstrating the effectiveness of πRL across various robotic manipulation benchmarks.

#### 4.1. In-Distribution (ID) RL Training

- **Significant Performance Gains:** πRL consistently yields substantial performance improvements over SFT baselines across all evaluated benchmarks (LIBERO, ManiSkill, MetaWorld, CALVIN).
 - For π0, maximum average improvement was +29.2% (Flow-Noise).
 - For π0.5, maximum average improvement was +31.0% (Flow-SDE).
- **Exceeding Full-Dataset SFT with Few-Shot RL:** On the LIBERO benchmark, few-shot SFT followed by πRL optimization achieved a 98.3% success rate with the π0.5 model, surpassing the 96.9% success rate of the full-dataset SFT baseline. This highlights the data efficiency and performance-boosting capabilities of πRL.
- **Robustness Across Complex Tasks:** Gains were observed in challenging environments like ManiSkill (4,352 pick-and-place combinations), MetaWorld (50 distinct manipulation primitives), and CALVIN (long-horizon sequential tasks), indicating broad applicability.
- **Enhanced Long-Horizon Performance:** For CALVIN, the performance gap between SFT and RL widened significantly with increasing sequence length, with π0.5's Len-5 success rate surging to 87.0% from 61.3% with SFT.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) RL Evaluation

- **Generalized Action-Level Refinement:** Performance gains from ID settings effectively transferred to OOD scenarios in ManiSkill (visual/environmental variations) and CALVIN (scene D evaluation). This suggests that RL promotes the acquisition of generalized *action representations* rather than narrow overfitting. Proportional improvements in OOD scenarios were comparable to ID gains (e.g., 102.3% OOD vs. 126.7% IND for π0.5 on ManiSkill).
- **Retention of SFT Skills (No Catastrophic Forgetting):** In MetaWorld (task-level generalization), while direct performance improvement on novel OOD tasks fluctuated, the RL-trained model *retained* the OOD skills inherited from the SFT phase. This indicates that RL avoids catastrophic forgetting and overfitting common in standard imitation learning, maintaining a balance between ID performance and OOD robustness.
- **Limitations in High-Level Generalization:** RL's benefits were primarily localized to action-level refinement. Transferring performance gains to *entirely novel task objectives* (as in MetaWorld's unseen tasks) remained a significant challenge. Freezing the VLM backbone during RL training for computational efficiency also limited adaptation to novel visual textures/backgrounds in OOD visual tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Focusing on Flow-SDE)

- **Critic Design:** Placing the critic after the VLM (Vvlm) yielded slightly superior performance, lower value loss, and higher explained variance compared to placing it after the action expert (Vexpert) for π0. A four-layer MLP structure for the critic also demonstrated stronger regression capabilities.
- **Flow Policy MDP:** The one-layer MDP (Flow-Noise formulation) converged most rapidly, but all three formulations (one-layer, two-layer, hybrid two-layer) achieved similar final success rates. The hybrid two-layer paradigm achieved a 2x speedup in update time over the standard two-layer model due to a shorter effective MDP chain. However, the one-layer MDP showed no substantial wall-clock advantage due to the need to recalculate full denoising trajectories for log-likelihood estimation.
- **Stochasticity Injection:** Both fixed and learnable noise injection strategies yielded similar converged performance when tuned appropriately, affirming their efficiency.

#### 4.4. Hyper-Parameters (Flow-SDE with π0 on LIBERO-Spatial)

- **Noise Level:** A trade-off exists; lower noise levels (e.g., a=0.2) mitigated exploration-induced performance degradation but led to instability (higher clip fraction) due to larger gradient magnitudes. Higher noise levels could distort flow trajectories.
- **Denoise Step (K):** K=1 resulted in significant ODE-to-SDE discretization error and plummeting training success. Larger K enhanced rollout performance but increased computational overhead.
- **Action Chunk Size:** Larger chunk sizes (e.g., 10 or 20) offered marginal SFT performance gains but paradoxically limited the ceiling for RL refinement due to reduced interaction frequency and obscured reward credit assignment (lower explained variance).

#### 4.5. Case Studies

- **SIMPLER:** πRL significantly improved success rates for single-task scenarios (e.g., π0 model increased average from 67.2% to 86.7%).
- **Real2Sim2Real:** Leveraging a simulator with 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic rendering, πRL enabled successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. An RL-tuned policy achieved a 40% success rate in the real world, while the SFT baseline failed, demonstrating the efficacy of reducing the visual domain gap and RL refinement.

#### 4.6. Insights from Large-Scale RL Training (Appendix)

- RL training effectively improves temporal efficiency, with episode lengths converging to the expert range (e.g., π0.5 in ManiSkill), driven by error correction and discounted rewards.
- A learning rate scheduler (cosine annealing) was crucial for stabilizing training by preventing KL divergence escalation.
- Replacing dropout layers with identity layers in the expert model was necessary for stable online RL training for architectures like GR00T, as dropout introduces instability in probability ratio updates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of πRL marks a significant advancement in the field of embodied intelligence and robotic control, with several key implications:

1. **Unlocking RL for Flow-Based VLAs:** The most crucial contribution is solving the long-standing challenge of applying online reinforcement learning to flow-based Vision-Language-Action models. By introducing Flow-Noise and Flow-SDE, the paper provides practical methods for intractable log-likelihood computation and stochastic exploration, thereby making a powerful class of VLA architectures amenable to RL fine-tuning. This bridges a critical gap in the existing research landscape.
2. **Substantial Performance Gains and Enhanced Generalization:** The empirical results demonstrate that πRL consistently and significantly boosts the performance of VLA models (π0, π0.5, and GR00T) across diverse and challenging robotic manipulation benchmarks. The ability of few-shot SFT + RL to outperform full-dataset SFT highlights a path toward more data-efficient training. Furthermore, the demonstrated transfer of performance gains to various out-of-distribution settings for action-level refinement indicates improved robustness and generalized skill acquisition.
3. **Path Towards More Capable Robots:** Flow-based VLAs are known for their ability to generate high-frequency, dexterous actions. By enhancing these models with RL, πRL enables robots to perform more complex, fine-grained, and long-horizon tasks with greater success, potentially leading to more adaptable and intelligent robots in unstructured environments.
4. **Advancing Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real deployment, facilitated by a high-fidelity simulator built with 3D Gaussian Splatting, showcases πRL's potential to accelerate the development and deployment of real-world robotic systems. This approach reduces the reliance on costly real-world data collection, leveraging the scalability of simulation for policy refinement.
5. **Robustness and Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation:** The finding that RL-tuned policies retain OOD skills learned during SFT (without catastrophic forgetting) is significant. This suggests that πRL provides a more balanced optimization strategy compared to traditional SFT, which often leads to overfitting and loss of general capabilities.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The comprehensive ablation studies and hyperparameter analysis provide valuable empirical insights into the critical factors affecting RL training for flow-based VLAs. This guidance can significantly accelerate future research in this area, informing architectural choices, noise injection strategies, and optimization setups.
7. **Open Science Contribution:** The release of open-source code and models ensures reproducibility and lowers the barrier for other researchers to build upon this work, fostering faster progress in the field of embodied AI.

**Potential Impact:**
The immediate impact is on the development of highly capable robotic manipulation systems that can learn and adapt more effectively. In the longer term, this work contributes to the vision of truly general-purpose robots capable of understanding and executing complex human instructions in diverse, real-world environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the low sample efficiency of online RL, which currently necessitates sim-to-real deployment. Future work aims to develop more efficient algorithms for real-world RL training. Additionally, improving noise injection strategies, exploring more advanced mixed ODE-SDE rollouts for training acceleration, and enhancing semantic generalization in OOD settings are identified as promising avenues for further research.
