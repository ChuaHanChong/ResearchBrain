# MMR-AD: A Large-Scale Multimodal Dataset for Benchmarking General Anomaly Detection with Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced MMR-AD, a large-scale multimodal dataset featuring reasoning-based text annotations for industrial anomaly detection, and proposed Anomaly-R1, a baseline multimodal large language model (MLLM) that leverages this data. Anomaly-R1 significantly improved anomaly localization accuracy and overall detection performance on industrial tasks compared to generalist MLLMs.

## Problem

- The field of industrial anomaly detection (IAD) lacks models capable of General Anomaly Detection (GAD) that can identify anomalies across diverse, novel classes without task-specific retraining.
- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are often pre-trained on general web data, creating a domain gap for industrial applications and lacking detailed multimodal textual annotations, especially reasoning processes, required for effective MLLM-based anomaly detection.
- Current MLLMs struggle with precise anomaly localization and articulating their detection rationale, hindering interpretability and robustness in industrial anomaly detection tasks.

## Method

- Developed **MMR-AD**, the largest-scale multimodal reasoning-based ==industrial anomaly== detection dataset to date, comprising 127,137 images from 14 diverse datasets with geometry-guided and 'think-then-answer' text annotations.
- Proposed Anomaly-R1, a baseline MLLM for GAD, which is post-trained on MMR-AD using a combination of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for initialization and reinforcement learning (RL) with a tailored reward function and contrastive sampling.
- Injected anomaly-related domain knowledge into prompts during RL training to guide the model towards more accurate and category-specific anomaly detection.

## Results

- Anomaly-R1-**7B**, post-trained on MMR-AD, achieved substantial performance gains, with anomaly localization accuracy increasing from **8.9%** to **66.3%** on the MVTecAD dataset, outperforming larger generalist MLLMs.
- Ablation studies confirmed that reasoning texts, normal reference images, cold-start SFT, contrastive sampling, and domain knowledge are vital components contributing to the enhanced performance of MLLM-based GAD.
- While performance significantly improved, a gap persists between MLLM-based general AD models and traditional full-shot AD models, indicating further potential for advancement in generalized anomaly detection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current generalist MLLMs demonstrate insufficient performance for industrial anomaly detection, particularly in fine-grained anomaly localization, underscoring the necessity for domain-specific adaptation.
> - The inclusion of reasoning-based text annotations and normal reference images during training is crucial for MLLMs to develop general comparative analysis and robust anomaly detection capabilities.
> - A staged training approach combining SFT for initial familiarization and RL with targeted rewards and contrastive sampling is effective for adapting MLLMs to complex, explainable anomaly detection tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of industrial anomaly detection (IAD) has evolved from methods focusing on single-class and multi-class anomalies to more advanced cross-class approaches. An emerging trend and ultimate objective in this domain is General Anomaly Detection (GAD). GAD aims to develop models capable of detecting anomalies across diverse, novel classes without requiring retraining or fine-tuning on target-specific data. This represents a shift towards more generalized and adaptable anomaly detection systems, akin to the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

Recently, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated advanced visual understanding and language reasoning capabilities, positioning them as potential enablers for achieving GAD. Several prior studies have explored MLLM-based anomaly detection. Some research has qualitatively evaluated MLLMs using specific prompts, indicating their generalization potential in IAD tasks. Other works, such as AnomalyGPT and Myriad, have fine-tuned MLLMs to predict anomalies directly, often through instruction tuning. However, these approaches frequently rely on simple text replies, which may limit interpretability and the full utilization of MLLMs' reasoning abilities. There has been a perceived gap in models that can articulate a step-by-step reasoning process, including identifying visual evidence, before providing a final anomaly detection decision.

A significant limitation identified in the broader landscape is the scarcity of suitable datasets for advancing MLLM-based GAD. Existing MLLMs are typically pre-trained on vast amounts of web-sourced data, which often exhibits a domain gap when applied to specific industrial anomaly detection scenarios. Furthermore, the image-text pairs used in their pre-training are not specifically curated for AD tasks. The majority of large-scale AD datasets currently available are image-based, primarily providing visual information (e.g., masks for anomaly localization) but lacking the detailed multimodal textual data, especially reasoning-based narratives, necessary for post-training and evaluating MLLMs effectively. This paper addresses this specific lacuna by introducing a large-scale, multimodal, and reasoning-based dataset designed to facilitate the development and benchmarking of MLLM-based GAD models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to facilitate and advance the field of Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)-based General Anomaly Detection (GAD). The authors outline several specific objectives to achieve this overarching goal:

1. **Develop a Large-Scale Multimodal Reasoning-Based Industrial Anomaly Detection Dataset**: A core objective is to construct MMR-AD, which is presented as the largest-scale multimodal reasoning-based industrial anomaly detection dataset to date. This dataset is designed to be comprehensive, suitable for both training and evaluating MLLM-based AD models.
2. **Benchmark Current MLLMs on General Anomaly Detection**: The work aims to conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the performance of state-of-the-art generalist MLLMs on industrial anomaly detection tasks using the newly developed MMR-AD dataset. This evaluation seeks to quantify their current capabilities, particularly in fine-grained anomaly detection and localization, and identify existing performance limitations.
3. **Propose a Baseline Model for MLLM-based GAD**: Based on the MMR-AD dataset, the authors aim to propose and demonstrate a baseline reasoning-based AD model, Anomaly-R1. This model is intended to showcase the potential for improving MLLM performance in GAD tasks through specialized post-training and reinforcement learning using the MMR-AD data.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several critical observations and needs within the anomaly detection community:

- **Emergence of General Anomaly Detection**: The industrial anomaly detection field is progressing towards GAD, where models can detect anomalies in novel classes without specific training. MLLMs, with their visual understanding and language reasoning capabilities, are considered promising candidates for achieving this generalization.
- **Domain Gap and Lack of Specialized Training Data for MLLMs**: Existing MLLMs are primarily pre-trained on general web data, which often differs significantly from industrial AD scenarios. Their pre-training data is not specifically designed for anomaly detection, leading to suboptimal performance in this domain.
- **Inadequacy of Existing AD Datasets for MLLMs**: Current mainstream AD datasets are predominantly image-based and lack the multimodal textual annotations, especially detailed reasoning steps, required for effectively post-training and evaluating MLLMs. This absence prevents MLLMs from leveraging their full reasoning potential for AD tasks.
- **Need for Explainability and Robustness**: The authors emphasize the importance of models that can explain *why* an image is anomalous and provide corresponding visual evidence. This "think-then-answer" approach is considered crucial for better interpretability, robustness, and generalizability, allowing models to detect novel anomalies through step-by-step analysis rather than mere memorization.
- **Observed Performance Limitations of Current MLLMs**: Initial assessments suggest that current generalist MLLMs fall short of industrial requirements for AD, particularly in precise anomaly localization. This highlights the necessity for specialized multimodal AD datasets to enhance their capabilities.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to bridge the gap between generalist MLLMs and the specialized requirements of industrial GAD, thereby accelerating progress in this area.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the creation of a large-scale multimodal dataset, MMR-AD, and the development of a baseline model, Anomaly-R1, to leverage this dataset.

#### 3.1. MMR-AD Dataset Construction

The construction of MMR-AD involved a multi-step process to ensure scale, diversity, quality, and multimodal reasoning capabilities:

- **Data Collection**: The authors extensively gathered and sampled approximately 190,000 images from 14 publicly available unimodal industrial anomaly detection datasets. These included diverse datasets like MVTecAD, VisA, RealIAD, MANTA, and others, aiming for a wide range of product categories and anomaly types from real-world industrial products.
 - **Quality Control**: All collected images underwent manual review to identify and remove low-quality samples, incorrect, or ambiguous anomaly annotations, resulting in a curated set of 127,137 samples.
 - **Manual Annotation**: For anomaly localization and subsequent text generation, bounding boxes and initial text labels for anomalous regions were manually annotated on the remaining samples. This step was crucial as original AD datasets often only provide masks, which are less suitable for text-based MLLM responses.

- **Text Generation**: An automatic pipeline was designed to generate detailed reasoning-based texts for each image, addressing the inefficiency and quality issues of manual text annotation.
 - **MLLM for Generation**: Qwen2.5-VL-72B, a strong MLLM, was utilized for text generation.
 - **Comparative Input**: Unlike previous methods relying on single images, the pipeline provided the MLLM with an (input image, normal reference image) pair. A spatially-aligned nearest sample was selected as the normal reference to enable comparative reasoning, reflecting the principle that anomalies are relative to normal states.
 - **Hints for MLLM**: To guide the MLLM, additional hints were provided:
 - **Visual Hints**: Red bounding boxes were plotted on the input image to highlight anomalous regions.
 - **Textual Hints**: Bounding box coordinates and corresponding anomaly types were explicitly provided in the text instruction.
 - **Instruction Template**: A "think-then-answer" instruction template (Table 2 in the paper) was designed to prompt the MLLM to first generate a detailed reasoning process (Chain-of-Thought, CoT) based on comparison and analysis, and then provide a structured answer with anomaly locations and labels. The template specifically constrained the output format to avoid simple copying of hints.

- **Text Verification**: After MLLM-generated texts were obtained, an automatic verification process was implemented to ensure consistency with ground-truth annotations.
 - **Consistency Check**: Predicted anomaly regions from the generated answer text were extracted and compared with true anomaly regions. A generated text was deemed correct if the predicted and true anomaly regions were consistent (IoU > 0.9 for bounding boxes and semantic similarity > 0.6 for labels).
 - **Manual Refinement**: Multiple annotators were employed to further check the consistency between the reasoning process (<think> tag) and the final answer (<answer> tag), removing any inconsistent texts.

- **Data Statistics**: The final MMR-AD dataset contains 127,137 images (28,222 normal, 98,915 anomalous) from 188 product categories across 14 public datasets, with 395 anomaly types and 112,875 annotated anomalous regions. It comprises 26 million text tokens, averaging 208 tokens per sample. The dataset offers high quality, better explainability through detailed reasoning texts, and is "improvable" as raw bounding box data is provided, allowing for future text generation with more advanced MLLMs.

#### 3.2. Anomaly-R1 Baseline Model

Anomaly-R1 is proposed as a reasoning-based AD model fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-VL-7B, employing a combination of supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.

- **Cold-Start Initialization**: Given that generalist MLLMs are not initially trained for AD, a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase was first performed on the MMR-AD dataset for 3 epochs. This phase aimed to familiarize the model with the industrial AD task, its specific output format, and basic domain knowledge. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) technique was used for efficient fine-tuning.

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Following SFT, the model was further enhanced using reinforcement learning based on the GRPO (Group-based Proximal Policy Optimization) algorithm. RL was chosen for its potential in improving generalization and reasoning capabilities.
 - **Reward Function**:
 - **Result Reward**: A reward of 1 was given if the model's final answer (Yes/No) matched the ground truth, and 0 otherwise.
 - **Consistency Penalty**: To ensure precise localization, a penalty of -0.2 was applied for each ground-truth bounding box that was not correctly detected (IoU ≤ 0.5 with any predicted bounding box). This prevented the model from getting a positive reward for simply predicting "Yes" without accurate localization.
 - **Contrastive Sampling**: To mitigate the issue of zero advantages in GRPO (where all sampled responses in a group might have the same reward), a contrastive sampling approach was used. For each query, the correct ground-truth text from MMR-AD was included as a positive response. If all other sampled responses were also positive, external MLLMs were used to regenerate responses with "opposite hints" (e.g., instructing an abnormal sample to be reported as normal) to ensure both positive and negative examples within a group.
 - **Anomaly-Related Domain Knowledge**: During RL training, domain-specific knowledge was injected into the prompts. For each product category, a list of potential anomaly types was provided, prompting the model to specifically look for these and disregard other minor differences as anomalies.

- **Implementation Details**: Qwen2.5-VL-7B was used as the base model. LoRA parameters were set (rank r=16, alpha=16). SFT involved a learning rate of 2e-5 and a batch size of 4. RL used a prompt batch size of 32, sampling 8 responses per prompt, with a learning rate of 2e-6. Min-p sampling was used during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MLLM-based general anomaly detection models yielded several key findings:

- **Inadequate Performance of Generalist MLLMs**: Current state-of-the-art generalist MLLMs, including powerful commercial models (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-pro, OpenAI-o4-mini, Qwen-QVQ-Max) and open-source models (e.g., Llama4-Maverick, InternVL3.5-38B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B), demonstrated insufficient performance for industrial anomaly detection tasks. This deficiency was particularly pronounced in precise anomaly localization, where many models achieved localization accuracies (at 0.1 IoU) below 10%. Some generalist MLLMs also exhibited a tendency to over-classify samples as abnormal, leading to high recall but low precision.
- **Significant Improvement with MMR-AD Post-Training**: The proposed baseline model, Anomaly-R1-7B, which was post-trained on the MMR-AD dataset, showed substantial performance improvements compared to its base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B. For instance, on the MVTecAD dataset, anomaly localization accuracy increased from 8.9% to 66.3%. Anomaly-R1-7B also outperformed other larger generalist MLLMs in both anomaly detection and localization, indicating the efficacy of domain-specific post-training on a specialized multimodal AD dataset.
- **Persistent Gap with Traditional Full-Shot AD Models**: Despite improvements, a performance gap remains between MLLM-based general AD models and conventional full-shot AD models (e.g., PaDiM, PatchCore, HGAD, Dinomaly, INP-Former). This gap is attributed to the fact that traditional models are typically trained using all normal samples from the specific datasets, whereas MLLM-based GAD models, including Anomaly-R1, are designed for generalization without retraining on target data. This suggests considerable room for further advancements in GAD.

#### 4.1. Further Analysis (Ablation Studies)

Ablation studies were conducted to investigate the impact of various components of the proposed methodology:

- **Necessity of Reasoning-Based Text**: Removing the reasoning texts from the `<think>` part during training resulted in a notable performance decrease. This finding suggests that explicit reasoning steps are crucial for MLLMs to develop general AD analysis and comparative abilities, preventing the model from merely memorizing trained anomalies.
- **Effectiveness of Normal Reference Image**: Omitting the normal reference image from the input prompt led to a significant reduction in the model's general AD ability. This indicates that MLLMs can effectively utilize a normal reference for comparative analysis, which is fundamental to anomaly detection.
- **Importance of Cold-Start Initialization**: Directly applying reinforcement learning without prior supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for cold-start initialization yielded poor results, particularly in anomaly localization and adherence to the specified output format. This demonstrates that SFT on MMR-AD is essential to familiarize MLLMs with AD tasks and provide foundational domain knowledge before advanced RL training.
- **Impact of Contrastive Sampling**: The removal of the contrastive sampling strategy resulted in decreased model performance. This confirms that the strategy is effective in mitigating the negative impact of "zero advantage" groups during GRPO training, thus improving learning efficiency.
- **Benefits of Anomaly-Related Domain Knowledge**: Including category-specific anomaly types in the prompt significantly improved performance. This highlights that a lack of domain knowledge is a major limitation for MLLMs in industrial AD, and providing such knowledge, even in textual form, enables more accurate decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of anomaly detection and the application of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in industrial settings, with several potential impacts:

- **Foundation for MLLM-based General Anomaly Detection (GAD)**: The introduction of MMR-AD, a large-scale multimodal reasoning-based industrial AD dataset, addresses a critical gap in resources. By providing high-quality images with detailed, explainable reasoning texts and precise anomaly localization (bounding boxes), MMR-AD creates a vital training and evaluation benchmark for MLLM-based GAD research. This dataset is "improvable," as the provision of raw bounding box data allows future researchers to leverage more powerful MLLMs for generating even better text annotations.
- **Benchmarking and Performance Assessment**: The comprehensive evaluation of current state-of-the-art MLLMs on industrial AD tasks, using MMR-AD, establishes a clear benchmark. The findings reveal the current limitations of generalist MLLMs, particularly their weakness in fine-grained anomaly detection and precise localization, underscoring the necessity of domain-specific post-training. This assessment provides a clear roadmap for future research directions.
- **Advancement in MLLM Training Methodologies for AD**: The proposed Anomaly-R1 baseline model demonstrates an effective methodology for enhancing MLLM capabilities in GAD. The combination of supervised fine-tuning for cold-start initialization, reinforcement learning with a tailored reward function and contrastive sampling, and the injection of anomaly-related domain knowledge provides a robust framework. This approach highlights how MLLMs can be adapted to learn complex reasoning and comparative analysis abilities for specialized tasks like AD.
- **Promotion of Explainable AI**: By emphasizing and incorporating detailed reasoning processes ("think-then-answer" format) in both the dataset and the baseline model, the research promotes the development of more explainable AI systems for anomaly detection. This interpretability can foster greater trust and facilitate deployment in critical industrial applications where understanding the "why" behind an anomaly detection decision is crucial.
- **Guidance for Future Research**: The identified performance gap between MLLM-based GAD models and traditional full-shot AD models points to substantial room for future improvement. The work implicitly encourages further research into novel architectures, training strategies, and data augmentation techniques to bridge this gap, ultimately fostering the development of MLLM-based general AD models that can meet stringent industrial requirements. The paper also opens avenues for exploring extensions such as combining MLLMs with segmentation models for pixel-level localization.

In conclusion, this work provides a foundational dataset and a strong baseline, thereby accelerating research and development towards achieving robust, generalizable, and explainable anomaly detection systems powered by multimodal large language models for industrial applications.
