# ForceSight: Text-Guided Mobile Manipulation with Visual-Force Goals

> [!summary] Summary
> ForceSight is a text-conditioned RGBD vision transformer that predicts visual-force goals for mobile manipulation: a 4-DOF kinematic goal (affordance map, depth map, yaw, gripper width) plus a force goal (grip force and wrist applied force), which a low-level visual-force servoing controller tracks. On a Stretch RE1 it reached 81% success across ten household tasks in unseen environments with unseen object instances.

## Problem

- Text-conditioned manipulation policies output kinematic goals only, relegating forces to low-level execution, so contact quality is never explicitly planned or predicted.
- Vision-only systems suffer occlusion and depth ambiguity, lacking direct measurement of mechanical interaction with surfaces, objects, and human hands during contact.
- Data-efficient methods stay tied to fixed cameras and narrow scenes, while versatile policies demand enormous datasets, leaving no middle ground for cross-environment generalization.

## Method

- **ForceSight**'s ==visual-force goal representation==: an ==affordance map== giving a pixel-space distribution over gripper position, an affordance-masked depth map, plus yaw, fingertip width, grip force, and applied force.
- Architecture: ImageNet-21k ==ViT-large== with a fourth depth channel added to patch embedding, ==cross-attention== to a frozen ==T5== text embedding after every block, convolutional decoders plus pooled-feature MLPs.
- Low-level controller and data pipeline: joint ==kinematic-plus-force servoing== objective with weighted force terms, action primitives appended to prompts, and 26,000 multi-view teleoperated datapoints including off-trajectory recovery states.

## Results

- **81%** task success (**81/100**) across ten tasks in three unseen environments with unseen object instances, at **0.036 m** mean fingertip error versus PerAct's **0.058 m**.
- Under matched initial conditions, ignoring force goals dropped success from **90%** (**18/20**) to **45%** (**9/20**), with failures from dropped objects and excessive surface force.
- Ablations degraded sharply: no text conditioning **0/20**, no depth **4/20**, no pretraining **4/20**, no augmentation **5/20**; force RMSE **0.404 N** applied, **1.524 N** grip.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting target forces alongside target poses, rather than delegating force to the controller, roughly doubles success on contact-rich tasks like thin-object grasping and handovers.
> - Forces for everyday mechanisms are visually predictable and camera-mount agnostic, so goals transfer across viewpoints and potentially embodiments better than pose-only representations.
> - Affordance maps as pixel-wise classification let one model hold multiple hypotheses; eye-in-hand servoing then absorbs residual depth error that grows with distance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

ForceSight operates at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields in robotics: mobile manipulation, multimodal learning (vision and language), and the integration of tactile/force sensing into high-level control. The broader research landscape is characterized by a growing effort to enable robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured, human-centric environments.

**Imitation Learning and Vision-based Control:** A dominant paradigm in modern robotics is imitation learning, where robots learn complex behaviors from human demonstrations. Recent advancements have focused on combining text and image inputs (e.g., CLIPort, Perceiver-Actor, RT-1, RT-2) to improve task generalization and interpretability. Vision-only systems, however, often face challenges with occlusions, depth ambiguity, and direct physical interaction. ForceSight builds upon this foundation by using a text-conditioned vision transformer, aligning with the trend of leveraging large pre-trained models for robotic tasks.

**Integration of Force/Tactile Sensing:** While force and tactile information are recognized as crucial for dexterous manipulation, they have traditionally been relegated to lower-level, reactive control loops (e.g., force-feedback control for precise insertions). Prior work has mostly used kinematic objectives for robotic planning, overlooking the explicit integration of force goals at a planning level. ForceSight addresses this gap by explicitly predicting "force goals" alongside kinematic ones, arguing that forces are not merely for low-level execution but can be planned and targeted. This is a key differentiator, as it elevates force perception from a reactive feedback mechanism to a predictive planning objective, drawing inspiration from human motor control where force anticipation is critical for tasks like precision lifting.

**Generalization and Data Efficiency:** Many state-of-the-art methods in robot learning either offer high versatility but demand extensive datasets (e.g., RT-1, BC-Z) or are data-efficient but restricted to narrow environments or fixed cameras (e.g., CLIPort, Perceiver-Actor, Coarse-to-fine visual servoing). ForceSight aims to strike a balance, achieving generalization across environments and perspectives with a "modest amount of data collection effort." Its use of an eye-in-hand camera and predictions relative to the camera frame also contributes to versatility, making the system less dependent on global calibration and more adaptable to mobile platforms. The approach of chaining "action primitives" for multi-step tasks also connects to the trend of hierarchical planning, often leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for high-level task decomposition.

**Novelty of Visual-Force Goals:** The core novelty of ForceSight lies in its conceptualization and prediction of "visual-force goals." While combined visual-force servoing has been explored with classical and data-driven methods, these were often limited to single-step tasks or seen objects. ForceSight extends this concept to multi-step tasks in novel environments with unseen object instances, making the force objectives integral to the higher-level planning output rather than just low-level servoing. This distinguishes it from prior work that might implicitly handle force or focus solely on kinematic poses.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ForceSight is to **enable text-guided mobile manipulation by predicting human-interpretable visual-force goals using a deep neural network.** This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging the Gap between Vision and Force:** The authors are motivated by the recognition that while vision provides crucial contextual information for robotic manipulation, force and tactile information are indispensable for direct perception and control of contact with the environment. Prior systems often relegated force control to low-level execution. ForceSight aims to elevate force information to a planning objective, arguing that explicitly predicting desired forces can significantly enhance performance in contact-rich tasks.
- **Enhancing Robustness and Generalization:** A significant motivation is to improve the robot's ability to perform tasks in novel, unseen environments and with object instances it has not specifically encountered during training. Vision-only systems can struggle with ambiguities and occlusions. By incorporating force goals, the system can leverage consistent mechanical interactions, making it more robust to visual variations and promoting generalization across different objects and scenarios.
- **Improving Performance in Contact-Rich Tasks:** Tasks like precision grasping, drawer opening, or object handovers inherently involve complex contact dynamics. The paper is motivated by the hypothesis that explicitly planning for target grip forces and applied forces will lead to higher success rates and more dexterous behaviors compared to relying solely on kinematic objectives.
- **Developing a Human-Interpretable Planning System:** ForceSight aims to produce "visual-force goals" that are human-interpretable (e.g., "move to this pose, apply this much grip force"). This facilitates understanding, debugging, and potential human-robot collaboration, as humans can intuitively grasp the robot's intended actions.
- **Efficient Data Collection and Scalability:** The research seeks to develop a system that achieves strong generalization without requiring an "extensive amount of data," a common challenge in imitation learning. The proposed data collection strategy (e.g., multiple views per goal, sampling error states) reflects this motivation. The choice of a transformer-based architecture also implicitly signals a motivation for scalability to larger datasets and more complex tasks in the future.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ForceSight employs a sophisticated, transformer-based architecture combined with a carefully designed data collection and control strategy to achieve text-guided mobile manipulation with visual-force goals.

**Core Concept: Visual-Force Goals:**
The system's central idea is to predict a "visual-force goal" (G), which comprises two components:
1. **Kinematic Goal (G_K):** Defines the desired 4-DOF pose of the gripper relative to the camera frame:
 - `C_xy`: 2D pixel coordinate (from an affordance map, A) representing the target gripper position.
 - `C_z`: Depth estimate (from a depth map, D) at `C_xy`, completing the 3D position.
 - `ψ`: Predicted yaw angle of the gripper.
 - `W`: Predicted gripper width (aperture).
2. **Force Goal (G_F):** Specifies desired forces:
 - `F_A`: Target applied force (3D vector) measured by a wrist-mounted force/torque sensor, transformed into the camera frame.
 - `F_G`: Target grip force (scalar) representing compressive force between fingertips. This is estimated by a small MLP trained separately.

These goals are predicted one keyframe into the future, relative to the current eye-in-hand camera frame, which aids in visual servoing robustness and generalizability across camera setups.

**Data Collection:**
- **Robot Platform:** Stretch RE1 mobile manipulator equipped with an Intel® RealSense™ D405 RGBD camera (eye-in-hand) and a wrist-mounted force/torque sensor.
- **Process:** Approximately 30 hours of teleoperation resulted in over 26,000 high-quality datapoints, equivalent to roughly 10,000 task demonstrations.
- **Efficiency Strategies:**
 - Associating multiple input images with the same visual-force goal (multiple views).
 - Sampling from states unlikely to occur during successful execution to promote error recovery (funneling/pre-image backchaining concepts).
 - Including objects relevant to other tasks in input images to generate more diverse data points per image.
- **Ground Truth:** ArUco tags on the gripper localize fingertips, then forward kinematics map 3D fingertip locations to the input camera frame. Ground truth for grip force is collected by grasping a force/torque sensor at various strengths.

**Network Architecture (ForceSight Model):**
- **Foundation:** Leverages a large-scale **Vision Transformer (ViT-large, 304M parameters)** initialized with weights from ImageNet 21k pre-training, and a frozen **T5 text encoder** for text embeddings.
- **RGBD Adaptation:** An enhanced patch embedding layer accommodates depth by adding a fourth input channel, initialized with the average of existing RGB channel weights, ensuring smooth integration.
- **Text Conditioning:** A **cross-attention mechanism** is incorporated across all layers of the Vision Transformer. Visual features act as queries, and text features as keys/values, allowing the network to condition visual understanding on the provided text prompt.
- **Outputs:**
 - **Affordance Map (A) & Depth Map (D):** Generated by convolutional decoders from vision transformer patch features. The affordance map is a pixel-wise probability distribution for the gripper goal. The depth map provides depth at the chosen pixel.
 - Loss: Weighted cross-entropy for affordance map (ground truth as 10-pixel radius circles for richer heatmaps). L1 loss for depth map, masked by affordance map ground truth.
 - **Gripper Width (W), Yaw (ψ), Applied Force (F_A), Grip Force (F_G):** Predicted by several Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) from average-pooled patch features.
 - Loss: Mean Squared Error (MSE) for all these regression outputs.
- **Overall Loss Function:** A weighted sum of all individual loss components.
- **Training:** 20 epochs (500,000 iterations), Adam optimizer, learning rate 5e-5. Extensive data augmentations (brightness, saturation, contrast, hue) for robustness.

**System Architecture and Low-Level Control:**
- **Hierarchical Control:** A high-level task planner (e.g., LLM or human) provides a task description. This is broken down into sequences of **action primitives** (e.g., "approach," "grasp," "lift"), which are appended to the text prompt for ForceSight.
- **Visual-Force Servoing:** The low-level controller receives the visual-force goals from ForceSight and executes incremental joint commands in a closed-loop fashion.
- **Joint Objective:** The controller minimizes a combined error: `M_end-effector = K_ee (T_k E_translation + λ_applied T_f E_applied-force)` and `M_gripper = K_g (E_width + λ_grip E_grip-force)`. This formulation explicitly integrates kinematic and force errors, with adjustable weights (λ) to prioritize modalities.
- **Primitive Switching:** The controller switches to the next action primitive when the current visual-force goals are adequately minimized, allowing for sequential task execution and error recovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on a Stretch RE1 mobile manipulator in held-out environments (mock bedroom, real kitchen) with unseen object instances (semantically similar but visually distinct from training data). The evaluation involved 10 household tasks, with 10 trials each, totaling 100 trials, along with ablation studies.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Average Fingertip Distance (m):** L2 distance between predicted and actual fingertip locations (kinematic accuracy).
- **RMSE (Applied/Grip Force):** Accuracy of force estimation.
- **Task Success Rate (%):** Percentage of completed tasks.

**Comparative Performance (Offline Goal Prediction):**
- **ForceSight (0.036m Avg Fingertip Dist)** significantly outperformed the adapted **Perceiver-Actor (PerAct) baseline (0.058m)** on kinematic goal prediction. This demonstrates ForceSight's capability to generate accurate target poses.

**Real-World Task Performance (Full System):**
- **Overall Success:** ForceSight achieved an impressive **81% task success rate (81/100 trials)** in unseen environments with unseen objects.
- **Significance of Force Goals:** This is the most critical finding.
 - In a direct ablation where the low-level controller *ignored force goals* ("w/o forces" ablation), the success rate **dropped dramatically from 81% to 50%**.
 - A separate, more controlled experiment further highlighted this: ForceSight achieved **90% success (18/20)** when using force goals, which plummeted to **45% (9/20)** when force goals were ignored.
 - Qualitative observations reinforced this: Without force goals, the robot often failed due to insufficient grip force (items falling), excessive applied force (damaging surfaces), or improper contact (failing to engage with drawers/objects).

**Impact of Ablations on Success Rate:**
The ablation studies clearly demonstrated the necessity of each component:
- **w/o forces:** 50% success (critical for performance).
- **w/o augmentation:** 25% success (highlighting the importance of data diversity for generalization).
- **w/o depth:** 20% success (compromised depth perception led to errors like early grasping).
- **w/o pre-training:** 20% success (underscoring the value of large-scale pre-training for visual features).
- **w/o text cond.:** **0% success** (confirming that text conditioning is absolutely essential for task specification and enabling varied behaviors).

**Emergent Behaviors (Discussion):**
- **Generalization to Unseen Object Instances:** Despite being trained on only one type of each object, ForceSight generalized effectively to visually distinct instances, attributed to high-level visual associations from pre-training.
- **Adaptability of Action Primitives:** The model could apply action primitives (e.g., "grasp," "push") to objects or scenarios not explicitly seen during training for those specific subtasks (e.g., "grasp" a light switch, "push" a medicine bottle), demonstrating a learned understanding of the primitive's intent rather than rote memorization.
- **Future Keyframe Prediction:** ForceSight showed an ability to predict goals "more than one keyframe into the future," implying a deeper understanding of task progression beyond immediate next steps.
- **Agent/Camera Agnosticism:** Predictions were shown to be effective regardless of the specific agent or camera perspective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ForceSight represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for mobile platforms operating in human environments. Its contributions and potential impacts are multifaceted:

- **Pioneering Visual-Force Goal Prediction:** The most significant contribution is the successful integration of explicit force goals alongside kinematic goals, predicted by a deep neural network from multimodal inputs. This elevates force sensing from a mere low-level feedback mechanism to a predictive planning objective, fundamentally changing how robots can perceive and interact with their environment. The compelling real-world results, especially the stark drop in success when force goals are ignored, definitively prove their value.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Dexterity:** By combining vision (for high-level context and target localization) with force (for precise contact and interaction), ForceSight enables the robot to perform contact-rich tasks with greater robustness and dexterity. This is crucial for tasks like picking small or delicate objects, opening drawers with varying resistance, or safely handing over objects to humans – areas where vision-only systems often falter.
- **Improved Generalization:** The ability of ForceSight to perform well in unseen environments with unseen object instances is a major leap. This addresses a critical limitation in many robot learning approaches and suggests a path towards more widely deployable and versatile robotic systems. The efficient data collection strategy and the power of pre-trained transformers are key enablers here.
- **Human-Interpretable and Controllable Robotics:** The output of ForceSight – explicit visual-force goals – is more human-interpretable than raw joint commands or complex policy networks. This transparency can facilitate debugging, allow for human oversight, and potentially enable more intuitive human-robot interaction and collaboration.
- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - **Broader Task Complexity:** The scalability of transformer models and the efficient data collection methodology suggest that ForceSight could be extended to an even wider array of complex, long-horizon tasks requiring intricate physical interaction.
 - **Deeper Integration of Modalities:** This work provides a strong case for further research into how different sensory modalities (vision, force, touch, proprioception) can be more deeply integrated at various levels of robotic planning and control.
 - **Generalization Across Robot Platforms:** While currently demonstrated on the Stretch RE1, the camera-frame relative goals suggest potential for adaptation to other mobile manipulation platforms, which would solidify its broader impact.
 - **LLM-Driven High-Level Planning:** The mention of LLMs for automating action primitive switching indicates a natural synergy with ongoing research in large language models for robotics, potentially leading to fully autonomous, language-guided execution of complex real-world tasks.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to releasing code, dataset, and trained models is highly significant, fostering reproducibility and accelerating future research by providing valuable resources to the wider robotics community.

In conclusion, ForceSight offers a compelling new paradigm for robotic manipulation by demonstrating the profound impact of incorporating force goals into high-level planning. Its blend of cutting-edge deep learning, robust data collection, and practical application paves the way for more capable, adaptable, and human-friendly robots in the future.
