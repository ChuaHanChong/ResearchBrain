# Koopman Representation of Nonlinear Virtual Environments in Kinesthetic Haptic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> This work represents a nonlinear virtual environment for kinesthetic haptic rendering with a Koopman operator model, lifting a Duffing-oscillator environment into a higher-dimensional space where its dynamics become linear. Closed-loop stability then reduces to an eigenvalue check on a combined device-environment matrix. Simulation, hardware experiments on a pantograph device, and an eleven-user perceptual study confirm the linear surrogate reproduces the nonlinear force feedback faithfully.

## Problem

- Nonlinear virtual environment models give realistic force feedback but complicate stability analysis and slow environment integration during real-time haptic rendering.
- Neural-network haptic controllers need costly online weight updates, and physics-driven network simulators only cover geometric and material nonlinearities.
- Passivity-based stability criteria are overly conservative and sacrifice transparency; absolute-stability alternatives assume passive or linear time-invariant virtual environments.

## Method

- ==Koopman operator== lifts the ==Duffing-oscillator== environment into nine third-order monomial states, fitted by ==extended dynamic mode decomposition== so lifted dynamics are linear with the coupling force as exogenous input.
- **Closed-loop matrix** assembles discretized rigid-body device dynamics with the lifted environment through ==virtual-coupling stiffness and damping==, reducing asymptotic stability to a unit-circle ==eigenvalue test==.
- States are ==retracted and relifted== at every step to block error propagation; training uses twenty-five simulated sine and cosine input trajectories spanning varied amplitudes, frequencies, and initial conditions.

## Results

- Against the ideal rigid-device simulation, the Koopman environment gave lower error than the nonlinear model, **0.002038** versus **0.002214** metres position and **0.01872** versus **0.02003** metres per second velocity, consistently across **nine** further trials.
- **Eleven** users rated similarity between the two environments at mean **8.364** and median **9** out of **10**; a Wilcoxon signed-rank test rejected the neutral threshold of six with p below **0.004**.
- All closed-loop eigenvalues lay strictly inside the unit circle, whereas Lyapunov candidate differences were inconclusive, peaking at **5.361e-4** for nonlinear and **2.376e-6** for Koopman.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Lifting a nonlinear plant into a linear space spreads sensitivity across many basis terms rather than one cubic term, buying robustness to small state perturbations.
> - Trading model dimension for linearity converts an intractable Lyapunov-candidate search into a routine eigenvalue check, and it certifies non-passive systems that passivity rejects.
> - The lifting-function set is not unique and strongly affects accuracy; no systematic design method exists, so basis choice remains an empirical, sensitive step.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Kinesthetic haptic systems are crucial for human perception and interaction with virtual environments (VEs) in various applications, including surgical training, pilot training, and medical robotics. Achieving accurate and stable force rendering is paramount for the safety and effectiveness of these operations. A significant challenge in this field lies in the necessity of simulating nonlinear VEs, as evidenced by studies demonstrating that nonlinear tissue models provide distinct and critical force feedback compared to linear models in surgical contexts. The use of nonlinear VEs is essential for realistic haptic feedback in applications demanding high accuracy.

However, the integration of nonlinear VEs introduces complexities. Traditional approaches using nonlinear dynamic models complicate stability analysis and can slow down VE integration due to their inherent computational demands. While neural network (NN)-based control methods exist to stabilize haptic systems by canceling lumped dynamics, they are often computationally intensive due to online weight updates. Physics-driven NN methods are proposed for nonlinear deformable VEs but are restricted to specific types of nonlinearities.

Stability assessment in haptic systems is a long-standing area of research. Passivity-based methods are commonly employed but often introduce conservatism, which can degrade the transparency and fidelity of the haptic system. Alternative frameworks, such as port-network approaches examining Llewellyn’s absolute stability criteria, have been developed. However, these criteria either require the VE to be passive or are limited to linear time-invariant (LTI) VEs, thus not addressing the general case of nonlinear, non-passive VEs without significant conservatism. Existing studies that specifically guarantee stability for haptic systems with nonlinear VEs also frequently rely on passivity, providing only sufficient and not necessary conditions for stability.

Against this background, the presented work introduces the Koopman operator framework as a novel approach. The Koopman operator offers a method to represent nonlinear dynamics as a linear model in a higher-dimensional space. This transformation allows the application of well-established linear system theory tools to the analysis and control of nonlinear systems, potentially circumventing the conservativeness and complexity associated with traditional nonlinear stability analysis methods in haptics. This research therefore aims to bridge the gap between the need for realistic nonlinear VEs and the challenges in their stable and efficient implementation within haptic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and investigate the use of the Koopman operator framework for representing nonlinear virtual environments (VEs) in kinesthetic haptic rendering. This foundational objective is supported by several more specific aims and driven by critical motivations in the field of haptics.

One key specific objective is to demonstrate that the Koopman operator can effectively learn and represent the dynamics of a nonlinear VE for the purpose of haptic interaction. This involves validating the accuracy of the Koopman representation against a known nonlinear model.

A second major objective is to leverage this Koopman representation to perform a closed-loop (CL) stability analysis of the entire haptic system. The goal is to show that the linear nature of the Koopman model in the lifted space simplifies this analysis compared to direct nonlinear methods, offering a less conservative alternative to traditional passivity-based approaches. This simplification could facilitate the design of stable haptic systems without sacrificing performance.

Furthermore, the research aims to assess the practical performance of the Koopman-model VE in comparison to traditional nonlinear models through both objective metrics (e.g., Root Mean Square Error) and subjective user perception studies. This includes evaluating the robustness of the Koopman representation to uncertainties inherent in the modeling of physical haptic devices.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in haptic system design and implementation:
- **Necessity of Nonlinear VEs:** Many advanced haptic applications, such as surgical simulators, require highly accurate force feedback that can only be generated by realistic, nonlinear VE models (e.g., simulating nonlinear tissue elasticity). Linear approximations often fail to capture critical perceptual cues.
- **Complexity of Nonlinear Dynamics:** Directly simulating nonlinear VEs introduces significant computational overhead and complicates the process of analyzing and guaranteeing the stability of the overall haptic system.
- **Limitations of Current Stability Analysis Methods:** Existing stability criteria for haptic systems are often either overly conservative (e.g., passivity-based methods that enforce artificial damping, thereby reducing transparency) or are restricted to linear or passive VEs, which limits their applicability to the full range of desired nonlinear interactions. Such conservativeness can compromise the fidelity and user experience.
- **Potential of Koopman Theory:** The Koopman operator offers a transformative approach by allowing nonlinear dynamics to be recast as a linear system in a higher-dimensional space. This opens the door to applying powerful, well-established linear system theory tools for analysis and control, potentially leading to more precise and less conservative stability guarantees without the computational burden of complex nonlinear solvers or online updates.
- **Robustness to Modeling Uncertainties:** The authors hypothesize that a Koopman representation, being a linear combination of lifted states, might exhibit greater robustness to small perturbations or uncertainties in the haptic device model compared to direct nonlinear models where specific nonlinear terms can amplify errors.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to advance the state-of-the-art in haptic rendering, enabling more realistic, stable, and robust interactions with complex virtual environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on leveraging the Koopman operator to model nonlinear VEs and integrating this model into a haptic system for stability analysis and performance evaluation.

#### 3.1. A. Koopman Operator Theory with Inputs

The study adopts the Koopman operator framework to represent discrete-time nonlinear dynamics `x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k)` as a linear system in a lifted space. A vector-valued lifting function `ψ(x_k, u_k)` maps the original states and inputs to a higher-dimensional space. This function is partitioned into `ϑ(x_k)` (state-dependent) and `υ(x_k, u_k)` (state- and input-dependent). Crucially, `ϑ(x_k)` is structured to contain the original states `x_k` as its initial terms, facilitating retraction back to the original state space. The Koopman operator linearly advances these lifted states, approximated by `ϑ(x_{k+1}) = Aϑ(x_k) + Bυ(x_k, u_k) + r_k`, where `A` and `B` form the Koopman matrix `U`, and `r_k` is the residual error. The Koopman matrix `U` is identified by minimizing the least-square error using a dataset of state-input snapshots, employing Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) or Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) algorithms. For the haptic VE, the input `u_k` (coupling force) is assumed to appear linearly and therefore does not necessitate lifting in concert with `x_k`.

#### 3.2. B. Closed-Loop Stability Analysis

For closed-loop stability analysis, a 1-degree-of-freedom (DoF) rigid-body model represents the haptic device: `m_d * ¨x_d(t) + b_d * ˙x_d(t) = F_c(t)`. The interaction between the device and VE is modeled by a virtual coupling force `F_c(t) = k_c(x_v(t) - x_d(t)) + b_c(˙x_v(t) - ˙x_d(t))`. Both the device dynamics and the Koopman-model VE dynamics are expressed in discrete-time state-space forms.
The Koopman representation of the VE is `ϑ_{k+1} = Aϑ_k + Bu_k`, where `u_k` is the coupling force. This coupling force is then expressed in terms of the device states `z_k` and VE states `x_k` (which are extracted from `ϑ_k`): `u_k = Mz_k + N x_k`. Substituting `x_k = Sϑ_k` where `S` extracts the original states from the lifted vector, the VE dynamics become `ϑ_{k+1} = (A + B S)ϑ_k + BMz_k`.
The device dynamics are `z_{k+1} = A_d z_k + B_d x_k`, which can be rewritten as `z_{k+1} = A_d z_k + B_d Sϑ_k`.
Combining these, a linear closed-loop system is formed: `x_{CL,k+1} = A_{CL} * x_{CL,k}`, where `x_{CL,k}` concatenates `z_k` and `ϑ_k`. Closed-loop asymptotic stability is determined by checking if all eigenvalues of the combined system matrix `A_{CL}` have magnitudes strictly less than 1. This method is posited as less conservative than passivity-based criteria.

#### 3.3. C. Lyapunov's Direct Method for Comparison

As a comparative approach, Lyapunov's direct method is reviewed. For the specific case study of a 1-DoF Duffing oscillator VE (`m_v * ¨x_v + b_v * ˙x_v + k_1 * x_v + k_2 * x_v^3 = -F_c(t)`), two Lyapunov candidate functions are proposed: `V_nonlinear` for the system with the original nonlinear VE model, and `V_Koopman` for the system with the Koopman-model VE. `V_nonlinear` is the total energy (kinetic and potential) of the device, virtual coupling, and nonlinear VE. `V_Koopman` includes device and coupling energy, plus a quadratic term `ϑ_k^T * P * ϑ_k` for the Koopman VE, where `P` satisfies a discrete-time Lyapunov equation. The aim is to highlight the challenges in definitively assessing stability using Lyapunov functions for nonlinear systems, in contrast to the eigenvalue check for the Koopman CL system.

#### 3.4. D. Experimental Validation

Experiments were conducted using a Quanser 2-DOF Pantograph, controlled via Quanser's Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) SDK in C. The pantograph's effective mass and damping coefficient were determined and tuned.
The VE was a Duffing oscillator with specific parameters for mass, damping, linear stiffness, and cubic nonlinear stiffness. Virtual coupling parameters (`k_c`, `b_c`) were selected to balance smoothness and user experience.
**Koopman Model Training**: 25 datasets were simulated from the nonlinear Duffing oscillator, using sine and cosine inputs of varied amplitudes and frequencies, and initial conditions spanning the pantograph's workspace. The lifting functions for the Koopman model were chosen as polynomials up to order 3, specifically `ϑ(x_v, ˙x_v) = [x_v ˙x_v x_v^2 ˙x_v^2 x_v˙x_v x_v^3 x_v^2˙x_v x_v˙x_v^2 ˙x_v^3]^T`, reflecting the cubic nonlinearity. The `pykoop` Python package was used for training. To prevent error propagation, states were retracted and relifted at each time step.
**Single-User Study**: A human user interacted with both the nonlinear-model VE (baseline) and the Koopman-model VE. The procedure involved displacing the VE from its equilibrium position and releasing it to observe its free response. Comparisons were made between simulated and experimental responses, and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) was used for quantitative assessment, with experimental data filtered to mitigate noise.
**Multi-User Study**: To evaluate perceptual similarity, participants interacted with both VEs by performing pulling and pushing motions. Reliable participants (consistent with baseline trials) rated the similarity between the two VEs on a 0-10 scale. A one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test was performed to determine if the median similarity rating was significantly above a neutral agreement threshold of 6.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Results span simulation, single- and multi-user studies, closed-loop stability analysis, and computational complexity, evaluated below in turn.

#### 4.1. A. Simulations

Simulated responses for both the Koopman-model VE and the nonlinear-model VE demonstrated close alignment in both position and velocity. This indicated that the Koopman representation effectively reproduced the dynamics of the nonlinear Duffing-oscillator VE under ideal simulation conditions. An initial velocity mismatch between the device end-effector (EE) and VE states was observed, attributed to the simplified device model's initial zero acceleration contrasting with the VE's non-zero acceleration. This mismatch was subsequently resolved by the virtual coupling.

#### 4.2. B. Single-User Study

Experimental results showed that the responses for the Koopman-model VE and the nonlinear-model VE were almost identical. Both VEs achieved a satisfactory level of fidelity, evidenced by the alignment between VE states and device EE states. While velocity curves exhibited less smoothness due to noise and quantization, the experiments confirmed the Koopman model's validity as a representation of the Duffing-oscillator VE.

Comparisons between simulated and experimental EE states revealed that experimental responses were "softer" than simulations. This discrepancy was attributed to simplifications in the device model (Equation 11), specifically the neglect of structural flexibility in the pantograph joints and capstan drive. Despite tuning the device damping to minimize RMSE, the simplified model could not fully capture the device's true dynamics.

A key finding was the comparative robustness of the Koopman-model VE. Across the initial trial and nine additional experimental groups with varying initial conditions (summarized in Table III and V), the Koopman-model VE consistently yielded lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) values compared to the nonlinear-model VE when both were compared against the simulated baseline (which assumed a rigid-body haptic device). This suggests that the Koopman model is more robust to structural uncertainties or unmodeled dynamics of the physical haptic device. This robustness is explained by the Koopman representation's linear combination of lifted states. While the nonlinear model amplifies small perturbations in VE position through its cubic term, the Koopman identification process learns a model that optimally represents the dynamics via a linear combination, thereby distributing the influence across multiple lifted states and making it less sensitive to errors in any single state variable.

#### 4.3. C. Stability Analysis

Application of Lyapunov's direct method proved inconclusive for both the nonlinear-model and Koopman-model VEs. The maximum per-step difference in the Lyapunov candidate (`∆V`) was 5.361 x 10^-4 for the nonlinear VE and 2.376 x 10^-6 for the Koopman VE, neither being strictly negative at all times. Although `∆V` for the Koopman model was negative or near zero more frequently, a definitive conclusion on stability using this method was challenging due to the difficulty in constructing a precise Lyapunov candidate for the nonlinear system.

In contrast, the closed-loop stability analysis leveraging the Koopman representation of the VE demonstrated asymptotic stability. An eigenvalue check on the `A_{CL}` matrix, representing the combined linear system in the lifted space, revealed that all eigenvalues lay strictly inside the unit circle. This provides a clear and direct confirmation of stability for the system. This finding underscores the advantage of the Koopman framework in simplifying stability analysis, making it more accessible and less conservative than traditional methods which often provide only sufficient conditions.

The study also noted that the choice of lifting functions impacts the model's accuracy, with a Koopman model using 8 (instead of 9) lifting functions yielding a higher RMSE, yet the closed-loop system remained asymptotically stable. This highlights a sensitivity to basis function selection while also indicating a degree of robustness in stability.

#### 4.4. D. Multi-User Study

The multi-user study involved 11 reliable participants. The mean similarity rating between the Koopman-model VE and the baseline nonlinear-model VE was 8.364, with a median of 9 (on a scale of 0-10). A one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirmed that this median rating was statistically significantly greater than 6 (p < 0.004), which was the predefined threshold for agreement. This outcome indicates a high degree of perceived similarity by users, supporting the conclusion that the Koopman model provides an effective and perceptually accurate representation of the nonlinear Duffing oscillator dynamics for haptic interaction.

#### 4.5. E. Computational Complexity

While the Koopman-model VE update involves matrix multiplication with a computational complexity of `O(p^2)` (where `p` is the dimension of the lifted-state vector), this is generally more complex than an explicit fourth-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) method for a nonlinear model (`O(k)`, `k` states). However, `p` is typically modest, making the cost manageable. Furthermore, if the nonlinear-model VE requires implicit RK methods for numerical stability (with `O(k^3)` complexity), the Koopman-model VE update becomes comparatively more efficient.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel application of the Koopman operator for representing nonlinear virtual environments (VEs) in kinesthetic haptic systems, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts.

Firstly, the work demonstrates the effectiveness of the Koopman framework in accurately modeling and rendering the dynamics of nonlinear VEs, as validated through both simulation and experimental results with a Duffing-oscillator VE. The high perceptual similarity confirmed by a multi-user study indicates that this data-driven linear representation can provide a realistic haptic experience comparable to a direct nonlinear model. This could facilitate the development of more complex and perceptually rich haptic simulations for applications such as surgical training, virtual prototyping, and rehabilitation, where accurate nonlinear force feedback is critical.

Secondly, a key contribution is the enabler for a more convenient and less conservative closed-loop stability analysis. By transforming the nonlinear VE into a linear system in a lifted space, the stability of the entire haptic system can be directly assessed through an eigenvalue analysis of the combined system matrix. This bypasses the challenges associated with constructing definitive Lyapunov candidates for nonlinear systems and offers a less conservative criterion compared to traditional passivity-based approaches, which often compromise transparency and fidelity to guarantee stability. This simplification in stability analysis can streamline the design and tuning of virtual coupling parameters and control strategies for stable haptic interaction with nonlinear VEs.

Thirdly, the research highlights an unexpected benefit: the Koopman-model VE demonstrated increased robustness to uncertainties in the haptic device's physical model. The consistent observation of lower RMSE values for the Koopman model compared to the nonlinear model, when evaluated against an ideal rigid-body simulation, suggests that the Koopman representation is less sensitive to small deviations or unmodeled dynamics in the device. This inherent robustness, stemming from the linear combination of lifted states, could lead to more reliable and adaptable haptic systems in real-world scenarios where precise device modeling is challenging.

Finally, regarding computational complexity, while the Koopman model's `O(p^2)` matrix computations are generally more complex than explicit RK4 methods for nonlinear VEs, they remain manageable for typical lifted-state dimensions. Furthermore, the Koopman approach proves to be computationally more efficient than implicit RK methods, which are often necessary for numerically stable updates of stiff or highly nonlinear VE models. This computational efficiency can be crucial for real-time haptic rendering applications.

In summary, this work provides a new paradigm for designing and analyzing haptic systems interacting with nonlinear VEs. By integrating Koopman operator theory, it offers a pathway to achieve greater realism, improved stability analysis, enhanced robustness to modeling uncertainties, and potentially better computational efficiency in specific contexts. This could have a substantial impact on the fidelity and reliability of next-generation haptic interfaces across diverse application domains. The acknowledged challenge of systematically selecting lifting functions points to a relevant area for future research to further broaden the applicability of this method.
