# SignBot: Learning Human-to-Humanoid Sign Language Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> SignBot introduces a framework that enables humanoid robots to autonomously perform interactive sign language communication by integrating precise motion control for full-body and dexterous hand gestures with a cognitive system for sign language translation, comprehension, and generation. The system successfully facilitates natural interaction with deaf and hard-of-hearing users in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing sign language AI systems primarily function in virtual environments, lacking the physical interaction capabilities essential for real-world communication with deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) individuals.
- Humanoid robots struggle with autonomously executing complex, dexterous sign language movements due to the high degrees of freedom required and inherent hardware limitations of robotic hands and wrists.
- Current robot imitation methods for sign language often rely on teleoperation or fail to accurately capture the nuances of hand gestures and full-body coordination, limiting autonomous interaction.

## Method

- A cerebellum-inspired motion control system employs a decoupled policy for upper-body sign tracking and lower-body stability, trained in simulation with precise motion retargeting for the robot's body and dexterous hands.
- A cerebral-oriented system integrates an LLM-based sign language translator, responder, and generator to enable real-time comprehension of user input and generate contextually appropriate sign language replies.
- Sim-to-real deployment is supported by domain randomization during policy training and the Ruckig algorithm for smooth, jerk-constrained online trajectory generation, ensuring safety and fluidity in physical interaction.

## Results

- SignBot achieved superior performance in simulation, demonstrating the lowest DoF position and yaw angle tracking errors and the highest cumulative reward values across various sign language difficulty levels compared to baseline methods.
- The framework exhibited strong generalization capabilities, successfully imitating sign language gestures across different robot embodiments (legged Unitree H1, wheeled W1) and diverse datasets (CSL-Daily, How2Sign).
- User studies with DHH evaluators indicated overall satisfaction and high perceived convenience, highlighting the system's potential for positive social impact in daily life, despite current limitations in facial expression and regional sign language variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling control policies for the upper and lower body effectively manages the high dimensionality of sign language gestures, enabling precise hand and arm movements while maintaining overall robot stability.
> - A meticulous motion retargeting pipeline, accounting for anatomical differences between humans and robots, is crucial for accurately translating nuanced human sign language into executable robot kinematics.
> - Integrating low-level motor control with high-level cognitive AI modules (translation, comprehension, generation) creates a comprehensive system for fully autonomous and natural human-robot sign language interaction.
