# SignBot: Learning Human-to-Humanoid Sign Language Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> SignBot introduces a framework that enables humanoid robots to autonomously perform interactive sign language communication by integrating precise motion control for full-body and dexterous hand gestures with a cognitive system for sign language translation, comprehension, and generation. The system successfully facilitates natural interaction with deaf and hard-of-hearing users in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing sign language AI systems primarily function in virtual environments, lacking the physical interaction capabilities essential for real-world communication with deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) individuals.
- Humanoid robots struggle with autonomously executing complex, dexterous sign language movements due to the high degrees of freedom required and inherent hardware limitations of robotic hands and wrists.
- Current robot imitation methods for sign language often rely on teleoperation or fail to accurately capture the nuances of hand gestures and full-body coordination, limiting autonomous interaction.

## Method

- A cerebellum-inspired motion control system employs a decoupled policy for upper-body sign tracking and lower-body stability, trained in simulation with precise motion retargeting for the robot's body and dexterous hands.
- A cerebral-oriented system integrates an LLM-based sign language translator, responder, and generator to enable real-time comprehension of user input and generate contextually appropriate sign language replies.
- Sim-to-real deployment is supported by domain randomization during policy training and the Ruckig algorithm for smooth, jerk-constrained online trajectory generation, ensuring safety and fluidity in physical interaction.

## Results

- SignBot achieved superior performance in simulation, demonstrating the lowest DoF position and yaw angle tracking errors and the highest cumulative reward values across various sign language difficulty levels compared to baseline methods.
- The framework exhibited strong generalization capabilities, successfully imitating sign language gestures across different robot embodiments (legged Unitree H1, wheeled W1) and diverse datasets (CSL-Daily, How2Sign).
- User studies with DHH evaluators indicated overall satisfaction and high perceived convenience, highlighting the system's potential for positive social impact in daily life, despite current limitations in facial expression and regional sign language variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling control policies for the upper and lower body effectively manages the high dimensionality of sign language gestures, enabling precise hand and arm movements while maintaining overall robot stability.
> - A meticulous motion retargeting pipeline, accounting for anatomical differences between humans and robots, is crucial for accurately translating nuanced human sign language into executable robot kinematics.
> - Integrating low-level motor control with high-level cognitive AI modules (translation, comprehension, generation) creates a comprehensive system for fully autonomous and natural human-robot sign language interaction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Sign language is a fundamental communication method for individuals who are deaf or hard-of-hearing (DHH). Advancements in fields such as computer vision and large language models (LLMs) have led to progress in sign language applications, including generation, translation, and recognition. These technologies can process and convert sign language representations into text, video, or mesh formats. However, the practical utility of these systems for assisting individuals with disabilities has been limited, primarily because they often lack the capacity for physical, real-world interaction.

Embodied Artificial Intelligence (EAI) aims to bridge this gap by integrating AI models into physical agents, enabling them to interact with and execute tasks in real-world environments. Humanoid robots, with their human-like structures, are considered suitable platforms for such integration, allowing them to perform daily tasks and foster natural human-robot interaction. Prior research in humanoid robot imitation of human behavior often employs whole-body control learning paradigms, sometimes decoupling upper and lower body policies or providing reference motions from human datasets. Existing methods for sign language applications on robots, however, typically face challenges: teleoperation-based approaches rely on human control rather than autonomous execution, and learning-based control methods often overlook the complexity of dexterous hand movements and the limited degrees of freedom (DoFs) in robotic hands and wrists, which are crucial for the rich and diverse movements of sign language.

SignBot positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical need for autonomous, physically interactive sign language systems on humanoid robot platforms. It seeks to integrate state-of-the-art sign language processing with robust humanoid control, overcoming the limitations of previous approaches by focusing on precise whole-body and dexterous hand movement imitation and integrating a comprehensive communicative AI system. This work aims to extend the capabilities of existing sign language AI from abstract models to embodied physical agents, thereby enhancing communication accessibility for the DHH community in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing SignBot stems from the existing communication disparities faced by the DHH community. The limited number of sign language proficient individuals necessitates technological solutions to bridge communication gaps. While recent advancements in computer vision and LLMs have improved sign language generation, translation, and recognition, their impact on facilitating real-world interaction for DHH individuals has been restricted. These systems predominantly operate in virtual or model-based environments and lack the physical embodiment required for natural, direct human-robot communication.

The emergence of embodied humanoid robots presents an opportunity to address this limitation. Humanoid robots, with their anthropomorphic form, possess the potential to seamlessly integrate into daily environments and interact physically with humans. However, leveraging humanoid robots for sign language interaction presents several technical challenges:
- **Lack of autonomous sign language execution:** Existing teleoperation methods require constant human input, preventing robots from performing sign language autonomously.
- **Complexity of dexterous hand movements:** Learning-based control methods often focus on gross body movements and struggle with the intricate and high-DoF requirements of dexterous hand gestures central to sign language.
- **Hardware limitations:** Many robotic hands and wrists have limited DoFs, restricting their ability to accurately express the nuanced movements of sign language.

Against this background, the key objectives of the SignBot research are:
1. **Develop a comprehensive framework for human-robot sign language interaction:** This involves integrating motion control for precise gesture execution with cognitive modules for comprehension and response.
2. **Enable precise sign language execution on humanoid robots:** The system aims to accurately track and imitate diverse human sign language motions, ensuring stability and fidelity in robotic performance. This includes addressing the challenges of dexterous hand movements and full-body coordination.
3. **Ensure generalization and domain adaptation:** SignBot should be capable of transferring learned policies to different robot embodiments (e.g., legged vs. wheeled) and adapting to various sign language datasets, demonstrating robustness across diverse scenarios.
4. **Facilitate natural and effective communication:** The framework aims to enable the robot to understand user-expressed sign language, interpret its semantic content, and generate appropriate sign language responses, thereby fostering natural communication between robots and humans in real-world settings.
5. **Contribute to communication accessibility for the DHH community:** Ultimately, the work seeks to provide a practical and promising solution to improve communication accessibility and foster interactions with minority communities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SignBot is presented as a novel framework for human-robot sign language interaction, integrating a cerebellum-inspired motion control component with a cerebral-oriented module for comprehension and interaction. The framework is structured into three main stages: Motion Retargeting, Policy Training (SignBot's Cerebellum), and Sign Language Interaction (SignBot's Cerebral).

**A. Motion Retargeting**
This initial stage focuses on converting human sign language data into a format compatible with robotic kinematics. The process involves separate retargeting for the human body and hands to account for anatomical differences between humans and robots.
- **Body Retargeting:** Utilizes a system based on established methods to map source keypoints to target keypoints using a dual T-Pose as a spatial alignment reference. Local quaternion joint representations are converted to axis angles. To enhance the naturalness of robot movements, two additional DoFs are introduced at the robot's wrist, transforming 1D wrist and shoulder joints into 3D joints.
- **Hand Retargeting:** Adapts a preprocessing method for the Linker hand, which is the dexterous robot hand used. This is formulated as an optimization problem to minimize the difference between human hand model keypoints and the dexterous hand's joint positions. Adjustments are made to the scale factor due to the Linker hand's size, and the regularization term is modified for smoother transitions between consecutive frames.

**B. SignBot’s Cerebellar: Control Policy Training**
This component is responsible for low-level movement control, enabling the humanoid robot to track and imitate sign language gestures within a simulated environment, with a focus on Sim-to-Real deployment.
- **Problem Formulation:** The task of tracking human sign language motions is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The objective is to learn a control policy that maximizes discounted cumulative rewards.
- **Observation Space:** The robot's observation ($o_t$) comprises proprioception ($o_p_t$) (joint positions, velocities, root linear/angular velocity, root projected gravity, previous action) and goal imitation ($o_y_t$) (positions of selected reference keypoints, positions and linear velocities of all reference joints). Domain randomization is applied in simulation to enhance robustness.
- **Decoupled Policy:** Given the high DoFs and the precise coordination required for sign language, a decoupled architecture is adopted:
 - **Upper-Body Policy ($\pi_{upper}$):** Primarily responsible for tracking the retargeted upper-body actions (including hands). This policy is learned through an imitation learning paradigm, minimizing an f-divergence (e.g., KL-divergence) between the robot's actions and the retargeted actions.
 - **Lower-Body Policy ($\pi_{lower}$):** Ensures the robot's balance in a default standing pose while adapting to the movements of the upper body. This policy is trained using a reinforcement learning algorithm, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for legged humanoid robots and Model Predictive Control (MPC) for wheeled humanoid robots, to maximize cumulative rewards.
- **Reward Design:** The reward function ($r_t$) is a weighted sum of:
 - **Task Rewards ($r_T$):** Measure the robot's performance in tracking joint motions and body velocities (e.g., DoF position, keypoint position, body linear velocity, body roll/pitch/yaw).
 - **Penalty Rewards ($r_P$):** Discourage undesirable outcomes such as falling or violating dynamic constraints (e.g., joint or torque limits, DoF position limits).
 - **Regularization Rewards ($r_R$):** Align the robot's sign language gestures with human preferences for motion style and safe deployment (e.g., time in air, new contact, drag, contact force, stumble, DoF acceleration, action rate, energy, DoF limit violation, DoF deviation, vertical linear velocity, horizontal angular velocity, projected gravity). Detailed reward terms are provided in Appendix A.1.

**C. SignBot’s Cerebral: Sign Language Reasoning**
This system handles high-level reasoning, enabling the robot to understand and respond to sign language. It processes user input and generates appropriate sign language responses.
- **Implementation:** The cerebral system integrates three main models:
 - **Sign Language Translator:** Implemented with an LLM. During pre-training, it extracts sign language features from videos and images, aligns them with the language model's dimensions, and then inputs them into the model. Fine-tuning uses translation text from sign language datasets to construct supervised labels.
 - **Sign Language Responder:** Utilizes the DeepSeek-Chat API for semantic comprehension and multi-turn conversations. A prompt template is designed to integrate text-format sign language into the prompt, generating contextually appropriate replies. The system is configured to be aware of the vocabulary and scope of the CSL dataset to manage output relevance.
 - **Sign Language Generator:** Autoregressively generates sign motions from text input using a multilingual LM. It employs a decoupled VQVAE tokenizer to map continuous sign motions into discrete tokens for the upper body (UB), left hand (LH), and right hand (RH) movements based on the SMPL-X model. Word-level signs, represented by discrete tokens, are retrieved from external dictionaries and fed along with the text sequence into an LM encoder. A multi-head decoding strategy, with three language modeling heads, predicts motion tokens for each body part simultaneously at each step. These derived motion tokens are then used to reconstruct sign motions.
- **Sim-to-Real Deployment:** For real-world deployment, the Ruckig algorithm is used for online trajectory generation. This algorithm computes time-optimal trajectories with third-order (jerk) constraints and complete kinematic targets, ensuring smooth transitions between robot positions. Intermediate values are interpolated to maintain motion smoothness, and joint angles near physical limits are avoided for safety.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The performance of SignBot was evaluated in both simulated (IsaacGym) and real-world environments across four key aspects: accuracy, generalization, naturalness, and interactivity. Experiments utilized the CSL-Daily and How2Sign (ASL) datasets, and involved Unitree H1 (legged), W1 (wheeled) robots, and the Linker hand.

**A. Accuracy: Alignment of Sign Language between Human and Robot**
- **Methodology:** The CSL-Daily dataset was used, segmented into three difficulty levels (simple, intermediate, difficult) based on sentence length. High-fidelity 3D SMPLX annotations were generated using OSX for body pose and WiLoR for refined hand pose. The Unitree H1 robot was modified to include two additional DoFs at the wrist to enable more flexible gestures.
- **Results:** Table I demonstrated that SignBot achieved the lowest error in DoF position tracking and yaw angle tracking, alongside the highest cumulative reward values, across all difficulty levels. This indicated superior control policy effectiveness compared to baselines such as Whole-Body Tracking, Whole-Body Tracking + AMP, and SignBot (w/o Lower-Body Tracking). The improvement was attributed to the provision of upper body DoF position data and lower body bent standing position tracking. SignBot also exhibited strong stability, reflected in controlled yaw and roll&pitch angles. The length of sentences did not significantly affect performance, though short sentences occasionally led to larger keypoint tracking errors due to frequent global pose adjustments and robot resets. A case study (Figure 7) visually illustrated SignBot's ability to maintain stability and motion tracking where other methods struggled, particularly with the increased upper body weight from the Linker hand and modified wrists.

**B. Generalization: Imitation across diverse datasets and robots**
- **Robot Embodiments:** Figure 3 illustrated the alignment between human and robot sign language gestures after SignBot's retargeting, demonstrating high accuracy on both the legged Unitree H1 and the wheeled W1 robots. This indicated SignBot's capability to generalize across different physical embodiments.
- **Diverse Datasets:** Table II presented SignBot's performance on the How2Sign (ASL) dataset, where it consistently achieved minimal tracking error across easy, medium, and hard difficulty levels. This result supported SignBot's generalization capability to different sign languages.

**C. Naturalness: Sim-to-Real Human-Robot Interaction**
- **Real-world Demonstration:** Figure 4 showcased a real-world interaction scenario with the W1 robot acting as a supermarket cashier, communicating with a sign language user.
- **Translation and Generation Module Evaluation:** Table III compared SignBot's sign language translation and generation modules against current state-of-the-art baselines on ASL and CSL datasets. SignBot's modules generally outperformed existing baselines in metrics such as BLEU-1, BLEU-4, and ROUGE for translation, and DTW-PA-JPE for generation. During real-world deployment, evaluation with non-CSL test sets showed that the performance degradation for CSL was not significant, suggesting a basic level of reliability for sign language interaction within the designed vocabulary.

**D. Interactivity: Human Feedback from the DHH Community**
- **User Study:** Ten DHH evaluators assessed the work based on comprehensive documentation and video recordings of the interaction process (direct interaction was not conducted due to venue and safety constraints). Evaluations covered satisfaction, enjoyment, naturalness, accuracy, understanding, and convenience, on a scale of 1-10.
- **Results:** Table IV showed that most evaluators provided scores above 6 across categories, indicating general satisfaction. The "convenience" criterion received the highest average score (6.60), suggesting that the DHH community perceived the research as meaningful with potential positive daily life impact. Excluding two likely outlier evaluators, average scores increased across all criteria (e.g., satisfaction average rose to 7.13, naturalness to 6.75, and accuracy to 6.50), despite the current absence of facial expressions or lip movements in the robot.
- **Limitations Identified:** Feedback from the DHH community highlighted several current limitations: persistent errors in state-of-the-art open-source sign language translation and generation models, regional variations in sign language not fully accounted for, and hardware limitations precluding facial expressions or lip movements, which can affect sign language understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SignBot represents a notable advancement in the field of automatic sign language interaction through embodied humanoid robots. The research introduces a novel framework that effectively integrates low-level motion control with high-level cognitive abilities for sign language comprehension and generation. This cerebellar and cerebral cooperation mechanism enables a humanoid robot to not only accurately execute diverse sign language motions but also to engage in meaningful communication.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Bridging the Physical Interaction Gap:** SignBot addresses a critical limitation of previous sign language AI systems, which primarily operated in virtual environments. By deploying on physical humanoid robots, it moves closer to facilitating real-world, direct communication for the DHH community.
- **Enhanced Precision and Stability in Robotic Sign Language:** The decoupled policy approach and meticulous reward design enable the robots to achieve high accuracy in tracking complex sign language gestures, including dexterous hand movements and maintaining whole-body stability, which is often a challenge for general humanoid control methods.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated ability of SignBot to generalize across different robot embodiments (legged and wheeled) and diverse sign language datasets (CSL-Daily and How2Sign) indicates a robust and adaptable framework. This scalability is crucial for broader deployment and impact.
- **Foundational Solution for Accessible Communication:** SignBot provides a foundational solution for future applications, such as daily service robots for the DHH community. The user study feedback, particularly the high scores for "convenience," indicates a perceived positive social value and potential to positively impact daily lives.
- **Integration of Multimodal AI:** The framework effectively integrates components for vision-based sign language translation, semantic interpretation via LLMs, and motion generation, showcasing a multimodal AI system for complex human-robot interaction.

The potential impact of SignBot is substantial for improving communication accessibility for the DHH community. By enabling humanoid robots to autonomously understand and produce sign language, this research contributes to fostering more inclusive environments and reducing communication barriers. Future developments, such as incorporating facial expressions and addressing regional sign language variations, could further enhance the naturalness and utility of such embodied systems, paving the way for ubiquitous sign language robots.
