# BridgeVLA++: A Data-Efficient, Generalizable, and Memory-Augmented Vision-Language-Action Framework for 3D Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> BridgeVLA++ introduces a Vision-Language-Action framework that enhances 3D manipulation by integrating data-efficient learning and robust generalization with an explicit spatio-temporal memory system. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulated benchmarks, including memory-dependent tasks, and demonstrates high sample efficiency on real-world robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Existing 3D VLA models often suffer from data scarcity, limited generalization, and a 'modality gap' when adapting 2D VLM priors to 3D inputs, hindering effective skill transfer.
- Most VLA policies lack temporal context and persistent spatial understanding, making them inadequate for long-horizon tasks, tracking sub-goals, distinguishing task stages, or handling occluded geometry.
- Converting 3D observations into 2D representations or token sequences for VLMs can discard crucial spatial correspondence, impacting the efficiency and precision of 3D manipulation policies.

## Method

- The **BridgeVLA++** framework employs a ==2D-heatmap pre-training== stage to align a VLM's output with language-conditioned spatial localization, leveraging large-scale 2D detection datasets to bridge the modality gap.
- It utilizes a ==coarse-to-fine 3D action fine-tuning== approach with multi-view orthographic projections of point clouds and heatmap-based translation prediction, preserving spatial correspondence.
- A unified ==spatio-temporal memory architecture== is introduced, comprising temporal memory (initial anchor views, recent keyframes, adaptive sub-goal keyframes) for coarse-stage reasoning and spatial memory (initial scene point cloud) for occlusion-robust fine localization.

## Results

- BridgeVLA++ achieved state-of-the-art average success rates of **93.7%** on RLBench, **96.0%** on RMBench (outperforming MemoryWAM by **13 points**), and **99.7%** on MemoryBench, demonstrating robust performance across diverse manipulation tasks.
- The model exhibited strong generalization, achieving **65.2%** on COLOSSEUM and **51.1%** on GemBench, while maintaining or slightly improving BridgeVLA's robustness to appearance-level and distribution shifts.
- In real-world experiments, BridgeVLA++ achieved a **95.4%** success rate on Franka with only 3 demonstrations per task and a **93.3%** average success rate on memory-dependent tasks with a Dobot, showing high sample efficiency and practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning the VLM's input and output in a shared 2D image space, through multi-view orthographic projections and heatmap-based action prediction, effectively unifies VLM semantic generalization with the data efficiency of 3D manipulation.
> - Explicitly differentiating between temporal memory (for interaction history and sub-goals) and persistent spatial memory (for scene geometry and occlusion recovery) at different stages of action prediction significantly improves performance on complex, memory-dependent tasks.
> - Language-conditioned heatmap pre-training proves crucial for transferring robust semantic grounding from pre-trained 2D VLMs to fine-grained robot manipulation, enabling high data efficiency and generalization even with limited robot-specific data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges within the field of robot manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The broader landscape of VLA models has seen increased interest in leveraging pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for learning generalizable and robust manipulation policies.

Current VLA approaches can be broadly categorized into:
- **2D Image-based Policies:** These models, often leveraging large VLM backbones, process 2D images and typically require extensive robot data for effective generalization. Their reliance on 2D images may not fully exploit the geometric structure inherent in 3D manipulation tasks.
- **3D Manipulation Policies:** These methods aim for higher sample efficiency by directly processing 3D representations such as point clouds or voxels. Examples include methods that construct 3D feature clouds (e.g., Act3D) or operate in voxel space (e.g., PerAct). Some recent works (e.g., RVT, RVT-2) use orthographic projections to convert 3D point clouds into 2D images, balancing geometric information with computational efficiency.

Existing 3D VLA models face specific challenges:
- **Data Scarcity and Generalization:** Many require substantial datasets and exhibit limited generalization, particularly under distribution shifts. Introducing 3D inputs into VLMs often creates a modality gap from their 2D image pre-training, hindering the transfer of VLM priors and exploitation of 3D structure. Some token-sequence based action predictions may also discard spatial correspondence.
- **Lack of Memory:** A significant limitation across many VLA and 3D manipulation policies is their predominantly Markovian formulation, where actions are predicted solely from the current observation. This approach is insufficient for tasks requiring temporal context (e.g., tracking sub-goals, distinguishing task stages) or when task-relevant geometry becomes occluded during execution.

BridgeVLA (the preceding work) positioned itself to unify the semantic generalization capabilities of pre-trained VLMs with the data efficiency of 3D manipulation policies. It addressed the data efficiency and generalization issues by aligning input and output in a unified 2D image space through multi-view orthographic projections of point clouds and heatmap-based action prediction.

BridgeVLA++ extends this line of research by specifically targeting the memory challenge within the unified 3D VLA framework. It aims to equip such a model with explicit spatio-temporal memory, thereby expanding its applicability to complex, long-horizon, and memory-dependent manipulation scenarios, without compromising its established data efficiency and generalization. This positions BridgeVLA++ as a contribution towards more capable and autonomous robotic systems that can operate effectively in open-world, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to develop a unified 3D vision-language-action (VLA) framework that is simultaneously data-efficient, robustly generalizable, and capable of effective memory-aware robot manipulation. This objective is broken down into specific motivations and aims for both the foundational BridgeVLA model and its memory-augmented extension, BridgeVLA++.

**Motivation and Objectives for BridgeVLA (Original Framework):**

1. **Addressing Data-Hungriness and Limited Generalization:** Existing 3D VLA methods often require substantial amounts of data for training and demonstrate restricted generalization capabilities, particularly when faced with shifts in data distribution. The motivation was to mitigate these limitations to enable application in data-scarce and open-world scenarios.
2. **Bridging the Modality Gap and Preserving Spatial Correspondence:** Many VLA models, especially those built on 2D image-pretrained VLMs, encounter a "modality gap" when adapted to 3D inputs. Additionally, methods that encode actions as token sequences may discard the inherent spatial correspondence between 3D observations and actions, which is a key factor in the efficiency of 3D policies.
 - **Objective:** To unify the semantic generalization of pre-trained VLMs with the geometric efficiency of 3D manipulation policies.
 - **Objective:** To achieve this by aligning the VLM's input and output within a shared 2D image space. This involves projecting 3D point clouds into multi-view orthographic images and predicting intermediate heatmaps before generating robot actions.
3. **Scalable Pre-training:** Developing a pre-training strategy that effectively transfers VLM priors to robot manipulation without extensive robot-specific data.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a scalable language-conditioned heatmap pre-training stage that teaches the VLM to ground language-specified objects in 2D heatmap space using large-scale 2D detection datasets.

**Motivation and Objectives for BridgeVLA++ (Memory-Augmented Extension):**

1. **Overcoming Memory Limitations:** A significant challenge for most VLA and 3D manipulation policies is their reliance on the current observation alone (Markovian assumption). This approach is inadequate for tasks requiring temporal context, such as tracking interaction history, distinguishing visually similar situations at different task stages, or when task-relevant geometry becomes occluded during execution.
 - **Objective:** To equip BridgeVLA with explicit memory capabilities to reason over observation histories.
2. **Unified Spatio-Temporal Memory for Comprehensive Context:** The memory system needs to provide both a sequential understanding of events and a persistent spatial understanding of the environment.
 - **Objective:** To develop a unified spatio-temporal memory architecture that models both persistent spatial context (e.g., initial scene geometry to recover occluded regions) and temporal interaction history (e.g., past actions and sub-goal completions).
3. **Preserving Core Capabilities:** The introduction of memory should enhance, not detract from, the existing strengths of BridgeVLA.
 - **Objective:** To integrate the memory architecture while preserving BridgeVLA's data efficiency and generalization capabilities.
4. **Extending to Complex Manipulation Scenarios:** Enabling the framework to handle more intricate and multi-robot tasks.
 - **Objective:** To demonstrate the scalability of the memory-augmented framework to bimanual manipulation settings and across different robotic platforms.

In summary, the motivation behind BridgeVLA++ is to create a robust, adaptable, and intelligent robotic manipulation system that can learn efficiently from limited data, generalize across diverse conditions, and effectively perform long-horizon tasks requiring memory of past observations and persistent spatial awareness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of BridgeVLA++ is built upon the foundational BridgeVLA framework, which emphasizes input-output alignment within a 2D image space, and extends it with a spatio-temporal memory architecture.

#### 3.1. BridgeVLA: Foundation for 3D Manipulation: Problem Formulation

The policy learns from expert demonstrations to predict the end-effector configuration of the next keyframe. An action `a_t` comprises 3D translation `x_t`, 6D rotation `R_t`, gripper state `g_t`, and a collision-avoidance flag `c_t`. The policy `π_B(o_t, l)` predicts `a_t` from the current observation `o_t` (RGB-D images) and a language instruction `l`.

#### 3.1.2. 2D-Heatmap Pre-Training

To align the VLM with spatial action prediction, an intermediate pre-training stage is introduced:
- **Dataset:** RoboPoint (120K object-detection split). Each sample includes an image, a text prompt, and bounding boxes.
- **Target Representation:** Ground-truth heatmaps are constructed by averaging normalized spatially truncated Gaussian probability maps centered at object bounding box locations.
- **VLM Backbone:** PaliGemma (SigLIP vision encoder and Gemma transformer). It processes the input image and text prompt.
- **Output:** Image tokens from the VLM are rearranged into a 2D feature grid, then decoded into a heatmap using a convex-upsampling module.
- **Loss:** Cross-entropy between the predicted and ground-truth heatmaps.
This stage modifies the VLM's output interface from token generation to language-conditioned spatial localization, preparing it for fine-grained manipulation.

#### 3.1.3. 3D Action Fine-Tuning

The pre-trained VLM and heatmap decoder are transferred for 3D action learning:
- **Input Processing:** RGB-D images are used to reconstruct a colored point cloud, which is then rendered into three orthographic projection images (top, front, right views). These images and the language instruction are fed to the VLM. Crucially, no proprioceptive signals are directly input to the VLM, maintaining input alignment with pre-training.
- **Translation Prediction:**
 - The VLM predicts a 2D translational heatmap for each orthographic view.
 - Candidate 3D locations in the workspace are sampled. Each candidate is projected onto the three views, and its score is calculated by summing the corresponding heatmap values.
 - The 3D candidate with the highest score is selected as the target end-effector translation.
- **Rotation, Gripper, and Collision Prediction:**
 - Global features (max-pooled VLM output tokens) and local features (VLM output token at the projected 2D location of the predicted 3D translation) from the three views are concatenated.
 - This combined feature vector is passed to a three-layer MLP.
 - Rotation is predicted using a continuous 6D representation. Gripper state and collision avoidance are predicted via two-class softmax.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Refinement:**
 - A first pass (coarse stage) predicts an approximate translation over the full workspace.
 - The point cloud is then cropped and magnified around this coarse prediction.
 - A second set of orthographic images (fine stage) is rendered from this zoomed local point cloud and processed by the same VLM backbone to refine the translation.
- **Fine-tuning Objective:** A composite loss `L_base` combines cross-entropy for translation heatmaps (at both coarse and fine stages), squared Frobenius norm for rotation, and cross-entropy for gripper state and collision.
- **Data Augmentation:** Random rigid-body transformations are applied to the point cloud and ground-truth actions.

#### 3.2. BridgeVLA++: Memory-Augmented Extension

BridgeVLA++ extends BridgeVLA by making the policy memory-conditioned: `a_t = π_M(o_t, l, M_t)`, where `M_t = (T_t, S_t)` represents temporal and spatial memory. Memory is integrated at specific stages: temporal memory for coarse-stage reasoning and spatial memory for fine-stage localization.

#### 3.2.1. Temporal Memory for Coarse-Stage Reasoning

Temporal memory `T_t` helps determine "what to do next" by providing interaction history:
- **Components:**
 - **Initial Anchor Views (A_0):** Visual tokens from the initial point cloud rendered into coarse-stage orthographic views, providing a fixed global reference.
 - **Neighboring Keyframes (H_nbr):** The `n` most recent executed keyframes (visual tokens), providing short-term context.
 - **Adaptive Sub-Goal Keyframes (H_sub):** Informative historical observations, adaptively selected to represent longer-term task progress.
- **Adaptive Selection Module:** A lightweight learnable module `D_ϕ` uses a query token attending to memory-conditioned tokens to predict a retention probability. Keyframes exceeding a threshold are stored as sub-goal keyframes.
- **Storage:** All temporal memory components are stored as coarse-stage visual tokens, avoiding repeated re-encoding.

#### 3.2.2. Spatial Memory for Occlusion-Robust Fine Localization

Spatial memory `S_t` helps determine "where exactly to act" by preserving previously observed scene geometry:
- **Storage:** The colored point cloud of the initial observation (`P_0`) is stored. This typically offers a less-occluded view.
- **On-Demand Re-rendering:** When the coarse stage predicts a waypoint, `P_0` is zoomed, re-rendered using the same virtual camera configuration as the current fine-stage crop, and encoded into visual tokens. This creates a geometrically aligned, less-occluded reference `S_t`.

#### 3.2.3. Memory Integration

- Temporal and spatial memories are injected using compact attention modules.
- Current coarse-stage visual tokens (`Z_c_t`) query temporal memory (`T_t`) to produce memory-conditioned `eZ_c_t`.
- Current fine-stage visual tokens (`Z_f_t`) query spatial memory (`S_t`) to produce memory-conditioned `eZ_f_t`.
- Each memory injection block consists of cross-attention (current tokens as queries, memory tokens as keys/values) followed by self-attention and feed-forward updates. The output token shape is preserved.

#### 3.2.4. Bimanual Extension

- The VLM backbone, temporal memory, spatial memory, and adaptive selection module are shared between two arms.
- Arm-specific action heads (duplicated convex-upsampling and MLP action heads) operate on the shared memory-conditioned representation to predict separate actions for each arm.

#### 3.2.5. Training and Inference Details

- **Training:** Memories are constructed from preceding expert demonstration observations. Random rigid-body augmentation is applied consistently across current and memory observations. The objective includes `L_base` and a binary cross-entropy loss `L_check` for the sub-goal gate.
- **Inference:** Initial observation populates anchor views and `P_0`. Encoded current observations update neighboring keyframes, and the adaptive module adds sub-goals. Spatial memory is rendered and encoded dynamically based on the coarse waypoint. The memory extension introduces additional parameters (approx. 9.2% of backbone) and latency (0.35s to 0.57s per step), considered minor compared to overall control loop time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations across simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating the capabilities of BridgeVLA and BridgeVLA++.

#### 4.1. General 3D Manipulation (RLBench)

- **BridgeVLA (Base Model):** Achieved an average success rate of 90.5% across 18 RLBench tasks, outperforming previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods like SAM2Act by 3.7 percentage points. It demonstrated strong performance, particularly in precision-critical tasks.
- **BridgeVLA++:** Further improved performance to an average success rate of 93.7%. This gain was notably significant in occlusion-heavy precision tasks such as Sort Shape (+16.8%) and Place Cups (+18.4%), indicating the effectiveness of the memory architecture in these scenarios.

#### 4.2. Generalization (COLOSSEUM & GemBench)

- **BridgeVLA:** Demonstrated robust out-of-distribution generalization. It achieved a SOTA average success rate of 64.0% on COLOSSEUM (outperforming RVT-2 by over 7 percentage points) and 50.0% on GemBench. Its strength was particularly evident under appearance-level shifts.
- **BridgeVLA++:** Maintained or slightly improved upon BridgeVLA's generalization capabilities, achieving 65.2% on COLOSSEUM and 51.1% on GemBench. This indicates that the memory augmentation did not compromise the framework's robustness to unseen conditions.

#### 4.3. Memory-Dependent Manipulation (RMBench & MemoryBench)

- **RMBench (Bimanual, Memory-Dependent):**
 - BridgeVLA (memory-free) exhibited a severe performance drop with an 18.9% overall success rate, confirming the intrinsic memory requirement of these tasks.
 - BridgeVLA++ achieved a 96.0% overall success rate, establishing SOTA performance and outperforming the strongest memory-augmented baseline, MemoryWAM, by 13.0 points. It ranked best or tied for best on 8 of 9 tasks. The temporal memory proved crucial for long-horizon tasks, with a 96% success rate in Battery Try compared to 41% for MemoryWAM.
- **MemoryBench (Single-Arm, Memory-Dependent):** BridgeVLA++ achieved a 99.7% success rate, reinforcing its efficacy in single-arm memory-dependent scenarios. BridgeVLA's success rate was 11.3%.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments (Franka Research 3 & Dobot CR5A)

- **Franka (General Manipulation):**
 - BridgeVLA achieved an average success rate of 96.9% when trained with 10 demonstrations per task, and remarkably, 95.4% with only 3 demonstrations, showcasing high sample efficiency.
 - It consistently outperformed RVT-2 across six challenging generalization settings (Distractor, Lighting, Background, Height, Combination, Category), with an average improvement of 32%. This indicated effective transfer of VLM semantic knowledge to real-world manipulation.
- **Dobot (Memory-Augmented Manipulation):**
 - On three real-world memory-dependent tasks, BridgeVLA++ achieved a 93.3% average success rate, a threefold improvement over the memory-augmented SAM2Act+ (30.0%). The memory-free BridgeVLA largely failed (20.0%).
 - For two memory-free standard manipulation tasks, BridgeVLA++ matched or exceeded BridgeVLA in both success rate and generalization across various settings, indicating no erosion of general manipulation competence due to memory integration.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Heatmap-based Action Decoding:** Replacing convex upsampling with a direct regression Transformer decoder significantly degraded performance (90.5% to 31.4% on RLBench), supporting the importance of dense heatmap supervision and spatial alignment.
- **3D Position Input to VLM:** Fusing per-pixel 3D positions into the VLM input degraded performance (90.5% to 56.2%), suggesting that preserving the VLM's native 2D image distribution is more beneficial than adding explicit 3D inputs that cause modality shifts.
- **2D Heatmap Pre-training:** Without this pre-training, BridgeVLA failed to generalize effectively in real-world language-related settings, demonstrating its criticality for transferring semantic knowledge.
- **Continuous Rotation Representation:** A continuous 6D rotation representation outperformed a discretized Euler-angle classification head (90.5% vs. 88.2% on RLBench), particularly for tasks requiring precise orientations.
- **Spatial Memory (S):** Removing spatial memory from BridgeVLA++ lowered RLBench performance (93.7% to 92.0%) and specifically impacted occlusion-heavy precision tasks (e.g., Sort Shape), confirming its role in fine-grained geometric alignment. Its removal had minimal impact on RMBench.
- **Temporal Memory (T):** Removing temporal memory from BridgeVLA++ caused a substantial collapse in RMBench success rate (96.0% to 21.3%), confirming its indispensability for long-horizon, memory-dependent tasks. It also provided benefits on RLBench, suggesting its utility extends to general manipulation by offering stable global references and local motion cues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in BridgeVLA++ offers several significant contributions and has the potential to impact the field of robotic manipulation in multiple ways:

1. **Unified and Comprehensive 3D VLA Framework:** The development of BridgeVLA++ establishes a unified framework that concurrently addresses three critical challenges in robotic manipulation: data-efficient learning, robust generalization to novel conditions, and effective reasoning over historical observations and persistent spatial context. This integration into a single model represents a notable advance over approaches that address these aspects in isolation.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse Benchmarks:** BridgeVLA++ consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance on a range of established benchmarks, including RLBench, COLOSSEUM, GemBench, RMBench, and MemoryBench. This broad validation in simulation underscores its effectiveness and robustness across various task complexities and evaluation criteria.

3. **Demonstrated Real-World Efficacy and Scalability:** The successful deployment and evaluation of BridgeVLA++ on two distinct real-world robotic platforms (Franka Research 3 and Dobot CR5A), for both memory-independent and memory-dependent tasks, signifies its practical applicability. Its ability to learn from a very limited number of real-world demonstrations (e.g., 3-10 trajectories) highlights its high sample efficiency, which is crucial for reducing the burden of data collection in robotics. The extension to bimanual manipulation further demonstrates its scalability to more complex coordinated tasks.

4. **Efficacy of Input-Output Alignment and Heatmap-based Action Prediction:** The research substantiates the core architectural principle of aligning the input and output spaces of a VLM in a shared 2D heatmap domain. The ablation studies confirm that this design choice, coupled with language-conditioned heatmap pre-training, is critical for achieving high data efficiency and generalization, particularly in transferring semantic knowledge from pre-trained VLMs.

5. **Targeted and Effective Memory Architecture:** The introduction of a distinct spatio-temporal memory architecture, designed to address specific types of memory requirements at different stages of action prediction (temporal memory for coarse-stage "what to do" decisions and spatial memory for fine-stage "where to act" localization), represents a robust solution for memory-dependent tasks. This targeted approach is shown to be more effective than generic memory mechanisms, as evidenced by its superior performance on RMBench and MemoryBench, without sacrificing general manipulation capabilities.

6. **Advancement Towards More Autonomous Robots:** By enabling robots to learn efficiently, generalize to unseen situations, and reason over past experiences, BridgeVLA++ moves towards creating more intelligent and autonomous robotic agents. Such capabilities are essential for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic, and open-world environments where tasks are complex and data is scarce.

Potential future impacts include:
- **Expanded Pre-training Paradigms:** The success of 2D heatmap pre-training could encourage exploration of more diverse pre-training tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation, keypoint detection) and alternative action decoders.
- **Long-Horizon and Life-Long Learning:** The memory injection mechanism, being agnostic to timescale in the token space, opens avenues for extending memory beyond single episodes to support cross-episode or life-long learning. This could enable robots to continuously accumulate and refine manipulation skills over extended operational periods.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** More capable and memory-aware robots could lead to more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration, as robots would better understand task context and adapt to changing instructions or environments.

In conclusion, BridgeVLA++ represents a substantial step forward in developing integrated and highly capable robot manipulation policies, laying groundwork for future advancements in general-purpose robotics.
