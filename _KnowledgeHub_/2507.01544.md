# MARVIS: Modality Adaptive Reasoning over VISualizations

> [!summary] Summary
> MARVIS introduces a training-free method that extends Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to predict across diverse data modalities, including vision, audio, biological, and tabular data, by transforming data embeddings into visualizations for VLM interpretation. The approach achieves performance within 2.5% of specialized models and significantly outperforms other generalist foundation models by an average of 16.7%, while also enhancing data privacy by avoiding direct exposure of raw data.

## Problem

- Large foundation models often underperform specialized models on non-traditional data modalities (e.g., audio, tabular) and 'long-tail' domains.
- There is no straightforward or efficient way to input many data modalities directly into text-centric foundation models, and existing serialization methods for data like tabular information suffer from privacy concerns and inefficient scaling.
- A gap exists between the high performance of specialized machine learning models within their narrow scope and the desired versatility of foundation models across diverse data types.

## Method

- A training-free, four-stage pipeline: (1) specialized embedding models generate dense vector representations from raw input data of any modality, then (2) ==dimensionality reduction (primarily t-SNE)== converts them into 2D scatter plot visualizations preserving local relationships.
- (3) A VLM (Qwen 2.5 VL 3B) queries the visualization, leveraging its pre-trained ==visual reasoning capabilities== with in-context learning to make predictions based on spatial relationships and provided semantic context.
- (4) A robust parser extracts the final prediction from the VLM's natural language response, handling consistent color schemes and legend naming.

## Results

- MARVIS-3B achieves performance within **2.5%** of best specialized models and improves upon other foundation models by an average of **16.7%** across vision, audio, biological, and tabular data modalities.
- For tabular data classification (OpenML CC18), MARVIS-3B attains **84.5%** mean accuracy, outperforming other LLM-based tabular methods by **34-44 percentage points**.
- VLMs demonstrate adaptive reasoning; correct predictions are associated with more sophisticated responses, increased spatial analysis (**33%** more color mentions, **18%** more distance reasoning), and distinct reasoning styles are observed for different visualization methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) pre-trained ability to reason over spatial relationships in images can serve as a "skeleton key" to interpret abstract data from any modality.
> - Transforming complex, high-dimensional data embeddings into human-interpretable 2D visualizations (e.g., using t-SNE) enables VLMs to make predictions without needing modality-specific projection layers or fine-tuning.
> - Empirical evidence suggests that VLMs engage in genuine, adaptive spatial reasoning over these abstract visual representations, adapting their analysis based on the visual information presented.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research landscape in machine learning is currently dominated by two parallel paradigms: highly specialized, small models and versatile, large foundation models (FMs). This paper critically positions MARVIS at the intersection of these two trends, aiming to leverage the strengths of each while mitigating their respective weaknesses.

Historically, machine learning applications have relied on **specialized models** (e.g., Prokhorenkova et al., 2018; He et al., 2015). These models, often small and fine-tuned to specific tasks or domains, achieve excellent performance within their narrow scope but inherently lack flexibility and generalize poorly to other data modalities or tasks. While intermediate embeddings from these models can sometimes be used for downstream tasks after fine-tuning (Devlin et al., 2019), this still requires adaptation.

The advent of **Foundation Models (FMs)**, exemplified by models like GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020), introduced the paradigm of in-context learning (ICL), allowing adaptation to new tasks without weight updates. However, despite their versatility, FMs often underperform specialized approaches, particularly on non-traditional modalities and "long-tail" domains (Zhang et al., 2024). Furthermore, for certain modalities like audio or structured tabular data, there is no straightforward "native" way to input them into traditional text-centric FMs. Attempts to serialize such data into text (e.g., Hegselmann et al., 2023b; Gardner et al., 2024; Shysheya et al., 2025) face significant challenges, including data privacy concerns (sharing PII with API endpoints), data leakage, and inefficient scaling with context length (van Breugel and van der Schaar, 2024).

**Multimodal Foundation Models** such as LLaVA (Liu et al., 2023a), BLIP (Li et al., 2022), Flamingo (Alayrac et al., 2022), and PaLI (Chen et al., 2022) have sought to align language models with vision and other modalities. These typically involve complex fusion mechanisms, projection layers, or multi-stage training (e.g., feature alignment followed by instruction tuning). While successful, they often require different translation mechanisms for each modality and can be computationally expensive to train or adapt. Tabular data, for instance, has proven particularly resistant to these efforts.

MARVIS distinguishes itself by offering a **training-free adaptation** strategy for *any* pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) to predict *any* data modality. Its core insight is to treat "vision as a skeleton key." This leverages the VLM's inherent ability to reason over spatial relationships in images, a capability honed during their pre-training on vast amounts of web-scraped visual data (Grounding DINO, KOSMOS-2, OtterHD, SleighVL, Emu3). By transforming specialized low-dimensional embeddings into visual representations (e.g., using t-SNE or UMAP, techniques rooted in representation learning and dimensionality reduction), MARVIS bypasses the need for modality-specific projection layers or fine-tuning. This approach builds on the concept of data visualization for human understanding (Unwin, 2020) and extends it to machine reasoning over abstract data.

In essence, MARVIS seeks to bridge the performance gap between specialized models and the versatility of FMs, particularly for challenging non-textual data, by introducing an ingenious visual intermediary. It offers a computationally efficient and privacy-preserving alternative to conventional multimodal FM adaptation, thereby expanding the applicability of smaller, more accessible VLMs across a universal range of data types.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the MARVIS project is to develop a **training-free method that enables even small vision-language models (VLMs) to predict any data modality with high accuracy.** This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the current limitations of both specialized machine learning models and large foundation models.

The primary motivations are:

1. **Bridging the Performance-Versatility Gap:** Traditional specialized models achieve excellent performance in their narrow domains but are inflexible and cannot easily generalize to other data types or tasks. Conversely, large foundation models (FMs), while highly versatile due to in-context learning, typically underperform specialized approaches, especially on non-traditional modalities (like audio, biological, or tabular data) and long-tail domains. MARVIS aims to create a system that can combine the reasoning capabilities and versatility of FMs with the high performance typically associated with specialized models.

2. **Addressing Challenges of Non-Traditional Modalities:** For many data types, such as audio, genomic sequences, or complex tabular data, there is no obvious or efficient way to natively utilize a traditional, often text-centric, FM. Existing serialization methods for tabular data, for example, suffer from issues like data privacy (exposure of Personally Identifiable Information - PII), potential data leakage, and inefficient scaling with context length. MARVIS seeks a method that avoids these pitfalls, particularly by not exposing raw, sensitive data.

3. **Leveraging VLM's Inherent Visual Reasoning Capabilities:** The authors posit a key insight: "visual reasoning, coupled with specialized low-dimensional embedding models, is a skeleton key that unlocks FMs and ICL for any modality of data." Humans often reason more effectively with data visualizations. Given that VLMs are pre-trained on vast amounts of web-scraped data, including scientific imagery and visualizations, they possess robust spatial and fine-grained reasoning skills. The motivation is to harness these existing capabilities of VLMs by presenting abstract data in a visual format, rather than attempting to teach VLMs to "understand" new raw data modalities from scratch.

4. **Achieving Computational Efficiency and Accessibility:** Many state-of-the-art FMs are massive, requiring significant computational resources for training and inference, making them inaccessible for many researchers and applications. MARVIS is motivated by the desire to achieve competitive performance using a relatively small 3B parameter VLM, thus making advanced multimodal prediction more efficient and broadly accessible without requiring substantial domain-specific training.

5. **Enhancing Data Privacy:** By transforming raw data into anonymized embedding visualizations, MARVIS inherently protects sensitive information (PII) from direct exposure to large language model APIs, which often retain and train on user input. This is a crucial motivation, especially for applications dealing with confidential scientific or personal data.

In summary, the core objective of MARVIS is to innovate a training-free, privacy-preserving, and computationally efficient method that universally extends the predictive power of smaller VLMs across an unprecedented range of data modalities by strategically leveraging their innate visual reasoning abilities on distilled data representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MARVIS (Modality Adaptive Reasoning over VISualizations) proposes an elegant, training-free, four-stage pipeline that fundamentally re-imagines how Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can process and make predictions on diverse data modalities. The core insight is that by transforming complex, high-dimensional data into human-interpretable visualizations of their latent embedding spaces, VLMs can leverage their strong spatial and fine-grained visual reasoning capabilities to interpret and utilize this information.

**The Four-Stage MARVIS Pipeline:**

1. **Embedding Generation:**
 - **Purpose:** To distill raw input data of *any* modality into a dense, numerical vector representation that captures its essential features.
 - **Implementation:** MARVIS utilizes pre-trained, domain-specific embedding models. These are "specialized low-dimensional embedding models" that have already learned effective representations for their respective data types.
 - **Vision:** DINOv2-ViT-L-14-reg (Oquab et al., 2023) is used for robust visual features.
 - **Audio:** Microsoft CLAP (Elizalde et al., 2023) provides contrastive audio-language embeddings, and OpenAI's Whisper-V2-Large (Radford et al., 2022) is also considered.
 - **Biological:** BioCLIP2 (Gu et al., 2025), part of a series of foundation models for biological applications, is employed for scientific vision understanding.
 - **Tabular:** TabPFNv2 (Hollmann et al., 2022, 2025), a transformer-based tabular foundation model, generates embeddings for structured data.
 - **Outcome:** A set of high-dimensional numerical embeddings (vectors) for both training data and the query point.

2. **Dimensionality Reduction:**
 - **Purpose:** To convert the high-dimensional embeddings into a lower-dimensional (typically 2D) visual representation suitable for image processing by a VLM. This step is crucial for rendering complex data as an interpretable "plot."
 - **Implementation:** The primary technique used is **t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE)** (Van der Maaten and Hinton, 2008). t-SNE is chosen for its ability to preserve local relationships within the data, which is vital for VLMs to understand proximity and clustering. Other dimensionality reduction techniques like PCA, UMAP, Spectral Embedding, and Isomap are also explored in ablation studies.
 - **Outcome:** A 2D (or 3D) scatter plot where each point represents an embedding, colored by its class label (for training data) or highlighted as a query point.

3. **Visual Reasoning (VLM Query):**
 - **Purpose:** To prompt a VLM to analyze the generated visualization and make a prediction based on the spatial relationships observed.
 - **Implementation:** The paper uses the **Qwen 2.5 VL 3B model** from Alibaba (Bai et al., 2025) as the VLM backbone. This model was chosen for specific architectural advantages:
 - **Patch-based processing:** Uses 14x14 patches with sliding window attention, emphasizing local interactions crucial for distance-based visualizations.
 - **Arbitrary aspect ratios:** Handles images without distorting distances.
 - **Scientific imagery training:** Qwen 2.5 VL series is trained to work with long context and scientific imagery.
 - **Prompting:** The VLM is given the visualization image, a clear instruction to classify the query point, a class legend (with color-to-label mapping), and optionally metadata or semantic context about the dataset (e.g., semantic axis interpretations, dataset description).
 - **In-context Learning (ICL):** The VLM's existing knowledge and reasoning abilities are leveraged without any fine-tuning or weight updates on the specific task or modality.

4. **Response Processing:**
 - **Purpose:** To extract the final prediction from the VLM's natural language response.
 - **Implementation:** A robust parser is implemented that is aware of class names and color names, ensuring correct extraction of the predicted class label. Consistent color schemes and legend naming across visualizations aid this process.

**Addressing Technical Challenges for Practical Implementation:**

The paper highlights several technical hurdles that needed to be cleared for MARVIS to work effectively:

- **VLM Architecture Selection:** Carefully selecting Qwen 2.5 VL 3B based on its "local patch interaction" and "arbitrary aspect ratios" capabilities, crucial for interpreting nuanced spatial information in scatter plots.
- **Resolution and "Zooming":** VLMs, unlike humans, require visualizations to be "zoomed in" to the region of interest for optimal performance, ensuring the target point and its neighbors are within the VLM's attention window. This scaling factor is set per benchmark.
- **Context Composition Strategy:** Extensive ablation studies (25 configurations) were conducted to determine the optimal way to present information. The "tsne knn" setting, which explicitly overlays k-nearest neighbor information (calculated from the *original* high-dimensional embeddings) onto the t-SNE plot, proved most effective, balancing speed and quality. The `nn` hyperparameter for KNN is set to `min(30, 10% of training data)`.
- **Robust Classname Extraction:** Standardizing color schemes and limiting legend class names to those present in the visualization, combined with a parser aware of class and color names, prevents common VLM parsing errors.

By meticulously addressing these technical details, MARVIS transforms a simple conceptual idea—visualizing embeddings for VLM interpretation—into a highly performant and practical system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in "MARVIS: Modality Adaptive Reasoning over VISualizations" demonstrate compelling results across diverse data modalities, highlighting the effectiveness and versatility of the proposed approach.

#### 4.1. Cross-Modal Performance

MARVIS-3B, utilizing the Qwen 2.5 VL 3B backbone, achieves competitive performance across four distinct modalities: vision, audio, biological, and tabular.

- **Overall Performance:** On average, MARVIS-3B achieves results that are within 2.5% of the best performing specialized models for each domain. Crucially, it significantly improves upon the best foundation model (FM) performance by an average of 16.7%, directly addressing the stated limitation of FMs underperforming specialized approaches.
- **Vision:** On CIFAR-10, MARVIS-3B achieves 98.0% accuracy, closely approaching the 99.0% of the specialized DINOv2 model and far surpassing Gemini's 85.7%. For CIFAR-100, MARVIS-3B scores 88.0% against DINOv2's 91.6% and Gemini's 64.3%.
- **Audio:** MARVIS-3B shows strong performance on audio classification benchmarks: ESC-50 (91.3% vs. CLAP's 90.5%), UrbanSound-8K (79.8% vs. CLAP's 77.1%). While slightly lower on RAVDESS (38.4% vs. Whisper's 47.9%), it still demonstrates robust capability for a generalist method. The paper notes that no generalist FM method exists for audio classification for direct comparison.
- **Biological:** MARVIS-3B is competitive on biological datasets: FishNet (80.2% vs. BioCLIP's 83.7% and Gemini's 59.5%), AWA2 (95.7% vs. BioCLIP's 97.1% and Gemini's 96.5%), and PlantDoc (67.4% vs. BioCLIP's 72.0% and Gemini's 74.2%).
- **Tabular Data (Detailed Analysis):**
 - **Classification (OpenML CC18):** MARVIS-3B achieves a mean accuracy of 84.5% on the OpenML CC18 benchmark. This places it competitively among traditional ML methods like TabPFNv2 (87.8%), CatBoost (87.0%), and Random Forest (86.5%). Most notably, MARVIS-3B significantly outperforms other LLM-based tabular methods (TabLLM with Gemini at 50.1%, TabLLM with Qwen at 42.9%, and JOLT at 41.0%) by 34-44 percentage points, validating the superiority of the visual reasoning approach over direct tabular-to-text serialization.
 - **Regression (Regression 2025):** For regression tasks, MARVIS-3B achieves a mean R² score of 0.532 (out of 100), ranking 5th among 7 algorithms, behind Random Forest (0.586), TabPFNv2 (0.585), and Gradient Boosting (0.564). While not top-performing, it shows reasonable capabilities for continuous prediction and again vastly outperforms LLM-based approaches like JOLT (0.051).
 - **Correlation with TabPFNv2:** A high Pearson correlation of 0.978 for classification and 0.884 for regression between MARVIS and TabPFNv2 indicates that both methods tend to perform well on similar datasets. This suggests MARVIS acts as a complementary alternative rather than a replacement, exhibiting similar strengths and weaknesses to existing tabular approaches.

#### 4.2. Evidence of VLM Adaptive Reasoning

A significant finding is empirical evidence that VLMs genuinely reason over the visual input and adapt their behavior, rather than merely copying predictions or relying on simple heuristics.

- **Performance-Driven Reasoning Patterns:** Analysis of VLM reasoning traces (e.g., response length, mentions of colors/distances, use of heuristics) reveals clear correlations with prediction accuracy. Correct predictions consistently exhibit:
 - **Enhanced response sophistication:** Responses are 12.9 characters longer on average (+4.8%).
 - **Increased spatial analysis:** 33% more color mentions and 18% more distance reasoning are observed.
 - **Reduced heuristic reliance:** 21% less usage of "closest" heuristics and 20% less cluster-based reasoning compared to incorrect predictions. This implies deeper analysis when the visual information supports an accurate classification.
- **Method-Specific Reasoning Signatures:** Different visualization methods (e.g., `tsne knn`, `tsne semantic axes`, `tsne perturbation axes`) elicit distinct reasoning styles from the VLM:
 - `tsne knn`: Prompts quantitative neighbor analysis with explicit distance calculations.
 - `tsne semantic axes`: Integrates semantic class information with spatial reasoning.
 - `tsne perturbation axes`: Generates the longest and most detailed responses, often including sophisticated uncertainty analysis.
 This systematic variation in reasoning style, directly correlated with the information content of each visualization, strongly supports that VLMs genuinely process and respond to different types of visual information.
- **Disagreement Patterns:** Only 35% of methods agree on all test cases, with 65% showing partial disagreement, indicating that different visualization strategies provide unique insights or information to the VLM.

#### 4.3. Computational Efficiency

MARVIS is computationally efficient. It uses a 3B parameter model, with average inference times ranging from 0.5-2.0 seconds per sample and peak GPU memory usage under 8GB (on H100 80GB GPUs). The total GPU utilization for the paper's creation was estimated at 1,500 H100-hours.

#### 4.4. Novel Tabular Benchmarks

As a secondary contribution, the paper introduces and open-sources two novel, large-scale, standardized tabular benchmarks: CC18-Semantic (for classification) and Regression2025-Semantic (for regression). These benchmarks include rich semantic metadata (class names, feature names, dataset context) generated using Claude Research from Anthropic, significantly enhancing dataset understanding and enabling more informed research in tabular ML.

Overall, the findings strongly validate MARVIS as a highly effective, versatile, and efficient approach for extending VLM capabilities across diverse data modalities, while also providing novel insights into VLM reasoning on abstract visual representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MARVIS represents a significant advancement in the field of machine learning, offering a novel paradigm for extending the capabilities of large models to a broader range of real-world data. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

1. **Universal Modality Support and Accessibility for Foundation Models:**
 - **Democratization of ML:** By enabling a single, relatively small 3B-parameter VLM (Qwen 2.5 VL) to achieve competitive performance across vision, audio, biological, and tabular data, MARVIS democratizes access to advanced multimodal ML capabilities. This reduces the need for massive, specialized models for every domain, lowering computational and financial barriers to entry.
 - **Bridging the Versatility-Performance Gap:** The method successfully combines the in-context learning versatility of foundation models with the high performance previously exclusive to specialized models. This is a crucial step towards truly general-purpose AI that can adapt to various data types without extensive retraining.
 - **Unlocking Long-Tail and Non-Traditional Modalities:** MARVIS provides a practical solution for modalities like audio, biological data, and tabular data, which have historically been challenging for FMs to process natively. It offers a "skeleton key" interface, making these data types interpretable by VLMs.

2. **Enhanced Data Privacy and Security:**
 - **PII Preservation:** A standout feature is its ability to operate without exposing raw Personally Identifiable Information (PII) or sensitive data. By transforming data into anonymized embedding visualizations, MARVIS significantly reduces the risks of data leakage and privacy breaches that can occur when raw data is serialized or sent to API endpoints for FM processing. This is particularly impactful for applications in sensitive domains like healthcare, finance, or scientific research.

3. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Resource Optimization:** Achieving competitive results with a 3B parameter model, requiring less than 8GB of GPU memory per inference, makes MARVIS highly efficient. This is a stark contrast to many state-of-the-art FMs that demand significantly more computational resources, making MARVIS attractive for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

4. **Insights into VLM Reasoning and Interpretability:**
 - **Beyond Pattern Matching:** The detailed analysis demonstrating that VLMs engage in genuine, adaptive spatial reasoning over abstract visual representations is profoundly significant. It provides strong empirical evidence against the notion that VLMs merely perform superficial pattern matching. This understanding can guide the development of more robust and reliable AI systems.
 - **Towards Explainable AI:** The VLM's natural language reasoning, which includes explicit references to spatial relationships, colors, and distances, offers a degree of interpretability into its decision-making process. This can be invaluable for debugging, building trust, and understanding why a model makes a particular prediction.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:**
 - **Novel Visualization Strategies:** The extensive ablation studies on context composition strategies open up a fertile area for future research into optimal visualization techniques for machine reasoning. How different plots (e.g., semantic axes, perturbation analysis, multi-view) influence VLM performance and reasoning styles is a critical avenue for exploration.
 - **VLM Fine-tuning for Abstract Imagery:** The paper explicitly calls for further investigation into fine-tuning strategies for base VLMs to improve their reasoning over scientific imagery post-training, potentially pushing performance even further.
 - **Semantic Metadata Integration:** The introduction of semantically rich tabular benchmarks (CC18-Semantic, Regression2025-Semantic) is a valuable contribution that will facilitate more meaningful evaluations and research in tabular machine learning, especially for LLM-based approaches.

In conclusion, MARVIS is more than just a new method; it introduces a paradigm shift in how foundation models can interact with the diverse, messy reality of real-world data. By leveraging the power of visualization and VLM spatial reasoning, it offers a practical, private, and efficient pathway to truly universal machine learning, with profound implications for accessibility, privacy, and the fundamental understanding of AI reasoning.
