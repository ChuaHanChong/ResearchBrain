# What Makes World Action Models Generalize? An Empirical Study of Test-Time Future Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Simple-WAM is a world action model that conditions its action expert on a single forward pass over fully noised future video tokens instead of iteratively denoising clean future frames, and trains with a mixed flow-time schedule that oversamples pure noise. A controlled study shows this keeps explicit WAMs' generalization, reaching 79.5% on LIBERO-Plus versus 67.7% explicit and 53.8% latent, at 3.8x explicit speed.

## Problem

- Explicit WAMs denoise future video into clean frames for every action chunk, so video denoising dominates per-chunk cost and blocks high-frequency closed-loop control.
- Latent WAMs drop future tokens at inference for speed, justified only by in-distribution parity that never tested the generalization benefits that originally motivated WAMs.
- Prior WAM generalization claims each come from one system with its own backbone, data and budget, so no matched comparison isolates inference-time future modeling.

## Method

- ==Three-axis generalization protocol==: environmental perturbation on LIBERO-Plus, data efficiency with reduced demonstrations, and task generalization via four-fold held-out suites with and without action-free video.
- **Single-pass noised-future inference**: the video expert runs once over future video tokens left at pure Gaussian noise, and that one feature conditions all K action denoising steps.
- ==Mixed flow-time training schedule==: future-token flow time is set to pure noise with probability p=0.5, otherwise drawn from the original schedule, aligning training with single-pass inference.

## Results

- Matched comparison: explicit and latent WAMs differ by **0.9** points in distribution, but latent trails by **13.97** on LIBERO-Plus, **8.45** at 10-shot, and **64.0** with action-free video.
- **Simple-WAM** scores **79.5** on LIBERO-Plus versus **67.7** explicit and **53.8** latent, and **73.6** on held-out tasks with video, at **74.7 ms** per chunk versus **286.9 ms** explicit.
- Replacing noise with learnable queries or zeros drops task generalization by **86.6** and **87.4** points; on a real-world held-out task with video, Simple-WAM reaches **87.8** versus **68.9** explicit and **10.0** latent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - In-distribution parity is not evidence of equal generalization; efficiency shortcuts for WAMs must be validated on perturbation, low-data, and held-out-task axes.
> - The generalization benefit comes from preparing a future representation, not generating it; one pass over noise recovers nearly everything that nine extra denoising passes add.
> - Conditioning inputs should stay aligned with pretraining: pure-noise video tokens the backbone already knows outperform novel learnable queries or zeros by large margins.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot policy must choose actions from what it sees. A world action model (WAM) is a robot policy trained to predict future visual frames alongside actions. At deployment, an explicit WAM iteratively denoises future-frame tokens while producing an action chunk, and a latent WAM drops those future tokens. Repeatedly running the video model is expensive because it processes many more tokens than the action model, and this slows closed-loop control. Dropping future information entirely is faster but may harm generalization. In the authors' controlled test, one pass over future slots filled with noise brings most of the benefit, and further video denoising adds little.

Success rate (%) on LIBERO-Spatial for environmental perturbation, data efficiency, task generalization without video, and task generalization with video, plotted against the number of video denoising steps. The legend distinguishes the latent reference, explicit without video denoising, and explicit with video denoising. The model was trained explicitly and was not retrained for the sweep.

### 2. One pass over noise

The test starts with a model trained in the explicit style. Its weights and action denoising stay fixed. Only the number of video-expert passes that update the future representation changes. With one pass, the future tokens are fully noised, and the action expert then reuses that feature for the rest of the action chunk.

Compared with the latent reference, the first pass gains 26.44, 8.20, 13.40, and 89.80 percentage points. These gains are for environmental perturbation, data efficiency, task generalization without video, and task generalization with video, respectively. In the "with video" setting, action-free video of held-out tasks trains the video branch; it contains no action labels. The next nine passes, which perform video denoising, add little compared with these first-pass gains.

The authors interpret this as evidence that the useful part is preparing the future representation, rather than generating a visually faithful future. The experiment establishes this result for one explicit-trained model and protocol. It does not identify a universal cause of WAM generalization.

### 3. What Simple-WAM changes

Simple-WAM keeps future tokens in the action model's context. It runs the video model just once, while the future tokens are still pure Gaussian noise. The resulting feature is reused for all action-denoising steps.

Training changes only the sampling of video noise times. With probability p = 0.5, the model trains at the fully noisy endpoint; otherwise it keeps the original flow-time schedule. The joint action/video training and video loss remain, and there is no added module or loss.

Computation paths, shown schematically. Explicit WAM keeps and iteratively updates future-video tokens (Video DiT × K, Action DiT × K). Latent WAM drops future tokens (Video DiT × 1, Action DiT × K). Simple-WAM keeps the future-token positions as noised tokens (Video DiT × 1, Action DiT × K).

### 4. Accuracy and speed

The closest prior work, [[2603.16666|Fast-WAM]], argues for skipping future-video generation at inference. The paper notes that this evidence was collected in distribution.

The paper tests Simple-WAM under LIBERO-Plus environmental perturbations, using success rates averaged across seven perturbation factors and weighted by their task counts. Simple-WAM scores 79.5%, explicit FastWAM-Joint scores 67.7%, and latent FastWAM scores 53.8%. The large-scale embodied-pretrained $\pi_{0.5}$ scores 84.4%, which is above Simple-WAM.

Simple-WAM leads the explicit model on seven of Table 2's eight measurements. The exception is RoboTwin task generalization with action-free video, where it scores 44.5% and the explicit model scores 47.3%.

On the paper's single RTX 5090 measurement setup, latency per action chunk is:

- 74.7 ms for Simple-WAM
- 286.9 ms for FastWAM-Joint
- 62.0 ms for FastWAM

Simple-WAM is therefore 3.8× faster than the explicit model and 12.7 ms slower than the latent model.

Left: per-chunk latency in milliseconds for FastWAM-Joint (286.9), Simple-WAM (74.7), FastWAM (62.0), and π0.5 (52.3). Right: mean success rate over the paper's four generalization settings (60.2, 65.1, 37.6, and 48.7, in the same order).

In this study, preserving a future-conditioned feature mattered more than spending extra passes to turn it into clean video. The conclusion rests on a Wan2.2-5B backbone without embodied pretraining. Whether it holds at larger scale or with embodied pretraining remains open.
