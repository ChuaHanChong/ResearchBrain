# Robot Squid Game: Quadrupedal Locomotion for Traversing Narrow Tunnels

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework called SQUID enables quadrupedal robots to autonomously navigate challenging 3D confined environments like tunnels by combining procedural environment generation with a multi-expert policy distillation strategy. The system achieved higher success rates, better efficiency, and lower collision rates in simulations compared to baseline methods, and demonstrated successful real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2 robot with up to 80% success in difficult tunnel geometries.

## Problem

- Quadrupedal robots struggle to navigate complex 3D confined environments such as tunnels and collapsed structures, which require 360-degree awareness and precise limb/body coordination.
- Existing locomotion strategies often rely on oversimplified training environments, leading to policies that are specialized and lack generalization to diverse, irregular tunnel geometries.
- Current methods are sensitive to real-world sensory noise, hindering robust performance during deployment in unpredictable confined spaces.

## Method

- The SQUID framework utilizes a procedural generation system to create diverse 3D tunnel geometries (e.g., triangular, circular, gap) with varying difficulty, preventing policies from overfitting to simplified training setups.
- Four specialized 'expert' policies are trained independently using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with privileged state information, each tailored to a specific tunnel class.
- These expert policies' knowledge is consolidated into a single vision-based student policy via Dataset Aggregation (DAgger), enabling navigation using depth images and proprioception while robust against sensor noise.

## Results

- SQUID consistently achieved the highest success rates across all tunnel configurations and difficulty levels in simulation, outperforming both a Vanilla RL agent and a Hierarchical Planner.
- In simulation, SQUID demonstrated superior traversal efficiency (faster completion times), lower collision frequency, and greater energy efficiency compared to baseline methods.
- Real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2 robot showed successful transfer, achieving **70%** success in circular, **80%** in gap, and **60%** in sloped triangular tunnels, validating performance under realistic sensor noise.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating diverse and complex tunnel geometries procedurally is critical for training generalizable locomotion policies that can adapt to a wide range of real-world confined spaces.
> - Decoupling perception from core locomotion by using multi-expert distillation with privileged teachers enhances robustness and generalization by allowing the student policy to effectively learn from vision.
> - A class-balanced replay buffer during distillation is essential to retain specialized behaviors from all experts, preventing bias towards any single tunnel geometry and ensuring comprehensive skill transfer.
