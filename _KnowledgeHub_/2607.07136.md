# Learning Spatiotemporal Tubes for Full Class of Signal Temporal Logic Tasks for Control of Unknown Systems under Input Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> A control framework, Physics-informed Neural Spatiotemporal Tube (PINSTT), enables unknown Euler-Lagrange systems with input constraints to satisfy complex Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications. It integrates neural network-based tube synthesis with a closed-form, approximation-free control law, demonstrating microsecond-level online control synthesis times and formal guarantees across various robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Existing Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) frameworks for STL control often rely on computationally intensive optimization, predefined templates, or assume unbounded control inputs, limiting practical applicability.
- Controlling real-world dynamic systems to satisfy complex, time-dependent STL tasks is challenging due to unknown system dynamics, disturbances, and inherent actuator input constraints.
- Many STL control approaches lack formal correctness guarantees over continuous domains or struggle with scalability and real-time performance due to high computational costs from online optimization.

## Method

- The **Physics-informed Neural Spatiotemporal Tube (PINSTT)** framework synthesizes time-varying state-space tubes (==STTs==) using a ==physics-informed neural network (PINN)==, encoding ==STL robustness== and tube evolution constraints as ==physics-based loss functions== for training.
- A ==two-stage, approximation-free control law== is designed to guide the system's trajectory within the synthesized STT, explicitly respecting system input constraints and without requiring full knowledge of system dynamics.
- Formal guarantees for STL satisfaction over continuous space-time are provided by leveraging ==Lipschitz continuity== properties of the STL robustness function and appropriately chosen sampling densities during PINN training.

## Results

- PINSTT achieved microsecond-level online control synthesis times (e.g., **3.17 μsec** for an omnidirectional robot, **5.42 μsec** for a 7-DOF manipulator), significantly outperforming optimization-based methods.
- Demonstrated successful STL task satisfaction for single-agent systems (**2D** omnidirectional robot, **3D** quadrotor, **7-DOF** Franka manipulator) and a multi-agent system (**four** omnidirectional robots) in both simulations and hardware experiments.
- The framework explicitly ensured that control inputs remained within prescribed limits under nominal and perturbed conditions, a key advantage over previous STT and other comparative methods which often required unbounded inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By modeling STT center and radius with a physics-informed neural network, the framework can synthesize tubes for the full class of STL specifications without predefined templates, directly integrating physical constraints.
> - Coupling the tube's evolution rates (Lipschitz constants for center and radius) with the system's velocity and actuation limits ensures the feasibility of the control law and adherence to input constraints.
> - A closed-form control law, derived from a bounded transformation function and funnel constraints for velocity error, enables microsecond-level online control synthesis, making it suitable for real-time applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenges of controlling dynamic systems to satisfy complex, time-dependent task specifications, moving beyond traditional control objectives like stabilization or trajectory tracking. Signal Temporal Logic (STL) has emerged as a formal language for describing such tasks, offering a quantitative measure of satisfaction through robustness metrics. STL's applicability spans various fields, including deep learning, reinforcement learning, and planning and control.

Despite its utility, controlling systems under STL specifications presents algorithmic and computational difficulties. Existing approaches include:
- **Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP) and gradient-based methods:** These rely on optimization, leading to scalability issues with increasing STL complexity or system dynamics.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** While effective within a receding-horizon framework, MPC often incurs high computational costs for complex tasks.
- **Symbolic control:** This method provides formal correctness guarantees but suffers from the curse of dimensionality due to state-space abstraction.
- **Prescribed Performance Control (PPC):** PPC can handle some STL fragments efficiently but is not applicable to the full class of STL specifications.
- **Control Barrier Functions (CBF):** CBF ensures safety with formal guarantees and has been extended to approximate predicate functions, but it requires accurate system dynamics and is also limited to fragments of STL.
- **Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) frameworks:** Previous STT approaches generated tubes by solving optimization problems, requiring predefined templates, which limited expressivity and increased computational complexity. They also typically relied on unbounded control inputs and did not account for input constraints, hindering real-world applicability.
- **Neural network-based methods:** Recent efforts leverage neural networks for STL-compliant control, but often lack formal correctness guarantees, require substantial state-space data (e.g., from simulators), and may only apply to fragments of STL.

This work positions itself by seeking to overcome these limitations. It aims to develop a framework that supports the full class of STL specifications, operates with unknown system dynamics, provides formal guarantees, handles input constraints explicitly, and offers computationally efficient, closed-form control, thereby enhancing the practical applicability of STL-based control for real-world cyber-physical systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a Spatiotemporal Tube (STT)-based control framework capable of enabling unknown nonlinear Euler-Lagrange (EL) systems, subject to input constraints, to satisfy general Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications. The core idea is to transform the complex problem of STL satisfaction into a more manageable geometric constraint satisfaction problem: ensuring the system's trajectory remains within a time-varying "tube" in the state space, which itself is guaranteed to encompass only STL-satisfying trajectories.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are:
- **Addressing limitations of prior STT frameworks:** Previous STT approaches, while promising, faced several challenges. They often required solving computationally intensive optimization problems to generate tubes, relied on predefined tube templates that restricted expressivity, and did not explicitly account for system input constraints. This paper aims to overcome these by directly synthesizing tubes for the full class of STL specifications without predefined templates and explicitly integrating input constraints into the design.
- **Handling unknown system dynamics:** Many real-world systems, particularly complex robots or multi-agent systems, have dynamics that are either partially known or subject to significant uncertainties and disturbances. The framework aims to operate effectively without requiring full knowledge of the system dynamics (e.g., mass matrix, Coriolis/centrifugal terms, gravity), relying instead on reasonable boundedness assumptions.
- **Ensuring formal guarantees for continuous domains:** Traditional neural network approaches, while powerful approximators, often lack formal guarantees over continuous time and space when trained on discrete samples. A key objective is to provide a mechanism for formal verification, ensuring that the learned STTs indeed satisfy the STL specifications over the entire continuous space-time horizon.
- **Integrating input constraints:** Actuator limits are inherent in physical systems. Previous STT methods often assumed unbounded control inputs, which is impractical. A significant motivation is to develop a control strategy that explicitly respects and accounts for these input constraints during both tube synthesis (by bounding tube evolution rates) and controller design.
- **Achieving computational efficiency:** Optimization-based methods for STL control often suffer from high computational costs, making them unsuitable for real-time applications. This research seeks to provide an approximation-free, closed-form control law that avoids online optimization, leading to microsecond-level online control synthesis times.
- **Supporting the full class of STL specifications:** Many existing methods are limited to fragments of STL. This work aims to support the entire expressive power of STL, allowing for a broader range of complex tasks to be specified and satisfied.

By pursuing these objectives, the research intends to provide a unified, robust, and efficient control framework for complex autonomous systems operating under high-level temporal logic requirements and physical limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Physics-informed Neural Spatiotemporal Tube (PINSTT), is structured into two main components: the synthesis of Spatiotemporal Tubes (STTs) using a neural network and the design of an input-constrained control law.

#### 3.1. System Definition and Assumptions

The work considers unknown nonlinear Euler-Lagrange (EL) systems, represented by the equation $M(x)\ddot{x} + V(x, \dot{x}) + G(x) = \tau(t) + w(t)$, where $x(t)$ is the system configuration, $\tau(t)$ is the control input, and $w(t)$ is an unknown disturbance. The control input is explicitly bounded, i.e., $|\tau(t)| \preceq \bar{\tau}$.
Four key assumptions are made regarding the system:
1. Boundedness of the external disturbance $w(t)$.
2. Existence of a positive constant $m$ relating the inverse of the mass matrix to the control bound, such that $m\bar{\tau} \preceq M^{-1}\bar{\tau}$.
3. Boundedness of the Coriolis/centrifugal and gravity terms, $V_M \preceq M^{-1}(V+G) \preceq \bar{V}_M$.
4. Boundedness of the disturbance scaled by the inverse mass matrix, implying existence of constants $m_i$ such that $-m_i w \preceq M^{-1}d \preceq m_i w$.
These assumptions provide bounds on system parameters crucial for controller design despite unknown exact dynamics.

#### 3.2. Signal Temporal Logic (STL) and Spatiotemporal Tubes (STT)

STL is used to formally define spatial, temporal, and logical properties of continuous-time signals. The satisfaction of an STL formula $\phi$ by a signal $x$ at time $t$ is quantified by a robustness metric $\rho_\phi(x,t)$, where $\rho_\phi(x,t) > 0$ implies satisfaction.
A Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) $\Gamma(t) = B(c(t), r(t))$ is defined as a time-varying ball (centered at $c(t)$ with radius $r(t)$) in the state space. The fundamental principle is that if a system's trajectory $x(t)$ remains within $\Gamma(t)$ for all $t \in [0, t_f]$, then the corresponding STL task $\phi$ is guaranteed to be satisfied.

#### 3.3. Neural STT Synthesis (PINSTT)

The core of the tube synthesis lies in modeling the STT center $c(t)$ and radius $r(t)$ using a Physics-informed Neural Network (PINN).
1. **Sufficient Conditions:** Lemma 3.1 establishes sufficient conditions for a time-varying ball to be a valid STT. These conditions ensure a positive tube radius ($r(t) \ge r_d$) and that all trajectories within the tube satisfy the STL specification (i.e., $\rho_\phi(x) > \eta$, where $\eta \le 0$).
2. **Sample Collection and Training:** Since the conditions involve continuous space-time domains, they are intractable directly. The approach samples $N$ points $({\theta}_r, {\lambda}_r, t_r)$ from an augmented space-time horizon $W$. The constraints are then encoded as physics-based loss functions for the PINN:
 - $L_1(\Upsilon)$: Ensures the tube radius $r(t_r;\Upsilon)$ remains above a user-defined minimum $r_d$.
 - $L_2(\Upsilon)$: Ensures the STL robustness metric $\rho_\phi(y(w_r))$ is strictly positive for trajectories $y(w_r)$ residing within the sampled tube boundaries.
 - $L_3(\Upsilon)$ and $L_4(\Upsilon)$: Enforce Lipschitz continuity on the derivatives of $c(t;\Upsilon)$ and $r(t;\Upsilon)$, respectively, bounding their rates of change by $L_c$ and $L_r$. These Lipschitz bounds are crucial for ensuring the tube's evolution is compatible with the system's input constraints.
 The total loss $L(\Upsilon)$ is a weighted sum of these sub-losses. An Adam/SGD optimizer is used to train the PINN parameters $\Upsilon$.
3. **Formal Verification:** To guarantee the learned PINSTT satisfies STL specifications over the *entire continuous* space-time horizon (not just sampled points), the framework leverages the Lipschitz continuity of the STL robustness function (Lemma 3.2). Theorem 3.3 formally states that if the constraints are met at sampled points and the Lipschitz constants are chosen appropriately (i.e., $\hat{\eta} + L\epsilon \le 0$, where $\hat{\eta}$ is related to the robustness margin, $L$ is a combined Lipschitz constant, and $\epsilon$ is the sampling density), then the STT provides formal guarantees over the continuous domain.

#### 3.4. Controller Synthesis

A two-stage, approximation-free control strategy is designed to keep the system trajectory within the synthesized STT while respecting input constraints:
1. **Velocity-level Control (Stage I):** A reference velocity $v_r(t)$ is designed using a bounded transformation function $\Psi$ (from) to guide the system configuration $x(t)$ towards the center of the tube and prevent it from exiting. The control structure is $v_r(t) = - \frac{x(t)-c(t)}{\|x(t)-c(t)\|} \Psi(e_x) \bar{v}$, where $e_x$ is the normalized position error.
2. **Acceleration-level Control (Stage II):** To track the reference velocity $v_r(t)$, a control input $\tau(t)$ is developed. This stage introduces funnel constraints for the velocity tracking error $e_v(t) = v(t) - v_r(t)$, forcing it to remain within exponentially decaying bounds $\pm\gamma_v(t)$. The closed-form control law is $\tau(t) = -\text{diag}(\Psi(\epsilon_v))\bar{\tau}$, where $\epsilon_v$ is the normalized velocity error, and $\bar{\tau}$ are the maximum permissible torques.
3. **Feasibility Conditions:** Crucially, the rates of change of the tube parameters ($L_c, L_r$) are coupled with the system's velocity and actuation limits.
 - Condition (17) ensures the maximum permissible velocity $\bar{v}$ is sufficient to track the tube's evolution and reject initial velocity errors: $\bar{v} \ge \Gamma + \|p_v\|$, where $\Gamma = L_c + L_r$.
 - Condition (18) ensures the maximum permissible torque $\bar{\tau}$ is sufficient to keep the velocity error within the funnel bounds, accounting for system dynamics and disturbances.
Theorem 4.2 formally proves that if these feasibility conditions hold and initial conditions are satisfied, the proposed control law guarantees the system remains within the STT and respects input constraints, thus satisfying the STL specification.

#### 3.5. Extension to Multi-Agent Systems

For multi-agent scenarios, the approach extends by defining a combined robustness metric for the cascaded formulation of individual and global STL specifications (e.g., formation keeping, collision avoidance). This global robustness metric is incorporated into the PINN training, and the same control strategy is applied to each agent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and validates its approach through a series of simulations and hardware experiments.

#### 4.1. Single-Agent Case Studies

The framework was validated on three distinct single-agent systems:
- **2D Omnidirectional Robot:** A surveillance task was specified, requiring the robot to repeatedly visit target regions and avoid obstacles over 360 seconds. The controller successfully guided the robot, both in simulation and hardware experiments (Agile LIMO robot), demonstrating robustness against unknown but bounded disturbances and payload variations. The control inputs remained within prescribed limits. Offline PINSTT computation was 51.410 seconds, with an online control synthesis time of 3.17 $\mu$sec.
- **3D Quadrotor:** A sequential reachability task in a 3D environment with static cylindrical obstacles was specified. The quadrotor successfully navigated the task, demonstrating adherence to velocity and acceleration limits. The offline computation was 5.284 seconds, and online control synthesis was 3.41 $\mu$sec.
- **7-DOF Franka Research 3 Manipulator:** A sequential reachability task in the task-space was defined for the manipulator. Experiments tested nominal control, payload variation, and external disturbances. Joint torques remained bounded within limits across all conditions. Offline PINSTT computation took approximately one minute, with an online control synthesis time of 5.42 $\mu$sec.

#### 4.2. Multi-Agent System

- The approach was extended to a multi-agent scenario involving four omnidirectional robots performing a formation-preserving navigation task. The agents navigated a 2D workspace with static circular obstacles, maintaining a diamond formation, and avoiding collisions with each other and the environment. The PINSTT framework successfully generated smooth, feasible trajectories while keeping velocity and acceleration profiles within bounds. Offline PINN training was 59.5 seconds, and per-step online control synthesis was 3.17 $\mu$s.

#### 4.3. Comparative Analysis

A qualitative and quantitative comparison against representative STL planning and control approaches (RRT*, MPC, CBF, PPC, MILP, prior STT) revealed the following:
- **Qualitative Comparison (Table 1):** PINSTT stands out by simultaneously offering closed-form control, formal STL guarantees, full STL support, applicability to unknown dynamics and bounded disturbances, and explicit input constraints, all while avoiding online optimization. Most other methods achieve only a subset of these properties.
- **Quantitative Computation Time (Table 2):** For benchmark STL tasks (stlcg-1, stlcg-2, stlfrag-1, stlfrag-2), PINSTT exhibited competitive or superior computation times. While the previous STT approach was faster for simpler STL fragments, PINSTT's offline computation scaled better with increased STL complexity. Importantly, PINSTT achieved significantly lower online control synthesis times (in the microsecond range) compared to optimization-based methods (MILP, MPC, CBF), which can take tens to hundreds of seconds.
- **Quantitative Input Comparison (Table 3):** PINSTT effectively ensured that control inputs remained within prescribed limits (e.g., 0.75) under both nominal and perturbed conditions. In contrast, the previous STT controller often required substantially larger (unbounded) control inputs, particularly under model perturbations. CBF and MILP-based approaches also showed larger control efforts, and their performance under perturbations was not evaluated in the comparison due to potential STL violation from model inaccuracies.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that the PINSTT framework is capable of generating STL-compliant trajectories for unknown EL systems under input constraints, providing formal guarantees and achieving real-time online performance, as evidenced by both simulations and hardware validations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers a significant contribution to the field of control systems, particularly in the domain of formal methods for robotic and autonomous systems. Its significance stems from the combination of several desirable properties within a single framework, addressing long-standing challenges in the control of complex systems.

Key aspects of significance and potential impact include:

- **Formal Guarantees with Full STL Support:** The framework provides formal guarantees for the satisfaction of the *full class* of Signal Temporal Logic specifications. This capability is crucial for mission-critical applications where complex, time-dependent tasks must be reliably met, and where failure to satisfy specifications could have serious consequences. This extends beyond the limited STL fragments often supported by other computationally efficient methods.
- **Control of Unknown Systems:** By operating effectively with unknown Euler-Lagrange system dynamics, the PINSTT framework broadens the applicability of STL control to a wider range of real-world robots and physical systems where precise models are difficult or impossible to obtain. This model-free aspect simplifies deployment in diverse and uncertain environments.
- **Explicit Handling of Input Constraints:** The explicit consideration and guarantee of control input bounds are paramount for practical implementation. Physical actuators have inherent limits, and controllers that ignore these often lead to infeasible or unsafe behaviors. PINSTT ensures that the control efforts remain within prescribed physical limits, enhancing the realism and safety of robotic operations.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The development of a closed-form, approximation-free control law that avoids online optimization is a major practical advantage. Achieving microsecond-level online control synthesis times makes the framework suitable for real-time applications, where rapid decision-making and control execution are essential for dynamic tasks and environments. This distinguishes it from optimization-heavy methods that often struggle with scalability.
- **Unified Architecture:** The framework integrates tube synthesis, formal verification, and input-constrained control into a cohesive architecture. This holistic approach simplifies the design and deployment process for complex control problems, providing a robust solution from specification to execution.
- **Multi-Agent System Scalability:** The successful extension to multi-agent systems, demonstrating formation-preserving navigation and collision avoidance, highlights the framework's potential for coordinating teams of autonomous agents in complex scenarios. This opens avenues for applications in drone swarms, collaborative robotics, and networked control systems.
- **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs):** The innovative use of PINNs to parameterize spatiotemporal tubes, incorporating STL robustness metrics and Lipschitz continuity constraints directly into the loss function, demonstrates a powerful paradigm for combining data-driven learning with physics-based and formal guarantees. This approach contributes to the growing field of explainable and verifiable AI in control.
- **Hardware Validation:** The experimental validation on real hardware (Agile LIMO robot, Franka Research 3 Manipulator) provides empirical evidence of the framework's robustness and practical viability in the face of real-world disturbances and model uncertainties.

The potential impact of this research is significant for various applications demanding high-assurance control, such as autonomous driving, aerospace systems, advanced manufacturing, and service robotics. By enabling the robust, real-time, and formally guaranteed execution of complex temporal tasks for unknown, input-constrained systems, this work moves closer to deploying more intelligent and reliable autonomous technologies. Future work, including extensions to stochastic systems and dynamic environments, indicates a path toward even broader applicability and impact.
