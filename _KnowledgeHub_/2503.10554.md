# NuExo: A Wearable Exoskeleton Covering all Upper Limb ROM for Outdoor Data Collection and Teleoperation of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Defense Technology introduce NuExo, a 5.2kg wearable exoskeleton that enables accurate upper limb motion tracking and humanoid robot teleoperation through a novel shoulder mechanism design, achieving 100% coverage of natural motion ranges while supporting outdoor data collection through a backpack-style portable system.

## Problem

- Existing motion capture and teleoperation systems struggle to simultaneously achieve accuracy, comfort, versatility, and outdoor usability
- Current exoskeletons have limited range of motion and are too heavy for extended outdoor use

## Method

- Developed lightweight exoskeleton with synchronized linkage and timing belt transmission for natural shoulder movement
- Created unified teleoperation system integrating multi-modal sensing and calibration-free control framework
- Implemented dual-actuator structure for elbow module to address mechanical constraints

## Results

- Achieved **100%** coverage of natural upper limb motion ranges
- Demonstrated superior accuracy compared to inertial motion capture systems, especially for shoulder joint kinematics
- Enabled untrained operators to successfully control various humanoid robots in manipulation tasks
- Maintained stable data collection and teleoperation accuracy in dynamic scenarios while weighing only 5.2kg

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Novel shoulder mechanism design enables full range of motion coverage while maintaining lightweight construction
> - Integration of force-torque, joint position, and egocentric vision data improves teleoperation accuracy
> - Backpack-style design allows practical outdoor deployment and extended use

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several active areas within robotics and human-machine interaction:

- **Imitation Learning and Robot Skill Acquisition:** The paper directly addresses the growing interest in using imitation learning to teach robots new skills. This approach relies on collecting human motion data to train robot controllers. The work aims to improve the quality and accessibility of this data.

- **Teleoperation of Humanoid Robots:** Teleoperation allows humans to remotely control robots, particularly in situations where robots need to perform tasks in hazardous or remote environments. The paper contributes to the development of more intuitive, versatile, and practical teleoperation systems.

- **Exoskeleton Technology:** Exoskeletons are wearable devices that augment human capabilities or provide assistance in rehabilitation. The paper focuses on upper-limb exoskeletons, which have seen increasing interest for both industrial and medical applications.

- **Multi-Modal Sensing and Data Fusion:** The paper emphasizes the importance of collecting diverse types of data (motion, force, vision) to provide a more complete understanding of human actions and the robot's environment. This is crucial for robust and adaptable robotic systems.

The paper builds upon existing research in these areas by addressing the limitations of current systems, such as trade-offs between accuracy and usability, lack of portability, and limited compatibility with different robot platforms. It distinguishes itself from existing works by aiming for a system that simultaneously achieves accuracy, comfort, versatility, and convenience. Compared to similar projects like Mobile Aloha or Gello, this project focuses on an exoskeleton approach, allowing for more natural and intuitive control. It also aims for a more comprehensive data collection platform than systems like AirExo. The work differentiates itself from existing exoskeleton teleoperation systems by focusing on lightweight design and outdoor applicability and by introducing an innovative shoulder mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's main objective is to develop a wearable exoskeleton system, named NuExo, that overcomes the limitations of existing motion capture and teleoperation systems. The key motivations behind this objective are:

- **Advancing Embodied Intelligence:** The authors believe that bridging the gap between human motion and robot skill learning is crucial for advancing embodied intelligence.

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Systems:** Existing systems often struggle to simultaneously achieve accuracy, comfort, versatility, and convenience in data collection and teleoperation.

- **Enabling Practical Outdoor Deployment:** The authors aim to create a system that can be used in real-world, unstructured outdoor environments and daily living scenarios.

Specifically, the paper aims to achieve the following:

- **Accurate Tracking of Full Upper Limb Movements:** The system should accurately capture the full range of motion of the human upper limb over extended durations.

- **Ergonomic Adaptation to Human Biomechanics:** The exoskeleton should be comfortable to wear and adapt to the natural movements of the human body.

- **Versatile Data Collection:** The system should be capable of collecting various data types, including force data, motion data, and visual data.

- **Compatibility with Humanoid Robots:** The system should be easily adaptable to different humanoid robot platforms.

- **Lightweight Design for Outdoor Use:** The exoskeleton should be lightweight and portable, allowing it to be used in outdoor environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a combination of mechanical design, control systems engineering, and experimental validation to achieve their objectives. The key elements of their methodology are:

- **Innovative Exoskeleton Design:** The authors designed a wearable, motor-driven exoskeleton with a novel shoulder mechanism featuring synchronized linkage and timing belt transmission. This design aims to replicate the full range of motion of the human upper limb while minimizing weight and mechanical interference. The elbow module also utilizes a dual-actuator structure to address mechanical constraints.

- **Unified Teleoperation System:** The authors developed a unified teleoperation system that integrates multi-modal sensing (force-torque, joint position, egocentric vision) and a calibration-free control framework. This system enables zero-shot operation of humanoid platforms with high manipulation accuracy in complex tasks.

- **Implementation on Different Humanoid Platforms:** The teleoperation framework and collection software were implemented on different humanoid platforms to demonstrate the system's versatility.

- **Experimental Validation:** The authors conducted a series of experiments to evaluate the system's performance, including range of motion measurements, data collection accuracy comparisons, and teleoperation trials.

Specifically, the methodology involves:

- Designing and fabricating a custom upper-limb exoskeleton using carbon fiber and 3D-printed PLA plastic components.
- Developing a control algorithm that maps human movements to robot actions while accounting for force feedback.
- Creating a software framework for data acquisition and teleoperation that is compatible with multiple robot platforms.
- Conducting experiments to measure the exoskeleton's range of motion, accuracy, and usability in real-world scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the following key findings:

- **Full Range of Motion Coverage:** The exoskeleton replicates 100% coverage of natural upper limb motion ranges due to the novel shoulder mechanism.

- **Lightweight and Portable Design:** The exoskeleton weighs 5.2 kg and supports backpack-type use, making it convenient for daily outdoor scenarios.

- **Stable Data Collection and Teleoperation Accuracy:** Experiments confirmed the stability of data collection and teleoperation accuracy in dynamic scenarios.

- **Versatile Performance Across Different Humanoid Platforms:** The system demonstrated strong manipulation performance with various operators and robots.

- **Superior Accuracy Compared to Inertial Motion Capture:** A direct comparison with inertial motion capture systems highlighted NuExo's superior accuracy in tracking shoulder joint kinematics during dynamic motions, especially after perturbations.

- **Intuitive Operation:** The system enables untrained operators to control different humanoid robots through diverse manipulation tasks with ease.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NuExo system has the potential to significantly impact the fields of robotics, human-machine interaction, and embodied intelligence. Its key contributions and potential impacts include:

- **Advancing Imitation Learning:** The system provides a more accurate and versatile platform for collecting human motion data, which can be used to train robots to perform complex tasks.

- **Enabling More Intuitive Teleoperation:** The exoskeleton-based teleoperation system allows humans to control robots more naturally and intuitively.

- **Facilitating Real-World Robot Deployment:** The lightweight and portable design of the exoskeleton makes it suitable for use in real-world, unstructured environments.

- **Improving Human-Robot Collaboration:** The system could be used to develop more effective human-robot collaboration strategies, where humans and robots work together to achieve common goals.

- **Potential Applications in Various Industries:** The system could be applied in various industries, including manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, and search and rescue.

- **Advancing Exoskeleton Technology:** The innovative shoulder mechanism and overall design of the exoskeleton contribute to the advancement of exoskeleton technology.

In summary, the NuExo system represents a significant step forward in the development of wearable exoskeletons for data collection and teleoperation. Its innovative design, versatile functionality, and strong performance make it a promising tool for advancing embodied intelligence and enabling real-world robot deployment.
