# QuadWBG: Generalizable Quadrupedal Whole-Body Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> QuadWBG is a modular framework developed by researchers at Peking University, Galbot, the University of Toronto, and BAAI, enabling quadrupedal robots to achieve robust and generalizable whole-body grasping. The system demonstrates an 89% one-time grasping success rate in real-world scenarios, including challenging transparent objects.

## Problem

- Integrating robust locomotion with precise manipulation on quadrupedal robots is challenging, especially in complex, unstructured environments.
- Existing end-to-end reinforcement learning methods for loco-manipulation often yield low accuracy and limited generalization across diverse objects and tasks.
- Traditional modular approaches, while simpler, restrict the robot's overall workspace and hinder truly integrated whole-body control.

## Method

- A modular framework named **QuadWBG** integrates Locomotion, Perception, Manipulation, and Planning modules to achieve coordinated whole-body control.
- It introduces the ==Generalized Oriented Reachability Map== (GORM), which guides a high-level reinforcement learning policy to find optimal 6-DoF robot base poses for grasping.
- The system leverages a learned agile locomotion policy, motion planning with differential kinematics for the arm, and robust off-the-shelf grasp detection modules to mitigate the sim-to-real gap for manipulation.

## Results

- QuadWBG achieved an **89%** one-time grasping success rate across 100 real-world trials involving diverse objects, significantly outperforming leading end-to-end and modular baselines.
- The system demonstrated an **80%** grasping accuracy for challenging transparent objects in cluttered real-world scenarios, attributed to its advanced perception module.
- Coordinated whole-body control, guided by GORM, expanded the effective workspace volume by **54%** and area by **33%**, enabling the robot to grasp objects from floor level to above its body height.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligently combining robust, pre-trained perception modules with learned locomotion and an advanced planning layer within a modular framework can achieve high accuracy and generalizability in robotic loco-manipulation.
> - The Generalized Oriented Reachability Map (GORM) serves as an efficient and effective metric for planning optimal 6-DoF robot base poses that maximize arm reachability for diverse grasp targets.
> - Coordinated whole-body movements, guided by a sophisticated planning mechanism, significantly expand the operational workspace of quadrupedal robots, allowing interaction with objects across a wide range of heights and positions.
