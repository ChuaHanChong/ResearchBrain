# QuadWBG: Generalizable Quadrupedal Whole-Body Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> QuadWBG is a modular framework developed by researchers at Peking University, Galbot, the University of Toronto, and BAAI, enabling quadrupedal robots to achieve robust and generalizable whole-body grasping. The system demonstrates an 89% one-time grasping success rate in real-world scenarios, including challenging transparent objects.

## Problem

- Integrating robust locomotion with precise manipulation on quadrupedal robots is challenging, especially in complex, unstructured environments.
- Existing end-to-end reinforcement learning methods for loco-manipulation often yield low accuracy and limited generalization across diverse objects and tasks.
- Traditional modular approaches, while simpler, restrict the robot's overall workspace and hinder truly integrated whole-body control.

## Method

- A modular framework named **QuadWBG** integrates Locomotion, Perception, Manipulation, and Planning modules to achieve coordinated whole-body control.
- It introduces the ==Generalized Oriented Reachability Map== (GORM), which guides a high-level reinforcement learning policy to find optimal 6-DoF robot base poses for grasping.
- The system leverages a learned agile locomotion policy, motion planning with differential kinematics for the arm, and robust off-the-shelf grasp detection modules to mitigate the sim-to-real gap for manipulation.

## Results

- QuadWBG achieved an **89%** one-time grasping success rate across 100 real-world trials involving diverse objects, significantly outperforming leading end-to-end and modular baselines.
- The system demonstrated an **80%** grasping accuracy for challenging transparent objects in cluttered real-world scenarios, attributed to its advanced perception module.
- Coordinated whole-body control, guided by GORM, expanded the effective workspace volume by **54%** and area by **33%**, enabling the robot to grasp objects from floor level to above its body height.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligently combining robust, pre-trained perception modules with learned locomotion and an advanced planning layer within a modular framework can achieve high accuracy and generalizability in robotic loco-manipulation.
> - The Generalized Oriented Reachability Map (GORM) serves as an efficient and effective metric for planning optimal 6-DoF robot base poses that maximize arm reachability for diverse grasp targets.
> - Coordinated whole-body movements, guided by a sophisticated planning mechanism, significantly expand the operational workspace of quadrupedal robots, allowing interaction with objects across a wide range of heights and positions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses one of the most significant challenges in modern robotics: the seamless integration of robust locomotion with precise manipulation in complex, unstructured environments, specifically for quadrupedal robots. This field, known as quadrupedal loco-manipulation, holds immense potential for applications ranging from household assistance to disaster relief and autonomous field operations.

Historically, advancements in legged locomotion and robotic manipulation often progressed independently. Legged locomotion has seen tremendous strides, particularly with the advent of reinforcement learning (RL), enabling robots to achieve agile motor skills, dynamic maneuvers, and robust navigation over challenging terrains. Similarly, robotic manipulation has benefited from sophisticated grasp detection techniques that allow for accurate grasping of diverse and even challenging objects (e.g., transparent surfaces).

The integration of these two capabilities has primarily followed two main paradigms:
- **Modular Methods:** These approaches typically separate locomotion and manipulation into distinct components, often utilizing off-the-shelf controllers for each. While simplifying design and enhancing stability, they can limit the robot's overall workspace and hinder truly integrated whole-body control. Examples like GAMMA demonstrate competence in specific settings (e.g., table-height grasping) but struggle with broader application (e.g., floor-level tasks).
- **Unified (End-to-End RL) Methods:** This paradigm aims for seamless loco-manipulation through a single, unified whole-body control policy, often trained with RL. These methods promise high integration but frequently encounter significant challenges such as increased action dimensionality, complex physical interactions, low one-time grasping accuracy, limited generalization across diverse objects, and the need for extensive training data, often relying on teleoperation. Baselines like VBC exemplify these limitations, showing low success rates and variability across object types.

"QuadWBG" strategically positions itself to overcome the limitations of both traditional modular and unified end-to-end RL approaches. It draws inspiration from the robustness of specialized grasp detection methods, aiming to integrate them effectively with learned locomotion. The authors recognize that simply using grasp detection for arm planning on a legged robot is insufficient, as it overlooks the crucial coordination between the robot's body and arm movements. Therefore, QuadWBG proposes a novel *modular framework with an intelligent planning layer* that leverages the strengths of both worlds: robust, off-the-shelf perception and precise manipulation, guided by an innovative whole-body planning metric, while retaining a learned, agile low-level locomotion policy. This hybrid approach seeks to deliver the high accuracy and generalizability that pure end-to-end RL methods often lack, without sacrificing the integrated whole-body control capabilities that separate modular systems restrict.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **robust and generalizable whole-body loco-manipulation controller for quadrupedal robots** that can effectively perform complex tasks in real-world environments. The authors are motivated by the vast potential applications of such capabilities, including household assistance, urban maintenance, disaster relief, and autonomous field operations.

The specific key objectives and underlying motivations can be broken down as follows:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - **Challenge with End-to-End RL:** The authors highlight that while end-to-end RL has significantly advanced locomotion, its application to loco-manipulation often results in policies with "mediocre accuracy and limited generalizability". This stems from the increased action dimensionality and complex physical interactions, making it difficult for a single policy to handle diverse object shapes, sizes, materials, and environmental conditions.
 - **Challenge with Modular Methods:** While modular strategies simplify design by optimizing arm and legs independently, they "can constrain the system’s potential for achieving a more extensive workspace and integrated whole-body control."
 - **Motivation:** To "take the best of both worlds," combining the robustness of specialized grasp detection with the adaptability of legged locomotion, while addressing the shortcomings of direct integration.

- **Achieving High Accuracy and Generalizability:**
 - **Objective:** Develop a system capable of high "one-time grasping accuracy" and "generalizability across tasks and objects," including challenging ones like transparent items.
 - **Motivation:** Grasp detection techniques have demonstrated robust performance in detecting grasp poses for diverse, unseen objects in cluttered environments. Integrating such perception with a robust control system can elevate the robot's ability to interact with the real world effectively.

- **Expanding the Robot's Workspace and Operational Range:**
 - **Objective:** Enable the robot to "effectively manage a large workspace, from floor level to above body height," performing diverse whole-body loco-manipulation tasks across various object locations.
 - **Motivation:** Real-world tasks demand robots to interact with objects at different heights and orientations, requiring coordinated body and arm movements beyond the reach of a fixed-base manipulator.

- **Mitigating the Sim-to-Real Gap:**
 - **Objective:** Reduce the common discrepancy between simulated and real-world performance.
 - **Motivation:** End-to-end visual policy learning often faces a significant sim-to-real gap, which can degrade performance in precision tasks. By incorporating highly robust, pre-trained grasp detection modules (which are often trained on large-scale real and synthetic data), the authors aim to bypass this issue for the manipulation aspect.

- **Enhancing System Understanding and Optimization:**
 - **Objective:** Provide "clearer insights into system performance and allow for targeted optimization of each module."
 - **Motivation:** Modular design inherently allows for easier debugging and improvement of individual components, making the overall system development and deployment more manageable and efficient compared to opaque end-to-end policies.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond the current limitations of loco-manipulation by designing a sophisticated modular framework that intelligently combines the strengths of advanced perception, agile locomotion, and precise manipulation through a novel planning mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The QuadWBG system is a modular framework designed for robust and generalizable quadrupedal whole-body grasping. It comprises four interconnected modules: **Locomotion, Perception, Manipulation, and Planning**, integrated to operate on a Unitree B1 quadruped robot equipped with a Unitree Z1 arm, Robotiq gripper, and a wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D415 camera. All policies are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in the Isaac Gym simulation environment.

**A. Locomotion Module:**
This module provides the robot with agile and robust mobility, crucial for whole-body loco-manipulation.
- **Policy Training:** A low-level RL policy is trained using a **teacher-student architecture**. The teacher policy utilizes both proprioceptive and privileged observations (e.g., base linear velocities, friction, mass, motor strengths not available in real-time) to learn robust control. The student policy then distills this knowledge using only proprioceptive observations (commands, gravity, angular velocity, joint positions/velocities, previous actions, phase variables) and a GRU network, ensuring real-world deployability.
- **Command Set:** The policy tracks 5-dimensional commands (pitch, height, and linear velocities in x, y, and yaw rate), allowing for versatile body posture adjustments. Command ranges are constrained based on height and pitch for stability.
- **Reward Structure:** Rewards are designed for accurate command tracking, efficient and natural walking, penalizing undesirable movements (vertical body velocity, roll/pitch angular velocities), and incorporating heuristics for foot placement, clearance, and action smoothness.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap Mitigation:** Extensive **domain randomization** (e.g., ground friction, payload mass, CoM variations, motor strength, proprioception latency) and observation noise are applied during training in Isaac Gym to enhance robustness in real-world scenarios. Proprioceptive latency was identified as crucial for stability.

**B. Perception Module:**
This module is responsible for real-time object tracking and precise grasp pose prediction, serving as the visual intelligence backbone.
- **Object Tracking:** It leverages state-of-the-art computer vision models. Initially, **SAM** generates object masks, followed by **XMem** for real-time object mask tracking.
- **Grasp Pose Detection:** **ASGrasp** (a system that takes IR and RGB inputs for accurate depth prediction, even for transparent/specular surfaces) is used to predict depth and initial grasp poses. These are then refined by **GSNet** to generate more accurate 6-DoF grasp poses.
- **Key Advantage:** By using robust, off-the-shelf grasp detection modules trained on large-scale data, the system bypasses the sim-to-real gap often encountered in end-to-end visual policies for manipulation.

**C. Manipulation Module:**
This module manages the arm's movement for tracking and grasping, coordinated with the robot's base motion.
- **Control Strategy:** It uses a **motion planning approach** with differential kinematics to achieve precise end-effector control, avoiding the inaccuracies inherent in whole-body RL policies for manipulation.
- **Two Phases:**
 - **Tracking:** The wrist-mounted camera's motion is constrained within a "tracking sphere" (parameterized by position and radius in the body frame). This sphere is defined using a **Reachability Map (RM)**, ensuring the camera operates within the arm's dexterous workspace to minimize motion blur and prevent loss of tracking.
 - **Grasping:** The system switches to this phase when the grasp pose reachability (calculated using the RM) meets a predefined threshold. The motion planner then generates trajectories online to guide the arm to the target and execute the grasp while the body remains stationary.

**D. Planning Module:**
This high-level controller determines the optimal base pose for grasping, integrating body and arm movements.
- **Generalized Oriented Reachability Map (GORM):** This is a core innovation. GORM extends the concept of Oriented Reachability Maps (ORM) by supporting robot base placements with **six degrees of freedom** (position and orientation), unlike traditional ORM for platforms restricted to flat surfaces.
 - **Function:** For any target grasp pose in the world frame, GORM computes a distribution of potential high-quality base poses via the inverse of the RM. It filters out invalid base poses (out of locomotion range, colliding with the environment, or below a reachability threshold).
 - **Efficiency:** GORM only needs to be calculated once for a given target pose, making it highly efficient for parallel training.
- **High-Level Policy Training:** An RL policy is trained to minimize the distance between the robot's current base pose and the nearest feasible base pose identified by GORM. The reward function is defined as `r_GORM = exp(-(min(d_GORM))^2)`.
- **Observations:** The policy's observations include the target grasp pose (rotation matrix, translation), the gravity vector in the body frame, joint positions, and the last executed 5D locomotion command.
- **Vision Integration:** Crucially, this module directly uses the 6-DoF grasp pose detection results from the perception module rather than relying on potentially noisy or inaccurate simulated depth images, further reducing the sim-to-real gap for grasping.

This modular structure, with its innovative GORM-guided planning, allows QuadWBG to leverage specialized solutions for perception and low-level control while coordinating them through an intelligent high-level planner, resulting in robust and generalizable whole-body loco-manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world to validate the performance of the QuadWBG system, deploying it on a Unitree B1 quadruped equipped with an Intel Core i9-13900H and NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Mobile.

**A. Evaluation in Simulation:**

- **Locomotion Module Evaluation:**
 - Compared to other locomotion controllers (AsymAC, ROA, StateEstimator, Teacher-student), the **teacher-student method was selected** due to its optimal trade-off between low energy consumption and high performance in tracking 5D velocity commands under significant randomization and observation noise.
- **Whole-Body Workspace Extension:**
 - The system demonstrated a substantial **expansion of the whole-body workspace**. By actively tracking vertical commands and utilizing arm proprioceptive feedback, the efficient workspace volume increased by **54%**, and the workspace area by **33%** compared to a fixed-arm configuration. This enables the robot to grasp objects from floor level to above its body height, adapting its body posture (e.g., leaning, crouching).
- **Generalizable Whole-Body Grasping (Table II):**
 - The system was benchmarked against VBC (a leading end-to-end RL method) and a version of QuadWBG without the GORM reward, across 7 object categories (Ball, Long Box, Square Box, Bottle, Cup, Bowl, Drill). Each object was tested 300 times from random positions and orientations.
 - **QuadWBG achieved significantly higher success rates** (ranging from 81.33% to 91.66%) across all object categories, with a low standard deviation of 3.46. This indicates consistent performance regardless of object complexity or size.
 - In contrast, VBC showed considerable variability (std dev 14.53), performing well on simple objects (Square Box: 80%) but struggling severely with complex or larger objects (Long Box: 28.57%, Drill: 53.33%).
 - The ablation study demonstrated the critical role of GORM: removing the GORM reward led to a significant drop in success rate (ranging from 50.66% to 57.09%), highlighting that basic rewards are insufficient for optimal base pose determination.
- **GORM Guided Grasping (Table IV):**
 - An ablation study focused on the "Cup" object at four different heights (Floor: 0.0m, Box: 0.3m, Table: 0.75m, Shelf: 1.0m) to evaluate the reachability of the target pose.
 - A masked depth-based policy consistently underperformed, especially for low-lying objects.
 - The policy without GORM reward showed a decline in reachability at various heights.
 - **QuadWBG with GORM maintained high reachability** (ranging from 76.0% to 99.6%) across all heights, proving its effectiveness in guiding the locomotion module to optimal base poses that maximize arm reachability for grasping.

**B. Evaluation in Real-World:**

- **Comparison on VBC Objects (Table III):**
 - QuadWBG was compared to end-to-end (VBC) and modular (GAMMA) methods using 14 real-world objects from VBC.
 - **QuadWBG demonstrated superior performance:** 90% success rate for objects on the floor, 75% for objects on a table.
 - GAMMA, a model-based low-level controller, failed to grasp objects on the floor and achieved 73% only on table height.
 - VBC exhibited very low success rates (30% on floor, 12% on table), reinforcing the challenges of end-to-end RL for robust loco-manipulation.
- **Experiment on Arbitrary Objects (Figure 5, Table V):**
 - Using a diverse dataset including YCB objects, recyclables (plastic bottles, cans, crumpled paper), small toys, and a significant portion of transparent objects, QuadWBG achieved an impressive **89% one-time grasping success rate** over 100 trials.
 - Performance breakdown: 6% track error, 3% grasp error, and 2% post-grasp drop.
 - This robust performance underscores the system's generalizability across varied object types and challenging environmental conditions, largely attributed to its modular design and off-the-shelf perception.
- **Experiment on Transparent Objects:**
 - Specifically tested on 10 trials involving transparent objects in cluttered scenarios, QuadWBG achieved **80% grasping accuracy**. This highlights the system's ability to tackle a notoriously difficult manipulation challenge, directly inheriting this capability from its advanced perception module without performance degradation.

In summary, QuadWBG significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of grasping accuracy, generalizability across diverse objects and heights, and robustness in real-world scenarios, particularly for challenging tasks like transparent object grasping. The effectiveness of the GORM planning module and the modular integration of robust perception and learned locomotion are key contributors to these state-of-the-art results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "QuadWBG: Generalizable Quadrupedal Whole-Body Grasping" paper presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic loco-manipulation, holding substantial implications for future robotic applications and research.

**A. Significance of Findings:**

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The most direct and impactful finding is the achievement of an **89% one-time grasping accuracy in real-world scenarios**, including the challenging task of grasping transparent objects (80% accuracy). This level of reliability and versatility positions QuadWBG as a benchmark for practical quadrupedal manipulation.
- **Enhanced Generalizability:** The system's ability to handle a wide range of objects—varying in shape, size, material, and initial pose—across a large workspace (from floor to above body height) demonstrates a high degree of generalizability. This is a critical improvement over previous end-to-end RL methods that often exhibit limited generalization and high variability.
- **Novelty of GORM:** The introduction of the **Generalized Oriented Reachability Map (GORM)** is a key methodological contribution. By extending the concept of reachability maps to support six degrees of freedom for the robot's base, GORM provides an efficient and effective metric for guiding the high-level planning policy. It ensures optimal base positioning for grasping while maintaining robot stability, effectively fusing locomotion and manipulation planning.
- **Effective Modular Design:** The paper validates the benefits of a modular framework. By integrating robust, off-the-shelf perception (grasp detection) with a learned, agile locomotion policy and a sophisticated planning module, QuadWBG successfully mitigates the "sim-to-real gap" for manipulation tasks and allows for targeted optimization of individual components. This approach offers a powerful alternative to the complexities and limitations of purely end-to-end learning for whole-body control.
- **Expanded Operational Workspace:** The demonstrated 54% increase in workspace volume and 33% increase in area through coordinated whole-body control significantly enhances the robot's practical utility, enabling interaction with objects at previously unreachable locations.

**B. Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Real-World Deployment:** The robust and generalized grasping capabilities of QuadWBG pave the way for accelerated deployment of quadrupedal robots in a myriad of real-world applications. These include:
 - **Household Assistance:** Performing chores like picking up items from the floor or shelves.
 - **Urban Maintenance:** Assisting with tasks in public spaces.
 - **Disaster Relief:** Handling debris and manipulating objects in hazardous, unstructured environments where human access is dangerous.
 - **Autonomous Field Operations:** Interacting with the environment in agriculture, inspection, or exploration tasks.
- **New Research Avenues:** The success of the GORM and the modular approach will likely inspire further research into hybrid control architectures that combine the strengths of classical control theory, advanced perception, and reinforcement learning. Future work might explore more complex planning scenarios, multi-robot coordination, and even human-robot interaction using similar principles.
- **Bridging the Gap in Robotics:** This work significantly bridges the gap between sophisticated individual robotic skills (locomotion, manipulation, perception) and their harmonious integration into complex whole-body behaviors. It provides a blueprint for how to overcome the scaling challenges associated with end-to-end learning in high-dimensional, real-world robotic tasks.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By leveraging modularity and off-the-shelf components for perception, the framework can potentially reduce the development costs and time associated with training complex end-to-end policies from scratch, allowing researchers and engineers to focus on optimizing specific aspects of the system.

**C. Limitations and Future Work:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations that provide clear directions for future research:
- **Collision Avoidance:** The current planning module lacks collision avoidance capabilities, which is crucial for safe operation in dynamic or cluttered environments.
- **Challenging Terrains:** Optimization for navigating extremely challenging terrains is an area for improvement.
- **Human Annotation Reliance:** The system still relies on human annotations for initial target object grounding rather than more intuitive language-based inputs. Integrating natural language processing for task specification would greatly enhance autonomy.

Despite these limitations, QuadWBG represents a monumental step forward in generalizable quadrupedal whole-body grasping, offering a robust and practical solution that pushes the boundaries of autonomous robot interaction with the physical world. Its modular design and innovative GORM planning are set to significantly influence the development of future intelligent robotic systems.
