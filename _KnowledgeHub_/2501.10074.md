# SpatialCoT: Advancing Spatial Reasoning through Coordinate Alignment and Chain-of-Thought for Embodied Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab introduces SpatialCoT, a two-stage fine-tuning approach for Vision-Language Models that integrates precise coordinate-based actions with Chain-of-Thought reasoning. This framework improves spatial reasoning for embodied tasks, achieving higher success rates in complex navigation and manipulation tasks in simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Traditional Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack explicit spatial data and understanding, which limits their capabilities in embodied AI tasks.
- Existing language-based spatial reasoning methods produce coarse outputs that are insufficient for the precise, fine-grained actions required in physical embodied tasks.
- Current Chain-of-Thought (CoT) applications for embodied AI primarily focus on high-level language planning without a clear mechanism to translate these thought processes into precise, coordinate-based physical actions.

## Method

- A novel ==two-stage fine-tuning paradigm== for Vision-Language Models is introduced, designed to enhance spatial reasoning and enable fine-grained action generation.
- Stage 1, ==Spatial Coordinate Bi-directional Alignment==, explicitly trains the VLM to understand and generate spatial coordinates from visual and linguistic inputs across diverse spatial data types.
- Stage 2, ==Chain-of-Thought Spatial Grounding==, leverages the VLM's language-based reasoning to produce both a natural language rationale and precise coordinate-based actions, supported by an automated data generation pipeline.

## Results

- SpatialCoT outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including GPT-4o and RoboPoint, achieving a Navigation Success Rate of **61.83%** and a Manipulation Success Rate of **82.57%** in simulated environments.
- The approach shows substantial performance gains in more challenging tasks (e.g., non-unique objects, crowded scenes, distant goals), indicating enhanced understanding of complex object relationships and improved physical cognitive abilities like collision avoidance.
- SpatialCoT demonstrates successful real-world transferability, performing complex manipulation tasks on a dual-arm robot by arranging various object combinations while adhering to physical constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining explicit spatial coordinate alignment with Chain-of-Thought reasoning significantly improves VLM performance in complex embodied tasks.
> - A two-stage fine-tuning process effectively bridges high-level language-based reasoning with low-level, precise physical actions.
> - Robust fundamental spatial capabilities, such as object understanding and spatial relationships, strongly correlate with improved performance in downstream embodied planning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spatial reasoning is a foundational capability for artificial intelligence, particularly in the burgeoning field of embodied AI. It enables agents to comprehend, visualize, and manipulate spatial relationships between objects and environments, which is crucial for tasks like navigation, manipulation, and interaction in the physical world. Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have propelled their application in embodied AI, yet their inherent limitations in spatial understanding have posed a significant challenge.

Traditional VLMs, primarily trained on 2D images and text, often lack the explicit spatial data necessary for robust spatial reasoning. Previous research has attempted to mitigate this deficiency through several avenues:
- **Supplementary Spatial Data and Fine-tuning:** Approaches like SpatialVLM and SpatialRGPT have integrated additional spatial data and fine-tuned models. However, these efforts predominantly focus on language-based reasoning, leading to coarse-grained results that fall short for tasks demanding precise, fine-grained actions (e.g., placing an object at an exact coordinate).
- **Point-Based Action Spaces:** Works like RoboPoint and RoboSpatial introduced a point-based action space to enable models to generate specific locations or regions. While effective for basic spatial referencing, these methods often bypass the inherent language-based reasoning capabilities of VLMs, directly translating instructions into points. This "direct action tuning" limits their ability to handle complex tasks requiring multi-step or intricate reasoning.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** CoT has emerged as a powerful technique to elicit step-by-step reasoning from LLMs and VLMs, improving their problem-solving abilities. In embodied AI, CoT has been explored for high-level language-based planning (e.g., InnerMonologue, CaP). However, a critical gap remains in effectively translating these high-level thought processes into fine-grained, low-level actions required for physical interaction.

SpatialCoT directly addresses this pivotal gap. It recognizes that while CoT enhances reasoning, it needs to be explicitly grounded in the physical world through precise coordinate-based actions. Conversely, while point-based methods provide fine-grained actions, they often lack the sophisticated reasoning capabilities of VLMs. This paper's contribution lies in synergizing these two aspects: enabling VLMs to reason deeply *and* translate that reasoning into precise spatial actions. By moving beyond open-loop evaluations on static VQA datasets to challenging closed-loop, dynamic embodied tasks in simulation and real-world, SpatialCoT pushes the boundaries of practical embodied intelligence. It positions itself as a significant advancement towards more capable and autonomous embodied agents that can truly understand and interact with their environment at a detailed level.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of SpatialCoT is to **significantly enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for effective embodied task planning, specifically focusing on the generation of fine-grained, coordinate-based actions.**

This objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of existing approaches:
- **Limitations of Current VLMs in Spatial Understanding:** Standard VLMs are not inherently equipped with sufficient spatial information from their 2D image and text training data, leading to restricted spatial reasoning.
- **Inadequacy of Language-Based Reasoning for Fine-Grained Actions:** While some methods improve language-based spatial reasoning, these coarse-grained outputs are insufficient for the precise action decisions required in complex embodied tasks (e.g., placing an object exactly "to the right of the plate" requires more than just a textual command).
- **Underutilization of VLM Reasoning in Point-Based Systems:** Existing point-based action space methods, like RoboPoint, bypass the sophisticated language-based reasoning capabilities of VLMs, leading to limitations in handling intricate or multi-step tasks. The motivation is to fully exploit the VLM's inherent "thinking" process.
- **Bridging High-Level CoT Reasoning with Low-Level Actions:** Current Chain-of-Thought (CoT) applications in embodied AI primarily focus on language-based planning, without a clear mechanism to translate these rich thought processes into precise, coordinate-based physical actions. SpatialCoT aims to bridge this critical gap.
- **Need for Robust Evaluation in Embodied Settings:** Previous spatial reasoning evaluations often rely on open-loop Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets. The motivation is to test models in more realistic and challenging closed-loop embodied settings, including navigation and manipulation tasks, both in simulation and the real world.
- **Efficiency in Data Acquisition:** Collecting high-quality, rationale-action data for fine-tuning VLMs is costly. A key motivation is to develop an efficient, automated pipeline for generating such data.

In essence, the authors are motivated to build more intelligent, dexterous, and general-purpose embodied agents by endowing VLMs with a deeper, more actionable understanding of space, facilitated by explicit reasoning and precise grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatialCoT proposes a novel **two-stage fine-tuning paradigm** for Vision-Language Models, designed to bolster their spatial reasoning capabilities and enable fine-grained action generation for embodied tasks.

**Stage 1: Spatial Coordinate Bi-directional Alignment**
The primary goal of this stage is to explicitly teach the VLM to understand and generate spatial coordinates in relation to visual and linguistic inputs. This is crucial for grounding abstract language concepts into precise spatial locations. Unlike prior work that co-trains on generic VQA data, SpatialCoT introduces a "bi-directional alignment" mechanism to strengthen this understanding.
- **Mechanism:**
 - **Coordinates Understanding (Xv, Xcoor -> Xlang):** The model is given an image and text containing coordinates (e.g., "What is the object at (0.81, 0.90)?") and must output a language-based description (e.g., "couch"). This teaches the VLM to interpret spatial coordinates visually.
 - **Coordinates Generation (Xv, Xlang -> Xcoor):** The model is given an image and language-only instructions (e.g., "Give the locations of all chairs") and must generate one or more spatial coordinates (e.g., "[(0.12, 0.31), (0.31, 0.35)]"). This teaches the VLM to translate linguistic spatial concepts into coordinates.
- **Diverse Data Types for Comprehensive Alignment:** To achieve a robust understanding, the authors categorize and generate data across four key areas:
 - **Object Understanding:** Identifying and locating objects based on natural language descriptions (visual grounding).
 - **Affordance Prediction:** Determining possible actions or properties an object/environment allows (e.g., navigable areas, graspable points).
 - **Spatial Relationship:** Understanding relationships between objects based on their layout (e.g., "left of," "above").
 - **Spatial Compatibility:** Predicting compatibility or potential conflicts (e.g., collisions) between objects when moved.
- **Implementation:** The Llama3.2-Vision 11B model serves as the VLM backbone. It is fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on a substantial dataset of 488,000 such bi-directional spatial coordinate alignment examples (Table 4).

**Stage 2: Chain-of-Thought Spatial Grounding**
Building upon the coordinate understanding capabilities acquired in Stage 1, this stage aims to explicitly leverage the VLM's language-based reasoning (Chain-of-Thought) to produce precise, coordinate-based actions. This contrasts with previous methods that directly output actions without an intermediate reasoning step.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of a direct `[Xv, Xlang] -> Xcoor` mapping, SpatialCoT outputs `[Xv, Xlang] -> [Rationale_Xlang, Action_Xcoor]`.
 - **Rationale:** The model articulates its thought process in natural language, utilizing spatial and commonsense reasoning to guide task completion. This "inner monologue" provides a logical bridge.
 - **Action:** Based on the generated rationale, the model produces appropriate coordinate-based actions. The alignment from Stage 1 ensures that the language-based rationale can be effectively translated into coordinates.
- **Automated Data Generation Pipeline:** A critical innovation is the pipeline for efficiently collecting high-quality rationale-action data, which is typically expensive and prone to inconsistencies.
 1. **Ground Truth Action Acquisition:** Given an image and task instruction, an optimal ground truth action (e.g., a trajectory or subgoal point) is acquired from a simulator using rule-based methods.
 2. **Action Annotation:** This ground truth action is visually annotated onto the input image.
 3. **Rationale Generation:** A powerful VLM (the backbone model itself) is prompted to generate a rationale based on the *action-labeled image* and the original task instruction.
 4. **Constraint Prompt:** An additional prompt is used to prevent information leakage, ensuring the rationale doesn't directly mention the ground truth action from the annotated image.
- **Implementation:** The Llama3.2-Vision 11B model, already fine-tuned in Stage 1, undergoes further LoRA fine-tuning on this newly generated rationale-action dataset, comprising 585,000 examples (Table 5).

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Data Collection:** For navigation tasks, the Habitat Synthetic Scenes Dataset (HSSD) and Habitat simulator are used. For manipulation tasks (tabletop rearrangement), Sapien is used, with Blender for high-quality image rendering. The manipulation data construction is semi-automated using LLMs.
- **Evaluation:** The paper emphasizes **closed-loop evaluation** in simulators (Habitat, Sapien) for navigation and manipulation, which is more challenging and realistic than prior open-loop VQA evaluations. They also perform qualitative evaluations on **real-world dual-arm robots** for manipulation. Furthermore, offline evaluations of VLMs' **fundamental spatial capabilities** (object understanding, affordance prediction, spatial relationships, spatial compatibility) are conducted to understand their correlation with embodied performance.
- **Baselines:** SpatialCoT is compared against state-of-the-art open-source (RoboPoint, Llama3.2V Zero-shot, Llama3.2V Direct-Action-Tuning) and closed-source (GPT-4o) VLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly demonstrate the superior performance of SpatialCoT, validating the effectiveness of its two-stage approach and the benefits of integrating Chain-of-Thought reasoning with spatial grounding.

**A. Effectiveness of the Two-Stage Training Process:**
- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** SpatialCoT consistently outperforms all baselines across both navigation and manipulation tasks, including state-of-the-art general-purpose VLMs like GPT-4o and specialized spatial reasoning models like RoboPoint.
 - **Navigation:** SpatialCoT achieves a Distance Gain (DG) of 3.33 and a Success Rate (SR) of 61.83%. This is a significant improvement over direct action tuning (DG 2.28, SR 57.40%), and far surpasses GPT-4o (DG -0.27, SR 56.21%) and RoboPoint (DG 0.21, SR 55.03%), which show either below-average action quality or limited success.
 - **Manipulation:** SpatialCoT attains an SR of 82.57% and a Collision Rate (CR) of 15.68%. This is a notable improvement over direct action tuning (SR 75.81%, CR 21.35%). Crucially, baseline models like GPT-4o and RoboPoint achieve 0% success rates, highlighting the complexity of the tasks and SpatialCoT's unique capability to handle them.
- **Ablation Studies:** The ablation results (Table 1) confirm the contribution of each stage:
 - Adding "Spatial Coordinate Alignment" to direct action tuning significantly boosts performance (DG from 2.28 to 3.23; SR from 57.40% to 60.65% for navigation, and SR from 75.81% to 81.48% for manipulation).
 - Adding "Chain-of-Thought Spatial Grounding" also provides a notable improvement (DG 2.83, SR 57.40% for navigation, SR 77.78% for manipulation).
 - The combination of both stages yields the best overall performance, demonstrating their synergistic effect.
- **Real-World Transferability (Figure 5):** SpatialCoT demonstrates impressive real-world transferability, successfully arranging various object combinations into reasonable layouts on a dual-arm robot while adhering to physical constraints and avoiding collisions.

**B. Benefits Across Different Task Difficulty Levels:**
- **Manipulation (Table 2):** SpatialCoT shows substantial improvements in tasks involving non-unique objects (Level 3, +8.68% SR over Direct Action Tuning) and a high number of objects leading to crowded scenes (Level 4, +20.00% SR). This indicates an enhanced understanding of complex object relationships and improved physical cognitive abilities, particularly collision avoidance.
- **Navigation (Table 3):** The most significant improvement for SpatialCoT is observed at the most challenging level (Level 4, +8.66% SR), characterized by fewer goals and greater distances. This suggests that SpatialCoT is particularly effective in scenarios with sparse reward signals, requiring advanced spatial understanding and reasoning to infer paths to distant, unseen objects.

**C. Correlation Between Fundamental Capabilities and Embodied Performance:**
- **Fundamental Capabilities Scores (Figure 6a):** SpatialCoT consistently outperforms other models across all evaluated fundamental capabilities: object understanding, affordance prediction, spatial relationships, and spatial compatibility.
- **Correlation Analysis (Figure 6b):** A clear positive correlation is observed between the scores on fundamental capabilities and the success rates in downstream embodied planning tasks. Object understanding and spatial relationships show particularly strong positive correlations, reinforcing the idea that strong foundational spatial understanding is critical for higher-level embodied tasks.

**D. Contribution of Chain-of-Thought (CoT):**
- **Case Study (Figure 7):** The CoT mechanism is shown to enable the model to leverage spatial and commonsense knowledge effectively. In the task of finding an alarm clock, SpatialCoT (with CoT) generates a rationale based on common locations (nightstand, bedroom) and inferred room layout, leading to an accurate subgoal. In contrast, the model without CoT produces disorganized and less logical movements. This demonstrates that CoT allows the model to "think" and infer unseen environmental aspects, leading to more intelligent and goal-oriented actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SpatialCoT framework represents a significant step forward in embodied AI research, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Pioneering Integration of CoT and Fine-Grained Actions:** SpatialCoT successfully addresses a critical challenge in embodied AI by bridging the gap between high-level language-based reasoning (Chain-of-Thought) and low-level, fine-grained, coordinate-based actions. This is a crucial conceptual and practical advancement, enabling VLMs to not only "think" but also precisely "act" in the physical world.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance in Complex Embodied Tasks:** By achieving superior results in challenging closed-loop navigation and manipulation tasks, both in simulation and real-world settings, SpatialCoT sets a new benchmark for VLM capabilities in embodied planning. Its ability to handle tasks with numerous objects, non-unique items, and sparse rewards demonstrates enhanced robustness and intelligence.
- **Enhanced Spatial and Physical Understanding:** The improvements in collision avoidance, understanding of object relationships, and the ability to reason about unseen areas highlight SpatialCoT's deeper grasp of spatial and physical concepts. This is vital for developing safer and more effective robotic systems.
- **Scalable Data Generation:** The proposed pipeline for automatic generation of high-quality rationale-action data is a significant practical contribution. It substantially reduces the cost and complexity of data acquisition, making it more feasible to train and fine-tune complex VLMs for embodied tasks on a larger scale.
- **Validation of Fundamental Spatial Capabilities:** The observed positive correlation between fundamental VLM capabilities (object understanding, spatial relationships, affordance, compatibility) and downstream embodied performance provides a valuable insight. It underscores the importance of focusing on these foundational skills for building more capable embodied agents, offering a roadmap for future research directions.
- **Towards More Autonomous and Versatile Robots:** By enabling robots to understand complex instructions, reason about their environment, and execute precise actions, SpatialCoT moves us closer to a future where autonomous agents can perform intricate tasks in diverse, unconstrained environments, significantly expanding the scope of robotic applications in homes, industries, and beyond.
- **Future Research Avenues:** The identified limitations (e.g., lack of complex actions like rotations, reliance on 2D inputs) also open promising avenues for future research, such as integrating 3D visual inputs and expanding the action space, which could further unlock the potential of spatially-aware VLMs.

In conclusion, SpatialCoT is a robust and innovative framework that effectively leverages the strengths of VLMs and Chain-of-Thought reasoning to advance spatial intelligence in embodied AI, paving the way for more capable and contextually aware robotic systems.
