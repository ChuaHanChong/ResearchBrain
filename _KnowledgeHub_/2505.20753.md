# Understand, Think, and Answer: Advancing Visual Reasoning with Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CASIA and collaborators developed Griffon-R, an LMM incorporating a unified "understand-think-answer" mechanism for compositional visual reasoning. The model achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on benchmarks like CLEVR (63.7%), VSR (70.9%), and V-Star Spat. (77.6%), while demonstrating a 13x speedup over toolkit-based methods and reducing object hallucination to 89.3% accuracy on POPE.

## Problem

- Current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) exhibit deficiencies in compositional visual reasoning, often resorting to "shortcut learning" that leads to unfaithful responses.
- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods are often task-specific and require multiple inference passes, reducing efficiency.
- Toolkit-based approaches for visual reasoning introduce significant computational load and latency by relying on external expert models.

## Method

- A unified =="Understand-Think-Answer" visual reasoning mechanism== that enables LMMs to sequentially perform complex reasoning within a single forward pass.
- A semi-automatic expert-supervised data engine to curate a large-scale (**334K** samples) visual reasoning dataset aligned with the "understand-think-answer" paradigm.
- The ==Griffon-R model==, built on a high-resolution single-branch architecture (Griffon v2 backbone with Gemma9B LLM), trained with the specialized data to intrinsically learn the reasoning process.

## Results

- Griffon-R achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on compositional visual reasoning benchmarks, including **63.7%** on CLEVR, **70.9%** on VSR, and **77.6%** on V-Star Spat.
- The model demonstrated comprehensive multimodal capabilities with a top score of **79.0** on MMBench and **87.0%** on ScienceQA, while also reducing object hallucination to **89.3%** on POPE.
- The unified reasoning mechanism provided a **13x** speedup over toolkit-based reasoning methods, with an inference time of **0.336s** per sample.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs can achieve human-like compositional visual reasoning intrinsically and efficiently in a single forward pass, without relying on external tools or multiple inference steps.
> - Training with a meticulously curated, mechanism-aligned dataset is essential for developing robust and accurate visual reasoning capabilities in LMMs.
> - Explicitly guiding LMMs through a structured "understand-think-answer" process significantly reduces object hallucination and leads to more faithful responses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT have spurred significant efforts to imbue them with visual understanding, leading to the emergence of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). These LMMs have demonstrated impressive performance on various vision-language tasks, from basic VQA and image captioning to more fine-grained tasks like visual grounding and object detection, sometimes even outperforming specialized models.

However, a persistent challenge in the LMM landscape is their deficiency in **compositional visual reasoning**. Existing LMMs primarily fall into three categories concerning their reasoning capabilities:

1. **Shortcut Learning Paradigm:** Many LMMs are trained to directly map questions to answers. While effective for straightforward tasks, this approach leads to "hallucinations" or unfaithful responses when confronted with complex questions requiring multi-step, compositional thinking. They struggle to infer answers based on their foundational visual capabilities in a structured manner.
2. **Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Methods:** Inspired by LLM CoT, these methods encourage LMMs to reason step-by-step. While improving reasoning quality, they often suffer from limitations such as being *task-specific* (requiring different reasoning paths for different question types) and necessitating *multiple forward passes* during inference, which reduces efficiency and increases latency. Examples include approaches that leverage bounding boxes, textual descriptions, or scene graphs.
3. **Toolkit-Based Visual Reasoning:** These approaches enhance LMMs by providing external "visual toolkits" (e.g., specialized vision expert models for object detection, segmentation, depth estimation) that supply structured information (e.g., bounding box coordinates, feature-based prompts). While providing accurate information, calling these external tools introduces significant *computational load and latency*, and the complexity scales with the number and type of tools required.

This paper positions itself as a significant departure and advancement from these existing paradigms. It aims to bridge the gap between LMMs' foundational visual capabilities and their ability to perform general, complex compositional question answering. The core contribution is to enable LMMs to perform human-like reasoning *intrinsically*, within a *single forward pass*, and *without reliance on external tools*. This unified approach seeks to combine the flexibility of human reasoning with the efficiency of end-to-end model inference, addressing the fundamental limitations of prior methods. By integrating an "understand-think-answer" process, the work pushes LMMs closer to becoming truly competent general vision models capable of generating faithful and traceable responses.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the recognized limitations of current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) in handling complex compositional visual reasoning tasks. Despite their impressive performance in foundational vision-language tasks, existing LMMs frequently resort to "shortcut learning," leading to superficial understanding and often hallucinated or unfaithful answers when confronted with questions demanding deeper inferential steps. While attempts to incorporate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or external toolkits have shown some promise, they often introduce inefficiencies (multiple inference passes, increased latency) or restrict generalizability due to their task-specific nature.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Unified Visual Reasoning Mechanism:** To enable LMMs to solve complex compositional visual reasoning problems by intrinsically leveraging their existing foundational capabilities (e.g., visual grounding, object understanding) in an integrated and holistic manner.
2. **Introduce a Human-like Reasoning Process:** To implement an "understand-think-answer" paradigm within LMMs. This process is designed to mimic human cognitive steps, allowing the model to analyze a question, gather relevant visual information, perform contextual reasoning, and formulate an answer, all within a single, end-to-end forward pass.
3. **Eliminate Reliance on External Tools and Multiple Inferences:** A core objective is to achieve advanced visual reasoning without the need for external vision expert models or repetitive inferences. This aims to improve efficiency, reduce computational overhead, and streamline the reasoning process.
4. **Promote Faithful and Traceable Responses:** By explicitly guiding the model through an internal reasoning process, the objective is to generate answers that are more reliable, accurate, and understandable in their derivation, thereby mitigating the problem of LMM hallucination.
5. **Curate a High-Quality Visual Reasoning Dataset:** To support the training of LMMs with this novel mechanism, a significant objective is to create a large-scale (334K samples) and meticulously annotated visual instruction dataset. This dataset is designed to align with the "understand-think-answer" paradigm, covering diverse natural and text-rich scenes and incorporating multiple foundational visual capabilities.
6. **Train and Validate Griffon-R:** To build a general-purpose LMM, Griffon-R, based on the proposed mechanism and curated data. The final objective is to demonstrate its superior performance on complex visual reasoning benchmarks (like VSR and CLEVR) and enhance its overall multimodal capabilities across various tasks (MMBench, ScienceQA, TextVQA).

In essence, the research aims to fundamentally advance the intelligence of LMMs by endowing them with intrinsic, efficient, and generalizable compositional reasoning abilities, moving beyond mere pattern recognition to genuine understanding and logical inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around a novel **unified visual reasoning mechanism** inspired by human cognition, supported by a meticulously designed **semi-automatic expert-supervised data engine**, and instantiated in the **Griffon-R model**.

#### 3.1. Unified Visual Reasoning Mechanism

The core innovation is the "Understand-Think-Answer" process, which allows LMMs to sequentially and continuously perform complex reasoning in a single forward pass.

- **Understand:** This initial phase is crucial for accuracy. The model first analyzes the input question and image to determine the specific information required and which intrinsic LMM capabilities (e.g., captioning, grounded captioning, visual grounding, text recognition) are needed to extract it. It then plans how to acquire this information and generates structured instructions to guide itself. For instance, for a question like "Which burger should I eat if I'm a vegetarian?", the model identifies the need to locate burgers, meat, and vegetables. This process is flexible, leveraging various capabilities rather than fixed ones, and outputs "none" if no relevant information is found, preventing misleading clues.
- **Think:** After gathering comprehensive visual cues during the "Understand" phase, the model engages in self-prompted contextual thinking. Leveraging the extensive knowledge base and contextual understanding inherent in its underlying LLM, it deduces the answer based on the generated visual cues. This stage avoids designing question-specific reasoning paths, promoting flexibility across diverse question types. Modern LMMs' ability to understand coordinate-format object references in context without extra forward passes is a key enabler here.
- **Answer:** Finally, based on the self-thinking process and a user-defined response template, the model generates the ultimate answer, concluding the visual reasoning process.

This entire "Understand-Think-Answer" sequence is designed to operate end-to-end within a *single forward pass*, meaning the model generates the understanding context ($X_U$), thinking context ($X_T$), and the final answer ($X_{ans}$) auto-regressively in one continuous sequence. This contrasts sharply with methods requiring multiple inference steps or external tool calls, offering both flexibility and efficiency.

#### 3.2. Expert-Supervised Data Engine

To train LMMs to adopt this unified reasoning mechanism, the authors developed a semi-automatic expert-supervised data engine to curate a high-quality visual reasoning dataset of 334K samples.

- **Progressive Annotation with AI Expert:** The process begins by leveraging a powerful state-of-the-art LMM, Qwen2-VL-72B, as an "AI Expert." This expert is prompted to:
 1. Analyze the question and image to identify key objects or information needed for problem-solving.
 2. Outline the understanding process by specifying intrinsic capabilities (e.g., "Generate a grounding caption").
 3. Generate initial annotations for tasks it excels at, such as general captioning or grounded captioning.
- **Curation with Human Expert:** Recognizing the limitations of even advanced AI in complex visual reasoning and precise multi-object grounding, human experts play a crucial role. They:
 1. Complete tasks where the AI Expert is deficient, particularly complex visual grounding involving multiple objects or partial text.
 2. Review and ensure the overall quality of annotations, verifying the soundness of the understanding process and the accuracy of task-specific annotations.
 3. Filter out overly simplistic questions to maintain data complexity and reasoning challenge.
- **Data Sources:** The 334K image-question pairs are curated from diverse public datasets, focusing on general and text-rich scenes that involve compositional reasoning. These include:
 - **VQA-based data:** GQA, VAW, VizWiz, ChartQA, DUE_Benchmark, TextVQA. Questions are filtered based on "Yes/No" answers or the presence of reasoning/attribute keywords.
 - **Instruction-following data:** LLaVA, ALLaVA, LVIS-Instruct4V.
 - **Caption data:** ShareGPT-4V, adapted to identify and ground key objects relevant to reasoning.

#### 3.3. Griffon-R Model

Griffon-R is the LMM developed using the proposed unified mechanism and curated dataset.

- **Architecture:** It adopts the advanced single-branch high-resolution structure from Griffon v2. This architecture is particularly adept at fine-grained object localization, critical for visual reasoning. It comprises three main components:
 1. A high-resolution visual encoder that processes images up to 1K resolution without partitioning.
 2. A vision-language connector that projects and compresses visual tokens into the textual embedding space.
 3. A Large Language Model (LLM), initialized with Gemma9B, to handle language processing and reasoning.
- **Training Pipeline:** Griffon-R follows a multi-stage training approach:
 1. **Stage I (Pre-training Projector):** The visual encoder and LLM are frozen, and only the vision-language projector is trained.
 2. **Stage II (Full Model Pre-training):** The entire model (visual encoder, connector, LLM) is updated using a broad mix of vision-language data, including referring expression comprehension/generation, and object detection data.
 3. **Stage III (Instruction Fine-tuning):** The full model is fine-tuned using the curated 334K visual reasoning data (aligned with the "understand-think-answer" mechanism), combined with general VQA and instruction-following datasets. Notably, only cross-entropy loss is used, avoiding task-specific losses, to promote generalizability.

This comprehensive approach, combining architectural innovation, a novel reasoning paradigm, and a specialized data curation strategy, aims to imbue Griffon-R with robust and efficient compositional visual reasoning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Griffon-R demonstrates its superior performance across a wide array of visual reasoning and general multimodal benchmarks, validating the effectiveness of the proposed unified mechanism and data curation.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Compositional Visual Reasoning Benchmarks

Griffon-R achieved state-of-the-art results on benchmarks specifically designed for complex visual reasoning, surpassing both foundational LMMs and methods employing MCoTs or toolkits.

- **CLEVR:** Griffon-R scored 63.7% accuracy, significantly outperforming the next best, LLaVA-1.5-13B (55.8%) and VolCano-7B (56.2%). This highlights its strong reasoning in structured, synthetic scenes.
- **VSR (Visual Spatial Reasoning):** It achieved 70.9% accuracy, surpassing LLaVA-1.5-13B (70.4%) and VolCano-7B (67.2%), demonstrating advanced spatial reasoning in natural images.
- **GQA:** Griffon-R obtained 65.1% accuracy, outperforming LLaVA-Next-7B (64.2%) and CogCom-17B (71.7%), although CogCom-17B has a higher score in this specific benchmark, it is a larger model.
- **V-Star Spat. (Spatial Reasoning):** In this challenging high-resolution, small-object compositional reasoning benchmark, Griffon-R achieved 77.6% accuracy, remarkably higher than SEAL-7B (76.3%) and LLaVA-Next-7B (63.2%), indicating its ability to handle fine-grained details for complex inferences.
- **TallyQA (Simple/Complex):** Griffon-R achieved 84.4% on Simple and 70.4% on Complex, showcasing strong counting and detailed reasoning abilities. It surpassed all other models in the simple category and matched the best (VPD-5B and VisualCoT-13B) in the complex category.

These results collectively confirm that Griffon-R, empowered by its unified mechanism, possesses robust visual reasoning capabilities across diverse tasks and scenarios, whether synthetic or natural, and whether requiring coarse or fine-grained understanding.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Multimodal Benchmarks

Beyond specialized reasoning tasks, Griffon-R also demonstrated comprehensive multimodal capabilities on general benchmarks.

- **MMBench:** Griffon-R achieved a top score of 79.0, outperforming leading LMMs like DeepSeek-VL-7B (71.3%) and LLaVA-NeXT-7B (69.0%), and multimodal CoT methods like CCoT-13B (70.7%). This indicates its general proficiency across various multimodal tasks.
- **ScienceQA:** It scored 87.0%, significantly higher than VPD-5B (83.1%) and CogCom-17B (84.0%), demonstrating its ability in scientific reasoning requiring common sense and knowledge.
- **TextVQA:** Griffon-R achieved 72.4% accuracy, surpassing the program-driven VPD model and other LMMs, highlighting its strong performance in text-rich scenes and OCR-based VQA.
- **SEED-Img and LLaVA-in-the-wild:** It achieved 73.8% and 76.2% respectively, further demonstrating its proficiency in real-world, challenging scenarios.
- **POPE (Hallucination Benchmark):** Griffon-R scored 89.3%, the highest among all compared models, suggesting that its structured reasoning mechanism effectively mitigates object hallucination, leading to more faithful responses.

These results indicate that the enhanced visual reasoning capabilities derived from the unified mechanism do not come at the expense of general multimodal performance but rather augment it.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies provided critical insights into the individual contributions of the proposed mechanism and data.

- **Discussion on Understanding Quality:** Using Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) as a proxy for understanding quality, Griffon-R-9B achieved 93.2% on RefCOCO val and 95.1% on test-A. This performance surpassed expert models like Ferret v2 and other reasoning-enhanced LMMs (CogCom, Visual CoT), confirming that Griffon-R develops accurate intrinsic understanding capabilities essential for its reasoning.
- **Discussion on the Unified Visual Reasoning Mechanism:** Comparing its performance on V-Star Spat. with a toolkit-based method (SEAL-7B), Griffon-R's unified mechanism achieved 77.6% accuracy (1.3 points higher than SEAL-7B's 76.3%) while being significantly faster (0.336s per sample vs. 4.586s for toolkit-based methods, indicating a 13x speedup). This directly validates the efficiency and effectiveness of the single-pass, intrinsic reasoning design.
- **Discussion on Training with Curated Data Annotations:** The study showed that while simply providing visual cues (UVRM without curated data) can slightly improve hallucination tasks (POPE), achieving accurate compositional reasoning requires training on the carefully curated dataset. Training with the curated annotations led to a substantial +2.6% improvement on V-Star Spat., underscoring the necessity and effectiveness of the expert-supervised data engine in fostering deep visual reasoning.

Qualitative examples provided in the paper (Figure 5) illustrate Griffon-R's ability to handle compositional visual reasoning by first comprehensively understanding the problem and extracting visual cues, and then performing context-based reasoning to generate accurate final answers. In summary, Griffon-R sets a new benchmark for compositional visual reasoning in LMMs, offering a more efficient, flexible, and robust approach that also generalizes well to broader multimodal tasks and reduces hallucination.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Understand, Think, and Answer: Advancing Visual Reasoning with Large Multimodal Models" marks a significant step forward in the development of truly intelligent Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

1. **Bridging the Reasoning Gap in LMMs:** The most profound impact is in directly addressing a critical limitation of current LMMs: their struggle with compositional visual reasoning. By enabling LMMs to perform a human-like "understand-think-answer" process intrinsically, this work moves beyond superficial "shortcut learning" to foster deeper, more faithful, and traceable visual intelligence. This brings LMMs closer to replicating complex human cognitive abilities in perception and reasoning.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** A key breakthrough is achieving complex reasoning in a *single forward pass* without relying on external expert models or requiring multiple inferences. This design choice dramatically reduces computational overhead and inference latency (demonstrated by the 13x speedup over toolkit-based methods). This efficiency is crucial for deploying LMMs in real-time applications and at scale, making advanced visual reasoning more practical and accessible.

3. **Increased Generality and Flexibility:** Unlike many task-specific multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or toolkit-based approaches, the proposed unified mechanism is designed to be flexible across diverse question types. By allowing the model to autonomously plan information acquisition and engage in self-prompted thinking, it avoids rigid, pre-defined reasoning paths. This inherent adaptability makes Griffon-R a more general-purpose reasoning agent.

4. **Mitigation of Hallucination:** The structured "understand-think-answer" process inherently encourages more grounded and faithful responses. By explicitly requiring the model to gather and process relevant visual cues before answering, the risk of hallucination—a common problem in LMMs—is significantly reduced, as evidenced by its leading performance on the POPE benchmark. This leads to more reliable and trustworthy AI outputs.

5. **Blueprint for High-Quality Data Curation:** The semi-automatic expert-supervised data engine offers a scalable and effective methodology for generating high-quality, mechanism-aligned visual reasoning data. This is crucial for training advanced LMMs, as data quality and annotation strategy directly influence model capabilities. This pipeline can serve as a valuable blueprint for future efforts in dataset creation for complex multimodal tasks.

6. **Advancement of the Griffon Model Family:** This work solidifies the position of the Griffon family of models (Griffon-R building upon Griffon v2) as leaders in high-resolution, fine-grained visual understanding and reasoning. It showcases a continuous research trajectory aimed at pushing the boundaries of LMM capabilities.

7. **Broader Applications:** The ability of LMMs to perform robust, efficient, and generalizable compositional visual reasoning has wide-ranging potential impacts across various domains:
 - **Advanced Visual Question Answering:** Enabling more sophisticated interactions where users can ask complex, multi-step questions about images.
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Enhancing agents' ability to understand their environment, plan actions, and execute tasks based on complex visual cues.
 - **Medical Imaging and Scientific Research:** Providing more accurate and interpretable analyses of visual data.
 - **Accessibility:** Improving tools for visually impaired individuals by offering more nuanced descriptions and answers to visual queries.
 - **Content Moderation and Generation:** Better understanding of complex visual scenarios for more intelligent content analysis.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the field of LMMs by moving them beyond basic visual recognition towards genuine, human-like compositional reasoning. Its focus on intrinsic, efficient, and generalizable mechanisms, supported by high-quality data, lays a crucial foundation for the development of more capable, reliable, and intelligent multimodal AI systems.
