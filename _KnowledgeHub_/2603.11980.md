# Learning Visuomotor Policy for Multi-Robot Laser Tag Game

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang and Westlake Universities developed a decentralized end-to-end visuomotor policy for multi-robot laser tag, directly mapping monocular camera images to robot actions. This approach improved hitting accuracy by 16.1% and reduced collision rates by 6% compared to classic modular methods that rely on explicit state estimation.

## Problem

- Traditional multi-robot combat approaches struggle with accurate enemy state estimation from monocular vision, often requiring specific motion patterns and being susceptible to noise in dynamic adversarial environments.
- Existing obstacle avoidance methods often depend on costly depth sensors or unavailable global localization, increasing hardware requirements and system complexity.
- Multi-robot cooperation frequently relies on inter-robot communication, which limits scalability and efficiency in environments with constrained or unreliable bandwidth.

## Method

- A two-stage privileged imitation learning approach is employed, first training a state-based "teacher" policy using Multi-Agent ==Reinforcement Learning== (MARL).
- Knowledge from the teacher policy is distilled into a vision-based "student" policy, mapping time series of monocular camera images directly to robot velocity commands.
- The student policy's input processing combines object detection (YOLOv5 Nano), non-metric depth estimation (Depth Anything v2), and dynamically scaled target area heatmaps, processed by a CNN and LSTM for temporal aggregation.

## Results

- The proposed visuomotor policy improved hitting accuracy (Hit@15) by **16.1%** and reduced the collision rate by **6%** compared to the strongest classic modular methods using monocular state estimation.
- The "depth + heatmaps" input consistently outperformed other visual inputs, with a SimpleCNN or ResNet18 encoder using this input surpassing larger pre-trained models reliant on RGB.
- Successful real-world deployment on robots equipped with an Nvidia Jetson Orin NX achieved a real-time control frequency of 20 Hz, demonstrating practicality and efficiency on edge computing platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining appearance-invariant depth information with explicit target heatmaps from monocular vision is crucial for robust performance and generalization in dynamic, adversarial environments.
> - A permutation-invariant feature extractor within the state-based teacher policy is essential for generating stable and smooth multi-robot behaviors in complex, multi-agent interactions.
> - LSTMs effectively aggregate temporal visual information, enhancing decision-making for vision-only policies under partial observability and mitigating temporal inconsistencies in depth estimates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the challenges inherent in multi-robot combat scenarios, specifically framed as a laser tag game. This domain serves as a dynamic competitive platform for evaluating the robustness of robotic algorithms and system designs, exemplified by competitions such as the ICRA AI Challenge adopting the DJI RoboMaster combat game. The algorithmic components of such tasks, including object detection, state estimation, autonomous aiming, planning, and control, are relevant to real-world applications such as the capture or interception of malicious drones.

Traditional approaches to shooting-game-style tasks typically employ a modular design, segmenting the problem into distinct stages like perception, state estimation, planning, and control. However, these classical methods encounter several limitations. First, accurate enemy state estimation from monocular vision often requires specific motion patterns to satisfy observability conditions, which are difficult to maintain in adversarial or dynamic environments. Observational noise further degrades reliability. Second, obstacle avoidance in many prior works relies on global localization or depth-sensor-based mapping, which increases hardware cost, system complexity, and may not be available in various real-world settings (e.g., GPS-denied environments). Third, in multi-robot systems, cooperation often depends on inter-robot communication, limiting scalability and efficiency.

The present work positions itself within the evolving landscape of end-to-end learning and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) in robotics. It aims to circumvent the limitations of modular pipelines by adopting an end-to-end visuomotor policy, where robot actions are directly generated from raw image observations. This approach is motivated by human capabilities in first-person shooting (FPS) games, where individuals operate cooperatively using only visual and proprioceptive cues, without explicit state estimation, global mapping, or constant communication. The paper builds upon existing works in visuomotor policies and privileged imitation learning, extending them to decentralized adversarial multi-robot interactions under partial observability, using only onboard monocular vision. This contributes to the development of simpler and more deployable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and decentralized end-to-end visuomotor policy for autonomous multi-robot laser tag games. This policy is designed to map visual observations directly to robot actions, enabling robots to operate effectively without relying on explicit state estimation, global localization, wireless communication, or depth-sensor-based mapping.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several challenges identified in classic modular approaches for shooting-game-style tasks:

1. **Limited Observability for State Estimation:** Accurately estimating an adversary's relative position and velocity from monocular vision is a critical but difficult component. It often necessitates specific robot motion patterns to satisfy observability conditions. In dynamic and adversarial scenarios, these conditions are frequently not met, leading to unreliable estimates and degraded shooting accuracy. The paper aims to bypass the need for explicit, accurate state estimation by learning behaviors directly from visual input.

2. **Reliance on Global Localization and Depth Sensors for Obstacle Avoidance:** Many existing obstacle avoidance strategies for multi-robot systems depend on global localization systems or depth sensors for environmental perception and planning. Global localization might be unavailable in real-world deployments (e.g., indoors or in GPS-denied areas), and depth sensors add hardware cost, weight, and system complexity, making them less suitable for low-cost or lightweight robotic platforms. The objective is to achieve effective obstacle avoidance using only monocular vision.

3. **Inter-Robot Communication Dependency in Multi-Robot Scenarios:** Previous cooperative multi-robot tasks often rely on robust inter-robot communication to share information (e.g., camera poses, estimated states). This dependency can limit the scalability and efficiency of the system, particularly in environments where communication bandwidth is constrained or unreliable. The research seeks to develop a decentralized policy that does not require explicit communication for cooperative or competitive tasks.

By developing an end-to-end visuomotor policy, the authors aim to overcome these challenges, drawing inspiration from human capabilities in competitive visual tasks. The chosen approach, privileged imitation learning, is motivated by the computational efficiency benefits of imitation learning compared to direct vision-based reinforcement learning, especially when dealing with high-dimensional visual inputs and multi-agent dynamics. Training a state-based "teacher" policy with privileged information allows for efficient data generation for the vision-based "student" policy, which then handles the full complexity of visual perception.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method employs an end-to-end visuomotor policy trained through privileged imitation learning. This involves a two-stage process: first, training a state-based teacher policy using multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), and second, distilling this teacher's knowledge into a vision-based student policy via imitation learning.

**Problem Formulation:**
The objective is to learn a policy $\pi$ that maps a time series of `N` image observations (embedded as $h_t$) directly to robot velocity commands $a_t = [v_x, v_y, \omega]$. The robots are divided into two adversarial teams: Team I (allies) aims to locate and shoot enemies while avoiding obstacles and teammates, and Team II (enemies) aims to escape while avoiding obstacles. Both teams use adversarial policies.

**A. Teacher Policy via Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning:**
The teacher policy is trained with privileged state information to generate expert demonstrations.

- **State and Action Space:** For the $i$-th robot at time $t$, the state $s^i_t$ includes:
 - Relative positions of the $K$ nearest obstacles ($p^o_t$).
 - Relative positions of the $K$ nearest teammates ($p^a_t$).
 - Relative positions of opponents ($p^e_t$).
 - The robot's own state (global 2D position and heading) ($p^s_t$).
 All relative positions are in the robot's local coordinate frame. A K-nearest selection strategy maintains fixed input dimensions. The action space $a^i_t$ is a 3D vector: linear velocities $[v_x, v_y]$ and angular velocity $\omega$.

- **Feature Extractor:** The raw state is processed by a permutation-invariant feature extractor (Fig. 3). Individual state components are first encoded by Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). These embeddings are then concatenated and passed through a self-attention layer, which processes them as keys, queries, and values. To obtain a fused embedding, a weighted summation pooling is applied along the embedded token axis. Attention weights are computed by applying softmax normalization to scores derived from the mean of each attention embedding. The fused embedding is then concatenated with the robot's own state embedding. This permutation-invariant design addresses potential input discontinuities from dynamic neighbor selection and changing order, leading to more robust and smoother policy behavior.

- **Reward Function:**
 - **Team I (Ally):** The reward function $r^1_t$ is a sum of components: distance to enemy ($r^1_{dist}$), laser aiming accuracy ($r^1_{aim}$), obstacle avoidance ($r^1_{obst}$), boundary avoidance ($r^1_{bound}$), and cross-fire avoidance ($r^1_{cf}$). Penalties are applied for collisions or accidental teammate shots. This encourages aiming at enemies from a desired distance while avoiding obstacles and friendly fire.
 - **Team II (Enemy):** The reward function $r^2_t$ focuses on avoiding being shot and avoiding obstacles. It includes rewards for increasing distance to the ally ($r^2_{dist}$), increasing aiming error for the ally ($r^2_{aim}$), obstacle avoidance ($r^2_{obst}$), and boundary avoidance ($r^2_{bound}$).

- **MARL Algorithm:** MADDPG (Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient) is used for training the adversarial policies, following a centralized training with decentralized execution paradigm.

**B. Student Policy via Vision-Based Imitation Learning:**
The student policy maps a time series of `N` onboard camera images to velocity commands.

- **Input Processing (Fig. 4):**
 1. A YOLOv5 Nano object detector identifies enemy and ally robots in the RGB image.
 2. Gaussian kernels are placed in the detected bounding box areas to create target area heatmaps ($I_{target}$). The kernel size is dynamically scaled by the estimated distance to the robot ($s = \lambda/D$), with closer robots having larger kernels. This emphasizes the center of the target for precise aiming. Distance $D$ is estimated from known physical robot size, bounding box width, and camera focal length, not from the non-metric depth map.
 3. The original RGB image is fed into Depth Anything v2 (DATv2) to generate a non-metric depth image ($I_{depth}$). Depth values are clipped and normalized.
 4. The heatmaps (for enemy and ally) and the depth image are concatenated along the channel axis to form the input tensor ($I_{input}$). This approach provides both target location cues and appearance-invariant environmental information.

- **Network Architecture:** The $I_{input}$ tensor is fed into a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) image encoder. The resulting image embeddings from a time series are then passed to a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) recurrent module, which aggregates temporal information. This is crucial for decision-making given the camera's limited field-of-view (FOV) and helps mitigate temporal inconsistencies in DATv2's depth estimates. The final fused embedding $h_t$ from the LSTM is then sent to an MLP to regress the action.

- **Imitation Learning:** The student policy is trained using dataset aggregation (DAgger), minimizing the squared L2 norm between the student's action $\pi_{stu,\theta}(h_t)$ and the teacher's action $\pi_{tea}(s_t)$. Data is collected from both simulation (ROS Gazebo digital twin) and real-world environments across various scenes to enrich the data distribution and reduce the sim-to-real gap.

**C. Implementation Details and Policy Training:**
- **Teacher Policy:** Trained with MADDPG, using 2-layer MLPs for actor (128 units) and feature extractor, and a 3-layer MLP for critic (512 units). K=3 nearest neighbors.
- **Student Policy:** Trained with DAgger for 100 epochs on 80k simulation and 20k real-world images. LSTM (1 layer, 128 units) with a sequence length of 5. Action net is a 2-layer MLP (128 units). Visual encoders (e.g., SimpleCNN, ResNet18) are trained end-to-end with the action network.
- **Real-World System:** Nvidia Jetson Orin NX onboard computer, omnidirectional robots with 5mW laser emitter, front-facing camera (110° FOV, 30 Hz). Enemy robots are red, allies white. Laser fires under specific conditions (enemy within 50 pixels of crosshair, bounding box width > 20 pixels, i.e., distance < 3m). Maximum velocities for ally/enemy varied from 0.05 m/s to 0.40 m/s. All visual modules (DATv2, YOLOv5, visual encoder) are converted to FP16 TensorRT models for onboard acceleration, achieving a control frequency of 20 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations were conducted in both simulation and real-world environments, measuring Action Error (AE), Hit@15, Hit@30 (number of consecutive frames hitting the enemy), and Collision Rate (CR).

**A. Ablation and Comparative Study of Visual Encoders and Input Types (Table I):**
- **Input Type Effectiveness:** The proposed "depth + heatmaps" input consistently achieved the best overall performance across all metrics and environments. Ablating heatmaps (depth-only input) degraded hitting performance, indicating the importance of explicit target cues. Pure RGB or edge-based inputs showed lower performance, especially in unseen test environments, suggesting that appearance-invariant depth information combined with target heatmaps improves generalization.
- **Visual Encoder Performance:** SimpleCNN and ResNet18, when combined with the proposed "depth + heatmaps" input, outperformed pre-trained high-capacity encoders (ResNet50, CLIP RNet, DINOv2, MAE) that used RGB input. This suggests that domain-specific input processing and end-to-end training are more critical than generic feature representations from large pre-trained models for this task.
- **Onboard Inference Time:** High-capacity encoders like DINOv2 (137.2 ms) were too slow for real-time control (target 30 Hz, achieving 20 Hz with other modules), limiting their practical usage on resource-constrained platforms. SimpleCNN and ResNet18 were significantly faster (7.8 ms and 16.7 ms respectively for the visual encoder), enabling real-time operation. The total control frequency, including DATv2 and YOLOv5, was 20 Hz.

**B. Performance of Teacher and Student Policies (Table II):**
- **Teacher Policy Feature Extractor:** The proposed "Teacher-SAP" (Self-Attention with Permutation-invariant Pooling) feature extractor achieved the highest Hit@15 (48.6%) and Hit@30 (22.1%), outperforming Teacher-MLP, Teacher-SA+SDF, and Teacher-SA. The permutation-invariant pooling was critical, as its absence (Teacher-SA) led to performance degradation and oscillatory robot movements in practice, hindering smooth chasing and aiming.
- **Student Policy Learning Methods:**
 - DAgger-based approaches significantly outperformed Behavioral Cloning (BC).
 - Combining DAgger with an LSTM (DAgger-LSTM) yielded the best student policy performance (Hit@15: 38.4%, Hit@30: 17.2%, CR: 36.0%), surpassing DAgger-MLP. This was attributed to the LSTM's ability to aggregate temporal information, enhancing decision-making robustness given the camera's limited FOV and mitigating temporal inconsistencies in depth estimates.

**C. Comparison with Classic Modular Methods (Table III):**
- **Ground Truth (GT) Baselines:** Policies with access to ground-truth states performed best. "GT Ours-Teacher" achieved the highest Hit@15 (48.6%) and lowest CR (10.0%), affirming the potential of the underlying learned policy when perfectly accurate state information is available.
- **Monocular-Vision Baselines:**
 - **State Estimation:** Bearing-Angle (BA) method was more accurate (0.094 ± 0.021m position error) than Spatial-Temporal-Triangulation (STT) (0.291 ± 0.072m), which suffered from reliance on global camera poses and communication.
 - **Control Policies:** Under the BA estimator, PD control and MLP control performed similarly and better than Image-Based Visual Servo (IBVS).
- **Overall Performance:** The proposed "Ours-Student" visuomotor policy, operating solely on onboard monocular vision, significantly outperformed all classic modular methods that used monocular state estimation.
 - It improved hitting accuracy (Hit@15) by 16.1% over the strongest baseline (BA + PD) (38.4% vs. 33.0%).
 - It reduced the collision rate by 6% (36.0% vs. 42.0%) compared to BA + PD.
- **Aiming Accuracy:** A Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of radial distances from the crosshair center (Fig. 8, left) showed that the state-based teacher policy had the highest aiming accuracy (37% of frames meeting fire condition), followed by the student policy (28%), which still outperformed other onboard-sensing baselines.
- **Velocity Robustness:** Across various maximum velocity limits for robots, the proposed visuomotor policy consistently achieved higher hit scores (Fig. 8, right), demonstrating its robustness in dynamic scenarios.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Full Gradient attention maps (Fig. 5) showed that the visuomotor policy focused its attention on task-relevant regions, such as enemy targets and environmental obstacles, aligning with human intuition. Real-world experiments (Fig. 6, 7, 9) demonstrated successful deployment, with allies maneuvering strategically to aim and attack enemies, and enemies evading.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a decentralized, end-to-end visuomotor policy for multi-robot laser tag, which offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Modular Approaches:** The work directly addresses and mitigates key challenges prevalent in classical modular robotic systems for dynamic tasks. By eliminating the reliance on explicit state estimation, global localization, and depth-sensor-based mapping, it circumvents inaccuracies from estimation errors, reduces hardware costs, simplifies system complexity, and enhances deployability in diverse real-world environments where such privileged information might be unavailable. The demonstrated performance improvement (16.1% in hitting accuracy, 6% in collision avoidance) over classic methods supports the efficacy of this end-to-end learning paradigm.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The use of a permutation-invariant feature extractor in the teacher policy contributes to smoother and more robust robot behavior in dynamic multi-robot, multi-obstacle environments. Furthermore, the vision-based student policy, leveraging depth maps and target heatmaps from monocular vision, provides appearance-invariant perception. This design choice contributes to the policy's robustness against varying environmental conditions and diverse enemy appearances, facilitating better generalization. The integration of an LSTM for temporal information aggregation further enhances decision-making in partial observability scenarios (limited camera FOV).

3. **Practical Real-World Deployment:** The successful deployment of the proposed policy on real-world multi-robot systems with limited onboard computational resources (Nvidia Jetson Orin NX) demonstrates its practicality. The optimization techniques (e.g., FP16 TensorRT models) enabling a 20 Hz control frequency underscore its viability for real-time robotic applications, showcasing that sophisticated learned policies can run effectively on edge computing platforms.

4. **Implications for Future Robotic Systems:** The findings suggest a viable pathway for developing autonomous robots capable of complex, adversarial interactions using minimal sensing modalities (monocular vision). This approach could be transferred to other challenging real-world tasks that share similar characteristics, such as autonomous drone interception, search-and-rescue operations in unknown environments, or inspection tasks where robots must navigate and interact with dynamic targets without external infrastructure. The work also contributes to the broader field of embodied AI, demonstrating how biologically inspired, vision-only control can lead to effective and robust robot behaviors.

In summary, this research advances the state-of-the-art in multi-robot control by offering a robust, efficient, and deployable end-to-end visuomotor learning framework that directly addresses fundamental limitations of traditional robotic pipelines, thereby opening avenues for more autonomous and adaptable robotic agents.
