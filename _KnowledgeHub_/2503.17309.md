# LLM+MAP: Bimanual Robot Task Planning using Large Language Models and Planning Domain Definition Language

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework combines Large Language Models with multi-agent planning to enable bimanual robots to execute complex tasks autonomously, achieving higher success rates and reduced planning times compared to direct LLM planning while maintaining coordination between robot arms through spatial scene understanding and PDDL-based task allocation.

## Problem

- LLMs alone struggle with long-horizon reasoning and logical correctness in robotic task planning
- Bimanual robots require complex spatial and temporal coordination between two arms that existing approaches don't adequately address
- Need for efficient task allocation and coordination in long-horizon bimanual manipulation tasks

## Method

- ==Integrate LLMs== with multi-agent planning through PDDL formalization, using iterative LLM prompting to generate valid PDDL problem instances
- Use spatial scene understanding to identify coordinated and uncoordinated work areas
- Apply symbolic planners (FMAP/BFWS) to generate executable partial-order plans

## Results

- Achieved highest success rates across all task domains and significantly reduced planning time compared to direct LLM planning and strong reasoning LLM models
- Demonstrated more efficient task allocation through improved Group Debits metric
- Successfully validated framework in simulated environment using NICOL bimanual robot

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing bimanual control as a multi-agent planning problem improves coordination
> - Combining LLM reasoning with formal planning provides better guarantees than pure LLM approaches
> - Spatial scene understanding helps optimize task allocation between arms

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in robotics: enabling robots, particularly bimanual robots, to perform complex, long-horizon tasks autonomously. The work fits into the broader research landscape in several ways:

- **Addresses the Limitations of LLMs in Robotics:** The paper directly tackles the shortcomings of using Large Language Models (LLMs) directly for robotic task planning. While LLMs have shown promise in generating plans from natural language instructions, they often struggle with long-horizon reasoning, hallucination, and guaranteeing the logical correctness of plans, especially in spatial scenarios.
- **Builds on LLM+P Paradigm:** The research extends the "LLM+P" (LLM + Planner) paradigm, which combines the reasoning abilities of LLMs with the formal guarantees of symbolic planners. Existing LLM+P approaches have shown success, but this paper innovates by applying it to the specific challenges of bimanual robots, where spatial and temporal coordination between two arms is crucial.
- **Connects to Multi-Agent Planning:** The paper introduces the novel approach of framing bimanual robot control as a multi-agent planning problem. It leverages techniques from the multi-agent planning community to allocate and coordinate tasks between the two robotic arms, thereby improving efficiency and robustness.
- **Bimanual Manipulation and Dexterity:** The research complements the growing body of work in bimanual manipulation, which aims to equip robots with the dexterity to perform complex tasks requiring the coordinated use of two hands. However, the paper distinguishes itself by focusing on high-level task planning, rather than low-level motor skills.
- **Classical Planning and PDDL:** The research leverages the long-standing techniques of classical planning and PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language). By translating a natural language problem into a PDDL representation, the authors are able to use existing, powerful planning algorithms to find provably correct solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables bimanual robots to autonomously plan and execute complex, long-horizon tasks with greater efficiency and reliability than existing methods.

The motivation behind this work stems from the following:

- **Versatility of Bimanual Robots:** Bimanual robots offer significant versatility in manipulation tasks, but their control is challenging due to the need for coordinated spatial and temporal actions between the two arms.
- **Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Existing approaches either focus on learning low-level manipulation skills or rely on LLMs, which suffer from reasoning errors and hallucinations in complex tasks.
- **Need for Efficient Task Allocation:** Bimanual manipulation requires not only effective task decomposition but also efficient task allocation between the two arms, which has not been adequately addressed by existing LLM+P methods.
- **Spatial and Temporal Coordination:** A need to improve spatial and temporal coordination in long-horizon bimanual robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes LLM+MAP, a bimanual planning framework that integrates LLM reasoning and multi-agent planning to automate bimanual task planning.

The methodology involves the following steps:

1. **Spatial Scene Understanding:** The system first identifies the location of objects and positions in the robot's workspace using object detection models (OWLv2) and rule-based reasoning. It distinguishes between "Uncoordinated Areas" (reachable by only one hand) and the "Coordinated Area" (reachable by both).
2. **PDDL Definition:** The bimanual robotic domain is formalized in PDDL, defining object types (hand, area, object, point), predicates (properties and relationships between objects), and actions (atomic manipulation skills for single and joint operations).
3. **LLM as PDDL Writer:** Given a task description in natural language and spatial scene information, the LLM (GPT-4o) is prompted to generate a PDDL problem instance (initial and goal states) compatible with the defined domain. Iterative re-prompting with error messages is used to reduce coding errors.
4. **Multi-Agent Planning:** A classical symbolic planner (FMAP) is used to generate a partial-order plan from the PDDL representation. In cases where FMAP is too time-consuming, it reverts to a single-robot planner (BFWS) and post-processes the plan to create a partial-order plan.
5. **Plan Execution:** The generated plan is executed on the NICOL bimanual robot in a simulated environment (CoppeliaSim).
6. **Ablation study**: The authors perform an ablation study to compare Multi-Agent Planning vs traditional sequential planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the superior performance of LLM+MAP compared to direct plan generation by LLMs (GPT-4o, V3, o1, and R1) in simulated long-horizon manipulation tasks.

The key findings include:

- **Reduced Planning Time:** LLM+MAP significantly reduces planning time compared to direct plan generation by LLMs, especially when using strong reasoning models (o1 and R1). The symbolic planning time increases with task complexity, but remains manageable.
- **Higher Success Rate:** LLM+MAP achieves the highest success rate across all task domains, indicating its improved ability to complete complex tasks successfully.
- **Efficient Task Allocation:** LLM+MAP demonstrates more efficient task allocation, as measured by the "Group Debits" metric. This indicates better coordination between the two arms and a more optimal plan overall.
- **PSRR over LLM+P**: LLM+MAP achieves a significant Planning Step Reduction Rate (PSRR) over LLM+P.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are considerable:

- **Improved Autonomy for Bimanual Robots:** The LLM+MAP framework enables bimanual robots to perform complex tasks more autonomously, reducing the need for human intervention and programming.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Reliability:** By integrating LLM reasoning with multi-agent planning, the framework improves the efficiency and reliability of bimanual robot task planning, leading to faster task completion and fewer errors.
- **Broader Applicability:** The approach of framing bimanual robot control as a multi-agent planning problem could be extended to other multi-robot systems, enabling more coordinated and efficient teamwork.
- **Real-World Applications:** The ability to plan complex tasks for bimanual robots has numerous potential applications in various domains, including manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, and home automation.
- **Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction:** The research contributes to the development of robots that can seamlessly interact with humans and assist them in everyday tasks, leading to improved quality of life.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work suggests several promising directions for future research, including integrating learning-based bimanual skills, extending the framework to larger multi-agent systems, and incorporating action durations and costs for more fine-grained planning.
