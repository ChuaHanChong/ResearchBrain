# PegasusFlow: Parallel Rolling-Denoising Score Sampling for Robot Diffusion Planner Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> PegasusFlow, a framework for training robot diffusion planners, directly samples trajectory score gradients from parallel environmental interaction, bypassing the need for expert demonstrations. This method enables a simulated hexapod to successfully navigate rough terrain, achieving a 100% success rate and faster task completion than existing approaches.

## Problem

- Diffusion models for robot planning rely heavily on costly and often impractical expert demonstration data for specialized robots.
- Training diffusion models through imitation learning from a separate policy is suboptimal and can introduce distribution shifts, degrading performance.
- Existing direct score estimation methods, like MPPI, require a large number of simulation rollouts, limiting their efficiency for parallel data generation and diffusion planner training.

## Method

- **PegasusFlow** introduces a hierarchical rolling-denoising framework for parallel and direct sampling of robot trajectory score gradients from environmental interaction.
- A novel Weighted Basis Function Optimization (WBFO) algorithm, including its Action-Value variant (AVWBFO), improves sample efficiency by representing trajectories with splines and applying spatiotemporal weighting to control points.
- The framework integrates efficient noise sampling (Latin Hypercube Sampling, Hierarchical Ramp Noise Scheduling) within an asynchronous parallel simulation architecture using IsaacGym for massively parallel rollouts and optional RL policy warm-starting.

## Results

- WBFO/AVWBFO demonstrated superior sample efficiency compared to MPPI in trajectory optimization, with WBFO achieving a final cost of -**0.095** versus MPPI's -**46.53** with 10 samples in 2D navigation.
- For Franka arm collision avoidance, AVWBFO combined with Latin Hypercube Sampling achieved a **100%** completion rate, surpassing MPPI's **90%** success rate with Latin Hypercube Sampling and reducing steps to completion to **63.9**±**15.6**.
- In hexapod barrier crossing, AVWBFO with RL warm-start achieved a **100%** success rate and required fewer steps (**165.1**±**47.0**) than MPPI with RL warm-start (**202.4**±**37.1** steps), significantly outperforming a vanilla RL policy (**0%** success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct estimation of trajectory score gradients from environmental interaction can effectively replace the need for expensive expert demonstrations in training diffusion planners.
> - Decomposing trajectories into spline basis functions with node-level importance weighting (WBFO/AVWBFO) significantly enhances sample efficiency and convergence speed in trajectory optimization tasks.
> - Combining advanced noise sampling techniques (LHS, HRNS) with a scalable parallel simulation architecture and RL warm-starting provides a robust and efficient method for generating high-quality motion plans for complex robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have emerged as a generative tool for trajectory planning in robotics, offering capabilities for refining motion plans in high-dimensional spaces and managing complex constraints. In particular, they hold potential for intricate navigation tasks for legged robots that pose challenges for traditional reinforcement learning (RL) policies. However, a significant barrier to their practical deployment is their reliance on extensive, high-quality expert demonstration data, typically acquired through imitation learning or behavior cloning from pre-trained RL policies or motion capture.

This imitation learning paradigm introduces several issues: it is often costly, labor-intensive, and sometimes infeasible to gather expert data for specialized robotic platforms. Furthermore, this indirect training process, where one policy generates data to train another, is theoretically suboptimal and can introduce distribution shifts, leading to performance degradation. This situation highlights a gap in the existing literature: the need for methods that can estimate trajectory score gradients directly from environmental interactions, thereby enabling more direct and efficient score-matching without expert data.

Existing approaches that explore direct score estimation, such as those inspired by Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, represent a step towards this goal. However, these methods typically require a substantial number of simulation rollouts (often thousands) to produce a single high-quality trajectory, which limits their efficiency for parallel data generation. At the time of this work, a framework capable of performing this score sampling process in a massively parallel manner for efficient data collection and diffusion planner training was not extensively available. PegasusFlow aims to address these limitations by providing a parallel and efficient framework for direct sampling of trajectory score gradients.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PegasusFlow is to address the reliance of diffusion-based robot planners on expert demonstration data, a bottleneck that hinders their practical application. The authors identify that collecting high-quality expert demonstrations for specialized robots is often impractical, costly, or even infeasible. Moreover, training diffusion models through imitation learning, where data is generated by a separate policy, is considered theoretically suboptimal and computationally inefficient, potentially leading to distribution shifts that degrade performance.

Given these challenges, the core objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Parallel Score Sampling Framework:** To create a system capable of directly sampling robot trajectory score gradients in parallel from environmental interaction. This aims to enable training of diffusion policies through pure score-matching, completely bypassing the need for expert demonstrations. This objective includes establishing a scalable mini-batch environment simulation system and a rolling-denoising optimization process utilizing IsaacGym with a coordinated main and rollout-environment architecture.
2. **Introduce an Efficient Trajectory Optimization Algorithm:** To design a novel sampling algorithm, named Weighted Basis Function Optimization (WBFO), that improves sample efficiency and convergence speed compared to existing methods like MPPI. This algorithm leverages spline basis representations with node-level importance weighting to achieve computational efficiency while maintaining trajectory quality through local optimality and temporal coherence.
3. **Implement an Efficient Noise Sampling Schema:** To enhance space exploration and convergence in trajectory optimization tasks by introducing a Model Predictive Control (MPC)-inspired structured noise scheduling approach. This schema combines Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS), Hierarchical Ramp Noise Scheduling (HRNS), and an RL Policy Warm Start.
4. **Integrate within a Scalable Parallel Architecture:** To embed the proposed optimization and sampling techniques within a scalable, asynchronous parallel simulation architecture (built on IsaacGym) that supports massively parallel rollouts, warm-starting with RL trajectories, and uses NVIDIA Warp for accelerated environmental queries.

Collectively, these objectives aim to provide a more direct, efficient, and scalable method for training diffusion models for robot planning, moving away from data-intensive imitation learning to interaction-based score gradient estimation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PegasusFlow introduces a hierarchical rolling-denoising framework designed for direct, parallel, and efficient sampling of trajectory score gradients, bypassing the need for expert demonstrations.

**Problem Formulation:**
The robotic planning problem is formulated as a discrete-time optimal control problem. The system dynamics are defined by $x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k, t_k)$, where $x_k$ is the state vector and $u_k$ is the control input. The objective is to minimize a finite-horizon cost function $J(u_{i:i+h})$, which includes a terminal cost $l_f$ and a stage cost $l(x_k, u_k, t_k; c_k, o_k)$. The stage cost further decomposes into task-related costs ($l_{task}$), obstacle avoidance penalties ($l_{obstacle}$), and control effort regularization ($l_{control}$). An exponential discount factor $\alpha$ prioritizes immediate costs.

**Estimating Score Gradient using MPPI:**
The framework draws theoretical inspiration from the connection between MPPI and diffusion processes. MPPI approximates optimal control by sampling noisy control sequences ($u_k^{(i)} = u_k + \sigma_k^{(i)}$), evaluating their costs, and computing an importance-weighted average to update the nominal control. This MPPI update is mathematically shown to be equivalent to a single-stage diffusion process, effectively acting as a gradient ascent using the score function $\nabla \log p(u)$. However, standard MPPI requires a large number of samples (N) for good approximation, which is computationally expensive for batch robot simulation.

**Weighted Basis Function Optimization (WBFO):**
To address MPPI's sampling inefficiency, PegasusFlow introduces WBFO, which decomposes trajectories into control points and applies spatiotemporal weighting.
- **Spline Trajectory Representation:** Cubic spline interpolation, specifically Catmull-Rom splines, is used to represent trajectories smoothly through a set of control nodes. A trajectory $u(t)$ is expressed as a linear combination of basis functions $\phi_k(t)$ and control point coefficients $P_k$: $u(t) = \sum_{k=1}^K P_k \phi_k(t)$. This allows for efficient conversion between dense trajectory samples ($u \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times T}$) and a smaller set of control nodes ($P \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times K}$) using a precomputed basis weight matrix $\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times T}$. The pseudo-inverse $\Phi^+$ is used for the reverse operation, minimizing reconstruction error.
- **Trajectory Sampling through Basis Function:** Instead of sampling entire trajectories, WBFO samples control points in a reduced-dimensional space ($K \ll T$). This approach improves exploration efficiency because it operates in a lower-dimensional space, allows local optimization through the influence of each control point, and inherently enforces smoothness.
- **WBFO Algorithm (Algorithm 1):** The algorithm computes discounted accumulated rewards $R_{acc}(i, t) = \sum_{s=t}^T r_{i,s} \cdot \gamma^{s-t}$ from step-wise rewards $r_{i,s}$ of sampled trajectories. $\gamma$ is a discount factor, allowing the algorithm to function for trajectory optimization ($\gamma=0$) or optimal control problems ($\gamma > 0$, termed Action-Value WBFO or AVWBFO). Node-specific weights $W$ are calculated by mapping $R_{acc}$ to control nodes using the transpose of the basis function mask: $W = R_{acc} \Phi^T$. These weights are then normalized at the node-level and converted into softmax weights $w_{ij}$. Finally, control points $P_j^+$ are updated by a weighted average of sampled control points. This method provides local optimality, temporal coherence, and efficient exploration.

**Efficient Noise Sampling Schema:**
A dual-component approach is proposed for noise sampling:
- **Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS):** Instead of traditional Monte Carlo sampling, LHS ensures uniform distribution across all noise dimensions by stratified sampling, improving parameter space coverage with fewer samples and promoting diverse exploration.
- **Hierarchical Ramp Noise Scheduling (HRNS):** This method applies a ramp structure to noise magnitudes, modulating them based on temporal distance from the current planning step. Near-term decisions receive focused exploration, while long-term planning maintains broader exploration. This combination aims for faster convergence and improved solution quality.

**Asynchronous Parallel Simulation:**
The framework leverages IsaacGym for massively parallel rollouts.
- **Batch Rollouts in Parallel:** A hierarchical environment structure consists of $N_m$ main environments and $N_r$ rollout environments for each main environment. Rollout environments are synchronized to match main environment states before each rollout, allowing parallel exploration of trajectory branches from identical initial states. Main environments remain frozen during rollouts and are restored to continue execution afterward.
- **Rolling-Denoising Optimization:** The system employs an MPC-like paradigm. Current node trajectories undergo $n_{denoise}$ denoising steps using WBFO in parallel rollout environments. The optimized trajectory is then executed for one timestep in the main environments. Trajectories are shifted forward in time, and new terminal actions are appended.
- **RL Policy Warm Start:** To improve initialization quality, optimization can be warm-started using action sequences from pre-trained RL policies. This mechanism initializes trajectories for the planning horizon and generates actions for appending during trajectory shifting, using an RL-style observation and reward setup.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of PegasusFlow focused on three aspects: effectiveness of parallel trajectory score evaluation, sample efficiency of WBFO/AVWBFO, and benefits of RL warm-starting.

**Various Tasks in PegasusFlow:**
The framework was tested on diverse robotic platforms and scenarios, including:
- Hexapod timber piles navigation (complex terrain).
- Franka arm collision avoidance planning (high-dimensional manipulation).
- Quadruped walking (demonstrating hundreds of rollout environments).
- Hexapod confined space navigation (spatially constrained environments).

**Optimization Algorithm Performance:**
1. **Algorithm Comparison under Varying Sample Numbers (2D Navigation & Inverted Pendulum):**
 - **2D Navigation (Trajectory Optimization):** WBFO (proposed) exhibited superior sample efficiency compared to MPPI, particularly with limited samples (e.g., with 10 samples, WBFO achieved a final cost of -0.095±1.46 versus MPPI's -46.53±4.60). AVWBFO performed intermediately in this trajectory optimization task.
 - **Inverted Pendulum (Optimal Control):** AVWBFO (proposed) slightly outperformed MPPI, while WBFO showed inferior performance. This suggests the importance of discounted rewards in dynamic systems with integrators.
2. **Franka Arm Collision Avoidance Planning:**
 - In a task requiring a Franka manipulator to reach a goal behind a wall gap within 150 steps, AVWBFO consistently achieved higher completion rates than MPPI (93.3% vs. 70% with Monte Carlo sampling, and 100% vs. 90% with Latin Hypercube Sampling).
 - LHS consistently improved performance across all methods. AVWBFO combined with LHS achieved the optimal result, with a 100% completion rate and an average of 63.9±15.6 steps to completion, demonstrating the combined benefit of the optimization algorithm and sampling strategy.

**Robot Navigation Performance in Rough Terrain (Hexapod Barrier Crossing):**
Experiments with 20 Hexapod robots navigating a 0.2m high, 0.35m wide square barrier revealed several insights:
- **RL Warm-starting Impact:** Both AVWBFO with RL warm-start and MPPI with RL warm-start achieved a 100% success rate. In contrast, AVWBFO without RL warm-start achieved 75% success, MPPI without RL warm-start achieved 40% success, and a Vanilla RL policy (pre-trained on flat terrain) completely failed (0% success). This highlights that trajectory optimization is essential for complex navigation, and RL warm-starting significantly enhances success rates.
- **Algorithm Superiority:** AVWBFO consistently outperformed MPPI in terms of completion efficiency. With RL warm-starting, AVWBFO required 165.1±47.0 steps on average, whereas MPPI required 202.4±37.1 steps. Without warm-starting, AVWBFO took 226.1±51.9 steps compared to MPPI's 237.7±48.2 steps. This indicates that AVWBFO is more efficient.
- **Solution Quality:** Mean reward analysis showed that AVWBFO with RL warm-start achieved the highest reward (0.024), followed by MPPI with RL warm-start (0.016). Higher rewards correlate with smoother, collision-free trajectories, suggesting superior trajectory optimality.

Overall, the experiments indicated that the combination of learned behaviors (via RL warm-start) with adaptive trajectory optimization (via AVWBFO and LHS) significantly improved both success rates and motion quality in challenging navigation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PegasusFlow addresses a fundamental challenge in the application of diffusion models to robot trajectory planning: the dependency on extensive, high-quality expert demonstration data. By introducing a framework for direct and parallel sampling of trajectory score gradients from environmental interaction, the work reduces the reliance on imitation learning, offering a pathway for training diffusion policies through pure score-matching. This has the potential to simplify the data collection process, reduce training costs, and enable the deployment of diffusion planners in scenarios where expert data is scarce or impractical to obtain, particularly for specialized or novel robotic platforms.

The introduction of the Weighted Basis Function Optimization (WBFO) algorithm, along with its action-value variant (AVWBFO), represents a methodological advancement in trajectory optimization. Its demonstrated superior sample efficiency and faster convergence, particularly when combined with Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS), indicates that it can yield high-quality trajectories with fewer computational resources compared to traditional sampling-based methods like MPPI. This efficiency is crucial for real-time robotic control and for enabling large-scale parallel simulations for data generation.

The integration of the framework within a scalable, asynchronous parallel simulation architecture built on IsaacGym, supporting massively parallel rollouts and leveraging GPU acceleration for environmental queries, contributes to practical feasibility. This parallelization capability is essential for generating the vast amounts of interaction data needed for training robust robot policies.

The experimental results, particularly the 100% success rate and faster navigation achieved in a challenging barrier-crossing task using AVWBFO with an RL warm-start, indicate the framework's effectiveness in complex locomotion scenarios where pure RL policies may fail. This suggests that PegasusFlow can enhance the agility and autonomy of legged robots in constrained and challenging environments.

By bridging the gap between the generative capabilities of diffusion models and the practical requirements of real-time robotic control, this research opens avenues for developing more capable and autonomous robotic systems. The work's implications extend to advancing diffusion models beyond imitation learning, potentially enabling more principled and efficient learning paradigms in robotics. Future applications could include full diffusion policy training, multi-robot coordination, and handling dynamic environments, further broadening the impact of diffusion-based planning.
