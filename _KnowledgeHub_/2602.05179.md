# From Sequential to Parallel: Reformulating Dynamic Programming as GPU Kernels for Large-Scale Stochastic Combinatorial Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A GPU-based framework reformulates sequential dynamic programming into parallel kernels, greatly enhancing the computational feasibility of large-scale stochastic combinatorial optimization problems with full-fidelity integer second-stage models. This enables processing millions of uncertainty realizations with speedups up to five orders of magnitude over CPU baselines, leading to higher-quality decisions through larger scenario sets.

## Problem

- High computational cost of solving exact, often NP-hard, combinatorial second-stage problems for each scenario in stochastic programming's Sample Average Approximation (SAA) methods.
- Current approaches often simplify second-stage problems to linear programs or less complex models, sacrificing realism and potentially degrading first-stage decision quality.
- The inherently sequential nature of traditional dynamic programming implementations limits their scalability for large-scale optimization tasks and prevents leveraging modern parallel computing.

## Method

- Reformulation of sequential dynamic programming (DP) algorithms into parallel, ==hardware-aware== GPU kernels designed for batched min-plus operations.
- Exploitation of multi-dimensional parallelism across scenarios, DP layers/stages, and routing/action options within a generic DP framework.
- Development of custom GPU kernels and a detailed recipe for converting DP subroutines into high-throughput primitives, demonstrated with CVRPSD split DP and DSIRP reinsertion DP.

## Results

- GPU implementations achieve substantial speedups, from **80x** over single-thread CPU for CVRPSD up to 9.3e4 for DSIRP, enabling the efficient processing of millions of scenarios.
- Larger scenario sets, facilitated by GPU acceleration, consistently improve the robustness and reduce the cost of first-stage solutions, with GPU-derived solutions closer to optimum under fixed time budgets.
- The GPU framework demonstrates superior scalability and solution quality compared to CPU baselines and traditional MILP solvers, also achieving a **5.09%** average improvement in objective value over a single-scenario DSIRP baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Statistically robust Sample Average Approximation (SAA) requires substantially larger scenario sets (tens of thousands to millions) than commonly used to effectively reduce bias and stabilize estimates.
> - GPUs can achieve dramatic speedups, ranging from one-two to four-five orders of magnitude over CPU baselines, by exploiting multi-dimensional parallelism across scenarios and within DP computations.
> - Enabling the evaluation of larger scenario sets and more first-stage candidates within practical time budgets directly translates to finding significantly higher-quality and more robust solutions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a computational challenge in stochastic programming (SP), particularly within scenario-based Sample Average Approximation (SAA) methods. In these approaches, a significant bottleneck arises from the necessity of solving an exact second-stage problem for each scenario, especially when these second-stage problems exhibit NP-hard combinatorial structures. Historically, this computational burden has led researchers to simplify second-stage problems, often by restricting them to linear programs or less complex models. Such simplifications can compromise the realism of the models and potentially degrade the quality of first-stage decisions.

Dynamic programming (DP) is a foundational paradigm in optimization and control, forming the basis for numerous algorithms in combinatorial optimization, including those for the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP), knapsack problems, and shortest path problems. DP subroutines are also integral to advanced heuristics and metaheuristics in areas like vehicle and inventory routing. Despite its widespread applicability, most DP implementations are inherently sequential, which limits their scalability for large-scale problems.

The broader landscape of optimization has seen increasing adoption of GPU acceleration, particularly benefiting continuous optimization problems such as those solved by ADMM, primal-dual methods, and interior-point algorithms. However, a comparable general-purpose framework for accelerating discrete, combinatorial, scenario-based problems, especially those relying on DP, has been largely absent. Existing GPU-accelerated approaches for discrete problems tend to be either highly specialized, requiring custom kernel designs and data layouts, or rely on higher-level libraries that necessitate significant reformulation to fit their underlying linear-algebra primitives. This work aims to bridge this gap by developing a generalized, GPU-based framework specifically for scenario-batched dynamic programming in stochastic combinatorial optimization, thereby enabling the use of full-fidelity integer second-stage models at scales previously considered impractical without relying on structural relaxations or surrogate learning models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the computational tractability of structured integer recourse operators in scenario-based Sample Average Approximation (SAA) for stochastic programming. The authors aim to enable the use of full-fidelity, integer second-stage models at scale, without resorting to simplifications or surrogate learning models that might compromise model realism or decision quality.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges prevalent in the field of stochastic programming:
- **Computational Bottleneck of Exact Second-Stage Problems**: A significant hurdle in scenario-based SAA is the computational expense of solving an exact second-stage problem for every individual scenario. This challenge is exacerbated when each scenario incorporates an NP-hard combinatorial structure, rendering the overall problem computationally demanding.
- **Limitations of Current Approaches**: Faced with the high computational cost, much of the existing stochastic programming literature has traditionally restricted second-stage problems to linear programs or simplified models. While these approaches offer computational feasibility, they often necessitate sacrificing model realism and can lead to suboptimal first-stage decisions.
- **Sequential Nature of Dynamic Programming**: Dynamic programming, a core component in many exact and heuristic combinatorial optimization algorithms, is typically implemented sequentially. This sequential nature obscures potential parallelism that exists both across multiple scenarios and within the DP transitions themselves, thus limiting the ability to leverage modern parallel computing architectures.
- **Absence of General GPU Frameworks for Discrete SP**: While GPU acceleration has been applied to various continuous optimization problems, a comprehensive and reusable GPU-based framework specifically designed for discrete, combinatorial, scenario-based stochastic optimization, particularly for DP-based recourse evaluations, has not been widely available. Existing GPU applications for discrete problems are often problem-specific, limiting their general applicability.
- **Need for Larger Scenario Sets**: For many real-world problems with complex or unknown demand distributions, a small number of scenarios is often insufficient to accurately capture uncertainty, leading to biased first-stage decisions. Statistically, larger scenario sets are required to reduce bias and achieve consistency in SAA estimates. However, the computational cost associated with evaluating these larger sets has historically prevented their practical application.

The authors are motivated to develop a framework that provides the computational leverage necessary to evaluate significantly larger scenario sets and more first-stage candidates within practical time budgets. This is intended to directly improve the quality and robustness of SAA solutions by allowing for a more comprehensive representation of uncertainty, ultimately leading to stronger first-stage decisions in large-scale, realistic stochastic discrete optimization problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology focuses on reformulating sequential dynamic programming (DP) algorithms into parallel GPU kernels to efficiently solve large-scale stochastic combinatorial optimization problems. The core approach involves a hardware-aware, scenario-batched framework designed to exploit multiple dimensions of parallelism.

**Generic Dynamic Programming Framework:**
The paper describes a generic DP formulation to facilitate GPU implementation.
1. **Preliminary Knowledge**: A finite-horizon DP is considered over stages $t=1, \ldots, T$, with states $s_t \in S_t$ and actions $a_t \in A_t(s_t)$. The system transitions from $s_t$ to $s_{t+1}$ deterministically or stochastically, incurring a stage cost $c_t(s_t, a_t; \omega)$ under scenario $\omega$. The objective is to minimize the expected terminal cost.
2. **Transition-Based Formulation**: The recursion is expressed in terms of state-to-state transition costs, $A^\omega_t(s, s') := \min_{a \in A_t(s), g_t(s,a)=s'} c^\omega_t(s, a)$. This transforms the Bellman update into $J^\omega_{t+1}(s') = \min_{s \in S_t} \{J^\omega_t(s) + A^\omega_t(s, s')\}$, which enables stage-wise min-plus updates suitable for batched GPU execution.
3. **Min-Plus Matrix Formulation**: By indexing state spaces $S_t = \{1, \ldots, m_t\}$, the Bellman update becomes a matrix-vector product in the $(\min, +)$ semiring: $J^\omega_{t+1} = (A^\omega_t)^\top \otimes J^\omega_t$. Infeasible transitions are masked with $+\infty$. This formulation allows for efficient GPU implementation via tensor broadcasting and dimension-wise minimization, with padding and masking handling variable-sized state spaces.

**Key Innovations in Parallelism:**
The framework’s core innovation is its ability to expose and exploit parallelism across multiple dimensions:
- **Across Scenarios ($\Omega$)**: Evaluations for different scenarios are independent once a first-stage decision is fixed, allowing simultaneous processing of a batch of scenarios.
- **Across DP Layers/Stages ($t$)**: The stage-wise min-plus updates in the generic DP framework are naturally parallelizable across states within a stage.
- **Across Route or Action Options ($a$ or $r$)**: Within a single state transition, multiple actions or routing options might be available, each contributing a cost. These can be evaluated in parallel before a minimum is taken.

**Hardware-Aware GPU Kernels:**
Custom GPU kernels are designed to leverage this multi-dimensional parallelism. These kernels implement Bellman updates using warp- and block-level reductions with numerically safe masking for $+\infty$ values. This design enables a single GPU pass to process over $10^6$ uncertainty realizations efficiently. The approach allows for dynamic batching and out-of-memory handling by adjusting batch sizes based on available GPU memory (Algorithm 4). The core batched DP (Algorithm 5) executes a backward DP in parallel over scenarios and states, making delivery decisions and recording transitions before backtracking to recover full solutions.

**Instantiation A: Split DP for Capacitated Vehicle Routing with Stochastic Demand (CVRPSD):**
- **Problem**: Given a "giant tour" (sequence of customers) and vehicle capacity $Q$, the second stage splits this tour into capacity-feasible routes based on scenario-dependent customer demands $q^\omega_{\sigma_k}$. The objective is to minimize total travel cost.
- **DP Formulation**: $J^\omega(i) = \min_{p<i} \{J^\omega(p) + A^\omega(p, i)\}$, where $J^\omega(i)$ is the optimal cost to serve customers up to $\sigma_i$, and $A^\omega(p, i)$ is the cost of serving customers $\sigma_{p+1}$ to $\sigma_i$ as a single route, masked with $+\infty$ if capacity is violated.
- **2D Parallelism**: The computation for a fixed destination state $i$ is parallelized across scenarios $\omega$ and predecessors $p < i$. Each thread computes $J^\omega(p) + A^\omega(p, i)$, followed by a warp/block-level min reduction across $p$ to yield $J^\omega(i)$ for each scenario (Figure 1, Algorithm 3).

**Instantiation B: Forward Inventory Reinsertion DP for Dynamic Stochastic Inventory Routing Problems (DSIRP):**
- **Problem**: For a single customer over a planning horizon, decide when to replenish inventory under uncertain demand to minimize expected routing, holding, and stockout costs, following an order-up-to (OU) policy. Decisions are scenario-independent for the first day, and scenario-dependent thereafter.
- **DP Formulation**: $C^{t+1}_i(I^{t+1,\omega}_i) = \min_{q^t_i \in \{0, U_i-I^{t,\omega}_i\}} \{C^t_i(I^{t,\omega}_i) + F_t(q^t_i) + h^t_i(I^{t+1,\omega}_i)\}$, where $C^t_i(I)$ is the minimum cumulative cost, $F_t(q)$ is routing cost, and $h^t_i(I)$ is holding/stockout cost. This is expressed as a masked min-plus matrix-vector product $J^{t+1}_i = (A^{t,\omega}_i)^\top \otimes J^t_i$.
- **3D Parallelism**: Computation for a stage $t$ is parallelized across scenarios $\omega$, state transitions $I \to J$, and candidate route options $r$. Threads evaluate $J^t_i(I) + A^{t,\omega}_i(I, J; r)$, with a two-level min reduction: first over route options $r$, then over predecessor states $I$, to yield $J^{t+1}_i(J)$ for each scenario (Figure 2).

The approach provides a detailed recipe for converting sequential DP subroutines into high-throughput GPU primitives by structuring them for batched min-plus operations and leveraging tensor operations inherent to GPU architectures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, scalability, and impact on decision quality of the proposed GPU-based DP framework.

**1. Statistical Benefits of Large Scenario Sets:**
- Experiments on the Dynamic Stochastic Inventory Routing Problem (DSIRP) demonstrate the empirical behavior of Sample Average Approximation (SAA) estimators (Figure 3).
- With a small number of scenarios, the estimated cost systematically underestimates the true expectation, indicating a bias effect.
- As the number of scenarios increases, the SAA estimator gradually stabilizes and approaches the true optimum, and its variance decreases at an approximate $O(1/\sqrt{m})$ rate, consistent with theoretical consistency guarantees.
- Improvements in estimates continue to accrue even with large sample sizes (tens of thousands to millions), suggesting that substantially larger scenario sets are required to reduce bias and improve solution quality when demand distributions are complex or unknown.

**2. Scalability with the Number of Scenarios:**
- Comparative runtime analysis between GPU and CPU (single-thread and multi-thread) implementations on CVRPSD split and DSIRP reinsertion DPs shows substantial performance gains (Figure 4).
- For **CVRPSD split operator** (Figure 4a):
 - Single-thread CPU runtime increases sharply, reaching minutes at $10^6$ scenarios.
 - 8-thread CPU provides moderate relief but saturates.
 - GPU runtime scales nearly linearly and remains in the seconds range at $10^6$ scenarios, achieving approximately an 80x speedup over single-thread CPU and 20x over 8-thread CPU at the largest scale.
- For **DSIRP reinsertion operator** (Figure 4b):
 - The speedup is more pronounced. At $2 \times 10^5$ scenarios, the GPU implementation achieves roughly $9.3 \times 10^4$ speedup over single-thread CPU and $2.26 \times 10^4$ speedup over multi-thread CPU.
 - These gains are attributed to 3D parallelism (scenarios × inventory transitions × route options), high arithmetic intensity, and on-chip warp/block-level reductions.
- Overall, GPU parallelization enables near-linear scaling in the number of scenarios, with speedups ranging from one-two to four-five orders of magnitude, making very large, data-driven scenario sets computationally feasible.

**3. Impact of Scenario Set Size on Decision Quality:**
- Evaluating the out-of-sample performance of first-stage solutions for CVRPSD instances (x-n128 and x-n105) under varying numbers of observed scenarios (1 to $10^4$) (Figure 5).
- Solutions derived from a small number of scenarios (e.g., a single scenario) exhibit significant bias and perform poorly.
- Increasing the number of observed scenarios consistently improves the robustness and reduces the cost of the resulting first-stage solutions. This outcome aligns with the theoretical principle that larger sample sizes reduce estimation bias in SAA.

**4. Decision Quality Under Fixed Time Budgets:**
- Comparison of decision quality achieved by CPU single-thread, CPU multi-thread, and GPU implementations under identical wall-clock time limits for the CVRPSD split algorithm (Figure 6).
- GPU consistently produces solutions closer to the true optimum than CPU baselines, even with small time budgets.
- The quality gap widens as the time limit increases, demonstrating that faster scenario evaluation on the GPU allows for the exploration of significantly more candidate first-stage tours, leading to higher-quality solutions.

**5. GPU Memory Usage Analysis:**
- Experiments for both CVRPSD and DSIRP solvers show that GPU memory usage grows linearly with the number of scenarios and state-space size (Figures 7 and 8).
- Even large configurations, such as $10^6$ scenarios for CVRPSD (3.98 GB) or $10^5$ scenarios for DSIRP (1.66 GB), fit within standard 11 GB GPU limits.
- This indicates that GPU memory capacity is not a practical bottleneck for the evaluated problem scales, with computation time being the limiting factor.

**6. Comparisons with Baseline Methods (Appendix A.6 & A.7):**
- For CVRPSD, the GPU-based splitting algorithm (GPUSPLIT) is compared against the MILP solver Gurobi (Table 1). GPUSPLIT demonstrates superior scalability, remaining tractable for larger scenario sets while Gurobi hits time limits or memory exhaustion. GPUSPLIT often achieves better objective values for larger instances where Gurobi cannot prove optimality.
- For DSIRP, the multi-scenario GPU approach is compared against a state-of-the-art single-scenario baseline (Coelho et al., 2012) (Table 2). The proposed method achieves 4.21%-6.37% lower objective values (5.09% on average) with comparable or faster runtimes, indicating that explicit multi-scenario handling leads to higher-quality solutions.

**7. Solution Quality vs. Number of Evaluated First-Stage Candidates (Appendix A.8):**
- Experiments on DSIRP and CVRPSD (Tables 3 and 4) show that as the time budget increases, leading to more GPU calls and a larger number of evaluated first-stage candidates (generated by a genetic algorithm), the framework consistently finds equal or better solutions. This demonstrates the framework's ability to leverage increased computational throughput for enhanced search effectiveness and solution quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a GPU-based framework that significantly advances the tractability of large-scale stochastic combinatorial optimization, particularly for problems involving dynamic programming in the second stage. The work's significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Full-Fidelity Models**: The primary impact is making full-fidelity, integer second-stage models computationally viable at scales previously considered impractical. This eliminates the need to resort to simplified models, surrogate learning, or structural relaxations, thereby allowing for more realistic and accurate representations of complex combinatorial structures and uncertainties in stochastic programming.
- **Overcoming Computational Bottlenecks**: By reformulating sequential dynamic programming as hardware-aware, scenario-batched GPU kernels, the framework addresses the major bottleneck of solving an exact NP-hard second-stage problem for every scenario. This enables the processing of over a million uncertainty realizations in a single GPU pass, a scale substantially beyond what traditional CPU-based or simplified approaches can handle.
- **Improved Decision Quality**: The demonstrated ability to evaluate far larger scenario sets and more first-stage candidates within fixed time budgets directly translates into improved decision quality. Larger scenario sets reduce the bias of SAA estimators, leading to more robust and consistently lower-cost first-stage solutions, as evidenced by the empirical convergence behavior and out-of-sample performance evaluations.
- **Generalizable Approach**: The generic DP framework and the detailed recipe for converting DP subroutines into high-throughput GPU primitives suggest a broader applicability beyond the specific CVRPSD and DSIRP problems studied. This framework could be adopted for a wide range of DP-based problems in optimization and control, offering a reusable acceleration method.
- **Scalability and Efficiency**: The achievement of nearly linear scaling with the number of scenarios and speedups of one-two to four-five orders of magnitude over multi-threaded CPU baselines redefines the computational frontier for scenario-based evaluation. This efficiency is critical for addressing real-world problems where large data-driven scenario sets are necessary for accurate modeling.
- **Practical Feasibility**: The GPU memory usage analysis confirms that realistic problem instances with up to millions of scenarios can fit within standard GPU memory capacities (e.g., 11GB), indicating that memory is not a limiting factor at these scales. This supports the practical deployability of the framework.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work serves as an early step toward hardware-aware algorithms for large-scale stochastic discrete optimization. It opens avenues for future research into handling constrained and risk-aware objectives, designing GPU-native abstractions for irregular DPs, integrating learned components while preserving numerical reliability, and extending these primitives to an even broader array of problem classes.

In summary, this research provides a practical and efficient pathway to solve complex stochastic optimization problems with combinatorial second stages, offering higher-quality solutions and enabling deeper insights into the impact of uncertainty, thus fostering advancements in operations research, logistics, and decision-making under uncertainty.
