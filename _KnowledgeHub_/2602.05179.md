# From Sequential to Parallel: Reformulating Dynamic Programming as GPU Kernels for Large-Scale Stochastic Combinatorial Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A GPU-based framework reformulates sequential dynamic programming into parallel kernels, greatly enhancing the computational feasibility of large-scale stochastic combinatorial optimization problems with full-fidelity integer second-stage models. This enables processing millions of uncertainty realizations with speedups up to five orders of magnitude over CPU baselines, leading to higher-quality decisions through larger scenario sets.

## Problem

- High computational cost of solving exact, often NP-hard, combinatorial second-stage problems for each scenario in stochastic programming's Sample Average Approximation (SAA) methods.
- Current approaches often simplify second-stage problems to linear programs or less complex models, sacrificing realism and potentially degrading first-stage decision quality.
- The inherently sequential nature of traditional dynamic programming implementations limits their scalability for large-scale optimization tasks and prevents leveraging modern parallel computing.

## Method

- Reformulation of sequential dynamic programming (DP) algorithms into parallel, ==hardware-aware== GPU kernels designed for batched min-plus operations.
- Exploitation of multi-dimensional parallelism across scenarios, DP layers/stages, and routing/action options within a generic DP framework.
- Development of custom GPU kernels and a detailed recipe for converting DP subroutines into high-throughput primitives, demonstrated with CVRPSD split DP and DSIRP reinsertion DP.

## Results

- GPU implementations achieve substantial speedups, from **80x** over single-thread CPU for CVRPSD up to 9.3e4 for DSIRP, enabling the efficient processing of millions of scenarios.
- Larger scenario sets, facilitated by GPU acceleration, consistently improve the robustness and reduce the cost of first-stage solutions, with GPU-derived solutions closer to optimum under fixed time budgets.
- The GPU framework demonstrates superior scalability and solution quality compared to CPU baselines and traditional MILP solvers, also achieving a **5.09%** average improvement in objective value over a single-scenario DSIRP baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Statistically robust Sample Average Approximation (SAA) requires substantially larger scenario sets (tens of thousands to millions) than commonly used to effectively reduce bias and stabilize estimates.
> - GPUs can achieve dramatic speedups, ranging from one-two to four-five orders of magnitude over CPU baselines, by exploiting multi-dimensional parallelism across scenarios and within DP computations.
> - Enabling the evaluation of larger scenario sets and more first-stage candidates within practical time budgets directly translates to finding significantly higher-quality and more robust solutions.
