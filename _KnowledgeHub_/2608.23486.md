# GeoWAM: Visual Geometry World Action Models for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoWAM is a world action model for autonomous driving that replaces future-image generation with future-geometry forecasting. A DVGT-2 geometry encoder builds multiview memory; a decoder predicts future point maps, and a stop-gradient, inverse-dynamics-style ego head turns forecast geometry into trajectories. It reaches 90.2 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2 navtest and 36.6 on navhard.

## Problem

- Pixel-space world models entangle geometry and motion with appearance, texture and illumination, forcing 3D transformations to be inferred indirectly from 2D observations.
- Video-generation objectives reward visually plausible futures without requiring recovery of the physical dynamics, so learned states are poorly aligned with planning needs.
- Occupancy world models need voxelized ground-truth annotations, while vision-language-action policies never explicitly supervise how the scene itself will evolve.

## Method

- ==Visual geometry world model== pretraining: a **DVGT-2** encoder yields multi-level geometry and ego tokens; learned future queries with ==causal temporal self-attention== plus cross-attention to history decode dense ==point maps==.
- Supervision combines ==cosine feature alignment== to ==stop-gradient== targets from future frames with a point objective of Euclidean regression, confidence-aware regression, and multi-scale ==surface-normal consistency==.
- **GeoWAM**'s geometry-conditioned action head: ego queries cross-attend history and stop-gradient future geometry tokens, then a causal temporal transformer and trajectory query regress one trajectory without anchors or sampling.

## Results

- Future geometry on nuScenes: mean Abs Rel **0.257** versus **0.274** for the strongest video-plus-reconstruction baseline, and mean threshold accuracy rises from **0.655** to **0.754**.
- NAVSIM v2 navtest: **90.2 EPDMS**, best in table, **+0.6** over the DVGT-2 initialization and above diffusion, latent-world and vision-language-action planners at **88.2 to 89.6**.
- Two-stage pseudo-closed-loop navhard: **36.6 EPDMS**, beating all baselines including reinforcement-learning and score-supervised methods at **31.9**, **34.1** and **36.1**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choose a state space aligned with the action space: forecasting 3D structure supervises exactly the transformations planning consumes, unlike photometric reconstruction.
> - Stop-gradient one-way conditioning preserves a pretrained predictive skill while a downstream head exploits it, preventing task loss from degrading the representation.
> - Forecasting geometry directly beats generating video then reconstructing it; the two-step pipeline loses metric structure, especially at longer two-to-four-second horizons.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems require robust anticipation capabilities, encompassing an understanding of the current scene, its probable future evolution, and how these dynamics constrain ego-vehicle actions. Recent advancements in end-to-end driving systems have increasingly integrated pretrained foundation models to develop comprehensive representations for this task.

One prominent approach involves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which transfer semantic knowledge and reasoning from vision-language models to ego-trajectory prediction. While effective for high-level scene understanding and decision-making, VLA policies typically employ action-centric training objectives that do not explicitly supervise future scene evolution, thereby leaving environmental dynamics implicitly represented.

Complementing VLA models are world models, which focus on learning predictive representations of how an environment evolves. In autonomous driving, many contemporary world models utilize high-capacity video-generation backbones to forecast future observations with visual fidelity. Pretraining these models on large, unlabeled driving datasets enables them to acquire priors regarding object motion, scene structure, and temporal evolution. However, future-observation prediction alone does not inherently specify the optimal ego-vehicle action.

World Action Models (WAMs) address this gap by connecting future-state forecasting with trajectory generation, thereby coupling environmental dynamics with ego-vehicle actions within a shared representation. Most existing WAMs for autonomous driving have adopted the design principles of visual world models, using images or video as their primary observation space. They learn scene dynamics through future-frame prediction and link these learned representations to trajectory generation.

The present work, GeoWAM, critically assesses the suitability of pixel-based representations for modeling driving dynamics. It argues that pixels, while visually rich, provide an indirect representation of scene geometry and motion, entangling them with appearance, texture, and illumination. This entanglement necessitates that models infer three-dimensional transformations from two-dimensional observations. Consequently, models optimized for visually plausible future-frame generation may capture spatiotemporal regularities without explicitly recovering the underlying physical dynamics, leading to predictions not directly grounded in a state representation aligned with driving requirements.

GeoWAM positions geometry, particularly point clouds, as a more direct and native state space for autonomous driving. Geometric representations explicitly encode three-dimensional environmental structure, and their temporal changes directly reveal object motion and ego-frame transformations without requiring photometric reconstruction. Furthermore, scene geometry and ego trajectories are defined within the same three-dimensional coordinate space. Therefore, forecasting future geometry provides direct supervision for the spatial structure and scene dynamics essential for safe planning and control.

This paper builds upon existing work in several related areas:
- **World Models for Autonomous Driving:** Prior models like GAIA-1, DriveDreamer, Vista, and GEM focus on future-video generation, capturing ego motion, agent behavior, and visual scene evolution. Other approaches, like OccWorld and Drive-OccWorld, use voxelized 3D occupancy forecasting. GeoWAM distinguishes itself by learning future geometry from dense metric point-map targets derived from geometry foundation models, without requiring explicit ground-truth occupancy annotations.
- **World Action Models for Autonomous Driving:** Models such as Driving into the Future, VaViM/VaVAM, Epona, WorldVLA, and PWM integrate future-state prediction with action generation. While diverse in architecture, most of these WAMs rely on image or video generation as their primary world-modeling interface. GeoWAM diverges by making metric geometry the primary prediction space and directly conditioning trajectory generation on the resulting future geometric dynamics.
- **Visual Geometry Models:** General visual geometry models, including DUSt3R, CUT3R, VGGT, MapAnything, and DVGT/DVGT-2, focus on feed-forward prediction of dense geometric quantities like point maps and ego poses. GeoWAM extends this line of research by moving from scene reconstruction to forecasting, using future point-map prediction to learn scene dynamics and integrate geometric representations with trajectory planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel World Action Model (WAM) for autonomous driving, named GeoWAM, which fundamentally shifts the underlying state representation from pixel space to visual geometry.

The motivation for this shift stems from perceived limitations of current pixel-based WAMs:
- **Indirect Representation:** Pixels provide an indirect representation of 3D scene dynamics, entwining geometry and motion with extraneous factors such as appearance, texture, and illumination. This entanglement complicates the inference of true three-dimensional transformations from two-dimensional observations.
- **Implicit Physical Dynamics:** Models optimized for visually plausible future-image generation may learn superficial spatiotemporal regularities without necessarily recovering the explicit underlying physical dynamics of the environment. Such predictions may lack grounding in a state representation that directly supports the demands of driving.
- **Misalignment with Action Space:** Driving actions are inherently executed in a three-dimensional coordinate space. Pixel-based future predictions require an additional interpretative step to translate visual changes into actionable 3D geometric and kinematic information.

Given these limitations, the authors motivate geometry as a more natural and advantageous state space:
- **Explicit Spatial Structure:** Representations like point clouds explicitly encode the three-dimensional structure of the environment. This directness eliminates the need to infer spatial relationships from indirect visual cues.
- **Direct Motion Capture:** Changes in geometry over time directly reveal object motion and transformations of the ego vehicle's reference frame, without requiring complex photometric reconstruction.
- **Alignment with Driving Actions:** Scene geometry and ego trajectories exist within the same three-dimensional coordinate system. Forecasting future geometry, therefore, provides direct supervision for the spatial structure and dynamic evolution of the scene, which are critical for safe and effective motion planning and control.

Based on this motivation, the specific objectives of the GeoWAM project are:
1. **To articulate and demonstrate** why geometry serves as a more native and appropriate state representation for world action models in autonomous driving, aligning scene dynamics directly with the 3D space of driving actions.
2. **To develop and pretrain** a visual geometry world model capable of forecasting future scene geometry from historical multiview observations, thereby capturing both spatial structure and temporal dynamics of driving scenes.
3. **To introduce GeoWAM** by extending this pretrained geometry world model with an inverse-dynamics-like formulation that infers future ego motion from the predicted geometry, subsequently mapping this motion to an ego trajectory.
4. **To validate the efficacy** of GeoWAM through quantitative evaluations of its future-geometry prediction accuracy and its performance in open-loop and closed-loop planning scenarios, thereby demonstrating the utility of visual geometry world modeling for autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoWAM employs a two-stage training approach, systematically building from geometric forecasting to action planning.

**Stage 1: Visual Geometry World Model Pretraining**

The first stage focuses on pretraining a visual geometry world model to predict dense future scene geometry from historical multiview images.

- **Multiview Geometry Encoding:**
 - **Input:** The model receives a sequence of `K` historical multiview frames, denoted `I_{t-K+1:t}`. Each `I_v^τ` represents an image from camera `v` at time `τ`.
 - **Encoder:** The DVGT-2 architecture is utilized as the geometry encoder (`E_θ`). This encoder processes the multiview image sequence to produce multi-level historical tokens.
 - **Outputs:** At each time step `τ` and for `L` selected feature levels, the encoder generates two types of tokens:
 - **Geometry Tokens (`X_ℓ^τ`):** Represent the spatial scene structure observed from each camera view.
 - **Ego Tokens (`E_ℓ^τ`):** Encode ego-motion context across the observation history.
 - These tokens are concatenated to form the complete historical geometry memory `Z_t`.

- **Future Geometry Decoding:**
 - The future-geometry branch predicts scene evolution over `F` future steps using a set of learned geometry queries (`Q_geom`).
 - **Query Construction:** A learned query seed `q_geom` is replicated and augmented with learned temporal, view, and 2D sinusoidal positional embeddings for each future step, camera view, and spatial location.
 - **Decoder Process:** The decoder first projects the historical memory `Z_t` to its hidden dimension. It then iteratively updates the geometry queries across multiple decoder layers. This update involves two steps:
 1. **Causal Temporal Self-Attention:** Models the evolution of each spatial location across the `F` future steps.
 2. **Cross-Attention:** The updated queries cross-attend to `Z_t` to retrieve relevant context from the observed historical scene.
 - **Output:** After decoding, a future geometry latent (`Û_{t+k}`) is obtained for each future step. This latent is then mapped by level-specific output projections to multi-level feature representations of the future scene (`X̂_ℓ^{t+k}`).
 - **Geometry Head:** A shared geometry head (`G_ψ`) decodes these multi-level features into a dense future point map (`P̂_{t+k}`) and a per-pixel confidence map (`Ĉ_{t+k}`). This direct prediction of 3D points in the ego coordinate system constitutes forecasting geometric scene evolution without generating future image appearance.

- **Future Geometry Supervision:**
 - **Feature Loss (`L_feat`):** Future images are processed by the same geometry encoder (in a detached target branch) to obtain patch-feature targets (`X̄_ℓ^{t+k}`). The predicted features (`X̂_ℓ^{t+k}`) are aligned with these targets using cosine distance.
 - **Point-Map Loss (`L_{future\_point}`):** Predicted point maps are supervised by ground-truth dense future point-map targets and their validity masks. This objective combines Euclidean point regression, confidence-aware regression, and multi-scale surface-normal consistency.
 - **Current Point Loss (`L_{current\_point}`):** The same point-map objective is applied to the encoded current frame to anchor the geometry encoder during forecasting.
 - **Overall Pretraining Objective (`L_{pre}`):** `L_{pre} = L_{feat} + L_{future\_point} + L_{current\_point}`.

**Stage 2: GeoWAM Finetuning (Visual Geometry World Action Model)**

After geometry pretraining, the model gains the ability to forecast scene geometry. This stage extends this capability to ego-trajectory planning through an action branch. GeoWAM adopts an inverse-dynamics-like formulation, inferring future ego motion from the predicted scene evolution and then mapping this motion to an ego trajectory.

- **Future Ego-Token Decoding:**
 - **Ego Query Construction:** `N_e` learned ego-query seeds (`q_ego_n`) are introduced, augmented with temporal and view embeddings for each future step and camera view (`Q_ego`).
 - **Ego Decoder (`D_ego_η`):** These queries undergo causal temporal self-attention across future steps and then cross-attend to both the historical geometry memory (`Z_t`) and the *predicted future geometry tokens* (`Û_{t+1:t+F}`).
 - **Stop-Gradient:** A stop-gradient operation is applied to `Û_{t+1:t+F}`. This design choice prevents the trajectory loss from propagating back through and altering the predicted future geometry, thus preserving the geometry forecasting capability acquired during pretraining. It also reflects the inverse-dynamics-like design: ego motion is inferred from scene evolution, but trajectory supervision does not reshape the geometry used for conditioning.
 - **Output:** The decoder produces predicted future ego tokens (`Ê_{t+1:t+F}`), which describe ego motion consistent with the forecast scene evolution.

- **Trajectory Decoding:**
 - **Action Head (`H_ω`):** The action head takes the deepest-level historical ego tokens (`E_L^{t-K+1:t}`) and the predicted future ego tokens (`Ê_{t+1:t+F}`).
 - **Refinement:** These sequences are appended and refined using a causal temporal transformer, allowing future ego tokens to integrate historical and predicted motion context.
 - **Trajectory Regression:** A learned trajectory query then cross-attends to the refined ego features, and a regression head maps the resulting query to a single future trajectory (`Â_t`). This trajectory (`â_{t+k} = (x̂_{t+k}, ŷ_{t+k}, θ̂_{t+k})`) specifies the planar position and heading of the ego vehicle. The model directly regresses one trajectory, without using trajectory anchors, mode classification, or iterative sampling.

- **Planning Objective (`L_{plan}`):**
 - During finetuning for planning, the future and current geometry objectives (`L_{pre}`) are retained.
 - **Trajectory Loss (`L_{traj}`):** An `ℓ_1` regression loss supervises the predicted trajectory.
 - **Auxiliary Pose Loss (`L_{pose}`):** An auxiliary `ℓ_1` loss is used to predict relative poses between historical frames.
 - **Complete Objective:** `L_{plan} = L_{pre} + λ_{traj}L_{traj} + λ_{pose}L_{pose}`, where `λ_{traj}` and `λ_{pose}` are loss weights.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GeoWAM was conducted across two primary capabilities: future geometry prediction and ego-trajectory planning.

### 5. Future Geometry Prediction on nuScenes

- **Metrics:** Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel) and threshold accuracy (δ < 1.25) were used, computed over eight future steps (up to 4 seconds). Lower Abs Rel and higher δ < 1.25 indicate better performance.
- **Baselines:** GeoWAM was compared against VGGT-World (a direct geometry world model) and video world models (Epona and Cosmos 3) combined with a geometry reconstruction model (DVGT) for a fair comparison of geometric outputs.
- **Results (Table 1):**
 - GeoWAM achieved the lowest Abs Rel across all evaluated horizons (1s, 2s, 3s, 4s) and significantly improved the aggregate mean Abs Rel from 0.274 (Epona+DVGT) to 0.257.
 - It also improved the mean δ < 1.25 from 0.655 to 0.754, performing notably better at horizons from two to four seconds.
 - These results suggest that directly forecasting visual geometry preserves future metric structure more effectively than reconstructing geometry from generated RGB frames and outperforms other direct geometry-forecasting methods.

### 6. Planning on NAVSIM v2

#### 6.1. navtest (Closed-Loop Planning)

- **Metrics:** Extended Predictive Driver Model Score (EPDMS), which aggregates no at-fault collision (NC), drivable-area compliance (DAC), driving-direction compliance (DDC), traffic-light compliance (TLC), ego progress (EP), time-to-collision (TTC), lane keeping (LK), history comfort (HC), and extended comfort (EC). Higher EPDMS is better.
- **Baselines:** Compared against several perception-based planners and recent world-action models.
- **Results (Table 2):**
 - GeoWAM achieved an EPDMS of 90.2, which was the highest score among all competing methods listed in the table.
 - This represents an improvement of 0.6 points over its DVGT-2 initialization.
 - GeoWAM also matched the best DDC and TLC scores and maintained competitive performance across other safety and progress metrics.
 - These findings support the effectiveness of GeoWAM's visual geometry formulation in combination with a deterministic trajectory decoder for closed-loop planning.

#### 6.2. Two-Stage Planning on navhard

- **Description:** The navhard split features challenging scenarios and utilizes a two-stage pseudo-closed-loop evaluation. This benchmark approximates real closed-loop behavior by rendering new observations from predicted ego poses using 3D Gaussian Splatting and feeding them back to the planner, allowing for assessment of robustness to accumulated deviations.
- **Baselines:** Compared against methods including those trained with reinforcement learning or direct PDMS-score supervision (e.g., LTFv6†, NavFormer†, EponaV2†).
- **Results (Table 3):**
 - GeoWAM achieved an EPDMS of 36.6 under this challenging setting, which was the highest score among all listed methods, including those optimized via reinforcement learning or direct metric supervision.
 - This performance indicates GeoWAM's ability to maintain effectiveness even when planning errors accumulate and affect subsequent observations.

### 7. Qualitative Visualization

- **Observations (Figure 3):** Visualizations of predicted future geometry for various maneuvers (left turn, straight driving, right turn) show that GeoWAM:
 - Preserves coherent scene structure over the prediction horizon.
 - Accurately reconstructs environmental elements such as trees, poles, and fine-grained road markings.
 - Captures the dynamics of surrounding agents (e.g., a following vehicle in a left turn scenario).
 - Provides actionable spatial context for planning, demonstrated by the ego trajectory steering around a roadside vehicle.

### 8. Significance and Potential Impact

The GeoWAM research contributes to the field of autonomous driving by proposing and validating a paradigm shift in world action modeling from pixel-based to geometry-based representations. This work has several significant implications:

- **Foundational Shift in State Representation:** The primary significance of GeoWAM lies in its argument for and empirical demonstration of geometry as a more native and direct state space for autonomous driving. By explicitly capturing 3D structure and transformations, geometry addresses limitations of pixel-based representations which indirectly encode spatial information and entangle it with irrelevant visual attributes. This provides a more physically grounded foundation for understanding and predicting scene dynamics.

- **Enhanced Predictive Accuracy:** The improved performance in future-geometry prediction on the nuScenes dataset demonstrates that directly forecasting 3D metric structure is more effective than inferring it from generated future images. This enhanced accuracy in predicting the physical environment can lead to more reliable downstream planning.

- **Stronger Driving Policies:** GeoWAM achieves superior performance in both open-loop and challenging pseudo-closed-loop planning scenarios on NAVSIM v2, reflected by its leading EPDMS scores. This indicates that grounding the world action model in geometric dynamics directly translates to stronger, safer, and more competent driving policies. The ability to outperform models explicitly trained with reinforcement learning or direct metric supervision on the navhard benchmark highlights the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed geometric approach in reactive and complex scenarios.

- **Direct Alignment with Planning:** By placing both world modeling and action prediction within a shared geometric space, GeoWAM ensures that the learned representations are explicitly organized around 3D structure and motion. This direct alignment between predicted environmental state and ego-vehicle actions simplifies the planning problem and provides a more intuitive and actionable context for trajectory generation. The inverse-dynamics-like formulation, where ego motion is inferred from predicted scene evolution, supports this direct connection.

- **Potential for Scalability and Interpretability:** A geometry-centric approach might offer advantages in terms of scalability and interpretability compared to complex pixel-level interpretations. While not explicitly detailed in the paper, direct 3D representations could facilitate easier debugging and understanding of model behavior, as well as more efficient computation of physical interactions relevant to driving.

- **New Pretraining Objective:** GeoWAM establishes future-geometry prediction as an effective pretraining objective for autonomous driving. This could encourage future research to explore similar geometric pretraining tasks, potentially leading to more robust and transferable foundation models for autonomous driving.

In summary, GeoWAM represents an advancement in autonomous driving world action models by explicitly leveraging visual geometry. Its demonstrated improvements in geometric forecasting and planning performance suggest that this approach could contribute to the development of more robust, reliable, and physically intelligent autonomous driving systems.
