# Generalized Parallel Scaling with Interdependent Generations

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces "Bridge," an architectural modification for large language models that enables information sharing among parallel response generations from the same input. This approach consistently improves individual response accuracy and enhances the consistency of correct answers within a set on complex reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional parallel LLM inference generates multiple responses independently for a single input prompt, underutilizing computational resources and potentially valuable information across paths.
- Existing methods integrating parallel dependence often funnel computation into a solitary output, rather than leveraging mid-generation interactions to produce multiple interdependent outputs simultaneously.
- Information from one generation path is not shared or leveraged by other concurrent paths, limiting the overall performance ceiling compared to sequential scaling where information is inherently shared.

## Method

- The authors introduce "Bridge" blocks, novel Transformer blocks inserted into existing LLMs that perform attention along the batch axis, enabling cross-sequence information sharing for tokens from the same input.
- ==Bridge blocks== are trained through an optional supervised fine-tuning warm-up followed by Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), adapting the objective to account for inter-sample dependence.
- The architectural addition is lightweight, contributing only 2.8% to 5.1% additional parameters to the base LLM, and is designed to be versatile across different generation widths without retraining.

## Results

- Bridge consistently achieved the highest average Pass@1 accuracy across three DeepSeek-R1-Distill models and 7 math benchmarks, demonstrating average relative improvements of **26%** to **39%** over the original models compared to the next best method.
- The method attained higher G-Pass@kτ values across nearly all τ fractions and models, indicating improved coverage and consistency in generating multiple correct responses within a single set.
- Bridge demonstrated versatility across varying generation widths (outperforming baselines for w > 1) and robust performance when extrapolating to longer sequence lengths, without degrading performance on non-math tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating batched LLM hidden states as a holistic 3D tensor, rather than independent 2D slices, facilitates effective cross-sequence information flow and enhances collective reasoning during parallel generation.
> - Enabling interdependence among parallel generations significantly improves both individual response accuracy (Pass@1) and the consistency/coverage of correct responses within a generated set (G-Pass@kτ).
> - Lightweight architectural modifications (Bridge blocks) can efficiently integrate cross-sequence information sharing with minimal parameter overhead, showcasing a computationally efficient path to improved parallel inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language model (LLM) inference has seen advancements through increased computational resources, which can be primarily scaled along two axes: generation length and the number of generations. Generation length scaling involves methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, where the model generates longer reasoning steps to arrive at a higher-quality single response. This approach ensures that past computations are utilized in subsequent steps for the final output.

In contrast, scaling along the number of generations typically involves Monte Carlo sampling of multiple responses for a single input prompt. This method, while increasing the probability of obtaining a high-quality response, traditionally treats each generated response independently. Consequently, information derived during one generation path is not shared with or leveraged by other parallel generation paths, even when all responses are prompted by the same input. This independent parallel generation approach partitions compute resources and may limit the overall performance ceiling compared to length scaling, where information is inherently shared sequentially.

Existing research has explored integrating parallel dependence for inference. Some methods, such as Hogwild! Inference and Group Think, share key-value caches across generation runs or concatenate outputs of parallel processes to aid in a single decoding thread. These approaches typically funnel parallel computation into a solitary output, which is suitable for generating one high-quality response but not a set of interdependent responses. Other works focus on pruning unpromising generation paths mid-generation to reallocate resources.

This paper addresses the gap in methods that leverage mid-generation interactions to produce *multiple* interdependent outputs simultaneously. It posits that, given a single input prompt, the multiple parallel generations exhibit a uniquely homogeneous structure, where information transfer across sequences could be beneficial. Traditional LLM hidden state operations, such as attention and feedforward blocks, primarily blend information within each sequence slice (S x D) while keeping the batch axis (B) independent. The work proposes to challenge this independence when multiple batch elements correspond to parallel generations of the *same* input, thereby seeking to generalize parallel inference scaling by introducing interdependence among concurrently generated responses. The concept of treating LLM hidden states as 3D tensors (B x S x D) rather than independent 2D slices for information diffusion aligns with principles observed in higher-order tensor operations and axial attention in other domains, like computer vision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that traditional parallel LLM inference, which samples multiple responses (N > 1) for a single input prompt, typically generates these responses independently. This independent generation paradigm leads to an underutilization of computational resources and potentially valuable information generated within one response path that could benefit others. Unlike sequential scaling methods (e.g., Chain-of-Thought), where each token generation builds upon prior computation, independent parallel generations operate in isolation, limiting their collective performance.

The paper's key objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Introduce Interdependent Parallel Generations:** The central objective is to develop a method that enables information sharing among parallel response generations stemming from the same input prompt. This involves rethinking how batched LLM hidden states are processed, moving from independent slices to holistic tensors that facilitate cross-sequence information flow. This aims to unlock a more general mode of parallel scaling that effectively leverages information between sequences.

2. **Enhance Reasoning Performance and Output Quality:** The method aims to improve both individual response quality (measured by accuracy) and the quality of the entire response set (measured by metrics such as coverage and consistency, e.g., G-Pass@kτ). The expectation is that shared information will lead to more accurate individual responses and a higher probability of obtaining multiple correct responses within a set.

3. **Achieve Low Computational Cost:** A significant objective is to integrate this interdependence with minimal architectural changes and parameter overhead. The proposed solution, "Bridge," is designed to be a minor architectural addition, adding only a small percentage (2.8% to 5.1%) of new parameters to existing LLMs, thereby maintaining efficiency.

4. **Ensure Versatility and Generalizability:** The method is intended to be versatile, supporting any generation width (number of parallel responses) without requiring retraining. It should also be robust to discrepancies between training and testing generation widths. Furthermore, it should operate without reliance on specific heuristics or interventions during the generation process and be compatible with various post-generation aggregation techniques.

5. **Address Limitations of Existing Parallel Methods:** While some prior works integrate parallel dependence, they often funnel computation into a single output. This work seeks to extend parallel scaling to allow all N output sequences for one prompt to utilize all available compute and information, producing N distinct, high-quality, and interdependent outputs simultaneously.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to transform parallel inference scaling from a resource-partitioning approach into a resource-pooling mechanism, where information from all parallel paths contributes to the generation of each individual response.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces "Bridge" (Batch reasoning with interdependent generations), an architectural modification to LLMs designed to enable information sharing across tokens that originate from the same prompt within a batch during parallel generation.

**Architectural Design of Bridge Blocks:**
Bridge blocks are implemented as novel Transformer blocks that are inserted into an existing LLM architecture. Specifically, a Bridge block and an input normalization layer are added after each feedforward block.
The core mechanism of a Bridge block is an attention-like operation that differs from standard self-attention in key aspects:
- **Attention Axis:** Unlike standard self-attention which operates along the sequence length axis (S x S) for each independent batch sample, Bridge blocks compute attention along the batch axis (B x B). This means that for a given token position 's', tokens from different sequences within the batch can attend to each other.
- **Masking:** A specific attention mask is applied. This mask prevents attention to tokens from sequences that originate from different prompts or sequences that have already completed their generation. This ensures that information sharing is restricted to relevant parallel generations for the same input and does not interfere with completed sequences.
- **Positional Encoding:** Positional encodings are explicitly omitted in Bridge blocks. This design choice maintains sample position invariance, contributing to the versatility of the method across varying generation widths.
- **Key-Value Cache:** Bridge's design is Markovian; it does not maintain a key-value cache, as it does not attend to previous tokens within the same sequence. It focuses on sharing information *across* sequences at the current timestep.

By operating along the batch axis, Bridge allows the LLM to treat the entire batch of hidden states as a 3D tensor (B x S x D), moving beyond the typical treatment of independent 2D slices. This concept is likened to axial attention, a technique previously used in computer vision for processing multi-dimensional inputs. The architectural addition is designed to be lightweight, contributing only 2.8% to 5.1% additional parameters to the base LLM, depending on the model size.

**Training Pipeline:**
The training of LLMs incorporating Bridge blocks follows a two-stage process:

1. **SFT Warm-up (Optional):**
 - **Motivation:** While Bridge blocks can be initialized to have zero contribution and immediately used with reinforcement learning, an optional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) warm-up stage is introduced to provide more sufficient training and improve downstream performance.
 - **Dataset Creation:** A custom SFT dataset is created by using the *original* LLM to generate multiple responses (e.g., 8) for prompts from a math dataset (e.g., GSM8K). These responses are then filtered for correctness. Only correct reasoning traces and problems with at least one correct response are compiled into the warm-up dataset.
 - **Training:** During this stage, only the newly added Bridge parameters are updated, while all other parameters of the base LLM remain frozen. Training is performed for a few epochs (e.g., 5 epochs), selecting the best checkpoint based on perplexity on a validation set.
 - **P-Match Baseline:** For comparative analysis, a "P-Match" baseline is introduced. This baseline replaces Bridge blocks with 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) of an equivalent parameter count, placed in the same architectural positions. This allows for distinguishing the benefits of Bridge's attention-based interaction from the mere addition of parameters. P-Match models are trained using the same warm-up and RLVR pipeline.

2. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):**
 - **Framework:** The RLVR stage utilizes the GRPO (Group-Reinforced Policy Optimization) algorithm (Shao et al., 2024), specifically a variant with token-level normalization to mitigate length bias (Yu et al., 2025).
 - **Reward Function:** The reward signal for RLVR is based solely on the correctness of the generated response.
 - **Objective Function Adaptation:** The key distinction in the RLVR objective with Bridge is the inherent inter-sample dependence introduced by the Bridge blocks. The ratio and KL divergence terms in the GRPO objective now reflect this dependence, meaning that gradients from all sequences within a group (which can have both positive and negative advantages) are backpropagated through each sequence. This contrasts with the original GRPO assumption of independent trajectories.
 - **Training Details:** The full model (base LLM with warmed-up Bridge blocks) is trained on a dedicated dataset (e.g., DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset) for a set number of gradient steps (e.g., 1000). Specific generation widths (4 or 8) are used during training depending on the model size.

The entire process ensures that Bridge blocks, initially contributing minimally, are progressively trained to facilitate effective information exchange among parallel generations, ultimately enhancing the model's reasoning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence supporting the effectiveness of Bridge in enhancing parallel LLM inference. The findings are categorized into accuracy improvements, set-level evaluations, and various ablations and analyses.

#### 4.1. Accuracy (Pass@1) Improvements

The paper evaluates Bridge's performance using standard Pass@1 accuracy across three DeepSeek-R1-Distill models (DS-Qwen-1.5B, DS-Qwen-7B, and DS-Llama-8B) on 7 math benchmarks.
- **Consistent Superiority:** Bridge consistently achieves the highest average accuracy across all tested models and benchmarks compared to the original model, the original model with only RLVR, and the P-Match baseline.
- **Quantified Gains:** On average, Bridge improves the performance over the original model by 26% (DS-Qwen-1.5B), 39% (DS-Qwen-7B), and 34% (DS-Llama-8B) relative to the next best method. For instance, on DS-Qwen-7B, Bridge achieves an average accuracy of 39.40%, compared to 37.75% for RLVR only and 36.68% for P-Match.
- **Beyond Parameter Addition:** The P-Match baseline, which adds an equivalent number of parameters in the form of MLPs, shows inconsistent improvements over RLVR-only and is generally outperformed by Bridge. This suggests that Bridge's superior performance is attributable to its mechanism of information sharing, rather than merely increased parameter count.
- **Generalizability Across Training/Evaluation Widths:** Models trained with a generation width of 4 (DS-Qwen-7B, DS-Llama-8B) demonstrated stronger evaluation results at width 8 than independent sampling methods, indicating robustness.
- **Model Scaling:** The observed improvements with Bridge are more pronounced for larger models, suggesting potential benefits with further scaling.
- **Non-Math Task Performance:** Despite being trained exclusively on math tasks, Bridge showed no degradation and, in some instances, improved performance on non-math tasks like summarization and logical reasoning (Table 2), indicating a broader applicability without negative transfer.

#### 4.2. Set Evaluations (G-Pass@kτ)

The quality of the generated response set is assessed using the G-Pass@kτ metric, which measures the probability of a certain fraction (τ) of *k* responses being correct.
- **Improved Coverage and Consistency:** Bridge generally achieves higher G-Pass@8τ values across nearly all τ values and models, compared to baseline methods (Figure 4). This indicates that Bridge not only increases the probability of having at least one correct response (coverage, τ → 0) but also enhances the consistency of generating multiple correct responses within a single set (τ > 0).
- **Specific Example:** For DS-Qwen-7B, Bridge increases the rate at which all 8 responses to a single competition math problem are correct (G-Pass@8_1) from 15.0% (RLVR only) to 17.8%.

#### 4.3. Ablations and Analysis

- **Generation Width Versatility:** Bridge's design allows for flexibility in generation width (w). Experiments on DS-Qwen-7B (trained at w=4) demonstrate that Bridge outperforms P-Match for all evaluated widths (w > 1). It generalizes effectively to widths both wider (e.g., w=16) and narrower (e.g., w=1) than its training width. Even at w=1 (equivalent to independent generation), Bridge's average accuracy (29.39%) falls between RLVR only (29.35%) and P-Match (28.32%), suggesting it does not harm independent reasoning.
- **Generation Length Extrapolation:** The method shows robust performance when extrapolating beyond its training sequence length (4096 tokens) to longer lengths (8K, 16K) on MATH-500. Bridge maintains the highest accuracy and improves set-level metrics like coverage and G-Pass@4τ across these extrapolated lengths (Figure 6). For instance, at 16K generation length, Bridge increased the number of sets with all correct answers by 6.0% compared to the next best method.
- **Cold Start vs. Warm-up:** The SFT warm-up procedure, although optional, was found to yield slight performance improvements. While not catastrophic to forgo, warming up Bridge blocks with SFT prior to RLVR is preferred for optimal results (Table 4).
- **Feature Contribution:** Analysis of the norm ratio (output norm / residual norm) indicates that Bridge blocks, despite their significant impact on performance, contribute relatively less to the residual features compared to the MLP blocks in P-Match (Figure 7). This suggests a subtle yet impactful integration into the model's internal representations.
- **Output Stability:** Bridge exhibits improved output stability. On MATH-500, it results in a slightly higher average pairwise BERTScore (90.41% for Bridge vs. 90.06% for RLVR only on DS-Qwen-7B), indicating marginally increased output similarity without collapsing the output distribution. For summarization tasks, Bridge shows the lowest variance in Rouge-1 scores (e.g., 14.44 for CNN/DailyMail vs. 16.14 for DS-Qwen-7B), implying a more consistent quality of generated outputs within a set.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Bridge presents a development in the domain of LLM inference scaling, particularly concerning parallel generation. By enabling interdependent generation, the work addresses a limitation of traditional Monte Carlo sampling, where parallel responses are typically generated in isolation.

The significance of this work can be understood through several points:

- **Enhanced Parallel Inference Scaling:** Bridge allows information to flow between parallel generations, transforming the process from one of partitioned compute resources to one where resources and derived information are pooled. This approach demonstrates improvements in both individual response accuracy and the consistency and coverage of correct responses within a generated set. This suggests that parallel scaling can become a more effective axis for improving LLM performance on complex tasks.
- **Architectural Innovation:** The concept of treating batched LLM hidden states as holistic 3D tensors and applying attention-like operations along the batch axis represents an architectural innovation. This design allows for cross-sequence information exchange without significantly increasing model parameters or computational overhead during inference, as demonstrated by the low additional parameter count (2.8%-5.1%).
- **Versatility and Generalizability:** The method's robustness across various generation widths (including widths different from training) and its ability to extrapolate to longer sequence lengths contributes to its practical utility. Its compatibility with existing post-generation aggregation techniques also broadens its applicability within current LLM pipelines.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays a foundation for several future research directions. These include exploring the impact of Bridge blocks during pretraining or mid-training stages of LLMs, integrating the method with other global objectives like Pass@k training, and investigating its benefits in other modalities or tasks where output homogeneity levels might vary. Such explorations could further refine the understanding and application of interdependent generation.
- **Improved Reliability of LLM Outputs:** By enhancing the consistency of correct responses within a set (higher G-Pass@kτ scores), Bridge could contribute to building more reliable LLM applications, particularly in domains requiring high accuracy and redundancy, such as code generation, scientific problem-solving, or complex reasoning tasks where multiple attempts are often aggregated.

Overall, Bridge offers a mechanism for LLMs to leverage the collective intelligence of parallel inference paths, which could contribute to more robust and accurate language model outputs.
