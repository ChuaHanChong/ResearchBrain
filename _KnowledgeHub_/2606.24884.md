# InSight: Self-Guided Skill Acquisition via Steerable VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> INSIGHT introduces a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to autonomously acquire new manipulation skills by identifying missing primitive actions and generating their own training data. The system enables robots to continually expand their learned skill vocabulary, achieving high success rates in novel tasks while retaining prior capabilities and improving execution efficiency.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have capabilities bounded by their initial training data, requiring extensive and costly human demonstrations for each new skill.
- Real-world robot skill acquisition via traditional reinforcement learning is often impractical due to high sample complexity, need for dense rewards, and safety constraints.
- Existing approaches improve robot control or enable composition of predefined skills, but they do not provide a mechanism for VLAs to autonomously learn and integrate genuinely new primitive actions into their underlying policy.

## Method

- A two-stage framework that first trains a steerable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of executing primitive-level language commands.
- An ==automatic primitive segmentation pipeline== uses a VLM to label human teleoperated demonstrations, enabling primitive-level steering for the VLA.
- A VLM-guided acquisition loop identifies missing primitive skills ("primitive gaps") for novel tasks, autonomously parameterizes and executes attempts for these primitives, and integrates successful rollouts into the VLA's training set through retraining.

## Results

- Achieved **75%** success on a block-flip task in simulation after acquiring 246 new primitive rollouts, outperforming an RL baseline which recorded **0%** success.
- Successfully acquired real-world primitives like "twist cap open" (**92%** success) and "pour beans" (**96%** success), demonstrating compositional generalization with **80%** end-to-end success on a 14-primitive 'twist-then-pour' task.
- Showcased efficient autonomous data acquisition (e.g., 20 successful 'twist' primitives in **39.7** minutes) and significantly faster execution times compared to zero-shot VLM planning approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Manipulation skills are inherently compositional, implying that VLAs can be designed to learn and combine fundamental primitive actions.
> - A Vision-Language Model (VLM) can serve as an active orchestrator for autonomous skill acquisition, identifying missing primitives, guiding data collection, and facilitating their integration.
> - Directly embedding newly acquired primitives into the VLA policy results in more efficient and reliable task execution compared to relying solely on test-time VLM planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressing with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which learn control policies from visual observations and language instructions. However, a recurring limitation of these models is that their capabilities are inherently bounded by the skills present in their initial training datasets. Acquiring new manipulation skills typically necessitates substantial human effort, including collecting new demonstrations and fine-tuning policies, a process that is both costly and time-consuming. Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) methods in simulation often require thousands of trials, and their application to real-world scenarios is frequently constrained by sample complexity and safety considerations.

Recent advancements have explored "steerable policies," where language commands enable finer-grained control over robot actions. Methods like STEER and Steerable Policies have demonstrated the utility of dense language relabeling for exposing expressive low-level control interfaces or guiding behavior at test time. Similarly, VLS employs Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to synthesize reward functions for guiding pretrained diffusion policies towards out-of-distribution configurations without explicit retraining. While these approaches enhance control, they generally treat the underlying set of available primitives as fixed, limiting the robot's ability to genuinely learn new fundamental actions.

Another significant area involves leveraging large language models (LLMs) and VLMs for high-level semantic guidance in robotics. Approaches such as SayCan, VoxPoser, and Code-as-Policies use these models as planners to compose pre-trained or pre-defined skills or generate code for execution. Hi Robot utilizes hierarchical VLAs to interpret complex instructions. These methods expand the robot's functional repertoire through test-time reasoning and composition of existing skills but do not fundamentally update or expand the learned policy's intrinsic skill vocabulary. The robot may perform a novel task, but the underlying policy remains unchanged, meaning newly composed behaviors are not integrated as reusable, learned capabilities.

Autonomous skill acquisition has also been explored, often involving foundation model-generated rewards, automated demonstration data, or language-labeled trajectories to reduce human supervision. However, these methods typically focus on acquiring skills at the level of full tasks or entire trajectories.

This work, INSIGHT, distinguishes itself by addressing the challenge of autonomous skill acquisition at the *primitive-action level*. It integrates the concept of steerability with a mechanism for continually expanding the VLA's primitive vocabulary. Unlike previous methods that maintain a fixed primitive set or use VLMs only for test-time planning, INSIGHT positions the VLM as an active agent in a data acquisition loop. This loop identifies missing primitives, generates training data for them through autonomous interaction, and integrates these new primitives into the VLA's learned policy through retraining. This approach provides a framework for continually expanding the robot's reusable skill set, moving beyond a fixed set of capabilities to enable lifelong, open-world skill learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable autonomous skill acquisition for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby overcoming the limitation that their capabilities are confined to the skills present in their initial training data. The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties and costs associated with traditional robot skill acquisition.

Specifically, the authors identify several driving motivations:

1. **High Cost of Skill Acquisition:** Teaching robots new manipulation skills typically involves substantial human effort for collecting demonstrations and subsequently fine-tuning policies for each new task. This process is time-consuming and expensive.
2. **Bounded Capabilities of Current VLAs:** Existing VLA models, despite their advancements in general-purpose manipulation, are limited by the scope of their training data. If a robot encounters a task requiring a skill not present in its initial dataset (e.g., a Mars rover trained for scooping needing to sweep dust off solar panels), it will likely fail.
3. **Impracticality of Real-World Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While RL offers a path to acquiring skills through interaction, its application in real-world robotics is often impractical due to high sample complexity, the need for dense rewards, and safety constraints.
4. **Compositional Nature of Manipulation Skills:** The authors hypothesize that manipulation skills are largely compositional, meaning new skills frequently reuse existing "primitive" actions in novel combinations. For instance, sweeping and scooping might share "approach" and "lowering" primitives but differ in a "lateral pushing" primitive. This suggests that VLAs might already implicitly encode reusable primitives, but these are not easily steerable or discoverable when needed for new tasks.
5. **Need for Active Skill Gap Identification and Acquisition:** An efficient method for acquiring new skills requires not only the ability to execute primitives (which steerable VLAs can achieve) but also the capacity to recognize *which* primitives are missing for a novel task. Furthermore, there is a need to autonomously generate successful demonstrations of these missing primitives and integrate them into the VLA's learned policy, rather than merely planning with a fixed set of pre-existing skills.

To address these motivations, the research outlines two primary contributions:

- **Development of an Automatic Primitive Segmentation Pipeline:** This pipeline aims to decompose teleoperated human demonstrations into labeled primitive trajectories without requiring manual annotation. This enables the fine-tuning of a VLA to be steerable at the primitive-action level.
- **Establishment of a VLM-Guided Primitive Acquisition Loop:** This loop is designed to identify "primitive gaps" (missing primitives) when a novel task is presented. It then autonomously attempts and collects successful rollouts of these missing primitives using VLM-proposed low-level control parameters. Finally, it labels, stores, and integrates these successful demonstrations into the VLA's training set through retraining, thereby expanding the VLA's intrinsic skill capabilities.

These objectives collectively aim to create a framework that enables robots to continually learn and expand their skill sets in a self-guided manner, analogous to how humans learn new capabilities through targeted practice.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

INSIGHT operates through a two-stage framework: (1) training a steerable VLA on automatically segmented primitives, and (2) autonomous skill acquisition orchestrated by a VLM.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: VLA with Steerable Primitives

This stage focuses on preparing a VLA to respond to primitive-level language commands.

- **Primitive and Skill Definition:**
 - A **skill** is defined as a target capability described by a language instruction (e.g., "unscrew the bottle cap and pour the contents").
 - A **plan** is the sequence of primitives generated by a VLM planner to complete a skill.
 - A **primitive** is a reusable action segment that the VLA executes when conditioned on its language label. Each primitive has a single dominant motion mode (e.g., translation or rotation along an axis, or a gripper transition) and is associated with a natural-language label (e.g., "move gripper to the yellow bottle", "lift upward", "twist", "pour"). Primitives are executed end-to-end until a termination signal.
 - The **primitive vocabulary (V)** is the set of primitive labels for which the VLA has been trained. A **primitive gap** is any primitive required by a plan that is not present in V.

- **Automatic Primitive Segmentation:**
 - Human teleoperated demonstrations of known tasks are collected.
 - A VLM is used to generate an ordered sequence of expected primitives for a given task description. The VLM is provided with example primitives to guide the granularity of its output.
 - The demonstrations are segmented by aligning the VLM-generated primitive plan with low-level robot state changes. Specifically, boundaries are identified at gripper open/close transitions using gripper command velocity.
 - For each frame, end-effector pose, derived motion magnitudes (in XY and Z axes), and a dominant-axis tag (e.g., {xy, z, rxy, rz}) are extracted. These signals are passed to the VLM to match each frame with its corresponding primitive name. This process ensures automatic labeling without manual annotation.
 - The segmented, primitive-labeled trajectories are used to fine-tune a pretrained VLA (specifically, π0.5 with a Gemma-2B backbone and Gemma-300M action expert, fine-tuned using LoRA). Each primitive segment is treated as a separate training episode, conditioned on its language label.
 - A learned "progress channel" in the action space (values [0, 1)) is added for each primitive, supervised with the normalized timestep within that primitive. This channel provides a primitive-level termination signal (e.g., when progress exceeds a threshold like 0.95).

#### 3.2. Stage 2: VLM-Guided Skill Acquisition

This stage describes how INSIGHT autonomously expands the VLA's skill set when encountering novel tasks requiring missing primitives.

- **Primitive Gap Identification:**
 - When presented with a novel task, a VLM first decomposes the task into a sequence of primitives.
 - This sequence is then compared against the VLA's current primitive vocabulary (V). Any primitive in the plan that is not in V is flagged as a "primitive gap."
 - The VLM planner is constrained to propose single-axis motions for each primitive gap (e.g., one translation or one rotation along a single axis in one direction). This means complex motions are broken down into multiple distinct primitive gaps.

- **Primitive Gap Parameterization:**
 - For each identified primitive gap, a pre-execution VLM call analyzes the current scene (using exterior and wrist camera images) and proposes specific low-level control parameters. These parameters include an axis of motion (translational: {dx, dy, dz} in the base frame; rotational: {drx, dry, drz} in the gripper's local frame) and a signed magnitude (in meters for translation, degrees for rotation).
 - For primitive gaps that are part of a chained sequence within a single rollout, the parameters of prior gaps are passed to the VLM to ensure consistency in axis and magnitude (e.g., for "pour forward" and then "tilt back upright").

- **Rollout Execution:**
 - During a plan execution, known primitives are executed by the steerable VLA.
 - Primitive gaps are executed by a low-level controller using the VLM-proposed axis and signed magnitude. The controller drives the arm along the specified axis until a termination signal fires (e.g., completion check or auto-advance threshold).

- **Success Evaluation and Data Integration:**
 - After the robot attempts the full task (skill `s`), a post-plan VLM oracle compares images of the scene before and after the execution to judge overall task success.
 - If the full task `s` is deemed successful, the rollouts corresponding to the newly acquired primitive(s) within `s` are automatically labeled, stored, and added to the VLA's training dataset.
 - The system includes a VLM-based "completion check" that periodically queries whether a current primitive is complete. This is used for termination, especially when executing from out-of-distribution (OOD) initial states, where the learned progress signal might degrade.

- **Retraining:**
 - After a sufficient number (n) of successful rollouts for a new primitive are acquired, the VLA is retrained. This fine-tuning process incorporates the existing primitive vocabulary V along with the newly acquired primitive(s).
 - The retrained model is then capable of executing the full composed task autonomously, with the VLM reverting to its role as a high-level planner for sequencing these now-known primitives.

This methodology aims to create a closed-loop system where robots can not only understand and execute known primitives but also actively identify, learn, and integrate new primitive actions into their operational policies, thus enabling continuous learning and adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of INSIGHT spanned simulation and real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating its capabilities in skill acquisition, out-of-distribution (OOD) execution, and compositional reuse without additional human demonstrations for target skills.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Block Flipping from Pick-and-Place Demonstrations:**
 - **Objective:** Acquire a `rotate-block` primitive from human demonstrations consisting only of `pick-and-place` actions. The task required flipping a block upright.
 - **Result:** INSIGHT achieved a 75% success rate on the block-flip task after acquiring 246 `rotate-block` primitive rollouts, collected over 479 total attempts. In contrast, an RL baseline (Soft Actor-Critic, SAC) with a comparable compute budget did not complete a single flip (0% success), though it learned to reach (23%) and grasp (10%) the block. This indicates the sample efficiency of INSIGHT's primitive acquisition.
- **Drawer Closing from Drawer Opening Demonstrations:**
 - **Objective:** Acquire a `push drawer closed` primitive when the VLA was initially trained only on `drawer-opening` demonstrations. This task presented an OOD initial state (drawer starting open).
 - **Result:** INSIGHT successfully acquired the missing `push drawer closed` primitive. It used a VLM completion check to manage imperfect primitive termination signals when operating from an OOD initial state. Across 82 episodes, 70 successful `close-drawer` primitives were acquired. After retraining, the unified VLA achieved 100% success over 25 evaluation trials for closing the drawer, while retaining its ability to open it. Incorrect axis selection was identified as the dominant failure mode during primitive acquisition.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

A VLA was initially trained on primitives segmented from 50 human `pick-and-place` demonstrations (top and side grasps). INSIGHT was then evaluated on tasks requiring new primitives: bottle twisting, pouring, and a compositional task combining these. Comparisons were made against CaP-X (a Code-as-Policies-style zero-shot baseline that does not expand the policy) and π0.5 (the base VLA fine-tuned only on human demos).

- **Bottle Twisting and Pouring (Individual Skill Acquisition):**
 - **Twist Cap Open:** INSIGHT achieved 92% end-to-end success. CaP-X achieved 32%, and the π0.5 baseline (lacking twisting demos) achieved 0%.
 - **Pour Beans into Bowl:** INSIGHT achieved 96% end-to-end success. CaP-X achieved 16%, and π0.5 achieved 0%.
 - **Per-primitive reliability:** INSIGHT maintained high success rates across individual primitives, contributing to high end-to-end success.
- **Compositional Reuse: Twist-then-Pour Task:**
 - **Objective:** Execute a long-horizon task requiring chaining primitives from two separately acquired skills (`twist` and `pour`), without any end-to-end human demonstrations of the combined task. The task involved 14 primitives (grasp from top, twist open, regrasp from side, pour, etc.).
 - **Result:** INSIGHT achieved 80% end-to-end success on this complex 14-primitive task. CaP-X achieved 4%. This demonstrates compositional generalization and the ability to combine newly acquired primitives with base skills.
- **Efficiency of Acquisition and Execution:**
 - **Acquisition Cost:** INSIGHT demonstrated efficient autonomous data acquisition, obtaining 20 successful `twist` primitives in 23 trials (39.7 minutes wall-clock time) and 20 successful `pour` primitives in 31 trials (85.3 minutes wall-clock time).
 - **Execution Time:** INSIGHT consistently spent substantially less wall-clock time per trial (VLM call latency + robot execution) compared to CaP-X for both `twist` (39.7s total for INSIGHT vs. 124.1s for CaP-X) and `pour` (85.3s total for INSIGHT vs. 206.9s for CaP-X). This indicates that integrating skills into the VLA policy leads to more efficient execution than zero-shot reasoning at test time.
- **Base Skill Retention:**
 - **Result:** The unified VLA, after incorporating the newly acquired `twist` and `pour` primitives, retained 100% success on the original `top-` and `side-pick-and-place` base skills (15 trials each). This suggests that INSIGHT's data-centric retraining approach effectively integrates new skills without degrading prior capabilities.
- **Sweeping from Scooping Demonstrations (Contact-Rich, Non-Prehensile Motion):**
 - **Objective:** Acquire a `sweeping` primitive from `scooping` demonstrations, extending beyond grasping to contact-rich, non-prehensile motions. Both skills share `approach` and `lowering` primitives but differ in the final contact motion (descent for scooping, lateral push for sweeping).
 - **Result:** INSIGHT autonomously acquired the `sweeping` primitive by identifying the `lateral-push` as the missing component. It achieved 5/5 success in evaluation trials, indicating its applicability to a broader range of manipulation types.

In summary, the findings indicate that INSIGHT provides a robust and efficient framework for autonomous skill acquisition in VLAs. It enhances robot capabilities by allowing them to learn new primitives from limited human demonstrations, generalize to OOD states, compose learned primitives for long-horizon tasks, and retain existing skills, all while demonstrating improved execution efficiency compared to zero-shot policy approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The INSIGHT framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in addressing the challenge of continually expanding robot capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several dimensions:

1. **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Bounded Capabilities:** A core limitation of current VLA models is their reliance on extensive, task-specific human demonstrations, which confines their capabilities to the training data. INSIGHT directly addresses this by enabling autonomous acquisition of new primitives without requiring human demonstrations for the *target* skill. This suggests a pathway to reduce the bottleneck of data collection, allowing robots to adapt to novel tasks in dynamic environments.
2. **Facilitating Lifelong and Continual Learning:** The framework allows for the persistent expansion of a VLA's skill vocabulary. By integrating newly acquired primitives into the policy through retraining, INSIGHT moves towards true lifelong learning, where robots can progressively accumulate and retain new capabilities. This contrasts with methods that plan over fixed primitive sets or rely on test-time reasoning without updating the underlying policy. The ability to retain existing skills while acquiring new ones is crucial for robots operating in evolving environments.
3. **Enhanced Autonomy and Adaptability:** By enabling robots to autonomously identify missing skills, generate their own training data, and integrate these skills, INSIGHT increases the level of robot autonomy. This has implications for deploying robots in remote, unstructured, or hazardous environments where human intervention is impractical (e.g., space exploration, disaster response), as illustrated by the Mars rover analogy. Robots could adapt to unforeseen challenges by learning necessary new behaviors on demand.
4. **Improved Efficiency in Skill Acquisition and Execution:** The empirical results demonstrate that INSIGHT is more sample-efficient than reinforcement learning baselines for acquiring new skills. Furthermore, by embedding learned primitives directly into the VLA policy, INSIGHT achieves substantially faster execution times compared to zero-shot VLM planning methods like Code-as-Policies, which incur significant latency due to repeated VLM calls. This efficiency is critical for real-world robot deployment.
5. **Compositional Generalization:** The ability to compose individually acquired primitives into complex, long-horizon tasks (e.g., twist-then-pour) signifies a step towards more general-purpose robot intelligence. It suggests that robots can build a repertoire of fundamental actions that are reusable across a wide array of novel and intricate tasks, without needing end-to-end demonstrations for every new composition.
6. **Broader Applicability to Manipulation Types:** The demonstration of acquiring `sweeping` from `scooping` indicates that INSIGHT is not limited to prehensile, object-centric manipulation but can extend to contact-rich and non-prehensile motions, thereby broadening the scope of skills that robots can autonomously learn.

While the work identifies limitations such as the restriction to single-axis motions for primitive acquisition and the need for human environment resets, it lays a practical foundation for advancing continual skill acquisition in VLA policies. Future work leveraging VLM-generated waypoints, trajectory optimization, or real-to-sim-to-real pipelines could further expand the complexity and robustness of acquirable primitives, enhancing the framework's overall impact on robotic autonomy.
