# Infrastructure-Centric World Models: Bridging Temporal Depth and Spatial Breadth for Roadside Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents the vision for Infrastructure-centric World Models (I-WM), a framework designed to leverage fixed roadside sensors for a comprehensive understanding of traffic dynamics. I-WM aims to provide "temporal depth" to complement ego-vehicle models' "spatial breadth," enabling system-level optimization for adaptive traffic control and proactive safety analysis.

## Problem

- Existing world models for autonomous driving are ego-centric, leading to limitations in global traffic situational awareness, persistent monitoring, and system-level optimization.
- Current roadside perception systems primarily focus on instantaneous object detection and tracking, lacking deeper understanding of underlying traffic rules, physical constraints, and causal relationships.
- Neither vehicle-borne sensors (spatial breadth) nor roadside sensors (temporal depth) alone can provide a complete model of traffic dynamics, highlighting a crucial spatio-temporal complementarity gap.

## Method

- Develop ==Infrastructure-centric World Models== (**I-WM**) using a ==dual-layer architecture== that separates annotation-free, modular perception from end-to-end, ==self-supervised generative world modeling==.
- Integrate ==multi-modal roadside sensor data== (LiDAR, camera, 4D radar, SPaT) in a phased strategy, leveraging each modality's strengths for robust and comprehensive ==3D/4D scene representation==.
- Implement a ==three-tier data strategy== combining proprietary roadside recordings, public ==V2X== datasets, and simulation-generated synthetic data to train the world model, including rare event scenarios.

## Results

- Conceptualized **I-WM** as a novel paradigm for driving world models, bridging the spatio-temporal understanding gap by integrating infrastructure-centric and ego-vehicle perspectives.
- Designed a practical dual-layer architecture that supports annotation-free perception and self-supervised generative world modeling, addressing data scarcity for roadside deployment.
- Outlined a three-phase research roadmap for **I-WM** development, progressing from generative scene understanding to physics-informed predictive dynamics and collaborative V2X world models, including the introduction of **Infrastructure VLA (I-VLA)**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Roadside sensors offer crucial "temporal depth" by continuously observing specific locations over long periods, allowing accumulation of comprehensive behavioral distributions and rare events, complementing vehicle-borne sensors' "spatial breadth."
> - A dual-layer architecture enables scalable roadside deployment by utilizing an annotation-free perception layer (Layer 1) to feed structured, uncertainty-annotated observations to a self-supervised generative world model (Layer 2).
> - Embedding physics-informed constraints into latent space dynamics and propagating quality-aware uncertainty are essential for creating robust, controllable, and reliable predictive models for intelligent infrastructure and counterfactual reasoning.
