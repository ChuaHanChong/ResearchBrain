# Infrastructure-Centric World Models: Bridging Temporal Depth and Spatial Breadth for Roadside Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents the vision for Infrastructure-centric World Models (I-WM), a framework designed to leverage fixed roadside sensors for a comprehensive understanding of traffic dynamics. I-WM aims to provide "temporal depth" to complement ego-vehicle models' "spatial breadth," enabling system-level optimization for adaptive traffic control and proactive safety analysis.

## Problem

- Existing world models for autonomous driving are ego-centric, leading to limitations in global traffic situational awareness, persistent monitoring, and system-level optimization.
- Current roadside perception systems primarily focus on instantaneous object detection and tracking, lacking deeper understanding of underlying traffic rules, physical constraints, and causal relationships.
- Neither vehicle-borne sensors (spatial breadth) nor roadside sensors (temporal depth) alone can provide a complete model of traffic dynamics, highlighting a crucial spatio-temporal complementarity gap.

## Method

- Develop ==Infrastructure-centric World Models== (**I-WM**) using a ==dual-layer architecture== that separates annotation-free, modular perception from end-to-end, ==self-supervised generative world modeling==.
- Integrate ==multi-modal roadside sensor data== (LiDAR, camera, 4D radar, SPaT) in a phased strategy, leveraging each modality's strengths for robust and comprehensive ==3D/4D scene representation==.
- Implement a ==three-tier data strategy== combining proprietary roadside recordings, public ==V2X== datasets, and simulation-generated synthetic data to train the world model, including rare event scenarios.

## Results

- Conceptualized **I-WM** as a novel paradigm for driving world models, bridging the spatio-temporal understanding gap by integrating infrastructure-centric and ego-vehicle perspectives.
- Designed a practical dual-layer architecture that supports annotation-free perception and self-supervised generative world modeling, addressing data scarcity for roadside deployment.
- Outlined a three-phase research roadmap for **I-WM** development, progressing from generative scene understanding to physics-informed predictive dynamics and collaborative V2X world models, including the introduction of **Infrastructure VLA (I-VLA)**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Roadside sensors offer crucial "temporal depth" by continuously observing specific locations over long periods, allowing accumulation of comprehensive behavioral distributions and rare events, complementing vehicle-borne sensors' "spatial breadth."
> - A dual-layer architecture enables scalable roadside deployment by utilizing an annotation-free perception layer (Layer 1) to feed structured, uncertainty-annotated observations to a self-supervised generative world model (Layer 2).
> - Embedding physics-informed constraints into latent space dynamics and propagating quality-aware uncertainty are essential for creating robust, controllable, and reliable predictive models for intelligent infrastructure and counterfactual reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The emergence of world models, defined as generative AI systems designed to simulate environmental evolution, represents a significant development in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly for autonomous driving. Industry initiatives like the Waymo World Model and NVIDIA Cosmos, alongside academic contributions such as HERMES, ResWorld, and DriveVLA-W0, exemplify the ongoing efforts in this domain. A common characteristic across existing driving world models is their adoption of an ego-vehicle perspective, which models the environment from the viewpoint of a single autonomous agent.

This paper identifies a critical gap in this landscape: the underexplored potential of infrastructure-centric world models. It positions its proposed Infrastructure-centric World Models (I-WM) as a complementary capability that leverages the unique advantages of fixed roadside sensors. A central argument is the fundamental spatio-temporal complementarity between roadside and vehicle-borne sensing platforms. Roadside sensors are described as excelling at "temporal depth," accumulating extensive behavioral distributions and rare, safety-critical events over long periods at specific locations. In contrast, vehicle-borne sensors are characterized by their "spatial breadth," sampling diverse scenes across large road networks but offering only transient observations at any single point. The paper posits that a complete model of traffic dynamics cannot be learned by either platform alone, thereby necessitating a framework that integrates both perspectives.

The work builds upon and aims to extend several active research areas:
- **World Models for Autonomous Driving:** It contrasts with existing ego-centric models by proposing a "bird's-eye, multi-sensor, persistent viewpoint" that addresses occlusions, provides persistent monitoring, and enables system-level optimization.
- **4D Occupancy Forecasting:** The paper aims to adapt existing 4D occupancy prediction techniques, such as those in OccWorld and Drive-OccWorld, to the roadside context, where the fixed, elevated viewpoint can facilitate more complete occupancy estimation.
- **Neural Scene Reconstruction and LiDAR Generation:** It intends to leverage advancements in realistic sensor data generation (e.g., NeRF-LiDAR, LiDAR Diffusion Models) but specifically targets roadside multi-modal outputs (LiDAR, camera, 4D radar) with an emphasis on quality-aware uncertainty annotation.
- **Physics-Informed Deep Learning and RL-Based Traffic Control:** The proposed I-WM framework integrates concepts from physics-informed neural networks (PINNs, PIDL) to embed traffic flow theory into latent dynamics and utilizes reinforcement learning (RL) for traffic signal control, transforming the world model into a "mental simulator" for signal optimization.
- **Cooperative Perception and V2X Communication:** While current V2X research primarily focuses on perception-level fusion, this paper proposes a deeper form of collaboration through "world model alignment," where latent representations of world models are shared between infrastructure and vehicles.
- **Broader AI Paradigms:**
 - **LeCun’s JEPA Framework:** The I-WM design aligns with JEPA's principle of predicting in abstract representation spaces (latent dynamics), but bridges this with explicit 3D/4D geometric understanding necessary for safety metrics.
 - **Li Fei-Fei’s Spatial Intelligence:** The paper positions roadside infrastructure as an ideal platform for developing spatial intelligence in transportation due to its comprehensive and persistent 3D scene understanding.
 - **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** It introduces the novel concept of Infrastructure VLA (I-VLA), recontextualizing vision, language, and action for roadside systems (multi-sensor perception, natural language traffic commands, and infrastructure control decisions).

This research seeks to fill a significant gap by proposing a unified infrastructure-centric world model with quality-aware uncertainty propagation and V2X latent sharing, leveraging the temporal-depth advantage of fixed roadside deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research vision is to develop Infrastructure-centric World Models (I-WM) that provide a fundamentally distinct and complementary perspective to existing ego-vehicle world models. The overarching goal is to enable intelligent infrastructure that does not merely observe traffic but actively understands and anticipates its dynamics.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations and unmet needs in the current state of autonomous driving and intelligent transportation systems:

- **Ego-Vehicle Perspective Limitations:** Current world models, such as Waymo's, are inherently ego-centric, reasoning about the immediate surroundings of a single agent. This approach struggles with occlusions, lacks persistent monitoring across all traffic participants, and cannot achieve system-level optimization (e.g., traffic signal control).
- **Lack of Deep Scene Understanding in Roadside Systems:** Existing roadside perception systems primarily focus on instantaneous object detection and tracking. They generally lack a deeper understanding of underlying "rules of the world," including physical constraints, causal relationships, and intent prediction, which are crucial for proactive safety and control.
- **Leveraging Spatio-Temporal Complementarity:** A core motivation is to harness the distinct strengths of roadside and vehicle-based sensors. Roadside sensors offer "temporal depth" by continuously observing specific locations over long periods, allowing the accumulation of complete behavioral distributions, including rare safety-critical events. Vehicle-borne sensors, conversely, provide "spatial breadth" by sampling diverse scenes across extensive road networks. The paper argues that combining these complementary viewpoints is essential for a complete model of traffic dynamics.
- **Enabling System-Level Applications:** I-WM is motivated by its potential to enable capabilities beyond single-vehicle autonomy, such as predictive analytics for entire traffic ecosystems, proactive safety interventions, and cognitive V2X collaboration. This includes applications like adaptive traffic signal control and comprehensive urban planning.
- **Data Efficiency for Rare Events:** Roadside deployments offer a data-efficient means to observe a high volume of unique vehicle interactions and rare conflict events, which are difficult to accumulate from transient vehicle perspectives. This is particularly relevant for training safety-critical applications.
- **Scalability and Annotation-Free Deployment:** For widespread deployment, roadside perception systems require methods that operate without extensive manual annotation, as each new intersection presents unique data characteristics. The proposed dual-layer architecture is designed to meet this practical demand.

The research vision outlines three phased objectives:

- **Phase I: Generative Scene Understanding (Years 1–2):** To address data scarcity by developing high-fidelity reconstruction and generation capabilities, incorporating quality-aware uncertainty propagation. This phase focuses on learning to predict future scene states from past observations in a self-supervised manner.
- **Phase II: Physics-Informed Predictive Dynamics (Years 2–3):** To instill "common sense," causality, and controllability into the world model. This involves learning latent space dynamics constrained by physical laws, enabling multi-agent counterfactual reasoning, and supporting natural language-conditioned scene editing with infrastructure-specific controls.
- **Phase III: Collaborative World Models for V2X (Years 3–5):** To achieve "cognitive alignment" between infrastructure and vehicles. This phase focuses on developing mechanisms for aligning infrastructure and vehicle world models in a shared latent space, facilitating V2X latent sharing, and applying multi-agent game theory.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Infrastructure-centric World Model (I-WM) is structured around a dual-layer architecture designed to balance practical deployment constraints with advanced generative AI capabilities.

#### 3.1. Dual-Layer I-WM Architecture

The architecture separates concerns into two distinct layers:

- **Layer 1: Modular Perception (Data Engine):** This layer is designed to be unsupervised, annotation-free, and adaptable to specific sites. It processes multi-modal inputs (LiDAR, Camera, 4D Radar, SPaT data) to produce structured, uncertainty-annotated observations.
 - **Components:**
 - **FRGB3D:** Performs background modeling to generate static scene priors and foreground segments. This leverages the temporal depth of roadside data to establish a baseline for "normal" scene behavior.
 - **MulDet3D:** Carries out multi-objective 3D object detection, propagating quality-aware uncertainty alongside detections.
 - **MulTrack3D:** Implements consensus-based tracking to generate robust trajectories for dynamic agents, complete with quality scores.
 - **Outputs:** This layer serves as a data engine for the world model, providing high-quality, structured representations such as 3D bounding boxes, trajectories, and static scene priors, all annotated with uncertainty or reliability scores. The annotation-free nature of this layer is deemed crucial for scalable roadside deployment where labeled data for each new intersection is typically scarce.

- **Layer 2: Generative World Model (End-to-End, Self-Supervised):** This layer builds upon the structured inputs provided by Layer 1, employing end-to-end generative architectures. It is trained through self-supervised objectives, predicting future scene states from past observations without requiring human-labeled ground truth beyond the perception layer's outputs.
 - **Techniques:** This layer incorporates diffusion-based 4D scene generation, autoregressive occupancy forecasting, and self-supervised latent dynamics to learn scene evolution, enable counterfactual reasoning, and perform future prediction. The separation between perception and world modeling is a deliberate design choice, allowing for the practical deployment of annotation-free perception while still benefiting from end-to-end learning over rich structured representations for the world model.

#### 3.2. Phased Multi-Modal Sensor Strategy

The I-WM framework adopts a phased approach to sensor integration, reflecting both deployment readiness and the value each modality contributes to world modeling:

- **Core Modalities (Phase I):**
 - **Multi-LiDAR:** Forms the geometric backbone, providing precise 3D structure essential for safety metrics (e.g., Time-to-Collision, Post-Encroachment Time).
 - **RGB Camera:** Complements LiDAR by providing dense appearance and semantic information (e.g., lane markings, traffic signs, pedestrian intent cues). The initial phase is LiDAR-centric, with camera integration progressing as deployment sites are augmented.
- **Extended Modalities (Phase I-II):**
 - **4D Radar:** Offers Doppler velocity measurements, crucial for dynamic prediction, and provides robustness in adverse weather conditions where LiDAR performance may degrade. This contributes to the quality-aware framework.
 - **Signal Phase and Timing (SPaT) data:** Provides causal information about traffic signal states, enabling more accurate counterfactual reasoning and informing closed-loop signal optimization.
- **Forward-Looking Modalities (Phase II-III):**
 - **Event Cameras:** Capture microsecond-level temporal dynamics for fast-changing events (e.g., sudden braking).
 - **Environmental Sensors:** Provide contextual data (e.g., temperature, precipitation, road surface condition) for weather-aware behavioral modeling.

#### 3.3. Data Acquisition Strategy

A three-tier data strategy is proposed to train the infrastructure-centric world model:

- **Tier 1: Proprietary Multi-LiDAR Roadside Data:** This includes existing deployments that provide synchronized multi-LiDAR point clouds, quality-aware annotations, 3D bounding boxes, trajectories, and long-term recordings necessary for temporal priors.
- **Tier 2: Public Roadside and V2X Datasets:** Leveraging established datasets such as DAIR-V2X, V2X-Seq, TUMTraf-V2X/I, V2X-Real, and V2XScenes for broader coverage and diverse traffic conditions.
- **Tier 3: Simulation-Generated Synthetic Data:** Utilizing CARLA-based roadside simulation to generate data with perfect ground truth and controllable scenarios, including rare events. The trained I-WM itself can also generate additional synthetic training data through self-play.

#### 3.4. Technical Strategy Across Phases

The I-WM is primarily positioned as a **3D/4D Scene Generation World Model** (Paradigm B), augmented with elements from **Decision/Planning-Oriented World Models** (Paradigm C) for closed-loop control.

- **Phase I (Generative Scene Understanding):**
 - **Foundation Model Adaptation:** Adapting open-source 3D/4D foundation models like DynamicCity's HexPlane VAE and DiT diffusion framework from ego-vehicle to roadside multi-LiDAR domain. Fine-tuning uses structured data generated by Layer 1 in a self-supervised manner.
 - **4D Occupancy Forecasting:** Developing autoregressive prediction algorithms for volumetric occupancy, leveraging the complete occupancy field offered by the roadside view.
 - **Quality-Aware Synthetic Data:** Extending representations like HexPlane with uncertainty channels to encode per-voxel reliability, distinguishing this from existing models that generate data without uncertainty.
- **Phase II (Physics-Informed Predictive Dynamics):**
 - **Latent Space Dynamics:** Learning a transition function `z_t+1 = T(z_t, a_t, η)` in a compressed latent space, with physical constraints (e.g., traffic flow theory, vehicle dynamics) embedded as loss functions (`L_physics`).
 - **Multi-Agent Counterfactual Reasoning:** Enabling "what-if" analyses for multiple agents, leveraging the temporal depth of roadside data to compare events against empirical behavioral distributions.
 - **Language-Conditioned Scene Editing:** Allowing natural language commands to modify scenario parameters, including infrastructure-specific controls like signal phase optimization. This integrates with RL-based planning loops (e.g., CarDreamer).
- **Phase III (Collaborative World Models for V2X):**
 - **World Model Alignment:** Developing protocols to align infrastructure (`q_I`) and vehicle (`q_V`) world models in a shared latent space, using metrics like KL divergence.
 - **V2I Latent Sharing:** Facilitating bandwidth-efficient communication of complex world states by sharing compressed latent representations.
 - **Multi-Agent Game Theory:** Modeling and analyzing competitive and cooperative behaviors of drivers and AVs, with the infrastructure acting as an impartial observer and information broker.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

This paper presents a research vision rather than reporting experimental results from a completed study. As such, the "main findings and results" pertain to the conceptual frameworks, identified gaps, and proposed solutions.

- **Identification of Spatio-Temporal Complementarity:** The paper establishes and articulates a fundamental insight: the distinct yet complementary nature of roadside and vehicle-borne sensing platforms. It posits that roadside sensors provide "temporal depth" (long-term, fixed-location behavioral distributions, including rare events), while vehicle-borne sensors offer "spatial breadth" (diverse scene sampling across networks). This complementarity is presented as a crucial, previously underexploited aspect for comprehensive traffic understanding.
- **Proposal of Infrastructure-Centric World Models (I-WM):** The core contribution is the conceptualization and detailed outline of I-WM as a novel paradigm in driving world modeling. This framework addresses the gap left by purely ego-vehicle-centric approaches, aiming to capture global traffic situational awareness and enable system-level optimization.
- **Dual-Layer Architecture Design:** The paper proposes a dual-layer architecture that separates annotation-free, modular perception (Layer 1) from end-to-end generative world modeling (Layer 2). This design is presented as a practical strategy for scalable roadside deployment, where perception components can operate without manual labeling, while the world model benefits from structured, self-supervised learning.
- **Phased Research Roadmap:** A concrete, three-phase (5-year) research trajectory is laid out, detailing the progressive development of I-WM from generative scene understanding (Phase I), through physics-informed predictive dynamics (Phase II), to collaborative world models for V2X (Phase III). Each phase outlines specific objectives, technical approaches, and relevant open-source foundations.
- **Introduction of Infrastructure VLA (I-VLA):** The concept of Infrastructure Vision-Language-Action (I-VLA) is introduced as a novel domain-specific application of the VLA paradigm. This redefines "action" for infrastructure, encompassing signal phase optimization, dynamic speed limits, and incident alerts, thereby enabling natural language control of traffic management.
- **Integration of Existing Technologies and Open-Source Foundations:** The paper demonstrates how I-WM can be constructed by adapting and extending established research and open-source models (e.g., DynamicCity, OccWorld, CarDreamer, HERMES). This approach suggests an actionable path by leveraging validated components.
- **Emphasis on Quality-Aware Uncertainty Propagation:** A specific technical "finding" is the necessity and proposed method for propagating sensor-level reliability and uncertainty throughout the generative pipeline, leading to quality-aware synthetic data. This is identified as a unique contribution compared to existing world models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Infrastructure-centric World Models (I-WM) framework carries several significant implications and potential impacts across various domains related to intelligent transportation and AI.

- **Expanded Applicability Beyond Autonomous Driving:** A key differentiating factor is I-WM's relevance beyond merely facilitating autonomous vehicle (AV) development, which is the primary focus of ego-centric world models. I-WM is positioned to serve a broader ecosystem by enabling:
 - **Proactive Safety Analysis:** Generating counterfactual near-miss scenarios and computing Surrogate Safety Measures at scale, allowing for data-driven safety improvements and infrastructure design evaluations.
 - **Adaptive Signal Control Optimization:** Utilizing the world model as a "mental simulator" for model-predictive control, potentially optimizing traffic flow and reducing congestion in real-time.
 - **Urban Planning and Design:** Simulating the impact of infrastructure changes (e.g., new lanes, signal timings) before physical construction, leading to more informed and efficient urban development.
 - **Training Data Generation for Agencies:** Providing synthetic datasets for the calibration and testing of detection and prediction systems used by traffic management agencies.
 - **Extreme Event Preparedness:** Simulating infrastructure responses to rare and severe events (e.g., natural disasters, major incidents) for emergency planning and resilience building.

- **Enhanced Environmental Understanding:** By explicitly bridging the "temporal depth" of roadside sensors with the "spatial breadth" of vehicle-based sensors through V2X latent space alignment, I-WM aims to achieve a level of environmental and behavioral understanding that neither platform can reach independently. This holistic view can lead to more robust and comprehensive models of traffic dynamics.

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Rare Event Learning:** The dual-layer architecture, with its annotation-free perception front-end, and the generative capabilities of the world model itself, can mitigate the challenges of data scarcity. Furthermore, the temporal depth afforded by roadside deployments is crucial for accumulating data on rare, safety-critical events, which are difficult to capture reliably from transient vehicle perspectives.

- **Foundation for Truly Intelligent Infrastructure:** The research contributes to the vision of intelligent infrastructure that progresses beyond passive observation. By understanding and anticipating traffic patterns, I-WM could enable proactive interventions, leading to safer, more efficient, and more resilient transportation systems.

- **Novel Research Directions:** The introduction of Infrastructure VLA (I-VLA) opens a new domain for research in multi-modal AI, integrating perception, language, and infrastructure control actions in a cohesive framework. The concept of "cognitive alignment" through world model sharing in V2X also presents a significant avenue for future exploration in cooperative autonomy.

- **Improved Safety and Efficiency:** The predictive capabilities and multi-agent counterfactual reasoning enabled by I-WM have the potential to significantly enhance traffic safety by identifying and mitigating conflict points before incidents occur. Simultaneously, optimizing traffic flow and management through intelligent signal control could lead to substantial improvements in efficiency and reduced travel times.

In summary, this research vision represents a directional shift in world model development, seeking to unlock the unique advantages of infrastructure-centric perception to create a more comprehensive, proactive, and collaborative intelligent transportation ecosystem.
