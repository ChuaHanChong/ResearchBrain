# PD-VLA: Accelerating Vision-Language-Action Model Integrated with Action Chunking via Parallel Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> PD-VLA introduces a training-free parallel decoding framework to accelerate Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly those using action chunking, by reformulating autoregressive decoding into fixed-point iterations. This approach boosts VLA model execution frequency by 2.52x and achieves higher success rates in real-world robotic manipulation tasks compared to standard autoregressive methods.

## Problem

- Action chunking, a technique used to improve VLA model performance and consistency in robotic manipulation, linearly increases the dimensionality of action sequences.
- Standard autoregressive decoding predicts each action token sequentially, leading to inference times that scale linearly with increased action dimensions, thereby limiting real-time control frequencies.
- Existing acceleration methods for VLA models often require re-training or architectural modifications, which can be computationally expensive and limit deployment flexibility.

## Method

- **PD-VLA** develops a training-free ==parallel decoding== mechanism that integrates into pre-trained VLA models without architectural changes or additional training.
- It re-frames autoregressive decoding by replacing the ==causal attention== mechanism with a ==bidirectional attention== mechanism within the LLM, enabling simultaneous updates of all action tokens in each iteration.
- The approach leverages ==Jacobi decoding== principles, iteratively predicting action tokens until a fixed point is reached, which typically requires fewer passes than sequential autoregressive decoding.

## Results

- The framework achieved a **2.52x** increase in execution frequency (**4.56 Hz** vs. **1.81 Hz**) for VLA models with action chunking on the CALVIN benchmark.
- PD-VLA demonstrated competitive manipulation performance, achieving a **94.7%** overall average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and **94.1%** for the 1/5 subtask on CALVIN.
- Real-world robotic experiments showed PD-VLA consistently outperforming standard LLaVA-VLA, with an **80%** success rate for "push button" (vs. **60%**) and **60%** for "pour water" (vs. **10%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parallel fixed-point iteration can efficiently transform sequential autoregressive generation into a faster, iterative process, providing substantial speedup without modifying the underlying VLA model architecture.
> - The inference bottleneck introduced by action chunking's increased action dimensionality is effectively mitigated by parallel decoding, allowing VLA models to maintain performance while achieving higher control frequencies.
> - The phenomenon of "fixed tokens" (correctly predicted tokens early in the parallel decoding process) contributes to faster convergence, particularly for low-entropy actions like gripper states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These advancements have led to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which represent a significant paradigm shift. VLA models aim to bridge the gap between high-level language instructions and low-level robot control by processing visual input, understanding natural language commands, and directly generating executable actions for robots. This end-to-end approach, trained on large-scale robotic datasets, promises greater generalizability and effectiveness in diverse manipulation scenarios.

A crucial technique for enhancing the performance of VLA models, especially in complex laboratory settings, is "action chunking." Action chunking involves predicting and executing sequences of actions over multiple time steps without immediate replanning, allowing the robot to capture temporal dependencies and generate more consistent and stable actions. While beneficial for performance, action chunking introduces a significant challenge: it linearly scales up the action dimensions in VLA models with increased chunking sizes. For example, a 7-Degrees of Freedom (DoF) manipulator with an `m`-step action chunk generates a 7m-dimensional action sequence.

The standard "autoregressive (AR) decoding" method, prevalent in VLA models, predicts each token (representing part of an action dimension) sequentially. This means that a longer action sequence, exacerbated by action chunking, directly translates to a proportionally longer inference time. This inefficiency hinders the achievement of high control frequencies, which are critical for real-time robotic applications and learning from high-frequency demonstrations.

The broader research landscape for VLA models includes ongoing efforts to improve their inference speed and efficiency. Existing acceleration strategies fall into several categories:
- **Architectural modifications:** Methods like DeeR-VLA (dynamic inference depth), QAIL (quantization-aware training), RoboMamba (replacing attention mechanisms), and TinyVLA (lightweight models) often require re-training the foundation models and sometimes additional data collection.
- **Token-level optimizations:** Techniques like token pruning (SparseVLM), compression-based tokenization (FAST), or adaptive token caching (VLA-Cache) aim to reduce the computational load during inference.

PD-VLA distinguishes itself by proposing a novel, "decoding-process-only" acceleration strategy. Unlike many prior works, PD-VLA requires no redesign or modification of the underlying VLA model architecture and no additional training (training-free and modification-free). This makes it highly "friendly for deployment" and capable of seamlessly synergizing with other existing acceleration techniques. By focusing on parallelizing the decoding of action tokens, PD-VLA directly addresses the fundamental bottleneck introduced by action chunking in an autoregressive setting, offering a practical solution to enhance the real-time capabilities of VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly accelerate the inference process of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that are integrated with action chunking, without compromising their manipulation performance.

The motivation stems from a critical bottleneck in current VLA models:
- **Performance vs. Efficiency Trade-off:** Action chunking has been shown to improve the consistency and stability of robotic actions, enhancing manipulation performance in complex tasks. However, it leads to a dramatic increase in the dimensionality of action sequences predicted in a single inference step.
- **Autoregressive Decoding Limitation:** Standard autoregressive decoding predicts each action token sequentially. When action dimensions increase, the inference time scales linearly, making the process slow. This "one-by-one" prediction method is a major impediment to achieving the high control frequencies (e.g., in Hertz) necessary for fluid, reactive, and real-time robotic operation.
- **Urgent Need for Acceleration:** The slow inference speed restricts VLA models from achieving desired control frequencies, thereby limiting their practical applicability in real-world scenarios, especially for dexterous tasks requiring rapid adjustments. Existing acceleration methods often involve architectural changes or re-training, which can be computationally expensive and limit deployment flexibility.

Therefore, the researchers are motivated to develop a framework that can overcome this inference efficiency challenge, specifically for VLA models augmented with action chunking, while being easy to deploy and compatible with existing VLA architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PD-VLA framework is built upon a fundamental VLA model and integrates action chunking before introducing a novel parallel decoding mechanism.

**A. Vision-Language-Action Model (LLaVA-VLA)**
The base VLA model, named LLaVA-VLA, is constructed upon the widely recognized LLaVA vision-language model. It utilizes `vicuna-7b-v1.5` as its Large Language Model (LLM) backbone and `clip-vit-large-patch14-336` as its vision encoder.
- **Input Processing:** The model takes two images (a static image `I_static` and a gripper image `I_gripper`) processed by the vision encoder `f_encoder` into visual tokens `h_I`. Simultaneously, text instructions and proprioceptive inputs are concatenated into a unified instruction `S`, tokenized into `h_S` by a tokenizer `T`.
- **Action Generation:** The LLM receives `h_I` and `h_S` and autoregressively generates action tokens `h_act`. These action tokens are then detokenized into a 7-dimensional continuous action `a`, comprising end-effector position (X, Y, Z), rotation angles (ϕ, θ, ψ), and gripper state (G).
- **Action Tokenization:** Continuous actions are discretized into 256 uniformly spaced bins, represented as integer indices. These indices are mapped to the 256 least frequently used tokens in the language model's vocabulary, allowing actions to be treated as textual strings for LLM generation.

**B. Action Chunking for VLA Models**
To enhance performance, action chunking is integrated. Instead of predicting a single action `a_t` at time `t`, the model predicts an action sequence `A_chunk = [a_t, a_t+1..., a_t+m-1]`, where `m` is the chunk size (set to 5 in this paper). This approach allows the VLA model to capture temporal dependencies, leading to more consistent and stable actions. However, this also means that a single inference now needs to generate a longer sequence of action tokens, specifically `l = 7m + 2` (7 dimensions per action * m actions, plus a beginning and ending token). With `m=5`, `l=37` tokens.

**C. Parallel Decoding for VLA Models**
The core innovation of PD-VLA lies in its parallel decoding mechanism, which re-frames autoregressive decoding to overcome its sequential bottleneck.
- **Revisiting Autoregressive Decoding:** Traditional AR decoding predicts tokens one by one (`y_i = argmax_y p(y|Y_i, x)`), requiring `n` sequential forward passes for `n` tokens, which is inefficient for long sequences.
- **Jacobi Decoding-based Acceleration:** PD-VLA leverages the concept of Jacobi decoding, which reformulates the inference process as solving a system of nonlinear equations. Instead of `y_i = argmax_y p(y|Y_i, x)`, the objective becomes finding `y_i` such that `f(y_i, Y_i, x) = 0`.
- **Breaking Sequential Dependencies:** To enable parallel updates, PD-VLA replaces the standard *causal attention mechanism* (where a token can only attend to previous tokens) with a *bidirectional attention mechanism* within the LLM. This allows all action tokens `Y = {y_1..., y_n}` to attend to each other, enabling simultaneous updates of all tokens in each iteration.
 The iterative process for parallel prediction is defined as: `y^(j+1)_i = argmax_y p(y|Y^(j), x)` for all `i=1..., n`, where `j` is the iteration count.
- **Convergence and Fixed Points:** The iterations continue until a fixed point is reached, i.e., `Y^(k) = Y^(k-1)`. The acceleration stems from the fact that the number of iterations `k` required to reach this fixed point is typically much smaller than the total number of tokens `n`, unlike AR decoding which always requires `n` sequential passes.
- **Decoding Horizon (`n`) Selection:** The decoding horizon `n` defines the number of tokens predicted in parallel. While arbitrary, the paper explores `n=7` (per-action dimension), `n=16`, and `n=37` (the full action chunk length, `l`). Setting `n=l=37` is considered effective as it allows the model to inherit the original action distribution modeling by predicting the entire sequence at once.
- **"Fixed Tokens" Phenomenon:** An interesting observation is that PD-VLA can preemptively predict some correct action tokens (termed "fixed tokens") even when other tokens are still incorrect. These fixed tokens remain unchanged in subsequent iterations, contributing to faster convergence. The gripper state, with only two values (open/close), is identified as particularly prone to becoming a fixed token early in the process.

This approach offers "training-free acceleration without redesign and modification of models," making it highly adaptable and deployable on pre-trained VLA models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PD-VLA is demonstrated through extensive simulations on the CALVIN and LIBERO benchmarks, detailed ablation studies, and real-world robot experiments.

**A. Simulation Experiments (CALVIN Benchmark)**
- **Performance Comparison:** PD-VLA significantly improves over the fundamental LLaVA-VLA model. Compared to other manipulation baselines like MCIL, HULC, and RT-1, PD-VLA achieves a competitive success rate of 94.1% for the 1/5 subtask and 50.5% for the 5/5 subtask, leading to the highest average completed length of 3.54 in the challenging CALVIN ABCD→D setup.
- **Acceleration Comparison:** When compared against other VLM acceleration methods (FastV and SparseVLM) deployed on VLA with action chunking, PD-VLA demonstrates superior performance. FastV, while preserving manipulation performance, incurred additional overhead leading to slower inference. SparseVLM suffered both decreased success rates and inference speed due to pruning, merging, and recycling costs. In contrast, PD-VLA achieves a 2.52x execution frequency (4.56 Hz) compared to the fundamental LLaVA-VLA (1.81 Hz), while also achieving higher success rates.

**B. Simulation Experiments (LIBERO Benchmark)**
- PD-VLA achieves state-of-the-art average performance on the LIBERO benchmark, attaining a 94.7% overall average success rate. Notably, it records 91.7% success on the most challenging LIBERO-Long suite, outperforming strong baselines like Diffusion Policy, Octo, OpenVLA, and π₀ (94.2%). This further validates its robust manipulation capabilities.

**C. Ablation Study**
Table III reveals the individual and synergistic contributions of action chunking (AC) and parallel decoding (PD):
- **Action Chunking (w/o PD):** Including AC alone significantly boosts performance (from LLaVA-VLA's 1.20 avg. len to 3.61 without PD), demonstrating its value in improving consistency and stability. However, its inference frequency (3.60 Hz) is still limited by AR decoding.
- **Parallel Decoding (w/o AC):** Applying PD without AC improves average decoding speed (39.56 to 39.86 tokens/s, 1.81 to 1.82 Hz), but without the benefits of chunking, the overall manipulation performance remains similar to the baseline.
- **PD-VLA (AC + PD):** The combination of AC and PD is crucial. AC provides the performance boost and consistency, while PD mitigates the inference inefficiency introduced by longer action sequences. PD-VLA achieves the highest average length (3.54) and execution frequency (4.56 Hz, a 2.52x improvement), demonstrating a successful balance between performance and real-time inference.

**D. Decoding Horizon and Acceleration Phenomenon**
- **Impact of Horizon:** The study on decoding horizons (Table V and Fig. 4) shows that `n=37` (the full chunk length) yields the best manipulation abilities (avg. length 3.64) and highest decoding speed (52.84 tokens/s, 4.56 Hz). This is attributed to its ability to inherit the full action distribution modeling.
- **Fixed Tokens:** With increasing decoding horizons, the number of "fixed tokens" (correctly predicted tokens that remain unchanged) also increases (e.g., from 5.17 for `n=7` to 8.75 for `n=37`), contributing significantly to faster convergence and speedup. The maximum decoding speed for `n=37` is approximately twice that of `n=7` and AR decoding.
- **Efficiency of `n=7`:** The `n=7` setting (corresponding to per-action dimension) performs better than `n=16` in terms of average length and frequency, suggesting that aligning the decoding horizon with the natural structure of actions can be more efficient than arbitrary intermediate chunking.

**E. Real-World Experiments**
- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted with a 6-DoF Unitree Z1-Pro mechanical arm and a 1-DoF gripper, using an ORBBEC Femto Mega camera. Three tasks were tested: "push button," "lift block," and "pour water."
- **Quantitative Results:** PD-VLA consistently achieves higher success rates across all tasks compared to LLaVA-VLA (Table VI).
 - "Push button": PD-VLA (80%) vs. LLaVA-VLA (60%)
 - "Lift block": PD-VLA (70%) vs. LLaVA-VLA (40%)
 - "Pour water": PD-VLA (60%) vs. LLaVA-VLA (10%)
- **Dexterous Manipulation:** The "pour water" task, requiring more flexible and consistent actions, highlights PD-VLA's advantage. LLaVA-VLA largely failed, while PD-VLA's higher execution frequency allowed it to adjust actions in real-time, leading to successful completion. Visualizations (Fig. 3) confirm smooth and robust manipulation in the presence of distractors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PD-VLA framework represents a significant advancement in making Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models more practical and efficient for real-world robotic applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Critical Bottleneck:** PD-VLA directly tackles the long-standing challenge of inference inefficiency in VLA models, particularly when integrated with performance-enhancing techniques like action chunking. By converting sequential autoregressive decoding into a parallel fixed-point iteration process, it fundamentally alters how action tokens are generated, leading to substantial speedups.
- **Enabling Real-time Robotic Control:** The 2.52x improvement in execution frequency (Hz) is crucial for robotic tasks that demand real-time responsiveness and continuous control. This enhancement allows VLA models to be deployed in scenarios requiring high-frequency adjustments, such as dexterous manipulation (e.g., "pour water" task), where timely feedback and consistent action generation are paramount.
- **Deployment-Friendly Solution:** One of the most impactful aspects of PD-VLA is its "training-free," "model-redesign-free," and "modification-free" nature. This means that existing, pre-trained VLA models can immediately benefit from PD-VLA without requiring costly re-training or architectural changes. This ease of deployment lowers the barrier to entry for practitioners and researchers, accelerating the adoption of VLA models in various robotic systems.
- **Synergy with Existing Methods:** The framework's design allows it to synergize seamlessly with other VLA acceleration techniques. This implies that future work could combine PD-VLA with other orthogonal optimization methods (e.g., model compression, token pruning) to achieve even greater efficiency gains.
- **Robust Performance and Generalizability:** PD-VLA not only achieves significant acceleration but also maintains or even improves manipulation performance across diverse simulation benchmarks (CALVIN, LIBERO) and real-world tasks. Its ability to handle long-horizon tasks and dexterous manipulations underscores its robustness and generalizability.
- **Mathematical Guarantees and Novel Formulation:** The reformulation of autoregressive decoding as a system of nonlinear equations solved by parallel fixed-point iterations provides a strong theoretical foundation for the approach, enhancing confidence in its stability and convergence properties.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens up new avenues for optimizing parallel decoding algorithms. Future research can focus on making these iterations even faster, perhaps by preventing redundant steps or improving the initial token sequence prediction for quicker convergence to a fixed point. This could lead to further breakthroughs in real-time AI for robotics.

In essence, PD-VLA moves VLA models closer to practical, high-performance deployment in real-world robotic environments, fostering a new generation of intelligent and agile robotic systems.
