# HMC: Learning Heterogeneous Meta-Control for Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The HMC framework developed at UC San Diego allows robots to perform complex, contact-rich loco-manipulation by adaptively blending heterogeneous control modalities directly in the torque space. This approach achieves high success rates, such as 93% on "Wipe Table," and improves stability and generalization for tasks on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Existing robot controllers, predominantly position-only, struggle with "contact-rich" tasks due to their inability to handle complex interaction dynamics effectively.
- No single control modality (position, impedance, hybrid force-position) is optimal for every phase of a complex task, leading to challenges with manual or discrete switching.
- Learning-based methods face data imbalance, with abundant positional demonstration data but scarce and expensive data for compliance or force-aware control.

## Method

- A low-level **HMC**-Controller unifies diverse control modalities by computing and blending their torques using a soft weighted average in the torque space.
- A high-level ==HMC-Policy==, featuring a shared Transformer trunk, modality-specific expert heads, and a soft router, learns to autonomously select and blend these heterogeneous control modes.
- A two-stage training strategy (pretraining on abundant positional data, fine-tuning on scarcer multi-expert data) addresses data imbalance and fosters robust, generalizable policies.

## Results

- HMC demonstrated superior performance, achieving a **93%** success rate on the "Wipe Table" task, significantly outperforming baseline policies that ranged from **33%** to **47%**.
- Adaptive control switching within the HMC-Controller proved essential for tasks like "Open Drawer," as fixed controllers showed poor performance in unseen settings due to inadequate compliance adaptation.
- Soft routing enhanced stability and generalization, improving the "Lift Bottle" task success rate in unseen settings from **53%** (with hard switching) to **80%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Blending control actions directly in the torque space allows for continuous and stable transitions between different control modalities, crucial for robust contact-rich interactions.
> - An architecture that decouples meta-level decision-making (via a soft router) from modality-specific action generation (via expert heads) provides enhanced robustness and stability.
> - A two-stage training paradigm effectively leverages imbalanced datasets, building a strong positional prior before integrating specialized force-aware behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards more autonomous and versatile systems capable of operating in unstructured, human-centric environments. A major thrust in this direction is **loco-manipulation**, where robots perform both locomotion and manipulation tasks in a coordinated manner, extending their utility beyond controlled factory settings to homes, warehouses, and disaster zones.

Recent breakthroughs in learning-based methods, including imitation learning, sim-to-real transfer, and zero-shot planning, coupled with internet-scale pre-trained visual models and sophisticated teleoperation interfaces, have significantly pushed the boundaries of robot capabilities. However, the paper identifies a critical limitation: most state-of-the-art methods rely on **position-only controllers**. While excellent for tracking accuracy, these controllers fundamentally neglect complex interaction dynamics, making them ill-suited for "contact-rich" tasks such as wiping surfaces, lifting delicate objects, or opening stuck drawers. These tasks require precise and adaptable regulation of both motion and force, where rigid position control can lead to instability, excessive forces, or even damage.

To address these limitations, **traditional compliance methodologies**, like impedance and admittance control or hybrid force-position control, have been developed. These methods dynamically adapt stiffness or manage forces in specific directions. However, they typically suffer from a lack of generalizability, often requiring manual tuning for specific tasks or environments. This work stands in contrast to these approaches by seeking a learned, adaptive solution.

More recent **learning-based approaches** have attempted to learn force-aware actions in a data-driven manner, predicting compliance parameters in real-time or integrating learning agents directly into control loops. However, these methods often depend on specific controller types, require expensive force sensors, and, critically, rely on small sets of domain-specific expert data collected using those exact controllers. This paper aims to overcome these data scarcity and specificity issues.

The concept of **meta-controllers** also forms a relevant backdrop, referring to systems that automate the selection and coordination of robot skills. Prior work includes skill libraries and approaches leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for skill guidance. While these offer high-level reasoning, the paper argues that LLM-based methods often face latency constraints and struggle to integrate real-time onboard observations, making them unsuitable for fast, reactive, multi-phase, and contact-rich tasks. HMC distinguishes itself by focusing on explicit, real-time, feedback-driven adaptation and expert policy selection, providing a more reactive solution to dynamic interaction challenges.

In this landscape, HMC positions itself as a novel framework that integrates the strengths of various control modalities with learning-based adaptability, specifically designed to handle the complexities of contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks on real robots, while addressing data limitations and ensuring smooth, stable transitions between control strategies. It pushes the frontier by providing a practical and robust solution for deploying compliant behaviors in learned policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the Heterogeneous Meta-Control (HMC) framework stems from the inherent limitations of current robot control paradigms when faced with real-world contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks. The authors identify three core challenges that existing methods fail to adequately address:

1. **Modality Mismatch:** No single control modality – be it pure position, impedance, or hybrid force-position control – is optimally suited for every phase of a complex task. For example, a precise positional controller might be ideal for reaching, but impedance control is necessary for compliant insertion, and force control for exerting pressure. Current systems often rely on a single mode or require manual, discrete switching, which limits versatility.
2. **Data Imbalance:** Learning-based robotic control heavily relies on demonstration data. However, there is a stark imbalance in the availability of this data. Large-scale teleoperation datasets are predominantly collected using position-only controllers, as they are simpler to operate and acquire data from. Conversely, fine-grained demonstrations leveraging compliance or explicit force control are scarce, expensive, and difficult to collect, creating a bottleneck for training robust force-aware policies.
3. **Abrupt Switching:** When different control modalities are employed, traditional methods often involve hard, discrete switching between them. This can lead to abrupt changes in torque commands, causing discontinuities that result in unstable, jerky, or even unsafe interactions with the environment and the robot hardware, especially during contact.

To overcome these significant challenges, the paper proposes the HMC framework with two main objectives:

- **To create a unified, adaptable low-level control interface (HMC-Controller)** that can seamlessly blend actions from multiple control modalities (position, impedance, hybrid force-position) directly in the torque space. This aims to provide continuous and stable control output, irrespective of the underlying control profile being utilized at any given moment, thus facilitating both teleoperation and autonomous policy deployment.
- **To develop a robust high-level learning policy (HMC-Policy)** that can autonomously select and blend these heterogeneous control modalities. This policy must be capable of learning from imbalanced datasets, effectively leveraging abundant positional data while still acquiring the critical force-aware behaviors from scarce expert demonstrations. The policy should also ensure smooth and interpretable transitions between control modes, eliminating the issues associated with abrupt switching.

In essence, the motivation is to enable humanoid robots to perform versatile, robust, and safe loco-manipulation in complex, contact-rich environments by intelligently and smoothly orchestrating different control strategies based on real-time task demands and environmental feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Heterogeneous Meta-Control (HMC) framework is designed as a two-tiered system comprising a low-level meta-control interface (HMC-Controller) and a high-level learning policy (HMC-Policy).

#### 3.1. HMC-Controller Interface

The HMC-Controller acts as a unified interface that takes actions from various low-level control modalities and blends them into a single torque command for execution. This controller operates on the upper body of the robot for manipulation, while an off-the-shelf whole-body humanoid controller handles lower-body locomotion.

- **Primitive Controllers:** The HMC-Controller encapsulates a suite of standard low-level controllers:
 - **Pure Position Controller:** A standard PD controller that tracks desired joint positions, outputting torques based on position and velocity errors.
 - **Joint-Space Impedance Controller:** Modulates joint-level compliance, regulating torques based on desired joint stiffness and damping.
 - **Cartesian-Space Impedance Controller:** Regulates end-effector compliance in Cartesian space, using the robot's Jacobian to convert Cartesian stiffness and damping into joint torques.
 - **Hybrid Position-Force Controller:** Tracks position along unconstrained axes while maintaining a specified force along constrained directions, using selection matrices to separate position and force control.
- **Torque-Space Blending:** The core innovation of HMC-Controller is its ability to compute torques for *each* active controller at a given timestamp and then perform a **soft weighted average** of these torques. This blending occurs directly in the torque space, allowing for continuous and smooth transitions between different control profiles. A low-pass filter is applied to the final blended torque commands to further ensure continuity and stability during execution.
- **Teleoperation Integration:** The HMC-Controller also serves as an interface for teleoperation. It uses OpenTV to track head and hand poses (e.g., from a VR system) at 50 Hz, which are then resolved into desired joint angles. A user-friendly dashboard allows a second operator to manually switch control modes (position, impedance, hybrid) and adjust parameters (e.g., impedance stiffness) on-the-fly. For force feedback in teleoperation without dedicated force sensors, the system performs an online, coarse estimation of contact forces based on joint motor torques and positional errors, visualizing these on a virtual robot arm.

#### 3.2. Learning Algorithm: Heterogeneous Meta-Control Policy (HMC-Policy)

HMC-Policy learns to orchestrate the HMC-Controller, formulating meta-control as a heterogeneous behavioral cloning problem. It addresses the challenges of modality mismatch, data imbalance, and abrupt switching through a specialized architecture and a two-stage training strategy.

- **Problem Formulation:** The policy learns from a demonstration dataset `τ = {(o_t, a_t, g_t)}`, where `o_t` are multi-sensory observations, `a_t` is a unified action vector including parameters for all potential controllers (e.g., EE pose, impedance stiffness, force vectors), and `g_t` is a soft belief state indicating the relative contribution of each expert controller. Zero-padding is used for irrelevant attributes to maintain a consistent action space.
- **HMC-Policy Architecture:**
 - **Shared Transformer Trunk:** This is the backbone of the policy. It takes multi-sensory observations (`o_vis_t` for visual, `o_prop_t` for proprioceptive) and tokenizes them using separate tokenizers. These tokens are then fed into a Transformer encoder, which outputs a shared latent embedding `z_t`. This trunk is responsible for general spatial reasoning and extracting task-relevant features.
 - **Modality-Specific Expert Heads:** Connected to the shared latent embedding `z_t` are `N` separate MLP "expert" heads. Each expert is specialized in predicting the action profile for a particular control modality (e.g., a "position expert" for EE poses, an "impedance expert" for Cartesian stiffness, a "hybrid force expert" for force vectors).
 - **Soft Router:** This critical component is an MLP parameterized by `ϕ`, which also takes `z_t` as input. It outputs a gating weight distribution `g_t` (a softmax over `N` experts), where `∑ g_t,i = 1`. This `g_t` represents the predicted contribution of each expert and enables continuous, smooth blending of expert outputs, preventing abrupt transitions. The final unified action `ˆa_t` is computed as a weighted sum: `ˆa_t = ∑ (g_t,i * a_t,i)`.
 - **Whole-Body Control (WBC) Commander:** A dedicated head `h_ψ(z_t)` continuously outputs high-level whole-body directives (e.g., base velocity, height, waist posture) to a separate low-level WBC controller, ensuring the robot's overall stability and coordination during loco-manipulation.
- **Two-Stage Training Strategy:**
 1. **Pretraining Stage (Positional Prior Learning):**
 - **Goal:** To establish a strong positional prior and general task representations from abundant data.
 - **Process:** Only the positional expert head and the WBC commander head are unfrozen. The Transformer trunk is trained extensively on large datasets consisting *solely of position-only demonstrations*.
 - **Loss Function:** An L1 loss `L_pretrain` is used to minimize the difference between the predicted and ground-truth positional actions, along with WBC commands.
 2. **Fine-tuning Stage (Multi-Expert Integration):**
 - **Goal:** To integrate force-aware behaviors, learn to blend control modalities, and refine the policy for heterogeneous control.
 - **Process:** All expert heads, the soft router, and the WBC commander are unfrozen. The Transformer trunk's learning rate is reduced. The policy is then fine-tuned on a *smaller, more fine-grained dataset containing multi-expert demonstrations* (i.e., data where different control modalities were used during teleoperation).
 - **Loss Function:** The fine-tuning objective `L_finetune` consists of two parts:
 - `L_action`: An L1 loss minimizing the difference between the blended predicted unified action `ˆa_t` and the ground-truth unified action `a_gt_t`.
 - `L_router`: A KL divergence term `D_KL(g_gt_t || g_t)` that encourages the predicted router weights `g_t` to match a smoothed ground-truth `g_gt_t` derived from the tele-operated modality IDs. This ensures the router learns appropriate switching behaviors.
 - **Benefits:** This two-stage approach mitigates data imbalance, prevents expert collapse (where one expert dominates), and enables smooth, continuous transitions by leveraging the well-initialized shared trunk and the soft router.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot demonstrate the significant efficacy of the HMC framework, particularly in complex, contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks.

- **Overall Performance Superiority (Q1):** HMC consistently and substantially outperforms various baselines across all evaluated tasks. For instance, on the "Wipe Table" task, HMC achieves a 93% success rate, dramatically higher than the "Stiff Policy" (33%) and ACT variants (33-47%). The baseline "Stiff Policy" often generated excessive torques, leading to motor overheating, highlighting HMC's ability to achieve compliant interaction. The "ACT (meta)" policy, despite predicting controller profiles, struggled with instability due to its single-head design for joint learning of low-level commands and high-level modes. HMC's architectural separation of meta-level decision-making and trajectory generation proved more robust.

- **Adaptive Control Switching via HMC-Controller (Q2):** Ablation studies confirm the necessity of the HMC-Controller's adaptive control switching. Fixed controllers (stiff-only or compliant-only) showed poor performance in unseen settings for tasks like "Open Drawer." This is because different task stages demand different compliance properties; compliant insertion might be needed to tolerate alignment errors, followed by stiff pulling to overcome resistance. HMC-Policy effectively routes between these expert controllers, dynamically increasing stiffness during the pulling phase, as illustrated by the routing logits visualization (Fig. 5). This phase-aware control routing leads to higher success rates.

- **Benefits of Soft Routing (Q3):** The "HMC (w/o soft routing)" baseline, which uses a hard argmax router for discrete switching, performs worse than the full HMC with soft routing. In the "Lift Bottle" task under unseen settings, hard switching often caused abrupt expert controller changes when the arm made contact, leading to subtle discrepancies in gripping force and occasional bottle slippage. Soft routing, by contrast, allowed for continuous blending of control outputs, maintaining stability during transitions and significantly improving the success rate in unseen scenarios (e.g., from 53% to 80% for Lift Bottle unseen). This highlights that continuous torque blending is crucial for stability and safety in real-world contact interactions.

- **Advantages of Two-Stage Training (Q4):** The "HMC (from scratch)" baseline, which skips the pretraining stage, consistently performs worse than the complete "HMC (ours)" approach. For example, in the "Open Drawer" task (unseen, Hand Insert phase), "HMC (from scratch)" achieved 47% success, while "HMC (ours)" achieved 67%. The pretrain-fine-tune strategy allows the shared Transformer trunk to learn fundamental task representations and spatial alignment from abundant positional data first. This strong positional prior then aids generalization and stabilizes gradient flow during fine-tuning with the scarcer, multi-expert data, preventing overfitting and mitigating conflicts between expert losses.

- **Generalization to Unseen Tasks (Q5):** HMC-Policy excels in contact-rich unseen tasks due to the combined strengths of its adaptive control routing, smooth transitions enabled by soft routing, and the robust initialization provided by the pretrain-finetune paradigm. This combination allows the policy to adapt to unexpected variations in contact dynamics and environmental configurations without degradation in performance seen in less sophisticated baselines. For instance, on the "Lift Bottle" task, HMC maintained an 80% success rate even in unseen scenarios, demonstrating its strong generalization capabilities.

In summary, the empirical results robustly validate HMC's design choices, demonstrating that its heterogeneous architecture, torque-space blending, soft routing, and two-stage training strategy collectively lead to significantly improved success rates, stability, and generalization abilities for complex, contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks on real humanoid robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HMC framework presents several significant advancements with substantial potential impact on the field of robotics:

- **Bridging the Gap in Robot Control:** HMC successfully addresses a critical limitation in current robotic systems: the struggle of position-only controllers with contact-rich interactions. By unifying and adaptively blending multiple control modalities (position, impedance, hybrid force-position), it enables robots to transition seamlessly between tasks requiring high precision and those demanding compliant, force-aware interaction. This is a fundamental step towards more dexterous and robust robot manipulation in diverse, unstructured environments.

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Versatility:** The ability to dynamically adapt control strategies in real-time empowers robots, particularly humanoids, to perform a wider array of complex loco-manipulation tasks with higher success rates and greater stability. This extends robot autonomy beyond structured industrial settings into challenging domains like homes, hospitals, disaster relief, and logistics, where varied objects, unpredictable contacts, and human-robot interaction are commonplace. Tasks like wiping surfaces, lifting delicate or heavy objects, and opening stubborn drawers become reliably achievable.

- **Improved Safety and Robustness for Real-World Deployment:** The continuous blending of control outputs in the torque space, facilitated by the HMC-Controller and governed by the HMC-Policy's soft routing mechanism, is crucial for real-world deployment. It eliminates abrupt torque discontinuities and hard switching, which are known causes of instability, damage to hardware, and unsafe interactions. This enhanced stability and robustness are paramount for deploying robots in close proximity to humans or in environments with sensitive equipment.

- **Efficient and Effective Data Utilization:** The two-stage pretrain-fine-tune paradigm is a highly impactful contribution for learning from demonstrations. It cleverly circumvents the "data imbalance" problem by first leveraging readily available, large-scale positional datasets to learn general task representations and a strong positional prior. Subsequently, it fine-tunes with smaller, scarcer, but critical force-aware demonstrations. This approach optimizes data efficiency, making it more practical to train complex, multi-modal policies without requiring prohibitive amounts of specialized force-controlled data.

- **Foundation for Future Advanced Robotics:**
 - **Scalability:** The heterogeneous architecture with modality-specific experts provides a flexible foundation for scaling to an even larger number of specialized control "experts," including more nuanced force control strategies or specialized gripper controls.
 - **Integration with High-Level Planning:** HMC's reactive, feedback-driven adaptation makes it an excellent low-level component for integration with higher-level task planners and symbolic reasoning systems (e.g., those involving LLMs). This could enable long-horizon autonomy for multi-stage, open-ended tasks where intelligent skill selection and execution are paramount.
 - **Humanoid Robotics Advancement:** The framework is particularly significant for humanoid platforms like the Unitree G1, which inherently demand sophisticated whole-body coordination, balance, and interaction capabilities. HMC directly enhances their ability to interact physically with the world in a compliant and controlled manner.

In conclusion, HMC represents a substantial step forward in developing generalist robot policies capable of nuanced and robust physical interaction. By elegantly combining heterogeneous control modalities with sophisticated learning strategies, it significantly expands the operational envelope for robots in complex, human-centric environments, laying critical groundwork for future generations of autonomous and versatile robotic systems.
