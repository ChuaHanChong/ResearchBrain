# Robotic Assembly Control Reconfiguration Based on Transfer Reinforcement Learning for Objects with Different Geometric Features

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University researchers developed a robotic assembly control reconfiguration method for Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC) systems, which enables adaptation to objects with diverse geometric features. The approach uses the Equivalent Theory of Compliance Law (ETCL) for accurate model-based parameter adjustment and Weighted Dimensional Policy Distillation (WDPD) for efficient reinforcement learning agent transfer, reducing re-learning costs and achieving superior control performance in diverse assembly tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic assembly controllers struggle to adapt to objects with diverse geometric features, especially asymmetric or irregular ones, necessitating costly re-tuning and re-training.
- Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC) requires reconfiguring both its model-based parameters and its reinforcement learning (RL) agent, where inaccurate model-based reconfiguration can lead to negative transfer.
- Existing Transfer Reinforcement Learning (TRL) methods often face challenges with low sampling efficiency and negative transfer when source and target tasks differ significantly.

## Method

- Proposed the Equivalent Theory of Compliance Law (ETCL) to analytically reconfigure model-based compliance parameters based on assembly object geometric features, thereby reducing the domain gap between tasks.
- Developed Weighted Dimensional Policy Distillation (WDPD), a transfer ==reinforcement learning== method that leverages dimensional similarity weights to guide the target RL agent in learning valuable sub-policies from relevant source domains.
- Integrated ETCL for initial model-based reconfiguration with WDPD for subsequent efficient RL agent transfer within the MHCC framework.

## Results

- ETCL successfully reconfigured compliance parameters for various assembly objects, demonstrating a positive influence on RL agent transfer by providing a more conducive learning environment.
- The WDPD method achieved the highest average reward curves and optimal final performance in training for diverse assembly tasks, outperforming direct training and other transfer baselines.
- Controllers reconfigured with WDPD demonstrated the smallest average force/moment during assembly among all tested methods, indicating superior rapidity and stability compared to constant compliance and other RL-enhanced approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate model-based reconfiguration, as achieved through ETCL, is critical for establishing a stable foundation for RL agent transfer, preventing negative transfer by minimizing the domain discrepancy.
> - Leveraging 'single-dimensional prior knowledge' from source domains, selectively weighted by their similarity to the target task, significantly accelerates the learning process for new assembly tasks.
> - The WDPD method effectively evaluates dimensional similarity using N-step rewards and continuous space serialized Shapley values to guide targeted and efficient policy distillation, enabling more effective knowledge transfer.
