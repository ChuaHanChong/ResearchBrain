# Robotic Assembly Control Reconfiguration Based on Transfer Reinforcement Learning for Objects with Different Geometric Features

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University researchers developed a robotic assembly control reconfiguration method for Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC) systems, which enables adaptation to objects with diverse geometric features. The approach uses the Equivalent Theory of Compliance Law (ETCL) for accurate model-based parameter adjustment and Weighted Dimensional Policy Distillation (WDPD) for efficient reinforcement learning agent transfer, reducing re-learning costs and achieving superior control performance in diverse assembly tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic assembly controllers struggle to adapt to objects with diverse geometric features, especially asymmetric or irregular ones, necessitating costly re-tuning and re-training.
- Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC) requires reconfiguring both its model-based parameters and its reinforcement learning (RL) agent, where inaccurate model-based reconfiguration can lead to negative transfer.
- Existing Transfer Reinforcement Learning (TRL) methods often face challenges with low sampling efficiency and negative transfer when source and target tasks differ significantly.

## Method

- Proposed the Equivalent Theory of Compliance Law (ETCL) to analytically reconfigure model-based compliance parameters based on assembly object geometric features, thereby reducing the domain gap between tasks.
- Developed Weighted Dimensional Policy Distillation (WDPD), a transfer ==reinforcement learning== method that leverages dimensional similarity weights to guide the target RL agent in learning valuable sub-policies from relevant source domains.
- Integrated ETCL for initial model-based reconfiguration with WDPD for subsequent efficient RL agent transfer within the MHCC framework.

## Results

- ETCL successfully reconfigured compliance parameters for various assembly objects, demonstrating a positive influence on RL agent transfer by providing a more conducive learning environment.
- The WDPD method achieved the highest average reward curves and optimal final performance in training for diverse assembly tasks, outperforming direct training and other transfer baselines.
- Controllers reconfigured with WDPD demonstrated the smallest average force/moment during assembly among all tested methods, indicating superior rapidity and stability compared to constant compliance and other RL-enhanced approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate model-based reconfiguration, as achieved through ETCL, is critical for establishing a stable foundation for RL agent transfer, preventing negative transfer by minimizing the domain discrepancy.
> - Leveraging 'single-dimensional prior knowledge' from source domains, selectively weighted by their similarity to the target task, significantly accelerates the learning process for new assembly tasks.
> - The WDPD method effectively evaluates dimensional similarity using N-step rewards and continuous space serialized Shapley values to guide targeted and efficient policy distillation, enabling more effective knowledge transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic assembly, particularly for high-precision tasks, has advanced beyond traditional off-line programming due to the dynamic nature of real-world environments and the need for precision. This has led to the adoption of external feedback systems, with force-guided control (also known as compliance control) often outperforming vision-based methods in terms of precision, especially when environmental factors affect visual signals.

Compliance control methods can be broadly categorized into model-based, model-free, and model-hybrid approaches. Model-based methods aim to analytically describe the force-pose relationship for robot path correction. While adaptive model-based methods address non-linearity and time-variance better than constant compliance, they still rely on simplified models and human experience, which limits performance for asymmetric or irregular objects due to the complexity of analytical modeling.

Model-free methods, predominantly data-driven learning approaches such as those using Gaussian mixture models or reinforcement learning (RL), are capable of handling asymmetric objects without explicit analytical models. However, a significant challenge for RL methods is their low sampling efficiency, even with various optimization techniques.

Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC) emerges as a strategy to combine the strengths of both model-based and model-free approaches, aiming to improve sampling efficiency while maintaining controller optimality, especially for complex assembly tasks involving asymmetric objects where both precision and training rapidity are critical.

A core challenge in deploying robotic assembly in varied scenarios is the high cost associated with re-tuning and re-learning controllers when the geometric features of assembly objects change. This problem is addressed by control reconfiguration, which involves adjusting controller structure and parameters to adapt to changes in plant dynamics. Within the context of MHCC, reconfiguring both the model-based parameters and the RL agent is necessary. The accuracy of model-based reconfiguration directly influences the complexity and effectiveness of RL agent transfer; an inaccurate initial reconfiguration can lead to negative transfer, where knowledge from source domains misguides learning in the target domain.

Transfer learning, particularly transfer reinforcement learning (TRL), is a relevant area for reconfiguring RL agents. Existing TRL methods, such as reward shaping and policy distillation, aim to leverage knowledge from source domains. However, they face dilemmas like negative transfer when source and target tasks are significantly different. This paper directly addresses these challenges by proposing methods for accurate model-based reconfiguration and efficient, similarity-weighted policy transfer for MHCC controllers, aiming to reduce learning costs and enhance performance across assembly tasks with diverse geometric features.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the challenges associated with robotic assembly of objects that possess diverse geometric features, especially those that are asymmetric or irregular. Traditional compliance controllers are typically optimized for specific geometric configurations. When these features change, the control system's performance degrades, necessitating a costly re-tuning of model-based parameters and re-training of reinforcement learning (RL) agents. This expense in time and resources hinders the practical deployment of robots in flexible and diversified assembly scenarios. Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC) offers a promising framework, but its multi-component nature (combining model-based and RL elements) complicates the reconfiguration process. A critical concern is that if the model-based components are not accurately reconfigured, the subsequent transfer of RL knowledge might be ineffective or even detrimental (negative transfer).

Against this background, the paper articulates two primary objectives for addressing the control reconfiguration problem in MHCC controllers for varying object geometries:

1. **To develop an accurate model-based reconfiguration method:** The aim is to precisely adjust the parameters of the model-based control modules (specifically, compliance parameters) in response to changes in assembly object geometry. This accurate reconfiguration is intended to minimize the discrepancy between the source (previously learned) and target (new) assembly tasks, thereby laying a stable foundation for subsequent RL agent transfer and preventing negative transfer. The paper proposes the **Equivalent Theory of Compliance Law (ETCL)** to achieve this, establishing an analytical relationship between compliance parameters and geometric features.
2. **To develop an efficient RL agent transfer method:** Building upon the accurately reconfigured model-based parameters, the objective is to transfer existing knowledge from RL agents trained on source tasks to accelerate the learning process for new target tasks. This transfer needs to be selective, ensuring that the target agent learns more effectively from source domains that are more relevant or similar. The paper proposes the **Weighted Dimensional Policy Distillation (WDPD)** method, which aims to leverage dimensional similarity weights to guide the target RL agent in learning specific, valuable sub-policies from various source domains, thereby improving sampling efficiency and overall control performance.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to enable robots to adapt quickly and efficiently to new assembly tasks involving objects with different geometric characteristics, reducing the significant re-learning overhead that currently limits their flexibility in industrial applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology for robotic assembly control reconfiguration is structured into two main sequential components: model-based reconfiguration and RL agent transfer, specifically for Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC) systems.

#### 3.1. Model of Assembly Process

The paper focuses on peg-in-hole assembly of columnar objects with equal cross-sections. It defines a system state **p** representing the relative pose between the peg and hole (RPY angles 𝛼, 𝛽, 𝛾; inserted length 𝑙; and biases d_x, d_y). The system output includes force/moment information **F** and Cartesian pose **r**. Incremental robot motion is the system input.

The state equation describes how robot motion changes the relative pose, incorporating kinematic functions (KNT) and coupling due to coordinate system misalignment (CS). The output equation details sensor-collected force/moment, comprising Force-Pose Mapping (FPM) and coupling due to coordinate system misalignment (CO). FPM is derived by integrating press (normal force) and friction across contact areas. The paper notes that asymmetric/irregular geometries lead to coupling in FPM, where one state component can cause multiple force/moment biases. It also highlights that M_z' (moment around the Z-axis) is difficult to model analytically due to the uncertain nature of tangential static friction (τ).

#### 3.2. Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC)

The MHCC controller combines model-based control modules with an RL agent to adapt parameters.

- **Model-Based Control Modules:** These modules include decoupling elements (DO and DS) to handle couplings arising from misaligned coordinate systems. The core model-based compliance law (Equation 9) assumes a proportional relationship between estimated force/moment in the assembly coordinate system (F_dec) and desired state adjustments (Δp_c). It employs a diagonal matrix **K** of compliance parameters (K_x, K_y, K_z, K_α, K_β, K_γ). The paper notes that couplings within FPM are ignored by these basic model-based modules and are intended to be handled by the RL agent.
- **RL Agent Control Module:** An RL agent is integrated to adaptively adjust the compliance parameters, addressing the unmodeled couplings and variable force-pose mapping.
 - **Markov Decision Process (MDP) Definition:**
 - **State (s):** Defined as the output of the assembly process, typically force/moment and robot pose.
 - **Action (a):** Defined as revision factors for the compliance parameters, ranging within [lb, ub].
 - **Reward Function (r):** Designed to incentivize successful assembly, penalizing high forces/moments and favoring insertion speed.
 - **Adaptive Compliance Law:** The overall compliance law (Equation 12) combines the initial constant compliance parameters (**K_0**) with adaptive revisions from the RL agent (**a**), using a Hadamard product. The DDPG (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient) algorithm, an off-policy actor-critic RL method, is used for training the RL agent.

#### 3.3. Control Reconfiguration

The reconfiguration method is designed for objects with different geometric features, treating pre-configured tasks as source domains and new tasks as target domains.

- **Model-Based Reconfiguration (Equivalent Theory of Compliance Law - ETCL):**
 - **Focus:** Reconfiguring proportional gains K_x, K_y, K_z, K_α, K_β, as K_γ is uncertain. Decoupling modules (DS, DO) are considered adaptable based on coordinate system misalignment, not geometric features.
 - **ETCL Principle:** It posits that critically stable compliance parameters K_i are inversely proportional to the expected change in force/moment with respect to pose (∂F_i/∂p_i) at the end-stage of insertion. More practically, K_i * E * R̂ * s_i = constant, where E is elasticity modulus, R̂ is the largest radius of the cross-section, and s_i is a shape scale factor dependent only on the cross-section's shape.
 - **Derivation:** The proof simplifies the control block diagram by merging decoupling modules and forming a virtual state feedback loop. By assuming the state feedback gains remain constant across assembly objects and relating them to the output feedback compliance law at the critical end stage, the constancy of K_i * E * R̂ * s_i is established.
 - **Practical Application Notes:** For irregular objects, ∂F_i/∂p_i is calculated numerically. For asymmetric objects, the expectation of ∂F_i/∂p_i is used, calculated from different state points to account for anisotropy. This allows for analytical reconfiguration based on geometric parameters.

- **RL Agent Transfer (Weighted Dimensional Policy Distillation - WDPD):**
 - **Objective:** To enable the target RL agent to learn specific, valuable dimensional knowledge from more similar source domains.
 - **Dimensional Similarity Evaluation (Equation 17):** Similarity (Sim_i_j) between sub-policy *j* of source domain *i* and the target domain is quantified using two criteria:
 - **N-step reward (N_i_j):** Measures the expected reward of applying sub-policy *j* in source domain *i*.
 - **Connection Graph Matrix (G_i(j,:) and G_θ(j,:)):** Utilizes continuous space serialized Shapley values to represent the influence of action *j* on all state components in a domain. The similarity component is based on the dot product of these row vectors, indicating how similarly action *j* affects states in source and target domains. A coefficient (υ) balances these two criteria.
 - **Dimensional Similarity Weight (Equation 20):** Calculated from the evaluated similarities (Sim_i_j) across all source domains and sub-policies to create a normalized weight (W_i_j). Higher weight signifies greater similarity and thus more relevant knowledge for transfer.
 - **Objective Function (Equation 21):** The RL training objective in the target domain is modified to include two components:
 - Standard reinforcement learning objective (maximizing expected discounted rewards for exploration).
 - A policy distillation term (weighted cross-entropy) that encourages the target policy (π_θ) to align with the sub-policies (π_i_j) from source domains, weighted by W_i_j. A scale factor (ω) balances these two parts.
 - **Implementation in DDPG (Equation 22):** The loss functions for both the actor and critic networks in DDPG are augmented. The actor loss includes the policy distillation term (simplified to MSE due to deterministic policy), encouraging the target actor to imitate weighted source policies. The critic loss also includes a similar weighted MSE term for target critic learning.

#### 3.4. Experiment Verifications

Experiments involved a robotic assembly system with a force-moment sensor and two groups of columnar assembly objects with varying geometric features (R̂, L, and cross-section shape). One object in each group served as a reference (manually tuned), while others were reconfigured. The experiments tested the effectiveness of ETCL in model-based reconfiguration and WDPD in RL agent transfer by comparing average reward curves and controller performance against various baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental verifications were structured to demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed Equivalent Theory of Compliance Law (ETCL) for model-based reconfiguration and the Weighted Dimensional Policy Distillation (WDPD) method for RL agent transfer.

#### 4.1. Model-Based Reconfiguration with ETCL

- **Reconfiguration of Compliance Parameters:** Table 1 presents the reconfigured compliance parameters (K_x, K_y, K_z, K_α, K_β, K_γ) for various assembly objects in two groups (A and B). For each group, one cuboid object's parameters were manually tuned as a reference, while the parameters for other objects with different geometric features were reconfigured using the ETCL. The shape scale (s_i) for each object component was computed relative to the reference cuboid. The parameter K_γ was kept constant across tasks due to its analytical modeling challenges.
- **Impact on RL Transfer:** Experiments specifically designed to evaluate ETCL's impact (Fig. 6) demonstrated that using compliance parameters reconfigured via ETCL had a positive effect on the RL agent transfer process. When the compliance parameters for a target object (e.g., label 5 in group A) were reconfigured accurately using ETCL, the transferred knowledge from a source object (label 3 in group A) facilitated faster and more effective learning, resulting in higher average rewards. Conversely, if compliance parameters deviated significantly from those suggested by ETCL, the transferred knowledge could mislead the agent, leading to poorer performance. This finding supports the premise that accurate model-based reconfiguration reduces the domain gap, providing a more conducive environment for positive knowledge transfer.

#### 4.2. RL Agent Transfer with WDPD

- **Dimensional Similarity Evaluation:** The paper computed dimensional similarity weights between sub-policies of source domains and the target domain (label 5) for both groups (Fig. 7). These weights reflected the relevance of different source sub-policies to the target task. For instance, in Group A, an assembly object shaped like a pentagonal prism was found to be most similar to a cuboid and least similar to a cylinder. In Group B, an object composed of a cylinder and a cuboid exhibited higher similarity to dual-component objects rather than single ones. This indicates that the similarity evaluation process, performed after model-based reconfiguration, successfully captures task-specific relationships influenced by both geometric features and the accuracy of force-pose mapping.
- **Transfer Performance Comparison:** Fig. 8 compares the average reward curves of the target object (label 5) under different transfer methods:
 - **WDPD Method:** Consistently achieved the highest increase in reward curves early in the training process and culminated in the largest final reward at the end stage for both Group A and Group B. This indicates superior acceleration of learning and optimal final performance.
 - **Transfer from Most Similar Source:** Showed a positive effect, but slightly weaker than WDPD.
 - **Equally Weighted Transfer from All Sources:** Initially performed better than direct training but eventually limited exploration, leading to lower final rewards compared to WDPD.
 - **Transfer from Least Similar Source:** Also initially improved over direct training, but the dissimilar knowledge hindered the agent's exploration and ultimately damaged the final reward.
 - **Directly Trained Agent:** Served as the baseline, showing the slowest learning progress.
- These results confirm that both the similarity weighting and dimensional transfer mechanisms within WDPD are effective in extracting and utilizing valuable single-dimensional knowledge from source domains, thereby accelerating the training process for the target domain more efficiently.

#### 4.3. Testing Process and Controller Performance

- **Controller Comparison (Fig. 9):** The paper compared the force/moment and compliance parameter curves during the testing phase for label 5 in Group A across different controller types:
 - **Constant Compliance Controllers:** Revealed a trade-off: small compliance parameters led to higher peak forces but more stable control, while large parameters resulted in smaller peak forces but reduced stability.
 - **RL-Enhanced Controllers (Directly Trained, WDPD, Equal Weighted):** Demonstrated adaptive adjustment of compliance parameters by the RL agents. This adaptive behavior allowed these controllers to simultaneously ensure rapidity (avoiding large force peaks) and stability, outperforming constant compliance methods.
 - **WDPD Reconfigured Controller:** Registered the smallest average force/moment during assembly among all RL-enhanced methods, aligning with its superior average reward during training.
- **Assembly Trajectories (Fig. 10):** The robot's TCP trajectories in the world coordinate system further illustrated the stable assembly process achieved by the different controllers, with the WDPD method contributing to efficient and controlled movement.

In summary, the experimental findings collectively validate that the proposed control reconfiguration method, encompassing ETCL for model-based reconfiguration and WDPD for RL agent transfer, yields superior control performance with reduced training costs compared to baseline methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a structured and experimentally validated control reconfiguration method for Model-Hybrid Compliance Control (MHCC) systems in robotic assembly. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Reduced Learning Cost and Enhanced Efficiency:** The primary contribution lies in providing a framework that significantly reduces the re-learning and re-tuning costs associated with adapting robotic assembly controllers to objects with varying geometric features. By leveraging existing knowledge, the method accelerates the training process for new tasks, which is critical for flexible manufacturing environments.
2. **Facilitating Model-Based Reconfiguration:** The introduction of the **Equivalent Theory of Compliance Law (ETCL)** provides a direct and analytical link between critical compliance parameters and the geometric features of assembly objects. This enables accurate model-based reconfiguration, which is crucial for narrowing the "domain gap" between different assembly tasks. By doing so, it creates a more favorable starting point for reinforcement learning agents, preventing potential negative transfer that could arise from poorly configured initial parameters.
3. **Optimizing Reinforcement Learning Agent Transfer:** The proposed **Weighted Dimensional Policy Distillation (WDPD)** method represents an advancement in transfer reinforcement learning. It allows for the selective transfer of "single-dimensional prior knowledge" by evaluating similarities between sub-policies of source and target domains. This targeted transfer mechanism ensures that the target agent learns more valuable and relevant information from similar source domains, thereby accelerating the learning process more efficiently and achieving better final control performance.
4. **Addressing Complexity of Asymmetric Objects:** The methodology is specifically designed to handle the challenges posed by asymmetric and irregular geometric features, which are difficult to model analytically using traditional model-based approaches. By integrating an adaptive RL agent with reconfigurable model-based modules, the system can achieve high precision and stability in complex assembly scenarios.
5. **Broad Practical Applicability:** The proposed control reconfiguration paradigm has significant practical implications for industries requiring flexible and diversified assembly. Fields such as electronics manufacturing, communication equipment assembly, and the automotive industry often encounter tasks involving numerous components with varying geometries. This method offers a pathway for rapidly deploying and adapting robotic systems to new products or product variations without extensive re-engineering for each new task.

Looking forward, the insights gained from this work could inform future research on bridging the simulation-to-real gap in contact-rich robotic assembly tasks, further extending the applicability and robustness of learning-based control strategies in real-world industrial settings.
