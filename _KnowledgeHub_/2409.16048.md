# Whole-body End-Effector Pose Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich’s Robotic Systems Lab developed a whole-body reinforcement learning controller that enables the ALMA quadrupedal mobile manipulator to precisely track 6-DoF end-effector poses across a large workspace on diverse, unstructured terrains. The system achieves an average tracking accuracy of 2.03 cm and 2.86 degrees on flat terrain, extending to 2.64 cm and 3.64 degrees on stairs, showcasing robust performance despite unmodeled disturbances and payloads.

## Problem

- Existing legged mobile manipulators struggle with precise 6-DoF end-effector pose tracking in large, whole-body workspaces, especially on rough or unstructured terrains.
- Model-based control methods for legged manipulators are highly sensitive to unmodeled disturbances, require explicit terrain models, and can struggle with real-time computation for complex, redundant systems.
- Prior learning-based approaches for legged manipulation often have limited operational workspaces, focus only on position tracking, or exhibit insufficient pose accuracy and poor generalization to varied terrains.

## Method

- Develops a whole-body reinforcement learning controller for a quadrupedal mobile manipulator using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) trained in a high-performance GPU-based simulation environment (Isaac Lab).
- Introduces a novel keypoint-based representation for 6-DoF end-effector pose commands, defining pose by three virtual keypoints rather than separate position and orientation parameters.
- Implements a comprehensive training methodology incorporating a terrain-aware command sampling strategy, a game-based curriculum for progressive difficulty, and extensive randomization (payloads, disturbances, observation noise) to enhance robustness and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The keypoint-based pose representation achieved the best tracking performance in simulation, with average errors 16.03 cm and 3.87° smaller than the next best 6D vector representation.
- On the physical ALMA robot, the controller demonstrated average pose tracking errors of 2.03 cm position and 2.86° orientation on flat terrain, and 2.64 cm position and 3.64° orientation on stairs.
- The system showed robustness to unmodeled payloads up to 3.75 kg (stationary) and 1.3 kg (moving), maintaining stable tracking, and outperformed a fine-tuned model-based MPC controller in mean errors on flat terrain (RL: 2.21 cm / 2.01°; MPC: 6.43 cm / 6.88°).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The novel keypoint-based pose representation effectively unifies position and orientation, simplifying reward tuning and achieving superior tracking accuracy compared to other representations like quaternions or Euler angles.
> - Training with terrain-aware sampling, a game-based curriculum, and extensive randomization enables a single RL policy to achieve precise whole-body 6-DoF end-effector pose tracking on diverse, unstructured terrains like stairs and slopes.
> - The learned policy demonstrates inherent robustness to unmodeled disturbances and payloads, adapting to varying conditions without explicit models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of manipulation capabilities with the mobility of legged robots represents a grand challenge in robotics, crucial for enabling robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured, human-designed, or hazardous environments. Traditionally, legged robots excel at traversing challenging terrains but lack the fine interaction capabilities of fixed-base manipulators. This paper directly addresses this gap by developing a robust and precise whole-body controller for legged mobile manipulators.

The research landscape for legged mobile manipulation can broadly be divided into two main control paradigms: model-based approaches and learning-based (Reinforcement Learning) approaches.

**Model-Based Approaches:**
Early efforts in legged manipulation largely relied on model-based control techniques, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC) and inverse dynamics. These methods leverage precise mathematical models of the robot and its environment to plan and execute motions.
- **Strengths:** Model-based controllers can achieve high precision and provide guarantees on flat or well-defined terrains, as they directly optimize for robot dynamics and contact forces. Examples include ZMP-based planning and unified MPC frameworks for locomotion and manipulation, which can even incorporate self and environment collision avoidance. Robustness to disturbances can also be incorporated through trajectory optimization.
- **Limitations:** Despite their strengths, model-based approaches face significant challenges. They are highly dependent on the accuracy of robot and environment models. Simplified models, often necessary for real-time computation with high-DOF legged manipulators, limit their robustness to unexpected disturbances (e.g., slipping, unmodeled payloads, unknown terrains). Re-planning in dynamic, unstructured environments is computationally intensive and difficult to achieve in real-time. Crucially, they often require pre-specified contact schedules and explicit terrain models, making them vulnerable to unmodeled terrain variations.

**Learning-Based (Reinforcement Learning - RL) Approaches:**
In recent years, RL has emerged as a powerful alternative for robot control, particularly for highly dynamic and robust behaviors in complex environments.
- **Strengths:** RL policies learn directly from interaction, often in simulation, and can develop remarkable resilience to external disturbances and environmental variability. This paradigm has been highly successful in quadrupedal locomotion.
- **Limitations & Previous Works in Legged Manipulation:** When extended to whole-body manipulation, existing RL works have encountered their own set of limitations:
 - **Hybrid Approaches:** Some approaches combine an RL policy for locomotion with a model-based planner (MPC) or inverse kinematics (IK) for the arm. While effective, fixing a locomotion policy for the base limits the synergistic use of the entire robot body, restricting the arm's effective workspace and overall adaptability.
 - **Unified RL (Locomotion + Manipulation):** More recent works have attempted to train a single RL policy for both locomotion and manipulation. However, these often suffer from:
 - **Limited Workspace:** Many implementations restrict the arm's workspace to be in front of the robot, which does not necessitate active base (leg) usage and thus limits the full potential of a mobile manipulator.
 - **Position vs. Pose Tracking:** Many focus only on 3D end-effector *position* tracking, neglecting orientation, or achieve poor *pose* (position and orientation) tracking accuracy (e.g., Fu et al. reported 22.2cm and 66.22 degrees error in a follow-up). While some recent works attempt pose tracking, they still face challenges with representation and generalization to full body motion or complex terrains. Ha et al. achieved good accuracy (2.12cm, 3.35deg) but primarily demonstrated it for motions in front of the robot, not fully exploiting whole-body capabilities.
 - **Terrain Limitations:** Most demonstrations of RL-based manipulation are restricted to flat terrains, failing to generalize to the rough, unstructured environments where legged robots are most valuable.

**This Work's Position:**
The presented paper directly addresses the aforementioned shortcomings. It proposes a whole-body RL formulation that specifically targets **precise 6-DoF end-effector *pose* tracking** in a **large, *whole-body* workspace** on **rough, unstructured terrains**, including stairs and slopes. By introducing a terrain-aware sampling strategy, a game-based curriculum, and, most notably, a novel keypoint-based pose representation, the authors overcome the limitations of both traditional model-based controllers (robustness to terrain and disturbances) and prior learning-based approaches (accuracy, workspace, terrain generalization, and effective pose representation). This work pushes the state-of-the-art by demonstrating highly accurate and robust performance in challenging real-world scenarios that were previously intractable for either paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a robust and high-performing whole-body controller that enables legged robots with attached arms to precisely track end-effector poses across a vast operational space and over diverse, challenging terrains.

The specific motivations driving this work stem directly from the identified limitations of existing control strategies:

- **Enhancing Practical Applicability of Legged Robots:** While legged robots have made significant strides in locomotion over complex terrains, their practical utility in real-world scenarios (beyond inspection or passive load transport) is severely limited by their lack of effective manipulation capabilities. Combining robust mobility with precise manipulation is essential for tasks like opening doors, operating machinery, or handling objects in disaster zones.
- **Addressing the Complexity of Legged Mobile Manipulators:** Integrating an arm onto a mobile legged base drastically increases the system's degrees of freedom (DoF), redundancy, and non-linear dynamics, making precise end-effector control extremely challenging. Traditional control designs struggle with this complexity.
- **Overcoming Model-Based Control Limitations:** Model Predictive Control (MPC) and other model-based methods, while precise on flat terrains, are vulnerable to unmodeled disturbances (e.g., slipping, unplanned contacts, unmodeled payloads) and require explicit terrain models. Their reliance on simplified models for real-time operation often compromises robustness.
- **Improving Upon Existing Reinforcement Learning Approaches:** Previous RL implementations for legged manipulation have their own set of drawbacks:
 - **Restricted Workspace:** Many RL solutions limit the arm's operational workspace (e.g., only in front of the robot), failing to utilize the full redundancy and mobility of the robot's base to extend reach.
 - **Position-Only Tracking or Poor Pose Accuracy:** Some approaches only track end-effector position, ignoring orientation, which is crucial for many manipulation tasks. Those that attempt pose tracking often achieve insufficient accuracy or struggle with effective representation of rotations, leading to difficult reward tuning and training instability.
 - **Lack of Terrain Generalization:** Most existing RL manipulators are demonstrated only on flat terrains, which limits their applicability in the unstructured environments that legged robots are designed for.
- **Achieving Stable and Smooth Policy Transitions:** In a practical scenario, a robot might switch between a locomotion policy and a manipulation policy. A major motivation is to ensure that such transitions are smooth and stable, avoiding abrupt movements or falls.

In summary, the key objective is to deliver a **general-purpose, robust, and precise whole-body controller** that can handle **6-DoF end-effector pose tracking** in a **large workspace** on **complex, unstructured terrains** (like stairs), outperforming the limitations of prior model-based and learning-based techniques regarding accuracy, robustness, and operational range.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a novel application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to achieve whole-body end-effector pose tracking for a quadrupedal mobile manipulator. The approach integrates several key components to address the challenges of workspace, terrain variability, and pose representation.

#### 3.1. Robot Platform and Simulation Environment

The system is developed for the **ALMA** platform, which consists of an **ANYmal D quadrupedal robot** (from ANYbotics) equipped with a **6 DoF Dynaarm** (from Duatic). This 18-DoF system (12 for legs, 6 for arm) is complex due to its high redundancy and non-linear dynamics.
Training is conducted entirely in simulation using **Isaac Lab**, a high-performance GPU-based physics simulation environment. This parallelized simulation (4000 robots in parallel) is crucial for efficient data collection and policy training in RL. The learned policy runs on the robot at 50Hz, with the main control loop and state estimator at 400Hz.

#### 3.2. Policy Architecture

The control policy is implemented using **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, a popular and effective RL algorithm. Both the actor and critic networks are structured as **multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs)** with hidden layers of size and ELU activation functions.

#### 3.3. Command Sampling Strategy (for End-Effector Poses)

To enable tracking across a large and challenging workspace, a sophisticated command sampling strategy is employed:
- **Initial Workspace Sampling:** 10,000 collision-free end-effector poses are pre-sampled by sweeping through the arm's joint angles with a fixed base. This defines the arm's intrinsic workspace.
- **Expanded Workspace (Whole-Body Utilization):** To encourage the robot to use its entire body for reach, a random body pose transformation (T_Δb ∈ R^6) is applied to each pre-sampled command. This effectively shifts and rotates the target pose relative to the base, forcing the policy to coordinate leg and arm movements. The transformations are sampled within specified ranges to keep them physically reasonable.
- **Terrain-Aware Sampling:** For unstructured terrains (like stairs), simple spatial sampling could lead to commands below the terrain surface. To counter this, a coarse terrain height map (20x20cm patches storing highest points) is pre-generated. Any command whose z-position falls below the terrain surface (plus an 8cm margin) is resampled. This ensures feasibility of commands on varying terrains.
- **Command Frequency:** A new command is generated every 4 seconds, and episodes last 12 seconds, exposing the robot to 3 different commands per episode. This forces the robot to learn smooth transitions between arbitrary arm configurations, rather than always starting from a default.
- **Spatially Uniform Sampling:** To prevent over-sampling dense regions of the workspace, a concentric cylinder binning strategy is used, ensuring a more uniform spatial distribution of sampled poses.

#### 3.4. Command Definition (Keypoint-Based Pose Representation)

A critical innovation is the **keypoint-based representation** for end-effector pose commands. Instead of separating position and orientation (which can lead to issues with rotation representations like quaternions or Euler angles, and difficult reward balancing), the pose is defined by **three keypoints** that correspond to the vertices of a virtual cube centered on the end-effector. These three points uniquely and completely define the 6-DoF pose. This unified representation simplifies reward tuning and avoids discontinuities often associated with other rotation representations.

#### 3.5. Action and Observation Space

- **Action Space (a_t ∈ R^18):** The policy outputs 18-dimensional joint position targets for a low-level proportional-derivative (PD) controller acting on each robot joint (12 for legs, 6 for arm). Actions are scaled and centered around a default arm-raised configuration.
- **Observation Space (o_t ∈ R^45):** The policy relies on proprioceptive information: the gravity vector projected in the base frame (3D), base linear and angular velocities (6D), all joint positions (18D), and previous actions (18D). Crucially, the policy input is augmented with the 9D keypoint-based end-effector pose command (positional difference between current and target keypoints in the base frame). While explicit terrain information is not provided, the robot can indirectly deduce terrain properties because initial poses are collision-free.

#### 3.6. Reward Function

The total reward `R` is a sum of task-specific rewards (`R_T`) and penalties (`R_P`):
- **Task Rewards (`R_T`):**
 - **Tracking Reward (`R_t`):** A delayed reward, active only during the last 2 seconds of a 4-second command cycle. This emphasizes reaching and holding the target pose, preventing undesired intermediate behaviors (like passing through the robot's body). It's based on the exponential of the negative squared distance between measured and commanded keypoints.
 - **Progress Reward (`R_p`):** Addresses sparsity of the tracking reward and reduces oscillation by incentivizing continuous reduction of distance to the target keypoints.
 - **Feet Contact Force Reward (`R_f`):** Encourages all four feet to maintain firm ground contact, crucial for stability.
 - **Initial Leg Joint Reward (`R_q`):** Encourages the robot to keep its leg joints close to a stable, pre-trained locomotion posture, aiding overall stability.
- **Penalties (`R_P`):** Standard penalties for high joint torques, joint accelerations, high action rates, and exceeding joint limits, promoting smooth and safe movements. Termination conditions are also set for knee and base contacts to prevent damage.

#### 3.7. Terrains and Curriculum Training

- **Terrains:** Training is conducted on procedurally generated terrains: flat, randomly rough, discrete obstacles, and stairs.
- **Curriculum Learning:** A "game-based" curriculum progressively increases task difficulty. If the robot achieves a low tracking error (position < 20cm, orientation < 20deg) within an episode, the terrain difficulty increases. Conversely, if errors are high, difficulty decreases. This prevents catastrophic forgetting and enables learning complex behaviors by starting simple.

#### 3.8. Initial Poses and Policy Transition

The manipulation policy is designed to operate alongside a separate locomotion policy, reflecting real-world task separation (rarely simultaneous high-precision manipulation and locomotion). To ensure smooth transitions between these policies, the robot's initial base pose and joint angles for each training episode are sampled from stable configurations achieved by a pre-trained locomotion policy after it performs some randomized movement. This prevents "jumps" when switching policies.

#### 3.9. Sim-to-Real Transfer (Robustness Training)

To bridge the sim-to-real gap and enhance robustness:
- **Payload Randomization:** Random masses (0-2.0 kg) are added to the end-effector.
- **External Disturbances:** Impulse forces are applied to the end-effector, and random pushes are applied to the robot's base.
- **Observation Noise:** Random noise is added to proprioceptive observations.

This comprehensive methodological framework, particularly the keypoint-based command representation and the tailored training strategies, underpins the reported high performance and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results from both simulation and hardware experiments, validating the efficacy and robustness of the proposed whole-body RL controller.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Pose Representation Comparison:** This was a critical evaluation, demonstrating the superiority of the proposed keypoint-based pose representation.
 - **Keypoint-based:** Achieved the best tracking performance.
 - **6D Vector Representation:** Ranked second, with average tracking errors 16.03 cm and 3.87° larger than the keypoint method.
 - **Quaternion and Euler Angles Representations:** Yielded significantly poorer results, with average errors exceeding the keypoint approach by 27 cm and 6.3°.
 - **Conclusion:** The authors attribute the keypoint method's superior performance to its continuous nature and unified representation of position and orientation, which also significantly simplified reward tuning compared to the difficult balancing act required for separate position/orientation terms.

- **Robustness to Added Mass on End-Effector:**
 - **Within Training Range (0-2.0 kg):** The controller maintained stable tracking, with an impressive average position error of 0.83 cm and orientation error of 3.45°.
 - **Beyond Training Range (e.g., 4.5 kg):** While performance degrades beyond the training distribution, the controller still showed some resilience, reaching errors of 15.33 cm and 45.02° for a 4.5 kg load. This demonstrates graceful degradation rather than catastrophic failure.

- **Comparison to Model-Based Control (MPC):**
 - The RL policy was compared against a fine-tuned model-based whole-body MPC controller on flat terrain.
 - **Median Errors:** Both controllers performed similarly (RL: 1.81 cm / 1.73°; MPC: 2.17 cm / 1.53°).
 - **Mean Errors:** The RL controller significantly outperformed MPC in mean errors (RL: 2.21 cm / 2.01°; MPC: 6.43 cm / 6.88°). This disparity highlights a key advantage: the MPC controller struggled with self-collision avoidance during transitions between distant poses, causing the arm to get stuck, an issue the RL controller effectively avoided.
 - **Terrain Generalization:** The MPC controller was not evaluated on stairs because it would require an explicit terrain model, which was outside the scope of the comparison, further emphasizing the RL controller's terrain-agnostic robustness.

#### 4.2. Hardware Experiments (on ALMA robot)

- **Pose Tracking Accuracy:** The results on the physical ANYmal robot demonstrated excellent sim-to-real transfer, confirming the effectiveness of the training methodology.
 - **On Flat Terrain:** The average tracking error for 20 randomly sampled poses in the expanded workspace was 2.03 cm for position and 2.86° for orientation.
 - **On Stairs (Challenging Terrain):** The controller maintained consistent performance on stairs (sideways, facing up, facing down) across 20 sampled poses. The average error was 2.64 cm for position and 3.64° for orientation. This is a significant achievement, as it demonstrates robust pose tracking on highly unstructured and uneven ground.
 - **Visual Confirmation:** Figure 1 visually illustrates the robot's ability to track poses across soft mattresses, stairs, and uneven natural ground, reinforcing the quantitative results.

- **Robustness to External Disturbances (Payloads):**
 - The physical robot was able to handle payloads up to 3.75 kg when stationary and up to 1.3 kg during movement, without explicit modeling of these weight changes. This is a crucial practical capability, allowing for dynamic changes in end-effector configuration and carried objects.

- **Smooth Policy Transitions:**
 - The initial pose sampling strategy, where the robot's starting configuration is drawn from a pre-trained locomotion policy, successfully ensured smooth transitions when switching between the locomotion policy and the whole-body manipulation policy on hardware. This prevents undesirable jumps or instabilities.

In summary, the results clearly demonstrate that the proposed whole-body RL controller achieves highly precise 6-DoF end-effector pose tracking, exhibits remarkable robustness to varying terrains and unmodeled payloads, and significantly outperforms existing baselines, especially in challenging, unstructured environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of legged mobile manipulation, addressing several long-standing challenges and paving the way for more capable and versatile robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Significance of the Work

- **High-Precision Whole-Body Pose Tracking on Unstructured Terrains:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration of precise 6-DoF end-effector *pose* tracking across a *large operational workspace* on *diverse and challenging terrains* (e.g., stairs, slopes, rough ground) using a whole-body RL approach. This moves beyond the limitations of previous works that were confined to flat terrains, limited workspaces, or position-only tracking.
- **Novel and Effective Pose Representation:** The introduction of the keypoint-based pose representation is a crucial methodological innovation. Its continuous nature and unified treatment of position and orientation simplify the RL learning process, enabling superior tracking accuracy compared to traditional quaternion, Euler, or 6D representations. This approach could be adopted by other RL tasks involving pose control.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Disturbances and Payloads:** The controller's learned robustness to external impulses and unmodeled payloads (up to 3.75 kg stationary, 1.3 kg in movement) signifies a major step towards practical deployment in real-world scenarios where precise system models are unavailable or conditions are unpredictable. This implicitly handles unmodeled dynamics, a key advantage over many model-based techniques.
- **Superior Performance over Baselines:** The direct quantitative comparison showing lower mean tracking errors against a highly-tuned model-based MPC controller and significantly better accuracy than previous RL methods (e.g., Fu et al.) validates the effectiveness of the proposed approach.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The minimal sim-to-real gap demonstrated in hardware experiments confirms the robustness of the training methodology, including terrain-aware sampling, curriculum learning, and various randomization techniques.
- **Smooth Policy Integration:** The method for initializing robot states from a pre-trained locomotion policy ensures smooth and stable transitions between locomotion and manipulation behaviors, which is vital for practical multi-modal robot operation.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Expanded Applications for Legged Robots:** This work unlocks a vast array of new applications for legged robots. Beyond simple inspection, ANYmal-like robots can now reliably perform complex manipulation tasks in dynamic and unstructured environments, such as:
 - **Industrial Inspections and Maintenance:** Operating valves, opening doors, or handling tools in hazardous industrial plants or construction sites.
 - **Disaster Response:** Interacting with debris, opening access points, or retrieving objects in collapsed buildings, where human access is dangerous.
 - **Logistics and Warehousing:** Manipulating packages on uneven floors or navigating stairs within complex storage facilities.
 - **Service Robotics:** Performing more sophisticated tasks in human-designed environments that involve interaction with objects at varying heights and orientations.
- **Advancement in Mobile Manipulation Research:** The paper sets a new benchmark for whole-body control of redundant legged manipulators. It provides a robust framework for future research in areas such as force control, compliant manipulation, and real-time environment interaction.
- **Inspiration for RL in Robotics:** The success of the keypoint-based representation and the integrated training strategies (terrain-aware sampling, curriculum, extensive randomization) offers valuable insights for designing more effective RL solutions for complex robotic tasks.
- **Reduced Reliance on Perfect Models:** By demonstrating high performance without relying on precise real-time terrain models or explicit payload knowledge, the work highlights the increasing capability of learning-based approaches to handle real-world uncertainties, potentially simplifying deployment requirements.

In conclusion, this research is a significant step towards developing truly versatile and autonomous legged mobile manipulators. By enabling precise, robust, and whole-body coordinated manipulation in challenging real-world environments, it promises to accelerate the deployment of these advanced robots in critical applications. Future work on integrating 3D environment representations for collision avoidance and enhancing performance with even heavier, unmodeled payloads suggests continued progress towards highly adaptive and capable systems.
