# Deep Reactive Policy: Learning Reactive Manipulator Motion Planning for Dynamic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Deep Reactive Policy (DRP) allows robotic manipulators to generate globally coherent, collision-free motion reactively in dynamic, partially observable environments directly from raw point cloud inputs. It achieved significantly higher success rates on challenging dynamic tasks in both simulation (e.g., 75.5% on Floating Dynamic Obstacles) and real-world settings (e.g., 93.33% on Dynamic Goal Blocking), running at 300 Hz.

## Problem

- Classical motion planners are computationally slow, require full environment knowledge, and produce open-loop trajectories, making them unsuitable for real-time operation in dynamic environments.
- Locally reactive controllers are fast and agile but frequently get trapped in local minima in cluttered scenes due to their lack of global environment understanding.
- Prior learning-based motion policies struggle with generalization to diverse or unseen environments, often incur minor collisions, and may sacrifice real-time reactivity for accuracy through test-time optimization.

## Method

- **IMPACT**: A ==transformer==-based neural policy is initially pretrained on over 1`cuRobo` million expert trajectories from a GPU-accelerated planner (`cuRobo`) to learn globally coherent, collision-free paths from raw point cloud inputs.
- Iterative Student-Teacher Finetuning: The policy is refined through an iterative process where a student learns from a teacher (pretrained IMPACT + `Geometric Fabrics`) to improve robustness and reduce collisions with static obstacles.
- **Dynamic Closest Point RMP (DCP-RMP)**: A non-learning, point cloud-based ==reactive control== module is integrated during inference to locally modify the goal, ensuring immediate repulsion from fast-moving dynamic obstacles without compromising global planning.

## Results

- DRP consistently outperformed both classical and prior learning-based methods in simulation, achieving **75.5%** success on Floating Dynamic Obstacles and **65.25%** on Dynamic Goal Blocking tasks.
- In real-world experiments, DRP demonstrated strong robustness to domain shift and noisy perception, achieving **70.00%** success on Floating Dynamic Obstacles and **93.33%** on Dynamic Goal Blocking, while baselines significantly degraded.
- The policy operates in real-time at **300 Hz** on an RTX 4090, with a cold-start time of **3.48 ms**, making it efficient for practical robot deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining large-scale imitation learning for global awareness with a dedicated local reactive controller effectively bridges the gap between globally optimal planning and real-time adaptability in dynamic environments.
> - An iterative student-teacher distillation process can significantly enhance a learned policy's robustness to static obstacles, refining learned actions into safer, collision-free trajectories.
> - Integrating a method-agnostic, point cloud-based reactive module (DCP-RMP) during inference enables robust handling of fast-moving dynamic obstacles, even with policies not explicitly trained for such scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Deep Reactive Policy: Learning Reactive Manipulator Motion Planning for Dynamic Environments

#### 1.1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic motion planning is crucial for enabling robots to operate autonomously and safely, especially in complex, human-centric environments. Historically, this field has been dominated by two main paradigms:

1. **Global Planning Methods:** These include search-based algorithms (e.g., A*, AIT*) and sampling-based algorithms (e.g., PRM, RRT, BIT*), as well as trajectory optimization methods (e.g., CHOMP, cuRobo). While these methods can compute globally optimal or near-optimal collision-free paths, they often suffer from several limitations. They typically require full, accurate knowledge of the environment, struggle with high-dimensional configuration spaces (like multi-joint manipulators), and are computationally intensive, making them too slow for real-time operation in dynamic scenes. When used, they usually generate open-loop trajectories, which lack adaptability to sudden environmental changes.

2. **Locally Reactive Methods:** Approaches like Riemannian Motion Policies (RMP) and Geometric Fabrics offer fast, closed-loop collision avoidance by steering robots away from nearby obstacles and towards goals. Their strength lies in their real-time adaptability to dynamic changes and inherent respect for robot dynamics. However, a significant drawback is their susceptibility to local minima in cluttered environments, as they lack global scene awareness and cannot plan around complex obstacles effectively.

More recently, **Learning-Based Methods** have emerged as a promising alternative, aiming to combine the benefits of both traditional paradigms while overcoming their limitations. These methods leverage neural networks to learn motion policies, often from demonstrations or self-supervision. Initial efforts augmented classical planners with learned components (e.g., biasing sampling, guiding search). However, the trend is moving towards **end-to-end visuo-motor neural policies** that directly map raw sensory inputs (like point clouds) to robot actions. These policies offer closed-loop adaptability and operate in real-time, crucial for dynamic and partially observable settings.

Prior learning-based methods, such as MπNets, MπFormer, and NeuralMP, have shown progress but still face challenges:
- **Generalization:** Many struggle to generalize to diverse, unseen, or out-of-distribution environments due to limited training data diversity.
- **Reactivity vs. Optimization:** Some methods, like NeuralMP, improve accuracy through test-time optimization (TTO), but this runtime search sacrifices the real-time reactivity needed for fast-changing environments.
- **Collision Avoidance:** Early models might fail to provide robust, collision-free motion, especially in complex or dynamic scenarios.

DRP fits into this landscape by explicitly addressing these gaps. It proposes a novel visuo-motor neural policy that aims to deliver **globally coherent, collision-free motion** while maintaining **real-time, reactive adaptability** in **diverse, dynamic, and partially observable environments**, all from **raw point cloud input**. It attempts to synthesize the strengths of large-scale imitation learning (for global awareness) with local reactive control (for dynamic obstacle avoidance), moving the field closer to robust, deployable robotic systems in unstructured settings.

#### 1.2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Deep Reactive Policy (DRP) is to enable robotic manipulators to generate **collision-free motion in dynamic, partially observable environments** in a **reactive and generalizable manner**, operating directly on **raw point cloud sensory inputs**.

The motivation for DRP stems from the limitations of existing motion planning approaches:
- **Classical motion planners** are slow, require full environment knowledge, and are ill-suited for dynamic scenes, limiting their deployment in fast-changing human environments. They typically produce open-loop trajectories that lack real-time adaptability.
- **Reactive controllers** (like RMP) are fast and excel at local collision avoidance but often get trapped in local minima in cluttered environments due to their lack of global scene understanding.
- **Prior neural motion policies** show promise by operating on raw sensory inputs in a closed-loop fashion. However, they frequently struggle with generalization to complex or diverse environments and often incur minor collisions, highlighting a trade-off between learning complex behaviors and ensuring absolute safety and reliability. Some (like NeuralMP) improve accuracy with runtime optimization, which sacrifices reactivity.

DRP seeks to overcome these challenges by:
- Developing a neural policy that can learn complex, globally aware motion strategies through massive-scale imitation learning.
- Enhancing this policy's robustness to static obstacles through an iterative student-teacher finetuning process that incorporates dynamics-aware local avoidance.
- Integrating a non-learning, locally reactive module to effectively handle fast-moving dynamic obstacles, ensuring immediate response without compromising global planning.
- Operating directly on point cloud data, making it suitable for real-world deployment without requiring explicit scene reconstruction or privileged information.

Ultimately, the motivation is to facilitate the deployment of robots in natural human environments, such as homes and kitchens, where safe, real-time, and adaptable collision-free motion generation is a fundamental requirement beneath higher-level cognitive and task planning layers.

#### 1.3. Methodology and Approach

DRP is a visuo-motor neural motion policy built upon three integrated components: a transformer-based neural policy called IMPACT, an iterative student-teacher finetuning process, and a locally reactive goal-proposal module called DCP-RMP.

##### 1.3.1. IMPACT: Imitating Motion Planning with Action-Chunking Transformer

IMPACT forms the core of DRP. It is a transformer-based neural network designed to generate joint position targets. Its inputs are the obstacle point cloud ($P_s$), robot point cloud ($P_r$), current joint configuration ($q_c$), and goal joint configuration ($q_{mg}$). The output is a sequence of delta joint actions.

- **Large-Scale Motion Pretraining:** IMPACT is initially pretrained via behavior cloning on an extensive dataset of over 10 million expert trajectories. This dataset is generated using `cuRobo`, a state-of-the-art GPU-accelerated optimization-based motion planner, in diverse simulation environments. Crucially, the dataset includes challenging scenarios where the goal is obstructed, teaching the robot to approach as closely as possible without colliding.
 - **Input Processing:** Point clouds are downsampled and converted into latent tokens using PointNet++'s set abstraction layers to reduce computational complexity. Joint configurations are encoded via MLPs.
 - **Transformer Architecture:** A transformer encoder processes the combined latent tokens (scene, robot, current, goal). A decoder then generates a sequence of delta joint actions, supervised by a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss against ground-truth actions. This action-chunking approach aims for efficiency and smoother trajectories.
 - **Real-World Deployment:** In real-world settings, scene point clouds are captured by calibrated depth cameras. Points near the robot are replaced with samples from its mesh model to ensure accurate self-representation.

##### 1.3.2. Iterative Student-Teacher Finetuning

While pretraining on `cuRobo` provides strong global awareness, the resulting IMPACT policy can still incur minor collisions due to compounding errors inherent in behavior cloning. To address this and improve static obstacle avoidance, DRP employs an iterative student-teacher finetuning process:
- **Teacher Policy:** The teacher combines the pretrained IMPACT policy ($\pi_b$) with `Geometric Fabrics` ($\pi_f$). `Geometric Fabrics` is a state-of-the-art closed-loop controller that excels at local obstacle avoidance and respects robot dynamics, but requires privileged information (ground-truth obstacle models). It refines IMPACT's initial joint position targets (`q_b`) into improved, collision-free targets (`q_e = \pi_f(obs, q_b)`).
- **Student Policy:** The student policy ($\pi_s$), an instance of IMPACT, learns to imitate these refined targets from the teacher, operating solely on point cloud inputs.
- **Iterative Distillation:** The entire process runs in parallel in IsaacGym. The teacher's base IMPACT policy ($\pi_b$) remains frozen for a set number of gradient steps (10,000), after which the fine-tuned student policy replaces $\pi_b$, and the cycle repeats. This progressive distillation improves local obstacle avoidance while preserving global planning capabilities.

##### 1.3.3. Riemannian Motion Policy with Dynamic Closest Point (DCP-RMP)

To enhance reactivity to fast-moving dynamic obstacles, which are not explicitly covered during pretraining or finetuning, DRP integrates a non-learning component called DCP-RMP during inference.
- **Goal-Proposal Module:** DCP-RMP acts as a local, reactive goal-proposal module that modifies the original joint-space goal ($q_g$) into a new goal ($q_{mg}$) passed to IMPACT. This modified goal prioritizes avoidance when dynamic obstacles approach, without compromising IMPACT's global awareness.
- **Point Cloud-Based Operation:** Unlike traditional RMP that requires ground-truth models, DCP-RMP operates directly on point cloud inputs. It identifies dynamic obstacles by comparing current and previous frame point clouds using a KDTree to find moving points.
- **Repulsive Motion Generation:** DCP-RMP calculates the minimal displacement between the robot and the closest dynamic obstacle. It then generates a repulsive acceleration to increase this separation, which is integrated with a goal-attracting policy to yield the modified joint goal ($q_{mg}$). This ensures that if a dynamic obstacle moves rapidly towards the robot, the modified goal will steer the robot away.
- **Robustness to Colliding Goals:** IMPACT's training includes scenarios where the goal is in collision with static obstacles, so it learns to stop safely rather than attempting to reach an unachievable modified goal.

#### 1.4. Main Findings and Results

DRP was evaluated extensively on `DRPBench`, a suite of challenging benchmark tasks in both simulation and the real world, designed to test navigation in cluttered static environments, reactivity to dynamic obstacles, and handling of temporarily obstructed goals. The primary metric was success rate (reaching end-effector goal pose within thresholds without collisions).

##### 1.4.1. Simulation Experiments and Results

On `DRPBench` (over 4000 instances) and the MπNets dataset, DRP demonstrated superior performance:

- **Overall Superiority:** DRP consistently outperformed both classical motion planners (AIT*, cuRobo, RMP, cuRobo-Vox) and prior learning-based methods (MπNets, MπFormer, NeuralMP) across diverse tasks (Table 1).
- **Dynamic and Goal-Blocking Tasks:** DRP particularly excelled in challenging dynamic and goal-blocking scenarios.
 - Classical sampling-based AIT* completely failed in dynamic environments (0% success).
 - Optimization-based `cuRobo` performed well in static settings (82.97% SE) but struggled in dynamic goal blocking (3.00% DGB).
 - RMP, while reactive (50.50% DGB), was significantly surpassed by DRP, highlighting the benefit of combining learning with reactivity.
 - DRP achieved 75.50% on Floating Dynamic Obstacles (FDO) and 65.25% on Dynamic Goal Blocking (DGB), far outperforming IMPACT alone (32.00% FDO, 0.25% DGB) and `cuRobo` (39.50% FDO, 3.00% DGB), directly demonstrating the effectiveness of DCP-RMP.
- **Generalization through Architecture and Data:** Learning-based models trained on narrower datasets (MπNets, MπFormer) showed poor generalization (0-2.5% on DRPBench). NeuralMP, which uses test-time optimization, performed better but was still limited in reactive contexts. IMPACT (without finetuning) already surpassed other learning-based methods due to its expressive transformer architecture and diverse large-scale pretraining data, without relying on post-hoc test-time optimization.
- **Impact of Finetuning:** DRP, though pretrained with `cuRobo`, significantly exceeded `cuRobo`'s performance. This is attributed to filtering for successful expert trajectories and the iterative student-teacher finetuning, which refined action generation and improved static obstacle avoidance (45% success rate increase over pretrained IMPACT, as detailed in Table 6).
- **DCP-RMP's Method-Agnostic Benefit:** Table 2 showed that integrating DCP-RMP improved performance across dynamic tasks even when applied to baseline methods like `cuRobo` and `NeuralMP`, confirming its broad utility for dynamic obstacle avoidance.

##### 1.4.2. Real-World Experiment Results

DRP's performance in real-world environments (using a Franka Panda robot with RGB-D cameras) further validated its robustness to domain shift and noisy perception (Table 3).
- **Robustness to Noise and Domain Shift:** DRP and IMPACT achieved near-perfect success rates on Static Environment (SE) and Suddenly Appearing Obstacle (SAO) tasks, significantly outperforming `cuRobo-Vox` and `NeuralMP`, which degraded severely under real-world conditions.
- **Complex Real-World Scenarios:** On the Goal Blocking (GB) task, DRP and IMPACT both achieved 92.86%, while baselines failed.
- **Crucial Role of DCP-RMP in Real-World Dynamics:** IMPACT alone failed to solve Floating Dynamic Obstacle (FDO) and Dynamic Goal Blocking (DGB) tasks in the real world, whereas DRP (with DCP-RMP) achieved 70.00% and 93.33% respectively. This underscores the critical importance of DCP-RMP for real-time reactive behavior in dynamic real-world settings.
- **Efficiency:** DRP runs at 300 Hz on an RTX 4090, with a cold-start time of 3.48 ms, significantly faster than NeuralMP (2970 ms) and MπFormer (30 ms) (Table 5).

#### 1.5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Deep Reactive Policy (DRP) represents a significant advancement in robot motion planning, particularly for manipulators operating in complex and dynamic human environments. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Bridging the Gap between Global Planning and Local Reactivity:** DRP effectively synthesizes the strengths of globally optimal planners (learned through large-scale imitation) with the real-time adaptability of local reactive controllers. This allows robots to maintain global awareness for complex path planning while instantly responding to unforeseen dynamic changes, a long-standing challenge in robotics.
- **Robust Generalization from Simulation to Reality:** By leveraging diverse, large-scale simulation data, an expressive transformer architecture, and a robust finetuning process, DRP demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, even to challenging, out-of-distribution real-world scenarios with noisy sensory inputs and semantic obstacles. This significantly reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world data collection and training.
- **Enhanced Safety and Adaptability in Dynamic Environments:** The integration of DCP-RMP, a point cloud-based reactive module, is crucial for handling fast-moving dynamic obstacles. This capability is paramount for safe human-robot collaboration and operation in unstructured environments where unexpected events (e.g., a person moving an object) are common.
- **Real-time Performance with Raw Sensory Input:** DRP operates directly on raw point cloud data in a closed-loop fashion at high inference speeds (300 Hz). This enables true real-time reactive decision-making without the need for computationally expensive scene reconstruction or offline planning, making it practical for real-world robotic applications.
- **Scalable Learning Paradigm:** The use of `cuRobo` for generating 10 million expert trajectories on GPU-accelerated simulation platforms like `IsaacGym` demonstrates a highly scalable data generation and training pipeline. This approach can be extended to acquire a vast repertoire of complex motion skills.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** DRP operates at a fundamental layer, providing safe and feasible motion generation beneath higher-level cognitive and task-planning systems, such as Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or behavior cloning agents. It ensures that abstract commands from these layers can be translated into physically executable, collision-free actions.
- **Open Research Contribution:** The commitment to release datasets, models, and benchmarks will foster further research and development in the robot learning community, providing a solid foundation for future comparisons and innovations.

While the current work is limited to a single robot embodiment (Franka Panda), the principles established by DRP for combining learned global awareness with reactive local control, all from raw sensory inputs, pave the way for more intelligent, safer, and highly adaptable robotic systems that can operate seamlessly in the complexity of human environments.
