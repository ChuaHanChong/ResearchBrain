# Can RL Improve Generalization of LLM Agents? An Empirical Study

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical study from Fudan NLP Lab systematically investigates whether Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) improves the generalization of Large Language Model (LLM) agents to unseen interactive environments. The research reveals RFT's robust generalization within environments and its capacity for positive transfer and anti-forgetting in sequential multi-environment training, while also identifying environment-dependent limitations and common failure modes.

## Problem

- Existing empirical evidence for Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) in LLM agents primarily relies on in-domain evaluations, which does not reflect real-world scenarios with unseen environments.
- A gap exists in understanding how RFT-trained LLM agents generalize across varying task difficulties and to fundamentally different environments with shifts in background knowledge, observation spaces, and action interfaces.
- Previous generalization research on LLMs has mostly focused on static/single-turn tasks or supervised fine-tuning (SFT) vs. RFT, without systematically exploring these effects in multi-turn LLM agents across diverse interactive environments.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical study using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==-based ==Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)== with Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct models.
- Evaluated RFT generalization across five diverse agentic environments (WebShop, SearchQA, TextCraft, Alf World, BabyAI) along three axes: intra-environment generalization (task difficulty), inter-environment generalization (unseen environments), and sequential multi-environment training (transfer and forgetting).
- Performed detailed failure mode analysis using GPT-5-mini to categorize common error types and provided qualitative case studies.

## Results

- Training on easy tasks improved performance on hard test sets by up to **60.1 points** (WebShop 7B model), and an easy-to-hard curriculum enhanced BabyAI performance by **2.0–3.3 points**.
- Held-in performance gains were substantial (e.g., Alf World **78.62 points** for 3B models), but average held-out improvements were more modest (**3.32–3.44 points**), with negative transfer observed in some cases (e.g., BabyAI-trained 7B model on WebShop dropped from **28.59 to 10.25**).
- Sequential RFT preserved upstream WebShop performance (**86.5 to 86.32**) while improving TextCraft (**80.88 to 82.50**) after fine-tuning, with 'Confirmation Bias' and 'Guessing or Fabrication' identified as prevalent failure modes across generalization settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RFT significantly enhances intra-environment generalization, with curriculum learning strategies (easy-to-hard) further improving performance and agent efficiency.
> - Positive transfer to unseen environments is observed, but a notable performance gap persists between held-in (training) and held-out (unseen) environments, with transfer effectiveness highly dependent on target environment characteristics.
> - Sequential RFT demonstrates strong anti-forgetting capabilities, largely preserving upstream task performance while achieving comparable or superior downstream performance to single-task training, and often matching joint training performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) has emerged as a method for training Large Language Model (LLM) agents to perform multi-turn decision-making tasks by leveraging environment feedback. This approach has been associated with enhancing agentic behaviors such as instruction following, planning, reasoning, and tool use in diverse applications like web navigation and software engineering.

However, the existing empirical evidence for RFT primarily stems from in-domain evaluations, where training and testing are conducted within the same environment or on closely related tasks. This practice leaves a gap in understanding how RFT-trained agents perform in real-world scenarios where they frequently encounter unseen environments. Such novel environments can present differences in background knowledge, observation spaces, and action interfaces, fundamentally altering interaction dynamics. Previous work on generalization in LLMs has largely focused on static or single-turn tasks, or on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) versus RFT in terms of catastrophic forgetting, but has not systematically explored these effects in multi-turn LLM agents across diverse interactive environments.

This paper addresses this gap by systematically investigating the generalization and transferability of RFT-trained LLM agents across varying task difficulties and distinct environments. It expands the current understanding beyond in-domain performance to characterize RFT's efficacy under distribution shifts, including its ability to transfer learned policies and its susceptibility to forgetting in sequential learning paradigms. By examining these aspects, the work aims to provide a more comprehensive profile of RFT's impact on LLM agent generalization, contributing to the development of more robust and deployable agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation of this research is to evaluate whether the performance improvements achieved through Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) for Large Language Model (LLM) agents generalize beyond their training distribution. While RFT has demonstrated effectiveness in enhancing LLM agents for complex interactive tasks, most evaluations have been confined to in-domain settings, where training and testing environments are identical or highly similar. In practical deployment, LLM agents are expected to operate in unseen environments with variations in background knowledge, observation spaces, and action interfaces. This disparity between current evaluation practices and real-world deployment needs raises a significant research question regarding the generalization capabilities of RFT-trained agents.

To systematically characterize the generalization profile of RFT under such environmental shifts, the study sets forth three specific objectives:

1. **To investigate intra-environment generalization across task difficulty:** This objective aims to determine if RFT policies learned on a subset of tasks can transfer to other tasks of varying difficulty levels within the *same* environment. This involves assessing performance when agents are trained on easy tasks and tested on hard tasks, or vice-versa, and exploring the impact of curriculum learning approaches.
2. **To examine inter-environment generalization to unseen environments:** This objective seeks to assess whether RFT maintains its efficacy when agents are deployed in *unseen* environments that differ not only in task instances but also in fundamental properties such as background knowledge, observation spaces, and action interfaces. The study quantifies performance shifts when an agent is trained in one environment and evaluated in another.
3. **To analyze sequential multi-environment training to quantify transfer and forgetting:** This objective focuses on understanding the dynamics of transfer and forgetting when RFT is applied sequentially across multiple environments. It compares the retention of upstream task performance with adaptation to new downstream environments, and also contrasts sequential training with joint training across a mixture of environments.

Collectively, these objectives aim to establish a systematic framework for understanding how RFT influences the generalization and transfer behaviors of LLM agents, thereby providing insights that can inform the development and practical deployment of more robust and adaptable agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic empirical study across five diverse agentic environments to assess the generalization capabilities of RFT-trained LLM agents.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation and Policy Optimization

The study adopts the ReAct interaction paradigm, formulating multi-turn decision-making tasks as a tuple (U, S, A, O, T, R), representing instruction space, state space, action space, observation space, state transition function, and reward function, respectively. An agent, parameterized by an LLM ($\pi_\theta$), generates actions based on the current state (previous dialogues and observations), and receives an observation from the environment. The process continues until task completion or maximum turns are reached, yielding a trajectory ($\tau$). A terminal reward $R(\tau)$ is provided upon task completion.

The objective is to optimize policy parameters $\theta$ to maximize the expected cumulative reward $J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta}[R(\tau)]$. Policy gradient methods are utilized for optimization, specifically the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm. GRPO reduces variance by estimating baselines using group relative advantages from a group of sampled outputs, eliminating the need for a separate critic network. It incorporates PPO-style clipping and a KL divergence penalty for training stability.

#### 3.2. Experimental Settings

- **LLM Models:** The study uses Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as base LLMs.
- **Environments:** Five distinct agentic environments are selected, varying in characteristics such as reward density, action validation strictness, provision of valid action lists, world knowledge requirements, and observation structure:
 - **WebShop:** Interactive web shopping (dense rewards, knowledge required, structured observations).
 - **SearchQA:** Q&A with search engine context (binary rewards, strict action validation, knowledge required, structured observations).
 - **TextCraft:** Text-based crafting game (binary rewards, strict action validation, unstructured observations).
 - **Alf World:** Household tasks (binary rewards, strict action validation, unstructured observations).
 - **BabyAI:** Interactive grid world simulator (dense rewards, valid action lists provided, unstructured observations).
 Task data is sourced from AgentGym.
- **Training Setup:** RFT training is performed using the AgentGym-RL framework. For each task, 8 trajectories are sampled, and the maximum response length is 8192 tokens. Maximum interaction turns ($K$) are set per environment (10 for WebShop, 5 for SearchQA, 30 for Alf World, 10 for BabyAI, 15 for TextCraft).
- **Evaluation Setup:** The primary metric is avg@8 (exact matching success rate). Average interaction turns ($\bar{k}$) and generated tokens ($\bar{l}$) are also measured for efficiency. All agents are evaluated with a maximum of 20 interaction turns.

#### 3.3. Experimental Axes

1. **Intra-Environment Generalization Across Task Difficulties:**
 - Tasks within each environment (U) are categorized into "easy" ($\text{U}_{\text{easy}}$) and "hard" ($\text{U}_{\text{hard}}$) based on the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct base model's performance, ensuring balanced data distribution for both training and test sets.
 - Agents are trained using RFT on $\text{U}_{\text{easy}}$, $\text{U}_{\text{hard}}$, the full mixture (U), and sequentially ($\text{U}_{\text{easy}} + \text{U}_{\text{hard}}$ or $\text{U}_{\text{hard}} + \text{U}_{\text{easy}}$).
 - Evaluation is performed on easy, hard, and all tasks within the same environment.

2. **Inter-Environment Generalization to Unseen Environments:**
 - RFT training is conducted exclusively within a *single* source environment.
 - The trained agent is then evaluated on *all other* (unseen, held-out) environments, as well as on its training (held-in) environment.
 - Three metrics quantify performance improvement over the baseline (base model):
 - $\Delta$Held-In: Average improvement when training and test environments coincide.
 - $\Delta$Held-Out: Average improvement when the agent is evaluated on environments different from the training environment.
 - $\Delta$Overall: Average improvement across all evaluation settings.

3. **Sequential RFT Across Environments (Transfer and Forgetting):**
 - **Two-stage experiments:** 20 experiments are conducted by sequentially training an agent on a first environment (upstream), followed by fine-tuning on a second environment (downstream). Performance retention on the upstream environment and gains on the downstream environment are assessed.
 - **Five-stage experiments:** Multi-stage sequential training is performed across all five environments in different orders.
 - **Joint training:** As a baseline for multi-environment training, an agent is trained simultaneously on a randomly mixed dataset from all five environments.
 - Anti-forgetting (upstream performance retention) and transferability (downstream performance gains) are key evaluation aspects.

#### 3.4. Further Analysis

- **Failure Mode Analysis:** A fine-grained error analysis is performed using GPT-5-mini to categorize failed trajectories into 8 common failure modes (e.g., Instruction Misinterpretation, Confirmation Bias, Action Execution Failure). This is applied to both intra- and inter-environment evaluations.
- **Case Studies:** Qualitative case studies are provided to illustrate mechanisms behind successful or failed transfers between environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical study produced several findings regarding the generalization capabilities of RFT-trained LLM agents across different experimental axes.

#### 4.1. Intra-Environment Generalization Across Task Difficulties

RFT demonstrated significant generalization robustness within a consistent environment, showing strong transferability across tasks of varying difficulty levels.
- **Performance Gains:** Training on easy tasks significantly improved performance on hard test sets. For example, the 7B model on WebShop, when trained on easy tasks, showed a 60.1-point improvement on the hard test set. Conversely, training on hard tasks generally yielded greater overall gains, which was attributed to the richer failure signals and longer-horizon exploration necessitated by harder tasks.
- **Curriculum Learning Benefits:** An easy-to-hard curriculum learning strategy (training on easy tasks first, then hard tasks) further enhanced performance. On BabyAI, this sequence improved performance by 2.0 to 3.3 points compared to training on single-difficulty levels.
- **Efficiency:** RFT training also led to more efficient exploration within the same environment. Agents showed a reduction in average interaction turns and generated tokens, indicating concise and goal-directed behavior (e.g., in BabyAI, average turns for the 3B model decreased from 10.76 to 4.19, and generated tokens from 624.58 to 160.60).

#### 4.2. Inter-Environment Generalization to Unseen Environments

Generalization across different environments was observed, but with a notable performance gap between held-in and held-out settings.
- **Held-in vs. Held-out Performance:** Substantial performance gains were achieved in held-in conditions (e.g., Alf World showed 78.62 and 65.44 points improvement for 3B and 7B models, respectively). In contrast, held-out (unseen) environments showed more modest improvements, averaging 3.32 and 3.44 points for 3B and 7B models, respectively.
- **Positive Transfer:** Most agents exhibited positive transferability to unseen environments. Agents trained on WebShop, Alf World, and SearchQA consistently showed positive $\Delta$Held-Out. The similarity between search-based environments like WebShop and SearchQA facilitated positive transfer, as agents learned to formulate flexible queries and extract information efficiently.
- **Negative Transfer:** Negative effects sometimes occurred. For instance, the 7B model trained on BabyAI showed an average negative improvement of -3.23 on held-out tasks, with a sharp drop on WebShop (from 28.59 to 10.25). This was attributed to the agent becoming over-reliant on BabyAI's specific feature of providing available actions per step, leading to a decline in long-horizon reasoning and an inability to adapt to environments without such explicit guidance.
- **Environment Dependency:** Cross-environment generalization performance varied significantly based on the target environment. Generalization was more effective to environments like TextCraft and BabyAI (less reliance on specific background knowledge), but more challenging for Alf World and SearchQA (strict action validation, sparse feedback, and lack of instructional guidance for invalid actions).

#### 4.3. Sequential RFT Across Environments (Transfer and Forgetting)

Sequential training strategies across multiple environments demonstrated effective transfer and resistance to catastrophic forgetting.
- **Anti-forgetting and Transferability:** Sequential RFT largely preserved upstream performance while matching or exceeding single-task performance on downstream environments. For example, an agent pre-trained on WebShop and then trained on TextCraft maintained its WebShop performance (86.5 to 86.32) while improving TextCraft performance (80.88 to 82.50). This suggests that sequential training equips agents with stable transfer capabilities and resistance to forgetting.
- **Correlation with Single-Environment Generalization:** Generalization patterns in multi-environment training aligned with those observed in single-environment settings. Environments that yielded poor single-environment generalization (e.g., BabyAI) continued to be detrimental in sequential training, negatively impacting performance on other environments.
- **Impact of Training Order:** The sequence of training environments significantly influenced generalization performance in held-out environments. An "easy-to-hard" curriculum, such as training on BabyAI then SearchQA (BA+SQ), outperformed the reverse order (SQ+BA) by substantial margins on target environments, likely due to the difficulty characteristics of the environments (BabyAI providing detailed feedback vs. SearchQA imposing strict constraints).
- **Comparison to Joint Training:** Sequential training achieved performance comparable to joint training (training on a mixture of data from all environments), even after training on five distinct tasks. The final performance was relatively insensitive to the training order, which was attributed to RFT's capability to preserve acquired knowledge. However, pronounced forgetting could occur for environments difficult to generalize to (e.g., Alf World and SearchQA) during long-term sequential training.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis: Failure Mode Analysis

An analysis of failure modes identified common error types across environments and generalization settings:
- **Confirmation Bias:** This was a prevalent failure mode across all scenarios (>10%), indicating agents' overconfidence and lack of self-reflection or verification based on environmental feedback.
- **Guessing or Fabrication:** Particularly widespread in SearchQA (23.8% in held-in, 21.2% in held-out settings), this suggested a deficiency in tool utilization and external information gathering.
- **Shift in Failure Modes:** When training and test environments differed, the distribution of failure modes shifted. For instance, "State or Memory Inconsistency" errors in WebShop increased from 4.3% (held-in) to 21.9% (held-out), indicating struggles with coherent decision-making and salient information extraction in novel contexts with large information volumes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes a systematic and comprehensive empirical study on how Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) influences the transfer and generalization capabilities of Large Language Model (LLM) agents in multi-turn decision-making tasks. Addressing a notable gap in the existing literature, which largely focuses on in-domain evaluations, this work provides a foundational understanding of RFT's performance under various distribution shifts.

The findings offer several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Comprehensive Generalization Profile:** By systematically investigating generalization across task difficulty within environments, transfer to unseen environments, and dynamics of multi-environment training, the study constructs a detailed generalization profile of RFT-trained LLM agents. This characterization helps to move beyond anecdotal evidence of RFT's efficacy.
- **Identification of Generalization Factors:** The research identifies specific factors that influence RFT's generalization, including shifts in semantic priors, observation spaces, action interfaces, and the benefits of curriculum learning and specific training orders. This provides actionable insights for researchers and practitioners aiming to improve agent robustness.
- **Understanding Transfer and Forgetting:** The analysis of sequential training elucidates RFT's capacity for anti-forgetting and positive transfer, demonstrating that it can build upon previously acquired knowledge without severe degradation of past performance. This understanding is crucial for designing continuously learning and adaptive agents.
- **Pinpointing Failure Mechanisms:** The detailed failure mode analysis highlights common breakdowns in agent reasoning and interaction in different generalization scenarios. Identifying issues like "Confirmation Bias" and "Guessing or Fabrication" provides specific targets for future agent design and training improvements, particularly concerning self-correction and tool utilization.
- **Practical Guidance for Deployment:** The results offer practical guidance for training and evaluating LLM agents intended for real-world deployment, where encountering unseen tasks and environments is a norm. The insights regarding environment characteristics that hinder or facilitate transfer can inform environment selection and curriculum design.
- **Advancing Generalizable LLM Agents:** Ultimately, this work informs the development of more generalizable and reliable LLM agents. By understanding the conditions under which RFT succeeds and fails in transferring learned behaviors, the research lays groundwork for building agents that can adapt more effectively to the complexities and variability of real-world interactive systems.
