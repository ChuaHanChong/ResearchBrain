# World in World: Explore the World with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> World in World (WiW) is a training-free interface for controlling a frozen causal video world model. It turns source-video frames, target-view depth projections, rendered subject geometry and retrieved generated history into camera- and time-labelled clean K/V states, read through native self-attention. Correspondence-guided routing and evidence-wise attention CFG steer and weight this evidence, giving the best VBench Overall and lowest camera errors in camera-controlled rerendering.

## Problem

- Camera-controlled rerendering and world models add each control type through its own conditioning module, so new evidence types or combinations need redesign or extra training.
- Rolling-cache causal video models only see recent states, so earlier generated appearance and layout are lost when the camera revisits a region.
- Appearance-based attention becomes ambiguous under large viewpoint changes, dynamic motion and repeated textures, and source views cannot supply subject surfaces the source video never observed.

## Method

- Shared ==visual-evidence interface==: each evidence channel is encoded at timestep zero into clean K/V with camera poses, ==RoPE== event-time coordinates, token-level spatial support weights and per-denoising-step activation, then attached as temporary auxiliary attention blocks.
- Complementary evidence sources: DepthCrafter-based target-view projection of source frames, LHM++ avatar renders driven by SMPL-X for newly exposed surfaces, and a history bank retrieving diverse top-K evicted states by coverage and viewing direction.
- ==Correspondence-guided attention routing== (**CGAR**) adds log correspondence weights from persistent point tracks to source-video attention logits; ==evidence-wise attention CFG== (**EWA**) amplifies each channel's response component orthogonal to the native response, adding no network evaluations.

## Results

- On DAVIS and OpenVid-1M rerendering, **WiW** reaches VBench Overall **85.19** versus **84.30** for UniWorld-View, the strongest of six baselines including ReCamMaster, TrajectoryCrafter and InSpatio-World.
- Camera control improves to **2.83** degrees rotation error versus **3.19** for CameraAnything, with translation error **0.0686** and the best PSNR (**23.15**), SSIM (**0.787**) and LPIPS (**0.1217**).
- Removing target-view warping is the most damaging ablation, raising rotation and translation errors about **3.4x** and **10.9x**; removing CGAR and EWA raises rotation error from **1.78** to **2.03** degrees.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adding controls to a pretrained causal video model can become an evidence construction and orchestration problem: write conditions as clean cached states the native self-attention already reads, instead of training adapters.
> - Explicit geometric layout references matter far more than attention-level refinements for camera following; routing and guidance tweaks give only small gains once warping is present.
> - Guiding each evidence channel along its direction orthogonal to the native attention response offers a cheap, per-source alternative to full classifier-free guidance passes, without extra network evaluations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges within the field of video generation, particularly focusing on autoregressive video world models. Recent advancements in this area have significantly improved visual quality, motion realism, and temporal coherence, enabling the development of models that support interactive, long-horizon exploration of virtual environments. Unlike conventional text- or image-conditioned systems that generate finite clips from pre-specified conditions, video world models aim to continually predict subsequent observations as users interactively change camera positions, viewing directions, or control actions. This capability extends video generation from merely producing recordings to supporting the exploration of evolving visual worlds, with implications for interactive content creation, virtual production, game generation, and embodied-agent simulation.

A key challenge within this landscape is to extend the controllability of large, pretrained world models without requiring extensive retraining or the development of task-specific modules. Existing methods for camera control, source-video rerendering, geometric control, and long-term memory often rely on control-specific pathways or representations. This necessitates redesign or additional training when new types of evidence or control combinations are introduced. For instance, maintaining synchronization with a recorded event from new viewpoints, accurately placing observed content, plausibly completing newly exposed regions, and recovering previously generated appearance on revisits are common requirements that existing methods typically address through specialized components.

This paper proposes an alternative by identifying that causal video world models, equipped with a clean-state cache, already possess a shared entry point for visual information: their native self-attention mechanism. The research positions itself as a solution to enhance the flexible controllability of these models by converting heterogeneous control information into a visual representation that the pretrained model inherently understands and processes, thereby reframing the problem from "model adaptation" to "visual evidence construction and orchestration." This approach aims to leverage the inherent capabilities of frozen backbones, making them more versatile across diverse control tasks without modification to their learned parameters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a training-free inference-time interface that significantly extends the flexible control capabilities of frozen causal video world models. The goal is to enable interactive, long-horizon exploration of dynamic events recorded in source videos, allowing users to navigate these worlds along new camera trajectories while preserving the original event's appearance and temporal progression.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current video world models and their control mechanisms:

- **Limited Flexible Control:** While autoregressive video world models support exploration, achieving flexible and diverse control over the generated content, especially from new viewpoints, remains challenging. Current systems often rely on control-specific modules or additional training for each new control modality, leading to a rigid and resource-intensive development cycle.
- **Maintaining Event Synchronization:** When exploring a source video from novel viewpoints, the generated rollout must remain temporally synchronized with the recorded event. This involves preserving the original dynamics and appearance of the scene.
- **Accurate Spatial Placement and Occlusion Handling:** Under varying camera poses, observed content needs to be correctly placed in the requested target view, respecting changes in perspective and occlusion relationships.
- **Plausible Completion of Novel Regions:** Large viewpoint changes frequently expose regions of the scene or subject surfaces that were not visible in the original source video. The model must be able to plausibly complete these newly exposed areas using its generative prior.
- **Long-Horizon Consistency and Revisiting:** For extended explorations, particularly when revisiting previously generated regions, the model needs to recover earlier appearance and spatial layout to maintain scene consistency. The finite rolling cache in causal models typically evicts older states, making this challenging.
- **Integration of Heterogeneous Evidence:** To achieve comprehensive control, the model needs to integrate complementary visual evidence from various sources, such as source observations, rendered geometry, and generated historical data. These sources often differ in their representation, spatial coverage, and temporal relevance, posing a challenge for unified integration.

The key observation motivating the proposed solution is that causal video world models already process visual information as "clean visual states" through their native self-attention during autoregressive generation. This pathway is used to read initial observations and recently finalized outputs, along with camera and temporal encodings. The researchers hypothesize that external control information can be converted into this same "clean visual state" representation, allowing the pretrained model to read and utilize it directly through its existing attention layers, thereby avoiding the need for model adaptation or learning separate control pathways. This reframes the problem of extending world-model control as one of constructing useful visual evidence, localizing the relevant evidence, and regulating its influence on generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

World in World (WiW) introduces a training-free, visual-evidence interface designed to extend the control capabilities of frozen causal video world models. The methodology focuses on converting diverse visual conditions into a standardized "clean visual state" representation that can be directly consumed by the model's native self-attention mechanism. The framework is instantiated on the publicly released causal-fast checkpoint of LingBot-World 2.0, with all pretrained parameters kept frozen.

**Shared Evidence Interface:**
At its core, WiW defines a shared interface for all types of visual evidence. At each denoising step `s`, raw evidence `E_b` from a channel `b` and a target camera trajectory `C` is mapped to a tuple `(X_b, P_b, ω_b, γ_b(s))`. `X_b` represents the visual content, `P_b` contains per-frame camera intrinsics, poses, and event-time indices, `ω_bj ∈` specifies the spatial support for each evidence token `j`, and `γ_b(s) ∈ {0, 1}` indicates whether the channel is active at step `s`.

To make this evidence usable, the visual content `X_b` and camera/temporal information `P_b` are fed into the frozen video backbone (with diffusion timestep `t=0`) to extract layer-specific keys (`K_l_b`) and values (`V_l_b`), which are then cached as clean K/V pairs. These cached features guide generation during the current denoising process. Source-video evidence occupies designated source-anchor slots within the native attention block, while other evidence types are attached as temporary auxiliary attention blocks, which are removed once the current chunk of frames is finalized. The model reuses its existing Q/K/V projections and preserves native text and camera conditioning pathways. Rotary position embeddings (RoPE) are employed to integrate relative temporal information, and spatial support weights `ω_bj` scale attention contributions, while `γ_b(s)` controls channel activation across denoising stages.

**Complementary Visual Evidence Construction:**
WiW constructs multiple forms of visual evidence to address various control requirements:

1. **Target-View Scene Evidence:** This evidence provides an explicit spatial layout reference. For a target frame `m` and a temporally aligned source view `r`, source RGB (`I_src_r,m`) and estimated depth (`D_src_r,m`) (from DepthCrafter) are used. Source pixels are back-projected into 3D and then reprojected from the source camera (`C_src_r,m`) to the target camera (`C_m`). The output includes target-view RGB (`X_proj_r,m`), a binary visibility mask (`M_proj_r,m`), and target-camera depth (`Z_proj_r,m`). This projected RGB is encoded with target-camera and event-time information, and the visibility mask is converted into token-level spatial support weights (`ω_proj`). This accounts for occlusion and geometric errors by leveraging geometric reliability.

2. **Rendered Geometry Evidence:** To guide the completion of newly exposed subject surfaces not visible in the source video, a renderable subject representation (`G_m`) is used. For human subjects, `G_m = (A, Θ_m)` where `A` is an avatar (reconstructed via LHM++) and `Θ_m` are per-frame SMPL-X parameters. This representation is rendered from the target camera (`C_m`) to produce target-view RGB (`X_geo_m`), a geometric support mask (`M_geo_m`), and target-camera depth (`Z_geo_m`). These renderings provide shape and appearance proposals for unseen regions, with the support mask converted to token-level weights.

3. **Retrieved Generated Evidence:** To enable consistent long-horizon revisits beyond the limited rolling cache, a rollout-wide history bank is maintained. This bank archives per-layer clean K/V features of evicted finalized states, along with their camera poses and temporal indices. When the camera revisits an earlier region, a bounded number of relevant historical states are selected from the bank (based on target-view surface coverage and viewing-direction compatibility) and retrieved. Their K/V features are directly read by the model through temporary auxiliary attention blocks.

**Attention Routing and Guidance:**
To ensure evidence is correctly localized and its influence regulated, WiW implements two mechanisms:

1. **Correspondence-Guided Attention Routing (CGAR):** This mechanism addresses ambiguities in appearance-based attention under viewpoint changes or repeated textures. It tracks points with persistent identities in the source video and combines depth and camera information to establish token-level correspondences between the current generation view and the source video. For geometrically matched query `i` and source-video key `j`, a logarithmic correspondence weight `log β_ij` is added to the attention score: `ℓ_corr_ij = (q_i^T k_j / √d_h) + log β_ij`. This guides queries towards geometrically corresponding source-video tokens.

2. **Evidence-Wise Attention CFG (EWA):** Inspired by classifier-free guidance, EWA independently regulates the guidance strength of each evidence source. For a given attention layer and queries, `o_0` represents the response from the native block, and `o_e` is the jointly normalized response with evidence `e`. EWA strengthens only the complementary information introduced by `o_e` relative to `o_0`. The updated response `˜o_e` is computed as `˜o_e = o_e + g_e * max(0, cos(o_e, o_0)) * [o_e - Proj_o0(o_e)]`, where `g_e` is an independent guidance strength. This formulation prevents redundant amplification of native responses. EWA operates directly on attention responses within the same denoising forward pass, reusing existing outputs, and thus introduces no additional network function evaluations (NFE).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of World in World (WiW) was evaluated through camera-controlled video rerendering experiments, long-horizon revisiting, human-motion transfer, and ablations of its individual components.

**Quantitative Comparisons:**
On the DAVIS and OpenVid-1M datasets, WiW was quantitatively compared against several baselines, including ReCamMaster, TrajectoryCrafter, WorldForge, InSpatio-World, UniWorld-View, and CameraAnything. Performance was assessed using VBench dimensions (Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality, Temporal Flickering, Motion Smoothness, Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Dynamic Degree), camera trajectory errors (Translation Error, Rotation Error), and image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS).

- **Overall Performance:** WiW achieved the highest overall VBench score (85.192), which is an average across the seven reported VBench dimensions, indicating superior perceptual quality, temporal consistency, and adherence to dynamics compared to all other methods.
- **Camera Accuracy:** WiW demonstrated the lowest camera trajectory errors, with a Translation Error of 0.068622 and a Rotation Error of 2.8326 degrees, suggesting its generated outputs align more precisely with the specified target camera paths.
- **Image Fidelity:** The method also achieved the highest PSNR (23.1511) and SSIM (0.787205) values, along with a tied-best LPIPS score (0.121664), indicating higher image fidelity and similarity to expected outputs.

**Qualitative Comparisons:**
Qualitative results illustrated camera-controlled rerendering under diverse camera motions (e.g., orbit, rotate, move). These examples visually confirmed WiW's ability to maintain subject and scene consistency, accurately control viewpoint, and plausibly complete newly exposed regions in the generated videos.

**Additional Applications:**
The framework’s versatility was demonstrated across a range of applications beyond basic rerendering, all utilizing the same frozen backbone and visual-evidence interface:

- **Bullet-time rendering:** Freezing the event's progression while allowing camera movement.
- **Video stabilization:** Correcting camera shake to produce a smoother video.
- **Video editing:** Modifying scene content, such as adding objects or changing appearances.
- **Cross-model communication (K/V sharing):** Sharing cached K/V features between different instances of the frozen model to guide new generations, showcasing flexible information transfer.
- **Human motion transfer:** Applying motion from a driving video to a subject in the generated scene.

These demonstrations support the claim that WiW provides a unified and training-free approach to extend world model control.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of individual components:

- **Impact of Target-View Warping and Source-Camera Conditioning:** Removing target-view warping resulted in the most significant degradation across all metrics, with rotation errors increasing approximately 3.4 times and translation errors increasing about 10.9 times compared to the full method. Removing source-camera Plücker conditioning also led to increased camera errors. These findings emphasize the importance of providing explicit spatial layout references and retaining source-view camera information for accurate control.
- **Impact of CGAR and EWA:** The absence of both Correspondence-Guided Attention Routing (CGAR) and Evidence-Wise Attention CFG (EWA) resulted in higher camera errors and degraded VBench scores. Separately, removing EWA led to an increase in rotation error (from 1.7823 to 1.9944 degrees), while removing CGAR had a smaller impact on rotation error but a noticeable effect on translation error, indicating both mechanisms contribute to maintaining subject appearance, background structure, and accurate routing.
- **Importance of Rendered Geometry and Historical Retrieval:** Qualitative ablations demonstrated that rendered 3D body geometry effectively guides the completion of newly exposed subject regions. Furthermore, historical retrieval was shown to be crucial for preserving appearance and layout when the camera revisits previously generated regions that have been evicted from the rolling cache.

These results collectively indicate that the proposed mechanisms and evidence sources are integral to WiW's performance in achieving controllable, consistent, and high-quality video generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The World in World (WiW) framework represents a significant contribution to the field of controllable video generation and world modeling. Its primary significance lies in introducing a novel, training-free visual-evidence interface that extends the control capabilities of frozen causal video world models. This approach shifts the paradigm from requiring specialized modules or additional training for each control task to a unified system for constructing, localizing, and regulating visual evidence.

The potential impact of WiW is multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Generalizability and Flexibility:** By leveraging the native self-attention mechanism of pretrained models, WiW allows a single, frozen backbone to accept diverse forms of visual control and world information. This generalizability is demonstrated through its ability to support camera-controlled rerendering, long-horizon revisiting, human-motion transfer, bullet-time effects, video stabilization, video editing, and cross-model communication. This flexibility significantly reduces the development overhead associated with adapting world models for new applications.
- **Improved Performance and Consistency:** The quantitative and qualitative evaluations show that WiW achieves state-of-the-art performance in camera-controlled video rerendering. Its superior scores in VBench dimensions, lower camera trajectory errors, and higher image fidelity metrics indicate that the framework produces visually consistent, temporally coherent, and accurately controlled video content. The ability to complete newly exposed regions plausibly and recover past generated states for long-horizon consistency addresses critical limitations of existing methods.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The Evidence-Wise Attention CFG (EWA) mechanism operates directly on attention responses within the same denoising forward pass and reuses existing computations. This design choice means that EWA introduces no additional network function evaluations (NFE) for guidance, contributing to the computational efficiency of the control process.
- **Broader Application Scope:** The ability to interactively explore and manipulate dynamic worlds within a video opens up new possibilities for various domains. This includes facilitating more intuitive and powerful tools for interactive content creation, virtual production pipelines, generation of dynamic game environments, and advanced simulation for embodied agents. The demonstrated applications in video editing and stabilization further broaden its utility.
- **Direction for Future Research:** WiW's success in decoupling model training from control extension suggests a promising direction for future research. Instead of continuously retraining or fine-tuning large generative models for specific control objectives, future efforts could focus on developing more sophisticated methods for constructing, localizing, and regulating external visual evidence. This could unlock even more advanced and fine-grained control over complex generative processes, potentially leading to foundational models that are inherently more adaptable and usable across an expansive range of tasks.
