# Efficient Real-World Online Reinforcement Learning for Robot Manipulation via Centralized Training and Critic Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> HARC is a real-world online RL framework for robot manipulation that splits hybrid actions into a continuous Cartesian arm actor and a discrete gripper actor trained under centralized training with decentralized execution, sharing one critic decomposed into task and grasp heads via a Hybrid Reward Architecture. Under 5-25x larger domain randomization, it lifts average real-task success from 40% to 75% and simulated block relocation from 25% to 95%.

## Problem

- Existing human-in-the-loop real-world RL systems train under tightly constrained workspaces, centimeter-scale randomization and narrow viewpoints, so learned policies generalize poorly.
- Hybrid manipulation actions mix continuous motion with discrete grasping; discretizing or independently optimizing separate policies creates non-stationary dynamics and unstable credit assignment.
- Noisy real RGB and proprioceptive observations inflate critic gradient norms, making a single monolithic Q-function's long-horizon temporal-difference regression unstable and prone to collapse.

## Method

- A ==CTDE== formulation replaces the prior discrete Q-learning gripper module with a discrete ==soft actor-critic== policy, so continuous arm and discrete gripper actors share one centralized critic evaluating joint actions.
- ==Hybrid Reward Architecture== splits the critic into task and grasp heads over a shared trunk, each regressing its own reward channel with separate temporal-difference targets and gradient paths.
- A ==potential-based grasp reward== derived from clipped normalized gripper torque or width, plus a switching penalty, feeds actor objectives recombined as task value plus weighted grasp value.
- A tendon-coupled compliant two-finger gripper adds passive fingertip and enveloping grasps without extra actuators or added action dimensions.

## Results

- Success rates rise from **60%** to **80%** on tennis-ball pick-and-place, **60%** to **90%** on banana pick-and-place, and **25%** to **95%** on simulated humanoid block relocation versus the baseline.
- The pot reset task reaches **55%** success where the baseline consistently fails at **0%**, with average real-task success climbing from **40%** to **75%** after **160 minutes** of training.
- Expert supervision drops substantially: equivalent demonstration episodes total **69**, **76**, **111** and **115** versus **80**, **102**, **132** and **189** for the baseline, with intervention rates converging to **0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing a value function along largely disjoint reward channels yields lower-variance targets, a cheap fix when noisy real observations, not representation quality, bottleneck critic learning.
> - A shared centralized critic can absorb the non-stationarity of concurrently trained heterogeneous actors while keeping execution decentralized on local, even restricted, observations.
> - Mechanical compliance can absorb task variability that would otherwise inflate the learned action space, keeping a binary gripper interface while widening the graspable object set.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Robotic manipulation remains a complex challenge, particularly regarding robust and efficient deployment in real-world scenarios. Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers an approach to learn behaviors through trial-and-error, bypassing manual controller design. However, policies trained in simulation often exhibit performance degradation when transferred to physical robots due to discrepancies between simulation and reality (the "sim-to-real gap"). This issue is particularly pronounced in contact-rich interaction tasks.

To address the sim-to-real gap, real-world online RL with human-in-the-loop (HIL) intervention has gained attention. Approaches like SERL and HIL-SERL have demonstrated sample-efficient learning by incorporating human guidance. However, these methods typically operate within limited domain randomization, constrained workspaces, and restricted viewpoints. This limitation hinders policy robustness and generalization, especially for long-horizon manipulation in diverse environments. Furthermore, real-world manipulation often involves hybrid action spaces, combining continuous end-effector motions with discrete gripper commands. Learning these hybrid actions with a single policy can introduce non-differentiable boundaries or lead to non-stationarity when optimized as independent policies. Existing solutions, such as discretizing continuous actions or using separate Q-learning agents, present their own challenges. Additionally, high-dimensional, noisy RGB observations in real-world settings can destabilize critic gradients and slow convergence, particularly in sparse-reward scenarios.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that aims to improve policy performance under large-scale domain randomization and enhance training stability and sample efficiency in real-world online RL. It positions itself as an advancement over existing HIL-SERL pipelines by introducing architectural enhancements specifically designed for robustness across diverse environments, tasks, and robotic platforms, while handling hybrid action spaces and noisy visual inputs.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a visual manipulation framework that improves sample efficiency and policy performance for robotic manipulation tasks under large-scale domain randomization. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Overcoming limitations of prior real-world online RL:** Existing methods are often evaluated in constrained settings with limited domain randomization (e.g., small x-y variations, restricted rotations). This limits policy robustness and generalization to more diverse and realistic environments. The goal is to enable robust policies that can operate across wide workspace variations and diverse object configurations.
- **Addressing challenges of hybrid action spaces:** Robotic manipulation tasks frequently require both continuous control (e.g., Cartesian arm pose) and discrete control (e.g., gripper open/close). Learning such hybrid action spaces with conventional RL policies is challenging. A single policy might face non-differentiable decision boundaries, while independently optimized policies can introduce non-stationarity, complicating credit assignment and leading to unstable optimization. The motivation is to cleanly represent and optimize both action modalities while stabilizing multi-agent learning.
- **Stabilizing learning from noisy real-world observations:** Real-world RGB observations are inherently noisier than low-dimensional state representations or simulated views. This noise can propagate through the visual encoder, destabilizing critic gradients and slowing convergence, particularly in sparse-reward settings. The objective is to develop a critic architecture that is more resilient to noisy high-dimensional observations, improving the stability and efficiency of value estimation.
- **Improving sample efficiency and training stability:** Despite advances in sample-efficient online RL, expert teleoperation remains time-consuming. Reducing the need for continuous human intervention and accelerating learning convergence are critical for practical real-world deployment. The framework aims to reduce the total expert data required for learning and achieve faster convergence.
- **Enabling practical deployment across diverse platforms:** The research aims to develop a unified framework capable of operating across various robotic platforms (different arms, humanoid robots) and tasks, demonstrating broad applicability and robustness.

To achieve these objectives, the paper introduces a framework combining centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) for hybrid action learning and a Hybrid Reward Architecture (HRA) with a centralized multi-head critic.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed methodology, termed Hybrid Actors with a Reward-decomposed Critic (HARC), integrates several components within an RLPD-based real-world online RL pipeline. The overall framework involves asynchronous training where local actors collect experience, and a cloud-based learner optimizes the actor-critic, followed by periodic policy synchronization.

**A. Sample-Efficient RL with Prior Data (RLPD-based Foundation)**
The foundation of the framework is RLPD, an off-policy model-free RL approach utilizing Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) as the underlying learner. Training leverages a replay buffer that combines autonomous policy rollouts, offline expert demonstrations, and human intervention data. The replay buffer is sampled with a predefined ratio (50% offline, 50% online data) to incorporate expert corrections and accelerate policy improvement. Sparse task rewards are used, derived from either a pre-trained ResNet-based classifier or human feedback. The critic and actor objectives follow standard SAC formulations, with a target state-value function and an entropy regularization term for the actor. A high Update-to-Data (UTD) ratio is used for critic optimization, and the entropy coefficient is adjusted automatically.

**B. Multi-agent RL with Hybrid Action Space and Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution (CTDE)**
To manage the hybrid action space of continuous Cartesian arm pose control and discrete gripper control, the method adopts a CTDE paradigm.
- **Action Decomposition:** The policy is decomposed into two actors: a continuous SAC policy for Cartesian end-effector control (π_c) and a discrete SAC policy for gripper control (π_d). Both actions are sampled simultaneously, and their joint probability is modeled as π(a|s) = π_c(a_c|s)π_d(a_d|s), assuming conditional independence.
- **Centralized Critic:** Unlike approaches with independent policies (e.g., SAC+DQN), which suffer from non-stationarity due to changing environment dynamics perceived by each agent, this method employs a single centralized critic. This critic jointly evaluates the actions of both the continuous arm actor and the discrete gripper actor during training, leveraging global observations. This mitigates non-stationarity and stabilizes value estimation.
- **Decentralized Execution:** During real-world execution, each actor (arm and gripper) operates independently using only local observations. The centralized critic is exclusively for training.
- **Discrete SAC Policy:** The discrete gripper actor directly outputs logits for a categorical distribution, which are converted into action probabilities. The discrete Q-network simultaneously outputs Q-values for all possible discrete actions, enhancing computational efficiency. The loss for the discrete actor is specifically formulated to account for the categorical distribution and incorporates the Q-values.

**C. Decomposed Critic via Hybrid Reward Architecture (HRA)**
To address the challenges of noisy real-world RGB observations and stabilize value estimation, the framework integrates HRA with a multi-head critic.
- **Reward Decomposition:** The total reward r(s,a) is decomposed into two components: r_task (sparse task reward, dependent on front-view and end-effector pose) and r_grasp (dense potential-based grasping reward, associated with wrist camera and gripper state). The grasp reward is derived from a grasp potential function (Φ(s)) based on clipped min-max normalized gripper torque or width, including a gripper-switching penalty.
- **Multi-Head Critic:** The centralized critic is split into one value head for each reward component (task and grasp), while sharing a common feature extraction trunk. Each critic head (Q_k(s,a)) estimates the expected return of its corresponding reward component. This simplifies the regression target for each head, reducing variance in Temporal-Difference (TD) targets and improving stability under noisy observations.
- **Loss Reformulation:** The critic loss is reformulated to sum the squared errors for each decomposed reward head. The TD targets (y_k) are calculated separately for task and grasp heads, with the grasp head's TD target incorporating the discrete policy's entropy. The actors' objective functions are then updated using a weighted combination of these decomposed Q-values (Q_task + λ_g Q_grasp), where λ_g controls the grasp value contribution to the discrete gripper objective.

**D. Compliant Gripper Design**
For experimental validation, a compliant two-finger gripper based on a modular 1-DoF revolute-joint gripper design was developed. This gripper supports both fingertip and enveloping grasps through passive adaptation, using articulated segments, tendons, and elastic elements. This design improves grasp stability for diverse object geometries and weights without increasing the dimensionality of the learned action space, as it maintains a binary open-close interface for the RL policy.

### 4. Main findings and results

The proposed framework, HARC, was evaluated against HIL-SERL, a state-of-the-art baseline, across two robotic arms and a simulated humanoid robot, on four tasks under large-scale domain randomization.

**A. Experimental Tasks and Setup:**
- **Robots:** 6-DOF custom manipulator, 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda arm, Unitree G1 (simulated humanoid).
- **Tasks:**
 1. Pick-and-place (P&P) a tennis ball (50 × 40 cm workspace).
 2. P&P a banana (30 × 30 cm workspace, 360° orientation variation).
 3. Pot reset (40 × 40 cm workspace, 90° orientation variation, reflective surface).
 4. Block relocation (simulated, two blocks, 20 × 10 cm and 10 × 10 cm regions, 360° orientation).
- **Domain Randomization:** Approximately 5-25 times larger than prior work (e.g., 6 × 6 cm x-y region, 10° z-axis rotation for initial end-effector pose, and large object randomization ranges).
- **Training:** 2.5 hours wall-clock training per task on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, 10 Hz environment operation. Initialized with 20 expert demonstrations.

**B. Sample Efficiency Improvement:**
- The framework required less total expert supervision compared to HIL-SERL.
- Across tasks, HARC's total expert data (initial demonstrations + training interventions) corresponded to 69, 76, 111, and 115 episodes for tennis ball, banana, pot reset, and block relocation, respectively.
- In contrast, HIL-SERL required 80, 102, 132, and 189 episodes for the same tasks.
- This indicates that HARC required approximately 14% to 39% less expert data during training.
- Human interventions were concentrated on correcting poor policy behavior, contributing to data quality and the learning of failure recovery mechanisms.

**C. Performance under Large-Scale Randomization:**
- **Success Rates:** After 160 minutes of training, HARC consistently achieved higher success rates across all benchmarks (Table II):
 - **P&P tennis ball:** 80% (HIL-SERL: 60%, +33% relative improvement).
 - **P&P banana:** 90% (HIL-SERL: 60%, +50% relative improvement).
 - **Pot reset:** 55% (HIL-SERL: 0%, successfully accomplished a task where the baseline consistently failed).
 - **Block relocation (simulated):** 95% (HIL-SERL: 25%, +280% relative improvement).
- **Overall Average Success Rate:** HARC increased the average success rate from 40% with HIL-SERL to 75%.
- **Training Curves (Fig. 4):** The learning curves illustrate that HARC converged faster, required fewer expert interventions (intervention rates decreased more rapidly), and completed episodes more efficiently (episode durations decreased faster) compared to HIL-SERL. Intervention rates ultimately reached 0% for HARC in several tasks.

These results demonstrate that the proposed method provides improved task performance, sample efficiency, and execution efficiency in the presence of large-scale domain randomization.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research presents a framework that addresses several challenges in real-world online reinforcement learning for robotic manipulation, contributing to the field in the following ways:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** By demonstrating successful policy learning under dimension-wise domain randomization 5-25 times larger than previously considered, the framework significantly advances the robustness and generalization capabilities of real-world online RL. This allows policies to operate effectively in more diverse and unpredictable physical environments, reducing the need for tightly controlled settings.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Reduced Human Intervention:** The framework's ability to achieve high success rates with less expert supervision and fewer training interventions (as evidenced by lower demo buffer transitions and faster convergence of intervention rates) is crucial for the practical deployment of robot learning systems. It makes online RL more feasible by mitigating the burden on human operators, a scarce resource.
- **Effective Handling of Hybrid Action Spaces:** The integration of CTDE with decoupled continuous arm and discrete gripper actors provides a robust solution for hybrid action spaces. By using a centralized critic to evaluate joint actions during training, it successfully addresses the non-stationarity and credit assignment problems typically associated with multi-agent or hybrid control, while maintaining the benefits of decentralized execution.
- **Stabilized Learning from Noisy Real-World Data:** The Hybrid Reward Architecture (HRA) with a multi-head critic effectively mitigates the challenges posed by noisy, high-dimensional RGB observations. By decomposing the value estimation into simpler, task-specific components, it stabilizes critic optimization and accelerates convergence, which is a critical factor for reliable real-world online learning.
- **Practical System Design and Hardware Integration:** The implementation of a complete real-world online RL system, including a novel task-adaptive compliant gripper, and its validation on multiple robotic arms and tasks, underscores the practical utility and deployability of the proposed methods. The compliant gripper, specifically, enhances grasping reliability for objects with diverse geometries without increasing control complexity.

The potential impact of this work includes enabling more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems for various manipulation tasks, from manufacturing to service robotics. The demonstrated improvements in robustness, efficiency, and generalization pave the way for wider adoption of online RL in complex, dynamic real-world scenarios, particularly where simulation is insufficient or costly. Future work focusing on collaborative dual-arm manipulation on humanoid platforms suggests further expansion of the framework's capabilities and impact.
