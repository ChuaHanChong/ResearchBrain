# VISTA: Visually Inferred Spatial ConTact Attention for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VISTA-Policy is an imitation-learning framework that reads contact state from the visible 3D deformation of a passive compliant gripper, called the Visual Deformation Field, instead of tactile hardware. It combines a real-time deformation encoder, energy-based contact gating, and a relative incremental gripper action space, reaching 100% grasping, 90% cap-unscrewing, and 90% writing success on a $77 gripper.

## Problem

- Vision-centric policies observe only appearance, geometry, and motion, giving indirect ambiguous contact evidence when contact regions are occluded or interaction-induced motion is subtle.
- Tactile and force sensors are fragile, costly, miss finger-level or large planar contacts, and suffer cross-device data inconsistency plus heavy computational overhead.
- Absolute gripper action representations bind policies to demonstrated apertures, causing spatial overfitting and blocking out-of-distribution actions on unseen object scales.

## Method

- ==Physics-Aware Encoding Engine==: streaming O(1)-memory tracker plus lightweight multi-scale segmentation and B-spline edge fitting, back-projecting gripper inner edges into a 3D displacement field (the ==Visual Deformation Field==, VDF) relative to the rigid root.
- ==Energy Aggregation Denoising Mechanism==: ReLU deadzone over per-point deformation magnitude, spatially averaged into an energy scalar, sigmoid-mapped and EMA-smoothed into a contact confidence gating a learnable neutral embedding.
- ==Deformation-Augmented Policy Network==: 1D CNN with dual max/average pooling over sampling points feeds a temporal Transformer with a readout token, driving a ==relative incremental gripper action space== inside the **VISTA-Policy** imitation-learning framework.

## Results

- Across three tasks VISTA reached **100%/90%/90%** success versus **40-60%** for 3D Diffusion Policy variants and the Daimon visuo-tactile baseline, with **zero** fall-off and dislocation rates.
- Trained on a single 4 cm object, VISTA held **100%** success on unseen scales while wrist-view and absolute-action ablations dropped to **40-50%**; absolute-action variants stalled or over-gripped.
- VISTA with **20** demonstrations beat the vision baseline with **40** demonstrations, recovered from knock-away disturbances in **4 of 5** trials, and handled tofu and cards without damage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instrumenting the robot's own compliant structure turns an ordinary camera into a contact sensor, sidestepping fragile tactile hardware while keeping high spatial resolution.
> - Relative incremental action spaces are prerequisite, not cosmetic: contact feedback cannot be exploited when absolute action labels numerically bound the reachable aperture.
> - More demonstrations do not fix a perceptual bottleneck; adding a physically invariant modality beats doubling data for contact-sensitive generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly in tasks requiring precise physical interaction such as surface finishing, handling fragile objects, and assembly, demands dynamic adaptability and compliant interaction capabilities. Contemporary manipulation policies frequently rely on visual inputs, leveraging advancements in deep learning and cost-effective cameras. While vision-centric approaches demonstrate efficacy in tasks involving object recognition and trajectory planning, they encounter limitations in contact-rich scenarios. This is primarily because conventional visual observations (e.g., object appearance, geometry, robot motion) provide indirect and ambiguous cues regarding contact states, especially when interaction regions are occluded or when induced motions are subtle. This indirect feedback can render learned policies vulnerable to variations in object compliance, geometry, and external disturbances, potentially increasing the data requirements for learning contact-sensitive behaviors.

Dedicated tactile and force/torque sensors offer direct interaction feedback, enhancing manipulation capabilities. However, these hardware solutions present practical challenges, including durability constraints, high deployment and maintenance costs, and complex calibration requirements. Visuo-tactile sensors often utilize soft elastomers susceptible to wear, while commercial force/torque sensors can be bulky or cost-prohibitive. Furthermore, perception gaps exist, as wrist-mounted force/torque sensors may not capture finger-level forces, and visuo-tactile sensors can struggle with large planar contacts. Data heterogeneity and computational overhead associated with high-dimensional tactile data also limit their widespread adoption and cross-platform generalization.

This research aims to bridge the gap between the limitations of pure-vision policies in contact-rich tasks and the practical constraints of dedicated tactile hardware. It draws inspiration from biological systems, where humans can perform complex tasks requiring fine motor control by visually monitoring object and hand deformation, even with impaired tactile sensing. By investigating whether robots can similarly exploit visible structural deformation as a proxy for contact interaction, VISTA-Policy proposes an alternative paradigm.

The work builds upon advancements in imitation learning (IL), which offers an efficient method for transferring expert experience compared to reinforcement learning (RL). Recent IL breakthroughs, such as the Action Chunking Transformer and Diffusion Policy (DP3), have improved action sequence prediction and complex behavioral distribution modeling. DP3, in particular, integrates 3D point clouds for scene geometry, demonstrating improved sample efficiency and generalizability. However, the precision of these policies is bounded by the representational capacity of their input observations, often lacking direct physical response information crucial for fine-grained manipulation. Previous IL frameworks also typically employed coarse or absolute action representations, limiting out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.

VISTA-Policy contributes to the landscape by introducing a visually inferred spatial contact attention mechanism that derives structured visuo-physical feedback from the deformation of a compliant gripper. This approach seeks to provide explicit, high-dimensional contact information that is naturally aligned with visual states, without the fragility, cost, or complexity associated with specialized tactile sensors. It also addresses OOD generalization by re-framing gripper actions into a relative incremental representation, aiming to unbind the policy from absolute geometric anchors and enable dynamic action modulation guided by real-time visuo-physical feedback.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the VISTA research are:

1. **Address Ambiguity of Conventional Visual Observations:** To overcome the limitation that standard visual observations (e.g., object appearance, geometry) provide only indirect and ambiguous cues about contact states, especially under occlusion or subtle interactions. This ambiguity makes vision-centric policies vulnerable to variations in object properties and external disturbances, hindering fine-grained manipulation.
2. **Mitigate Practical Challenges of Dedicated Tactile/Force Sensors:** To develop an alternative to costly, fragile, and complex dedicated tactile or force/torque sensors. The motivation is to reduce hardware complexity, calibration requirements, and deployment costs associated with such sensors, which currently limit their suitability for long-term, high-intensity operations and large-scale deployment in general-purpose robotic systems.
3. **Extract Physically Meaningful Contact Feedback from Vision:** To investigate whether a robot can utilize the visually observable deformation of a passive compliant gripper as a direct and physically interpretable indicator of interaction state. This aims to provide rich contact information without adding specialized internal sensing hardware, thereby offering a low-cost, durable, and low-maintenance solution.
4. **Enhance Execution Performance in Contact-Rich Tasks:** To improve the success rate, precision, and quality of robotic manipulation in tasks that fundamentally depend on delicate physical interaction, such as adaptive grasping, cap handling, and tool use.
5. **Improve Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** To enable robotic policies to generalize effectively to unseen object scales, geometries, and environmental conditions. This involves developing a representation that captures physical invariants of contact, allowing the policy to adapt beyond the exact configurations seen during training.
6. **Increase Robustness and Self-Recovery:** To empower robots to withstand dynamic disturbances and safely manipulate fragile objects. The motivation is to enable reactive control and secondary recovery grasps, ensuring stable operation in unstructured and unpredictable environments.
7. **Boost Sample Efficiency:** To reduce the amount of expert demonstration data required to train effective manipulation policies. By providing a structured and unambiguous contact cue, the goal is to facilitate more efficient learning of contact-sensitive behaviors.
8. **Develop a Cost-Effective and Durable Solution:** To propose a hardware and software paradigm that is both economically viable and robust enough for long-term practical deployment, reducing the fragility and operational overhead associated with existing high-resolution tactile sensors.

In essence, the central motivation is to create a robust, cost-effective, and generalizable imitation learning paradigm for contact-rich manipulation by leveraging a visually inferred, physically meaningful representation of gripper deformation, thereby bridging a critical gap in current robotic sensing and control approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VISTA-Policy is an imitation learning paradigm designed for contact-rich manipulation. Its core approach relies on extracting a 3D displacement representation, termed the Visual Deformation Field (VDF), from the observed deformation of a passive compliant gripper using external visual observations. The framework integrates three main components: a Physics-Aware Encoding Engine, an Energy Aggregation Denoising Mechanism, and a Deformation-Augmented Policy Network.

#### 3.1. A. Overview

The VISTA-Policy framework operates in three sequential stages:
1. **Physics-Aware Encoding Engine:** This component processes real-time camera inputs (I_t) to extract a structured 3D VDF (D), which encodes the deformation of the compliant gripper.
2. **Energy Aggregation Denoising Mechanism:** This module analyzes the VDF to compute a continuous contact confidence (c_t), which guides a gating mechanism to produce a fused global representation (F) by filtering out environmental noise.
3. **Deformation-Augmented Policy Network:** This network utilizes the fused multimodal state (F) and a relative incremental gripper action space to generate robust manipulation commands.

#### 3.2. B. Physics-Aware Encoding Engine

The Physics-Aware Encoding Engine is responsible for real-time extraction of the 3D VDF from camera inputs.

- **Real-time Gripper Segmentation and Tracking:** The method builds upon Track-Anything for 2D segmentation and tracking of the compliant gripper. To meet real-time requirements for closed-loop control (20 Hz), the architecture is reconstructed into a state-machine-driven online streaming pipeline. This modification enables single-step inference using only the current frame (I_t) and an implicit memory feature (M_{t-1}), ensuring O(1) memory complexity and mitigating memory overflow risks.
- **Feature Extraction and Segmentation Network:** A lightweight multi-scale semantic segmentation network focuses feature extraction on the anterior inner edge line of the gripper. This network uses large convolutional kernels and residual modules for rapid downsampling, a pyramid scene parsing module for multi-scale context fusion to mend occlusion-induced fragmentation, and a Fire module for feature dimension compression.
- **Rule-Based Post-Processing:** Segmented masks undergo post-processing to rectify artifacts and fractures. Morphological opening and area-threshold filtering extract clean edge mask clusters. Left and right edge point sets are then parameterized using B-spline curves to reconstruct discrete masks into smooth, continuous geometric profiles.
- **3D VDF Estimation:** Aligned RGB-D depth maps are used to backproject 2D edge pixels into 3D space. A local 3D coordinate system is established at the gripper’s rigid root, and N equidistant points are sampled along the longitudinal y-axis. The structural deformation for each point (i) at timestep (t) on both left and right fingers is formalized as a combined relative displacement vector: d_i,t = [∆x_L, ∆z_L, ∆x_R, ∆z_R] ∈ R^4. This definition measures deformation relative to the rigid root, aiming to eliminate spurious correlations with absolute gripper positions. Depth completion via 1D linear interpolation handles missing values caused by adverse lighting or occlusions. The final output is a structured 3D VDF, D ∈ R^(T × N × C), where T is the observation horizon, N is the spatial sampling dimension, and C=4 denotes displacement feature channels.

#### 3.3. C. Energy Aggregation Denoising Mechanism

This mechanism processes the raw VDF to generate a compact, physically meaningful contact confidence (c_t), filtering out background and tracking noise.

- **Deformation Noise Floor Calibration:** A deformation noise floor threshold (τ_noise) is calibrated to eliminate minor perturbations during non-contact states.
- **Effective Spatial Energy Calculation:** The effective spatial energy (E_spatial,t) is computed by integrating deformation vectors across the global field, using a ReLU activation function as a deadzone filter to truncate signals below τ_noise.
 E_spatial,t = (1/N) * Σ_{i=1 to N} ReLU(||d_i,t||_2 - τ_noise)
- **Contact Confidence Mapping:** A sigmoid function maps E_spatial,t to a normalized raw contact confidence (c_raw,t), with parameters k (gain coefficient) and E_mid (nominal trigger center) to control sensitivity and define a state-switching feature.
- **Temporal Smoothing:** An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is applied to c_raw,t to obtain a temporally continuous contact confidence (c_t), preventing transient jumps.
- **Gating Fusion Mechanism:** To resolve interference from deformation data during non-contact phases, c_t is used to perform a soft gating operation on the deformation features. A learnable neutral embedding vector (e_neutral) represents the non-contact state. The gated feature (f̃_def) routed to the downstream network is computed as: f̃_def = c_t · f_def + (1 − c_t) · e_neutral.
- **Multimodal State Representation:** The gated deformation features (f̃_def), scene visual features (f_vision), and robot proprioceptive state (f_state) are concatenated to form a comprehensive multimodal state representation F = [f_vision, f_state, f̃_def] for the policy network.

#### 3.4. D. Deformation-Augmented Policy Network

This network generates manipulation actions based on the multimodal state representation, emphasizing adaptive gripper control.

- **Relative Incremental Gripper Action Space:** Unlike traditional IL frameworks using absolute positions, VISTA-Policy reconstructs the gripper action space into a relative increment representation (positional delta between consecutive timesteps). This design unbinds the policy from absolute numerical boundaries, enabling dynamic adjustment of gripper closure guided by real-time deformation states. Arm actions retain their original representation.
- **Spatio-Temporal Deformation Encoder:**
 - **1D CNN Processing:** A 1D CNN processes the single-frame VDF, treating the N sampling points as a sequence with inherent physical topology to learn localized deformation patterns.
 - **Dual-Pooling Strategy:** Adaptive max pooling captures peak deformation, while adaptive average pooling encodes the overall contact distribution, enhancing robustness across varying contact areas.
 - **Temporal Transformer Network:** The fused spatial embedding sequence ({f_1, f_2..., f_T}) is fed into a temporal Transformer network. A learnable readout token (x_readout) serves as a global proxy, aggregating spatio-temporal contact dynamics through a multi-head self-attention mechanism. The final readout token feature serves as the global contact-state representation, aligned with the downstream policy network.

#### 3.5. E. Experimental Setup

The hardware platform consists of a UR3 robotic arm, a UMI-based passive compliant gripper, and RealSense L515 and D405 depth cameras for global and local observations, respectively. The research employs three contact-rich manipulation tasks: Cross-Scale Object Grasping, Cap Unscrewing, and Calligraphy Writing, with progressive difficulty gradients.

- **Comparative Baselines:**
 - **DP3:** Standard 3D Diffusion Policy using only global point cloud.
 - **DP3-Wrist:** DP3 augmented with local wrist-view point cloud.
 - **TDF-DM:** A hardware tactile baseline replacing the compliant gripper with a commercial Daimon visuo-tactile sensor.
 - **VISTA-Policy (ours):** Full pipeline with VDF feedback and relative incremental gripper actions.
- **Ablation Baselines:**
 - **Action Space Ablations:** VISTA-Abs, DP3-Wrist-Abs (models output absolute gripper actions).
 - **Architecture Ablations:** VISTA-Cat (concatenates deformation features without energy denoising), VISTA-MLP (replaces spatio-temporal encoder with an MLP).
- **Sample Size Variants:** DP3-Wrist-40 (DP3-Wrist trained with 40 demonstrations, compared to 20 for others).
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR), Fall-off Rate (FR), Dislocation Rate (DR), Holding Success Rate (HSR), Writing Success Rate (WSR), and Stroke Quality Index (SQI = sqrt(LCR × Solidity)) for calligraphy. Statistical analysis of VDF characteristics is also performed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to address three core research questions (Q1: VDF consistency and robustness; Q2: VISTA-Policy performance and OOD generalization; Q3: VISTA-Policy robustness, sample efficiency, and safety).

#### 4.1. A. General Analysis

VISTA-Policy demonstrated superior performance across all three contact-rich tasks (Cross-Scale Object Grasping, Cap Unscrewing, Calligraphy Writing), outperforming all baselines including pure-vision (DP3, DP3-Wrist) and tactile (TDF-DM) methods in success rate, data efficiency, and generalization capabilities (Table II), affirming Q2. Furthermore, statistical analysis of the VDF during contact phases revealed that VISTA-Policy exhibited the narrowest fluctuation range compared to baselines (Fig. 5), indicating more consistent contact-induced deformation and reliable control commands, which supports Q1.

#### 4.2. B. Cross-Scale Object Grasping

1. **Single-Object Training and Cross-Scale Generalization:** When trained on a single 4 cm-wide object and evaluated on unseen scales, VISTA-Policy achieved 100% success rate (SR) with 0% fall-off rate (FR) across all object scales, including those narrower than the training object. In contrast, DP3-Wrist, trained on the same data, achieved only 50% SR on unseen scales (Table III), demonstrating premature lifting or inability to adapt gripper closure. This result substantiates VISTA-Policy’s OOD generalization (Q2) by capturing underlying grasping mechanics rather than absolute object dimensions.
2. **Gripper Action Representation Ablation:** Ablation studies showed that policies using absolute gripper action spaces (VISTA-Abs, DP3-Wrist-Abs) generalized poorly to unseen scales, despite successfully grasping the training object. VISTA-Abs exhibited over-gripping or repetitive opening-and-closing oscillations when facing narrow targets, indicating a modality conflict between the absolute action space and VDF feedback. This highlights that the relative incremental gripper action space is crucial for VISTA-Policy to leverage VDF as a dynamic criterion for adaptive gripper regulation, further supporting Q2.
3. **Multi-Object Training and Sample Efficiency:** In multi-object grasping experiments with five object diameters (1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6 cm), VISTA-Policy achieved 100% grasping SR across all scales with zero drops (Table IV). DP3-Wrist struggled, particularly with narrow objects and showed excessive gripping on larger ones. Notably, DP3-Wrist-40 (trained with 40 demonstrations) remained inferior to VISTA-Policy (trained with 20 demonstrations) (Fig. 7a), demonstrating that VDF guidance provides superior sample efficiency and effective contact cues (Q3).
4. **Robustness and Recovery Evaluation:** VISTA-Policy exhibited substantial robustness against dynamic disturbances. When an object was manually knocked back onto the tabletop after an initial grasp, VISTA-Policy successfully triggered secondary recovery grasps in 80% of trials, whereas DP3-Wrist failed completely (Fig. 7b). Additionally, VISTA-Policy successfully manipulated fragile tofu and delicate playing cards without damage, tasks where DP3-Wrist either failed or caused damage. These results confirm VISTA-Policy’s resilience, real-time perception-action self-recovery, and versatility (Q3).

#### 4.3. C. Cap Unscrewing

1. **Boundary-Scale Single-Object Training:** VISTA-Policy demonstrated superior cross-scale generalization when trained on minimum (2 cm) and maximum (6 cm) diameter caps (Table V). When trained on 2 cm caps, VISTA-Policy achieved 80% SR on unseen scales (compared to 20% for DP3-Wrist), with smoother execution and effective contact-state guidance. In the maximum-scale setting, VISTA-Policy dynamically regulated gripping for smaller caps, maintaining stable engagement, while DP3-Wrist failed entirely on small caps. This sustained performance under extreme boundary conditions supports Q2.
2. **Multi-Object Training:** Even with exposure to all cap sizes during training, DP3-Wrist’s performance did not improve, still suffering from over-gripping and bottle dislocation. VISTA-Policy, however, achieved 100% SR (Table VI), demonstrating smoother motions and successful multi-stage execution, including secondary attempts. This indicates that VDF-provided contact signals guide the model to overcome noise and execute intended actions, supporting Q1 and Q2.

#### 4.4. D. Calligraphy Writing

1. **Main Results and Stroke Quality Evaluation:** This task required stringent z-axis compliance and real-time contact perception. VISTA-Policy achieved 100% Holding Success Rate (HSR) and 90% Writing Success Rate (WSR) (Table VII), maintaining robust adaptability across various brush types and surface heights. The policy smoothly transitioned into the horizontal writing phase once sufficient z-axis deformation was registered by the VDF, contrasting with DP3-Wrist which struggled to discern this transition, often oscillating vertically or descending abruptly. This precision validates VDF as an explicit physical indicator (Q1, Q2). DP3-Wrist-40 (40 demos) still lagged VISTA-Policy (20 demos), reinforcing sample efficiency findings (Fig. 9).
 Stroke quality evaluation (Fig. 8a, 8b) showed that VISTA-Policy’s Stroke Quality Index (SQI) and stroke length distribution closely approximated expert demonstrations, clustering in the upper-right quadrant (high SQI, high length). Baselines, particularly DP3-Wrist, frequently produced short, dot-like strokes with high SQI but insufficient length, indicating unstable performance.
2. **OOD Generalization and Dynamic Disturbance Robustness:** VISTA-Policy outperformed DP3-Wrist in OOD height evaluations, maintaining reliable paper contact and writing even at an extreme lower bound of 15 cm. During dynamic-disturbance tests (online surface height changes), VISTA-Policy adaptively modulated its vertical action to complete effective strokes, whereas DP3-Wrist’s control stability collapsed (Fig. 9). This demonstrates VISTA-Policy’s OOD generalization and robustness (Q2).

#### 4.5. E. Ablation Studies

Ablation variants (VISTA-Cat and VISTA-MLP) showed performance degradation across all tasks. VISTA-Cat (without energy denoising) resulted in stuttered motions, prolonged execution times, and task failures due to noise injection. VISTA-MLP (with a simpler encoder) suffered from severe vertical bouncing and degraded stroke quality in writing, indicating its inability to accurately capture spatio-temporal VDF characteristics. These results validate the contributions of the Energy Aggregation Denoising Mechanism and the spatio-temporal deformation encoder.

#### 4.6. F. Interpretability Analysis on Feature Effectiveness

Feature similarity analysis demonstrated that VISTA-Policy reliably maps diverse object instances into a unified, physically invariant contact representation (Q1). Objects with distinct global geometries but similar local contact states yielded high cosine similarity among their VDF features (Fig. 10a). Furthermore, the VDF similarity matrix exhibited a distinct block-diagonal topology, clearly separating non-contact and contact phases and showing progressive evolution of contact features, unlike cluttered correlations from the baseline DP3 encoder (Fig. 10b). This confirmed that the VDF encoder effectively reduces contact-state ambiguity, providing clean and meaningful contact representations (Q1).

#### 4.7. G. Discussion and Practical Implications

The research provides mechanistic insights into pure-vision failures, attributing them to limited physical closed-loop feedback and entanglement of contact cues with object geometry. It also highlighted limitations of tactile hardware baselines like TDF-DM, which frequently suffered from stuttering, scraping, and protective stops, leading to suboptimal performance and safety concerns compared to VISTA-Policy. From a deployment perspective, the compliant gripper used in VISTA-Policy costs approximately $77 and demonstrated durability over 1,000 high-intensity contact-rich trials without structural degradation, offering a cost-effective and reliable solution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VISTA-Policy framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical limitations inherent in current approaches.

- **Bridging the Perception Gap:** This work provides a method to bridge the gap between the ambiguity of conventional vision-only policies in contact-rich tasks and the practical challenges (cost, fragility, complexity) associated with dedicated tactile or force/torque sensors. By leveraging visually observable deformation, VISTA-Policy offers a low-cost, durable alternative for obtaining rich, direct interaction feedback.
- **Enhanced Performance in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The demonstrated superior success rates, precision, and execution quality across challenging tasks like cross-scale grasping, cap unscrewing, and calligraphy writing suggest that VISTA-Policy can significantly improve robotic capabilities in fine-grained manipulation where delicate physical interaction is paramount.
- **Robust Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** The ability of VISTA-Policy to generalize to unseen object scales and environmental conditions, as well as its robustness against dynamic disturbances and capacity for self-recovery, is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and unpredictable real-world environments. This suggests that the VDF provides a robust physical invariant that transcends specific object geometries.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The finding that VISTA-Policy achieves superior performance with a limited number of demonstrations compared to vision-only baselines (even those with more data) has practical implications for reducing the time and resources required for policy training, making imitation learning more accessible.
- **Cost-Effectiveness and Durability:** The use of a simple, inexpensive, and robust compliant gripper, coupled with external cameras, offers a scalable and low-maintenance hardware solution compared to intricate and fragile tactile sensors. This economic viability and operational longevity can accelerate the adoption of advanced manipulation capabilities in various industries.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The VISTA paradigm opens avenues for future research, including:
 - Improving robustness in extreme visual conditions (occlusion, low light) through temporal memory or multi-view fusion.
 - Exploring the quantitative mapping between deformation patterns, object compliance, and contact forces, potentially extending VDF from pure feedback to compliance perception and force estimation.
 - Integrating VDF feedback with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to combine high-level semantic understanding with low-level fine-grained control, enabling broader applications in areas such as sorting, delicate packaging, and service robotics.

In summary, VISTA-Policy offers a viable and effective pathway toward developing more general-purpose, robust, and cost-efficient robotic systems capable of performing intricate contact-rich manipulation tasks, thus having a significant impact on advancing the capabilities and deployment of robotic autonomy.
