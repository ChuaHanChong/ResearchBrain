# Closing the Reality Gap: Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Deployment for Dexterous Force-Based Grasping and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a reinforcement learning framework that enables zero-shot deployment of dexterous manipulation policies, trained entirely in simulation, onto real multi-finger robotic hands for force-based tasks. The approach integrates efficient tactile simulation, data-driven current-to-torque calibration, and randomized actuator modeling, demonstrating controllable grasp force tracking and robust in-hand object reorientation on physical robots.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation with multi-finger dexterous hands faces challenges due to high degrees of freedom, complex contact dynamics, and the need for precise physical interaction.
- The 'sim-to-real' gap hinders effective transfer of deep reinforcement learning policies, especially for contact-rich tasks, without extensive real-world fine-tuning.
- Specific practical hurdles include computationally expensive high-resolution tactile simulation and discrepancies in actuator dynamics like unmodeled nonlinearities and the absence of direct torque sensors.

## Method

- A sim-to-real reinforcement learning framework was developed, employing an ==asymmetric actor-critic PPO== pipeline that leverages dense tactile sensing and joint torque feedback.
- A fast, high-resolution tactile sensor simulation method was implemented, approximating contacts via distance computation and a ==Mooney-Rivlin stress-strain model== for force.
- A data-driven ==current-to-torque calibration== and a ==randomized actuator model==, incorporating ==backlash== and ==velocity-dependent saturation==, were used to bridge the actuation gap.

## Results

- Policies trained entirely in simulation achieved zero-shot deployment on a real multi-finger hand, successfully demonstrating controllable grasp force tracking and robust in-hand object reorientation.
- The force-adaptive grasping policy generalized to novel, irregularly shaped objects, modulating grasp intensity based on commanded force, achieving contact forces up to **93.92 N**.
- In-hand object rotation policies achieved up to **25.1** consecutive successful trials on the real robot, with contact information and a **6D** orientation representation significantly improving performance over baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit dense tactile and joint torque feedback into policy observations is crucial for robust force-based dexterous manipulation policies transferable to the real world.
> - The combination of rapid, high-resolution tactile simulation and data-driven, randomized actuator modeling effectively closes the sim-to-real gap for contact-rich tasks, enabling zero-shot transfer.
> - Comprehensive contact information (force-weighted contact center, contact force) and a continuous 6D orientation representation are vital for stable and successful in-hand object reorientation policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly with multi-finger dexterous hands, aims to achieve human-level interaction capabilities with objects. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has shown promise in this domain, with notable successes in tasks like object reorientation. However, a significant challenge remains in bridging the "sim-to-real" gap, where policies trained entirely in simulation fail to transfer effectively to physical robots without extensive fine-tuning. This gap is particularly pronounced in contact-rich tasks due to discrepancies in contact physics, imperfect actuation, and the difficulty of simulating dense tactile feedback at scale.

Previous work has explored large-scale domain randomization, improved simulation fidelity, and various forms of tactile sensing, including visuotactile fusion and binary touch signals. While these efforts have demonstrated transferable policies for tasks like in-hand object rotation, challenges persist in achieving robust, force-sensitive control and zero-shot deployment for general dexterous manipulation. Specifically, the computational cost of simulating high-resolution tactile contacts for DRL exploration and the discrepancies in actuator dynamics (especially the absence of direct torque sensors on most dexterous hands) are identified as major practical hurdles.

This paper addresses these specific limitations by focusing on integrating dense tactile feedback with joint torque sensing in a sim-to-real DRL framework. It builds upon the foundation of DRL for dexterous manipulation but introduces novel methods for efficient tactile simulation, data-driven actuator modeling, and current-to-torque calibration to enable zero-shot transfer for force-based tasks. The work distinguishes itself by claiming the first demonstration of controllable grasping with grip-force tracking and force-adaptive grasping on a multi-finger hand trained purely in simulation and deployed zero-shot on real hardware. It moves beyond vision- or kinematics-only approaches by emphasizing full-state feedback to explicitly regulate physical interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reliable sim-to-real framework for training full-state policies that leverage both dense tactile sensing and joint torque/current feedback, enabling zero-shot deployment onto real multi-finger dexterous hands for contact-rich manipulation tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from two core challenges that limit the practical application of dexterous robotic hands:

1. **Complexity of Dexterous Hand Control:** Multi-finger hands offer human-like manipulation capabilities but are inherently difficult to control due to their high degrees of freedom, complex contact dynamics, and the need for precise physical interaction. Current real-world applications often resort to simpler grippers, indicating that robust in-hand manipulation remains an unsolved problem.
2. **Sim-to-Real Transfer Hurdles for Contact-Rich Tasks:** While deep reinforcement learning has advanced dexterous manipulation in simulation, transferring these policies to the real world, especially for tasks involving sensitive physical contact and force regulation, is problematic. The authors identify two specific practical hurdles:
 - **Computationally Expensive Tactile Simulation:** Simulating high-resolution, dense tactile contacts is computationally demanding, making it too slow for the massive exploration required during DRL training. This limitation often leads to policies that are brittle and fail to generalize to real-world contact dynamics.
 - **Actuator Dynamics Discrepancies:** Most dexterous hands lack direct joint torque sensors, relying instead on motor current estimates. The absence of accurate motor dynamics modeling in simulation, including nonlinear torque-speed effects and backlash, creates a significant "actuation gap" that hinders effective sim-to-real transfer.

To address these motivations, the authors aim to:
- Formulate policy observations that combine dense tactile sensing and joint torques to infer contact states and forces explicitly.
- Develop a fast and high-resolution tactile simulation method suitable for large-scale DRL training.
- Implement a data-driven actuator modeling approach that includes current-to-torque calibration and randomization to account for real-world motor uncertainties.
- Demonstrate the zero-shot deployment of simulation-trained policies for two specific contact-rich skills on a real multi-finger dexterous hand: controllable grasp force tracking and in-hand object reorientation.

Ultimately, the goal is to provide a practical and replicable solution for achieving reliable dexterous manipulation by effectively closing the reality gap through scalable sensing and actuation modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology focuses on a sim-to-real reinforcement learning framework designed to train dexterous manipulation policies entirely in simulation for zero-shot deployment on a physical five-finger robotic hand. The approach addresses specific sim-to-real gaps through tailored sensing, actuation modeling, and reward design.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The work addresses two key dexterous manipulation tasks:
1. **Force-Adaptive Grasping:** The policy learns to achieve stable grasps with commanded force levels across objects of varying properties.
2. **In-Hand Object Rotation:** The policy learns to precisely rotate a grasped object around a defined axis using multi-finger coordination.
Experiments utilized a 12-DoF direct-drive dexterous hand (XHand) and were conducted within the IsaacLab simulation environment.

**B. Identification of Sim-to-Real Gaps**
The study identified four primary gaps:
1. **Perceptual Gap:** Discrepancies between ground-truth simulation states and noisy real-world visual sensing.
2. **Actuator Dynamics Discrepancies:** Unmodeled joint-level issues like backlash, nonlinear motor control, and response delays.
3. **Contact Physics Discrepancies:** Inaccurate contact modeling due to simplified surface geometry, material properties, and restitution.
4. **Unseen State Distribution Shift:** Mismatch in observation distributions between training and deployment environments.

**C. Task Setup**
1. **Force-Adaptive Grasping:** To simplify learning and improve sim-to-real transfer, an "inverted catch-the-object" setup was used. The hand is fixed upside down, and objects are dropped towards the palm. The policy learns to catch and stably grasp objects under randomized properties and external perturbations, focusing on real-world measurable observations.
2. **In-Hand Object Rotation:** A cube is rotated along a single axis. Upon successful 90-degree rotation, the target pose updates, enabling continuous rotation.

**D. Policy Learning in Simulation**
The policy utilizes an asymmetric actor-critic PPO pipeline. The actor receives a subset of observations (real-world measurable), while the critic has access to full state information.

1. **Force-adaptive grasping:**
 - **Observations (Actor/Critic):** Hand joint angles (12D), hand joint torque (12D), object position (3D), object linear velocity (3D), contact force (5D), contact center (15D), fingertip positions (15D), and a force command (1D). For the real world, object Z-coordinate is estimated from a vision model (SAM), and XY coordinates are fixed. Linear velocity is derived from positional changes.
 - **Rewards:**
 - **R_torque (Joint Torque Reward):** Encourages specific joint torques when fingers are in contact. The thumb has a binary reward for being within a valid torque range, while other fingers use a Gaussian function centered on the target torque, multiplied by a validity mask.
 - **R_force (Contact Force Reward):** Similar to R_torque, encourages appropriate contact forces at the fingertips, with distinct handling for the thumb.
 - **R_diff (Four-Finger Consistency Reward):** Penalizes variance in the first joint positions of the four homologous fingers to promote coordinated, human-like motion.
 - **R_outer (Outer Finger Penalty):** Penalizes deviation of outer finger joints from a central position to encourage effective grasping.
 - **R_action (Action Rate Penalty):** Penalizes large changes in action commands to promote smoother control.
 - **R_vel (Joint Velocity L2 Penalty):** Discourages high joint velocities for stable movements.
 - A terminal state penalty is also applied.

2. **In-Hand Object Rotation:**
 - **Observations (Actor):** Hand joint angles (12D), relative target orientation (6D continuous representation), last actions (12D), contact center (15D), contact force (5D), fingertip positions (15D).
 - **Observations (Critic):** Adds object orientation (6D), fingertip velocities (30D), fingertip rotations (30D), hand joint velocities (12D), object linear velocity (3D), object angular velocity (3D).
 - **Two Policy Types:** One relies solely on proprioceptive information, excluding direct object orientation. The other uses an embedded IMU for object orientation feedback.
 - **Orientation Representation:** 6D continuous representation is used instead of quaternions to avoid discontinuity issues in the parameter space during learning.
 - **Rewards:** A composite reward combines proximity to goal rotation and position, preventing local optima where the agent avoids rotation. Reach goal bonus is given for achieving specified rotation and position thresholds. An action penalty is also included.

**E. Sim-to-Real Transfer Techniques**
The approach addresses the identified sim-to-real gaps with specific techniques:

1. **Fast, High-Resolution Tactile Sensor Simulation:**
 - Approximates tactile contacts by computing distances between a dense array of virtual tactile units (120 per fingertip, 600 total for the hand) and the object surface.
 - Uses parallel forward kinematics for rapid distance computation to the N-nearest surface points.
 - **Activation:** A virtual sensor is activated if it "penetrates" the object surface, determined by a negative dot product between the vector from the nearest object point to the sensor and the object's normal vector.
 - **Force:** Contact force for each sensor is approximated using a Mooney-Rivlin stress-strain relationship model based on the distance between the sensor and the object surface.
 - **Tactile Information Representation (for policy):** Each fingertip's tactile data (T_i) is represented by its total contact force (F_i) and a force-weighted mean contact position (μ_i), calculated from active sensors. This captures critical contact states while being spatially sparse.

2. **Robust Complex Contact Simulation:**
 - The simulation of in-hand contacts is made more robust by randomizing coefficients for surface friction, damping, and restitution between the hand and the object.

3. **Current-Torque Calibration:**
 - To align real-world motor current with simulated joint torque, a calibration process is performed. Varying forces are applied to each fingertip on both the real robot and in simulation.
 - Real-world motor current and tactile sensor readings are recorded. In simulation, joint torque and applied force magnitudes are collected.
 - A proportional relationship between contact force and both current/torque is established (Fig. 2). Maximum current, torque, and corresponding contact forces are normalized to align real-world current with simulated torque.

4. **Actuator Model Randomization:**
 - A randomized actuator model is used to capture real-world motor nonlinearities, enhancing policy robustness.
 - **Torque Calculation:** A PD controller (τ_c) with randomized proportional (k_p) and derivative (k_d) gains is used.
 - **Backlash:** A deadband function (τ_b) simulates mechanical dead zones, setting torque to zero if the error between reference and measured joint position is below a randomized threshold (ϵ).
 - **Velocity-Dependent Saturation:** Torque is constrained by velocity-dependent saturation functions (τ⁺_sat, τ⁻_sat) derived from DC motor characteristics (stall torque τ_0, max no-load velocity q̇_max).
 - **Efficiency Factor:** A randomized factor (η) accounts for variations in motor torque constant and driver efficiency.
 - All model parameters (k_p, k_d, ϵ, τ_0, q̇_max, η) are resampled for each actuator at the start of every training episode, forcing the policy to adapt to a wide range of imperfections.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both qualitative and quantitative results demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed sim-to-real framework for dexterous manipulation.

**A. Qualitative Observations:**
- **Consistent Behavior:** Policies trained entirely in simulation demonstrated consistent behavior when deployed zero-shot on the real robot for both force-adaptive grasping and in-hand object rotation (Fig. 7, Fig. 8).
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** The force-adaptive grasping policy successfully grasped novel, irregularly shaped objects (e.g., gamepad, bowl) that were not encountered during training, exhibiting stable and conforming contact (Fig. 9).
- **Controllable Grasp Force:** The policy could modulate grasp intensity based on commanded force, applying low, medium, or high strength grasps to various objects (e.g., toy, spring, pliers, chips, doll, protein bar) (Fig. 5).

**B. Quantitative Ablation Studies:**

1. **Force-Adaptive Grasping Reward Ablation (Table V, Fig. 6):**
 - The study investigated the impact of the R_force and R_torque reward terms on grasping performance.
 - **Combined Rewards:** Using both R_force and R_torque together yielded the widest and strongest ranges of contact forces (44.50-93.92 N) and joint torques (1.09-1.52 percent), indicating robust and dynamic grasping.
 - **R_force Only:** When only R_force was used, both contact force (37.29-70.02 N) and joint torque (0.69-0.75 percent) ranges were reduced, suggesting less assertive grasping.
 - **R_torque Only:** Relying solely on R_torque also resulted in reduced ranges for contact force (38.45-47.12 N) and joint torque (0.85-1.02 percent).
 - **Correlation with Force Command:** Analysis showed an approximately linear positive correlation between commanded force intensity and both contact force and joint torque. The strength of this correlation was highest when both reward terms were used, followed by R_force only, and then R_torque only (Fig. 6). These findings confirm the necessity of both reward components for achieving robust and adaptive force-based grasping.

2. **In-Hand Object Rotation Observation Ablation (Table IV):**
 - Ablation experiments evaluated the contribution of different observation components to real-world performance using metrics like consecutive successful trials, average duration, and time to fall.
 - **Full Configuration:** The policy with force-weighted contact center, contact force, and 6D orientation achieved the highest performance (25.1 consecutive successes, 3.36 seconds average duration).
 - **Orientation Representation:** Replacing 6D orientation with quaternions drastically reduced performance (2.9 successes), highlighting the importance of a continuous rotation representation for stable learning.
 - **Contact Information:**
 - Removing the force-weighted contact center reduced successes to 12.2.
 - Removing the contact force reduced successes to 13.2.
 - Ablating both contact components (contact center and contact force) further decreased performance to 7.5 successes.
 - **Baseline (No Contact Sensing):** A baseline without any contact sensing performed poorly (1.1 successes), underscoring the critical role of tactile feedback.
 - **No Explicit Orientation Encoding:** A model without explicit orientation encoding performed with 11.2 successes, underperforming the full configuration, demonstrating the value of precise orientation feedback.
 - These results collectively indicate that comprehensive contact information (force-weighted contact center, contact force), along with a continuous 6D rotation representation, are crucial for robust in-hand object rotation.

**C. Sim-to-Real Fidelity:**
- Visualizations of contact data during an in-hand rotation task showed high fidelity between real-world and simulated contact points and force magnitudes (Fig. 4). The real-world contact region was observed to be contained within the simulation contact region, suggesting effective transfer of contact features.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a reinforcement learning framework coupled with a suite of sim-to-real techniques that address fundamental challenges in deploying dexterous manipulation policies on physical robots. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer for Force-Based Tasks:** The work provides a practical and effective solution for zero-shot deployment of policies trained entirely in simulation to real hardware for complex, force-aware dexterous manipulation. This is particularly impactful for tasks like controllable grasping and in-hand reorientation, which have historically been challenging due to the inherent difficulties in simulating contact-rich physics and imperfect actuation. The claim of being the first demonstration of controllable grasping with grip-force tracking and force-adaptive grasping on a multi-finger hand with zero-shot transfer underscores its novelty.

2. **Addressing Key Sim-to-Real Gaps:** The methodology systematically tackles major limitations that often impede sim-to-real transfer:
 - **Efficient Tactile Simulation:** The introduction of a rapid, massively parallel tactile sensor simulation method based on distance computation mitigates the computational cost barrier, allowing for extensive exploration of contact dynamics during RL training without sacrificing fidelity. This approach generates high-rate, high-resolution touch signals essential for learning fine manipulation skills.
 - **Robust Actuator Modeling:** The data-driven current-to-torque calibration, coupled with a randomized actuator model that accounts for nonlinearities like torque-velocity saturation and backlash, effectively bridges the actuation gap. This eliminates the dependency on expensive joint torque sensors and improves policy robustness against real-world hardware variations.
 - **Refined Contact Physics:** Randomization of surface friction, damping, and restitution coefficients, alongside a novel representation of tactile information (total contact force and force-weighted mean contact position), contributes to a more robust simulation of complex in-hand contacts.

3. **Advancing Dexterous Manipulation Capabilities:** The demonstrated skills—command-based controllable grasp force tracking and robust in-hand object reorientation—represent significant progress toward achieving human-level dexterity. The ability to control grasp force adaptively and to reorient objects accurately without fine-tuning on the robot extends the practical utility of multi-finger hands beyond simple parallel-jaw grippers. The generalization to unseen, irregularly shaped objects further highlights the robustness of the learned policies.

4. **Broader Applicability and Replicability:** The authors suggest that their simulation-based RL techniques and "easy-to-replicate recipe" are broadly applicable to a wide range of commercial humanoid hands. This has the potential to facilitate broader research progress in dexterous manipulation by offering a scalable and efficient training paradigm, ultimately accelerating the development of robots capable of intricate human-like interactions with the physical world.

In summary, this research provides a comprehensive and practical framework that integrates advanced sensing, modeling, and learning techniques to overcome critical barriers in sim-to-real transfer for dexterous manipulation, paving the way for more capable and robust robotic systems.
