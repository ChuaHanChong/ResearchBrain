# HomeRobot: Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HomeRobot introduces Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM) as a unified problem for household robots, presenting the first reproducible real-world benchmark with a complementary simulation environment and open-source software stack. Baseline experiments reveal that current open-vocabulary perception systems are a primary bottleneck, leading to low overall success rates (e.g., 15-20% in real-world scenarios) for the complex end-to-end task.

## Problem

- Mobile manipulation robots were typically limited to predefined object sets and constrained environments, hindering generalization to novel items and complex multi-room homes.
- A lack of standardized hardware platforms and software stacks made it difficult to reproduce results and compare different approaches fairly across research labs.
- Research in embodied AI often addressed fragmented sub-problems (e.g., navigation or manipulation) rather than integrating perception, navigation, and manipulation for complex, open-vocabulary tasks.

## Method

- Formalizes the Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM) task, requiring robots to find, pick, and place any object in any unseen home environment based on natural language commands.
- Establishes the first reproducible benchmark for OVMM, comprising a large-scale simulation environment using Habitat HSSD and a real-world component utilizing the standardized Hello Robot Stretch platform.
- Provides the HomeRobot open-source software framework with unified APIs for both simulated and real-world execution, designed to facilitate modular development and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- Overall success rates in simulation dropped significantly from 5.1-14.8% with ground-truth perception to 0.4-0.6% when using the DETIC open-vocabulary object detector.
- Real-world experiments showed very low overall success rates (15% for heuristic baseline, 20% for RL baseline), primarily limited by the robustness of open-vocabulary perception systems.
- While reinforcement learning (RL) policies demonstrated improved performance in specific manipulation sub-tasks (Gaze, Pick, Place), integrating these skills with noisy perceptual inputs proved challenging, highlighting the need for more robust, integrated perception-action systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust open-vocabulary perception in cluttered, real-world environments is currently the most critical bottleneck for successful end-to-end mobile manipulation.
> - While individual sub-skills like grasping and placement can be improved with learned policies, their integration into a full system reveals the immense challenge posed by noisy perception and the sim-to-real gap.
> - The HomeRobot benchmark serves as a crucial tool for exposing current limitations and driving future research towards more robust and integrated perception-action systems for household robots.
