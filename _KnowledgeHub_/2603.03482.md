# Beyond Pixel Histories: World Models with Persistent 3D State

> [!summary] Summary
> PERSIST establishes a world modeling framework that explicitly tracks a latent 3D state, generating more coherent and stable interactive experiences over extended durations compared to pixel-history methods. The approach yields lower Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) scores and enables precise 3D scene editing and persistent off-screen dynamics.

## Problem

- Existing autoregressive world models rely on pixel-based histories, which are computationally intensive, have limited context windows, and struggle with long-horizon 3D consistency.
- Pixel observations provide partial, redundant, and viewpoint-dependent information, making it difficult and costly to identify relevant past evidence for extended, coherent generation in complex 3D environments.
- Current 3D generative environments often remain static, limiting dynamic interactions primarily to navigation within a fixed world.

## Method

- **PERSIST** maintains and evolves a ==latent 3D world-frame== and camera parameters, forming a proxy for the environment's hidden state.
- Interactive world simulation is decomposed into three learnable components: a ==world-frame model== (for 3D state prediction), a ==camera model== (for camera state prediction), and a ==world-to-pixel generation module== (for rendering pixel frames).
- A ==differentiable projection operator== transforms the 3D world-frame into screen-space, providing pixel-aligned 3D information to guide the world-to-pixel generation, acting as a ==learned deferred shader==.

## Results

- **PERSIST** achieved substantially lower Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores over **600-step** episodes, maintaining visual quality while pixel-history baselines degraded significantly.
- Human evaluations confirmed **PERSIST**'s superior performance across 'Visual Fidelity,' '3D Consistency,' 'Temporal Consistency,' and 'Overall Quality' compared to pixel-history baselines.
- The model demonstrated new capabilities, including generating diverse 3D environments from a single initial image, enabling precise mid-generation 3D world editing, and exhibiting persistent off-screen dynamics that affect later observations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling a persistent, dynamically evolving 3D state provides superior spatial memory and 3D consistency, significantly outperforming implicit learning from pixel-based histories.
> - Decomposing world simulation into 3D state evolution and 2D rendering enables stable long-horizon rollouts with a fixed-cost memory representation, independent of episode length.
> - Mitigating exposure bias through diffusion forcing and noise augmentation allows for robust separate training of world-frame and pixel generation components in an autoregressive pipeline.
