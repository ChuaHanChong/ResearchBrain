# Beyond Pixel Histories: World Models with Persistent 3D State

> [!summary] Summary
> PERSIST establishes a world modeling framework that explicitly tracks a latent 3D state, generating more coherent and stable interactive experiences over extended durations compared to pixel-history methods. The approach yields lower Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) scores and enables precise 3D scene editing and persistent off-screen dynamics.

## Problem

- Existing autoregressive world models rely on pixel-based histories, which are computationally intensive, have limited context windows, and struggle with long-horizon 3D consistency.
- Pixel observations provide partial, redundant, and viewpoint-dependent information, making it difficult and costly to identify relevant past evidence for extended, coherent generation in complex 3D environments.
- Current 3D generative environments often remain static, limiting dynamic interactions primarily to navigation within a fixed world.

## Method

- **PERSIST** maintains and evolves a ==latent 3D world-frame== and camera parameters, forming a proxy for the environment's hidden state.
- Interactive world simulation is decomposed into three learnable components: a ==world-frame model== (for 3D state prediction), a ==camera model== (for camera state prediction), and a ==world-to-pixel generation module== (for rendering pixel frames).
- A ==differentiable projection operator== transforms the 3D world-frame into screen-space, providing pixel-aligned 3D information to guide the world-to-pixel generation, acting as a ==learned deferred shader==.

## Results

- **PERSIST** achieved substantially lower Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores over **600-step** episodes, maintaining visual quality while pixel-history baselines degraded significantly.
- Human evaluations confirmed **PERSIST**'s superior performance across 'Visual Fidelity,' '3D Consistency,' 'Temporal Consistency,' and 'Overall Quality' compared to pixel-history baselines.
- The model demonstrated new capabilities, including generating diverse 3D environments from a single initial image, enabling precise mid-generation 3D world editing, and exhibiting persistent off-screen dynamics that affect later observations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling a persistent, dynamically evolving 3D state provides superior spatial memory and 3D consistency, significantly outperforming implicit learning from pixel-based histories.
> - Decomposing world simulation into 3D state evolution and 2D rendering enables stable long-horizon rollouts with a fixed-cost memory representation, independent of episode length.
> - Mitigating exposure bias through diffusion forcing and noise augmentation allows for robust separate training of world-frame and pixel generation components in an autoregressive pipeline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI has seen significant advancements in creating realistic media, including images and videos. Interactive world models represent a specific sub-area focused on generating dynamic experiences that evolve in response to user actions. These models hold potential for applications ranging from personalized interactive experiences for human users, such as neural game engines, to training embodied agents safely within learned simulators.

Current approaches to interactive video generation commonly employ autoregressive (AR) models, often based on diffusion transformers with causal attention mechanisms. These models typically condition their generation on a history of past observations and actions to produce the next frame. This framework allows for real-time action conditioning and indefinite generation, leveraging progress in large-scale video modeling.

However, existing AR models face several limitations. Conditioning on high-dimensional visual observations is computationally intensive, and hardware constraints limit the number of prior frames (context window) that can be ingested. This typically restricts AR generation to only a few seconds of past footage, even with compressed observations. To mitigate this, a common strategy involves heuristic key-frame retrieval from a memory bank of past observations. A fundamental challenge with such pixel-based histories is that each pixel observation provides only partial, redundant, and viewpoint-dependent information. As the memory bank grows, identifying relevant past evidence becomes increasingly difficult and costly, hindering the generation of extended and coherent experiences within complex 3D environments.

In parallel, there has been a growing interest in incorporating explicit 3D representations into generative architectures to produce explorable and spatially consistent environments. However, these 3D environments often remain static, limiting interactions primarily to navigation within a fixed world.

This work, "Beyond Pixel Histories: World Models with Persistent 3D State," addresses these limitations by proposing a departure from pixel-based histories. It introduces a paradigm where interactive world models simulate the evolution of a latent 3D scene directly. This approach is inspired by traditional 3D simulators and game engines, which maintain coherence by rendering pixel frames from a persistent and dynamically evolving 3D state of the world. By doing so, the authors aim to overcome the challenges of spatial memory, 3D consistency, and long-horizon stability that are prevalent in existing interactive world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a new paradigm for interactive world models that can generate coherent, evolving 3D worlds over extended durations. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Address Limitations of Pixel-Based Histories:** Current autoregressive world models primarily rely on pixel-based histories, which offer limited spatial memory and struggle with 3D consistency. The authors aim to move "beyond pixel histories" by introducing an explicit 3D representation as the core memory and generation mechanism.
2. **Enhance Spatial Memory and 3D Consistency:** Existing models often exhibit unrealistic user experiences and inconsistencies in 3D geometry over long rollouts because 3D consistency must be implicitly learned from limited temporal context. A key objective is to explicitly model a persistent 3D state of the environment, camera, and renderer to synthesize new frames with persistent spatial memory and consistent geometry by construction.
3. **Improve Long-Horizon Stability:** The degradation of generation quality and introduction of artifacts are common issues in long interactive sequences with current models. The research seeks to enable long-horizon rollouts with stable dynamics by using a fixed-cost memory representation (the 3D world-frame) whose information retrieval mechanism is independent of episode length.
4. **Facilitate Downstream Tasks and Agent Training:** The lack of robust 3D representations and consistent environments in current models presents obstacles for tasks such as training embodied agents within learned simulators. By improving the realism and consistency of generated worlds, the authors aim to create more suitable environments for such applications.
5. **Enable Novel Generative Capabilities:** Beyond improving existing metrics, the research is motivated by the desire to unlock new functionalities. This includes the ability to:
 - Synthesize diverse 3D environments from a single initial image.
 - Support fine-grained, geometry-aware control over generated experiences through environment editing and specification directly in 3D space.
 - Allow for the emergence of off-screen dynamic processes that evolve even when not directly observed by the agent.
6. **Decompose World Simulation into Manageable Components:** The authors propose to decompose the complex task of world simulation into three coupled and learnable components: a world-frame model, a camera model, and a world-to-pixel generation module. This modularity is intended to simplify the learning process and improve model interpretability and control.

In essence, the motivation is to create more robust, realistic, and controllable interactive world models by grounding them in an explicit, dynamically evolving 3D understanding of the environment, rather than relying solely on sequences of 2D pixel observations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of PERSIST involves maintaining and evolving a latent 3D representation of the environment, referred to as a "world-frame," alongside camera parameters, which together form a proxy for the environment's hidden state (`˜s = ⟨w, c⟩`). This framework decomposes interactive world simulation into three primary components: a world-frame model, a camera model, and a world-to-pixel generation module.

**Dataset Construction and Pre-processing:**
The models are trained using a dataset collected from Luanti, an open-source voxel-based game engine. This dataset comprises approximately 40 million environment interactions, including sequences of pixel observations (O), actions (A), ground-truth world-frames (W), and camera views (C).
- **Actions:** Encoded as a 23-dimensional multi-hot vector representing key presses and discretized mouse movements.
- **World-frames:** Represented as 48x48x48 voxel grids centered on the agent, with voxels containing semantic integer labels.
- **Latent Representations:** A 2D-VAE (Vision Transformer architecture) is trained to encode pixel-frames into latent patches (`¯o` of 10x10 pixels). A 3D-VAE (3D-ResNet architecture) encodes 3D world-frames into latent patches (`¯w` of 4x4x4 voxels). These VAEs are trained separately prior to the dynamic models.

**System Components:**

1. **World-Frame Prediction (`Wθ`):**
 - **Objective:** To predict the next latent world-frame (`¯wt`) based on past world-frames, actions, camera states, and pixel observations.
 - **Architecture:** Implemented as a rectified flow model with a causal Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone. It utilizes interleaved spatial, temporal, and cross-attention modules.
 - **Spatial Module:** Modified to handle three spatial dimensions, incorporating absolute position embeddings for XYZ coordinates of each voxel token.
 - **Conditioning:** Actions and camera parameters are jointly embedded via an MLP and injected using Adaptive Layer Normalisation (AdaLN). Pixel patches are concatenated channel-wise with Plücker embeddings (conveying 3D projection information from `C`) and injected via cross-attention.
 - **Initialization:** Crucially, `Wθ` supports generating the initial world-frame `w0` from an initial observation `⟨o0, c0⟩` at inference time, meaning ground-truth 3D conditioning is not always required.
 - **Decoding:** The predicted latent `¯wt` is decoded to its native 3D resolution using the 3D-VAE.

2. **Camera Model (`Cθ`):**
 - **Objective:** To predict the camera state (`ct`) at each timestep.
 - **Representation:** Camera state is a 10-dimensional vector `⟨pos, rot, fov⟩` (position, 6D rotation, field-of-view).
 - **Architecture:** A 1D causal transformer backbone with RoPE temporal embeddings.
 - **Conditioning:** Takes past camera states, current and past world-frames (cropped to inner 4x4x4 voxels), and actions as input.
 - **Prediction:** Predicts residuals (`∆pitch`, `∆yaw`, `∆fov`) relative to the previous camera state, enhancing stability.
 - **Training:** Trained using Mean Squared Error (MSE) losses for each component of the residuals.

3. **World-to-Pixel Generation (`Pθ`):**
 - **Objective:** To generate the pixel-frame (`¯ot`) based on the projected 3D information from the world-frame and other contextual cues.
 - **World Projection (`R`):** A differentiable projection operator (`R(c, w)`) transforms the 3D world-frame (`w`) into screen-space (`w2D`). This output consists of `˜w2D` (a per-pixel, depth-ordered list of voxel features) and `d` (linear depth of each voxel feature). This projection uses GPU-native triangle rasterization with depth-peeling to create pixel-aligned 3D information.
 - **Pixel Frame Prediction (`Pθ`):** Implemented as a rectified flow model with a causal DiT backbone (interleaved spatial and temporal modules). `Pθ` functions as a learned deferred shader, responsible for generating textures, lighting, and other visual details not explicitly present in the 3D latents.
 - **Conditioning:** `w2D` is projected to latent space via a 1D-convolution and channel-wise concatenated with `¯o` (pixel latents). `Pθ` is biased to use `w2D` as the primary information source by assigning it more latent channels. Actions are embedded and injected via AdaLN.

**Mitigating Exposure Bias:**
To address the distribution shift between training (ground truth) and inference (autoregressive predictions), two strategies are employed:
- **Diffusion Forcing:** Each denoiser is trained with diffusion forcing to improve robustness to its own predictions.
- **Noise Augmentation:** A flat 10% random noise augmentation is applied to `¯O` when training `Wθ` and to `¯W` when training `Pθ`. This enables separate training of components while reducing distributional shift during inference.

The overall approach creates a pipeline where a persistent 3D world-frame evolves, the camera moves within it, and pixels are rendered consistently from this 3D state, providing a framework for long-horizon, consistent interactive experiences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PERSIST focused on its ability to generate spatially and temporally consistent interactive experiences in procedurally generated 3D game worlds (Luanti), comparing it against pixel-history baselines and several ablations.

**Quantitative Metrics:**

- **Fréchet Video Distance (FVD):** PERSIST demonstrated substantial improvements in FVD scores across different episode lengths (200, 400, 600 frames) compared to pixel-history baselines (Oasis, WorldMem). While baselines showed significant degradation in quality over time, PERSIST maintained stable FVD scores, indicating better long-horizon consistency.
 - Ablation studies revealed that `No-3D-Upscale` (skipping 3D-VAE enhancement) resulted in the highest FVD penalty, confirming the importance of high-resolution 3D latent rendering for depth resolution and 3D-to-pixel alignment.
 - `PERSIST-S` (smaller 3D denoiser) performed only marginally worse than the base configuration, suggesting robustness of the underlying 3D representation to spatial compression.
 - `Camera-GT` (bypassing the learned camera model) achieved an FVD similar to the base model but introduced physical inconsistencies not captured by FVD (e.g., agent phasing through terrain).

- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID):** FID scores measured at each generation timestep corroborated the FVD findings. Pixel-history baselines consistently produced irrecoverable artifacts by the 200th timestep. In contrast, PERSIST exhibited virtually no degradation in FID over 600-step episodes, maintaining visual quality.
 - `PERSIST+w0` (a variant initialized with a ground-truth world-frame) started with a slightly lower FID, indicating that the initial FID penalty for the base PERSIST configuration was primarily due to regenerating the initial pixel observation to ensure alignment.
 - The world-frames generated by `Wθ` were observed to remain coherent for several thousand steps, providing stable structural guidance that allowed `Pθ` to recover from visual artifacts, even over a 2000-step episode.

**Qualitative Analysis and Human Study:**

- **Generation Stability:** Qualitative review of sample rollouts showed that pixel-history baselines rapidly developed artifacts, while PERSIST produced notably stable and coherent rollouts. The persistent 3D world-frame provided reliable structural guidance, enabling the pixel denoiser to recover from visual inconsistencies.
- **Human Evaluation:** A user study involving 28 participants rating over 800 videos confirmed the quantitative findings. Users rated videos on "Visual Fidelity," "3D Consistency," "Temporal Consistency," and "Overall Quality." PERSIST configurations consistently outperformed baselines across all metrics.
 - Interestingly, `PERSIST-S` performed similarly to the base PERSIST configuration in human ratings, suggesting that its higher FVD score did not translate to a perceptually significant degradation in generation quality for human observers.
 - `PERSIST+w0` achieved the highest human ratings across all categories, indicating the benefit of explicit 3D conditioning.
 - While PERSIST significantly improved quality, ground-truth videos were consistently preferred by users, highlighting remaining challenges.

**Inference Efficiency:**

- Preliminary investigations indicated that `PERSIST-S` was faster than baselines (2672.4 ms/frame vs. 3007.4 ms/frame for Oasis, and 6387.1 ms/frame for WorldMem) despite the overhead of tracking a 3D state, partly due to key-value caching.
- Reducing denoising steps for `Wθ` (to 2 steps) and `Pθ` (to 4 steps) resulted in a 3x speed-up (886 ms/frame, or 1.13 FPS) with only a moderate impact on FVD and without requiring fine-tuning, suggesting potential for further optimization towards real-time performance.

**New Applications and Emerging Capabilities:**

- **Diverse 3D Generation from Single Image:** PERSIST demonstrated the ability to generate plausible and diverse initial world frames from a single RGB conditioning observation, including outpainting unseen regions differently across episodes.
- **Explicit 3D Initialization:** The capability to initialize generation with a ground-truth world-frame (`PERSIST+w0`) provided a greater degree of control and resulted in improved quality metrics and human ratings.
- **Mid-Generation World Edits:** The explicit 3D representation allowed for precise 3D edits to the world mid-episode by modifying the `wt` state and restarting generation, offering a novel method of control.
- **Persistent World Dynamics:** The model learned to capture and evolve environmental processes even for elements outside the agent's immediate view. Examples included out-of-view collisions and dynamic processes (e.g., a cave filling with water), which could subsequently produce emergent on-screen effects.

These findings collectively indicate that PERSIST's integration of a persistent 3D state significantly enhances the capabilities of interactive world models in terms of consistency, stability, and control, addressing key limitations of prior pixel-history-based approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of PERSIST, a world modeling framework that explicitly tracks the evolution of a persistent latent 3D state, represents a notable advancement in the field of generative artificial intelligence and interactive simulation.

**Scientific Significance:**

- **Paradigm Shift:** PERSIST proposes a fundamental shift from implicitly learning 3D consistency from pixel histories to explicitly modeling a dynamic 3D scene. This provides a more robust foundation for generating coherent and stable long-horizon interactive experiences.
- **Improved Consistency and Stability:** The research demonstrates that actively generating guidance frames from a 3D state substantially improves spatial memory, temporal coherence, and overall generation quality and stability compared to methods relying on pixel-based histories. This addresses a critical limitation in current autoregressive video generation models, which often suffer from rapid degradation and accumulation of artifacts over extended rollouts.
- **Modular Architecture:** The decomposition of world simulation into world-frame, camera, and world-to-pixel components offers a structured approach to learning complex environment dynamics and rendering, potentially simplifying future research and development.
- **Addressing Exposure Bias:** The use of diffusion forcing and noise augmentation to mitigate exposure bias, while allowing separate training of components, contributes to the practical robustness of complex autoregressive generative pipelines.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Interactive Experiences:** The ability to generate coherent, evolving 3D worlds enables more immersive and realistic interactive experiences for human users in applications such as neural game engines. This could lead to a new class of personalized and dynamic digital environments.
- **Advanced Embodied AI Training:** For embodied agents, PERSIST offers a promising avenue for training within simulators that are learned directly from data but maintain high fidelity and consistency. Such simulators can provide safer and more diverse training grounds, accelerating research in reinforcement learning and robotics. The modeling of off-screen dynamics and robust collision detection are particularly valuable for agent training.
- **Novel Creative Tools:** The emergent capabilities of PERSIST, such as explicit 3D scene initialization and mid-episode scene editing directly in 3D space, open new possibilities for content creation and control. Game developers and content creators could leverage these tools to rapidly prototype environments or dynamically modify interactive narratives.
- **Digital Twin Simulations:** The framework's ability to maintain a reliable 3D scene representation and model persistent world dynamics could be applicable to digital twin simulations, where accurate and evolving virtual replicas of real-world systems are crucial for monitoring, analysis, and prediction.

**Future Directions and Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations that outline a clear roadmap for future research:
- **Reliance on Ground-Truth 3D Supervision:** Current training requires 3D annotations, limiting its applicability to simulators or datasets with such information. Future work aims to relax this dependency by learning 3D representations directly from pixels or leveraging 2D-to-3D foundation models for synthetic annotations.
- **Exposure Bias Mitigation:** Despite improvements, human evaluators still prefer ground-truth videos. Further research will explore end-to-end post-training on generated rollouts to better align training and inference distributions and mitigate autoregressive drift over very long horizons.
- **Unconstrained Spatial Memory:** PERSIST currently tracks a finite region of 3D space. Future work could investigate using a 3D memory bank to retain spatial information over arbitrarily large environments, which would be inherently de-duplicated and spatially organized.
- **Inference Speed Optimization:** While improvements were shown, achieving real-time inference speed is a goal for future optimization through increased compression, smaller models, and more efficient implementations.

In conclusion, PERSIST establishes a new foundation for interactive world models by integrating persistent 3D state, yielding substantial improvements in consistency and opening doors for more advanced applications in interactive AI and generative content creation.
