# Procedural Generation of Articulated Simulation-Ready Assets

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Princeton University and McGill University developed Infinigen-Articulated, a toolkit for procedurally generating diverse, high-quality articulated 3D assets for robotics simulation. The toolkit significantly improves movable part segmentation, boosts reinforcement learning policy generalization by an average of 2.86-fold, and enhances zero-shot sim-to-real transfer performance across various manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- A scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality datasets of articulated objects exists, which are crucial for training robotics and embodied AI systems in physical simulations.
- Current methods for acquiring articulated assets, such as internet curation or 3D scanning, often lack essential joint annotations (e.g., pivot points, axes), while manual annotation is labor-intensive, costly, and limits diversity and accuracy.
- Existing datasets frequently offer limited kinematic and geometric variations, making it difficult to develop robust robotic policies that generalize to novel object configurations or to accurately model small or occluded articulated parts.

## Method

- The team developed **Infinigen-Articulated**, a toolkit leveraging ==Blender's Geometry Nodes and Shader Nodes==, by introducing ==custom node-groups== for implementing revolute and prismatic joints.
- These specialized nodes establish ==parent-child relationships==, parameterize joints with dynamic expressions, provide visual feedback, and standardize ==joint metadata== for consistent asset generation.
- An ==automated three-step pipeline== converts procedural assets from Blender into ==simulation-ready formats (URDF, USD, MJCF)==, supporting dynamic control over asset parameters and the construction of kinematic trees.

## Results

- Co-training vision models with **Infinigen-Articulated** assets significantly improved movable part segmentation, particularly for small components, with the mAP for "Lamp Switch" increasing from **11.45** to **17.44**.
- Reinforcement learning policies trained exclusively on **Infinigen-Articulated** assets demonstrated a **2.86-fold** average improvement in success rate across three manipulation tasks compared to policies trained on PartNet-Mobility assets.
- For zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, policies trained with **Infinigen-Articulated** assets achieved substantially higher success rates on a physical robot (e.g., **18/30** for door opening versus **3/30** for PartNet-Mobility).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Procedural generation offers a scalable solution for creating an effectively unlimited number of high-fidelity articulated objects with continuous variations in geometry, kinematics, and visual appearance, overcoming the limitations of manual data acquisition.
> - By embedding joint definitions and metadata directly into the generation process, the toolkit ensures precise ground truth for articulation properties, which is crucial for accurate physics simulations and robust robot learning.
> - Diverse, procedurally generated datasets enhance the generalization capabilities of robot learning policies and improve sim-to-real transfer, even when the generated assets are out-of-distribution relative to traditional test sets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a recognized challenge within the fields of robotics and embodied artificial intelligence: the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality datasets of articulated objects suitable for physical simulation. Robotics applications frequently necessitate interaction with articulated objects, such as opening doors, manipulating drawers, or pressing buttons. Learning these manipulation skills often relies on training within simulated environments, which requires extensive and varied object datasets to ensure policy robustness and generalizability.

Current approaches to acquiring articulated assets for simulation face several limitations. Datasets obtained from internet curation or 3D scanning typically consist of static models that lack crucial joint annotations (e.g., pivot points, axes, ranges of motion). Datasets that do include articulation information, such as PartNet-Mobility and SAPIEN, often rely on labor-intensive human annotation. This manual process restricts the quantity, quality, and diversity of unique geometries and articulation configurations that can be achieved. Manual annotation can also lead to inaccuracies in joint parameters. Recent efforts have explored AI-based generation techniques, but these often lack precise control over geometry, may not guarantee correct joint configurations, and struggle with generating high-quality assets or modeling occluded articulated parts. Real-to-sim pipelines aimed at reconstructing articulated objects from real-world data similarly encounter difficulties in producing accurate geometries and joint annotations, especially for hidden components.

The broader research landscape for procedural generation has seen success in creating diverse static environments, including indoor scenes, natural landscapes, and cityscapes. However, the application of procedural generation to detailed, multi-level articulated objects, specifically for robotics simulation, has been less developed. Existing procedural generators for articulated assets tend to use simpler geometries or offer limited extensibility.

Infinigen-Articulated positions itself as a system designed to overcome these limitations by providing a procedural generation toolkit. It aims to deliver a scalable method for creating virtually unlimited variations of articulated objects with accurate physical and kinematic properties, thereby providing richer training data for robotic learning and perception tasks without the constraints of manual data acquisition or annotation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Infinigen-Articulated, a toolkit designed to procedurally generate realistic, simulation-ready articulated assets for robotics applications. This toolkit aims to facilitate the creation of high-quality, diverse articulated objects that can be directly integrated into common robotics simulators.

The motivation for developing Infinigen-Articulated stems from several critical needs within the field of robotics:

- **Addressing the Need for Articulated Object Interaction:** Many real-world robotics tasks, such as opening household appliances (fridges, dishwashers), operating mechanisms (pushing buttons, traversing doors), or manipulating tools, inherently involve interacting with articulated objects. Robots must be proficient in manipulating these objects to perform useful functions.
- **Improving Simulation-Based Learning for Robotics:** Learning in physical simulation is a well-established strategy for developing robotic policies due to its safety, scalability, and ability to generate large quantities of training data. For this strategy to be effective in articulated object manipulation, access to extensive and varied datasets of articulated assets is essential. These datasets must encompass diverse geometries, joint types, and visual appearances.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Asset Acquisition Methods:**
 - **Lack of Joint Information:** Many readily available 3D models online or from 3D scanning are static and do not contain annotations for articulated joints, making them unsuitable for simulation without extensive manual post-processing.
 - **Labor-Intensive Manual Annotation:** Widely used articulated object datasets often rely on human annotators to specify joint positions, axes, and ranges. This process is time-consuming, expensive, and intrinsically limits the quantity, quality, and diversity of unique object geometries and articulation configurations that can be created. It also introduces potential for human error in annotation.
 - **Limited Kinematic and Geometric Diversity:** Existing datasets often present a restricted range of kinematic variations, which can hinder the development of generalizable robotic policies. For instance, a policy trained on doors with only one type of handle and hinge-to-handle distance may not generalize to novel configurations.
 - **Difficulty with Small/Occluded Parts:** Manually annotating and articulating small or occluded components (e.g., tiny buttons, internal racks) is particularly challenging and prone to inaccuracy, but these details can be critical for robust manipulation.

Infinigen-Articulated aims to provide a solution that offers:
1. **Unlimited Kinematic Variation:** Allowing for continuous sampling of task-relevant parameters (e.g., handle-to-hinge distance, edge bevel radii, toaster slot width) to generate diverse training data that covers a dense space of configurations.
2. **High-Quality Assets with Verified Annotations:** Ensuring the correctness of joint locations, axes, and ranges, which contrasts with methods relying on human annotation or less controllable generative techniques. The toolkit also integrates physics-based rendering materials and physical properties (e.g., friction, density).
3. **Combinatorial Coverage:** Facilitating the random swapping of sub-parts (e.g., different handle styles) and the easy expression of variable quantities of articulated components, including small or occluded parts that are difficult to capture or annotate manually.

By addressing these motivations, Infinigen-Articulated seeks to enable the development of more robust and generalizable robotic systems by providing a scalable source of high-fidelity, procedurally generated articulated simulation assets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Infinigen-Articulated is a toolkit that employs procedural generation techniques within a 3D graphics environment to create articulated simulation assets. The methodology encompasses the development of specialized tools within Blender and an automated export pipeline to various robotics simulation formats.

#### 3.1. Articulated Procedural Generator Tools

The toolkit is built upon Blender's procedural modeling capabilities, specifically leveraging its Geometry Nodes and Shader Nodes. These Blender features offer a graphical user interface (GUI)-based, node-centric domain-specific language that allows users to construct meshes and materials through the composition of various nodes representing primitives, geometric transformations, and arithmetic operations.

To enable the creation of articulated assets, Infinigen-Articulated introduces two custom "node-groups" within Blender's Geometry Nodes system: one for implementing revolute (hinged) joints and another for prismatic (sliding) joints. These specialized nodes are designed to:

- **Define Joint Relationships:** They accept two incoming geometries, establishing a parent-child relationship between them, and annotate this relationship with the specified joint type.
- **Parameterize Joints:** Users can define the pivot position, axis, and joint range for each joint. These parameters can be set as fixed values or dynamically computed through arithmetic expressions within the node graph, deriving values directly from the asset's geometry.
- **Streamline Generation:** These node-groups encapsulate common articulation operations, reducing repetitive implementation efforts for each new object.
- **Provide Visual Feedback:** The nodes offer immediate visual feedback in the Blender GUI, demonstrating the effect of joint parameters (e.g., the transformation of a child geometry based on hinge origin or angle extent). This feature aids in debugging and validating the calculated joint parameters.
- **Standardize Metadata:** The standardized implementation of joints allows for consistent storage of all joint metadata in a uniform format, which is crucial for downstream processing.
- **Ensure Semantic Consistency:** The nodes are designed to store semantic information, contributing to consistent and fine-grained naming conventions for joints and geometries across all procedurally generated assets within a given category.

The joint nodes support various articulated structures, including sequential rigid bodies, multiple child bodies attached to a single parent, and multiple degrees of freedom between bodies. An additional custom node group is provided for duplicating jointed bodies at defined points, facilitating the procedural generation of repetitive articulations like multiple burners on a cooktop or shelves in a dishwasher. Furthermore, a utility script is included to detect and report self-penetration or collision issues between rigid parts of an asset within its defined range of motion, assisting users in refining joint configurations and part geometries.

#### 3.2. Exporting Assets to Simulation

The toolkit includes an automated three-step pipeline for converting procedurally generated articulated assets from Blender into simulation-ready formats:

1. **Transpilation:** The procedural asset's node graph in Blender is first converted into Python code using Infinigen's transpiler. This step grants users the flexibility to modify the generated code, allowing for customization of asset parameter distributions or further adjustments to the node graph structures. This provides dynamic control over asset generation.
2. **Asset Instantiation and Kinematic Tree Construction:** Random values are sampled for each procedural parameter to create an instance of the asset within Blender. The Blender Python API is then used to parse and update the asset's node graph. During this process, Blender-provided nodes are injected into the graph to assign attribute values to different parts of the geometry. These attributes enable the construction of a kinematic tree for the asset instance, which abstracts the complete procedural graph while retaining essential articulation details.
3. **Simulation Export:** The instantiated asset and its corresponding kinematic tree are then passed to a native exporter. This exporter supports popular simulation file formats, including URDF, USD, and MJCF. The exporter recursively traverses the kinematic tree depth-first. For each rigid body, it queries the asset instance using attributes from the tree's current path, extracts the matching part of the asset, and saves it as an individual mesh. Users have the option to generate convex-decomposed collision meshes using third-party tools such as CoACD. This comprehensive process culminates in the assembly of the final simulation-ready asset, complete with all joints, rigid geometries, and semantic metadata.

#### 3.3. Articulated Asset Procedural Generators

Using the Infinigen-Articulated toolkit, the researchers developed procedural generators for 18 common articulated object categories (e.g., doors, refrigerators, toasters, lamps, dishwashers, cabinets). Each generator is equipped with distributions for joint dynamics parameters, such as stiffness and damping, and is capable of producing kinematically diverse and photorealistic articulated assets. The input parameters for these assets can be continuously varied, either manually or randomized according to defined distributions, providing extensive control over the generated objects. A key feature is the ability to generate detailed, small components (like locks or switches) with high accuracy and minimal manual overhead, which is often a bottleneck in other asset creation methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents experimental results demonstrating the efficacy of Infinigen-Articulated assets in three primary areas: movable part segmentation, reinforcement learning (RL) generalization, and sim-to-real transfer.

#### 4.1. Movable Part Segmentation

The objective of this experiment was to evaluate the utility of Infinigen-Articulated assets for movable part segmentation, a vision task where a model segments articulated parts from an RGB image.
- **Setup:** Experiments focused on five object categories: doors, toasters, refrigerators, dishwashers, and lamps. 250 diverse assets were generated per category using Infinigen-Articulated. Image datasets were created for both PartNet-Mobility and Infinigen-Articulated assets, rendered at 1024x1024 resolution with randomized camera positions, lighting, and joint configurations. A pre-trained Mask R-CNN model with a ResNet-50-FPN backbone was fine-tuned on three datasets:
 1. **P15k:** ~15,000 images of PartNet assets only.
 2. **P30k:** ~30,000 images of PartNet assets only (doubling the training data from P15k).
 3. **P15k+I:** ~15,000 images of PartNet assets combined with ~15,000 images of Infinigen-Articulated assets.
 All models were evaluated exclusively on images of unseen PartNet assets.
- **Results:**
 - Doubling the size of the PartNet-only dataset (P30k vs. P15k) yielded only marginal improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP) and, in some cases, a decrease in performance. This suggested a saturation point for learning new features from the existing PartNet distribution.
 - In contrast, co-training with Infinigen-Articulated assets (P15k+I) resulted in a significant improvement in mAP, particularly for smaller articulated parts such as door handles, toaster levers, dishwasher buttons, and lamp switches. For instance, the mAP for "Door Handle" improved from 41.08 (P15k) to 44.97 (P15k+I), "Toaster Lever" from 59.76 to 64.31, and "Lamp Switch" from 11.45 to 17.44.
 - This indicates that the procedural generation tool's ability to scale diverse and detailed articulated parts, which are labor-intensive for manual annotation, enhances model generalization even when the generated assets are out-of-distribution relative to the evaluation set.

#### 4.2. RL Generalization

These experiments assessed whether Infinigen-Articulated assets improve the generalization capabilities of reinforcement learning policies to novel object instances.
- **Setup:** Three robotics tasks were defined: "Push Door with Handle," "Push Down Toaster Lever," and "Open Fridge Door." For each task, PPO policies were trained in ManiSkill3 using GPU-parallelized heterogeneous simulation across 128 environments, each potentially containing a different asset. Policies took end-effector pose and an RGB image as input and outputted delta pose commands. Policies were trained using:
 1. Exclusively Infinigen-Articulated assets.
 2. Exclusively PartNet-Mobility assets.
 3. A combination of both.
 Evaluation was conducted on a test set comprising 5 unseen Infinigen-Articulated assets and 5 unseen PartNet-Mobility assets.
- **Results:**
 - Policies trained solely on Infinigen-Articulated assets demonstrated a 2.86-fold average improvement in success rate across all three tasks (measured over the last 1 million timesteps) compared to policies trained only on PartNet-Mobility assets.
 - This performance increase was attributed to the scale, quality, and diversity offered by Infinigen-Articulated assets. The limited number of PartNet-Mobility assets, coupled with potential joint misalignments and suboptimal geometries, were hypothesized to increase the difficulty of training effective policies.
 - It was noted that during earlier training stages for the door and fridge opening tasks, PartNet-Mobility policies sometimes performed on par with or better than Infinigen-Articulated policies, suggesting that the full benefits of diverse assets might require longer training periods.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Transfer

The final set of experiments investigated the effectiveness of Infinigen-Articulated assets in enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation.
- **Setup:** Real-world versions of the door opening, toaster pushing, and fridge opening tasks were set up using a Franka Panda robot. Expert simulation trajectories were collected in an IsaacGym environment using cuRobo, with randomization applied to lighting, joint initialization, object position, and camera views. An ACT policy was trained on this simulated expert data (fingertip poses and RGB images from an on-the-shoulder camera) and then transferred zero-shot to the physical robot. Policies were trained using assets from either Infinigen-Articulated or PartNet-Mobility.
- **Results:**
 - Policies trained using Infinigen-Articulated assets exhibited significantly better performance across all three real-world tasks compared to policies trained with PartNet-Mobility assets (Table II). For the door opening task, Infinigen-Articulated achieved 18/30 successes versus 3/30 for PartNet-Mobility. For toaster pushing, it was 11/30 versus 0/30, and for fridge opening, 10/30 versus 0/30.
 - Qualitative observations indicated that policies trained with Infinigen-Articulated assets demonstrated smoother movements and failed less aggressively. For instance, fridge task failures with Infinigen-Articulated policies typically involved nearing the handle or just short of the target door opening angle, whereas PartNet-Mobility policies often missed the handle entirely.
 - This success was attributed to the increased diversity and quantity of assets generated by Infinigen-Articulated, which is postulated to contribute to effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Infinigen-Articulated represents a contribution to the field of robotics by providing a scalable, high-quality, and diverse source of articulated assets for simulation. Its significance lies in addressing a fundamental bottleneck in robot learning: the availability of rich, accurate, and varied training data for tasks involving articulated object manipulation.

**Significance:**

- **Scalability and Diversity:** The toolkit enables the procedural generation of an effectively unlimited number of articulated object instances with continuous variation in geometry, kinematics, and visual appearance. This overcomes the inherent limitations of manual annotation or 3D scanning, which are constrained by human effort, time, and the diversity of real-world objects.
- **Accuracy and Ground Truth:** By generating assets procedurally, Infinigen-Articulated ensures precise ground truth for joint locations, axes, and ranges of motion, eliminating the inaccuracies often associated with human-annotated datasets. This intrinsic accuracy is critical for physics-based simulations and for training robust manipulation policies.
- **High Fidelity:** The integration with Blender's rendering capabilities and physics-based materials ensures that the generated assets are photorealistic and possess realistic physical properties, enhancing the realism of simulation environments.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The development of this open-source toolkit and its accompanying library of 18 procedural generators is intended to foster further research and development in robot learning and perception.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Robot Learning Generalization:** The experimental results demonstrate that using Infinigen-Articulated assets significantly improves the generalization capabilities of robot learning policies across diverse instances of articulated objects. This can lead to robots that are more adaptable and robust when encountering novel objects in real-world scenarios.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The success in zero-shot sim-to-real transfer indicates that the diversity and quality of procedurally generated assets can effectively bridge the reality gap, allowing policies trained purely in simulation to perform reliably on physical robots. This can accelerate the development and deployment of robotic systems.
- **Advancements in Perception Tasks:** The observed improvements in movable part segmentation suggest that the toolkit can provide valuable training data for computer vision models, enabling them to better understand and segment the functional components of articulated objects.
- **Reduced Data Acquisition Costs:** By automating the generation of articulated assets, Infinigen-Articulated reduces the substantial human labor and financial costs associated with creating large-scale, high-quality datasets, making advanced robot learning more accessible.
- **Enabling Research into Complex Articulations:** The toolkit's ability to generate objects with intricate, multi-level, and occluded articulations opens avenues for research into more complex manipulation tasks that are currently difficult to address due to data limitations.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The paper identifies certain limitations. Currently, joints are modeled independently; future work could explore the procedural generation of coupled joints (e.g., a trash can pedal linked to its lid). Additionally, while collision meshes for individual parts are generated using CoACD, this process can potentially slow down simulation depending on the level of convex decomposition. A prospective area for future work involves leveraging the asset's node graph to generate corresponding sets of primitives as collision meshes, potentially enhancing simulation speed.
