# RoboBRIDGE: A Modular Framework for Bridging Policies to Robust Real-World Robotic Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> A modular framework developed at Sungkyunkwan University, ROBOBRIDGE, enhances any action-generating policy into a robust robotic agent by adding capabilities like two-phase monitoring and reactive planning. This framework notably increased the average task success rate of GR00T-N1.5 on RoboCasa benchmark tasks from 6.2% to 14.7% when pick-and-place tasks were excluded.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, as direct action predictors, lack inherent mechanisms for detecting and recovering from execution failures in real-world settings.
- Current VLA deployments often exhibit inconsistent behavior over extended task horizons due to independent inference steps and insufficient tracking of task progress.
- Existing VLA models show reduced robustness to shifts in observations, tasks, or robot embodiments, complicating sim-to-real transfer and cross-robot deployment.

## Method

- **ROBOBRIDGE** introduces a policy-agnostic, ==modular orchestration framework== comprising Perceptor, Planner, Controller, Robot Interface, and Monitor modules.
- It integrates a ==two-phase monitoring== system for continuous failure detection (lightweight check, then diagnosis) that triggers hierarchical recovery strategies (retry, regenerate, replan, re-perceive), combined with ==reactive planning== via asynchronous perception that replans only when significant divergence is detected between planned and observed environments.
- For VLA controllers, it utilizes primitive skill fine-tuning by attaching lightweight ==LoRA adapters== to a frozen VLA backbone, enabling dynamic selection of primitive-specific controllers.

## Results

- ROBOBRIDGE improved the GR00T-N1.5 VLA backbone's average task success rate on the LIBERO benchmark from **35.5% to 39.7%**.
- On the RoboCasa benchmark, ROBOBRIDGE increased average success rates for various VLA backbones (SmolVLA, π0.5, GR00T-N1.5), with GR00T-N1.5's non-PnP success rate rising from **6.2% to 14.7%**.
- The framework demonstrated real-world deployment on a Franka Emika Research 3 arm, effectively detecting execution misalignment early and performing minimal recovery actions in unseen environments, showing robustness to visual variations and actuation uncertainties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving reliable robotic agency necessitates structured orchestration around action predictors, rather than solely relying on scaling the predictors themselves.
> - Effective failure recovery and long-horizon consistency are enabled by combining continuous two-phase monitoring with hierarchical recovery strategies.
> - Asynchronous perception coupled with divergence-triggered replanning allows for dynamic adaptation to environmental changes without requiring full high-level re-reasoning at every step.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as a scalable approach for learning robot control. These models, exemplified by systems such as RT-1, DROID, Open X-Embodiment, Octo, π0.5, GR00T, and SmolVLA, aim to map multimodal observations and language instructions directly to low-level actions, with an increasing scope of tasks and embodiments. This trend mirrors the development of Large Language Models (LLMs) in natural language processing, which have advanced from simple text generators to more versatile agents through external orchestration.

Despite their capabilities in action prediction, current VLA models, when deployed as monolithic forward-pass policies, exhibit limitations. These include the absence of mechanisms for detecting and recovering from execution failures, a lack of consistent behavior over extended task horizons, and reduced robustness to shifts in observations, tasks, or robot embodiments. Existing research has addressed these limitations individually, through approaches like runtime monitoring for failure detection, grounded replanning for plan-execution misalignment, LLM-guided task decomposition, and parameter-efficient adaptation for cross-domain robustness. However, these solutions often target specific failure modes or environmental settings, resulting in a fragmented landscape of fixes rather than a unified framework.

This work positions itself within the broader context of developing general-purpose orchestration frameworks, similar to those that have enhanced LLM capabilities through tool use, planning, and verification modules. ROBOBRIDGE proposes to address the identified gaps by providing a policy-agnostic, modular framework that systematizes the transformation of any action-generating policy, including pretrained VLAs, into a robust robotic agent. The paper argues that reliable robotic agency requires structured orchestration around action predictors, rather than solely relying on scaling the predictors themselves.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a modular and generalizable orchestration framework, named ROBOBRIDGE, that can transform any action-generating policy into a robust robotic agent. The motivation stems from three critical gaps observed when deploying advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in real-world robotic scenarios:

1. **Lack of Failure Recovery Mechanisms:** VLA models, as direct action predictors, do not intrinsically possess capabilities to detect or recover from execution failures. A single missed grasp, collision, or kinematic infeasibility can go unnoticed, leading to cascading errors that destabilize multi-step plans and prevent task completion. The motivation here is to enable prompt error correction to prevent minor failures from escalating.
2. **Inconsistent Execution Over Long Horizons:** Existing VLA deployments typically treat each inference step independently, without inherent mechanisms to track task progress or correct compounding errors across extended operational sequences. This leads to inconsistent behavior, especially in complex, multi-step tasks where the environment can change or diverge from initial assumptions. The objective is to maintain consistent task progression by dynamically adapting to environmental changes and ensuring the plan remains aligned with the actual scene state.
3. **Limited Robustness to Domain Shifts:** The performance of VLA models can degrade significantly when deployed in environments that differ from their training distribution. This includes shifts in observations (e.g., lighting, object appearance), tasks, or robot embodiments (e.g., different kinematics, grippers). This lack of generalization makes sim-to-real transfer and cross-robot deployment brittle. The motivation is to enhance the robustness and adaptability of VLA-based agents to various operational conditions without necessitating extensive retraining of the base model.

To address these motivations, ROBOBRIDGE aims to provide a unified solution by wrapping any action-generating policy within a structured control stack. This includes supplying capabilities such as continuous monitoring for failure detection, hierarchical recovery strategies, reactive planning with asynchronous scene understanding, and domain-invariant primitive skill execution. The overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful action prediction models and the requirements for reliable, autonomous robotic agency in dynamic real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ROBOBRIDGE is presented as a modular, generalizable orchestration framework designed to convert an action-generating policy into a robust robotic agent. The framework is composed of five coordinated modules and integrates three core mechanisms to address the identified limitations of standalone VLA models.

**Modular Framework Components:**

1. **Perceptor:** This module converts raw sensory observations into an object-centric scene state. This state includes object identities, 3D poses, and semantic attributes relevant to the task, such as affordances. The Perceptor operates asynchronously to avoid blocking execution.
2. **Planner:** The Planner receives the refined object-centric state from the Perceptor and a language instruction. It decomposes the overall task into an ordered sequence of primitive skills, which may be parameterized by additional contextual information. The Planner is designed to trigger replanning reactively when environmental conditions diverge.
3. **Controller:** This module is the designated slot for the action-generating policy, such as a pretrained VLA. It translates each primitive skill specified by the Planner into low-level actions. The framework is designed to be policy-agnostic, allowing various action generators to be plugged in.
4. **Robot Interface:** This module abstracts embodiment-specific APIs, timing constraints, safety limits, and coordinate transformations. Its role is to enable the same upstream control logic to operate across different robot platforms and simulators without modification.
5. **Monitor:** The Monitor module closes the loop by continuously verifying the outcomes of executed actions. Upon detecting failures, it triggers hierarchical recovery strategies.

**Core Mechanisms:**

The framework integrates three core mechanisms:

1. **Two-phase Monitoring for Failure Recovery:**
 - **Phase 1: Lightweight Success Check:** During execution, a success check model (Dcheck), often a vision-language model, periodically evaluates the current observation (ot) against the expected plan context (ct). It produces a binary success flag (suct) and a confidence score (cont). This check is designed to be low-latency and operate continuously without introducing significant overhead.
 - **Phase 2: Failure Diagnosis and Recovery:** If a failure is detected with high confidence (suct =false and cont ≥γthresh), the system halts execution and invokes a diagnosis model (Ddiag). This model infers the root cause of the failure and selects a recovery target (rt) and reason (reasont). The recovery strategy is hierarchical, ranging from least to most costly:
 - **Retry:** Re-executes the current primitive.
 - **Regenerate:** Regenerates the trajectory for the current primitive, retaining the current plan and perception state.
 - **Replan:** Replans from the latest asynchronous perception, regenerating primitive skills for subsequent actions.
 - **Re-perceive:** Forces a full re-perception before replanning, used when the object state is deemed unreliable.

2. **Reactive Planning with Asynchronous Perception:**
 - **Asynchronous Perception:** The system employs two concurrent threads. A perception thread continuously updates a thread-safe buffer (B) with the latest object-centric scene state (¯ot) from the Perceptor. This runs at the Perceptor's inference speed, decoupling perception latency from execution.
 - **Divergence-Triggered Replanning:** The execution thread sequentially runs primitive skills. After each primitive completes, it compares the perception observation (¯oplan) used for the current plan with the latest observation (¯olat) from the asynchronous buffer using a divergence measure (∆). This measure quantifies changes in object positions and counts object appearances/disappearances. If the divergence exceeds a threshold (τ), the high-level action sequence is preserved, but the primitive skills for the current and subsequent actions are regenerated based on ¯olat. This avoids redundant high-level reasoning while updating spatial parameters.

3. **Primitive Skill Fine-tuning (for VLA Controllers):**
 - **Factoring Manipulation into Primitives:** When a VLA acts as the Controller, manipulation is factored into domain-invariant primitive skills (e.g., MOVE, GRIP, ROTATE). The Planner decomposes tasks into sequences of these primitives.
 - **Primitive-Specific LoRA Adapters:** A lightweight Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module is attached to a frozen VLA backbone. Each primitive (pk) has a dedicated LoRA adapter (∆θk) trained on demonstrations specific to that primitive.
 - **Controller Switching:** The Controller dynamically selects the appropriate adapter based on the current primitive (RESOLVE function). If a specific adapter for a primitive exists, it is used; otherwise, an averaged adapter across all registered LoRA adapters is employed. LoRA module swapping is performed in-place. The action prediction from the selected adapter outputs a 7-DoF delta action (translational, rotational, gripper command).
 - **Action Execution:** The predicted delta is dispatched as a Cartesian velocity command. If an inverse-kinematics (IK) solver is available, the delta is converted to an absolute target for joint position control; otherwise, Cartesian velocity commands are used. This dual-path execution strategy supports various robot kinematic interfaces.

The overall pipeline orchestrates these modules and mechanisms: the task starts with initial perception and high-level planning. An asynchronous perception thread is launched. The system then iterates through planned primitives, executing actions, continuously monitoring for failures, and invoking hierarchical recovery if needed. After each primitive, it checks for environmental divergence and triggers replanning if the scene state has significantly changed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental evaluations of ROBOBRIDGE in both simulation and real-world environments, using three VLA backbones: SmolVLA, π0.5, and GR00T-N1.5.

**Simulation Results:**

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** On the LIBERO benchmark, which includes diverse manipulation tasks across different generalization categories (Object, Spatial, Goal, Long), ROBOBRIDGE consistently improved the performance of the GR00T-N1.5 VLA backbone. The average task success rate across all LIBERO tasks increased from 35.5% for the standalone VLA to 39.7% with ROBOBRIDGE. This demonstrates a benefit even when standalone VLA performance is moderate.
- **RoboCasa Benchmark:** RoboCasa, characterized by longer horizons and frequent execution drift, proved more challenging for standalone VLAs, with many tasks consistently failing. When wrapping the same controllers with ROBOBRIDGE, performance improved across all three VLA backbones (SmolVLA, π0.5, GR00T-N1.5) and a broad subset of tasks.
 - For SmolVLA, average success rose from 3.4% to 6.9%.
 - For π0.5, average success rose from 3.4% to 5.9%.
 - For GR00T-N1.5, average success rose from 4.2% to 9.8%.
 - Excluding pick-and-place tasks, the average success rate for GR00T-N1.5 increased from 6.2% to 14.7%. These improvements were achieved without scaling or retraining the base VLA policies, attributed to the agent capabilities provided by ROBOBRIDGE, such as two-phase monitoring with hierarchical recovery and reactive planning with asynchronous perception.

**Real-World Case Study:**

- ROBOBRIDGE was deployed on a Franka Emika Research 3 arm for long-horizon, complex tasks.
- The system demonstrated the ability to detect execution misalignment early (indicated by red boxes in provided figures) and trigger minimal recovery actions, allowing the agent to resume progress without restarting the entire plan.
- This failure-aware monitoring and recovery behavior transferred to unseen environments, showing robustness to visual variations and actuation uncertainties without additional adaptation. The agent completed tasks successfully (indicated by green boxes) in both training and unseen environments.

**Analysis:**

- **Effect of LLM Backbone and Monitoring:** An analysis on RoboCasa tasks (excluding PnP) showed that the choice of LLM backbone for the Planner and Phase-2 Monitor impacts performance.
 - Without monitoring, LLM backbones performed within a narrow range (1.8–8.0% success), suggesting planning alone is insufficient.
 - With two-phase monitoring enabled, Claude Opus 4.6 achieved the highest gain (+8.1 percentage points, reaching 14.7% success), followed by GPT-5 mini (+6.2 percentage points). Smaller models like Gemini-3 Flash and Claude Haiku 4.5 showed marginal improvements. This indicates that effective failure diagnosis benefits from sufficient reasoning capacity in the LLM backbone, and that both planning and monitoring contribute synergistically.
- **Analysis of Controller:** Comparisons of different controller configurations on a subset of RoboCasa tasks revealed:
 - ROBOBRIDGE improved both LoRA fine-tuning (+11.8 percentage points) and full fine-tuning (+8.1 percentage points) strategies, confirming its generalizability across controller types.
 - LoRA fine-tuning within ROBOBRIDGE (27.1%) approached the standalone full fine-tuning baseline (31.9%) despite fewer updated parameters. Full fine-tuning itself further benefited from ROBOBRIDGE, reaching 40.0%.
 - A classical inverse-kinematics (IK) controller achieved 22.1% success within ROBOBRIDGE, demonstrating the framework's policy-agnostic design extends to non-learned controllers.
 - CycleVLA, a VLA variant with model-internal subtask backtracking, achieved only 7.5% success without ROBOBRIDGE, suggesting that external orchestration provided by ROBOBRIDGE offers more comprehensive correction than model-internal mechanisms alone.
- **Failure Cases Analysis:** Two dominant failure modes were identified:
 - **Perception errors:** Misidentification or mislocalization of target objects, often in cluttered or occluded scenes, leading to incorrect primitive execution.
 - **Unrecoverable manipulation failures:** Irreversible alterations to the environment state during contact-rich interactions, rendering further retries futile. This highlights the need for stronger verification and validation to detect when success is unlikely and to trigger alternative strategies or safe termination.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ROBOBRIDGE represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a modular, generalizable orchestration framework that enhances the reliability and robustness of robotic agents built upon existing action-generating policies, including advanced VLA models. Its significance lies in several key aspects:

1. **Bridging the Gap from Policy to Agent:** The work addresses the observation that powerful action predictors, such as VLA models, do not inherently constitute robust robotic agents. ROBOBRIDGE provides the surrounding infrastructure—monitoring, recovery, planning, and interface—that elevates these predictors into capable agents without requiring re-training or architectural changes to the base policy. This shift in focus from merely scaling predictors to structured orchestration is a central thesis of the paper and aligns with advancements seen in LLM agent development.
2. **Generalizability and Policy Agnosticism:** The framework's modular design allows it to wrap any action-generating policy, demonstrated by its compatibility with various VLA backbones (SmolVLA, π0.5, GR00T-N1.5) and even classical IK controllers. This policy agnosticism means that improvements in base policies can be seamlessly integrated and augmented by ROBOBRIDGE's capabilities, fostering broader applicability across diverse robotic systems and research efforts.
3. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** By systematically addressing critical limitations—failure recovery, long-horizon consistency, and domain robustness—ROBOBRIDGE facilitates more reliable operation in dynamic and complex real-world settings. The two-phase monitoring with hierarchical recovery enables prompt error correction, preventing cascading failures. Reactive planning with asynchronous perception ensures adaptability to environmental changes, improving consistency over long task horizons. Primitive skill fine-tuning with LoRA adapters reduces sensitivity to domain shifts, promoting effective sim-to-real transfer and cross-robot deployment.
4. **Practical Implications for Deployment:** The framework's ability to operate in real-world settings on multiple robot platforms (Franka Emika Research 3, UR7e) without extensive re-adaptation demonstrates its practical utility. This makes it a candidate for more robust deployment of learned policies in industrial or service robotics where reliability is paramount. The modularity could also simplify the development process for robot applications by allowing researchers and engineers to compose robust agents from off-the-shelf components.
5. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies areas for future work, such as expanding the primitive skill vocabulary for more complex interactions, learning monitoring thresholds and recovery rules from data, and integrating explicit verification and validation layers. These directions point towards further enhancing the autonomy and intelligence of robotic systems, potentially leading to robots that can assess task feasibility, select optimal strategies, and manage uncertain outcomes more effectively.

In summary, ROBOBRIDGE provides a systematic approach to developing more robust and reliable robotic agents by offering a structured orchestration layer around existing control policies. Its focus on modularity, policy agnosticism, and comprehensive error handling contributes to advancing the deployment capabilities of learned robotic manipulation systems.
