# HELM: Harness-Enhanced Long-horizon Memory for Vision-Language-Action Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HELM, a model-agnostic framework developed by Tsinghua University and Alibaba Group, enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for complex robotic manipulation by integrating episodic memory, a learned pre-execution state verifier, and a robust recovery controller. This system improved task success rates by 23.1 percentage points on LIBERO-LONG and achieved a 54.2% recovery success rate on perturbed tasks, addressing limitations in long-horizon task performance.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models systematically fail on long-horizon manipulation tasks despite strong performance on shorter tasks, with merely increasing context window length offering only marginal improvements.
- The 'Memory Gap' in VLA models causes them to discard past task context, leading to 'forgetting' completed subgoals and inconsistent actions.
- The 'Verification Gap' results from VLA models reactively proposing actions without pre-execution validation of their feasibility or correctness, leading to propagation of errors.

## Method

- **HELM** introduces an ==Episodic Memory Module (EMM)== that maintains a persistent key-value store of past keyframes and task context, retrieved using ==CLIP embeddings== to augment the VLA's input.
- A lightweight, learned ==State Verifier (SV)== predicts the probability of action failure *before* execution, reasoning jointly over current visual state, proposed action, subgoal, and retrieved memory.
- A ==Harness Controller (HC)== manages subgoal execution and initiates recovery procedures (rollback or forward recovery) based on SV predictions, providing failure context to the VLA.

## Results

- HELM achieved **81.5%** TSR on LIBERO-LONG (**+23.1pp** over OpenVLA's **58.4%**) and **54.2%** RSR on LIBERO-Recovery vs OpenVLA's **12.3%**.
- Ablations: **EMM +11.2pp TSR**, **SV +8.4pp TSR**; learned SV outperformed rule-based and ensemble verifiers.
- Model-agnostic: improved Octo VLA from **51.2% → 72.8%** on LIBERO-LONG (**+21.6pp**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simply extending the VLA model's context window is insufficient for resolving performance issues in long-horizon tasks; structural architectural augmentations are required.
> - Pre-execution verification, particularly when conditioned on retrieved episodic memory, is crucial for preventing errors and enabling robust execution, offering advantages over reactive or post-hoc correction methods.
> - A modular, model-agnostic framework can significantly enhance the reliability and performance of various existing VLA backbones without requiring core model retraining or modification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. Existing VLA models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, and Octo, have demonstrated proficiency in short-horizon tasks. However, the authors identify a systematic limitation in their ability to perform complex, long-horizon manipulation sequences. This limitation stems from their predominantly reactive execution paradigm, which typically lacks persistent memory for past task context, pre-execution action validation, and robust failure recovery mechanisms.

The paper contrasts its approach with several related areas:
- **VLA models:** Current VLA models operate in a reactive loop, mapping observations directly to actions without retaining long-term task history or performing proactive checks. HELM seeks to augment these models, rather than replace them, by addressing these architectural gaps.
- **Memory for embodied agents:** Previous work on memory for agents, such as FILM (semantic maps for navigation), Voyager (skill libraries for exploration), and Generative Agents (importance-weighted memory streams), typically addresses different aspects of memory or application domains. HELM's Episodic Memory Module (EMM) distinguishes itself by focusing on multi-stage manipulation with physical state constraints, utilizing keyframe-boundary write triggers, CLIP-based visual retrieval, and being tightly integrated with a learned failure predictor.
- **LLM agent frameworks:** Frameworks like ReAct, Reflexion, and CRITIC employ Large Language Models (LLMs) for reasoning and post-hoc textual reflection or correction. HELM's State Verifier (SV) operates on visual-physical state and performs *pre-execution* failure prediction, which is presented as a fundamentally different and more challenging problem than post-hoc text-based correction. The SV also contrasts with systems like Inner Monologue, which rely on hand-crafted success detectors.
- **Failure recovery:** Many existing robot planning and control systems, including SayCan, ProgPrompt, Code as Policies, and PaLM-E, do not explicitly model execution-time failures or lack comprehensive recovery loops. While Inner Monologue does incorporate replanning on failure, it uses hand-crafted detectors, unlike HELM's learned SV and integrated recovery system.
- **Pre-execution verification:** The concept of learned feasibility predictors has been explored in general motion planning, but the authors state that HELM's SV is the first learned pre-execution failure predictor in the VLA context that conditions on episodic memory. This is noted as significant due to the high-dimensional action space and the semantic nature of failure signals in VLA manipulation, which often depend on historical context.

Overall, this work extends the capabilities of existing VLA models by introducing a modular, model-agnostic framework designed to bridge critical deficiencies that emerge in long-horizon tasks, thereby enhancing their reliability and robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research arises from an observed systematic failure of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on long-horizon manipulation tasks, despite their strong performance on shorter, simpler tasks. For example, OpenVLA achieves a 91.2% success rate on LIBERO-SPATIAL (average 2.3 subgoals) but drops to 58.4% on LIBERO-LONG (average 5.8 subgoals). Initial investigations by the authors reveal that this performance gap is not closed by merely increasing the VLA model's context window length. Even with a context history (H) extended to 32 steps (4x the default), the task success rate (TSR) for OpenVLA on LIBERO-LONG only rises to 63.8%, leaving a significant portion of the performance gap unexplained. This observation suggests that the issue is structural, rather than solely a limitation of memory capacity within the immediate context window.

The authors identify three specific deficiencies within the current reactive execution loop of VLA models that contribute to these failures:

1. **Memory Gap (F_M):** The fixed-size context window employed by VLA models discards information about previously completed subgoals. This leads to situations where the model "forgets" past actions or states, potentially attempting to re-execute an already completed step or taking an action inconsistent with the established task history. For instance, the model might attempt to place an object into a cabinet that it already placed there earlier in the task, corrupting the overall task state.
2. **Verification Gap (F_V):** Current VLA models propose actions reactively without any mechanism to validate their feasibility or correctness *before* execution. This means that physically infeasible grasps, unintended object contacts, or motions outside the workspace can be silently executed. Such errors propagate through the task, leading to subsequent failures.
3. **Recovery Gap (F_R):** When an action fails, the reactive execution paradigm typically continues from a corrupted state. Without explicit failure detection and a recovery strategy, these initial failures can cascade, rendering subsequent subgoals unachievable and leading to overall task failure.

The key objective of HELM is to develop a model-agnostic framework that addresses each of these identified gaps with dedicated, learned components. Specifically, the framework aims to:
- Introduce a mechanism for long-term task context retrieval to bridge the memory gap.
- Implement a pre-execution action validation component to close the verification gap.
- Establish a robust failure detection and recovery system to address the recovery gap.

The State Verifier (SV) is highlighted as the core learning contribution, motivated by the need for a mechanism that can jointly reason over the current visual state, the proposed action, the subgoal context, and the task history to predict action failure *before* execution. This pre-execution verification is deemed qualitatively different from post-hoc reflection or uncertainty-based filtering, necessitating a learned component augmented by long-horizon memory.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HELM (Harness-Enhanced Long-horizon Memory) is designed as a model-agnostic framework that wraps any frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) backbone, augmenting its capabilities for long-horizon manipulation. The framework operates by introducing three learned components to address the identified memory, verification, and recovery gaps.

The HELM execution loop, at each timestep $t$, proceeds as follows:
1. **Memory Retrieval:** The Episodic Memory Module (EMM) retrieves relevant historical context, denoted $m_t$.
2. **Action Proposal:** The VLA backbone ($\pi_\theta$) proposes an action $a_{prop_t}$ conditioned on the current observation $o_t$, the overall task instruction $\tau$, the current subgoal $g_t$, and the retrieved memory $m_t$.
3. **Action Verification:** The State Verifier (SV) predicts the probability of failure ($p_{fail}$) for the proposed action.
4. **Execution or Recovery:** The Harness Controller (HC) then decides whether to execute the proposed action $a_{prop_t}$ or trigger a recovery procedure based on the SV's prediction.

Details of each component are as follows:

#### 3.1. Episodic Memory Module (EMM)

The EMM addresses the *memory gap (F_M)* by maintaining a persistent key-value store, $M = \{(k_i, v_i)\}$.
- **Key ($k_i$):** A CLIP ViT-B/32 embedding of a past observation $o_i$, providing a semantically rich representation of the visual state. These embeddings are L2-normalized.
- **Value ($v_i$):** A tuple containing the keyframe ($o_i$), the active subgoal ($g_i$), its completion status, the timestep ($t_i$), and a compact state delta (gripper pose + object positions from depth).
- **Write Policy:** New entries are written to memory under three conditions: upon subgoal completion (success), upon detected failure, or at regular checkpoints every $\Delta_c = 20$ steps.
- **Retrieval:** At each step $t$, the EMM retrieves the top-$k$ (with $k=3$ being the chosen value) memory entries. Retrieval is based on cosine similarity between the current observation's CLIP embedding $\phi(o_t)$ and the stored key embeddings $k_i$. The retrieved values are then serialized into structured text and appended to the VLA's language input, providing task-history context.
- **Compression:** To manage memory size, if $|M|$ exceeds $N_{max}=50$ entries, the EMM retains only the most recent checkpoint associated with each subgoal.

#### 3.2. State Verifier (SV): Core Learning Contribution

The SV is designed to address the *verification gap (F_V)* by predicting the probability of action failure *before* execution.
- **Motivation:** This component is central because pre-execution failure prediction requires reasoning about the complex interplay of visual state, proposed action, subgoal context, and task history. The authors argue this is beyond the capabilities of simple rule-based checks (which miss semantic errors and require extensive engineering) or uncertainty heuristics (which are action-agnostic). The reliance on memory-augmented context ($M_t$) is crucial for semantic reasoning.
- **Model Architecture:** A lightweight 3-layer MLP with dimensions [1024→512→256→1], utilizing ReLU activation and 0.1 dropout. This architecture is chosen for its low latency (12 ms/step).
- **Input:** The SV takes a concatenated input:
 - `ˆo_t = [ϕ(o_t); k_{top-1}] ∈ R^{1024}`: The current observation's CLIP embedding concatenated with the CLIP embedding of the top-1 retrieved keyframe from the EMM. This explicitly injects memory context.
 - `W_a * a_t ∈ R^{256}`: A learned projection of the proposed action.
 - `ϕ_{text}(g_t) ∈ R^{256}`: An embedding of the current subgoal text.
- **Training:** The SV is trained on a dataset of 50,000 `(o_t, a_t, g_t, y_t)` triples collected from VLA rollouts on training tasks. The label $y_t=1$ indicates that a failure occurred within 5 subsequent steps. Binary cross-entropy loss is used, with a positive weight of 4.0 to account for class imbalance. Training is performed using Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ and a batch size of 256, taking approximately 2 hours on an A100 GPU.
- **Threshold:** A predicted failure probability $p_{fail} > \theta_v = 0.65$ triggers the Harness Controller's recovery mechanism.

#### 3.3. Harness Controller (HC)

The HC addresses the *recovery gap (F_R)* by orchestrating the execution flow and implementing recovery strategies.
- **Subgoal Management:** The HC maintains a subgoal stack `S`, which is initialized by prompting the VLA backbone ($\pi_\theta$) to decompose the overall task instruction $\tau$ into sequential subgoals. It also utilizes a separate completion detector (with an architecture similar to the SV) to verify subgoal achievement.
- **Recovery Mechanism:** If the SV predicts a high probability of failure ($p_{fail} > \theta_v$) or the completion detector indicates that a subgoal was not met, the HC initiates a recovery sequence. This involves:
 1. Retrieving the most recent checkpoint or successful entry from the EMM.
 2. Issuing a goal-conditioned recovery prompt (e.g., "return to the state shown") to the VLA.
 3. Re-pushing the failed subgoal onto the stack.
 4. Appending the failure entry to the context for future reference.
 A maximum of $R_{max}=3$ recovery attempts are allowed before marking the entire task as failed.
- **Forward Recovery Variant (HELM-Fwd):** For environments where physically reversing actions (rollback) is not feasible, the HC can instead generate a *forward recovery plan* from the current corrupted state. This variant is evaluated separately to assess the necessity of rollback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of HELM was evaluated across three benchmarks: LIBERO-LONG, CALVIN ABC→D, and a newly introduced LIBERO-Recovery protocol designed for perturbation injection. The experiments compared HELM against nine baselines, including extended context windows, oracle memory, rule-based verifiers, ensemble methods, same-budget backbone fine-tuning (LoRA), and an alternative reflection mechanism.

**Key Findings:**

- **Longer Context Alone is Insufficient:** Increasing the OpenVLA context window from the default H=8 to H=32 resulted in a marginal task success rate (TSR) increase of only 5.4 percentage points (pp) on LIBERO-LONG (from 58.4% to 63.8%). This observation supports the claim that the limitations of VLA models in long-horizon tasks are structural and not merely a function of insufficient immediate history.
- **HELM Achieves Substantial Performance Gains:**
 - On **LIBERO-LONG**, HELM (using OpenVLA as backbone) achieved an 81.5% TSR, representing a 23.1 pp improvement over the baseline OpenVLA (58.4%). The Subgoal Completion Rate (SCR) also increased from 74.2% to 89.3%.
 - On the novel **LIBERO-Recovery** protocol, HELM demonstrated a Recovery Success Rate (RSR) of 54.2%, significantly higher than OpenVLA's 12.3%.
 - For **CALVIN ABC→D**, HELM completed an average of 3.58 chains, compared to OpenVLA's 3.02.
- **Learned State Verifier Outperforms Alternatives:**
 - A rule-based verifier, while providing some improvement, yielded only +6.8 pp TSR over OpenVLA.
 - An ensemble of 5 SV models achieved +9.5 pp TSR but at a 5x inference cost.
 - HELM's learned SV, through ablation, was shown to contribute +8.4 pp TSR at 1x inference cost, indicating its superior efficiency and effectiveness compared to both rule-based and uncertainty-based (ensemble) methods.
- **Memory-Conditioned Verification is Critical:** Ablation studies showed that removing memory context from the SV's input (`SV w/o m_t`) degraded its contribution to TSR by 6.1 pp (from 8.4 pp to 2.3 pp), confirming the crucial coupling between the Episodic Memory Module (EMM) and the State Verifier (SV).
- **Rollback Enhances Recovery:** The HELM-Fwd variant (using forward recovery) achieved 76.3% TSR and 38.7% RSR. However, the full HELM system, which incorporates rollback capabilities, provided an additional 5.2 pp TSR and 15.5 pp RSR, indicating the significant benefit of being able to revert to a previous state when feasible.
- **Model-Agnosticism Demonstrated:** When HELM was applied to the Octo VLA backbone, it improved Octo's TSR by 21.6 pp (from 51.2% to 72.8%), a relative gain comparable to that observed with OpenVLA. This demonstrates the framework's generalizability across different VLA architectures.
- **Ablation Study (Component Contributions):**
 - Removing the EMM resulted in the largest performance drop, reducing TSR by 11.2 pp.
 - Removing the SV decreased TSR by 8.4 pp.
 - Removing rollback reduced TSR by 6.3 pp and RSR by 31.1 pp.
 - Removing both EMM and SV led to a cumulative drop of 19.1 pp TSR.
- **Mechanism Analysis Insights:**
 - **Retrieval Method:** CLIP-based retrieval for EMM achieved 81.5% TSR, matching a fine-tuned learned retriever (82.1%) with no additional training cost, and significantly outperforming random (64.3%) or recency-based (71.4%) methods.
 - **Failure Label Horizon:** The SV's AUROC for failure prediction peaked at a 5-step horizon (0.847), confirming the training strategy.
 - **Memory Size:** $k=3$ retrieved keyframes for EMM was found to be optimal, with larger $k$ providing negligible additional benefit.
 - **Subgoal Decomposition:** Using oracle (ground-truth) subgoal decomposition resulted in 84.2% TSR, a minor improvement of 2.7 pp over HELM's VLA-generated decomposition, suggesting decomposition quality is a secondary bottleneck.
 - **Failure Mode Reduction:** HELM effectively reduced all three identified failure modes: F_M (memory gap) by 76%, F_V (verification gap) by 61%, and F_R (recovery gap) by 82%. The most substantial reduction was observed in F_R, attributed to the rollback mechanism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HELM framework presents a significant advancement in enhancing the reliability and performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. By systematically addressing the identified memory, verification, and recovery gaps, HELM contributes to developing more robust and autonomous embodied AI systems.

The significance of this work can be understood through several key points:

- **Bridging a Critical Performance Gap:** HELM directly tackles the long-standing challenge of VLA models failing systematically on complex, multi-stage tasks. The empirical evidence that simply extending context length is insufficient underscores the structural nature of these failures, which HELM effectively resolves. This suggests that complementary execution-time augmentations, rather than solely scaling backbone models, are crucial for progress in embodied AI.
- **Novel Learned Pre-execution Verification:** The State Verifier (SV) is a core contribution, introducing a memory-conditioned, learned component for predicting action failure *before* execution. This approach offers a distinct advantage over post-hoc reflection, rule-based checks, or ensemble uncertainty methods, providing a more proactive and semantically aware mechanism for error prevention and detection. Its reliance on episodic memory to understand task history for verification represents a new direction in VLA capabilities.
- **Model-Agnostic Augmentation:** HELM's design as a wrapper for existing VLA backbones (demonstrated with both OpenVLA and Octo) means it can enhance a broad range of current and future VLA architectures. This positions HELM as a framework for improving the practical deployment of various VLA models without requiring extensive retraining or modification of their core components.
- **Enhanced Fault Tolerance and Recovery:** The Harness Controller, with its ability to orchestrate memory retrieval, action verification, and strategic recovery (including rollback or forward recovery), significantly improves the agent's ability to handle unexpected perturbations and recover from errors. The introduction of LIBERO-Recovery as a standardized evaluation protocol for failure recovery is also a valuable contribution, encouraging further research in this critical area.
- **Practical Implications for Robotics:** For real-world robotic applications, robustness and reliability are paramount. HELM's ability to reduce task failures and enable recovery from unforeseen events or execution errors could lead to more dependable robotic systems in manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics. The HELM-Fwd variant, in particular, offers a pathway for deploying the framework in environments where physical rollback is not feasible.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Positive Societal Impact:** More reliable and capable robots could reduce the need for human intervention in repetitive, dangerous, or physically demanding tasks, leading to improved workplace safety and efficiency. This could free human workers for more creative and complex roles.
- **Negative Societal Impact (Long-term Consideration):** As robotic capabilities advance, concerns about job displacement in certain sectors may arise. While HELM is a research contribution focused on fundamental robotic intelligence, the broader trajectory towards more autonomous systems warrants careful consideration of societal transitions and policies to support affected workforces.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also highlight avenues for future research:
- **SV Data Dependency:** The SV requires a significant amount of rollout data (approx. 50K steps) for training, limiting its zero-shot deployment in new environments. Future work could explore data-efficient learning or transfer learning for the SV.
- **Real-Robot Rollback:** The effectiveness of rollback in real-world scenarios is constrained by the reversibility of physical actions. While HELM-Fwd addresses this, further real-robot validation is necessary.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** All experiments are conducted in simulation. The generalization of the learned SV and CLIP embeddings to real-world visual domain shifts and sensor noise, as well as the adequacy of simulated perturbations, requires further study.
- **Subgoal Decomposition Quality:** Although a minor bottleneck, improving the VLA's ability to decompose tasks into optimal subgoals could yield further gains.
- **Computational Overhead:** While the SV is lightweight, the additional computational overhead (12 ms/step) compared to the VLA backbone alone warrants consideration for latency-critical applications.

Overall, HELM provides a robust framework for augmenting VLA models, contributing to the development of more intelligent and fault-tolerant robotic systems capable of performing complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks with greater reliability.
