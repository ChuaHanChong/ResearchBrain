# CoRT: Code-integrated Reasoning within Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> The CoRT framework enables Large Reasoning Models to efficiently leverage Code Interpreters for complex mathematical reasoning, achieving higher accuracy and significantly reducing token usage by optimizing interaction patterns. It introduces a novel hint-engineering data synthesis method and an end-to-end training pipeline combining supervised, rejection, and reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) struggle with precision and efficiency in complex mathematical operations and symbolic problem-solving, areas where Code Interpreters (CIs) excel.
- High-quality, human-annotated data for code-integrated reasoning is scarce, making it difficult to teach LRMs effective CI interaction.
- Existing tool-integrated reasoning approaches often exhibit inefficiencies such as "delayed code computation" (performing text-based reasoning before using CI) and "code result distrust" (unnecessarily verifying CI outputs).

## Method

- The **CoRT** framework introduces "==Hint-engineering==," a novel and sample-efficient method for synthesizing high-quality code-integrated reasoning data by strategically inserting hints to guide LRM behavior.
- An end-to-end training pipeline combines Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for initial learning, Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT) to filter suboptimal reasoning traces, and Code-integrated Reinforcement Learning (RL) for refined policy learning.
- A "strong-to-weak distillation" strategy facilitates RL training on smaller models, incorporating a persistent execution environment and a custom dual reward system to enhance code integration and reduce errors.

## Results

- Hint-Engineering-RFT-**32B** achieved **81.3%** average accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, competitive with leading models, despite being trained on only 30 manually annotated samples.
- Hint-Engineering models demonstrated 30-**50%** lower token consumption compared to baselines while maintaining or improving performance, notably achieving similar performance to QwQ-**32B** with **50%** fewer tokens.
- Code behavior analysis showed Hint-Engineering shifted CI usage from primarily verification (**82.4%**) to a balanced mix of direct calculation (**51.1%**) and verification (**48.9%**), indicating more efficient and appropriate tool integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Hint-engineering," which uses a small set of 30 high-quality, strategically hinted samples, significantly improves model efficiency by guiding immediate CI use for calculations and fostering trust in CI outputs.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) yields substantial performance gains (approximately 8% absolute accuracy) for smaller models, fundamentally enhancing their ability to consistently find correct solutions.
> - A dual reward system in RL, including a code execution penalty, effectively encourages correct CI usage and further improves performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) has witnessed remarkable advancements, particularly with the adoption of techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. Models such as OpenAI's o1, QwQ, and DeepSeek-R1 have demonstrated impressive human-like cognitive strategies, including self-refinement and multi-strategy exploration, enabling them to tackle complex reasoning tasks in natural language. However, a significant limitation persists: LRMs often struggle with precision and efficiency in complex mathematical operations, precise computations, and symbolic problem-solving. These tasks are inherently deterministic and are better suited for computational tools like Code Interpreters (CIs).

The integration of CIs with LRMs has emerged as a promising avenue to overcome these limitations, as evidenced by models like o3 and o4-mini, which have shown enhanced mathematical reasoning capabilities. However, this integration presents a unique technical challenge: CIs introduce external, deterministic knowledge beyond the model's internal text representations, making direct and efficient combination non-trivial. The core problem is not just *whether* LRMs can use tools, but *when* and *how* they can use them effectively and efficiently to generate structured, precise reasoning.

CoRT positions itself within this evolving landscape by directly addressing the inefficiencies and sub-optimal behaviors observed in existing tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) approaches. Prior works like ToRA pioneered code-integrated reasoning for math, while others like COA and rStar-Math explored structured reasoning chains and data synthesis. Concurrent efforts such as START and STILL3 also incorporate hints or prompting for CI integration. However, CoRT distinguishes itself by focusing on the *quality* and *strategic placement* of interaction signals (hints) and efficient post-training methods. Specifically, it tackles:
- **Data Scarcity:** High-quality, human-annotated code-integrated reasoning data is rare. CoRT proposes a novel data synthesis framework ("Hint-Engineering") that leverages a small number of carefully curated samples.
- **Efficiency:** Many existing approaches, even if accurate, suffer from "delayed code computation" (models perform text-based reasoning before using CI for verification) and "code result distrust" (models unnecessarily verify CI outputs). CoRT aims to significantly reduce token usage by optimizing these interaction patterns.
- **Training Scalability:** The paper explores efficient post-training pipelines (SFT, RFT, RL) across different model scales (1.5B to 32B), demonstrating that substantial improvements can be achieved with minimal high-quality data.

By focusing on these challenges, CoRT contributes to pushing the boundaries of LRM capabilities, moving beyond mere tool *usage* to truly *integrated* and *efficient* reasoning. It demonstrates that strategic data curation and targeted fine-tuning can lead to significant gains in both accuracy and computational efficiency, making tool-integrated LRMs more practical and performant for real-world mathematical problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the CoRT framework is to teach Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) to leverage Code Interpreters (CIs) effectively and efficiently, particularly for complex mathematical operations. The motivation stems from the observed limitations of current LRMs in handling precise computation and complex equation solving, areas where CIs excel but are not seamlessly integrated.

The paper identifies three key open challenges that motivate the CoRT framework:
1. **Data Scarcity for High-Quality Training:** How can high-quality training data for code-integrated reasoning be synthesized, especially when the detailed reasoning traces of frontier models (like o3 and o4-mini) are not exposed? This challenge is critical because effective CI integration requires teaching LRMs not only *when* to use external tools but also *how* to structure their reasoning in conjunction with them.
2. **Coordination Between CI Precision and CoT Abstraction:** How can the deterministic computational precision of CIs be effectively coordinated with the abstract, flexible reasoning capabilities of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) processes? This involves teaching the model to judiciously switch between natural language reasoning and code execution.
3. **Reconciling Self-Reflection with External Knowledge:** How can the inherent self-reflection mechanisms of LRMs be reconciled with the exact, external knowledge provided by CIs? Current models often exhibit "code result distrust," where they unnecessarily verify CI outputs, leading to inefficiencies.

Driven by these challenges, CoRT aims to develop methods that not only improve the accuracy of mathematical reasoning but also significantly enhance the *token efficiency* of LRMs when interacting with CIs. The motivation is to overcome the observed inefficiencies such as "delayed code computation" and "code result distrust" that lead to redundant steps and higher computational costs. The ultimate goal is to enable LRMs to understand the outputs of CIs deeply, mitigate meaningless reflection behaviors, and integrate external knowledge seamlessly into their reasoning processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoRT proposes a multi-faceted framework, illustrated in Figure 2, comprising data synthesis, model training pipelines, and a specialized reinforcement learning approach.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

Code-integrated reasoning is modeled as an iterative process involving a problem input ($P$), a language model ($\pi$), and an executor environment ($E$). The system constructs a sequence $\tau_t$ at each time step $t$, where $\tau_t = \{(n_1, p_1, o_1)..., (n_t, p_t, o_t)\}$. Here, $n_i$ is a textual reasoning step, $p_i$ is a program snippet generated by the model, and $o_i$ is its execution output from $E$. This iterative mechanism creates a dynamic feedback loop, allowing each reasoning step to be informed by prior computational results.

#### 3.2. Cold Start Methods

##### 3.2.1. Prompt-hint

As a preliminary step to data generation, the authors developed a basic prompting strategy to encourage initial CI usage. By inserting a simple hint—"Okay, let’s try to solve this problem step by step using multiple python code calls"—immediately after the model's thinking token `<think>`, they observed a significant increase in code triggering rates (from 50% to 90%). This "prompt-hint" approach, applied to the R1 model and STILL3 dataset (820 math problems), generated 800 training instances ($D_{prompt-hint}$), used for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B model, creating Prompt-Hint-SFT-32B. While effective in initiating code usage, it revealed inefficiencies: "delayed code computation" and "code result distrust."

##### 3.2.2. Hint-engineering

To address the inefficiencies of the prompt-hint approach, the paper introduces "hint-engineering," a core novelty. This method involves strategically inserting different hints at appropriate positions throughout the reasoning process to guide the LRM towards more efficient CI utilization.
- **Addressing Delayed Code Computation:** When the model begins manual calculation of complex operations, a hint like "It looks tedious, and we can use python code to simplify the reasoning.```python" is inserted to encourage immediate CI use.
- **Addressing Code Result Distrust:** When the model shows signs of re-verifying CI outputs, a hint like "We don’t need to doubt the accuracy of python calculations." is introduced to redirect its focus.
A critical aspect of hint-engineering is the *manual* generation of hints for 30 high-quality problems from AIME, based on the principle that "data quality outweighs quantity" (less is more). This small, high-quality dataset forms $D_{Hint-engineering-SFT}$ and is used to train Hint-Engineering-SFT-32B.

To further refine performance, **Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT)** is employed. The Hint-Engineering-SFT-32B model is used to sample multiple trajectories on the 820 STILL3 problems. Incorrect trajectories and those exhibiting delayed computation or distrust are filtered out. The remaining high-quality trajectories are combined with $D_{Hint-engineering-SFT}$ to create $D_{Hint-engineering-RFT}$ (830 examples), used to fine-tune DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, resulting in Hint-Engineering-RFT-32B.

#### 3.3. Strong-to-weak Distillation

Due to computational constraints for RL on 32B models, a "strong-to-weak distillation" strategy is used. Both Prompt-Hint-SFT-32B and Hint-Engineering-RFT-32B are distilled into the smaller DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B architecture using 10k selected examples from public datasets. This yields Prompt-Hint-1.5B-SFT and Hint-Engineering-SFT-1.5B, which serve as base models for RL experiments.

#### 3.4. Code-integrated Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is applied to the distilled 1.5B models using the GRPO algorithm, with several key modifications for code integration:
- **Rollout with Code Interpreter:** Multiple model-CI interactions are enabled during rollouts, with a maximum tool usage limit ($T$) to manage overhead.
- **Persistent Execution Environment:** A Jupyter-like environment is maintained where variables and functions persist across code blocks, enhancing code efficiency and reducing errors.
- **Output Masking:** Execution results are masked to ensure training stability and prevent model collapse due to potential code failures.
- **Reward Design:** A dual reward system is implemented:
 - **Accuracy Reward ($R_a$):** 1 if the final answer matches ground truth, 0 otherwise.
 - **Code Execution Reward ($R_c$):** -1 if all code execution attempts fail (penalty for infinite loops), 0 otherwise.
 - **Total Reward ($R = R_a + \omega R_c$):** $\omega$ controls the contribution of the code execution penalty, tuned to encourage correct code usage without excessively penalizing exploration.

This comprehensive methodology, ranging from targeted data synthesis to advanced RL, aims to imbue LRMs with sophisticated and efficient code-integrated reasoning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on five challenging mathematical reasoning datasets (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, and OlympiadBench) consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of the CoRT framework.

#### 4.1. Performance Improvements

- **32B Models:** Prompt-Hint-SFT-32B achieves competitive performance (81.8% average accuracy), slightly outperforming other tool-integrated models. Notably, **Hint-Engineering-RFT-32B, despite being initially trained on only 30 manually annotated samples, achieves a highly competitive 81.3% average accuracy.** This underscores the efficacy of high-quality data and rejection fine-tuning.
- **1.5B Models:** The reinforcement learning (RL) stage yields substantial gains. Prompt-Hint-1.5B-RL achieves state-of-the-art performance among lightweight models (58.3% average accuracy), surpassing non-tool-using baselines. Hint-Engineering-1.5B-RL also shows strong results at 56.4%. The **dramatic improvement from SFT to RL stages (approximately 8% absolute gain)** highlights the power of RL for tool-integrated reasoning.

#### 4.2. Token Efficiency Analysis

- **Superior Efficiency of Hint-Engineering:** Hint-Engineering models consistently demonstrate higher token efficiency. For instance, Hint-Engineering-RFT-32B achieves performance comparable to QwQ-32B while using **50% fewer tokens (7K vs 14K)**. Compared to R1-distill-32B, Hint-Engineering-SFT-32B reduces token consumption by **approximately 30%** while maintaining comparable performance, despite only 30 training examples.
- **Efficiency Across All Responses:** Hint-Engineering significantly reduces token consumption for *both correct and incorrect responses*, indicating that it not only solves problems efficiently but also minimizes wasted tokens during unsuccessful attempts. This is attributed to its design promoting immediate code use for computations and increased confidence in CI outputs.
- **Optimal Performance-to-Efficiency Ratio:** When visualizing performance against token usage (Figure 1), Hint-Engineering-RFT-32B occupies an optimal region, showcasing the best performance-to-efficiency ratio among all compared models.

#### 4.3. Code Behavior Analysis

A qualitative analysis reveals significant differences in code usage patterns:
- **Prompt-Hint Approach:** Code is predominantly used for *verification* (82.4%) of results derived through natural language reasoning, showing an inefficient pattern.
- **Hint-Engineering Approach:** Exhibits a more balanced usage, with **51.1% of code dedicated to direct *calculation* and 48.9% for verification.** This reflects a more sophisticated understanding of when to leverage computational tools.
- **Diversity of Operations:** Hint-Engineering also shows greater diversity in the types of mathematical operations performed by the CI (e.g., symbolic mathematics, equation solving), implying a broader and more appropriate understanding of CI capabilities.

#### 4.4. Impact of Code Reward in RL

Ablation studies confirm the positive impact of the code execution reward. Both Prompt-Hint-1.5B-RL and Hint-Engineering-1.5B-RL show **consistent performance improvements (3-5% absolute gain)** when trained with this reward. A modest penalty ratio ($\omega=0.1$) was found to be optimal, suggesting that while encouraging code correctness is beneficial, overly penalizing experimental code can hinder exploration.

#### 4.5. Pass@K Analysis

- **SFT vs. RL Impact:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) alone does not significantly raise the Pass@k upper bound for 1.5B models; it primarily teaches format and basic tool usage. In contrast, **Reinforcement Learning (RL) substantially elevates performance across all k values**, especially at lower sampling budgets, indicating a fundamental enhancement in the model's ability to consistently find correct solutions.
- **Sample Efficiency:** For 32B models, RFT demonstrates remarkable sample efficiency, achieving high accuracy with significantly fewer samples compared to other approaches, making it valuable for resource-constrained scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CoRT framework makes several significant contributions to the field of Large Reasoning Models and their practical applications, particularly in complex domains like mathematical problem-solving.

**Key Contributions Summarized:**
1. **Novel Data Synthesis Framework:** CoRT introduces "Hint-Engineering," a highly effective and sample-efficient method for synthesizing code-integrated reasoning data. By demonstrating that high-quality, strategically hinted data (even just 30 samples) can yield substantial improvements, the work addresses the critical data scarcity challenge in this emerging domain. This "less is more" principle is a crucial insight for future LRM training.
2. **Efficient and Scalable Training Pipeline:** The paper presents an end-to-end training pipeline combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT), and Reinforcement Learning (RL). This pipeline enables LRMs to acquire sophisticated code-integrated reasoning capabilities across different model scales (1.5B to 32B), making the approach practical for models of varying sizes and computational budgets.
3. **Comprehensive Empirical Validation:** CoRT provides extensive experimental evaluations across five challenging mathematical benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in both absolute accuracy and, critically, token efficiency. The remarkable **30-50% reduction in token usage** by Hint-Engineering models, while maintaining or improving performance, represents a major step towards making powerful LRMs more cost-effective and environmentally sustainable.

**Broader Significance and Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Mathematical Reasoning Capabilities:** CoRT significantly improves LRMs' ability to solve complex mathematical problems by seamlessly integrating precise computational tools. This can lead to more reliable and accurate AI systems for scientific research, engineering, and financial modeling, where precision is paramount.
- **Educational Applications:** The enhanced reasoning and problem-solving abilities could revolutionize educational tools, offering students more accurate and step-by-step explanations for mathematical concepts, and helping them understand how to combine abstract reasoning with practical computation.
- **AI System Reliability:** By reducing "code result distrust" and promoting more efficient tool utilization, CoRT contributes to building more robust and trustworthy AI systems. Models that understand when to trust external computational results and how to integrate them effectively will be less prone to subtle errors that can arise from purely natural language-based reasoning.
- **Computational Efficiency and Sustainability:** The significant reduction in token usage directly translates to lower computational costs and reduced energy consumption. This aligns with growing concerns about the environmental footprint of large AI models, making their deployment more sustainable in the long run.
- **Foundation for Future Agentic Systems:** The work advances the understanding of how to train AI agents that can effectively decide when to use external tools, how to construct tool calls, and how to interpret their outputs. This lays crucial groundwork for developing more autonomous and capable AI systems that can interact with the real world through a diverse set of tools.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the computational requirements for training and inference, which may affect broader accessibility, despite using publicly available models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen). They also note the rapid evolution of LRM capabilities, suggesting continuous opportunities for enhancement. Future work could explore further synergies between external tools beyond code interpreters and the inherent reasoning capabilities of language models, and investigate responsible deployment strategies for these advanced AI systems.

In conclusion, CoRT represents a significant advance in teaching LRMs to integrate external tools for complex reasoning. By focusing on data quality, strategic prompting, and efficient post-training, it offers a path towards more accurate, efficient, and reliable AI systems capable of tackling challenging real-world problems.
