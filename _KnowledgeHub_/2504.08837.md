# VL-Rethinker: Incentivizing Self-Reflection of Vision-Language Models with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> VL-Rethinker enhances the multimodal reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models through a direct reinforcement learning approach. The work introduces Selective Sample Replay to stabilize RL training and Forced Rethinking to incentivize adaptive self-reflection, resulting in new state-of-the-art performance on various multimodal benchmarks, including MathVista and MathVerse.

## Problem

- Text-only 'slow-thinking' LLMs excel in complex reasoning tasks (e.g., math), but this capability does not readily transfer to multimodal reasoning in VLMs, leaving a performance gap.
- Existing methods for multimodal reasoning often depend on resource-intensive, multi-stage pipelines involving supervised fine-tuning on detailed reasoning traces, often distilled from powerful teacher models.
- Standard reinforcement learning for VLMs, especially with binary rewards, faces training instability due to 'vanishing advantages,' which hinders the emergence of deep, explicit reasoning patterns.

## Method

- The VL-Rethinker project employs a two-stage reinforcement learning methodology using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== on a base instruction-tuned VLM.
- ==Selective Sample Replay (SSR)== is introduced to address the 'vanishing advantages' problem in GRPO by maintaining a replay buffer that only stores and prioritizes samples with non-zero advantage signals.
- A ==Forced Rethinking== mechanism compels the model to generate self-reflection steps (e.g., 'Wait, does it seem right?'), and successful rethinking trajectories are reinforced with an additional Supervised Fine-tuning loss.

## Results

- VL-Rethinker-72B achieved new state-of-the-art results on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, scoring **80.4%** on MathVista and **63.5%** on MathVerse, outperforming OpenAI-o1 and other open-source models.
- The model also set new open-source state-of-the-art on multi-disciplinary benchmarks like MMMU-Pro (**55.9%**) and EMMA (**38.5%**), narrowing the gap with proprietary models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Selective Sample Replay (SSR) effectively mitigates vanishing advantages, stabilizing training, while Forced Rethinking cultivates adaptive self-reflection, leading to a higher Pass@1 than passive prompting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-language models possess latent self-correction capabilities that can be activated and enhanced through targeted training.
> - Reinforcement learning, when properly stabilized with techniques like Selective Sample Replay, can directly incentivize complex reasoning and self-reflection in VLMs without relying on distillation from larger models or pre-generated reasoning traces.
> - Forcing a VLM to rethink during training, coupled with an SFT loss on successful attempts, enables the model to learn adaptive metacognitive behaviors, strategically engaging in self-correction only when necessary.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and reinforcement learning (RL), addressing a critical gap in multimodal reasoning capabilities.

**The Rise of "Slow-Thinking" Systems:** The AI landscape has recently seen a significant paradigm shift with the emergence of "slow-thinking" systems (e.g., OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1). These models differentiate themselves from "fast-thinking" counterparts (e.g., GPT-4o) by engaging in extended, explicit reasoning and reflection steps before generating a final answer. This deliberation has demonstrably led to superior performance on complex, text-based benchmarks, particularly in challenging mathematical and scientific problem-solving. They often achieve accuracy gains of 10% to over 30% compared to their fast-thinking peers on such tasks.

**The Multimodal Reasoning Gap:** Despite the success of slow-thinking in text-only domains, the paper identifies a crucial limitation: their multimodal reasoning capabilities have not kept pace. Proprietary slow-thinking models like GPT-o1 perform similarly to fast-thinking models on multimodal benchmarks such as MathVista, MathVerse, and MathVision. This indicates that the reflective abilities cultivated for text-based tasks do not automatically transfer or emerge effectively when visual information is involved. This observation poses a significant challenge, as real-world problems often require seamless integration of visual and textual understanding.

**Existing Approaches to Multimodal Reasoning:** Previous efforts to enhance multimodal reasoning, particularly through reinforcement learning, often rely on multi-stage pipelines. A common approach involves supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on large datasets of vision-language data that have been explicitly augmented with detailed reasoning traces. These traces are frequently generated by strong text-only LLMs after converting visual inputs into textual descriptions (distillation). While effective, these methods are resource-intensive, requiring complex visual captioning, teacher model distillation, and intricate SFT+RL orchestration.

**VL-Rethinker's Novel Contribution:** VL-Rethinker carves out a unique niche by proposing a *direct and lightweight RL-only approach* to incentivize slow-thinking in VLMs, without relying on distillation from stronger teacher models or extensive, pre-designed supervised reasoning traces. This represents a significant deviation from the prevailing paradigm, aiming for a more efficient and potentially more scalable way to imbue VLMs with self-reflection. The work extends the proven benefits of RL for reasoning (observed in text-only models) to the multimodal domain, focusing on making VLMs *inherently* more deliberate and self-correcting.

In essence, VL-Rethinker addresses the pressing question of "How can we effectively incentivize multimodal slow-thinking capabilities in Vision-Language Models?" by pushing the boundaries of reinforcement learning applications in the multimodal space, seeking an organic emergence of reflective behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the "VL-Rethinker" project is to **enhance the slow-thinking capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using reinforcement learning (RL), without relying on distillation from stronger teacher models.** This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims:

1. **Bridging the Multimodal Reasoning Gap:** The primary motivation stems from the observed discrepancy between the advanced reasoning capabilities of slow-thinking LLMs in text-only domains (e.g., math and science) and their comparatively stagnant performance in multimodal reasoning tasks. While models like GPT-o1 excel in text-based math, their performance on visual-mathematical benchmarks (e.g., MathVista, MathVerse) is often no better than fast-thinking models. The paper aims to close this gap, enabling VLMs to leverage slow-thinking for complex multimodal problems.

2. **Incentivizing Explicit Self-Reflection:** A core objective is to explicitly cultivate deliberate reasoning patterns within VLMs. The authors note that standard RL training on multimodal data does not naturally induce longer chains of thought or spontaneous self-reflection. They aim to design methods that encourage VLMs to engage in explicit self-verification, self-correction, and self-questioning before arriving at a final answer, mirroring the reflective processes seen in human problem-solving.

3. **Developing a Direct and Efficient RL Approach:** Unlike existing multimodal reasoning systems that often depend on complex pipelines involving supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on distilled reasoning traces from powerful teacher models, a key objective is to explore and validate a *direct RL approach*. This aims to demonstrate a more streamlined and potentially less resource-intensive method for fostering advanced reasoning, offering a viable alternative to complex supervised fine-tuning and distillation pipelines.

4. **Addressing RL Training Instabilities:** Within the chosen RL framework (GRPO), the authors identify a specific technical challenge: the "vanishing advantages problem." A critical objective is to devise a solution to this instability, ensuring that the RL training process remains effective and provides consistent gradient signals, particularly for high-capacity models.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to advance the state-of-the-art in multimodal AI, producing VLMs that are not only capable of understanding complex visual and textual information but also adept at reasoning through challenging problems with human-like deliberation and self-correction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VL-Rethinker project proposes a novel two-stage reinforcement learning (RL) methodology to instill slow-thinking capabilities in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), building upon a base instruction-tuned VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Reward

The task is defined as generating a textual response `y` to a multimodal input `x = (I, Q)` (image `I` and textual query `Q`). The goal is to maximize the expected reward `r(y, x)`, which is a simple binary function: `r(y, x) = 1` if `y` is correct, `0` otherwise. This simple reward signal, common in reasoning tasks, emphasizes factual correctness.

#### 3.2. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

The core RL algorithm employed is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an on-policy method inspired by PPO.
- **Group Generation:** For each input `x`, the policy generates a group `G` of candidate responses `{y_i}`.
- **Advantage Calculation:** The advantage `Â_{i,t}` for the `i`-th response at time `t` is calculated by normalizing its reward `r(x, y_i)` against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the *same query group*. This relative comparison aims to guide policy updates more effectively than absolute rewards.
- **Clipped Surrogate Loss:** GRPO uses a clipped surrogate loss, similar to PPO, to prevent large policy updates and maintain training stability.

#### 3.3. Addressing the "Vanishing Advantages" Problem

A critical limitation identified in standard GRPO, especially for high-capacity models and binary rewards, is the "Vanishing Advantages" problem. When all responses in a group are either all correct or all incorrect, the standard deviation of rewards becomes zero, leading to zero advantages and thus no gradient signals for policy updates. This causes training instability and premature convergence to shallow reasoning.

To mitigate this, the paper introduces **Selective Sample Replay (SSR)**:
- **Experience Replay Buffer:** SSR maintains a replay buffer `B_replay` that stores tuples `(x, y_i, Â_i)` from past iterations.
- **Selective Filtering:** Crucially, the buffer *only* stores samples where the corresponding query group exhibited non-zero advantages (`|Â_k| > 0`). This filters out uninformative experiences.
- **Prioritized Sampling:** At each training step, the current batch is augmented with samples drawn from `B_replay`. Sampling from the buffer is prioritized based on the *absolute magnitude* of the advantages (`P(select j) = |Â_j|^α / Σ_k∈B_replay |Â_k|^α`). This ensures that experiences with significant positive or negative advantage signals (i.e., those near the decision boundaries, indicating challenging or highly informative examples) are replayed more frequently.
- **Benefits:** SSR counteracts vanishing advantages by ensuring a consistent flow of informative gradient signals, stabilizes training, and acts as an online curriculum learning mechanism by dynamically focusing on high-value experiences.

#### 3.4. Incentivizing Explicit Self-Reflection with "Forced Rethinking"

Despite SSR improving optimization, the authors observed that complex, explicit self-correction behaviors did not consistently emerge from standard VLM RL, unlike in text-only models. To address this, they propose **Forced Rethinking**:
- **Instruction Prompt Hint:** A subtle initial hint within the instruction prompt encourages the model to "regularly perform self-questioning, self-verification, self-correction."
- **Rethinking Trigger Injection:** The core of the method involves a targeted intervention during RL rollouts. After the VLM generates an initial response `y_1`, a specific textual "rethinking trigger" (e.g., "Wait, does it seem right?", "Wait, there might be a mistake") is appended to `y_1`. This augmented sequence is then fed back to the model, compelling it to generate a subsequent response segment `y_2`. The full response becomes `y = y_1 ⊕ trigger ⊕ y_2`.
- **Diverse Triggers:** Three categories of triggers are designed: self-verification, self-correction, and self-questioning.
- **Controlled Application:** To avoid disrupting the policy's native distribution, forced rethinking is applied to only a *fraction* (`q < 1`) of the generated responses.
- **Successful Trajectory Reinforcement:** Only rethinking trajectories that lead to a *correct final answer* are retained. An additional Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) loss is applied to these successful trajectories, explicitly incentivizing the model to generate the desired deliberate thinking patterns.
- **Outcome:** This trains VL-Rethinker to strategically engage in rethinking only when necessary, demonstrating an emergent metacognitive ability.

#### 3.5. Training Data and Benchmarks

- **Training Data:** A curated dataset of approximately 38,870 multimodal queries, combining publicly available datasets with novel web-collected data. Rigorous cleaning, filtering (for programmatic verifiability), and augmentation (rephrasing, option permutation) were applied. Smaller, specialized subsets (16K for 7B models, 20K for 32B/72B) were created by selecting queries where the base model exhibited a non-zero PassRate@8, ensuring the RL process refines existing abilities.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** A diverse suite of challenging multimodal benchmarks covering:
 - **Math-related reasoning:** MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision.
 - **Multi-discipline understanding:** MMMU, MMMU-Pro, EMMA.
 - **Real-world tasks:** MegaBench.
- **Metrics:** Pass@1 accuracy using greedy decoding.

The methodology combines a principled RL algorithm (GRPO) with innovative techniques (SSR and Forced Rethinking) to address specific challenges in VLM training, aiming to foster genuinely reflective and robust multimodal reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate significant advancements in multimodal reasoning capabilities through VL-Rethinker, validating the effectiveness of the proposed SSR and Forced Rethinking techniques.

#### 4.1. Method Effectiveness (Q1)

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** VL-Rethinker-72B achieves **new state-of-the-art (SoTA)** results on key mathematical benchmarks among *all* models, including proprietary ones like OpenAI-o1. Specifically:
 - **MathVista:** 80.4% (vs. 73.9% for OpenAI-o1, 74.8% for open-source SoTA Qwen2.5-VL-72B)
 - **MathVerse:** 63.5% (vs. 57.0% for OpenAI-o1, 57.2% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B)
- **Open-Source SoTA on General Benchmarks:** VL-Rethinker-72B also sets a new **open-source SoTA** on multi-disciplinary benchmarks, narrowing the gap with OpenAI-o1:
 - **MathVision:** 44.9% (+6.8% over open-source SoTA)
 - **MMMU-Pro:** 55.9% (+3.7% over open-source SoTA)
 - **EMMA:** 38.5% (+4.4% over open-source SoTA)
 - **MEGA-Bench:** 51.3% (+2.3% over open-source SoTA)
- **Scalability:** The 7B version, VL-Rethinker-7B, also outperforms other 7B-scale reasoning-focused models, demonstrating the approach's effectiveness across different model capacities.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Q2 & Q3)

- **Ablation on Data Quality and Diversity:**
 - Removing text-only queries did not significantly impact performance, suggesting that multimodal contexts drive the primary learning.
 - However, removing queries from broader *scientific domains* (beyond math) resulted in a pronounced performance drop. This underscores the critical importance of diverse scientific data for improving a model's general reasoning ability, even in a multimodal context.

- **Ablation on Selective Sample Replay (SSR):**
 - **Performance:** The full GRPO-SSR algorithm consistently achieved superior performance compared to ablated versions (GRPO-Filter, which only filters zero-advantage examples, and plain GRPO, which neither filters nor replays). This strongly supports the benefits of both filtering and selective replay.
 - **Training Dynamics (Fig. 5):**
 - Standard GRPO exhibited significant overfitting and performance degradation due to vanishing advantages.
 - GRPO-Filter showed more stability but still suffered.
 - GRPO-SSR demonstrated the most stable training process and best convergence, indicating its effectiveness in providing consistent learning signals.
 - **Advantage Distribution (Fig. 6):**
 - Standard GRPO and GRPO-Filter showed highly skewed advantage distributions, with a large mass centered around zero (vanishing advantages).
 - GRPO-SSR significantly *redistributed* the probability mass away from zero, emphasizing examples with large absolute advantages. This confirms SSR's ability to ensure a more balanced and effective learning process by focusing on informative experiences.

- **Analysis on Forced Rethinking (Q3):**
 - **Latent Rethinking Capability:** Forcing the baseline model (VL-Reasoner) to perform a rethinking step at test time yielded positive relative improvements across all benchmarks, indicating that VLMs possess *latent* capabilities for self-correction.
 - **Superiority of Training:** VL-Rethinker, trained with the Forced Rethinking technique, consistently *outperformed* the baseline model when it was passively forced to rethink at test time. For instance, on MathVision, VL-Rethinker achieved an 8.46% relative improvement, significantly higher than the 2.49% from passively forcing the baseline. This demonstrates that *training* the model for self-reflection is much more effective than merely prompting it at inference time.
 - **Adaptive Metacognition:** Crucially, VL-Rethinker demonstrated an *adaptive* rethinking behavior. Its "Rethinking Ratio" (the proportion of instances where it spontaneously engaged in rethinking) varied substantially across different benchmarks, unlike the fixed 100% application in the forced scenario. This indicates that the model learned to strategically trigger rethinking based on its perceived difficulty or confidence, showcasing an emergent metacognitive ability. The paper even notes that VL-Rethinker can identify flaws in the problem itself during rethinking.

In summary, the results conclusively show that VL-Rethinker's novel combination of SSR and Forced Rethinking leads to substantial performance gains, effectively addresses RL training challenges, and successfully cultivates adaptive self-reflection in multimodal models, advancing them beyond the capabilities of even leading proprietary "slow-thinking" systems on certain benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VL-Rethinker project makes several significant contributions to the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of multimodal reasoning, with notable potential impacts:

1. **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning State-of-the-Art:** VL-Rethinker significantly pushes the performance boundaries for Vision-Language Models on complex reasoning benchmarks. By achieving new state-of-the-art scores on MathVista and MathVerse (surpassing even OpenAI-o1) and setting new open-source records on multi-disciplinary and real-world tasks, the work demonstrates that VLMs can achieve genuinely "slow-thinking" capabilities in multimodal contexts, comparable to, and in some cases exceeding, their text-only counterparts. This is a crucial step towards more generally intelligent AI systems that can understand and reason about the world through multiple sensory inputs.

2. **Novel and Efficient RL Training Paradigm:** A major impact lies in demonstrating a viable and *direct RL approach* for enhancing VLM reasoning. Unlike prior methods that often rely on resource-intensive distillation from large teacher models or complex supervised fine-tuning with synthetically generated reasoning traces, VL-Rethinker shows that explicit self-reflection can be incentivized directly through targeted RL. This could simplify the training pipeline for advanced VLMs, making sophisticated reasoning capabilities more accessible and potentially reducing the computational overhead and reliance on massive, human-annotated reasoning datasets.

3. **Robust Reinforcement Learning for VLMs:** The introduction of Selective Sample Replay (SSR) is a significant technical contribution that addresses a fundamental challenge ("vanishing advantages") in applying GRPO-based reinforcement learning to VLMs. By stabilizing training and ensuring consistent, informative gradient signals, SSR makes RL a more reliable and effective tool for optimizing VLM behavior. This method's effectiveness might also extend to other domains of large language model RL where similar reward sparsity or uniformity issues arise.

4. **Cultivating Emergent Metacognition:** Forced Rethinking offers a lightweight yet powerful strategy to explicitly guide VLMs towards self-reflection and self-correction. The demonstration that VL-Rethinker learns to *adaptively* trigger rethinking, rather than rigidly applying it, is particularly impactful. This indicates an emergent metacognitive ability, where the model can assess its own uncertainty or potential for error and strategically engage in deeper deliberation. Such self-awareness is a hallmark of intelligent systems and could lead to more reliable, trustworthy, and human-aligned AI outputs, especially in high-stakes applications. The ability to identify flaws in the problem itself, as shown in the example, highlights a higher level of understanding.

5. **Broad Applications:** Improved multimodal reasoning has wide-ranging implications across various fields:
 - **Education:** Enhanced AI tutors that can solve complex problems presented with diagrams and text, helping students learn.
 - **Science and Engineering:** AI systems capable of analyzing scientific diagrams, charts, and experimental results alongside textual descriptions, accelerating research and discovery.
 - **Medical Diagnosis:** Assisting medical professionals by reasoning over medical images, patient records, and research literature.
 - **Robotics:** Enabling robots to understand and execute complex tasks in visually rich environments by integrating perception with reasoning.
 - **Accessibility:** Developing AI tools that can interpret complex visual information for visually impaired individuals.

Despite its breakthroughs, the paper also points to future work, acknowledging the models still lag behind human experts on some general multimodal tasks, primarily due to the ongoing challenge of acquiring high-quality, diverse multimodal training datasets. This highlights a critical bottleneck for future research, even as the proposed methods demonstrate how to effectively leverage existing data for more intelligent reasoning. The observation about differing RL incentives for slow-thinking across modalities also opens up new avenues for foundational research in AI learning mechanisms.
