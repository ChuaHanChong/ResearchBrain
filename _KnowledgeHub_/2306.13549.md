# A Survey on Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive synthesis of the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models, detailing their common architectures, multi-stage training paradigms, and diverse evaluation methodologies. It highlights the importance of data quality for instruction tuning and addresses key challenges, including the pervasive issue of multimodal hallucination.

## Problem

- The rapid and uncoordinated progress in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has created a fragmented research landscape, making it difficult for researchers to keep pace with developments.
- There was a lack of a comprehensive and structured overview that synthesizes the state-of-the-art, core components, and emerging trends in the nascent MLLM field.
- Researchers needed a unified resource to understand MLLM architectures, training strategies, data considerations, evaluation methodologies, and prevalent challenges like multimodal hallucination.

## Method

- The survey deconstructs MLLMs into fundamental architectural components (==modality encoders, LLMs, modality interfaces==) and analyzes their typical lifecycle-based training paradigms (pre-training, instruction-tuning, alignment tuning).
- It provides a categorical framework for MLLM evaluation, distinguishing between ==closed-set and open-set assessment methods==.
- The paper systematically discusses key challenges such as ==multimodal hallucination==, outlining its types, evaluation methods, and mitigation strategies, and details advanced techniques like M-ICL, M-CoT, and LLM-Aided Visual Reasoning.

## Results

- MLLMs show improved performance and **emergent zero-shot capabilities** with increased LLM parameter size and higher input resolutions, with MoE architectures showing promise for scaling.
- Evaluation methodologies for MLLMs are evolving from traditional closed-set benchmarks to more comprehensive open-ended assessments, often relying on human or AI-based scoring.
- MLLMs are expanding their capabilities to finer granularities (region/pixel-level), diverse modalities (3D, audio, video), multiple languages, and specialized real-world scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs typically follow a three-module architecture: a pre-trained modality encoder, a pre-trained Large Language Model, and a learnable modality interface.
> - A multi-stage training paradigm involving pre-training, instruction-tuning, and alignment tuning is common, with data quality being more critical than quantity, especially for instruction tuning.
> - Multimodal hallucination remains a persistent and critical challenge, with ongoing efforts in data engineering, architectural fixes, and post-correction strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper emerges at a critical juncture in the evolution of artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal understanding. Recent years have witnessed an explosion in the capabilities of LLMs, exemplified by models like GPT-3 and ChatGPT, which demonstrate extraordinary emergent abilities such as instruction following, in-context learning (ICL), and chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. However, these models are inherently limited to processing discrete text, rendering them "blind" to other forms of information like images, video, and audio. Concurrently, Large Vision Models (LVMs) have made significant strides in visual perception but typically lack the advanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

Recognizing this complementarity, the burgeoning field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) seeks to integrate the perceptual prowess of LVMs with the reasoning and generative capabilities of LLMs. This paper positions MLLMs, particularly those represented by GPT-4V, as a new and rapidly rising research hotspot. Unlike traditional multimodal methods, which include discriminative paradigms (e.g., CLIP for unified representation) and generative paradigms (e.g., OFA for sequence-to-sequence unification), MLLMs leverage billion-scale parameters and novel training paradigms like multimodal instruction tuning. This enables MLLMs to exhibit surprising emergent capabilities, such as generating stories from images, performing OCR-free mathematical reasoning, and understanding complex cultural nuances like memes – abilities that were rare in preceding models. These emergent properties are highlighted as a "potential path to artificial general intelligence (AGI)."

The rapid development in the MLLM field, fueled by both academia and industry, has created an urgent need for a comprehensive synthesis of the progress. This survey aims to fulfill that need by tracing and summarizing the advancements made since the public debut of models like GPT-4V. The authors assert that "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first survey on MLLM," which, if accurate, establishes this paper as a foundational reference for researchers seeking to understand the current state, core components, and future directions of this dynamic field. By providing a structured overview and an associated GitHub repository for continuous updates, the authors position this work not just as a static review but as a living resource for the research community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the exceptionally rapid progress and promising results observed in the nascent field of Multimodal Large Language Models. The advent of models like GPT-4V has galvanized efforts in both academic and industrial research, leading to a proliferation of new architectures, training strategies, and applications at an unprecedented pace. This rapid evolution, while exciting, also creates a challenge for researchers to keep abreast of the latest developments, foundational concepts, and emerging trends.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this paper are clearly defined:

1. **Summarize Recent Progress:** The overarching goal is to "trace and summarize the recent progress of MLLMs," providing a consolidated view of the state-of-the-art.
2. **Delineate Basic Formulation:** To establish a common understanding, the survey aims to present the fundamental concepts of MLLMs, including their core architectural components (modality encoders, LLMs, modality interfaces), various training strategies, data considerations, and standard evaluation methodologies.
3. **Explore Research Extensions:** Beyond the basics, the survey intends to delve into how MLLMs are being extended to support more granular interactions, incorporate diverse modalities (beyond just vision and language), handle multiple languages, and adapt to specific real-world scenarios.
4. **Address Critical Issues:** The paper specifically aims to discuss significant challenges facing MLLMs, with a particular focus on multimodal hallucination—the phenomenon where models generate responses inconsistent with the input visual content. It seeks to outline existing evaluation methods and mitigation strategies for this problem.
5. **Introduce Key Techniques:** The survey is motivated to highlight and explain advanced techniques that are crucial for MLLM capabilities, such as Multimodal In-Context Learning (M-ICL), Multimodal Chain of Thought (M-CoT), and LLM-Aided Visual Reasoning (LAVR), detailing their mechanisms and applications.
6. **Identify Challenges and Future Directions:** To guide future research, the authors aim to identify current limitations and unresolved problems in the MLLM landscape, offering promising avenues for further exploration.

In essence, the motivation is to serve as a vital guide for researchers entering or operating within this fast-moving field, enabling them to quickly grasp the core ideas, primary methods, and current state of MLLMs, thereby inspiring new and impactful research contributions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, its "methodology" pertains to its systematic approach to reviewing, categorizing, and synthesizing existing literature. The authors employ a structured and comprehensive framework to dissect the rapidly evolving MLLM landscape, ensuring a holistic understanding for the reader.

The survey's approach can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Component-wise Deconstruction:** The paper begins by deconstructing a typical MLLM into its fundamental building blocks: a pre-trained modality encoder (e.g., visual, audio), a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM), and a modality interface (connector) that bridges the two. This systematic breakdown clarifies the architectural commonalities across diverse MLLMs.
2. **Lifecycle-based Training Analysis:** The training process is presented in a phased manner, reflecting the typical development lifecycle of MLLMs:
- **Pre-training:** Focuses on initial modality alignment and learning multimodal world knowledge using large-scale, often noisy, text-paired data (e.g., image-caption pairs).
- **Instruction-tuning:** Aims to teach models to understand and follow human instructions, enhancing zero-shot generalization and conversational abilities through structured instruction-response pairs.
- **Alignment tuning:** Involves aligning model responses with specific human preferences, often using techniques like Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), particularly for reducing undesirable behaviors like hallucinations.
3. **Data-centric Perspective:** Throughout the training discussion, the authors meticulously detail the types, sources, characteristics, and quality considerations of the data used at each stage (e.g., coarse-grained vs. fine-grained caption data, self-instruction techniques for instruction data). This emphasizes the critical role of data in MLLM development.
4. **Categorical Evaluation Framework:** The evaluation of MLLMs is categorized into two main types:
- **Closed-set evaluation:** Involves predefined answer options, often using standard benchmarks and metrics (e.g., VQA, ScienceQA).
- **Open-set evaluation:** Pertains to flexible, chatbot-like responses, requiring more nuanced assessment methods such as manual scoring, GPT scoring, or GPT-4V scoring, along with qualitative case studies.
5. **Problem- and Solution-Oriented Discussion:** The survey dedicates significant sections to specific challenges and advanced techniques. For instance, the section on Multimodal Hallucination systematically defines different types of hallucinations, reviews evaluation methods, and then categorizes mitigation strategies into pre-correction, in-process-correction, and post-correction.
6. **Technique-Specific Deep Dives:** Key enabling techniques like M-ICL, M-CoT, and LLM-Aided Visual Reasoning are given dedicated subsections. For each, the core idea, learning paradigms, configurations, and applications are explored, providing detailed insights into how MLLMs achieve their advanced reasoning and interaction capabilities.
7. **Future-Oriented Outlook:** The paper concludes by summarizing existing challenges and pointing out promising future research directions, framing the current state as "rudimentary" and highlighting areas for significant improvement.
8. **Dynamic Resource Provision:** A crucial methodological choice is the provision of an associated GitHub link, which is explicitly stated to be updated regularly. This commitment transforms the survey from a static document into a dynamic resource, essential for a field undergoing rapid transformation.

The authors maintain a focus primarily on visual and language modalities, acknowledging the inclusion of video and audio in selected works. The report is rich with specific examples of models, datasets, and techniques, supported by tables summarizing common components and datasets, making it a practical reference for researchers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey, this paper does not present novel experimental results but rather synthesizes the collective "findings" and trends observed across the current landscape of Multimodal Large Language Models. The main insights derived from this comprehensive review are:

1. **Architectural Commonality and Modularity:** Despite diverse implementations, MLLMs generally adhere to a three-module architecture: a pre-trained modality encoder (e.g., CLIP-variants for vision), a pre-trained Large Language Model (e.g., LLaMA, Qwen, Flan-T5), and a learnable modality interface (connector). The interface is crucial for aligning different modalities, with token-level (e.g., Q-Former, MLP) and feature-level fusion (e.g., cross-attention within LLM layers) being the primary approaches. The parameter size of the connector is notably small compared to the encoder and LLM, highlighting parameter efficiency.
2. **Multi-Stage Training Paradigm:** The development of MLLMs typically involves three distinct training stages:
- **Pre-training:** Focuses on coarse-grained alignment between modalities using massive web-scale data (e.g., LAION-5B, COYO-700M), primarily for foundational world knowledge and inter-modal connection.
- **Instruction-tuning:** Leverages instruction-formatted data (often adapted from existing datasets or generated via self-instruction using powerful LLMs like GPT-4V) to teach models to understand and follow diverse human commands and engage in multi-turn conversations.
- **Alignment Tuning:** Utilizes human preference data (or AI-generated feedback from models like GPT-4V) via techniques like RLHF or DPO to fine-tune MLLMs for specific human preferences, notably reducing issues like hallucination and improving helpfulness.
3. **Data Quality Over Quantity:** A significant finding reiterated across multiple works is that the quality of training data, especially for instruction tuning, is often more critical than sheer volume. High-quality, fine-grained data (e.g., from GPT-4V generation) and diversity in prompts and task coverage are empirically shown to yield better model performance and generalization.
4. **Scaling Laws and Emergent Capabilities:** Increasing the parameter size of the LLM component (e.g., from 7B to 13B or 34B) and utilizing higher input resolutions for visual encoders consistently lead to performance improvements and the emergence of new zero-shot capabilities, including multilingual understanding, even when trained predominantly on English data. The exploration of Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures also shows promise for scaling without proportional computational cost.
5. **Evolving Evaluation Methodologies:** MLLM evaluation is shifting from traditional, task-specific closed-set benchmarks (e.g., VQA, ScienceQA) towards more comprehensive and open-ended assessments. New benchmarks like MME and MMBench offer multi-dimensional evaluations, while open-set evaluation increasingly relies on human or AI-based (GPT/GPT-4V) scoring for nuanced qualitative aspects like conversational fluency and reasoning accuracy.
6. **Broadening Scope through Extensions:** MLLMs are rapidly extending their capabilities:
- **Granularity:** Evolving from image-level understanding to region-level (bounding boxes) and pixel-level (points, masks) control for input and output grounding.
- **Modalities:** Expanding input support to 3D point clouds, audio, and video, and output generation to images, audio, and video, often facilitated by diffusion models.
- **Language:** Growing efforts towards multilingual models, particularly for under-resourced languages like Chinese, often by leveraging bilingual LLMs and translated data.
- **Scenarios/Tasks:** Adaptation to specialized domains (medical imaging, document understanding) and real-world interactive agents (mobile, embodied, GUI agents) requiring planning and execution capabilities.
7. **Hallucination as a Central Challenge:** Multimodal hallucination (existence, attribute, relationship) is identified as a persistent and critical issue. Current mitigation strategies target different stages: pre-correction (data engineering), in-process-correction (architectural/decoding fixes), and post-correction (external models for verification).
8. **Foundational Role of Advanced Techniques:**
- **Multimodal In-Context Learning (M-ICL):** Enables few-shot learning by analogy from multimodal examples, crucial for visual reasoning and teaching tool use.
- **Multimodal Chain of Thought (M-CoT):** Promotes step-by-step reasoning for complex tasks, with explorations into single vs. tree-shaped chains and infilling vs. predicting patterns.
- **LLM-Aided Visual Reasoning (LAVR):** Positions LLMs as controllers (task decomposition, tool invocation), decision-makers (iterative reasoning, context summarization), or semantics refiners (fluent text generation), leading to systems with strong generalization and interactivity.

These findings collectively paint a picture of MLLMs as powerful, yet still developing, systems at the forefront of AI research, marked by rapid innovation, a reliance on diverse data, and a continuous push towards broader capabilities and reliability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the rapidly advancing field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with a broad potential impact on future research and development.

1. **Establishing a Foundational Reference:** By claiming to be the "first survey on MLLM," the paper aims to serve as a foundational, canonical reference for researchers entering or already engaged in this domain. It consolidates disparate knowledge, architectural patterns, training paradigms, and evaluation metrics into a single, organized document, significantly reducing the overhead for newcomers to grasp the field's complexities.
2. **Structuring a Nascent Field:** The MLLM landscape is characterized by its explosive growth and fragmented contributions. This survey brings much-needed structure by systematically categorizing architectures, training methodologies, data types, evaluation approaches, and advanced techniques. This categorization helps to delineate the core components and sub-areas, facilitating clearer communication and more targeted research efforts.
3. **Highlighting Key Trends and Best Practices:** The report distills critical insights, such as the importance of data quality over quantity, the benefits of scaling LLM parameters and input resolution, and the effectiveness of multi-stage training (pre-training, instruction-tuning, alignment tuning). These insights provide actionable guidance for practitioners and researchers in designing and training more effective MLLMs.
4. **Addressing Core Challenges:** By dedicating a substantial portion to multimodal hallucination, the survey underscores a critical limitation of current MLLMs. It meticulously details the types of hallucinations, methods for their evaluation, and various mitigation strategies, thereby galvanizing future research to enhance model reliability and trustworthiness—a crucial step for real-world deployment.
5. **Catalyzing Advanced Research:** The in-depth exploration of advanced techniques like M-ICL, M-CoT, and LLM-Aided Visual Reasoning provides a roadmap for developing more sophisticated MLLM capabilities. By showcasing how LLMs can act as controllers, decision-makers, and semantics refiners, the paper inspires further innovation in compositional reasoning, tool augmentation, and interactive AI systems.
6. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The concluding section explicitly outlines open challenges and promising future directions, including processing long multimodal contexts, improving complex instruction following, strengthening M-ICL and M-CoT capabilities, developing embodied agents, and enhancing model safety. This forward-looking perspective is invaluable for researchers seeking impactful problems to solve, steering the collective effort towards high-priority areas.
7. **Fostering Community Engagement:** The provision of an associated, actively updated GitHub repository transforms the survey into a dynamic, living resource. This not only ensures its continued relevance in a fast-moving field but also promotes community engagement by providing a centralized, evolving list of papers and resources.
8. **Bridging Academia and Industry:** The collaboration between USTC and Tencent YouTu Lab reflects a synergy that is increasingly vital in AI research. This work, combining academic rigor with industrial relevance, serves as an example of how such partnerships can yield comprehensive and impactful analyses that benefit both theoretical understanding and practical application.

In summary, this survey is a significant contribution that serves as an essential guide and comprehensive resource for the MLLM community. It provides a much-needed overview of current progress, clarifies fundamental concepts, highlights challenges, and points towards the most promising avenues for future research, ultimately accelerating the development of more capable, reliable, and versatile multimodal AI systems.
