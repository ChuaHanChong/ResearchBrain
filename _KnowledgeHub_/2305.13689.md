# Know Your Self-supervised Learning: A Survey on Image-based Generative and Discriminative Training

> [!summary] Summary
> This comprehensive survey outlines the evolution and current landscape of image-based self-supervised learning, dissecting its methodologies into generative and discriminative paradigms. It elucidates core technical concepts and evaluates modern SSL frameworks, highlighting their ability to learn powerful representations competitive with supervised methods, often using Vision Transformers.

## Problem

- Traditional supervised learning requires vast amounts of manually labeled data, which is often expensive and time-consuming to obtain, limiting scalability and application in data-scarce domains.
- Supervised learning approaches, despite strong performance on benchmarks, are approaching performance plateaus and face inherent limitations due to their dependence on explicit labels.
- The challenge of effectively leveraging abundant unlabeled data for learning robust and generalizable visual representations.

## Method

- The survey examines self-supervised learning as a paradigm that generates supervisory signals from unlabeled data itself, allowing models to learn meaningful representations.
- It categorizes existing image-based SSL approaches into two main groups: ==discriminative SSL== (e.g., contrastive learning, non-contrastive methods, clustering) and ==generative SSL== (e.g., masked image modeling, denoising).
- It details the technical concepts and architectural components common across SSL frameworks, such as ==Siamese networks, stop-gradient operations, momentum encoders==, and various loss functions.

## Results

- Both discriminative and generative SSL methods have achieved performance levels **comparable to or exceeding** supervised learning benchmarks on various computer vision tasks.
- Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as the dominant architecture, particularly for generative SSL methods like Masked Image Modeling, demonstrating improved performance with increased model size.
- Discriminative SSL methods often yield strong results with **linear evaluation** on frozen features, while generative methods typically show superior performance when fine-tuned on downstream tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning (SSL) has evolved from diverse pretext tasks (e.g., colorization, inpainting) to sophisticated frameworks like contrastive learning and masked image modeling.
> - SSL methods can be broadly categorized into discriminative approaches, which learn to distinguish instances, and generative approaches, which focus on reconstructing or generating data.
> - Key technical concepts such as Siamese networks, stop-gradient, momentum encoders, and projection heads are fundamental to modern SSL framework design, particularly for preventing representation collapse and improving feature quality.
