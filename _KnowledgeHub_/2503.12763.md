# A Survey on Human Interaction Motion Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> This IJCV survey delivers the first comprehensive review of human interaction motion generation, unifying human-human, human-object, human-scene, and human-mix interaction research under one taxonomy. It systematizes entity representations, conditioning modalities, and eight generative paradigms spanning motion graphs to LLM-based planning, catalogs over 110 methods and 68 datasets, and proposes a standardized fidelity, diversity, and coherence evaluation framework, identifying data, physics, representation, and controllability as open gaps.

## Problem

- Prior surveys cover single-person motion generation, pose estimation, or action recognition, leaving interactive motion generation without a dedicated comprehensive review.
- Research on human-human, human-object, and human-scene interaction generation has evolved in fragmented, largely disconnected sub-communities without a unified taxonomy.
- No consolidated resource compares datasets and evaluation metrics across interaction types, making cross-task comparison and method selection difficult for researchers.

## Method

- A four-category ==taxonomy==, human-human, human-object, human-scene, human-mix, organizing all reviewed generation methods by interacting entity type and technical challenge.
- A preliminaries framework covering interactive entities (human, object, scene capture and representation), conditioning modalities (text, audio, action, spatial signals), and eight fundamental generation paradigms spanning ==motion graphs== to ==LLM-based planning==.
- A structured evaluation-metrics taxonomy grouping ==fidelity==, ==diversity==, and ==condition-coherence== metrics across all four interaction categories into one unified comparison table.

## Results

- Reviews and tabulates over **110** generation methods across four interaction categories (**29** human-human, **38** human-object, **40** human-scene, **3** human-mix) in structured comparison tables.
- Catalogs **68** interaction datasets across the four categories (**29** human-human, **21** human-object, **16** human-scene, **2** human-mix), summarizing subjects, sequences, and modalities.
- Human-mix interaction remains critically underexplored, with only **3** methods and **2** datasets identified versus **29** to **40** for single-relation interaction categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Four open directions, data scarcity, physical plausibility, efficient representation, and controllable editing, recur across all interaction categories, suggesting shared bottlenecks rather than category-specific fixes.
> - Representations encoding relative, contact-centric geometry, such as object motion relative to contact points, generalize better than absolute-position representations across interaction types.
> - Physics-simulation and diffusion-based generative approaches remain largely incompatible, so hybrid architectures combining reinforcement-learning physical accuracy with diffusion's generative flexibility are a promising unification path.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Human motion generation is a fundamental problem in computer graphics, vision, and robotics. It aims to synthesize realistic and controllable human movements for various applications, ranging from animated characters in entertainment to autonomous agents in virtual environments and humanoid robots. Historically, this field has progressed from manual animation and motion capture editing to data-driven techniques, leveraging large datasets and statistical models.

This survey specifically carves out a niche within this broader landscape by focusing on "human **interaction** motion generation." It distinguishes itself from related but distinct areas such as single-person motion generation, human motion style transfer, human pose estimation, and human action recognition. The core challenge in interaction motion generation lies in modeling the intricate dependencies between humans and external entities (other humans, objects, or environments) while maintaining physical plausibility and semantic coherence.

The emergence of deep generative models (e.g., Variational Autoencoders, Generative Adversarial Networks, Diffusion Models, Large Language Models) has revolutionized various domains, including text, image, and video generation. This paper explicitly contextualizes human interaction motion generation within this wave of deep learning advancements, noting how these models have accelerated progress in synthesizing diverse and natural 3D human motions. However, it emphasizes that interactions introduce unique complexities beyond standard generative modeling, such as inherent stochasticity, environmental awareness, physical constraints (e.g., preventing inter-penetration), and the significant challenge of acquiring sufficiently rich interaction data. Therefore, this survey positions itself as a critical resource for understanding how these modern generative techniques are adapted and extended to tackle the multi-entity dynamics of human interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide, for the first time, a comprehensive overview of the literature in human interaction motion generation. The authors aim to establish foundational concepts, systematically review existing solutions and datasets, discuss evaluation metrics, and identify open research directions.

The motivation behind this comprehensive review stems from several critical aspects:

- **Ubiquity of Interaction:** Human life is fundamentally characterized by interactions with the external world through motion. These movements convey relationships and demonstrate how humans perceive and communicate. Replicating these complex behaviors is crucial for creating human-like entities in digital systems.
- **Diverse Applications:** The ability to generate realistic interactive human motions is vital for a wide array of applications, including:
 - **3D Virtual Characters:** In entertainment media (games, movies), for enhanced realism and immersion.
 - **Humanoid Robots:** For developing robots that can perform complex tasks and interact naturally with humans and objects.
 - **Digital Avatars:** In virtual reality and augmented reality, for personalized and expressive online representations.
- **Recent Progress and Lingering Challenges:** While deep generative models and new datasets have significantly advanced the field, substantial challenges persist. These include:
 - **Stochasticity and Coherence:** Human interaction is inherently stochastic, yet generated movements must maintain spatial and temporal coherence aligned with specific intentions.
 - **Environmental Awareness and Physical Constraints:** Interacting with the external world demands adaptation to diverse scene layouts, understanding object properties and affordances, and compliance with physical laws (e.g., collision avoidance, gravity).
 - **Data Scarcity:** Collecting high-quality, large-scale human interaction data is resource-intensive and difficult to scale, necessitating creative approaches to learning.
- **Need for a Unified Resource:** The rapid advancements and fragmented nature of research across various interaction types (human-human, human-object, human-scene) create a need for a unified framework to understand the state of the art, identify commonalities, and pinpoint future research opportunities. This survey aims to fill that gap.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, its methodology primarily involves structuring, categorizing, and synthesizing existing knowledge. The authors' approach is systematic and multi-faceted, covering foundational concepts, technical solutions, datasets, and evaluation.

#### 3.1. Survey Structure

The survey is meticulously organized to guide readers through the complex landscape of human interaction motion generation. It begins by defining its scope and distinguishing it from related fields. The core structure includes:

- **Foundational Concepts (Preliminaries):** This section lays the groundwork by explaining key aspects like interactive entities (human motion, objects, scenes), conditioning modalities (text, audio, action class, spatial/temporal signals), and fundamental methods for motion synthesis (Motion Graphs, Deterministic Regression, GANs, VAEs, Diffusion Models, Transformer-Based Language Models, RL + Physics Simulation, LLM-Based Motion Planning).
- **Categorization of Interactions:** The paper systematically reviews existing solutions by categorizing human interaction scenarios into four primary types:
 - Human-Human Interaction (HHI)
 - Human-Object Interaction (HOI)
 - Human-Scene Interaction (HSI)
 - Human-Mix Interaction (involving multiple interaction types simultaneously)
- **Dedicated Sections for Datasets and Evaluation:** The survey provides comprehensive overviews of commonly used datasets for each interaction category, highlighting their unique features and acquisition methods. It also details the various evaluation metrics employed to measure performance across dimensions like fidelity, diversity, and condition coherence.
- **Discussion of Open Research Directions:** The paper concludes by identifying and elaborating on promising avenues for future research, offering a roadmap for the field.
- **Supplementary Resources:** A public GitHub repository listing relevant papers further enhances the utility of the survey as a research tool.

#### 3.2. Fundamental Generation Methods Reviewed

The survey delves into the core technical approaches that underpin human interaction motion generation:

- **Motion Graphs:** These foundational methods represent motion segments as nodes and transitions as edges, allowing for novel motion synthesis through graph traversal. While enabling diverse motions, they face scalability limitations.
- **Deterministic Regression:** Models that aim for a one-to-one mapping from input conditions to target motions, often using RNNs or Transformers. Their limitation is the struggle to capture the inherent stochasticity of human movement, often leading to "mean poses."
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs):** Comprising a generator and a discriminator in an adversarial setup, GANs learn to produce realistic interaction motions. However, they are known for training instability, mode collapse, and convergence issues.
- **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs):** These models encode data into a structured latent space for generation. Conditional VAEs (cVAEs) are widely used for controllable motion synthesis by incorporating various conditioning variables (e.g., action labels, text).
- **Diffusion Models:** A more recent and powerful generative framework that progressively denoises corrupted data to reconstruct realistic motions. They offer inherent stability and capture fine-grained details, making them increasingly popular for complex interactive motion generation.
- **Transformer-Based Language Models:** Leveraging self-attention mechanisms, Transformers (like GPTs) are used to model sequential relationships in discrete motion tokens (often derived from VQ-VAEs), enabling text-to-motion generation and complex sequencing.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) + Physics Simulation:** This approach aims for physically plausible motions by training an RL agent in a physics-based environment. It excels at enforcing physical laws (balance, collisions) but often faces training convergence challenges and limited generalization.
- **LLM-Based Motion Planning:** An emerging paradigm where Large Language Models are used as high-level planners, translating abstract interaction goals into detailed, step-by-step motion sequences, reducing reliance on extensive datasets.

The survey analyzes how these fundamental methods are adapted and combined to address the specific challenges within each interaction category.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey distills key insights and commonalities across the diverse literature of human interaction motion generation:

#### 4.1. Interaction Categories and Challenges

The paper effectively categorizes the field into HHI, HOI, HSI, and Human-Mix, revealing distinct challenges for each:

- **Human-Human Interaction (HHI):** Requires addressing semantic consistency (intention-driven movements), global interpersonal coordination (relative positions, orientations), and fine-grained local interaction (precise contact between body parts). Recent solutions leverage past interactions, partner motions, action categories, text, and audio as conditioning signals, often employing distance-aware loss functions and attention mechanisms to achieve coordination and contact realism.
- **Human-Object Interaction (HOI):** Focuses on interaction semantic relevance (authentically reflecting intent and object functionality) and strict spatial/physical constraints (precise alignment, adherence to gravity/friction). Diffusion models are prominent, often conditioned by text or object motions. Contact-guided methods and physics-based simulations are crucial for ensuring physical plausibility and accurate contact.
- **Human-Scene Interaction (HSI):** Involves modeling human movement and interaction within complex environments while adhering to physical rules (collision avoidance) and semantic constraints. Approaches often involve hierarchical motion generation systems, robust environment constraint modeling (e.g., using occupancy grids, signed distance fields), and contextual interaction understanding (scene graphs, affordance maps, LLM-based planning).
- **Human-Mix Interaction:** This is the most complex category, combining multiple interaction types (e.g., human-human-object). The survey notes the extreme scarcity of dedicated datasets and solutions, highlighting it as a significant open area. Existing work often simplifies complexities by abstracting entities or unifying information into comprehensive embeddings.

#### 4.2. Dominant Methodologies and Conditioning

- **Deep Generative Models Dominance:** VAEs, GANs, and especially Diffusion Models have become the workhorse of the field, demonstrating strong capabilities in generating diverse and natural motions. Transformers are widely adopted for capturing temporal and spatial dependencies.
- **Multimodal Conditioning:** The trend is towards rich, multimodal conditioning. Text, audio, action labels, and explicit spatial/temporal signals are frequently used to guide generation, enabling more controllable and semantically consistent outputs. Large Language Models (LLMs) are emerging as powerful tools for high-level planning and understanding complex interaction goals.
- **Physics-Aware Learning:** While purely kinematic methods are common, there's a growing recognition of the importance of physical plausibility. Hybrid approaches combining deep learning with reinforcement learning and physics simulations are explored to ensure motions adhere to real-world physical laws, especially for contact-rich interactions.

#### 4.3. Dataset Landscape

The survey details a rich but often specialized dataset landscape:

- **Growth in Quantity and Quality:** Over the past decades, datasets have grown significantly in scale and richness, incorporating 3D skeletal data, parametric body models (SMPL, SMPL-X), and even custom 3D meshes.
- **Modality Expansion:** Beyond kinematic data, datasets now frequently include text annotations, audio, multi-view RGB-D videos, and even IMU sensor data. Synthetic data generation (e.g., using gaming engines or VR environments) is increasingly employed to overcome real-world capture limitations.
- **Category-Specific Focus:** Most datasets are tailored to specific interaction types (e.g., GRAB for HOI, NTU RGB+D for HHI, PROX for HSI).
- **Human-Mix Scarcity:** A notable finding is the severe lack of comprehensive datasets for human-mix interactions, pointing to a critical bottleneck for research in this complex area.

#### 4.4. Evaluation Landscape

The survey provides a structured overview of evaluation metrics, emphasizing the need for multi-dimensional assessment:

- **Fidelity:** Measured by direct comparison to ground truth (e.g., MPJPE, MPJVE), naturalness (e.g., FID/FVD, action recognition accuracy, critic scores), and physical plausibility (e.g., foot skating ratio, penetration metrics).
- **Diversity:** Assessed using metrics like inter-motion diversity and multimodality to ensure the model generates a wide range of plausible outputs for a given input.
- **Condition Coherence:** Task-specific metrics are crucial to ensure the generated motions align with the input conditions (e.g., Mutual Consistency for HHI, Chamfer Distance/Penetration for HOI, Collision Ratio/Semantic Contact Score for HSI, R-Precision/MultiModal Distance for text, Beat-Align Score for audio).
- **User Studies:** Acknowledged as indispensable for subjective quality assessment, capturing human preferences, and evaluating perceptual realism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a significant contribution to the field of human motion generation and its related disciplines. Its impact is multi-fold:

- **Unified and Comprehensive Resource:** By providing the "first comprehensive overview" of human interaction motion generation, the survey serves as an invaluable, centralized resource for researchers and practitioners. It synthesizes a vast and fragmented body of literature, making it accessible and understandable. This organization and classification of methods, datasets, and evaluation metrics provide a much-needed roadmap for navigating the complexities of the field.
- **Clarity on Challenges and Progress:** The report clearly articulates the unique challenges associated with generating interactive motions, distinguishing them from simpler single-person motions. By analyzing how different methods tackle these challenges across various interaction types, it highlights successful strategies and areas where further innovation is needed.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The identification of "open research directions" is perhaps the most impactful aspect of the survey. By pinpointing the critical limitations in data acquisition (scarcity, coverage, efficiency), physical plausibility (generalization of physics models), representation (interaction-aware features, scene complexity), and editing/controllability, the authors provide clear avenues for future work. This guidance can help focus research efforts, leading to more impactful breakthroughs.
- **Catalyst for Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The survey, itself a product of industry-academia collaboration, underscores the interdisciplinary nature of the field, bridging computer graphics, computer vision, robotics, and artificial intelligence. By consolidating knowledge, it can foster further collaboration to address complex problems.
- **Enabling Real-World Applications:** The advancements in human interaction motion generation directly contribute to enhancing the realism and interactivity of digital systems. This has profound implications for:
 - **Robotics:** Developing more intelligent and dexterous robots capable of natural human-robot interaction and object manipulation.
 - **Virtual Reality/Augmented Reality:** Creating highly immersive experiences with realistic and responsive avatars, improving user engagement and presence.
 - **Animation and Game Development:** Automating character animation processes, reducing manual labor, and enabling the creation of dynamic, believable virtual worlds.

In essence, "A Survey on Human Interaction Motion Generation" does not just summarize existing work; it frames the problem, dissects the solutions, assesses the tools, and, most importantly, illuminates the path forward for a field critical to the future of human-computer and human-robot interaction.
