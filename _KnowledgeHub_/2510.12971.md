# Actron3D: Learning Actionable Neural Functions from Videos for Transferable Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Actron3D distills 2-3 monocular human videos per task into a Neural Affordance Function, a compact object-centric neural field jointly encoding geometry, appearance, contact probability, and 3D point-flow actions. At deployment it retrieves the best-matched function and aligns it to the scene via coarse-to-fine differentiable optimization, yielding zero-shot 6-DoF manipulation with a 14.9pp success-rate gain over baselines.

## Problem

- Pixel-space affordance transfer from human videos lacks explicit 3D reasoning, causing ambiguity when lifting 2D predictions into spatially grounded robot actions.
- 3D actionable-cue methods depend on manually specified contact points or goal images and cannot reliably infer gripper orientation for full 6-DoF control.
- Existing video-based affordance transfer degrades sharply under domain shift, such as unseen objects or large viewpoint mismatches between human and robot views.

## Method

- **Neural Affordance Function**: a unified ==continuous field== with geometry, color, visual-descriptor, contact, and action heads fit per object from an uncalibrated video via ==multimodal differentiable rendering==.
- Two-phase fitting protocol: a ==static phase== optimizing geometry/appearance/contact losses, then a ==dynamic phase== supervising the action head with 3D point-flow and visibility targets.
- ==MLLM==-based affordance retrieval plus ==coarse-to-fine differentiable alignment==: contact-guided hypothesis sampling, feature-similarity ranking, and ==SIM(3) pose optimization== to transfer flows to a novel scene.

## Results

- Achieves **90.8%** average success rate across **13** RLBench tasks, a **14.9 percentage-point** improvement over the strongest baseline VidBot at **75.9%**.
- Real-robot evaluation on **5** tasks with a Stretch 3 platform reaches **76%** average success rate versus **50%** for VidBot and **32%** for RAM.
- Ablations show removing the neural function drops success to **30%**, disabling affordance optimization drops it to **21.1%**, and removing contact refinement drops it to **66.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding actionable cues as a continuous, queryable neural field rather than discrete keypoints enables iterative render-and-compare alignment that generalizes better across viewpoint and instance gaps.
> - Coarse-to-fine optimization, first global feature/geometry alignment then contact-region refinement, avoids local minima that direct contact-only optimization falls into.
> - Compact per-task neural representations fit from only 2-3 demonstrations can outperform baselines needing an order of magnitude more data, suggesting representation quality can substitute for data volume.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "generalist robots" capable of acquiring and transferring diverse interaction skills to novel scenarios is a central challenge in contemporary robotics. Actron3D addresses this by proposing a novel approach to learning transferable 6-DoF manipulation skills from readily available, uncalibrated human videos.

**Current Landscape and Limitations:**

- **Data-Hungry Robotics Systems:** Many modern robotic systems require extensive, carefully curated datasets or teleoperated demonstrations, which are costly and time-consuming to collect. This limits scalability and generalization to new tasks or objects.
- **Learning from Human Videos (2D Approaches):** Previous attempts to leverage "out-of-domain" data, such as human videos, often predict or transfer affordance trajectories in pixel space (e.g., [2–4]). While improving sample efficiency, these 2D methods suffer from:
 - **3D Ambiguity:** Difficulty in lifting pixel-level predictions into precise 3D actions, even with local geometric priors.
 - **Viewpoint Sensitivity:** Performance degrades significantly under large viewpoint mismatches between human-centric demonstrations and robot-centric observations.
 - **Scalability Issues:** Often rely on large, manually curated memory banks for skill retrieval.
- **Learning from Human Videos (3D Approaches):** Some works extract 3D actionable cues like point flows or 3D waypoints (e.g., [5–8]). However, these methods frequently:
 - **Require Manual Input:** Depend on manually specified contact points or goal images.
 - **Lack Gripper Orientation:** Cannot reliably infer precise robot gripper orientations (6-DoF poses).
 - **Struggle with Domain Shifts:** Performance deteriorates with unseen objects or significant domain shifts.
- **Simulation-based Affordance Learning:** While spatially expressive, these methods require extensive synthetic assets and face significant "sim-to-real" transfer challenges (e.g.,).
- **Vision Foundation Models for Manipulation:** Approaches using visual descriptors from foundation models (e.g., DINO) for keypoint correspondence (e.g.,) improve generalization from in-the-wild demonstrations but still struggle with 3D spatial grounding and viewpoint changes. Lifting 2D features to 3D often requires controlled multi-view capture setups (e.g., [24–28]), limiting scalability.
- **Generative Video Models:** Recent advances allow robots to "imagine" actions from synthesized videos (e.g., [38–40]), but video generation at test time can be computationally expensive.

**Actron3D's Position:**
Actron3D directly tackles the aforementioned limitations by introducing a novel "distill-then-transfer" framework that emphasizes a compact, continuous 3D neural representation: the Neural Affordance Function (NAF). By distilling multimodal information (geometry, visual appearance, affordance, and actions) from diverse, uncalibrated monocular videos into NAFs, the framework aims to achieve:
1. **3D Spatial Grounding:** Overcoming the inherent ambiguity of 2D cues.
2. **Viewpoint Invariance:** Robustness to varying observation viewpoints.
3. **Data Efficiency:** Learning from a few demonstrations, avoiding large datasets.
4. **6-DoF Precision:** Generating precise end-effector poses without manual intervention.
5. **Zero-Shot Transfer:** Enabling generalization to novel objects and scenarios.

The work leverages cutting-edge techniques in novel view synthesis (e.g., Wonder3D, InstantMesh), 3D reconstruction (SfM, metric depth estimation), and large multimodal models (MLLMs) for intelligent retrieval, effectively integrating these advancements into a cohesive robot learning framework. It bridges the gap between purely 2D and fragmented 3D affordance learning methods by providing a unified, differentiable 3D representation and an optimization-based transfer mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of Actron3D is to advance the field of robotic manipulation by enabling robots to acquire and transfer complex 6-DoF skills efficiently from readily available human video demonstrations. This is driven by several key motivations:

- **Building Generalist Robots:** The ultimate goal is to create robots that can generalize skills across diverse objects, environments, and robot embodiments, much like humans do. Current data-hungry systems fall short in this regard.
- **Efficient Skill Acquisition from Human Data:** Humans learn complex tasks by observing a few demonstrations. The authors are motivated by the vast potential of "in-the-wild" human videos (casual recordings, human-object interaction datasets, or even generated content) as a rich, scalable, and informative source of actionable knowledge for robots.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Methods:**
 - **2D Ambiguity:** Prior 2D affordance learning methods, despite improved sample efficiency, struggle with the fundamental ambiguity of lifting pixel-space predictions to precise 3D actions. Actron3D aims to provide explicit 3D reasoning.
 - **Lack of 6-DoF Precision:** Existing 3D methods often require manual input (e.g., contact points) or simplify actions to 3D waypoints, making it difficult to perform dexterous tasks requiring full 6-DoF gripper control (e.g., pouring).
 - **Poor Generalization under Domain Shifts:** Many methods struggle with transferring skills to unseen objects or under significant viewpoint changes between demonstration and deployment.
- **Distilling Compact, Transferable Representations:** A core motivation is to find a way to distill the information-rich but noisy and uncalibrated video data into a compact, object-centric representation that is reliable and transferable. This led to the concept of the Neural Affordance Function (NAF), which jointly encodes geometry, visual features, and actionable cues in a continuous 3D space.
- **Effective Adaptation and Execution:** Beyond learning the representation, the authors aim for a robust mechanism to adapt and execute these learned skills in novel scenarios. This led to the development of an optimization-based transfer pipeline, inspired by computer graphics and SLAM, which allows for iterative, precise cross-modal alignment in a continuous energy landscape, a departure from typical one-step feature matching.
- **High Sample Efficiency:** To overcome the data bottleneck, the project aims to achieve high performance with only a few demonstration videos per task, making the system practical and scalable for real-world deployment.

In essence, Actron3D seeks to answer two central questions:
1. How can we distill compact, actionable information from diverse videos into representations that transfer reliably across embodiments, object instances, and viewpoints?
2. How can such representations be effectively adapted and executed in novel scenarios?

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Actron3D is conceptualized as a "distill-then-transfer" framework, comprising two main phases: distilling multimodal knowledge into Neural Affordance Functions (NAFs) and then transferring these functions to perform zero-shot robotic manipulation.

**A. Neural Functions Distillation from Videos (Distillation Phase - G_act)**

The core innovation is the **Neural Affordance Function (NAF)**, an object-centric, continuous 3D representation that jointly encodes geometry, color, dense visual features, contact probability, and action flows within a unified neural network structure.
- **Formulation:** An NAF (F) is parameterized by multiple MLPs: `F(x) = {f_geo, f_col, f_fea, f_cnt, f_act}`.
 - `f_enc`: A shared encoder head producing a latent geometry feature `z` from a 3D point `x`.
 - `f_geo`: Predicts the Signed Distance Function (SDF) value `s` from `z` and `x`, enabling accurate 3D geometry reconstruction (building on NeuS).
 - `f_col`: Outputs RGB radiance `c` from `z` and `x`.
 - `f_fea`: Generates `d`-dimensional visual descriptors `g` (e.g., DINO-like features) for robust retrieval and alignment.
 - `f_cnt`: Estimates the probability `r` that `x` lies on a feasible contact region.
 - `f_act`: Defined *on the object surface*, maps surface points `x'` (augmented with `z'` and `g'`) to H-step 3D point flows (P) and visibility scores (V). This restriction ensures compactness and motion knowledge on relevant surfaces.
- **Multimodal Differentiable Rendering:** NAFs enable rendering any encoded modality (geometry, color, features, contact probability) from arbitrary viewpoints using standard volume rendering techniques. This allows for consistent comparison and alignment across different views and modalities, bridging instance and viewpoint gaps.
- **Multimodal Knowledge Extraction:** Given an uncalibrated RGB video (`V`) and narration (`l`):
 1. **Static State Extraction:** The initial "hands-free" frame is passed through an image-to-3D model (e.g., Wonder3D, InstantMesh) to generate multi-view, pose-consistent images, foreground masks (Segment Anything), and dense visual features (DINOv2).
 2. **Affordance Cue Extraction:** The entire video is processed using a Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipeline (e.g., MegaSAM) and a metric depth estimator (e.g., UniDepthV2) to recover camera poses and depth maps.
 - **Contact Regions:** The first contact frame is identified, and contact pixels are warped to the initial hands-free frame to create contact masks for various views.
 - **Point Flows:** Keypoints sampled within the object's foreground are tracked in 3D using a point tracker (e.g., TapiP3D), yielding 3D point flows in the camera frame.
 3. **Canonicalization:** A similarity transformation (SIM(3)) is estimated to map the extracted camera-centric 3D point flows into the object's canonical frame used by the NAF, minimizing SDF values over the transformed points.
- **Neural Function Fitting:** The NAF is trained in two phases:
 1. **Static Phase:** `f_geo, f_col, f_fea, f_cnt` are fitted using a combined loss function (`L_static`) including color, regression (SDF), mask, feature (L2), and contact (BCE) losses.
 2. **Dynamic Phase:** With static heads frozen, `f_act` is fitted using an L2 loss (`L_dynamic`) to predict the canonicalized point flows and their visibility. This process requires only 2-3 demonstration videos per task.

**B. Affordance Transfer via Neural Functions (Transfer Phase - G_map)**

At test time, given an RGB-D observation (`{I, D}`) and a language instruction (`l`), the system transfers action knowledge from a retrieved NAF.
- **Affordance Knowledge Retrieval:** A Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM, e.g., GPT-4o) is used to retrieve the best-matched NAF (F*) from the memory bank. The MLLM jointly reasons over visual appearance (first frame of the demo video) and linguistic context (demo narration vs. task instruction), enabling more precise retrieval than CLIP-based methods.
- **Contact-Guided Hypothesis Sampling and Ranking:**
 1. **Sampling:** Multiple viewpoint hypotheses are sampled around the retrieved NAF's origin.
 2. **Filtering:** Invalid viewpoints where contact regions are occluded are filtered by querying `f_cnt`.
 3. **Ranking:** Remaining candidates are ranked by visual feature similarity to the target observation. Feature maps are rendered from candidate NAF views (`Q_fea`) and compared to the target scene's feature map (`G_t_fea`) using dense correspondence matching (best-buddy matching) and an L2 similarity metric. The top `k` poses are selected.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Affordance Optimization:** This strategy ensures robust alignment of F* with the target scene.
 1. **Coarse Alignment:** An initial SIM(3) transformation (`T_ot`) is optimized by minimizing `L_coarse`, a combination of:
 - **Feature Loss (`L_fea`):** Cosine similarity between rendered and target feature maps.
 - **Surface Loss (`L_surf`):** Huber loss on SDF values of transformed target points, ensuring geometric alignment.
 - **Depth Loss (`L_depth`):** Huber loss comparing rendered and observed depth maps.
 2. **Fine Alignment (Contact Refinement):** Building on the coarse alignment, `T_ot` is further refined by minimizing `L_fine`, which adds a contact loss (`L_cnt`) to `L_fea` and `L_surf`. `L_cnt` measures the distance between contact points extracted from F* and visually matched contact points in the target scene, ensuring precise alignment in the action-critical region.
- **6-DoF Manipulation Policy from Flows:**
 1. **Flow Extraction:** The aligned NAF's `f_act` is queried on the transformed target point cloud to generate 3D point flows (`P`).
 2. **EEF Pose Derivation:** Valid flows are converted into a sequence of relative 6-DoF end-effector (EEF) poses using a weighted SVD algorithm, where weights are inversely proportional to distance from the gripper.
 3. **Grasp Estimation:** An initial contact point (`x*`) is determined by maximizing `f_cnt` on the transformed target point cloud. A grasp detector (e.g., AnyGrasp) estimates an initial 6-DoF grasp pose (`T_hat`) around `x*`.
 4. **Absolute Trajectory:** The relative EEF motions are composed with `T_hat` to yield the final absolute 6-DoF end-effector trajectory (`T`) for execution.

**C. Implementation Details:** The paper specifies network architectures (MLPs, hash-grid, positional encoding), optimizer details (Adam, learning rates, loss weights), and the number of iterations for both training and inference phases, demonstrating careful engineering.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Actron3D demonstrates superior performance in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, particularly highlighting the advantages of its 3D neural affordance representation and optimization-based transfer mechanism.

**A. Simulator Benchmark (RLBench)**
- **Overall Performance:** Actron3D achieved an average success rate (SR) of **90.8%** across 13 diverse household tasks (including opening/closing various articulated objects like drawers, microwaves, cabinets, and portable objects like cups and bottles).
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** This represents a significant **14.9 percentage point improvement** over the best-performing baseline, VidBot (75.9%), and even larger margins against other methods like RAM (58.5%), Where2Act (56.4%), VRB (42.6%), and GFlow (39.6%).
- **Task-Specific Strengths:**
 - Actron3D consistently achieved high success rates (often 100%) on tasks involving both articulated (e.g., opening/closing drawers, microwaves, hinge cabinets) and portable objects (picking up cups/bottles).
 - It particularly excelled on tasks requiring complex, curved 3D motions, which are challenging for 2D-based methods (e.g., T03: Open microwave, T05: Open hinge cabinet). The paper attributes the failure of 2D methods to oversimplified 2D motion cues leading to infeasible linear motions and gripper slips.
- **Efficiency:** The superior performance was achieved using only 2-3 demonstration videos per task, a stark contrast to data-hungry baselines.

**B. Real Robot Experiments (Hello Robot Stretch 3)**
- **Real-World Generalization:** Actron3D was evaluated on five real-world tasks in previously unseen human-suited environments, achieving an average SR of **76.0%**.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** It significantly outperformed RAM (32.0%) and VidBot (50.0%) in real-world settings.
- **6-DoF Capability:** Crucially, Actron3D was the *only* method that generated full 6-DoF motions, which are essential for complex tasks like "Pour water." Baselines were limited to purely translational motions, leading to grasp slips and task failures.

**C. Ablation Studies**
A detailed ablation study highlighted the importance of each core component:
- **Impact of Neural Representation (V1: w/o Neural Function):** Replacing NAF with direct 2D feature-based transfer (like RAM) caused a drastic drop in average SR to **30.0%** (a >50% reduction). This unequivocally demonstrates the critical role of the continuous 3D neural function for robust transfer across viewpoint gaps.
- **Impact of Initialization Strategy (V2: w/o Contact Sampling, V3: w/o Viewpoint Ranking):**
 - Disabling contact-guided sampling (V2) reduced SR to **56.6%** (a 30.1% drop), as it allowed infeasible poses to enter optimization, increasing local minima.
 - Removing feature-based viewpoint ranking (V3) led to an SR of **57.8%** (a 28.9% drop), emphasizing that feature-informed ranking is crucial for guiding the optimizer to high-quality initial poses.
- **Impact of Optimization Scheme (V4: w/o Affordance Optimization, V5: w/o Contact Refinement):**
 - Disabling the entire coarse-to-fine optimization (V4) and relying on direct transfer from the most similar view resulted in an extremely low SR of **21.1%**. This confirms that sparse 2D visual alignment alone is insufficient for handling viewpoint variations and achieving precise actions.
 - Removing the final contact-centric refinement (V5) decreased SR to **66.7%** (a 20% drop). This indicates that while coarse alignment helps, precise alignment in the critical contact area requires this dedicated refinement step for accurate motion execution.

**D. Downstream Applications**
- **Long-horizon Manipulation:** Actron3D seamlessly integrated with MLLM-based task and motion planning frameworks to execute complex, multi-step instructions (e.g., "Use the sponge in the drawer to clean the table" decomposed into "Open drawer," "Take sponge," "Wipe table").
- **Policy Fine-tuning:** The action head of a retrieved NAF could be fine-tuned online for novel objects. This led to absolute SR improvements of 6.7% to 13.3% on various tasks and accelerated convergence by approximately **4 times** compared to training from scratch, validating the utility of NAF's latent features for action learning.

In summary, the main findings confirm that Actron3D’s novel 3D Neural Affordance Function and its differentiable, optimization-based transfer pipeline effectively address the challenges of generalization, data efficiency, and 6-DoF precision in robotic manipulation, significantly outperforming existing methods across a wide range of tasks and environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Actron3D represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and manipulation, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

- **Paving the Way for Generalist Robots:** The framework takes a crucial step towards building truly generalist robots that can efficiently acquire and apply a wide array of manipulation skills. By enabling learning from diverse, "in-the-wild" human videos with minimal demonstrations, it moves beyond the limitations of highly curated datasets or task-specific training.
- **Novelty of Neural Affordance Functions (NAFs):** The NAF is a powerful, compact, and continuous 3D object-centric representation. Its ability to jointly encode geometry, visual features, contact priors, and 6-DoF action flows in a unified differentiable framework is a key conceptual breakthrough. This solves the long-standing problem of 2D ambiguity in lifting to 3D actions and offers a robust, viewpoint-invariant representation.
- **Exceptional Data Efficiency:** Achieving state-of-the-art performance with only 2-3 demonstration videos per task is a game-changer. This dramatically lowers the data collection burden, making it feasible to rapidly deploy new skills for a robot in novel environments or with new objects, bypassing the need for extensive teleoperation or simulation assets.
- **Robustness and Generalization Capabilities:** The combination of MLLM-based affordance retrieval, contact-guided sampling, and coarse-to-fine differentiable optimization ensures robust zero-shot transfer of precise 6-DoF manipulation policies to novel objects, unseen viewpoints, and different robot embodiments. This level of generalization is critical for real-world deployment.
- **Embodiment-Agnostic Skill Learning:** By representing actions as object-centric 3D point flows, Actron3D decouples skill representation from specific robot embodiments. This makes the learned skills highly transferable across different robot platforms, enhancing the versatility and adaptability of robotic systems.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Dexterity:** The ability to infer dense 6-DoF end-effector poses enables robots to perform more dexterous and complex tasks (e.g., pouring water) that previous methods, often limited to translational motions or requiring manual inputs, could not handle effectively. This moves robots closer to human-level manipulation capabilities.
- **Integration with Advanced AI:** The seamless integration with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for intelligent skill retrieval and with 3D AIGC techniques for knowledge extraction demonstrates the potential of leveraging frontier AI advancements for robotics. This also opens avenues for more sophisticated language-guided, long-horizon task planning.
- **Acceleration of Robot Deployment and Adoption:** By making skill acquisition faster, cheaper, and more generalizable, Actron3D has the potential to accelerate the deployment of robots in various domains, from household assistance and logistics to manufacturing, reducing the need for expert programmers and extensive re-training.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a strong foundation for future work. Addressing current limitations, such as inference latency for strict real-time applications and robustness to AIGC artifacts, will further solidify its impact. Investigating vectorized optimization and second-order solvers for acceleration, as suggested by the authors, could push the boundaries of real-time transferable skill execution.

In conclusion, Actron3D's contributions are significant for advancing robot learning from human videos. Its innovative neural representation and optimization-based transfer pipeline offer a compelling solution to critical challenges in robotic manipulation, paving the way for more autonomous, adaptable, and generalist robots in the near future.
