# OSIRIS: Bridging Analog Circuit Design and Machine Learning with Scalable Dataset Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Politecnico di Milano introduced OSIRIS, a scalable pipeline for generating large, physically accurate datasets for analog integrated circuit layout design, producing 87,100 DRC-clean and LVS-verified variants. This framework enabled an RL-based optimization method to achieve PEX scores over an order of magnitude smaller for some circuits and nearly 50% faster acquisition times, also demonstrating its utility in fine-tuning an LLM for capacitor layout generation.

## Problem

- Analog integrated circuit (IC) back-end design, particularly physical layout, is highly complex and sensitive to parasitic effects, making automation difficult and reliant on manual expertise.
- Existing machine learning (ML) frameworks for analog design primarily address front-end optimizations, leaving a gap for iterative, performance-driven physical layout refinement based on post-layout feedback.
- The scarcity of open, high-quality datasets specifically for analog back-end design, including detailed physical layouts, parasitic extractions, and post-layout performance metrics, hinders ML research in this domain.

## Method

- **OSIRIS**, a scalable dataset generation pipeline, systematically explores the analog IC ==layout== design space by varying transistor finger counts and component placements.
- The pipeline generates physically accurate, DRC-clean, and LVS-verified layouts through a two-stage process: enumerating finger permutations and then generating layout variants via baseline place-and-route followed by spatial perturbations.
- It outputs GDSII files and comprehensive Quality of Solution (QoS) reports, including parasitic extraction (PEX) scores and layout area, for each generated variant.

## Results

- The OSIRIS pipeline generated a dataset of 87,100 DRC-clean and LVS-verified layout variants for five analog circuits, encompassing 871 netlist permutations and 100 spatial perturbations each.
- An RL-based optimization method, integrated with OSIRIS, achieved PEX scores more than an order of magnitude smaller for Miller and Feed Forward amplifiers, and reduced acquisition times by up to **50%** for several circuits compared to existing automated flows.
- The OSIRIS dataset successfully fine-tuned a Qwen3-**14B** LLM to generate **100%** valid capacitor layouts with perfect dimensional fidelity, a task a vanilla LLM could not perform.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scalable dataset generation is crucial for enabling machine learning applications in the traditionally manual and expert-driven analog integrated circuit back-end design.
> - Iterative, performance-driven design space exploration, facilitated by comprehensive post-layout feedback (e.g., PEX scores), can significantly improve the quality and efficiency of analog layouts compared to single-pass automation.
> - Domain-specific, physically accurate datasets are essential for fine-tuning large language models or other ML techniques to achieve manufacturable and functionally correct physical layout generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The automation of analog integrated circuit (IC) design has historically presented significant challenges compared to digital IC design. This disparity arises from the intricate interdependencies among physical layout, parasitic effects, and circuit-level performance, which are difficult to model and optimize using conventional electronic design automation (EDA) methodologies. While digital design flows have largely benefited from advanced automation, including machine learning (ML) integration, analog design remains a predominantly manual, expert-driven process.

Within the analog design flow, two main phases are distinguished: the front-end (schematic-level optimization) and the back-end (physical layout generation). Recent ML advancements have shown promise primarily in front-end optimizations, such as topology generation and device sizing, utilizing techniques like Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), Large Language Models (LLMs), and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Examples include CktGNN for operational amplifier optimization, AnalogCoder and LaMAGIC for specification-driven design, and EVA for circuit discovery. These efforts address the abstract representation and functional structure of circuits.

In contrast, the back-end phase, which involves translating optimized schematics into manufacturable physical layouts, remains comparatively underexplored by ML-driven automation. This phase is characterized by extreme sensitivity to layout geometry, parasitic effects, technology-node-specific constraints, and complex manufacturability rules. Existing back-end automation frameworks, such as Berkeley Analog Generator (BAG), ALIGN, and MAGICAL, provide automated place-and-route capabilities. However, these systems often rely on predefined templates, necessitate substantial domain expertise for parameter tuning, and typically operate as single-pass automation flows. While some methodologies integrate these layouters into closed-loop systems for iterative sizing adjustments, a fundamental gap exists in frameworks specifically designed for *iterative, performance-driven layout design space exploration* and optimization.

A critical impediment to advancing ML-based analog back-end automation is the limited availability of open, high-quality datasets tailored to this domain. Existing analog datasets primarily focus on front-end aspects (netlists, topologies, schematic-level performance), providing insufficient information on physically accurate, parasitic-aware layout variations. This scarcity restricts the development, benchmarking, and generalizability of ML techniques for layout-dependent effects.

OSIRIS is positioned to address these limitations by providing a scalable dataset generation pipeline and an accompanying dataset focused on the back-end design phase. It aims to bridge the gap between analog layout circuit design and machine learning by enabling the creation of large volumes of physically accurate, parasitic-aware layout data, thereby supporting the development of iterative, ML-driven optimization strategies for analog layout.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the enduring challenges in automating analog integrated circuit (IC) design, particularly in the back-end physical layout phase. The authors identify several key issues that necessitate a new approach:

1. **Complexity and Sensitivity of Analog Layout**: Analog circuit performance is highly sensitive to physical layout geometry. Subtle changes in component placement and routing can drastically alter parasitic effects (e.g., capacitance, resistance), which in turn degrade critical circuit-level performance metrics such as gain, bandwidth, noise, and stability. Managing these layout-dependent effects, process variations, and stringent manufacturing constraints makes the back-end design process iterative, time-consuming, and heavily reliant on expert human intervention. This constitutes a major bottleneck in the overall IC design flow.

2. **Lack of Holistic ML Frameworks for End-to-End Layout Optimization**: While machine learning (ML) has demonstrated potential in automating specific stages of analog design, particularly in the front-end, the development of comprehensive, end-to-end frameworks that iteratively refine layouts based on post-layout, parasitic-aware performance feedback is still in nascent stages. Existing back-end automation tools primarily offer single-pass solutions, which are insufficient for exploring complex design trade-offs under parasitic influences. There is a need for methodologies that enable adaptive layout refinement through repeated evaluation and learning in performance-constrained environments.

3. **Limited Availability of High-Quality, Open Datasets for Back-End Analog Design**: Progress in ML-driven electronic design automation (EDA) is significantly hindered by the scarcity of open, high-quality datasets specifically tailored to the analog back-end domain. Most existing datasets focus on front-end (schematic-level) information, lacking the detailed physical layout data, parasitic extraction results, and post-layout performance metrics necessary for training robust ML models for layout-dependent effects. This limitation restricts both the benchmarking of new ML techniques and their generalizability across different circuit types and technology nodes.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of OSIRIS are:

- **To develop OSIRIS: A Scalable Dataset Generation Pipeline**: Create an efficient and extensible pipeline capable of systematically exploring the design space of analog circuits. This pipeline is designed to generate large volumes of physically accurate, DRC-clean, and LVS-verified layouts, accompanied by comprehensive performance metrics and metadata.
- **To Enable ML-driven Research in Analog Back-End Automation**: Provide the necessary data infrastructure and framework to foster machine learning research in the automation of analog physical layout, moving beyond manual trial-and-error methods.
- **To Support Iterative, Performance-Driven Layout Optimization**: Design OSIRIS to facilitate iterative design space exploration strategies, contrasting with single-pass automation. This allows for continuous refinement of layouts based on parasitic-aware performance feedback.
- **To Release a Comprehensive Analog Layout Dataset**: Make a substantial, open-source dataset publicly available, demonstrating OSIRIS's capabilities and providing a resource for the ML-for-EDA community to develop and benchmark novel back-end optimization strategies.
- **To Demonstrate an RL-driven Optimization Baseline**: Develop and integrate a reinforcement learning framework that leverages OSIRIS to iteratively explore layout design spaces and optimize circuit implementations based on parasitic-aware performance feedback, thereby showcasing a practical application of the generated data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OSIRIS is presented as a scalable dataset generation pipeline designed to systematically explore the analog IC layout design space and produce comprehensive performance metrics. The pipeline takes a circuit netlist template, transistor matching specifications, and a simulation testbench as input, generating physical layout files (GDS) and quality of solution (QoS) reports. The process is divided into two primary stages: Fingers Permutation and Variants Generation.

**Design Space Dimensions Explored:**
OSIRIS primarily explores two hierarchical dimensions to capture parasitic effects:
1. **Number of Transistor Fingers**: For a given transistor, the count of gate segments (fingers) sharing source/drain regions can be varied. This affects layout compactness, parasitic effects, and performance without altering logical function.
2. **Component Placement**: A "halo" mechanism allows each component (transistors, resistors, capacitors) to move freely within a defined bounding box, enabling spatial perturbations.

The underlying infrastructure is designed for extensibility beyond these two degrees of freedom.

**OSIRIS Pipeline Stages:**

1. **Fingers Permutation**:
 - **Input**: Circuit netlist template (TP) and specified transistor pairs (Ps) for matching requirements.
 - **Process**: This stage enumerates all valid combinations of finger assignments for transistors. Validity is determined by adherence to matching requirements (e.g., current mirrors having identical finger counts) and minimum gate dimensions specified by the Process Design Kit (PDK). Each valid finger assignment creates a distinct netlist.
 - **Output**: A set of *M* distinct netlists (NL\_i).

2. **Variants Generation**:
 - **Input**: The set of *M* netlists from the previous stage and a simulation testbench (TB).
 - **Process**: For each netlist NL\_i, this stage generates *N* layout variants. It is further divided into two steps:
 - **Baseline Place-and-Route (P&R)**:
 - Synthesizes an initial, manufacturable baseline layout for a given netlist NL\_i.
 - **Placer**: Encodes circuits using sequence-pairs, derives placement constraints solved with a geometric Integer Linear Programming (ILP) formulation, and uses simulated annealing as a meta-heuristic to minimize half-perimeter wire length (HPWL).
 - **Router**: Employs Dijkstra's algorithm for global routing and the A\* search algorithm for detailed routing to resolve local congestion.
 - **Validation**: The generated layout undergoes Design Rule Checking (DRC) (respected by construction), Layout Versus Schematic (LVS) verification (using Netgen), Parasitic Extraction (PEX), and circuit simulation (using Ngspice) to assess functionality and performance.
 - **Output**: A baseline layout (GDS\_b\_i), corresponding quality metrics (QoS\_b\_i), and an internal representation (IR\_b\_i) containing component coordinates and types. This baseline serves as the starting point for further perturbations.
 - **Random Exploration (Default Strategy)**:
 - **Input**: IR\_b\_i.
 - **Process**: Iteratively generates *N* incremental variants. In each iteration *j*:
 - **Shift**: A component is randomly selected, and a cardinal direction (up, down, left, right) is randomly chosen to perturb its position within its halo. The IR is updated.
 - **P&R**: The layout is re-routed to ensure electrical connectivity based on the new component placement.
 - **Validate**: DRC, LVS, PEX, and simulation are performed. If any validation fails, the perturbation is reverted, and the iteration is repeated until a successful layout is generated.
 - **Output**: A set of *N* GDS and QoS files for the *N* variants derived from each NL\_i.
 - **Overall Output**: A total of *M* × *N* layout variants, each with its GDSII file and QoS report.

**Metrics for Layout Evaluation:**
OSIRIS uses two primary metrics to quantify the quality of generated layouts:
1. **PEX Score**: Quantifies performance degradation due to parasitic effects. It is computed as the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) between pre-layout (schematic-level) and post-layout simulation results (specifically AC small-signal simulations across a frequency sweep). Lower pscore values indicate better performance and less parasitic impact.
2. **Area**: Measures the total silicon footprint of the layout, calculated as the sum of the bounding box areas of all components. Lower area values are generally preferred for cost and integration density.

**RL-driven Baseline Methodology (Alternative Variants Generation Strategy):**
As an alternative to random exploration, OSIRIS can employ a two-level iterative Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach for variants generation.

1. **Outer Loop (FinPerm Search)**:
 - **Agent**: A fully connected neural network trained with REINFORCE.
 - **Action**: Selects the next netlist (NL\_i) to explore by choosing finger counts for transistors.
 - **Reward**: Derived from the best performance (lowest pscore and area) achieved by the inner loop for the selected netlist. A negative reward is assigned for invalid finger configurations.

2. **Inner Loop (RL Exploration)**:
 - **Agent**: An actor-critic agent trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The input encodes component types and coordinates.
 - **Action**: Selects a component to move and a cardinal direction.
 - **Process**: Similar to Random Exploration, including Shift, P&R, and Validate steps.
 - **Reward**: Computed based on changes in pscore and area relative to the baseline layout (weighted by tuning parameters α and β). Unlike random exploration, an unsuccessful iteration (validation failure) results in a penalty, and the episode ends.

**Technology Node**: All circuits are implemented using the open-source SkyWater 130nm Process Design Kit (PDK).

**Dataset Structure**: The generated dataset is organized into `netlists` (containing circuit templates, testbenches, matching pairs, and finger-permutated netlists) and `data` (comprising `simulations` for pre/post-layout results, `metrics` for pscore and area, `layouts` in GDSII format, and `metadata` for component-level GDS and movement traces).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across dataset generation, RL-driven optimization performance, and an LLM-based use case.

#### 4.1. Dataset Generation and Statistics

- **Scale and Scope**: OSIRIS successfully generated a substantial dataset consisting of 87,100 DRC-clean and LVS-verified layout variants. This was achieved by exploring 871 distinct netlist permutations (derived from finger count variations) across five representative analog circuits: Miller amplifier, Ahuja amplifier, Feed Forward amplifier, 5-Transistors amplifier, and a Low-Pass Filter (LPF). For each netlist, 100 layout variants were generated through spatial perturbations.
- **Circuit Diversity**: The selected circuits feature a mix of NMOS/PMOS transistors, resistors, and capacitors, encompassing structural diversity with current mirrors and differential pairs, making them suitable templates for layout automation research.
- **Acquisition Efficiency**: The generation of a single layout required an average of 39 seconds. The entire dataset acquisition took approximately 37 days (156 to 195 hours per circuit) on a Rocky Linux machine with a 32-core Intel Xeon CPU and 128GB RAM. No GPU was used for the dataset generation itself.
- **Data Characteristics**: The dataset, totaling 5 GB, includes comprehensive information: pre- and post-layout simulation results (AC small-signal over 1kHz to 1GHz), PEX scores (quantifying parasitic degradation), layout area, GDSII files for physical layouts, and metadata detailing component displacements and individual component GDSII.
- **Comparative Advantage**: OSIRIS is identified as the first framework specifically designed for back-end analog design that systematically explores and extensively covers the layout design space. It provides physically valid, parasitic-aware data, distinguishing it from existing front-end focused datasets (e.g., CktGNN, AnalogGenie, AMSNet) and even back-end netlist collections (e.g., ALIGN) by providing actual layout files and detailed post-layout performance metrics.

#### 4.2. RL-driven Optimization Performance

To evaluate the efficacy of learning-driven strategies within OSIRIS, an RL-based exploration method was compared against state-of-the-art single-pass layout generators (MAGICAL, ALIGN) and the random exploration strategy within OSIRIS.

- **PEX Score (Parasitic Awareness)**: The RL-based approach consistently achieved lower pscore values across all five circuit benchmarks. For the Miller and Feed Forward amplifiers, the RL method produced pscore values more than an order of magnitude smaller than those from ALIGN and MAGICAL. This indicates that the learning-guided placement strategies effectively mitigate parasitic effects in the generated layouts, leading to better electrical performance.
- **Area**: The RL baseline generally yielded layouts with comparable or smaller areas compared to other methods. For instance, it produced the most compact layouts for Miller and Feed Forward amplifiers. For the Ahuja amplifier, the RL method achieved a slightly smaller area (1,797 µm²) with the same pscore as the Random baseline (1,805 µm²). An exception was the 5-Transistors OTA, where the RL approach resulted in a larger area (444.5 µm²) but still achieved a lower pscore (0.047 V vs. 0.050 V for Random), indicating a trade-off favoring electrical performance. For the LPF, RL achieved both the smallest area and lowest pscore.
- **Acquisition Time**: The RL-based method demonstrated reduced acquisition time compared to the Random baseline. For example, it achieved nearly a 50% reduction in runtime for the Miller and Ahuja circuits, reporting the shortest acquisition times across all benchmarks (e.g., 96 hours for Miller vs. 195 hours for Random; 70 hours for Ahuja vs. 191 hours for Random). This suggests that RL enables more efficient exploration of the solution space, converging faster to high-quality solutions.
- **Robustness**: MAGICAL failed to generate any valid layouts for the LPF benchmark, whereas OSIRIS (both Random and RL variants) successfully processed it.

#### 4.3. OSIRIS Dataset Use Case: LLM-based Component Layout Generation

The OSIRIS-generated dataset was utilized to fine-tune a Large Language Model (LLM), Qwen3-14B, for automated component-level layout generation, specifically for capacitors in the SkyWater 130nm PDK.

- **Fine-tuning Effectiveness**: Using 10,000 capacitor layouts extracted from the OSIRIS dataset, the Qwen3-14B model was fine-tuned with LoRA. The fine-tuned model successfully generated 100% valid outputs with perfect dimensional fidelity for capacitor layouts (DRC-free, LVS-verified, and physically correct structures, including overlapping metal plates and connection pins).
- **Contrast with Vanilla LLM**: The vanilla (not fine-tuned) Qwen3-14B model, even with highly structured prompts, failed to generate geometrically valid or functional capacitor layouts, demonstrating a complete lack of PDK-specific geometric competence. This highlights the necessity and utility of domain-specific datasets like OSIRIS for specialized ML tasks in EDA.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of OSIRIS and its accompanying dataset holds significant implications for the field of analog integrated circuit (IC) design and the broader application of machine learning (ML) in Electronic Design Automation (EDA).

1. **Enabling ML-driven Analog Back-End Automation**: OSIRIS addresses a critical bottleneck in analog IC design by providing a scalable framework for generating large volumes of high-quality, physically accurate, and parasitic-aware layout data. This is crucial for training and validating ML models designed to automate complex back-end tasks such as placement, routing, and parasitic optimization, which have traditionally been manual and expert-intensive.
2. **Bridging the Data Gap**: The release of a substantial, open-source dataset (87,100 layout variants) specifically targeting analog back-end design fills a long-standing void. Prior datasets predominantly focused on front-end aspects, limiting ML's applicability to layout-dependent effects. OSIRIS's dataset, with its detailed GDSII files, pre/post-layout simulations, and parasitic scores, provides the necessary supervision signals for learning layout behaviors.
3. **Facilitating Iterative Design Space Exploration**: Unlike existing single-pass automation tools, OSIRIS is designed to enable iterative, performance-driven layout design. This paradigm shift allows ML algorithms to learn from post-layout feedback and progressively refine designs, leading to superior optimization outcomes, as demonstrated by the RL-driven baseline.
4. **Improved Design Quality and Efficiency**: The experimental results show that ML-guided approaches, when integrated with OSIRIS, can yield layouts with significantly reduced parasitic effects (lower pscore) and often comparable or smaller areas compared to established automated flows. The RL-based method also demonstrated faster convergence, leading to reduced acquisition times. This translates to higher-performance circuits and accelerated design cycles.
5. **Foundation for Future EDA Advancements**: OSIRIS provides a modular and open-source foundation that is extensible. This extensibility allows for future development, including support for a wider range of circuit families, more sophisticated perturbation operators, and capabilities for transfer learning across different PDKs. This lays the groundwork for increasingly sophisticated, learning-driven analog back-end flows.
6. **Unlocking Novel Automation Paradigms**: The successful application of the OSIRIS dataset to fine-tune an LLM for component-level layout generation (e.g., capacitors) demonstrates the potential for ML to automate even highly granular, PDK-specific tasks. This opens new avenues for synthesizing manufacturable geometries directly from specifications, a task previously requiring significant manual effort and specialized knowledge.
7. **Promoting Open Science and Reproducibility**: By making the OSIRIS code and dataset publicly available, the authors foster reproducibility, encourage broader participation from the ML-for-EDA community, and enable independent benchmarking and further research. This contributes to a more transparent and collaborative research environment.
8. **Indirect Societal Impact**: Accelerating the development of high-performance, energy-efficient analog ICs through automation could indirectly benefit various industries, including healthcare (e.g., advanced medical devices), autonomous systems (e.g., enhanced sensing platforms), and communications. While such advancements carry ethical considerations regarding responsible use, OSIRIS's open nature aims to ensure transparency and broad access.
