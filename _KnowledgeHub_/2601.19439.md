# OSIRIS: Bridging Analog Circuit Design and Machine Learning with Scalable Dataset Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Politecnico di Milano introduced OSIRIS, a scalable pipeline for generating large, physically accurate datasets for analog integrated circuit layout design, producing 87,100 DRC-clean and LVS-verified variants. This framework enabled an RL-based optimization method to achieve PEX scores over an order of magnitude smaller for some circuits and nearly 50% faster acquisition times, also demonstrating its utility in fine-tuning an LLM for capacitor layout generation.

## Problem

- Analog integrated circuit (IC) back-end design, particularly physical layout, is highly complex and sensitive to parasitic effects, making automation difficult and reliant on manual expertise.
- Existing machine learning (ML) frameworks for analog design primarily address front-end optimizations, leaving a gap for iterative, performance-driven physical layout refinement based on post-layout feedback.
- The scarcity of open, high-quality datasets specifically for analog back-end design, including detailed physical layouts, parasitic extractions, and post-layout performance metrics, hinders ML research in this domain.

## Method

- **OSIRIS**, a scalable dataset generation pipeline, systematically explores the analog IC ==layout== design space by varying transistor finger counts and component placements.
- The pipeline generates physically accurate, DRC-clean, and LVS-verified layouts through a two-stage process: enumerating finger permutations and then generating layout variants via baseline place-and-route followed by spatial perturbations.
- It outputs GDSII files and comprehensive Quality of Solution (QoS) reports, including parasitic extraction (PEX) scores and layout area, for each generated variant.

## Results

- The OSIRIS pipeline generated a dataset of 87,100 DRC-clean and LVS-verified layout variants for five analog circuits, encompassing 871 netlist permutations and 100 spatial perturbations each.
- An RL-based optimization method, integrated with OSIRIS, achieved PEX scores more than an order of magnitude smaller for Miller and Feed Forward amplifiers, and reduced acquisition times by up to **50%** for several circuits compared to existing automated flows.
- The OSIRIS dataset successfully fine-tuned a Qwen3-**14B** LLM to generate **100%** valid capacitor layouts with perfect dimensional fidelity, a task a vanilla LLM could not perform.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scalable dataset generation is crucial for enabling machine learning applications in the traditionally manual and expert-driven analog integrated circuit back-end design.
> - Iterative, performance-driven design space exploration, facilitated by comprehensive post-layout feedback (e.g., PEX scores), can significantly improve the quality and efficiency of analog layouts compared to single-pass automation.
> - Domain-specific, physically accurate datasets are essential for fine-tuning large language models or other ML techniques to achieve manufacturable and functionally correct physical layout generation.
