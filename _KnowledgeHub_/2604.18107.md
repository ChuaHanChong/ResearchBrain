# Test-Time Perturbation Learning with Delayed Feedback for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Tsinghua University developed Perturbation learning with Delayed Feedback (PDF), a lightweight, verifier-free test-time adaptation framework that enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' robustness against trajectory overfitting. PDF improved average success rate on the LIBERO robotic manipulation benchmark by 8 percentage points over the OpenVLA baseline and achieved a Human-Normalized Score of 1.07 on Atari, a 0.10 improvement over the Jat base model.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit fragility to minor environmental perturbations due to "trajectory overfitting," where they rely on spurious correlations from training data rather than robust semantic understanding.
- Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for VLAs are either computationally expensive (verifier-based) or unstable and unreliable (verifier-free methods relying on miscalibrated self-supervised confidence metrics).
- VLAs tend to reproduce memorized action patterns, leading to suboptimal or incorrect action sequences when inputs deviate slightly from training data, without dynamic, goal-oriented decision-making.

## Method

- The **PDF** framework introduces an ==Uncertainty-Based Action Voting== mechanism that adaptively applies data augmentation based on the model's decision uncertainty, generating multiple perturbed action logits.
- A lightweight, trainable ==Perturbation head $h_\theta$== adds learnable perturbations to the base VLA's action logits, while the large base model's parameters remain frozen.
- ==Delayed Feedback-Guided Adaptation== optimizes the P head using a ==REINFORCE-style objective== and a ==KL divergence regularizer==, utilizing environmental feedback to refine action logits and correct overconfident incorrect decisions.

## Results

- PDF achieved **0.77** average success rate on LIBERO, **+8pp** over OpenVLA baseline (**0.69**) and nearly **+6pp** over MG-Select.
- On Atari-57, PDF reached HNS **1.07**, surpassing Jat base model's **0.97** by **+0.10**, with positive performance changes in **47/57** games.
- Ablation confirmed both uncertainty-based data augmentation and delayed feedback-guided adaptation contribute synergistically.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Trajectory overfitting in VLAs can be effectively mitigated at test time by adaptively perturbing action logits and refining these perturbations with delayed environmental feedback.
> - Quantifying decision uncertainty allows for efficient allocation of data augmentation resources, balancing improved robustness with computational overhead during inference.
> - A lightweight perturbation module, trained with a specialized loss function leveraging delayed rewards, can enable stable and efficient test-time adaptation without requiring computationally intensive fine-tuning of the entire base VLA model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an advancement in sequential decision-making systems, integrating visual perception, language understanding, and action generation within a single framework. These models have demonstrated capabilities in domains such as robotic manipulation and visually grounded control, indicating progress towards general-purpose embodied intelligence. Examples include OpenVLA, Gato, RT-1, and RT-2.

Despite these capabilities, a recognized challenge for VLAs is their fragility to minor environmental perturbations, such as subtle changes in object pose, which can lead to performance degradation or task failure. This brittleness is attributed, in part, to what the authors term "trajectory overfitting." This phenomenon involves VLAs relying on spurious correlations between actions and contextual visual patterns—like background textures or gripper appearance—memorized from training trajectories, rather than forming a robust semantic understanding of the task. Consequently, when inputs deviate slightly from training data, the model may reproduce suboptimal or incorrect action sequences.

To address this brittleness, test-time adaptation (TTA) methods have been explored. Traditional TTA approaches often fall into two categories: verifier-based and verifier-free. Verifier-based methods, such as V-GPS and RoboMonkey, utilize a separate, pretrained verifier to evaluate candidate actions, incurring significant computational overhead and potentially struggling with generalization to novel environments. Verifier-free TTA strategies, like MG-Select, aim to adapt models in real-time using only unlabeled test samples, avoiding the cost of a verifier. However, these methods frequently rely on self-supervised confidence metrics (e.g., entropy minimization) which can become unreliable under significant distribution shifts or when the model's predictions are miscalibrated. This limitation can lead to adaptation instability or suboptimal accuracy.

This paper positions itself within the verifier-free TTA landscape for VLAs. It aims to develop a lightweight and stable adaptation framework that mitigates trajectory overfitting and improves decision performance during inference without requiring explicit fine-tuning of the base VLA model's parameters or the use of an external verifier.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and reliability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models during test-time inference, particularly when faced with environmental shifts. The authors identify a specific failure mode, termed "trajectory overfitting," as a primary motivation for their work.

**Detailed Motivation:**

1. **Addressing Trajectory Overfitting:** The paper posits that VLAs, despite their performance, often "over-attend to the spurious correlation between actions and entities," leading them to "reproduce memorized action patterns." This means the models learn to execute sequences of actions based on superficial visual cues or spatial arrangements observed during training, rather than a deep semantic understanding of the task and its objectives. For instance, as illustrated in Figure 1, a robot instructed to grasp a bowl might proceed with a movement towards a plate even if it failed to grasp the bowl, or even if the target bowl is visually obscured. This behavior indicates a reliance on learned motion patterns or contextual information rather than dynamic, goal-oriented decision-making. The authors argue that this overfitting makes VLAs fragile to minor environmental changes.
2. **Improving Test-Time Decision Performance:** The core motivation is to develop a method that can adapt the VLA's policy during inference to mitigate the effects of trajectory overfitting and improve task success rates. This adaptation should occur without costly fine-tuning of the large base VLA model, which is often computationally expensive and impractical in real-time deployment.
3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) Methods:**
 - **Verifier-based TTA:** While effective, these methods introduce substantial computational overhead due to the need for a pre-trained verifier and multiple rollouts. They also may not generalize well to unseen environments with different dynamics.
 - **Verifier-free TTA:** Current verifier-free methods often rely on self-supervised confidence metrics (e.g., entropy minimization), which can be unreliable if the model's initial predictions are overconfident in incorrect actions. This can lead to adaptation instability or even amplification of errors, as depicted in Figure 2. The authors aim to provide a more stable and accurate verifier-free adaptation mechanism.
4. **Maintaining Efficiency and Stability:** The proposed solution must be lightweight and operate in real-time, making it practical for deployment. This implies a strategy that minimizes computational overhead and ensures stable adaptation without causing new vulnerabilities.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations, the key objectives are to:
- Propose a verifier-free test-time adaptation framework that enhances VLA decision performance.
- Mitigate trajectory overfitting by making the VLA less reliant on spurious correlations and more on semantic task grounding.
- Introduce a mechanism that adaptively allocates augmentation resources based on decision uncertainty to balance performance and inference cost.
- Develop a method to refine action logits guided by delayed feedback from the environment, specifically to correct overconfident incorrect decisions.
- Demonstrate consistent performance gains across diverse VLA benchmarks (robotic manipulation and visual control games) while maintaining real-time efficiency and keeping the base model parameters frozen.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Perturbation learning with Delayed Feedback (PDF), is a plug-and-play, verifier-free test-time adaptation strategy for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. It operates without fine-tuning the base VLA model parameters and consists of two primary components: Uncertainty-Based Action Voting and Delayed Feedback-Guided Adaptation.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The VLA problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where at each time step `t`, the agent observes a multimodal state `s_t = (o_t, c_t)` comprising a pixel observation `o_t` and a textual instruction `c_t`. The agent selects an action `a_t`, leading to a new state `s_{t+1}`. Upon episode completion, the agent receives delayed feedback `r` reflecting task performance.

#### 3.2. Uncertainty-Based Action Voting

This component aims to improve the robustness of VLAs during inference by adaptively applying data augmentation based on the model's decision uncertainty.

1. **Feature Extraction and Initial Logits:** Given a state `s_t = (o_t, c_t)`, the pixel observation `o_t` is encoded by a visual encoder into pixel embeddings `e_{o_t}`, and the textual instruction `c_t` is tokenized and embedded into text embeddings `e_{c_t}`. These are concatenated, projected into multimodal features `f_t`, and then processed by an MLP (the Large Model (LM) head `h_ϕ(·)`) to produce initial logits `z_t` for each action token.

2. **Uncertainty Estimation:** The predictive uncertainty of the model's decision is quantified using the normalized Shannon entropy of the predicted action distribution:
 `U_t = - (1 / log K) * Σ_{k=1}^K p(a_k | s_k) log p(a_k | s_k)`
 where `K` is the number of possible action tokens and `p(a_t = a_k | s_t) = softmax(z_t)_k`. High uncertainty indicates a potentially unreliable decision.

3. **Adaptive Data Augmentation:** To mitigate uncertainty and potential trajectory overfitting, data augmentation is employed. The augmentation budget `N_t` (number of augmented views) is adaptively determined by the estimated uncertainty:
 `N_t = N_{max} * U_t`
 where `N_{max}` is the maximum allowed augmentation budget. This approach allocates more augmentation resources when uncertainty is high, balancing computational overhead with the need for robustness.

4. **Multi-View Processing and Logit Perturbation:** Both the original observation `o_t` and its `N_t` augmented views `\{T_j(o_t)\}_{j=1}^{N_t}` are processed. Each view is divided into patches, encoded into pixel embeddings, concatenated with text embeddings, and processed by a causal transformer to produce features `f_t`. These features are then projected by two parallel heads:
 - The LM head `h_ϕ(·)` (from the base VLA) generates the original decision logits.
 - A newly introduced, lightweight **Perturbation head (P head) `h_θ(·)`** outputs learnable perturbations.
 The final logits `˜z_t` for each view are computed as:
 `˜z_t = h_ϕ(f_t) + λh_θ(f_t)`
 where `λ` is a hyper-parameter controlling the perturbation scale.

5. **Action Voting:** The final logits `˜z_t` from the original and augmented views yield a set of candidate actions. The final action `a_t` for the current time step is selected through majority voting among these candidates. The features `f_t` and the voted final logits `˜z_t` (before voting) are stored in a rollout buffer `D`.

Crucially, throughout this process, all parameters of the base VLA model (visual encoder, token embedding layers, causal transformer, LM head) remain **frozen**. Only the P head `h_θ(·)` contains trainable parameters.

#### 3.3. Delayed Feedback-Guided Adaptation

This component refines the P head's parameters using delayed environmental feedback, correcting overconfident incorrect decisions.

1. **Feedback Reception:** After an episode completes, the agent receives delayed feedback `r` (e.g., task success/failure, cumulative reward), reflecting the overall performance.

2. **P Head Optimization:** A batch of features `f_b` is sampled from the rollout buffer `D`. The perturbed logits `˜z_b` for these features are recomputed:
 `˜z_b = h_ϕ(f_b) + λh_θ(f_b)`
 A perturbed policy `˜π_b = softmax(˜z_b)` is then generated. The P head `h_θ` is optimized using the following PDF loss function:
 `L_{PDF} = -(r - b) log π_ϕ + λ_{KL} I[r > b] KL(π_ϕ || ˜π)`
 where:
 - The first term is a REINFORCE-style objective. It increases the likelihood of actions from the perturbed policy `˜π` when the feedback `r` exceeds a baseline `b`. This encourages the P head to steer the base VLA towards actions associated with positive outcomes.
 - The second term is a KL divergence regularizer, `KL(π_ϕ || ˜π)`. It is gated by `I[r > b]`, meaning it is applied only when positive feedback is received. This regularizer encourages the perturbed policy `˜π` to remain close to the original base policy `π_ϕ`, promoting stability and preventing drastic, potentially unstable, deviations when successful outcomes are observed.
 - `λ_{KL}` is a hyper-parameter.

This adaptation process focuses solely on updating the lightweight P head `h_θ`, ensuring efficient and stable test-time adaptation while keeping the large VLA base model frozen.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PDF was evaluated across two distinct decision-making environments: the LIBERO robotic manipulation benchmark and the Atari suite, using OpenVLA and Jat (GATO implementation) as base models, respectively.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison Across Benchmarks

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** On the LIBERO benchmark, which includes Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long-horizon task suites, PDF demonstrated consistent improvements. It achieved an average success rate (SR) of **0.77**, with a mean rank of **2.5** among all compared methods, including various VLA variants and other TTA baselines. This represents a gain over the reproduced OpenVLA baseline (0.69 SR). Specifically, PDF achieved 0.90 SR on Spatial tasks, 0.86 SR on Goal tasks, and 0.59 SR on Long-horizon tasks. The most notable gains were observed in Long-horizon tasks, where PDF improved by 4.1 percentage points over the best baseline, suggesting enhanced sequential decision quality. Compared to MG-Select, another verifier-free TTA method, PDF improved average SR by nearly 6 percentage points.
- **Atari Benchmark:** On Atari-57, PDF achieved a Human-Normalized Score (HNS) of **1.07**, surpassing the Jat base model's 0.97 (a +0.10 improvement). Out of 57 games, PDF showed positive performance changes in 47 games, with an average improvement of 11.28%. Significant gains were observed in games such as BOXING (+60.25%), TIME PILOT (+53%), ATLANTIS (+49%), and ASSAULT (+45%).
- **Efficiency:** The PDF framework uses a lightweight perturbation module with only 9M trainable parameters, while the base models use 93–130M parameters, and requires no gradient access to the base model. This indicates its efficiency for test-time adaptation.

#### 4.2. Impact of Data Augmentation and Delayed Feedback

An ablation study on LIBERO demonstrated the individual and combined contributions of Data Augmentation (DA) and Delayed Feedback (DF):

- **PDF w/o DF (only DA):** This variant showed a substantial performance drop compared to the full PDF model (e.g., Object SR dropped to 0.50 from 0.72, Goal SR to 0.77 from 0.85). This indicates that DF is a critical component for robustness, especially in object manipulation and goal-conditioned tasks.
- **PDF w/o DA (only DF):** This variant generally surpassed the OpenVLA baseline on most tasks, suggesting that DF alone contributes positively.
- **Full PDF (with both DF and DA):** This model achieved the highest average success rates across all four LIBERO suites, outperforming both individual variants. This confirms a synergistic relationship between DA and DF, where both components contribute integrally to the observed performance gains.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Data Augmentation Budget

Experiments on varying data augmentation budgets (number of augmented views) revealed that:

- Increasing the augmentation budget beyond a certain point (e.g., from 0 to 4) generally led to performance degradation across LIBERO and Atari benchmarks.
- A moderate augmentation budget (up to a maximum of 3 augmented views) offered the best trade-off, mitigating trajectory overfitting without introducing excessive noise or low-quality views.
- The findings suggest that adaptive, benchmark-specific budget ceilings for data augmentation are preferable to uniform maximization.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Loss Functions

Analysis of the PDF loss function (comprising a REINFORCE-style term and a KL regularization term) indicated that both components contribute to the overall performance:

- The full PDF objective yielded the highest scores across benchmarks.
- Removing the KL divergence term ("w/o KL") resulted in notable performance changes, sometimes positive on Atari (+0.07 HNS) but with varied impacts on LIBERO.
- Removing the REINFORCE-style term ("w/o RE") generally led to slight performance reductions on Atari (-0.01 HNS) but some improvements on specific LIBERO suites (e.g., Object +0.07 SR).
- These results imply that the KL term provides stability and calibrated exploration, while the REINFORCE-style term focuses on reward-aligned action refinement. Their combined use provides complementary benefits.

#### 4.5. Comparison between Voting Mechanisms

Comparing dimension-wise and action-wise voting strategies:

- **Dimension-wise voting** (majority vote per action dimension) was generally more effective, particularly on LIBERO. It allowed for more flexible deviations from the original policy by enabling cross-view agreement on individual action components, thus better mitigating trajectory overfitting.
- **Action-wise voting** (voting over full action tuples) often resulted in performance closer to the baseline, as achieving consensus on an entire action tuple across different augmented views was less frequent.
- On Atari, where actions are effectively one-dimensional, both methods were equivalent.

#### 4.6. Visualization and Case Study

Qualitative analysis on LIBERO tasks (e.g., "pick up the cream cheese") provided visual evidence:

- **OpenVLA (baseline)** often exhibited trajectory overfitting, executing pre-learned motion plans even if the target object was not successfully manipulated, leading to task failure.
- **PDF** demonstrated more robust and state-aware behavior. In successful cases, it correctly interacted with the target object. In instances of initial failure (e.g., failing to grasp an object), PDF exhibited corrective actions, reattempting the maneuver until success, indicating dynamic decision-making rather than a fixed open-loop replay. These observations highlight PDF's ability to utilize feedback for corrective actions and its resilience to demonstration bias.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Perturbation learning with Delayed Feedback (PDF) as a contribution to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly in enhancing their robustness during test-time operation.

#### 5.1. Significance

1. **Addressing Trajectory Overfitting:** The work systematically identifies and analyzes "trajectory overfitting" as a fundamental cause of VLA fragility under environmental shifts. By proposing PDF, the paper offers a method to mitigate this behavior, encouraging VLA agents to ground their decisions in semantic understanding rather than spurious correlations.
2. **Verifier-Free and Lightweight Adaptation:** PDF provides a verifier-free test-time adaptation framework, which is computationally more efficient than verifier-based methods. The framework achieves adaptation by learning a lightweight perturbation module rather than requiring full base model fine-tuning or gradient access, making it practical for real-world deployment where computational resources might be constrained.
3. **Improved Decision Performance:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across diverse benchmarks (LIBERO robotic manipulation and Atari games) indicate that PDF effectively improves VLA decision quality, leading to higher task success rates and human-normalized scores.
4. **Synergistic Components:** The methodology integrates uncertainty-based data augmentation for adaptive resource allocation and a delayed feedback-guided adaptation mechanism, showing that these components provide complementary benefits for stability and performance.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **More Robust Embodied AI:** By increasing the robustness of VLAs to subtle environmental variations, PDF contributes to the development of more reliable and trustworthy embodied AI agents for applications in robotics and other interactive environments.
2. **Practical Deployment of VLAs:** The lightweight and verifier-free nature of PDF could facilitate the practical deployment of advanced VLA models in real-world scenarios, where online adaptation and efficiency are critical.
3. **Foundation for Future TTA Research:** This work establishes a path toward reliable verifier-free test-time adaptation in multimodal decision-making agents. The identified problem of trajectory overfitting and the proposed solution could inspire further research into more fundamental ways to prevent such biases in VLA training.

**Limitations:**

The authors acknowledge that PDF does not fully solve the issue of trajectory overfitting but rather alleviates its negative impact. This suggests that while PDF improves robustness, the underlying propensity for VLAs to overfit trajectories remains. Future work aims to address this challenge more fundamentally. The effectiveness of the chosen data augmentation types and the generalizability of the uncertainty estimation method to all possible environmental shifts are also aspects that could be further explored.
