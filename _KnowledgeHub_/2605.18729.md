# Robo-Cortex: A Self-Evolving Embodied Agent via Dual-Grain Cognitive Memory and Autonomous Knowledge Induction

> [!summary] Summary
> A self-evolving embodied agent framework, Robo-Cortex, addresses "experiential amnesia" in autonomous navigation by converting interaction history into generalizable decision heuristics, demonstrating improved task success and generalization across diverse environments in simulation and preliminary real-world tests.

## Problem

- Embodied agents frequently suffer from "experiential amnesia," failing to abstract generalizable strategies from past interactions.
- Existing trajectory-driven or reactive policies often lead to weak, local improvements that struggle to transfer to unseen or novel environments.
- Current methods typically do not explicitly induce transferable embodied heuristics that abstract knowledge beyond individual episodes or specific environmental layouts.

## Method

- A multimodal Imagine-then-Verify planning loop provides robust, short-horizon decision-making, simulating outcomes and validating action plans.
- A Dual-Grain Cognitive Memory system manages experience with Short-term Reflective Memory (SRM) for within-episode reflection and Long-term Principle Memory (LPM) for cross-episode principle abstraction.
- An Autonomous Knowledge Induction (AKI) mechanism continuously distills recurring success and failure patterns from interaction history into reusable, natural-language heuristics for strategy evolution.

## Results

- Robo-Cortex consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines on Image-Goal Navigation, Active Recognition, and Active Embodied Question Answering tasks, achieving **41.26%** SR and **31.66%** SPL on IGNav.
- The adaptive Robo-Cortex++ variant further improved performance, reaching **45.07%** SR and **35.06%** SPL on IGNav through continual online heuristic updating.
- Heuristics autonomously induced from "seen" environments demonstrated effective generalization to "unseen" environments, yielding **48.61%** SR on IGNav and **23.02%** SR on AR, and preliminary real-world tests showed SRM's ability to recover from navigation errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating multimodal decision-making, reflective memory, and cross-episode heuristic induction into a unified framework enables continuous cognitive strategy evolution.
> - Dual-grain memory, encompassing both local trajectory analysis and abstracted principles, facilitates adaptation across short-term and long-term temporal scales.
> - Automatically inducing and consolidating natural-language heuristics from past experiences can generate transferable strategic knowledge that enhances future perception, reasoning, and action.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation is a foundational capability for embodied agents operating in complex, real-world environments. Significant progress has been made in embodied artificial intelligence (AI) through advancements in planning, memory mechanisms, and reasoning capabilities. Existing methods frequently employ trajectory-driven or reactive policies, which may involve imitating successful paths, retrieving episode-specific memories, or selecting actions based on local observations. These approaches often struggle to synthesize generalizable strategies from accumulated past interactions, leading to what the authors term "experiential amnesia." This refers to the inability of an agent to transform specific prior interactions into portable decision knowledge that can generalize effectively across different environments or novel situations.

The broader research landscape for embodied agents includes various paradigms, such as those focusing on preserving scene context, trajectory context, or improving through critique, skill accumulation, or model updates. However, these paradigms typically do not explicitly induce *transferable embodied heuristics* that abstract knowledge beyond individual episodes or specific environmental layouts.

Robo-Cortex positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitation of "experiential amnesia." It proposes a framework that aims to enable agents to evolve their cognition by transforming raw experiences into progressively more effective strategies over time. Unlike methods that primarily emphasize isolated components such as planning, memory, or reflection, Robo-Cortex integrates multimodal decision-making, reflective memory, and cross-episode heuristic induction into a unified system. This approach is intended to facilitate continual strategy evolution, moving beyond mere experience accumulation to the active transformation of experience into reusable decision knowledge. This aligns with a growing interest in self-improving agents that can reflect on their behavior, abstract lessons, and adapt their strategies for future performance, though Robo-Cortex specifically applies this to closed-loop embodied interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for Robo-Cortex stems from a recognized limitation in current embodied AI: the phenomenon of "experiential amnesia." Despite advancements in various aspects of embodied navigation, agents frequently fail to abstract generalizable strategies from their past interactions. This results in behavior improvements that are often weak, local, and struggle to transfer to unseen or novel environments. The authors observe that existing trajectory-driven or reactive policies, while capable of local optimization, do not efficiently convert individual experiences into broadly applicable decision knowledge.

Inspired by human cognitive development, where continuous interaction, reflection, abstraction, and adaptation processes shape and refine cognitive abilities, the authors envision embodied agents that can similarly evolve their strategies. Humans transform raw experiences into reusable knowledge, leading to improved decision-making over time. The core motivation is to equip embodied agents with a comparable capacity: to learn not merely by accumulating experiences, but by transforming these experiences into increasingly effective strategies for perception, reasoning, and action, thereby enabling their cognition to evolve continually.

The key objectives of the Robo-Cortex framework are detailed as follows:

- **Autonomous Heuristic Induction:** To enable robots to autonomously identify and distill recurring patterns of success and failure from their multimodal interaction histories into structured navigation heuristics.
- **Cognitive Strategy Refinement:** To facilitate the continuous refinement of these cognitive strategies through an ongoing reflection-adaptation loop. This involves moving beyond passive execution to active strategy evolution.
- **Knowledge Generalization:** To abstract successful patterns and identify failure pitfalls into natural-language heuristics that are transferable and generalizable across diverse environments, including those not previously encountered.
- **Robust Decision-Making:** To ensure robust real-time decision-making through a multimodal Imagine-then-Verify planning loop that simulates potential outcomes and validates action plans against task goals.
- **Dual-Scale Adaptation:** To support adaptation at both short-term (within-episode) and long-term (cross-episode) temporal scales through a dual-grain cognitive memory system.

These objectives collectively aim to empower embodied agents to transcend the limitations of episode-specific learning and develop a more robust, adaptable, and generalized understanding of navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Robo-Cortex is designed as a self-evolving embodied navigation framework structured around three interconnected components: an Imagine-then-Verify planning loop, a Dual-Grain Cognitive Memory system, and an Autonomous Knowledge Induction (AKI) mechanism. These components are integrated to form a continuous interaction-reflection-conceptualization-adaptation loop for strategy evolution.

#### 3.1. Imagine-then-Verify Planning Loop

This component addresses the challenge of accumulated model error in long-range predictions under partial observability by implementing a short-horizon, closed-loop decision-making process. The agent avoids committing to extended open-loop action sequences, instead performing frequent replanning.

- **Observation and Candidate Generation:** At each time step *t*, the agent observes the current state, denoted as *o_t*. A planner generates a set of *N* task-conditioned candidate plans, *P_t = {p^(1)_t..., p^(N)_t}*. Each candidate plan *p^(i)_t* comprises a low-level action sequence *a^(i)_t* and associated high-level reasoning *r^(i)_t*.
- **Subtask Distribution:** A subtask distributor, *D(·)*, aligns the discrete actions with their corresponding semantic rationales, producing a sequence of semantically grounded subtask units, *S^(i)_t = D(a^(i)_t, r^(i)_t)*. This ensures interpretability and semantic grounding for each action.
- **World Model Imagination:** For each semantically grounded candidate *S^(i)_t*, a world model, *W(·)*, predicts a short future rollout of visual outcomes over a horizon *h*, denoted as *ˆo^(i)_t+1:t+h = W(o_t, S^(i)_t)*.
- **Vision-Language Evaluation and Selection:** A vision-language evaluator then assesses these imagined future rollouts. It scores each candidate based on its expected progress towards the task goal *g*. The plan with the highest score is selected for execution: *p*^∗*t = arg max_{p^(i)_t ∈P_t} Score(ˆo^(i)_t+1:t+h, g)*.
- **Execution and Replanning:** The selected decision is executed, the agent's state is updated, and the planning loop is re-entered. This closed-loop design allows for continuous revision of decisions as new observations become available.

#### 3.2. Dual-Grain Cognitive Memory

Robo-Cortex manages experience through a structured memory graph *G = (V, E)*, designed to support reasoning at two complementary granularities. The node set *V* includes root nodes (representing episodes), trajectory nodes (representing decision steps), and subtask nodes (representing executed semantic units). The edge set *E* contains hierarchical parent-child links and temporal links between consecutive subtasks, enabling both local trajectory analysis and cross-episode memory consolidation.

- **Short-term Reflective Memory (SRM):**
 - **Operation:** SRM functions over a sliding window of recent subtasks, *W_t = {v_{sub, t-w+1}..., v_{sub, t}}*, within a single episode.
 - **Analysis:** An SRM analyzer, *f_srm*, summarizes this local trajectory segment into a reflective cue, *s_SRM_t*. This summary captures local progress, identifies emerging failure patterns, and provides ongoing subgoal context.
 - **Application:** This reflective cue is appended to the planner's context for subsequent decisions within the same episode. This mechanism promotes short-term behavioral continuity, helps the agent avoid repetitive ineffective actions, and allows for the continuation of partially completed approaches without a full strategy replan.
- **Long-term Principle Memory (LPM):**
 - **Construction:** LPM is built after each episode concludes. For every subtask within an episode, Robo-Cortex traverses the temporal graph forward to collect the downstream trajectory *τ_i = {v_{sub, i}, v_{sub, i+1}..., v_{sub, i+k}}*, where *k* is a configured horizon.
 - **Principle Abstraction:** A principle analyzer converts these trajectories into either guiding principles (for successful episodes) or cautionary principles (for failed episodes).
 - **Retrieval:** During operation, Robo-Cortex retrieves relevant prior subtasks by performing nearest-neighbor matching in a goal-conditioned state embedding space, retaining matches above a threshold *δ_lpm*. LPM provides not just episodic trajectories, but also principle-level abstractions distilled from past successes and failures, offering transferable strategic knowledge rather than mere scene-specific action replays.
- **Memory Consolidation:** To maintain an efficient and informative memory, Robo-Cortex performs goal-wise consolidation. Successful episodes are prioritized, with shorter, more efficient successful trajectories being preferred. If no successful episodes are available for a given goal, diverse failed episodes are preserved to document complementary failure modes for future principle induction.

#### 3.3. Autonomous Knowledge Induction (AKI)

AKI is the mechanism that drives the self-evolving capability of Robo-Cortex, abstracting accumulated interaction history into reusable and generalized behavioral heuristics that are fed back into future reflection and planning. This allows Robo-Cortex to convert episodic traces into transferable strategic knowledge.

- **Heuristic Extraction:** After each episode, a knowledge agent loads the corresponding structured trajectory from the memory graph. A vision-language heuristic extractor identifies recurring success and failure patterns from this multimodal data.
- **Heuristic Representation:** Each extracted heuristic *h* is represented as a tuple: *(ρ, d, a, c, y)*, where *ρ* is a pattern identifier, *d* is a natural-language problem description, *a* is a recommended strategy, *c ∈* is a confidence score, and *y ∈ {success, failure}* indicates its derivation source.
- **Knowledge Consolidation:** As more episodes accumulate, extracted heuristics are merged into a shared knowledge base. They are grouped by pattern identifier and can be further refined by semantic similarity of their descriptions and strategies. A merger model then produces a generalized heuristic *˜h_C = (ρ, ˜d, ˜a, ˜c)* for each cluster *C*, where *˜d* and *˜a* are merged descriptions and strategies, and *˜c* is the aggregated confidence.
- **Feedback Loop:** High-confidence, recurrent heuristics are provided as structured guidance to the online system for short-term reflective analysis (SRM) and planning. These heuristics act as priors on likely failure modes and promising behaviors, improving the agent's interpretation of recent interaction history and the generation of informative reflective summaries. This closes the self-evolving loop: experience leads to abstracted knowledge, which then enhances future behavior. AKI differentiates itself from LPM by inducing general heuristics applicable across episodes, rather than retrieving specific past experiences.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

Simulation experiments were conducted using Habitat-sim. Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct-AWQ served as the vision-language backbone for planning, memory analysis, and AKI. Wan2.1-I2V-A14B-480P-Diffusers was used as the world-model imagination backbone. For efficiency, planning and memory modules were deployed as separate vLLM services, sharing backbone weights. The navigation action space was discretized into 0.20 m forward motion and 22.5° left/right rotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Robo-Cortex was conducted on three embodied tasks: Image-Goal Navigation (IGNav), Active Recognition (AR), and Active Embodied Question Answering (AEQA). These tasks were performed in Habitat-sim environments, utilizing HM3D for IGNav and AEQA, and MP3D for AR. Performance was measured using Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), Mean Trajectory Length, and Answer Score (for AEQA). The framework was evaluated in two configurations: Robo-Cortex (static, with fixed heuristic guidance) and Robo-Cortex++ (adaptive, with online heuristic updating).

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

Robo-Cortex consistently outperformed three strong baselines—MapGPT, WMNav, and World-In-World—across all three embodied tasks (Table 1).

- **Static Robo-Cortex:** Achieved the strongest overall performance.
 - **IGNav:** 41.26% SR and 31.66% SPL, representing improvements of +2.69% SR and +4.16% SPL over the strongest prior method.
 - **AR:** Achieved the highest SR (22.39%) and the shortest mean trajectory length (6.97).
 - **AEQA:** Recorded the best Answer Score (29.78) and shortest mean trajectory length (17.34).
 These results indicate improvements in task success, exploration quality, and decision efficiency.

- **Adaptive Robo-Cortex++:** Demonstrated further gains due to continual self-evolution through online heuristic updating.
 - **IGNav:** Improved to 45.07% SR and 35.06% SPL.
 - **AR:** Increased to 23.88% SR.
 - **AEQA:** Reached 30.59 Answer Score and 25.57% SPL.
 This variant showcased the capacity of the framework to continually refine its strategy during inference.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation study investigated the contributions of the Dual-Grain Cognitive Memory and heuristic transfer capabilities on separate "seen" and "unseen" evaluation splits. A "Basic Pipeline" served as a baseline, including only core planning, world-model imagination, evaluation, and execution components without SRM, LPM, or AKI.

- **Seen Split (Memory Accumulation Study - Table 2):**
 - **SRM:** Provided immediate gains in the first round (e.g., IGNav SR increased from 36.11% to 40.18%, AEQA Answer Score from 25.55 to 30.83), highlighting its role in within-episode reflection and failure recovery.
 - **LPM:** Its benefits became more substantial with accumulated experience. While modest in Round 2, LPM showed stronger improvements by Round 3 (e.g., IGNav SR reaching 43.06%). This supports LPM's role in providing cross-episode strategic guidance.
 - **Full Model (+LPM & SRM):** Consistently yielded the strongest overall results, confirming the complementary nature of the two memory components. In Round 3, it achieved 44.29% SR on IGNav, 24.03% SR on AR, and 31.71 Answer Score on AEQA.

- **Unseen Split (Heuristic Transfer Study - Table 3):**
 - **Manual Heuristics:** Offered a useful prior for IGNav (improving SR from 34.72% to 38.89%) but showed less consistent benefits across AR and AEQA.
 - **Transferred Heuristics:** Heuristics induced from the "seen" split generalized effectively to unseen environments. The "w/ transferred heur." variant achieved the best IGNav performance (48.61% SR) and highest AR success rate (23.02%) without further online updating. This indicates that AKI captures portable success and failure patterns.
 - **Online Updating on Unseen Split:** The effect of continued online updating on the unseen split was nuanced. While "w/ transferred heur. + update" generally outperformed the Basic Pipeline, it did not consistently surpass the fixed "w/ transferred heur." setting. This suggests that strong transferred heuristics provide a high-quality initial prior, and aggressive online rewriting on limited unseen episodes might sometimes overwrite beneficial guidance. AKI's primary value was identified as supplying compact strategic priors that complement online adaptation.

- **Round-wise Update Dynamics on IGNav (Figure 4):**
 - **Transferred Heuristics:** Provided the strongest initial prior (41.67% SR, 34.84% SPL in Round 1) on unseen split, demonstrating immediate utility. However, performance plateaued relatively quickly.
 - **From-scratch Updating:** Showed more steady improvement over rounds, eventually surpassing initial transferred heuristics performance (36.62% SR in Round 1 to 51.39% in Round 5).
 - **On-split Heuristics:** Achieved the strongest long-term performance, with a notable sharp improvement after Round 3 (rising from 38.89% SR in Round 1 to 55.56% SR in Round 5). This suggests that sufficient target-split experience enables significantly more effective induced heuristics.
 These dynamics illustrate a trade-off between rapid initial transfer from generalized priors and stronger long-term adaptation through continued learning on the target domain.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiment

A preliminary real-world study was conducted for image-goal navigation in indoor environments (living room, supermarket).

- **Basic Pipeline:** Succeeded on 4 out of 10 goals, indicating transferability of the core Imagine-then-Verify pipeline to physical settings, but also its vulnerability to appearance ambiguity and local drift.
- **With SRM (using simulation-derived heuristics):** Demonstrated recovery capabilities. In cases where the robot lost alignment with key visual cues and began to drift, SRM detected this misalignment, identified the last known goal-consistent region, and redirected the robot to recover, leading to successful task completion. This suggests that the failure modes addressed by Robo-Cortex are relevant in real-world scenarios and that the navigation heuristics remain useful in physical environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Robo-Cortex framework represents a notable advancement in the field of embodied AI by reframing autonomous navigation from a problem of isolated action selection to one of continual strategy evolution. Its primary significance lies in addressing the challenge of "experiential amnesia," where agents struggle to generalize effective decision strategies from past interactions.

The key contributions and their potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Unified Self-Evolving Framework:** Robo-Cortex integrates three distinct yet synergistic components—Imagine-then-Verify planning, Dual-Grain Cognitive Memory, and Autonomous Knowledge Induction—into a cohesive system. This unification fosters a continuous interaction-reflection-conceptualization-adaptation loop, which enables the agent to progressively improve its cognitive strategies over time. This approach offers a paradigm shift from static policy learning to dynamic, adaptive intelligence in embodied agents.
- **Enhanced Decision-Making Robustness:** The Imagine-then-Verify planning loop, with its short-horizon, closed-loop design and multimodal evaluation, provides a robust mechanism for real-time decision-making. This reduces susceptibility to accumulated errors common in long open-loop planning, leading to more reliable navigation.
- **Dual-Grain Memory for Comprehensive Learning:** The Dual-Grain Cognitive Memory system effectively supports learning at different temporal scales. Short-term Reflective Memory (SRM) facilitates immediate, within-episode reflection and recovery from errors, while Long-term Principle Memory (LPM) abstracts cross-episode guiding and cautionary principles. This dual approach ensures both tactical responsiveness and strategic accumulation of wisdom.
- **Autonomous Knowledge Induction for Generalization:** The Autonomous Knowledge Induction (AKI) mechanism is central to Robo-Cortex's self-evolving capability. By distilling recurring success and failure patterns into natural-language heuristics, AKI enables the agent to learn portable strategic knowledge. The strong transfer performance of these induced heuristics to unseen environments demonstrates that Robo-Cortex can capture generalizable principles rather than merely benchmark-specific behaviors. This capability is crucial for deploying robust agents in novel and unpredictable real-world settings.
- **Demonstrated Performance and Adaptability:** Extensive evaluations on IGNav, AR, and AEQA tasks show that Robo-Cortex consistently outperforms strong baselines in terms of task success, exploration efficiency, and generalization. The adaptive Robo-Cortex++ variant further highlights the potential for sustained improvement through online memory and heuristic updating during inference, suggesting a pathway for agents to continuously learn and refine their strategies throughout their operational lifetime.
- **Validation in Real-World Settings:** Preliminary real-world robotic experiments, particularly the demonstration of SRM's ability to facilitate recovery from navigation errors in physical environments, suggest that the principles and heuristics learned in simulation hold practical utility and are not confined to the simulated domain. This indicates a promising step towards robust real-world robotic deployment.

The potential impact of Robo-Cortex extends to developing more intelligent, autonomous, and adaptable robotic systems. By fostering "cognitive evolution," this work could lead to agents capable of learning complex tasks more efficiently, generalizing their skills to diverse new environments, and operating more reliably in dynamic and partially observable real-world scenarios. This has implications for various applications, including autonomous exploration, service robotics, and human-robot interaction in unstructured environments.

However, the authors acknowledge limitations. The real-world validation is preliminary and limited in scale, necessitating broader physical experiments for comprehensive robustness assessment. Furthermore, while the framework is designed to be model-agnostic, Robo-Cortex has not been systematically evaluated with a diverse range of vision-language backbones or world models, presenting an area for future investigation.
