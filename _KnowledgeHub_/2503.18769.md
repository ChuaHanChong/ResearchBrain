# AlphaSpace: Enabling Robotic Actions through Semantic Tokenization and Symbolic Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Menlo Research's AlphaSpace enables Large Language Models to perform precise 3D spatial reasoning for robotic manipulation through semantic tokenization and symbolic reasoning. The approach achieved 66.67% total accuracy on the EmbodiedBench Manipulation Subtask, surpassing GPT-4o's 37.5% and Claude 3.5 Sonnet's 29.17%.

## Problem

- Existing vision-centric and end-to-end robotic models are computationally expensive and require vast datasets.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with fine-grained 3D spatial reasoning and precise object manipulation due to reliance on implicit visual features.
- High-dimensional control spaces in robotic arms make existing methods difficult to scale efficiently and adapt to minor task variations.

## Method

- **AlphaSpace** generates a ==synthetic dataset== of 3D object configurations and corresponding 7-dimensional action plans with explicit reasoning annotations.
- Employs an enhanced ==semantic tokenization== strategy to represent 3D spatial information, including object locations (x,y,z) and orientations.
- Fine-tunes a lightweight ==decoder-only LLM== (==DeepSeek-R1-distil-Qwen-1.5B==) using ==supervised learning== on the tokenized data.

## Results

- Achieved a total accuracy of **66.67%** on the EmbodiedBench Manipulation Subtask, a benchmark for multimodal LLMs in embodied agents.
- Outperformed leading general-purpose LLMs, with GPT-4o at **37.5%** and Claude 3.5 Sonnet at **29.17%** accuracy.
- Demonstrated an **83.3%** success rate on "picking" tasks and a **50%** success rate on more complex "stacking" tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoder-only LLMs can effectively perform 3D spatial reasoning for robotic manipulation when provided with explicitly structured semantic tokens.
> - Specialized training on synthetic data with detailed reasoning annotations allows LLMs to learn precise 3D object placement and manipulation.
> - Explicit spatial encoding through tokenization offers a computationally efficient and potentially more interpretable alternative to implicit visual feature extraction for embodied AI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of AI and robotics is rapidly advancing, with Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) playing increasingly central roles. AlphaSpace positions itself within this dynamic landscape by addressing a critical challenge: enabling LLMs to perform precise 3D spatial reasoning and robotic manipulation effectively and efficiently.

Traditionally, robotic manipulation and spatial understanding in AI have relied heavily on vision-centric approaches. These include:
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Models like GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet, which excel at interpreting visual scenes and responding to natural language queries. However, as the paper points out, they often "struggle with fine-grained spatial reasoning and object manipulation in 3D environments" because they "rely on implicit visual features rather than explicitly structured spatial representations." This can lead to inconsistencies in precise tasks and limits generalization due to a lack of dedicated spatial priors.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** More specialized models (e.g., OpenVLA, Kim et al.) that are trained on vast datasets of robot episodes to directly predict robotic arm joint angles from visual inputs. While powerful, these models are computationally expensive, require extensive training data, and often face challenges with adaptability to minor task variations and scaling efficiently to more complex tasks due to their high-dimensional control space.
- **General-Purpose Robot Foundation Models:** Emerging models like DeepMind's Gemini Robotics (2025) and π0 (Black et al.), which aim to leverage LLM reasoning abilities for complex robotic tasks and exhibit zero-shot capabilities. These models represent a significant step toward more adaptable and generalist robots but still often depend on substantial visual and action data.

AlphaSpace carves out a distinct niche by proposing an alternative paradigm: **explicit semantic tokenization and symbolic reasoning for 3D spatial understanding**, rather than relying primarily on high-dimensional visual embeddings or end-to-end learning of joint angles. The paper builds on a growing body of work that highlights the potential of decoder-based models to perform spatial reasoning through structured tokenization (e.g., Wu et al., Yamada et al., Li et al.). By converting 3D environments and robotic actions into structured symbolic tokens, AlphaSpace aims to provide LLMs with a more direct and interpretable way to reason about spatial relationships.

This approach offers several advantages:
- **Computational Efficiency:** By reducing reliance on computationally intensive visual encoders and large VLA models, AlphaSpace aims to be a "lightweight yet effective" solution.
- **Precision and Interpretability:** Explicitly encoding spatial semantics, including height, allows for more precise manipulation and potentially more interpretable reasoning processes compared to implicit visual feature extraction.
- **Generalizability:** Structured symbolic reasoning can potentially lead to better generalization across diverse manipulation settings, as the model learns to infer logical relationships rather than just memorizing visual patterns.

In essence, AlphaSpace contributes to the ongoing effort to imbue AI systems with robust spatial intelligence, offering a unique, token-based perspective that complements and, in some cases, aims to surpass the capabilities of prevailing vision-heavy approaches for specific fine-grained manipulation tasks. It explores the frontier of how LLMs can directly understand and operate in 3D physical spaces without a continuous, high-fidelity visual stream, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with symbolic representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AlphaSpace is to **substantially improve the spatial reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) for robotic manipulation in 3D Cartesian space**. Specifically, it aims to enable LLMs to accurately manipulate objects by positioning them at precise (x, y, z) coordinates.

This core objective is driven by several key motivations, stemming from the limitations of existing approaches in embodied AI:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Robotics Models:**
 - **Computational Cost:** Many state-of-the-art robotics models, including generally pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, are "computationally expensive" during both training and inference. They require vast datasets and significant computational resources. AlphaSpace seeks a "lightweight yet effective approach."
 - **Lack of Fine-Grained Spatial Reasoning:** While VLMs excel at general scene interpretation, they "struggle with fine-grained spatial reasoning and object manipulation in 3D environments." They often rely on implicit visual features, which leads to "inconsistencies when performing precise object placement or navigation tasks" and limits their ability to generalize without "dedicated spatial priors."
 - **Adaptability and Generalization Hurdles:** Approaches that directly predict robotic arm joint angles from visual inputs often face "substantial hurdles" in adaptability. Minor variations in task conditions can degrade performance, and the "high-dimensional control space of robotic arms makes it difficult to scale these methods efficiently to more complex tasks."

- **Leveraging the Power of Structured Tokenization:** The motivation for AlphaSpace also comes from the proven success of semantic tokenization in 2D spatial reasoning. The authors' previous work, AlphaMaze (Dao and Vu), demonstrated that "semantics tokenization strategy has demonstrated that it can provide decoder models with sufficient information to reason about coordinates and spatial attributes in 2D spaces." This suggests that by extending this principle to 3D, a similar, if not greater, benefit could be achieved.

- **Enabling Explicit and Interpretable Spatial Understanding:** By encoding spatial semantics explicitly, AlphaSpace aims to allow models to achieve "more structured and interpretable spatial understanding." This contrasts with the 'black-box' nature of some end-to-end learning approaches, offering potential for better debugging and analysis of the model's reasoning process.

In summary, AlphaSpace is motivated by the need for a more efficient, precise, and interpretable method for LLMs to understand and interact with 3D physical environments, moving beyond the limitations of purely vision-based or high-dimensional control prediction models by leveraging a novel, semantics-based tokenization and symbolic reasoning strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AlphaSpace's methodology is built upon a two-stage synthetic dataset generation process, followed by model training using a decoder-only architecture and an enhanced semantic tokenization strategy. This approach is explicitly inspired by their previous work, AlphaMaze, and targets robotic manipulation within a tabletop environment, specifically designed to be evaluated on the EmbodiedBench benchmark.

#### 3.1. Synthetic Dataset Generation

The core of AlphaSpace's learning process relies on a synthetically generated dataset, comprising "object configurations" and their corresponding "action plans."

- **Object Configurations:**
 - **Environment:** A planar tabletop workspace is discretized into a 100x100 grid. To support hierarchical reasoning, this fine grid is overlaid with a coarser 25x25 grid, where each coarse cell contains a 4x4 subgrid.
 - **Object Representation:** An object's location is defined by a tuple (r_g, c_g, r_l, c_l), specifying global (coarse grid) and local (fine subgrid) row/column indices.
 - **Attributes:** Objects have (color, shape) attributes. Colors are selected from 19 distinct options, and shapes include cube, cylinder, triangular prism, star, moon, or container.
 - **Spatial Constraints:** To ensure realism and diversity, non-overlapping objects are enforced (minimum 4-unit Euclidean distance between centers), objects are uniformly spatially distributed, and variable heights are assigned (uniformly sampled from). This introduction of height (z-coordinate) is crucial for 3D reasoning.
 - **Task Categories:** The dataset includes three types of manipulation tasks:
 1. **Placement:** Pick an object and place it into a container.
 2. **Stacking:** Position one object on top of another.
 3. **Movement:** Relocate an object to specific coordinates.
 - **Scene Complexity:** Environments contain 4-7 objects, including those necessary for the task and additional "distractor" objects to increase complexity.
 - **Uniqueness Constraints:** For a subset of the data, object uniqueness constraints were applied to prevent the model from learning spurious correlations.

- **Action Plan Generation:**
 - For each object configuration, a corresponding "action plan" is generated, outlining the sequence of robot actions required to achieve the manipulation goal.
 - **Action Encoding:** Each individual action is encoded as a 7-dimensional vector:
 - (r_g, c_g): Global position in the 25x25 grid.
 - (r_l, c_l): Local position in the 4x4 subgrid.
 - Z-axis height: Vertical distance from gripper to table surface.
 - Roll, Pitch, Yaw: Orientation (each ranging 0-120, 3 degrees per unit).
 - Gripper state: 0 for closed, 1 for open.
 - **Action Sequence:** A typical manipulation sequence consists of seven predefined steps: approach above source, lower to object, close gripper, lift, move above target, lower to placement, open gripper.
 - **Reasoning Annotations:** Crucially, each sequence is accompanied by "explicit reasoning annotations" that detail the spatial problem-solving strategy. This includes object localization (identifying relevant objects' positions and properties) and action planning (step-by-step breakdown). These annotations serve as vital "supervision signals" for training the model to link perception, spatial reasoning, and action planning.
 - **Dataset Size:** The final dataset comprises approximately 260,000 synthetic samples: 100,000 placement, 120,000 stacking, and 40,000 movement tasks.

#### 3.2. Model Training Pipeline

- **Architecture:** A decoder-only architecture is used, specifically based on **DeepSeek-R1-distil-Qwen-1.5B**. This choice is significant as it demonstrates that a model without explicit 3D geometric encoders or vision modules can perform 3D spatial reasoning when provided with structured tokens.
- **Training Method:** The model is trained using **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** on the synthetically generated reasoning dataset. This process enables AlphaSpace to learn spatial relationships and object manipulation tasks by mapping tokenized environmental states and natural language instructions to precise action sequences.
- **Training Details:** A learning rate of 1.0 x 10^-4 with a cosine decay schedule and a warm-up ratio of 0.1 was used. Training was conducted on 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs, with a batch size of 16 samples per device and a maximum context length of 4096 tokens.

#### 3.3. Evaluation

- **Benchmark:** AlphaSpace is evaluated using **EmbodiedBench (Yang et al.)**, a benchmark for multimodal LLMs in embodied agents.
- **Specific Subset:** The focus is on the **EB-Manipulation subset**, specifically the "spatial tasks category," which includes pick-and-place scenarios. The evaluation set consists of 12 pick tasks and 12 place tasks, totaling 24 actions.
- **Baselines:** AlphaSpace's performance is compared against leading commercial LLMs: **GPT-4o** and **Claude 3.5 Sonnet**, with their performance re-run on the specified benchmark subset for direct comparison.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that AlphaSpace is trained on a rich, structured dataset that explicitly encodes 3D spatial information and reasoning steps, allowing a lightweight LLM to learn complex manipulation tasks that typically require more computationally intensive vision-based approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the EmbodiedBench Manipulation Subtask clearly demonstrate AlphaSpace's superior performance compared to leading general-purpose LLMs.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

The model used for AlphaSpace is based on **DeepSeek-R1-distil-Qwen-1.5B**, a decoder-only architecture. It was fine-tuned using supervised learning on the custom synthetic dataset described in the methodology, which includes 260,000 samples of 3D object manipulation tasks. The training utilized 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs, with a batch size of 16 and a context length of 4096 tokens.

For evaluation, AlphaSpace was tested against GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet on a specific subset of the EmbodiedBench Manipulation Subtask. This subset consists of 24 actions: 12 "picking" tasks and 12 "stacking" tasks (the paper uses "picking" and "stacking" in the table and text, which appears to be a more specific breakdown of the "pick and place" described in the methodology). The success rate for each task category and overall accuracy were measured.

#### 4.2. Evaluation Results

The quantitative results are summarized as follows:

| Model | Picking (Successes/Total) | Stacking (Successes/Total) | Total Accuracy (%) |
| :------------------- | :------------------------ | :------------------------- | :----------------- |
| **AlphaSpace (Ours)** | **10/12** | **6/12** | **66.67%** |
| GPT-4o | 6/12 | 3/12 | 37.5% |
| Claude 3.5 Sonnet | 5/12 | 2/12 | 29.17% |

Key observations from these results include:

- **Significant Outperformance:** AlphaSpace achieved a total accuracy of 66.67%, which is a substantial improvement over GPT-4o (37.5%) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (29.17%). This performance gap highlights the effectiveness of AlphaSpace's specialized training approach and its structured spatial encoding.
- **Strong Picking Performance:** AlphaSpace demonstrated particularly strong performance in "picking" tasks, successfully completing 10 out of 12 (83.3% success rate). This indicates a robust ability to accurately identify and initiate interaction with target objects.
- **Challenging Stacking Tasks:** While outperforming baselines, AlphaSpace's success rate on "stacking" tasks was lower (6 out of 12, or 50%). This suggests that "compositional manipulation tasks," which require precise sequential actions and understanding of object relationships for stable placement, remain more challenging even for this specialized model.
- **Advantages of Specialized Training:** The results underscore the benefits of a specialized training approach involving synthetic data generation and explicit reasoning supervision. General-purpose LLMs, despite their vast pre-training, struggle with the precise spatial reasoning required for these embodied manipulation tasks when relying solely on their inherent capabilities or implicitly learned visual features.

In essence, the findings validate AlphaSpace's core hypothesis: that structured semantic tokenization combined with symbolic reasoning can effectively empower decoder-only LLMs to perform 3D spatial reasoning for robotic manipulation, surpassing the capabilities of leading general-purpose multimodal models on specific fine-grained tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AlphaSpace introduces a significant advancement in empowering Large Language Models with 3D spatial reasoning for robotic manipulation. Its approach, centered on semantic tokenization and symbolic reasoning, carries several implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Significance and Strengths

1. **Enabling 3D Spatial Reasoning in Decoder-Only LLMs:** The most significant contribution is the empirical demonstration that a decoder-only LLM, without reliance on explicit 3D geometric encoders or complex vision modules, can effectively reason in a 3D Cartesian space. By encoding height (z-coordinate) information through enhanced semantic tokens, AlphaSpace bypasses the traditional reliance on computationally expensive vision-based embeddings (like those used in VLMs or VLAs). This is a paradigm shift, proving that "structured tokenization" can directly represent spatial semantics for precise and efficient reasoning about object relationships.
2. **Computational Efficiency and Lightweight Approach:** By minimizing the need for heavy visual processing, AlphaSpace presents itself as a "lightweight yet effective alternative for robotic manipulation tasks." This has a profound impact on deployment scenarios, potentially enabling robotics with lower computational footprints and reducing the barrier to entry for developing spatial reasoning capabilities.
3. **Enhanced Generalizability through Symbolic Reasoning:** The reliance on structured symbolic reasoning data helps the model learn a more fundamental and structured form of spatial reasoning. This is evident in its superior performance on embodied manipulation tasks, suggesting that it can infer correct object placements and transformations in novel spatial arrangements more effectively than end-to-end visual learning approaches. This could lead to more robust and adaptable robotic systems.
4. **Interpretability:** Directly representing spatial semantics through tokens can make the model's decision-making process more interpretable, as one can potentially trace the logical steps derived from the structured input, unlike the often opaque nature of implicit visual feature extraction.

#### 5.2. Limitations

Despite its strengths, AlphaSpace has notable limitations that also highlight avenues for future work:

1. **Static vs. Dynamic Environments:** The reliance on pre-tokenized spatial representations makes AlphaSpace less suitable for "highly dynamic environments where real-time sensory feedback is crucial." Unlike VLMs that continuously process visual input, AlphaSpace would struggle with rapidly changing scenarios, occlusions, or unpredictable external forces without a hybrid approach.
2. **Assumption of Structured Environments:** The current experimental setup assumes controlled object placements and clear spatial references. Real-world complexity, including complex object geometries or ambiguous scenes, would likely pose significant challenges.
3. **Scalability to Complex Robotic Scenarios:** While effective for tabletop object manipulation, the scalability of AlphaSpace to more intricate multi-step assembly tasks or dynamic obstacle avoidance remains an open question.
4. **Lack of Reinforcement Learning Integration:** The model does not leverage recent advancements in reinforcement learning for LLMs, which have proven effective in improving the accuracy of decoder models for spatial reasoning tasks with visual components. This suggests a missed opportunity for further performance gains.
5. **Incomplete Evaluation Scope:** The project concluded prematurely, covering only two out of four planned task categories from the EB-Manipulation benchmark. This limits the comprehensive assessment of AlphaSpace's full capabilities and leaves gaps in understanding its performance across the broader spectrum of embodied spatial tasks.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact and Future Directions

The work paves the way for more structured and efficient spatial reasoning in robotics and AI, reducing reliance on expensive vision-based encoders and high-dimensional control models. The potential impacts include:

- **More Accessible Robotics:** By reducing computational demands, AlphaSpace's principles could make advanced robotic manipulation more accessible for systems with limited processing power.
- **Enhanced LLM Capabilities:** It pushes the boundary of what LLMs can do in the physical world, suggesting new architectures and training paradigms for embodied AI.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** A more explicit spatial understanding could lead to more intuitive and precise human-robot collaboration, as robots better understand and execute commands related to object placement and movement.

Future research directions suggested by the authors include:

1. **Integration of Reinforcement Learning:** Incorporating RL-based fine-tuning could enable real-time decision-making and adaptation to unexpected scenarios, addressing the limitation of static reasoning.
2. **Hybrid Modeling:** Combining AlphaSpace with "lightweight vision modules" could bridge the gap between symbolic reasoning and real-world perception, enhancing adaptability without incurring the full computational cost of traditional VLMs.
3. **Dynamic Spatial Transformations:** Expanding the tokenization approach to support dynamic object properties (e.g., rotation, deformation) would be crucial for more complex real-world tasks that involve continuous adjustments.
4. **Real-World Deployment:** The ultimate goal is to move beyond synthetic environments and explore real-world deployment and integration with multimodal systems to validate its robustness and practical utility.

In conclusion, AlphaSpace represents a compelling step forward in the quest to imbue LLMs with robust spatial intelligence, offering a computationally efficient and highly structured approach that could redefine how AI systems understand and interact with the 3D physical world. While current limitations exist, the proposed future directions suggest a clear path toward bridging the gap between symbolic reasoning and complex embodied AI applications.
