# Back to Basics: Let Denoising Generative Models Denoise

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT propose a re-evaluation of denoising diffusion models, demonstrating that directly predicting clean data ("x-prediction") with a minimalist "Just image Transformers" (JiT) architecture overcomes catastrophic failures in high-dimensional pixel spaces. This approach achieved competitive image generation quality on ImageNet, reaching an FID of 1.82 at 256x256 without reliance on complex components like latent tokenizers or pre-training.

## Problem

- Denoising diffusion models commonly predict noise or a combination of noise and data, which may be suboptimal for high-dimensional pixel spaces.
- High-dimensional pixel-space diffusion models often face challenges such as catastrophic failures or a need for complex architectures, latent tokenizers, or extensive pre-training.
- Many state-of-the-art generative models rely on multi-stage pipelines and auxiliary losses, making them less self-contained and harder to generalize across domains.

## Method

- The authors advocate for directly predicting clean data ("x-prediction") in denoising ==diffusion== models, supported by the classical manifold hypothesis.
- They introduce "Just image Transformers" (**JiT**), a minimalist ==Vision Transformer== architecture that operates directly on raw pixel patches, eschewing tokenizers, pre-training, or auxiliary losses.
- A systematic empirical comparison of "x", "epsilon", and "v" prediction targets across various loss spaces and network capacities was conducted to validate the proposed approach.

## Results

- "X-prediction" successfully prevented catastrophic failures observed with "epsilon"- and "v"-prediction methods on ImageNet 256x256, where it yielded good FID scores (e.g., 8.62) while others failed (FID > 300).
- The JiT-G/16 model achieved an FID of 1.82 on ImageNet 256x256, outperforming or matching many complex latent diffusion models (e.g., DiT-XL/2: 2.27, SiT-XL/2: 2.06) without additional components.
- JiT models demonstrated strong scalability, achieving FID 1.78 on ImageNet 512x512 and FID 4.82 on 1024x1024, by proportionally scaling patch sizes to maintain sequence length and computational cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting on-manifold clean data ("x") is more robust and inherently easier for neural networks than predicting off-manifold quantities (noise or "v") in high-dimensional input spaces.
> - The "x-prediction" strategy effectively mitigates the "curse of dimensionality" for ViT-based pixel-space diffusion models, allowing them to function stably on raw, high-dimensional inputs.
> - A simplified, self-contained "Diffusion + Transformer" framework, operating solely on pixels, can achieve competitive image generation performance without requiring complex external components.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is strategically positioned within the highly active and rapidly evolving landscape of generative AI, specifically focusing on denoising diffusion models and Vision Transformers (ViTs).

**Evolution of Diffusion Models:** Diffusion models have emerged as the state-of-the-art for image synthesis, surpassing Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in quality and diversity. Their genesis involved the idea of learning a reverse diffusion process by "denoising" corrupted images. However, the paper points out a critical historical deviation: early popularization of diffusion models, notably DDPMs, pivoted to predicting the *noise* itself (`ϵ-prediction`), followed by `v-prediction` which combines data and noise. Today, `ϵ`- or `v`-prediction are the dominant paradigms. This paper directly challenges this status quo by questioning whether these predictions are truly optimal, particularly in high-dimensional settings.

**The Manifold Hypothesis and Data Representation:** The work explicitly harkens back to the classical "manifold assumption" in machine learning, which posits that natural high-dimensional data (like images) lie on a lower-dimensional manifold embedded within the higher-dimensional ambient space. This fundamental principle has historically underpinned various representation learning techniques, including Denoising Autoencoders (DAEs). By re-emphasizing this, the paper connects modern, complex diffusion models back to a foundational concept, suggesting that the choice of prediction target should be informed by the data's intrinsic structure.

**Latent Diffusion Models vs. Pixel-Space Diffusion:** The current dominant approach for high-resolution image generation is Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs), such as Stable Diffusion. LDMs first learn a compressed latent representation using an autoencoder (a "tokenizer") and then perform diffusion in this lower-dimensional latent space. While effective, this approach introduces an additional complex component (the VAE or tokenizer), often trained with auxiliary losses (adversarial, perceptual), making the overall system less "self-contained." Pixel-space diffusion, which operates directly on raw pixels, offers greater generality but has traditionally struggled with high resolutions due to the "curse of dimensionality" and computational expense, often relying on dense convolutional U-Net architectures or complex hierarchical ViT designs. This paper aims to make pixel-space diffusion viable and competitive without these complex additions, promoting a "self-contained" paradigm.

**Vision Transformers in Generative Modeling:** Following the success of Vision Transformers (ViTs) in discriminative tasks, models like Diffusion Transformers (DiT) adapted ViTs for diffusion. DiT applied Transformers to *latent tokens* generated by a VAE, achieving impressive results. This paper takes the idea a step further: "Just image Transformers" (JiT) applies plain ViTs directly to *pixel patches*, a much higher-dimensional input. It addresses the reported challenges of ViT-style diffusion models degrading rapidly in high-dimensional input spaces, often necessitating self-supervised pre-training or large hidden sizes in prior works.

**Minimalism and Generalization:** The authors explicitly advocate for a minimalist design, rejecting tokenizers, pre-training, adversarial losses, or perceptual losses. This stands in contrast to many state-of-the-art generative models that incorporate numerous auxiliary components and training strategies. By stripping down the complexity, the paper seeks to uncover more fundamental principles and enable a "Diffusion + Transformer" paradigm that is self-contained and widely applicable beyond computer vision, especially to domains (e.g., biological sequences, climate data) where domain-specific tokenizers are hard to design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's core objectives are driven by a belief that the current dominant practices in denoising diffusion models overlook a fundamental principle regarding data representation, especially when dealing with high-dimensional natural data.

1. **Re-evaluate the Prediction Target in Diffusion Models:** The primary objective is to challenge the widely adopted `ϵ`-prediction and `v`-prediction strategies in diffusion models. The authors propose that directly predicting the clean image (`x-prediction`) is a more principled and effective approach, particularly in high-dimensional pixel spaces.

2. **Leverage the Manifold Assumption:** The central motivation stems from the classical manifold hypothesis. The authors hypothesize that natural data points reside on a low-dimensional manifold, while noise or noised quantities (`ϵ` or `v`) are distributed across the full high-dimensional space. Therefore, asking a neural network to predict `x` (on-manifold) should be an inherently easier task, requiring less capacity, than predicting `ϵ` or `v` (off-manifold). This forms the theoretical cornerstone for their proposed shift.

3. **Develop a Self-Contained and Minimalist Generative Model:** A key goal is to create a diffusion model that is self-contained and conceptually simple. This means eliminating reliance on external components like pre-trained latent tokenizers (VAEs), auxiliary losses (adversarial, perceptual), or self-supervised pre-training for the backbone network. They aim for a "Just image Transformers" (JiT) model that applies a plain Vision Transformer directly to raw pixels.

4. **Overcome High-Dimensional Challenges in Pixel Space:** Prior pixel-space diffusion models or ViT-based latent diffusion models often struggle when per-token dimensionality increases, leading to catastrophic failures or requiring extremely large models. The paper aims to demonstrate that `x-prediction` can naturally mitigate these issues, allowing apparently under-capacity networks to operate effectively in very high-dimensional pixel spaces (e.g., large patches of raw pixels).

5. **Advance the Generalizability of "Diffusion + Transformer":** By minimizing domain-specific designs and focusing on fundamental principles, the authors seek to build a more broadly applicable "Diffusion + Transformer" paradigm. This paradigm would be particularly valuable for scientific domains beyond computer vision, where raw natural data is prevalent and crafting specialized tokenizers or pre-training schemes is complex or infeasible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a combination of theoretical grounding, controlled empirical studies (including a toy experiment), and practical model design to support their claims.

1. **Theoretical Framework and Reparameterization Analysis:** The paper begins by formally outlining the relationships between `x`, `ϵ`, and `v` prediction spaces within the diffusion and flow-based modeling paradigms. They present a comprehensive table (Table 1) detailing all nine possible combinations of network output targets (`x`, `ϵ`, `v`) and loss spaces (`x`, `ϵ`, `v`). This analysis highlights that while different prediction targets can be reparameterized to define losses in other spaces, the fundamental nature of *what the network directly predicts* remains distinct and has significant implications.

2. **Manifold Hypothesis Validation with a Toy Experiment:** To empirically demonstrate the core hypothesis that predicting on-manifold data (`x`) is easier than off-manifold quantities (`ϵ` or `v`) in high-dimensional spaces, a controlled toy experiment is designed. A low-dimensional (d=2) dataset is synthetically embedded into a much higher-dimensional (D=2 to D=512) observation space via an unknown projection matrix. A simple 5-layer MLP is trained as the generator, and its performance is evaluated across different D for `x`, `ϵ`, and `v`-prediction (all using `v-loss`). This experiment clearly shows that only `x-prediction` maintains performance as D increases, even when the network's hidden dimension is much smaller than D.

3. **"Just image Transformers" (JiT) Architecture:** For real-world image generation, the authors propose JiT, a minimalist architecture based on a plain Vision Transformer (ViT). Instead of operating on latent tokens, JiT directly processes raw pixel patches (e.g., 16x16 or 32x32 pixels) as tokens. The architecture consists of linear patch embedding, positional encoding, a stack of Transformer blocks, and a linear predictor to map tokens back to pixel patches. This design is essentially DiT applied directly to pixels, avoiding any specialized pixel-space designs like dense convolutions or hierarchical structures often found in other pixel-space diffusion models.

4. **Systematic Empirical Comparison of Prediction Targets on ImageNet:** The core of their empirical methodology involves training JiT models on the ImageNet dataset across all nine combinations of prediction and loss spaces identified in Table 1.
- **High-Dimensional Setting:** JiT-B/16 is tested on 256x256 ImageNet, where each 16x16x3 patch results in a 768-dimensional token, comparable to the Transformer's hidden dimension. This setup reveals catastrophic failures for `ϵ`- and `v`-prediction, while `x-prediction` performs well across all loss types.
- **Low-Dimensional Setting:** JiT-B/4 is tested on 64x64 ImageNet, where 4x4x3 patches yield 48-dimensional tokens, much smaller than the Transformer's hidden dimension. In this scenario, all nine combinations perform reasonably well, suggesting the issue is specific to high-dimensional inputs.

5. **Analysis of Model Capacity and Design Choices:**
- **Loss Weighting and Noise-Level Shift:** They demonstrate that simply reweighting losses (implicitly done by changing loss space) or adjusting noise schedules, while sometimes beneficial, cannot remedy the fundamental failure of `ϵ`- or `v`-prediction in high-dimensional settings.
- **Under-capacity Networks and Bottlenecks:** They show that `x-prediction` works well even when the Transformer's hidden dimension is significantly smaller than the input patch dimension (e.g., 768-dim hidden for 3072-dim or 12288-dim patches). Furthermore, they introduce a *bottleneck* in the linear patch embedding layer (reducing the patch dimension before expanding to the Transformer's hidden size) and find that it can actually *improve* performance, reinforcing the idea that the network is implicitly learning lower-dimensional representations.

6. **"Just Advanced" Transformers for Performance Optimization:** While advocating for simplicity, the authors also incorporate general-purpose architectural improvements from the broader Transformer literature, especially from large language models. These include SwiGLU, RMSNorm, RoPE, qk-norm, and an enhanced in-context class conditioning (multiple class tokens). This demonstrates that the JiT paradigm can benefit from general Transformer advancements without requiring domain-specific modifications.

7. **Algorithmic Formulation:** The paper specifies their chosen algorithm, which leverages `x-prediction` with `v-loss` as the objective function (Algorithm 1 for training, Algorithm 2 for sampling), including a clipping mechanism for numerical stability.

8. **Scalability Experiments:** JiT models of varying sizes (Base, Large, Huge, Giant) are evaluated on ImageNet at 256x256, 512x512, and 1024x1024 resolutions. A crucial aspect is that for higher resolutions, they increase the patch size proportionally (e.g., JiT/32 for 512x512, JiT/64 for 1024x1024) to maintain the same sequence length (number of tokens), ensuring comparable computational cost across resolutions for a given model size. They also adjust the noise scale proportionally to maintain SNR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several compelling findings that fundamentally question current practices in diffusion modeling and highlight the power of direct data prediction.

1. **X-Prediction is Critical for High-Dimensional Pixel Space:** The most significant finding is the stark contrast in performance between `x-prediction` and `ϵ`/`v`-prediction in high-dimensional settings. On ImageNet 256x256 with 16x16 patches (768-dim tokens), `ϵ`- and `v`-prediction methods fail catastrophically, yielding extremely high FID scores (e.g., FID > 300), regardless of the loss space used. In contrast, `x-prediction` consistently achieves good performance (e.g., FID 8.62 with `v-loss`). This effect is not observed in lower-dimensional settings (ImageNet 64x64 with 4x4 patches, 48-dim tokens), where all prediction types perform comparably, indicating that the problem is specifically related to the "curse of dimensionality" when modeling off-manifold quantities.

2. **Validation of the Manifold Hypothesis:** The toy experiment (Fig. 2) provides clear, controlled evidence supporting the manifold assumption. As the ambient observation dimension (D) increases, only `x-prediction` (which implicitly models the low-dimensional underlying data) maintains its ability to generate meaningful samples. `ϵ`/`v`-prediction degrades and fails catastrophically in high D-spaces, even with an under-capacity network, echoing the real-world ImageNet results.

3. **Bottleneck Embeddings Can Be Beneficial:** Surprisingly, introducing a bottleneck (reducing dimensionality) in the initial patch embedding layer for `x-prediction` can improve image quality (FID scores). For JiT-B/16 on ImageNet 256x256, a bottleneck dimension between 32 and 512 yielded better FIDs than no bottleneck, suggesting that forcing the network to focus on lower-dimensional representations of the input patch aids learning. This further reinforces the manifold learning perspective.

4. **Robustness to Network Capacity for X-Prediction:** `x-prediction` proves remarkably robust. Even when the per-patch dimension is significantly higher than the Transformer's hidden dimension (e.g., 3072-dim patches for JiT-B/32 or 12288-dim patches for JiT-B/64, with a 768-dim hidden size), the models can still produce strong results. This implies that for `x-prediction`, the network doesn't need to match or exceed the observed dimensionality, as it only needs to retain the lower-dimensional manifold information.

5. **Minimalist and Self-Contained Design Achieves Competitive Results:** The "Just image Transformers" (JiT) model, without tokenizers, pre-training (self-supervised or otherwise), adversarial losses, or perceptual losses, achieves highly competitive FID scores on ImageNet.
- **ImageNet 256x256:** JiT-G/16 achieves FID 1.82, comparable to or surpassing many complex latent diffusion models (e.g., DiT-XL/2: 2.27, SiT-XL/2: 2.06) and most pixel-space diffusion models, some of which use pre-training or specialized architectures.
- **ImageNet 512x512:** JiT-G/32 achieves FID 1.78, again competitive with or outperforming complex counterparts (e.g., DiT-XL/2: 3.04, SiT-XL/2: 2.62).
- **ImageNet 1024x1024:** Even at this high resolution, JiT-B/64 achieves a respectable FID of 4.82 with a relatively small model.

6. **Scalability Across Resolutions and Model Sizes:** JiT models demonstrate strong scalability. By keeping the sequence length constant and proportionally increasing patch size with resolution, the computational cost remains similar across 256x256, 512x512, and 1024x1024 for a given model size. Performance generally improves with larger model sizes (B to G), and the gap between 256x256 and 512x512 performance diminishes, with JiT-G even showing slightly better FID at 512x512 than 256x256, possibly due to reduced overfitting on the more challenging 512x512 task.

7. **Improved Training Stability and Lower Loss with X-Prediction:** Comparisons of training loss curves show that `x-prediction` leads to significantly lower and more stable training losses compared to `v-prediction` (Fig. 7), suggesting an inherently easier optimization landscape. Visualizations of single-step denoised images also confirm that `v-prediction` suffers from noticeable artifacts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work has profound significance, challenging established norms in diffusion modeling and opening new avenues for research and application.

1. **Conceptual Recalibration of Diffusion Models:** The paper's most significant contribution is its conceptual shift. By convincingly arguing that denoising generative models should "denoise" in the classical sense—by directly predicting clean data (`x`)—it challenges the prevailing wisdom that `ϵ`-prediction or `v`-prediction are universally superior. This re-emphasizes the importance of designing networks that align with the intrinsic structure of natural data, rather than merely reparameterizing loss functions. It suggests that the success of `ϵ`-prediction in early DDPMs might have been a consequence of other factors (e.g., specific noise schedules, model capacities in low-dimensional settings) rather than its inherent superiority as a prediction target for general high-dimensional data.

2. **Simplification and Democratization of Generative AI:** By achieving state-of-the-art or highly competitive results without relying on complex, multi-stage pipelines (like VAE tokenizers) or extensive pre-training schemes, JiT offers a dramatically simplified approach to high-resolution image generation. This "back to basics" minimalism could significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, reducing computational overhead and intellectual property concerns associated with proprietary pre-trained components. It promotes a more transparent and understandable generative modeling framework.

3. **Broadened Applicability Across Scientific Domains:** The self-contained nature of JiT, devoid of domain-specific tokenizers or pre-training, makes the "Diffusion + Transformer" paradigm highly attractive for applications beyond computer vision. Many scientific fields (e.g., computational biology for protein design, materials science for molecular generation, climate modeling) deal with raw, high-dimensional data where designing effective tokenizers or finding large-scale pre-training datasets is challenging or impossible. JiT provides a general-purpose foundation that could be directly applied to such data, potentially accelerating discovery and innovation in these areas.

4. **Efficiency and Resource Optimization:** The ability of `x-prediction` to work effectively even with network capacities significantly lower than the observed patch dimensionality (e.g., 768 hidden units for 12288-dim patches) implies a more efficient use of model parameters. This could lead to more compact and computationally less demanding models for high-resolution generation, making advanced generative AI more accessible to researchers with limited computational resources.

5. **Guidance for Future Architectural Design:** The unexpected benefit of bottleneck patch embeddings provides a crucial insight for future architecture design in high-dimensional generative models. It suggests that explicitly encouraging low-dimensional representation learning within the network, even for ostensibly "simple" components like patch embeddings, can be highly effective. This opens new research directions into how to best integrate manifold learning principles into modern deep generative architectures.

6. **Catalyst for Foundational Research:** This paper serves as a potent reminder that fundamental principles from classical machine learning, like the manifold hypothesis, remain highly relevant and can offer fresh perspectives on contemporary challenges. It encourages researchers to constantly question assumptions and revisit foundational concepts, potentially leading to more robust, efficient, and interpretable AI systems.

In conclusion, "Back to Basics: Let Denoising Generative Models Denoise" is not just an incremental improvement but a significant conceptual contribution that could re-steer the development of denoising diffusion models towards simpler, more principled, and broadly applicable paradigms.
