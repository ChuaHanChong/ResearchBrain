# Back to Basics: Let Denoising Generative Models Denoise

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT propose a re-evaluation of denoising diffusion models, demonstrating that directly predicting clean data ("x-prediction") with a minimalist "Just image Transformers" (JiT) architecture overcomes catastrophic failures in high-dimensional pixel spaces. This approach achieved competitive image generation quality on ImageNet, reaching an FID of 1.82 at 256x256 without reliance on complex components like latent tokenizers or pre-training.

## Problem

- Denoising diffusion models commonly predict noise or a combination of noise and data, which may be suboptimal for high-dimensional pixel spaces.
- High-dimensional pixel-space diffusion models often face challenges such as catastrophic failures or a need for complex architectures, latent tokenizers, or extensive pre-training.
- Many state-of-the-art generative models rely on multi-stage pipelines and auxiliary losses, making them less self-contained and harder to generalize across domains.

## Method

- The authors advocate for directly predicting clean data ("x-prediction") in denoising ==diffusion== models, supported by the classical manifold hypothesis.
- They introduce "Just image Transformers" (**JiT**), a minimalist ==Vision Transformer== architecture that operates directly on raw pixel patches, eschewing tokenizers, pre-training, or auxiliary losses.
- A systematic empirical comparison of "x", "epsilon", and "v" prediction targets across various loss spaces and network capacities was conducted to validate the proposed approach.

## Results

- "X-prediction" successfully prevented catastrophic failures observed with "epsilon"- and "v"-prediction methods on ImageNet 256x256, where it yielded good FID scores (e.g., 8.62) while others failed (FID > 300).
- The JiT-G/16 model achieved an FID of 1.82 on ImageNet 256x256, outperforming or matching many complex latent diffusion models (e.g., DiT-XL/2: 2.27, SiT-XL/2: 2.06) without additional components.
- JiT models demonstrated strong scalability, achieving FID 1.78 on ImageNet 512x512 and FID 4.82 on 1024x1024, by proportionally scaling patch sizes to maintain sequence length and computational cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting on-manifold clean data ("x") is more robust and inherently easier for neural networks than predicting off-manifold quantities (noise or "v") in high-dimensional input spaces.
> - The "x-prediction" strategy effectively mitigates the "curse of dimensionality" for ViT-based pixel-space diffusion models, allowing them to function stably on raw, high-dimensional inputs.
> - A simplified, self-contained "Diffusion + Transformer" framework, operating solely on pixels, can achieve competitive image generation performance without requiring complex external components.
