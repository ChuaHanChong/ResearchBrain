# The Quadruped Soft Tail: Compliant Grasping and Swabbing for Contamination Surveys in Harsh Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CERN and the Norwegian University of Science and Technology developed a quadruped robot equipped with a soft, compliant tail for contamination surveys in hazardous environments. This system successfully grasps thin tissues, swabs surfaces with controlled compliance, and releases samples, demonstrating a 65% improvement in wipe grasping success over a rigid gripper and achieving an effective stiffness increase from 3.9 N/m to 16.7 N/m via common-mode control.

## Problem

- Performing beryllium contamination surveys in highly radioactive and cluttered environments at CERN necessitates human entry, leading to radiation exposure.
- Traditional wheeled and tracked robots struggle with the complex, cable-strewn terrains of these facilities, with tracked robots risking damage to infrastructure.
- Existing robotic manipulators lack the combined compliance for gentle swabbing and the specific dexterity needed for reliably picking up thin, flat sampling tissues in situ.

## Method

- A **Continuous Tendon-Actuated Manipulator (CTAM)** soft tail, integrated onto a quadruped robot, was developed, featuring a tapered TPU backbone, rigid discs, and ==tendon routing== for bending and gripper actuation.
- A tendon-actuated ==soft gripper==, 3D printed in TPU 95A, was designed and optimized for grasping delicate objects like cotton tissues and ensuring compliant surface contact during swabbing.
- A closed-form ==Euler-Bernoulli-based kinematic model== and a ==singularity-robust task-space controller== were implemented, incorporating ==common-mode tendon actuation== for decoupled stiffness modulation and axial preload control.

## Results

- The optimized **N=6**, **Φ=20°** soft gripper design showed superior performance, achieving up to **65%** higher success rates in grasping wipes compared to a baseline rigid gripper.
- Common-mode tendon actuation allowed for significant stiffness modulation, increasing the effective stiffness against a **0.20 N** tip push from **3.9 N/m** to **16.7 N/m**, enabling adjustable interaction forces.
- The developed kinematic model provided sufficient accuracy for real-time control, with a root-mean-square prediction error of **5.6 deg** for the tip angle and **7.3 deg** for the disc angle, after empirical calibration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining the agile locomotion of quadruped robots with the compliant manipulation of a soft tail enables safe and effective intervention in highly cluttered and hazardous environments.
> - Optimizing soft gripper design parameters (finger count, angle, material infill) is crucial for versatile and reliable grasping of various objects, including challenging thin, flat items.
> - Common-mode tendon actuation provides an effective mechanism to modulate robot stiffness and control interaction forces independently of primary bending, enhancing adaptability for tasks like delicate grasping and firm swabbing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in robotic deployment for inspection and intervention in hazardous and complex environments, specifically focusing on radioactive areas at CERN.

**Robotics in Hazardous Environments:** Facilities like CERN present high-radiation zones that necessitate robotic solutions to minimize human exposure during tasks such as inspection, maintenance, and repair. Traditional wheeled and tracked platforms often face limitations in these environments due to terrain complexity. Wheeled robots may struggle with high, gradient, or dynamic obstacles like cables and pipes. Tracked platforms, while more capable, risk damaging or disconnecting critical infrastructure.

**Legged Robotics for Mobility:** Quadruped robots have emerged as an alternative for navigating such challenging terrains. Their ability to make and break contact with the environment allows them to climb over non-rigid obstacles without causing damage, proving effective in cluttered areas previously inaccessible to other robotic systems. This has been demonstrated in CERN's North Area for tasks like leak detection and machine inspection.

**Robotic Manipulators and Tails:** Equipping mobile robots with manipulation capabilities is crucial for intervention tasks beyond mere inspection. In the context of quadrupeds, various robotic manipulators have been explored. Bio-inspired tails, in particular, are a developing area. Existing literature primarily focuses on rigid-link tails for functions such as locomotion assistance, stabilization, tool carrying, and proprioceptive sensing. While some flexible tails have been implemented for compliance, compactness, and lower mass, their primary function typically remains locomotion enhancement rather than offering additional manipulation capabilities. Designs featuring discs and tendon-actuated variable stiffness tails have been documented, but again, mainly for locomotion improvement.

**Soft Robotic Grippers:** Concurrently, soft robotic grippers have gained attention due to their compliance, which makes them suitable for grasping delicate or irregularly shaped objects in applications like factory automation, agriculture, and biomedicine. Specific gripper designs exist for picking up flat objects, often involving multiple actuating fingers, crawling mechanisms, rolling actions, or vibrations, frequently requiring complex and heavy actuation systems. Wiping tools for contaminated environments have also been developed, incorporating passively compliant sections to improve surface contact, but these are typically specialized for wiping only and lack grasping versatility.

**Research Gap:** The current work identifies a gap where a compact, low-mass, and versatile soft gripper solution is needed to overcome the limitations of existing designs, especially one capable of both compliant grasping of thin, flat objects (like tissues) and swabbing, while being integrated onto a quadruped robot for navigating hazardous, cluttered terrain. This paper directly addresses the need for a system that combines the agility of legged locomotion with the compliance of soft robotic manipulation to perform complex intervention procedures without human exposure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the necessity to perform beryllium contamination surveys in highly radioactive and cluttered environments at CERN, without exposing human personnel to radiation. The key objectives were therefore to develop a robotic system capable of executing the entire survey procedure autonomously or via teleoperation.

Specifically, the system needed to address the following detailed requirements and motivations:

- **Hazardous Environment Operation:** The overarching goal was to eliminate human exposure to radiation during contamination surveys. This mandated a fully robotic solution for tasks within radioactive areas.
- **Navigation in Cluttered Terrain:** CERN's experimental areas are characterized by floors heavily cluttered with electrical cables, cooling pipes, and other obstacles. These environments are difficult or impossible for wheeled robots to traverse and risky for tracked robots which could damage infrastructure. Quadruped robots have proven successful in these conditions by stepping over obstacles without disruption. Thus, the robotic system needed to leverage the mobility of a quadruped.
- **Specific Task Requirements (Beryllium Contamination Survey):** The requested procedure involved a sequence of precise and gentle manipulations:
 - **Tissue Pickup:** The robot must be able to reliably pick up a cotton tissue from a designated pickup point. A critical sub-objective was to handle "worst-case scenarios" where tissues might be lying flat and unfolded on a surface.
 - **Compliant Swabbing:** After picking up a tissue, the robot needed to gently swab designated wall surfaces. This required compliant interaction to avoid damaging the walls or any installed equipment, and to ensure proper contact for effective sampling.
 - **Tissue Release:** The collected tissue must then be released at a designated collection point for subsequent beryllium analysis by the Radiation Protection (RP) group.
 - **Repetitive Operations:** The entire pick-swab-release cycle needed to be repeatable for multiple tissues at various locations.
- **System Integration and Physical Constraints:**
 - **Low Profile:** To access all necessary areas, the robot system (quadruped plus manipulator) had a maximum height constraint of 616 mm. This implied that any manipulator installed on the quadruped needed to be capable of folding to not exceed 183 mm from its mounting plate.
 - **Lightweight Design:** To minimize impact on the quadruped's dynamics and allow for the use of existing quadruped controllers, the manipulator system needed to be lightweight, keeping the center of gravity as close as possible to that of the unmodified quadruped.
 - **Reach and Flexibility:** The manipulator needed to bend its tip towards the floor and extend horizontally to reach walls for swabbing, including reaching past the quadruped's rear legs to curved tunnel walls.
- **Intuitive Control:** For effective teleoperation by human operators, the system required a control architecture that could translate operator commands into precise robot motions. This necessitated the development of a suitable kinematic model and a singularity-robust task-space controller.
- **Broad Applicability:** While specifically motivated by CERN's beryllium surveys, the research aimed to develop a concept broadly applicable to legged robots operating in diverse cluttered, confined, or hazardous environments where conventional rigid-link manipulators might be impractical or undesirable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research involved the development of a novel quadruped soft tail system, comprising a compliant manipulator and a soft gripper, integrated with a kinematic model and a control strategy for teleoperation.

**System Overview and Design (CTAM Soft Tail):**
The proposed solution utilizes the Continuous Tendon-Actuated Manipulator (CTAM) from a previous project at CERN, adapted as a quadruped tail. CTAM is designed to be lightweight, flexible, and compliant.
- **Backbone Structure:** It features a hollow, tapered backbone made of TPU 95A, incorporating friction-fitted rigid discs. The tapering and logarithmic spacing of the discs ensure greater bending distally, allowing the backbone to form a spiral in its maximum curled state. This design is inspired by previous work for versatile grasping.
- **Tendon Routing:** Tendons are fastened to the distal disc, routed through cylindrical holes in each disc, and terminate at motor-mounted spools within an actuation box. The hollow backbone allows an additional tendon to be routed through its center to actuate the gripper.
- **Dimensions and Mass:** The CTAM has a length of 405 mm, enabling the gripper to reach the floor and walls. The entire system (CTAM, actuation, endoscopic camera, gripper, cover, electronics, cabling) has a total mass of 902.8 g, designed to minimize impact on quadruped dynamics. An endoscopic camera (4.5 mm wide) can also be routed through the backbone.
- **Control System:** The full system is controlled by a computer integrated into the quadruped and teleoperated via WiFi, using UDP packets from a remote controller.

**Soft Gripper Design and Testing:**
A tendon-actuated soft gripper was designed for tissue manipulation.
- **Material and Actuation:** The gripper is 3D printed in TPU 95A for simplified fabrication and material consistency with the backbone. A helically routed tendon causes fingers to close upon tensioning, and the TPU's shape memory facilitates opening upon tension release.
- **Design Parameters:** The design was parameterized by the number of fingers (N), finger angle (Φ) relative to the centerline, and approximated effective contact areas (A_o for open, A_c for closed). The interior side of each finger has a flat edge angled at 20 degrees. The gripper used in the video had N=6 and Φ=20°.
- **Experimental Testing:**
 - **Material Properties:** Grippers were tested with varying infill densities (20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, 100%) and number of tendon traverses (1, 2) to assess required actuation force and rebound time.
 - **Geometric Parameters:** Seven grippers with N ∈ {2-8} fingers (Φ=20°) and four grippers with N=6 fingers (Φ ∈ {0, 20, 40, 60}°) were tested. Actuation force, contact areas, and rebound time were measured.
 - **Pick-and-Place:** A comparative pick-and-place study was conducted using soft grippers (at 20% infill) and a 3-fingered rigid, spring-loaded gripper (baseline). Test objects included a 140x140 mm² beryllium testing wipe, an M6x10 screw, an M5 nut, and a 38 mm diameter rubber gasket. The gripper approached objects from 10 cm height at varying entry angles (Ω = 0, Φ/2, Φ, 3Φ/2), with the worst-case orientation (one finger contact). Success rate was evaluated based on stable grasping during a return motion and subsequent drop.

**Kinematic Model:**
A closed-form Euler-Bernoulli-based varying curvature kinematic model was developed to map tendon displacements to robot curvature profiles, enabling real-time control.
- **Actuation Map:** A linear mapping (q = Bl) relates tendon displacements (l) to a 2D bending vector (q), whose amplitude (ρ) and direction (ϕ) define the bending.
- **Taper Curvature Profile:** The tapered backbone geometry (radius r(s) and tendon offset d(s) varying linearly with length s) is incorporated. The curvature κ(s) is derived from the Euler-Bernoulli assumption, considering the hollow circular cross-section. This leads to a spatially varying curvature profile κ(s) = κ₀w(s), where κ₀ is the base curvature and w(s) is a local curvature weight. The cumulative bending angle θ(s) is obtained by integrating κ(s). Closed-form solutions for these integrals are provided. The base curvature κ₀ is constrained by the tendon displacement magnitude ρ, establishing a mapping from l → ρ → κ₀ → θ(s).
- **Forward Kinematics:** Under the assumption of pure bending without torsion (constant ϕ), the robot's orientation R(s) and position p(s) along the backbone can be derived in closed form, culminating in a homogeneous centerline point transform T(s).
- **Differential Kinematics/Jacobian:** A Jacobian J is derived to map tendon velocities to end-effector velocities. It is decomposed into a linear part (K_κ mapping tendon displacements to a base curvature vector κ*) and a nonlinear part (J_xκ mapping κ* to end-effector pose), ensuring the Jacobian is well-defined even at zero-curvature singularities.

**Control Strategy:**
A closed-loop inverse-kinematics (CLIK) task-space controller was implemented, featuring a two-loop architecture.
- **Inner Loop:** Calculates commanded tendon velocities (˙l) from desired base curvature vector rates (˙κ*d) using the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of K_κ. Crucially, it preserves a common-mode degree of freedom (γ) that is orthogonal to the bending subspace.
- **Common-Mode Control:** This common-mode actuation, which does not affect the robot's curvature configuration, is used to control the axial preload (pretension) of the robot. This allows the robot to modulate its stiffness and exert varying interaction forces (e.g., for gentle grasping versus firm swabbing).
- **Outer Loop:** A singularity-robust CLIK law generates the desired curvature-vector motions (˙κ*d) from task-space tracking objectives (desired end-effector velocity ˙p_d and position error e). A λ-damped pseudo-inverse is used to handle situations where the desired motion leaves the reachable manifold and to regularize the zero-curvature singularity.

**Experimental Results (Kinematic Model Assessment):**
The developed prototype, with specific geometric parameters, was used for experimental validation.
- **Hardware:** Dynamixel XH430-W210T servo motors (current mode) drove the tendons.
- **Motion Capture:** Vicon Tracker 3.10.2 system with Vero cameras tracked visual markers affixed to the robot's base, a disc at 107 mm, and the tip, providing 0.02 mm position resolution.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Shape-independence:** The extent to which gripper actuation and common-mode actuation affected the robot's tip and disc orientation was assessed during various maneuvers (85 gripper events, 97 bending, 49 common-mode).
 - **Model Calibration:** A scalar scaling factor (k_cal) was applied to the kinematic model, determined by a one-dimensional least-squares fit against measured tip positions, to account for compliance and manufacturing deviations.
 - **Stiffness Modulation:** The stiffening effect of common-mode pretension was quantified by pushing the robot tip with a 0.20 N horizontal force while varying common-mode tendon displacements, recording tip deflections and calculating effective stiffness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation provided insights into the performance of the soft gripper, the kinematic model, and the effects of common-mode actuation.

**Soft Gripper Performance:**
- **Material and Tendon Configuration:**
 - Increasing infill density and the number of tendon traverses generally increased the required actuation force to close the gripper.
 - For single tendon traverses, rebound time decreased significantly as infill density increased from 20% to 60% (from 10.408 s to 0.982 s), indicating faster shape recovery. However, at higher infill densities (80% and 100%), rebound time increased again (to 1.468 s and 6.492 s, respectively), which was attributed to increased internal friction and localized stress concentrations.
 - Grippers with more than one tendon traverse exhibited a greater tendency toward residual deformation and did not consistently return to their original open state, leading to omission of their mean rebound times.
 - For light object grasping, lower infill densities (e.g., 20%) with a single tendon traverse were identified as providing a suitable balance between actuation force and shape recovery.
- **Geometric Parameters (N, Φ) and Pick-and-Place:**
 - The gripper with N=6 fingers and Φ=20° was identified as the most successful configuration for grasping wipes.
 - All tested soft gripper designs demonstrated acceptable performance for grasping a wipe at entry angles (Ω) of 0 and Φ/2. Success rates decreased when the entry angle became equal to or greater than the finger angle Φ.
 - In comparison, the baseline rigid gripper exhibited a success rate below 50% for all entry angles when grasping a wipe, performing 65% less successfully than its 3-fingered soft gripper counterpart for this task. The rigid gripper was more successful at grasping the gasket, but demonstrated less versatility across all object types compared to the soft grippers.
 - Observations of failure modes indicated that grippers with N < 5 fingers often failed due to poor object-tendon contact and lateral movement of the "tied" finger. Grippers with N > 5 mitigated this issue but performed poorly with smaller objects due to reduced effective contact areas. The N=5 gripper was the most successful for picking up the M5 nut.
 - For large objects like the gasket, grippers with more fingers (N > 5) performed better at normal entry angles. Conversely, as the entry angle Ω increased, the larger gaps between fingers in grippers with N < 6 became advantageous.
 - The gripper with a 20° finger angle (Φ=20°) achieved the highest mean success rate overall. Grippers with larger finger angles performed better only for the gasket, where their effective contact areas exceeded the gasket's surface area. However, the increased travel distance and force required for actuation at larger angles caused small test objects to be pushed away.

**Kinematic Model and Control System Validation:**
- **Shape-Independence of Gripper Actuation:** Experimental results indicated that gripper actuation had a negligible effect on the overall robot shape. The median tip orientation sensitivity was 0.047 deg/mm and for the disc was 0.022 deg/mm, which are only 3.6% and 3.2% respectively, relative to the sensitivity caused by bending tendons. This supported the assumption that the backbone could be treated as load-free with respect to gripper operations.
- **Common-Mode Actuation and Stiffness Modulation:**
 - Common-mode tendon actuation provided a mechanism for stiffness modulation and preload control. When tendons were taut, common-mode actuation still induced some kinematic coupling, with tip orientation sensitivity of 0.285 deg/mm (21.6% relative to bending tendons) and disc sensitivity of 0.116 deg/mm (16.6% relative).
 - When tendons became slack, the kinematic decoupling was voided, leading to even higher sensitivities (0.881 deg/mm for tip, 0.224 deg/mm for disc), as motor positions no longer accurately determined the configuration.
 - A direct assessment of stiffness demonstrated that common-mode pretension significantly increased the robot's effective stiffness against external forces. For example, with a 0.20 N tip push, the mean tip deflection decreased from 50.2 mm (0 mm common-mode travel) to 11.7 mm (28.1 mm common-mode travel), resulting in an increase in effective stiffness (k_eff) from 3.9 N/m to 16.7 N/m. This confirmed the utility of common-mode control for adapting interaction forces.
- **Kinematic Model Accuracy:** The proposed closed-form kinematic model, when scaled by an empirically derived factor (k_cal = 0.529 ± 0.060), provided a suitable approximation of the backbone deformation behavior. The resulting root-mean-square prediction error across all trials was 5.6 deg for the tip angle and 7.3 deg for the disc angle. This level of accuracy was deemed sufficient for practical real-time task-space control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a robotic system that addresses a critical operational need in hazardous environments and offers broader implications for field robotics.

**Direct Impact on Beryllium Contamination Surveys at CERN:**
The primary significance lies in providing a complete robotic solution for beryllium contamination surveys in the challenging, cluttered, and radioactive environments of CERN.
- **Human Safety:** By enabling the entire procedure—from picking up sampling tissues, swabbing contaminated surfaces, to releasing tissues at collection points—to be performed robotically, the system eliminates the need for human personnel to enter high-radiation areas. This directly improves worker safety and reduces radiation exposure.
- **Operational Efficiency:** The system allows for repeated, consistent sampling tasks in areas previously difficult or impossible for human access or less agile robotic platforms.

**Advancements in Field Robotics:**
The proposed quadruped soft tail concept integrates several key robotic capabilities, contributing to the development of autonomous systems for complex interventions.
- **Combined Agility and Compliance:** The system effectively combines the robust mobility and agility of a quadruped robot for navigating highly cluttered and uneven terrain with the compliant and dexterous manipulation capabilities of a soft robot. This hybrid approach overcomes limitations inherent in single-modality systems.
- **Versatile Soft Manipulation:** The soft gripper's design and experimental validation demonstrate its capability to reliably grasp thin, flat objects like wipes, which is a common challenge for conventional grippers, in addition to other objects. Its compliance allows for gentle interaction with delicate surfaces during swabbing, minimizing damage risk.
- **Intuitive and Robust Control:** The development of a closed-form kinematic model and a singularity-robust task-space controller provides a basis for intuitive teleoperation, making the complex manipulation tasks accessible to human operators. The innovative use of common-mode tendon actuation for decoupled stiffness modulation allows the robot to adapt its interaction forces to task requirements (e.g., delicate grasping vs. firm swabbing), enhancing its versatility and robustness.
- **Lightweight and Compact Design:** The lightweight nature of the soft tail (total mass ~900g) minimizes its impact on the quadruped's dynamics, allowing for integration without significantly altering the base robot's locomotion characteristics or requiring extensive controller redesign for the base platform. The ability of the manipulator to fold to a low profile enables access to confined spaces.

**Broader Applicability:**
While specifically motivated by CERN's needs, the quadruped soft-tail concept is broadly applicable to a range of other hazardous, cluttered, or confined environments where conventional rigid-link manipulators are undesirable or impractical. Potential applications include:
- **Nuclear Decommissioning:** Tasks like radiation mapping, waste handling, and decontamination in nuclear facilities.
- **Disaster Response:** Inspection, sampling, and light manipulation in collapsed structures or chemically contaminated zones.
- **Industrial Inspection and Maintenance:** Navigating complex pipe networks, machinery, or infrastructure in hazardous industrial settings.
- **Space Exploration:** Operations in extraterrestrial environments with unpredictable terrain and sensitive equipment.

The work advances the state of the art in combining mobile legged platforms with soft, compliant manipulators, paving the way for more capable and safer robotic interventions in real-world challenging scenarios. Future user operation studies are indicated to further refine and validate the system's practical deployment.
