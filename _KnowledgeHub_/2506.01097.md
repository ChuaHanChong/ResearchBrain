# Generic Token Compression in Multimodal Large Language Models from an Explainability Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an explainability-guided method for visual token compression in Multimodal Large Language Models, enabling efficient processing by identifying and retaining only semantically important visual information. The approach demonstrates that significant compression at the MLLM input stage can maintain over 98% of original performance on image tasks and over 99% on video tasks, even with 50% token retention, offering improved computational efficiency and practical deployment.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) face significant computational burden due to the exponential growth of visual tokens with increasing image resolutions and video lengths.
- Existing visual token compression methods often assume that shallow layers of MLLMs are critically important and should be preserved, limiting compression efficiency.
- Current compression approaches lead to bottlenecks in memory usage and slow inference speeds, hindering practical deployment of MLLMs.

## Method

- The approach uses ==relevance propagation== (explainability methods) to identify and quantify the semantic importance of each visual token, generating ground-truth relevance scores.
- A lightweight, ==five-layer 1D convolutional network== learns to predict these relevance scores from the first-layer attention map, enabling efficient, real-time compression.
- The learned compressor compresses visual tokens at the ==input stage== of the MLLM, retaining only the most relevant information before it enters the main LLM architecture.

## Results

- The method achieves over **98%** of original performance on image tasks and over **99%** on video tasks while retaining only **50%** of visual tokens.
- The learned compressor consistently outperformed FastV and other baselines across ten benchmarks for image and video tasks (e.g., MME, MMStar, MVBench), demonstrating superior efficiency gains.
- Video tasks generally show better performance preservation after compression compared to image tasks, attributed to higher temporal redundancy in video content.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggressive visual token compression at the MLLM input stage can maintain high performance, challenging the assumption that shallow layers require all visual tokens.
> - Compressing tokens before LLM processing provides superior computational savings across all subsequent layers, including prefill and decode phases.
> - The learned compressor, based on a fully convolutional design, demonstrates strong generalization to visual inputs much larger than those seen during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) represents a significant advancement, building upon the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to integrate and process information from multiple modalities, particularly vision and language. These models commonly ingest visual tokens, derived from a vision encoder, alongside textual tokens into a unified LLM architecture. While this paradigm has led to progress in image- and video-related tasks, it introduces substantial computational and memory overhead. The number of visual tokens can be considerable, increasing with higher input resolutions or frame rates, necessitating efficient token compression techniques.

Existing visual token compression methods for MLLMs generally fall into two categories. The first category focuses on task- or instruction-agnostic compression, aiming to generate more compact visual representations independent of the LLM. Many contemporary MLLMs already incorporate such built-in mechanisms (e.g., spatial and temporal pooling) to reduce general redundancy. The second category, to which this work primarily contributes, centers on task- or instruction-related compression. These methods aim to select visual tokens most relevant to a given instruction, thereby reducing redundancy specific to the task at hand.

Previous research in task-related compression, exemplified by works like FastV, has often operated under the assumption that visual tokens in the shallow layers of the LLM are crucial for maintaining vision-language alignment. Consequently, such compression techniques have predominantly been applied in intermediate LLM layers. This paper directly questions this prevailing assumption, investigating whether effective task-related token compression can be achieved at the *input stage* of the LLM without significant performance degradation. This exploration positions the work as an early effort to challenge and potentially shift the paradigm of where and how task-related visual token compression is applied within MLLM architectures. By proposing a model-agnostic design leveraging explainability methods, the authors aim to introduce a compression approach that is broadly applicable without requiring modifications to the LLM architecture, offering a potentially more efficient and transferable solution to the MLLM computational bottleneck.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the computational and memory inefficiencies inherent in current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models process a substantial volume of visual tokens, especially when handling high-resolution images or long video sequences, leading to significant computational costs and memory overhead. This issue restricts the scalability and practical deployment of MLLMs.

The overarching goal of this work is to develop and validate an effective token compression strategy that mitigates these inefficiencies while maintaining model performance. Specifically, the paper addresses a critical, underexplored question: whether task-related visual token compression can be effectively and efficiently performed at the *input stage* of the Large Language Model (LLM), prior to its main computation. This contrasts with previous research that predominantly applies such compression within intermediate LLM layers, often assuming the indispensability of all shallow-layer visual tokens.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Challenge Existing Assumptions:** The authors explicitly seek to answer whether an effective task-related token compression approach exists prior to the LLM input, or if it is inherently infeasible. This objective challenges the common belief that all visual tokens in shallow LLM layers are indispensable for task-related compression.
2. **Leverage Explainability for Token Importance Assessment:** A core objective is to demonstrate that explainability methods, particularly those designed for transformer-based architectures, can effectively evaluate the global importance of each visual token with respect to a given instruction. This allows for the precise identification and selective pruning of task-irrelevant visual tokens.
3. **Validate Input-Stage Compression Feasibility:** Through experimentation, the research aims to show that with proper selection based on explainability scores, task-related token compression at the LLM input stage is indeed feasible, incurring only negligible performance loss.
4. **Develop an Efficient and MLLM-Agnostic Compressor:** Recognizing that full explainability computation is resource-intensive, a key objective is to propose and train a lightweight, standalone convolutional network (denoted as fθ). This network is designed to learn a mapping from the LLM's first-layer attention map to the explainability-derived relevance scores. The motivation is to create a compressor that:
 - Avoids the need for a full inference pass (forward and backward) during deployment.
 - Is highly efficient in terms of model size and training time.
 - Is MLLM-agnostic, meaning it can be applied to different MLLM architectures without requiring modifications or retraining of the core MLLM.
5. **Achieve Substantial Inference Cost Reductions:** By performing token compression prior to the LLM's main computation, the method aims to significantly reduce both the prefilling time and KV-cache memory requirements during inference. This objective directly addresses the practical limitations of deploying high-capacity MLLMs.
6. **Demonstrate Broad Generalization:** The research is motivated by the need for a robust compression solution. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation across multiple leading MLLMs (Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, VILA1.5) and a diverse set of 13 image and video benchmarks is planned to demonstrate the effectiveness and strong generalization capabilities of the proposed method.

In summary, the motivation is to resolve the efficiency bottleneck in MLLMs by introducing a novel, explainability-guided, input-stage token compression paradigm that is both effective in preserving performance and efficient in deployment, thereby enhancing the scalability and practicality of MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on a two-phase approach: first, establishing the efficacy of explainability methods for visual token compression; second, developing an efficient, learnable compressor that approximates this explainability for practical deployment at the LLM input stage.

**Background and Motivation (Formalized):**
Current MLLMs typically consist of a vision encoder (VM) that generates visual token embeddings (E_v) from an input image or video (V). These E_v, along with system prompts (E_s) and user instructions (E_u), are fed into a Large Language Model (LM) to produce a textual response (Y). The challenge lies in the large number of visual tokens (N_v) in E_v, leading to computational and memory burdens. The objective is to introduce a compression module (`Comp`) that prunes E_v to a smaller set ˆE_v = `Comp`(E_v | E_u) *before* it reaches the LLM, without altering the VM or LM architecture. This approach aims to address task-related redundancy, complementing any built-in task-agnostic compression in the VM.

**Phase 1: Token Compression with Explainability (Ground Truth Relevance)**

1. **Relevance Maps by Explainability Method:**
 - The authors adopt a Generic Attention Explainability (GAE) framework, similar to methods by Chefer et al. (2021b) and Yao et al. (2024), to compute the relevance of the LLM's response to the visual input.
 - The process involves tracing the semantic relevance flow from the generated textual tokens (Y = {y0, y1..., yT-1}) back to the raw visual inputs.
 - For each generated token y_t, a relevance map R_t is initialized as an identity matrix.
 - R_t is iteratively updated across the LLM's L layers using the equation: R_t = R_t + E_h((A_l^t ⊙ ∇A_l^t)^+) · R_t.
 - A_l^t represents the multi-head attention map in the l-th layer.
 - ∇A_l^t denotes the corresponding gradients with respect to the output y_t, obtained during a backward pass.
 - ⊙ is the Hadamard product, capturing the interaction between attention weights and their influence.
 - (.)^+ sets negative values to zero, ensuring a causal direction of influence.
 - E_h averages across the attention heads, effectively performing a gradient-weighted expectation.
 - The relevance of y_t to visual signals is extracted from the last row of the final R_t.
 - Finally, the visual relevance across all time steps (t) is aggregated by averaging to obtain an overall visual relevance score, R_v ∈ ℝ^(1×N_v), which represents the importance of each visual token with respect to the current response.
2. **Visual Token Compression Using Relevance Scores:**
 - Based on the computed R_v, visual tokens are ranked by their importance.
 - Less important tokens are pruned, retaining a target count ˆN_v, resulting in compressed token embeddings ˆE_v for LLM input.
 - This phase serves as a proof-of-concept, demonstrating that R_v can effectively guide lossless compression at the input stage.

**Phase 2: Explainability-Based Compressor Learning (Efficient Deployment)**

1. **Motivation for Learning:** Directly using the explainability method (Phase 1) is impractical for inference because it requires a full forward and backward pass through the LLM. An efficient, standalone module is needed to predict relevance scores pre-LLM.
2. **Model Architecture (`fθ`):**
 - The authors propose a lightweight, fully convolutional network (`fθ`) to approximate R_v.
 - Input to `fθ`: The attention map from the *first layer* of the LLM (A_0). Specifically, the submap A_0_u→v ∈ ℝ^(N_u × N_v) is extracted, representing attention scores from textual instruction tokens to visual tokens.
 - This submap is then averaged across the N_u textual instruction tokens to obtain a compact representation A_0_v ∈ ℝ^(1×N_v).
 - `fθ` is implemented as a five-layer 1D depthwise separable convolutional network.
 - The output `˜R_v = fθ(A_0_v)` is a predicted visual relevance distribution, with a softmax operation applied at the final layer.
 - A separate `fθ` instance is trained for each MLLM to capture its specific explainability patterns.
3. **Training Objectives:**
 - **Training Labels (R*_v):** Derived from the ground-truth R_v (from Phase 1). The bottom 50% of R_v values are masked (set to zero), and the remaining values are normalized into a probability distribution (by dividing by their sum, not softmax) to preserve relative differences.
 - **Loss Function:** Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence is used to minimize the difference between the predicted `˜R_v` and the target `R*_v`: L_KL = KL(R*_v || ˜R_v).
 - **Training Data:** Subsets of high-quality, general-domain instruction-following datasets (LLaVA-Video for videos, Infinity-MM for images), each comprising approximately 10K samples. A crucial filtering step is applied: only samples for which the target MLLM produces a *correct* answer are retained for training `fθ`. This ensures `fθ` learns from high-quality, relevant importance signals.
 - **Efficiency:** Training of `fθ` is highly efficient (e.g., half an hour for image data, less than four hours for video data on a single A100 GPU).
4. **Overall Inference Framework:** The trained `fθ` is integrated into the MLLM inference pipeline. It takes the first-layer attention map (A_0_v) to predict `˜R_v`, which then guides the token compression of E_v into ˆE_v *before* any further LLM computation. This significantly reduces computational and memory costs during both the prefill and decode phases.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** LLaVA-OneVision-7B, Qwen2-VL-7B, VILA1.5-8B (chosen for their architectural diversity and advanced visual processing capabilities).
- **Evaluation Tasks:** 13 benchmarks covering a wide range of image (MME, MMStar, MMVet, SEED-Bench, POPE, TextVQA, MMBench) and video (Video-MME, MVBench, MMBench-Video, NExT-QA, ActivityNetQA) understanding abilities.
- **Baselines:** State-of-the-art task-related token compression methods (FastV, Pyramid-Drop, Dart) that primarily operate in intermediate LLM layers.
- **Metrics:** Performance preservation (accuracy/score), FLOPs, total inference time, prefilling time, and KV cache memory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed explainability-based visual token compression method.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of Compression with Explainability (R_v-Guided Compression)

Initial experiments validated the core hypothesis: explainability methods can effectively identify task-relevant visual tokens.
- **Performance Preservation:** When using the relevance scores (R_v) directly derived from the explainability method to guide token compression, performance was well-preserved across models and tasks.
 - For Qwen2-VL, a 50% reduction in visual tokens preserved over 99% of its original performance on both image and video benchmarks.
 - LLaVA-OneVision retained 99.1% of its video performance even with only 25% of tokens.
 - VILA1.5 maintained 98% of its image performance and nearly unchanged video performance when visual tokens were reduced by 50%.
- **Conclusion:** These results indicate that the explanation outcomes faithfully capture the visual information essential for the MLLM to correctly answer questions, and pruning less relevant tokens based on R_v does not substantially compromise model performance.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Explainability-Based Compressor Learning (˜R_v-Guided Compression)

The learned lightweight compressor (fθ), which predicts relevance scores (˜R_v) from the first-layer attention map, demonstrated strong performance and efficiency.

- **Performance on Image Benchmarks (Table 2 & 8):**
 - At a 50% token retention rate, the proposed compressor consistently outperformed baselines (FastV, Pyramid-Drop, Dart) at comparable FLOPs. Average performance improvements were 2.6% for LLaVA-OneVision and 1.2% for Qwen2-VL across all evaluated benchmarks (MME, MMStar, MMVet, SEED-Bench, POPE, TextVQA, MMBench, GQA, SQA, VizWiz, ChartQA, DocVQA, OCRBench).
 - Under a more aggressive 25% retention rate, these performance gains increased to 4.7% and 1.6% respectively, highlighting the robustness of the method at higher compression rates.
- **Performance on Video Benchmarks (Table 3 & 9):**
 - The compressor consistently outperformed baselines across all evaluated models (LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL, VILA1.5) and retention ratios (50%, 25%, 10%).
 - LLaVA-OneVision and VILA1.5 maintained 100% performance when 50% of visual tokens were pruned.
 - Even at a 10% retention ratio, the method achieved average improvements of 3.7%, 3.2%, and 1.9% for LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL, and VILA1.5, respectively, over the best-performing baseline, while achieving the lowest FLOPs.
 - This indicates that the task-related compression complements existing task-agnostic compression mechanisms in models like Qwen2-VL and VILA1.5.
- **Generalization to Larger Images and Longer Videos (Figure 3, Tables 10-13):**
 - The trained fθ generalized well to unseen larger image sizes and longer video durations, consistently outperforming FastV and achieving competitive results with specialized video compression methods (e.g., PruneVID, FastVID) on challenging benchmarks like MMStar and MVBench, often with lower FLOPs.
- **Inference Efficiency (Table 4 & 14):**
 - The method achieved substantial reductions in computational and memory overhead.
 - **FLOPs:** Achieved the lowest FLOPs compared to baselines. For Qwen2-VL at 25% retention, FLOPs were reduced to 0.24x compared to the baseline 1.00x (Table 4).
 - **Prefilling Time:** Demonstrated significant speedups in the prefilling stage (e.g., 1.60x for Qwen2-VL on MMStar, 1.67x on MMVet).
 - **KV Cache Memory:** Substantially reduced KV cache footprint (e.g., from 71.2MB to 17.8MB for Qwen2-VL on MMStar).
 - **Total Inference Time:** While providing significant prefill and KV-cache benefits, the total inference time remained comparable to other methods.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Explainability Method Aggregation (Table 5):** Gradient-weighted aggregation of multi-head attention maps for R_v generation consistently yielded better performance than simple averaging, supporting the importance of differentiated head contributions.
- **Convolutional Network Depth (Table 6):** A 5-layer convolutional network for fθ achieved the best performance. Shallower networks lacked capacity, while deeper networks (7 or 10 layers) showed slight degradation, suggesting potential overfitting or optimization difficulties for the task's complexity.
- **Attention Layer Index as Input (Table 7):** Using the first-layer attention map (A_0) as input for fθ was found to be sufficient and efficient. While deeper layers (up to layer 4) offered marginal performance improvements (+0.1% to +0.6%), they incurred significant computational overhead, contradicting the goal of an efficient pre-LLM compressor.

#### 4.4. Discussion

The effectiveness of the lightweight fθ is attributed to three factors: (i) `fθ` learns a structurally matched input-output mapping (attention maps compose relevance maps); (ii) shallow-layer attention maps contain vital information for output generation; and (iii) the learning target is simplified to identifying relatively large relevance values (top 50%), rather than a precise one-to-one mapping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel and validated paradigm for visual token compression in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Compression Location:** The work fundamentally challenges the prevailing assumption that task-related visual token compression must occur in the intermediate layers of the LLM. By demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness of compression at the *LLM input stage*, it opens a new avenue for research and development in MLLM efficiency. This input-stage compression is particularly impactful as it reduces the computational burden from the earliest point in the LLM processing pipeline.

2. **Enhanced Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The most immediate impact is the significant reduction in computational costs (FLOPs) and memory footprint (KV-cache) during MLLM inference. By pruning task-irrelevant tokens before LLM computation, the method achieves faster prefilling times and lower memory requirements. This directly addresses critical bottlenecks that limit the deployment of MLLMs, enabling them to process higher-resolution images and longer video sequences more efficiently. This advancement can facilitate broader application of MLLMs in resource-constrained environments or for real-time tasks.

3. **Model-Agnostic and Architecture-Independent Deployment:** The proposed compressor design, which learns to approximate explainability, is MLLM-agnostic. This means it can be applied to diverse MLLM architectures (demonstrated across LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL, and VILA1.5) without requiring modifications or retraining of the underlying LLM. This broad applicability enhances the transferability and utility of the method, making it a versatile tool for optimizing various MLLM frameworks.

4. **High Performance Preservation:** Crucially, the efficiency gains are achieved with minimal or negligible performance loss. The method consistently retains a high percentage of baseline performance (often >97% or even 100% in some cases) even at substantial compression ratios (e.g., 75% for video tokens). This balance between efficiency and performance is vital for practical adoption.

5. **Leveraging Explainability for Optimization:** The research highlights the utility of explainability methods not just for interpreting model decisions, but also for *guiding model optimization*. By using relevance scores to identify essential tokens, explainability transitions from a diagnostic tool to a prescriptive mechanism for improving model efficiency. This may inspire further integration of interpretability techniques into the design and optimization of complex deep learning models.

6. **Foundation for Future MLLM Development:** The findings lay a foundation for future research. The authors note that the current generation of relevance scores is resource-intensive, prompting future exploration into more efficient compressor training for higher-resolution and longer sequences. Additionally, the concept of using relevance scores to guide token compression *during MLLM training* (rather than as a post-hoc learning task) could lead to end-to-end optimized models that are inherently more efficient and task-aware from their inception.

In conclusion, this work represents a significant step towards making MLLMs more efficient, scalable, and practically deployable. By introducing a novel, explainability-guided approach to input-stage token compression, it offers a versatile solution to a prevalent challenge in multimodal AI, with the potential to broadly impact the development and application of advanced vision-language models.
