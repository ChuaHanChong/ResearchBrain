# TIR-Bench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Agentic Thinking-with-Images Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Laboratory and collaborating institutions developed TIR-BENCH, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate multimodal large language models' ability to 'think with images' by dynamically manipulating visual inputs with tools. The benchmark revealed that models with explicit agentic tool-use capabilities achieved up to 46% accuracy, significantly outperforming non-agentic models on tasks requiring active visual interaction.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily evaluate static visual input and recognition, neglecting dynamic, tool-based image manipulation capabilities.
- Current MLLMs often treat visual inputs as unalterable, limiting their reasoning to linguistic domains despite the emergence of agentic functionalities.
- There was a lack of a comprehensive evaluation framework for "thinking-with-images" where models actively transform visual information to inform subsequent reasoning.

## Method

- Introduced ==TIR-BENCH==, a benchmark featuring 13 diverse tasks spanning spatial reasoning, visual perception, and mathematics, requiring active, ==tool-based visual manipulation== (e.g., drawing lines, pixel analysis, image enhancement).
- Designed tasks to be inherently difficult, demanding multi-step, programmatic strategies to differentiate genuine =="thinking-with-images"== from superficial recognition.
- Evaluated 22 leading MLLMs, including open-source, proprietary, and explicit ==tool-using models==, using objective accuracy metrics and pilot studies on ==function-calling== and fine-tuning strategies.

## Results

- TIR-BENCH proved universally challenging, with the highest overall accuracy being **46%** achieved by o3-TU, underscoring its effectiveness in assessing advanced reasoning.
- Agentic tool-using MLLMs (e.g., o3-TU) demonstrated substantially higher performance, outperforming the best non-tool-using model (Gemini-2.5-pro) by nearly **17%** on average.
- Pilot studies showed o3 had superior function-calling ability, explicit prompting strategies improved performance, and "Tool-use SFT" significantly surpassed "Direct SFT" on the Rotated Image OCR task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agentic tool-use capabilities are essential for solving tasks requiring dynamic visual interaction, with models like o3-TU significantly outperforming non-tool-using MLLMs.
> - Complex tasks, such as the Jigsaw Game, remain highly challenging even for advanced tool-using models, indicating specific areas for future development in multimodal reasoning.
> - "Tool-use SFT" (fine-tuning models to leverage tools) is more effective and scales better with data size than "Direct SFT" for image-operation tasks, suggesting a promising training paradigm.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is strategically positioned at the cutting edge of multimodal AI, specifically addressing the emerging paradigm of "thinking-with-images" in agentic MLLMs.

**Evolution of Visual Reasoning:** Historically, MLLMs have advanced significantly through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which decomposes complex problems into sequential textual steps. However, as the authors note, these methods predominantly treat visual inputs as "static, unalterable input," limiting reasoning to the linguistic domain. The advent of models like OpenAI's o3 has introduced a new capability: dynamically generating and executing tools (e.g., Python code) to manipulate images, effectively creating new visual information to inform subsequent reasoning steps. This active interaction with images is termed "thinking-with-images" and represents a crucial shift from passive visual understanding to active visual problem-solving.

**Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** The paper critically points out a significant gap in current evaluation methodologies. While numerous MLLM benchmarks exist (e.g., V* Bench, HR-Bench), even those designed for agentic capabilities, such as visual search in high-resolution images, are largely confined to basic operations like localization and cropping. These benchmarks fail to capture the broader spectrum of "thinking-with-images" abilities, which includes diverse tool-based manipulations such as zooming, rotating, enhancing contrast, drawing auxiliary lines, or performing pixel-level analysis. The authors argue that such a narrow focus overlooks the sophisticated, dynamic, and tool-dependent reasoning that advanced MLLMs are beginning to exhibit.

**The Rise of Thinking-with-Images:** The paper cites several contemporary works that attempt to integrate tool-use for visual reasoning, such as Visual Sketchpad, CoGCoM, DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner, and others. However, it distinguishes between methods that rely on fixed external visual parsers (e.g., detection/segmentation models) and those that adopt general-purpose programming languages like Python (e.g., ViperGPT, OpenAI's o-series, PyVision). The latter approach, by enabling MLLMs to dynamically write and execute code, supports more general and flexible reasoning, aligning with the "thinking-with-images" paradigm. TIR-Bench is designed to specifically evaluate this more advanced, flexible form of agentic visual reasoning. By doing so, it directly contributes to the development and understanding of MLLMs that can autonomously interact with and transform visual information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the introduction of TIR-Bench are clear and directly address the identified gaps in the research landscape:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive Benchmark for Agentic Thinking-with-Images:** The core objective is to create "TIR-Bench," a diverse and challenging benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the advanced "thinking-with-images" capabilities of agentic MLLMs. This aims to move beyond simple visual search tasks to encompass a wide array of tool-based visual manipulations.
2. **Assess Integrated Multimodal Reasoning:** The benchmark seeks to evaluate models that can integrate linguistic reasoning with active, tool-driven visual processing in a chain-of-thought manner. The motivation is to test models' abilities to generate new visual information by manipulating input images and then using these transformed visuals to inform subsequent linguistic reasoning steps.
3. **Identify Limitations of Current MLLMs:** By designing tasks that are intentionally unsolvable without multi-step, tool-based strategies, the authors aim to probe the limitations of current MLLMs. This includes distinguishing true "thinking-with-images" reasoning from simpler visual recognition and highlighting areas where models struggle.
4. **Guide Future MLLM Development:** The benchmark is motivated by the need to provide a robust evaluation framework that can guide the development of future MLLMs. By revealing performance disparities and specific challenges, TIR-Bench aims to encourage research into more sophisticated agentic architectures and training methodologies.
5. **Evaluate Tool-Use Proficiency and Fine-Tuning Strategies:** The paper also aims to assess the function-calling proficiency of various MLLMs and conduct pilot studies comparing direct supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with agentic SFT. This provides initial insights into effective training strategies for models that perform image operations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing and evaluating TIR-Bench is multifaceted, focusing on task design, data collection, and comprehensive model assessment.

**TIR-Bench Design Philosophy:**
The benchmark is meticulously designed around four key guidelines:
1. **Diverse, Application-Grounded Tasks:** TIR-Bench comprises 13 distinct tasks spanning spatial reasoning, visual perception, and mathematics, mirroring real-world scenarios where static image analysis is insufficient.
2. **Comprehensive Skill Assessment:** Tasks require a range of programmatic skills, such as drawing auxiliary lines, pixel-level analysis, enhancing low-light images, correcting image orientation, solving mazes, and reassembling jigsaw pieces. This ensures a holistic evaluation of agentic capabilities.
3. **Probing Model Limitations:** Each task is inherently difficult, demanding multi-step, tool-based strategies, which serves to differentiate genuine "thinking-with-images" from superficial recognition.
4. **Deterministic Evaluation:** All tasks are designed with objectively verifiable answers, ensuring reliable and reproducible evaluations. Many samples are newly annotated or generated to minimize data contamination risks.

**Task Descriptions:**
TIR-Bench includes 13 tasks, each requiring specific tool-based interactions:
- **Color VQA, Proportion VQA:** Programmatic analysis of color composition or object proportion.
- **Rotated Image OCR:** Identifying and correcting image orientation before OCR.
- **Symbolic Reasoning, Math Problems:** Applying abstract logic or geometric reasoning by drawing auxiliary lines or coordinate systems.
- **Maze:** Advanced spatial planning and pathfinding with image processing tools.
- **Word Search, Spot the Difference:** Fine-grained visual discrimination via pixel-level comparison or programmatic image subtraction.
- **Low-Light Image VQA:** Programmatic image enhancement (contrast/brightness) to reveal details.
- **Instrument Reading, Visual Search:** Locating, cropping, and enlarging specific regions for accurate reading or multi-turn iterative search.
- **Jigsaw Puzzle, Rotation Game:** Complex spatial reasoning and iterative action-correction loops (segmentation, reassembly; testing rotation angles).

**Data Collection:**
Data for the 13 tasks was collected through a combination of newly created/annotated data (Math VQA, Symbolic Reasoning, Low-Light VQA, Instrument Reading), curation from existing datasets with specific filtering for tool-use necessity (Color VQA, Proportion VQA, Rotated Image OCR, Visual Search), and programmatic generation (Jigsaw, Maze, Rotation, Word Search). This hybrid approach ensures both diversity and the challenging nature of the tasks. The total benchmark comprises 1215 examples, with a mix of multi-choice and free-form questions.

**Model Evaluation Setup:**
- **Model Selection:** 22 leading MLLMs were evaluated, categorized into:
 - **Open-source models:** 11 models from LLaVA, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL3 families (3B to 78B parameters).
 - **Proprietary models:** 7 models including GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5 (Flash and Pro), Grok-4, o4-mini, and o3.
 - **Tool-using MLLMs:** DeepEyes, PyVision, o4-mini-TU, and o3-TU (models with explicit tool-use augmentation, e.g., code interpreters).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Accuracy was calculated by comparing extracted answers (via GPT-4o) against ground truth. For grounding-type problems (e.g., Jigsaw Game, Spot the Difference), Intersection over Union (IoU) was used.
- **Inference:** All evaluations were conducted in a zero-shot manner for fair comparison and generalization. Proprietary models used official APIs, while open models ran on NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
- **Pilot Studies:**
 - **Function Calling:** The Rotation Game task was used to test models' abilities to output correct degree parameters for a predefined rotate function and to generate the full rotation code. Three prompt variations (baseline, hint for function, explicit systematic testing) were used.
 - **Fine-tuning Comparison:** The Rotated Image OCR task was used to compare "Direct SFT" (mapping rotated image to text) vs. "Tool-Use SFT" (learning to rotate, then read) on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B across varying data scales (1k-15k samples).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments reveal several critical insights into the capabilities of current MLLMs concerning agentic thinking-with-images.

1. **TIR-Bench is Universally Challenging:** The benchmark proved exceptionally difficult across all models tested, with the highest overall accuracy being only 46% (achieved by o3-TU). This underscores TIR-Bench's effectiveness in assessing advanced reasoning capabilities that go beyond current state-of-the-art MLLMs.

2. **Traditional Non-Agentic Models Perform Poorly:** MLLMs without explicit tool-use capabilities, including most open-source and proprietary models, showed significantly poor performance. Many open-source models performed close to or slightly above random guess levels. Even the best non-tool-using model, Gemini-2.5-pro, surpassed random guess by only 15%, highlighting their inability to solve tasks requiring dynamic visual manipulation.

3. **Agentic Tool-Use is Essential:** Models equipped with agentic tool-use capabilities, such as o3-TU and o4-mini-TU, demonstrated substantially higher performance. o3-TU achieved the highest average accuracy (46%), outperforming Gemini-2.5-Pro by nearly 17% and its non-tool-using counterpart (o3) by 19%. These models excelled particularly in tasks involving straightforward image manipulation like the Rotation Game, Rotated Image OCR, and Word Search, winning 10 out of 14 categories. PyVision also showed a 13% accuracy improvement over its base GPT-4.1.

4. **Nuanced Performance and Remaining Challenges:** While tool-using models significantly improved, their performance was not uniform. Complex tasks like Jigsaw Game still presented a major challenge, with o3-TU's accuracy remaining low. Interestingly, o3-TU's performance on Proportion VQA slightly decreased compared to o3, which the authors attribute to its current lack of capability to call external segmentation models (like SAM) required for accurate object mask generation, instead relying on its general code-writing ability for image manipulation. This finding points to a specific limitation in tool-use integration, where models might need more specialized external tools for certain tasks.

5. **Function Calling and Prompting Strategies:**
 - o3 demonstrated superior function-calling ability, with o4-mini closely following.
 - Explicit prompting strategies, particularly those instructing models to systematically test options, significantly improved performance. This suggests that models may require explicit guidance to effectively utilize their function-calling capabilities.
 - Newer models (e.g., o3) exhibited a much higher average number of function calls, indicating better training for iterative tool interaction.

6. **Fine-Tuning Implications:**
 - "Tool-use SFT" significantly outperformed "Direct SFT" on the Rotated Image OCR task, suggesting that training models to leverage tools for image manipulation is more effective than directly fine-tuning them on transformed visual inputs.
 - Tool-use SFT showed positive scaling with data size, whereas Direct SFT did not, implying that simply scaling data is insufficient for tasks requiring image-based reasoning without an agentic approach.
 - The faster loss reduction in Tool-use SFT, despite a higher initial loss (due to the base model not being pretrained with function-calling), suggests that restoring image orientation before text output avoids "forgetting" issues common when models are directly fine-tuned on unfamiliar data distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TIR-Bench makes a highly significant contribution to the field of multimodal AI, with several potential impacts:

1. **Advancing MLLM Evaluation:** By providing the first comprehensive benchmark for agentic "thinking-with-images" capabilities, TIR-Bench fills a critical gap in existing evaluation frameworks. It pushes the boundaries of MLLM assessment beyond static image understanding, reflecting the capabilities of next-generation models. This will enable researchers to more accurately gauge the progress of MLLMs in complex, interactive visual reasoning.

2. **Guiding Future Research and Development:** The benchmark's challenging nature and the detailed performance analysis provide clear directions for future research. It highlights the paramount importance of developing robust tool-use integration and agentic reasoning architectures in MLLMs. Future models will need to not only understand images but also actively manipulate them as part of their problem-solving process. The insights into specific task failures (e.g., Jigsaw, Proportion VQA) offer concrete targets for improvement.

3. **Encouraging Agentic AI Systems:** TIR-Bench explicitly promotes the development of more autonomous and capable AI agents that can interact dynamically with their environment, including actively transforming visual information. This aligns with a broader trend in AI toward more general-purpose, agentic intelligence.

4. **Informing Training Strategies:** The pilot studies on function calling and fine-tuning offer valuable initial guidance for practitioners. The finding that agentic fine-tuning is more effective than direct SFT for image-operation tasks suggests that training models on "how to use tools" rather than simply "what to see" is a more promising avenue for building robust MLLMs. This has direct implications for data generation and instructional fine-tuning methodologies.

5. **Benchmarking for Real-World Applications:** The diverse, application-grounded tasks in TIR-Bench are relevant to numerous real-world applications where dynamic image processing is crucial. This includes fields like medical imaging, robotics, scientific discovery, and advanced user interfaces, where AI agents might need to enhance, manipulate, or analyze images in an interactive fashion.

In conclusion, TIR-Bench is a timely and essential contribution that not only rigorously evaluates the nascent capabilities of agentic MLLMs but also lays down a challenge for the field, urging researchers to develop models that genuinely "think with images" to solve complex visual problems.
