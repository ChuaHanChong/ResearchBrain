# EditReward: A Human-Aligned Reward Model for Instruction-Guided Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> A human-aligned reward model, EDITREWARD, accurately assesses instruction-guided image edits, leveraging a meticulously annotated 200K preference pair dataset. This model achieves state-of-the-art evaluation performance and enables the curation of high-quality training data, significantly advancing open-source image editing capabilities.

## Problem

- Open-source instruction-guided image editing models lag behind proprietary solutions due to a lack of reliable, human-aligned reward mechanisms.
- Existing evaluation methods like perceptual scores, feature scores, and general Vision-Language Models (VLMs) fail to capture precise editing semantics or align with nuanced human preferences.
- Fine-tuning general VLMs for this task is hindered by noisy, low inter-annotator agreement in crowd-sourced annotations or biased pseudo-labels from proprietary models.

## Method

- Constructed ==EDITREWARD-DATA==, a large-scale (over 200K preference pairs) and high-fidelity human preference dataset annotated by experts across two dimensions: Instruction Following and Visual Quality.
- Developed ==EDITREWARD==, a specialized reward model using VLM backbones, a ==multi-dimensional uncertainty-aware ranking framework==, and a novel strategy to disentangle ties in human preferences.
- Introduced ==EDITREWARD-BENCH==, a robust benchmark with challenging multi-way preference comparison tasks to more accurately evaluate and differentiate reward models.

## Results

- EDITREWARD achieved new state-of-the-art results on GenAI-Bench (**65.72%** accuracy) and AURORA-Bench (**63.62%** accuracy), outperforming proprietary models like GPT-5 and OpenAI-GPT-4o.
- On the challenging EDITREWARD-BENCH, EDITREWARD scored **38.42%**, surpassing leading proprietary models like Gemini-2.5-Flash (**38.02%**) and GPT-5 (**37.81%**).
- Utilizing EDITREWARD for data curation, fine-tuning the Step1X-Edit model on a 20K high-quality subset improved its GEdit-Bench score from **6.444** to **7.086**, making it competitive with top-tier editors like Doubao.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-dimensional human annotation and uncertainty-aware ranking are critical for accurately capturing the nuanced quality of instruction-guided image edits.
> - Decomposing tied preference pairs into distinct dimensional preferences provides richer supervisory signals, leading to more stable and performant model training.
> - Data quality, as assessed by a highly human-aligned reward model, is more impactful than mere data quantity for training superior downstream image editing models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Instruction-guided image editing is a rapidly evolving field, enabling users to modify images intuitively using natural language commands. Recent advancements have been largely driven by sophisticated deep learning models, particularly those leveraging diffusion models. The research landscape is broadly divided between powerful, proprietary closed-source models and their open-source counterparts.

**Closed-source models** like OpenAI's GPT-Image-1, Seedream, and Google's Nano-Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) have demonstrated impressive capabilities, often attributed to their access to vast, high-quality, in-house private training datasets. These models set the frontier for performance and user experience.

**Open-source models**, while making progress, still lag behind their closed-source counterparts. The paper identifies a critical bottleneck for open-source development: the **lack of a reliable, human-aligned reward model** to effectively scale up high-quality synthetic training data. Existing open-source image editing datasets (e.g., ImgEdit, HQ-Edit, GPT-Image-Edit-1.5M) are often generated through automatic data synthesis pipelines, filtered by various ad-hoc reward mechanisms.

The limitations of these existing reward mechanisms are categorized by the authors:
- **Perceptual scores** (e.g., LPIPS) fail to capture semantic alignment with user instructions.
- **Feature scores** (e.g., CLIP) struggle to capture editing semantics comprehensively.
- **VLM-as-a-judge models** (e.g., VIEScore), while powerful, are general-purpose Vision-Language Models not specifically optimized for the nuances of image editing tasks, leading to weak alignment with human preferences.
- **Fine-tuned VLMs** based on noisy, crowd-sourced preference annotations suffer from inconsistency and low inter-annotator agreement. Others relying on pseudo-labels from proprietary models inherit their biases and noise.

EDITREWARD directly addresses this critical gap. By constructing a large-scale, high-fidelity human preference dataset (EDITREWARD-DATA) specifically for instruction-guided image editing, and then training a specialized reward model (EDITREWARD) on this data, the authors aim to provide the open-source community with a robust tool. This work positions itself as a foundational step to enable open-source models to catch up with proprietary frontiers by providing a means to generate and filter high-quality training data, thereby improving model alignment with human intent and visual quality standards. It contributes to the broader field of AI evaluation and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), extending these principles to the complex domain of multimodal image editing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **address the critical bottleneck hindering the advancement of open-source instruction-guided image editing models: the absence of a reliable, human-aligned reward model capable of scaling up high-quality synthetic training data.**

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Bridging the Gap:** To help open-source image editing models close the performance gap with advanced closed-source counterparts, whose superiority is partly attributed to their proprietary, high-quality training data and reward systems.
- **Improving Data Quality:** To overcome the limitations of existing automatically synthesized datasets and ad-hoc reward mechanisms, which suffer from weak human alignment, noise, and biases. The goal is to provide a more accurate signal for what constitutes a "good" image edit according to human preferences.
- **Enabling Scalable Data Curation:** To develop a reward model that can effectively filter large pools of synthetically generated data, identifying high-quality examples suitable for training next-generation image editing models, thereby reducing the need for costly manual annotation for every training sample.
- **Robust Evaluation:** To introduce a more challenging and reliable benchmark for evaluating instruction-guided image editing models, moving beyond simpler pairwise comparisons to multi-way preference rankings that better reflect human discriminative capabilities.
- **Community Empowerment:** To release a high-quality dataset, a trained reward model, and a new benchmark to foster open research and accelerate progress in the field.

In essence, the authors are motivated to establish a new gold standard for evaluating and curating data for instruction-guided image editing, thereby democratizing access to tools that can elevate the quality of open-source generative AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research revolves around the meticulous construction of a high-quality human preference dataset, the development of a specialized reward model trained on this data, and the creation of a robust evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. EDITREWARD-DATA Construction

The foundation of EDITREWARD is **EDITREWARD-DATA**, a large-scale, high-fidelity human preference dataset comprising over 200,000 preference pairs.
- **Source Data Collection:** The dataset started with 9,557 unique instruction-image pairs collected from six established editing benchmarks (GEdit-Bench, ImgEdit-Bench, MagicBrush, AnyEdit, EmuEdit) and an internal set, ensuring broad coverage and ecological validity.
- **Candidate Generation:** For each instruction-image pair, 12 candidate edited images were generated using six state-of-the-art models (Step1X-Edit, Flux-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit, BAGEL, Ovis-U1, OmniGen2) with multiple random seeds. This strategy aimed to create a diverse pool of edits spanning a wide range of quality and to mitigate model-specific biases.
- **Multi-Dimensional Human Annotation:** Seven candidates were randomly sampled from the pool for human evaluation. Trained annotators, following a rigorous and standardized protocol, scored each image on a 4-point Likert scale (1=Poor to 4=Excellent) along two critical dimensions:
 1. **Instruction Following (IF):** Assessing semantic accuracy, completeness, and absence of unprompted changes.
 2. **Visual Quality (VQ):** Evaluating plausibility, artifact-free rendering, and aesthetics.
 This multi-dimensional rubric provides more granular and informative feedback than a single holistic score.
- **Quality Control:** The annotation process included initial pilot studies, formal training and calibration of annotators, and continuous random sampling with cross-checking to maintain high Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA). Krippendorff's Alpha (α) values of 0.668 for IF and 0.597 for VQ confirmed strong human consistency and empirically validated the inherent subjectivity difference between IF and VQ, supporting the multi-dimensional design.

#### 3.2. EDITREWARD-BENCH Construction

To rigorously assess reward models, the authors introduce **EDITREWARD-BENCH**, a new benchmark built upon 500 high-quality groups from the EDITREWARD-DATA candidate pool.
- **Curated for Challenge:** The benchmark prioritizes challenging cases with small score differences between competing edits to enhance discriminative power.
- **Multi-Way Preference Comparison:** The key innovation is a multi-way preference comparison protocol, extending beyond pairwise judgments to include ternary (A, B, C) and quaternary (A, B, C, D) tuples. Correctness for a tuple requires simultaneously predicting all pairwise relations within it, offering a more comprehensive test of ranking consistency.
- **Expert Annotation:** Each group was annotated by three independent experts using the same two-dimensional rubric, with a cross-check protocol to resolve disagreements and ensure reliability.

#### 3.3. EDITREWARD Model Architecture

The EDITREWARD model leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM) as its backbone, recognizing the inherent tri-modal nature of image editing evaluation (source image, text prompt, edited image).
- **Backbone:** The model uses powerful VLMs like Qwen2.5-VL or MiMo-VL as feature extractors (Hψ). All parameters of the backbone are unfrozen and trainable during fine-tuning.
- **Reward Head:** An MLP reward head (Rω) projects the latent representation from the VLM backbone to a scalar score.
- **Multi-Dimensional Output:** Departing from previous approaches, the reward head outputs a separate Gaussian distribution (sᵢ,d ∼ N(μᵢ,d, σ²ᵢ,d)) for each evaluation dimension (IF and VQ), allowing it to capture the multi-faceted nature of edit quality and inherent uncertainty.

#### 3.4. Multi-Dimensional Uncertainty-Aware Ranking and Loss Functions

To train the model with its multi-dimensional output, two distinct loss functions were explored:
- **Multi-Dimensional Uncertainty-Aware Ranking Loss:** This approach extends the probabilistic ranking framework by HPSv3. The two dimensional mean scores (μᵢ,1, μᵢ,2) for each candidate image are aggregated into a single effective mean score (μaggᵢ). Three aggregation strategies were investigated: pessimistic minimum, balanced average (1/2(μᵢ,1 + μᵢ,2)), and direct summation (μᵢ,1 + μᵢ,2). The aggregated means and predicted uncertainties (σ²) are then used to compute preference probability, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth preference (Lrank).
- **Aggregated Score Regression:** Alternatively, training is framed as a direct regression task. The aggregated score distribution is treated as a Gaussian, and the model is optimized by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the mean of this aggregated distribution and a transformed sum of the ground-truth scores (Lreg).

Ablation studies confirmed that the pair-wise uncertainty model with multiple independent heads and a balanced mean aggregation strategy performed best.

#### 3.5. Disentangling Ties via Dimensional Preference

A novel data augmentation strategy was introduced to extract richer supervisory signals from "tied" preference pairs (where overall quality is similar).
- **Decomposition of Ties:** For tie pairs (IA, IB)tie, if one image is preferred on one dimension and the other on the second (e.g., IA is better on IF, IB is better on VQ), the tie is decomposed into two new training samples with opposing preference labels (IA ≻ IB and IB ≻ IA) based on their dimensional advantages. This forces the model to learn nuanced trade-offs and provides a more stable training dynamic.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

- **Training Data:** 200K pairwise preference samples from EDITREWARD-DATA.
- **Backbones:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B and MiMo-VL-7B.
- **Hardware:** 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs.
- **Epochs:** 2 epochs.
- **Hyperparameters:** Learning rate of 2 × 10⁻⁶ with cosine learning rate schedule and 0.05 warm-up ratio. Per-GPU batch size of 2, total effective batch size of 16.
- **Preprocessing:** Images resized to 448 × 448 pixels while preserving aspect ratios.

This comprehensive methodology, from data curation to model training and robust evaluation, underscores a rigorous scientific approach to developing a highly aligned reward model for image editing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results demonstrating EDITREWARD's superior performance and practical utility.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Human Alignment

- **Public Benchmarks:** EDITREWARD (on MiMo-VL-7B) achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on key public benchmarks:
 - **GenAI-Bench:** 65.72%, significantly outperforming strong proprietary models like GPT-5 (59.61%) and the leading open-source method ADIEE (59.96%).
 - **AURORA-Bench:** 63.62%, showing a substantial gain over OpenAI-GPT-4o (50.81%).
 - **ImagenHub:** EDITREWARD (on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves a competitive Spearman rank correlation of 36.18%, closely following GPT-4o.
- **EDITREWARD-BENCH:** On the newly proposed, challenging EDITREWARD-BENCH, EDITREWARD (on MiMo-VL-7B) achieves the highest score of 38.42%, surpassing specialized proprietary models like Gemini-2.5-Flash (38.02%) and GPT-5 (37.81%). This validates the benchmark's ability to differentiate between high-performing models and highlights EDITREWARD's fine-grained capabilities.

#### 4.2. Impact of the Training Paradigm

- The study demonstrates a massive performance uplift by applying the EDITREWARD training methodology to base VLMs. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Inst's performance on GenAI-Bench jumped from 40.48% to 63.97% after being trained as EDITREWARD (on Qwen2.5-VL-7B), a **+23.5 point improvement**. This highlights that the proposed framework dramatically enhances a VLM's alignment with human judgments, regardless of the strong backbone.
- The framework's robustness is confirmed by consistent strong performance across different backbones (Qwen and MiMo-VL), indicating its generalizability.

#### 4.3. Application: EDITREWARD as a Reward Model for Data Curation

- **Data Filtering for Fine-tuning:** EDITREWARD was used to score 46,000 examples from the noisy ShareGPT-4o-Image dataset. A high-quality subset of 20,000 examples (Top 20K) was selected for fine-tuning the Step1X-Edit model.
- **Significant Performance Improvement:** Step1X-Edit, when fine-tuned on the EDITREWARD-filtered Top 20K subset, achieved an overall GEdit-Bench-EN score of 7.086. This is a substantial improvement over the original Step1X-Edit baseline (6.444) and the same model trained on the *full, noisy* 46K dataset (6.780).
- **Data Quality over Quantity:** Sensitivity analysis revealed that the Top 20K subset was optimal, outperforming both the Top 10K (higher signal-to-noise ratio but less volume) and the Top 30K (more data but diluted by lower quality). This finding confirms that data quality, as judged by EDITREWARD, is more impactful than sheer quantity, effectively pruning noisy examples.
- **Competitiveness:** This uplift made the open-source Step1X-Edit model competitive with, and in some cases superior to, top-tier proprietary editors like Doubao (6.983).

#### 4.4. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization Analysis

- On challenging OOD categories (Text-in-Image / OCR and Style Transfer) sourced from Open Images, EDITREWARD (on MiMo-VL-7B-SFT) achieved competitive performance compared to GPT-4o, maintaining strong alignment despite the inherent difficulties of these tasks for VLMs. This demonstrates EDITREWARD's robustness beyond its training distribution.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Loss Type:** Pair-wise uncertainty model significantly outperformed point-wise regression (63.97% vs. 49.62% on GenAI-Bench), confirming the effectiveness of modeling relative preferences.
- **Head Type:** Using multiple independent heads for IF and VQ predictions yielded better performance (63.97%) than a shared head architecture (60.17%), indicating that specialized heads better capture disentangled evaluation dimensions.
- **Aggregation Strategy:** The balanced mean aggregation strategy (for combining IF and VQ scores) provided the most consistent and highest performance.
- **Model Backbone:** Performance consistently increased with stronger foundation models (Qwen2.5-VL-3B to 7B, then MiMo-VL-7B), demonstrating the framework's backbone-agnostic nature and scalability.
- **Data Scale & Tie Disentanglement:** Increasing data from 130K to 200K and implementing the tie-disentanglement strategy led to consistent performance gains across most benchmarks, with notable improvements on ImagenHub.

These findings collectively underscore EDITREWARD's effectiveness as a human-aligned reward model, its practical utility in data curation, and the robustness of its underlying methodology.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EDITREWARD project makes several significant contributions that could profoundly impact the field of instruction-guided image editing, particularly for the open-source community.

- **Addressing a Critical Bottleneck:** The most direct significance is the solution to the long-standing bottleneck of lacking a reliable, human-aligned reward model for open-source image editing. This work provides a concrete and effective tool to overcome this limitation, which has historically hindered open-source models from matching the performance of proprietary systems.
- **High-Quality Open Resources:** The release of **EDITREWARD-DATA** (a large-scale, 200K preference dataset meticulously annotated by experts), the trained **EDITREWARD** model, and the challenging **EDITREWARD-BENCH** benchmark represents a substantial contribution to public research. These resources will foster transparency, enable comparative studies, and accelerate innovation across the community by providing high-fidelity ground truth and evaluation tools.
- **Enabling Scalable Data Curation:** EDITREWARD's demonstrated ability to effectively filter noisy datasets and curate high-quality subsets for training is a game-changer. This mechanism allows researchers and developers to scale up training data without requiring prohibitively expensive and time-consuming manual annotation for every example. By improving the quality of training data, EDITREWARD paves the way for the development of more powerful, robust, and human-aligned next-generation image editing models.
- **Advancing Evaluation Standards:** EDITREWARD-BENCH, with its multi-way preference ranking, pushes the boundaries of image editing evaluation. It offers a more nuanced and challenging assessment than traditional pairwise comparisons, revealing fine-grained differences between models and providing a more robust measure of ranking consistency and reasoning capabilities.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The multi-dimensional uncertainty-aware ranking framework and the novel "Disentangling Ties via Dimensional Preference" strategy offer new methodological avenues for research in human preference modeling, particularly in complex multimodal generative tasks.
- **Democratizing AI Capabilities:** By providing open-source tools that were previously only available in closed, proprietary systems, EDITREWARD contributes to democratizing access to cutting-edge AI capabilities. This can empower a wider range of researchers, developers, and creative professionals to build and experiment with advanced image editing technologies.

**Potential Impact:**
The immediate impact will be observed in improved performance of open-source image editing models, making them more competitive with their commercial counterparts. Longer-term, it could lead to faster iteration cycles for new generative models, as data quality issues become more manageable.

**Ethical Considerations:** The authors responsibly acknowledge the dual-use potential of advanced image editing technologies, including the risk of misuse for deepfakes or misinformation. Their commitment to mitigating these risks through measures like dataset curation from non-sensitive sources, PII filtering, and licensing (CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0, prohibiting commercial use) highlights a conscientious approach to AI development. By promoting transparency and providing tools for rigorous evaluation, EDITREWARD contributes to fostering a safer and more ethical AI research ecosystem.

In conclusion, EDITREWARD represents a significant leap forward for open-source instruction-guided image editing, providing essential tools and methodologies that will undoubtedly accelerate progress in this dynamic field.
