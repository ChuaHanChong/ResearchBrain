# A Survey of Behavior Foundation Model: Next-Generation Whole-Body Control System of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper systematically reviews Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) as a next-generation approach for humanoid robot whole-body control, categorizing their pre-training pipelines into goal-conditioned, intrinsic reward-driven, and forward-backward representation learning, and detailing their adaptation strategies. It demonstrates how BFMs enable versatile, general-purpose behaviors, including zero-shot or rapid adaptation to diverse tasks, with some models successfully deployed on real-world humanoids.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face significant challenges in achieving versatile and general-purpose whole-body control (WBC) due to their complex dynamics, high degrees of freedom, and the diverse task requirements.
- Traditional model-based WBC methods are robust but demand labor-intensive tuning, struggle with real-time performance for high-dimensional systems, and lack flexibility for dynamic skills or robustness to unforeseen disturbances.
- Learning-based, task-specific control approaches (e.g., RL, IL) suffer from sample inefficiency, sensitivity to reward function design, the simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap, and poor generalization across tasks, requiring extensive retraining for new scenarios.

## Method

- ==Introduce Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs)== that leverage large-scale pre-training on diverse behavioral data to learn reusable primitive skills and broad behavioral priors.
- Categorize BFM pre-training into three main types: goal-conditioned learning (e.g., motion tracking, high-level goal execution), intrinsic reward-driven learning (e.g., skill discovery via curiosity), and forward-backward representation learning (e.g., universal policy representations).
- Examine adaptation strategies for BFMs, including fine-tuning techniques for task-specific enhancement and hierarchical control architectures that integrate BFMs as low-level executors with high-level planners like LLMs.

## Results

- Goal-conditioned BFMs demonstrate efficient skill acquisition from large datasets and achieve rapid adaptation and generalization across a wide range of dynamic behaviors.
- Forward-Backward (FB) based BFMs, such as BFM-Zero, have been successfully deployed on real-world humanoid robots (Unitree G1, Booster T1), achieving zero-shot adaptation for motion tracking, goal reaching, and reward optimization without fine-tuning.
- Fine-tuning techniques like ReLA and LoLA can improve the task performance of BFMs by up to **40%** and enhance robustness to unseen dynamics with minimal online interaction, bridging the gap between broad priors and precise task requirements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BFMs can acquire a comprehensive understanding of motor skills and interaction patterns through large-scale pre-training, enabling humanoid robots to generate complex, dynamic, and naturalistic motions.
> - The forward-backward representation learning framework provides a universal policy representation that facilitates zero-shot adaptation to diverse tasks and reward functions without requiring additional environment interaction.
> - BFMs serve as a crucial interface between high-level semantic instructions (e.g., from Large Language Models) and low-level physical execution, enabling the handling of complex, long-horizon tasks and multi-task generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is positioned within the evolving landscape of humanoid robot control, specifically addressing the challenge of achieving versatile and general-purpose whole-body control (WBC). Historically, WBC methods have progressed from traditional model-based approaches, which rely on explicit physics models and optimization, to learning-based, task-specific controllers utilizing reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL).

Traditional model-based methods, such as Centroidal Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Whole-Body Operational Space Control (WBOSC), have provided foundational robustness for locomotion and manipulation in early humanoids (e.g., Atlas, HRP-2). However, these methods are characterized by labor-intensive configuration, sensitivity to model inaccuracies, computational demands, and limited flexibility for highly dynamic or complex behaviors and adaptation to disturbances.

Learning-based methods, exemplified by frameworks like DeepMimic and AMP, offered increased flexibility in task design, enabling robots to acquire complex skills through interaction or demonstrations. Despite their successes, these approaches face significant limitations, including sample inefficiency, sensitivity to reward function design, the simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap, and poor generalization across tasks, often requiring extensive retraining for new scenarios.

Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) emerge as a paradigm intended to address the limitations of prior approaches. Inspired by large-scale foundation models in fields like natural language processing and computer vision, BFMs aim to leverage extensive pre-training on diverse behavioral data to learn reusable primitive skills and broad behavioral priors. This pre-training is designed to enable zero-shot or rapid adaptation to a wide range of downstream tasks, moving towards scalable and general-purpose humanoid intelligence. This survey systematically reviews the development of BFMs for humanoid WBC, contextualizing their emergence as a "next-generation" control system by tracing their evolutionary trajectory and outlining their potential to overcome persistent challenges in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the fundamental challenges inherent in achieving efficient and general whole-body control (WBC) in humanoid robots. These challenges include:

- **Complex Dynamics and Underactuation:** Humanoid robots possess sophisticated dynamics, high degrees of freedom, and underactuation, making coordinated control difficult.
- **Diverse Task Requirements:** Humanoids are expected to perform a wide array of tasks, from locomotion and manipulation to human-robot interaction, often requiring agile and adaptable full-body motions.
- **Limitations of Traditional Model-Based Control:** These methods are robust but demand labor-intensive tuning, struggle with real-time performance for high-dimensional systems, lack flexibility for dynamic skills, and exhibit weak robustness to unforeseen disturbances.
- **Limitations of Learning-Based, Task-Specific Control:** While offering flexibility, RL-based approaches suffer from sample inefficiency and reward function sensitivity, while IL-based methods face significant data collection challenges. Both paradigms generally struggle with cross-task generalization, requiring costly retraining for new scenarios and often failing to transfer well from simulation to reality (Sim2Real gap).

Against this backdrop, Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) are presented as a transformative approach. The core motivation behind BFMs is to develop a control paradigm that:

- **Learns Reusable Primitive Skills and Broad Behavioral Priors:** By pre-training on large-scale, diverse behavioral data, BFMs aim to acquire a comprehensive understanding of motor skills and interaction patterns.
- **Enables Zero-shot or Rapid Adaptation:** This foundational knowledge should allow robots to adapt to new, unseen tasks with minimal or no additional training, overcoming the generalization limitations of previous methods.
- **Achieves Scalable and General-Purpose Humanoid Intelligence:** The ultimate goal is to create a control system that can handle a wide variety of tasks and environments without requiring specialized controllers for each scenario.

The objective of *this survey paper* is to provide a comprehensive and systematic overview of BFMs for humanoid WBC. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. Trace the historical development of WBC methods, highlighting the trajectory leading to BFMs.
2. Categorize and discuss diverse pre-training pipelines and adaptation strategies employed in current BFM research.
3. Explore real-world applications and industries that stand to benefit from BFM adoption.
4. Identify and critically analyze the current limitations and urgent challenges facing BFM development, such as the Sim2Real gap, data bottlenecks, and embodiment generalization.
5. Highlight future research opportunities and potential risks, including ethical considerations and safety mechanisms, to guide subsequent advancements in the field.
6. Offer a curated collection of relevant papers and projects to facilitate further research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology involves a structured and comprehensive review of existing literature and projects related to Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) in the context of humanoid whole-body control (WBC). The approach can be broken down into the following components:

1. **Contextualization of Humanoid WBC Evolution:** The paper begins by establishing the historical context of humanoid WBC, categorizing past approaches into:
 - **Traditional Model-based Controllers:** Discussing their reliance on physics-based models (e.g., MPC, WBOSC), their successes in early humanoids, and their inherent limitations (labor-intensive, low robustness, poor flexibility).
 - **Learning-based and Task-specific Controllers:** Reviewing the emergence of reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL) (e.g., DeepMimic, AMP), their ability to acquire complex skills, and their challenges (sample inefficiency, Sim2Real gap, poor generalization).

2. **Definition and Characterization of BFMs:** The survey formally defines BFMs as a specialized class of foundation models designed for agent behavior control in dynamic environments. It differentiates BFMs from general foundation models (e.g., GPT-4) and vision-language-action (VLA) models by emphasizing their focus on direct agent behavior control (locomotion, manipulation, interaction) and their applicability to sophisticated WBC of full-size humanoids.

3. **Taxonomy of BFM Pre-training Pipelines:** The core of the methodology involves categorizing and detailing current BFM pre-training approaches into three main types:
 - **Goal-conditioned Learning:** Policies are trained to achieve specific goals (e.g., target states, trajectories, text instructions) often using dense reference supervision from motion capture data or self-supervised command tracking. Representative methods like DeepMimic, ASE, HugWBC, MoConVQ, and MaskedMimic are discussed, illustrating how diverse skills are acquired and generalized.
 - **Intrinsic Reward-driven Learning:** Agents explore environments without explicit task-specific rewards, guided by self-generated intrinsic rewards that encourage curiosity, skill discovery, or state coverage (e.g., ICM, DIAYN, RE3, ODPP). This section positions these methods as influential prototypes for unsupervised behavioral prior acquisition.
 - **Forward-Backward Representation Learning (FB):** This framework decouples policy learning from task-specific objectives by learning finite-rank approximations of successor measures. Methods like FB-IL, Motivo, and BFM-Zero are analyzed, demonstrating how a universal policy representation can enable zero-shot adaptation through reward inference or demonstration alignment, often combined with regularization techniques (FB-CPR) and architectural innovations for Sim2Real robustness.

4. **Analysis of BFM Adaptation Strategies:** The paper examines how pre-trained BFMs are adapted to downstream tasks, classifying strategies into:
 - **Fine-tuning Techniques:** Approaches that involve refining model parameters or introducing small modules for task-specific enhancement (e.g., ReLA, LoLA, TaskTokens, belief-FB).
 - **Towards Hierarchical Control:** Architectures that integrate BFMs as low-level execution layers with high-level planners (e.g., LLMs, diffusion models) for complex, long-horizon tasks, as seen in UniHSI, TokenHSI, CLoSD, and LangWBC.

5. **Discussion of Applications and Limitations:** The survey extends its analysis to practical implications, outlining potential applications of BFMs across various industries (humanoid robotics acceleration, virtual agents/gaming, Industry 5.0, healthcare, and assistive robotics). Simultaneously, it critically identifies current limitations, including the Sim2Real gap, computational/system integration challenges, data bottlenecks (e.g., lack of large-scale multimodal datasets), and embodiment generalization issues.

6. **Identification of Opportunities and Risks:** The paper concludes by projecting future research directions and considerations. Opportunities include multimodal BFMs, integration with high-level ML systems, scaling laws, advanced post-training techniques, multi-agent systems, and developing robust evaluation mechanisms. Risks cover ethical issues (bias, privacy, unsafe behaviors) and the need for rigorous safety mechanisms in real-world deployment.

Throughout the review, the authors maintain a neutral, factual tone, relying on existing literature to support their categorization and analysis. They explicitly mention providing a curated and regularly updated collection of BFM papers and projects on GitHub to further facilitate research in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, this paper's "findings" are the synthesized categorization, detailed analysis, and critical assessment of the current state and future trajectory of Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) for humanoid whole-body control (WBC). The main findings and results are structured around the taxonomy developed by the authors:

**A. Pre-training Pipelines for BFMs:**
The survey identifies three primary approaches for pre-training BFMs:

1. **Goal-conditioned Learning:**
 - **Mechanism:** Agents learn to achieve diverse target states, poses, or follow explicit instructions (e.g., text) by conditioning their policies on goal embeddings. This often involves tracking reference motions or self-supervised command tracking.
 - **Representative Works:**
 - **Skill Learning from Motion Tracking (e.g., DeepMimic, ASE, CALM, CASE):** These frameworks leverage motion capture data to learn dynamic, physically-based character skills, often using adversarial imitation to acquire reusable latent skill spaces.
 - **High-level Goal Execution (e.g., MoConVQ, MaskedMimic, HOVER):** More advanced BFMs integrate with large language models (LLMs) or utilize masked motion inpainting to interpret and execute high-level goals, enabling versatile control across diverse tasks like text-to-motion generation, human-object interaction (HOI), and multi-mode task switching in real robots.
 - **Outcome:** These methods demonstrate efficient skill acquisition from large datasets and rapid adaptation to downstream tasks, generalizing across a wide range of behaviors.

2. **Intrinsic Reward-driven Learning:**
 - **Mechanism:** Agents are motivated by self-generated intrinsic rewards (e.g., curiosity, skill diversity, state coverage) rather than explicit task-specific rewards, encouraging exploration and non-directional skill acquisition.
 - **Representative Works (e.g., ICM, DIAYN, RE3, ODPP):** These methods focus on enhancing exploration (e.g., by minimizing prediction error), discovering diverse behaviors (e.g., mutual information maximization between skill and state), or maximizing state visitation frequency.
 - **Outcome:** While often trained on single environments, these models show promise in discovering generalizable and latent behavioral priors, laying conceptual groundwork for unsupervised BFM pre-training, but typically yield less intuitive behavioral priors compared to goal-conditioned models.

3. **Forward-Backward (FB) Representation Learning:**
 - **Mechanism:** This framework decouples policy learning from task-specific objectives by learning a universal policy representation through the approximation of successor measures. This allows for zero-shot evaluation of policies for arbitrary reward functions. It relies on learning forward and backward embeddings.
 - **Representative Works:**
 - **Theoretical Foundations (e.g., FB-IL, FB-AW, FB-AWARE):** Established the concept of BFMs and demonstrated their ability to support multiple imitation learning principles without task-specific RL fine-tuning, improving expressivity with auto-regressive encoding.
 - **Addressing Limitations (e.g., Motivo, BFM-Zero):** Motivo enhanced FB by combining it with conditional policy regularization (FB-CPR) to overcome limitations like low-rank dynamics assumptions and poor dataset coverage. It achieved zero-shot adaptation for motion tracking, goal reaching, and reward optimization in simulation, showing improved diversity and efficiency. BFM-Zero successfully deployed an FB-derived BFM on real-world humanoid robots (Unitree G1, Booster T1) by incorporating architectural innovations (dual-critic, domain randomization, history-based policies) for Sim2Real robustness without fine-tuning.
 - **Outcome:** FB models enable zero-shot adaptation to diverse tasks without additional environment interaction, representing a significant step towards general-purpose controllers.

**B. Adaptation Strategies for BFMs:**
The survey identifies two main categories for adapting pre-trained BFMs:

1. **Fine-tuning Techniques:**
 - **Mechanism:** Methods that refine pre-trained BFM parameters or introduce additional modules to enhance performance on specific tasks or environments.
 - **Representative Works (e.g., ReLA, LoLA, TaskTokens, Belief-FB):** These techniques enable rapid adaptation with minimal online interaction, improve task performance (e.g., up to 40% improvement with ReLA/LoLA), and enhance robustness to unseen dynamics (e.g., Belief-FB's transformer-based dynamics inference module).
 - **Outcome:** These strategies bridge the gap between broad behavioral priors and precise task-specific requirements, often achieving high success rates.

2. **Towards Hierarchical Control:**
 - **Mechanism:** Architectures that decouple high-level planning (interpreting abstract goals) from low-level motion execution (handled by BFMs) to achieve scalable and flexible control.
 - **Representative Works (e.g., UniHSI, TokenHSI, CLoSD, UniPhys, LangWBC, LeVERB):** These frameworks integrate BFMs with LLMs or diffusion models to translate language commands or abstract plans into low-level physical actions. Examples include guiding human-scene interaction via chain-of-contacts, multi-skill unification through task tokenization, and text-driven multi-task control via diffusion planners.
 - **Outcome:** BFMs serve as a crucial interface between semantic instructions and physical execution, enabling complex, long-horizon tasks, multi-task generalization, and physics-aware motion synthesis.

**C. Applications and Limitations:**
The survey outlines broad potential applications in humanoid robotics, virtual agents/gaming, Industry 5.0 (human-centric manufacturing), and healthcare/assistive robotics. However, it also highlights critical limitations:
- **Sim2Real Gap:** The challenge of transferring policies from simulation to real hardware remains, especially for diverse BFM behaviors.
- **Computational and System Integration Challenges:** Real-time control demands high frequencies (50-100Hz) under strict hardware constraints (latency, power), which larger BFM models and multimodal inputs exacerbate.
- **Data Bottleneck:** Current datasets for humanoid motion are significantly smaller and less diverse than those for LLMs/vision models, hindering large-scale BFM training and multimodal integration.
- **Embodiment Generalization:** BFMs typically struggle to generalize to robots with different morphologies, actuator dynamics, or sensor configurations, limiting their "foundation" capability to specific embodiments.

**D. Opportunities and Risks:**
The paper identifies key opportunities, including developing multimodal BFMs, integrating them into high-level ML systems (e.g., with LLMs), investigating scaling laws, employing advanced post-training techniques (e.g., RL from human feedback), enabling multi-agent coordination, and establishing robust evaluation mechanisms. Concurrently, it raises significant risks:
- **Ethical Issues:** Concerns about demographic biases from non-diverse training data, privacy risks from movement analytics, and the potential for BFMs to reproduce unsafe or socially harmful behaviors.
- **Safety Mechanisms:** The necessity for robust protection against adversarial attacks, sensor noise, and cross-modal confusion to ensure predictable and trustworthy robot behavior in safety-critical real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper asserts that Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) represent a significant shift in the field of humanoid robot control, positioning them as a "next-generation" solution with the potential to fundamentally transform humanoid robotics.

The primary significance lies in the BFM paradigm's capacity to address long-standing limitations of previous Whole-Body Control (WBC) approaches. By leveraging large-scale pre-training on diverse behavioral data, BFMs can learn broad behavioral priors and reusable primitive skills. This foundational knowledge enables humanoid robots to achieve:

- **Enhanced Generalization:** Unlike task-specific controllers that require extensive retraining for new scenarios, BFMs are designed for zero-shot or rapid adaptation to a wide variety of downstream tasks. This reduces development costs and accelerates deployment cycles.
- **Increased Versatility and Agility:** BFMs can generate complex, dynamic, and naturalistic motions by coordinating numerous joints and contacts across the entire robot body, improving the robot's ability to operate in unstructured human-centric environments.
- **Scalable Intelligence:** By providing a unified control framework, BFMs contribute to the vision of scalable and general-purpose humanoid intelligence, where a single model can underpin a vast repertoire of behaviors.

The potential impact of BFMs extends across multiple domains:

- **Humanoid Robotics Development:** BFMs act as a "general accelerator," streamlining the prototyping and evaluation of robot behaviors by bypassing traditional, time-consuming RL loops and enabling rapid transfer of skills.
- **Virtual Agents and Gaming:** They can revolutionize virtual characters by enabling lifelike, context-aware non-player character (NPC) behaviors, enriching immersive experiences and fostering intuitive interactions, especially when integrated with large language models (LLMs).
- **Industry 5.0:** BFMs are critical for the transition towards human-centric, resilient, and sustainable manufacturing, empowering humanoid robots to exhibit adaptive, collaborative, and explainable behaviors in shared workspaces, performing tasks that require both precision and flexibility.
- **Healthcare and Assistive Robotics:** They can facilitate the development of more adaptable assistive robots for tasks like mobility support, daily activities, and personalized rehabilitation, adjusting to diverse user needs and environments with minimal tuning.

Beyond these applications, the survey itself is significant as it provides the first comprehensive overview of BFMs, systematically categorizing their development, identifying crucial limitations (Sim2Real gap, data scarcity, embodiment generalization), and outlining future opportunities (multimodal integration, scaling laws, post-training, multi-agent systems, evaluation mechanisms). By highlighting these critical areas for future research and underscoring potential ethical and safety risks, the paper serves as a foundational resource to guide the robotics community toward developing more reliable, adaptable, and socially responsible humanoid systems.
