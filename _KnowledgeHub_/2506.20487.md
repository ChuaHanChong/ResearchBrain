# A Survey of Behavior Foundation Model: Next-Generation Whole-Body Control System of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper systematically reviews Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) as a next-generation approach for humanoid robot whole-body control, categorizing their pre-training pipelines into goal-conditioned, intrinsic reward-driven, and forward-backward representation learning, and detailing their adaptation strategies. It demonstrates how BFMs enable versatile, general-purpose behaviors, including zero-shot or rapid adaptation to diverse tasks, with some models successfully deployed on real-world humanoids.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face significant challenges in achieving versatile and general-purpose whole-body control (WBC) due to their complex dynamics, high degrees of freedom, and the diverse task requirements.
- Traditional model-based WBC methods are robust but demand labor-intensive tuning, struggle with real-time performance for high-dimensional systems, and lack flexibility for dynamic skills or robustness to unforeseen disturbances.
- Learning-based, task-specific control approaches (e.g., RL, IL) suffer from sample inefficiency, sensitivity to reward function design, the simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap, and poor generalization across tasks, requiring extensive retraining for new scenarios.

## Method

- ==Introduce Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs)== that leverage large-scale pre-training on diverse behavioral data to learn reusable primitive skills and broad behavioral priors.
- Categorize BFM pre-training into three main types: goal-conditioned learning (e.g., motion tracking, high-level goal execution), intrinsic reward-driven learning (e.g., skill discovery via curiosity), and forward-backward representation learning (e.g., universal policy representations).
- Examine adaptation strategies for BFMs, including fine-tuning techniques for task-specific enhancement and hierarchical control architectures that integrate BFMs as low-level executors with high-level planners like LLMs.

## Results

- Goal-conditioned BFMs demonstrate efficient skill acquisition from large datasets and achieve rapid adaptation and generalization across a wide range of dynamic behaviors.
- Forward-Backward (FB) based BFMs, such as BFM-Zero, have been successfully deployed on real-world humanoid robots (Unitree G1, Booster T1), achieving zero-shot adaptation for motion tracking, goal reaching, and reward optimization without fine-tuning.
- Fine-tuning techniques like ReLA and LoLA can improve the task performance of BFMs by up to **40%** and enhance robustness to unseen dynamics with minimal online interaction, bridging the gap between broad priors and precise task requirements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BFMs can acquire a comprehensive understanding of motor skills and interaction patterns through large-scale pre-training, enabling humanoid robots to generate complex, dynamic, and naturalistic motions.
> - The forward-backward representation learning framework provides a universal policy representation that facilitates zero-shot adaptation to diverse tasks and reward functions without requiring additional environment interaction.
> - BFMs serve as a crucial interface between high-level semantic instructions (e.g., from Large Language Models) and low-level physical execution, enabling the handling of complex, long-horizon tasks and multi-task generalization.
