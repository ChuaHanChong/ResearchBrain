# GeoPredict: Leveraging Predictive Kinematics and 3D Gaussian Geometry for Precise VLA Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoPredict augments Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with predictive kinematic priors and 3D Gaussian geometry, enhancing robotic manipulation precision and 3D spatial reasoning. It achieved substantial success rate improvements, such as 10.1 percentage points on RoboCasa Human-50, by using these predictive modules exclusively during training to maintain inference efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models are primarily reactive and 2D-centric, limiting precise 3D spatial reasoning, object pose understanding, and collision avoidance.
- Current VLA models often lack physically consistent long-horizon control due to an absence of foresight regarding future robot and scene states.
- Directly integrating high-capacity 3D prediction modules with VLA backbones introduces significant computational overhead during inference, making them impractical for real-time robotic control.

## Method

- The framework jointly learns future robot ==keypoint trajectories==, providing a compact summary of past kinematics and an explicit prediction of future robot motion.
- It predicts future scene geometry using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which is refined using predicted robot keypoint tracks to concentrate modeling capacity in interaction regions.
- The predictive kinematic and 3D geometric modules serve purely as training-time supervision, shaping the VLA transformer's internal representations without adding computational burden during inference.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **52.4%** on RoboCasa Human-50, an improvement of 10.1 percentage points over the baseline (**42.3%**), and surpassed other methods.
- Demonstrated an average success rate of **96.5%** across the four LIBERO suites, outperforming the UniVLA baseline (**95.2%**) and π0 baseline (**93.9%**).
- Showed substantial gains in real-world experiments, with an **85.0%** success rate for spatial generalization (vs. **60.0%** for baseline) and **95.0%** for geometry generalization (vs. **50.0%** for baseline).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit predictive kinematic and 3D geometric priors (via 3D Gaussian Splatting) significantly improves VLA model performance in tasks requiring precise 3D manipulation.
> - Complex 3D world models can effectively provide supervision during training to instill intrinsic spatial reasoning into VLA policies, without needing to be executed at inference time.
> - Dynamically allocating geometric modeling capacity to task-relevant areas, such as using track-guided refinement for 3DGS, enhances precision and efficiency by focusing on critical interaction zones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "GeoPredict" is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLA models have demonstrated generalization capabilities in robotics by mapping visual observations and language instructions to actions, leveraging priors from large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). However, current VLA paradigms often operate reactively and are primarily 2D-centric, which limits their performance in tasks requiring precise 3D spatial reasoning, object pose understanding, collision avoidance, and physically consistent long-horizon control.

Previous efforts to move beyond purely reactive policies in visuomotor models have incorporated predictive structures, such as learning latent dynamics, temporal abstractions, or predicting future observations like RGB frames, depths, or point-based representations. While these methods provide temporal signals, they frequently lack explicit multi-view or 3D geometric consistency. This deficiency makes it challenging to reason about object poses and end-effector motion in a consistent 3D workspace. Additionally, integrating complex 3D prediction modules with large VLA backbones can introduce significant computational overhead during inference, hindering practical deployment for real-time robotic control.

Concurrent work in 3D scene representation has seen advancements from implicit neural representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to explicit methods such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). 3DGS offers efficient rendering and high-fidelity scene modeling. Some applications have begun to leverage 3DGS for robotic world modeling. However, directly forecasting massive Gaussian attributes for future timesteps remains computationally demanding if required during inference.

GeoPredict addresses these limitations by introducing a framework that augments VLA models with both predictive kinematic priors and predictive 3D Gaussian geometry. It distinguishes itself by:
1. **Emphasizing 3D geometric consistency**: Unlike many 2D-centric prediction methods, GeoPredict forecasts explicit 3D scene geometry.
2. **Integrating kinematic and geometric priors**: It combines predictions of robot keypoint trajectories with evolving 3D scene geometry.
3. **Ensuring inference efficiency**: The predictive modules are exclusively used during training, providing supervision that shapes the transformer's internal representations without incurring computational costs during deployment.

By addressing the reactive and 2D-centric nature of existing VLA models while maintaining inference efficiency, GeoPredict aims to enhance precision and robust 3D reasoning in robotic manipulation, positioning it as an advancement towards more capable and deployable generalist robot policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a geometry-aware VLA framework, named GeoPredict, that augments continuous-action policies with predictive kinematic and geometric priors to improve precision in robotic manipulation.

The motivation behind GeoPredict stems from several identified limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

1. **2D-centric and Reactive Nature**: While VLA models demonstrate strong generalization by leveraging large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), they primarily operate on 2D image observations and generate actions reactively. This formulation limits their ability to perform tasks that require explicit 3D reasoning, such as understanding object poses, clearances, and the complex interplay between the robot and its environment in 3D space. Such limitations can lead to unreliability in spatially demanding scenarios.
2. **Lack of Physically Consistent Long-Horizon Control**: The reactive nature of current VLA models means they often lack an understanding of how the robot and the scene will evolve over multiple future steps. This absence of foresight hinders their capacity for physically consistent long-horizon control, where anticipating future states is crucial for successful task completion.
3. **Inefficiency of Direct 3D Prediction at Inference**: While incorporating predictive structure into visuomotor models has been explored, tightly coupling high-capacity 3D prediction modules with VLA backbones can introduce significant computational overhead. This overhead, if present during inference, can make such systems impractical for real-time robotic control applications.
4. **Need for Specific Predictive Capabilities in Robotics**: The authors argue that for robotic manipulation, two specific predictive capabilities are particularly valuable:
 - **Predictive Kinematic Priors**: The policy should anticipate how the robot is likely to move over future steps, rather than relying solely on instantaneous joint states. This involves summarizing past kinematics and forecasting future trajectories.
 - **Predictive 3D Gaussian Geometry**: The policy needs to reason about the evolving 3D geometry of the workspace through an explicit, differentiable representation. This representation should be aligned with the robot's workspace and amenable to supervision from depth and multi-view cues, enabling better understanding of object configurations and potential interactions.

GeoPredict is motivated by the necessity to address these gaps by seamlessly integrating these two predictive signals with VLA architectures. A crucial aspect of this motivation is to design these predictive modules such that they primarily serve as training-time supervision, thus keeping inference-time overhead minimal to enable practical deployment. The goal is to endow the VLA model with intrinsic, foresight-driven spatial reasoning abilities without sacrificing efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoPredict is a geometry-aware VLA framework designed to equip continuous-control policies with predictive kinematic and geometric priors. It achieves this by jointly learning future robot keypoint trajectories and future scene geometry, represented by 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), and conditioning the underlying VLA policy on these predictions. A key design principle is that the predictive modules operate exclusively during training, preserving efficiency during inference.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Base VLA Model and Problem Definition

The framework builds upon $\pi^0$ as its base VLA model, which employs PaliGemma (with a SigLIP vision encoder) and a robotics-specific action expert that generates continuous actions via conditional flow matching. The problem involves learning a policy that maps a language instruction, current multi-view image observations, and robot proprioceptive state to a continuous action chunk for a future horizon $H=50$ timesteps. GeoPredict augments this by conditioning not only on past observations but also on internally predicted future robot motion and scene geometry.

#### 3.2. Trajectory-Level Kinematic Prediction

This module provides a compact summary of past kinematics and an explicit prediction of future trajectories, serving as a kinematic prior and guidance for spatial refinement.

- **Track Encoder**: This component compresses the motion history of $K$ robot keypoints (joints and end-effector) into compact tokens. For each keypoint $k$, its 3D coordinates from time 0 to $t-1$ form a trajectory $T_k$. A shared learnable history query $Q_{hist}$ performs cross-attention over the embedded trajectory to produce a history track token $Z_{hist_k}$. These tokens capture inertia, joint limits, and motion regularities.
- **Future Track Query**: $K$ learnable future track queries $\{q_{fut_k}\}$ are introduced. These queries are processed by the transformer along with instruction, images, and history tokens, generating future track embeddings $e_{fut_k}$. Each embedding is decoded by a shared MLP into an explicit spatio-temporal trajectory $\hat{p}_{k,t+\tau}$ for $H+1$ timesteps (current and $H$ future), incorporating a 1D sinusoidal temporal encoding $PE_{time}$.
- **Supervision**: The predicted trajectories are supervised using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, $L_{track}$, over all keypoints and timesteps, encouraging the transformer to learn dynamically consistent motion representations.

#### 3.3. Predictive 3D Gaussian Geometry

This module estimates the future geometry of the robot workspace, anticipating scene evolution to enhance the policy's spatial reasoning.

- **3D Spatial Query**: A 3D volume representing the robot workspace is discretized into coarse voxels. Each voxel is assigned a learnable C-dimensional embedding, forming an initial spatial query tensor $Q_{init}$. A 3D sinusoidal positional encoding $PE_{spatial}$ is added to incorporate geometric structure. These queries are flattened and provided to the transformer.
- **Voxel Decoder**: After processing by the transformer, spatial embeddings $E_{spatial}$ are obtained. These are temporally shifted using $PE_{time}$ to obtain $E_{spatial_{t+\tau}}$ for future timesteps. Each is reshaped into a grid and passed through a 3D voxel decoder (transposed convolutions and upsampling) to restore the original voxel resolution. This yields a dense voxel feature volume $F_{voxel}$. A final 3D convolution maps each voxel feature to a set of $N_G$ Gaussian primitives (center $\mu$, opacity $\alpha$, covariance $\Sigma$), forming the initial 3D scene representation $G_{init_{t+\tau}}$. Color coefficients are omitted as the focus is on geometry.
- **Track-based Gaussian Refinement**: To enhance fidelity near interaction regions, the predicted future keypoint trajectories from the kinematic module are used. At each timestep $t+\tau$, a binary refinement mask $M_{refine}$ is created for voxels containing any predicted keypoint position. For these refined voxels, a shared MLP maps their $F_{voxel}$ features to an additional $N'_G$ finer-grained Gaussian primitives ($N'_G > N_G$), forming a refined set $G_{refine_{t+\tau}}$. The complete Gaussian representation is $G_{total_{t+\tau}} = G_{init_{t+\tau}} \cup G_{refine_{t+\tau}}$. This concentrates modeling capacity in task-relevant areas.
- **Future Depth Rendering**: The predictive 3DGS model is supervised by rendering depth maps from $G_{total_{t+\tau}}$ at all future timesteps using differentiable alpha compositing. A masked L1 loss, $L_{depth}$, is applied, restricting supervision to depth values within the robot's operational workspace via a spatial mask $M_{spatial}(r)$.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference

- **Block-wise Causal Attention Mechanism**: A block-wise causal attention structure, adapted from $\pi^0$, is used. Tokens are partitioned into five ordered groups: 2D (text, image), 3D (history track), 3D Query (future track, spatial), State (proprioceptive), and Action Noise. Attention is bidirectional within blocks and causal across blocks, imposing a perceptual-to-predictive-to-control hierarchy.
- **Training Objective**: The model is trained end-to-end using a weighted sum of three loss terms: $L_{total} = \lambda_1 L_{action} + \lambda_2 L_{track} + \lambda_3 L_{depth}$. $L_{action}$ is the conditional flow-matching loss from $\pi^0$.
- **Inference**: Critically, during inference, the predictive 3D Gaussian geometry modules (voxel decoder, depth-rendering) are *not* executed. The transformer processes all context tokens (including lightweight additional query tokens for the predictive modules) in a single forward pass, caching Key-Value pairs. The action expert then iteratively denoises to generate the action chunk, reusing these cached states. The predictive modules function purely as training-time supervision, shaping the transformer's internal representations without adding runtime computational burden.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GeoPredict involved both simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa Human-50, LIBERO) and real-world manipulation tasks, with comparisons against state-of-the-art VLA and world-modeling approaches.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **RoboCasa Human-50**: GeoPredict achieved an average success rate of 52.4% across 24 sub-tasks. This represents a substantial improvement of 10.1 percentage points over the $\pi^0$ baseline (42.3%), and significantly outperformed other methods such as GWM (39.2%) and BC-Transformer (28.8%). The gains were particularly noted in scenarios requiring generalization from limited human demonstrations and handling unseen object instances and scene styles.

- **LIBERO**: GeoPredict demonstrated an average success rate of 96.5% across the four LIBERO suites (LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Long). This performance surpassed the UniVLA baseline (95.2%) and the $\pi^0$ baseline (93.9%), indicating a high level of effectiveness and robustness. The model maintained consistently high success rates across all suites: 98.0% for Spatial, 98.2% for Object, 95.7% for Goal, and 94.0% for the challenging Long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the RoboCasa benchmark analyzed the contribution of each proposed module:

- **History Track Encoder**: Adding the history Track Encoder to the $\pi^0$ baseline improved the average success rate from 42.3% to 44.8%. This suggests that encoding past kinematic context provides a beneficial prior.
- **Future Track Query (L_track)**: Incorporating explicit motion prediction via the Future Track Query further increased the success rate to 47.2%, indicating the value of forecasting future robot trajectories.
- **Predictive 3D Gaussian Splatting (L_depth)**:
 - Supervising with $L_{depth}$ using only initial global Gaussians ($G_{init}$) yielded 49.4%.
 - Jointly training with $L_{track}$ and $L_{depth}$ (without track-guided refinement) achieved 50.5%.
 - The full GeoPredict model, which includes **track-guided refinement** of the 3DGS representation, reached the peak average success rate of 52.4%. This 1.9 percentage point gain (from 50.5% to 52.4%) validates that dynamically allocating geometric capacity along predicted future robot tracks enhances the geometric prior for the policy.
- **Ablation on Depth Rendering**: Experiments showed that depth-only rendering (49.4%) performed comparably to rendering with color (49.2%), suggesting that geometric information alone is sufficient. Increasing the global density of Gaussians (e.g., $N_G=8$ for 51.4% success rate) increased training time significantly (19.1 hours/epoch). In contrast, the track-guided refinement strategy (e.g., $N_G=4, N'_G=64$) achieved a comparable or better success rate (52.4%) with a more efficient training time (15.7 hours/epoch), highlighting the efficiency of focused geometric capacity.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative visualizations of the predictive 3DGS geometry module demonstrated that the refined Gaussians ($G_{total}$) produced significantly sharper geometric details, particularly around the robotic arm, compared to the initial Gaussians ($G_{init}$). This confirmed that the refinement mechanism generated more precise and geometrically faithful future predictions.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

GeoPredict was evaluated on a DISCOVER robotic arm across three real-world task categories:

- **Spatial Generalization**: GeoPredict achieved an 85.0% success rate, significantly outperforming the $\pi^0$ baseline (60.0%).
- **Geometry Generalization**: GeoPredict attained a 95.0% success rate, compared to 50.0% for the baseline, demonstrating robust adaptive grasping capabilities for novel object sizes and orientations.
- **Visual Robustness**: GeoPredict achieved a 90.0% success rate, substantially higher than the baseline's 35.0%, indicating greater resilience to visual distractors.

These real-world results underscore GeoPredict's practical efficacy in tasks demanding explicit 3D reasoning, spatial generalization, and geometric robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GeoPredict framework presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its primary significance lies in the integration of predictive kinematic and 3D Gaussian geometry priors into continuous-action VLA policies, which enhances precision and robust 3D reasoning without incurring significant computational overhead during inference.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Enhanced 3D Reasoning and Precision**: GeoPredict moves beyond the reactive and 2D-centric nature of many current VLA models. By explicitly forecasting future robot trajectories and workspace geometry, it provides the policy with a richer, foresight-driven understanding of 3D spatial dynamics. This is crucial for tasks demanding high precision, object interaction, and collision avoidance in complex environments.
- **Generalization Capabilities**: The consistent and substantial improvements observed across diverse benchmarks like RoboCasa Human-50 (few-shot learning) and LIBERO (knowledge transfer) indicate that GeoPredict fosters stronger generalization. Its ability to handle unseen object instances, scene styles, and novel target locations suggests a more robust and adaptable policy.
- **Inference Efficiency**: A key innovation is that the sophisticated 3D prediction and refinement modules are used *exclusively during training*. This ensures that the VLA model's internal representations are imbued with geometric and physical structure without adding computational burden to the action generation process at inference time, making the approach practical for real-time robotic control.
- **Adaptive Geometric Modeling**: The track-guided refinement mechanism for 3D Gaussian Splatting intelligently allocates modeling capacity to task-relevant interaction regions. This provides high-fidelity geometry where it matters most (e.g., around the end-effector and objects in motion) without requiring a globally dense and computationally expensive 3D scene representation.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Broader Adoption of VLA Models**: By addressing limitations in 3D reasoning and precision while maintaining efficiency, GeoPredict could facilitate the wider deployment of VLA models in real-world robotic applications that require high reliability and dexterity. This could include complex assembly, domestic assistance, and industrial manipulation tasks.
- **New Directions in Robot Learning**: The training-time predictive supervision paradigm offers a promising avenue for researchers to incorporate richer physical and geometric priors into end-to-end learning systems. It suggests that complex 3D world models need not be run at inference time to benefit action generation, opening opportunities for more efficient knowledge transfer.
- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration**: Robots capable of precise 3D reasoning and anticipating future states can interact more safely and effectively in shared workspaces with humans.
- **Advancements in Data Efficiency**: The notable performance gains in few-shot settings (like RoboCasa Human-50) suggest that GeoPredict's structured priors can help policies learn more effectively from limited demonstration data.

While the requirement for multi-view RGB-D data and calibrated camera extrinsics presents scaling challenges, the increasing availability of such hardware and datasets may mitigate these concerns. Ultimately, GeoPredict contributes to developing more reliable, geometry-aware, and physically grounded VLA controllers, pushing the capabilities of generalist robots towards more complex and precise tasks.
