# RynnVLA-001: Using Human Demonstrations to Improve Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RynnVLA-001 is a VLA model pretrained via a two-stage video-generative curriculum: an autoregressive Image-to-Video model on 12M ego-centric human manipulation videos, then joint frame-and-keypoint-trajectory prediction, plus ActionVAE compressing action chunks into compact latent embeddings. Finetuned on SO100 robot data it beats GR00T N1.5 and Pi0, reaching 90.6% average success rate.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation datasets remain far smaller than the web-scale corpora that power LLMs, VLMs, and generative models, limiting VLA scaling.
- Prior video-pretraining VLA methods leave a gap between high-level visual frame prediction and the low-level action space needed for real robot control.
- Existing VLA action heads predict raw or discretized action sequences directly, losing temporal smoothness and wasting output-space capacity.

## Method

- ==Ego-Centric Video Generative Pretraining==: an autoregressive ==Chameleon==-based Image-to-Video transformer trained on 12M curated ego-centric manipulation videos to predict future frames from an image and instruction.
- ==Human-Centric Trajectory-Aware Video Modeling==: finetunes the I2V model on EgoDex wrist-keypoint trajectories with a linear action head, jointly predicting frames and human motion embeddings.
- **ActionVAE**: a ==variational autoencoder== that encodes action/trajectory chunks into compact continuous latent embeddings, decoded back into executable action sequences at inference.

## Results

- RynnVLA-001 reaches **90.6%** average success rate across three SO100 tasks versus **70.4%** for Pi0 and **55.6%** for GR00T N1.5.
- Ablations show pretraining stages compound: Scratch **4.4%**, Chameleon-init **50.0%**, plus video pretraining **84.4%**, full model with trajectory stage **90.6%**.
- On Calvin ABC->D, ActionVAE embeddings beat raw action prediction (**4.161** vs **4.019** avg length) and a single linear action head beats a deeper 5-layer MLP head (**4.161** vs **3.323**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video-generative pretraining on ego-centric human data transfers manipulation priors even without any robot action labels, cutting reliance on scarce teleoperation data.
> - Compressing action chunks into a VAE latent space, rather than predicting raw or discretized actions, improves temporal coherence and simplifies the output space.
> - A shallow linear action head can outperform a deeper MLP, suggesting the pretrained transformer backbone already carries most of the useful representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large models, multimodal AI, and robotics, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot manipulation. The broader AI landscape has witnessed unprecedented advancements in large language models (LLMs), large multimodal models (LMMs), vision-based recognition models, and generative models. These successes are largely attributed to the availability of massive-scale datasets readily accessible from the internet.

However, the field of robotics, particularly for VLA models, faces a significant bottleneck: the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality robot manipulation data. Unlike the internet-scale datasets for text and images, collecting robot data typically involves labor-intensive and costly human teleoperation on physical robots.

Existing efforts to address this data scarcity problem broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Directly building large-scale robot manipulation datasets:** While initiatives like Open X-Embodiment and DROID have made strides, these datasets remain orders of magnitude smaller than those used for LLMs and LMMs.
2. **Exploiting massive prior knowledge:** This involves leveraging representations and capabilities from pretrained generative models or vision-language models (VLMs) to alleviate the need for extensive robot-specific data.

RynnVLA-001 firmly belongs to the second category, advancing the paradigm of leveraging prior knowledge. Its distinct contribution lies in a novel multi-stage pretraining strategy that specifically taps into readily available *human ego-centric video demonstrations*. Unlike some prior works that rely on static images or focus solely on generating future *states*, RynnVLA-001 emphasizes *video generation* and, critically, *trajectory prediction* from human movements. It aims to progressively transfer skills from general visual dynamics to explicit action understanding.

The paper positions RynnVLA-001 against state-of-the-art baselines like GR00T N1.5 and Pi0. These models also represent significant advancements in VLA, often integrating powerful VLMs with specialized policy heads for continuous control. RynnVLA-001 aims to demonstrate that its proposed pretraining curriculum offers a more effective initialization for such VLA models, leading to superior performance, particularly in robustness and instruction-following. This work thus represents a crucial step towards scaling robot learning by effectively bridging the data gap between the vast human demonstration space and the limited robot data domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly improve the performance and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot manipulation**, particularly in executing complex and long-horizon tasks based on high-level language instructions.

The core motivation stems from the **fundamental challenge of data scarcity in robot learning**. While other AI domains benefit from vast internet-scale datasets, collecting comparable amounts of diverse, high-quality robot manipulation data is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This limitation hinders the development of truly generalist robot agents.

To address this, the authors propose a key insight: to **implicitly transfer manipulation skills learned from readily available human demonstrations, specifically in ego-centric videos, to robot manipulation**. This leverages the abundance of human video data to bootstrap robot learning.

More specifically, the key objectives driving the RynnVLA-001 project are:
- **Develop a novel two-stage pretraining methodology:** This methodology aims to progressively connect general visual dynamics learned from human videos with explicit action prediction relevant for robot control.
- **Bridge the gap between high-level visual observations and low-level robot actions:** By introducing an intermediate stage of human keypoint trajectory prediction, the model is guided to understand the underlying motions that lead to visual changes, thus facilitating the transfer to robot actions.
- **Enhance action representation for robotic control:** To handle continuous, multi-step robot actions effectively, the paper aims to develop a compact and coherent action representation using a Variational Autoencoder (ActionVAE).
- **Demonstrate superior performance and generalization:** The ultimate goal is to achieve higher success rates and greater robustness against variations in scenes and the presence of distractors, thereby outperforming existing state-of-the-art VLA models when finetuned on the same limited robot-specific data.
- **Enable robots to accurately follow high-level language instructions:** This is crucial for practical robot deployment where intuitive, natural language commands are preferred.

By achieving these objectives, RynnVLA-001 seeks to lay a foundation for more scalable, generalized, and practical robot manipulation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RynnVLA-001 proposes a novel VLA model built upon large-scale video generative pretraining from human demonstrations, employing a two-stage pretraining methodology followed by robot-centric finetuning. The core idea is to progressively transfer manipulation priors from abundant human ego-centric videos to robot control. The overall architecture is an autoregressive (AR) Transformer, extending an AR image generation model (Chameleon) for video tasks.

**ActionVAE: Action Representation Learning**
A crucial component is the **Action Variational Autoencoder (ActionVAE)**, designed to compress sequences of actions (action chunks) into compact, continuous latent embeddings. This addresses the issues of repetitive single-step predictions and improves efficiency. Two domain-specific ActionVAEs are trained: one for human trajectories (used in Stage 2) and another for robot actions (used in Stage 3), ensuring tailored and accurate representations for each embodiment.

**Three-Stage Progressive Training Pipeline:**

**Stage 1: Ego-Centric Video Generative Pretraining**
- **Objective:** To learn general physical dynamics and visual manipulation patterns from a first-person perspective, mimicking the inference process of a VLA model by predicting future video frames.
- **Data:** A meticulously curated dataset of **12 million ego-centric human manipulation videos** filtered from various web sources, supplemented by **244K robotic manipulation videos** from open-source datasets. The human videos emphasize hand operations analogous to robot gripper movements.
- **Task:** Image-to-Video (I2V) generation. The model takes an initial visual observation and a language instruction as input and predicts subsequent frames.
- **Architecture:** An autoregressive Transformer (adapted from Chameleon), where language tokens are interleaved with visual tokens to align with VLA inference.
- **Supervision:** Cross-entropy loss over discrete visual tokens and language tokens.

**Stage 2: Human-Centric Trajectory-Aware Video Modeling**
- **Objective:** To bridge the gap between purely visual prediction from Stage 1 and explicit action generation required for robot control. The model learns to associate visual changes with underlying motion trajectories.
- **Data:** The EgoDex dataset, providing human upper-body joint trajectories, specifically using wrist keypoints as approximations for end-effector positions.
- **Task:** Multi-task prediction: jointly predicting future visual frames and the corresponding human wrist keypoint trajectories. These trajectories are encoded by a pretrained human-specific ActionVAE into compact embeddings.
- **Input Enhancement:** Introduction of **state embeddings** (blue blocks in Fig. 2) representing current human wrist keypoint positions, projected into the Transformer's input dimension and interleaved with other tokens.
- **Architecture:** Extends the Stage 1 model with a lightweight action head (a single linear layer) mapping the Transformer's hidden state to the continuous latent space of action embeddings.
- **Supervision:** L1 loss for action embeddings (at `<ACTION_PLACEHOLDER>` tokens) and cross-entropy loss for visual tokens.

**Stage 3: Robot-Centric Vision-Language-Action Modeling**
- **Objective:** To adapt the pretrained trajectory-aware model for real-world robot control, learning to predict executable robot actions.
- **Data:** A **newly collected real-world manipulation dataset** using a LeRobot SO100 robotic arm, comprising approximately 800 expert demonstrations across three tasks (pick and place blocks, strawberries, pen into holder). The data includes diverse scenes (single target, multi-target, distractors) and varying environments.
- **Input:** Language instruction, current RGB observations (two camera views: front and wrist), and current robot joint states.
- **Architecture:** Inherits weights from Stage 2. The human action head is discarded, and a new lightweight action head (single linear layer) is initialized to predict robot action embeddings, which are decoded by a robot-specific ActionVAE.
- **Supervision:** L1 loss between predicted and ground-truth robot action embeddings, and cross-entropy loss for future visual token prediction (as an auxiliary task).

**Inference:**
During inference, RynnVLA-001 operates in a closed-loop control cycle. It takes the language instruction, current RGB observations, and robot states as input. For efficiency, the model *only predicts the action embedding*, discarding future vision token generation. This predicted embedding is then decoded by the ActionVAE into an executable sequence of low-level robot actions, which the robot executes before the cycle repeats.

**Ego-Centric Video Data Curation Pipeline:**
To create the large-scale human ego-centric video dataset for Stage 1, a multi-stage pipeline was developed:
1. **Keypoint Detection:** Using a pose estimation model (Yang et al., 2023) to extract human keypoints from each video frame.
2. **Ego-centric Filtering:** Discarding videos with facial keypoints (indicating third-person view) and retaining only frames with visible wrist and hand keypoints (strong indicators of ego-centric manipulation).
3. **Text Description Annotation:** Using Qwen2-VL-7B to generate concise textual descriptions for each curated video, mimicking natural language instructions for robot tasks.

This comprehensive methodology, from large-scale data curation to progressive pretraining and specialized action representation, forms the backbone of RynnVLA-001's approach to enhanced robot manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RynnVLA-001 demonstrates significant advancements in robot manipulation performance, particularly when compared to state-of-the-art baselines and through detailed ablation studies.

**Superior Performance Against State-of-the-Art Baselines:**
RynnVLA-001 was finetuned on a newly collected dataset using a LeRobot SO100 robotic arm and compared against GR00T N1.5 and Pi0.
- **Task-Specific Success Rates:** RynnVLA-001 consistently achieved substantially higher success rates across all three evaluation tasks: "Pick up and place green blocks" (90.0% vs. 65.0% for GR00T, 75.6% for Pi0), "Pick up and place strawberries" (91.7% vs. 53.3% for GR00T, 71.1% for Pi0), and "Grab pen and put it into holder" (90.0% vs. 48.3% for GR00T, 64.4% for Pi0).
- **Average Success Rate:** RynnVLA-001 achieved an impressive average success rate of 90.6%, significantly outperforming GR00T N1.5 (55.6%) and Pi0 (70.4%).
- **Success Rate@1 (SR@1):** RynnVLA-001 achieved an SR@1 of 56.7%, comparable to Pi0 (56.3%), suggesting that while overall success is high, there's still room for improvement in achieving task completion in a single trial for all models.

**Robustness to Diverse Evaluation Settings:**
The model's performance was evaluated under increasing scene complexity: single-target, multi-target, and instruction-following with distractors.
- RynnVLA-001 exhibited remarkable stability in its performance, with success rates of 93.3% (single-target), 86.7% (multi-target), and 91.7% (distractors).
- In contrast, baselines like GR00T N1.5 and Pi0 showed noticeable drops in success rates as scene complexity increased, particularly with distractors (e.g., Pi0 dropped from 80.0% to 60.0%). This highlights RynnVLA-001's superior instruction-following capabilities and robustness in cluttered environments.

**Effectiveness of the Two-Stage Pretraining Pipeline (Ablation Study):**
A comprehensive ablation study confirmed the critical contribution of each pretraining stage:
- **RynnVLA-001-Scratch (no pretraining):** Achieved an abysmal average success rate of 4.4%, demonstrating that training from scratch is largely ineffective.
- **RynnVLA-001-Chameleon (initialized with T2I weights):** Achieved 50.0% success, indicating that general visual priors from image generation are beneficial but insufficient for complex manipulation.
- **RynnVLA-001-Video (after Stage 1: Ego-Centric Video Generative Pretraining):** Jumped to 84.4% average success, proving the immense value of pretraining on large-scale ego-centric human videos for learning manipulation dynamics.
- **RynnVLA-001 (Full model, after Stage 2: Human-Centric Trajectory-Aware Video Modeling):** Achieved the highest average success rate of 90.6%, affirming that explicitly bridging visual prediction with action generation via human keypoint trajectories further refines the model's understanding and performance.

**Ablation Study on Model Designs:**
- **Image Resolution:** A resolution of 384x384 proved optimal. Reducing it to 256x256 significantly degraded performance (e.g., from 4.161 to 3.652 on Calvin Task ABC->D), attributed to a mismatch with the VQGAN component and reduced reconstruction quality.
- **Action Representations:** Predicting actions in the latent space of the ActionVAE consistently outperformed direct prediction of raw action sequences (4.161 vs. 4.019 on Calvin Task ABC->D), validating ActionVAE's role in providing efficient, compact, and temporally consistent action representations.
- **Size of Action Head:** A simple linear layer for the action head was found to be superior to a deeper five-layer MLP head (4.161 vs. 3.323 on Calvin Task ABC->D). This suggests that the Transformer's output already contains rich representations, and additional complexity in the head can introduce noise or overfitting.

**Further Analysis:**
- **Video Pretraining Visualization:** The Stage 1 I2V model was shown to generate plausible motions and content consistent with input images and text prompts, demonstrating its ability to learn manipulation dynamics at a visual level.
- **Importance of Distractors in Training Data:** Training with distractors was confirmed as crucial for instruction-following. A model trained without distractors achieved 0% success on a cluttered "pick up strawberry" task, consistently grasping distractors, whereas the full RynnVLA-001 achieved 90% success.
- **Functional Analysis of Cameras:** Experiments revealed that the front camera is vital for coarse object localization and providing critical 3D projective information for tasks requiring depth perception (e.g., inserting a pen). The wrist camera then handles precise local adjustments. Disabling or altering the front camera's perspective severely impacted task success, especially for targets outside the wrist camera's initial field of view or for tasks requiring careful 3D reasoning.

In summary, RynnVLA-001 significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robot manipulation by proposing a highly effective, data-efficient pretraining strategy that leverages human demonstrations, leading to superior robustness and instruction-following capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RynnVLA-001 represents a significant step forward in the field of robot manipulation and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with profound implications for the development of more capable and general-purpose robots.

**Addressing the Data Scarcity Problem:** The most significant contribution of RynnVLA-001 is its highly effective strategy for overcoming the pervasive data scarcity problem in robot learning. By demonstrating that large-scale human ego-centric video demonstrations can be effectively leveraged through a progressive pretraining curriculum, the work opens new avenues for scaling VLA models without relying exclusively on expensive robot-specific data collection. This approach is critical for the future development of robotics, as it allows access to a virtually limitless pool of general manipulation priors.

**Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The proposed two-stage pretraining methodology, combined with the comprehensive data curation that includes diverse scenarios and distractors, results in a VLA model that exhibits superior generalization and robustness. The ability to maintain high success rates in cluttered environments and accurately follow nuanced instructions is crucial for real-world deployment, where environments are dynamic and tasks are rarely simplistic. This enhanced robustness differentiates RynnVLA-001 from prior models that often struggle with variations beyond their narrow training distribution.

**Bridging the Perception-Action Gap:** The "Human-Centric Trajectory-Aware Video Modeling" stage is particularly impactful. By explicitly teaching the model to predict human keypoint trajectories in addition to visual frames, RynnVLA-001 creates a more direct and effective bridge between high-level visual observations and the low-level action spaces required for robot control. This curriculum learning approach facilitates the transfer of abstract visual understanding into concrete, actionable motion plans.

**Novel Methodological Contributions:** The paper introduces several key methodological innovations:
- **Two-stage pretraining curriculum:** This structured approach progressively refines general visual dynamics into action-aware representations.
- **ActionVAE for action chunk representation:** This method efficiently encodes complex action sequences into compact latent embeddings, leading to smoother and more coherent robot movements and improved performance.
- **Dedicated data curation pipeline:** The rigorous filtering and annotation of 12 million ego-centric human videos provide a high-quality foundation for pretraining, ensuring the learned priors are highly relevant to manipulation tasks.

**Practical Implications for Robot Deployment:** The superior performance, especially in instruction-following and robustness against distractors, means that robots trained with RynnVLA-001 can potentially perform tasks more reliably and with less supervision. This has significant practical implications for various applications, including:
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** More robust pick-and-place operations in dynamic environments.
- **Domestic Robotics:** Enabling robots to understand and execute tasks in complex home settings.
- **Manufacturing and Assembly:** Enhancing precision and adaptability for robotic workforces.
- **Reducing "Sim-to-Real" Gap:** By learning from real-world human videos, the model might inherently capture more realistic physical dynamics than purely simulated data, aiding in successful transfer to physical robots.

**Catalyst for Future Research:** This work is likely to inspire further research in several directions:
- **Scaling human demonstration data:** Exploring even larger and more diverse human video datasets, possibly incorporating multimodal cues beyond ego-centric views.
- **Multi-embodiment transfer:** Investigating how the RynnVLA-001 framework can be extended to enable seamless transfer of skills across a wider range of robot hardware and kinematic configurations.
- **Long-horizon and complex tasks:** Applying the robust pretraining to tackle even more intricate and multi-step manipulation challenges.
- **Open-Source Impact:** The availability of the GitHub repository implies an open-source release, which will allow the broader research community to build upon, validate, and extend RynnVLA-001, accelerating progress in the field.

In conclusion, RynnVLA-001 offers a compelling solution to a core problem in robotics, demonstrating a highly effective pathway to leveraging human intelligence for robot skill acquisition. Its advancements in data efficiency, generalization, and action representation promise to accelerate the development of truly capable and adaptable robotic systems.
