# From Pairs to Sequences: Track-Aware Policy Gradients for Keypoint Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Wuhan University and Xiaomi EV developed TraqPoint, a reinforcement learning framework that optimizes keypoint detection for long-term trackability across image sequences, moving beyond traditional pairwise matching. It achieves improved performance on 3D vision tasks, for example, a 5.9 AUC@5 increase on MegaDepth for relative pose estimation and higher Average Keypoint Tracking Length in visual odometry.

## Problem

- Most existing learning-based keypoint detection methods optimize for instantaneous 'pairwise matchability', which is insufficient for continuous perception systems.
- This pairwise training paradigm leads to keypoints that may drift, become inconsistent, or disappear over longer image sequences, compromising the stability and accuracy of 3D vision systems.
- The discrete and non-differentiable nature of keypoint selection poses a challenge for direct optimization with standard deep learning techniques.

## Method

- **TraqPoint** reformulates keypoint detection as a ==sequential decision-making== problem, utilizing a Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach to optimize for long-term trackability.
- A novel composite reward function is introduced, assessing each keypoint's trackability across an entire sequence by combining saliency consistency (rank reward) and descriptor distinctiveness.
- A policy gradient method, coupled with a hybrid global and grid sampling strategy and spatial entropy regularization, is used to optimize the keypoint detection policy.

## Results

- TraqPoint achieved superior relative pose estimation, outperforming state-of-the-art methods with a **5.9** AUC@5 boost on MegaDepth and a **2.9** AUC@5 boost on ScanNet over existing pairwise methods.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced visual odometry, showing substantial gains in Average Keypoint Tracking Length (AKTL), for instance, achieving **7.3** vs. **4.6** for RDD on KITTI sequence 01.
- Improved 3D reconstruction capabilities, registering more images (e.g., **693** vs. **632** for RDD on Madrid Metropolis) and generating denser sparse point clouds with longer average track lengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly optimizing for 'long-term trackability' across image sequences, rather than just 'pairwise matchability', significantly enhances keypoint performance in sequential 3D vision tasks.
> - The proposed track-aware composite reward, integrating both cross-view saliency consistency and descriptor distinctiveness, is essential for learning robust keypoints that persist over time.
> - An RL framework can effectively handle the discrete nature of keypoint selection and complements the quality of underlying feature backbones, providing consistent gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Keypoint detection and description constitute a foundational element in contemporary 3D computer vision systems, impacting applications such as Structure-from-Motion (SfM), Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), and relocalization. The performance of these systems is critically dependent on the quality of detected keypoints, specifically their stability under varying viewpoint and illumination conditions.

Historically, keypoint detection relied on handcrafted algorithms. Examples include SIFT, which emphasizes scale and rotation invariance through scale-space extrema, and FAST and ORB, which prioritize efficiency for corner detection. TBMR utilized Morse theory for contrast and affine invariance. These methods generally aimed to identify features in textured regions with robust geometric signatures.

With advancements in deep learning, the paradigm has shifted towards data-driven approaches. Learning-based methods can be broadly categorized:
- **Self-supervised methods:** These leverage geometric transformations, such as homographies, to generate pseudo-labels for image pairs. Examples include SuperPoint, which trains a detector and descriptor jointly, R2D2, which optimizes for repeatability and reliability, and SiLK, which uses descriptor matchability for supervision.
- **Supervised methods:** These train on large-scale 3D datasets, such as MegaDepth, utilizing ground-truth correspondences derived from COLMAP reconstructions. This category includes D2-Net, ALIKED, XFeat, DeDoDe, and RDD.
- **Dense and Semi-dense matching methods:** Recent work like RoMa, Mast3r, Dust3r, LoFTR, and Efficient LoFTR also predominantly adopt a pairwise training paradigm.

A distinct line of research frames keypoint detection as a Reinforcement Learning (RL) problem, addressing the discrete and non-differentiable nature of keypoint selection. Early RL-based approaches, such as, used Q-learning with rewards derived from handcrafted descriptors. More recent methods employ policy gradient optimization. For instance, Reinforced Feature Points (RFP) uses final pose estimation error as a reward, DISK utilizes geometrically consistent inliers, and RIPE employs an unsupervised approach based on inlier ratio from epipolar constraints.

A common principle underlying most existing learning-based methods, including many RL-based ones, is the definition of keypoint quality through its repeatability and matchability within an *image pair*. This pairwise training paradigm, while improving performance on matching tasks, primarily optimizes for instantaneous "matchability." This objective is inherently misaligned with the requirements of sequential applications like SLAM and SfM, where keypoints must exhibit "long-term trackability"—the ability to persist and remain stable across extended sequences under significant viewpoint, illumination, or motion blur changes. Failures in long-term tracking can compromise the stability and accuracy of the overall 3D vision system. This paper identifies this gap and aims to address it by shifting from pairwise to sequence-level optimization for keypoint trackability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from a identified misalignment between the training paradigms of most existing keypoint detection methods and the demands of sequential 3D vision applications. Current learning-based approaches, including those leveraging reinforcement learning, primarily define and optimize keypoint quality based on their matchability within isolated *image pairs*. While this paradigm has led to performance improvements in pairwise matching tasks, it implicitly optimizes for "instantaneous matchability" rather than the "long-term trackability" required for robust performance in applications like Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM).

In sequential tasks, keypoints need to maintain their identity and stability across multiple frames under varying conditions, including significant viewpoint changes, illumination fluctuations, and motion blur. Keypoints that exhibit strong matchability in a single pair may drift, become inconsistent, or disappear over longer trajectories, leading to compromised system stability and accuracy. The paper argues that this fundamental objective mismatch—optimizing for pairwise matching when sequential tracking is the ultimate goal—limits the practical utility of current methods in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

To address this limitation, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **Paradigm Shift:** To advocate for and implement a paradigm shift from optimizing "pairwise matchability" to directly learning for "long-term trackability" of keypoints.
2. **Sequential Decision-Making Formulation:** To reframe keypoint detection as a sequential decision-making problem, where the goal is to identify points that consistently form stable and persistent tracks across an entire image sequence.
3. **Reinforcement Learning Framework Development:** To introduce a novel, end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework, named TraqPoint, specifically designed to optimize the "Track-quality" (Traq) of keypoints directly on image sequences.
4. **Track-Aware Reward Mechanism:** To develop a composite, track-aware reward mechanism within the RL framework. This mechanism aims to jointly encourage both the consistency (saliency across views) and distinctiveness (global uniqueness) of keypoints throughout their entire track.
5. **Policy Gradient Optimization:** To utilize a policy gradient method to guide the learning process, enabling the policy network to produce detection probability maps that inherently favor keypoints with high potential for long-term stability.
6. **Performance Validation:** To demonstrate through extensive evaluation that the proposed TraqPoint framework significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art keypoint detection and description methods on both pairwise and, critically, sequential downstream tasks, including relative pose estimation, visual localization, visual odometry, and 3D reconstruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TraqPoint framework reframes keypoint detection as a sequential decision-making problem, utilizing a Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach to optimize for long-term keypoint trackability.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Network Architecture and Pre-training

The method builds upon a dual-branch network architecture, similar to RDD, and employs a "describe-then-detect" training paradigm.

- **Network Architecture:**
 - **Backbone:** The original ResNet-50 backbone from RDD is replaced with a DINOv3-ConvNeXt (base) model, utilizing its features from four different scales.
 - **Descriptor Branch (Φ):** This branch processes the multi-scale features from the backbone using a multi-scale deformable transformer module to produce a dense descriptor map (D ∈ R^(H/4 × W/4 × 256)).
 - **Keypoint Branch (πθ):** This branch, serving as the policy network, adopts a lightweight 4-layer convolutional design from ALIKED. Its final 1x1 convolutional layer, termed the policy head, outputs a logit map (L ∈ R^(H×W)).
- **Descriptor Branch Pre-training:**
 - The descriptor branch (Φ) is pre-trained on image pairs from the MegaDepth dataset.
 - Ground-truth pixel correspondences (Mgt) are generated using camera poses and depth maps.
 - A focal loss function (L_desc) is applied to optimize the similarity between descriptors of corresponding points, computed via a dual-softmax operation on the similarity matrix.
 - After training, the parameters of the descriptor branch (Φ) are frozen, providing a stable signal for subsequent reward calculations.

#### 3.2. Problem Setup: Reinforcement Learning Formulation

- **Agent:** The keypoint branch, parameterized by θ, acts as the policy network (πθ).
- **State (s):** The input is a single reference image (I_ref).
- **Action (A):** The policy network outputs a pixel-wise probability distribution (Pθ = πθ(s)). An action consists of sampling a set of N keypoints (A = {xi}N_i=1) independently from this distribution.
- **Objective:** To maximize the expected reward J(θ) = E_A∼Pθ[R(A)], where R(A) is the average long-term trackability score for the sampled keypoints.

#### 3.3. Hybrid Sampling Strategy

To mitigate keypoint clustering and ensure adequate spatial coverage, a hybrid sampling strategy is proposed:

- **Global Sampling:** N_g keypoints are sampled directly from the global probability distribution Pθ, prioritizing regions with high detection probabilities.
- **Grid Sampling:** The image is divided into a G × G grid. For each grid cell, a local logit submap is extracted from L, and a spatial softmax is applied to obtain a local policy distribution. One keypoint is then sampled from each cell based on this local distribution.
- The total action set A comprises N = N_g + N_grid keypoints. The probability of each sampled keypoint x_i (Pθ(x_i)) is defined by its value in the global distribution for policy gradient calculations.

#### 3.4. Trackability Reward Formulation

The core innovation is a composite reward function R_i for each sampled keypoint x_i, designed to assess its long-term trackability across a sequence.

- **Visibility Determination:** For each x_i in the reference image (I_ref), it is projected to other target frames (I_t) in the sequence using known relative poses and depth, yielding projected coordinates x_t_i. A projection is considered "visible" if it falls within image bounds and passes geometric verification (e.g., depth consistency). V_i denotes the set of frame indices where x_i is visible.
- **Per-Frame Reward (R_t_i):** For each visible projection x_t_i (t ∈ V_i), a per-frame reward is calculated as the average of two linear scores:
 - **Rank Reward (R_t_rank,i):** Quantifies the cross-view consistency of saliency. It measures the logit value L_t(x_t_i) relative to its local K × K patch in the target frame. A saliency percentile score (rank prop) is computed. The reward is linearly scaled from 0 based on rank prop exceeding a threshold (τ_rank = 0.2).
 - **Distinctiveness Reward (R_t_dist,i):** Measures the distinctiveness of the keypoint using the frozen descriptor branch. The reference descriptor d_i = Φ(I_ref, x_i) is compared to all projected descriptors in the target frame. Let d_1 and d_2 be the distances to the nearest and second-nearest neighbors. The reward is linearly scaled based on the ratio d_1/d_2 being below a threshold (τ_dist = 0.85).
- **Final Trackability Reward (R_i):** The aggregate reward for keypoint x_i is the average of its per-frame rewards across all visible instances: R_i = (1/|V_i|) * Σ_(t∈V_i) R_t_i. If a keypoint is never visible, its reward is zero.

#### 3.5. Policy Optimization

The policy network (πθ) is optimized using a composite loss function L(θ):

- **Policy Gradient Term:** The primary component is derived from the policy gradient, minimizing the negative expected reward. To reduce variance, the average reward of the entire action set R(A) = (1/N) * Σ_(i=1)^N R_i is used, weighted by the average log-probability of sampled actions (log Pθ(x_i)).
- **Spatial Entropy Regularization:** An entropy term, -λH(Pθ) = -λΣ_x Pθ(x) log Pθ(x), is added to encourage spatial diversity in keypoint detection and prevent mode collapse. The hyperparameter λ is set to 0.001.
- **Warm-up Loss (L_w):** For the initial 10% of training epochs, a warm-up loss α_t L_w is included. This term weakly supervises the sigmoid of the policy's logit map using keypoint locations from the FAST detector (e.g., via Binary Cross-Entropy loss). The weight α_t linearly anneals from 1.0 to 0.0 during this warm-up phase.
- **Training Details:** Images are resized to 480px for keypoint policy learning. Fixed-length sequences of 5 images are used, with N = 256 keypoints sampled per RL step. Training is conducted for 50,000 steps using the Adam optimizer with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule (2e-4 to 5e-6). During inference, a sigmoid function is applied to the keypoint branch output, followed by 5x5 Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS). Soft mutual nearest neighbor (MNN) operation is used for feature matching, discarding matches with probability below 0.01.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates TraqPoint against state-of-the-art methods across a range of 3D vision tasks, encompassing both pairwise and sequential applications, along with ablation studies to analyze individual components.

#### 4.1. Relative Pose Estimation

- **Datasets:** MegaDepth (outdoor scenes) and ScanNet (indoor scenes), testing robustness to viewpoint changes, illumination variations, and repetitive textures.
- **Metrics:** Area Under the Curve (AUC) for pose errors at thresholds of 5°, 10°, and 20°, using OpenCV's RANSAC for essential matrix estimation. Top 4,096 keypoints were used.
- **Results:** TraqPoint consistently achieved higher AUC scores than existing state-of-the-art pairwise methods and other RL-based approaches. For instance, on MegaDepth, TraqPoint improved AUC@5 by 3.9 compared to RDD, and by 2.9 on ScanNet. It also showed notable advantages over RL-based methods like DISK and RIPE. Furthermore, TraqPoint, using only the MNN matcher, demonstrated a 5.9 AUC@5 boost on MegaDepth compared to methods employing additional matchers (e.g., SP+LG) and exhibited comparable accuracy on ScanNet, despite not being trained on indoor data. Qualitative results indicated that TraqPoint generated more structurally salient keypoints in texture-rich areas with strong cross-view consistency, contributing to enhanced pairwise matching accuracy.

#### 4.2. Visual Localization

- **Dataset:** Aachen Day-Night dataset, which poses challenges related to weather, season, viewpoint, and day-night cycles for localizing nighttime images against a daytime 3D model.
- **Metrics:** AUC of estimated camera poses under translation error thresholds of {0.25m, 0.5m, 1.0m} and rotation error thresholds of {2°, 5°, 10°}, evaluated using HLoc. Top 4,096 keypoints were used.
- **Results:** TraqPoint achieved superior performance across all daytime settings and for two out of three nighttime settings, demonstrating robustness to varying environmental conditions.

#### 4.3. Visual Odometry (VO)

- **Dataset:** KITTI Odometry Benchmark, sequences 01-03, covering real-world driving scenarios with diverse conditions.
- **Metrics:** Average Trajectory Error (ATE) and Maximum Trajectory Error (MTE) for trajectory accuracy, and Average Keypoint Tracking Length (AKTL) for keypoint tracking consistency. Top 4,096 keypoints and MNN matching were used.
- **Results:** TraqPoint achieved lower ATE and MTE, indicating improved trajectory estimation accuracy. It also demonstrated substantial gains in AKTL, outperforming RDD and RIPE by significant margins. For example, on sequence 01, TraqPoint achieved an AKTL of 7.3 compared to RDD's 4.6 and RIPE's 4.1. This suggested that TraqPoint's keypoints maintained better spatial consistency in temporal sequences, contributing to more stable camera pose and trajectory estimation under challenging outdoor conditions.

#### 4.4. 3D Reconstruction

- **Dataset:** ETH benchmark, specifically three medium-scale scenes.
- **Metrics:** Number of registered images (Reg. Images), number of sparse points in the point cloud (Sparse Pts), average track length (Track Len), and mean reprojection error (Reproj. Error), using COLMAP with a ratio test of 0.8 and mutual check. Maximum 20,000 keypoints were used.
- **Results:** TraqPoint achieved the highest number of registered images, generated sparse points, and average track length across all evaluated scenes. For instance, on Madrid Metropolis, it registered 693 images and generated 254k sparse points, with an AKTL of 11.14, compared to RDD's 632 images, 154k points, and AKTL of 9.40. The reprojection error was marginally higher in some cases, which the authors attributed to retaining "hard" keypoints crucial for increasing reconstruction density and track length. Qualitative visualizations indicated a more rational distribution of keypoints, avoiding textureless regions and exhibiting a structured arrangement in texture-rich areas, which contributed to improved reconstruction quality.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Sequential vs. Pairwise Reinforcement Learning:**
 - A two-frame simplified RL variant (pairwise reward) resulted in an AUC@5 of 53.3 and AKTL of 4.3, compared to TraqPoint's 55.8 AUC@5 and 6.6 AKTL, indicating the benefits of sequence-level optimization.
 - A basic matching reward (awarding keypoints forming nearest-neighbor matches) yielded lower performance (AUC@5 of 49.7, AKTL of 2.8), demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed composite trackability reward.
- **Reward Function Design:**
 - Removing the ranking reward led to a decrease in AUC@5 (52.6) and AKTL (4.0).
 - Removing the distinctiveness reward also reduced AUC@5 (54.6) and AKTL (5.9).
 - A supervised baseline (without RL learning) showed significant performance degradation (AUC@5 of 52.0, AKTL of 3.8), underscoring the critical role of the RL framework for long-term trackability.
- **Backbone Architecture Analysis:**
 - While replacing the DINOv3-ConvNeXt backbone with DINOv2-ViT or ResNet-50 led to minor performance variations, the sequence-aware RL paradigm consistently provided gains across all backbones. This suggests that the proposed RL approach complements descriptor quality rather than solely depending on it. DINOv3-ConvNeXt was chosen for its combined multi-scale and strong semantic representation capabilities.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
 - A study on sequence length and number of sampled keypoints indicated optimal performance with a training sequence length of 5 and 256 sampled keypoints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the field of 3D computer vision by addressing a fundamental limitation in keypoint detection and description: the insufficient optimization for long-term trackability in sequential visual data.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Conceptual Reframing:** By reformulating keypoint detection as a sequential decision-making problem within a Reinforcement Learning framework, the paper introduces a new conceptual approach that departs from the prevalent pairwise matching paradigm. This perspective is more aligned with the actual requirements of continuous perception systems.
- **Direct Optimization for Trackability:** The development of TraqPoint, with its novel track-aware reward mechanism, allows for direct optimization of keypoint quality for long-term consistency and distinctiveness across multiple views. This explicit focus on "trackability" rather than merely "matchability" addresses a critical gap that compromises the robustness of 3D vision systems.
- **Methodological Advancements:** The proposed hybrid sampling strategy effectively balances exploitation and exploration, ensuring spatially diverse keypoint detection while focusing on high-probability regions. The composite reward function, combining rank-based saliency consistency and Lowe-ratio-inspired distinctiveness, provides a more comprehensive signal for learning robust keypoints.
- **Demonstrated Performance Gains:** The extensive experimental validation across diverse and challenging benchmarks (relative pose estimation, visual localization, visual odometry, 3D reconstruction) demonstrates that TraqPoint consistently outperforms established state-of-the-art methods. The significant improvements in metrics like Average Keypoint Tracking Length (AKTL) directly substantiate the enhanced long-term stability of the learned keypoints.
- **Broad Applicability:** The improvements in keypoint trackability have direct implications for the robustness and accuracy of a wide array of 3D vision applications. These include, but are not limited to, Structure-from-Motion (SfM), Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), visual odometry for autonomous systems, and augmented reality/virtual reality (AR/VR) environments.

The potential impact of this work is in fostering more stable, accurate, and reliable performance of sequential vision systems, particularly in real-world scenarios characterized by dynamic environments, significant viewpoint changes, and challenging illumination conditions. This novel RL-based approach provides a new direction for research in learning local features that are intrinsically suited for long-term tracking tasks, potentially influencing future designs of keypoint detectors and descriptor learning architectures.
