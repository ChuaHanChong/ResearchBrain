# WorldPlanner: Monte Carlo Tree Search and MPC with Action-Conditioned Visual World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldPlanner introduces a model-based framework for robotic manipulation that integrates action-conditioned visual world models with Monte Carlo Tree Search and Model Predictive Control. The system demonstrates superior performance over behavior cloning methods on real-world manipulation tasks, learning from only a few hours of unstructured play data.

## Problem

- Behavior Cloning (BC) for robotic control requires extensive, high-quality, task-specific demonstrations, struggles to generalize to novel situations, and cannot recover from out-of-distribution states.
- Long-horizon planning in real-world robotics using learned visual world models is challenging due to error accumulation in predictions and prohibitive computational requirements for high-fidelity models.
- Data collection for robotics is often costly, demanding frequent environment resets and careful curation of goal-directed demonstrations.

## Method

- A two-stage framework is developed: first, learning a diffusion-based visual ==world model==, a diffusion action generator (action prior), and image-conditioned reward models from unstructured 'play data'.
- Global planning is performed using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), which leverages the world model for rollouts and the action prior to guide action sampling within the learned dynamics distribution.
- A local, zeroth-order Model Predictive Control (MPC) system executes MCTS-generated plans on the real robot in a closed-loop fashion, using the world and reward models for optimization and visual feedback.

## Results

- The diffusion world model consistently predicted future states over 11 seconds (64 steps) with high visual fidelity across rigid, multi-object, and deformable interactions.
- WorldPlanner achieved a **97%** success rate on the 'push-T' task with learned video rewards, outperforming Diffusion Policy (**88%**) and ACT (**60%**) baselines.
- The system successfully manipulated deformable objects and showed adaptability to various task objectives (e.g., multi-object interaction) by simply changing the image-conditioned reward function, all trained on a few hours of unstructured data using a single GPU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion-based visual world models can maintain high fidelity in auto-regressive state predictions for long horizons (over 11 seconds or 64 steps) in complex real-world environments, enabling multi-step planning.
> - Unstructured, goal-independent 'play data' is sufficient to learn effective world models and action priors, drastically reducing data collection costs compared to explicit task demonstrations.
> - The combination of MCTS for global reasoning and MPC for robust local control allows robots to synthesize novel behaviors, recover from errors, and achieve high success rates in complex manipulation tasks on physical hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "WorldPlanner" positions itself at a critical juncture in robotic control and artificial intelligence, specifically within the evolving landscape of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) and the application of generative AI to robotics.

Traditionally, a common approach for robotic task execution has been **Behavior Cloning (BC)**, where a robot learns an end-to-end policy by imitating human demonstrations. While straightforward, BC suffers from significant limitations: it requires extensive, high-quality, task-specific demonstrations, often necessitating frequent environment resets, which is prohibitively expensive to collect at scale. Furthermore, BC policies struggle to generalize to novel situations or recover from out-of-distribution states, as they merely memorize observed behaviors rather than understanding underlying dynamics.

"WorldPlanner" explicitly contrasts itself with BC, advocating for a **model-based approach**. This aligns with a growing trend in robotics to move beyond purely policy-driven methods towards systems that can reason about their environment and the consequences of their actions. The advent of high-fidelity video generative models and diffusion architectures has spurred significant breakthroughs in **explicit environment modeling directly in raw image space**. Works like, and exemplify this progress, demonstrating the capability of these models to predict future states with remarkable accuracy. "WorldPlanner" builds directly on this progress by adopting an action-conditioned visual world model.

A key aspect distinguishing this work is its focus on **long-horizon planning** in real-world robotic settings using *learned* visual world models. The paper notes that most prior contributions in this area have been limited to short-horizon planning, primarily due to issues like error accumulation in models with highly compressed latent spaces or the prohibitive computational requirements of larger "foundation world models" like Cosmos. By demonstrating long-horizon planning with a relatively small, locally trainable diffusion-based world model, WorldPlanner addresses a significant practical challenge, making advanced planning more accessible and deployable. It implicitly proposes that such smaller, task-specific models could serve as efficient "local world models" distilled from larger foundation models in the future.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the crucial aspect of **data efficiency** and **generalization**. Instead of relying on costly goal-directed demonstrations, it leverages "unstructured play data" – easily collectible interactions with no specific task objectives or environment reset requirements. This paradigm shift in data collection is vital for scaling robotic learning. The idea that action primitives useful for solving new tasks can be extracted from this general play distribution, combined with MCTS, allows for the synthesis of behaviors not explicitly seen during data collection, offering a clear advantage over BC in terms of generalization and adaptability. This resonates with the broader AI trend of learning powerful representations from diverse, uncurated data.

The integration of **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)** and **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** forms the algorithmic core. While MCTS has seen remarkable success in game environments (e.g., AlphaGo), its application in real-world robotics with *learned visual world models* for low-level action planning is highly sparse. "WorldPlanner" claims to be one of the first to achieve multi-step long-horizon planning in this context, demonstrating that such run-time reasoning is feasible. The use of a diffusion model for action sampling within MCTS is also innovative, helping to constrain the search space to the world model's training distribution and thus mitigating "hallucinations" – a common problem when world models encounter out-of-distribution inputs. The adoption of a zeroth-order MPC further contributes to robustness by closing the loop with visual feedback and handling model errors without requiring gradient computations, aligning with recent advancements in robust control.

In summary, "WorldPlanner" makes a significant contribution by presenting a practical, data-efficient, and robust model-based framework for long-horizon planning and control in real-world robotics. It bridges advancements in generative AI with classical control and planning techniques, offering a compelling alternative to traditional policy-based methods and addressing key scalability and generalization challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and underlying motivations of the "WorldPlanner" research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Behavior Cloning (BC):** The central motivation is to move beyond the constraints of BC, which relies on implicitly encoding task knowledge from human demonstrations. The authors highlight BC's demanding data requirements (low entropy, sufficient coverage, frequent resets), poor transferability to new tasks, and inability to recover from unseen states. "WorldPlanner" seeks a more robust and scalable alternative.

2. **Leverage Unstructured, Easily Collectible Data:** A critical objective is to demonstrate that rich interaction knowledge can be extracted from easily collectible "unstructured play data." This "play data" imposes no constraints on entropy or environment resetting, making it significantly less expensive and time-consuming to acquire compared to goal-directed, optimal demonstrations. The aim is to show that this play distribution contains "action primitives useful for solving new goal-specific tasks."

3. **Enable Long-Horizon Reasoning and Novel Behavior Synthesis:** The paper aims to develop a framework that can synthesize long sequences of actions and generate behaviors not directly observed in the initial training data. This contrasts with BC's memorization paradigm and is crucial for generalizable and adaptive robotic intelligence. This objective is directly supported by the planning capabilities of MCTS.

4. **Develop a High-Fidelity, Action-Conditioned Visual World Model:** A core technical objective is to train a visual world model that can accurately predict future states from raw sensory inputs (images) conditioned on robot actions over extended horizons. The model needs to be computationally efficient enough for real-time planning, avoid excessive error accumulation, and capture the multi-modality inherent in play behavior. The choice of a diffusion-based architecture and its training on a single GPU addresses these practical concerns.

5. **Ensure Reliable Planning in the Learned Model:** A common challenge with learned world models is "hallucination" when queried with out-of-distribution actions. The authors motivate the integration of a stochastic diffusion-based action sampler to keep the MCTS search within the world model's training distribution, thereby ensuring more reliable and plausible rollouts.

6. **Validate on Diverse Real-World Robotic Tasks:** The ultimate objective is to provide strong empirical validation on real robotic hardware across a range of manipulation tasks with varying levels of complexity (rigid objects, multi-object interactions, deformable objects). This real-world validation is crucial to demonstrate the practical viability and robustness of the proposed framework, moving beyond purely simulated environments.

7. **Integrate Planning with Robust Control:** To bridge the gap between idealized plans and real-world execution, the authors aim to integrate the global MCTS planner with a local, closed-loop Model Predictive Controller (MPC). This MPC, using the same world and reward models, would handle real-time feedback, small disturbances, and model errors, thereby ensuring reliable task completion on the physical robot.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more intelligent, autonomous, and generalizable robotic system that learns a deep understanding of its environment through unsupervised interaction and leverages this understanding to plan and execute complex tasks with minimal reliance on costly human demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "WorldPlanner" framework is structured into a two-stage process: first, learning foundational models from unstructured play data, and second, employing these models for active planning and control.

**Stage 1: Learning from Unstructured Play Data**

The initial stage focuses on training three core components from a dataset of "unstructured play trajectories." This dataset comprises several hours of a 7-DoF robotic arm interacting randomly with its environment, collected via teleoperation. Crucially, this data is "goal-independent" and "high-entropy," requiring no environment resets, making it significantly easier to collect than task-specific demonstrations.

1. **Diffusion World Model:**
- **Architecture:** The method adopts the DIAMOND Atari world model formulation, chosen for its high visual fidelity, fast training, and efficient inference on a local GPU. It is an auto-regressive, diffusion-based generative model.
- **Function:** Its purpose is to capture the distribution of future states ($s_{t+1}$) conditioned on a history of past states and actions ($s_{t:t-h}$, $a_{t:t-h}$).
- **Training:** A denoiser $D_\theta$ (realized by a U-Net) is trained using a score-matching loss to predict clean future states from noisy versions.
- **Inference:** During planning, it samples the next state by iteratively solving a reverse diffusion process. The use of EDM-based noise scheduling and preconditioning allows for high-quality sampling with only a few (3) denoising steps, crucial for fast parallel rollouts in MCTS.
- **Key Advantage:** Its diffusion-based nature allows it to capture multi-modality in environment dynamics and "retract to the image manifold at each prediction step," which helps prevent error accumulation and maintain visual fidelity over long horizons, even for complex real-world dynamics.

2. **Action Generator Model (Action Prior):**
- **Architecture:** A diffusion policy is trained using the same unstructured play dataset.
- **Function:** It learns the distribution of robot actions ($a_t$ | $s_t$) inherent in the play data, serving as a "prior policy" ($\pi_{prior}$).
- **Role in Planning:** This policy provides diverse action primitives, guiding the MCTS search. Importantly, actions sampled from this model remain within the world model's training distribution, which is hypothesized to minimize "hallucinations" during planning rollouts.

3. **Reward Models:** Three types of image-conditioned reward functions are considered for evaluating MCTS rollouts:
- **Fully Geometric:** For tasks with explicit geometric objectives (e.g., target pose). It uses an off-the-shelf object pose tracker to compute reward based on position and orientation differences.
- **Latent Space Image Distance (DINOv2):** A more general approach where a goal image ($s_{goal}$) and current state image ($s_t$) are encoded into a high-dimensional embedding space using a pretrained foundation image encoder (DINOv2). The reward is based on the geometric distance (e.g., Euclidean or cosine similarity) in this latent space. An optional object-centric attention mechanism (using SAM2) can be applied to mask images, focusing the reward on specific objects and improving planning performance.
- **Training Reward Functions from Video (Bradley-Terry):** A model is trained to encode temporal progress from passive video observations. It learns an energy function $f_\psi(s_t | s_{start}, s_{goal})$ that assigns higher values to states closer to the goal. This is trained using the Bradley-Terry objective on DINOv2 features from video chunks, capturing fine-grained notions of progress towards a goal.

**Stage 2: Planning and Control**

Using the learned world model, action prior, and reward models, the system implements a global planner and a local controller.

1. **Global MCTS Planner (Algorithm 1):**
- **Objective:** To find a long sequence of actions to transition the robot from an initial state ($s_s$) to a target state ($s_g$).
- **Tree Building:** MCTS iteratively builds a search tree where nodes are states and edges are short trajectories.
- **Steps:**
- **Traversal:** Selects the most promising node using the UCB1 criterion (Upper Confidence Bound 1), balancing exploration and exploitation.
- **Expansion:** From a leaf node, the action prior ($\pi_{prior}$) generates short sequences of actions, which are rolled out in parallel using the world model (simulator). New states at the end of these rollouts become new nodes in the tree.
- **Simulation:** From newly added nodes, multiple longer rollouts (M_{sim} rollouts for H_{sim} steps) are run using the action prior and world model. The reward model evaluates these rollouts.
- **Backpropagation:** The total reward from the simulations is accumulated up the path to the root, updating node values and visit counts.
- **Output:** A plan (sequence of actions and states) from the root to the node with the maximum average value and sufficient visits.

2. **Local Zeroth-Order MPC Controller (Algorithm 2):**
- **Objective:** To execute the MCTS-generated plan on the real robot in a closed-loop fashion, mitigating deviations due to noise, model errors, and unmodeled dynamics.
- **Approach:** Uses a zeroth-order optimizer (specifically, a CEM-like update rule).
- **Process:**
- **Population Generation:** Around the MCTS-planned actions within a short optimization window ($H_{MPC}$), Gaussian distributions are defined. Multiple action trajectories are sampled from these distributions, augmented with zero-mean trajectories for broader exploration.
- **Rollout and Evaluation:** Each sampled trajectory is rolled out using the learned world model from the robot's current camera observation ($s_{cam}$). Rewards are computed using the reward model (e.g., unmasked DINOv2 distance to the plan states).
- **Update:** The top-K$_{elite}$ samples (those with the best rewards) are used to update the mean ($\mu_t$) and covariance ($\Sigma_t$) of the Gaussian distributions for the next iteration (Eq. 6), focusing the search.
- **Execution:** After $N_{MPC}$ iterations, a short chunk ($h_{exec}$) of the optimized action sequence is executed on the robot. The process repeats with a new $s_{cam}$.
- **Re-planning:** If the robot's state deviates too much from the MCTS plan ($d(s_{cam}, s_{P_t}) > d_{max}$), the MCTS planner is re-triggered.

This comprehensive methodology, combining generative visual models, advanced planning, and robust control, aims to create a highly capable and adaptable robotic system from easily acquired interaction data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "WorldPlanner" paper presents several compelling findings validated through extensive real-world robotic experiments.

1. **High-Fidelity Long-Horizon World Model Predictions:**
- The learned diffusion-based visual world model demonstrates remarkable fidelity in predicting future states. Figure 1 clearly shows that model rollouts remain "highly consistent with the state of the world even after 11.28 seconds (64 steps) of auto-regressive forward integration." This is crucial for long-horizon planning and is demonstrated across diverse tasks involving rigid, multi-object, and deformable interactions. This finding supports the hypothesis that a relatively small, task-specific diffusion model can capture complex real-world dynamics.

2. **Superior Performance over Behavior Cloning on Manipulation Tasks:**
- For the "push-T" task (pushing a T-shaped object to the board's center), MCTS significantly outperforms Behavior Cloning (BC) baselines (Diffusion Policy and ACT). As shown in Table I, MCTS with a learned video reward achieves a 97% success rate for translation errors up to 10cm, compared to 88% for Diffusion Policy and 60% for ACT. Even with a simpler geometric reward, MCTS outperforms BC. This quantitative evidence supports the core claim that planning leads to a significant improvement over BC, especially due to the planner's ability to "generate new behaviors online" and recover from errors, unlike BC policies that merely memorize demonstrations.

3. **Effectiveness of Learned Reward Models:**
- The comparison between the geometric reward and the reward learned from passive videos (Sec. III-D.3) shows that the latter leads to higher planning performance. Figure 3 illustrates that the video-learned reward model produces "clear, low-variance monotonic progress" towards the goal, providing a more nuanced and effective signal for planning compared to simple geodesic distance. This highlights the power of learning rich reward functions from uncurated visual data.

4. **Flexible Goal Specification and Multi-Object Interaction:**
- The framework demonstrates adaptability to different task objectives by simply changing the reward function. For multi-object interaction (push-cubes task), the masked DINOv2-based reward (Sec. III-D.2) enables selective manipulation, allowing the robot to focus on moving specific objects while potentially leveraging other objects for indirect pushing (Figure 4). This showcases the ability to optimize for diverse, user-defined goals.

5. **Handling Deformable Objects and Diverse Planning Strategies:**
- The planner successfully manipulates a flexible chain to a desired form. Interestingly, due to the stochasticity of the action sampler and the multi-modality of the diffusion world model, different MCTS executions from identical start and goal states can yield distinct, equally valid strategies (e.g., behaviors B1 and B2 in Figure 6). This demonstrates the planner's capacity to explore a diverse range of solutions for complex deformable object manipulation.
- An analysis of problem length vs. performance for deformable object tasks shows an expected negative correlation, but even for challenging scenarios, the terminal states are qualitatively close to the goal (Figure 7 and 8).

6. **Robust Closed-Loop Execution with MPC:**
- The integration of MCTS with a zeroth-order MPC controller for real-time, closed-loop execution is successful. Figure 5 demonstrates the real robot tracking MCTS-generated plans for the push-T task, tolerating small disturbances and modeling errors.
- The planning time for MCTS is reported to be "under five minutes on average," indicating practicality for real-world scenarios, which is attributed to the lightweight world model and the guided search strategy from the action-prior policy. MPC iterations are also efficient, taking "a couple of seconds" per control step.

7. **Data Efficiency:**
- All models (world model, action prior, reward models) are successfully trained on "a few hours of unstructured play data" using a single local GPU. This underscores the data-efficient nature of the approach, making it more accessible and scalable compared to methods requiring massive curated datasets or powerful computational clusters.

In essence, the findings collectively demonstrate that learning action-conditioned visual world models from readily available unstructured data, combined with advanced planning (MCTS) and control (MPC) techniques, offers a powerful, robust, and generalizable framework for solving complex real-world robotic manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming traditional behavior cloning methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "WorldPlanner" research carries significant implications for the future of robotic autonomy and AI, with potential impacts across several dimensions:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Robotic Learning:** The most profound impact lies in its strong advocacy for a model-based approach as a powerful alternative, and in many cases superior, to policy-based Behavior Cloning (BC) for complex real-world tasks. By demonstrating that robots can synthesize novel behaviors through explicit reasoning rather than memorizing demonstrations, it challenges the prevailing BC paradigm and offers a path towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.

2. **Reduced Data Collection Burden and Scalability:** The reliance on "unstructured play data" rather than costly, task-specific, and low-entropy demonstrations is a game-changer for scaling robotic learning. This drastically lowers the barrier to data collection, making it feasible to gather large, diverse datasets without constant human supervision or environment resets. This could accelerate progress in various robotics domains where data acquisition is a major bottleneck.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Task Transfer:** The ability to generate new behaviors and adapt to unseen situations, which is a key advantage of planning with a learned world model, dramatically improves generalization. This framework has the potential to enable robots to tackle a wider array of tasks, including those not explicitly present in the initial play data, and to transfer knowledge more effectively across different task variations or even new environments. This is a critical step towards truly general-purpose robots.

4. **Robustness and Reliability in Real-World Deployment:** The combination of global MCTS planning with local MPC control provides a robust framework for real-world deployment. MCTS handles long-horizon decision-making, while MPC offers real-time feedback and disturbance rejection. The use of a diffusion-based action sampler also mitigates model "hallucinations," leading to more reliable plans and executions. This increased robustness is essential for moving robots out of controlled lab settings into more dynamic and unpredictable environments.

5. **Practicality and Accessibility:** The demonstration that a high-fidelity visual world model can be trained on "a single local GPU within a few days" from "a few hours of play data" makes advanced model-based planning more accessible to a broader research community and smaller labs. This avoids the need for massive computational resources or enormous pre-trained foundation models, fostering innovation and rapid iteration. It also suggests the possibility of personalized "local world models" for specific robotic platforms or environments.

6. **Foundation for Future AI-Robotics Integration:** This work lays a strong foundation for integrating ever more powerful generative AI models and reasoning capabilities into robotics. The authors explicitly mention future work involving "foundation joint action and world models to enable hierarchical planning and zero-shot task execution in novel settings." This paper provides a concrete blueprint for how such advanced models can be effectively deployed and leveraged in practical robotic control systems.

7. **Advancements in Visual World Models:** The paper contributes to the understanding and application of diffusion-based visual world models, particularly highlighting their capacity to maintain long-term fidelity and capture multi-modality in complex real-world dynamics. This pushes the boundaries of what is possible with explicit visual modeling for robot control.

In conclusion, "WorldPlanner" represents a significant stride towards more intelligent, autonomous, and capable robots. By offering a robust, data-efficient, and generalizable model-based planning and control framework, it paves the way for robots that can learn from minimal unstructured interaction, understand their environment deeply, and execute complex tasks with unprecedented flexibility and reliability.
