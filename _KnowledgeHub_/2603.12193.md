# SaPaVe: Towards Active Perception and Manipulation in Vision-Language-Action Models for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> SaPaVe presents an end-to-end framework for robots to perform active manipulation by jointly learning semantic active perception and active-view execution with data efficiency. The framework, developed by researchers at Peking University and Beihang University, utilizes a decoupled training strategy and Universal Spatial Knowledge Injection, achieving superior success rates in real-world active manipulation tasks and robust generalization across various out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing active perception methods often lack semantic understanding and fine-grained continuous camera control.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are sensitive to viewpoint shifts, as they are often trained on fixed, near-optimal views, limiting their robustness in active-view execution.
- Augmenting VLA models with continuous camera movement is data-intensive and can lead to conflicts between learning camera control and manipulation due to a unified action space.

## Method

- A decoupled architecture with a lightweight ==Camera Adapter== (LoRA) for the VLM and separate action decoders for camera and manipulation actions.
- A two-stage, bottom-up training strategy: first, learning semantic ==active perception== using a large-scale dataset (ActiveViewPose-200K), then fine-tuning for active manipulation with hybrid data.
- Integration of Universal Spatial Knowledge Injection to enhance 3D geometric consistency and view-invariant understanding for action guidance.

## Results

- SaPaVe achieved an **84.3%** average success rate on semantic active perception tasks (ActiveViewPose-200K), outperforming generalist VLMs like Gemini-2.5-Pro (**72.7%**).
- In real-world active manipulation, SaPaVe demonstrated a superior average success rate of **85.00%**, significantly exceeding existing VLA models such as π0 (**45.00%**) and GR00T-N1 (**53.75%**).
- The framework exhibited robust generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios, maintaining high success rates (e.g., 85-**90%** for unseen objects in occluded pick-and-place tasks) in real-world settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct training on specialized data for semantic camera control (ActiveViewPose-200K) is more effective for precise active perception than relying on emergent properties of generalist VLMs.
> - Decoupling camera and manipulation action learning prevents interference and is crucial for robust active-view execution and data-efficient skill acquisition.
> - Integrating 3D geometric knowledge significantly enhances spatial awareness, improving robustness to viewpoint changes and occlusions in active manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied agents capable of actively perceiving and interacting with complex, dynamic environments represents a long-standing goal in robotics and artificial intelligence. This work addresses the challenges associated with active manipulation, which necessitates both semantic active perception and robust active-view execution.

Historically, active perception has been approached through methods such as Next-Best-View (NBV) planning, which often rely on non-end-to-end pipelines and lack semantic understanding, limiting their scalability and adaptability to diverse tasks. With the advent of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), some research has reframed active perception as a Visual Question Answering (VQA) task, where VLMs select optimal viewpoints from discrete sets. However, this discrete approach limits fine-grained camera control and struggles to connect high-level semantics with continuous camera pose spaces.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models aim to provide end-to-end solutions by integrating camera control with manipulation actions. However, many existing VLA models are trained on fixed, near-optimal head-camera views, rendering them susceptible to viewpoint shifts and restricting their robustness in active-view execution. Furthermore, directly augmenting the action space of VLA models to include continuous camera movement often necessitates large volumes of costly real-world data, which is scarce for concurrent camera movement and manipulation. This scarcity leads to conflicts and suboptimal performance when fine-tuning VLA models in a unified action space.

This paper positions itself by proposing an end-to-end framework that addresses these limitations through a decoupled learning strategy and the introduction of new datasets and benchmarks. It aims to bridge the gap between semantic understanding provided by VLMs and the precise, continuous control required for active manipulation, while also improving data efficiency and robustness to dynamic viewpoints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end framework, named SaPaVe, that enables robots to perform active manipulation by jointly learning semantic active perception and active-view execution in a data-efficient manner.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified challenges in current robotic systems:
- **Unifying Semantic Perception and Robust Execution:** Existing methods frequently struggle to integrate semantic-driven perception, which involves understanding task-critical cues, with robust execution, which must adapt to dynamic and potentially suboptimal viewpoints. An example is the need for a robot to proactively shift its viewpoint to reveal a hidden object (semantic active perception) and then manipulate it effectively even if the view is not perfectly centered (active-view execution).
- **Limitations of Discrete Viewpoint Selection:** While VLMs have improved semantic understanding, formulating active perception as selecting from a discrete set of viewpoints restricts the robot's flexibility and precision in continuous camera control.
- **Sensitivity of Fixed-View VLA Models:** Current end-to-end VLA models, often trained on fixed near-optimal camera views, exhibit sensitivity to viewpoint changes. This limits their applicability in cluttered or dynamic real-world environments where objects might be occluded or out-of-view.
- **Data Scarcity and Training Challenges:** Collecting large-scale real-world datasets with concurrent head-camera and manipulation actions is expensive and complex. Simply fine-tuning existing VLA models by adding camera movement to a unified action space often leads to increased data demands and potential interference between learning camera control and manipulation, resulting in suboptimal performance.
- **Lack of 3D Geometric Awareness:** Many current VLA models do not fully leverage 3D geometric priors, making them sensitive to camera perturbations and hindering effective reasoning under viewpoint changes.
- **Absence of Standardized Benchmarks:** There is a lack of established simulation benchmarks specifically designed for evaluating active perception and manipulation beyond fixed-view settings, making rigorous comparison and development challenging.

To address these motivations, SaPaVe aims to decouple camera and manipulation actions for learning, first focusing on semantic camera control with a large-scale dataset, and then jointly optimizing both action types using hybrid data. This approach is intended to minimize cross-interference, leverage VLM capabilities for semantic understanding, and enhance robustness through 3D geometry integration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SaPaVe framework is designed to facilitate active manipulation through a specific problem formulation, a modular architecture, and a two-stage training strategy, supported by novel datasets and benchmarks.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The active manipulation task is defined as learning a policy $\pi_{\theta}: \mathcal{O} \times \mathcal{L} \rightarrow \mathcal{A}$. At each timestep $t$, the policy predicts a joint action trajectory $\mathcal{A}_t = \{\mathcal{A}_{\text{head},t}, \mathcal{A}_{\text{other},t}\}$ given an observation $\mathcal{O}_t$ and a language instruction $\mathcal{L}$.
- **Observation ($\mathcal{O}_t$):** Comprises an RGB image ($\mathcal{I}_t$) and optional 3D geometric information ($\mathcal{G}_t$), such as depth maps and camera intrinsics/extrinsics.
- **Action ($\mathcal{A}_t$):** Decoupled into two sequences over a horizon $k$:
 - **Head Camera Action ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{head},t}$):** Consists of relative pitch and yaw adjustments for the robot's head.
 - **Manipulation Action ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{other},t}$):** For the Unitree G1 humanoid with dual 7-DoF arms and dual 6-DoF Inspire hands, this represents 26-DoF joint position deltas.
This decoupling is central to the proposed two-stage learning strategy, which first enriches the model with semantic active perception priors and then optimizes both camera control and manipulation actions for active-view execution.

#### 3.2. Architecture

SaPaVe's architecture is designed to acquire semantic active perception priors and robust active-view execution capabilities.
- **Decoupled Action Heads and Camera Adapter:**
 - **Camera Adapter:** A LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) module is added to the VLM backbone (Eagle-2, integrating a SigLIP-2 image encoder with a SmolLM2 LLM). This adapter is trained on a large-scale semantic camera movement dataset to learn active perception priors without significantly altering the original VLM's weights or incurring high computational costs. This preserves general semantic knowledge while specializing for camera control.
 - **Decoupled Action Head:** The model features two distinct decoders: a Camera Action Decoder (2-DoF MLP) for head movement (pitch/yaw) and an Other Action Decoder (26-DoF MLP) for manipulation. This decoupling aims to prevent interference between the learning processes for these distinct action types.
- **Universal Spatial Knowledge Injection:**
 - To enhance 3D geometric consistency and view-invariant understanding, a Universal Spatial Encoder, inherited from the MapAnything 3D geometry model, is incorporated. This encoder can process various 3D geometry configurations (e.g., depth maps, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, ray directions).
 - The encoded spatial tokens are fused with the VLM's output semantic tokens via element-wise addition, forming a combined multi-modal context. This fused representation then serves as the Key and Value in the cross-attention layers of the Decoupled Action Heads (which are implemented as Diffusion Transformer blocks), guiding the action-denoising process with both semantic intent and precise 3D geometry.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Strategy

A bottom-up, curriculum-style training strategy is employed to sequentially acquire active perception and manipulation skills.
- **Stage 1: Semantic Active Perception Alignment:**
 - **Objective:** To equip the model with robust semantic active perception priors, enabling it to understand "where to look" based on language instructions.
 - **Data:** Uses the proposed ActiveViewPose-200K dataset, comprising 200k image-language-camera movement pairs.
 - **Optimization:** The Universal Spatial Encoder and Manipulation Action Decoder are frozen. Only the Camera Adapter (LoRA layers on Eagle-2) and the Camera Action Decoder are updated.
 - **Loss Function:** Minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted head camera movement ($\hat{A}_{\text{head},t}$) and the ground-truth ($A^*_{\text{head},t}$).
- **Stage 2: Active Manipulation Fine-tuning:**
 - **Objective:** To enable effective active manipulation by coordinating learned gaze control with dexterous arm movements.
 - **Data:** A mixture of ActiveViewPose-200K data and active manipulation robot data (from ActiveManip-Bench and real-world collection).
 - **Optimization:** The Camera Adapter remains frozen, but both the Camera Action Decoder and the Other Action Decoder are trained. The Universal Spatial Encoder is also involved in providing 3D context.
 - **Loss Function:** A weighted sum of MSE losses for head movement and other actions: $L_{\text{stage2}} = \lambda_{\text{head}} L_{\text{head}} + \lambda_{\text{other}} L_{\text{other}}$. This prioritizes the higher-dimensional manipulation space while maintaining visual stability.

#### 3.4. ActiveViewPose-200K and ActiveManip-Bench

Two novel contributions support the training and evaluation:
- **ActiveViewPose-200K Dataset:**
 - **Purpose:** A large-scale, high-quality dataset of 200k image-language-optimal camera movement pairs for semantic active perception learning.
 - **Construction:** Created through a semi-automatic process involving 3D asset curation (from Objaverse), procedural scene synthesis (using Infinigen), task-driven view annotation (heuristic algorithm for optimal viewpoints), and instruction augmentation (GPT-4o for linguistic diversity, followed by manual refinement). It includes diverse task templates and prompt modalities (visual centering, spatial directive, common sense).
- **ActiveManip-Bench Benchmark:**
 - **Purpose:** The first simulated active manipulation benchmark designed to evaluate capabilities beyond fixed-view settings.
 - **Details:** Built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, featuring a G1 humanoid robot with Inspire Hands and an active head-mounted camera. It includes 100 objects across 20 diverse scenes and 12 semantic active manipulation tasks.
 - **Task Structure:** Tasks are categorized by horizon length (atomic, short-horizon composite, long-horizon sequential) and visual complexity (unoccluded, occluded, out-of-view).
 - **Data Collection:** Uses VR-based human teleoperation, scalable data augmentation (visual randomization, self-improving trajectory generation), and semi-automatic semantic enrichment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings to evaluate SaPaVe's performance across various aspects.

#### 4.1. Semantic Active Perception Evaluation (ActiveViewPose-200K)

- SaPaVe, trained in Stage 1, demonstrated a notably higher success rate (84.3% average) on the ActiveViewPose-200K test sets compared to general VLMs like Gemini-2.5-Pro (72.7%) and specialized spatial VLMs like Multi-SpatialMLLM (70.2%).
- The performance difference was particularly pronounced in Test2, where instructions omitted explicit camera movements, requiring deeper semantic understanding. SaPaVe achieved 78.3% compared to Gemini-2.5-Pro's 68.2%, suggesting that direct training on specialized data is crucial for learning precise semantic active perception, rather than relying solely on emergent properties of generalist VLMs.

#### 4.2. Fixed and Dynamic Cameras Evaluation (ActiveManip-Bench Simulation)

- **Impact of Dynamic Viewpoints:** A fixed camera setup resulted in substantially lower success rates across all task types, especially for "Out-of-View" tasks (11% success rate), which dropped by over 60% compared to active camera setups. This indicated that fixed views severely limit exploration and active manipulation.
- **Fixed + Wrist Camera Limitations:** While combining a fixed head camera with wrist cameras improved performance over a single fixed camera (52.33% average success rate vs. 36.17%), it still underperformed significantly compared to active camera configurations. This suggests that wrist cameras provide complementary information but cannot fully compensate for the lack of active head movement, particularly for tasks requiring broad environmental scanning.
- **Minimal Gain from Active + Wrist Camera:** The integration of an active head camera with wrist cameras showed only a marginal improvement (73.16% average) compared to using only an active head camera (74.83% average). This finding suggests that once robust dynamic perception and strong 3D spatial understanding are established via an active head camera, additional dynamic viewpoints might not provide significant performance benefits, and could potentially introduce noise or data mismatch issues if not carefully integrated.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Existing VLA Models (Real-World Active Manipulation)

- SaPaVe demonstrated superior performance in real-world active manipulation tasks compared to existing VLA models (π0 and GR00T-N1) when fine-tuned for active manipulation.
- SaPaVe achieved an average success rate of 85.00%, significantly outperforming π0 (45.00%) and GR00T-N1 (53.75%).
- This performance gap was attributed to two main factors: (1) direct VLA fine-tuning typically lacks sufficient active perception priors, and (2) existing VLA models often lack dedicated modules for active-view manipulation. The results validated the necessity and effectiveness of SaPaVe's bottom-up strategy, which first establishes robust semantic active perception priors and then learns active manipulation.

#### 4.4. Generalization Ability Evaluation (Real-World OOD Scenarios)

- SaPaVe exhibited robust generalization capabilities across out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, including unseen objects, varying lighting conditions, and diverse scenes.
- For "Occluded Pick-and-Place," success rates remained high (e.g., 85-90% for unseen objects, 80-90% for different lighting, 85-90% for unseen scenes). Similar strong performance was observed for other task categories.
- This suggests that the model developed robust high-level semantic understanding for interpreting OOD objects and instructions, and that the incorporation of 3D information bolstered its spatial perception, enabling reliable operations in diverse environments.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Real-World Tasks)

- **Bottom-Up Training Strategy:** Both Stage 1 (Semantic Active Perception Alignment) and Stage 2 (Active Manipulation Fine-tuning) were crucial. Omitting Stage 1 drastically reduced success rates, particularly for "Out-of-View" tasks (e.g., halving performance in articulated manipulation), underscoring the importance of initial semantic active perception priors. Omitting Stage 2 also reduced overall success, highlighting the necessity of fine-tuning for active manipulation.
- **Decoupled Formulation:** Using decoupled action heads (Camera Action Decoder and Other Action Decoder) outperformed a unified single action decoder. This supported the hypothesis that camera movement is embodiment-agnostic, and a decoupled approach prevents disruption of semantic active perception priors and improves active manipulation capability.
- **Camera Adapter:** Employing a lightweight Camera Adapter (LoRA) proved superior to fully fine-tuning the VLM. Full fine-tuning led to performance drops across all tasks, indicating that the adapter effectively learns camera rotation while preserving the VLM's general high-level semantic information essential for task understanding.
- **Universal Spatial Knowledge Injection:** Omitting Universal Spatial Knowledge Injection resulted in a significant performance drop (e.g., 15% in "Occluded Pick-and-Place"). This confirmed that consistent 3D spatial understanding is critical for active manipulation and that injecting 3D knowledge effectively guides action output and facilitates implicit 3D learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SaPaVe represents a contribution towards the development of embodied agents capable of active perception and manipulation in complex, dynamic environments. The framework’s key aspects contribute to addressing existing challenges in robotics.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Unified Active Perception and Manipulation:** SaPaVe provides an end-to-end framework that explicitly integrates semantic active perception with active-view execution. This is a departure from systems that treat these capabilities as separate or rely on fixed viewpoints, offering a more holistic approach to robotic interaction.
- **Data-Efficient Learning:** The proposed bottom-up, two-stage training strategy, combined with the decoupled action space and parameter-efficient Camera Adapter, enables data-efficient acquisition of complex skills. This addresses a critical challenge in robotics, where collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world demonstration data is often prohibitive.
- **Enhanced Semantic Understanding and Robustness:** By leveraging VLMs with a specialized Camera Adapter, the model demonstrates advanced semantic active perception capabilities, outperforming generalist VLMs in translating high-level instructions into precise camera movements. The Universal Spatial Knowledge Injection further enhances the model's 3D geometric awareness, contributing to robustness against viewpoint changes and complex occlusions.
- **Novel Datasets and Benchmarks:** The introduction of ActiveViewPose-200K and ActiveManip-Bench addresses a gap in existing resources. ActiveViewPose-200K provides a large-scale dataset for training semantic camera control, while ActiveManip-Bench offers the first simulation benchmark tailored for evaluating active manipulation, facilitating standardized comparisons and future research in this domain.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated ability of SaPaVe to generalize to unseen objects, lighting conditions, and scenes in real-world scenarios suggests a robust and adaptable policy, which is a key requirement for deployment in unstructured environments.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement of Humanoid Robotics:** SaPaVe's approach, particularly its focus on human-like active perception and manipulation on a humanoid platform like the Unitree G1, could contribute to the development of more capable and autonomous humanoid robots for various applications, including household assistance, industrial tasks, and exploration.
- **Foundation for Future Robotics Research:** The proposed framework, datasets, and benchmarks offer a foundation for subsequent research in areas such as learning more complex, long-horizon active manipulation tasks, developing more sophisticated mechanisms for fusing multi-modal and 3D geometric information, and improving sim-to-real transfer.
- **Mitigation of Data Scarcity in Robot Learning:** The data-efficient learning strategy could reduce the reliance on extensive and costly real-world data collection, making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible to researchers and developers.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy in Unstructured Environments:** By enabling robots to actively perceive and adapt to changing visual conditions, SaPaVe could significantly improve their ability to operate autonomously in less structured and more dynamic real-world settings, beyond controlled laboratory environments.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The primary limitation is that the robot operates with a fixed base, confining active manipulation to "local" exploration. Future work aims to extend SaPaVe to mobile manipulation settings, integrating active perception with mobile base navigation. This would enable the robot to navigate to look for objects in different rooms and approach to manipulate objects in confined spaces, moving towards truly general-purpose embodied agents.
