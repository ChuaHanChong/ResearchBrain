# Load-bearing Assessment for Safe Locomotion of Quadruped Robots on Collapsing Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> A locomotion framework for quadruped robots assesses terrain load-bearing capacity using only joint measurements, eliminating the need for specialized sensors, enabling safe traversal and recovery on challenging collapsing terrains. The system integrates trajectory optimization, model predictive control, and a state machine to proactively probe and adapt to unstable ground.

## Problem

- Quadruped robots face significant risks when navigating unstable or collapsing terrains, as traditional exteroceptive sensing provides only surface-level observations of stability.
- Existing terrain probing methods often require specialized hardware, such as dedicated robotic arms or custom foot-mounted force/torque sensors, which adds complexity, weight, and cost.
- Maintaining static stability in quadruped robots during probing is challenging, as the robot operates with only three legs in contact, creating a reduced support polygon.

## Method

- A framework integrating Trajectory Optimization (TO), Perceptive ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) with probing-specific constraints, and a State Machine for coordinated actions.
- Leverages existing robot joint measurements (torques and positions) to infer terrain stability and perform load-bearing assessment, eliminating the need for specialized hardware.
- Proactively computes an adaptive Ground Reaction Force (GRF) envelope for each probing leg and implements real-time collapse detection and recovery mechanisms.

## Results

- The Aliengo robot successfully traversed custom-made tilting plank platforms and natural loose rock terrains, consistently maintaining stability and recovering from detected collapses.
- The proposed framework significantly outperformed a baseline MPC controller that lacked probing constraints, demonstrating its necessity for safe navigation on collapsing terrains.
- Experiments showed that sufficient probing force was applied to detect terrain collapse and prevent falls, even when the support polygon constraint led to minor deviations from the optimized GRF tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective terrain load-bearing assessment can be achieved using only internal robot joint measurements and sophisticated control, making the solution hardware-agnostic and more accessible.
> - A trade-off exists between precisely tracking the optimized probing force and maintaining the robot's static stability within the support polygon, requiring careful balancing for safety.
> - The 'probe around' strategy, which tests points within a small radius of an initially stable foothold, enhances safety by verifying the immediate surrounding area.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of quadruped robots for real-world applications has advanced significantly, moving from research labs to commercial availability. Consequently, a major focus of current research is to enhance the robustness of legged locomotion systems across diverse and challenging environments. This paper directly addresses a critical and high-risk scenario: **navigating collapsing or unstable terrains**, commonly encountered in applications such as search and rescue missions, planetary exploration, remote inspections, or hazardous construction sites.

Traditionally, quadruped robots assess terrain traversability primarily through **exteroceptive sensing**, utilizing cameras, LiDAR, or other perception systems to generate terrain maps. These maps help identify and avoid potentially unsafe paths by segmenting or classifying terrain types based on visual appearance. However, this approach has inherent limitations. Exteroceptive sensors can be degraded by environmental factors like poor lighting, reflective surfaces, or sensor occlusion. More critically, they offer only *surface-level observations*, providing an indirect estimate of terrain stability based on visual cues rather than direct physical interaction. A visually flat surface, for instance, might conceal underlying structural instability, rendering it unsafe.

To overcome these limitations, recent research has integrated **proprioceptive information** (internal sensor data like joint measurements, IMUs) with visual or depth data. These multimodal approaches enhance terrain understanding by considering physical interaction, but often still prioritize surface geometry and may assume structural soundness based on visual flatness. While they improve safety by acknowledging physical interaction, they generally do not directly assess the *load-bearing capacity* of the terrain—a crucial factor for preventing collapse.

**Terrain probing** has emerged as a direct and effective method for analyzing terrain stability. However, existing probing techniques frequently rely on specialized hardware. Some approaches utilize a dedicated robotic arm for probing, which adds complexity, weight, and energy consumption to the robot. Others involve installing special foot sensors, such as force/torque sensors or IMUs, within the robot's feet. While effective, these solutions can be expensive, require significant hardware modifications to the robot's leg design, and may introduce fragility.

A few studies have attempted locomotion on collapsing terrains *without* specialized force sensors. Haddeler et al. developed a digital twin framework to predict terrain collapsibility, but lacked reactive actions to prevent falls. Tennakoon et al. integrated terrain probing into a hexapod robot's walking cycle, testing footholds before committing to a step, validated on relatively simpler collapsing materials like styrofoam or hidden gaps. While a step in the right direction, hexapods inherently offer a larger support polygon during probing due to their higher number of legs, making stability less challenging than for quadruped robots, which face a reduced support polygon when one leg is airborne for probing.

This paper addresses a significant gap by proposing a novel locomotion framework for quadruped robots that performs load-bearing assessment *without requiring specialized hardware modifications or external sensors*. It builds upon and extends previous work by:
1. Leveraging existing **joint measurements** to infer terrain stability, making the approach hardware-agnostic and cost-effective.
2. Integrating advanced **motion planning (Trajectory Optimization and Model Predictive Control)** with **terrain probing** to not just detect collapse but proactively determine the necessary forces for a stable stance.
3. Focusing on **quadruped robots**, which face unique stability challenges during probing compared to hexapods.
4. Demonstrating robust performance on more challenging and realistic collapsing terrains.

In essence, this work pushes the boundaries of autonomous legged locomotion by providing a practical, robust, and hardware-efficient solution for navigating unpredictable and unstable environments, moving beyond indirect visual cues to a direct, physical assessment of terrain load-bearing capacity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a **robust and safe locomotion framework for quadruped robots operating on challenging, unstable, or collapsing terrains**. This capability is vital for enabling robots to effectively assist in high-risk real-world applications where such environments are common.

The specific key objectives and motivations driving this work can be detailed as follows:

- **Enhance Safety in Hazardous Environments:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent risks associated with deploying quadruped robots in scenarios like search and rescue missions, planetary exploration, or remote inspections. These environments frequently present unstable or collapsing surfaces (e.g., loose soil, sliding rocks, compromised structures) that pose significant risks of robot instability, falls, and potential damage or mission failure. Current methods often provide insufficient safety guarantees.
- **Proactive Load-bearing Assessment:** To move beyond reactive responses, the authors aim to implement a **proactive terrain assessment mechanism**. This involves not just detecting terrain collapse *after* it happens, but actively probing the terrain *before* committing to a full step, ensuring the chosen foothold can support the robot's anticipated load.
- **Hardware-Agnostic Stability Assessment:** A critical objective is to perform this load-bearing assessment using **existing robot hardware and onboard sensors**, specifically leveraging joint measurements (torques and positions). This eliminates the need for expensive, heavy, or fragile specialized foot-mounted force/torque sensors or dedicated robotic arms, which simplifies robot design, reduces cost, and improves robustness. This makes the solution more practical and accessible for existing quadruped platforms.
- **Integration of Multi-Layered Control and Perception:** The paper seeks to integrate a comprehensive suite of robotic capabilities, including **terrain probing, load-bearing analysis, sophisticated motion planning (Trajectory Optimization and Model Predictive Control), and reactive control strategies**. This holistic approach aims to create a seamlessly operating system capable of dynamically adapting to unstable terrain.
- **Adaptive Probing Force Determination:** A specific objective is to determine the appropriate probing force adaptively. Instead of applying a fixed, potentially over- or underestimated force, the system should compute the **Ground Reaction Force (GRF) envelope** required for each leg's subsequent motion. This ensures optimal probing, preventing unnecessary energy expenditure or insufficient assessment.
- **Maintain Stability During Probing:** Quadruped robots face a unique challenge during probing: a reduced support polygon with only three legs in contact. A key objective is to ensure the robot's **stability and balance** are maintained throughout the probing process, allowing for quick recovery in case a collapse is detected. This requires robust control strategies that explicitly account for stability constraints.
- **Experimental Validation on Challenging Terrains:** The research aims to rigorously validate the proposed framework not only in simulation but also through **real-world experiments** on customized collapsing platforms (e.g., tilting planks) and natural challenging terrains (e.g., loose rocks). This demonstrates the framework's practical applicability and robustness in realistic scenarios.
- **Improve Efficiency and Robustness over Baseline Methods:** By incorporating advanced planning and adaptive probing, the authors seek to improve the robot's speed and stability compared to simpler approaches (e.g., MPC without probing) and to outperform previous probing methods that might be slower or less adaptable.

In summary, the motivation is to empower quadruped robots to navigate truly challenging and unpredictable environments safely and effectively, by providing them with an intrinsic, hardware-independent capability to assess the physical integrity of their intended footholds.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed locomotion framework for safe navigation on collapsing terrain is inspired by how humans cautiously explore unstable ground: by probing the surface before committing their full weight. This framework integrates several key components: perception for terrain mapping, trajectory optimization for proactive force estimation, Model Predictive Control (MPC) for real-time motion planning and stability, and a state machine for coordinating the complex probing and recovery actions.

**A. Framework Overview (Figure 2):**
The system begins with terrain mapping (optional, via VFA) to select potential footholds. A Trajectory Optimization (TO) then computes the necessary GRF envelope for these footholds and the robot's full stride. This information feeds into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) system, which executes real-time motion planning, incorporating probing-specific constraints. A Whole-Body Controller (WBC) translates MPC outputs into joint torques. Crucially, a State Machine orchestrates the entire probing process, including collapse detection and recovery, using joint data and IMU measurements as primary feedback.

**B. Terrain Mapping and Visual Foothold Adaptation (VFA) [Sec. II-A]:**
To enhance efficiency, a robot-centric 2.5D elevation map can be used to pre-select probing points and avoid visibly unsafe regions. The Visual Foothold Adaptation (VFA) algorithm classifies the safety of grid cells based on heuristic criteria such as terrain roughness, potential leg collision, and map uncertainty. VFA identifies safe regions and optimal foot placement positions, generating 2D convex polygons that serve as constraints for foothold placement in the Trajectory Optimization. While VFA guides selection, probing remains essential for load-bearing verification. In "blind locomotion" or when map data is unavailable, VFA is disabled, and TO outputs default probing footholds.

**C. Trajectory Optimization (TO) [Sec. II-B]:**
This is a critical, proactive component. Before each probing step, a full stride trajectory is optimized to determine the GRF envelope (maximum and minimum forces in x, y, and z directions) that the probing leg will need to exert at its next foothold. This adaptive approach avoids fixed probing forces, optimizing energy use and effectiveness.
- **Formulation:** The optimization problem minimizes a cost function (L) over time, subject to system dynamics (Centroidal Dynamics model), initial state, and equality/inequality constraints.
- **Inputs:** Robot's current state, target base position/orientation, reference touch-down positions (potentially from VFA), elevation map, and the index of the current probing leg.
- **Outputs:** VFA-adapted touch-down positions and the GRF envelope for the probing leg.
- **Constraints:** Stance legs are constrained by zero end-effector velocity and a friction cone (μ=0.5). Swing legs have zero contact force and constrained normal velocity, allowing tangential foot placement optimization. Foot placement is further constrained by the 2D convex polygons from VFA (Equation 4). Joint position, velocity, and contact force limits are also included as relaxed log-barrier functions.

**D. Perceptive Model Predictive Control (MPC) [Sec. II-C]:**
After TO provides the desired contact positions and GRF envelope, an MPC system performs real-time motion planning at 100 Hz. It continuously solves an optimization problem (similar to TO) over a short horizon (1.0 s) using the latest state measurements.
- **Base Formulation:** Uses an MPC formulation based on, adapting VFA for foothold placement and using TO-generated reference footholds.
- **Probing-Specific Constraints (Soft):** Two crucial soft constraints are added to handle terrain probing:
 - **Support Polygon Constraint (Equation 5):** Activated from lift-off until probing completion, ensuring the robot's CoM projection (r_x,y_b) remains within the support polygon formed by the *three non-probing legs*. A safety margin (α) is applied. This prioritizes static stability during probing.
 - **Probing Force Constraint (Equation 6):** Active only during the probing phase, this constraint forces the contact forces (f_i) on the probing foot to track the desired force profile (f_p_i) from the GRF envelope.
- **Soft Constraints:** Both constraints are implemented as relaxed log-barrier functions in the cost function, allowing flexibility and preventing convergence issues when these (sometimes opposing) objectives are weighted. The support polygon constraint is prioritized for safety.

**E. Whole-Body Controller (WBC):**
The WBC operates at 250 Hz, tracking the MPC-generated trajectories. It uses a virtual Cartesian impedance approach to compute the necessary joint actuation torques and compensates for external disturbances estimated by a momentum observer.

**F. State Machine [Sec. II-D]:**
The state machine is the orchestrator of the probing actions, managing the sequence of operations for each leg:
1. **PRE OPT:** Defines the target robot state (e.g., 10 cm forward) and reference footholds based on nominal joint configuration. Base height and orientation adapt to the local surface normal.
2. **SEND:** Inputs target state and reference footholds to VFA (if enabled) for safe foothold selection.
3. **OPT:** Executes the Trajectory Optimization (TO) to compute the ideal probing position and the GRF envelope. It stores the current foot positions as "latest safe" and defines three alternative probing positions (7.5 cm apart) in case of collapse.
4. **MOVE & DETECT CONTACT:** The robot moves its base to maintain stability within the three non-probing legs' support polygon. The probing leg then swings to the calculated probing point. Contact detection occurs when the normal component of the estimated contact force (derived from joint torques via Equation 7) exceeds a threshold for three successive measurements. If early contact is detected, probing starts there; if no contact is detected at max leg extension, it tries alternative points.
5. **PROBE:**
 - The MPC's support polygon and probing force constraints are activated.
 - The robot applies the forces from the GRF envelope for 100 ms each.
 - **Collapse Detection:** If the foot's displacement (difference between current position and stored contact position) during probing exceeds 3 cm, a collapse is detected.
 - **PROBE AROUND:** As an additional safety measure, if the initial probing point is deemed stable, the robot also probes four points within a 2.5 cm radius around the original foothold to verify the stability of the immediate surrounding area.
 - **Recovery:** If collapse is detected, the leg is immediately lifted (LEG UP), and the TO is triggered to compute a new alternative probing point. If all alternative points fail, the leg returns to the previous *known safe* position (RETURN SAFE).

This comprehensive methodology allows the quadruped robot to proactively assess terrain stability, adapt its locomotion plan in real-time, and recover safely from unexpected collapses, all while minimizing hardware complexity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research framework was implemented in C++ using ROS for communication and OCS2 for solving the optimal control problems (TO and MPC via Sequential Quadratic Programming). The MPC ran at 100 Hz (1.0 s horizon, 0.015 s sampling), and the WBC at 250 Hz.

**A. Experimental Scenarios:**
The approach was validated through simulations and extensive experimental tests on the Aliengo robot in two primary real-world scenarios:
1. **Custom-made platform with tilting planks:** Designed to test load-bearing capacity where visual cues (elevation map) were insufficient (Fig. 7).
2. **Terrain with unstable and collapsing rocks:** Representing a more natural, unpredictable collapsing environment (Fig. 12).

**B. Performance on Tilting Planks Terrain:**
- **Successful Traversal:** The robot successfully traversed the collapsing terrain with tilting planks in both blind locomotion (without VFA) and with VFA information enabled. Traversal was completed in approximately 6.5 minutes on average, demonstrating maintained stability throughout.
- **Collapse Reaction and Recovery:** Snapshots (Fig. 8a, 1 & 2) illustrate the robot's reactive capability. Upon detecting a plank collapse during probing, the robot immediately removed its foot from the unstable surface while maintaining its base in a stable condition using the remaining three legs.
- **Adaptive Foothold Selection:** The framework effectively identified stable foothold positions. The robot frequently found stable points on the underlying beams or in the transition areas where support was sufficient (Fig. 8a, 3 & 4).
- **"Probe Around" Strategy Effectiveness:** The "probe around" safety measure proved crucial. In one instance, it prevented the robot from placing its foot dangerously close to a plank edge. A collapse detected at a point forward to the initial target prompted the selection of a new, safer foothold (Fig. 8b).
- **Superiority Over Baseline MPC:** A crucial comparison was made with a baseline MPC controller that lacked the proposed probing constraints. This baseline approach failed to traverse the terrain, as the robot's legs became trapped beneath the planks (Fig. 8c). This definitively demonstrated that the integrated probing framework is essential for handling collapsing terrains where dynamic recovery alone is insufficient.

**C. Probing Force Tracking vs. Robot Stability:**
- **Trade-off Identified:** A key finding concerned the trade-off between precisely tracking the optimized GRF envelope and maintaining the robot's static stability via the support polygon constraint during probing (Fig. 9).
 - **Without Support Polygon Constraint:** The MPC could perfectly track the desired GRF envelope. However, this allowed the robot's Center of Mass (CoM) projection to move outside the support polygon formed by the non-probing legs, making the robot susceptible to falling if collapse occurred.
 - **With Support Polygon Constraint:** Prioritizing safety, the MPC maintained the CoM within the stable support polygon. This, however, introduced a tracking error in the GRF envelope, meaning the *applied* probing force was slightly lower than the *optimized* required force.
- **Sufficient Probing:** Despite the GRF tracking error when the support polygon constraint was active, the experimental results confirmed that the *applied probing force remained sufficient* to detect terrain collapse and prevent falls.
- **WBC Performance:** The Whole-Body Controller (WBC) successfully tracked the desired contact forces generated by the MPC, as validated by real robot joint torque measurements (Fig. 10).
- **Hardware Limitations:** Attempts to reduce the GRF tracking error while maintaining the support polygon constraint (by reducing base pose tracking gains) led to excessive motor current and power limits on the experimental hardware, highlighting a practical limitation of the robot platform used.

**D. Synergy with Visual Foothold Adaptation:**
- **Guiding Foothold Selection:** In a scenario with missing planks, the VFA algorithm effectively identified these gaps as unsafe regions, guiding the foothold selection away from them. This demonstrates how perception can accelerate initial safe region identification (Fig. 11).
- **Probing Still Essential:** Even with VFA, probing remained necessary to verify the load-bearing capacity of visually "safe" regions, underscoring the complementary roles of perception and physical interaction.

**E. Performance on Loose Rocks Terrain:**
- **Robustness in Natural Environments:** The robot successfully traversed a terrain composed of loose rocks and bricks, even in blind locomotion (without elevation mapping). This scenario is highly challenging due to the unpredictable nature of loose material (Fig. 12).
- **Iterative Probing:** The robot consistently probed multiple positions until it identified stable footholds, demonstrating its ability to adapt to highly unstable, real-world conditions and maintain balance throughout the path.

In summary, the experiments robustly validated the proposed framework, demonstrating its ability to safely traverse complex collapsing terrains by proactively assessing load-bearing capacity, adapting its motion, and recovering from detected collapses, all without requiring specialized sensing hardware. The trade-off between precise force tracking and stability was managed effectively to ensure safety.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper presents a significant advancement in the field of quadrupedal robotics, offering a novel, practical, and highly impactful solution for robust locomotion in hazardous, unpredictable environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Pioneering Hardware-Agnostic Load-Bearing Assessment:** The most profound contribution is the ability to assess terrain load-bearing capacity **solely through joint measurements and trajectory optimization**, without requiring specialized, expensive, or fragile foot-mounted force/torque sensors or robotic arms. This radically simplifies robot design, reduces cost, increases robustness, and makes the technology more accessible for integration into a wide range of existing quadruped platforms. It shifts the paradigm from requiring explicit force sensing to inferring stability from the robot's own dynamic response.
- **Enhanced Safety and Fall Prevention:** By integrating a proactive terrain probing strategy into the locomotion cycle, the framework drastically improves robot safety. The ability to detect collapses *before* fully committing to a step and dynamically adjust footholds in real-time significantly reduces the risk of falls, damage to the robot, and mission failure in critical applications. The "probe around" strategy further bolsters this safety by verifying the stability of the immediate vicinity of a foothold.
- **Robustness in Unstructured and Dangerous Environments:** The framework directly addresses a critical challenge for robot deployment in real-world scenarios, particularly in search and rescue, planetary exploration, industrial inspection of compromised structures, and military applications. By enabling reliable navigation over collapsing terrain, it opens up new operational possibilities for quadruped robots in environments previously considered too dangerous or inaccessible for autonomous systems.
- **Adaptive and Energy-Efficient Probing:** The adaptive computation of the Ground Reaction Force (GRF) envelope for probing is more intelligent and energy-efficient than applying a fixed, arbitrary force. It ensures the robot applies precisely the force required to test the terrain for its intended movement, avoiding unnecessary strain on the robot and improving mission endurance.
- **Integrated Multi-Layered Control Architecture:** The successful integration of perception (VFA), sophisticated motion planning (Trajectory Optimization and Model Predictive Control with specialized constraints), and low-level whole-body control within a coordinated state machine represents a robust and comprehensive solution. This holistic approach handles complex interactions between sensing, decision-making, and physical execution, setting a high standard for autonomous legged locomotion.
- **Empirical Validation on Challenging Real-World Terrains:** The rigorous experimental validation on custom-made tilting platforms and loose rocky terrains—including direct comparisons to a baseline MPC that failed—underscores the practical effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework in challenging and realistic conditions. This strong experimental evidence boosts confidence in the method's real-world applicability.

**Potential Future Impact:**

- **Expanded Operational Domains:** Robots equipped with this capability can be deployed in disaster zones, extraterrestrial landscapes, or hazardous industrial sites with greater confidence, reducing human exposure to danger.
- **Advanced Autonomy:** It contributes to developing more intelligent, terrain-aware autonomous robots that can make complex decisions based on physical interaction with the environment, moving beyond purely visual navigation.
- **Foundation for High-Speed Locomotion:** While the current method is slower due to probing, the authors' future work on scaling to higher speeds and leveraging visual semantics to *reduce unnecessary probing* will be crucial. This suggests a future where robots can intelligently decide *when* to probe based on perceived risk, leading to more efficient and dynamic navigation.
- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration:** By increasing robot autonomy and reliability in dangerous terrains, this research facilitates more effective human-robot collaboration in critical missions.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a significant step forward in making quadruped robots truly robust and reliable for real-world applications in hazardous environments. By providing a hardware-efficient and intelligent method for load-bearing assessment, it not only enhances safety and expands operational capabilities but also lays a strong foundation for future advancements in adaptive and autonomous legged locomotion.
