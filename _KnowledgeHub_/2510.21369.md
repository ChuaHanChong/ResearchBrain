# Load-bearing Assessment for Safe Locomotion of Quadruped Robots on Collapsing Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> A locomotion framework for quadruped robots assesses terrain load-bearing capacity using only joint measurements, eliminating the need for specialized sensors, enabling safe traversal and recovery on challenging collapsing terrains. The system integrates trajectory optimization, model predictive control, and a state machine to proactively probe and adapt to unstable ground.

## Problem

- Quadruped robots face significant risks when navigating unstable or collapsing terrains, as traditional exteroceptive sensing provides only surface-level observations of stability.
- Existing terrain probing methods often require specialized hardware, such as dedicated robotic arms or custom foot-mounted force/torque sensors, which adds complexity, weight, and cost.
- Maintaining static stability in quadruped robots during probing is challenging, as the robot operates with only three legs in contact, creating a reduced support polygon.

## Method

- A framework integrating Trajectory Optimization (TO), Perceptive ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) with probing-specific constraints, and a State Machine for coordinated actions.
- Leverages existing robot joint measurements (torques and positions) to infer terrain stability and perform load-bearing assessment, eliminating the need for specialized hardware.
- Proactively computes an adaptive Ground Reaction Force (GRF) envelope for each probing leg and implements real-time collapse detection and recovery mechanisms.

## Results

- The Aliengo robot successfully traversed custom-made tilting plank platforms and natural loose rock terrains, consistently maintaining stability and recovering from detected collapses.
- The proposed framework significantly outperformed a baseline MPC controller that lacked probing constraints, demonstrating its necessity for safe navigation on collapsing terrains.
- Experiments showed that sufficient probing force was applied to detect terrain collapse and prevent falls, even when the support polygon constraint led to minor deviations from the optimized GRF tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective terrain load-bearing assessment can be achieved using only internal robot joint measurements and sophisticated control, making the solution hardware-agnostic and more accessible.
> - A trade-off exists between precisely tracking the optimized probing force and maintaining the robot's static stability within the support polygon, requiring careful balancing for safety.
> - The 'probe around' strategy, which tests points within a small radius of an initially stable foothold, enhances safety by verifying the immediate surrounding area.
