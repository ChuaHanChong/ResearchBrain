# GR-MG: Leveraging Partially Annotated Data via Multi-Modal Goal-Conditioned Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Chinese Academy of Sciences and ByteDance Research introduce GR-MG, a method to enhance robot manipulation generalization by utilizing various partially-annotated datasets. This approach combines a progress-guided goal image generation model with a multi-modal goal-conditioned policy, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the CALVIN benchmark with an average of 4.04 tasks completed in a row and improving real-robot generalization success rates from 44.4% to 60.6%.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation tasks suffer from data scarcity, as collecting fully-annotated robot trajectories (with actions and language) is time-consuming and expensive.
- Abundant partially-annotated data, such as human activity videos without action labels or robot trajectories without text labels, are largely underutilized.
- Existing goal-conditioned policies often lack robust multi-modal conditioning and struggle with generating accurate visual goals for sequential manipulation tasks without considering task progress.

## Method

- GR-MG leverages three data types: fully-annotated trajectories, text-annotated human videos (without actions), and robot trajectories (without text) through a two-module architecture.
- A progress-guided goal image generation model, built on InstructPix2Pix, synthesizes future visual sub-goals by incorporating explicit task progress information alongside current observation and text instruction.
- A multi-modal goal-conditioned GPT-style transformer policy predicts actions and current task progress, conditioning on both the language instruction and the generated goal image for robustness.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art on the CALVIN benchmark's generalization split, increasing the average number of tasks completed in a row of 5 from 3.35 to 4.04 and the 5-task success rate from 41.2% to 64.4%.
- Demonstrated superior real-robot generalization, improving average success rates from 44.4% to 60.6% across unseen distractors, instructions, backgrounds, and objects on 58 tasks, outperforming OpenVLA, Octo, and GR-1.
- Showcased enhanced few-shot learning capabilities for novel skills, achieving a 17.5% success rate with only 10 demonstrations, a substantial improvement over baselines (0.0% to 2.5%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining both natural language instructions and generated visual goal images in a multi-modal policy provides superior robustness and performance compared to relying on a single modality.
> - Injecting task progress information into the diffusion-based goal generation process significantly improves the accuracy and semantic relevance of synthesized goal images for complex, sequential manipulation tasks.
> - Effectively integrating diverse partially-annotated datasets—specifically, text-annotated human videos for goal generation and action-labeled robot data for policy pre-training—is crucial for scaling robot learning and improving generalization under data scarcity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation, particularly with language-based instructions, is rapidly evolving, driven by the desire for more generalizable and intuitive human-robot interaction. This work directly addresses one of the most significant bottlenecks in this domain: data scarcity for fully-annotated robot trajectories.

Historically, robot manipulation policies have relied heavily on imitation learning from human demonstrations. However, collecting such data, especially with precise action and corresponding natural language labels, is time-consuming and labor-intensive. This has led researchers to explore alternative data sources and methodologies.

One prominent direction involves **goal-conditioned policies**, where a robot learns to reach a desired state specified by a goal image or video. While effective, acquiring these goal images during deployment can be challenging. More recently, **multi-modal goal conditioning** has gained traction, using both language and visual goals (images or videos) during training. However, many of these approaches default to relying solely on language during inference when visual goals are not naturally available.

A critical area of exploration is **leveraging partially-annotated data**. This includes:
- **Data without action labels:** Large public datasets of text-annotated human activity videos (e.g., Something-Something-V2, HowTo100M, Ego4D) contain rich visual and linguistic information about tasks but lack explicit robot actions. Methods like RT-2 and those learning representations from such videos have shown promise.
- **Data without text labels:** Unstructured robot play data or automatically collected trajectories offer abundant action-labeled sequences but lack explicit language instructions. Hindsight goal relabeling is a common technique to make such data useful by treating any future frame as a goal image.

Recent advancements in **generative models, especially diffusion models**, have opened new avenues for creating desired states. Models like Susie and video generation models have been used to generate goal images or future videos, which then serve as inputs for goal-conditioned policies or inverse dynamics models. This allows for leveraging partially-annotated data where visual goals can be synthesized.

GR-MG positions itself at the intersection of these advancements. It builds upon the idea of using diffusion models for goal image generation, similar to Susie. However, it identifies and addresses key limitations:
1. **Lack of task progress information:** Previous diffusion-based goal generation often ignores the sequential nature of manipulation, potentially generating ambiguous or inaccurate goals.
2. **Over-reliance on generated goals:** Policies conditioned solely on potentially inaccurate generated images can be brittle.

Furthermore, GR-MG distinguishes itself from prior work like GR-1 (which pioneered large-scale video generative pre-training for robot manipulation) by explicitly incorporating *both* data without action labels (for goal image generation) *and* data without text labels (for policy training), and by using multi-modal (text + generated image) conditioning during *both* training and inference. This comprehensive approach to data utilization and multi-modal conditioning aims to push the boundaries of generalizable robot manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable **generalizable robot manipulation** using flexible natural language instructions, primarily by overcoming the prohibitive costs and labor associated with acquiring fully-annotated robot demonstration data. The authors are motivated by the inherent abundance of partially-annotated data that is currently underutilized in robot learning.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be broken down as follows:

1. **Address Data Scarcity:** The primary motivation stems from the recognition that obtaining robot trajectories fully annotated with both actions and corresponding natural language descriptions is extremely time-consuming and resource-intensive. This scarcity limits the scale and diversity of tasks robots can learn through imitation.
2. **Leverage Partially-Annotated Data:** The core idea is to tap into readily available, but incomplete, datasets:
 - **Human activity videos with text annotations (without action labels):** These public datasets contain valuable semantic information about how actions change the environment based on textual descriptions, but lack robot-specific action labels.
 - **Robot trajectories with action labels (without text labels):** These can be collected at scale with less human supervision (e.g., unstructured play data) and contain rich kinematic and visual information, but lack high-level language context.
 The objective is to develop a unified framework that can effectively utilize *both* types of partially-annotated data alongside traditional fully-annotated datasets.
3. **Enhance Goal Image Generation with Task Progress:** Previous approaches generating goal images from text and current observations often overlook the sequential nature of robot manipulation. Ambiguous observations or intermediate states can lead to inaccurate goal image generation. A key objective is to **incorporate task progress information** into the goal image generation process. This aims to provide crucial temporal context, ensuring the generated goal image accurately reflects the desired next stage of the task.
4. **Develop a Robust Multi-Modal Goal-Conditioned Policy:** While generating goal images can be powerful, relying solely on them for action prediction can make the policy brittle, especially if the generated image is inaccurate or hallucinates. The motivation is to design a policy that can condition on **both the text instruction and the generated goal image simultaneously**. This dual conditioning provides redundancy and robustness, allowing the policy to fall back on the language instruction even if the visual goal is imperfect, thereby improving overall reliability in challenging, out-of-distribution scenarios.
5. **Achieve Strong Generalization and Few-Shot Learning:** Ultimately, the aim is to create a robot policy that not only performs well on seen tasks but also exhibits powerful generalization capabilities to unseen environments, distractors, instructions, and objects. Furthermore, the ability to rapidly learn novel skills with very few demonstrations (few-shot learning) is a crucial objective for practical robot deployment.

In essence, GR-MG is motivated by the vision of a robot learning paradigm that is data-efficient, robust, and highly generalizable, by intelligently combining different data modalities and leveraging advanced generative models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GR-MG proposes a novel architecture composed of two interacting modules: a **progress-guided goal image generation model** and a **multi-modal goal-conditioned policy**. This dual-module design allows for the effective integration of diverse partially-annotated data types during training.

#### 3.1. Data Utilization Strategy

The methodology explicitly distinguishes and leverages three types of data:
- **Fully-annotated robot trajectories:** Contains language, observations, states, and actions. Used for training both modules.
- **Data without action labels:** Videos with text annotations (e.g., human activity videos). Primarily used to train the goal image generation model.
- **Data without text labels:** Robot trajectories with action labels but no language instructions (e.g., unstructured play data). Used to pre-train/finetune the multi-modal goal-conditioned policy.

#### 3.2. Progress-guided Goal Image Generation Model

This module is responsible for synthesizing a visual representation of the desired future state (a sub-goal image) given the current context.
- **Base Model:** It utilizes **InstructPix2Pix**, a diffusion-based image-editing model, as its foundation. This model is adept at generating images conditioned on both an input image and a text instruction.
- **Key Innovation: Task Progress Condition:** Unlike previous approaches (e.g., Susie), GR-MG injects **task progress information** into the generation process. Robot manipulation is a sequential task, and an observation can be ambiguous (e.g., "moving object back-and-forth"). Task progress provides a crucial global cue, indicating how far along the task the robot should be.
 - **Implementation:** The progress is appended to the natural language instruction as a sentence (e.g., "pick up the red block. And 60% of the instruction has been completed."). During training, progress is derived from the timestep within a trajectory. During inference, the policy predicts the current task progress, which is then fed into this generation model. The progress is discretized into 10 bins (0-100%) for stability.
 - **Encoders:** T5-Base is used to encode the augmented text instruction.
- **Training:** The model is trained as a noise prediction model, similar to DDPM. It samples a frame `N` steps ahead as the goal image. Critically, it can be trained on **data without action labels** (text-annotated videos) in addition to fully-annotated robot trajectories, thereby enriching its understanding of semantic changes from language.

#### 3.3. Multi-Modal Goal-Conditioned Policy

This module predicts robot actions and task progress based on both language and the generated goal image.
- **Base Architecture:** It is a **GPT-style transformer network**, inspired by GR-1. This architecture is known for its ability to process sequences of multi-modal tokens (vision, language, robot states).
- **Inputs:**
 - **Text Instruction:** Encoded via T5-Base.
 - **Observation Images:** A sequence of RGB images from both a static camera and a wrist-mounted camera (from `t-h` to `t`), tokenized using MAE.
 - **Robot States:** A sequence of 6-DoF end-effector poses and binary gripper states (from `t-h` to `t`), encoded via linear layers.
 - **Goal Image:** The image generated by the progress-guided goal image generation model is tokenized by MAE and prepended to the input sequence, allowing all subsequent tokens to attend to it.
- **Outputs:**
 - **Action Trajectory:** Instead of a single action, the policy predicts a trajectory of `k` actions using a conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE). This is achieved by concatenating a VAE-encoded style vector, the output embedding of an `[ACT]` query token, and `k` learnable token embeddings, which are then fed into a transformer.
 - **Task Progress:** A novel `[PROG]` query token is introduced. Its output embedding is passed through linear layers to regress the current task progress value (0-100%). This predicted progress is crucial for closing the loop with the goal image generation model during inference.
- **Training:**
 - **Pre-training:** The policy's weights are initialized from a model pre-trained on large-scale generative video data (Ego4D), similar to GR-1. This provides strong visual and temporal representations.
 - **Leveraging Data w/o Text Labels:** A significant aspect is that the policy can be first trained with **data without text labels** (using a null string as the text condition), then finetuned on fully-annotated trajectories. This allows the policy to learn robust visual-kinematic mappings from abundant robot interaction data.
 - **Decoupled Training:** The training of the goal image generation model and the policy are performed independently, sampling ground-truth goal images and progress for the policy from the trajectories.

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, the process becomes a closed-loop system:
1. Initial task progress is set to zero.
2. The **progress-guided goal image generation model** takes the current observation image, the text instruction, and the *current predicted task progress* to generate a goal image.
3. This generated goal image, along with the text instruction, current observations, and robot states, is fed into the **multi-modal goal-conditioned policy**.
4. The policy predicts an action trajectory and the updated task progress.
5. The goal image generation model is re-run at fixed intervals (`n` steps, where `n < N`, `N` being the training goal distance) to update the sub-goal, maintaining efficiency.

This methodology represents a comprehensive approach to data utilization and robust policy learning, particularly emphasizing the benefits of multi-modal conditioning and context-aware goal generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both a challenging simulation benchmark (CALVIN) and on a real-robot platform, providing robust validation for GR-MG's capabilities.

#### 4.1. CALVIN Benchmark Experiments

**Generalization to Unseen Environments (ABC→D Split):**
- **Superior Performance:** GR-MG established a new state-of-the-art on the CALVIN benchmark, which tests generalization to unseen environments (Env D after training on Env A, B, C).
- **Quantitative Improvement:** It increased the average number of tasks completed in a row of 5 (Avg. Len.) from the previous best of 3.35 (3D Diff Actor) to **4.04**. The success rate for completing 1 task improved from 93.8% to **96.8%**, and for 5 tasks in a row, it significantly jumped from 41.2% to **64.4%**. These results demonstrate strong multi-task learning and generalization capabilities.

**Data Scarcity (10% Fully-Annotated Data):**
- **Effectiveness of Partially-Annotated Data:** When trained with only 10% of the fully-annotated data but incorporating the additional data without text labels, GR-MG achieved an average length of **3.11**. This dramatically outperformed GR-1 (which only uses fully-annotated data) at 10% (1.41 Avg. Len.) and even outperformed GR-1 when it used *100%* of the fully-annotated data (3.06 Avg. Len.).
- **Validation of `data w/o text labels`:** Comparing GR-MG with "GR-MG w/o part. ann. data" (which did not use data w/o text labels), GR-MG's performance (3.11 Avg. Len.) was significantly better than the variant's (2.33 Avg. Len.). This confirms the crucial role of data without text labels in enhancing policy learning, especially when fully-annotated data is scarce.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Importance of Multi-Modal Goals:** "GR-MG w/o text" (policy uses only goal image) and "GR-MG w/o image" (policy uses only text) variants both performed worse than the full GR-MG (Avg. Len. 3.46 and 3.42 vs. 4.04). This unequivocally shows that both language and goal images are essential for effective policy conditioning and robust performance.
- **Impact of Task Progress:** "GR-MG w/o progress" (goal image generation without task progress information) resulted in an average length of 3.76, notably lower than GR-MG's 4.04. This highlights the effectiveness of incorporating task progress.
- **Quantitative Goal Image Quality:** GR-MG significantly outperformed "GR-MG w/o progress" across all four metrics (MSE, PSNR, SSIM, CD-ResNet50), confirming that task progress leads to more accurate goal images.
- **Qualitative Goal Image Quality:** Visualizations in Fig. 4(a) showed that GR-MG generates goal images closely aligned with ground truths and text instructions, whereas GR-MG w/o progress produced visually plausible but semantically misaligned goals.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiments

**Generalization Across Diverse Settings:**
- GR-MG demonstrated superior performance on a Kinova Gen-3 robot across 58 tasks, including 37 seen and 21 unseen tasks.
- **Average Success Rates:**
 - **Simple Setting:** GR-MG improved the average success rate from 68.7% to **78.1%** compared to baselines.
 - **Generalization Settings** (Unseen Distractors, Unseen Instructions, Unseen Backgrounds, Unseen Objects): GR-MG boosted the average success rate from 44.4% to **60.6%**.
- **Outperformance:** GR-MG consistently outperformed OpenVLA, Octo, and GR-1 in all five evaluation settings, demonstrating its strong real-world generalization capabilities, which the authors attribute to powerful video pre-training for both generative and policy models.
- **Validation of `data w/o action labels`:** Comparison with "GR-MG w/o part. ann. data" (which did not use text-annotated videos like Something-Something-V2/RT-1 for goal image generation) showed that the additional partially-annotated data without action labels were crucial. This variant struggled with hallucination and generated goal images that did not adhere to instructions, especially for unseen objects (Fig. 4(b)). This confirms these data enhance language understanding and robustness to out-of-distribution inputs.

**Few-Shot Learning of Novel Skills:**
- GR-MG demonstrated enhanced few-shot learning capabilities for 8 held-out tasks (7 novel skills).
- **10-shot learning:** GR-MG achieved a 17.5% success rate, significantly outperforming GR-1 (2.5%), OpenVLA (0.0%), and Octo (0.0%).
- **30-shot learning:** GR-MG improved to 37.5% success, still leading GR-1 (22.5%) and other baselines.
- **Observed Bottleneck:** While the goal image generation model performed well with few-shot finetuning, the policy itself still struggled, indicating a potential area for future improvement through scaling policy training with more real-world data without text labels.

In summary, the results consistently highlight GR-MG's ability to effectively utilize partially-annotated data, its robustness through multi-modal conditioning and progress-guided goal generation, and its superior generalization and few-shot learning performance across both simulated and real-world robot manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "GR-MG: Leveraging Partially-Annotated Data via Multi-Modal Goal-Conditioned Policy" carries significant implications for the future of robot learning and manipulation, addressing several fundamental challenges in the field.

#### 5.1. Addressing the Data Scarcity Problem

The most profound impact of GR-MG lies in its effective strategy for mitigating the notorious data scarcity problem in robot learning. By demonstrating how to concurrently leverage three distinct data types—fully-annotated, robot trajectories without text labels, and human activity videos without action labels—GR-MG provides a robust framework for learning from abundant, yet imperfect, data sources. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing complex robot behaviors, reducing the reliance on costly and labor-intensive fully-annotated datasets. This approach can unlock the potential of vast amounts of readily available online videos and passively collected robot interaction data.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Generalization Capabilities

GR-MG's strong performance across diverse generalization settings (unseen environments, distractors, instructions, backgrounds, and objects) in both simulation and real-world experiments is a critical step towards deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic environments. The ability to understand novel instructions (synonyms) and manipulate unseen objects in varying contexts makes robots more versatile and adaptable, moving beyond brittle, task-specific solutions. This generalized understanding is paramount for real-world robotic applications, where conditions are rarely identical to training scenarios.

#### 5.3. Robustness through Multi-Modal Conditioning

The novel multi-modal goal conditioning strategy, combining both natural language instructions and generated goal images, significantly enhances the robustness of the policy. By not relying solely on the generated visual goal, GR-MG mitigates the risks associated with potential inaccuracies or hallucinations from generative models. This dual-modality provides a crucial fail-safe, allowing the language semantics to guide the robot even when the visual goal is ambiguous or incorrect. This inherent resilience is vital for reliable robot operation in complex and unpredictable situations.

#### 5.4. Improved Goal Image Generation via Task Progress

The introduction of task progress information into the goal image generation process is a subtle yet powerful innovation. By providing temporal context, GR-MG ensures that the generated visual goals are not only plausible but also contextually appropriate for the current stage of the task. This leads to more accurate and reliable intermediate goals, which are essential for long-horizon tasks and for disambiguating identical visual states that occur at different points in a sequence. This concept could be broadly applicable to other temporal generative tasks in robotics.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Faster Skill Acquisition (Few-Shot Learning)

GR-MG's demonstrated superiority in few-shot learning scenarios indicates its potential to accelerate the development and deployment of novel robot skills. The ability to learn new tasks from a handful of demonstrations drastically reduces the re-training effort for new applications, making robots more agile and economical to adapt. While the authors note a bottleneck in the policy for few-shot learning, the progress in goal image generation is already a promising sign.

#### 5.6. Broader Impact and Future Directions

This work pushes the boundaries of embodied AI, accelerating the convergence of large language models, vision models, and robotic control. It lays the groundwork for more intelligent, autonomous robots that can interpret human intent with greater flexibility and learn complex behaviors from a wider array of data sources. Future work, such as joint training of the modules, incorporating depth information, and scaling policy training with even more real-world data without text labels, holds the promise of further enhancing GR-MG's capabilities. Ultimately, GR-MG contributes significantly to the long-term vision of general-purpose robotic agents capable of robustly performing diverse manipulation tasks in human-centric environments.
