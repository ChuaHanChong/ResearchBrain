# GR-MG: Leveraging Partially Annotated Data via Multi-Modal Goal-Conditioned Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Chinese Academy of Sciences and ByteDance Research introduce GR-MG, a method to enhance robot manipulation generalization by utilizing various partially-annotated datasets. This approach combines a progress-guided goal image generation model with a multi-modal goal-conditioned policy, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the CALVIN benchmark with an average of 4.04 tasks completed in a row and improving real-robot generalization success rates from 44.4% to 60.6%.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation tasks suffer from data scarcity, as collecting fully-annotated robot trajectories (with actions and language) is time-consuming and expensive.
- Abundant partially-annotated data, such as human activity videos without action labels or robot trajectories without text labels, are largely underutilized.
- Existing goal-conditioned policies often lack robust multi-modal conditioning and struggle with generating accurate visual goals for sequential manipulation tasks without considering task progress.

## Method

- GR-MG leverages three data types: fully-annotated trajectories, text-annotated human videos (without actions), and robot trajectories (without text) through a two-module architecture.
- A progress-guided goal image generation model, built on InstructPix2Pix, synthesizes future visual sub-goals by incorporating explicit task progress information alongside current observation and text instruction.
- A multi-modal goal-conditioned GPT-style transformer policy predicts actions and current task progress, conditioning on both the language instruction and the generated goal image for robustness.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art on the CALVIN benchmark's generalization split, increasing the average number of tasks completed in a row of 5 from 3.35 to 4.04 and the 5-task success rate from 41.2% to 64.4%.
- Demonstrated superior real-robot generalization, improving average success rates from 44.4% to 60.6% across unseen distractors, instructions, backgrounds, and objects on 58 tasks, outperforming OpenVLA, Octo, and GR-1.
- Showcased enhanced few-shot learning capabilities for novel skills, achieving a 17.5% success rate with only 10 demonstrations, a substantial improvement over baselines (0.0% to 2.5%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining both natural language instructions and generated visual goal images in a multi-modal policy provides superior robustness and performance compared to relying on a single modality.
> - Injecting task progress information into the diffusion-based goal generation process significantly improves the accuracy and semantic relevance of synthesized goal images for complex, sequential manipulation tasks.
> - Effectively integrating diverse partially-annotated datasets—specifically, text-annotated human videos for goal generation and action-labeled robot data for policy pre-training—is crucial for scaling robot learning and improving generalization under data scarcity.
