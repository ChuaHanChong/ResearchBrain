# RoboHarn-Evo: Evolving Hierarchical Physical Knowledge for Self-Improving Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboHarn-Evo is a two-loop harness that improves robot manipulation while its VLM and executor stay fixed. It builds and revises Hierarchical Physical Knowledge from experience. Task Knowledge records which subtask to run and when it is done. Action Knowledge records object-relative geometry and its physical effects. Each level is checked against its own criterion. Success rises by up to 24.2 points on RMBench, and the knowledge transfers zero-shot to RoboDojo and real robots.

## Problem

- Existing robot memory and skill-evolution systems store experience but do not reliably turn it into later improvement, because wrong stored entries are rarely revised or removed.
- Prior feedback schemes treat a local action success as task progress, but a successful grasp does not show that a cover was placed on the right target.
- Memories that only append, or only verify new additions, leave old knowledge errors in place, while policy-improvement methods must retrain the base model instead of reusing physical experience.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical Physical Knowledge (HPK)==: ==Task Knowledge== entries hold the subtask, its purpose and its completion condition, and each links to ==Action Knowledge== entries holding the operation, object-relative geometry and expected physical effect.
- **RoboHarn-Evo**'s ==dual-loop harness==: the inner loop retrieves Task Knowledge, then Action Knowledge, through ==skill-indexed summaries== to guide the VLM's tool calls, and the outer loop's ==VLM reflector== turns episode records into atomic entries.
- ==Evidence-driven maintenance==: Task entries are checked against subtask completion and Action entries against their intended effect, and the resulting support, oppose or unverified evidence decides whether entries are merged, revised or suspended.

## Results

- On **six** RMBench tasks, full HPK raises mean success from **58.3%** to **82.5%** with GPT-5.5 and from **72.5%** to **91.7%** with GPT-6, beating Task-only (**70.8%**) and Action-only (**66.7%**).
- Over **80** online rollouts, held-out success rises from **48.3%** to **75.0%** with GPT-5.5 and from **70.0%** to **88.3%** with GPT-6, beating raw memory by **16.7** and **15.0** points.
- Maintenance repairs or deactivates **83.3-91.7%** of seeded errors and keeps **95.8%** of valid entries, and frozen RMBench knowledge lifts RoboDojo success from **40.0%** to **75.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Check each piece of knowledge at the decision level it governs, so that a physically successful action cannot validate a wrong subtask choice or a premature completion.
> - A frozen foundation model can keep improving after deployment through a revisable external knowledge store, but memory that only appends stalls because old errors are never removed.
> - The same experience works better when organized hierarchically than as flat reflection, and object-relative knowledge with geometry recomputed for each scene transfers across benchmarks and from simulation to real robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

In ordered block manipulation, a robot can select the right covered block but release its lid off target. A plausible plan, or even a successful grasp, is not enough. The robot must check whether the physical result completed the intended subtask. A local action can succeed without the larger task being complete: grasping a lid successfully does not prove it was placed over the intended block.

The authors keep experience at two linked levels. One records what subtask to do and how to know it is complete. The other records how to act and the physical effects those actions should produce. The system checks those two kinds of success separately and uses physical feedback to revise knowledge before later reuse. The system, RoboHarn-Evo, does this without changing its vision-language model (VLM) or low-level executor. With the model and executor fixed, the learned knowledge improves later held-out performance.

### 2. Two kinds of "done"

**Task Knowledge** records what subtask to do and how to know it is complete. For example, it can say "after red is visible, uncover green next" and specify what counts as completing that subtask. **Action Knowledge** records object-relative ways of acting and the physical effects those actions should produce, such as how to approach a lid's handle and what physical effect to check.

Task retrieval answers what to do. Conditioned on that choice, action retrieval answers how to act. The robot grounds geometry in the current scene rather than reusing a stored robot pose.

An outer loop maintains this knowledge:

- Observations, tool calls, and physical outcomes become an episode record.
- A reflector extracts task and action entries.
- Maintenance merges equivalents, revises challenged content, and updates affected skill indexes.

Each level has its own check. Task entries are checked against subtask completion, and action entries are checked against intended local physical effects. Knowledge becomes retrievable only when it is supported and no applicable opposing evidence exists. This means a successful grasp can support the grasp strategy even when the enclosing uncover-or-place task remains incomplete.

### 3. Does experience become better behavior?

The continued-interaction study used RMBench's Swap Blocks and Put Back Block tasks. The protocol had these conditions:

- Three learning histories were run per model, each with 80 learning rollouts.
- Evaluation used frozen knowledge snapshots on the same 20 held-out configurations.
- Evaluation feedback was excluded from knowledge updates.
- The VLM and execution tools remained fixed.

Mean held-out task success rose from 48.3% to 75.0% for GPT-5.5, and from 70.0% to 88.3% for GPT-6. These are success rates, not token counts, number of attempts, or elapsed-time savings.

A skeptical reader might ask whether simply storing experience explains the gain. The endpoint control addresses this by building knowledge from the same source experience in different ways. At K=80, full RoboHarn-Evo exceeded knowledge built without physical-evidence verification by 8.3 percentage points for GPT-5.5 and 6.7 for GPT-6. It also exceeded raw memory by 16.7 and 15.0 points, respectively. Runtime execution checks remained enabled in the variants. The result therefore supports the authors' interpretation that verified knowledge construction matters, but it does not establish that verification is the only cause of the gains.

### 4. Contribution and boundary

The system's claim is that interaction can improve later manipulation by changing reusable knowledge rather than model parameters. Its Hierarchical Physical Knowledge links subtask decisions to local physical effects. In the reported study, success rose with a fixed model and executor, and same-experience controls support a contribution from knowledge construction and verification. Other aspects of the method remain coupled with verification.

Separating "what remains to be done" from "what physical effect just occurred" gives experience a way to guide later decisions without mistaking a successful local action for completion of the whole task.
