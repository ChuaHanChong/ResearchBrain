# D-OPSD: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Continuously Tuning Step-Distilled Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed D-OPSD, an on-policy self-distillation framework, to enable continuous fine-tuning of efficient few-step diffusion models without sacrificing their original inference speed or image quality. This method notably outperformed standard fine-tuning and reinforcement learning approaches in learning new concepts and generalizing them while preserving core capabilities.

## Problem

- Step-distilled diffusion models face a train-inference mismatch when fine-tuned with standard supervised methods, leading to degradation of their efficient few-step generation quality.
- Existing online reinforcement learning (RL) methods for adaptation often require complex reward functions or preference signals, which are typically unavailable to developers with only image-text pairs.
- The challenge of continuously adapting few-step diffusion models to new concepts, styles, or domains while strictly preserving their inherent few-step inference capability and high image quality.

## Method

- **D-OPSD** introduces an ==on-policy self-distillation== paradigm where the diffusion model simultaneously acts as a 'student' (text-conditioned) and a 'teacher' (multimodal-conditioned).
- The teacher model leverages the ==emergent in-context capabilities== of LLM/VLM encoders by being conditioned on both the text prompt and the target image features, providing a stronger supervision signal.
- The student model is trained to match the teacher's predictions along its own generated few-step trajectories, using an ==MSE loss between predicted velocities==, with an ==EMA teacher== for stability.

## Results

- In LoRA training for learning new concepts, D-OPSD achieved **lowest DINO distance** and **lowest LPIPS distance**, and **highest VLM-judgment scores** for learning and generalization, while preserving image quality.
- For full fine-tuning on larger datasets (e.g., anime domain), D-OPSD yielded **lowest FID**, **lowest DINO-D**, and **lowest LPIPS-D**, indicating closer distribution to the target, and maintained high Quality Score for few-step inference.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of both on-policy sampling and self-distillation, with the **EMA teacher** configuration providing the most stable and effective training compared to off-policy or SFT variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern text-to-image diffusion models with LLM/VLM encoders exhibit emergent in-context capabilities, allowing them to generate concept-consistent variations when provided with multimodal conditions without additional training.
> - Optimizing diffusion models directly on their own generated few-step trajectories (on-policy) is crucial for preserving their original few-step inference behavior and image quality during continuous adaptation.
> - Using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher model provides a stable and effective target for self-distillation, outperforming a frozen base model or direct student copy.
