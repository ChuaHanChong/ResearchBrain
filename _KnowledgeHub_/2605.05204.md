# D-OPSD: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Continuously Tuning Step-Distilled Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed D-OPSD, an on-policy self-distillation framework, to enable continuous fine-tuning of efficient few-step diffusion models without sacrificing their original inference speed or image quality. This method notably outperformed standard fine-tuning and reinforcement learning approaches in learning new concepts and generalizing them while preserving core capabilities.

## Problem

- Step-distilled diffusion models face a train-inference mismatch when fine-tuned with standard supervised methods, leading to degradation of their efficient few-step generation quality.
- Existing online reinforcement learning (RL) methods for adaptation often require complex reward functions or preference signals, which are typically unavailable to developers with only image-text pairs.
- The challenge of continuously adapting few-step diffusion models to new concepts, styles, or domains while strictly preserving their inherent few-step inference capability and high image quality.

## Method

- **D-OPSD** introduces an ==on-policy self-distillation== paradigm where the diffusion model simultaneously acts as a 'student' (text-conditioned) and a 'teacher' (multimodal-conditioned).
- The teacher model leverages the ==emergent in-context capabilities== of LLM/VLM encoders by being conditioned on both the text prompt and the target image features, providing a stronger supervision signal.
- The student model is trained to match the teacher's predictions along its own generated few-step trajectories, using an ==MSE loss between predicted velocities==, with an ==EMA teacher== for stability.

## Results

- In LoRA training for learning new concepts, D-OPSD achieved **lowest DINO distance** and **lowest LPIPS distance**, and **highest VLM-judgment scores** for learning and generalization, while preserving image quality.
- For full fine-tuning on larger datasets (e.g., anime domain), D-OPSD yielded **lowest FID**, **lowest DINO-D**, and **lowest LPIPS-D**, indicating closer distribution to the target, and maintained high Quality Score for few-step inference.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of both on-policy sampling and self-distillation, with the **EMA teacher** configuration providing the most stable and effective training compared to off-policy or SFT variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern text-to-image diffusion models with LLM/VLM encoders exhibit emergent in-context capabilities, allowing them to generate concept-consistent variations when provided with multimodal conditions without additional training.
> - Optimizing diffusion models directly on their own generated few-step trajectories (on-policy) is crucial for preserving their original few-step inference behavior and image quality during continuous adaptation.
> - Using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher model provides a stable and effective target for self-distillation, outperforming a frozen base model or direct student copy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of high-performance image generation has seen a shift towards efficient few-step diffusion models (e.g., Z-Image-Turbo, FLUX.2-klein), which require significantly fewer denoising steps compared to their multi-step predecessors. This development has led to reduced latency and computational costs, making these models increasingly suitable for practical deployment.

However, a challenge in this evolving landscape is the continuous fine-tuning of these step-distilled models. Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods, commonly applied to multi-step diffusion models, often compromise the inherent few-step inference capability of distilled models. This is because standard SFT optimizes the model on externally induced states of target images, which do not align with the states visited by the model's own few-step sampler. This train-inference mismatch can perturb the learned few-step dynamics, leading to degradation in image quality.

Online Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches, in contrast, optimize models on their own generated samples, thus preserving few-step capabilities by deriving learning signals from the same sampled trajectories. However, online RL typically necessitates a well-designed reward function or preference model, which may not be readily available to secondary developers who primarily possess image-text pairs for customization.

This research addresses the gap between the efficiency of few-step diffusion models and the need for adaptable, high-quality continuous fine-tuning. It proposes an on-policy self-distillation framework that aims to combine the benefits of on-policy learning (preserving few-step capacity) with supervised learning from paired image-text data (learning new concepts/styles) without requiring explicit reward functions. This work is positioned to enable more flexible and effective adaptation of state-of-the-art efficient generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel training paradigm that enables continuous supervised fine-tuning of step-distilled diffusion models while preserving their original few-step inference capability.

This objective is motivated by several considerations:

- **Preserving Few-Step Inference Capability:** State-of-the-art diffusion models are increasingly optimized for efficient, few-step image generation. A crucial challenge for continuous tuning is to acquire new knowledge (concepts, styles, domains) without degrading this efficiency. Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods for multi-step models typically involve optimizing on noised target images, which creates a mismatch with the actual few-step sampling trajectories of distilled models. This mismatch frequently perturbs the learned few-step dynamics, leading to a noticeable decline in generation quality.
- **Addressing Train-Inference Mismatch:** The core issue with conventional SFT is that the training signals are derived from an offline data distribution (noised target images) rather than the model's own on-policy roll-outs. For models whose quality relies on a small number of carefully distilled denoising updates, this discrepancy is particularly detrimental, as even minor perturbations can severely impact performance.
- **Avoiding Complex Reward Functions:** While online Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches naturally address the train-inference mismatch by optimizing on the model's own trajectories, they depend on carefully designed reward functions or preference signals. These are often unavailable or impractical for most secondary developers who aim to customize models using only image-text pairs. The goal is to provide a practical solution that does not require such auxiliary components.
- **Leveraging Emerging Model Properties:** The authors identify an emergent property in modern diffusion models that utilize Large Language Model (LLM) or Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones as encoders. They observe that these diffusion models can inherit their encoders' "in-context capabilities." Specifically, when conditioned on multimodal features extracted from both a text prompt and a target image, the model can generate variations that preserve the target concept or style, even without explicit additional training for this behavior. This observation forms the foundational motivation for formulating the training as an on-policy self-distillation process.
- **Combining On-Policy Learning with Supervised Data:** The motivation is to create a fine-tuning strategy that combines the benefits of on-policy learning (optimizing on the model's own roll-outs) with the practicality of supervised learning from paired image-text data, all without requiring an explicit reward function or external modules. This approach aims to allow the model to learn new information on the states it actually visits during inference, thereby maintaining its few-step performance.

In summary, the key motivation is to enable practical and effective continuous adaptation of efficient, step-distilled diffusion models, allowing them to learn new concepts and styles from paired data while robustly preserving their efficiency and original generation quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, D-OPSD (Diffusion On-Policy Self-Distillation), addresses the challenge of continuously fine-tuning step-distilled diffusion models. It frames the training as an on-policy self-distillation process, leveraging an emergent property of modern diffusion models with LLM/VLM encoders.

**Core Idea: On-Policy Self-Distillation**
D-OPSD draws inspiration from On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) in large language models (LLMs). In LLMs, OPSD involves a single model acting as both a "student" and a "teacher" under different contexts. The student predicts under a weaker context (e.g., query only), while the teacher predicts under a stronger context (e.g., query with additional in-context information or ground-truth response). The student is then optimized on its own sampled outputs to align with the teacher's predictions, thereby learning on its own trajectory.

**Adaptation to Diffusion Models:**
Directly applying OPSD to diffusion models is non-trivial because simply feeding a target image into the denoising process would alter the sampling trajectory, reverting to an off-policy SFT regime. D-OPSD overcomes this by introducing target-image information through a representation that enriches the model's conditioning space without changing the student's denoising roll-outs.

**Key Mechanism: Leveraging In-Context Capability of LLM/VLM Encoders**
The foundation of D-OPSD is the observation that modern text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models, which incorporate LLM/VLM backbones as their encoders (e.g., Z-Image-Turbo, FLUX.2-klein), can inherit the encoder's in-context capabilities. This means that if the diffusion model is conditioned on multimodal features extracted from *both* the text prompt and a target image, it can generate images that preserve the target concept or style, even without any specific training for this multimodal conditioning. This "emergent behavior" allows the target image to serve as an in-context supervision signal rather than a direct denoising target.

**Student and Teacher Roles:**
During training, the single diffusion model is assigned two roles:
1. **Student:** Conditioned solely on the text feature ($c_s = f_{\text{text}}(y)$), where $y$ is the text prompt. The student performs the standard few-step denoising process, generating an on-policy trajectory of latent states ($x_t^s$) using its current parameters $\theta$.
2. **Teacher:** Conditioned on a multimodal feature ($c_t = f_{\text{mm}}(y, x_0)$), where $y$ is the text prompt and $x_0$ is the target image. This multimodal conditioning provides richer information about the concept, style, or preference to be learned. The teacher's parameters $\bar{\theta}$ are typically an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's parameters or a frozen base model.

**Training Process (Algorithm 1):**
1. **Initialize:** Student weights $\theta$ and teacher weights $\bar{\theta}$ are initialized from the base model $\phi$.
2. **Condition Encoding:** For each training pair $(x_0, y)$:
 - The student condition $c_s$ is encoded from the text prompt $y$.
 - The teacher condition $c_t$ is encoded from the multimodal context of $y$ and the target image $x_0$.
3. **On-Policy Sampling (Student Roll-outs):** The student model (conditioned on $c_s$) generates a few-step denoising trajectory. Starting from Gaussian noise $x_t^K$, the student iteratively denoises using its velocity field $v_\theta(x_t, t, c_s)$ and a few-step solver $\Phi$. Crucially, these intermediate states $x_t^s$ constitute the *on-policy roll-outs*.
 - The generation of $x_t^s$ is performed with stop-gradient to ensure that the student's sampling process is not directly influenced by the teacher's supervision.
4. **Teacher and Student Velocity Prediction:** On each state $x_t^k$ of the *student's own on-policy trajectory*, both the student and the teacher predict the denoising velocity:
 - Student velocity: $u_k^s = v_\theta(x_t^k, t_k, c_s)$
 - Teacher velocity: $u_k^t = v_{\bar{\theta}}(x_t^k, t_k, c_t)$
5. **Self-Distillation Loss:** The student is trained to match the teacher's velocity predictions using a mean-squared error (MSE) loss:
 - $L_{\text{D-OPSD}} = E_{(x_0,y)} \left[ \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \|u_k^s - \text{sg}(u_k^t)\|^2_2 \right]$
 - The $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ (stop-gradient) operation ensures that gradients flow only to the student network, making the teacher a fixed target during each optimization step.
6. **Model Update:** The student model parameters $\theta$ are updated by minimizing $L_{\text{D-OPSD}}$. The teacher parameters $\bar{\theta}$ are updated via EMA of the student parameters.

**Preservation of Few-Step Capability:**
This methodology preserves few-step capability because both the states on which the optimization occurs and the supervision signal itself are derived from the student's own few-step roll-outs. This significantly reduces the mismatch between training and inference that often plagues traditional SFT methods, which would otherwise perturb the finely tuned few-step dynamics of distilled models.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Encoder Setup:** For the student, the original text encoder (e.g., Qwen3-4B) is used. For the teacher, a multimodal encoder (e.g., Qwen3-VL-4B) is employed. To mitigate feature space mismatch issues that can arise from direct substitution, the LLM component weights of the VLM are replaced with those from the text-only LLM (e.g., Qwen3-4B), while keeping VLM-specific components (ViT, Connector) unchanged. This balance ensures multimodal capability while maintaining consistency with the diffusion model's original training distribution.
- **Training Scales:** D-OPSD is evaluated in both LoRA training settings (small customized datasets, e.g., DreamBooth) and full fine-tuning settings (larger datasets, e.g., high-quality anime data). EMA momentum for the teacher is typically set to a high value (e.g., 0.9999) for stability and tracking student progress.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from evaluating D-OPSD in two distinct settings: LoRA training on small customized datasets and full fine-tuning on larger-scale datasets, comparing it against several baseline methods.

#### 4.1. D-OPSD for LoRA Training on Small Customized Datasets

In this setting, the objective was to learn new concepts or styles from a limited number of image-text pairs (e.g., 4 examples) while maintaining generalization and preserving few-step inference capability.

- **Learning New Concepts/Styles:** D-OPSD demonstrated superior performance in acquiring new knowledge. It substantially outperformed SFT-style training on DINO Distance (DINO-D), LPIPS Distance (LPIPS-D), and VLM’s Judgment of Subject or Style Consistency (VLM-J). Lower DINO-D and LPIPS-D indicate better alignment with target images, while higher VLM-J suggests stronger consistency in learned concepts or styles.
- **Generalization Beyond Training Set:** D-OPSD showed strong generalization capabilities. The model could generate newly learned concepts in novel scenes not present in the training data, as indicated by a higher CLIP Score (CLIP-S) compared to PSO. PSO, while capturing target concepts, tended to overfit, leading to a decline in CLIP-S and reduced ability to follow novel instructions.
- **Preservation of Few-Step Inference Capability:** A critical finding was D-OPSD's ability to maintain the original model's capacity to produce high-quality images with a small number of inference steps. This was reflected in significantly higher Quality Score (Quality-S) and Aesthetic Score (Aesthetic-S) compared to vanilla SFT and DreamBooth, which showed large drops in these metrics (Table 1). Visual comparisons (Figure 3) further illustrated that SFT and DreamBooth often produced blurry images, whereas D-OPSD maintained visual quality.

#### 4.2. D-OPSD for Full Fine-tuning on Larger Scale Datasets

This setting evaluated whether D-OPSD could bias the model towards a specific preference or domain (e.g., "anime" domain) without catastrophic forgetting of previously learned knowledge.

- **Adaptation to New Domains:** D-OPSD significantly outperformed baseline methods and the base model in adapting to the target domain, indicated by lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), DINO-D, and LPIPS-D (Table 2). This suggests that images generated by the D-OPSD-tuned model were statistically closer to the target distribution.
- **Retention of Prior Knowledge:** The method demonstrated the ability to retain previously learned knowledge, evidenced by GenEval and DPG scores that showed no catastrophic degradation after fine-tuning. While a slight drop in benchmark scores was noted compared to the base model, this was attributed to a trade-off associated with adapting to a new domain. In contrast, SFT and PSO exhibited sharp declines across multiple metrics, indicating failures in both domain adaptation and prior knowledge retention (Table 2, Figure 4).
- **Preservation of Few-Step Inference Capability:** Similar to the LoRA setting, D-OPSD maintained high Quality-S and Aesthetic-S, affirming its ability to preserve few-step inference capabilities even during large-scale fine-tuning. SFT and PSO, conversely, failed to uphold this capacity, resulting in lower scores and blurry outputs (Figure 4).

#### 4.3. User Study Results

A user study (Figure 5) further corroborated the quantitative metrics, showing that D-OPSD was preferred over PSO, Vanilla SFT, and even the base model across three dimensions: image quality, aesthetics, and prompt following. The substantial preference margin against SFT highlighted D-OPSD's effectiveness in preserving few-step generation quality.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Effect of On-Policy Self-Distillation:** Ablation studies confirmed that self-distillation-based schemes effectively mitigate the issue of quality degradation caused by vanilla SFT. D-OPSD, specifically, achieved faster training convergence (highest DINO similarity) while maintaining the best generation quality compared to off-policy distillation variants (Figure 6). This underscores the importance of both on-policy sampling and on-policy distillation.
- **Construction of the Teacher Model:** The research explored teacher model construction. Using an EMA teacher with a high momentum coefficient (0.9999) yielded the best results, providing stable training by smoothing the alignment target while tracking student progress. The frozen base model was also found to be a stable teacher.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Two-stage Training Pipeline (Appendix C)

A comparison with a two-stage approach (fine-tuning a multi-step model then re-distilling it using DMD) revealed D-OPSD's advantages. While multi-step SFT achieved strong target domain fitting, it required 100 NFEs. The two-stage pipeline reduced NFEs to 8 but incurred substantial extra training costs and still resulted in lower Quality-S and GenEval scores compared to D-OPSD. D-OPSD, despite a higher per-iteration cost than vanilla SFT, proved more resource-efficient overall when considering the alternative of re-distillation, while achieving better few-step quality and prior knowledge retention.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents D-OPSD as a notable advancement in the continuous tuning of step-distilled diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key areas:

- **Enabling Continuous Adaptation of Efficient Generative Models:** D-OPSD directly addresses a critical challenge in the practical deployment of state-of-the-art few-step diffusion models: how to continuously adapt them to new concepts, styles, or domains without compromising their inherent efficiency. By preserving the few-step inference capability, the method ensures that these models can remain fast and cost-effective even after customization.
- **Bridging the Gap Between Supervised Learning and On-Policy Optimization:** The proposed framework offers a practical alternative to both traditional supervised fine-tuning (which degrades few-step performance) and online reinforcement learning (which requires complex reward functions). By formulating training as on-policy self-distillation, D-OPSD allows models to learn from paired image-text data on their own generated trajectories, combining the strengths of supervised learning with the benefits of on-policy updates.
- **Leveraging Intrinsic Model Properties:** The identification and utilization of the emergent in-context capability of modern diffusion models with LLM/VLM encoders is a significant insight. This property enables the self-distillation mechanism without external teachers or complex architectural changes, simplifying the adaptation process.
- **Improved Quality, Generalization, and Knowledge Retention:** The experimental results consistently demonstrate that D-OPSD not only allows for effective acquisition of new knowledge but also maintains high image quality, promotes strong generalization to unseen prompts, and retains previously learned knowledge, unlike other baseline methods that often suffer from trade-offs.
- **Practical Applicability for Developers:** The method simplifies the customization workflow for secondary developers. Without the need for designing explicit reward functions or undergoing a separate, complex re-distillation stage, developers with only image-text pairs can fine-tune efficient models more effectively.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** D-OPSD provides a promising framework for further exploration in post-training and continuous adaptation of diffusion models. The authors suggest several future directions, including:
 - **Richer Teacher-Side Context:** Investigating more sophisticated conditional signals from image editing or video generation models to create stronger teacher contexts.
 - **Combining with Other Constraints:** Integrating D-OPSD with additional training constraints for enhanced learning.
 - **Multi-Expert On-Policy Distillation:** Exploring the distillation of multiple domain-specific experts into a single base model within the D-OPSD framework.

While acknowledging limitations such as increased computational cost per iteration compared to vanilla SFT and reliance on the base model's intrinsic teacher capability, the overall contribution of D-OPSD is its provision of a robust and effective single-stage solution for continuously adapting efficient text-to-image diffusion models. This can significantly impact the accessibility and versatility of advanced generative AI in various application scenarios.
