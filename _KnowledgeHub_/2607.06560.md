# Vision as Unified Multimodal Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> SenseTime Research presents a framework that unifies diverse computer vision tasks, spanning structured understanding, dense geometry, segmentation, and multi-view 3D, into a single multimodal generation paradigm. This approach enables a unified multimodal model to natively produce text, image, or mixed outputs for these tasks, achieving competitive performance across various benchmarks while retaining general multimodal abilities.

## Problem

- Computer vision traditionally relies on specialized systems for different tasks, leading to fragmentation and diverse architectures, loss functions, and evaluation protocols.
- The task-specific nature of existing systems hinders the sharing, reuse, and composability of visual supervision across heterogeneous tasks.
- A lack of a comprehensive unified generative interface that can natively support both text and image outputs for the full spectrum of computer vision tasks.

## Method

- Formulated computer vision as a ==unified multimodal generation== task, expressing a wide range of visual tasks through the native text and image generation spaces of a single ==unified multimodal model (UMM)==.
- Developed a ==data protocol== to convert diverse computer vision annotations into a common ==instruction-response sample schema==, leveraging natural language instructions and optional visual prompts.
- Constructed the **SenseNova-Vision Corpus (SN-VC)** by converting public datasets and generating new data, then fine-tuned an existing UMM (==Bagel-7B-MoT==) on this corpus with a ==rectified-flow objective== and without task-specific prediction heads.

## Results

- Achieved strong performance on structured visual understanding benchmarks, matching Grounding DINO-Swin-T on COCO-Common (**56.6 F1@mIoU**) and surpassing specialist models on OCR and GUI grounding.
- Demonstrated strong performance in dense geometric prediction (e.g., **4.0 abs rel.** / **98.1 δ₁** on NYUv2 depth) and competitive results in segmentation (e.g., **63.2 gIoU** on reasoning segmentation).
- Showed competitive results for multi-view visual geometry tasks like point map reconstruction (**0.301 Acc.** / **0.175 Comp.** / **72.2 F1** on ETH3D) and camera pose estimation (**80.1 AUC@30** on CO3Dv2).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single unified multimodal model can effectively perform a wide array of heterogeneous computer vision tasks by natively generating text, image, or mixed outputs.
> - Standardizing diverse visual annotations into an instruction-response format enables consolidated training and output parsing across different vision tasks.
> - The unified framework fosters programmable perception, allowing dynamic task definition and interaction through natural language instructions and visual prompts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has historically developed through the creation of specialized systems tailored for individual task families, such as object detection, segmentation, or 3D reconstruction. These specialist systems often employ task-specific architectures, loss functions, decoding rules, and evaluation protocols, leading to fragmentation and limiting the shared utility and composability of visual supervision.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated the potential for consolidating diverse language tasks through prompting and generation. This paradigm has been extended to unified multimodal models (UMMs) which combine text and image generation. This paper positions itself at the intersection of these trends, investigating whether the entire spectrum of classical computer vision can be integrated into a single UMM framework using unified multimodal generation, without the need for task-specific prediction heads.

Prior efforts toward unification have explored several complementary avenues:
- **Sequence-format unifications** (e.g., Pix2Seq, UniTAB, OFA, Unified-IO, Florence-2) aim to convert diverse visual annotations into shared token or sequence formats for common model interfaces. However, they often still rely on serialization and parsing conventions, which can be less natural for dense spatial outputs.
- **Representation-centric models** (e.g., MAE, DINOv2, SAM, Painter, SegGPT, Depth Anything, VGGT) focus on learning generalized visual representations or developing models that generalize within visually coherent task families (e.g., promptable segmentation). While effective within their domains, they typically lack a unified output space and comprehensive language control across heterogeneous tasks.
- **Image-generative models for dense perception** (e.g., Marigold, Lotus, DICEPTION, Vision Banana) leverage diffusion models to produce spatially aligned visual outputs like depth maps or surface normals. These methods make dense targets compatible with native image generation but often struggle with symbolic records or mixed outputs, as their unification remains largely image-centric.
- **MLLM-based perception and task modules** (e.g., Kosmos-2, Shikra, Ferret, LISA, PixelLM, X-SAM, G2VLM, VisionLLM v2) integrate language instructions and reasoning into visual perception. While enhancing language grounding, many still route dense outputs through task-specific decoders, thus fragmenting the output spaces.

The present work addresses the gap by proposing a single generative interface within a UMM that natively supports both text and image generation, and their combination, for all major computer vision tasks. This approach contrasts with prior methods by seeking a holistic unification of input-output spaces, aiming to make computer vision a native, programmable capability of foundation models rather than a collection of specialized systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to formulate computer vision as a unified multimodal generation task. This involves expressing a wide range of heterogeneous visual tasks through the native text and image generation spaces of a single unified multimodal model (UMM), eliminating the requirement for task-specific architectures or prediction heads.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several limitations observed in the traditional computer vision paradigm:
- **Fragmentation of Task-Specific Systems:** Computer vision has advanced through specialist systems, each optimized for specific task families (e.g., detection, segmentation, 3D geometry). This specialization leads to diverse output formats (bounding boxes, masks, dense maps, 3D poses), each typically requiring distinct architectures, loss functions, decoding rules, and evaluation protocols.
- **Difficulty in Sharing and Composing Supervision:** The task-specific nature of existing systems makes it challenging to share, reuse, or compose visual supervision across different tasks, hindering the development of more generalist vision systems.
- **Lack of a Unified Generative Interface:** While LLMs have demonstrated the power of a unified generative interface for language tasks, and UMMs extend this to text and image generation, a comprehensive framework for casting all of computer vision into such a native multimodal generation paradigm has been largely absent. Existing approaches either rely on sequence serialization, generalize only within task families, or handle dense outputs via specialized decoders.
- **Scalability and Programmability:** The authors are motivated by the premise that computer vision should evolve from a collection of isolated task-specific systems to a programmable, generative, and extensible capability inherent in unified foundation models. This would provide a reusable foundation for future research and integration into general-purpose AI systems.

To achieve this, the research aims to:
1. **Develop a unified multimodal generation formulation:** This involves defining how heterogeneous vision tasks can be mapped to native input-output spaces of UMMs, utilizing natural language instructions and optional visual prompts to specify tasks, targets, and output conventions.
2. **Construct a large-scale instruction-response corpus:** This requires converting diverse computer vision annotations into instruction-response examples compatible with the UMM's native generation spaces, encompassing text, image, and mixed text-and-image targets.
3. **Train and evaluate a UMM on this corpus:** The objective is to demonstrate that a single UMM, trained primarily on this newly constructed corpus, can achieve competitive performance across various perception families without architectural modifications, while also retaining general multimodal abilities and supporting language-defined task variants not explicitly seen during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on formulating heterogeneous computer vision tasks as unified multimodal generation. This involves a data conversion protocol, corpus construction, and a mixed-task fine-tuning strategy for an existing UMM.

#### 3.1. Data Protocol

The core of the methodology is a data protocol that converts diverse computer vision annotations into a common sample schema for UMM training. This schema comprises one or more visual inputs, a natural-language instruction, and a decodable target response (text, image, or mixed).
- **Visual Inputs:** Can be a single image, an image with auxiliary visual prompts (e.g., points, boxes), or an ordered set of images for multi-view tasks.
- **Language Instruction:** Specifies the task intent, output schema, and decoding convention. Multiple instruction variants are used for robustness.
- **Target Response:** Is represented as text, an image, or a mixed text-image output. Lightweight textual markers (e.g., `<p>`, `<bbox>`, `<point>`, `<kpt>`, `<ins>`, `<polygon>`, `<color>`) and reserved special tokens are used to standardize output formats.

The protocol covers four main computer vision families:
1. **Structured Visual Understanding:** Tasks like detection (object, referring, pointing, visual-prompt), OCR, keypoint localization, layout analysis, and GUI grounding. Outputs are represented as text generation targets, including labels, transcripts, and normalized spatial coordinates, parsed back into benchmark records.
2. **Dense Geometric Prediction:** Tasks like depth and surface normal estimation. These are formulated as conditional image generation, where the instruction specifies the geometric quantity, and the response image stores the signal in a deterministic visual encoding (e.g., inverse depth as grayscale, normals as RGB).
3. **Segmentation:** Covers single-target (referring, reasoning, interactive) and multi-region (semantic, panoptic, grounded conversation generation) segmentation.
 - **Single-target:** Instruction identifies the target, response is a binary mask image. Interactive segmentation adds visual prompts.
 - **Multi-region:** Uses a mixed text-image response, with text listing regions and color legends (`<color>` markers), and the image rendering the corresponding color-coded mask. This combines language for categories/descriptions with image for dense pixels.
4. **Multi-view Visual Geometry:** Tasks like point map reconstruction and camera pose estimation from an ordered image set.
 - **Point Maps:** Dense scene geometry represented as per-view dense XYZ point maps encoded in RGB channels of image outputs.
 - **Camera Pose:** Structured sequences of text, encoding relative pose (quaternion rotation, translation direction, scale) with reserved tokens (e.g., `<frame>`, `<quat>`). This mixes dense and discrete geometric outputs.

#### 3.2. Corpus Construction (SenseNova-Vision Corpus - SN-VC)

The authors constructed the SenseNova-Vision Corpus (SN-VC) by converting publicly available computer vision datasets into instruction-response examples according to the data protocol.
- **Source Data:** Images are drawn from public sources. Annotations are directly converted when possible.
- **Generated/Curated Data (SN-VC-50M):** For incomplete, unavailable, or insufficiently diverse supervision, additional targets are generated or curated. This 50-million-example subset is publicly released. The remaining public-source portion of SN-VC can be reproduced using released source lists, prompt templates, and conversion rules.
- **Overlap Handling:** Official benchmark splits are preserved, and corresponding evaluation images/annotations are excluded from training data.
- **Corpus Organization:** SN-VC is organized into four families: structured visual understanding (detection, OCR, GUI, layout, keypoint), dense geometric prediction (depth, normal), segmentation (referring, generic, interactive, GCG, reasoning), and multi-view visual geometry (reconstruction, camera pose).

#### 3.3. Training

SenseNova-Vision is trained by adapting an off-the-shelf UMM, Bagel-7B-MoT, without task-specific prediction heads or architectural changes.
- **Mixed-Task Fine-tuning:** Supervised fine-tuning is performed on the SenseNova-Vision Corpus combined with general-purpose multimodal data (VQA, text-to-image, image-to-image). This mixture aims to teach benchmark-compatible vision abilities while preserving the base UMM's open-ended capabilities.
- **Joint Sampling Strategy:** Mini-batches are drawn from a weighted mixture of converted computer vision samples and auxiliary multimodal samples.
- **Joint Supervision:** Text and visual targets are interleaved in the same optimization process.
 - **Text Outputs:** Tokenized and optimized with standard cross-entropy (CE) loss under a next-token-prediction paradigm.
 - **Visual Outputs:** Encoded into a VAE latent space and optimized with the rectified-flow training objective, inherited from Bagel.
- **High-Resolution Training:** For image-input tasks requiring fine spatial conditioning (especially segmentation), the SigLIP2 input resolution is kept up to 980 pixels for understanding and image-conditioned generation, exceeding Bagel's default for generation.
- **Multi-View Training:** Each training sample selects at most 10 views from a scene. Point map reconstruction aligns views to the first, center-normalizes, and maps invalid pixels to a distant sky box. Camera pose estimation uses 2,009 reserved special tokens to encode quantized pose parameters (quaternion rotations, unit translation vectors, scales) and structural placeholders.
- **Optimization:** SFT is performed with the VAE visual encoder frozen, while other modules are updated using AdamW optimizer, a learning rate of 2.5 × 10⁻⁵, and no weight decay. The model is trained for 50K steps with warm-up and EMA, using the EMA checkpoint for evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SenseNova-Vision demonstrates competitive performance across the four major computer vision task families, suggesting the viability of unified multimodal generation.

#### 4.1. Structured Visual Understanding (Table 1)

- **Performance:** Achieves strong overall performance on benchmarks covering box- and point-based detection, referring, OCR localization, GUI grounding, and keypoint detection.
- **Highlights:** Matches the performance of Grounding DINO-Swin-T on COCO-Common (56.6 F1@mIoU) and surpasses specialist models like Rex-Omni and LocateAnything on several OCR and GUI grounding tasks (e.g., 49.5 F1@mIoU on ICDAR15 OCR, 34.6 F1@mOKS on COCO-Kpt). This indicates proficiency in generating structured textual predictions, including long object lists and precise coordinates.

#### 4.2. Dense Geometric Prediction (Table 2)

- **Performance:** Demonstrates strong performance in monocular depth estimation and surface normal estimation.
- **Highlights:** Outperforms several generation-based baselines (e.g., Marigold, DICEPTION, FE2E) and remains competitive with geometry-specialized models (e.g., DepthAnything, MoGe-2). For instance, it achieves 4.0 abs rel. / 98.1 δ₁ on NYUv2 depth, and 12.8 mean err. / 68.9 δ₁₁.₂₅ on ScanNet normals. This suggests effective generation of dense geometric maps as image outputs.

#### 4.3. Segmentation (Table 3)

- **Performance:** Achieves competitive overall performance among unified segmentation and multimodal baselines.
- **Highlights:** Shows strong results on reasoning segmentation (63.2 gIoU) and Grounded Conversation Generation (GCG) segmentation (65.7 mIoU). While competitive, a performance gap remains against specialized segmentation models (e.g., X-SAM) on certain generic and referring segmentation metrics, particularly where strong mask priors from models like SAM are leveraged.

#### 4.4. Multi-View Visual Geometry (Table 4)

- **Performance:** Achieves strong results among generalist geometric approaches for multi-view point map reconstruction and camera pose estimation.
- **Highlights:** Competitive on ETH3D reconstruction (0.301 Acc. / 0.175 Comp. / 72.2 F1) and CO3Dv2 camera pose estimation (80.1 AUC@30). A performance gap exists compared to specialized feed-forward geometric models like VGGT and DepthAnything3, indicating the continued benefit of geometry-focused training and inductive biases for these tasks.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Generalist Vision Models (Table 5)

- **Broader Coverage:** SenseNova-Vision demonstrates broader task coverage compared to other generalist visual models like Youtu-VL (vision-language understanding-focused) and Vision Banana (image-generation-centered). It performs strongly against Youtu-VL on structured, semantic, and depth benchmarks, and remains competitive with Vision Banana on image-space segmentation and dense prediction. This broader coverage is attributed to its unified multimodal generation over text, image, and mixed outputs.
- **Retention of General Abilities:** After mixed-task fine-tuning, SenseNova-Vision largely preserves the base UMM's general multimodal abilities, achieving 79.0 on MMVP for multimodal understanding (compared to Bagel's 83.3) and 0.85 on GenEval for text-to-image generation (compared to Bagel's 0.82).

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results and Additional Analysis

- **Convergence Analysis (Figure 6):** Depth and surface normal estimation converge fastest, possibly due to their spatial alignment with input images and proximity to pretraining patterns. Multi-view reconstruction converges moderately, requiring cross-view alignment. Camera pose estimation is slower, needing new pose tokens and deeper geometric understanding. Common detection and generic segmentation show intermediate convergence, while referring segmentation and dense detection are slower, reflecting higher demands on language conditioning, fine-grained localization, and serialization of many small objects.
- **Overall Qualitative Results (Figure 7):** Illustrates that the model follows diverse task prompts, producing decodable outputs across structured 2D, dense geometry, segmentation, and multi-view 3D tasks. Predictions for crowded scenes, small objects, and documents are complete, and dense maps, masks, and multi-view outputs show reasonable consistency.
- **Referring-Style Interactive Segmentation (Figure 8):** The model successfully interprets text-encoded point coordinates (`<p><point>[x,y]</point></p>`) for interactive segmentation, generating accurate binary masks. This demonstrates interpolation of coordinate understanding from structured prediction into mask generation through the unified text-and-image interface, highlighting a language-defined task variant beyond explicit training.
- **Free-Form Language-to-Mask Generation (Figures 9-12):**
 - **Dense Instance Segmentation:** Adapting detection instructions to request instance-level mask outputs in crowded or tiny-object scenes (e.g., Dense200, VisDrone), SenseNova-Vision extracts numerous instances and assigns distinct colors, extending mask generation to data distributions better covered by detection.
 - **Visual Grounded (VGD) Segmentation with Text Cues:** The model segments same-class objects based on a text-specified reference point or box, demonstrating adaptation of grounding supervision to a new segmentation task.
 - **Free-Form Color-Coded Mask Generation:** The model generates mask-like outputs that approximately follow natural language descriptions of region-color correspondences, suggesting control over the mask representation protocol.
 - **Text Segmentation:** By recombining OCR localization with mask generation, the model segments specific words or even individual letters as text-shaped regions, broadening segmentation target types to lexical units.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces unified multimodal generation as a formulation for computer vision, positing it as an analogous development to GPT-style generative modeling in natural language processing. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Unified Foundation for Computer Vision:** By demonstrating that heterogeneous visual tasks—from structured understanding to dense geometry, segmentation, and multi-view 3D—can be expressed and performed through the native text and image generation spaces of a single UMM, the work proposes a paradigm shift from task-specific systems to a unified framework. This unification simplifies the architecture and training process, eliminating the need for separate prediction heads for each task.
- **Scalable Integration into Foundation Models:** The approach provides a scalable route for integrating diverse computer vision supervision into general-purpose foundation models. This means future foundation models could inherently possess a broad range of visual perception capabilities, making them more versatile and powerful.
- **Programmable and Extensible Perception:** Computer vision becomes a programmable and extensible capability. Users can specify tasks, target regions, output schemas, and decoding conventions using natural language instructions and optional visual prompts, allowing for dynamic task definition and interaction beyond predefined benchmarks. This flexibility enables the model to follow language-defined task variants that combine different visual cues (category, color, region) even if not explicitly enumerated in the training corpus.
- **Enhanced Cross-Modal Correspondences:** Joint training under this unified formulation strengthens cross-modal correspondences, allowing information to be flexibly represented and utilized across modalities (e.g., spatial locations specified by text or image cues, textual-spatial information as symbolic text or visual masks). This suggests a deeper integration of visual, linguistic, and spatial understanding within one model.
- **Cultivation of Implicit Spatial Understanding:** The generative modeling of diverse 2D and 3D perception tasks is posited to cultivate implicit spatial understanding, which could naturally connect this paradigm to emerging frontiers like physical intelligence and world models capable of perceiving, reasoning about, and interacting with the physical world.
- **Public Availability:** The public release of the SenseNova-Vision model and the SenseNova-Vision Corpus is a significant contribution, providing researchers with resources to further explore and build upon this unified vision paradigm.

The authors identify several future directions that this formulation opens, including stronger in-context learning for specifying new visual tasks through examples, extending unified multimodal generation from images to video to incorporate temporal dynamics, and scaling the corpus and model capacity for deeper integration with advanced language models to absorb richer visual and spatial knowledge.
