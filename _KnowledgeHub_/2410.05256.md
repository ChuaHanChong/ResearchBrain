# Proprioceptive State Estimation for Quadruped Robots using Invariant Kalman Filtering and Scale-Variant Robust Cost Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a Robust Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) for quadruped robots, integrating scale-variant robust cost functions to address pose drift caused by contact estimation errors. The method reduced pose drift by up to 40% on public datasets and improved accuracy in challenging high-slippage terrains compared to a standard IEKF.

## Problem

- Proprioceptive-only state estimators for legged robots suffer from high pose drift, primarily due to inaccuracies in contact estimation from phenomena like slippage or deformable terrain.
- Exteroceptive sensors, while accurate, are vulnerable to environmental conditions (e.g., lighting, low-texture, motion blur), necessitating robust proprioceptive-only solutions.
- Standard IEKF implementations for legged robots, while addressing some EKF limitations, still exhibit significant drift, particularly in the vertical direction and during slippage events, making them less suitable for precise navigation.

## Method

- The approach combines the ==Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF)== framework with ==scale-variant robust cost functions (Huber and Tukey)== to model state on Lie groups and handle outliers.
- Robust cost functions are applied within the IEKF's measurement update step using ==Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS)==, effectively down-weighting measurements with large residuals caused by contact perturbations.
- A ==right-invariant error formulation== is utilized for consistent and accurate error propagation, enhancing the filter's stability without frequent re-linearization.

## Results

- The robust IEKF achieved up to a **40%** reduction in pose drift on a public dataset compared to a state-of-the-art standard IEKF, with the Huber function (**c=0.5**) reducing ATE RMSE from **9.237m** to **5.499m**.
- In high-slippage indoor experiments with an AlienGo robot, robust IEKF configurations consistently reduced error compared to the standard IEKF baseline, with the Tukey cost function (**c=9.0**) identified as most effective.
- While visual-inertial SLAM (T265) demonstrated high accuracy, the best robust IEKF results (e.g., Tukey c=9.0 with ATE RMSE **0.071m**) were competitive or superior in overall ATE for specific high-slippage scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating robust cost functions into the IEKF framework significantly mitigates pose drift in proprioceptive state estimation by effectively handling outlier measurements from contact perturbations like slippage.
> - The choice of robust cost function (Huber or Tukey) and the tuning of its associated scale parameter 'c' critically influence estimation accuracy and the trade-off between vertical and horizontal drift.
> - The method demonstrates that advanced invariant filtering theory, combined with robust statistical techniques, can yield highly accurate and reliable state estimates for legged robots in environments where exteroceptive sensing is compromised.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate state estimation is a foundational requirement for autonomous legged robot locomotion, providing essential information for control, stability, and navigation, particularly in complex and unstructured environments. The broader landscape of state estimation for legged robots typically categorizes approaches based on sensor modality: proprioceptive sensors (e.g., joint encoders, Inertial Measurement Units - IMUs, torque sensors), exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDARs), or a combination of both.

Many contemporary, highly accurate state estimation frameworks, such as VILENS, Cerberus, and STEP, integrate both proprioceptive and exteroceptive measurements. These multi-sensor approaches offer advantages in accuracy and capabilities like environmental mapping. However, exteroceptive sensors exhibit vulnerabilities, including susceptibility to lighting changes, low-texture environments, featureless corridors, and motion blur during rapid movements, which can lead to system failures or reduced performance. Consequently, there is a recognized need for robust state estimation systems that rely solely on proprioceptive data.

Proprioceptive-only state estimation methods generally employ either filtering or smoothing techniques. Smoothing methods, while often more accurate due to their use of past estimations in optimization, can be less suitable for real-time applications. Filtering methods, conversely, process data sequentially, offering greater efficiency. Early filtering-based estimators, such as the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) proposed by Bloesch et al. (QEKF) and the eXogenous Kalman Filter (XKF) by Fink and Semini, used quaternions for rotation representation. These approaches faced limitations, including state-dependent error propagation models that could lead to linearization inaccuracies and the decoupling of rotation and translation uncertainty propagation.

The Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF), introduced by Barrau and Bonnabel, addressed these issues by modeling the state as a Lie group, enabling on-manifold filtering. Subsequent works applied IEKF to legged robots, with some efforts to handle complex scenarios like slippage by fusing leg kinematics with inertial-velocity measurements. Despite these advancements, many proprioceptive estimators were not specifically designed for challenging terrains (slippery, rough) and consequently suffered from high pose drift. This drift, particularly in the vertical direction, often renders proprioceptive state estimators impractical for autonomous navigation. The challenge of accurately estimating contact, especially in scenarios involving slippage or deformable terrain, remains a significant hurdle.

Previous attempts to address contact estimation and slippage include slip detection based on leg kinematics, probabilistic contact estimation (though gait-dependent and not explicitly handling slippage), and learning-based contact estimation (which did not model uncertainty). Robust statistical techniques have also been explored, such as Mahalanobis distance rejection, though this can increase estimation variance by discarding information. More recently, Gao et al. combined an IEKF with an adaptive filter and robust cost functions in the propagation model, noting a reduction in foot speed estimation error but lacking explicit details on the functions used, their parameter influence, or impact on trajectory error.

This work contributes to the landscape by specifically developing a robust IEKF using scale-variant robust cost functions within the *measurement update* step, aiming to mitigate pose drift caused by contact perturbations. This represents an explicit application of robust filtering techniques in the measurement model of an IEKF for quadruped robots to address the drift problem, a gap not fully explored in prior IEKF literature for legged robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a proprioceptive state estimator for quadruped robots that can maintain high accuracy and reduce pose drift, particularly in challenging environments characterized by uneven or slippery terrain. This is achieved by combining the mathematical framework of the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) with scale-variant robust cost functions.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges in legged robot state estimation:

1. **Vulnerability of Exteroceptive Sensors:** While multi-sensor fusion (proprioceptive + exteroceptive) approaches often achieve high accuracy, exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDARs) are inherently vulnerable to environmental conditions such as poor lighting, low-texture scenes, featureless environments, and motion blur. This necessitates robust state estimation capabilities that can function reliably using only proprioceptive data when exteroceptive information is compromised or unavailable.
2. **Proprioceptive Drift and Contact Estimation Errors:** A significant limitation of existing proprioceptive-only state estimators is their susceptibility to high pose drift. This drift is predominantly caused by inaccuracies in contact estimation, which arise from phenomena like foot slippage, deformable terrain, or deformable legs. Traditional approaches often assume rigid contact, leading to estimation errors when these assumptions are violated.
3. **Limitations of Standard IEKF Implementations:**
 - **Drift in Complex Scenarios:** While IEKF offers advantages over conventional EKFs by modeling states on Lie groups and providing consistent error propagation, some implementations (e.g.,) have been observed to suffer from significant drift in complex scenarios, particularly when slippage occurs.
 - **Lack of Explicit Slippage Handling:** Many proprioceptive state estimators, including some EKF and IEKF variants, do not explicitly formulate or experimentally evaluate mechanisms to handle slippage events.
 - **Vertical Drift:** Previous research has demonstrated that traditional IEKF implementations, which often use simple thresholds on ground reaction forces for contact determination, are prone to high drifts in the vertical direction, making them less suitable for precise navigation.
 - **Uncertainty in Learning-based Contact Estimation:** While learning approaches for contact estimation have shown promising results, they sometimes do not explicitly model the uncertainty of contact estimation, potentially leading to errors under high foot slippage.
4. **Inadequacy of Traditional Robust Techniques:** Methods like Mahalanobis distance rejection, used to remove outliers, can inadvertently increase estimation variance by discarding potentially useful information within the residual, highlighting a need for more nuanced robust approaches.
5. **Limited Analysis of Robust Cost Functions in IEKF:** Although some work has explored robust adaptive filters with IEKF, it often lacked specific details on the robust cost functions used, a comprehensive analysis of their parameter influence, or a thorough evaluation of their impact on trajectory error for legged robots.

To address these motivations, the specific contributions outlined by the authors are:
- To present a novel derivation of a robust right-invariant IEKF for quadruped robots, based on recent theoretical formulations, and integrate it with scale-variant robust cost functions. This is positioned as the first application of a robust filter in the measurement model of the IEKF for quadruped robot state estimation to specifically address drift caused by leg perturbations.
- To demonstrate, through experimental validation and public datasets, the superior performance of the proposed robust IEKF in terms of absolute pose accuracy, aiming for a significant reduction in pose drift compared to a state-of-the-art IEKF.
- To provide a detailed analysis of how different robust cost functions and their associated scale parameters influence the accuracy of state estimation, thereby offering insights into their practical application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper combines the principles of Invariant Extended Kalman Filtering (IEKF) with scale-variant robust cost functions. The core idea is to leverage the mathematical properties of Lie groups for state representation and error propagation, while simultaneously employing robust statistical techniques in the measurement update step to mitigate the impact of outliers caused by contact perturbations.

**A. Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF)**

1. **Robot Model and State Variables:**
 - The robot's state is defined at an instant $t_i$ as $X_i \in SE_{2+N}(3)$, a Lie group representing orientations, positions, and velocities. Specifically, $X_i$ encapsulates the IMU's orientation ($R_i \in SO_3$), velocity ($v_i \in R^3$), position ($p_i \in R^3$) in the world frame, and the contact positions ($p^1_f..., p^N_f \in R^3$) of $N$ feet also in the world frame.
 - $SE_{2+N}(3)$ is a semi-direct product group, where composition rules are defined for elements $(R_i, x_i)$ and $(R_j, x_j)$ as $(R_i R_j, x_i + R_i * x_j)$, where $*$ denotes term-by-term multiplication.
 - The Lie algebra ($se_{2+N}(3)$) corresponding to $SE_{2+N}(3)$ is utilized via the exponential and adjoint maps, facilitating on-manifold filtering.
 - The paper primarily focuses on the right-invariant error, which is defined at the identity element of the Lie group.

2. **Discrete Stochastic Process Model (Prediction Step):**
 - **Sensor Noise:** IMU measurements (angular velocity $\tilde{\omega}_{I,i}$ and linear acceleration $\tilde{a}_{I,i}$) are assumed to be corrupted by white Gaussian noise ($w_{g,i}$, $w_{a,i}$). Foot contact position is assumed fixed in the world frame, but its velocity is corrupted by white Gaussian noise ($w_{f,i}$) in the IMU frame to account for potential slippage.
 - **State Propagation:** The nominal state $X_i$ is propagated using discrete evolution dynamics derived from the IMU measurements and gravity. Approximations for exponential maps and Jacobians are applied to simplify the dynamics.
 - **Error Propagation:** A key aspect of IEKF is the exact propagation of the right-invariant error. For the noise-free case, the error $\xi_{r,i+1}$ propagates linearly through a matrix $A_i$ that is state-independent. For the noisy system, the error propagation is derived to include noise terms, leading to the covariance propagation equation: $P_{i+1|i} = A_i P_i A_i^T + B_i Q_i B_i^T$, where $Q_i$ is the covariance of the process noise. This formulation ensures that the error dynamics remain consistent and accurate without requiring re-linearization around a new state estimate at each step, a common issue in traditional EKFs.

3. **Measurement Update Step:**
 - **Measurement Model:** The measurement model is based on the forward kinematics of the leg in contact. Joint encoder measurements ($\tilde{q}_i$) are assumed to be corrupted by white Gaussian noise ($w_q$). The measured foot contact position ($\tilde{z}_{I,i}$) in the IMU frame is obtained by applying the forward kinematics function $K(q)$ and a transformation from the body to the IMU frame. Kinematics calibration errors are also modeled as white Gaussian noise ($w_K$). The total covariance of the measurement noise $N_i$ is derived.
 - **Innovation Definition:** The innovation $\delta z_i$ is defined as the difference between the measured foot contact position and the predicted position, transformed into the world frame. Uniquely, this innovation is defined in a frame opposite to that of the measurement, ensuring the update step is state-trajectory independent (a property of invariant filters).
 - **Kalman Filter Equations:** The standard EKF equations are then applied to calculate the Kalman gain $K_i$, the estimated error $\hat{\xi}_{r,i}$, and the updated error covariance $P_{i+1}$.
 - **State Update:** The estimated error $\hat{\xi}_{r,i}$ is used to update the current state estimate $\hat{X}_i$ on the Lie group via the right-invariant group action: $\hat{X}_i = \hat{\xi}_{r,i} \oplus X_{i+1|i}$.

**B. Robust Invariant Extended Kalman Filter**

1. **Addressing Outliers:** The standard Kalman Filter, based on an $l_2$ norm (least squares) formulation, is sensitive to outliers. In the context of legged robots, these outliers commonly arise from contact estimation errors due to slippage or uneven terrain. Large residuals disproportionately influence the state estimate in a standard EKF.
2. **Robust Cost Functions:** To mitigate this sensitivity, the paper proposes replacing the $l_2$ norm in the measurement update's underlying linear regression problem with scale-variant robust cost functions. Two functions are specifically investigated:
 - **Huber Function:** Optimal for contaminated Gaussian densities, it treats small errors as $l_2$ errors and large errors as $l_1$ errors, limiting their influence.
 - **Tukey Function:** Provides even stronger resistance to extreme outliers by assigning zero weight to errors above a certain scale parameter $c$.
3. **Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS):** The modified optimization problem, now using robust cost functions, is solved iteratively using the IRLS method. This approach effectively down-weights measurements whose residuals exceed a predetermined threshold, thereby making the state estimation process less susceptible to the negative impact of outliers caused by perturbations in leg contact.

**C. Implementation and Evaluation**
The methodology was implemented in C++ using ROS Noetic. Performance evaluation utilized Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) metrics, comparing the proposed method against an existing IEKF and, where applicable, visual-inertial odometry solutions. Experiments were conducted on a public dataset (Street dataset from) and an indoor experiment with an AlienGo robot on challenging terrains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the proposed Robust Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (Robust IEKF) using two distinct test scenarios: a public dataset (Street) and a controlled indoor experiment with an AlienGo robot. The performance was assessed using Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) metrics, and comparisons were made against a standard IEKF and, in one case, a visual-inertial SLAM system.

**A. Street Dataset (Unitree A1 Robot)**

- **Baseline Performance:** The standard IEKF exhibited the highest pose drift among all tested methods on this dataset, which covered trajectories exceeding 450 meters.
- **Robust IEKF Performance:** The introduction of robust cost functions significantly reduced pose drift.
 - The Robust IEKF, utilizing either the Huber or Tukey cost function, consistently outperformed the standard IEKF in terms of absolute pose accuracy.
 - Specifically, the Robust IEKF achieved up to a 40% reduction in pose drift compared to the state-of-the-art IEKF (presumably referring to the `IEKF - Huber (c=0.5)` run which showed an ATE RMSE of 5.499m compared to `IEKF`'s 9.237m).
 - **Influence of Scale Parameter `c`:** A clear trend was observed where decreasing the scale parameter `c` (which dictates the threshold for outlier influence) generally led to a reduction in drift in both the X-Y (horizontal) and Z-Y (vertical) planes. This indicates that a more aggressive down-weighting of potential outliers improves accuracy in this scenario.
 - **Cost Function Comparison:** The Tukey cost function with a parameter `c = 9.0` yielded results comparable to the Huber function with `c = 2.0`, suggesting that different functions achieve similar performance levels with distinct parameter settings.

**B. AlienGo Robot on Uneven and Slippery Terrain**

- **Challenging Environment:** This experiment involved the AlienGo robot navigating over rocks and a slippery surface covered with liquid soap, presenting a high-slippage scenario. Ground truth was provided by a Vicon motion capture system, and a RealSense T265 visual-inertial SLAM system was used for additional comparison.
- **Overall Improvement over IEKF:** All robust IEKF configurations demonstrated a reduction in error (either ATE or RPE) compared to the standard IEKF baseline.
 - The Tukey cost function with `c = 9.0` was identified as the most effective in this particular high-slippage environment.
- **Trade-off in High Slippage:** Unlike the Street dataset, which involved a longer trajectory where drift accumulated over time, the high-slippage AlienGo experiment revealed a potential trade-off. While increased robustness effectively reduced vertical (Z-direction) drift, it sometimes resulted in a slight increase in drift within the X-Y plane.
- **Comparison to Visual-Inertial SLAM (T265):** The T265 system (ATE RMSE 0.0978m) demonstrated higher absolute accuracy than most IEKF variants in this specific short-distance, high-slippage environment, particularly in X-Y plane. However, the best robust IEKF results (e.g., Tukey c=9.0 with ATE RMSE 0.071m) were competitive or superior in some aspects of overall ATE to the T265. The IEKF variants generally showed higher RPE for rotation than the T265.

**C. Discussion and General Conclusions**

- The research consistently demonstrated the advantage of incorporating robust cost functions into the IEKF framework for proprioceptive state estimation.
- In scenarios dominated by cumulative error propagation over long distances without explicit high slippage (Street dataset), increasing the robustness (by decreasing `c`) reduced drift in both horizontal and vertical directions.
- In scenarios with acute external disturbances and high slippage (AlienGo experiment), robust cost functions effectively mitigated vertical drift, although this could come with a marginal compromise in horizontal accuracy in some cases. This suggests the robust IEKF offers a more stable vertical estimate under contact perturbations.
- The analysis confirmed that the choice of robust cost function (Huber or Tukey) and the tuning of the scale parameter `c` significantly influence the estimation accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of legged robot state estimation, particularly for scenarios where reliance on proprioceptive sensors is paramount. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The primary impact is the demonstrated ability to significantly reduce pose drift in proprioceptive state estimation for quadruped robots, especially in challenging environments characterized by uneven terrain, slippage, and external disturbances. This directly addresses a critical limitation of existing proprioceptive estimators that often fail or accumulate substantial errors in such conditions.
2. **Increased Autonomy and Reliability:** By providing more accurate and consistent state estimates without relying on exteroceptive sensors, the proposed robust IEKF can enhance the autonomy and reliability of legged robots. This is crucial for applications where visual or LiDAR data may be unreliable (e.g., low-light, featureless environments, heavy dust/smoke, or high-speed motion causing blur). Robots equipped with this system could navigate and perform tasks more effectively in a wider range of unstructured and dynamic real-world settings.
3. **Advancement in Invariant Filtering Theory Application:** The paper contributes to the practical application of advanced invariant filtering theory by integrating recent theoretical formulations of the IEKF (based on Barrau and Bonnabel) with robust statistics. This provides a refined and theoretically grounded approach to handling noise and outliers in state estimation on Lie groups.
4. **Mitigation of Contact Estimation Errors:** The core problem of drift caused by inaccurate contact estimation (e.g., slippage, deformable terrain) is directly tackled. By applying robust cost functions in the measurement update, the system effectively down-weights outlier measurements originating from unmodeled contact dynamics, leading to more stable and accurate pose estimates. This is a crucial step towards more realistic and adaptable locomotion control.
5. **Foundation for Further Research and Optimization:** The detailed analysis of how different robust cost functions (Huber, Tukey) and their scale parameters (`c`) influence estimation accuracy provides valuable insights. This lays a foundation for future work, explicitly noting the need to explore optimal scale values and their interaction with sensor covariance values, which could further improve the adaptability and performance of such systems.
6. **Practical Tool for Robot Development:** The methodology is implemented in C++ with ROS, indicating its potential for practical integration into real-world quadruped robot systems. The empirical validation using both a public dataset and an actual robot in an experimental setting demonstrates its feasibility and effectiveness.

In conclusion, this research significantly strengthens the capability of legged robots to perform robust state estimation using only proprioceptive sensors, thereby expanding their operational envelopes and contributing to their broader deployment in complex, unstructured, and potentially hazardous environments where exteroceptive sensing may be compromised.
