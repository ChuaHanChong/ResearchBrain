# DyFo: A Training-Free Dynamic Focus Visual Search for Enhancing LMMs in Fine-Grained Visual Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> DyFo, developed by researchers at Peking University and Tencent, is a training-free dynamic focus visual search method designed to enhance Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) in fine-grained visual understanding and reduce visual hallucinations. It achieves this by orchestrating a bidirectional interaction between LMMs and visual experts via a Monte Carlo Tree Search, demonstrating improved performance on benchmarks such as POPE and V* Bench by enabling LMMs to effectively filter irrelevant visual content.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with fine-grained visual understanding, especially in high-resolution or cluttered images, due to fixed-resolution encoders that lose details during downsampling.
- Dynamic-resolution LMMs, while processing more data, can be overwhelmed by irrelevant visual information, which paradoxically exacerbates hallucination issues by increasing noise.
- Existing solutions for fine-grained understanding often require significant architectural modifications, extensive data collection, or costly fine-tuning, limiting their generalizability and practicality.

## Method

- Researchers at Peking University and Tencent developed ==DyFo (Dynamic Focus)==, a training-free visual search method that enables LMMs to dynamically focus on crucial visual regions.
- DyFo orchestrates a ==bidirectional interaction== between an LMM (for text refinement) and an off-the-shelf visual expert (for image region updates) using a compact action space (==semantic focus and semantic scatter==).
- A ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== algorithm guides this dynamic focusing process, exploring potential focus regions and leveraging a reward mechanism based on consistency and conciseness to efficiently identify the most relevant visual information.

## Results

- DyFo consistently improved LMM performance on the POPE benchmark, reducing visual hallucinations; for example, it boosted Qwen2-VL's F1 Score on POPE-COCO (random) from **89.22** to **91.71**.
- On the V* Bench, designed for fine-grained understanding in high-resolution images, DyFo-Q (Qwen2-VL with DyFo) achieved an overall accuracy of **81.15%**, surpassing fine-tuned methods like SEAL (**75.39%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the combined semantic focus and scatter actions, and demonstrated that Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) was the most efficient search algorithm, requiring fewer steps (**3.20** on V* Bench) compared to alternatives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A training-free method can effectively integrate off-the-shelf visual experts with LMMs, providing an "out-of-the-box" solution that avoids costly fine-tuning or architectural changes.
> - Orchestrating a bidirectional interaction between LMMs (for semantic understanding) and visual experts (for precise localization) through a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithm enhances fine-grained understanding and reduces visual hallucinations.
> - A human-inspired dynamic focus mechanism, utilizing actions like semantic focus and semantic scatter, efficiently guides the visual search, allowing LMMs to filter irrelevant content and concentrate on critical details.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses critical limitations in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), a rapidly evolving field at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing. LMMs, such as BLIP-2 and LLaVA, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in general multimodal perception and reasoning. However, as the authors highlight, they face significant challenges in fine-grained visual understanding, particularly when dealing with high-resolution and cluttered images.

**Current LMM Limitations:**
- **Fixed-resolution encoders:** Many LMMs use fixed-resolution encoders (e.g., 224x224 or 336x336 pixels) which, when applied to high-resolution images, necessitate downsampling. This process invariably leads to the loss of crucial fine-grained details, such as small objects, ultimately contributing to "hallucinations" – instances where the LMM generates plausible but factually incorrect information.
- **Dynamic-resolution approaches:** While newer LMMs like Qwen2-VL and LLaVA-Next attempt to mitigate this by dividing images into sections and processing them separately, the authors argue that simply increasing input capacity can introduce vast amounts of irrelevant content. This "noise" can interfere with task-related information and, paradoxically, *exacerbate* hallucination issues, as supported by recent studies.

**The Role of Visual Search:**
The paper draws inspiration from human visual search, a cognitive mechanism that allows humans to efficiently filter irrelevant information and focus on task-related regions in cluttered environments. This mechanism, characterized by dynamic focusing adjustments (like fixations and saccades during reading), is a key cognitive ability that current LMMs lack intrinsically.

**Existing Solutions for Fine-Grained Understanding:**
Previous attempts to enhance LMMs' fine-grained understanding include:
- **Region-based training:** Some methods integrate bounding boxes or masks during training to enable local region understanding. However, these often require explicit spatial prompts at inference time, limiting their real-world applicability.
- **Integrated visual search (e.g., SEAL):** SEAL is a notable prior work that combines visual search with LMMs for automatic localization. While effective, SEAL necessitates significant modifications to the LMM (vocabulary expansion, additional localization modules like object detection and heatmap generation) and requires extensive data collection and fine-tuning. This makes it less transferable and "general-purpose."

**DyFo's Unique Positioning:**
DyFo differentiates itself by proposing a **training-free** approach. Unlike SEAL, it does not require architectural changes, vocabulary expansion, or costly fine-tuning of the LMM itself. Instead, it leverages the complementary strengths of existing, powerful **visual expert models** (like Grounding DINO, Lang SAM, SAM) for precise visual grounding and LMMs for comprehensive semantic understanding. By orchestrating a bidirectional interaction between these two distinct types of models via a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithm, DyFo offers a "plug-and-play" solution. This positions DyFo as a highly adaptable and efficient method that can enhance the performance of general-purpose LMMs on fine-grained tasks without burdensome retraining, directly addressing the hallucination problem stemming from visual ambiguity or irrelevant context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind DyFo stems from observing the inherent limitations of current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) in handling fine-grained visual understanding tasks, especially in complex and cluttered scenes. Humans effortlessly perform "visual search" by dynamically adjusting their focus, efficiently filtering out irrelevant information to concentrate on critical details. LMMs, however, often struggle with this, leading to two primary issues:

1. **Loss of Detail (for fixed-resolution models):** Downsampling high-resolution images to fit fixed-resolution encoders (e.g., in LLaVA) results in the irreversible loss of crucial small objects or subtle attributes, making accurate fine-grained understanding impossible.
2. **Interference from Irrelevant Content (for dynamic-resolution models):** While dynamic-resolution models (e.g., Qwen2-VL) can process more visual tokens, simply feeding more raw visual data can introduce an overwhelming amount of irrelevant information. This "noise" can confuse the LMM, making it harder to discern salient features and, counterintuitively, leading to an *increase* in visual hallucinations.

Existing solutions, while attempting to improve fine-grained capabilities, typically come with significant drawbacks: they often require specific architectural modifications to the LMM, costly fine-tuning on new datasets (e.g., region-caption pairs), or the integration of specialized, trainable localization modules. This makes them less generalizable and more expensive to deploy.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of the DyFo research are:

- To propose **DyFo (Dynamic Focus)**, a novel visual search method that enables LMMs to dynamically focus on crucial visual regions, mimicking human-like visual attention.
- To ensure this method is **training-free**, meaning it does not require additional data collection, fine-tuning of the LMM, or architectural changes to the underlying LMM. This makes it an "out-of-the-box" solution.
- To achieve this by leveraging a **bidirectional interaction** between LMMs (for semantic understanding) and off-the-shelf visual experts (for precise visual localization), orchestrated by a **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)** algorithm.
- To demonstrate that DyFo can **significantly improve fine-grained visual understanding** across both fixed and dynamic resolution LMMs.
- To show that DyFo can **effectively reduce visual hallucination issues** in LMMs by enabling them to filter out irrelevant visual context and concentrate on task-relevant information.

In essence, DyFo aims to bridge the gap between an LMM's broad understanding and a visual expert's precise localization, all while maintaining the versatility and cost-effectiveness of a training-free approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DyFo operates on the principle of dynamic focusing, inspired by human visual search, to enable Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to selectively process critical visual information. The method is "training-free," meaning it does not require retraining or fine-tuning the LMM or introducing new trainable modules. It achieves this by orchestrating a collaborative interaction between an LMM and a separate visual expert model, guided by a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithm.

The methodology can be broken down into two main components: the **Focus Adjuster** and the **Focus Tree Search (FTS)**.

#### 3.1. Focus Adjuster

The Focus Adjuster is the core mechanism that facilitates the bidirectional interaction between the LMM and the visual expert. Its goal is to fuse the LMM's comprehensive semantic understanding with the visual expert's precise feature extraction capabilities.

- **Definition of Focus:** A "focus" is defined as a pair `f = (I, T)`, where `I` is a relevant image region selected from the original image `I_o`, and `T` is the primary semantic cue (textual description) associated with that region.
- **Iterative Focus Update:** The focus `f` is iteratively updated based on an external "action" `A_i` that guides adjustments:
 - `T_i+1 = L(f_i, A_i)`: The LMM `L` refines the textual focus based on the current focus `f_i` and the guiding action `A_i`. This means the LMM interprets the current visual context and the action to suggest what to "look for" next in terms of text.
 - `I_i+1 = E(T_i+1, I_i, A_i)`: The visual expert `E` then updates the image region `I` based on the refined text cue `T_i+1` and the action `A_i`. The visual expert effectively "finds" the visual region corresponding to the LMM's refined textual query.
 - `f_i+1 = (I_i+1, T_i+1)`: The new image region and refined text cue form the next focus state.

- **Action Space:** To simulate human focus adjustments, DyFo defines a compact action space:
 - **A1: Semantic Focus:** This action directs the system to identify contextually relevant visual targets based on semantic information (e.g., objects related to the query). It simulates the human tendency to zoom in on specific elements.
 - **A2: Semantic Scatter:** This action enlarges the current focused area. It prevents overly precise focusing from missing crucial, slightly out-of-focus information, mimicking the human eye's divergence to gather broader context if needed.

#### 3.2. Focus Tree Search (FTS)

FTS is a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithm that navigates through potential focus regions (represented as "focus nodes" in a tree structure). MCTS is well-suited for this task because it balances exploration (discovering new focus regions) and exploitation (refining promising focus regions) to efficiently explore the visual space.

- **Tree Structure:** Each node in the tree represents a focus state `f`. Edges connecting nodes correspond to the focus-shift actions (`A1` or `A2`) that transition to a new focus region.
- **State-Action Value Function (Q):** A `Q(f, a)` function estimates the expected future reward of performing action `a` in focus state `f`. This guides the search towards more promising paths.
- **Phases of MCTS:**
 - **Selection Phase:** Starting from the root (initial image and query), the algorithm iteratively selects the next node using the Upper Confidence bounds applied to Trees (UCT) formula. This formula balances the `Q` value (exploitation) with a term that encourages visiting less-explored nodes (exploration).
 - **Expansion Phase:** When a leaf node (unexplored path) is reached, new child nodes are added by randomly sampling unexplored actions.
 - **Backpropagation Phase:** Once a terminal state (e.g., maximum search count reached) is encountered, rewards are propagated back up the search path to update the `Q` values for all visited state-action pairs.
- **Reward Mechanism:** The reward `R_f_i` for a node `f_i` is crucial for guiding the search. It's designed to encourage:
 - **Consistency:** `I_{I=T}` is an indicator function (1 or 0) that checks if the image region `I` (found by the visual expert) is semantically consistent with the text `T` (generated/refined by the LMM). This ensures agreement between the two models.
 - **Efficiency/Conciseness:** `s_f_i / s_o` is the ratio of the effective area of the focused region `s_f_i` to the original image area `s_o`. This incentivizes the model to find the most compact and relevant region, filtering out irrelevant content.
 - The reward formula is `R_f_i = I_{I=T} * (s_f_i / s_o)`. A higher reward is given for smaller, more consistent focus regions.

#### 3.3. Multi-grained Voting

After the FTS constructs the focus tree, a "multi-grained voting" mechanism is employed to derive the final answer. Instead of relying on a single best path, this approach aggregates insights from multiple promising nodes within the tree.

- The final answer `A` is obtained through a weighted voting scheme. Each node `f` in the tree provides a prediction `L(f)`, and its weight in the voting process is its accumulated reward `R_f`.
- `A = arg max_L(f) (Sum_f∈Tf R_f * L(f))` where `T_f` is the focus tree. This ensures that predictions from high-reward (consistent and focused) nodes contribute more to the final decision.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

For practical implementation, DyFo uses **Lang-Segment-Anything** as the visual expert. This choice is noted for its efficiency in providing detection boxes and masks based on text inputs through batch processing, which is crucial for minimizing operational costs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate DyFo's effectiveness in enhancing LMMs' fine-grained visual understanding and mitigating hallucination across various benchmarks and LMM architectures.

#### 4.1. POPE Benchmark (Hallucination Reduction)

The POPE (Polling-based Object Probing Evaluation) benchmark assesses visual hallucination by presenting balanced queries about existent and non-existent objects, across three datasets (MSCOCO, A-OKVQA, GQA) and three negative sampling strategies (random, popular, adversarial).

- **Consistent Performance Improvement:** DyFo consistently improved the performance of both LLaVA-1.5 (a fixed-resolution model) and Qwen2-VL (a dynamic-resolution model) across all datasets and sampling settings. For instance, on MSCOCO, DyFo improved Qwen2-VL's F1 Score from 89.22 to 91.71 in the random setting, and LLaVA-1.5's from 89.58 to 91.46. Similar improvements were observed for A-OKVQA and GQA.
- **Superiority over Other LMMs:** When compared against various existing LMMs and methods (including InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, Ferret, Shikra, SEAL, and commercial chatbots), DyFo consistently outperformed them on the POPE-COCO benchmark across all sampling settings (random, popular, adversarial). For example, DyFo-Q (Qwen2-VL with DyFo) achieved an F1 Score of 91.71 (random) and 89.70 (popular), significantly higher than other models. This strong performance indicates that DyFo effectively filters irrelevant or misleading visual content, thereby reducing visual hallucinations.

#### 4.2. V* Bench (Fine-Grained Understanding in High-Resolution Images)

V* Bench is a challenging benchmark specifically designed to test LMMs on fine-grained detail recognition in high-resolution images, covering attribute recognition and spatial relationship reasoning.

- **Significant Gains on Challenging Tasks:** Most baseline LMMs perform poorly on this benchmark, often near random guess levels, highlighting the difficulty of the task. However, DyFo achieved substantial improvements. DyFo-Q (Qwen2-VL with DyFo) achieved an overall accuracy of 81.15%, remarkably surpassing SEAL's 75.39%. DyFo-L (LLaVA-1.5 with DyFo) also showed a considerable improvement to 59.16% overall.
- **Outperforming Fine-Tuned Baselines:** The fact that DyFo, a training-free method, outperforms SEAL (which requires specific architectural fine-tuning for visual search) underscores the efficiency and effectiveness of its approach in integrating visual experts with LMMs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Action Space:** The ablation study on action space design confirmed that combining both "Semantic Focus" (A1) and "Semantic Scatter" (A2) actions yielded the best performance on V* Bench for both LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2-VL. This validates the design of the human-inspired focus-shifting actions.
- **Impact of Visual Expert:** Comparisons with standalone visual experts on POPE-COCO showed that DyFo consistently surpassed the visual expert alone. This indicates that the collaboration mechanism allows the LMM to mitigate biases or limitations inherent in the visual expert, leading to mutual enhancement, not just leveraging the expert's capabilities.
- **Efficiency Study:**
 - **Search Space Design:** DyFo's "Dynamical Focus" space design required fewer search steps (3.20 on V* Bench) compared to the "Uniform Dividing Policy" (4.90) used by methods like SEAL. This suggests that DyFo's adaptive focusing strategy is more efficient and avoids ambiguous search steps.
 - **Search Algorithm:** Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) proved to be the most efficient search algorithm (3.20 search length) compared to A* (4.33), BFS (5.65), and DFS (6.15). The authors attribute MCTS's superiority to its reward function modeling, which allows it to gradually approximate the true reward distribution and avoid greedy traps, even when finding optimal heuristics for A* is challenging.

#### 4.4. Case Studies

Visual case studies further illustrate DyFo's practical advantages. In both low-resolution (POPE) and high-resolution (V* Bench) examples, DyFo dynamically adjusted the focal area (highlighted by red bounding boxes), allowing the LMM to correctly identify partially obscured, off-center, or tiny objects. This contrasts sharply with baseline LMMs (LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2-VL) which, without DyFo, often hallucinated or failed to detect the objects due to overwhelming irrelevant content or detail loss.

In summary, the results strongly support DyFo's ability to significantly improve LMM performance in fine-grained visual understanding and hallucination reduction through its training-free, dynamic focusing mechanism and effective LMM-visual expert collaboration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DyFo research offers several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact on the development and application of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs):

1. **Addresses a Core LMM Limitation (Hallucination and Fine-Grained Understanding):** Hallucination is a pervasive and critical issue in LMMs, undermining their trustworthiness and reliability, especially in real-world applications. DyFo directly tackles this by enabling LMMs to focus on relevant visual information and filter out distracting or irrelevant content. Its demonstrated improvements on benchmarks like POPE and V* Bench show a tangible step towards more accurate and dependable LMMs in fine-grained tasks.

2. **"Training-Free" and "Plug-and-Play" Paradigm:** This is arguably DyFo's most significant practical contribution. Unlike many existing approaches that demand extensive re-training, costly fine-tuning, or architectural modifications to LMMs, DyFo operates as an "out-of-the-box" solution. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for developers and researchers, allowing them to enhance the capabilities of pre-trained LMMs without incurring prohibitive computational costs or requiring specialized expertise in model fine-tuning. This greatly expands the applicability of LMMs to a wider range of high-resolution, detail-oriented vision tasks.

3. **Effective Synergy Between LMMs and Visual Experts:** DyFo provides a compelling blueprint for how to combine the strengths of different AI paradigms. It intelligently leverages the LMM's robust semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities with the visual expert's unparalleled precision in localization and segmentation. The mutual enhancement observed in ablation studies (where DyFo outperforms standalone experts) validates this collaborative approach, suggesting that LMMs can even help mitigate biases or limitations inherent in visual experts. This paradigm could inspire similar integration strategies for other specialized AI models with general-purpose LMMs.

4. **Cognitive Inspiration for AI:** The method's foundation in human visual search and dynamic focus adjustments, simulated through MCTS and a tailored action space, represents a valuable step towards building more cognitively-plausible AI systems. This bio-inspired approach can lead to more robust and efficient visual processing in AI, making them more adept at navigating complex visual scenes akin to human perception.

5. **Broad Applicability:** DyFo is shown to work effectively with both fixed-resolution (e.g., LLaVA-1.5) and dynamic-resolution (e.g., Qwen2-VL) LMMs. This versatility ensures that its benefits are not limited to a specific LMM architecture but can be broadly applied across the evolving landscape of multimodal models.

6. **Efficiency in Search:** The efficiency study demonstrates that DyFo's dynamic focus search space and the use of MCTS lead to more efficient exploration compared to uniform division or other search algorithms. This means that the enhanced performance is achieved without disproportionately increasing inference time or computational overhead, making it practical for deployment.

In conclusion, DyFo provides a practical, efficient, and highly effective solution for improving the fine-grained visual understanding and reducing hallucinations in LMMs. Its training-free nature and intelligent integration of visual experts position it as a significant advancement that could accelerate the deployment of more reliable and capable multimodal AI systems in various real-world applications, from intelligent assistants to complex visual analysis platforms.
