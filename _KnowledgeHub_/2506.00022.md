# Scaling Physical Reasoning with the PHYSICS Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces the PHYSICS dataset, a large-scale collection of 16,568 bilingual physics questions, and a Rule+Model hybrid evaluation framework to address limitations in current LLMs' physical reasoning. This work provides a more accurate assessment tool and demonstrates that fine-tuning models with PHYSICS leads to improved performance on physics, mathematical, and general reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) demonstrate significant progress in domains like mathematics and coding, but show limited understanding and application of physical principles.
- Existing physics datasets for LLMs suffer from a lack of high-quality training data, imbalanced test data distribution across difficulty levels and subject areas, and insufficient scale.
- Current LLM evaluation frameworks, largely adapted from mathematics, are inadequate for physics-specific challenges such as unit conversion, numerical simplification, and physical equivalence.

## Method

- Constructed the **PHYSICS dataset**, comprising 16,568 high-quality physics questions and answers, collected from textbooks and exercise books, annotated for language, difficulty, subject, and answer type.
- Developed a robust data collection and quality control pipeline using GPT-4o for extraction, error correction, and bilingual translation, complemented by expert review and n-gram leakage detection.
- Designed a Rule+Model hybrid evaluation framework that combines rule-based verification (augmented with unit conversion rules) with a fine-tuned model-verifier (physics-xVerify) to accurately assess physics problem solutions.

## Results

- The PHYSICS dataset was compiled with **16,568** bilingual (English/Chinese) questions (**2,000** test samples) balanced across five subfields and four difficulty levels, and the Rule+Model evaluation framework improved accuracy by 12.58% over existing verification methods.
- LLM evaluations showed that even leading proprietary and open-source models achieved accuracies of only 50-60% on the PHYSICS test set, with specialized reasoning models generally outperforming general chat models.
- Fine-tuning models like Qwen2.5, Llama3.x, and Mistral with the PHYSICS dataset resulted in improved performance on physics-specific benchmarks (e.g., GPQA, OlympiadBench), as well as gains in mathematical (e.g., AIME-2025) and general reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strong mathematical reasoning in LLMs does not directly translate to equally strong physical reasoning (top-performing models achieve around 50-60% accuracy on physics benchmarks), with error analysis revealing that knowledge deficits, particularly conceptual and modeling errors, constitute the majority of failures.
> - The Rule+Model hybrid evaluation framework significantly enhances evaluation accuracy for physics problems (over 20% improvement) compared to purely rule-based methods, addressing physics-specific nuances.
> - Supervised fine-tuning with the PHYSICS dataset leads to notable performance improvements across various physics, mathematical, and general reasoning benchmarks, indicating a positive transfer of learned skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated substantial progress in complex reasoning domains such as mathematics and coding, with some models achieving or exceeding human expert performance on challenging benchmarks. However, the application of LLMs to physics, a fundamental natural science requiring intensive reasoning, has received comparatively limited attention. This has resulted in observed limitations in current models' understanding and application of physical principles, which is considered crucial for AI systems to accurately model and interact with the real world.

Existing efforts to integrate physics into LLM evaluation and training have encountered several challenges. Prior physics datasets are frequently characterized by a lack of high-quality training data, imbalanced test data distribution (covering narrow difficulty levels or subject areas), and insufficient scale. Multi-discipline datasets typically include only a small proportion of physics content, while existing physics-specific datasets often suffer from quality control issues, limited quantity, and an absence of dedicated training-test splits.

Furthermore, current evaluation frameworks for LLMs primarily borrow metrics from mathematics, which are not adequately equipped to handle physics-specific evaluation challenges such as unit conversion, numerical simplification, and physical equivalence. These limitations lead to biased assessments of model performance and hinder targeted improvements in physical reasoning capabilities.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing a large-scale, high-quality, and comprehensively structured physics dataset, accompanied by a specialized evaluation framework. It positions itself as an effort to provide foundational resources necessary to advance LLMs' capabilities in physics understanding and reasoning, thereby aligning AI with the physical nature of reality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from the identified deficiencies in current large language models concerning physical reasoning. These include:

- **Addressing the scarcity of high-quality physics training data:** A significant motivation is the observed lack of comprehensive and high-quality datasets specifically designed for training LLMs in physics. Existing datasets are often limited in scope, quality, or quantity, hindering the effective enhancement of models' abilities in this domain. The authors aim to overcome this by creating a large-scale dataset that can facilitate the development of sophisticated physics understanding and reasoning skills in LLMs.
- **Improving the diversity and balance of physics test data:** Current physics test sets are frequently criticized for their narrow coverage of difficulty levels and subject areas. This limitation restricts the discriminability and diversity of evaluations, preventing a thorough assessment of a model's physics capabilities across a broad spectrum of topics and skill requirements. A key objective is to construct a test set with balanced distributions across difficulty and subject domains to enable more comprehensive and accurate evaluations.
- **Developing a dedicated evaluation framework for physics problems:** The authors identify that existing evaluation frameworks, largely adapted from mathematics, are inadequate for physics tasks. Physics problems introduce unique challenges such as unit conversion, numerical simplification, and the recognition of physically equivalent expressions, which current methods struggle to handle. The motivation here is to design a specialized, hybrid evaluation framework that balances accuracy and efficiency, specifically tailored to these characteristics of physics problems. This framework is intended to provide a more precise assessment of model performance and guide future model improvements.
- **Benchmarking current LLM performance in physics:** The research is motivated by the need to quantify the current state of LLMs' physical reasoning capabilities. By conducting extensive evaluations on both open-source and proprietary models using the new dataset and evaluation framework, the authors aim to highlight existing limitations and identify areas requiring further development. This benchmarking serves to establish a baseline and inform future research directions in physics-centric AI.
- **Facilitating the advancement of LLMs in physics:** Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to accelerate the progress of LLMs in physics. By providing a robust dataset for training and evaluation, along with an optimized evaluation methodology, the authors intend to equip the academic and industrial communities with the tools necessary to develop LLMs that can effectively understand, apply, and assist in physics-related real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the PHYSICS dataset and a novel evaluation framework, employing a structured approach for both.

#### 3.1. PHYSICS Dataset Construction

The PHYSICS dataset is designed to be large-scale, high-quality, and cover a broad range of physics subjects and difficulty levels.

#### 3.1.1. Data Collection

The dataset compilation process involved three main stages:
- **PDF to Markdown Conversion:** Physics textbooks and exercise books in PDF format were initially processed using Optical Character Recognition (OCR) tools to convert them into Markdown files. This step facilitated text-based manipulation.
- **Question Extraction:** A sliding window approach was applied to traverse the Markdown documents. GPT-4o was utilized to extract questions, answers, and complete question-answer pairs. To mitigate hallucination and ensure fidelity to the source material, each extracted pair was cross-referenced with its original location in the document.
- **Matching Questions and Answers:** For source materials where questions and answers were presented in separate sections, metadata such as chapter numbers, problem indices, and formatting patterns were leveraged to accurately associate questions with their corresponding solutions.

#### 3.1.2. Quality Control

To ensure the reliability and integrity of the collected data, several quality control measures were implemented:
- **OCR Error Correction:** GPT-4o was employed for contextual understanding to detect and rectify errors arising from OCR processing, such as misrecognized digits or mathematical expressions.
- **Data Filtering:** Multi-modal data requiring visual input (e.g., images), questions heavily dependent on external textual context, and unmatchable question-answer pairs were systematically removed.
- **Expert Review:** Human experts conducted a final review to identify and correct any remaining issues, including incorrect extractions, mismatched pairs, or erroneous answers, ensuring high data quality. After this process, 8,284 high-quality physics questions and answers were obtained.
- **Bilingual Translation:** All problems were translated between English and Chinese using GPT-4o. The translation quality was assessed using GEMBA-MQM (LLM-based) and MQM (human expert review), with retranslation or manual correction for low-quality outputs. This process doubled the dataset size to 16,568 questions.

#### 3.1.3. Data Annotation

The collected data was annotated across multiple dimensions for effective utilization:
- **Language:** Problems were categorized as English or Chinese based on their source.
- **Difficulty Level:** Questions were classified into four levels: High School, High School Olympiad, Undergraduate (Non-Physics Major), and Undergraduate/Postgraduate (Physics Major). This classification primarily relied on data sources, supplemented by expert review.
- **Subject Classification:** Each question was assigned to one of five physics subfields: Modern Physics, Mechanics, Electromagnetism, Thermodynamics, and Optics. Labeling was primarily source-based, with LLM assistance and expert annotation for ambiguous cases.
- **Answer Types:** Seven distinct answer types were identified: Interval, Expression, Equation, True/False, Multiple Choice, Numerical Value, and Open-End.

#### 3.1.4. Data Split

The dataset was divided into training and test sets to support model development and evaluation:
- **Test Set:** 2,000 samples were carefully selected for the test set, ensuring a balanced distribution across subjects and difficulty levels.
- **Training Set:** The remaining 14,568 samples were allocated for training. For 4,000 samples within the training set, detailed reasoning paths generated by the QwQ-32B reasoning model were provided to facilitate supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of other models. Translation pairs were maintained within the same set to prevent information leakage.

#### 3.1.5. Data Leakage Detection

To prevent potential data leakage from influencing evaluation results, n-gram matching was employed. This method involved randomly selecting 5-gram positions from each test sample and checking if a model's predicted 5-gram matched the actual 5-gram, indicating potential contamination. The results for several LLMs indicated minimal data leakage, suggesting negligible impact on evaluation validity.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Framework

The authors developed a specialized Rule+Model hybrid evaluation framework to address the limitations of existing methods in handling physics-specific challenges.

#### 3.2.1. Identified Challenges

Existing mathematical evaluation frameworks were found to exhibit biases due to:
- **Unit Conversion:** Inability to properly handle dimensional physical units.
- **Simplification and Approximation:** Lack of capability to recognize various forms of numerical simplification and approximation common in physics.

#### 3.2.2. Rule+Model Framework

The proposed framework combines rule-based and model-based approaches to balance accuracy and efficiency (Algorithm 1):
1. **Rule-based Verification:** An initial assessment is performed using predefined rules.
2. **Model-based Verification:** If the rule-based check deems the answer incorrect, a fine-tuned model-verifier performs a second check. An answer is considered incorrect only if both methods judge it as wrong.

#### 3.2.3. Physics-specific Optimization

- **Rule-based Component:** The `math-verify` library was adapted as the base rule-verifier, augmented with a set of pre-defined unit conversion rules.
- **Model-based Component:** The `xVerify-8B-I` model, originally for mathematical reasoning judgment, was fine-tuned to create `physics-xVerify`. This involved constructing a training dataset by using GPT-4o to generate multiple equivalent forms of physics answers, followed by human verification for accuracy and diversity. This fine-tuned model demonstrated a 12.58% accuracy improvement over existing methods on a 2,000-sample human-annotated test set, with acceptable time costs, confirming its effectiveness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's experimental results and analyses provide insights into the current state of LLM performance in physics reasoning and the impact of the proposed dataset and evaluation framework.

#### 4.1. LLM Performance Evaluation (Test Set)

Evaluations were conducted on a diverse set of closed-source and open-source models, categorized into reasoning and chat models, across English and Chinese questions, and five physics domains.

- **Overall Performance Limitations:** Even the top-performing models, such as OpenAI-o3 (high) with an accuracy of 58.90% and DeepSeek-R1 at 55.30%, exhibited significant limitations. These scores are considerably lower than their reported performance on challenging mathematics tasks (e.g., AIME2025), indicating that strong mathematical reasoning does not directly translate to equally strong complex physics reasoning.
- **Subject-Specific Strengths and Weaknesses:** Models generally showed poorer performance on Thermodynamics compared to Mechanics and Electromagnetism. This disparity is attributed to potential differences in inherent subject difficulty and imbalanced distribution of physics content in pre-training datasets.
- **Open-source vs. Closed-source Models:** While closed-source models generally maintained an overall lead (e.g., OpenAI-o3), open-source models, particularly DeepSeek-R1, demonstrated competitive performance, surpassing some closed-source counterparts like Gemini-2.5-Pro. This indicates a closing performance gap.
- **Reasoning Models Outperform Chat Models:** A consistent trend observed was that specialized reasoning models (e.g., o3, DeepSeek-R1) outperformed general chat models (e.g., GPT-4.1, DeepSeek-V3) within the same generation or model family, highlighting the importance of explicit reasoning capabilities.
- **Difficulty Level Correlation:** Model accuracy generally decreased with increasing problem difficulty. An observation was that models performed worse on High School Olympiad problems than on undergraduate non-physics-major questions. This was hypothesized to be due to Olympiad problems demanding strong reasoning skills, which models often lack, despite undergraduate problems requiring broader knowledge bases that models may partially possess.
- **Language Impact:** Models exhibited varying performance across English and Chinese questions. Smaller models, such as DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMa-7B, showed larger performance disparities between languages, suggesting potential limitations in multilingual capacity or training data imbalances.
- **Effectiveness of Hybrid Verification:** The Rule+Model hybrid evaluation framework consistently yielded significantly higher accuracy scores (often exceeding 20% improvement) compared to the rule-based verifier alone, underscoring its importance for accurate physics problem evaluation.

#### 4.2. Training Results and Analysis

Models of various parameter sizes (Qwen2.5, Llama3.x, Mistral series) were fine-tuned using the PHYSICS training dataset.

- **Improved Physics Performance:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with the PHYSICS dataset led to notable improvements in model performance across various physics benchmarks, including GPQA, OlympiadBench, UGPhysics, and the PHYSICS test set. This indicates that the dataset's comprehensive coverage of sub-disciplines and difficulty levels effectively strengthens models' physics problem-solving abilities. The PHYSICS-SFT models also outperformed other reasoning enhancement methods on physics-specific datasets.
- **Math Performance Gains:** The physics-based training also resulted in improvements in mathematical reasoning capabilities, as evidenced by gains on MATH500 and AIME-2025. This suggests a transfer of learned reasoning skills from physics contexts to general mathematical problems.
- **General Domain Performance Gains:** Fine-tuning with the PHYSICS data enhanced models' general reasoning abilities, with observed improvements on benchmarks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro. Larger models tended to show greater improvements, indicating better capacity for learning complex patterns and generalization.

#### 4.3. Error Analysis

A manual inspection of 100 incorrect answers from Grok 3 (Think) categorized errors into two main types:

- **Knowledge Deficit:** Comprising Conceptual Errors (incorrect understanding or application of fundamental physics concepts) and Modeling Errors (issues like mixing ideal/non-ideal conditions or ignoring constraints). This category accounted for nearly 60% of all errors, suggesting a persistent lack of sufficient domain-specific physics knowledge in advanced LLMs.
- **Reasoning Flaw:** Including Comprehension Misunderstanding (misinterpreting questions or context) and Computational Errors (arithmetic mistakes or incorrect formula application). These errors highlight challenges in language understanding, consistent long-chain reasoning, and complex operation handling.

The analysis concludes that physics reasoning is inherently more complex than mathematical deduction, requiring additional domain-specific knowledge and real-world grounding, posing significant challenges for LLM advancement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of artificial intelligence by addressing recognized limitations in large language models' capabilities for physical reasoning. The introduction of the PHYSICS dataset and the Rule+Model evaluation framework carries several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Advancement of LLM Capabilities:** By providing a large-scale, high-quality, and comprehensively annotated dataset, this work offers a critical resource for training LLMs to develop more robust physics understanding and reasoning skills. The empirical results demonstrate that fine-tuning with the PHYSICS dataset significantly enhances model performance across diverse physics benchmarks, and even shows positive transfer to mathematical and general reasoning tasks. This suggests a pathway for extending the general intelligence of LLMs to encompass a deeper understanding of the physical world.
- **Robust and Accurate Evaluation:** The proposed Rule+Model evaluation framework specifically designed for physics problems addresses the unique challenges of unit conversion, numerical simplification, and physical equivalence. This framework provides a more accurate and nuanced assessment of LLM performance, moving beyond the biases of existing math-centric metrics. Its adoption could lead to more reliable benchmarking and clearer guidance for model development, fostering more effective iteration in research.
- **Identification of Research Gaps:** The extensive evaluation of state-of-the-art LLMs, both open-source and proprietary, clearly highlights the current limitations in physics reasoning. The detailed error analysis further categorizes these shortcomings into knowledge deficits and reasoning flaws, providing specific targets for future research and model improvements. This systematic identification of weaknesses can accelerate focused efforts to enhance domain-specific knowledge integration and refine complex reasoning architectures.
- **Foundation for Multimodal Physics AI:** While the current dataset is text-based, the authors acknowledge the prevalence of multimodal problems in physics and plan for a future iteration incorporating visual information. This foundational text-based dataset and evaluation framework lay groundwork for future developments in multimodal AI systems that can interpret and reason about physics problems involving diagrams, graphs, and simulations.
- **Impact on Science Education and Research Assistance:** LLMs with enhanced physical reasoning could have practical applications in science education, providing more accurate explanations, solving complex problems, and assisting students. In scientific research, such models could potentially aid in hypothesis generation, experimental design, and data analysis by leveraging a deeper understanding of physical principles.
- **Promoting Interdisciplinary AI Research:** The observed transfer of skills between physics and mathematics, and the improvements in general reasoning, underscore the interconnectedness of these domains. This work encourages interdisciplinary research that leverages insights from various scientific fields to build more universally capable AI systems.

In conclusion, this research provides essential tools and insights for advancing LLMs in the domain of physics. It sets a new benchmark for dataset quality and evaluation rigor, laying a foundation for future breakthroughs in AI's ability to comprehend and interact with the physical world.
