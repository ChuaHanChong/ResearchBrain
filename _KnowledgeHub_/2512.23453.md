# CoFi-Dec: Hallucination-Resistant Decoding via Coarse-to-Fine Generative Feedback in Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CoFi-Dec introduces a training-free decoding framework for Large Vision-Language Models, leveraging coarse-to-fine generative self-feedback and a Wasserstein-based fusion mechanism to reduce visual hallucinations. The approach consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple hallucination benchmarks, enhancing factual consistency and visual grounding of generated content.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate content inconsistent with visual input, known as hallucinations, limiting their trustworthiness and real-world applicability.
- Existing training-time hallucination mitigation methods are resource-intensive and unscalable for rapidly evolving foundation models.
- Prior decoding-time strategies often process visual information at a single resolution and apply feedback post-hoc, lacking fine-grained, step-by-step integration during token generation.

## Method

- The framework performs ==hierarchical visual decomposition==, generating ==coarse-grained== (downsampled) and ==fine-grained== (cropped salient regions) views of the input image.
- It employs a ==text-to-image generative model== to synthesize ==pseudo-images== from initial textual hypotheses derived from both coarse and fine visual conditions, creating =='generative self-feedback'==.
- A ==Wasserstein barycenter optimization== is used to fuse the ==conditional probability distributions== from the original image and the synthesized multi-granular pseudo-images, guiding token generation at each step.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art accuracy, precision, and F1 scores on the POPE benchmark for object hallucination, demonstrating robustness against challenging negative sampling strategies.
- Significantly reduced CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I scores on the CHAIR benchmark, indicating a substantial decrease in subject and instance hallucinations in free-form image captions.
- Outperformed competitors on MME-Hallucination and MMBench, showing improved object-level and attribute-level hallucination mitigation, alongside gains in general multimodal reasoning accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mimicking human coarse-to-fine visual perception by integrating multi-granular feedback during autoregressive decoding significantly improves visual grounding.
> - Leveraging text-to-image generative models to create 'pseudo-visual' feedback from textual hypotheses offers an effective self-verification mechanism for LVLMs.
> - A principled Wasserstein-based fusion across diverse semantic probabilities leads to more robust and accurate token predictions by respecting the underlying semantic space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs): the phenomenon of "hallucination." LVLMs, such such as LLaVA, InstructBLIP, and Qwen-VL, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in multi-modal understanding and generation, driving progress in tasks like image captioning and visual question answering. However, a persistent and significant limitation is their tendency to generate content that is inconsistent with or entirely fabricated from the visual input. This unfaithfulness, or hallucination, severely undermines the trustworthiness and reliability of LVLMs, thereby limiting their deployment in real-world, safety-critical applications.

Existing efforts to mitigate hallucinations broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Training-time interventions:** These approaches involve modifying the model architecture, fine-tuning with additional annotated data, or using enhanced training schemes (e.g., reinforcement learning with human feedback, adversarial training). While effective, these methods are often resource-intensive, requiring extensive retraining, substantial computational power, and large, carefully curated datasets. This high overhead limits their scalability and practicality for widespread adoption with continuously evolving foundation models.
2. **Decoding-time strategies:** A newer and increasingly popular class of approaches focuses on interventions during the inference phase, without altering the pre-trained model weights. These "training-free" or "zero-tuning" methods offer a more efficient and generalizable pathway to combat hallucinations. Within this category, several sub-approaches exist:
 - **Contrastive decoding:** Suppresses hallucinations by contrasting token predictions generated under faithful visual input against those from biased or weakened conditions. Examples include VCD and M3ID.
 - **Guided decoding:** Injects auxiliary signals directly into the token generation loop, aiming to steer the model towards more grounded outputs.
 - **Feedback-based decoding:** Explores revising or verifying model predictions by incorporating auxiliary signals, often in the form of retrieved factual knowledge or, more recently, synthetic images generated from textual hypotheses. This is the most direct lineage for CoFi-Dec. DeGF (Generative Feedback) is a recent example of this.

CoFi-Dec positions itself as an advancement within the decoding-time, feedback-based strategy. The authors identify three key limitations of prior feedback methods:
1. **Single-resolution visual processing:** Most existing methods rely solely on the original image, overlooking the inherent multi-scale nature of visual information. This can lead to missed inconsistencies at different semantic levels (e.g., global layout vs. object details).
2. **Post-hoc feedback application:** Generative feedback is typically used after initial generation for re-ranking or answer replacement, having a weak influence on the token-level generation process itself.
3. **Lack of fine-grained, step-by-step integration:** Feedback is rarely incorporated dynamically during the cumulative token generation, limiting continuous refinement.

CoFi-Dec directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel framework that integrates generative self-feedback with coarse-to-fine visual conditioning *during* the autoregressive decoding process. By mimicking human visual perception (coarse scan followed by detailed inspection) and employing a principled fusion mechanism, CoFi-Dec extends the state-of-the-art in training-free hallucination mitigation, aiming for more robust, faithful, and visually grounded LVLM outputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **mitigate hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs)**, thereby enhancing their reliability and trustworthiness for real-world applications. The authors aim to develop a novel, efficient, and effective decoding framework that reduces the generation of content inconsistent with visual input.

The motivation behind CoFi-Dec stems from several critical observations and identified gaps in the current landscape of LVLM research:

1. **Prevalence and Risk of Hallucinations:** Despite their impressive advancements in multimodal understanding and generation, LVLMs frequently produce "hallucinated content." This behavior is not merely an inconvenience but poses serious risks of misinformation, especially in sensitive domains, and severely limits the applicability of these powerful models in safety-critical or real-world scenarios. The inherent unreliability due to hallucinations is a major barrier to wider adoption.
2. **Limitations of Existing Mitigation Strategies:**
 - **Cost of Training-time Interventions:** Many effective hallucination suppression methods require extensive retraining, fine-tuning, or large amounts of annotated data. These dependencies incur significant computational and data annotation overheads, making them unscalable and impractical for rapidly evolving and increasingly massive LVLMs.
 - **Ineffectiveness of Current Decoding-time Strategies:** While decoding-time strategies offer a training-free alternative, existing feedback-based methods suffer from fundamental shortcomings. They often process visual information at a single scale, which can lead to overlooking critical nuances or inconsistencies that manifest at different granularities (e.g., global scene versus local object details). Furthermore, generative feedback in these methods is typically applied in a post-hoc manner, merely serving as a reference for re-ranking or replacement, rather than actively guiding the token-level generation process in a fine-grained, step-by-step fashion.
3. **Inspiration from Human Visual Cognition:** The authors draw inspiration from the human visual system, which naturally processes visual information in a coarse-to-fine manner—first perceiving the global scene and then inspecting specific details. This cognitive strategy suggests that integrating multi-scale visual conditioning could be a more robust approach to visual grounding for LVLMs.
4. **Need for a Generalizable and Practical Solution:** There is a clear need for a hallucination mitigation framework that is **training-free**, **model-agnostic**, and **modular**. Such a framework could be seamlessly applied to a wide range of pre-trained LVLMs, enabling immediate improvements in their fidelity without the burden of extensive modifications or retraining.

By addressing these motivations, CoFi-Dec seeks to provide a principled, efficient, and cognitively inspired method to enhance the factual consistency and visual grounding of LVLM outputs, thereby significantly improving their trustworthiness and utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoFi-Dec is proposed as a novel, training-free decoding framework that integrates generative self-feedback with coarse-to-fine visual conditioning to mitigate hallucinations in LVLMs. The methodology is inspired by human visual processing, moving from global scene perception to detailed inspection, and dynamically applies this principle during token generation.

The approach can be broken down into three core stages:

1. **Generative Feedback with Multi-Granular Conditioning:**
 - **Hierarchical Visual Decomposition:** Given an input image ($I_0$) and a textual prompt ($T$), the framework first decomposes the image into multiple granularities to capture both global context and fine details.
 - **Coarse-grained views ($I_c$):** The original image is uniformly downsampled into low-resolution patches. This captures the global spatial structure and contextual semantics, akin to a quick, overall scan.
 - **Fine-grained views ($I_f$):** High-resolution crops are extracted, focusing on salient or ambiguous regions. These regions can be identified using learned attention maps or region proposal algorithms, providing localized discriminative features often missed by coarse processing.
 - **Initial and Conditional Response Generation:**
 - An initial textual response ($R_0$) is generated by the LVLM conditioned on the original image ($I_0$) and the prompt ($T$).
 - Two additional, independent textual responses are generated: $R_c$ (conditioned on coarse-grained views $I_c$ and $T$) and $R_f$ (conditioned on fine-grained views $I_f$ and $T$). Each of these serves as a semantic hypothesis derived from a different "perceptual lens."
 - **Generative Self-Verification (Pseudo-Image Synthesis):** To convert these textual hypotheses into actionable visual feedback, a text-to-image generative vision model (e.g., Stable Diffusion, denoted $G$) is employed. This model synthesizes two pseudo-images: $v_c$ from $R_c$ and $v_f$ from $R_f$. These synthesized images represent the model's "self-imagined reflections" of its own beliefs at different levels of abstraction, enriching the grounding cues.

2. **Self-Correcting Decoding with Generative Feedback (Token-level Integration):**
 - Unlike previous methods that use feedback post-hoc, CoFi-Dec integrates these multi-granular visual cues directly into the autoregressive decoding loop for each generated token.
 - At every decoding timestep $t$, the LVLM is prompted to generate the next token ($y_t$) under three distinct visual conditions:
 - The original image embedding ($v$).
 - The synthesized coarse-grained pseudo-image embedding ($v_c$).
 - The synthesized fine-grained pseudo-image embedding ($v_f$).
 - This yields three conditional probability distributions over the vocabulary for the next token: $P(y_t|v, \text{x}, y_{<t})$, $P(y_t|v_c, \text{x}, y_{<t})$, and $P(y_t|v_f, \text{x}, y_{<t})$.

3. **Wasserstein-based Fusion Mechanism:**
 - To obtain a unified, robust prediction that reconciles potential discrepancies and leverages the strengths of each perspective, CoFi-Dec employs a **Wasserstein barycenter optimization**.
 - Instead of simple averaging, this method computes a "geometry-aware consensus" across the semantic token space. The Wasserstein distance, also known as Earth Mover's Distance, measures the minimum "cost" to transform one probability distribution into another.
 - The barycenter is the distribution that minimizes the sum of Wasserstein distances to all three input distributions ($P_{(v)t}$, $P_{(c)t}$, $P_{(f)t}$). This principled fusion ensures that the resulting fused distribution ($P_{(\text{fused})t}$) is not only probabilistically valid but also geometrically faithful to the underlying structure of the token space.
 - This fused distribution is then used for token selection during generation, allowing the model to adaptively weigh global context, structural composition, and localized evidence at each step, leading to more faithful and grounded outputs.

The entire framework is designed to be **training-free, modular, and compatible** with a wide range of pre-trained LVLMs, making it highly practical for real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation of CoFi-Dec across six hallucination-focused benchmarks consistently demonstrates its superior performance in mitigating hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). The core findings highlight the framework's effectiveness in enhancing visual grounding and factual consistency.

1. **POPE Benchmark (Object Hallucination):** CoFi-Dec achieved state-of-the-art results on the POPE benchmark, which specifically targets object-level hallucinations. Across three LVLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) and three negative sampling strategies (Random, Popular, Adversarial), CoFi-Dec consistently yielded the highest accuracy, precision, and F1 scores. A notable finding was its robustness in the more challenging "Popular" and "Adversarial" settings, where it experienced significantly smaller performance drops compared to baselines. This indicates CoFi-Dec's enhanced ability to discern non-existent objects and resist context-driven hallucinations. Its consistently higher precision also suggests a more cautious and reliable response pattern, minimizing false positives.

2. **CHAIR Benchmark (Free-form Image Captioning Hallucination):** For open-ended image captioning, CoFi-Dec significantly outperformed all tested baselines on the CHAIR benchmark. It achieved lower CHAIR$_S$ and CHAIR$_I$ scores (where lower is better, indicating fewer subject and instance hallucinations), demonstrating a substantial reduction in hallucinated objects within free-form captions. Furthermore, CoFi-Dec generated more informative responses, reflected by higher recall values and longer average output lengths, indicating an improved ability to ground descriptions in visual content while providing richer detail.

3. **MME-Hallucination and MMBench (Object and Attribute Hallucination & General Multimodal Reasoning):** On the MME-Hallucination benchmark, designed to evaluate both object-level and attribute-level hallucinations, CoFi-Dec consistently surpassed competitors. It achieved notable improvements in the total MME score and secured the highest performance in six out of eight subcategories (Existence, Count, Position, Color). The significant gains in the "Color" subset highlight its capacity for accurate interpretation of subtle visual attributes, indicating strong fine-grained hallucination mitigation. On the broader MMBench benchmark, CoFi-Dec also showed an increase in overall accuracy, suggesting that improving hallucination resistance contributes to better general multimodal reasoning capabilities.

4. **MMVP (Fine-grained Visual Discrimination):** Experiments on the MMVP benchmark, which assesses fine-grained visual discrimination, further reinforced CoFi-Dec's strengths. It elevated the performance of LLaVA-1.5 significantly, demonstrating improved precision in visual recognition tasks that require distinguishing subtle differences between visually similar images.

5. **LLaVA-Bench (Qualitative and GPT-4V-aided Evaluation):** Qualitative case studies using the LLaVA-Bench, augmented by GPT-4V for evaluation, provided compelling evidence. While standard decoding often produced vague or hallucinated descriptions, CoFi-Dec consistently generated more grounded, accurate, and informative outputs, precisely identifying elements like specific landforms, infrastructure, and vegetation. This human-like (or strong AI-aided) evaluation underscores the practical improvements in response quality.

6. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Coarse-to-Fine Reasoning:** The ablation studies confirmed that combining both fine-grained and coarse-grained visual responses ("Fine-grained - Coarse Response") consistently outperformed using either alone. This validates the effectiveness of the multi-level semantic aggregation strategy in improving factual grounding and descriptive accuracy.
 - **Wasserstein Fusion:** Removing the Wasserstein fusion module led to noticeable drops in performance across all evaluated metrics. This critical finding confirms the indispensable role of the principled Wasserstein-based fusion strategy in consolidating diverse semantic cues into a unified and accurate token-level prediction.
 - **Generative Model Choice:** The performance of CoFi-Dec remained stable across various versions of Stable Diffusion used for generative feedback (SD-v1.1, SD-v1.5, SD-v2.1, SD-XL-v0.9, SD-XL-v1.0). While SD-XL-v1.0 offered marginal improvements, SD-v1.5 was chosen as the default for its superior efficiency, demonstrating the framework's adaptability.

7. **Efficiency Comparison:** While CoFi-Dec requires multiple forward passes and a text-to-image generation step, making it less efficient than simple regular or contrastive decoding (approx. 6.05x latency and 1.28x GPU memory overhead), it remains more efficient than other computationally intensive hallucination mitigation methods like OPERA and HALC. The authors justify this overhead by the substantial and consistent improvements in hallucination rates across all benchmarks.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that CoFi-Dec offers a robust and effective solution for mitigating various types of hallucinations in LVLMs by dynamically integrating multi-granular visual feedback through a principled fusion mechanism during the decoding process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of CoFi-Dec carries significant implications for the reliability and practical application of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). Its contributions extend beyond incremental improvements, offering a novel paradigm for enhancing the trustworthiness of multimodal AI.

1. **Addressing a Critical Reliability Issue:** Hallucination is a pervasive and debilitating problem for LVLMs, directly hindering their deployment in sensitive and high-stakes environments. CoFi-Dec directly tackles this fundamental flaw, offering a robust solution that substantially reduces both object-level and semantic-level inconsistencies. By improving the factual accuracy and visual grounding of LVLM outputs, CoFi-Dec takes a crucial step towards making these powerful models more dependable.

2. **Novel and Cognitively Inspired Approach:** The framework's inspiration from human visual processing—the coarse-to-fine inspection strategy—is a significant conceptual contribution. This biologically plausible approach, translating into hierarchical visual decomposition and dynamic feedback, allows LVLMs to mimic a more robust form of perception. This multi-granular reasoning, coupled with generative self-verification, represents a departure from single-scale or post-hoc correction methods, providing a more deeply integrated and effective mechanism for grounding.

3. **Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Design:** One of the most impactful aspects of CoFi-Dec is its training-free and model-agnostic nature. This means it can be seamlessly applied to a wide array of existing, pre-trained LVLMs without requiring expensive retraining, fine-tuning, or architectural modifications. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for improving hallucination resistance, making advanced reliability features accessible to a broader range of developers and practitioners. Its modularity ensures compatibility and ease of integration, accelerating the adoption of more robust LVLMs.

4. **Principled Fusion Mechanism:** The introduction of a Wasserstein-based barycenter for fusing conditional token distributions is a significant technical advancement. Unlike simpler averaging schemes, this method respects the underlying semantic geometry of the vocabulary space, leading to more coherent, smooth, and semantically meaningful corrections during decoding. This principled approach ensures that the integration of diverse visual cues is mathematically sound and contributes to the overall robustness of the generated output.

5. **Broad Applicability and Performance Consistency:** CoFi-Dec's demonstrated effectiveness across numerous benchmarks (POPE, CHAIR, MME-Hallucination, MMBench, MMVP, LLaVA-Bench) and multiple LVLM backbones (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) highlights its generalizability. Its consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art decoding strategies, even in challenging adversarial scenarios, confirms its reliability in diverse contexts.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Trust in AI:** By mitigating hallucinations, CoFi-Dec directly contributes to building greater trust in multimodal AI systems. This is paramount for expanding the use of LVLMs in sensitive domains such as medical diagnosis, autonomous navigation, scientific research, and legal analysis, where factual accuracy is non-negotiable.
- **Wider Adoption of LVLMs:** A more reliable LVLM can unlock new applications and accelerate the deployment of existing ones. Industries currently hesitant due to hallucination risks may now find LVLMs more viable for tasks like automated content generation, intelligent assistants, accessibility tools, and complex data analysis requiring visual and textual reasoning.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** CoFi-Dec's success in leveraging coarse-to-fine reasoning, generative self-feedback, and principled fusion can inspire new research directions. Future work might explore adaptive granularity selection, dynamic weighting of feedback sources, or even more sophisticated cognitive architectures for grounding multimodal AI. The approach of integrating generative models for "self-imagined reflections" also opens avenues for enhancing internal model coherence and reasoning capabilities.
- **Improved User Experience:** For end-users, this translates to more accurate, relevant, and believable interactions with AI systems that interpret images and generate text, reducing frustration and the need for manual verification of AI-generated content.

In conclusion, CoFi-Dec represents a significant step forward in making LVLMs not just powerful, but also dependably accurate. Its innovative, training-free, and principled approach offers a practical and impactful solution to a core challenge, paving the way for more robust and trustworthy multimodal AI applications.
