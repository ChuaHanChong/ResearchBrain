# Robotic Contextual Awareness for Human-Robot Collaboration and Environmental Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Federico Rollo's doctoral thesis develops methods for robots to achieve comprehensive contextual awareness for safe and autonomous operation in human-centric environments. The work introduces techniques for robust human re-identification and advanced multi-modal environmental mapping, which incorporates both geometric and semantic understanding. The developed systems demonstrate improved tracking capabilities for human collaborators and enhanced accuracy in 3D mapping and object localization, enabling complex interaction tasks.

## Problem

- Robots often lack comprehensive contextual awareness, moving beyond purely geometric understanding, to operate safely and adaptively in dynamic, human-centric environments.
- Existing human-robot interaction (HRI) systems struggle with persistent human re-identification amidst appearance changes and occlusions, limiting personalized and reliable collaboration.
- Traditional SLAM and mapping techniques face challenges with accumulated odometry drift, sensor noise, spurious reflections, and lack the integration of high-level semantic information for intelligent decision-making.

## Method

- Developed **CARPE-ID**, an adaptive human re-identification framework with ==unsupervised continual learning== to reliably track specific individuals, incorporating ==Multi-Object Tracking== to address dynamic changes and catastrophic forgetting.
- Introduced **LEO-SLAM**, a 3D ==LiDAR SLAM== algorithm featuring multi-level alignment, submap-based ==loop closure detection== with enhanced ==Gaussian Scan Context== descriptors, and ground-aware intensity filtering for robust geometric mapping.
- Created a multi-modal (RGB-D camera and LiDAR) online ==semantic mapping== framework for accurate object detection, localization, and high-level scene comprehension, enabling semantic-driven robot interaction.

## Results

- CARPE-ID with continual learning achieved a mean tracking time of **121.8 seconds** (matching the ground truth mean of **122.7 seconds**) and **7 successful Re-IDs**, significantly outperforming previous CARPE-ID and standard MOT methods.
- LEO-SLAM demonstrated competitive **Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE)** and superior **Relative Pose Error (RPE)** against state-of-the-art algorithms on public datasets, operating in real-time at an average of **185 ms** per loop.
- Multi-modal semantic mapping achieved **98%** correct object mapping in real-world experiments, extending object detection range up to **10.37m**, and successfully enabled semantic loco-manipulation tasks in simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous, online adaptation and unsupervised continual learning for Re-ID models are crucial for robust human tracking in dynamic environments, enabling recovery from track losses and preventing catastrophic forgetting.
> - Multi-level alignment combined with submap-based loop closure and advanced descriptors like Gaussian Scan Context significantly improves LiDAR SLAM accuracy and robustness against noise and sparse data.
> - Fusing multi-modal sensor data (RGB-D and LiDAR) provides more accurate and extended-range object detection and semantic mapping compared to single-sensor approaches, forming a richer basis for cognitive robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the evolving field of robotics, which is moving towards enabling autonomous mobile robots to operate effectively and safely in dynamic, human-centric environments. Historically, industrial robots have functioned as "blind" machines in highly structured, static settings. The integration of sensing capabilities marked an initial step towards adaptability, primarily focusing on geometric understanding for tasks like path planning and obstacle avoidance.

This thesis contributes to the paradigm shift from purely geometric perception to comprehensive contextual awareness, encompassing both geometric and semantic understanding of the environment and its dynamic entities, particularly humans. This interdisciplinary area, often termed Spatial AI, aims to imbue robots with cognitive capabilities for higher-level reasoning and decision-making. The work addresses limitations in current Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), and semantic mapping techniques. It seeks to develop robots that can reliably identify human collaborators, accurately map complex workspaces, and semantically interpret objects and areas, thereby enhancing their ability to engage in socially compliant navigation, intuitive collaboration, and adaptive task execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this thesis is to achieve comprehensive robotic contextual awareness, enabling robots to safely and autonomously operate in complex, human-inhabited environments. This overarching goal is motivated by the limitations of conventional robotic systems that lack the perceptual and cognitive capabilities required for nuanced interaction and adaptive behavior in unstructured settings.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing the Gap in Contextual Awareness:** Existing robots often possess only a geometric understanding of their surroundings, which is insufficient for complex tasks requiring an understanding of "what" objects are, "who" is present, and "how" these elements relate within a dynamic scene. This limits their ability to plan adaptive behaviors or engage in meaningful interaction.
- **Enhancing Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC) through Reliable Human Re-identification (Re-ID):** A core motivation is to allow robots to persistently track and recognize specific individuals despite changes in appearance, viewpoint, or temporary occlusions. This capability is essential for personalized interactions and human-aware navigation, moving beyond simple person-following based on transient features. The thesis aims to develop solutions that minimize human intervention in the robot's recognition process and overcome issues like catastrophic forgetting.
- **Improving Environmental Understanding through Robust Geometric Mapping:** The thesis seeks to enable robots to build accurate and consistent maps of their operational space. This involves advancing LiDAR-based SLAM techniques to overcome challenges such as accumulated odometry drift, sensor noise, and spurious reflections from transparent or reflective surfaces, which compromise map accuracy and reliability.
- **Integrating Semantic Information for Higher-Level Scene Comprehension:** Beyond geometry, the work aims to endow robots with the ability to assign high-level semantic meaning to objects and places. This semantic enrichment of maps is crucial for enabling robots to make informed decisions, perform complex manipulation tasks, and interact more intelligently with their environment, mimicking human perceptual capabilities.
- **Developing Multi-modal Sensor Fusion for Enhanced Perception:** A key objective is to leverage the complementary strengths of different sensor modalities (e.g., RGB-D cameras and LiDAR) to achieve more robust and accurate object detection and localization across various distances and environmental conditions, addressing the limitations of single-sensor approaches.

By addressing these objectives, the thesis aims to facilitate the transition of robots from controlled industrial settings to active, intelligent participants in our evolving human world, capable of deep environmental and human understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research is structured around two interconnected pillars: reliable object detection and re-identification (primarily focusing on humans), and robust environmental mapping and semantic understanding.

#### 3.1. Human-Robot Collaboration through Re-Identification

The approach to human Re-ID evolved through several stages:

- **FollowMe (Baseline):** This initial framework uses visual and depth data from an RGB-D camera.
 - **People Detection:** YOLACT++ instance segmentation identifies persons and generates masks.
 - **Person Re-ID:** A pre-trained IBN-ResNet-50 network (fine-tuned with MMT pre-training) extracts distinctive features. A calibration phase collects images to compute a mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) of target features, along with a statistical distance threshold (λ_d). Individuals are re-identified if their feature distance to the target model is below λ_d.
 - **Localization and Filtering:** The re-identified person's mask and depth data are used to compute a 3D centroid in the camera frame, which is then filtered with a Kalman Filter (KF) and transformed to the map frame.
 - **Gesture Detection:** MediaPipe hand tracking extracts 3D hand key points, which are fed into a Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifier to recognize gestures (e.g., "wait," "follow").
 - **Navigation and Decision Making:** The ROS navigation stack handles robot movement and obstacle avoidance. A Finite State Machine (FSM) manages the robot's behavior (Steady, Follow, Search, Wait) based on Re-ID and gesture commands, incorporating a safety circle.
- **CARPE-ID (Continuous Adaptation for Personalized Re-identification):** This iteration addresses the limitation of static appearance models.
 - It integrates a Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) algorithm (yolo_tracking/StrongSORT) with an adaptive Re-ID layer.
 - The core innovation is the continuous, online adjustment of the ideal target representation (μ, σ) and the Re-ID threshold (λ_d) using a Damped Exponential Moving Average (DEMA). Damping factors (∆_f, ∆_λ_d) mitigate high-frequency noise and prevent overfitting, allowing adaptation to appearance changes.
 - A blacklist manager is employed to filter out distractors.
- **Unsupervised Continual Learning for Robustness:** To counter catastrophic forgetting in CARPE-ID, an enhanced approach is introduced.
 - **Parallel Twin Neural Network:** A duplicate feature extractor network is trained online in parallel while the primary network handles real-time tracking, minimizing interruptions.
 - **Continual Training Setup:** Training uses a custom dataset of target and distractor images from the CARPE-ID buffer. Soft Triplet loss is employed to learn discriminative feature embeddings, ensuring anchor samples are closer to positive samples and farther from negative ones.
 - **Smart Image Pool Acquisition:** A mechanism selects older images with significant appearance differences from the current statistical model, merging them with recent images. This dynamic dataset generation prevents overfitting to recent data and helps retain memory of past appearances.
 - **Post-training Statistical Model Adaptation:** After the twin network is trained, its new weights are transferred, and the CARPE-ID statistical models (μ, σ, λ_d) are refined to ensure compatibility.
 - **Early Training:** If the target is lost for an extended period before a full training batch is accumulated, an early training session is triggered with available images to quickly adapt.

#### 3.2. Environmental Perception through Geometrical Matching

This pillar focuses on robust 3D mapping and place recognition using LiDAR.

- **LEO-SLAM (LiDAR-based SLAM):** A novel 3D LiDAR SLAM algorithm is developed.
 - **Point Cloud Preprocessing:** Raw point clouds undergo 3D crop box filtering (removing robot and distant points), statistical outlier rejection, and a ground-aware intensity filter. Voxelization is then applied for density control.
 - **Multi-level Alignment Strategy:**
 - **Scan-To-Scan (s2s):** Aligns the current scan with the previous one.
 - **Scan-To-Submaps (s2S):** Aligns the current scan with a collection of the N_s most recent submaps.
 - **Submap-To-Submaps (S2S):** Performed when a new submap keyframe is finalized, aligning it with N_s previous submaps. All alignments use Generalized Iterative Closest Point (GICP).
 - **Submap-based Loop Closure Detection (LCD):**
 - Utilizes Scan Context++ (SC++) computed from submaps (aggregations of multiple scans) to provide denser, richer descriptors, addressing limitations of sparse LiDAR data.
 - Ceilings are explicitly removed in indoor environments to prevent uniform height values from obscuring structural details.
 - An adaptive search area ellipsoid is introduced to narrow the loop closure search to spatially relevant past locations, reducing false positives.
 - Upon loop detection, an S_c2S_l alignment (current submap to loop submaps) refines the match.
 - A pose graph is constructed and optimized using iSAM2 after successful loop closures.
- **Point Cloud Ground-Aware Intensity Filtering:**
 - This technique addresses erroneous reflections from transparent/reflective surfaces and the unintended removal of low-intensity ground points by standard filters.
 - **Naive Intensity Filter:** Filters points with intensities outside a specified range (ψ*) but explicitly preserves all points below the robot's footprint height (z < l_z_f). This is a computationally efficient O(n) approach.
 - **Normal Intensity Filter:** A more complex (O(n^2)) alternative that computes normal vectors for points and retains those whose normal is parallel to the robot's z-axis, in addition to intensity filtering. The naive filter was preferred in experiments due to sparsity issues with the normal computation.
- **Gaussian Scan Context (GSC):** An extension to SC++ to enhance robustness to noise and outliers.
 - Instead of storing the maximum height value per bin, each bin (b_i,j) in the context matrix is represented by a multivariate normal distribution (G_i,j) computed from all points within it.
 - The context matrix entry (C_i,j) is then defined as the mean height (μ_z_i,j) plus a scaled variance term (α Σ_zz_i,j).
 - **Huber-weighted GSC:** Further refines the statistical estimation by applying Huber weighting to the points within each bin, thereby reducing the influence of outlier points on the mean and covariance computations.

#### 3.3. Robot Environmental Contextual Understanding and Interaction with Semantics

This section integrates semantic information for higher-level robotic understanding and interaction.

- **Artifacts Mapping:** A multi-modal (RGB-D camera and LiDAR) online semantic mapping framework is developed.
 - **Artifacts Detection and Position Estimation:**
 - **2D Object Segmentation:** A deep neural network (Yolact++/YolactEdge) infers object classes and masks from RGB images.
 - **3D Object Position Estimation using Camera-LiDAR Filtering:** Depth information from both sensors is fused. Camera depth is masked and projected to 3D for a camera centroid (c_ζ) and radius (c_ρ). LiDAR points are projected onto 2D masks for a LiDAR centroid (l_ζ) and radius (l_ρ). These are fused (ζ, ρ) using a piecewise function based on object distance and sensor accuracy ranges, giving more weight to the camera at closer distances and LiDAR at farther distances. The object's view angle (ϕ) is also computed.
 - **Artifacts Manager for Data Association:**
 - **Position Filtering:** Perceived artifacts are stored in a temporary buffer. A moving-average filter tracks centroids and radii, computing variance.
 - **Position Stabilization:** Artifacts deemed "stable" (low variance, sufficient measurements) are moved to a stable buffer, and spatially overlapping duplicates of the same class are merged. Stable artifacts are saved to a YAML file.
 - **User Interface:** An RViz plugin allows visualization of mapped artifacts, sending navigation goals (offset to be in front of the object), and deleting incorrect entries.
- **Example of Semantic Mapping Usage in Loco-Manipulation:** A "bring me" service framework demonstrates semantic map utility.
 - A Behavior Tree (BT) orchestrates actions: navigate_to_object (using semantic map data), approach_object (using instance segmentation and proportional controllers to center/distance the object), pose_estimation, pick_object, bring_to_user, release_object. An `abort` action handles failures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation demonstrated the effectiveness and improvements offered by the proposed methodologies across various domains.

#### 4.1. Re-identification for Human-Robot Collaboration

- **FollowMe Baseline:** Achieved high precision (0.98-0.99) and recall (0.96-0.99) for hand gesture classification. The Re-ID module attained 96% precision and 91% recall for target identification, demonstrating a desired conservative behavior (favoring false negatives over false positives). The full framework successfully tracked subjects in a 100 m² laboratory, operating at 7-10 Hz, validating its applicability for HRC.
- **CARPE-ID (Autonomous Adaptation):** In custom dataset experiments designed for challenging scenarios (occlusions, appearance changes), the framework demonstrated significant improvements over baseline MOT. While MOT algorithms exhibited frequent track losses (mean tracking time 21.2s vs. 176s video length), CARPE-ID achieved a low mean Re-ID delay (1.1s) and only 2 total Re-ID errors across 113 minutes of video. It reliably recovered MOT errors. In person-following tasks, the robot successfully tracked targets across 837 meters without human intervention.
- **Continual Learning (Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting):** The continual learning approach, integrated with CARPE-ID, substantially outperformed CARPE-ID and standard MOT. It achieved a mean tracking time of 121.8 seconds (matching the ground truth mean of 122.7 seconds) and 7 successful Re-IDs (also matching ground truth), compared to CARPE-ID's 102.3 seconds and 5 Re-IDs. The system maintained real-time performance (7 Hz). An ablation study identified optimal training parameters (batch size 16, 18 iterations) and confirmed the critical role of both the statistical model update and early training for robustness. Saliency maps visually demonstrated that the continually trained network specialized in consistent visual cues (e.g., faces, trousers), explaining its ability to overcome appearance changes.

#### 4.2. Geometric Environmental Perception

- **LEO-SLAM:** Evaluated on the VBR dataset, LEO-SLAM showed competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art (SoTA) algorithms (KISS-ICP, PIN-SLAM). It achieved better Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) in 2 out of 4 sequences and superior Relative Pose Error (RPE) in almost all sequences. It operated in real-time on CPU (average 185 ms per loop). The use of submap keyframes and multi-level alignment contributed to denser point clouds and improved accuracy, particularly for LiDARs with fewer channels.
- **Point Cloud Ground-Aware Intensity Filtering:** Tested with KISS-ICP and DLO on a custom indoor dataset with numerous reflections. For KISS-ICP, the ground-aware filter significantly reduced translation error (from 2.585m to 1.112m) and z-axis translational error (from 2.297m to 0.187m), by preserving crucial ground points that standard intensity filters would remove. DLO showed robustness to reflections even without the filter, but the filter still produced a cleaner map without degrading odometry. The naive O(n) filter was found to be practical and effective for real-time applications.
- **Gaussian Scan Context (GSC):** Compared against SC++ on KITTI and VBR datasets. GSC, especially its Huber-weighted height variant, consistently outperformed SC++ in recall while maintaining 100% precision. For example, in KITTI sequence 02, GSC Huber achieved 69.7% recall compared to SC++'s 57.8%. In the noisier VBR Pincio sequence, it achieved 49.3% recall versus 36.1% for SC++. GSC showed greater robustness to threshold variations. The computational overhead was modest (67 ms vs. 54 ms for SC++), deemed acceptable for the performance gains.

#### 4.3. Semantic Mapping for Environmental Understanding and Interaction

- **Artifacts Mapping:** Achieved high accuracy in object detection, localization, and mapping through multi-modal sensor fusion (RGB-D camera and LiDAR). In simulation, 99% of objects were correctly mapped with fusion, outperforming camera-only (92%) and LiDAR-only (93%) setups. In real-world laboratory experiments, fusion mapped 98% of objects correctly, significantly better than camera-only (85%) and LiDAR-only (80%). The fusion extended the detection range (up to 15.47m in simulation, 10.37m in real-world) and provided more precise centroids by leveraging LiDAR for distant objects and cameras for dense, near-field information.
- **Loco-Manipulation Demonstration:** The developed semantic map was successfully used in a simulated "bring me" loco-manipulation task, demonstrating the robot's ability to navigate to, approach, pick, and deliver objects based on high-level semantic requests.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This thesis represents a notable contribution to the field of robotics by advancing the concept and implementation of robotic contextual awareness. The developed methodologies collectively push robots beyond rudimentary geometric understanding, enabling them to operate more intelligently, safely, and collaboratively in dynamic, human-centric environments.

The **significance in Human-Robot Collaboration** is profound. By providing robust, adaptive, and continually learning human Re-ID capabilities, the work allows robots to recognize and track specific individuals with high reliability, even amidst appearance changes and occlusions. This facilitates personalized interactions, enhances human-aware navigation, and reduces the need for constant human intervention, thereby fostering more intuitive and effective collaboration. The ability to distinguish human collaborators from distractors is crucial for applications ranging from industrial assistance (e.g., carrying items between workstations, manipulation support) to personalized care.

In **environmental understanding**, the advancements in geometric and semantic mapping are critical. LEO-SLAM provides a more accurate and robust foundation for robot self-localization and map building, particularly beneficial for platforms with resource constraints or operating in challenging environments prone to sensor noise and reflections. The ground-aware intensity filtering addresses a common LiDAR limitation, ensuring clean and geometrically accurate maps without losing vital ground information. The Gaussian Scan Context significantly improves loop closure detection, a cornerstone for maintaining global map consistency over large-scale operations.

The **integration of semantics** represents a crucial step towards cognitive robotics. By enabling robots to not only localize objects but also understand their semantic meaning, the work provides a richer foundation for higher-level reasoning and decision-making. The multi-modal sensor fusion approach for semantic mapping efficiently leverages the complementary strengths of different sensors, achieving high accuracy in object detection and localization across diverse ranges. This semantic understanding is demonstrated to be highly impactful in enabling complex tasks, such as autonomous loco-manipulation services (e.g., "bring me" tasks), which are essential for true robotic assistance in industrial and domestic settings.

Overall, this research contributes to the vision of a new generation of autonomous systems that are not merely machines performing predefined tasks but active, understanding participants in the human world. The proposed solutions lay foundational groundwork for developing more sophisticated, trustworthy, and adaptable robots capable of living and working alongside humans with enhanced perception and intelligence. Future work, as noted by the author, involves further deep comprehension of environmental semantics, relations, and their utility for robot control, potentially mimicking or surpassing human capabilities in environmental interaction.
