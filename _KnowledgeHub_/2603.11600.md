# Hybrid Energy-Aware Reward Shaping: A Unified Lightweight Physics-Guided Methodology for Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hybrid Energy-Aware Reward Shaping (H-EARS) framework integrates lightweight physics priors into model-free deep reinforcement learning by combining a dual-potential function with energy-based action regularization. This approach improves policy convergence speed, stability, and energy efficiency, achieving quantifiable gains across various continuous control benchmarks and in a high-fidelity vehicle simulation.

## Problem

- Model-free Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies often exhibit high variance, low energy efficiency, and limited generalization, potentially learning unstable or physically implausible behaviors.
- Integrating physical priors into DRL is challenging due to the high computational complexity and modeling costs of full physics-based approaches, and conventional reward shaping lacks physical interpretability or direct action-level constraints.
- Existing reward shaping methods, while accelerating convergence, often fail to ensure physical consistency or prevent high-frequency control oscillations, leading to policies that may overfit to simulation dynamics.

## Method

- Developed **H-EARS** (Hybrid Energy-Aware Reward Shaping), a systematic framework unifying ==potential-based reward shaping== with ==energy-aware action regularization==.
- Introduced a ==dual-potential function== `Φ(s) = α_task Φ_task(s) + α_energy Φ_energy(s)` where `Φ_task` guides task performance and `Φ_energy = -E(q, q̇)` incorporates mechanical energy.
- Integrated an ==energy-based action regularization== functional `E(a) = aᵀQa` into the shaped reward, directly penalizing action magnitude to prevent high-frequency oscillations.

## Results

- H-EARS+SAC achieved up to a **32.5%** performance gain, **28.2%** faster convergence, and **27.6%** variance reduction in standard continuous control benchmarks like Ant-v5.
- In high-fidelity vehicle simulations, H-EARS+SAC reduced average speed error by **43.9%** and maximum sideslip angle by **49.5%**, demonstrating enhanced lateral stability in extreme conditions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both task and energy potentials, and action regularization, while empirical validation in vehicle simulations showed a **96.0%** performance retention even with approximately **0.15** energy model approximation error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The potential shaping term and action regularization term operate on functionally independent domains, allowing for independent optimization of task performance and energy efficiency.
> - Energy potentials derived from mechanical systems, especially those with positive definite Hessian conditions, can accelerate DRL convergence by introducing a directional bias towards energy-reducing actions.
> - Lightweight physics modeling, focusing on dominant energy components (O(n) complexity), can provide substantial performance benefits without requiring complete, high-fidelity dynamic models, enabling practical deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has demonstrated capabilities in continuous control tasks, such as trajectory tracking and motion control. Model-free DRL algorithms, exemplified by Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), learn control policies through extensive environmental interaction without explicit system models. However, policies derived from such trial-and-error approaches often exhibit characteristics including high variance, low energy efficiency, and limited generalization capabilities. These issues can arise because agents, treated as *tabula rasa*, may need to independently discover fundamental physical principles, leading to unstable or physically implausible behaviors, and potentially exploiting simulator-specific dynamics rather than generalized control strategies.

The integration of physical priors into DRL has emerged as a strategy to address the gap between simulation performance and real-world reliability. Existing physics-based models, such as Lagrangian or Hamiltonian neural networks, offer guarantees of physical consistency but typically require complete system equations and incur cubic computational complexity, which can limit their applicability in real-world scenarios marked by modeling uncertainties or constrained computational resources. Conversely, purely model-free methods are computationally efficient but lack intrinsic physical constraints, potentially resulting in "shortcut" policies that perform poorly when real-world dynamics diverge from training conditions. This trade-off between physical precision, computational efficiency, and broad applicability is a significant challenge, particularly in safety-critical domains like vehicle dynamics.

Reward shaping is a technique employed to enhance sparse reward signals and accelerate reinforcement learning. Potential-Based Reward Shaping (PBRS) frameworks have been developed to preserve policy optimality under reward transformations. While PBRS research has primarily focused on accelerating convergence, less attention has been directed toward the physical interpretability or stability of the learned policies. Furthermore, many PBRS schemes operate solely on the state space, making them unable to directly constrain action-level behavior, which can lead to physically implausible, high-frequency control oscillations even with optimal policies.

This study positions itself within this landscape by seeking to reconcile the benefits of model-free DRL with the stability and efficiency offered by physical principles, without incurring the high computational and modeling costs of full physics-based approaches. It addresses the challenges of achieving a balance between modeling cost, computational complexity, and performance, unifying convergence acceleration and physical stability, and mitigating policy overfitting to simulation dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a systematic framework, named Hybrid Energy-Aware Reward Shaping (H-EARS), that unifies potential-based reward shaping with energy-aware action regularization. This framework aims to integrate lightweight physics priors into model-free deep reinforcement learning to enhance policy optimization across several critical dimensions, including convergence speed, stability, and energy efficiency.

The motivation for H-EARS stems from several observed limitations in current DRL and physics-guided learning methodologies:

1. **Bridging Model-Free and Physics-Based Paradigms:** Model-free DRL often struggles with efficiency, stability, and generalization due to a lack of physical understanding, while explicit physics-based models are often computationally intensive and require complete system equations, which may not be feasible for complex real-world systems with uncertainties. H-EARS seeks to offer an intermediate approach that leverages fundamental physical principles without requiring complete dynamics modeling.
2. **Addressing Shortcomings of Conventional Reward Shaping:** While PBRS accelerates learning, existing methods often lack physical interpretability and do not directly constrain action-level behavior. Policies can still exhibit high-frequency oscillations or energy-inefficient actions. H-EARS aims to introduce physical guidance, specifically energy minimization, directly into the reward structure to yield more physically reasonable policies.
3. **Improving Policy Robustness and Generalization:** Learned DRL policies frequently exploit simulator artifacts, leading to brittleness when deployed in real-world environments that deviate from training conditions. By embedding physics-aware reward shaping, H-EARS seeks to encourage policies that adhere to physical laws, thereby improving their robustness and generalization capabilities.
4. **Optimizing Computational and Modeling Costs:** Deriving complete Euler-Lagrange equations for complex systems can be time-consuming and requires expert knowledge. H-EARS aims to reduce this burden by only modeling dominant energy components, thereby simplifying the modeling process and reducing engineering effort, making it more practical for industrial deployment.
5. **Enhancing Stability in Safety-Critical Domains:** In applications such as vehicle control, stability and robustness are paramount. Current DRL approaches may lack theoretical stability guarantees. H-EARS is motivated to explicitly incorporate energy minimization with provable convergence properties and a heuristic connection to Lyapunov stability to enhance safety and reliability in such domains.
6. **Establishing Theoretical Foundations for Physics-Guided DRL:** The work is motivated to provide a comprehensive theoretical foundation for its proposed methodology. This includes demonstrating functional independence, the necessity of action regularization, the mechanism of energy-based convergence acceleration, and providing convergence guarantees and error bounds for approximate physical models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

H-EARS is presented as a systematic framework that modifies the reward signal in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) to integrate physics-guided priors without altering the underlying DRL algorithm architecture.

**A. Preliminaries and Framework Definition**

The foundation is built upon an MDP, defined by state space `S`, action space `A`, transition probability `P`, reward function `R`, and discount factor `γ`. The objective remains to maximize the expected cumulative return. Potential-Based Reward Shaping (PBRS) theory, which states that shaped rewards `R̃(s, a, s') = R(s, a, s') + γΦ(s') - Φ(s)` preserve optimal policies for any potential function `Φ(s)`, is adopted.

The H-EARS framework defines its shaped reward as:
`R_H-EARS(s, a, s') = R(s, a, s') + [γΦ(s') - Φ(s)] - λ * E(a)`
where:
- `Φ(s) = α_task Φ_task(s) + α_energy Φ_energy(s)` is a **dual-potential function**.
 - `Φ_task(s)` encodes task-oriented guidance (e.g., distance to goal).
 - `Φ_energy(s) = -E(q(s), q̇(s))` encodes the mechanical energy structure, with `E` representing total energy (kinetic + potential).
- `E(a) = aᵀQa` is an **energy-based action regularization functional**, with `Q` being a positive semi-definite matrix.
- `λ ≥ 0` is the regularization coefficient.

This formulation is interpreted as a two-step process: first, an intermediate MDP is created via action regularization, and then standard PBRS is applied.

**B. Theoretical Contributions**

The methodology is supported by several theoretical developments:

1. **Functional Independence Property (Lemma II.1):** This lemma formally establishes that the potential shaping term `γΦ(s') - Φ(s)` and the action regularization term `-λE(a)` operate on disjoint functional domains (state transitions vs. actions). This functional independence implies that task performance and energy efficiency can be optimized independently by adjusting `Φ` and `λ`, respectively, without invalidating PBRS policy invariance.
2. **Regularization Necessity (Theorem II.2):** This theorem classifies mechanical systems into two categories: Class I (energy dissipation naturally promotes task objectives) and Class II (requiring transient energy injection or exhibiting high-frequency modes due to discretization). It posits that for Class I systems, potential shaping alone (`λ = 0`) suffices. However, for Class II systems, action regularization (`λ > 0`) is necessary to prevent pathological behaviors, such as high-frequency oscillations, where rapid action changes in discrete time might not lead to proportional energy changes, thus evading energy-based penalties. Regularization directly penalizes action magnitude.
3. **Energy-Based Convergence Acceleration Mechanism (Theorem II.4):** This is a core contribution. It demonstrates that if the mechanical system's energy function `E(q, q̇)` satisfies a positive definite Hessian condition (`∂²E/∂q² ≻ 0`), then the energy potential `Φ_energy(s) = -E(q, q̇)` accelerates DRL convergence. The shaped reward introduces an additional directional bias in the policy gradient toward energy-reducing actions. This acceleration is quantified by `ρ_accel`, which is proportional to the ratio of informative energy gradients to sparse task rewards, potentially leading to orders-of-magnitude faster learning in domains where task rewards are sparse but energy gradients are rich. The physical self-consistency of energy potentials, derived from intrinsic mechanical components, is highlighted as enabling design-free construction, physical grounding, and graceful degradation compared to artificial potentials.
4. **Dual-Potential Decomposition Necessity (Proposition II.7):** This proposition explains why a single potential function cannot simultaneously satisfy both task directivity and energy awareness when these objectives conflict. The dual decomposition (`Φ(s) = α_task Φ_task(s) + α_energy Φ_energy(s)`) resolves this by allowing independent tuning of the task-oriented and energy-oriented components, enabling staged optimization where one can prioritize task completion early and then refine for energy efficiency.
5. **Convergence Guarantees (Theorem II.9):** The framework provides convergence guarantees, stating that under standard assumptions, H-EARS achieves an `O(1/√N)` convergence rate. An upper bound for the regularization coefficient `λ_max` is derived to ensure numerical stability by keeping the shaped reward magnitude bounded.
6. **Parameter Continuity and Sensitivity Analysis (Theorem II.10):** This theorem asserts that the optimal policy exhibits continuity with respect to the regularization coefficient `λ`, indicating bounded hyperparameter sensitivity for robust deployment.
7. **Approximate Potential Error Bounds (Lemma II.11):** This lemma quantifies the trade-off between energy modeling accuracy and performance. It provides a bound on performance loss as a function of the approximation error in the energy potential, suggesting that even simplified, approximate energy models can retain significant performance benefits.
8. **Energy-Based Shaping as Stability-Oriented Guidance (Proposition II.12):** This proposition draws a heuristic connection between maximizing `Φ_energy = -E` and guiding policies toward Lyapunov-stable behaviors. By encouraging actions that dissipate energy (`Ė < 0`), the shaped reward implicitly promotes stability in systems where energy dissipation aligns with task objectives. This is presented as a heuristic rather than a rigorous guarantee due to discretization errors and the complexities of closed-loop stability.

**C. Actor-Critic Integration**

H-EARS integrates with standard actor-critic algorithms by modifying the reward signal fed into the Temporal Difference (TD) learning update for the critic. The actor update remains conceptually unchanged, but its advantage function is computed from Q-values or value estimates trained on the `R_H-EARS` shaped reward. This ensures that H-EARS guidance propagates through learned value functions without requiring direct modifications to the actor's policy structure, preserving properties like entropy regularization or trust regions. The framework allows for the computation of potential functions and action regularization terms during environment interaction, which are then stored alongside standard transition tuples.

**D. Lightweight Physics Modeling**

A crucial aspect of the methodology is the emphasis on "lightweight" physics priors. Instead of requiring complete, high-fidelity dynamic models (which might entail O(n³) complexity), H-EARS focuses on selectively capturing dominant energy components (e.g., torso and limb kinetic energy plus gravitational potential for locomotion). This approach yields linear modeling complexity (O(n)), making it accessible to general engineers without requiring deep expertise in analytical mechanics and facilitating rapid engineering deployment and maintenance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical validation across standard continuous control benchmarks and a high-fidelity vehicle simulation, demonstrating the efficacy and theoretical alignment of the H-EARS framework.

**A. Standard Environment Experiments**

Experiments were conducted on Ant-v5, Hopper-v5, LunarLander-v3, and Humanoid-v5, using four baseline DRL algorithms: SAC, TD3, PPO, and DDPG, with and without H-EARS integration.

- **SAC Integration:** H-EARS+SAC consistently showed improvements. In Ant-v5, it achieved a 32.5% performance gain, 28.2% faster convergence, and 27.6% variance reduction. LunarLander-v3 saw a 53.3% faster convergence and 41.1% variance reduction. Hopper-v5 showed a 33.1% performance increase. These results support Theorem II.4, indicating that energy potentials reshape the value landscape for faster and more stable learning, particularly where energy-based constraints align with task objectives. In Humanoid-v5, a high-dimensional system, a modest 4.8% gain was observed, primarily enhancing stability (10.1% to 9.0% CV), consistent with Lemma II.11 on the utility of O(n) approximate energy models.
- **TD3 Integration:** H-EARS+TD3 showed environment-selective benefits. Ant-v5 improved by 15.5% with 25.8% faster convergence, and Hopper-v5 gained 11.7%. However, LunarLander-v3 showed minimal change, suggesting that TD3's deterministic policy gradient already incorporates a degree of smoothness, saturating the marginal benefit from additional energy constraints. Still, variance reduction (e.g., 11.8% in Hopper-v5) confirmed the independent stability contribution of energy potentials (Lemma II.1).
- **PPO Integration:** PPO+H-EARS exhibited mixed results. It rescued Ant-v5 from catastrophic collapse (376 to 501, CV 74.7% to 25.3%), validating Theorem II.2 (regularization necessity) where energy potentials prevent divergence from competing gradients. LunarLander-v3 showed 9.8% improvement and 47.1% variance reduction. However, Hopper-v5 showed a slight regression, suggesting a conflict between PPO's clipped objective and energy shaping when rapid policy adjustments are needed. Humanoid-v5 showed delayed collapse but lower final return, indicating energy constraints slow but don't prevent instability in critically unstable systems, aligning with Proposition II.12's discrete-time approximation limits.
- **DDPG Integration:** DDPG+H-EARS demonstrated framework limitations. While LunarLander-v3 showed an 8.2% improvement and 35.4% variance reduction, Ant-v5 experienced severe degradation (610 to 456, CV 37.9% to 92.5%). This was attributed to DDPG's Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise requiring high-frequency perturbations for exploration, which energy minimization suppressed, leading to insufficient exploration. This outcome served to validate the theory, demonstrating that the energy potential's constraint effect can cause predictable degradation when it conflicts with the base algorithm's design principles.

Overall, the cross-algorithm analysis revealed systematic patterns: H-EARS performed strongest in tasks naturally aligned with energy dynamics (e.g., LunarLander), was effective for stochastic off-policy methods (SAC, TD3), faced limitations with algorithm incompatibility (DDPG high-DOF exploration), and was bounded by physical characteristics (Humanoid complexity).

**B. Ablation Experiments**

Ablation studies on Ant-v5 and Hopper-v5 validated the necessity of each H-EARS component:

- **Task Potential Necessity:** Variants without `Φ_task` experienced significant performance degradation (22.2% in Ant, 17.2% in Hopper), confirming that `Φ_task` provides crucial gradient-based exploration guidance for rapid early convergence, while `Φ_energy` handles asymptotic refinement. This supports Lemma II.1 on functional independence.
- **Energy Potential as Stability Constraint:** In Hopper-v5 (an inherently unstable system), "Without Energy" variants exhibited critical instability and 37.2% higher variance, supporting Theorem II.4. "Energy Only" variants showed superior stability (7.1% CV) despite lower returns, confirming energy minimization as a Lyapunov-like convergence accelerator. In Ant-v5 (statically stable), the `Φ_energy` impact was less pronounced, indicating its primary benefit for dynamically unstable systems.
- **Action Regularization Necessity:** "Without Regularization" variants showed substantial degradation (17.2% in Ant, 20.7% in Hopper) and increased variance. This confirmed Theorem II.2, demonstrating that regularization `λ||a||²` is necessary to mediate conflicts between task and energy potentials that might otherwise lead to oscillatory training dynamics or extreme control outputs. "Regularization Only" improved performance but with reduced convergence efficiency compared to full H-EARS, highlighting that explicit physics priors (potentials) complement, but cannot be replaced by, implicit constraints.

**C. Vehicle Simulation Validation**

High-fidelity simulations of a four-wheel distributed-drive electric MPV in TruckSim, under extreme conditions (low adhesion, compound slopes), validated H-EARS in a safety-critical domain.

- **Algorithm Convergence:** H-EARS+SAC demonstrated rapid convergence, reaching a performance threshold 32.1% faster than standard SAC and maintaining 56.0% lower variance. This initialization advantage and long-term stability were attributed to the physically-grounded priors provided by the hybrid potential function, supporting Theorem II.4's predicted convergence acceleration via mechanical stability.
- **Quantitative Performance:** H-EARS+SAC achieved a 43.9% reduction in average speed error (0.23 ± 0.12 m/s vs. 0.41 ± 0.19 m/s for SAC) and a 49.5% reduction in maximum sideslip angle (0.52° vs. 1.03° for SAC). Yaw rate oscillations were also significantly constrained. These improvements confirm that energy-aware shaping promotes coordinated control and enhances tracking accuracy and lateral stability.
- **Lyapunov Stability Empirical Validation:** By defining a Lyapunov candidate function `L` related to the energy potential `Φ_energy`, H-EARS empirically demonstrated monotonic convergence characteristics (L(t) decreasing) for sideslip angle and yaw rate, especially in challenging road segments. This provided direct empirical evidence for Theorem II.12's heuristic connection between energy potentials and Lyapunov stability, showing that H-EARS implicitly guides policies toward energy-dissipating, stable behaviors.
- **Approximate Model Robustness:** H-EARS used a simplified energy model (dominant kinetic and yaw energy terms, omitting inter-wheel coupling, suspension). Despite an empirical approximation error of approximately 0.15, the system retained 96.0% of its performance, validating Lemma II.11. This supports the practical utility of selective energy modeling, demonstrating that simplified potentials can capture sufficient dynamics for effective guidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study presents H-EARS as a robust framework with several significant implications for the field of reinforcement learning and its practical applications:

1. **Reconciling Model-Free Efficiency with Physical Principles:** H-EARS offers a pathway to combine the data-driven adaptive capabilities of model-free DRL with the stability, efficiency, and robustness typically associated with physics-based models. It addresses a fundamental trade-off in the field by providing a lightweight method that avoids the high computational complexity and stringent modeling requirements of full physics-based approaches, making DRL more applicable to real-world engineering systems.
2. **Enhanced Policy Quality and Robustness:** By systematically embedding physics-aware guidance, specifically energy minimization, into the reward structure, H-EARS demonstrably improves convergence speed, stability, and energy efficiency of learned policies. This leads to policies that are not only effective but also physically plausible, less prone to high-frequency oscillations, and more generalizable, reducing the risk of performance degradation under out-of-distribution conditions or in physical systems that deviate from perfect simulation.
3. **Comprehensive Theoretical Foundation:** The framework is supported by a set of theoretical contributions, including proofs of functional independence, regularization necessity, energy-based convergence acceleration mechanisms, dual-potential decomposition, and convergence guarantees. The derivation of approximate potential error bounds is particularly significant for engineering, quantifying the acceptable trade-off between modeling complexity and performance. The heuristic connection to Lyapunov stability provides intuitive design guidance grounded in control theory.
4. **Practical Accessibility and Deployability:** The methodology's emphasis on "lightweight physics priors" means that complete, expert-derived system equations are not required. By focusing on dominant energy components, the modeling complexity is reduced to O(n), enabling general engineers, rather than only analytical mechanics experts, to implement and maintain these systems within practical timeframes. This feature significantly lowers the barrier for integrating advanced DRL into industrial applications.
5. **Broad Applicability Across DRL Algorithms:** The experimental results demonstrate that H-EARS can be integrated with various off-policy and on-policy actor-critic algorithms (SAC, TD3, PPO, DDPG) and consistently yields improvements, albeit with context-dependent nuances. This algorithmic generality enhances its potential impact across diverse continuous control tasks.
6. **Validation in Safety-Critical Domains:** The high-fidelity vehicle simulation in TruckSim, under extreme conditions, provides compelling evidence of H-EARS's utility in safety-critical applications. The framework's ability to enhance speed tracking accuracy and, crucially, lateral stability (reducing sideslip and constraining yaw rates) through implicit Lyapunov-like stability, highlights its potential for robust autonomous system control.

In summary, H-EARS presents a unified, theoretically grounded, and practically feasible methodology that significantly advances the integration of physics priors into DRL. Its focus on lightweight, interpretable guidance, coupled with robust empirical validation, positions it as a valuable contribution toward transferring deep reinforcement learning from academic research to reliable industrial deployment, particularly in domains demanding high performance, stability, and safety.
