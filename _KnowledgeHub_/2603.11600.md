# Hybrid Energy-Aware Reward Shaping: A Unified Lightweight Physics-Guided Methodology for Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hybrid Energy-Aware Reward Shaping (H-EARS) framework integrates lightweight physics priors into model-free deep reinforcement learning by combining a dual-potential function with energy-based action regularization. This approach improves policy convergence speed, stability, and energy efficiency, achieving quantifiable gains across various continuous control benchmarks and in a high-fidelity vehicle simulation.

## Problem

- Model-free Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies often exhibit high variance, low energy efficiency, and limited generalization, potentially learning unstable or physically implausible behaviors.
- Integrating physical priors into DRL is challenging due to the high computational complexity and modeling costs of full physics-based approaches, and conventional reward shaping lacks physical interpretability or direct action-level constraints.
- Existing reward shaping methods, while accelerating convergence, often fail to ensure physical consistency or prevent high-frequency control oscillations, leading to policies that may overfit to simulation dynamics.

## Method

- Developed **H-EARS** (Hybrid Energy-Aware Reward Shaping), a systematic framework unifying ==potential-based reward shaping== with ==energy-aware action regularization==.
- Introduced a ==dual-potential function== `Φ(s) = α_task Φ_task(s) + α_energy Φ_energy(s)` where `Φ_task` guides task performance and `Φ_energy = -E(q, q̇)` incorporates mechanical energy.
- Integrated an ==energy-based action regularization== functional `E(a) = aᵀQa` into the shaped reward, directly penalizing action magnitude to prevent high-frequency oscillations.

## Results

- H-EARS+SAC achieved up to a **32.5%** performance gain, **28.2%** faster convergence, and **27.6%** variance reduction in standard continuous control benchmarks like Ant-v5.
- In high-fidelity vehicle simulations, H-EARS+SAC reduced average speed error by **43.9%** and maximum sideslip angle by **49.5%**, demonstrating enhanced lateral stability in extreme conditions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both task and energy potentials, and action regularization, while empirical validation in vehicle simulations showed a **96.0%** performance retention even with approximately **0.15** energy model approximation error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The potential shaping term and action regularization term operate on functionally independent domains, allowing for independent optimization of task performance and energy efficiency.
> - Energy potentials derived from mechanical systems, especially those with positive definite Hessian conditions, can accelerate DRL convergence by introducing a directional bias towards energy-reducing actions.
> - Lightweight physics modeling, focusing on dominant energy components (O(n) complexity), can provide substantial performance benefits without requiring complete, high-fidelity dynamic models, enabling practical deployment.
