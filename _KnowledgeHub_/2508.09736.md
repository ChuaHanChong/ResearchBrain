# Seeing, Listening, Remembering, and Reasoning: A Multimodal Agent with Long-Term Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> M3-Agent, developed by ByteDance Seed, introduces a multimodal AI agent framework that processes continuous video and audio streams, constructs an entity-centric long-term memory, and performs multi-turn reasoning. The system achieves 30.7% accuracy on the M3-Bench-robot dataset and 48.9% on M3-Bench-web, demonstrating enhanced person understanding and cross-modal reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal agents struggle to process infinitely long input streams and are limited by finite context windows or ineffective feature compression.
- Current AI memory systems often fail to form consistent, entity-centric world knowledge from diverse multimodal content, leading to ambiguity in long-term understanding.
- Standard Long Video Question Answering (LVQA) benchmarks primarily assess low-level visual tasks, lacking the evaluation of higher-level cognitive abilities essential for truly intelligent agents.

## Method

- M3-Agent employs an ==entity-centric multimodal graph== as long-term memory, continuously generating episodic and semantic memories from incoming video and audio streams.
- It integrates external tools for facial and speaker recognition to assign and maintain ==consistent character identities== across different modalities and clips.
- A reinforcement learning (DAPO) based ==control workflow== enables multi-turn reasoning, allowing the agent to iteratively query its comprehensive memory to answer complex, instruction-based questions.

## Results

- M3-Agent achieved an overall accuracy of **30.7%** on the M3-Bench-robot dataset, surpassing the strongest baseline, MA-LMM, by **6.3%**.
- On M3-Bench-web, it obtained **48.9%** accuracy, outperforming the Gemini-GPT4o-Hybrid baseline by **7.7%**, with notable improvements of **15.5%** in Person Understanding and **6.7%** in Cross-modal Reasoning.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that removing character identity equivalence or semantic memory resulted in substantial accuracy drops (e.g., up to **19.2%** on M3-Bench-web), affirming their critical roles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly managing character identity equivalence and integrating multimodal features into memory is crucial for maintaining long-term consistency and improving agent understanding.
> - The construction of detailed semantic memory, including character attributes, relationships, and general world knowledge, is essential for enabling higher-level cognitive reasoning in agents.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically DAPO, is effective in training agents to perform flexible, multi-turn reasoning and strategic memory retrieval in complex, dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent AI agents capable of operating autonomously in complex, dynamic environments is a central goal in artificial intelligence. This paper addresses a critical challenge in this domain: equipping multimodal agents with robust, human-like long-term memory and reasoning capabilities.

The existing research landscape for AI agents has seen significant advancements, particularly with the rise of large language models (LLMs). Many efforts have focused on enabling LLM agents with memory by appending entire trajectories, summaries, latent embeddings, or structured knowledge representations. However, as the paper notes, most of these approaches are geared towards LLM agents, which primarily handle textual or symbolic information. Multimodal agents, which interact with the world through vision and audio, face unique challenges in processing diverse inputs, maintaining consistency of multimodal content, and constructing internal world knowledge over extended periods. Current multimodal models, while powerful, often struggle with "infinitely long" input streams, relying on context window extensions or feature compression that do not scale effectively for continuous online processing.

Furthermore, the field of **online video understanding** (Section 2.2) has explored memory-based methods to process long video streams. While these approaches improve scalability by storing encoded features, they often fall short in maintaining long-term consistency, especially in tracking evolving entities like human identities or complex events. Socratic Models, which generate language-based descriptions as memory, also encounter similar consistency issues across complex and evolving video content.

The current work, M3-Agent, distinguishes itself by directly tackling these limitations. It positions itself as a crucial step towards truly embodied and continuously learning AI agents by:
1. **Enabling Infinite Information Processing**: Unlike methods limited by finite context windows, M3-Agent continuously processes arbitrary long multimodal streams online, mimicking human perception and incremental experience integration.
2. **Building World Knowledge**: It moves beyond merely storing descriptions of experience to actively constructing "world knowledge" through an entity-centric, multimodal memory structure. This addresses the ambiguity and inconsistency inherent in language-based descriptions by grounding knowledge with persistent multimodal representations (e.g., faces, voices).
3. **Evaluating Higher-Level Cognition**: By introducing M3-Bench, the paper highlights a significant gap in existing Long Video Question Answering (LVQA) benchmarks. Traditional LVQA often focuses on low-level visual tasks, whereas M3-Bench specifically targets higher-level cognitive abilities essential for agents, such as person understanding, general knowledge extraction, and cross-modal reasoning, which are crucial for real-world agent applications.

Thus, M3-Agent represents an evolution in multimodal agent design, emphasizing continuous learning, robust memory formation, and sophisticated reasoning to enable agents to operate more intelligently and autonomously in dynamic, real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the intelligence of multimodal AI agents, enabling them to perceive, remember, and reason about their environments in a manner analogous to human cognition. The authors articulate this goal through the lens of a future household robot, envisioning a system that can autonomously perform tasks, learn user preferences, and adapt to its surroundings without explicit, continuous instruction.

The key motivations driving the development of M3-Agent are threefold:

1. **Continuous Multimodal Perception**: Real-world agents must continuously process vast streams of information from various sensors (e.g., cameras, microphones). Current AI systems often handle inputs in discrete, finite chunks, which is insufficient for truly intelligent agents that need to operate in an ongoing, open-ended manner. M3-Agent aims to address this by developing a framework for continuous, real-time visual and auditory input processing.
2. **Long-Term Memory and World Knowledge Construction**: For an agent to act intelligently and adapt over time, it needs to form and continually update a rich, long-term memory. This memory should not merely be a log of observations but should evolve into structured "world knowledge" – understanding of entities, their attributes, relationships, and environmental rules. Existing memory systems in AI often struggle with maintaining consistency and constructing high-level knowledge over extended durations, leading to ambiguity and a lack of coherent understanding. The paper motivates an entity-centric, multimodal memory system to overcome these limitations.
3. **Memory-Based Reasoning for Complex Tasks**: The ultimate utility of long-term memory lies in its ability to inform intelligent decision-making and task completion. Agents need to be able to actively retrieve relevant information from their vast memory banks and perform multi-turn reasoning to answer complex questions or execute instructions. This goes beyond simple retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and necessitates more sophisticated, iterative reasoning processes. The paper also highlights that existing benchmarks for long-video understanding do not adequately assess these higher-level cognitive abilities crucial for real-world agent applications.

In summary, the authors are motivated to create a more robust, adaptive, and human-like multimodal agent by addressing the fundamental challenges of continuous perception, coherent long-term memory formation, and sophisticated memory-based reasoning, thereby bridging the gap between current AI capabilities and the requirements for truly autonomous, intelligent agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The M3-Agent framework is designed with two parallel, continuously operating processes: **memorization** and **control**, supported by a novel **long-term memory** architecture.

#### 3.1. Long-Term Memory Architecture

M3-Agent implements long-term memory as an external database, structured as an **entity-centric multimodal graph**. This graph stores information in various modalities (text, images, audio), where each node represents a distinct memory item (Table 3). Nodes possess attributes like unique ID, type, raw content, embedding, weight (for confidence), and metadata. Crucially, nodes are connected by undirected edges indicating logical relationships, particularly for forming entity-centric graphs where information related to the same person (face, voice, knowledge) is linked. This structure supports both sequential and associative retrieval.

A **weight-based voting mechanism** is used to resolve conflicting information, ensuring robustness and consistency by allowing frequently activated, correct entries to override less confident ones over time. The memory module provides specialized `search_node` and `search_clip` functions for multimodal and clip-level retrieval, respectively (Table 4).

#### 3.2. Memorization Workflow

This process continuously perceives incoming video streams (visual and auditory) in a clip-by-clip manner, generating and updating memory:
- **Episodic Memory**: Captures concrete events and fine-grained details observed in video (e.g., "Alice takes the coffee").
- **Semantic Memory**: Extracts higher-level general knowledge, such as character identities, attributes, relationships, and world knowledge (e.g., "Alice prefers coffee," "green bin for recycling"). Semantic memory also enriches retrieval cues.
- **Consistent Entity Representation**: To overcome ambiguity of textual descriptions ("a man with a beard"), M3-Agent preserves original multimodal features. It integrates external tools for **facial recognition** and **speaker identification** (Appendix C) to extract and assign persistent identity representations (e.g., `<face_id>`, `<voice_id>`). These IDs are globally maintained within the memory graph, ensuring consistent character tracking across different clips.
- **Memory Generation**: A Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) generates episodic and semantic memories as text entries, explicitly referencing characters via their `face_id` or `voice_id`. This allows cross-modal reasoning to infer equivalences (e.g., linking a face to a voice for the same person), which are then updated in the graph, unifying them under a shared `<character_id>`.

#### 3.3. Control Workflow

Upon receiving an instruction, the control process initiates multi-turn reasoning and iterative memory retrieval (Algorithm 1).
- Unlike single-turn RAG, M3-Agent uses **reinforcement learning (RL)** to enable a flexible, multi-turn reasoning approach.
- In each round, a policy model (`πθ`) generates reasoning, an action (either `[Search]` or `[Answer]`), and arguments.
- If `[Search]`, the system queries the long-term memory using the argument, and retrieved results are appended to the context for the next round. Different search functions (`search_node`, `search_clip`) can be invoked based on the context.
- If `[Answer]`, the system returns the content, and the process terminates.

#### 3.4. Training

To achieve optimal performance, M3-Agent employs separate policy models for memorization and control, leveraging different foundation models:
- **Memorization Training**: Qwen2.5-Omni, an advanced open-source multimodal model, is fine-tuned via imitation learning (`memory-7b-sft`). This involves a sophisticated three-stage synthetic data generation process:
 1. **Episodic Memory Synthesis**: GPT-4o generates visual descriptions, which are then used as context for Gemini-1.5-Pro to generate richer, audio-informed episodic memories (Table 11).
 2. **Entity ID Relationship Detection**: A novel algorithm identifies "meta-clips" (segments with one face, one voice) to build a high-confidence meta-dictionary for global face-voice ID mapping (Algorithm 2). This automatically annotates identity equivalences for training clips.
 3. **Semantic Memory Synthesis**: Prompt templates are designed to extract various types of semantic memory (character attributes, relationships, contextual knowledge) from videos (Table 12).
- **Control Training**: Qwen3, a powerful open-source LLM, is trained using **Direct Advantage Policy Optimization (DAPO)** (Yu et al., 2025).
 - The environment for RL training uses long-term memories generated by `memory-7b-sft`.
 - GPT-4o acts as an automatic evaluator to provide a binary reward signal (correct/incorrect) for agent responses.
 - The policy is optimized to maximize this reward, learning to generate effective reasoning, search queries, and answers (Equation 3).

#### 3.5. M3-Bench Benchmark

To evaluate the effectiveness of memory and memory-based reasoning in multimodal agents, the authors developed **M3-Bench**, a novel Long Video Question Answering (LVQA) benchmark.
- **M3-Bench-robot**: 100 real-world, robot-perspective videos (average ~34 minutes) recorded with human actors simulating robot interactions in diverse domestic and office scenarios. This dataset focuses on agent-centric, real-world tasks and perceptions.
- **M3-Bench-web**: 920 diverse web-sourced videos (average ~27 minutes) from YouTube, collected specifically to support complex narratives and inter-entity relationships.
- **Question Types**: M3-Bench defines five challenging question types (Table 1): Multi-evidence Reasoning, Multi-hop Reasoning, Cross-modal Reasoning, Person Understanding, and General Knowledge Extraction. These are designed to assess higher-level cognitive abilities beyond simple visual perception, crucial for intelligent agents.
- **Automatic Evaluation**: GPT-4o is used as an automatic evaluator, validated to achieve 96% agreement with human judges (Table 18).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of M3-Agent on the newly introduced M3-Bench and existing VideoMME-long benchmarks reveals significant advancements over various baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (Table 5)

M3-Agent consistently outperforms all categories of baselines across all three benchmarks:
- **M3-Bench-robot**: M3-Agent achieves an overall accuracy of 30.7%, surpassing the strongest baseline, MA-LMM, by a substantial 6.3%.
- **M3-Bench-web**: M3-Agent achieves an overall accuracy of 48.9%, outperforming the Gemini-GPT4o-Hybrid baseline by 7.7%.
- **VideoMME-long**: M3-Agent achieves an accuracy of 61.8%, marking a 5.3% improvement over the Gemini-GPT4o-Hybrid baseline.

#### 4.2. Performance by Question Type

Analysis across M3-Bench question types highlights M3-Agent's particular strengths in human understanding and cross-modal reasoning:
- On M3-Bench-robot, M3-Agent shows improvements of 4.2% in Person Understanding and 8.5% in Cross-modal Reasoning compared to the best-performing baseline, MA-LMM.
- On M3-Bench-web, M3-Agent demonstrates even greater gains, with 15.5% in Person Understanding and 6.7% in Cross-modal Reasoning over the Gemini-GPT4o-Hybrid baseline. These results underscore M3-Agent's enhanced ability to maintain character consistency, integrate multimodal information, and deepen its understanding of individuals.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies provide critical insights into the importance of M3-Agent's core components:

- **Impact of Memorization Model (Table 6)**:
 - Replacing M3-Agent's optimized memorization model (`memory-7b-sft`) with prompt-based commercial models (`memory-gemini-prompt` or `memory-7b-prompt`) leads to noticeable accuracy drops (2.0-11.0% across benchmarks), indicating the superior quality of memory generated by the fine-tuned M3-Agent memorization module.
 - Crucially, removing **character identity equivalence** (a core feature for consistency) from memory generation results in substantial performance degradation (e.g., 11.2% drop on M3-Bench-robot).
 - The removal of **semantic memory** completely leads to the most significant drops in accuracy (17.1% on M3-Bench-robot, 19.2% on M3-Bench-web, 13.1% on VideoMME-long), demonstrating its critical role in enabling higher-level reasoning.

- **Impact of Control Method (Table 7)**:
 - The **DAPO reinforcement learning algorithm** for control consistently outperforms GRPO and prompt-based approaches.
 - RL training significantly boosts performance: `control-32b-rl` improves accuracy by 10.0%, 8.0%, and 9.3% over its prompt-based counterpart (`control-32b-prompt`) on M3-Bench-robot, M3-Bench-web, and VideoMME-long, respectively. This highlights the effectiveness of RL in optimizing multi-turn reasoning and memory retrieval.
 - Both **inter-turn instructions** (which guide the agent between reasoning steps) and the **reasoning mode** (the explicit step-by-step thought process) are shown to be critical. Removing inter-turn instructions causes accuracy drops of 5.8-10.5%, while disabling reasoning leads to declines of 8.8-11.7%.

#### 4.4. Case Studies (Appendix G)

- **Memorization**: A comparison between `memory-7b-sft` and `memory-gemini-prompt` shows that M3-Agent generates more detailed episodic memory, more accurate identity equivalence, and richer, proactively generated semantic knowledge about characters and environments.
- **Control**: A detailed trajectory demonstrates M3-Agent's multi-turn reasoning. When asked about Tomasz's imagination, the agent first identifies his character ID, then attempts a direct query about his personality, and when that fails, generates a more targeted query about "creative problem-solving methods" based on his role as CTO, successfully retrieving relevant semantic memory to form an answer.

#### 4.5. Hard Cases

The paper acknowledges that M3-Bench remains challenging, identifying two categories of hard cases:
- **Fine-grained details**: Questions requiring precise extraction of minute details (e.g., "Who wants to eat the ham sausage?").
- **Spatial reasoning**: Questions demanding an understanding of spatial layouts and changes over time (e.g., "Where can the robot get the snacks?"). These suggest avenues for future research in selective memorization and richer visual memory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The M3-Agent framework represents a significant step forward in the development of truly intelligent and autonomous multimodal AI agents. Its contributions carry profound implications for both fundamental AI research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Advancing Multimodal AI Agents

By introducing a novel framework that integrates continuous perception, entity-centric multimodal long-term memory, and multi-turn reinforcement learning-based reasoning, M3-Agent pushes the boundaries of what multimodal agents can achieve. It effectively addresses critical limitations of prior work, particularly the challenges of managing infinitely long multimodal streams, maintaining long-term consistency of entities, and constructing robust world knowledge from experience. This work lays a foundation for agents that can learn and adapt over prolonged interactions, moving closer to human-like cognitive capabilities.

#### 5.2. Enabling More Capable and Autonomous Applications

The practical implications of M3-Agent are substantial, especially for embodied AI and robotics. The ability of an agent to "see, listen, remember, and reason" continuously over long periods is paramount for developing highly autonomous and context-aware systems:
- **Household Robots**: The initial motivation of a household robot understanding user preferences and routines without explicit instructions becomes more attainable. Such robots could provide personalized assistance, manage complex environments, and even anticipate needs, significantly enhancing daily life.
- **Intelligent Assistants**: Advanced multimodal memory could power next-generation virtual assistants capable of remembering past conversations, visual interactions, and preferences across various devices and timeframes.
- **Long-Term Scene Understanding**: Applications in surveillance, monitoring, or even interactive educational systems could benefit from agents that can build a cumulative understanding of dynamic scenes and provide high-level insights.

#### 5.3. A Foundational Benchmark for Memory-Based Reasoning

The introduction of M3-Bench is a critical contribution to the research community. By focusing on higher-level cognitive abilities such as person understanding, general knowledge extraction, and cross-modal reasoning, M3-Bench fills a significant gap in existing Long Video Question Answering (LVQA) benchmarks. It provides a challenging and realistic evaluation platform for future multimodal agent research, driving innovation towards capabilities essential for real-world deployment rather than solely focusing on low-level perceptual tasks. The inclusion of robot-perspective videos further grounds the benchmark in practical agent scenarios.

#### 5.4. Insights for Future Research and Design

The detailed ablation studies offer invaluable insights into the design of future multimodal memory systems and agent architectures. The clear demonstration of the critical roles played by semantic memory, character identity equivalence, and reinforcement learning-driven multi-turn reasoning provides a roadmap for optimizing agent performance. The identification of "hard cases" related to fine-grained details and spatial reasoning also highlights specific areas for targeted future research, such as developing more selective attention mechanisms for memory formation and incorporating richer visual representations for spatial cognition.

#### 5.5. Open Science Contribution

The commitment to making the model, code, and data publicly available (via GitHub and project page) significantly enhances the impact of this work. It fosters reproducibility, encourages community engagement, and provides resources for other researchers to build upon M3-Agent's advancements, accelerating progress in the field of multimodal AI agents.

In conclusion, M3-Agent represents a robust and well-validated framework that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in multimodal AI agents. Its principled approach to long-term memory and reasoning, coupled with a novel evaluation benchmark, is poised to inspire and guide future research towards the realization of truly intelligent and autonomous AI entities.
