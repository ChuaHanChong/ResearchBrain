# villa-X: Enhancing Latent Action Modeling in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research and collaborating universities introduce `villa-X`, a framework that enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by developing physically grounded latent actions and integrating them via a joint diffusion policy. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across various simulation benchmarks and real-world robot manipulation tasks, exhibiting strong zero-shot generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- The scarcity of large-scale, high-quality robot data with corresponding action labels limits the training of robust Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Existing latent action models, primarily based on visual signals, often produce physically ungrounded representations, hindering effective knowledge transfer to real robot control.
- Current methods for integrating latent actions into VLA pre-training lack structured and effective mechanisms for transferring information to low-level robot policies.

## Method

- A Latent Action Model (**LAM**) is developed with a proprioceptive ==Forward Dynamics Model== (proprio-FDM) as an auxiliary decoder, compelling latent tokens to emphasize physical dynamics alongside visual changes.
- An ==embodiment context vector== is incorporated into the **LAM**'s proprio-FDM to disambiguate heterogeneous robot morphologies and control frequencies present in large datasets.
- An ACTor Module (**ACT**) employs a ==joint diffusion framework==, explicitly modeling both latent and robot actions, where robot actions are conditioned on predicted latent plans, observation, and language features.

## Results

- `villa-X` achieved state-of-the-art performance on the SIMPLER benchmark, with an average success rate of **77.7%** for the Google robot and **62.5%** for the WidowX robot, surpassing prior VLA and latent-action methods.
- The framework demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization, successfully planning and executing tasks on unseen Realman and XArm robots (the latter without dexterous-hand pretraining) with novel objects and open-vocabulary commands.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the proprioceptive FDM enhanced latent action quality, leading to improved downstream policy performance, and the embodiment context vector improved reconstruction losses and generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physically grounding latent actions with proprioceptive feedback significantly improves their quality and utility for low-level robot control, bridging abstract visual changes with concrete physical dynamics.
> - A joint diffusion framework, which explicitly factors the policy into latent and robot action experts, provides a more structured and potent mechanism for transferring planning knowledge to robot execution.
> - Leveraging diverse, action-free human videos through physically grounded latent actions enables robust zero-shot generalization to unseen robot embodiments and open-vocabulary instructions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards developing general-purpose agents capable of performing a wide array of tasks in complex, unstructured environments, often guided by human language instructions. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a prominent paradigm for achieving this, allowing robots to learn manipulation policies that can interpret natural language and generalize across various scenarios. These models typically leverage pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and adapt them for action prediction.

A key challenge in training robust VLA models is the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality robot data with corresponding action labels, especially compared to the abundance of human video data or web images. Latent actions have gained traction as a promising solution to bridge this data gap. Latent actions are abstract, compact representations of motion between successive frames, derived from both robot and human video data. By converting diverse motion types into a unified "pseudo-action label" space, latent actions enable VLA models to learn from readily available, action-free human videos, thereby enriching robot policy training through imitation learning.

`villa-X` (Vision-Language-Latent-Action) explicitly positions itself within this landscape by addressing two fundamental limitations of existing latent action modeling approaches:
1. **Physical Ungrounding of Latent Actions:** Prior latent action models primarily compress visual signals. However, subtle but crucial physical movements (e.g., end-effector rotations, gripper actions) may not manifest as significant pixel changes, leading to physically ungrounded latent actions that hinder effective knowledge transfer to real robot control.
2. **Ineffective Integration into VLA Pre-training:** While latent actions are increasingly used, their integration into VLA pre-training often lacks a structured and effective mechanism for information transfer, limiting their full potential. Existing methods might rely on latent actions only for pre-trained weight initialization (e.g., LAPA) or treat them as separate planners (e.g., Go-1), potentially leading to issues like teacher-forcing inconsistencies or insufficient context.

`villa-X` advances the state-of-the-art by proposing innovations in both how latent actions are learned and how they are incorporated, aiming to establish a more principled and scalable paradigm for generalizable robot manipulation policies. It builds upon and extends approaches from works like LAPA, GR00T, Go-1, and Moto-GPT, aiming for tighter integration and more robust reasoning by providing physically grounded latent actions and a novel joint diffusion policy architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind `villa-X` is to significantly improve the utility and effectiveness of latent actions in learning generalizable robot manipulation policies within the VLA framework. The authors articulate two pivotal questions that drive their investigation:
1. **How to better learn latent actions?** The objective here is to overcome the limitation of existing latent action models that primarily rely on visual changes for compression. While visual changes often correlate with motion, they can overlook subtle yet critical physical dynamics (e.g., specific end-effector rotations or gripper movements) that are crucial for precise robot control. This leads to latent actions that are "physically ungrounded," making knowledge transfer to robot policy training less effective.
2. **How to more effectively integrate latent actions into VLA pre-training?** Current methods for incorporating latent actions vary, but often lack a robust and structured mechanism for transferring the valuable information encoded in these latent representations to the low-level robot control policies. The goal is to design an integration strategy that facilitates a tighter and more efficient flow of information, enabling the robot to leverage latent plans for improved control and generalization.

In pursuit of these objectives, `villa-X` aims to enable robot policies to:
- **Achieve better physical grounding:** By incorporating proprioceptive feedback during latent action learning, the model should learn latent actions that are inherently more aligned with actual robot physical dynamics.
- **Facilitate effective knowledge transfer:** The improved learning and integration of latent actions should lead to better transfer of learned skills from diverse (including action-free human) datasets to various robot embodiments.
- **Demonstrate strong zero-shot generalization:** The ultimate goal is for the `villa-X` framework to produce policies that can generalize zero-shot to unseen robot embodiments, novel tasks, and open-vocabulary symbolic understanding, without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Establish state-of-the-art performance:** The culmination of these improvements should result in superior performance across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic setups.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The `villa-X` framework is comprised of two core components designed to address the aforementioned challenges: the Latent Action Model (LAM) and the ACTor Module (ACT). Training proceeds in three stages: LAM pretraining, ACT pretraining, and embodiment-specific finetuning.

#### 3.1. Latent Action Model (LAM)

The LAM is responsible for inferring physically grounded latent actions from observation pairs. It extends traditional visual-only latent action models by incorporating proprioceptive grounding and disambiguating heterogeneous embodiments.

- **Traditional LAM Limitations:** Existing LAMs typically use an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) to predict a latent token `z_t` from a frame pair `(o_t, o_{t+K})` and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) to reconstruct the future observation `hat{o}_{t+K}` from `(o_t, z_t)`. This ensures visual consistency but neglects physical dynamics.
- **Proprioceptive Grounding Innovation:** `villa-X` introduces a **proprioceptive Forward Dynamics Model (proprio-FDM)** as an auxiliary decoder. This module predicts future robot proprioceptive states `(hat{q}_{t+1}..., hat{q}_{t+K})` and actions `(hat{a}_{t+1}..., hat{a}_{t+K})` given the current state `q_t` and latent `z_t`. By optimizing visual and proprioceptive forecasting jointly, the latent tokens are compelled to emphasize physical dynamics alongside visual changes, making them "better grounded." This module is generic and could be extended to other structural cues.
- **Disambiguating Heterogeneous Embodiments:** Large-scale datasets often contain data from robots with different morphologies and control frequencies. To prevent latent actions from encoding embodiment-specific features, `villa-X` conditions the proprio-FDM on an **embodiment context vector `c_e`**. This vector is derived from learnable embeddings of the dataset ID and sinusoidal features of the control frequency, allowing the model to separate embodiment-specific dynamics while maintaining latent action consistency across datasets.
- **Implementation Details:** The LAM uses a Spatial–Temporal Transformer for the IDM, a Vector Quantization (VQ) module (codebook size 32) for discretization (though continuous codebook centers are used downstream), a 12-layer Vision Transformer for the visual FDM, and a 2-layer MLP for the proprio-FDM. The overall loss includes image reconstruction, proprioceptive prediction, and vector-quantization commitments. For human videos lacking proprioceptive labels, the proprio term is omitted.

#### 3.2. ACTor Module (ACT)

The ACT module is designed to effectively leverage the learned latent actions by explicitly modeling both latent and robot actions within a joint diffusion framework.

- **Joint Diffusion Framework:** `villa-X` factorizes the policy into two conditional distributions: a robot action expert (`pi_robot`) conditioned on latent actions, observations, language, proprioceptive state, and embodiment context, and a latent action expert (`pi_latent`) conditioned on observations and language. This explicit factorization treats latent actions as a distinct mid-level representation, bridging high-level vision-language prompts with low-level robot actions.
- **Architecture:** The ACT module (Figure 2 in the paper) comprises three experts:
 - **VLM:** A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (PaliGemma) encodes visual-language inputs into high-level features.
 - **ACT-latent:** A latent action expert predicts sequences of latent action tokens for mid-level planning, conditioned on VLM features.
 - **ACT-robot:** A robot action expert generates low-level action chunks, conditioned on VLM features, predicted latents, proprioceptive states, and embodiment context.
 - Wrist camera input is optionally incorporated via a cross-attention head.
- **Attention Masking Strategies:** To ensure the robot action expert doesn't overly rely on latent actions or learn trivial shortcuts, `villa-X` employs a stochastic masking strategy. During training, 50% of the time, all robot-to-latent attention is masked; otherwise, 50% of the latent tokens are randomly masked. This regularization promotes robustness.
- **Joint Diffusion Modeling:** The joint distribution of future robot and latent actions is modeled using a conditional flow matching framework. A network `v_theta_tau` is trained to minimize a flow matching loss, predicting a denoising vector field. This effectively trains both experts within a unified probabilistic framework.
- **Implementation Details:** The VLM is based on PaliGemma (3B parameters). Both `ACT-latent` and `ACT-robot` are 18-layer Transformer networks (300M parameters each), mirroring PaliGemma's design, and trained from scratch. Different beta distributions are used for sampling `tau` for latent and robot actions, biasing latent actions towards noisier regimes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on `villa-X` aimed to validate the quality of its latent actions, the effectiveness of its actor module, its zero-shot generalization capabilities, and its overall performance against state-of-the-art baselines in both simulation and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Improved Latent Action Quality

- **Proprioceptive Grounding (LAM):** Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the proprio-FDM module.
 - **Probing Experiment:** A 3-layer MLP trained to predict robot actions from frozen latent actions (on the LIBERO dataset, unseen during LAM training) showed that the `w/pp` (with proprio-FDM) variant produced more samples with lower maximum L1 error compared to `wo/pp` (without proprio-FDM) (Figure 3, Appendix D.3). This demonstrates `proprio-FDM`'s ability to capture more useful information for low-level robot actions.
 - **Policy Pre-training:** Policies pre-trained with `w/pp` latent actions significantly outperformed those using `wo/pp` latent actions in the SIMPLER simulation environment (Table 1), validating the impact of proprioceptive grounding on downstream policy performance.
 - **Embodiment Context:** Further ablations in Appendix D.3 confirmed that the embodiment context vector `c_e` improved both visual and proprioceptive FDM reconstruction losses and enhanced zero-shot generalization of latent actions to a novel Realman robot arm, especially for rotation and gripper actions.

#### 4.2. Effective Actor Module Design

- **Leveraging Latent Actions:** `villa-X`'s ACT module design was compared against alternative latent action integration strategies using the same base architecture.
 - `villa-X` significantly outperformed `LAPA-style` (two-stage pre-training, latent actions for VLM initialization) and `GO-1-style` (separate autoregressive latent planner) models in SIMPLER tasks (Table 1). This confirms that `villa-X`'s joint diffusion process, conditioning robot action prediction on latent actions, achieves a more effective and structured transfer of information.
 - A baseline `wo/LAM` (no latent actions) showed significantly worse performance, highlighting the essential role of latent action pre-training.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Generalization for Latent Actor Module

- **Unseen Embodiments and Open-Vocabulary Understanding:** A real-world visualization experiment (Figure 4) demonstrated `ACT-latent`'s strong zero-shot generalization.
 - Using a Realman robot arm (unseen during training) and novel open-vocabulary symbol cards (e.g., "touch the corn"), `ACT-latent` successfully generated sequences of latent actions.
 - These latent plans, when rendered into videos by a world model, showed correct task execution, confirming `ACT-latent`'s ability to:
 - Identify and control an unseen robot arm (embodiment generalization).
 - Comprehend and interact with novel symbolic concepts (open-vocabulary understanding).

#### 4.4. State-of-the-Art Performance in Simulation

- **SIMPLER Benchmark:** `villa-X` achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across diverse simulation tasks in the SIMPLER benchmark (Table 2).
 - It secured the highest average success rates on both the Google robot (77.7%) and the WidowX robot (62.5%).
 - `villa-X` consistently surpassed other VLA models (e.g., RT-1-X, Octo-base, OpenVLA, RoboVLMs, `pi_0`) and other latent-action based methods (MoTo, LAPA), demonstrating the superiority of its approach to leveraging unlabelled video data.
 - The "Ours w/o latent" ablation showed a significant performance drop, reinforcing that the latent-action expert is critical for `villa-X`'s gains.

#### 4.5. Superior Performance on Real-world Robots

- **Realman Arm with Gripper:** `villa-X` outperformed all baselines in five tasks on a 6-DoF Realman RM 75 robot arm with an Inspire gripper (Table 4). This included both in-task evaluation and generalization evaluation where block color and table cover were changed, demonstrating strong real-world transfer and robustness to visual variations.
- **XArm with XHand Dexterous Hand:** Despite no dexterous-hand data being used during pretraining, `villa-X` achieved superior results on a 7-DoF XArm with a 12-DoF XHand (Table 3). This was demonstrated across five complex manipulation tasks, including both "seen" (randomized objects/distractors) and "unseen" (novel objects/backgrounds) conditions, highlighting its embodiment transfer ability.
- **LIBERO Benchmark (Appendix H):** `villa-X` also achieved SOTA performance across all four LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), indicating strong knowledge transfer and generalization capabilities for multitask and lifelong robot learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

`villa-X` introduces a significant advancement in the development of generalizable robot manipulation policies, establishing itself as a "principled and scalable paradigm." Its main contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Latent Action Quality through Physical Grounding:** By integrating a proprioceptive Forward Dynamics Model (proprio-FDM), `villa-X` moves beyond purely visual signals to ground latent actions in physical robot dynamics. This innovation ensures that the learned latent representations are more meaningful for actual robot control, bridging the gap between abstract visual changes and concrete physical actions. This is crucial for precise and reliable robot behavior.
2. **More Effective Latent Action Integration:** The novel joint diffusion framework, which explicitly models both latent and robot actions and conditions robot action generation on latent action plans, represents a more structured and potent mechanism for information transfer. This design avoids limitations of prior integration strategies, leading to more robust reasoning and improved control policies.
3. **Strong Zero-Shot Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated ability of the latent action expert to plan effectively in a zero-shot fashion for unseen embodiments and open-vocabulary symbolic understanding is a major breakthrough. This implies that `villa-X` can leverage its pre-trained knowledge to control entirely new robotic platforms and comprehend novel task instructions without requiring extensive re-training or fine-tuning, significantly reducing the cost and effort of deploying robots in new scenarios.
4. **Superior Performance Across Diverse Benchmarks:** `villa-X` consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance in both high-fidelity simulation environments (SIMPLER, LIBERO) and challenging real-world robotic setups (gripper-based and dexterous hand manipulation). This validates the robustness and effectiveness of the `villa-X` framework across a broad spectrum of tasks and robot morphologies.
5. **Scalable Learning from Heterogeneous Data:** By enabling effective learning from a mixture of action-free human videos and robot trajectories, `villa-X` provides a scalable approach to policy learning. It addresses the critical data scarcity problem in robotics by efficiently leveraging abundant, readily available data, thereby accelerating the development of more capable and autonomous robots.

**Potential Impact:**
`villa-X` has the potential to significantly impact the field of embodied AI and robotics by:
- **Accelerating Robot Deployment:** The zero-shot generalization capabilities could drastically reduce the time and resources required to deploy robots in new environments or for new tasks, making robotic solutions more accessible and practical.
- **Enabling More Generalist Robots:** By learning from diverse data and achieving broad generalization, `villa-X` pushes towards the creation of truly generalist robots that can adapt to a wide range of instructions and physical interactions.
- **Fostering Future Research:** The framework provides a strong foundation for future research, particularly in exploring how to further enhance the planning capabilities of the latent expert (e.g., integrating critics with prior VLM knowledge for trajectory rejection) or extending the physical grounding to other structural cues like end-effector keypoints.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge one key limitation: the full potential of the proposed latent expert for future planning (through both visual and proprioceptive state planning) is not entirely explored. Future work could focus on learning a critic from foundation vision-language models to sample multiple latent plans and reject those that do not align with language instructions, thereby improving the robustness of planning and control. This indicates a clear path for further developing the cognitive and decision-making aspects of the `ViLLA` framework.
