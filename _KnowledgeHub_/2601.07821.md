# Failure-Aware RL: Reliable Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning with Self-Recovery for Real-World Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Failure-Aware RL (FARL) introduces a framework for reliable offline-to-online reinforcement learning, mitigating human-intervention-requiring failures during real-world robotic manipulation by integrating a world-model-based safety critic and a pre-trained recovery policy. The system reduced failure episodes by an average of 43.6% in simulation and achieved dramatic reductions on a real Franka Emika Panda robot, while maintaining or improving task performance.

## Problem

- Online fine-tuning of pre-trained policies in real-world reinforcement learning often leads to "Intervention-requiring Failures" (IR Failures) due to inherent exploration randomness.
- IR Failures are costly, demanding human intervention, causing damage, and significantly hindering the practical deployment and scalability of robotic systems.
- Existing safe RL methods often prematurely constrain exploration or are incompatible with the offline-to-online paradigm, leading to suboptimal performance when fine-tuning pre-trained policies.

## Method

- **FARL** pre-trains a task policy, a ==recovery policy== from specific recovery demonstrations, and a ==latent world model== augmented with a ==constraint prediction head== to forecast future failures.
- During online fine-tuning, the world model predicts the safety of an action sampled from the task policy by rolling out future steps.
- If an action is predicted as risky, it is discarded and replaced with a safer alternative sampled from the pre-trained recovery policy, guiding exploration away from failures.

## Results

- FARL achieved an average **43.6%** reduction in failure episodes across four FailureBench simulation tasks and demonstrated a dramatic reduction in real-world IR Failures on a Franka Emika Panda robot (e.g., from 10 to 2 failures in Franka Disturbed Push).
- The framework maintained or improved average task returns compared to baseline methods like Uni-O4 in simulation and showed enhanced performance with lower standard deviations in real-world experiments.
- FARL significantly outperformed state-of-the-art online safe RL algorithms (PPO-Lagrangian, P3O, CPO) by over **800%** in average returns when applied to fine-tuning pre-trained policies, highlighting the incompatibility of traditional safe RL with this paradigm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly predicting future constraint violations with a world-model-based safety critic is crucial for effective risk assessment in dynamic online fine-tuning, outperforming simpler safety metrics.
> - Leveraging a pre-trained recovery policy, derived from expert recovery demonstrations, provides a more robust and effective safety mechanism than implicit planning from a world model alone.
> - A "failure-aware" approach can achieve a balance between drastically reducing intervention-requiring failures and maintaining or improving overall task performance and robustness.
