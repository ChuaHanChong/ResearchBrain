# Implicit Neural-Representation Learning for Elastic Deformable-Object Manipulations

> [!summary] Summary
> KAIST researchers introduce INR-DOM, a framework combining implicit neural representations with contrastive learning for robotic manipulation of elastic objects, achieving 40.3% higher task success rates compared to existing methods while demonstrating robust performance under occlusion through a two-stage learning process of reconstruction and policy optimization.

## Problem

- Traditional methods struggle to represent deformable objects due to infinite degrees of freedom, non-linear dynamics, and partial observations
- Learning effective manipulation policies for elastic objects is sample inefficient and fails to generalize well across different tasks
- Prior particle-based and graph-based object representations lack the continuous, fine-grained surface detail needed to accurately model complex elastic deformations

## Method

- Two-stage learning approach combining reconstruction-based pre-training with ==contrastive learning== for policy optimization
- ==Partial-to-complete VAE architecture== using ==signed distance functions (SDFs)== for continuous surface representation
- Novel ==query-key selection strategy== for identifying similar temporal sequences during training

## Results

- Outperformed baseline methods by **40.3%** on task success rates across sealing, installation, and disentanglement tasks, while achieving faster learning convergence through improved representation quality and sample efficiency
- Successfully transferred learned policies to real-world manipulation tasks using a Franka Emika Panda robot
- Demonstrated superior occlusion recovery compared to existing methods even for previously unseen objects

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit neural representations can effectively capture complex deformable object geometries while being robust to occlusion
> - Contrastive learning helps refine representations to be more task-relevant and improves sample efficiency
> - Combining shape and structure reconstruction losses enables better handling of partial observations

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a significant challenge in robotics: manipulating deformable objects (DOM). The work builds upon existing research in several areas:

- **Deformable Object Manipulation (DOM):** DOM is a well-established field, with previous work focusing on tasks like grasping, folding, wearing, and knotting. This paper contributes to this field by proposing a novel representation learning method specifically tailored for elastic objects.
- **Representation Learning:** The paper leverages recent advances in representation learning, particularly implicit neural representations (INRs) and contrastive learning. It addresses the limitations of traditional representations (particle-based, graph-based) by using SDFs for continuous and detailed surface representation.
- **Implicit Neural Representations (INRs):** INRs, especially signed distance functions (SDFs), have gained traction for representing complex geometries. This work extends the use of SDFs in DOM by incorporating a two-stage learning process to improve occlusion robustness and task-relevance.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The paper integrates INR learning with RL to enable robots to learn manipulation policies. It addresses the sample inefficiency of RL by using contrastive learning to refine representations and improve exploration.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The research focuses on training in simulation and transferring the learned policies to real-world robotic systems. This is a crucial aspect of practical robotics research, and the paper demonstrates the applicability of the proposed method in real-world scenarios.

The paper distinguishes itself from existing work by:

- **Focus on Elastic Objects:** Many previous DOM studies have focused on cloth or other deformable materials. This paper specifically addresses the challenges of manipulating highly elastic objects like rubber bands.
- **Occlusion Robustness:** The proposed method emphasizes learning representations that are robust to partial observations and self-occlusions, which are common in DOM tasks.
- **Two-Stage Learning:** The two-stage learning approach (pre-training with reconstruction and fine-tuning with contrastive learning) is a key contribution, allowing the model to learn both general and task-specific representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the research are:

- **Develop a consistent and occlusion-robust state representation for deformable objects from visual observations.** The motivation is to overcome the limitations of traditional representations, which struggle with the infinite degrees of freedom, non-linear dynamics, and partial observations associated with deformable objects.
- **Develop an effective representation-refining approach using contrastive learning to capture task-relevant state information for RL.** The motivation is to improve the sample efficiency and generalization ability of RL-based manipulation policies.
- **Demonstrate that the proposed method improves convergence stability in policy learning and success rate for DOM tasks, in both simulations and real-world settings.** The ultimate goal is to enable robots to reliably and efficiently manipulate deformable objects in real-world environments.

The underlying motivation is to advance the capabilities of robots in tasks that require interaction with deformable objects, which are prevalent in various applications such as manufacturing, healthcare, and logistics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes a novel method called INR-DOM, which combines implicit neural representations (INRs) and contrastive learning for deformable object manipulation. The methodology consists of two main stages:

- **Stage I: Reconstruction-Based Pre-training:**
 - **Network Architecture:** A partial-to-complete variational autoencoder (VAE) is used. It consists of a PointNet-based encoder, a hypernetwork decoder, and an implicit neural SDF network.
 - **Loss Functions:** The network is trained using a combination of shape and structure reconstruction losses (SDF loss and skeleton loss) and regularization losses (KL divergence loss, weight regularization loss, and consistency loss). The shape and structure reconstruction losses make the representations robust to occlusion.
- **Stage II: Policy Learning with Fine-tuning:**
 - **Reinforcement Learning:** A deep RL framework (Soft Actor-Critic) is used to learn a manipulation policy. The state space includes proprioceptive information and the latent representation from the pre-trained encoder.
 - **Contrastive Learning:** Contrastive learning is integrated to improve sample efficiency in RL. A novel query-key selection strategy for time series data is introduced to capture similarities between temporally correlated sequences.
 - Query-Key Selection Strategy: The method computes top-M similar episodes using minimal start-and-goal embedding distance, which is based on dot products of start and goal embeddings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents both quantitative and qualitative results to demonstrate the effectiveness of INR-DOM:

- **Quantitative Results (Simulation):**
 - **Occlusion Recovery:** INR-DOM achieves the lowest reconstruction errors compared to baseline methods in completing partially observed rubber bands, even for unseen objects.
 - **Manipulation Tasks:** INR-DOM outperforms baseline methods in three simulated DOM tasks (sealing, installation, and disentanglement), achieving significantly higher task success rates. The authors specifically note an average task success increase of 40.3% when compared to ACID (Action-Conditional Implicit visual Dynamics), which was the next best approach.
 - **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of both pre-training and contrastive learning for achieving optimal performance.
 - **Learning Curve Analysis:** INR-DOM shows superior learning efficiency compared to baseline methods, achieving higher accumulated rewards during training.
- **Qualitative Results (Real-World):**
 - INR-DOM successfully manipulates a Franka Emika Panda robot to perform the three DOM tasks in real-world settings, achieving a high task-success rate.
 - Comparison with SERL (a representative image-based manipulation method) demonstrates the advantage of INR-DOM in distinguishing between twisted directions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research has significant implications for the field of robotics:

- **Improved Deformable Object Manipulation:** The proposed method enables robots to more effectively manipulate deformable objects, which is crucial for various real-world applications.
- **Enhanced Occlusion Robustness:** The ability to handle partial observations and self-occlusions makes the method more practical for real-world scenarios where visual information may be limited.
- **Sample-Efficient Learning:** The integration of contrastive learning improves the sample efficiency of RL, reducing the amount of training data required to learn effective manipulation policies.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The demonstration of INR-DOM on a real robot validates the potential of the method for deployment in real-world applications.

The potential impact of this research includes:

- **Automation of complex tasks:** Enabling robots to automate tasks involving deformable objects, such as assembly, packaging, and surgical procedures.
- **Improved human-robot collaboration:** Allowing robots to assist humans in tasks that require manipulation of deformable objects, such as healthcare and manufacturing.
- **Advancement of AI and robotics research:** Providing a novel and effective approach for representation learning and control in robotics, which can inspire further research in this area.

#### 5.1. Areas for Future Research

The authors acknowledge several limitations and areas for future research:

- **Extending to other Deformable Objects:** The current work focuses on linear objects. Future research could explore the application of INR-DOM to more complex deformable objects.
- **Improving Segmentation:** The method's performance depends on the accuracy of the point-cloud segmentation model. Future work should focus on improving the robustness of the segmentation process.
- **Multi-Task Learning:** The current fine-tuning process is task-specific. Future research could explore multi-task learning approaches to improve the generalization ability of the model.
- **Dynamic Interaction Modeling:** The work does not extensively investigate the dynamics of interaction between the robot and deformable objects. Future research could focus on modeling these interactions for more complex manipulation tasks.
