# ReinforceGen: Hybrid Skill Policies with Automated Data Generation and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> REINFORCEGEN introduces a framework that enables robust robotic manipulation across long-horizon tasks by synergistically combining automated data generation, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning. The system achieves an 80% success rate on challenging Robosuite tasks using visuomotor control, demonstrating improved performance and efficiency by systematically fine-tuning hierarchical policy components through online interaction.

## Problem

- Long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks are difficult due to compounding errors, sparse rewards, and the need for precise, contact-rich interactions.
- Imitation Learning (IL) methods are limited by data scarcity and struggle with out-of-distribution states, while Reinforcement Learning (RL) faces significant exploration challenges for complex tasks.
- Prior automated data generation techniques are open-loop and bounded by the quality and diversity of initial human demonstrations, preventing the discovery of truly novel behaviors.

## Method

- The framework decomposes long-horizon tasks into hierarchical stages, each with an initiation pose predictor, a skill policy, and a termination predictor.
- It uses automated, object-centric data generation from a minimal set (e.g., 10) of human demonstrations to pre-train these hierarchical policy components via imitation learning.
- An iterative online phase fine-tunes each policy component using real-time replanning for initiation, residual reinforcement learning for skills, and a success probability-based filtering mechanism for terminations.

## Results

- ReinforceGen achieved an 80% success rate across challenging long-horizon Robosuite tasks (e.g., Nut Assembly, Threading, Three Piece, Coffee) using visuomotor control in the highest reset range setting.
- It demonstrated an 89% average relative performance increase over baseline Hybrid Skill Policies (HSP) without state observability and remained competitive with baselines using privileged state information.
- Ablation studies showed real-time replanning for initiation poses improved HSP success rates by 22% (a 50% relative improvement), and skill policy fine-tuning contributed a 24.41% increase in task completion rates and a 9.74% reduction in steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating online exploration and environmental feedback through reinforcement learning into automated data generation allows agents to learn beyond initial demonstrations, leading to more robust and efficient behaviors.
> - Systematic, component-wise fine-tuning of a hierarchical policy (initiation, skill, termination) through real-time adaptation and residual RL significantly improves performance in complex, contact-rich tasks.
> - The combination of a privileged teacher for initiation pose prediction and filtering false positive terminations is crucial for improving cross-stage causal effects and overall task success with partial observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the long-standing and critical challenge of **long-horizon manipulation** in robotics. This domain is particularly difficult due to the compounding errors over time, the need for precise contact-rich interactions, and the inherent complexity of obtaining sufficient, high-quality training data.

The current research landscape for robot learning is largely dominated by two paradigms:

- **Imitation Learning (IL):** This approach trains robots by mimicking human demonstrations. While effective for many tasks, IL suffers from several limitations for long-horizon problems. Collecting a diverse and extensive dataset of human demonstrations is time-consuming and expensive, often requiring teleoperation. Furthermore, IL policies can struggle with out-of-distribution states, leading to compounding errors when they deviate from the demonstrated trajectories.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL allows agents to learn through trial and error, leveraging environmental feedback (rewards). However, in long-horizon tasks, especially with sparse reward signals, RL faces significant exploration challenges. The agent may struggle to discover successful behaviors within a vast state-action space, leading to inefficient learning or even outright failure.

To mitigate these issues, several hybrid approaches have emerged:

- **Combining IL and RL:** Researchers have explored using IL to provide a warm-start or initial policy, which is then fine-tuned or improved using RL (Nair et al., 2017; Johannink et al., 2018). This leverages the initial guidance from demonstrations while allowing RL to explore and refine behaviors. The paper explicitly references SPIRE (Zhou et al., 2024), co-authored by Zihan Zhou, which fine-tunes IL-learned skills with RL, indicating a direct lineage.
- **Automated Data Generation:** A parallel trend focuses on augmenting or synthetically generating demonstrations to overcome the data scarcity for IL. This includes:
 - **Object-centric demonstration adaptation:** Methods like MimicGen (Mandlekar et al., 2023b) and SkillMimicGen (Garrett et al., 2024) generate new demonstrations by transforming and replaying a small set of human demonstrations based on new object configurations. ReinforceGen directly builds upon this foundational work.
 - **Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) for data generation:** Other approaches use TAMP to generate symbolic or low-level demonstrations, although these often struggle with contact-rich manipulation.
- **Hierarchical Decomposition and Hybrid Planning-Learning:** Breaking down complex tasks into simpler sub-goals or skill segments (e.g., using Task and Motion Planning, TAMP) significantly reduces the complexity. Learned policies are then integrated into these hierarchical frameworks, focusing learning efforts on specific, precision-intensive skill segments while using planners for transitions (Wang ets al., 2021; Mandlekar et al., 2023a; Garrett et al., 2024).

ReinforceGen positions itself at the intersection of these advancements. It explicitly addresses a key limitation of prior automated data generation systems: that they are **open-loop and fundamentally bounded by the quality and diversity of the initial human demonstrations**. ReinforceGen breaks this barrier by integrating **online exploration and environmental feedback through reinforcement learning**. By fine-tuning *each component* of a hierarchical policy (initiation, skill, termination) with RL, it allows the system to discover more successful and efficient behaviors that might not be present in the original human demonstrations or their simple transformations. This represents a significant step towards creating more autonomous and robust robot learning systems that can transcend their initial training data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ReinforceGen is to **enable robust and efficient learning of long-horizon, contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks using a minimal number of human demonstrations.** The researchers are motivated by several critical challenges inherent in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Overcoming the Data Bottleneck in Imitation Learning (IL):** Collecting high-quality, diverse, and sufficiently numerous human demonstrations for complex robotic tasks is a major impediment to the scalability of IL. The cost and effort associated with teleoperation platforms and human operators are significant.
2. **Addressing Exploration Challenges in Reinforcement Learning (RL):** For long-horizon tasks, especially those with sparse reward signals (e.g., a single reward upon task completion), traditional RL struggles with efficient exploration. Finding the optimal sequence of actions through random exploration can be prohibitively slow or even impossible.
3. **Improving Beyond the Limitations of Offline Data Generation:** While automated data generation methods (like MimicGen) have shown promise in augmenting datasets, they are inherently limited by the quality and coverage of the initial "source" human demonstrations. These methods are open-loop and do not allow the agent to learn novel, more successful behaviors not present in the original data. The motivation is to allow the agent to **explore and improve beyond existing demonstrations**.
4. **Enhancing Robustness and Generalization:** Agents trained solely on offline data or demonstrations often struggle with out-of-distribution states, leading to brittle policies. The goal is to create policies that are more resilient to minor variations and can generalize better to unseen scenarios by incorporating online feedback.
5. **Developing a Flexible and Hybrid Control Strategy:** Many manipulation tasks involve segments requiring high precision (e.g., inserting a peg) and segments requiring broader movements (e.g., moving to a general location). The motivation is to leverage the strengths of both symbolic planning (for structured movements) and learned policies (for contact-rich, fine-grained control) to reduce the overall learning burden and improve reliability.
6. **Enabling Visuomotor Control with Minimal Supervision:** The ultimate aim is to achieve high success rates for complex tasks using only visual observations and robot proprioception, reducing reliance on privileged state information that is often unavailable in real-world deployments.

In essence, ReinforceGen seeks to bridge the gap between the efficiency of demonstration-based learning and the adaptability of reinforcement learning, specifically by making automated data generation an *active* and *improving* process rather than a static one.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReinforceGen proposes a sophisticated hybrid system that iteratively refines its components using reinforcement learning and online adaptation. The methodology can be broken down into an initial solution phase and an iterative fine-tuning phase:

**Initial Solution (Offline Phase):**

1. **Task Decomposition:** The long-horizon manipulation task is first broken down into a sequence of *n* stages, each with a specific sub-goal. Each stage is further decomposed into a "connect segment" (where the robot moves to an initiation position) and a "skill segment" (where a precision-intensive skill is executed). This hierarchical structure is based on the Hybrid Skill Policy (HSP) framework.
2. **Object-Centric Data Generation:** From a *very small set* (e.g., 10) of human demonstrations, ReinforceGen automatically generates a much larger synthetic dataset (e.g., 1,000+ demonstrations). This process involves transforming and replaying segments of the human demonstrations by adapting them to new object poses in different environment instantiations. This ensures a diverse initial training dataset.
3. **Imitation Learning (Behavior Cloning - BC):** An initial Hybrid Skill Policy (HSP) agent is trained using behavior cloning on the generated dataset. This HSP agent consists of three parameterized components for each skill `ψ_i = ⟨I_θ_i, π_θ_i, T_θ_i⟩`:
 - **Initiation Pose Predictor (I_θ_i):** Predicts the target end-effector pose for the motion planner to reach before a skill begins.
 - **Skill Policy (π_θ_i):** Directly controls the robot's actions during the precision-intensive skill segment.
 - **Termination Predictor (T_θ_i):** A binary classifier that determines when the current stage's sub-goal has been achieved.
4. **Motion Planning:** To connect the skill segments, a motion planner is used. It plans collision-free trajectories to move the robot from its current position to the predicted initiation pose for the next skill.

**Improvement and Fine-tuning (Online Phase with Reinforcement Learning):**

This is the core contribution of ReinforceGen, where each component of the initial HSP policy is systematically improved through online interaction and RL:

1. **Initiation Pose Prediction Fine-tuning (I_θ_i):**
 - **Real-time Replanning:** During the connect segment (motion planning phase), the pose predictor continuously updates its target prediction based on new observations. If the difference between the newly predicted pose and the current planned target exceeds a threshold, the motion planner re-plans to the updated pose. This allows for dynamic correction and adaptation to perception errors.
 - **Student Predictor from Privileged Teacher:** To improve the predictor, a privileged teacher (I_Priv) that has access to ground-truth object state information is used to generate accurate target poses during training. The learned predictor (I_θ_i) is then distilled from this teacher. This process starts by using the privileged teacher to generate trajectories and then transitions to online distillation where the student predictor itself generates trajectories to bridge the distribution gap.
2. **Skill Policy Fine-tuning (π_θ_i):**
 - **Residual Reinforcement Learning:** The initial BC-trained skill policy (π_base) serves as a warm-start. An additional *residual policy* (π_res_θ_i) is trained using an off-the-shelf RL algorithm (DrQ-v2). The final action is `a = π_base(o) + π_res_θ_i(o)`. This approach allows the RL agent to explore "differences" from the base policy, making exploration more efficient and effective, especially with sparse 0-1 rewards derived from the skill's termination condition. A regularization term is added to penalize large deviations of the residual actions.
3. **Termination Classification Fine-tuning (T_θ_i):**
 - **Purging False Positives:** The termination predictor, initially trained with binary cross-entropy, can suffer from inaccuracies, particularly false positives (terminating a stage prematurely). Instead of full RL for termination (which is challenging), ReinforceGen employs a greedy solution. A separate predictor (p_i) is trained to estimate the probability of *overall task success* if the current stage were to terminate at the current state. Hand-crafted termination conditions (T_r_i) are then filtered: a termination is only accepted if p_i(s) exceeds a certain threshold (ϵ_term). This prevents the agent from exploiting premature terminations during RL training.

**Deployment and End-to-End Distillation:**

- **ReinforceGen Hybrid Policy:** The fine-tuned components (initiation predictor, skill policy, termination predictor) are deployed in sequence. The system alternates between motion planning (with real-time replanning) to skill initiation poses and executing the fine-tuned skill policies until termination.
- **End-to-End Visuomotor Policy (Optional):** The successful trajectories generated by the high-performing hybrid ReinforceGen policy can be used as a dataset to train a fully end-to-end visuomotor policy via Behavior Cloning. This eliminates the need for a motion planner at deployment, providing flexibility for different application requirements.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology is evaluated on the Robosuite benchmark, specifically challenging D2 variants (largest initialization range) of tasks like Nut Assembly, Threading, Three Piece, Coffee, and Coffee Preparation, involving up to 5 stages and contact-rich interactions. Observations consist of RGB images from multiple cameras and robot proprioception, ensuring a partially observable setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Robosuite dataset demonstrate the significant efficacy and robustness of the ReinforceGen framework:

- **High Task Success Rates:** ReinforceGen achieves an impressive 80% success rate across all evaluated tasks (Nut Assembly, Threading, Three Piece, Coffee, Coffee Preparation). These include long-horizon tasks with up to five stages and complex contact-rich manipulations, all performed using visuomotor controls in the highest reset range setting.
- **Substantial Performance Improvement over Baselines:**
 - Compared to baseline HSP (Hybrid Skill Policy) without state observability, ReinforceGen achieves an **89% average relative performance increase** in overall success rate. This highlights its ability to compensate for partial observability through its fine-tuning mechanisms.
 - Even when compared to baselines with privileged state information (HSP-Priv + Skill-FT), which represent a near upper-bound on performance, ReinforceGen remains highly competitive. It shows only a maximum deficit of 8% in the challenging Threading task and a mere 2% decrease in the overall success rate, demonstrating its effectiveness despite relying only on observations.
- **Effectiveness of Real-time Replanning:** Ablation studies reveal that the real-time replanning mechanism for the initiation pose predictor is crucial. It increases the overall success rate of HSP by 22% (a **50% relative improvement** from 44.68% to 66.88%) by significantly reducing target prediction errors across all stages.
- **Contribution of Skill Policy Fine-tuning:** The RL-based fine-tuning of skill policies proves highly effective. On average, it contributes to a **24.41% increase in task completion rates** and a **9.74% reduction in the number of steps** required to complete skills, demonstrating improved efficiency. This fine-tuning is particularly impactful in precision-demanding stages, such as the second stage of Threading.
- **Impact of Termination Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning the termination predictor, specifically by rejecting false positives, helps in repairing cross-stage causal effects. For instance, in the Three Piece task, it improved HSP-Priv's success rate from 66.44% to 70.77%, and further to 82.24% when combined with skill fine-tuning. This ensures that stages are terminated appropriately, preventing compounding errors.
- **Viability of Learned Termination Predictors:** The performance of a learned termination predictor, relying only on partial observations, shows only minor drops in success rates compared to an "oracle" termination condition that has access to ground-truth state information. This indicates that the learned predictor is robust and practically viable.
- **End-to-End Distillation Results:** While ReinforceGen can successfully distill its hybrid policy into proficient end-to-end visuomotor agents for some tasks (e.g., Threading, Coffee, showing significant improvements over baselines), it encounters limitations in tasks with long-range transfers and significant rotational movements (Nut Assembly, Three Piece). This is attributed to joint lock-up states in the connect phases, highlighting an area for future improvement.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Noise:** ReinforceGen agents exhibit greater resistance to artificial action noise and trajectory deviations compared to baselines, affirming their improved robustness stemming from RL-based exploration and fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReinforceGen represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Bridging the IL-RL Divide for Scalable Robotics:** The most profound significance lies in its ability to effectively combine the strengths of data-efficient imitation learning (from a handful of human demos) with the exploratory and adaptive power of reinforcement learning. This synergistic approach overcomes the inherent limitations of purely offline data generation, enabling robots to *explore beyond* initial demonstrations and discover more robust and efficient behaviors. This is crucial for scaling robot learning to a wider array of real-world tasks.
2. **Democratizing Robot Learning:** By dramatically reducing the need for extensive human demonstrations (from hundreds or thousands to merely 10), ReinforceGen lowers a major barrier to entry for developing and deploying robotic systems. This makes advanced manipulation capabilities more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited resources for data collection, potentially accelerating innovation in various domains from manufacturing to service robotics.
3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The iterative fine-tuning using online RL, especially the real-time replanning and residual RL for skills, directly addresses the issue of policy brittleness and out-of-distribution state failures. Robots trained with ReinforceGen are inherently more robust to environmental variations and perception errors, leading to more reliable performance in unstructured or semi-structured environments.
4. **Flexible Deployment Strategies:** The framework's ability to support both a hybrid planning-learning control strategy and, optionally, distill into a fully end-to-end visuomotor policy offers considerable flexibility. Users can choose the deployment method that best suits their application's requirements, trading off the need for planning models with the complexity of end-to-end learning. This allows for adaptability to different hardware and software stacks.
5. **Advancement in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Contact-rich manipulation tasks, often requiring fine-grained control and precise interactions, remain a frontier in robotics. ReinforceGen's demonstrated success in these challenging scenarios highlights its contribution to solving a historically difficult class of problems, particularly in visuomotor settings.
6. **Paving the Way for Autonomous Skill Acquisition:** The iterative, self-improving nature of ReinforceGen points towards a future where robots can autonomously refine and expand their skill repertoire with minimal human intervention. This vision is a cornerstone of advanced robotic intelligence.

While the end-to-end distillation still presents challenges for certain task types (e.g., those with large rotational movements leading to joint lock-ups), ReinforceGen lays a strong foundation. Future work could focus on improving the robustness of end-to-end policies, enhancing cross-task generalization of skills, and dealing with even greater levels of partial observability in motion planning, pushing the boundaries of autonomous robot skill learning.
