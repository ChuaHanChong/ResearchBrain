# DexTeleop-0: Force-Aware Bimanual Dexterous Teleoperation with Ego-Centric Perception towards Shared Autonomy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University developed DexTeleop-0, a bimanual dexterous teleoperation framework that integrates ego-centric perception with tactile-driven shared autonomy to translate human intents into force-compliant robotic commands. This system achieved higher task success rates, up to 97% in simulated ball assembly, and maintained safer contact force distributions compared to baselines in various contact-rich and bimanual manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robotic teleoperation systems face an "embodiment gap" and often lack haptic/tactile feedback, making precise, contact-rich manipulation challenging for multi-fingered hands.
- Traditional teleoperation methods like kinesthetic rigs are rigid, exoskeletons are cumbersome, and vision-only systems suffer from occlusions and tracking drift, particularly in bimanual scenarios.
- The absence of force transparency and real-time physical awareness leads to low data collection efficiency and difficulties in safely executing delicate tasks.

## Method

- **DexTeleop-0**, a bimanual dexterous teleoperation framework, combines ego-centric perception (from VR headsets) with a tactile-driven adaptation strategy to generate force-compliant robotic commands.
- A vector-based "==Project-and-Escape==" tracking logic and a constrained non-linear optimization (SLSQP) are used for real-time dexterous hand retargeting, ensuring stable mapping and enforcing contact geometries.
- A contact-rich force-aware shared autonomy layer computes residual actions through a unified Quadratic Programming (QP) problem, integrating localized force tracking and multi-contact force-torque balance to modulate teleoperated commands.

## Results

- DexTeleop-0 consistently demonstrated higher task success rates, achieving **97%** in simulated Ball Assembly and **57.14%** in real-robot Gear Mesh, significantly outperforming baselines.
- The framework effectively regulated contact forces, maintaining safer distributions (e.g., **11.15** "N" in Ball Assembly) and preventing object slippage or damage, which was a common failure mode for other methods.
- In coordinated bimanual tasks like Food Sorting and Tube Operation, DexTeleop-0 exhibited robust stability and higher operational mastery, with up to **74.29%** Stage 4 success in Food Sorting, attributing this to its object-centric force-torque balance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time integration of tactile and force feedback through a shared autonomy framework is critical for bridging the embodiment gap and enabling precise, safe, and stable dexterous teleoperation.
> - Tactile-driven compliant adjustments can effectively translate coarse human intentions into refined robotic actions, preventing excessive interaction forces and maintaining cooperative grasp stability.
> - A unified optimization approach, encompassing both localized force regulation and global force-torque balance, significantly enhances task success rates and execution efficiency in complex bimanual manipulation scenarios.
