# DexTeleop-0: Force-Aware Bimanual Dexterous Teleoperation with Ego-Centric Perception towards Shared Autonomy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University developed DexTeleop-0, a bimanual dexterous teleoperation framework that integrates ego-centric perception with tactile-driven shared autonomy to translate human intents into force-compliant robotic commands. This system achieved higher task success rates, up to 97% in simulated ball assembly, and maintained safer contact force distributions compared to baselines in various contact-rich and bimanual manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robotic teleoperation systems face an "embodiment gap" and often lack haptic/tactile feedback, making precise, contact-rich manipulation challenging for multi-fingered hands.
- Traditional teleoperation methods like kinesthetic rigs are rigid, exoskeletons are cumbersome, and vision-only systems suffer from occlusions and tracking drift, particularly in bimanual scenarios.
- The absence of force transparency and real-time physical awareness leads to low data collection efficiency and difficulties in safely executing delicate tasks.

## Method

- **DexTeleop-0**, a bimanual dexterous teleoperation framework, combines ego-centric perception (from VR headsets) with a tactile-driven adaptation strategy to generate force-compliant robotic commands.
- A vector-based "==Project-and-Escape==" tracking logic and a constrained non-linear optimization (SLSQP) are used for real-time dexterous hand retargeting, ensuring stable mapping and enforcing contact geometries.
- A contact-rich force-aware shared autonomy layer computes residual actions through a unified Quadratic Programming (QP) problem, integrating localized force tracking and multi-contact force-torque balance to modulate teleoperated commands.

## Results

- DexTeleop-0 consistently demonstrated higher task success rates, achieving **97%** in simulated Ball Assembly and **57.14%** in real-robot Gear Mesh, significantly outperforming baselines.
- The framework effectively regulated contact forces, maintaining safer distributions (e.g., **11.15** "N" in Ball Assembly) and preventing object slippage or damage, which was a common failure mode for other methods.
- In coordinated bimanual tasks like Food Sorting and Tube Operation, DexTeleop-0 exhibited robust stability and higher operational mastery, with up to **74.29%** Stage 4 success in Food Sorting, attributing this to its object-centric force-torque balance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time integration of tactile and force feedback through a shared autonomy framework is critical for bridging the embodiment gap and enabling precise, safe, and stable dexterous teleoperation.
> - Tactile-driven compliant adjustments can effectively translate coarse human intentions into refined robotic actions, preventing excessive interaction forces and maintaining cooperative grasp stability.
> - A unified optimization approach, encompassing both localized force regulation and global force-torque balance, significantly enhances task success rates and execution efficiency in complex bimanual manipulation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a foundational challenge in robotics: achieving human-level, fine-grained, bimanual dexterous manipulation, particularly in contact-rich tasks. This area of robotics has seen significant advancements with multi-fingered robotic hands, which offer the structural versatility for complex interactions beyond the capabilities of parallel grippers. However, successfully executing such delicate tasks necessitates high-resolution tactile and force feedback, which provides critical insights into localized pressure, slip conditions, and multi-point contact dynamics.

Existing robotic teleoperation systems, which are crucial for collecting high-quality data for imitation learning and data-driven policies, frequently encounter limitations. These limitations stem from an "embodiment gap" – the kinematic and morphological differences between human and robotic hands – and the common absence of haptic or tactile feedback. Traditional teleoperation approaches include kinesthetic rigs, mechanical exoskeletons, and vision-only retargeting methods. Kinesthetic rigs are often rigid and embodiment-specific, exoskeletons can be cumbersome, and vision-only systems suffer from occlusions and tracking drift, especially in bimanual scenarios.

Recent efforts have leveraged egocentric vision via VR/AR headsets for real-time hand tracking and visual feedback, offering more flexible teleoperation. However, these systems often lack the physical awareness provided by force and tactile feedback, making precise, contact-rich manipulation challenging and leading to low data collection efficiency.

This work specifically aims to bridge this feedback gap by introducing a tactile-driven adaptation strategy on top of existing egocentric vision-based teleoperation pipelines. It fits into the broader trend of shared autonomy, where autonomous systems assist human operators by applying real-time corrections to human inputs, thereby enhancing robustness and safety in complex robotic manipulation tasks. The integration of tactile sensing for real-time optimization represents an evolution beyond purely kinematic or vision-based teleoperation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to overcome the limitations inherent in current teleoperation systems for fine-grained, bimanual, dexterous manipulation, specifically the "tactile and force feedback missing problem" and the "embodiment gap." These issues lead to low data collection efficiency and difficulties in executing contact-rich tasks safely and precisely.

The key objectives of the proposed DexTeleop-0 framework are:

1. **Bridge the Embodiment Gap and Lack of Force Transparency**: The core objective is to translate coarse human tracking intents, captured via egocentric perception, into precise, force-compliant robotic commands. This addresses the mismatch between human and robot kinematics and dynamics, which causes inaccuracies and instability during teleoperation.
2. **Enable High-Precision, Contact-Rich Adjustments**: The system aims to facilitate delicate interactions by providing real-time, localized, compliant corrections. This is achieved by dynamically adjusting the robot's posture based on tactile feedback, ensuring stability and preventing damage during physical engagement.
3. **Improve Data Collection Efficiency for Dexterous Manipulation**: By enhancing the robustness and success rate of complex tasks through shared autonomy, the framework aims to reduce the effort and time required to collect high-quality demonstration data for machine learning algorithms, particularly for data-hungry reinforcement learning and imitation learning approaches.
4. **Develop a Tactile-Driven Shared Autonomy Strategy**: The research seeks to propose and implement a novel adaptation strategy that uses real-time fingertip force sensing to formulate a localized optimization problem. This strategy continuously projects user commands onto a physically consistent, force-compliant manifold.
5. **Design a Lightweight, Hardware-Agnostic Teleoperation Framework**: An objective is to create a system that is not dependent on specialized or cumbersome mechanical rigs but rather utilizes readily available commercial egocentric vision systems (like VR headsets) for human motion capture.
6. **Validate the Full-Stack Solution Across Simulation and Real Hardware**: The work aims to rigorously evaluate the proposed method in both simulated environments and on physical robotic platforms, demonstrating its effectiveness in terms of task success rates and execution efficiency compared to representative baselines.

In essence, the research seeks to enhance the utility of high-DoF bimanual robotic systems by making them more controllable, safer, and more efficient for executing intricate manipulation tasks through the strategic integration of tactile feedback into a shared autonomy teleoperation pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexTeleop-0 is a bimanual dexterous teleoperation framework that integrates ego-centric perception with a tactile-driven adaptation strategy. The system is designed to translate human tracking intents into force-compliant robotic commands.

The methodology can be broken down into the following components:

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The system takes tracking data from a VR headset, which provides 𝑀 transformations for human hand joints (H) and a global transform for the human wrist (T_w). The goal is to map these observations onto a bimanual robotic system, consisting of dual 6-DoF arms and 22-DoF dexterous hands. An inverse kinematics (IK) solver maps the human wrist pose to the robotic arm joint angles (q_a), and a hand retargeting function maps human hand joint vectors to robotic hand joint angles (q_h). The raw target commands (q_raw) are then modulated by a force-aware shared autonomy layer, which computes a residual action (Δq) to produce a stable command (q_final = q_raw + Δq).

#### 3.2. Dexterous Hand Retargeting

To map the 26 human hand joints to the 22 DoFs of the robotic hand, a vector-based retargeting paradigm is employed.
- **Vector-based Approach**: Instead of directly mapping absolute joint angles, which are prone to morphological mismatches, the system extracts 𝐾 critical inter-joint and finger-to-palm target vectors from the human hand tracking data. The corresponding robotic hand vectors are computed via forward kinematics.
- **"Project-and-Escape" Tracking Logic**: For a subset of 𝐾_p tip-to-tip vectors, a hysteresis-based logic is applied. If a human reference vector's length (ℓ_k) falls below a `d_project` threshold, it is "projected" to a predefined contact distance `d_k`, and its optimization weight is augmented (high weight). If ℓ_k exceeds `d_escape`, it "escapes" and is used directly with a nominal weight. This mechanism enforces specific contact geometries during grasping.
- **Constrained Non-linear Optimization**: The real-time kinematic retargeting is formulated as an optimization problem:
 - Minimize the weighted sum of Huber loss between robotic and human reference vectors, plus a quadratic regularization term that penalizes deviations from the previous frame's joint configuration, to suppress jitter.
 - Subject to physical lower and upper joint limits (q_lb ≤ q_h ≤ q_ub).
- **Solver**: This optimization is solved in real time ( < 15 ms) using the Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP) algorithm, leveraging analytical gradients propagated through the operational space Jacobian for rapid convergence.

#### 3.3. Contact-Rich Force-Aware Shared Autonomy

This layer introduces real-time modulation of the nominal teleoperated joint commands (q_tele) by computing a compliant residual action (Δq), addressing the lack of haptic feedback.

- **Tactile Sensing Processing and Contact State Estimation**:
 - Multi-contact interaction profiles are continuously sampled at each fingertip, providing world-frame contact forces (f_w_i) and contact point locations (p_w_i).
 - An instantaneous object reference center (p_obj) is estimated as the mean of active contact positions. Localized contact point position vectors (p_i) are then calculated relative to this object center.
 - A continuous logistic activation weight function, incorporating hysteresis, is applied to the magnitude of raw contact forces. This function (w_i ∈) scales the participation of each finger in optimization based on its contact reliability, filtering noise and handling transient contact phases.

- **Localized Force Feedback Tracking**:
 - For active fingers, the objective is to regulate fingertip interaction forces within a desired operational window ([f_min, f_max]).
 - The linear contribution of joint angle increments to force dampening is captured by an interaction matrix (A_F), which sums the negative operational space contact Jacobians of active contacts.
 - Scalar force errors (r_F_i) exceeding safety boundaries are projected into displacement domain residual vectors (ρ_F_i) via a virtual force residual stiffness (K_F).
 - This forms a quadratic error minimization cost (ℓ_F) for Δq, weighted by a prioritized penalty (λ_F) proportional to the contact activation parameter.

- **Multi-Contact Force-Torque Balance Evaluation**:
 - To maintain cooperative grasp stability and prevent slippage, the system evaluates the instantaneous net force (F) and net torque (τ) acting on the object reference center.
 - Global force and torque discrepancies (r_F, r_τ) are computed against target specifications (F\*, τ\*).
 - A filtering deadband parameter (ε) is applied to these residuals to prevent minor fluctuations from overriding primary safety tracking.
 - The torque variation is mapped to joint space using a skew-symmetric cross-product formulation (A_τ) involving the contact arm vectors.
 - This results in a secondary soft quadratic task (ℓ_B) for multi-contact balance, with force and torque balance prioritization hyperparameters (λ_fb, λ_tb).

- **Unified Force Balance Optimization**:
 - All localized safety constraints and cooperative balance requirements are unified into a single box-constrained Quadratic Programming (QP) problem.
 - This QP minimizes a cost function combining nominal stabilization terms (H_0, g_0), the localized force tracking cost (ℓ_F), and the multi-contact balance cost (ℓ_B).
 - It is subject to physical joint limits (q_min, q_max) and a slew-rate saturation limit (δ) derived from maximum permissible joint velocities.
 - The problem is solved at 30 Hz using an efficient projected gradient box-QP solver. This ensures that teleoperated commands are projected onto a force-compliant, physically stable manifold.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated DexTeleop-0 against three baselines: No-Residual (direct kinematic mapping), Joint-Level PD Control (localized PD loops for force damping), and Single-Force Tracking (localized force feedback without global force-torque balance). Evaluations were conducted in both NVIDIA IsaacSim 4.5 and on a physical robotic platform comprising twin UR7e arms with 22-DoF Sharpa Wave dexterous hands, controlled by a Meta Quest 3 VR headset (56 DoFs total). Performance was measured by Multi-Stage Success Rate and Force Statistical Data (mean and variance of thumb contact force).

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance Analysis

- **Ball Assembly (Contact-rich dynamic grasping and relocation)**:
 - No Residual and PD Control methods failed significantly after Stage 1, achieving 4% and 1% Stage 3 success rates, respectively. This was attributed to rigid kinematic overrides causing excessive interaction pressures (e.g., 31.58 ± 10.48 N for No Residual), leading to object slippage.
 - DexTeleop-0 maintained a 97% success rate through Stage 3, regulating contact pressures to a safer distribution (11.15 ± 5.01 N).
 - Analysis (Fig. 5) showed that DexTeleop-0's real-time joint angle adjustments (Δq) actively alleviated excessive fingertip contact forces, ensuring safe interaction.
- **Stir in Cup (Delicate tool use and bimanual trajectories)**:
 - No Residual achieved a 60% Stage 3 completion rate at the cost of severe interaction (15.56 ± 6.59 N mean tactile force), twice that of DexTeleop-0.
 - DexTeleop-0 achieved a comparable 59% Stage 3 success rate with significantly lower interaction forces (7.93 ± 2.67 N), demonstrating a trade-off where force-safe compliance was prioritized without a substantial drop in task completion.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experimental Verification

Experiments involved five inexperienced and two professional operators, each performing five trials per task per method.
- **Single-Arm Precision Manipulations (Gear Mesh and Peg Insertion)**:
 - **Gear Mesh (Sub-centimeter alignment)**: DexTeleop-0 achieved a 57.14% Stage 2 assembly success rate, significantly outperforming No Residual (11.43%) and PD Control (37.14%).
 - **Peg Insertion (Precise peg-to-hole clearance)**: DexTeleop-0 maintained a 97.14% Stage 1 capture success and 60.00% Stage 2 insertion completion rate. Baselines frequently caused parts to slip due to uncompensated contact vectors.
 - DexTeleop-0 effectively modulated user intent into compliant alignment trajectories, mitigating errors that lead to mechanical binding. While not always yielding the *absolute lowest* tactile force in these tasks, this was attributed to operators' conservative strategies to protect the hardware.
- **Coordinated Bimanual Tasks (Food Sorting and Tube Operation)**:
 - **Food Sorting (Handling irregular geometries)**: DexTeleop-0 achieved the highest end-to-end operational mastery with a 74.29% Stage 4 success rate and the lowest overall contact profile (4.94 ± 1.80 N), preventing damage to delicate objects.
 - **Tube Operation (Fluid transfer with long-horizon stability)**: DexTeleop-0 showed robust stability, achieving a 100.00% Stage 2 success rate and a 77.14% Stage 3 completion rate. The No Residual method dropped to 34.29% due to continuous multi-contact distribution mismatches. The inclusion of object-centric force-torque balances was critical for stability in these bimanual tasks.

**Summary of Findings:**
DexTeleop-0 consistently demonstrated higher task success rates and improved execution efficiency compared to baselines in both simulated and real-world environments. The system effectively regulated contact forces, preventing destructive interactions and maintaining grasp stability, particularly in contact-rich and bimanual tasks. The shared autonomy framework, with its tactile-driven adaptation, was shown to be critical for bridging the embodiment gap and enhancing the robustness and safety of dexterous teleoperation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in DexTeleop-0 holds significance for the field of robotics by addressing a fundamental challenge in dexterous manipulation and teleoperation. Its potential impacts are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation Capabilities**: By directly addressing the "tactile and force feedback missing problem" and the "embodiment gap," DexTeleop-0 enables robots to perform fine-grained, contact-rich bimanual tasks with greater precision, stability, and safety. This capability expands the operational envelope of multi-fingered robotic hands, making them viable for a broader range of complex real-world applications.

2. **Improved Data Collection for AI/ML in Robotics**: The system's ability to facilitate robust and successful execution of complex manipulation tasks directly translates to more efficient collection of high-quality demonstration data. This is crucial for training data-hungry machine learning models, such as reinforcement learning and imitation learning policies, which are essential for developing generalizable and adaptable robot behaviors. High-quality visual-tactile trajectories collected through DexTeleop-0 can form the foundation for more advanced robotic intelligence.

3. **Safer Human-Robot Interaction and Teleoperation**: The real-time force-balanced residual optimization loop prevents excessive interaction forces that could lead to object damage, robot wear, or even unsafe conditions. This inherent compliance and safety mechanism is vital for deploying dexterous robots in environments where delicate objects are handled or where physical interaction with the environment is frequent. It makes teleoperation more forgiving and intuitive for human operators, reducing the cognitive load associated with purely kinematic control.

4. **Advancement of Shared Autonomy and Human-in-the-Loop Systems**: The proposed tactile-driven adaptation strategy represents a step in shared autonomy, where the robotic system intelligently assists the human operator by interpreting coarse intents and refining them into physically consistent actions. This paradigm could lead to more effective human-robot collaboration in tasks requiring both human intuition and robotic precision.

5. **Hardware-Agnostic and Accessible Teleoperation**: By utilizing readily available commercial VR headsets for egocentric perception, the framework lowers the barrier to entry for high-dimensional dexterous teleoperation. This makes the technology more accessible for research, development, and eventual practical applications, avoiding the need for expensive and cumbersome specialized hardware rigs.

6. **Insights into Embodied Manipulation**: The finding that localized tactile corrections and physical force-balancing are critical for bridging the embodiment gap and ensuring interaction safety – potentially more so than merely increasing baseline tracking resolution – offers a fundamental insight for future research in embodied manipulation. It highlights the importance of multi-modal feedback beyond just visual or kinematic information.

In the future, the integration of predictive slip-detection algorithms could allow the system to adapt dynamically to unexpected object physical properties, further enhancing robustness. Leveraging the high-quality, visual-tactile demonstration trajectories natively collected through DexTeleop-0 for training generalizable imitation learning policies also represents a clear path toward broader impact in complex industrial and laboratory assembly tasks.
