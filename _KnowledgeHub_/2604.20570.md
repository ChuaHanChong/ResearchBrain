# Exploring Spatial Intelligence from a Generative Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and collaborators introduce Generative Spatial Intelligence (GSI), a new capability for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to manipulate 3D spatial constraints during image generation. They develop GSI-Bench, a benchmark with synthetic and real-world data, demonstrating that training MLLMs on synthetic GSI data improves generative spatial editing and boosts spatial understanding performance across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing research on spatial intelligence in MLLMs primarily focuses on an "understanding" perspective, evaluating perception and recognition rather than active generation.
- A lack of reliable, scalable, and model-agnostic methods to quantitatively measure MLLMs' ability to generate and modify visual content while adhering to real-world 3D spatial constraints.
- The relationship between generative processes and improved spatial understanding in MLLMs remains largely unexplored, with most work focusing on understanding enhancing generation.

## Method

- Defined **Generative Spatial Intelligence (GSI)** as the ability to respect and manipulate ==3D spatial constraints== during image generation, operationalized through a ==spatially grounded image editing task==.
- Developed **GSI-Bench**, comprising **GSI-Syn** (large-scale synthetic data with precise 3D ground truth from simulators) and **GSI-Real** (real-world images from ==ScanNet++== with reconstructed 3D priors).
- Implemented a multi-faceted evaluation protocol with metrics for ==Instruction Compliance==, ==Spatial Accuracy==, ==Edit Locality==, and ==Appearance Consistency==, and fine-tuned a unified MLLM (**BAGEL**) on GSI-Syn data.

## Results

- Closed-source models like NanoBanana and GPT-Image showed moderate GSI, while open-source Emu3.5 achieved the highest average score on GSI-Real (**43.52**), outperforming other baselines significantly.
- Fine-tuning BAGEL on GSI-Syn data resulted in substantial improvements, with an average gain of **+7.83 points** on GSI-Real (from **28.46 to 36.28**) and even higher gains on synthetic benchmarks.
- Generative spatial training significantly improved spatial understanding, with BAGEL+GSI-Syn showing gains of **+0.95% to +2.00%** across dimensions of OmniSpatial and an overall gain of **+4.00%** on SAT-Real.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GSI is a measurable and distinct capability for MLLMs, addressing a gap in current evaluation that largely overlooks generative spatial reasoning.
> - Synthetic data, when carefully designed with explicit 3D geometric grounding, can effectively train MLLMs for complex spatial editing tasks and demonstrate strong sim-to-real transfer to real-world images.
> - Generative training on spatial editing tasks can tangibly enhance a model's performance on downstream spatial understanding benchmarks, suggesting a reciprocal relationship between generation and understanding.
