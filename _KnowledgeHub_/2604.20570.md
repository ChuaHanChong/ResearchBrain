# Exploring Spatial Intelligence from a Generative Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and collaborators introduce Generative Spatial Intelligence (GSI), a new capability for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to manipulate 3D spatial constraints during image generation. They develop GSI-Bench, a benchmark with synthetic and real-world data, demonstrating that training MLLMs on synthetic GSI data improves generative spatial editing and boosts spatial understanding performance across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing research on spatial intelligence in MLLMs primarily focuses on an "understanding" perspective, evaluating perception and recognition rather than active generation.
- A lack of reliable, scalable, and model-agnostic methods to quantitatively measure MLLMs' ability to generate and modify visual content while adhering to real-world 3D spatial constraints.
- The relationship between generative processes and improved spatial understanding in MLLMs remains largely unexplored, with most work focusing on understanding enhancing generation.

## Method

- Defined **Generative Spatial Intelligence (GSI)** as the ability to respect and manipulate ==3D spatial constraints== during image generation, operationalized through a ==spatially grounded image editing task==.
- Developed **GSI-Bench**, comprising **GSI-Syn** (large-scale synthetic data with precise 3D ground truth from simulators) and **GSI-Real** (real-world images from ==ScanNet++== with reconstructed 3D priors).
- Implemented a multi-faceted evaluation protocol with metrics for ==Instruction Compliance==, ==Spatial Accuracy==, ==Edit Locality==, and ==Appearance Consistency==, and fine-tuned a unified MLLM (**BAGEL**) on GSI-Syn data.

## Results

- Closed-source models like NanoBanana and GPT-Image showed moderate GSI, while open-source Emu3.5 achieved the highest average score on GSI-Real (**43.52**), outperforming other baselines significantly.
- Fine-tuning BAGEL on GSI-Syn data resulted in substantial improvements, with an average gain of **+7.83 points** on GSI-Real (from **28.46 to 36.28**) and even higher gains on synthetic benchmarks.
- Generative spatial training significantly improved spatial understanding, with BAGEL+GSI-Syn showing gains of **+0.95% to +2.00%** across dimensions of OmniSpatial and an overall gain of **+4.00%** on SAT-Real.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GSI is a measurable and distinct capability for MLLMs, addressing a gap in current evaluation that largely overlooks generative spatial reasoning.
> - Synthetic data, when carefully designed with explicit 3D geometric grounding, can effectively train MLLMs for complex spatial editing tasks and demonstrate strong sim-to-real transfer to real-world images.
> - Generative training on spatial editing tasks can tangibly enhance a model's performance on downstream spatial understanding benchmarks, suggesting a reciprocal relationship between generation and understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a specific area within the broader field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and their capacity for spatial reasoning. Prior research has predominantly concentrated on evaluating and enhancing spatial intelligence from an "understanding" perspective. This involves tasks such as perception, recognition, and question-answering related to objects, scenes, and their geometric relationships in 3D physical environments. Existing benchmarks and methodologies largely focus on assessing how well MLLMs can interpret or describe spatial configurations. Examples include benchmarks like VSI-Bench for video-based spatial reasoning, MindCube for 3D spatial modeling from sparse views, and OmniSpatial for comprehensive spatial reasoning dimensions. Methodological advancements have introduced auxiliary spatial encoders or leveraged simulation environments to generate rule-based spatial reasoning data for training.

Concurrently, there has been a significant development in "unified multimodal models" that aim to perform both understanding and generation tasks. While existing research has provided evidence that robust visual understanding can contribute to improved image generation quality, the inverse relationship—whether generative processes can, in turn, enhance models' spatial understanding—remains less explored.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two trends. It investigates "generative spatial intelligence" (GSI), defined as the ability of MLLMs to respect and manipulate 3D spatial constraints during image generation. By focusing on image-to-image editing as an operationalization of GSI, the authors aim to quantitatively measure this capability and explore its potential for reciprocal benefits to spatial understanding. This approach seeks to bridge the gap between understanding-centric spatial intelligence research and the emerging paradigm of unified understanding-generation models, laying groundwork for applications in embodied AI, robotic manipulation, and world modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that while spatial intelligence is foundational for MLLMs to interact with the physical world, its assessment has been largely confined to an "understanding" framework. The authors propose that MLLMs, particularly unified multimodal models that handle both perception and generation, should also possess generative spatial intelligence (GSI) to create and modify visual content while adhering to real-world 3D spatial constraints. The paper articulates three primary objectives:

1. **To ascertain the existence and extent of Generative Spatial Intelligence (GSI) in modern generative and unified multimodal models.** This involves investigating whether current state-of-the-art models inherently possess the capability to respect and manipulate spatial constraints during image generation. The hypothesis is that while these models may exhibit some level of general image generation quality, their proficiency in precise, physically grounded spatial manipulation might be limited without explicit training or architectural biases.

2. **To develop a reliable, scalable, and model-agnostic method for measuring GSI.** Recognizing the limitations of qualitative assessments and the challenges of creating precisely annotated real-world data for spatial manipulations, a key objective is to establish a quantitative benchmarking framework. This framework needs to objectively evaluate spatial compliance and editing fidelity, requiring novel data generation and evaluation protocols that can handle the complexity of 3D spatial transformations in 2D image generation.

3. **To investigate whether GSI can be enhanced through targeted interventions, and if such enhancement transfers to downstream spatial understanding tasks.** This objective explores the underexplored reverse direction of the understanding-generation relationship. Specifically, the authors aim to determine if training models on generative spatial editing tasks can not only improve their GSI but also lead to a deeper grasp of spatial concepts that benefits their spatial understanding capabilities. This inquiry seeks to establish a new pathway for advancing spatial intelligence in MLLMs by leveraging generative training.

The overarching motivation is to move beyond mere recognition of spatial concepts to actively enabling MLLMs to "reason with space" in a generative capacity. This is considered crucial for applications requiring models to ground language in physical space, such as embodied navigation, robotic manipulation, and advanced 3D scene understanding, where the ability to predict and generate spatially consistent environments or object manipulations is vital.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research operationalizes Generative Spatial Intelligence (GSI) through a spatially grounded image editing task, where a model receives an input image and a natural language instruction for a spatial manipulation, and generates an output image satisfying the specified constraints. The methodology involves the construction of a novel benchmark, GSI-Bench, comprising both synthetic and real-world components, along with a unified evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Generative Spatial Intelligence (GSI) Definition

GSI is defined as the capability of a unified multimodal model to respect, reason about, and manipulate spatial constraints during image generation. Unlike traditional spatial understanding, which is passive (perceiving spatial configurations), GSI is active, reflecting a model's ability to enforce spatial relationships in generated content. The image-to-image editing formulation is adopted for quantitative assessment, providing a reference image and a spatially grounded instruction to produce an edited image adhering to the transformation.

#### 3.2. Task Formulation and Spatial Operations

Visual scenes are modeled using a latent 3D structure, including object centers, sizes, rotations, and camera parameters. Spatial instructions are structured as a target object reference, an action, and a 3D geometric transformation (Φ₃D) that updates object poses or camera parameters. This explicit link between language and 3D geometry facilitates data synthesis and evaluation. Seven categories of quantitatively grounded spatial operations are defined, spanning object-, camera-, and scene-level transformations:
- **Camera-Relative Move (CM):** Movement along camera axes.
- **Object-Relative Place (OP):** Positioning relative to a reference object.
- **Object Rotation (OR):** Rotating objects in 3D.
- **Receptacle Placement (RP):** Placing objects within containers.
- **Perspective Control (PC):** Changing camera viewpoint.
- **Spatial Removal (SR):** Deleting objects based on spatial criteria.
- **Object Scaling (OS):** Uniformly scaling objects.

#### 3.3. GSI-Syn: Synthetic Benchmark Construction

GSI-Syn is a large-scale synthetic benchmark built using open-source simulators (AI2-THOR and Mesa-Task) to provide precise ground truth. The automated synthesis pipeline consists of:
- **Scene Initialization and Viewpoint Curation:** DBSCAN clustering on floor plans partitions spaces, followed by maximally dispersed viewpoint sampling. Viewpoints are prioritized based on the presence of manipulable objects.
- **Action Candidate Generation and Geometric Grounding:** A target object (and reference object for relational operations) is randomly selected. Rigorous 3D geometric checks ensure physical plausibility (e.g., object visibility, stable surface, collision avoidance). Template-based modules generate corresponding textual instructions.
- **Simulated Execution and Success Validation:** The proposed action's ideal destination state is computed, then executed in the physics-enabled simulator. Success is validated by comparing the actual outcome with the ideal state, ensuring object position and visibility match. Failed executions are resampled.
- **Post-Generation Filtering and Quality Assurance:** Two-stage filtering is applied: (1) instance segmentation masks filter out samples with negligible pixel-level change, and (2) an MLLM (Qwen3-VL-235B) identifies and discards subtle anomalies like simulation artifacts, physical implausibility, or severe occlusions.

#### 3.4. GSI-Real: Real-World Benchmark Construction

GSI-Real complements GSI-Syn by evaluating GSI in natural images. Without direct 3D scene representations or physics simulation, an alternative protocol is used, where success is evaluated by analyzing spatial consistency between the model's predicted edit and the specified 3D transformation.
- **Image Source and Frame Selection:** Images are sourced from ScanNet++ (indoor RGB-D dataset). Multi-criteria filtering prioritizes sharp frames with minimal motion blur and rich manipulable object content, using frequency-domain analysis and a 3D object grounding model.
- **3D Scene Reconstruction and Operation Generation:** DetAny3D, an open-vocabulary 3D grounding model, reconstructs the source scene, extracting object-level 3D bounding boxes, poses, and semantic labels. Rule-based procedures generate candidate spatial operations (move, rotate, remove) and compute destination states.
- **Visualization-based Verification and MLLM Gating:** To filter invalid operations, before-and-after visualizations of projected bounding boxes are generated. An MLLM (Qwen3-VL-235B) identifies and discards physically implausible operations, corrects annotation errors, and generates diverse natural language instructions.
- **Human Review and Refinement:** A final manual review identifies and corrects residual annotation inaccuracies or ambiguous instructions to ensure high data quality.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Protocol

A multi-faceted protocol assesses GSI with four core metrics:
- **Instruction Compliance (IC):** Binary metric evaluating if the edited scene satisfies the spatial semantics of the instruction within a reasonable tolerance.
- **Spatial Accuracy (SA):** Fine-grained geometric precision measured by normalized translation error, relative pose error, and geodesic rotation error.
- **Edit Locality (EL):** Assesses localized editing by computing LPIPS on non-target regions, comparing original and edited images (higher score indicates better consistency). A dataset-specific locality gate filters samples before IC and SA scoring.
- **Appearance Consistency (AC):** An MLLM (Qwen3-VL-235B) verifies edited object visual attributes for transformations or background inpainting quality for removals.

#### 3.6. Fine-tuning Unified MLLMs for GSI

The automated pipeline of GSI-Syn allows for large-scale training data generation. BAGEL, a unified multimodal model, is chosen as the base model due to its native image editing support and self-attention mechanism for perception-generation interaction. It is fine-tuned on a GSI-Syn training set comprising diverse spatial operations, with strict scene separation from test sets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on GSI-Bench and other spatial understanding benchmarks yielded several findings regarding the capabilities of MLLMs in generative spatial intelligence and the impact of specialized training.

#### 4.1. Benchmarking Generative Spatial Intelligence

**Performance Disparities Across Models:**
- **Closed-Source Models:** NanoBanana and GPT-Image exhibited relatively strong performance on GSI-Syn-Table (37.03 and 33.97 average, respectively), particularly in Instruction Compliance (IC) and Appearance Consistency (AC). However, their performance on GSI-Real (33.52 and 34.70 average) was comparable to, or sometimes lower than, leading open-source models, suggesting challenges with fine-grained spatial manipulations in real-world contexts despite general visual generation capabilities.
- **Open-Source Baselines:** Emu3.5 emerged as the strongest open-source performer, achieving the highest average score on GSI-Real (43.52) and strong results across all evaluation dimensions. This suggests that Emu3.5, potentially due to its video-centric training, possesses stronger spatial priors. Other general-purpose models like Uniworld, Ultra, and Omnigen2 exhibited substantially lower scores, with very low AC or IC values, indicating difficulties in following structured spatial instructions and maintaining visual fidelity during spatial transformations.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visual results indicated that most models performed better on object removal tasks than on precise geometric manipulations (e.g., rotation, movement, scaling). Emu3.5 generally produced cleaner removals with stronger spatial consistency. However, models like Ultra and AnyEdit frequently failed to preserve object identity. AnyEdit, BAGEL, and Omnigen2 often introduced artifacts. BAGEL sometimes misinterpreted translation instructions as camera motion. While BAGEL, Emu3.5, and Qwen demonstrated reliability in following referential cues, they occasionally removed unintended content, suggesting that fine-grained localization remains an area for improvement.

#### 4.2. Impact of Fine-tuning on GSI-Syn

**Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Fine-tuning the BAGEL model exclusively on synthetic GSI-Syn data resulted in consistent and substantial improvements across both synthetic and real-world benchmarks:
- **On GSI-Real:** BAGEL+GSI-Syn achieved an average gain of 7.83 points over the base BAGEL model (from 28.46 to 36.28). Significant improvements were observed in Edit Locality (+9.22), Appearance Consistency (+8.25), and Instruction Compliance (+8.16). Spatial Accuracy also improved by +5.68 points. These results indicate that synthetic, geometrically grounded supervision effectively transfers to real images, enhancing the model's ability to preserve object identity and perform precise, spatially constrained edits without requiring real-world annotations for training.
- **On Synthetic Benchmarks:** The improvements were even more pronounced on synthetic data, with gains of +22.15 on GSI-Syn-Table and +7.05 on GSI-Syn-Room. The larger gains on GSI-Syn-Table are attributed to its structured geometric variations and localized editing focus. The more modest gains on GSI-Syn-Room suggest that increased scene complexity and potential spatial ambiguities in more varied environments still pose challenges for global spatial reasoning.

**Enhanced Spatial Understanding through Generative Training:** A notable finding was the positive impact of generative spatial training on downstream spatial understanding tasks, even without explicit understanding-based supervision during fine-tuning.
- **OmniSpatial Benchmark:** Fine-tuning BAGEL solely on GSI-Syn improved its performance on the OmniSpatial benchmark, showing consistent gains in relevant dimensions: Dynamic Reasoning (+0.95%), Spatial Interaction (+2.00%), and Perspective Taking (+1.07%). While a moderate decrease in Complex Logic was noted, the overall improvement indicates that generative spatial training can substantially enhance spatial understanding capabilities.
- **SAT-Real Benchmark:** Similar improvements were observed on the SAT-Real benchmark, with BAGEL+GSI-Syn achieving an overall gain of +4.00%. Specific improvements were seen in Allocentric Perspective, Goal Aiming, and Egocentric Movement.

These findings suggest a reciprocal relationship where generative training can strengthen spatial reasoning, providing evidence for a new approach to advancing spatial intelligence in unified MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Generative Spatial Intelligence (GSI) as a distinct and measurable capability for multimodal large language models, moving beyond traditional understanding-centric evaluations. The development of GSI-Bench, comprising both GSI-Real and GSI-Syn, provides a novel and comprehensive framework for assessing this intelligence. This benchmark is significant because it allows for scalable, model-agnostic quantification of a model's ability to manipulate 3D spatial constraints during image generation, addressing a critical gap in existing evaluation methodologies. The structured approach to dataset construction, leveraging 3D priors, rule-based generation, MLLM validation, and human review, contributes a methodology for creating high-quality, precise spatial editing benchmarks.

The empirical findings hold several implications:
1. **Current Model Limitations:** The experiments reveal that even state-of-the-art MLLMs, including powerful proprietary models, exhibit limitations in performing precise, physically grounded spatial manipulations. While they may excel at general image generation, their capacity to consistently respect and enforce 3D spatial constraints during editing is often insufficient. This highlights a specific area for improvement in current MLLM architectures and training paradigms.
2. **Effectiveness of Generative Training:** A key finding is that fine-tuning unified MLLMs on synthetic, geometrically grounded spatial editing data (GSI-Syn) substantially enhances their generative spatial intelligence. This improvement not only applies to synthetic tasks but also demonstrates effective sim-to-real transfer, leading to gains on real-world spatial editing scenarios without requiring real-world annotations. This suggests that high-quality synthetic data, when carefully designed for precise spatial reasoning, can be a valuable resource for training.
3. **Reciprocal Relationship between Generation and Understanding:** The most impactful finding is the demonstration that generative training can tangibly strengthen a model's spatial understanding capabilities. Improvements observed on benchmarks like OmniSpatial and SAT-Real, across dimensions such as dynamic reasoning, spatial interaction, and perspective taking, indicate a novel pathway for advancing MLLMs. This suggests that models that are proficient at *generating* spatially coherent content may also possess a deeper, more robust internal representation of space, which in turn benefits their *understanding* tasks.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted. It establishes GSI as a critical component of multimodal intelligence, akin to how language generation complements language understanding. For embodied AI, robotics, and interactive systems, equipping models with robust GSI is fundamental for realistic world modeling, effective navigation, and precise manipulation. If models can accurately generate spatially consistent visual outcomes based on instructions, it brings them closer to real-world interactive capabilities where understanding and acting in 3D space are paramount. Furthermore, the discovery that generative training enhances understanding opens new avenues for research into jointly optimizing understanding and generation objectives within unified multimodal models, potentially leading to more efficient and capable AI systems.
