# Beyond Flat Policies: Hierarchical Post-Training for Embodied Agents in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HiRoC, a hierarchical post-training framework, enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to achieve robust long-horizon robotic manipulation by explicitly decoupling high-level task planning from low-level action execution. It established a new state-of-the-art with an average success rate of 93.5% on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization across diverse perturbations.

## Problem

- VLA models using "flat policies" struggle with long-horizon, multi-stage robotic manipulation due to difficulties in explicitly modeling task progression and accumulating errors without semantic anchors.
- Existing hierarchical approaches often rely on supervised learning from offline demonstrations, which limits continuous improvement through online interaction.
- Directly integrating a subgoal-generating planner with a pre-trained VLA executor leads to a severe "cold start" problem due to distribution misalignment.

## Method

- Proposes Hierarchical Robotic Control (**HiRoC**), which separates a planner for high-level task decomposition from an executor for low-level action execution.
- The planner is trained via ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== to dynamically generate executable subgoals based on current observations and global task descriptions.
- The executor undergoes ==subgoal-conditioned SFT== to mitigate distribution misalignment, followed by online reinforcement learning using a novel ==hierarchical Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== objective.

## Results

- HiRoC achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of **93.5%** on the LIBERO benchmark, improving over baselines by **10.06%**, and demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization across all seven perturbation types on LIBERO-Plus.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the custom-trained planner, the distribution misalignment training, and both global and local GRPO components for robust performance and stable learning.
- Case studies and a sim-to-real experiment validated HiRoC's ability to dynamically decompose tasks and perform successful long-horizon manipulation on a physical robotic platform.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit hierarchical task decomposition, guided by a specialized planner, is essential for VLA models to effectively handle long-horizon, multi-stage robotic manipulation.
> - Mitigating distribution misalignment between the planner's subgoals and the executor's expectations through subgoal-conditioned supervised fine-tuning is critical for successful hierarchical integration.
> - A hierarchical Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective, adaptively combining global task-level and intermediate subgoal-level advantages, provides more robust learning signals for policy optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models represent an emergent paradigm in robotic manipulation, integrating capabilities from visual perception, language understanding, and action generation. Leveraging large pre-trained vision-language models, these VLA agents have demonstrated proficiency in various manipulation tasks and across different robotic embodiments, particularly for short-horizon and primitive skills.

However, the existing landscape of VLA post-training methods predominantly employs a "flat policy" formulation. In this approach, models typically map visual observations and a single, global task instruction directly to low-level actions. This flat structure makes it challenging for agents to explicitly model task progression, identify their current stage within a complex task, or reason about sequential intermediate objectives. Consequently, such policies often struggle with robust long-horizon and multi-stage manipulation tasks, where minor execution deviations can accumulate and lead to task failure without semantic anchors for self-correction.

While hierarchical approaches have been explored to introduce task decomposition and structured planning, many of these methods rely on supervised learning from pre-collected offline demonstrations. This reliance limits their ability to continuously improve execution through online interaction, which is a key advantage of reinforcement learning. The broader research landscape thus identifies a gap: a need for VLA models that can perform hierarchical reasoning and leverage online interaction to enhance long-horizon task completion, moving beyond the limitations of both flat policies and purely supervised hierarchical methods.

This research introduces Hierarchical Robotic Control (HiRoC) to address this gap. It aims to develop a hierarchical post-training framework that decouples high-level task planning from low-level action execution within VLA models, and crucially, integrates online reinforcement learning for continuous improvement. By doing so, HiRoC positions itself at the intersection of VLA development, hierarchical control, and online learning, offering a method to imbue VLA agents with more structured reasoning and adaptive self-correction capabilities essential for complex real-world robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from observed limitations in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models when applied to complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. While VLA models have demonstrated notable capabilities in general-purpose robotics, their effectiveness is often constrained by the predominant "flat policy" post-training methods. These methods typically condition actions on a single, global task instruction throughout an entire execution episode.

The authors identify several specific issues arising from this flat policy formulation:
- **Difficulty in Modeling Task Progression:** Complex tasks are inherently multi-stage, each with distinct semantic objectives. A flat policy struggles to explicitly recognize its current stage or the immediate intermediate objective, making it difficult to plan and execute a sequence of actions effectively.
- **Accumulation of Errors:** Without explicit subgoals or semantic anchors, minor execution deviations can compound over time, leading to degraded decision-making and eventual task failure in long-horizon scenarios. The policy lacks the ability for stage-wise self-correction.
- **Lack of Hierarchical Reasoning:** Flat policies do not inherently possess the hierarchical reasoning capabilities needed to decompose high-level instructions into manageable intermediate steps, which is crucial for robust multi-stage execution.

Existing hierarchical approaches attempt to address these issues by introducing task decomposition. However, the authors point out a significant limitation: these methods primarily rely on supervised learning from offline demonstrations. This dependence means they cannot effectively leverage online interaction to refine their policies and improve execution performance in dynamic or unseen scenarios.

To bridge these gaps, the authors articulate three key challenges that need to be addressed for effective hierarchical post-training of VLA models:
1. **Executable Subgoal Generation:** The high-level planner must be capable of generating correct, executable subgoals that enable appropriate transitions between stages of a complex task.
2. **Overcoming Distribution Misalignment:** The pre-trained VLA executor is typically optimized for global task instructions, not fine-grained intermediate subgoals. Directly integrating a subgoal-generating planner with such an executor leads to a severe "cold start" problem due due to a distribution mismatch, hindering effective subgoal-conditioned execution.
3. **Exploiting Subgoal-level Progress in Optimization:** Policy optimization often relies on sparse, trajectory-level rewards (e.g., success/failure at task completion). For long-horizon tasks, this signal is insufficient for effective learning. The optimization process needs to exploit more granular, intermediate subgoal progress to provide structured learning signals.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:
- To propose and develop Hierarchical Robotic Control (HiRoC), a hierarchical post-training framework that explicitly separates high-level task planning from low-level action execution for VLA models.
- To design a planner module capable of dynamically decomposing global task instructions into a sequence of executable subgoals, thereby providing explicit semantic guidance.
- To develop a collaborative training strategy that aligns the executor with planner-generated subgoals, specifically through subgoal-conditioned supervised fine-tuning, to mitigate the distribution misalignment issue.
- To integrate online reinforcement learning (RL) for the executor, incorporating a novel hierarchical Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective that leverages both global task-level and intermediate subgoal-level progress for more effective policy optimization.
- To experimentally validate that HiRoC consistently outperforms existing strong baselines across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks, particularly on long-horizon and complex tasks, and to provide comprehensive analyses of its components.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Hierarchical Robotic Control (HiRoC) framework is designed to overcome the limitations of flat policies in VLA models by decoupling high-level task planning from low-level action execution. It comprises two main modules: a planner and an executor, which are trained through a multi-stage process involving Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) tuning.

#### 3.1. Training the Planner via SFT

The planner's role is to decompose high-level task instructions into executable subgoals.
- **Data Preparation:** Existing multimodal datasets (e.g., from VLA-OS) containing text and images are reused. This data is meticulously cleaned and reorganized into a LlamaFactory-compatible format. The process involves unifying the label space, removing ambiguous or duplicate entries, and reannotating conflicting samples to align with the planner's objective of predicting the immediate next subgoal based on the current context. This results in the dataset `D_plan`.
- **Planner Training:** A Vision-Language Model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B in this work) is fine-tuned via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the `D_plan` dataset. The objective is to minimize an autoregressive cross-entropy loss, where the planner learns to generate a sequence of subgoal tokens (`l_t`) conditioned on the current multimodal observation (`s_t`, comprising visual and language inputs). After this SFT phase, the planner's parameters (`w_p`) are frozen and it acts as a fixed semantic planning module during subsequent executor optimization.

#### 3.2. Training the Executor via SFT and RL Tuning

The executor's function is to generate low-level actions conditioned on the current observation and the subgoal provided by the planner.

- **Addressing Distribution Misalignment (SFT Pre-training):**
 - **Problem:** Pre-trained VLA executors are typically optimized for global task instructions. When coupled with a planner generating fine-grained intermediate subgoals, a "distribution misalignment" occurs, leading to a severe cold start problem for the executor.
 - **Solution:** To mitigate this, the collected trajectories are reorganized into an executor-specific dataset, `D_exe`. Each trajectory is broken down into subgoal-level training samples `(s_i, l_i, a_i)`, where `s_i` is the multimodal observation for a subgoal, `l_i` is the associated subgoal, and `a_i` is the action chunk for accomplishing `l_i`.
 - **Executor SFT:** The executor (OpenVLA-OFT in this work) is initialized by supervised fine-tuning on `D_exe`. This process involves minimizing a cross-entropy loss, where the executor learns to predict the action sequence (`a_i`) conditioned on the current observation (`s_i`) and the specific subgoal (`l_i`). This pre-training step equips the executor with basic subgoal-following capabilities, effectively alleviating the cold start issue and providing a better initialization for subsequent reinforcement learning.

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Tuning:**
 - **Online Interaction:** Once the executor has basic subgoal-following capabilities, it is further optimized through online interaction with the environment. The high-level planner, which remains frozen, guides this interaction by dynamically generating subgoals (`l_i,t`) based on the current observation (`o_i,t`) and the global task description. The executor's policy `π_θ(· | o_i,t, l_i,t)` then predicts action chunks (`a_i,t:t+H−1`) for a given horizon H. Collected online trajectories are stored in an on-policy rollout buffer `D`.
 - **Hierarchical Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** To provide richer learning signals, HiRoC augments the standard GRPO with a hierarchical advantage mechanism:
 - **Task-level Advantage (Global GRPO):** For a group of N trajectories sampled from the same task instance, the cumulative rewards `R_i` for each trajectory `i` are standardized to obtain a task-level advantage (`A_task_i`). This captures the overall success or failure of the trajectory.
 - **Subtask-level Advantage (Local GRPO):** The subgoal structure recorded during rollout is utilized to compute an aggregated subtask score `S_i` for each trajectory. This `S_i` is then standardized within the group to derive a subtask-level advantage (`A_sub_i`). This provides intermediate supervision by evaluating progress towards individual subgoals.
 - **Combined Advantage:** The final advantage (`e_A_i`) used for optimizing the executor is a weighted sum of the task-level and subtask-level advantages: `e_A_i = w_t A_task_i + w_s A_sub_i`. The weights (`w_t`, `w_s`) are adaptively balanced based on the average number of recorded subgoals per trajectory (`K`) and the length of collected trajectories (`L`), allowing for dynamic adjustment based on task complexity.
 - **Policy Optimization:** The low-level executor's policy is optimized using a PPO-style clipped actor objective. This objective minimizes a loss function based on the probability ratio `ρ_i,t,j(θ)` between the current policy and the behavior policy (used for data collection), scaled by the combined hierarchical advantage (`e_A_i`). A clipping operation limits the magnitude of updates to maintain policy stability. A binary mask ensures only valid action tokens contribute to the loss.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted on the LIBERO benchmark suites (Goal, Spatial, Object, Long) and LIBERO-Plus for generalization evaluation. Qwen2.5-VL-3B is used for the planner (LoRA fine-tuned), and OpenVLA-OFT for the executor (LoRA SFT, then RL). The group size for GRPO is 8. Evaluation involves 50 test episodes per task, reporting average success rates. All experiments are distributed across 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs. Sim-to-real transfer experiments are performed using JoySim and JoyRA-0.1 to validate real-world applicability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of HiRoC in enhancing VLA models for robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance on LIBERO Benchmark

- HiRoC achieved an average success rate of 93.5% across the LIBERO benchmark, establishing a new state-of-the-art. This represents an average improvement of 10.06% over strong baselines.
- Specifically, HiRoC demonstrated high success rates on the Long (98%) and Spatial (95.6%) suites, indicating its capability in complex, long-horizon tasks and those requiring intricate spatial reasoning. It also performed strongly on Object (96%) and Goal (84.4%) suites.
- The hierarchical approach, with the planner providing informative guidance, led to superior performance compared to flat RL-based methods such as VLA-RL and TGRPO.
- HiRoC also outperformed world-model-based methods like World-Env and GRAPE, suggesting that explicit task decomposition contributes more effectively to complex task completion than environmental modeling alone.

#### 4.2. Transfer Ability (Zero-shot Performance) on LIBERO-Plus

- HiRoC exhibited the best generalization capability across all seven perturbation types in the LIBERO-Plus benchmark.
- Its performance notably surpassed SFT-based models like OpenVLA, indicating that online interaction through RL contributes to exploring more diverse trajectories and improving robustness beyond static offline datasets.
- HiRoC also outperformed WorldVLA, suggesting that while world models can improve generalization, the subgoal decomposition strategy provides a more resilient approach to environmental changes without requiring additional adaptation data.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Distribution Misalignment

- Experiments revealed that without the proposed distribution misalignment (DM) training (i.e., SFT pre-training of the executor on subgoal-conditioned data), the initial success rate of the executor was significantly lower.
- Even with subsequent RL tuning, the final performance without DM training remained suboptimal.
- This finding validates the necessity of DM training, as it enables the executor to effectively interpret and follow planner-generated subgoals, leading to superior overall performance.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Training Scheme

- Comparing different integration strategies for task-level and subtask-level learning signals, the results showed that combining their advantage functions (as done in HiRoC) achieved better performance than directly summing their losses.
- This suggests that an advantage-based integration strategy better reconciles the different optimization objectives derived from full and subtask trajectories, leading to more stable and effective policy learning.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Planner Effectiveness

- A 5-fold cross-validation confirmed that the HiRoC-Planner, trained on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, achieved near-perfect accuracy (e.g., 90.20% average overall similarity for mixed trajectory lengths) in decomposing high-level tasks into subgoals.
- In contrast, state-of-the-art multi-step task planning models like RoboBrain2-3B and RoboBrain2-7B demonstrated substantially lower accuracy (e.g., 0.03% and 0.33% respectively for mixed trajectories) under the same settings. This highlights the critical role and necessity of the custom-trained planner within HiRoC.
- Ablation studies further showed that removing the planner ("w/oPlan") drastically degraded the overall success rate (e.g., from 96% to 4% on the Object suite) and resulted in unstable learning curves, affirming the planner's essential contribution to task execution and learning stability.

#### 4.6. Case Studies and Real-World Experiments

- Case studies on LIBERO tasks (e.g., placing alphabet soup in a basket, placing a bowl in a drawer, placing moka pots on a stove) illustrated HiRoC's ability to dynamically decompose complex tasks into sequential subgoals. The executor successfully adapted its actions based on these stage-specific semantic guidances, demonstrating robust long-horizon manipulation.
- A sim-to-real experiment involving placing correction fluid in a box on a real robotic platform confirmed the practical applicability of HiRoC. The policy, trained entirely in a high-fidelity simulator, was directly deployed for zero-shot evaluation and successfully completed the task, showcasing its sim-to-real transfer capability.

#### 4.7. Ablation Studies

- Removing the planner ("w/oPlan") significantly degraded performance, reinforcing its role in task decomposition and guidance.
- Removing the global GRPO ("w/oG") also led to a substantial drop in success rate, indicating its primary contribution to overall task-level optimization.
- Removing the local GRPO ("w/oL") resulted in a performance decrease, confirming its complementary role in providing additional, more granular learning signals for intermediate subgoal progress.
- The planner was also observed to contribute to more stable learning dynamics, as its absence led to increased fluctuations in learning curves.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Hierarchical Robotic Control (HiRoC), a framework that contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Advancement in Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation:** HiRoC demonstrates a robust approach to managing complex, multi-stage robotic tasks. By explicitly modeling task progression through hierarchical planning, it enables VLA agents to effectively tackle long-horizon challenges where flat policies typically fail. This has implications for real-world applications requiring sequential, multi-step operations.
- **Effective Integration of Hierarchical Planning and Online Reinforcement Learning:** The work successfully bridges the gap between existing hierarchical methods (often reliant on offline supervised learning) and online reinforcement learning. By combining a supervised-tuned planner with an RL-optimized executor, HiRoC leverages the strengths of both paradigms, allowing for continuous policy improvement through interaction while maintaining structured task decomposition.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The observed superior performance on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, which includes diverse perturbations, suggests that HiRoC improves the generalization and robustness of VLA models. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in varied and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Practical Solution for Distribution Misalignment:** The proposed strategy of subgoal-conditioned supervised fine-tuning for the executor offers a practical and effective solution to the distribution misalignment problem. This cold-start issue is a common impediment in hierarchical control systems where high-level guidance needs to be reconciled with low-level execution, and addressing it can accelerate the development of more complex systems.
- **Structured Learning Signals for Complex Tasks:** The hierarchical GRPO objective, which adaptively combines task-level and subgoal-level advantages, provides a more informative and structured learning signal for reinforcement learning in long-horizon tasks. This allows the policy to learn from intermediate progress rather than solely sparse terminal rewards, potentially leading to more efficient and stable learning.
- **Demonstrated Sim-to-Real Transferability:** The successful zero-shot deployment of HiRoC policies on a real robotic platform highlights its practical applicability and promising sim-to-real transfer capabilities. This is a significant step towards bridging the gap between simulated training environments and real-world robotic systems, accelerating the development and deployment of VLA-controlled robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies future research directions, such as exploring end-to-end training for hierarchical systems and developing more robust decomposition mechanisms. HiRoC thus serves as a foundational framework that underscores the value of hierarchical decomposition combined with RL-based policy optimization, potentially inspiring further advancements in embodied AI and robotic control.

In summary, HiRoC provides a principled and empirically validated approach to imbue VLA models with hierarchical reasoning and online learning capabilities, positioning it as a significant contribution towards more intelligent, autonomous, and capable robotic manipulation systems.
