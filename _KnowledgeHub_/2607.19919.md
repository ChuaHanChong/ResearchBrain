# Diffusion ReRoll: Revisable Denoising for Robotic Sequential Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Diffusion ReRoll, a framework developed by researchers at the Agency for Defense Development, integrates active, iterative revision into the denoising process for robotic sequential prediction. It manages token-wise noise levels via a programmable schedule matrix, preventing premature commitment and leading to improved long-horizon consistency in planning, policy learning, and unified video-action modeling.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion-based sequence predictors employ a single, monotonic denoising process, limiting direct control over temporal dependencies and leading to "premature commitment."
- Early errors in sequential predictions, especially in long-horizon tasks, become difficult to rectify even when later context or terminal constraints are available.
- Prior methods introducing temporal structure into diffusion models often do not directly address the mutual influence of distant horizon segments during denoising or the core issue of premature commitment.

## Method

- **Diffusion ReRoll (DR)** is a diffusion-based framework that actively manages token-wise noise levels during denoising via a ==programmable schedule matrix==.
- It organizes trajectories into virtual piecewise-linear noise-level patterns, termed ==linear chunks==, within the schedule matrix, which dictate denoising and re-noising behavior.
- **DR** introduces ==ReRoll events== where specific token regions are re-noised (noise level reset to maximum) and then re-denoised, utilizing updated context to prevent premature commitment and enable iterative revision.

## Results

- DR achieved an average success rate of **88.1%** in guidance-based replanning on OGBench, a **21%** relative gain over Diffusion Forcing, and **90.8%** in goal inpainting, a **23%** relative gain over Diffuser.
- On the LIBERO-10 multi-task benchmark, DR improved average success in policy learning by **56.5%** relative to Diffusion Policy (from **40.1%** to **62.7%**).
- In unified video-action modeling, DR increased out-of-distribution (OOD) joint-policy success from **57.2%** to **71.0%** and achieved a lower zero-action error (**0.301m** compared to **0.324m**), indicating stronger action-video consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Actively managing token-wise noise levels through a schedule matrix allows for explicit cross-horizon revision within the diffusion denoising process, preventing premature commitment.
> - Structured stochastic noise-level patterns can provide an inductive bias for generative policies, enabling different denoising steps to serve distinct roles such as exploration, correction, and consolidation.
> - Integrating the revision mechanism directly into the diffusion denoising process, rather than relying on external repair modules, provides a streamlined and programmable interface for temporal control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Sequential prediction is a fundamental component of various robotic and embodied intelligence applications, encompassing tasks such as video prediction, planning, policy learning, and behavior synthesis. Diffusion models, which have demonstrated proficiency in capturing high-dimensional, temporally correlated, and multimodal outputs, have been increasingly adopted for these sequential tasks.

However, existing diffusion-based sequence predictors commonly employ a single, monotonic denoising process across the entire prediction horizon. This approach limits direct control over temporal dependencies and can lead to "premature commitment," where errors or suboptimal predictions made early in the denoising process become difficult to rectify later, even when subsequent context or terminal constraints become available. This limitation is particularly pronounced in long-horizon tasks where early decisions have cascading effects.

Prior research has attempted to introduce temporal structure into diffusion-based sequential generation. Some methods compose smaller generative components for hierarchical or stitched long-horizon planning, such as CompDiffuser. Others, like Diffusion Forcing, assign timestep-dependent noise levels, enabling flexible schedules such as causal denoising, where earlier tokens are resolved before later ones. While these approaches make sequence organization more explicit, they often do not directly address the mutual influence of distant horizon segments during denoising or the problem of premature commitment. For instance, in causal denoising, resolving early segments to a low-noise state can effectively "lock in" an erroneous prefix, rendering it uncorrectable by later, more informed denoising steps.

The concept of revision has been explored in other contexts, including noise injection for editing, diffusion resampling for inpainting, masked re-prediction for iterative token generation, and repair-based refinement for infeasible diffusion plans. These methods highlight the value of iterative refinement but typically involve external modules or separate mechanisms for correction, rather than integrating revisability directly into the core diffusion denoising process itself.

This work addresses the gap by proposing Diffusion ReRoll, a framework that integrates revisability into the generative process by actively managing token-wise noise levels within the diffusion denoising framework. It draws inspiration from observations that stochasticity and iterative denoising can provide beneficial inductive biases for generative policies and that different denoising steps may serve distinct roles such as exploration, correction, and consolidation. Diffusion ReRoll aims to leverage structured stochastic noise-level patterns to enable explicit cross-horizon revision, allowing segments of a sequence to be refined multiple times with updated context, thereby preventing premature commitment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Diffusion ReRoll is to enable revisable denoising over horizons for robotic sequential prediction tasks. This addresses the limitations of conventional diffusion models that typically perform a single, monotonic denoising process, often leading to premature commitment to early predictions.

The core motivations behind this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Premature Commitment**: In many sequential prediction tasks, especially in robotics, an initial prediction might be locally plausible but globally inconsistent or suboptimal when considered with later parts of the sequence or terminal constraints. Standard diffusion models tend to commit early segments to a low-noise state, making it challenging to correct these errors as more context becomes available. Diffusion ReRoll seeks to prevent this by allowing specific regions to revert to a higher noise state, thus remaining revisable.

2. **Enabling Iterative Cross-Horizon Revision**: The authors observed that the diffusion process involves different stages (exploration, correction, consolidation) at varying noise levels. They hypothesize that by strategically manipulating noise levels across the sequence and over denoising steps, information flow and revision can be explicitly controlled. The goal is to allow earlier and later segments of a predicted sequence to mutually revise one another iteratively throughout the denoising process, fostering global consistency.

3. **Integrating Revision within Denoising**: Instead of implementing a separate repair or refinement module, the motivation is to embed the revision mechanism directly within the diffusion denoising process. This streamlines the generative process and leverages the inherent capabilities of diffusion models to refine noisy inputs into coherent outputs. The authors explore whether structured stochastic noise-level patterns can serve as an inductive bias for this revisable denoising.

4. **Providing a Programmable Interface for Temporal Control**: A key objective is to offer a flexible and programmable mechanism to control how information propagates across the prediction horizon (e.g., forward, backward, or endpoint-conditioned influence). This is achieved through a schedule matrix that can be adjusted to emphasize different types of influence without altering the underlying model architecture.

By achieving these objectives, Diffusion ReRoll aims to generate more robust, consistent, and accurate long-horizon predictions in robotic applications, particularly where temporal dependencies and dynamic revisions are critical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Diffusion ReRoll (DR) is a diffusion-based framework designed for revisable robotic sequential prediction, implemented through active management of token-wise noise levels via a schedule matrix. The framework builds upon the concept of per-token noise conditioning, as introduced in Diffusion Forcing (DF).

**Core Mechanism:**
The central idea involves organizing a single trajectory into virtual piecewise-linear noise-level patterns, termed "linear chunks," within the schedule matrix. These chunks are not explicit partitions of the trajectory but rather patterns that dictate the denoising and re-noising behavior across different horizon regions.

**Schedule Matrix for Diffusion ReRoll:**
The schedule matrix $S$ is an $M \times T$ matrix, where $M$ is the number of denoising steps and $T$ is the prediction horizon. $S_{m,t}$ specifies the noise level of token $t$ at denoising step row $m$. Unlike full-sequence diffusion, which applies a uniform noise level, DR constructs $S$ using these virtual piecewise-linear chunks, conceptualized as "denoising waves."

A key feature is the "ReRoll event," triggered when the leading edge of a chunk reaches a predefined "reset noise level." Upon this event, the corresponding token region is re-noised (noise level reset to maximum $K$) and then refined again, utilizing updated context from the rest of the sequence. This mechanism prevents premature commitment by allowing selected regions to be iteratively revised.

The schedule matrix's behavior is parameterized by three main factors:
1. **Denoising slope ($s_{dns}$)**: Defined as $T/n_{dns}$, where $T$ is the prediction horizon and $n_{dns}$ is the number of DDIM rows for one token to denoise from $K$ to $0$. A larger slope results in shorter chunks and more localized refinement, while a smaller slope provides broader temporal context.
2. **Reset noise level ($k_{reset}$)**: The noise level threshold that initiates a ReRoll event. If too high, re-noising occurs prematurely; if too low, the region becomes too deterministic before revision. An intermediate value is generally preferred.
3. **Number of ReRoll events ($N_{roll}$)**: This parameter controls how often full noise is reintroduced to tokens, distributing structured revision across the horizon.

**Bidirectional Schedule Matrix:**
For tasks with both initial and terminal constraints (e.g., goal inpainting), DR employs bidirectional schedule matrices where denoising waves originate from both ends of the horizon. Two variants are explored:
- **Taking Over Matrix**: Backward waves provide early goal-side context but are later superseded by forward causal waves, promoting a more causal final prediction structure.
- **Meeting Matrix**: Forward and backward waves continuously revise the sequence from both directions, maintaining persistent bidirectional influence.

**Training with Linear Chunks:**
To prepare the model for diverse noise configurations at deployment, DR is trained with randomized linear chunks rather than a fixed schedule. This involves sampling:
- The number of chunks ($N_{chunk}$).
- Chunk slopes (from a range around the deployment denoising slope).
- Starting noise levels (from a zero-to-middle noise range, with a wider range for the first chunk to cover initial high-noise and already-denoised states).
- Masking lengths to mimic unresolved regions.
Bidirectional training further includes mixing forward, masked bidirectional (masking around the meeting region), and full-horizon bidirectional patterns, allowing some right-side chunks to be reversed. This ensures the model is exposed to structured local noise patterns across a broad distribution.

**DDIM Updates with Schedule Matrices:**
DR's denoising process utilizes Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) updates. For a denoising transition where the noise level decreases, standard DDIM updates are applied (deterministically with $\eta=0$ in experiments). For a ReRoll event where the noise level increases, an explicit forward noising transition is applied, re-injecting new Gaussian noise and making the reset transition stochastic.

**Model Architecture:**
DR does not require a new backbone architecture. It necessitates per-token noise-level conditioning, typically achieved by providing a noise-level embedding to each token. The implementation often uses a transformer backbone, where each sequence timestep (action, state, or video latent) is represented as a token. Minor modifications were made to baseline architectures (e.g., Diffusion Policy's Chi-Transformer, UWM's DiT-style transformer) to accommodate per-token noise levels instead of global timestep conditioning.

**Action Representation for Manipulation:**
For manipulation tasks, DR employs an anchor-relative cumulative action representation, where future end-effector targets are expressed relative to the current pose. This representation, combined with horizon-dependent normalization, enhances the stability of token-wise revision as each horizon token represents a more self-contained future pose target.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Diffusion ReRoll (DR) was conducted across long-horizon planning, policy learning, and unified video-action modeling, consistently demonstrating performance improvements over baseline diffusion methods.

**1. Effect of ReRoll Events:**
An initial ablation study isolated the effect of ReRoll events. The results indicated that increasing the number of ReRoll events generally improves performance across all evaluated task categories (planning, policy learning, unified video-action modeling) up to a saturation point. This supports the hypothesis that selective re-noising, enabling repeated revision, is a primary driver of DR's performance gains.

**2. Long-Horizon Planning:**
Evaluated on OGBench PointMaze and AntMaze, DR was compared against Diffuser (full-sequence diffusion) and Diffusion Forcing (causal denoising).
- **Guidance-based Replanning**: DR achieved an average success rate of 88.1%, representing a 21% relative gain over Diffusion Forcing (72.8%).
- **Goal Inpainting**: DR achieved an average success rate of 90.8%, demonstrating a 23% relative gain over Diffuser (73.8%).
- Across both protocols and various maze sizes (especially Large and Giant mazes), DR consistently outperformed baselines, indicating its ability to maintain trajectory consistency and recover from early planning errors. Qualitative visualizations showed that unlike full-sequence diffusion, which can commit to early incorrect plans, ReRoll's revisable nature allowed it to explore alternatives and recover valid paths.

**3. Policy Learning:**
Evaluated on LIBERO-10 multi-task and RoboCasa single-task benchmarks, focusing on action-chunk prediction.
- **LIBERO-10 Multi-task**: DR improved average success by 56.5% relative to Diffusion Policy (from 40.1% to 62.7% under the official protocol) across different prediction horizons and history lengths. The gains were more pronounced in the multi-task setting, where diverse contexts benefit from continued cross-horizon revision. The study also highlighted the sensitivity of LIBERO-10 evaluations to initial gripper reset protocols, with DR maintaining strong performance under both "official" and "open-gripper" initializations.
- **RoboCasa Single-task**: DR showed less consistent superiority on single-task learning, potentially because narrower behavior distributions in these datasets offer less scope for repeated revision to resolve competing action structures.

**4. Unified Video-Action Modeling:**
Applied to Unified World Models (UWM) for joint action and future visual prediction, evaluated on LIBERO-10.
- DR (UWM + DR) consistently improved performance in policy generation, inverse dynamics, and especially under out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation.
- **OOD Joint-Policy Success**: DR increased OOD joint-policy success from 57.2% (UWM) to 71.0%.
- **Action-Video Consistency**: DR achieved the lowest zero-action error (0.301m compared to 0.324m for UWM), indicating a stronger consistency between imposed actions (e.g., zero actions) and the generated visual dynamics.
- Analysis of bidirectional schedules showed that the "meeting matrix," which preserves continuous bidirectional interaction between action and image tokens, consistently outperformed the "taking over matrix," suggesting persistent cross-modal revision is beneficial for video-action modeling.

In summary, Diffusion ReRoll consistently improved performance across diverse robotic sequential prediction tasks by enabling active, iterative revision within the diffusion denoising process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Diffusion ReRoll represents a development in diffusion-based sequential prediction for robotics, primarily by integrating a mechanism for active, iterative revision directly into the denoising process.

**Significance:**

1. **Addressing Premature Commitment**: The core contribution is the framework's ability to prevent premature commitment to early predictions. By selectively re-noising regions that have become locally stable, Diffusion ReRoll allows for later refinement using updated context from the rest of the prediction horizon. This iterative cross-horizon revision mitigates a common challenge in long-horizon sequential generation, where initial errors can become difficult to rectify.
2. **Programmable Revision Interface**: The schedule matrix serves as a flexible, programmable interface that enables explicit control over how information flows and how revision occurs across the horizon. This mechanism allows the same model architecture to adapt its generative behavior, emphasizing forward, backward, or endpoint-conditioned influence without requiring architectural changes, thereby enhancing the model's adaptability to various task structures.
3. **Empirical Performance Improvement**: The consistent empirical gains across diverse and representative robotic tasks—including long-horizon planning (OGBench PointMaze and AntMaze), policy learning (LIBERO-10, RoboCasa), and unified video-action modeling—demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the revisable denoising approach. The improvements in long-horizon recovery, robust action prediction, and action-video consistency are noteworthy.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **More Robust Robotic Systems**: By making sequential predictions more revisable and less prone to early commitment, Diffusion ReRoll could lead to the development of more robust and fault-tolerant robotic control policies and planners. Robots could potentially recover from suboptimal intermediate decisions by leveraging future context, leading to more reliable execution in complex, uncertain environments.
2. **Enhanced Long-Horizon Planning**: The framework's ability to improve long-horizon planning suggests its utility in tasks requiring foresight and global consistency. This could enable robots to execute more intricate and extended sequences of actions with greater success rates.
3. **Improved Generalization and Adaptation**: The structured training with randomized linear chunks, coupled with the flexible schedule matrix, may contribute to models that are more capable of handling varied temporal dynamics and adapting to different task constraints without extensive re-training.
4. **Advancements in Multimodal Robotic Models**: In unified video-action modeling, the improvement in action-video consistency and out-of-distribution performance suggests a potential for more coherent and trustworthy generative world models that better integrate visual and action modalities.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**

1. **Simulation-Only Validation**: All experiments were conducted in simulation. Real-world robotic validation is necessary to confirm the framework's applicability and robustness in physical environments.
2. **Schedule Tuning Requirements**: While a shared default matrix was used for most experiments, Diffusion ReRoll still requires tuning of schedule parameters (denoising slope, reset noise level, number of ReRoll events) for new tasks, which can impact both performance and inference speed.
3. **Scale and Architecture**: The approach was tested with transformer denoisers and benchmark-scale datasets. Its effectiveness on more general robot models or larger Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models remains to be explored in future work.

Despite these limitations, Diffusion ReRoll offers a novel perspective on managing temporal dependencies in diffusion models, proposing that the denoising process itself can serve as a powerful and programmable interface for generating revisable and robust robotic sequences.
