# CrayonRobo: Object-Centric Prompt-Driven Vision-Language-Action Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A multi-modal prompt-driven framework enables robots to perform complex manipulation tasks through crayon-style visual overlays combined with language instructions, demonstrating improved success rates on manipulation tasks while introducing a novel keyframe-based approach for long-horizon task execution through the integration of contact points, end-effector orientation, and movement direction specifications.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation approaches struggle with precise task specification and complex, long-horizon tasks
- Current methods (pure vision, language, or goal-image based) lack either functional understanding or contain excessive irrelevant information

## Method

- Developed **CrayonRobo**, combining visual prompts (contact points, orientations, movement directions) with language instructions
- Implemented a keyframe sequencing system to break down complex tasks into manageable steps
- Created a ==Vision-Language-Action== model using LLaMA-adapter and CLIP's visual encoder

## Results

- Achieved higher success rates compared to baseline methods in manipulation tasks
- Demonstrated robustness to noise in visual prompts while maintaining performance
- Successfully transferred to real-world robot deployment without sim-to-real fine-tuning
- Showed effective generalization to unseen tasks and objects

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal prompts (visual + language) outperform single-modality approaches
> - Progressive training strategy improves model comprehension of different prompt types
> - Visual prompts can be automatically extracted using Grounded-DINO and GPT-4

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a significant challenge in robotics: how to effectively communicate task goals to robots. It sits at the intersection of several active research areas:

- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Robotics:** The paper builds upon the recent surge in using VLMs for robotic manipulation. VLMs allow robots to understand both visual inputs (images, videos) and language instructions, enabling more flexible and intuitive task specification. This paper extends this area by introducing visual prompts.
- **Robotic Manipulation:** The core focus is on enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks. This field encompasses various approaches, including deep learning, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning. CrayonRobo contributes by providing a new method for guiding robot actions using a combination of visual and language cues.
- **Prompt Engineering for Robotics:** The paper aligns with the trend of using prompts (visual, language, or multi-modal) to guide robotic behavior. Similar to how prompt engineering improves the performance of large language models, it aims to improve the robot's task execution. The method is most closely related to visual prompt-conditioned manipulation
- **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** By focusing on intuitive and expressive task specifications, this work contributes to improved HRI. The crayon-style prompts offer a potentially more user-friendly way to instruct robots compared to complex code or demonstrations.

The paper acknowledges and differentiates itself from existing approaches:

- **Pure Vision-Based Manipulation:** It argues that while these approaches focus on actionability, they may lack the functional understanding necessary for complex tasks.
- **Language-Conditioned Manipulation:** It points out the ambiguity and lack of detail in language instructions, often requiring human assistance.
- **Goal Image or Video-Conditioned Manipulation:** It argues that these methods encode excessive and often irrelevant information.
- **Visual Prompt-Conditioned Manipulation:** This is the most related field, and the paper directly compares itself to methods like RT-Sketch and RT-Trajectory, highlighting its advantages in conveying both positional and directional information and avoiding trajectory overlap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a novel method for task specification in robotic manipulation.** The goal is to overcome the limitations of existing methods by providing a more precise, expressive, and user-friendly way to communicate task goals to robots.
- **Enable robots to understand and execute complex, long-horizon tasks.** The paper aims to develop a system that can break down complex tasks into a sequence of keyframes and execute them sequentially based on visual and language prompts.
- **Enhance the robustness and generalization ability of robotic manipulation systems.** By providing clear and interpretable prompts, the authors aim to improve the robot's ability to handle novel tasks and adapt to different environments.

The motivation behind this work stems from the desire to create more capable and versatile robots that can effectively assist humans in various tasks. The limitations of current approaches to task specification motivate the development of a new method that combines the strengths of both visual and language modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the CrayonRobo approach involves:

- **Multi-Modal Prompts:** Using a combination of visual (crayon-style overlays on RGB images) and language prompts to convey task goals.
- **Keyframe Sequencing:** Representing long-horizon tasks as a sequence of keyframes, each with its own set of prompts.
- **Prompt Components:** The visual prompts consist of:
 - Contact Point (blue): Indicates where the robot should make contact with the object.
 - End-Effector Orientation (red and green): Defines the desired orientation of the robot's end-effector at the contact point.
 - Movement Direction (yellow): Specifies the direction the robot should move after making contact.
- **Model Architecture:** Utilizing a Vision-Language-Action model (VLA) based on LLaMA-adapter and CLIP's visual encoder to process visual and language prompts.
- **Training Strategy:** A progressive training strategy is used to enhance the model's ability to comprehend each type of prompt, which involves training the model on different input pairs. Training objectives include a text supervision loss, an orthogonal loss, and a projection loss.
- **Data Collection:** Using a simulator (SAPIEN) and the PartNet-Mobility dataset to generate training data consisting of images, prompts, and corresponding robot actions.
- **Inference Strategy:** Using the Grounded-DINO model and GPT-4, the visual prompts can be extracted automatically. The system can determine whether the movement direction after contact is needed depending on the task characteristics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents experimental results in both simulated and real-world environments that demonstrate the effectiveness of CrayonRobo:

- **Superior Performance:** CrayonRobo achieves significantly higher success rates in manipulation tasks compared to baseline methods, including pure vision-based, language-conditioned, goal image/video-conditioned, and existing visual prompt-conditioned approaches.
- **Robustness to Noise:** The model exhibits tolerance to noise in the visual prompts, maintaining good performance even when the prompts are not perfectly accurate.
- **Generalization Ability:** The model demonstrates promising performance on unseen tasks, indicating its ability to generalize to new objects and environments.
- **Real-World Feasibility:** The real-world experiments show that CrayonRobo can be successfully deployed on a real robot without additional sim-to-real fine-tuning.

The ablation studies provide insights into the importance of different components of the approach:

- **Importance of Multi-Modal Prompts:** Combining both visual and language prompts leads to better performance than relying on either modality alone.
- **Effectiveness of Training strategy:** Progressive introduction of each type of prompt helps improve the performance.
- **Importance of Individual Loss Functions:** Each loss term (text supervision, orthogonal, and projection) contributes to the overall performance of the model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CrayonRobo approach has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Improved Task Specification for Robotics:** It offers a more intuitive and expressive way to communicate task goals to robots, bridging the gap between human intention and robot execution.
- **Increased Robot Versatility:** By enabling robots to understand and execute complex, long-horizon tasks, CrayonRobo can significantly expand the range of tasks that robots can perform.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** The user-friendly interface and the ability to provide feedback through prompts can foster more effective collaboration between humans and robots.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated performance in real-world experiments suggests that CrayonRobo has the potential to be applied to various real-world scenarios, such as manufacturing, healthcare, and service robotics.

Potential limitations include:

- **Obstacle Avoidance:** The current system does not explicitly address obstacle avoidance, which is a critical aspect of real-world robotic manipulation.
- **Dependency on Simulator:** The reliance on simulated data for training may limit the model's ability to generalize to highly complex or unstructured real-world environments.

Overall, the "CrayonRobo" paper presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation. By introducing a novel approach for task specification that combines visual and language prompts, the authors have demonstrated a promising path toward creating more versatile, robust, and user-friendly robotic systems. Future work could focus on addressing the limitations mentioned above and exploring the application of CrayonRobo to a wider range of real-world tasks.
