# MM-CoT:A Benchmark for Probing Visual Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-sen University and industry partners introduced MM-CoT, a diagnostic benchmark designed to evaluate visual grounding and logical coherence in Chain-of-Thought reasoning for multimodal models. The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art models like GPT-5 achieve only 19.47% accuracy on verification tasks, highlighting a substantial gap between their generative fluency and genuine reasoning fidelity.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal Chain-of-Thought benchmarks primarily focus on generating plausible answers and fluent rationales, overlooking the verification of visual grounding and logical coherence.
- Multimodal models often produce explanations that are linguistically plausible but unfaithful to visual evidence, leading to hallucination or misinterpretation of visual cues.
- Current evaluation methods fail to diagnose specific reasoning failures where models might rely on linguistic shortcuts or dataset priors without true visual understanding.

## Method

- MM-CoT was developed as a novel, large-scale diagnostic benchmark that re-frames multimodal CoT reasoning as a ==discriminative verification task==.
- A ==triadic event-chain format (A→B→C)== was designed, requiring models to simultaneously satisfy visual grounding and logical coherence for each step.
- The benchmark uses a =='Generate-then-Disturb' pipeline==, leveraging GPT-4o for generating valid chains and adversarial distractors (visually inconsistent or logically incoherent), followed by rigorous human verification.

## Results

- Leading multimodal models, including proprietary ones like GPT-5 (**19.47%** accuracy) and open-source models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B (**32%** accuracy), perform significantly below human experts (**82.57%** accuracy) on MM-CoT.
- Performance on video tasks notably degrades with increased temporal span and complexity, indicating limitations in current LVLMs for genuine causal event reconstruction.
- Utilizing Chain-of-Thought and Reflective Reasoning paradigms consistently improved model performance (e.g., GPT-5's video extreme tasks improved from **7.54%** to **13.82%** with Reflective Reasoning), validating the benchmark's sensitivity to enhanced reasoning structures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Despite their generative fluency, current state-of-the-art multimodal models demonstrate significant deficiencies in verifying true visual grounding and logical coherence, falling far behind human performance.
> - MM-CoT successfully differentiates model capabilities, exposing specific challenges in multi-step reasoning for images and cross-frame state tracking for videos.
> - The benchmark tasks genuinely require multimodal perception, as removing visual signals dramatically reduces model accuracy, preventing reliance on purely linguistic patterns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of multimodal models (MMs), particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has witnessed rapid advancements, enabling them to generate sophisticated multi-step explanations for complex visual tasks, often through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. These models represent a major milestone, pushing the capabilities of AI in understanding and interpreting the visual world alongside natural language. However, the authors identify a critical gap in the current research landscape: despite the apparent fluency of these models in generating explanations, there remains an unaddressed question of whether such reasoning is genuinely *grounded in visual evidence* and *logically coherent*.

Existing multimodal benchmarks, such as VQAv2, GQA, VCR, MMBench, and MMMU, primarily emphasize the *generation* of plausible answers or fluent rationales. They reward models for producing narratives that sound correct, but largely overlook the *verification* aspect – the capacity to assess whether a reasoning chain is both visually consistent and logically valid. This oversight can lead to models replicating familiar reasoning templates or exploiting linguistic shortcuts and dataset priors without truly understanding the underlying causal or visual structure of a scene. Consequently, models can produce explanations that reference non-existent objects, misinterpret visual cues, or violate causal ordering, a phenomenon often referred to as hallucination or unfaithful reasoning.

MM-CoT directly addresses this fundamental evaluation gap. By reframing multimodal CoT reasoning as a *discriminative verification task*, it shifts the focus from simply generating plausible output to verifying the fidelity and coherence of reasoning steps. This approach is distinct from purely text-centric CoT methods, where intermediate steps, even if linguistically plausible, might still be useful. For multimodal reasoning, however, each intermediate step *must be visually warranted* by the scene. MM-CoT operationalizes this by designing a triadic event-chain format (A→B→C) that demands step-level scrutiny, compelling models to simultaneously satisfy what is seen (visual grounding) and what follows (logical/causal coherence). This enables the disentanglement of perceptual evidence from inferential soundness, surfacing distinct error profiles that are obscured by conventional answer-only or generation-based benchmarks. Thus, MM-CoT provides a unique and complementary dimension to the existing suite of multimodal evaluation tools, crucial for advancing AI systems toward genuine, evidence-driven understanding rather than mere pattern matching.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind the MM-CoT benchmark stems from a critical observation about the current state of multimodal models: their impressive ability to generate detailed, multi-step explanations (Chain-of-Thought) for visual tasks often conceals a deeper deficiency. While these explanations may be remarkably fluent and linguistically plausible, they frequently lack genuine grounding in the visual evidence and may violate logical or causal coherence.

The key objectives of this research are therefore:
1. **Identify and highlight a fundamental evaluation gap:** To formally recognize that existing multimodal CoT benchmarks are predominantly generative, rewarding fluency and plausibility, but fail to adequately assess the *verification* of visual grounding and structural validity of reasoning chains.
2. **Introduce a diagnostic benchmark:** To develop MM-CoT, a novel, large-scale benchmark specifically designed to probe the visual grounding and logical coherence of CoT reasoning in multimodal models. This involves reframing the evaluation task from open-ended generation to discriminative verification.
3. **Disentangle and diagnose reasoning failures:** To create a benchmark that can differentiate between distinct reasoning failure modes, such as visual evidence mismatches, causal misalignments, and multi-step brittleness. This aims to provide fine-grained diagnostic insights into *where* and *how* multimodal reasoning breaks down, rather than just *whether* it arrives at a correct answer.
4. **Drive the development of truly grounded models:** By exposing the limitations of current models in faithfully reasoning within the visual world, MM-CoT seeks to provide a principled foundation for guiding future research toward developing models that reason not just plausibly, but with genuine fidelity and coherence.

In essence, the motivation is to push beyond the "surface sophistication" of current multimodal AI and assess its true inferential depth, ensuring that generated explanations are not just narratives but reflections of sound, evidence-based reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodological innovation of MM-CoT lies in its shift from an open-ended explanation *generation* task to a *discriminative verification* task. Instead of asking models to produce a Chain-of-Thought, MM-CoT presents them with an image or video and a set of candidate event chains, requiring them to select the *unique valid chain*.

#### 3.1. Formalism of Visually Grounded Reasoning Chains

A reasoning chain `c` is defined as a Logical-Sequential Chain (A → B → C), where:
- **A (Initiating Condition):** An event grounded in the visual input `V`. It can be a physical change, a deliberate human action, or an external entity interaction.
- **B (Mediating Event):** A visually evidenced causal mediator that is observably consistent with `V` and causally linked to A.
- **C (Outcome):** A logically consequent outcome that follows from B under commonsense or physical regularities.

A chain is considered valid only if it satisfies two orthogonal constraints:
- **Visual Grounding (Φ_vis):** All events (A, B, C) must be factually anchored to observable evidence in the visual input `V` without hallucination.
- **Logical Coherence (Φ_log):** The causal or temporal transitions between events must be plausible, adhering to physical and commonsense principles.

The model's objective is to identify `c*` (the uniquely valid chain) from a set of candidates, where each distractor intentionally violates exactly one of these two constraints.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction Pipeline

The MM-CoT benchmark consists of 5,616 image-based and 2,100 video-based reasoning instances. The construction follows a "Generate-then-Disturb" pipeline, leveraging GPT-4o for automated chain generation and distractor engineering, followed by rigorous human verification.

- **Data Sourcing and Pre-processing:**
 - **Image Modality:** 5,615 instances derived from Flickr30k. Images were selected using a "Semantic Density Filtering" strategy, prioritizing those rich in human-object or multi-agent interactions to ensure high causal potential, and then sampled for diversity using CLIP embeddings.
 - **Video Modality:** 2,100 instances from ShareGPT4Video. Videos were temporally sliced into short (<5s), medium (5-15s), and long (>10s) segments, and filtered by "Motion Salience" using optical flow to ensure dynamic visual changes.

- **Automated Chain Generation and Distractor Engineering:**
 - **Step 1: Valid Chain Generation:** GPT-4o acts as a "visual reasoning expert" to generate three distinct A→B→C chains for each visual input. Diversity is enforced by constraining A to three types: Physical Causality, Behavior Causality, and External Inference.
 - **Step 2: Distractor Engineering:** Adversarial distractors are created for each valid chain:
 - **Visual Inconsistency Distractors (¬Φ_vis):** Generated via "Counterfactual Hallucination." The model imagines a scene `V'` different from `V` (e.g., changing objects, environment) and creates a chain that is logically consistent with `V'` but factually false for `V`. The text is linguistically plausible to prevent easy detection.
 - **Logical Incoherence Distractors (¬Φ_log):** Generated via "Causal Perturbation." The model uses *only* elements present in `V` but disrupts the causal order (e.g., reversing cause and effect) or introduces spurious correlations, making the error purely inferential.

- **Filtering and Human Verification:**
 - **De-duplication:** BERTScore is used to discard chains with excessive semantic overlap (ambiguous) or extremely low overlap (too easy).
 - **Human Expert Review:** A random 10% subset underwent a two-stage human review by 15 expert annotators (PhD students/researchers). They verified strict adherence to visual grounding and logical validity. Batches with >10% error rate were rejected and regenerated. Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA) was high (Fleiss’ Kappa > 0.79), confirming objectivity. Ambiguous cases were filtered out to maintain a "Single-Truth" policy.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics

- **Primary Metric: Chain Selection Accuracy (Acc):** The percentage of instances where the model correctly selects the unique valid chain. This evaluates full-chain verification.
- **Diagnostic Dimensions:**
 - **Visual Grounding Verification:** Measures robustness against visually inconsistent distractors.
 - **Logical Coherence Verification:** Measures rejection of causally flawed distractors that are visually compatible.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The benchmark evaluates leading proprietary models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-Sonnet-4, Grok-2-Vision-1212) and open-source VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, LLaMA-3.2-90B, GLM-4.5V, InternVL3, Ovis-2.5, LLaVA-1.5, Idefics2), ensuring fair comparisons with aligned inference configurations. Human performance is also measured as a benchmark.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies

- **Reasoning Paradigms:** Comparison of Direct Answer, Standard CoT, and Reflective Reasoning to quantify performance gains.
- **Verification of Visual Dependency (Text-Only):** Removing visual signals to confirm that MM-CoT tasks genuinely require multimodal perception.
- **Error Analysis:** In-depth examination of failure cases for Qwen2.5-VL-72B and GPT-5 to identify error categories (e.g., Semantic Redundancy Failure, Non-Causal Attribute Selection).

This comprehensive methodology ensures that MM-CoT is a robust, diagnostic tool capable of uncovering the nuanced deficiencies in current multimodal reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluations on MM-CoT revealed several key insights into the capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art multimodal models:

1. **Significant Performance Gap:** Even the most advanced proprietary and open-source VLMs struggle considerably on MM-CoT. The overall accuracy across models is low (e.g., GPT-5 at 19.47%, Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 32%), revealing a sharp discrepancy between their generative fluency (as observed in other benchmarks) and their true reasoning fidelity, visual grounding, and logical coherence. Human performance, in contrast, consistently achieves high accuracy (82.57% overall), highlighting the substantial room for improvement in AI.

2. **Differentiation of Capabilities:** MM-CoT effectively differentiates model capabilities, particularly when shifting from single-step to multi-step reasoning in images, and across varying difficulty tiers in videos.
 - **Image Reasoning:** Models show a notable performance drop from "Single" (direct visual evidence) to "Multi" (requiring bridging unobserved intermediate states), indicating challenges with inferential depth.
 - **Video Reasoning:** Performance generally degrades significantly as video temporal span and complexity increase (from Easy to Hard/Extreme). This suggests a persistent limitation in current LVLMs for cross-frame state tracking and genuine causal event reconstruction. Interestingly, some models performed *better* on long videos than short ones, which the authors attribute not to superior long-range temporal reasoning, but to stronger *narrative priors* in longer clips, allowing models to generate plausible responses through story completion rather than fine-grained dynamic visual reasoning.

3. **Sensitivity to Reasoning Paradigms:** The benchmark demonstrates sensitivity to different reasoning strategies. Transitioning from a "Direct Answer" approach to more structured "Chain-of-Thought" and "Reflective Reasoning" paradigms consistently led to performance improvements across various models. For example, GPT-5's accuracy on video extreme tasks improved from 7.54% (Direct Answer) to 13.82% (Reflective Reasoning). This validates MM-CoT's effectiveness in capturing the marginal contributions of enhanced reasoning structures. Reflective Reasoning, in particular, showed critical gains in highly complex scenarios, suggesting that self-correction mechanisms are vital for robust long-context causal tracking.

4. **Verification of Visual Dependency:** A crucial "Text-Only" control experiment, where visual signals were removed, resulted in a dramatic accuracy collapse (30-70% drop) across all tested models and tasks. This unequivocally confirms that MM-CoT tasks cannot be solved by relying on linguistic patterns or superficial logical inference alone; they demand genuine multimodal perception and visual-semantic alignment, effectively preventing language-only shortcut strategies.

5. **Detailed Error Analysis:** An in-depth error analysis on Qwen2.5-VL-72B and GPT-5 revealed specific and pervasive failure modes:
 - **Image-based Errors:** "Semantic Redundancy Failure" (42-54%) was dominant, where models merely restate existing content without advancing the causal chain. "Visual Distraction Error" and "Hypothetical Condition Dependence" were also significant, indicating vulnerability to salient but irrelevant visual elements and over-reliance on textual premises during counterfactual reasoning.
 - **Video-based Errors:** "Non-Causal Attribute Selection" (34-44%) and "Direct Cause Omission" (20-22%) were most prevalent, showing struggles in identifying the minimal causal set and immediate causal antecedents. Models also exhibited "Causal Over-Specification" and "Counterfactual Modeling Errors," highlighting limitations in causal factor selection, chain localization, and counterfactual inference.

6. **Uniqueness of MM-CoT:** The benchmark exhibits consistently low correlation with existing multimodal metrics, confirming that it measures a distinct and previously unassessed dimension of reasoning—specifically, the simultaneous verification of visual grounding and logical coherence.

In summary, the findings indicate that despite rapid progress, current multimodal models have fundamental weaknesses in truly understanding and reasoning about visual causality and evidence. MM-CoT successfully exposes these limitations and provides a rigorous framework for diagnosing them.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MM-CoT benchmark carries significant implications for the future direction of multimodal AI research and development. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Diagnostic Precision for Model Development:** The most immediate and profound impact of MM-CoT is its role as a precise diagnostic tool. By exposing specific failure modes—visual inconsistency, logical incoherence, and multi-step brittleness—it offers granular insights into *where* models fall short, rather than just *whether* they fail. This fine-grained error analysis is invaluable for researchers, allowing them to pinpoint weaknesses in model architectures, training methodologies, and data curation. This specificity will guide the development of more robust, visually grounded, and causally aware multimodal models.

2. **Elevating the Standard of Multimodal Reasoning:** MM-CoT fundamentally raises the bar for evaluating multimodal AI. By shifting the paradigm from generative fluency to discriminative verification, it pushes models beyond superficial pattern matching and plausible narration towards a deeper, evidence-based inferential capability. Future models will need to demonstrate not just the ability to generate coherent text about a scene but also to faithfully anchor every reasoning step to observable visual evidence and maintain logical validity throughout a causal chain.

3. **Addressing Hallucination and Unfaithful Reasoning:** Hallucination and unfaithful reasoning remain critical challenges in large language and multimodal models. MM-CoT directly confronts these issues by penalizing explanations that are linguistically plausible but visually ungrounded or logically flawed. By training models to reject such distractors, the benchmark implicitly encourages the development of systems that are more truthful and reliable in their reasoning, reducing the risk of generating misleading or factually incorrect information.

4. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The benchmark's findings highlight specific areas needing intensive research, such as improving causal factor selection, enhancing cross-frame state tracking in videos, strengthening counterfactual reasoning mechanisms, and balancing text-visual information integration. The consistent struggle of even state-of-the-art models on MM-CoT suggests that significant breakthroughs are still required in these foundational aspects of multimodal intelligence.

5. **Impact on Real-World Applications:** Models capable of truly grounded and logically coherent reasoning are essential for numerous high-stakes real-world applications. These include autonomous driving (where misinterpreting a visual cue or a causal sequence could be catastrophic), medical diagnosis (requiring precise interpretation of visual data and logical inference), scientific discovery (demanding accurate causal reasoning from experimental observations), and even advanced human-AI interaction. By fostering models with higher reasoning fidelity, MM-CoT contributes to building more trustworthy and effective AI systems for these critical domains.

In conclusion, MM-CoT is not merely another benchmark; it represents a crucial step forward in the rigorous evaluation of multimodal AI. By challenging models on fundamental aspects of visual grounding and logical coherence, it provides the tools necessary to diagnose current limitations and pave the way for a new generation of multimodal models that can reason not just plausibly, but faithfully and coherently within the complex visual world.
