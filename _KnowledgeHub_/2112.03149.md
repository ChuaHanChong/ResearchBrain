# Distilled Domain Randomization

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Distilled Domain Randomization (DiDoR), an algorithm that enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for robot control policies by training multiple specialized "teacher" policies on fixed randomized simulation environments and then distilling their collective knowledge into a single "student" policy. The approach achieves performance comparable to or superior to baseline methods in locomotion and manipulation tasks while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for real-time robotic applications.

## Problem

- Training deep reinforcement learning policies on physical robots is costly, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe due to extensive data requirements.
- The 'reality gap' causes policies trained solely in simulation to perform poorly on real robots due to inevitable discrepancies between simulated and physical environments.
- Naive domain randomization methods can lead to unstable training for a single policy if the parameter distribution is too broad, hindering convergence.
- Ensemble methods, while robust, impose significant memory and computational overhead at deployment, which is a critical limitation for real-time robot control.

## Method

- **DiDoR** trains a set of individual 'teacher' policies in parallel, with each teacher assigned to a unique, fixed instance of a randomized simulation environment.
- After teachers are trained, their combined knowledge is transferred to a single 'student' policy through ==policy distillation==. The student is updated by minimizing the sum of Kullback-Leibler divergences between its action distribution and those of each teacher.
- The student policy is a single, smaller neural network designed to synthesize the diverse expertise of the teachers, offering computational efficiency during deployment.

## Results

- DiDoR demonstrated competitive or superior performance compared to Uniform Domain Randomization, Ensemble Methods, and P2PDRL baselines in both sim-to-sim (Hopper, Half-Cheetah, Ant) and sim-to-real (Furuta pendulum, Cart-pole) environments.
- The method achieved successful zero-shot transfer to a physical Furuta pendulum, and showed effective generalization to unseen simulation instances across all tasks, though the Cart-pole task presented challenges due to complex friction modeling.
- DiDoR maintained computational efficiency for real-time control, requiring no increased memory capacity or action computation time during deployment compared to single-policy baselines, unlike ensemble methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training individual teacher policies on fixed domain instances promotes more stable and reproducible reinforcement learning than continuously varying environments.
> - Consolidating knowledge from multiple teachers into a single student policy significantly reduces memory footprint and action computation time at deployment, crucial for real-time robot control applications.
> - The distillation process enables the student policy to effectively generalize by synthesizing diverse expertise, leading to robustness against model mismatches during sim-to-real transfer.
