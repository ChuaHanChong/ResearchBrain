# DRPO: Efficient Reasoning via Decoupled Reward Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Decoupled Reward Policy Optimization (DRPO), developed by researchers at Texas A&M University and the University of Virginia, enhances Large Reasoning Models by significantly reducing the length of generated reasoning paths while preserving performance. The method achieves this by decoupling reward signals for correct and incorrect answers and normalizing length-based rewards solely within correct samples, resulting in a 51% length reduction with only a 2.6% performance loss on a 7B model.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) frequently exhibit "overthinking," generating verbose and redundant reasoning steps that increase computational costs and inference latency.
- Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, particularly those based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), incur substantial performance degradation when employing length penalties to encourage conciseness.
- GRPO's group-relative advantage function can inadvertently assign negative learning signals to correct but lengthy outputs, mistakenly discouraging otherwise valid reasoning pathways.

## Method

- DRPO ==decouples== the reward processing for positive (correct) and negative (incorrect) samples, ensuring that length penalties only modulate the strength of a positive signal without rendering it negative.
- The framework integrates an "optimized positive data distribution" that explicitly maximizes length-based rewards under a KL regularization into a discriminative RL objective, deriving a closed-form analytical solution.
- Learning signals are efficiently computed using only on-policy data and importance weighting, where each positive data point is assigned a weight proportional to its length reward, normalized exclusively within the positive samples.

## Results

- DRPO reduced reasoning length by **51%** (from 3053 to 1502 tokens) on a **7B** model with only a **2.6%** performance loss, outperforming all six baseline methods that exhibited higher performance degradation for similar or lesser length reductions.
- The method consistently achieved positive Accuracy Efficiency Scores (AES) across **1.5B**, **7B**, and **8B** models (e.g., **0.249** for **7B**), indicating a superior trade-off between accuracy and computational cost compared to baselines, which mostly yielded negative AES scores.
- On easy questions (GSM8K), DRPO achieved a **73.1%** length reduction (from 969 to 261 tokens) for the **7B** model with only a **0.6%** performance loss, demonstrating significant efficiency gains while preserving reflection capabilities and eliminating redundant reasoning steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Widely adopted RL frameworks like GRPO are fundamentally limited in multi-objective optimization (e.g., correctness and conciseness) because their group-relative advantage functions can misinterpret correct but verbose outputs as undesirable.
> - Decoupling reward normalization for correct answers prevents interference from incorrect examples, allowing length penalties to guide toward conciseness without corrupting the fundamental positive learning signal.
> - A principled mathematical formulation for an optimized positive data distribution, incorporating length rewards and a KL constraint, enables tractable and effective optimization for efficient reasoning using only on-policy data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) represents a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, with models demonstrating proficiency in complex tasks such as mathematics, coding, and scientific problem-solving. These models, often leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and extended Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, enhance performance by generating elaborate reasoning paths, allowing for self-correction and exploration of alternatives. This paradigm contrasts with earlier language models that focused on direct answers, highlighting a shift towards more deliberative, multi-step reasoning capabilities.

Despite their reasoning power, existing LRMs frequently exhibit a phenomenon termed "overthinking," producing verbose and redundant reasoning steps even for straightforward questions. This overthinking leads to increased computational costs and higher inference latency, limiting their practical deployment and scalability. The broader research landscape has, therefore, seen efforts to mitigate this issue, categorized into training-free methods (e.g., prompt manipulation, decoding process adjustments), Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) methods (e.g., curating compressed reasoning datasets), and RL-based methods.

Among these, RL-based methods are considered particularly effective due to their scalability and flexibility. They often incorporate explicit reward shaping, such as length penalties, to guide models toward concise reasoning. However, a consistent challenge across most existing RL-based approaches (e.g., RLOO-LP, ALP, HAPO, L1-max, ShorterBetter, LASER-D) is their tendency to incur substantial performance degradation when attempting to shorten reasoning paths. This work identifies a fundamental limitation within widely adopted RL frameworks, particularly GRPO, that explains this trade-off. It also relates to discriminative learning paradigms, which have been explored for LRM training to increase the likelihood of positive samples and decrease negative ones, but have generally not addressed the challenge of reasoning efficiency with flexible, non-binary rewards. DRPO positions itself within this context by addressing the performance degradation issue in RL-based efficient reasoning, offering a mechanism that aims to achieve a more favorable balance between reasoning accuracy and conciseness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient reasoning framework for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) that reduces the length and redundancy of generated reasoning paths while preserving or minimally impacting their performance on complex tasks. This objective is motivated by the recognized problem of "overthinking" in current LRMs, which leads to increased computational costs and inference latency, thereby hindering their practical applicability.

The authors identify a specific limitation in existing RL-based efficient reasoning methods, particularly those built upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework. GRPO, while effective for distinguishing correct from incorrect reasoning, proves ill-suited for composite reward functions that include length penalties alongside correctness. When a length penalty is applied, the reward for a correct but verbose reasoning path can be reduced. Within GRPO's group-relative advantage function, this reduced reward can cause a valid, correct output to be assigned a negative advantage if its reward falls below the group average, which includes both positive and negative examples. Consequently, GRPO's learning signal is corrupted, inadvertently discouraging otherwise valid reasoning and leading to a significant optimization barrier where efficiency gains come at the cost of accuracy.

Therefore, the key motivations and specific objectives are:
1. **Diagnose the Root Cause:** To formally identify and demonstrate the mechanism by which existing RL frameworks, like GRPO, generate misleading learning signals when length penalties are incorporated, leading to performance degradation.
2. **Propose a Novel Framework:** To introduce Decoupled Reward Policy Optimization (DRPO), an RL framework designed to compute learning signals that are robust to composite rewards. DRPO aims to differentiate not only between correct and incorrect reasoning but also between efficient and inefficient correct reasoning, ensuring positive reinforcement for all correct answers while proportionally reducing the signal for longer ones.
3. **Formulate a Principled Objective:** To derive a rigorous mathematical formulation for DRPO within a discriminative RL framework. This involves integrating an optimized positive data distribution, which maximizes length-based rewards under a KL regularization, into a discriminative objective. The goal is to obtain a tractable, closed-form solution for this perturbed distribution, allowing for efficient optimization using only on-policy data and importance weighting without additional data collection overhead.
4. **Empirical Validation:** To experimentally demonstrate DRPO's superiority over existing efficient reasoning baselines across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks and model sizes, quantifying its ability to achieve substantial length reduction with minimal performance loss.

In essence, the work seeks to resolve the fundamental trade-off observed in prior efficient reasoning methods, enabling LRMs to reason both effectively and concisely.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on diagnosing a limitation in existing reinforcement learning frameworks for efficient reasoning and subsequently proposing a novel framework, Decoupled Reward Policy Optimization (DRPO), to overcome it.

#### 3.1. Problem Diagnosis: Limitations of GRPO with Length Penalties

The paper first analyzes the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework, which is widely adopted for training LRMs. GRPO's objective function (Equation 1) aims to maximize expected rewards, incorporating a group-relative advantage function $A(o_i|q)$ (Equation 2). This advantage function normalizes the reward of an output $o_i$ against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within a batch of generated outputs.

The critical insight presented is that when a length penalty (e.g., $r_l(o)$) is integrated into the reward function (e.g., $r(o|q) = r_c(o|q) - r_l(o)$), the total reward for a correct but lengthy output decreases. If this decreased reward falls below the average reward of the entire group (which includes both correct and incorrect answers), GRPO's relative advantage function can assign a negative value to that correct output. This negative advantage, as illustrated in Figure 1 and Appendix A.3 (Table 3), mistakenly signals to the policy that a valid, albeit verbose, correct answer is an undesirable outcome, leading to performance degradation as the model actively discourages effective reasoning pathways. This issue is identified as common to other RL methods that couple reward calculations for correct and incorrect answers.

#### 3.2. Proposed Solution: Decoupled Reward Policy Optimization (DRPO)

To address the identified limitation, DRPO introduces a mechanism that decouples the reward processing for positive (correct) and negative (incorrect) samples. This ensures that rewards for correct rollouts are normalized exclusively within the group of correct rollouts, preventing interference from negative examples and ensuring that length penalties only modulate the *strength* of a positive signal, never rendering it negative.

DRPO builds upon Discriminative Constrained Policy Optimization (DisCO) (Li et al., 2025a), a framework designed to increase the generative likelihood of positive answers and decrease that of negative answers, while maintaining training stability through a KL-divergence constraint ($D_{KL}(\pi_{old}||\pi_\theta) \le \delta$).

The core innovation is the integration of length rewards into DisCO's objective. Instead of directly modifying the reward, DRPO conceptualizes an "optimized positive data distribution" ($P^*_q$) that explicitly maximizes length-based rewards. This distribution is defined as:
$P^*_q = \text{arg max}_{P \in \mathcal{P}} E_{o \sim P} r_l(o) - \lambda D_{KL}(P, \pi^+_{old}(\cdot|q))$,
where $r_l(o) = 1 - \frac{|o|}{C}$ (a linear length reward with $C$ as maximum length) is the length reward for a correct response $o$, and $\lambda > 0$ is a regularization parameter controlling the trade-off. This formulation aims to find a distribution of correct outputs that favors shorter responses while staying close to the current policy's correct output distribution.

The paper derives a closed-form analytical solution for $P^*_q(o)$ (Equation 6), which is:
$P^*_q(o) = \frac{\pi^+_{old}(o|q) \exp(r_l(o)/\lambda)}{E_{o \sim \pi^+_{old}(\cdot|q)} \exp(r_l(o)/\lambda)}$
This solution indicates that each positive data point $o$ is assigned a weight $\omega(o|q) = \frac{\exp(r_l(o)/\lambda)}{E_{o \sim \pi^+_{old}(\cdot|q)} \exp(r_l(o)/\lambda)}$ that is proportional to its length reward, normalized only within the positive samples. Crucially, since $\exp(\cdot)$ is always positive, the learning signal for correct samples remains positive, regardless of length.

By substituting this $P^*_q$ into the DisCO objective (Equation 4), the final DRPO objective function (Equation 7) is obtained:
$\max E_q \left[ E_{o \sim \pi^+_{old}(\cdot|q)} \omega(o|q) s_\theta(o,q) - \tau \log \left( E_{o' \sim \pi^-_{old}(\cdot|q)} \exp\left(\frac{s_\theta(o',q)}{\tau}\right) \right) \right]$
subject to $D_{KL}(\pi_{old}||\pi_\theta) \le \delta$.
Here, $s_\theta(o,q)$ is a scoring function measuring the generative likelihood of $o$ given $q$, and $\tau$ is a temperature parameter. The KL constraint ensures training stability.

The optimization problem is solved using empirical averages for expectations and a penalty function for the KL constraint, allowing efficient computation using only on-policy data via importance weighting (Algorithm 1 in Appendix). DisCO is shown to be a special case of DRPO when $\lambda = +\infty$, meaning no length reward is applied.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Training was conducted on the DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset (40.3k math Q&A pairs). Evaluation used GSM8K (easy), MATH-500 (medium), Olympiad-Bench (hard), and AIME (very hard) for mathematical reasoning. An additional experiment on the K&K logic puzzle dataset (non-mathematical) is included in Appendix A.4.
- **Models:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, -7B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B were used as base models for RL fine-tuning.
- **Baselines:** Six state-of-the-art efficient reasoning methods were compared: RLOO-LP, ALP, HAPO, L1-max, ShorterBetter (SB), and LASER-D.
- **Training Details:** AdamW optimizer, specific learning rates (2e-6 for 1.5B, 1e-6 for 7B, 5e-7 for 8B), batch size 128, mini-batch size 32, 8 responses sampled per question. Generation budget was 8k tokens. Hyperparameters for baselines (e.g., $\alpha$, $\beta$, $w$) and DRPO ($\lambda$) were tuned.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Pass@1 (average over 16 generated answers), average number of tokens (length), and Accuracy Efficiency Score (AES), which quantifies the trade-off between accuracy and computational cost.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate DRPO's efficacy in reducing reasoning length while maintaining or minimally affecting performance across various benchmarks and model sizes.

#### 4.1. Visualization of Learning Process (Figure 2)

During training, DRPO models with smaller $\lambda$ values (indicating stronger length regularization) showed comparable or marginally worse Pass@1 scores compared to DisCO (DRPO with $\lambda = +\infty$, no length reward). Simultaneously, these smaller $\lambda$ values led to substantial reductions in response length, with $\lambda = 0.1$ achieving over 50% length reduction relative to DisCO, across both 1.5B and 7B models. This suggests that DRPO effectively guides the model toward conciseness without significantly impairing its ability to find correct solutions during the training phase.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines: Performance-Efficiency Trade-off (Figure 3 and Table 1)

DRPO consistently exhibited a superior performance-efficiency trade-off compared to all six baselines across 1.5B, 7B, and 8B model sizes.
- **Quantitative Example (7B Model):** DRPO reduced reasoning length from 3053 tokens (reference learning method) to 1502 tokens (a 51% reduction) with a performance loss of only 2.6%. In contrast, RLOO-LP, for instance, achieved a 38% length reduction (from 2975 to 1841 tokens) but incurred a 7.1% performance loss on the 7B model. ALP showed a similar trend of higher performance degradation for less length reduction.
- **Accuracy Efficiency Score (AES):** AES integrates performance and length into a single metric, emphasizing minimal performance degradation. DRPO consistently achieved positive AES scores for 1.5B, 7B, and 8B models (e.g., 0.178 for 1.5B, 0.249 for 7B, 0.297 for 8B). Conversely, almost all baseline methods exhibited negative AES scores, indicating that their length reductions came with a disproportionately larger drop in accuracy. This highlights DRPO's capability to improve efficiency without substantially sacrificing reasoning ability.

#### 4.3. Length Reduction for Different Problem Difficulty (Figure 4)

The study analyzed the impact of length reduction on problems of varying difficulty:
- **Easy Questions (GSM8K):** DRPO demonstrated significant length reduction with minimal performance impact. For the 1.5B model, it achieved a 77.2% length reduction (from 1563 to 356 tokens) with only a 1.1% performance loss. For the 7B model, a 73.1% length reduction (from 969 to 261 tokens) resulted in a 0.6% performance loss. Baselines, in comparison, showed steeper performance declines for similar or lesser length reductions on these easy problems.
- **Increasing Difficulty:** As problem difficulty increased from GSM8K to AIME, all models exhibited longer reasoning lengths and greater performance drops, which is consistent with the inherent complexity of such tasks. However, DRPO consistently maintained a better trade-off between performance and length across all difficulty levels.
- **Model Size Influence:** Larger models (7B and 8B) generally required shorter reasoning paths than the 1.5B model and were more resilient to length reduction, indicated by flatter slopes in the trade-off plots.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Length Reward Design (Appendix A.5, Figure 6)

An ablation study comparing linear, concave, and cosine length reward functions revealed that the linear reward provided the broadest trade-off spectrum, allowing for more aggressive length reduction. Concave and cosine rewards tended to prioritize higher accuracy but resulted in longer reasoning paths.

#### 4.5. Case Studies (Appendix A.7, Figures 7, 8, 9)

Case studies comparing DRPO-trained models with DisCO (without length regularization) showed that DRPO effectively reduced redundant reasoning. For an easy prompt, DRPO produced a correct answer in 89 tokens (6x reduction) with clear reasoning, while DisCO required 526 tokens, including multiple reflections and repeated answers. For a challenging prompt, DRPO used 455 tokens (10x reduction) and produced two correct answers with five reflection words, compared to DisCO's 4497 tokens, 78 reflection words, and six incorrect answers. This suggests DRPO preserves reflection capabilities while eliminating unnecessary iterative reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research identifies a notable limitation in existing reinforcement learning frameworks, specifically GRPO, when applied to multi-objective optimization problems like simultaneous correctness and reasoning efficiency in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). The finding that group-relative advantage functions can produce misleading negative learning signals for correct but verbose reasoning paths is a significant diagnostic contribution to the field of RL for LLMs.

The proposed Decoupled Reward Policy Optimization (DRPO) framework offers a principled solution to this issue. By decoupling the reward signals for correct and incorrect answers and normalizing length-based rewards solely within the positive group, DRPO ensures that length penalties modulate the strength of a positive signal without ever converting it into a negative one. This approach fundamentally rethinks how learning signals are constructed for composite rewards, leading to a more robust and effective training paradigm.

The empirical results demonstrate DRPO's capability to achieve a superior trade-off between reasoning performance and efficiency across various model sizes and mathematical reasoning tasks. The ability to attain substantial length reductions (e.g., 77% on easy questions like GSM8K) with minimal performance loss (e.g., 1.1%) addresses a critical bottleneck in LRM deployment. This efficiency gain translates directly into reduced computational costs and lower inference latency, which are crucial for scaling LRMs to real-world applications.

The formulation of DRPO is noted for its generality, allowing for the incorporation of other preference rewards on positive data beyond just length, such as process-based or user-defined preferences. This flexibility suggests broad applicability of the framework to various LRM fine-tuning objectives. Furthermore, the effectiveness across different difficulty levels and its generalizability to non-mathematical reasoning tasks (as shown in the appendix) imply a robust and versatile method.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Practicality of LRMs:** More efficient reasoning enables LRMs to be deployed in latency-sensitive applications and environments with computational constraints, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and cost-effective.
- **Improved RL Algorithms for LLMs:** The diagnosis of GRPO's limitations for composite rewards can inform the development of more robust and nuanced RL algorithms for LLM alignment and optimization.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper suggests avenues for future work, such as incorporating adaptive mechanisms for the regularization parameter $\lambda$ based on problem difficulty, which could lead to even more fine-grained control over the efficiency-accuracy trade-off. Extending DRPO to other types of positive preference rewards beyond length also presents a promising direction for advancing LRM capabilities.

In summary, DRPO represents a meaningful advancement in training efficient reasoning models, offering a method to mitigate the "overthinking" problem without significantly compromising reasoning accuracy, thereby enhancing the utility and scalability of LRMs.
