# MatchingPolicy: Correspondence-Aware Policy Enables Cross-Object In-Context Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> MatchingPolicy decouples correspondence extraction from action prediction for in-context robotic imitation: vision foundation models compute dense semantic correspondences between demonstrations and a novel scene, and a graph-based diffusion policy conditions actions on these correspondence-indexed keypoints. Outperforms InstantPolicy, KAT, and RDT-1B on RLBench and real-world tasks with only two demonstrations, no fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Prior in-context imitation methods force one model to jointly infer demonstration-scene correspondences and adapt motions, hurting both tasks in visually diverse or geometrically complex scenes.
- Existing in-context policies fail to generalize to novel objects and tasks even when demonstrations show the exact unseen task and object.
- Keypoint-based policies rely on static, pre-defined keypoints chosen manually or via clustering, limiting adaptability to novel scenarios and requiring human effort.

## Method

- **Correspondence-Aware Diffusion Policy**: a graph neural network routes ==cross-frame correspondence edges==, ==intra-frame spatial edges==, and ==action-frame consistency edges== to decode gripper-keypoint displacements via ==denoising diffusion==.
- **MatchingDataset** and **MatchingEngine**: a synthetic dataset of 15K trajectory sets, 150K+ trajectories, and 50K+ ShapeNet object instances with ground-truth 3D correspondences via ==canonical-anchor spatial retargeting==.
- Two-stage online matching: ==adaptive multi-view semantic alignment== plus ==dense VFM feature matching== for inter-scene correspondence, combined with ==point-tracker-based intra-scene tracking== and ==geometric 3D lifting==.

## Results

- On 36 RLBench tasks MatchingPolicy averages **0.92/0.99**, **0.83/0.86**, and **0.72/0.73** (NoFT/FT) across Adaptation, OOD-Easy, and OOD-Hard groups, versus InstantPolicy's near-zero OOD-Hard performance.
- With only two demonstrations MatchingPolicy reaches **0.81** average success on six hard tasks, beating KAT with ten demos (**0.34**) and fine-tuned RDT-1B with twenty demos (**0.62**).
- Real-world four-task evaluation shows **77.5%/65.0%/72.5%** average success across Layout-Easy/Layout-Hard/Shape settings versus InstantPolicy's **22.5%/0.0%/10.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating correspondence discovery from action adaptation into distinct modules resolves a dual-burden conflict that limits single-model in-context policies.
> - Conditioning policies on correspondence features rather than raw object representations yields object-agnostic behavior that transfers across instances and semantic categories.
> - Synthetic ground-truth correspondence supervision from canonical mesh anchors can substitute for learned feature matchers during training, deferring VFM reliance to test time only.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of robotic imitation learning, specifically addressing the challenges of generalization in few-shot, in-context learning settings. Traditional imitation learning methods typically necessitate extensive, task-specific demonstration data, limiting their adaptability to new tasks without substantial fine-tuning. While advancements in universal, multi-task imitation policies, trained on large and diverse datasets, have emerged, they continue to encounter difficulties in generalizing to entirely novel tasks and often require task-specific fine-tuning for satisfactory performance.

Inspired by progress in language and vision domains, in-context learning (ICL) has been introduced to robotic policy learning. This paradigm aims to enable models to adapt to unseen tasks by conditioning them on a few task demonstrations at test time, bypassing the need for explicit fine-tuning. However, prior ICL approaches in robotics have shown limitations in generalizing to novel objects and tasks, even when demonstrations explicitly feature the target unseen task and object. The authors attribute this shortcoming to the expectation that a single model simultaneously infer precise correspondences between the demonstration and the current scene and adapt the demonstrated motions to new objects and scenes. This dual requirement is argued to hinder both correspondence identification and action adaptation, particularly in visually complex or diverse environments.

The paper differentiates itself from existing approaches in several ways. While some ICL methods rely on explicit correspondence establishment followed by heuristic-based trajectory transformations, these often require considerable human effort and have constrained adaptability. Other recent efforts involve training direct in-context policy models using transformers or graph neural networks to reduce human intervention. MatchingPolicy, in contrast, adopts a direct and modular strategy by explicitly extracting dense point-wise correspondence information using Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), thereby enabling the policy to concentrate solely on adapting robot actions. This modular design also contrasts with retrieval-augmented generation methods that decouple relationship extraction but may not provide fine-grained spatial cues. Furthermore, the work distinguishes itself from existing keypoint-based policy learning methods, which often focus on single-task imitation and rely on static, pre-defined keypoints. MatchingPolicy extends to in-context multi-task learning by dynamically generating keypoints based on matching confidence and visibility.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust robotic policy capable of few-shot generalization to unseen objects and novel manipulation scenarios within an in-context imitation learning framework.

The motivation for this work stems from an identified limitation in current in-context learning (ICL) approaches for robotics. The authors observe that existing methods struggle to generalize effectively to novel objects and tasks, even when provided with demonstrations that directly illustrate the target task and object. This deficiency is posited to arise from an inherent conflict within a single model architecture, which is simultaneously tasked with:
1. **Inferring precise correspondences:** Identifying accurate mappings between the given demonstration and the current novel scene. This often needs to be done from scratch, using potentially limited robotic-domain data.
2. **Adapting demonstrated motions:** Translating the observed actions from the demonstration to the new objects and scene context.

The authors argue that this dual burden compromises both the accuracy of correspondence identification and the efficacy of action adaptation, especially in environments characterized by visual diversity or geometric complexity.

To address this challenge, the research proposes the following key objectives:
- **Decoupling Correspondence Extraction and Policy Learning:** Introduce a novel framework, MatchingPolicy, that explicitly separates the process of identifying semantic correspondences between demonstrations and the current scene from the process of learning and executing robotic policies. This architectural separation is designed to resolve the aforementioned inherent conflict.
- **Developing a Correspondence-Aware Policy:** Design a diffusion policy that conditions robotic actions directly on dense semantic correspondences. This explicit conditioning aims to improve the policy's ability to precisely localize task-relevant interaction regions and generate accurate, adaptive actions.
- **Integrating Vision Foundation Models (VFMs):** Leverage powerful, off-the-shelf VFMs in conjunction with a novel two-stage matching algorithm to dynamically establish reliable and dense correspondences in real-world settings. This approach aims to minimize reliance on manually defined correspondences or domain-specific data for matching.

The motivations underlying this decoupled design and correspondence-aware policy include:
- **Enhanced Generalization:** By operating on correspondence features rather than raw visual object representations, the policy is expected to become inherently object-agnostic, thereby substantially improving its generalization capabilities across different object instances and semantic categories.
- **Increased Precision and Adaptability:** Explicit correspondences provide fine-grained spatial cues that guide the policy more effectively, leading to more accurate and adaptable action generation in novel scenarios.
- **Improved Flexibility and Scalability:** The modular nature of the framework allows for the seamless integration of advancements in visual and semantic matching methods (e.g., VFMs) without requiring retraining of the policy's action prediction module. This design aims to reduce manual intervention and enhance the system's overall robustness and scalability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MatchingPolicy framework addresses robotic manipulation in a few-shot, in-context learning setting, aiming to perform tasks in novel scenes based on a small set of demonstrations (N=1 or 2). The policy predicts a horizon of K future actions, consisting of relative gripper transformations and gripper states, conditioned on the current scene state (point cloud and gripper state) and the demonstration set.

The core of the methodology lies in explicitly decoupling correspondence extraction from action prediction. The overall pipeline operates in two main phases:
1. **Correspondence Establishment:** Semantic correspondences are explicitly established between the current observation and demonstration frames.
2. **Action Prediction:** A sparse set of keypoints, identified based on these correspondences, serves as a structured context for a correspondence-aware policy to predict end-effector motions. These motions are then converted into robot actions via inverse kinematics.

**Training Phase:** During training, the system utilizes a synthetic dataset, MATCHINGDATASET, which provides ground-truth correspondences to supervise the correspondence-aware policy.

**Deployment Phase:** In real-world deployment, where ground-truth correspondences are unavailable, the system integrates Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) with visual point trackers to extract semantic and spatio-temporal correspondences from real-world observations. The same action prediction module of the policy is used without modification.

#### 3.1. Correspondence-Aware Diffusion Policy

The policy architecture is designed around explicitly leveraged correspondence information:

- **Keypoint Selection with Correspondence:** To achieve robustness and reduce computational load, keypoints are selected from the current scene based on both geometric coverage and the reliability of their correspondence across demonstrations. Only points with semantically matched counterparts in a majority of demonstration frames are considered, filtering out unstable matches. Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is then applied to these candidates. A fixed set of gripper keypoints, with known one-to-one correspondences, is also incorporated. These selected keypoints form "correspondence-indexed entities," where each current-frame keypoint is explicitly linked to its matched counterparts in the demonstrations.

- **Correspondence-Conditioned Action Prediction:** The policy organizes its input as a graph structure (Fig. 2), with nodes grouped into demonstration frames, the current frame, and K future action frames. Each frame contains a fixed number of gripper (M_g=6) and scene (M_s=16) nodes. Nodes sharing the same index across frames are treated as corresponding entities. Gripper nodes store 3D positions and gripper states, while scene nodes store 3D keypoint positions. For action frames, gripper nodes store predicted displacement relative to the current frame, and scene nodes serve as a fixed scene reference.
 - **Graph Structure:** Information flow is defined by three types of edges:
 - **Cross-frame correspondence edges:** Connect current-frame nodes to their corresponding nodes in demonstration frames, and also corresponding nodes between adjacent demonstration frames. These edges facilitate the direct transfer of demonstrated intent.
 - **Intra-frame spatial edges:** Fully connect gripper and scene nodes within each frame, modeling spatial configurations and propagating correspondence information. Relative Cartesian displacement serves as the edge attribute.
 - **Action-frame consistency edges:** Link gripper nodes in each action frame to their counterparts in the current and preceding action frames, ensuring temporal consistency and anchoring future predictions.
 - After predicting gripper-keypoint displacements, the relative gripper pose transformation (ΔT) for each action frame is recovered using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).

- **Local Geometric Refinement:** To address sparsity and potential noise in selected keypoints, local geometric evidence is incorporated. Unselected points in the scene's point cloud are assigned to their nearest selected keypoint, forming local groups. These groups are processed by a pre-trained PointNet encoder, which summarizes fine-grained local shape information. This geometric data is propagated to gripper nodes via intra-frame edges, refining action predictions.

- **Diffusion-based Action Decoding:** The policy is trained using a diffusion-based objective. Ground-truth gripper-keypoint positions and gripper states are corrupted with Gaussian noise. The policy's task is to predict the denoising target, comprising translation, rotation, and gripper-state residuals, optimized with a standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective.

#### 3.2. MATCHINGDATASET: Correspondence-Rich In-context Learning Data

A large-scale synthetic dataset was created to provide the necessary intent-consistent trajectories and ground-truth 3D correspondences for training.
- **Composition:** The dataset includes 15,000 task-consistent sets, over 150,000 trajectories, and more than 50,000 manipulated object instances (using ShapeNet meshes). It covers a variety of manipulation behaviors.
- **Generation Process (Fig. 3, 6):**
 - Each trajectory set originates from a procedurally constructed "reference demonstration."
 - **Spatial Retargeting:** Additional trajectory realizations are generated by varying object poses and scene layouts while preserving the robot-object relative poses at key frames. This ensures diverse observations and robot motions while maintaining consistent task intent.
 - **Observations:** RGB-D point clouds are captured from virtual cameras.
 - **Ground-Truth Correspondence Annotation:** For each object mesh, 2048 canonical surface anchors are sampled. These anchors define correspondence identities across all frames and trajectories within a set. Using ground-truth object poses, these anchors are transformed into each scene and time step. Observed object points are associated with their nearest transformed anchor. An anchor is marked "visible" if its associated observed point is within a specified distance tolerance, accounting for partial observations due to occlusion or being out-of-view. This process provides explicit ground-truth 3D correspondence supervision for training.

#### 3.3. Real-world Correspondence Integration via VLMs

For real-world deployment, the framework incorporates off-the-shelf vision foundation models:
- **Process:** Object-centric candidate points are sampled from initial observations. These points are matched across scenes using VFM features. The matched points are then propagated over time using a point tracker. Finally, valid 2D matches and tracks are lifted to a shared 3D world frame.
- **Inter-scene Correspondence via Semantic Matching:** Dense feature matching is performed between the initial frames of demonstrations and the current scene. This involves segmenting target objects, extracting L2-normalized dense descriptors, and finding 2D correspondences via nearest-neighbor search in descriptor space.
 - **Adaptive View Alignment (Fig. 4b):** To enhance robustness against viewpoint changes, the system uses three calibrated RGB-D cameras. It compares pooled view-level descriptors (average features of segmented objects) to select the most compatible camera view assignment, then performs dense matching on the best two aligned view pairs.
- **Intra-scene Correspondence via Visual Tracking:** An off-the-shelf point tracker is used to propagate 512 object-centric points per object per camera view over time, generating temporally consistent 2D image-space trajectories.
- **Geometry-based 3D Lifting and Point Filtering:** A validity mask ensures that only points with available depth and confirmed visibility are processed. Valid 2D points are back-projected into the robot-aligned world frame using depth and camera calibration. These lifted 3D points are then transformed into the corresponding gripper frame for policy input.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The model was trained using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁵. Training required seven days on four NVIDIA A6000 GPUs for 1 million steps. Evaluation was conducted on a PC equipped with an RTX 4080 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MatchingPolicy was conducted in both simulation (RLBench) and real-world manipulation tasks, focusing on few-shot performance and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (RLBench)

MatchingPolicy was evaluated against InstantPolicy, KAT, and RDT-1B on 36 RLBench tasks, categorized into Adaptation, OOD-Easy, and OOD-Hard tasks.

- **Comparison with InstantPolicy (Table 1):**
 - MatchingPolicy achieved higher average success rates across all three task groups compared to InstantPolicy.
 - On Adaptation Tasks, MatchingPolicy (0.92 average without fine-tuning, 0.99 with fine-tuning) outperformed InstantPolicy (0.79 without fine-tuning, 0.97 with fine-tuning).
 - On OOD-Easy Tasks, MatchingPolicy (0.83 without fine-tuning, 0.86 with fine-tuning) also outperformed InstantPolicy (0.60 without fine-tuning, 0.82 with fine-tuning).
 - The most significant difference was observed on OOD-Hard Tasks, where MatchingPolicy achieved an average success rate of 0.72 (without fine-tuning) and 0.73 (with fine-tuning), while InstantPolicy achieved near-zero performance (0.00) even with fine-tuning.
 - MatchingPolicy demonstrated the ability to generalize without task-specific fine-tuning, outperforming a fine-tuned InstantPolicy on several unseen tasks like "Open Jar" and "Lamp On." This suggests that InstantPolicy's reliance on object geometry makes it less effective when interaction patterns change, whereas MatchingPolicy's conditioning on explicit correspondences allows for better transfer of interaction patterns irrespective of exact object geometry.

- **Comparison with KAT and RDT-1B (Table 2):**
 - On six challenging RLBench tasks, MatchingPolicy, using only two demonstrations, consistently outperformed KAT (an LLM-based ICL method), even when KAT was provided with ten demonstrations. MatchingPolicy achieved an average success rate of 0.81, compared to KAT's 0.17 (2 demos) and 0.34 (10 demos).
 - MatchingPolicy also achieved higher success rates than RDT-1B (a VLA model), even after RDT-1B was fine-tuned on 20 demonstrations. RDT-1B's average success rate was 0.62 with 20 demonstrations, while MatchingPolicy achieved 0.81 with only two. These results highlight the efficiency and effectiveness of explicit correspondence modeling compared to LLM-based ICL and VLA fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

MatchingPolicy was evaluated on four real-world manipulation tasks (Pour Water, Put Lid, Open Box, Cut Egg) using an AirBot Pro 6-DoF robotic arm and three RGB-D cameras. The policy was deployed without real-world fine-tuning. Three levels of generalization were tested: Layout-Easy, Layout-Hard, and Shape.

- **Overall Performance (Table 3):**
 - MatchingPolicy consistently and substantially outperformed InstantPolicy across all three generalization settings.
 - **Layout-Easy:** MatchingPolicy 77.5% average success rate vs. InstantPolicy 22.5%.
 - **Layout-Hard:** MatchingPolicy 65.0% vs. InstantPolicy 0.0%.
 - **Shape:** MatchingPolicy 72.5% vs. InstantPolicy 10.0%.
 - The results demonstrated MatchingPolicy's robust generalization to both layout and object variations in real-world scenarios.

- **Generalization to Novel Layout and Objects (Fig. 5):**
 - InstantPolicy's performance dropped significantly (e.g., 0% on "Put Lid" in Layout-Hard) due to misidentifying interaction objects. MatchingPolicy, leveraging VFMs for precise semantic correspondences, could correctly identify and interact with the target object.
 - MatchingPolicy exhibited the ability to generate successful trajectories that approached objects from directions substantially different from the demonstrations (e.g., "Open Box"), indicating that it follows the motion of matched keypoints rather than merely replaying trajectories.
 - In the "Shape" generalization setting, MatchingPolicy maintained a high success rate, successfully completing tasks like "Pour Water" even with geometrically different teapots.
 - The policy also demonstrated cross-category generalization, such as placing a lid on a teapot after being shown a demonstration of placing a lid on a pot (Fig. 9), confirming its reliance on learned correspondences and object-agnostic nature.
 - MatchingPolicy also demonstrated interaction following, replicating distinct grasping styles shown in demonstrations for the "Pour Water" task (Fig. 10).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Two-Stage Matching vs. Naive 2D-to-3D Matching (Table 4):**
 - The two-stage matching approach of MatchingPolicy yielded superior performance across all generalization settings compared to a 3D naive matching baseline. For instance, in Layout-Easy, it achieved 77.5% versus 60.0% for the naive baseline.
 - The naive baseline's performance degraded particularly in "Shape" and "Layout-Hard" settings, indicating that less accurate matching leads to task failure.

- **Test-time Planning Methods (Table 4):**
 - The "overlapping chunks" planning strategy significantly improved policy performance and trajectory smoothness compared to "whole chunk" (executing all K actions) and "half chunk" (executing K/2 actions and re-predicting) strategies. The "overlap" strategy achieved the highest success rates (77.5% Layout-Easy, 65.0% Layout-Hard, 72.5% Shape).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MatchingPolicy framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic imitation learning by addressing a fundamental limitation in the generalization capabilities of in-context learning (ICL) methods. By explicitly decoupling the process of correspondence extraction from policy learning, the research resolves an identified conflict that previously hindered robust transferability to novel scenarios.

The key significance and potential impacts include:

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The work demonstrates robust few-shot generalization across unseen object instances, novel scene layouts, and even semantic categories in both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks. This represents a step towards robots that can adapt quickly to environmental changes with minimal retraining.
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** MatchingPolicy achieves high success rates with a minimal number of demonstrations (e.g., two demonstrations outperforming baselines that use ten or twenty), which is crucial for reducing the costly and time-consuming process of data collection in robotics.
- **Modular and Scalable Design:** The decoupled architecture allows for the integration of powerful, off-the-shelf vision foundation models (VFMs) for correspondence extraction without requiring modifications or retraining of the core policy. This modularity enhances the flexibility of the system and its potential to scale with advancements in visual perception.
- **Object-Agnostic Policy Learning:** By conditioning robotic actions directly on dense semantic correspondences rather than raw visual features or object geometry, the policy becomes inherently more object-agnostic. This characteristic is critical for deploying robots in diverse, unstructured environments where specific object models may not be available.
- **Leveraging Synthetic Data for Training:** The development of MATCHINGDATASET, a large-scale synthetic dataset rich in ground-truth 3D correspondences, provides a scalable and efficient means to train complex correspondence-aware policies. This approach mitigates the reliance on extensive and expensive real-world data collection for initial policy learning.
- **Advancement in ICL Paradigms:** The research reinforces the principle that explicit modeling of visual and semantic relationships between demonstrations and target scenes is a key factor for effective in-context imitation learning. This extends the understanding and applicability of ICL beyond its origins in language and vision to complex physical interactions.

The potential impact of MatchingPolicy extends to developing more adaptable and autonomous robotic systems that can learn new skills more efficiently and reliably from human demonstrations. This could lead to broader deployment of robots in various applications, from flexible manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics, where rapid adaptation to new tasks and environments is required.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge several limitations. The current method primarily focuses on short-horizon manipulation tasks, indicating a direction for future work to extend it to long-horizon tasks. The operating speed is relatively slow, which might limit its applicability to highly dynamic tasks. Furthermore, the view selection process used in the real-world setup is somewhat platform-dependent, implying a need for adaptation when deploying on different robotic platforms.
