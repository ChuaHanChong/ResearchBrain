# Reasoning or Pattern Matching? Probing Large Vision-Language Models with Visual Puzzles

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper from the National Technical University of Athens and Instituto de Telecomunicações recasts visual puzzles as diagnostic instruments to probe whether Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) exhibit genuine reasoning or sophisticated pattern-matching, revealing that current LVLMs often display an "illusion of reasoning" where fluent explanations mask fundamental cognitive limitations across various reasoning types.

## Problem

- A critical question exists whether LVLMs possess genuine reasoning capabilities or primarily rely on complex pattern-matching, despite impressive performance on multimodal tasks.
- Traditional multimodal benchmarks often entangle reasoning with confounding factors, making it difficult to diagnose specific failure modes or ascertain true visual reasoning.
- There is a need for robust diagnostic tools to evaluate LVLMs' cognitive foundations beyond perception and high-level recognition.

## Method

- The authors formally define ==visual puzzles== as diagnostic instruments, characterized by essential reliance on visual information, explicitly constrained structure, and ==reasoning-centric challenges==.
- A comprehensive ==taxonomy== organizes existing ==visual puzzle benchmarks== into five distinct reasoning dimensions: ==inductive==, ==analogical==, ==algorithmic==, ==deductive==, and ==geometric/spatial reasoning==.
- The paper provides an ==empirical synthesis== of findings across these categories to identify consistent limitations and vulnerabilities in current LVLMs.

## Results

- LVLMs demonstrate brittle generalization, struggling to induce invariant rules or abstract structural patterns and degrading sharply under minor variations or out-of-distribution shifts.
- Models show a perception-reasoning entanglement, where limitations in fine-grained visual perception bottleneck downstream reasoning, and they often fail in multi-step execution and maintaining internal state for algorithmic tasks.
- LVLMs struggle with constraint integration in deductive tasks and often rely on superficial spatial cues rather than abstract geometric relations, leading to error accumulation in geometric/spatial reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current LVLMs exhibit an "illusion of reasoning," where fluent and rational verbal explanations often do not correspond to faithful execution or comprehension of visual evidence.
> - Visual puzzles serve as uniquely sharp diagnostic tools by providing isolated environments and controlled conditions that decouple domain knowledge from pure logic.
> - Future research should focus on multi-agent collaboration, long-horizon training objectives, compositional generalization, and abductive/lateral visual reasoning to foster more reasoning-aware LVLMs.
