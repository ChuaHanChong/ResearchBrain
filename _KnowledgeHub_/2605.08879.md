# Preserving Foundational Capabilities in Flow-Matching VLAs through Conservative SFT

> [!summary] Summary
> The Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory introduces Conservative Supervised Fine-Tuning (ConSFT) for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, a method to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during downstream fine-tuning. ConSFT dynamically weights learning signals based on model confidence, achieving superior retention of foundational capabilities (e.g., 34% average on LIBERO benchmark) while matching target task performance, all without requiring prior data or architectural modifications.

## Problem

- Catastrophic forgetting in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models during downstream adaptation via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), leading to degradation of foundational capabilities.
- Computational challenges of directly applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) trust-region constraints to continuous-time flow-matching VLAs due to the need for evaluating action likelihoods and integrating continuous trajectories.
- Limitations of existing anti-forgetting methods (Experience Replay, Policy Regularization, PEFT) such as high data requirements, increased computational overhead, or reduced model plasticity.

## Method

- Proposed **Conservative Supervised Fine-Tuning (ConSFT)**, an SFT objective that ==dynamically modulates learning signals== based on per-sample model confidence.
- Down-weighted gradients from poorly modeled, low-confidence transitions using an ==exponential conservative importance weight== ω_ConSFT(θ) = exp(−L_SFT(θ) / τ) applied to the standard SFT loss.
- Employed a ==stop-gradient operator== on the weight and an ==exponential annealing schedule== for the temperature parameter τ to balance capability retention and target task convergence without prior data or architectural changes.

## Results

- **ConSFT** attenuated catastrophic forgetting, preserving foundational capabilities (e.g., **34%** average retention on LIBERO, **28%** on RoboTwin) while matching target-task performance, outperforming established baselines (KL-Regularization, LoRA, Experience Replay) without external data or architectural modifications.
- Mechanistic analysis confirmed ConSFT induced a controlled, uniformly decaying sparsity path in parameter updates, mitigating disruptive weight deviations during early adaptation and preventing the rapid global overwrites seen in vanilla SFT.
- In physical robotic deployments, ConSFT prevented spatial drift and preserved foundational spatial capabilities (e.g., semantic grasping under visual clutter) while achieving proficiency in high-precision, long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unconstrained Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) leads to dense parameter overwrites and catastrophic forgetting in flow-matching VLAs, while bounded optimization (like RL trust regions) induces structurally sparse updates that preserve prior capabilities.
> - The proposed Conservative Supervised Fine-Tuning (ConSFT) objective analytically bounds parameter disruption risk, ensuring that for unfamiliar, high-loss transitions, the forgetting risk attenuates exponentially towards zero.
> - Dynamically weighting learning signals based on model confidence can emulate the benefits of RL's bounded optimization dynamics in SFT, facilitating progressive learning without the computational overhead of explicit RL or additional data/architecture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large-scale pre-training is a foundational step for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, endowing them with general capabilities relevant to robotic manipulation and control. However, the subsequent process of adapting these models to specific downstream tasks in novel environments often involves fine-tuning, which can lead to a phenomenon known as catastrophic forgetting. This issue manifests as the degradation of previously acquired, pre-trained capabilities when the model's parameters are extensively updated to fit a new, often narrow, target distribution. Catastrophic forgetting is identified as a significant bottleneck in the post-training phase of VLA models.

Recent advancements in VLA models have increasingly adopted flow-matching architectures for generating continuous actions. Despite their state-of-the-art performance, mitigating catastrophic forgetting within these continuous-time frameworks remains an open challenge. Previous research in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suggests that Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, particularly those employing trust-region constraints, are more resistant to forgetting. This resilience is attributed to the induction of structurally sparse parameter updates, which restrict the divergence between updated and reference policies.

However, directly applying explicit RL boundary constraints to flow-matching VLA models introduces substantial computational overhead. This is due to the requirement for evaluating action likelihoods by solving probability-flow Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) and integrating full continuous trajectories across parallel active and reference networks simultaneously, making such approaches unscalable for large-capacity VLAs. Existing alternative methods for mitigating catastrophic forgetting include data replay (e.g., Experience Replay), policy regularization, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA). Each of these approaches has limitations: data replay requires access to often proprietary pre-training corpora, regularization methods necessitate parallel reference networks that double memory consumption and computational overhead, and parameter-efficient methods like LoRA can restrict the model's plasticity for acquiring new downstream skills.

This research aims to address the identified gap by developing an optimization objective that mitigates catastrophic forgetting in flow-matching VLAs. The proposed method seeks to emulate the bounded optimization dynamics observed in RL trust regions, but without incurring the computational penalties, data dependencies, or architectural constraints associated with existing anti-forgetting strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an optimization objective that effectively preserves pre-trained foundational capabilities in flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models during downstream adaptation, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting. This objective is pursued while adhering to specific constraints regarding computational resources and data requirements.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in the deployment and continuous adaptation of modern VLA models:
1. **Catastrophic Forgetting in VLA Models**: Large-scale pre-training establishes broad foundational capabilities in VLA models. However, when these models are fine-tuned for specific tasks in new environments using vanilla Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), the unconstrained parameter updates often lead to dense overwrites. This process degrades the agent's general robustness and erodes pre-trained foundational spatial and action priors, constituting a primary bottleneck for effective VLA post-training.
2. **Challenges with Flow-Matching Architectures**: State-of-the-art VLA models increasingly employ continuous-time flow-matching architectures. Mitigating catastrophic forgetting within these specific models presents an open optimization problem. Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques that prevent forgetting through bounded policy updates (e.g., trust-region constraints) are computationally prohibitive for flow-matching models. This is because enforcing exact Kullback-Leibler (KL) limits necessitates evaluating action likelihoods by solving complex probability-flow Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) and integrating continuous trajectories across both active and reference networks simultaneously, which is unscalable for large-capacity models.
3. **Limitations of Existing Anti-Forgetting Methods**: Current approaches to combat catastrophic forgetting, such as Experience Replay (ER), regularization methods like Learning without Forgetting (LwF), and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., Low-Rank Adaptation - LoRA), each possess limitations. ER requires access to original pre-training data, which is often proprietary. Regularization methods that constrain policy shifts by penalizing divergence from a reference model demand a parallel network, doubling memory and computational overhead. LoRA, while preserving some capabilities by restricting updates to low-rank subspaces, can simultaneously limit the model's plasticity for acquiring novel downstream skills.
4. **Empirical Evidence of Trust Regions' Efficacy**: Prior research has indicated that RL's trust-region mechanisms induce structurally sparse parameter updates, which correlate with better retention of foundational capabilities compared to the dense parameter overwrites typical of vanilla SFT. The motivation is to capture these beneficial optimization dynamics without the associated computational complexity.

Therefore, the key motivation is to develop an efficient, data-independent, and architecturally light solution—Conservative Supervised Fine-Tuning (ConSFT)—that can replicate the capability-preserving effects of bounded optimization in flow-matching VLAs, thereby enabling more robust and continuously adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology introduces Conservative Supervised Fine-Tuning (ConSFT), an optimization objective designed to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in flow-matching VLA models. The approach is inspired by the structural sparsity observed in RL trust-region methods but engineered to operate within a standard supervised regression paradigm, thereby avoiding the computational overhead associated with online policy evaluation or parallel network architectures.

**Core Mechanism: Conservative Weighting via Oracle Policy Substitution**
ConSFT dynamically modulates the regression objective based on per-sample model confidence. The foundational concept draws from trust-region methods, which bound parameter updates using an importance sampling ratio, $r(\theta) = \frac{\pi_{\theta}(a|s)}{\pi_{behavior}(a|s)}$. For continuous-time generative models like flow-matching VLAs, exact action likelihoods are intractable. The paper leverages an approximation from Flow Policy Optimization (FPO), where the flow-matching objective $L_{flow}$ serves as a tractable proxy for the negative log-likelihood.
To adapt this to a supervised context lacking an explicit behavior policy, the research conceptually defines the behavior policy as an "ideal expert distribution" that perfectly generates offline demonstrations, for which the theoretical flow matching loss ($L_{behavior}$) is assumed to be zero. By substituting this and introducing a temperature parameter $\tau$, the Conservative Importance Weight, $\omega_{ConSFT}(\theta)$, is formulated as:
$\omega_{ConSFT}(\theta) = \exp\left(-\frac{L_{SFT}(\theta)}{\tau}\right)$

The final ConSFT objective is then defined as:
$J_{ConSFT}(\theta) = \text{sg}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{L_{SFT}(\theta)}{\tau}\right)\right] \cdot L_{SFT}(\theta)$
where $\text{sg}$ denotes a stop-gradient operator. This ensures the exponential weight acts as a dynamic modulation scale rather than a differentiable target.

**Progressive Learning Dynamic**
This exponentiated weighting induces a progressive learning dynamic. It addresses the issue in vanilla SFT where high-loss (low-confidence) transitions generate large gradients that disrupt foundational weights. ConSFT mitigates this by down-weighting learning signals from poorly modeled samples, initially prioritizing structurally aligned, easier samples. As the network adapts and confidence increases (loss decreases), the update weights for previously difficult transitions amplify. This continuous feedback loop facilitates an ordered assimilation of the target distribution and prevents dense, unbounded parameter overwrites.

**Analytical Bounding of Parameter Disruption Risk**
The paper provides a mathematical formalization of how ConSFT protects foundational skills. Using the concept of forgetting risk, $R(g) = g^T F g$, where $F$ is the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) representing the curvature of the pre-training objective, the impact of gradient updates on previously acquired capabilities is quantified.
For vanilla SFT, the unconstrained gradient $g_{SFT} = \nabla_{\theta} L_{SFT}(\theta)$ drives optimization, leading to a baseline forgetting risk $R(g_{SFT})$. Since $L_{SFT}(\theta)$ is positively correlated with gradient magnitude, high-loss samples disproportionately amplify this risk.
In ConSFT, the gradient is modulated by the scalar weight $\omega$, so $g_{ConSFT} = \omega \cdot g_{SFT}$. The resulting forgetting risk becomes:
$R(g_{ConSFT}) = \omega^2 \cdot R(g_{SFT}) = \exp\left(-\frac{2L_{SFT}(\theta)}{\tau}\right) R(g_{SFT})$
This demonstrates that ConSFT exponentially attenuates the forgetting risk for high-loss samples, effectively counteracting the generation of large gradients that disrupt foundational knowledge.

**Temperature Annealing Mechanism**
A dynamic temperature schedule is employed, where $\tau$ monotonically increases over training. This annealing relaxes the optimization from conservative bounded updates towards an unconstrained SFT objective, ensuring both initial capability preservation and eventual target task convergence. A lower bound ($\omega_{min}$) is applied to prevent gradient vanishing for high-variance samples.

**Experimental Setup**
- **Benchmarks**: Evaluated on LIBERO (Spatial, Object, Goal suites for capability retention) and RoboTwin (for bimanual coordination, testing retention under structural task discrepancies).
- **Architectures**: Tested on state-of-the-art flow-matching VLA models: $\pi^0$, $\pi^{0.5}$, and GR00T-N1.6-3B.
- **Baselines**: Compared against vanilla SFT, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with and without clipping (for mechanistic understanding), KL-Regularization (LwF), Experience Replay (ER), and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).
- **Metrics**: Target task success rates, prior task retention (measured by absolute drops from base model performance), and parameter update sparsity (quantified by a relative deviation threshold).
- **Real-world Deployment**: Practical evaluation involved fine-tuning a $\pi^{0.5}$ model on a high-precision test-tube transfer task and re-evaluating its performance on prior semantic grasping tasks, including under visually cluttered conditions.
- **Computational Efficiency**: ConSFT operates with zero auxiliary memory overhead and does not require parallel reference networks. Distributed training using Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP) on NVIDIA A100 GPUs was used for large VLA backbones.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings derived from mechanistic ablations, extensive benchmark evaluations, and real-world robotic deployments.

**Mechanistic Analysis of Optimization Dynamics (Section 3)**
- **Impact of Trust Regions**: Empirical analysis on the $\pi^0$ model revealed that unconstrained optimization methods (vanilla SFT and PPO-NoClip) resulted in severe capability degradation, reducing average prior task performance to 9% and 3% respectively on held-out LIBERO suites. In contrast, PPO with a clipping boundary preserved an average foundational capability of 39%, limiting degradation on the Object suite to 13% compared to a 57% drop without clipping. This isolates the explicit trust-region constraint as a critical stabilizer.
- **Parameter Sparsity**: Trust-region constraints significantly reduced the global parameter update volume by half, restricting modifications to approximately 15% of the parameter space compared to 30% under unconstrained SFT. PPO yielded over 99% sparsity across core Attention and MLP weights, demonstrating a correlation between constrained updates, parameter sparsity, and capability retention.

**Capability Retention in Flow-Matching VLAs (Section 5.2, Table 2)**
- **Attenuation of Forgetting Risk**: ConSFT consistently attenuated catastrophic forgetting across all evaluated flow-matching VLA architectures ($\pi^0, \pi^{0.5}$, GR00T) and benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin), while ensuring target-task convergence. For instance, fine-tuning the $\pi^0$ architecture on a LIBERO-Spatial task via SFT led to a 56% drop in LIBERO-Object success rate (from 58% to 2%). ConSFT, however, limited this degradation to 26% (from 58% to 32%) while achieving the same 90% target-task performance as SFT.
- **Robustness to Distribution Shifts**: On the RoboTwin benchmark, which involved structural discrepancies between target (independent dual-arm) and held-out (single-arm, coordinated bimanual) tasks, vanilla SFT resulted in near-total skill degradation (e.g., 0% success on RoboTwin-Coord. for $\pi^0$ and $\pi^{0.5}$). ConSFT preserved these held-out capabilities (e.g., 13% for $\pi^0$ and 10% for $\pi^{0.5}$ on RoboTwin-Coord.) without compromising target-task execution, even slightly outperforming SFT on the target task for $\pi^0$ (60% vs. 55% on RoboTwin-Indep.).

**Mitigating Forgetting Without Data Replay or Structural Constraints (Section 5.3, Table 3)**
- ConSFT outperformed established anti-forgetting baselines (LwF, ER, LoRA) in average prior capability retention.
- **KL-Regularization (LwF)**: Provided insufficient protection (0% success on LIBERO-Goal and RoboTwin-Coord.) and limited target-task convergence (45% on RoboTwin-Indep.).
- **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**: Showed inconsistent preservation, with a 48% drop on LIBERO-Object but only a 2% drop on LIBERO-Goal, and restricted target-task adaptation (51% on RoboTwin-Indep.).
- **Experience Replay (ER)**: Despite its data dependency, ER achieved lower average retention (18% on LIBERO, 15% on RoboTwin) compared to ConSFT (34% on LIBERO, 28% on RoboTwin).
- ConSFT achieved the highest average prior capability retention across both domains while matching or exceeding the target-task performance of all baselines in a prior-data-free regime.

**Mechanistic Validation: Stable Sparsity Decay (Section 5.4, Figure 2)**
- Vanilla SFT was observed to trigger rapid, global parameter overwrites early in training, leading to a quick loss of parameter sparsity.
- ConSFT fundamentally altered this trajectory by bounding the intrinsic disruption risk, leading to a controlled and uniformly decaying sparsity path. It substantially delayed the onset of severe sparsity collapse, enabling necessary parameter shifts for target-task convergence while strictly bounding overall disruption. This effectively replicated the capability retention properties of on-policy RL without its computational overhead.

**Real-world Deployment (Section 5.5, Figure 3)**
- **Preventing Spatial Drift**: In real-world robotic tasks, ConSFT prevented "spatial overfitting" observed in baselines. Baselines (vanilla SFT, LwF, ER, LoRA) overfit to the spatial bias of a high-altitude test-tube transfer task, causing premature gripper closure at elevated z-axis positions when performing prior semantic grasping tasks.
- ConSFT successfully preserved foundational spatial precision and pre-trained physical skills while acquiring sequential target tasks, achieving robust performance even under visually cluttered conditions for prior tasks and a 70% success rate on the long-horizon test-tube insertion task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Conservative Supervised Fine-Tuning (ConSFT) as a significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly in the context of continuous learning and robotic deployment.

**Addressing a Critical Bottleneck**: The work directly addresses catastrophic forgetting, a pervasive problem that hinders the practical deployment and lifelong learning capabilities of VLA models. By enabling models to adapt to new tasks without severely degrading pre-trained foundational skills, ConSFT enhances the robustness and utility of these advanced robotic systems.

**Efficiency and Scalability**: ConSFT's ability to mitigate forgetting without requiring prior pre-training data or additional architectural overhead (such as parallel reference networks) represents a substantial practical advantage. This makes the method highly scalable and applicable to large-capacity VLA models, where memory and computational resources are often constrained, differentiating it from data-intensive (e.g., Experience Replay) or computationally expensive (e.g., explicit RL trust regions for flow models) alternatives.

**Broader Applicability**: The empirical validation across diverse state-of-the-art flow-matching VLA architectures ($\pi^0, \pi^{0.5}$, GR00T) and challenging benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin), including real-world robotic deployments, indicates the method's broad applicability. This suggests that ConSFT can be effectively used to fine-tune various continuous-control VLA models for complex manipulation tasks.

**Enhanced Mechanistic Understanding**: The research contributes to a deeper mechanistic understanding of why reinforcement learning trust regions prevent forgetting, linking this phenomenon to induced parameter sparsity. ConSFT demonstrates that these beneficial dynamics can be effectively emulated within a simpler supervised fine-tuning framework, offering insights into how to design more resilient learning algorithms.

**Practical Impact on Robotics**: For real-world robotics, the ability to acquire new, high-precision skills without losing general robustness or foundational physical competencies (e.g., preventing spatial overfitting) is critical. ConSFT's demonstrated performance in physical deployments suggests that it can facilitate more seamless and reliable adaptation of robots to new environments and tasks, thereby accelerating the development of more versatile and long-lived robotic agents.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding the deceleration of novel skill acquisition and the lack of formal theoretical guarantees on flow-matching ODE trajectory errors, ConSFT offers a compelling and practical solution to a pressing challenge in VLA model adaptation, paving the way for more capable and continuously learning robotic systems.
