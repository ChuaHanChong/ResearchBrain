# NeRF in the Palm of Your Hand: Corrective Augmentation for Robotics via Novel-View Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> SPARTN is a fully offline data augmentation scheme for eye-in-hand robot manipulation policies. It trains a per-demonstration neural radiance field, renders novel views from noise-perturbed gripper poses, and pairs each render with the corrective action returning the gripper to the expert trajectory. Behavior cloning on this augmented data yields reactive, real-time, RGB-only 6-DoF grasping, improving simulated success 2.8x and real-world success by 22.5 points.

## Problem

- Behavior cloning suffers compounding errors, so complex visual tasks need online expert relabeling or environment interaction, both expensive and slow to collect.
- Offline corrective noise injection perturbs only low-dimensional proprioceptive state, leaving visual observations unaugmented, which severely limits its usefulness for image-based policies.
- Closed-loop 6-DoF grasping usually demands depth or point clouds, which fail on transparent, thin and reflective objects; RGB pipelines stay open-loop and unreactive.

## Method

- **SPARTN** trains one ==Instant-NeRF== per demonstration trajectory from the eye-in-hand posed images, using ==COLMAP==-estimated camera poses plus ==hand-eye calibration== to place renders in the world frame.
- ==Corrective relative-action augmentation== samples ==SE(3) noise==, post-multiplies it onto the measured end-effector pose and pre-multiplies its inverse onto the action, copying non-SE(3) gripper dimensions unchanged.
- A ==static-scene protocol== restricts augmentation to pre-grasp timesteps and splices the real gripper back into renders through a ==fixed binary gripper mask== that is excluded from NeRF training.

## Results

- On the simulated 6-DoF grasping benchmark, SPARTN reaches **74.7%** YCB success versus **28.9%** for RGB behavior cloning, a **2.8x** gain, and **51.2%** for DART.
- SPARTN's RGB policy beats DAgger with RGB (**52.3%**) and DART with point clouds (**65.6%**), closing the RGB-versus-depth gap; RL with point clouds still leads at **88.2%**.
- Across eight real-world Franka tasks with 14-25 demonstrations each, SPARTN averaged **61.25%** versus **38.75%** for behavior cloning, winning every object including wine glass **90%** versus **70%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Novel-view synthesis can substitute for online expert queries: rendered off-distribution observations plus geometrically derived labels give DAgger-like recovery data entirely offline.
> - Augmentation only needs local fidelity; because perturbations are small, mediocre NeRFs trained on few demonstration views still work, unlike typical view-synthesis applications.
> - Corrective augmentation that relabels actions dominates label-preserving image augmentation, but assumes static scenes and needs per-platform noise-scale tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly object grasping, presents significant challenges. While advances have been made in controlled settings like top-down bin picking, 6-Degree-of-Freedom (6-DoF) grasping of arbitrary objects within cluttered or complex environments remains an area of active research. This is especially true for objects that are difficult for traditional depth cameras to perceive, such as shiny or transparent items.

Current approaches to 6-DoF grasping often fall into two main categories:
1. **Open-loop trajectory execution:** Methods that synthesize a single grasp pose and then use a motion planner to reach it. These systems typically lack reactive perceptual feedback, making them susceptible to errors or unforeseen environmental changes.
2. **Closed-loop policies:** These approaches, often trained via imitation learning (IL) or reinforcement learning (RL), aim to provide reactive behavior based on continuous perceptual feedback. However, IL, while promising, is known to suffer from *compounding errors* or *covariate shift*. Small deviations from the expert's data distribution can lead to a policy encountering states for which it has no expert guidance, leading to escalating errors. To mitigate this, IL often requires either a large volume of demonstrations, expensive online expert supervision (e.g., DAgger), or extensive environment interaction (e.g., in RL), all of which are resource-intensive.

Previous efforts to address compounding errors through "feedback augmentation" have typically been applied to low-dimensional system states and have not extended to complex visual observations. Additionally, while eye-in-hand cameras have been shown to improve visuomotor policies by providing a consistent, egocentric view, they do not intrinsically resolve the issue of compounding errors.

More recently, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have emerged as a powerful tool for novel-view synthesis, enabling the generation of high-fidelity images from arbitrary camera poses. NeRFs have found applications in various robotics domains, including localization, navigation, dynamics modeling, and data generation for other learning-based methods, and some works have leveraged NeRF for open-loop grasp synthesis. However, the direct application of NeRFs for *offline, visual corrective feedback augmentation* to train *closed-loop, reactive, RGB-only* policies for 6-DoF grasping has not been widely explored.

This work, SPARTN, positions itself by addressing the limitations of existing imitation learning and data augmentation techniques. It specifically extends the concept of corrective feedback augmentation to the visual domain by leveraging NeRFs, thereby enabling the training of robust closed-loop 6-DoF grasping policies from RGB-only inputs in a fully offline manner. The research contrasts itself with prior grasping methods by uniquely combining RGB-only input, 6-DoF control, and closed-loop reactive policy learning within an offline augmentation framework (as summarized in Table 1 of the paper).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and performance of visual robotic manipulation policies, specifically for 6-DoF grasping tasks, using eye-in-hand cameras. This is to be achieved through a novel, fully-offline data augmentation scheme that addresses the inherent limitations of imitation learning.

The detailed objectives and motivations include:

1. **Mitigating Compounding Errors in Imitation Learning:** A significant motivation stems from the susceptibility of behavior cloning (a common imitation learning technique) to compounding errors. When a robot deviates slightly from the expert's trajectory, it encounters states not present in the training data, leading to a cascade of errors. The objective is to introduce a mechanism that prepares the policy to recover from such deviations.
2. **Developing an Offline Data Augmentation Scheme:** The authors aim to create a data augmentation method that operates entirely offline. This is motivated by the high cost and time consumption associated with collecting large numbers of expert demonstrations or providing online expert supervision, as well as the need for environment interaction in alternative methods like reinforcement learning or DAgger variants. An offline approach minimizes additional expert effort and computational resources during policy training.
3. **Extending Corrective Feedback to the Visual Domain:** Previous offline "feedback augmentation" techniques, which inject noise into system states to generate corrective actions, have been limited to low-dimensional state representations. A key objective is to extend this concept to complex visual observations (RGB images), enabling policies to learn visual recovery behaviors.
4. **Leveraging Novel-View Synthesis (NeRFs):** The motivation behind using NeRFs is to synthetically generate diverse, high-fidelity visual observations from perturbed camera poses without requiring real-world interaction. This allows for the creation of a rich dataset of "what-if" scenarios where the robot slightly deviates and then receives a corrective visual input and action. This effectively "distills" the underlying 3D geometric understanding of the scene from the NeRF into a 2D image-based policy.
5. **Enabling Reactive, Real-Time, RGB-Only Policies for 6-DoF Grasping:** The ultimate goal is to produce policies that can perform 6-DoF grasping with full 3D translation and rotation control, respond in real-time to sensory feedback, and operate solely on RGB camera inputs. This is particularly motivated by the challenges posed by transparent, reflective, or thin objects, which are problematic for depth sensors. By relying only on RGB, the method aims to improve robustness for a broader range of real-world objects.
6. **Building on Eye-in-Hand Camera Success:** Recognizing the benefits of eye-in-hand cameras for visuomotor policies (consistent perspective), the work aims to build upon this foundation by integrating the corrective augmentation to address their remaining limitation concerning compounding errors.

In essence, the research seeks to overcome the data inefficiency and error propagation issues of imitation learning in visuomotor control by synthetically generating diverse training data that includes corrective feedback, all while maintaining a fully offline, RGB-only paradigm suitable for complex 6-DoF manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, SPARTN (Synthetic Perturbations for Augmenting Robot Trajectories via NeRF), is an offline data augmentation scheme designed to improve robot policies that utilize eye-in-hand cameras. The core idea involves using Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) to generate synthetic perturbed viewpoints and corresponding corrective actions from expert demonstrations.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key stages.

### 4. Preliminaries

- **Imitation Learning (Behavior Cloning):** The foundation is standard behavior cloning, where a policy $\pi_\theta(s)$ is trained to mimic expert actions $a_k$ given states $s_k$ from a dataset of expert trajectories $D = \{\tau_i\}_{i=1}^N$. The objective is to minimize a loss function in the action space.
- **Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF):** NeRFs are used for novel-view synthesis. For each scene, a NeRF ($F_\Theta$) is trained from a set of posed images $\{(I_k, T_k)\}$, allowing it to render new views $I = \text{NeRF-Render}(T; F_\Theta)$ from any requested camera pose $T$. The paper utilizes an accelerated NeRF implementation (Instant-NeRF) due to the need for training multiple NeRFs.
- **Corrective Noise Augmentation:** This technique aims to make policies robust to small errors. By injecting noise ($\epsilon$) into a nominal state ($s_k \rightarrow \tilde{s}_k = s_k + \epsilon$), a corrective action ($\tilde{a}_k$) is computed that would return the system to the nominal trajectory ($s_{k+1}$). Prior work applied this to low-dimensional states, but not visual observations.

### 5. Visual Corrective Augmentation Overview

SPARTN extends corrective noise augmentation to the visual domain for eye-in-hand robot policies operating in static scenes. The process for each demonstration involves:
1. **NeRF Training:** A scene-specific NeRF ($F_\Theta^\tau$) is trained for each demonstration trajectory ($\tau$) using its sequence of posed images $\{(I_k, T_k)\}$.
2. **Perturbation and Corrective Action Calculation:** For each timestep $k$ in a demonstration, the robot's end-effector pose $T_k$ is perturbed with sampled noise $\epsilon$ to create a noisy pose $\tilde{T}_k$. Concurrently, a corrective action $\tilde{a}_k$ is calculated that would steer the robot back towards the original expert trajectory from this perturbed state.
3. **Novel View Rendering:** Using the trained NeRF $F_\Theta^\tau$, a novel, high-fidelity visual observation $\tilde{I}_k$ is rendered from the perturbed camera pose corresponding to $\tilde{T}_k$.
4. **Augmented Data Generation:** The triplet $(\tilde{I}_k, \tilde{T}_k, \tilde{a}_k)$ forms an augmented transition, which is added to a dataset $\tilde{D}$. This process is repeated $N_{aug}$ times for each original transition.
5. **Policy Training:** The augmented dataset $\tilde{D}$ is combined with the original dataset $D$ to train an RGB-only policy using standard behavior cloning.

### 6. Training NeRFs from Robot Demonstrations

- **Camera Pose Transformation:** The robot's end-effector poses are transformed into camera poses using a known hand-eye calibration matrix ($E T_C$).
- **COLMAP for Pose Refinement:** For real-world data, raw robot-estimated camera poses are often noisy. SPARTN uses COLMAP to estimate more accurate camera poses ($V H_C k$) in an arbitrary reference frame ($V$). A conversion process (involving estimating a scale factor $\beta$ and a transformation $V T_W$) is applied to transform perturbed poses from the robot's world frame to COLMAP's frame for rendering. In simulation, where calibration is exact, COLMAP is not used.
- **Static Scene Assumption Handling:** Standard NeRFs assume a static scene. For manipulation tasks, SPARTN applies NeRF training and augmentation only to the portions of a demonstration where no robot-object interaction occurs (e.g., before the gripper closes).
- **Gripper Masking:** The robot's gripper, often visible in eye-in-hand views, also violates the static scene assumption. A binary mask $M$ is used to mask out gripper pixels during NeRF training. After rendering a perturbed image $\tilde{I}_k$, the gripper from the original image $I_k$ is spliced back in: $\tilde{I}_k \leftarrow \neg M \tilde{I}_k + M I_k$.
- **NeRF Quality:** The method posits that even with limited training views, NeRF's generalization in the local region around the demonstration trajectory (where perturbations are small) is sufficient for effective augmentation.

### 7. NeRFing Corrective Noise Augmentation Details

- **Noise Sampling:** Noise $\epsilon$ is sampled from a parameterized distribution (e.g., uniform for Euler angles and translations) in SE(3).
- **Relative Actions:** Policies are parameterized to output relative actions (changes in end-effector pose) rather than global poses. If the un-noised relative action is $a_k$, and the noise is defined as transforming from the noisy end-effector frame to the measured one ($E T_{\tilde{E}} = \epsilon$), then the corrective relative action becomes $\tilde{a}_k = \epsilon^{-1} a_k$. This means perturbing the measured pose by post-multiplying with $\epsilon$ and pre-multiplying the un-noised relative action with $\epsilon^{-1}$.
- **Non-SE(3) Actions:** For action components not related to pose (e.g., gripper open/close), the original expert actions are simply copied to the augmented transitions.

### 8. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation:** Evaluated on a simulated 6-DoF grasping benchmark using a Franka Emika Panda arm. Policies control 6-DoF relative pose changes. Training data includes 2,500 demonstrations.
 - Augmentation parameters: $N_{aug} = 100$, rotation noise $\alpha = 0.2$ radians, translation noise $\beta = 3$ mm.
 - Policy architecture: ResNet-18 image encoder (pretrained on ImageNet) followed by an MLP.
- **Real-World:** Tested on a Franka Emika Panda robot with a wrist-mounted webcam performing 6-DoF grasping on eight challenging target objects from limited human demonstrations.
 - Augmentation parameters: $N_{aug} = 50$, rotation noise $\alpha = 0.05$ radians, translation noise $\beta = 0.4$ mm.
 - Policy architecture: A custom four-layer convolutional network followed by an MLP.
 - Special detail: A few images of the scene from fixed poses were collected automatically before each real-world demonstration to improve COLMAP pose estimation and NeRF training quality.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The research presents evaluation results in both simulated and real-world 6-DoF grasping tasks, comparing SPARTN against various baseline methods.

### 10. Simulation Experiments

- **Improved Success Rates:** In a simulated 6-DoF grasping benchmark, SPARTN significantly improved grasp success rates for RGB-only policies.
 - It achieved a **2.8x increase** in success rate over imitation learning (Behavior Cloning, BC) without corrective augmentations on the challenging YCB object set (74.7% vs. 28.9%).
 - SPARTN outperformed DART (an offline method that injects noise during expert demonstration) with RGB input (74.7% vs. 51.2% on YCB).
 - It also surpassed some methods requiring online supervision, specifically DAgger for RGB policies (74.7% vs. 52.3% on YCB).
- **Closing the RGB-D Gap:** SPARTN's RGB-only performance on YCB objects was comparable to or better than methods utilizing depth or point cloud information, such as DART with point clouds (74.7% vs. 65.6%) and DAgger with point clouds (74.7% vs. 77.2% - a close comparison). This indicates that SPARTN can effectively distill 3D geometric information into an RGB policy, reducing or eliminating the need for depth sensors.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Distribution Objects:** SPARTN showed larger performance improvements on the YCB dataset (held-out, more "out-of-distribution" objects) compared to the ShapeNet dataset (which is closer to the training distribution), suggesting improved generalization capabilities.
- **Effect of Standard Image Augmentations:** Ablation studies demonstrated that standard image augmentations (random crop, color jitter) had a minor effect on performance across various methods, in contrast to the substantial impact of SPARTN's corrective augmentations.

### 11. Real-World Experiments

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** On eight challenging real-world 6-DoF grasping tasks using a Franka Emika Panda robot with limited human demonstrations, SPARTN consistently outperformed vanilla BC policies.
 - It achieved an average absolute success rate increase of **22.5%** across all tasks (average 61.25% for SPARTN vs. 38.75% for BC).
 - Specific examples included improving grasping success for a banana (from 55% to 75%), a thin box (from 35% to 65%), a steel water bottle (from 20% to 40%), and a wine glass (from 70% to 90%).
- **Qualitative Improvements in Reactive Behavior:** Qualitative analysis of policy rollouts revealed that SPARTN-trained policies exhibited more reactive and robust behaviors compared to baseline BC policies.
 - This included improved navigation towards target objects, occasional obstacle avoidance, more precise action execution, and successful reattempts after an initial missed grasp.
 - In some instances, SPARTN policies successfully completed tasks where BC policies failed to even reach the object. Even in cases where both methods failed, SPARTN often performed qualitatively better, such as nearly grasping the object or making contact where BC completely missed.

These results indicate that SPARTN effectively addresses the compounding error problem in imitation learning, enabling the training of more robust and reactive RGB-only policies for complex 6-DoF grasping, both in simulation and the real world, without requiring additional online supervision or interaction.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SPARTN presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation and machine learning:

1. **Enhanced Policy Robustness and Reactivity:** By explicitly training policies to recover from small deviations, SPARTN directly addresses the critical problem of compounding errors in imitation learning. This leads to more robust, real-time, and reactive closed-loop policies that can adapt to minor unforeseen circumstances during execution, a crucial factor for reliable real-world robotic deployment.
2. **Reduced Data Collection Costs and Effort:** SPARTN's fully-offline nature is a substantial advantage. It eliminates the need for expensive online expert supervision or extensive environmental interactions that are often required by other methods like DAgger or reinforcement learning. This makes the process of training visuomotor policies more accessible and less resource-intensive, as it only requires a pre-collected set of expert demonstrations.
3. **Elimination of Depth Sensor Dependency:** A key impact is demonstrating that RGB-only policies, when augmented with SPARTN, can achieve performance comparable to or even surpassing some methods that rely on depth information. This is particularly significant for grasping challenging objects like transparent or reflective items, which are notoriously difficult for depth cameras but are common in real-world scenarios. By removing the need for depth sensors, SPARTN simplifies robot hardware requirements and broadens the range of graspable objects.
4. **Effective Distillation of 3D Information into 2D Policies:** The method effectively leverages NeRFs, which inherently capture 3D scene geometry, to generate 2D RGB image augmentations. This process distills complex 3D geometric understanding into a real-time, RGB-only policy, allowing it to benefit from 3D context without explicit 3D input at inference time.
5. **Generalization to Novel Scenarios:** The simulation results, particularly the stronger performance on out-of-distribution YCB objects compared to in-distribution ShapeNet objects, suggest that SPARTN improves the generalization capabilities of learned policies. This indicates a greater ability to handle novel objects and variations in object placement.
6. **Paving the Way for Future Research:** SPARTN introduces a novel paradigm of using NeRFs for visual corrective feedback augmentation. This opens avenues for future research into dynamic scene synthesis for more complex manipulation tasks, the development of amortized NeRF models to reduce computational overhead during augmentation, and potentially a fully offline variant of DAgger by using policy actions to generate noise for NeRF rendering.

In summary, SPARTN offers a practical and effective solution to a fundamental challenge in imitation learning for robotics. By making policies more robust, reducing training costs, and expanding the capabilities of RGB-only vision systems, it contributes significantly to the development of more capable and deployable robotic manipulation systems.
