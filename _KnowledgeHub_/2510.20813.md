# GSWorld: Closed-Loop Photo-Realistic Simulation Suite for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GSWorld is a photo-realistic simulation suite for robotic manipulation that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with physics engines, enabling a closed-loop development cycle from real-world scene reconstruction to policy training and iterative refinement. It demonstrates zero-shot sim2real transfer and improves policy performance through automated DAgger data collection in simulation.

## Problem

- Traditional robot training methods (simulation, human videos, real teleoperation) exhibit trade-offs, particularly the visual fidelity and action-space mismatch, leading to a persistent sim-to-real gap.
- Existing photo-realistic real2sim approaches using NeRFs or early 3DGS often struggle with object motion, visual artifacts, extensive manual setup, single-scene overfitting, and lack standardized asset formats.
- Collecting high-quality corrective data for policy adaptation (e.g., DAgger) in the real world is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale.

## Method

- Integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with conventional physics engines to provide photo-realistic rendering that closely mirrors real scenes while maintaining accurate physics interactions.
- Introduces the Gaussian Scene Description File (GSDF), a portable asset format combining Gaussian-on-Mesh representation with robot URDFs and other object properties for cross-embodiment compatibility.
- Provides a streamlined 'real-to-sim' reconstruction pipeline utilizing ArUco markers for metric-scale alignment and ICP for robust robot/table alignment, significantly reducing manual effort.

## Results

- Policies trained exclusively in GSWorld's simulation achieve promising zero-shot sim2real transfer success rates on real robots, such as 68% for 'Place Box' and 52% for 'Pour Sauce' tasks with a Franka Research 3 robot.
- The closed-loop DAgger pipeline significantly improves policy performance, boosting success rates for tasks like 'Place Box' from 68% to 76% in simulation and enhancing real-world policies through targeted corrective data.
- Visual Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies trained with GSWorld achieve improved real-world success rates (e.g., 30% for 'Grasp Banana' vs. 0% for baseline simulator) and demonstrate faster convergence during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining the photo-realism of 3DGS with physics engines and a standardized asset format (GSDF) effectively bridges both the visual and action-space sim-to-real gaps for robotic manipulation.
> - Automated DAgger data collection within a high-fidelity simulation environment provides an efficient and scalable mechanism for iterative policy refinement and adaptation, significantly reducing the cost and effort compared to real-world collection.
> - GSWorld's simulation performance strongly correlates with real-world policy outcomes, establishing it as a reliable and reproducible platform for benchmarking and evaluating algorithmic progress.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

GSWorld addresses several critical challenges at the intersection of robotics simulation, machine learning for robotics, and neural rendering, aiming to bridge the persistent "sim-to-real" gap.

**Current Landscape Challenges:**
Traditional methods for training robot manipulation policies typically rely on three main data sources, each with inherent trade-offs:
1. **Simulation:** Offers perfectly aligned action spaces and scalability but suffers from a significant visual fidelity gap (sim2real visual gap) compared to the real world, often requiring extensive domain randomization or adaptation techniques.
2. **Human Videos:** Provide photo-realistic scenes and real physics but lack temporally aligned robot actions and operate in a mismatched action space.
3. **Real Teleoperation:** Offers perception-action alignment but is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, especially for collecting large, diverse datasets or corrective data after policy failures.

**Evolution of Real2Sim Approaches:**
The paper acknowledges a growing trend in "real2sim" methods, which attempt to build simulation assets by virtualizing real-world environments. This area has seen advancements in:
- **Photo-realistic 3D Reconstruction:** Early methods used NeRFs, Diffusion models, or meshes. NeRFs, while photo-realistic, struggled with object motion due to their implicit representation. Mesh-based approaches often had visual artifacts. Recent works like Simpler aimed for photo-realistic benchmarking but required extensive manual labor.
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** The emergence of 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) has revolutionized real-time radiance field rendering. Prior works leveraging 3DGS for robotics include SplatSim, which combined 3DGS with physics engines for zero-shot sim2real, but was limited by manual segmentation and single-scene overfitting. Other parallel efforts include Embodied-GS, Robo-GS, and ManiGaussian, each focusing on specific aspects like interaction or optimization. Most recently, Re3Sim extended SplatSim by focusing on robust manipulation through domain randomization and mixed simulation.

**GSWorld's Position and Advancements:**
GSWorld builds directly upon these advancements in 3DGS-based simulators while addressing their key limitations:
- **Comprehensive Closed-Loop Framework:** Unlike prior 3DGS simulators that often target single-setup rendering or lack portable asset standards, GSWorld provides a "robust real-to-sim-to-real pipeline." This "closed-loop" concept is central, allowing the same environment to be used for training, evaluation, failure diagnosis, and relabeling, facilitating rapid iteration in policy development.
- **Standardized Asset Format (GSDF):** A crucial contribution is the introduction of GSDF (Gaussian Scene Description File), which infuses Gaussian-on-Mesh representation with robot URDF and other object properties. This standardized, portable format tackles the "engine-tied pipelines" limitation of previous works, enabling reproducible cross-embodiment benchmarking.
- **Streamlined Reconstruction Pipeline:** GSWorld offers an easy-to-use, streamlined procedure to reduce manual alignment efforts, overcoming the manual segmentation and single-scene overfitting issues of earlier 3DGS simulators. The use of ArUco markers for metric-scale alignment and ICP for robot/table alignment automates critical steps.
- **Broad Application Scope:** GSWorld is not just a rendering engine; it's a comprehensive suite supporting:
 - **Zero-shot Sim2Real Transfer:** Directly deploying policies trained in simulation to the real world.
 - **Closed-Loop DAgger:** Automated, high-quality corrective data collection to adapt policies to deployment environments, a significant improvement over costly real-world teleoperation.
 - **Reproducible Visual Benchmarking:** Standardized assets and environments enable fair, apples-to-apples comparisons of algorithms.
 - **Virtual Teleoperation:** Efficient simulation data collection using human operators.
 - **Visual Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Reducing visual gaps for more effective RL training.

In essence, GSWorld significantly advances the state-of-the-art by providing a more generalized, scalable, and user-friendly photo-realistic simulation platform that effectively addresses both visual and action-space sim-to-real gaps, thus accelerating the development and deployment of robust robotic manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind GSWorld is to overcome the inherent trade-offs associated with existing methods for training robot manipulation policies, particularly the persistent sim-to-real gap. The paper aims to create a robust and photo-realistic simulation suite that enables a "closed-loop" development cycle for robotic manipulation.

The key objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Narrowing the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The core objective is to reduce both the visual and action-space discrepancies between simulated and real-world environments. By integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with physics engines, GSWorld seeks to achieve photo-realistic rendering that closely mirrors real scenes, allowing policies to perceive realistic observations while issuing controls in the robot's native action space.
- **Enabling Closed-Loop Policy Development:** To facilitate rapid iteration and continuous improvement, GSWorld aims to create a single environment that can be used for training, reproducible evaluation, diagnosing failures, and efficiently collecting corrective data (re-labeling). This "closed-loop" capability is designed to streamline the entire policy development process.
- **Developing a Standardized Asset Format (GSDF):** To ensure portability, generalizability, and ease of use across diverse scenes and robot embodiments, a critical objective is to propose a new asset format, GSDF (Gaussian Scene Description File). This format combines the photo-realistic Gaussian-on-Mesh representation with robot URDF and other object properties, making it compatible with existing physics engines.
- **Facilitating Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer:** A major goal is to enable policies trained entirely within the photo-realistic GSWorld simulator to be directly deployed on real hardware without requiring extensive domain adaptation or sim2real translation techniques. This includes both imitation learning (IL) and visual reinforcement learning (RL) policies.
- **Automating High-Quality DAgger Data Collection:** To address the challenge of gathering corrective data for policy adaptation during deployment, GSWorld aims to provide an automated, efficient mechanism for collecting DAgger-style data. This involves reproducing failure cases in simulation and generating expert corrections.
- **Establishing Reproducible Visual Benchmarking:** The creation of standardized GSDF assets, fixed camera parameters, consistent lighting, and action semantics seeks to enable "apples-to-apples" comparisons of different manipulation policies and algorithms, ensuring that performance improvements reflect algorithmic progress rather than environmental variance.
- **Supporting Diverse Downstream Applications:** Beyond its core capabilities, GSWorld is designed to support a range of immediate and impactful applications, including:
 - Learning zero-shot sim2real pixel-to-action manipulation policies.
 - Automated high-quality DAgger data collection for policy adaptation.
 - Reproducible benchmarking of real-robot manipulation policies.
 - Simulation data collection through virtual teleoperation.
 - Zero-shot sim2real visual reinforcement learning.

In essence, GSWorld is motivated by the desire to accelerate robotic manipulation research by providing a highly realistic, efficient, and scalable simulation platform that significantly reduces the friction in moving from simulation to real-world deployment and continuous policy improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GSWorld's methodology hinges on a novel integration of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with conventional physics engines, establishing a bidirectional "real-to-sim-to-real" pipeline and a new asset format.

**A. Problem Formulation (Policy Learning with GSWorld)**
The core idea is to replace raw real-world RGB observations ($I_{real}$) with 3DGS-rendered images ($I_{gs}$) for various robot learning tasks (Imitation Learning, Reinforcement Learning, DAgger). The policy $\pi_\theta$ takes $I_{gs}$ and robot proprioceptions $q_t$ as input to predict actions $a_t$. The success criterion is the generalization of this policy to real-world observations at test time.

**B. Recipe for Real2Sim Reconstruction (Creating GSDF Assets)**
This is the "real-to-sim" direction, focusing on creating photo-realistic digital twins as GSDF assets.
1. **Collecting Training Views:** Multi-view RGB images of the scene (including robots and objects) are captured using robot wrist/third-person cameras and mobile phones. Crucially, the robot's joint pose is recorded during capture.
2. **Aligning Scale for Metric Representation:** To overcome the scale ambiguity inherent in Structure-from-Motion (SfM) methods like COLMAP, GSWorld uses ArUco markers placed on the tabletop. These markers, with known physical dimensions, allow for scaling the 3DGS-reconstructed point cloud to a metric representation, also aiding in identifying support surfaces and gravity direction.
3. **Aligning Robots and Table:**
 - A static robot (R) is reconstructed as part of the scene.
 - The simulation robot's URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) is then rigidly aligned with the 3DGS reconstruction of the real robot using Iterative Closest Point (ICP). The fixed metric scale from the ArUco step simplifies this alignment by reducing degrees of freedom.
 - K-NN segmentation is used on the 3DGS point cloud to associate points with specific robot links.
4. **Object Assets:** For movable objects (O), GSWorld integrates existing large-scale datasets like DTC and YCB. For custom objects, 2DGS is used for reconstruction, generating both Gaussian and mesh representations. Mass is estimated by weighing. Unobserved regions (e.g., object bottoms) can be inpainted. Similar to robots, ICP is used to align object reconstructions with their simulation counterparts.

**C. GSDF (Gaussian Scene Description File):**
The proposed GSDF format is key to unifying the Gaussian-on-Mesh representation with robot URDFs and object properties. It allows for physics simulation (collision meshes, material properties) alongside photo-realistic rendering from 3DGS. GSDF assets are compatible with existing simulators, utilizing standard formats for depth and segmentation while providing a rendering wrapper for photo-realistic RGB.

**D. Applications - Closing the Loop for Developing Visual Manipulation Policies**
This section describes the "sim-to-real" and iterative aspects:

1. **Closed-loop DAgger Training:**
 - GSWorld automates DAgger data collection in simulation.
 - When a policy fails during simulation rollout, the environment is reset to a preceding solvable state.
 - A motion planner then generates "corrective data" from this state to successfully complete the task.
 - This process iteratively refines policies by adding targeted corrective trajectories to the training dataset. It applies to both policies initially trained on simulation data (sim2real) and those trained on real-world data (real2sim2real DAgger).
 - The "privileged information" in simulation allows for efficient reproduction and correction of failures, which is arduous in the real world.
2. **Visual Benchmarking:**
 - GSWorld provides a standardized, photo-realistic environment (GSDF assets, fixed cameras, consistent lighting/materials, standardized actions) for evaluating manipulation policies across different robots and tasks.
 - It allows for "apples-to-apples" comparisons, ensuring that algorithmic improvements are genuinely reflected.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - To support the high data requirements of RL, GSWorld optimizes its implementation for parallel environments.
 - It parallelizes only the 3DGS points associated with moving parts (robot, objects), caching static background points to efficiently leverage a single GPU for large-scale parallelism, thereby accelerating RL convergence and reducing sim2real visual gaps.
4. **Virtual Teleoperation:**
 - GSWorld supports human teleoperation within the simulation, allowing for efficient collection of high-quality, photo-realistic demonstration data using standard input devices (keyboard/mouse).

The methodology of GSWorld thus presents a comprehensive ecosystem that not only generates highly realistic simulation environments but also leverages these environments for efficient data collection, iterative policy improvement, and reliable benchmarking, effectively "closing the loop" in robot learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GSWorld's effectiveness is demonstrated across a suite of manipulation tasks and learning paradigms using various robot platforms (Franka Research 3, xArm6, Galaxea R1) and policy architectures (ACT, Pi0).

**A. Zero-shot Sim2Real Imitation Learning (IL)**
- **Finding:** Policies trained exclusively with GSWorld-generated simulation data exhibit promising success rates when deployed directly on real robots without any further fine-tuning or domain adaptation.
- **Result (Fig. 7, Table II):** For FR3 tasks like "Place Box," "Pour Sauce," "Stack Cans," and "Arrange Cans," the "TfS-Sim" (Train from Scratch with Sim data) consistently shows non-zero and often significant success rates (e.g., 68% for Place Box, 52% for Pour Sauce after 500 demonstrations in sim). This directly validates GSWorld's ability to bridge the sim-to-real visual gap for IL.

**B. Closed-loop DAgger for Continuous Policy Improvement**
- **Finding:** The automated DAgger data collection pipeline in GSWorld significantly improves policy performance, both for policies initially trained in simulation and for refining existing real-world policies.
- **Result (Fig. 7, Table II - FR3 Sim2Real DAgger):** DAgger consistently outperforms policies trained from scratch (TfS-Sim/Real). For instance, on "Place Box," DAgger increases success from 68% (TfS-Sim) to 76% (DAgger-Sim) in simulation, and from 65% (TfS-Real) to 70% (DAgger-Real) on the real robot after 5 iterations. Similar improvements are observed across all FR3 tasks (e.g., "Stack Cans" shows a jump from 60% to 88% in sim).
- **Result (Fig. 8 - xArm Real2Sim2Real DAgger):** DAgger applied to initially real-world trained policies on xArm tasks like "Align Cans," "Grasp Banana," and "Tidy Table" also demonstrates significant performance boosts, highlighting its utility for continual improvement of deployed real-world agents.

**C. Visual Benchmarking**
- **Finding:** GSWorld accurately predicts real-world policy outcomes based on simulation performance, establishing a reliable benchmarking framework. There is a strong positive correlation between simulated and real-world success rates.
- **Result (Fig. 9, Table I):** Across various FR3 tasks and different policy architectures (ACT and Pi0), higher simulated success rates consistently correspond to higher success rates in real-world experiments. For example, for "Place Box," ACT achieved 44% in sim and 50% in real, while Pi0 achieved 52% in sim and 60% in real. The average real-world success rates (ACT: 50.0%, Pi0: 55.0%) are closely tracked by the simulated rates (ACT: 41.0%, Pi0: 43.5%). This correlation is maintained regardless of the overall quality of the policy, making GSWorld a robust tool for evaluating algorithmic progress.

**D. Virtual Teleoperation**
- **Finding:** GSWorld enables efficient simulation data collection through virtual teleoperation, providing photo-realistic visual feedback.
- **Result (Fig. 10):** A qualitative example using the bimanual Galaxea R1 robot demonstrates that users can teleoperate the robot in the simulated environment with photo-realistic renderings, facilitating easy data collection for complex tasks.

**E. Visual Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
- **Finding:** GSWorld helps narrow the sim2real visual gap for visual RL policies, leading to improved real-world performance compared to standard simulators.
- **Result (Fig. 11):** For tasks like "Grasp Banana" and "Tidy Table," SAC policies trained with GSWorld achieved real-world success rates of 30% and 20% respectively. In contrast, policies trained with baseline ManiSkill (a standard simulator) achieved only 0% and 5%. The learning curves (Fig. 11) also show faster convergence and higher final rewards for GSWorld environments during training, demonstrating its benefits for RL.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that GSWorld effectively achieves its objectives, providing a powerful and versatile platform for training, evaluating, and improving robot manipulation policies with high fidelity and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GSWorld represents a significant advancement in the field of robotics, particularly in manipulation policy learning, with a substantial potential impact across research and development.

**1. Accelerating Robot Learning and Development Cycles:**
The "closed-loop" nature of GSWorld is its most impactful contribution. By enabling rapid iteration between training, evaluation, failure diagnosis, and targeted data collection (via DAgger), it drastically reduces the time and resources required to develop and refine manipulation policies. This iterative refinement process, previously bottlenecked by costly and difficult real-world data collection, can now be executed efficiently in a high-fidelity simulation. This acceleration will allow researchers and engineers to explore more complex algorithms and tackle more challenging manipulation tasks.

**2. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap with Unprecedented Fidelity:**
GSWorld's combination of 3D Gaussian Splatting with physics engines delivers photo-realistic rendering that closely mirrors real-world environments. This directly addresses the visual sim-to-real gap, which has historically been a major hurdle for deploying simulation-trained policies on physical robots. The demonstrated zero-shot sim2real transfer for both imitation learning and reinforcement learning is a strong testament to this fidelity, reducing the need for extensive domain randomization or real-world fine-tuning.

**3. Democratizing Access to Complex Robotic Scenarios:**
By providing a robust and streamlined pipeline for creating digital twins (GSDF assets) from real-world scenes, GSWorld lowers the barrier for researchers and small teams to experiment with diverse robot embodiments and complex environments. It eliminates the need for expensive, time-consuming manual asset creation and meticulous alignment, making advanced simulation accessible to a wider community. This could lead to a proliferation of novel manipulation research in scenarios that were previously too difficult or costly to set up.

**4. Enabling Standardized and Reproducible Benchmarking:**
The introduction of standardized GSDF assets and a consistent simulation environment allows for truly "apples-to-apples" comparisons of different robot learning algorithms. This addresses a critical need in robotics research for reproducible benchmarks, where algorithmic improvements can be clearly attributed to the methods themselves rather than environmental variations or subtle implementation differences. This will foster more rigorous scientific evaluation and accelerate consensus on effective techniques.

**5. Advancing Continual Learning and Adaptation:**
The automated DAgger data collection mechanism is particularly significant for lifelong and continual learning paradigms. Policies can be continually improved even after deployment, as failures can be reproduced in simulation, diagnosed, and corrected with targeted data. This enables robots to adapt to changing environments or unforeseen circumstances, moving towards more robust and adaptive autonomous systems.

**6. Fostering Innovation in Neural Rendering for Robotics:**
GSWorld's successful integration of 3DGS with robotics physics engines further validates the utility of neural rendering techniques beyond view synthesis. It showcases how these advanced rendering capabilities can directly contribute to solving real-world robotics problems, likely inspiring more research at this intersection.

In summary, GSWorld is more than just another simulator; it's a comprehensive platform that promises to redefine how robotic manipulation policies are developed, evaluated, and deployed. Its emphasis on closed-loop development, photo-realistic fidelity, and ease of use positions it as a foundational tool for accelerating progress towards more capable and adaptable autonomous robots.
