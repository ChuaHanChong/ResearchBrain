# QDTraj: Exploration of Diverse Trajectory Primitives for Articulated Objects Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> QDTraj generates diverse low-level trajectory primitives for manipulating articulated objects. It runs MAP-Elites quality-diversity search over 6-DoF grasp start frames, scored by an adaptive compliant interaction policy where the object joint is actuated while the robot goes soft, recording the resulting end-effector path. It yields thousands of distinct successful trajectories per task, averaging 704 primitives across 30 PartNet-Mobility articulations, and transfers to a real Franka arm.

## Problem

- Expert demonstration data for contact-rich articulated manipulation is costly to teleoperate, while simulated collection suffers sim-to-real inconsistency and modeling error.
- Prior interaction policies rely on hand-designed, geometry-specific primitives or fixed Cartesian directions, limiting adaptability to misaligned handles, blocked parts, or missing handles.
- Existing methods optimize toward a single solution and ignore redundancy, so robots lack alternative ways to achieve a task under live constraints or unexpected changes.

## Method

- Articulation-centred task formalism: an activation task is object, joint type plus index, and initial and target joint values, decomposing complex tasks into single-degree-of-freedom activations.
- MAP-Elites archive whose genotype is a 6-DoF end-effector start pose, behavioural descriptor is its 3D Cartesian position on a 1 cm grid, with half-elite half-random selection plus bounded noise mutation.
- Adaptive interaction policy as fitness evaluator: after force-closure grasp, robot gains drop to compliant while the object joint is driven stiffly, and the dragged end-effector path becomes the trajectory.

## Results

- MAP-Elites-Explore produced 5 times more successful trajectories than the success-only variant on the hinge task and 6 times more on the slider task.
- The adaptive action space gave 2.3 times more successful trajectories on average, and 3.5 times more for hinge versus 1.6 times for slider, over Where2Act and VAT-Mart spaces.
- Combining both contributions lifted hinge successes from 27 to 2,944 and slider from 532 to 3,988; 30 PartNet-Mobility articulations averaged 704 trajectories and 645 grasps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating diversity as the optimization target, not a byproduct, surfaces non-intuitive solutions such as closing a door without the handle that human demonstrators rarely produce.
> - Letting the object actuate a compliant robot inverts the usual control direction and yields physically grounded trajectories without hand-coded motion primitives or precomputed interaction paths.
> - Benefit is joint-type dependent: prismatic tasks are handled well by simple parameterized primitives, so adaptive search pays off most on rotational, geometry-coupled articulations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is transitioning from controlled industrial settings to more unstructured, everyday environments, such as homes. A significant challenge in these open-ended environments is the autonomous manipulation of articulated objects, which possess movable parts connected by joints (e.g., oven doors, drawers). Current approaches for acquiring expert manipulation data typically rely on time-consuming and expensive real-world teleoperation or simulated data collection that can suffer from sim-to-real inconsistencies.

Existing methods for articulated object manipulation often focus on learning grasp areas or generating interaction policies. Approaches like AO-Grasp and GAPartNet provide actionable grasps, but some are highly hand-designed or geometry-specific, limiting their adaptability. Other methods, such as PartManip using PPO or AdaRPG leveraging foundation models, may restrict robots to predefined orientations or action types. Works like Where2Act and Where2Explore often rely on randomly sampled or hard-coded Cartesian motion primitives, which can lead to low sampling efficiency or limited diversity. VAT-Mart focuses on predicting residual gripper poses for more realistic motions, but its diversity primarily affects trajectories rather than initial interaction points.

A critical limitation identified in robotic learning, particularly for articulated object manipulation, is the lack of diversity in learned policies or demonstrations. Recent research has highlighted that diversity in data, environments, and demonstrations can significantly improve robot performance, generalization capabilities, and robustness in real-world scenarios.

This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a method that generates a diverse set of high-quality, physically-grounded low-level trajectory primitives for manipulating articulated objects. It aims to overcome the challenges of data acquisition, ensure solution diversity, and manage the inherent complexity of articulated object tasks by leveraging Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms, specifically MAP-Elites, for trajectory generation. This represents an application of QD optimization to the specific domain of articulated object manipulation trajectories, moving beyond traditional focus on grasping. The use of compliant control is also incorporated to mitigate sim-to-real discrepancies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that enables a robot to autonomously manipulate a wide range of articulated objects by generating diverse and high-performing low-level trajectory primitives. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and addresses key challenges in robotic manipulation:

- **Addressing the Expert Data Acquisition Challenge:** Traditional methods for acquiring manipulation demonstrations are often resource-intensive (time-consuming, expensive) and prone to sim-to-real gaps. The motivation is to automatically generate physically grounded expert demonstrations in simulation, thereby reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection while ensuring the generated trajectories are robust enough for real-world deployment. The use of compliant control is integrated to absorb modeling errors and facilitate sim-to-real transfer.

- **Ensuring Diversity in Manipulation Solutions:** A central motivation is to generate not just one, but multiple distinct ways to achieve a given manipulation task. This redundancy and novelty are considered crucial for deploying robots in open-ended, dynamic environments where constraints and unexpected changes are common. Having a diverse repertoire of trajectories allows the robot to select the most appropriate solution based on real-time conditions or higher-level task requirements. This contrasts with traditional optimization methods that typically aim for a single optimal solution.

- **Managing the Complexity of Articulated Object Tasks:** Articulated objects exhibit diverse functionalities and contexts, leading to complex manipulation requirements. The approach addresses this by breaking down complex manipulation tasks into simpler sub-tasks, each involving the activation of a single degree of freedom (e.g., opening a hinge joint, extending a slider joint). This simplification allows for more focused exploration and generation of primitives for fundamental object interactions.

- **Leveraging Quality-Diversity Algorithms:** The paper is motivated to apply Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms, particularly MAP-Elites, to the problem of generating articulated object manipulation trajectories. QD algorithms are well-suited for discovering diverse repertoires of high-performing solutions by systematically exploring a behavioral space. The authors aim to demonstrate that this gradient-free learning strategy can effectively uncover multiple, sometimes non-intuitive, methods for manipulating the same object, which might be missed by other learning paradigms or human demonstrations.

In summary, the core objective is to develop "QDTraj," a plug-and-play methodology that, given an articulated object's URDF and an activation task, automatically produces a structured archive of diverse, high-performing, and adaptive robot trajectories for manipulating articulated objects, validated through simulation and real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The QDTraj method is designed to explore diverse trajectory primitives for activating a specific articulation on an articulated object, assuming the availability of the object's URDF (Unified Robot Description Format). The methodology integrates concepts from Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms, specifically MAP-Elites, with an adaptive interaction policy.

**A. Movement Primitive Representation**

- **Action Primitive:** Each robot activation task is defined by the specific articulated joint it aims to activate. The system focuses on single-degree-of-freedom joints, such as prismatic (slider) or revolute (hinge) joints. An activation task is formally represented as Γ = (object, articulation+index, s_init, s_target), where `object` is the articulated object, `articulation+index` specifies the joint type and identifier, and `s_init`, `s_target` denote the initial and target configuration values for that articulation. The method concentrates on prehensile manipulation involving contact-rich interactions.
- **Trajectory Primitive:** A trajectory primitive, TΓ, is defined as a sequence of robot end-effector poses, expressed in the object's reference frame, from an initial grasp to the completion of the task. Formally, TΓ = {F_ee/object(k)} from k=0 to N, where F_ee/object(k) is the end-effector pose at step k.

**B. Learning Algorithm (MAP-Elites-based)**

QDTraj utilizes a MAP-Elites algorithm to generate a diverse set of high-scoring trajectory primitives. The output is a structured archive of solutions.

- **Genotype Representation:** Each candidate trajectory primitive is initiated by a grasp candidate, which is parameterized by the robot's end-effector pose in the object's reference frame. The genotype `g_i` of an individual `i` is a 7-dimensional vector: (x_i, y_i, z_i, q_1i, q_2i, q_3i, q_4i), representing the Cartesian position and unit quaternion orientation of the end-effector.
- **Initialization:** An initial population of `N` end-effector positions (grasp candidates) is generated around the target object.
- **Selection-Mutation Loop (Iterative Process):**
 1. **Parallelized Evaluation:** Each genotype `g_i` (grasp candidate) is evaluated in parallel across `N` simulation environments. During evaluation, an adaptive prehensile trajectory `T_i` is generated using the adaptive interaction policy (described below). A fitness score `f_i` is then assigned to each trajectory, quantifying the percentage of articulation activation achieved before detachment.
 2. **MAP-Elites Behavioral Descriptors:** Individuals are stored in a MAP-Elites archive `A` based on their behavioral descriptor. The behavioral descriptor `b(g_i)` is defined as the three-dimensional Cartesian starting position of the genotype (b_xyz), rounded to the nearest hundredth of a centimeter. The archive is conceptualized as a 3D grid, where each cell stores the individual with the highest fitness score for that specific behavioral descriptor.
 3. **Selection:** In each generation, a new population is formed by selecting individuals. Half of the genotypes are chosen from the highest-performing cells in the MAP-Elites archive, and the other half are selected randomly. This strategy, termed "MAP-Elites Explore," encourages both exploitation of successful solutions and exploration of new areas.
 4. **Mutation:** Each selected individual from the new population undergoes mutation to create offspring. This involves adding bounded noise to both the Cartesian coordinates and the quaternion orientation of the starting poses, promoting exploration of the genotype space.

This loop iterates for a predefined number of `n_generations`, progressively populating and refining the archive `A` with diverse and high-performing trajectory primitives.

**C. Adaptive Interaction Policy (Evaluation Function)**

The core of QDTraj's evaluation function is an adaptive interaction policy that generates a prehensile trajectory for a given joint activation task and a candidate starting point.

- **Inverse Kinematics and Grasping:** For a given starting point `g_i`, the corresponding robot joint configuration is computed via inverse kinematics. The two-finger end-effector then closes, aiming for force closure.
- **Contact Detection:** A contact evaluation function determines if the gripper's utility surfaces are in contact with the target object part, signifying a successful grasp.
- **Compliant Control Mode:** Upon successful grasping, the robot transitions to compliant control. Robot joint gains are set low to reduce stiffness and damping, allowing the robot to respond softly to external forces and absorb modeling errors. Simultaneously, the gains of the object's joint of interest are set high, ensuring precise control over its motion.
- **Virtual Object Joint Actuation:** The object's joint is then virtually actuated from `s_init` to `s_target` through intermediate target values. During this actuation, the QDTraj contact function continuously monitors the interaction between the end-effector and the object.
- **Trajectory Recording and Fitness Assignment:** At the end of the object actuation, the full end-effector trajectory is recorded. The fitness score `f_i` for the trajectory is calculated as the percentage of the articulation's target value achieved before any detachment between the robot and the object occurs. This indicates the quality of the trajectory.

**D. Experimental Setup and Baselines**

- **Robot Hardware & Simulation:** Experiments utilize a Franka Emika FR3 robotic arm with a two-finger gripper. Simulations are conducted using the Genesis physics engine, leveraging 10,000 parallelized environments on a GPU.
- **Objects:** A custom experimental articulated box (with accurate CAD and URDF models) is used, alongside objects from the PartNet-Mobility dataset for generalization studies.
- **Baselines for Comparison:**
 - **Interaction Policy Baselines:** QDTraj's adaptive interaction policy (Adaptive-AS) is compared against two literature-based action spaces: Where2Act-AS (six hard-coded Cartesian motion primitives) and VAT-Mart-AS (stochastic step-by-step trajectories generated by sampling residual gripper poses).
 - **Grasp Strategy Ablation:** The "MAP-Elites Explore" selection strategy is compared with "MAP-Elites Success" (no cloning of successful individuals) and "Random Search" (random sampling without selection or mutation), serving as a lower-bound baseline.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Experiments involved 180 runs for a controlled comparison on the experimental object (3 grasp strategies x 3 interaction strategies x 2 tasks x 10 seeds). Generalization was assessed on 30 action primitives across 10 PartNet-Mobility objects over 50 generations.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Trajectory Success:** Number of trajectories in the archive achieving >= 65% of the joint activation requirement.
 - **Grasp Success:** Number of successful grasps found in the archive (reflects spatial diversity of initial grasp points).
 - **Real Robot Deployment:** Successful trajectories are deployed using ROS2 Cartesian controller with impedance control for sim-to-real transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative results demonstrating the performance and generalization capabilities of QDTraj.

**A. Qualitative Results and Analysis:**

- **Diversity in Trajectories:** Figure 6 illustrates the spatial diversity captured by QDTraj. The generated archives show that successful trajectories occupy distinct grid cells across the behavioral space, indicating that solutions are not clustered. This suggests the method successfully explores various regions, uncovering diverse approaches to the task that might not be found by gradient-based methods.
- **Non-Intuitive Solutions:** For opening tasks, trajectory variations are concentrated around the handle. For closing tasks, diversity increases as grasping is not limited to the handle region. The algorithm generates non-intuitive trajectories, such as closing a door without using the handle, highlighting its exploratory capabilities beyond human-demonstrated or classical approaches.
- **Real-World Deployment and Adaptability:** A crucial finding is the successful deployment of generated trajectories on a real robot, even for non-intuitive paths (e.g., grasping the door above the handle when the handle is unreachable). This validates the sim-to-real transfer and the practical benefit of having a diverse archive, allowing the robot to select a reachable and effective trajectory in constrained real-world scenarios.
- **Task-Specific Adaptation:** Trajectories differ for the same articulation based on the initial and final joint values, confirming that not all trajectories are reversible. This supports the physically grounded activation task classification and the method's ability to adapt to specific task contexts.
- **Generalization to Object Geometry:** QDTraj demonstrates adaptation to object geometry, exemplified by faucet manipulation where grasps further from the rotation axis result in larger rotation radii. The method's plug-and-play nature allows it to generate physically-grounded diversity automatically without explicit priors.
- **Performance Across Object Types:** Qualitative observations on PartNet-Mobility objects confirm the method's generalization, with effective adaptation, particularly for hinge joint trajectory generation.

**B. Quantitative Results and Analysis:**

- **Superior Trajectory Success:** Table I shows that "MAP-Elite-Explore" consistently yields the highest number of successful trajectories. It generates approximately 5 times more successful trajectories than "MAP-Elite-Success" for hinge tasks and 6 times more for slider tasks. This indicates that the strategy of cloning and evolving successful individuals significantly enhances exploration.
- **Effectiveness of Adaptive Interaction Policy:** The proposed "Adaptive-AS" (Adaptive Interaction policy Action Space) generates an average of 2.3 times more successful trajectories compared to baseline interaction policies (Where2Act-AS and VAT-Mart-AS), across all grasp selection strategies (column mean).
- **Combined Method Performance:** The combination of "QDTraj-MAP-Elite-Explore-Adaptive-AS" significantly outperforms all other configurations.
 - For hinge joints (Γ1), it generates 2,944 successful trajectories, a substantial increase from the 27 trajectories found by the least effective baseline. It improves the number of discovered trajectories by 3.5 times compared to other action spaces when combined with MAP-Elite-Explore.
 - For slider joints (Γ2), it generates 3,988 successful trajectories, compared to 532 for the least effective baseline. The improvement factor over other action spaces is 1.6 times.
- **Task Complexity and Adaptive-AS Advantage:** The greater advantage of Adaptive-AS for hinge tasks (3.5x improvement) compared to slider tasks (1.6x improvement) is attributed to the inherent complexity of hinge movements. Hinge tasks benefit more from the method's adaptability in generating richer, diverse trajectories that align with rotational constraints, whereas simpler parameterized motion primitives can be adequate for slider tasks.
- **Correlation of Grasps and Trajectories:** A strong correlation is observed between the number of discovered successful grasps and the number of successful trajectories. However, a high number of grasps does not automatically guarantee a proportionally large number of trajectories, as trajectory diversity also depends on the optimization and generation methods.
- **Generalization on PartNet-Mobility:** Across 30 action primitives on 10 PartNet-Mobility objects (Figure 7, right), QDTraj generated an average of 704 trajectories per object after 50 generations (1,006 for hinge joints and 445 for slider joints), and an average of 645 successful grasps. This indicates robust generalization across varied object types, with particular effectiveness for hinge joints.
- **Archive Filling Dynamics:** Figure 7 (left) shows that the "Adaptive-AS" achieves faster performance in filling the archive with successful trajectories from the very first generation, validating its efficiency. The initial rapid increase in successful trajectories, followed by a smoother slowdown, suggests potential for online Quality-Diversity applications if sufficient parallelization is available.

**C. Limitations:**

- The method requires accurate physical models and inertia parameters, which were available for the experimental object but may not always be for complex datasets like PartNet-Mobility or reconstructed scans.
- The simulator occasionally produces false contacts, which can create spurious clusters in the results. These instances are rare and can potentially be minimized by fine-tuning simulator parameters such as spatial discretization, integrator time step, and contact stiffness/friction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The QDTraj method represents an advancement in robotic manipulation by addressing several critical challenges associated with operating in open-ended, unstructured environments.

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** By automatically generating a diverse repertoire of physically-grounded, high-performing, and adaptive low-level trajectory primitives, QDTraj contributes to increased robot autonomy. This capability enables robots to perform complex manipulation tasks on articulated objects without extensive manual programming or costly data acquisition processes.
- **Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** The generation of diverse solutions for the same task is a key innovation. This allows a robot to select the most suitable trajectory in real-time, adapting to unexpected changes, live constraints, or partial occlusions in its environment. Such adaptability improves the robustness and reliability of robot operations in real-world scenarios where conditions are rarely ideal or static.
- **Generalization Across Object Categories:** The demonstrated ability of QDTraj to generalize across different articulated objects from the PartNet-Mobility dataset, including various types of hinge and slider joints, indicates its potential for broad applicability. This reduces the need for object-specific training or policy design, making the system more versatile for a wide array of household or industrial objects.
- **Efficiency in Data Generation:** By leveraging parallelized simulation and Quality-Diversity algorithms, the method offers an efficient means of generating a large volume of high-quality expert demonstrations. This addresses the bottleneck of data acquisition in robot learning, facilitating the development and deployment of more capable manipulation systems.
- **Plug-and-Play Methodology:** The "plug-and-play" nature of QDTraj, where it can take an object's URDF and an activation task as input to generate primitives, simplifies the integration of new objects or tasks into a robot's capabilities.
- **Foundation for Hierarchical Control:** The work lays a foundation for future hierarchical robotic control systems. The authors suggest that coupling QDTraj with a high-level task sequencer that orchestrates these diverse low-level primitives could enable fully autonomous and intelligent robotic systems capable of more complex, multi-step manipulation tasks.
- **Exploration of Non-Intuitive Solutions:** The method's ability to discover non-intuitive, yet effective, manipulation trajectories highlights the power of Quality-Diversity algorithms in exploring solution spaces beyond human biases or conventional approaches. These novel solutions could lead to more efficient or robust manipulation strategies in challenging scenarios.

In summary, QDTraj's contribution lies in providing a principled, automated, and scalable approach to equipping robots with a rich library of manipulation skills for articulated objects, fostering greater adaptability and autonomy in diverse real-world applications.
