# Unveiling Causal Reasoning in Large Language Models: Reality or Mirage?

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from the National University of Defense Technology and collaborators assessed large language models' causal reasoning capabilities, revealing their reliance on memorized patterns rather than genuine understanding when evaluated on the novel CausalProbe-2024 benchmark. The proposed G2-Reasoner, which integrates external general knowledge and goal-oriented prompts, improved performance on these fresh causal tasks.

## Problem

- Determining whether large language models exhibit genuine causal reasoning or merely sophisticated pattern matching based on training data.
- The inadequacy of existing causal reasoning benchmarks due to potential data contamination, hindering accurate evaluation of LLMs' generalization abilities.
- Understanding the theoretical limitations of autoregressive transformer-based LLMs in performing true causal inference.

## Method

- Differentiating between ==Level-1 (retrieval-based)== and ==Level-2 (deductive, genuine)== causal reasoning to categorize LLM capabilities.
- Constructing ==CausalProbe-2024==, a novel causal reasoning benchmark utilizing news articles published after LLM training cut-offs, to mitigate data contamination.
- Designing ==G2-Reasoner==, a method that combines retrieved external general knowledge with ==goal-oriented prompting== to guide LLMs toward more robust causal inference.

## Results

- Large language models experienced a substantial decline in exact match accuracy on the novel CausalProbe-2024 benchmark (e.g., Claude 3 Opus from **0.991** to **0.758** EM on CausalProbe-E), confirming their struggle with unseen causal reasoning tasks.
- G2-Reasoner consistently improved LLM performance on causal reasoning, including on challenging fresh and counterfactual scenarios (e.g., LLaMA 2 7B improved on CausalProbe-H from **0.565** to **0.582** EM), surpassing baseline prompting and RAG-only approaches.
- The goal-oriented prompting component of G2-Reasoner proved crucial, as retrieval-augmented generation alone did not consistently lead to performance gains for causal inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models predominantly perform Level-1 causal reasoning by retrieving patterns from training data, rather than deep Level-2 deduction.
> - The autoregressive nature of LLMs inherently limits their capacity for genuine causal inference, as sequential dependencies do not directly equate to logical causality.
> - Effective integration of external knowledge for causal reasoning requires specific goal-oriented prompting to guide the LLM's inference process.
