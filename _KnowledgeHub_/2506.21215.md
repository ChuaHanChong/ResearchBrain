# Unveiling Causal Reasoning in Large Language Models: Reality or Mirage?

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from the National University of Defense Technology and collaborators assessed large language models' causal reasoning capabilities, revealing their reliance on memorized patterns rather than genuine understanding when evaluated on the novel CausalProbe-2024 benchmark. The proposed G2-Reasoner, which integrates external general knowledge and goal-oriented prompts, improved performance on these fresh causal tasks.

## Problem

- Determining whether large language models exhibit genuine causal reasoning or merely sophisticated pattern matching based on training data.
- The inadequacy of existing causal reasoning benchmarks due to potential data contamination, hindering accurate evaluation of LLMs' generalization abilities.
- Understanding the theoretical limitations of autoregressive transformer-based LLMs in performing true causal inference.

## Method

- Differentiating between ==Level-1 (retrieval-based)== and ==Level-2 (deductive, genuine)== causal reasoning to categorize LLM capabilities.
- Constructing ==CausalProbe-2024==, a novel causal reasoning benchmark utilizing news articles published after LLM training cut-offs, to mitigate data contamination.
- Designing ==G2-Reasoner==, a method that combines retrieved external general knowledge with ==goal-oriented prompting== to guide LLMs toward more robust causal inference.

## Results

- Large language models experienced a substantial decline in exact match accuracy on the novel CausalProbe-2024 benchmark (e.g., Claude 3 Opus from **0.991** to **0.758** EM on CausalProbe-E), confirming their struggle with unseen causal reasoning tasks.
- G2-Reasoner consistently improved LLM performance on causal reasoning, including on challenging fresh and counterfactual scenarios (e.g., LLaMA 2 7B improved on CausalProbe-H from **0.565** to **0.582** EM), surpassing baseline prompting and RAG-only approaches.
- The goal-oriented prompting component of G2-Reasoner proved crucial, as retrieval-augmented generation alone did not consistently lead to performance gains for causal inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models predominantly perform Level-1 causal reasoning by retrieving patterns from training data, rather than deep Level-2 deduction.
> - The autoregressive nature of LLMs inherently limits their capacity for genuine causal inference, as sequential dependencies do not directly equate to logical causality.
> - Effective integration of external knowledge for causal reasoning requires specific goal-oriented prompting to guide the LLM's inference process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the forefront of the ongoing and critical debate regarding the true capabilities of large language models (LLMs). While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable emergent abilities across various tasks—including instruction following, in-context learning, and certain forms of reasoning—there is a growing skepticism about whether these abilities reflect genuine understanding or merely sophisticated pattern matching and statistical associations learned from vast datasets. This work specifically targets *causal reasoning*, a fundamental aspect of human intelligence that involves understanding cause-and-effect relationships, distinguishing it from mere correlation, and engaging in counterfactual thinking.

Previous research has explored LLMs' general reasoning capabilities, including logical, mathematical, and commonsense reasoning. Some studies have indicated LLMs' apparent proficiency in causal tasks, sometimes providing seemingly accurate cause-and-effect responses. However, as the authors note, these observations have often been qualitative or based on benchmarks that might suffer from data contamination (i.e., the LLMs having seen similar or identical examples during their pre-training). The question of whether LLMs possess "genuine causal reasoning capability or only a 'mirage'" lies at the heart of this paper.

The paper argues against the notion of genuine causal reasoning in current LLMs, positing that their apparent abilities are primarily a result of *Level-1 causal reasoning*, which involves retrieving embedded causal knowledge from their training corpora. This contrasts with *Level-2 causal reasoning*, defined as sophisticated, human-like deduction of new or unseen causal knowledge, including counterfactual scenarios. This distinction aligns with broader discussions in cognitive science, such as Daniel Kahneman's "Thinking, Fast and Slow," differentiating intuitive, pattern-based thinking from more deliberate, analytical reasoning.

By introducing a new, "fresh" benchmark (CausalProbe-2024) whose content is explicitly designed to be outside the training data cut-off of current LLMs, the authors aim to circumvent the data contamination issue that has plagued earlier evaluations. This methodological rigor allows for a more definitive assessment of whether LLMs can generalize causal reasoning to truly novel contexts. Furthermore, the proposed solution, G2-Reasoner, attempts to bridge the identified gap by integrating external knowledge and goal-oriented prompting, drawing inspiration from how humans leverage general knowledge and specific objectives during reasoning. This places the work within the growing field of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and advanced prompting techniques, but with a specific focus on enhancing a core cognitive function rather than just improving task performance. The paper thus contributes to both the evaluation of LLM limitations and the development of strategies to overcome them, pushing the boundaries towards stronger artificial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to rigorously investigate the extent to which large language models (LLMs) possess genuine causal reasoning capabilities, moving beyond anecdotal observations or performance on potentially "seen" data. The authors distill this into a pointed question: "Does this reflect LLMs’ genuine causal reasoning capability or only a 'mirage'?"

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Challenging the Notion of "Genuine" Reasoning:** Despite impressive LLM performance on various reasoning tasks, the authors are motivated by the need to critically assess if this performance stems from deep, human-like understanding or from a more superficial ability to mimic patterns. Causal reasoning, being an advanced cognitive function involving discerning cause-and-effect and counterfactuals, serves as an ideal testbed for this challenge.
2. **Addressing Data Contamination in Benchmarking:** Existing causal reasoning benchmarks (e.g., COPA, e-CARE) are acknowledged for their contributions but are criticized for potentially having corpora that were included in LLMs' pre-training data. This makes it difficult to ascertain if LLMs are truly reasoning or merely recalling memorized causal statements. A key motivation is to create a benchmark (CausalProbe-2024) with fresh, unseen data to provide a more accurate evaluation.
3. **Understanding LLM Mechanisms:** The authors are motivated to delve into the fundamental mechanisms of transformer-based LLMs, specifically their autoregressive nature, to understand *why* they might struggle with genuine causal reasoning. This methodological analysis aims to provide a theoretical underpinning for observed empirical limitations.
4. **Bridging the Gap Towards Strong AI:** Recognizing the critical role of causal reasoning in advancing LLMs towards strong artificial intelligence, the ultimate motivation is to identify the limitations and then propose concrete methods to enhance LLMs' capabilities beyond simple pattern retrieval. By drawing inspiration from human cognition, they aim to guide the development of LLM architectures or prompting strategies that foster deeper causal understanding.
5. **Differentiating Levels of Causal Reasoning:** The paper is motivated to formalize a distinction between "Level-1" (retrieval-based, shallow) and "Level-2" (deductive, genuine) causal reasoning. This categorization provides a clearer framework for evaluating and discussing LLM capabilities and identifying the specific areas where improvement is needed.

In essence, the paper aims to provide both diagnostic tools (CausalProbe-2024 and autoregression analysis) to accurately assess the current state of LLM causal reasoning and a prescriptive solution (G2-Reasoner) to guide future research towards achieving more robust and human-like causal intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-pronged methodology, combining a theoretical analysis of LLM mechanisms with empirical evaluation and the development of a novel reasoning framework.

#### 3.1. Conceptual Framework: Levels of Causal Reasoning

The foundation of the paper's argument rests on a distinction between two levels of causal reasoning, inspired by cognitive science insights (e.g., "Thinking, Fast and Slow" by Kahneman).

- **Level-1 Causal Reasoning:** This refers to the ability to retrieve and apply causal knowledge that is explicitly or implicitly embedded in the LLM's parameters, primarily derived from its vast training corpora. This process is characterized as "fast" and effective for common, well-documented cause-and-effect relationships.
- **Level-2 Causal Reasoning:** This denotes a more sophisticated form of reasoning where an LLM can deduce new or unseen causal knowledge, leveraging internal parametric knowledge and context through advanced reasoning mechanisms. This process is typically "slower" and capable of handling novel, complex, or counterfactual scenarios.

The paper's hypothesis is that current LLMs primarily exhibit Level-1 reasoning, lacking true Level-2 capabilities.

#### 3.2. Methodological Critique: Autoregressive LLMs and Causality

The authors delve into the inherent limitations of transformer-based LLMs from a methodological perspective. They argue that the **autoregressive mechanism**, which drives next-token prediction, is not inherently causal. While autoregressive models assume that the current value depends on past values, this sequential dependency in text does not necessarily equate to a logical causal relationship. They illustrate this with an example where the sequential order of concepts in a sentence (e.g., "rain" -> "school closure" -> "cannot go to school" -> "learn at home") does not perfectly align with the logical causality.

Using **Structural Causal Models (SCMs)**, the paper formalizes textual causal reasoning. They depict a causal graph (Figure 4) where a common cause variable `C` (world knowledge) influences a cause semantic variable `X` and an effect semantic variable `Y`. Natural language `T` is then a conditioned collider, a function of `X`, `Y`, and an exogenous variable `ϵ` (textual variability). This formalization highlights that while natural language `T` contains valuable information about causal relationships, the autoregressive training primarily optimizes for token prediction based on sequence patterns, making LLMs adept at memorizing common causal expressions (Level-1) but poor at comprehending genuine causal knowledge in unfamiliar or complex texts (Level-2).

#### 3.3. Empirical Validation: CausalProbe-2024 Benchmark

To empirically validate their hypothesis, the authors introduce a novel causal question-answering (Q&A) benchmark called **CausalProbe-2024**. The core innovation of this benchmark is its **freshness**: its corpora were collected from reputable news sources (BBC and The Guardian) between January 1, 2024, and April 29, 2024, which is *later* than the training data cut-off times for all evaluated LLMs (LLaMA 2 7B, LLaMA 3 8B, GPT 3.5 turbo, Claude 3 Opus). This design aims to ensure that LLMs cannot rely on memorized knowledge.

CausalProbe-2024 consists of three progressively challenging datasets:
- **CausalProbe-E (Easy):** Assesses genuine causal reasoning on novel problems with straightforward cause-effect relationships.
- **CausalProbe-H (Hard):** Probes LLMs' ability to distinguish misleading or false causal relationships in new tasks, involving counterfactual elements.
- **CausalProbe-M (Multiple-choice):** Features questions with an uncertain number of correct answers (1 to 4), requiring LLMs to discern the correctness of each causal proposition rather than guessing.

The benchmark also includes explicit **contexts** for each question, a feature the authors argue is often lacking in earlier benchmarks and is crucial for realistic causal reasoning. To further confirm the freshness, the authors employ **Min-K% Prob**, a membership inference attack method, which quantitatively shows CausalProbe-2024's corpora are less likely to be part of the LLM training data compared to older benchmarks like COPA, e-CARE, and CausalNet.

#### 3.4. Proposed Solution: G2-Reasoner

To address the identified limitations and move towards Level-2 causal reasoning, the authors propose **G2-Reasoner** (General-Knowledge-Assisted and Goal-Driven Reasoner). This framework draws inspiration from human reasoning, which typically involves leveraging general knowledge and pursuing specific goals.

G2-Reasoner integrates two main components:
1. **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** This module incorporates external general knowledge into the LLM's reasoning process. A small (∼16 MB) general knowledge Q&A dataset is used to construct a vector database. When a causal reasoning question is posed, relevant general knowledge is retrieved from this database using a retriever (Meta's Contriever and Faiss for vector storage). This external knowledge acts as the "P_C" (general knowledge base) in their formalization, helping to account for confounding variables in causal inference.
2. **Goal-Oriented Prompts:** To counteract the tendency of autoregressive LLMs to lose coherence or deviate from the task, a specialized prompt is designed. This prompt explicitly instructs the LLM to act as an "intelligent causal reasoner," guiding it to "carefully analyz[e] the available information and logically infer[r] the most probable causal relationship during reasoning," with "related general knowledge" as a reference. This aims to steer the LLM towards a targeted, deductive reasoning process.

Experiments with G2-Reasoner involve comparing its performance against baseline methods such as vanilla zero-shot inference, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, and RAG alone on both the CausalProbe-2024 and older benchmarks, using exact match (EM) as the primary metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings that collectively support the authors' hypothesis and demonstrate the potential of their proposed solution.

#### 4.1. Limitations of Autoregressive LLMs

The theoretical analysis strongly suggests that the **autoregressive nature of transformer-based LLMs is not inherently designed for genuine causal reasoning**. While effective for next-token prediction and pattern recognition, this mechanism primarily enables LLMs to memorize and reproduce causal knowledge observed in their training data (Level-1 reasoning). It struggles to infer novel or abstract causal relationships, especially when sequential causality in text diverges from logical causality.

#### 4.2. Performance on CausalProbe-2024

Empirically, the most striking finding is the **significant performance drop** exhibited by all evaluated LLMs (LLaMA 2 7B, LLaMA 3 8B, GPT 3.5 turbo, Claude 3 Opus) on the novel **CausalProbe-2024 benchmark** compared to earlier, potentially "seen" benchmarks (COPA, e-CARE, CausalNet).

- On older benchmarks like COPA, state-of-the-art models like Claude 3 Opus achieved near-perfect scores (0.991 EM), and even LLaMA 2 7B performed reasonably well (0.752 EM).
- However, on CausalProbe-E, Claude 3 Opus's performance dropped to 0.758 EM, and LLaMA 2 7B to 0.616 EM.
- The performance decline was even more pronounced on CausalProbe-H, where Claude 3 Opus scored only 0.692 EM, and LLaMA 2 7B achieved a mere 0.565 EM.
- The Min-K% Prob analysis confirmed that CausalProbe-2024's corpora were indeed significantly "fresher" for the LLMs, reinforcing that the performance drop was due to the novelty of the causal tasks rather than other factors.

This substantial decrease in accuracy on unseen causal contexts provides strong evidence that LLMs primarily rely on Level-1 causal reasoning—retrieving embedded knowledge—and largely lack the capacity for genuine Level-2 human-like causal deduction.

Furthermore, on CausalProbe-M (multiple-choice with uncertain correct answers), LLMs showed a significant struggle with exact match, indicating difficulty in identifying *all* correct causal propositions. However, when evaluated with a "partial match" metric (allowing for missing correct options but penalizing incorrect ones), GPT and Claude achieved higher scores (around 75-85%). This suggests that while LLMs struggle with comprehensive causal understanding in complex novel scenarios, they are relatively good at avoiding *false positive* causal relationships.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of G2-Reasoner

The proposed **G2-Reasoner** demonstrated its ability to **significantly enhance LLMs' causal reasoning capabilities**, particularly on fresh and counterfactual contexts.

- Across most benchmarks, including the challenging CausalProbe-2024, G2-Reasoner generally **outperformed baseline methods** such as vanilla zero-shot inference, Chain-of-Thought (CoT), and RAG without goal-oriented prompting.
- For instance, on CausalProbe-H, G2-Reasoner improved LLaMA 2 7B from 0.565 to 0.582 EM, GPT 3.5 turbo from 0.671 to 0.693 EM, and Claude 3 Opus from 0.692 to 0.696 EM.
- The ablative analysis showed that RAG alone (without goal-oriented prompts) often did not match or even fell below the vanilla method's performance. This highlights the crucial role of the **goal-oriented prompt** in effectively steering LLMs to utilize the retrieved general knowledge for targeted causal reasoning, rather than simply having access to it.
- The authors note that the improvements, while consistent, were not "significantly" large, attributing this partly to the small scale of the general knowledge base used (16 MB). They suggest that a larger knowledge base (like Wikipedia) could yield more substantial gains, indicating a promising direction for future work.

In summary, the findings provide compelling evidence that current LLMs primarily function as "causal parrots," echoing patterns from their training data, rather than performing genuine causal inference. G2-Reasoner offers a validated first step towards equipping LLMs with a more robust, human-inspired approach to causal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries substantial significance for the field of artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the development and understanding of large language models.

1. **Reframing LLM Causal Reasoning Capabilities:** The paper offers a critical and empirically grounded perspective on LLM causal reasoning, moving beyond optimistic claims of "emergent abilities." By introducing the distinction between Level-1 (retrieval-based) and Level-2 (genuine, deductive) causal reasoning, the authors provide a more nuanced framework for evaluating LLM intelligence. The strong evidence that current LLMs predominantly operate at Level-1 suggests that their impressive causal outputs often represent sophisticated pattern matching rather than deep causal understanding. This insight is crucial for setting realistic expectations and guiding future research directions.

2. **Addressing Data Contamination in Benchmarking:** The creation of **CausalProbe-2024** is a significant contribution to the field of LLM evaluation. Its rigorous design, which ensures the novelty of its corpora relative to LLM training data cut-offs, establishes a new standard for assessing true generalization capabilities. This benchmark provides a reliable tool for researchers to test whether LLMs can genuinely reason about unseen causal events, rather than merely recalling information. This will help prevent inflated performance metrics due to data leakage and foster more accurate comparisons between models.

3. **Insights into Autoregressive Limitations:** The methodological analysis of the autoregressive mechanism provides a theoretical explanation for why LLMs struggle with genuine causality. Understanding that sequential correlation in text does not equate to logical causality is fundamental. This insight could influence the development of next-generation LLM architectures, potentially leading to models that incorporate explicit causal modules or different training objectives that go beyond next-token prediction.

4. **A Promising Path Towards Stronger AI (G2-Reasoner):** G2-Reasoner represents an important step towards bridging the gap between Level-1 and Level-2 causal reasoning. By demonstrating that integrating external general knowledge (via RAG) and goal-oriented prompts can enhance causal reasoning, especially in novel and counterfactual contexts, the authors provide a blueprint for future advancements. This approach, inspired by human cognitive processes, suggests that hybrid AI systems—combining LLM capabilities with explicit knowledge bases and reasoning guidance—might be necessary to achieve genuine causal intelligence. The finding that goal-oriented prompts are crucial (as RAG alone was less effective) highlights the importance of guiding the LLM's reasoning process.

5. **Potential for Real-World Impact:** Genuine causal reasoning is critical for many high-stakes AI applications, including scientific discovery, medical diagnosis, policy-making, and autonomous systems. If LLMs can be reliably equipped with Level-2 causal reasoning, they could move beyond being powerful assistants to becoming true collaborators that can understand complex systems, predict unforeseen consequences, and generate novel solutions. While G2-Reasoner is a first step, its success hints at a future where LLMs can contribute more profoundly to problem-solving in dynamic and unpredictable environments.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust critique of current LLM causal reasoning abilities, offers a critical new evaluation tool, and proposes a valuable initial strategy for fostering more genuine understanding. Its impact lies in shifting the discourse from mere performance to deeper cognitive capabilities, ultimately guiding the field towards building more intelligent and trustworthy AI systems.
