# MiCo: Multi-image Contrast for Reinforcement Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> MiCo (Multi-image Contrast) introduces a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enhance their Chain-of-Thought reasoning across multiple images without relying on human-annotated reasoning steps. The framework leverages inherent visual constraints from video frames and image editing datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multi-image reasoning benchmarks like VLM2-Bench and generalizing effectively to a range of other vision tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to robustly link fine-grained visual cues and perform complex logical reasoning when presented with multiple related images.
- Existing methods for improving VLM reasoning, especially those using reinforcement learning, heavily depend on prohibitively expensive and time-consuming human-annotated question-answer pairs with ground-truth reasoning steps.
- Current VLMs often lack the "meta-cognitive" ability to effectively compare and connect visual information across different views, leading to failures or hallucinations in multi-image tasks.

## Method

- ==MiCo== develops a ==self-supervised framework== that constructs challenging visual comparison tasks by selecting visually similar but meaningfully different image pairs from video frames and image editing datasets.
- It formulates training triplets using augmented views of these image pairs, implicitly supervising the VLM to identify subtle differences and similarities.
- An ==Augmented Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) strategy== is employed, where reasoning trajectories sampled from weakly augmented inputs are used to optimize the VLM's policy for stronger augmentations, improving generalization.

## Results

- MiCo-7B achieved state-of-the-art performance on the VLM2-Bench benchmark with an average accuracy of **61.06%**, outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by **+12.93%** and surpassing larger models like GPT-4o in multi-image comparison.
- The framework demonstrated strong performance on other multi-image benchmarks (MuirBench, BLINK) in correspondence and spatial relation tasks, and showed steady improvements across general vision benchmarks (MMStar, MMMU, HallusionBench, MathVista).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of CoT reinforcement learning, diverse data sources, the Augmented GRPO strategy, and varied prompt templates in achieving robust and generalized multi-image reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning, leveraging inherent visual constraints, can effectively instill complex multi-image Chain-of-Thought reasoning capabilities in VLMs without the need for explicit human labels.
> - The Augmented GRPO strategy successfully transfers high-quality CoT reasoning behaviors learned from simpler visual conditions (weak augmentations) to more challenging ones (strong augmentations), significantly improving model generalization.
> - Training VLMs on visual comparison skills through contrastive learning not only enhances multi-image reasoning but also generalizes to a broader range of general vision tasks, indicating the acquisition of a fundamental "meta-cognitive" visual skill.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Chen et al. directly addresses a critical and evolving challenge in the field of artificial intelligence: enabling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform sophisticated reasoning across multiple images. While VLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and interpreting single images, a significant limitation persists in their ability to robustly link fine-grained visual cues and execute complex logical reasoning when presented with a sequence or set of related images. This deficiency hinders their application in crucial real-world scenarios such as video analysis, 3D reconstruction from multi-view inputs, change detection, and event understanding.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have highlighted the power of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting combined with rule-based reinforcement learning (e.g., GRPO) for improving complex logical reasoning. This success has naturally led to efforts to extend CoT reasoning to the vision-language domain, with works like MM-Eureka, NoisyRollout, LVAA-Thinking, and ThinkLite. However, a common bottleneck in these approaches is their heavy reliance on meticulously curated question-answer (QA) pairs with ground-truth reasoning steps. Such manual annotation is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and particularly challenging to create for tasks involving subtle visual differences or complex multi-image logic.

MiCo distinguishes itself by circumventing this data annotation bottleneck. It draws inspiration from the paradigm of self-supervised visual representation learning, where images serve as their own source of supervision (e.g., contrastive learning methods like SimCLR and MoCo). By leveraging the inherent constraints and relationships within visual data, MiCo proposes a novel self-supervised framework to incentivize VLMs to develop multi-image reasoning capabilities without requiring explicit human-annotated QA pairs. This approach positions MiCo at the forefront of research into data-efficient and scalable methods for training reasoning-capable VLMs, pushing the boundaries of what these models can achieve in complex visual understanding tasks. It addresses the fundamental problem that while VLMs are excellent at single-image perception, they often lack the "meta-cognitive" ability to compare and connect information across images, which is essential for true multi-image intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the MiCo research is to empower Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning capabilities to effectively link and interpret visual cues across multiple images. The authors identify a significant gap in current VLM performance, despite their strong perceptual abilities on single images: a struggle to perform sophisticated logical reasoning and comparison when presented with several related visual inputs.

The primary motivations behind this work are multifaceted:
1. **Bridging the Multi-Image Reasoning Gap:** Many real-world applications (e.g., understanding actions in videos, inferring 3D structures from multiple views, detecting subtle changes in medical images or surveillance footage) inherently require processing and comparing information from multiple images. Current VLMs, as highlighted by the paper's pilot study, often fail or hallucinate when faced with such tasks, demonstrating a lack of robust visual comparison and inter-image reasoning.
2. **Addressing Data Annotation Challenges:** Traditional methods for enhancing reasoning in VLMs, particularly those involving reinforcement learning, typically demand extensive datasets of manually curated question-answer pairs with gold-standard reasoning processes. Creating these annotations for fine-grained visual tasks and complex multi-image scenarios is exceedingly difficult, costly, and often subjective. The authors are motivated to find a more scalable and cost-effective approach.
3. **Leveraging Inherent Visual Structure:** Inspired by the success of self-supervised learning in computer vision, the researchers hypothesize that images themselves contain sufficient inherent constraints and relationships that can serve as a potent form of supervision for learning reasoning, without explicit human labels. This insight drives the development of a self-supervised approach to elicit reasoning.
4. **Enhancing "Meta-Cognitive" Visual Skills:** The core hypothesis is that VLMs already possess strong foundational perception but lack the meta-cognitive ability to "compare and connect" visual information across different views. The objective is to specifically train and enhance this fundamental skill, believing it will generalize to a wider array of multi-image understanding tasks.

In essence, MiCo aims to develop a robust, scalable, and self-supervised method to instill advanced multi-image reasoning capabilities in VLMs, overcoming the limitations of current models and reducing the reliance on costly human annotation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MiCo (Multi-image Contrast) introduces a novel self-supervised framework to enable Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in VLMs for multi-image understanding, primarily by leveraging inherent visual constraints and a tailored reinforcement learning strategy.

#### 3.1. MiCo Framework Overview

The methodology revolves around constructing challenging visual comparison tasks without human annotations. The framework consists of three main steps:
1. **Contrastive Sample Construction:** Identifying and selecting image pairs that are visually similar but contain subtle, meaningful differences.
2. **Training Triplet Formulation:** Applying data augmentation to these pairs to create informative triplets, designed to compel the model to perform detailed comparisons.
3. **Augmented GRPO:** Employing a modified rule-based reinforcement learning strategy to optimize the VLM, encouraging the generation of structured reasoning trajectories.

#### 3.2. Contrastive Sample Construction

To obtain suitable training data, MiCo utilizes two primary data sources that naturally provide visually similar yet distinct images:
- **Video Frames:** Pairs of frames (I$_a$, I$_b$) are randomly sampled from the same video with a temporal gap of 2 seconds. A Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) score is computed, and pairs with SSIM greater than 0.95 are filtered out to ensure they are not nearly identical, but still closely related.
- **Image Editing Datasets:** "Before" and "after" images from datasets like OmniEdit are used as (I$_a$, I$_b$) pairs. Pixel-wise Mean Squared Error (MSE) is calculated, and pairs with excessively large differences (e.g., difference ratio exceeding 0.8 across pixels with value > 30) are discarded. This ensures subtle but perceptible changes.
These filtering criteria are crucial to create "hard negatives" – similar enough to require careful inspection, but distinct enough to necessitate reasoning.

#### 3.3. Training Triplet Formulation

Once contrastive image pairs are selected, they are used to form training triplets. Given a source image I, two augmented versions, T$_1$(I) and T$_2$(I), are generated via random cropping and resizing. For each (I$_a$, I$_b$) pair, a triplet is constructed as:
$T = \{T_1(I_a), T_2(I_a), T_3(I_b)\}$
Here, $T_1(I_a)$ and $T_2(I_a)$ are two augmented views of the same image $I_a$, while $T_3(I_b)$ is an augmented view of a *different but similar* image $I_b$. The implicit supervision is that $T_1(I_a)$ and $T_2(I_a)$ are "the same" (content-wise, despite augmentation), whereas $T_3(I_b)$ is "different" from $T_1(I_a)$ and $T_2(I_a)$.

#### 3.4. Question-Answer Formulation for Reinforcement Learning

For reinforcement learning, the model is prompted with a structured reasoning template and a user question:
- **Reasoning Prompt:** "First output the thinking process in <think> </think> and give the final answer in <answer> </answer> tags."
- **User Question:** "Regardless of the augmentation, are image1 and image2 the same? How about image2 and image3, image1 and image3? Only return T(True) or F(False) in <answer> </answer>, for example <think> </think> <answer>TFT</answer>."
To increase robustness and generalization, the authors also incorporate binary image-pair comparisons and utilize GPT-4o to generate diverse phrasings for the user questions, ensuring a balanced distribution of answer types.

#### 3.5. Augmented GRPO

MiCo introduces a novel reinforcement learning strategy called Augmented GRPO, which builds upon the Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework. The core idea is to sample reasoning trajectories under "weak augmentations" and then use these trajectories to optimize the policy for "stronger augmentations."
- **Standard GRPO:** Samples a group of outputs {o_i} from the old policy on a question `q` and computes rewards `R_i` to optimize the policy.
- **MiCo's Augmented GRPO:**
 1. Weakly augmented inputs (T$_w$) are used to construct a "weak prompt" (`q_w`).
 2. The VLM (policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$) generates a group of CoT responses {o_i} by performing rollouts on `q_w`. The assumption is that weak augmentations make it easier to produce valid and high-quality reasoning chains.
 3. Rewards `R_i` and advantages `A_i` are computed based on these rollouts from `q_w`.
 4. The policy $\pi_{\theta}$ is then optimized using these advantages on a "strong prompt" (`q_s`), which is constructed from the *strongly augmented inputs* (T$_s$).
This strategy effectively transfers reliable CoT behavior learned from easier visual conditions (weak augmentations) to more challenging ones (strong augmentations), thereby improving the model's generalization.

#### 3.6. Training Objective and Details

The training objective is a PPO-like formulation that encourages the policy to assign higher likelihoods to responses with higher relative rewards within each group, incorporating a KL regularization term. Rewards are based on both the binary format of the output and the accuracy of the final answer (1:1 weight). A correct reward for a triplet requires all three comparisons (image1 vs. image2, image2 vs. image3, image1 vs. image3) to be accurate.
MiCo is built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, trained for 600 iterations on 8x A100 GPUs, with a learning rate of 1e-6 and a batch size of 16, generating 8 rollouts per sample.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on MiCo demonstrate its effectiveness in enhancing multi-image reasoning capabilities in VLMs, alongside strong generalization to various vision tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Multi-Image Benchmarks

- **VLM2-Bench:** MiCo achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on the VLM2-Bench benchmark, which specifically evaluates the ability to compare and link fine-grained visual cues across multiple images. Notably, the MiCo-7B model surpassed larger, closed-source models like GPT-4o, achieving an average accuracy of 61.06%, an improvement of +12.93% over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline with CoT prompting. This highlights MiCo's superior ability in image comparison and relational reasoning.
- **Task-Specific Performance on VLM2-Bench:** While CoT reasoning generally boosted performance, it yielded limited or even negative gains for tasks involving human faces (Person track). The authors hypothesize that fine nuances of human identity might be difficult to verbalize for language-based reasoning. Conversely, object-level identity differences (e.g., logos, textures) benefited significantly from CoT, suggesting that these are more readily describable and thus better leveraged by language-guided reasoning.
- **MuirBench and BLINK:** MiCo also showed strong performance on other representative multi-image understanding benchmarks, MuirBench and BLINK. It demonstrated clear advantages in correspondence-style tasks (e.g., Visual Retrieval, Semantic Correspondence, Jigsaw, Functional Correspondence) and Spatial Relation tasks, outperforming other reasoning models. This indicates its strength in aligning multi-modal signals and understanding spatial relationships through comparative reasoning. However, it lagged behind on tasks like Scene Understanding and Forensic Detection (requiring domain-specific priors) and struggled with Relative Depth, suggesting areas for future improvement.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Other Vision Tasks

- **Overall Performance:** MiCo brought steady improvements over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline across a broader range of general vision benchmarks including MMStar, MMMU, HallusionBench, and MathVista. This suggests that the visual comparison skill learned in a self-supervised manner generalizes beyond the specific training task.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** MiCo showed notable gains in Instance Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, and Math on MMStar, implying enhanced multi-step inference capabilities. However, its performance on fine-grained perception tasks sometimes slightly decreased, indicating that while it excels at high-level reasoning, there might be a trade-off or room for improvement in low-level precise visual attribute understanding.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies rigorously validated the core design choices of MiCo:
- **Training Paradigm:** CoT reinforcement learning significantly outperformed supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and no-CoT reinforcement learning, underscoring the critical role of explicit reasoning chains.
- **Data Sources:** Combining diverse data sources like image editing datasets (OmniEdit) and video frames (Vidgen-1M) yielded the best results, demonstrating their complementary nature. The framework also showed robustness to variations in editing domains.
- **Rollout Augmentation:** The Augmented GRPO strategy (sampling with weak augmentations, optimizing with strong augmentations) proved superior, confirming its effectiveness in transferring high-quality reasoning from simpler to more challenging visual contexts. Strong augmentations were found vital for contrastive learning.
- **Sample Formulation:** A combination of image pair and image triplet formulations led to the best performance, ensuring comprehensive coverage of comparison scenarios.
- **Prompt Diversity:** Increasing the variety of prompt templates (up to 50 variations) was crucial for preventing overfitting and enhancing generalization.
- **Image Augmentations:** Random cropping and resizing were empirically identified as the most effective augmentations, with other techniques like flipping, rotation, or color augmentation not consistently improving performance.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples demonstrate MiCo's ability to generate detailed, step-by-step reasoning chains. For questions requiring object comparison, counting, jigsaw solving, IQ-style pattern recognition, or functional correspondence, the model actively analyzes fine-grained differences, similarities, and spatial relationships across images before deriving the final answer. This illustrates that MiCo performs systematic, multi-step reasoning grounded in visual understanding, rather than superficial matching.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MiCo framework represents a significant step forward in developing more capable and data-efficient Vision-Language Models for multi-image reasoning.

#### 5.1. Significance

1. **Reduced Data Annotation Burden:** MiCo's most significant contribution is its ability to learn robust multi-image reasoning *without relying on human-annotated question-answer pairs*. By leveraging inherent visual constraints and self-supervision, it addresses a major bottleneck in VLM development, which traditionally requires expensive and labor-intensive data curation for complex reasoning tasks.
2. **Enhanced Multi-Image Reasoning Prowess:** The proposed contrastive learning paradigm and the Augmented GRPO strategy effectively teach VLMs to link fine-grained visual cues across images. This leads to state-of-the-art performance on challenging multi-image benchmarks, surpassing even larger models, and significantly closing the gap towards human-level performance in specific visual comparison tasks.
3. **Generalization of Reasoning Abilities:** The learned ability to compare and contrast images generalizes effectively to a wide range of other vision tasks, including both multi-image and some single-image understanding scenarios. This indicates that MiCo instills a fundamental "meta-cognitive" visual skill rather than just task-specific knowledge, making VLMs more versatile.
4. **Novel Reinforcement Learning Strategy:** Augmented GRPO, which samples high-quality CoT trajectories from weakly augmented examples and uses them to optimize the model on strongly augmented contexts, offers a novel and effective technique for improving generalization in VLM fine-tuning, potentially applicable beyond this specific problem.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Democratization of AI:** By reducing the need for costly human annotations, MiCo can make advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible for development in various domains, including those with limited resources or for underrepresented languages and visual contexts where curated datasets are scarce.
- **Advancements in Real-World Applications:** Improved multi-image reasoning has direct implications for a host of real-world applications, including:
 - **Video Understanding:** More accurate action recognition, event detection, and temporal reasoning.
 - **3D Vision:** Enhanced 3D reconstruction and understanding from multi-view images.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Better detection of subtle changes or anomalies across sequential scans.
 - **Surveillance and Monitoring:** Improved anomaly detection and tracking across different camera feeds or timeframes.
 - **Robotics:** More robust object manipulation and scene understanding from diverse sensory inputs.
- **Future Research Directions:** MiCo opens up new avenues for research into combining self-supervised learning with reinforcement learning for multi-modal models. It also highlights the need to explore integrating more structured priors for highly specialized tasks (e.g., face verification, visual mathematics, precise spatial understanding) where current self-supervised signals might be insufficient.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Social Impact (as per Appendix D)

While transformative, the authors acknowledge limitations. MiCo shows limited effectiveness on highly specialized tasks such as face verification, visual math, and certain spatial understanding challenges, which might require domain-specific knowledge or structured representations not explicitly captured by its current training paradigm. Future work plans to explore more efficient data construction strategies tailored to these domains.

From a social impact perspective, MiCo's ability to perform fine-grained comparisons across images, while beneficial, also carries risks. This enhanced capability could be misused in privacy-invading scenarios like surveillance, misinformation generation, or unauthorized inference of sensitive information. The authors advocate for responsible AI deployment, emphasizing transparency, user consent, clear boundaries in application domains, robust sensitive content filtering, and human-in-the-loop mechanisms for critical decision-making processes.
