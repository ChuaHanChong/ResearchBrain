# R2BC: Multi-Agent Imitation Learning from Single-Agent Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC) was developed by researchers at the University of Utah and DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory to enable multi-agent imitation learning from single-human, one-agent-at-a-time demonstrations, overcoming the challenges of joint-action expert data. This method matched or exceeded the performance of "super-human" oracle baselines in simulated cooperative tasks and reliably outperformed centralized behavior cloning on physical robots.

## Problem

- Traditional Multi-Agent Imitation Learning (MAIL) often assumes access to coordinated, synchronous "joint-action demonstrations" where an expert controls all agents simultaneously.
- Providing such synchronized joint-action demonstrations is impractical for human operators due to high cognitive burden, physical limitations, and underactuated control interfaces.
- Many existing MAIL methods rely on pre-trained reinforcement learning policies or planners to generate expert data, limiting their applicability to novel, "in-the-wild" tasks.

## Method

- The paper proposes Round-Robin ==Behavior Cloning== (**R2BC**), an online imitation learning algorithm designed for sequential, single-agent demonstrations.
- A single human demonstrator cycles through agents, teleoperating one agent at a time to provide demonstrations, while all other agents execute their current learned policies.
- Individual agent policies are decentralized and updated online using standard Behavior Cloning (BC) loss on their respective demonstration datasets, collected during these interactive sessions.

## Results

- In simulated cooperative tasks, R2BC consistently matched or surpassed the performance of oracle Joint Behavior Cloning (JBC) and online imitation learning baselines (DAgger, DART) which relied on "super-human" joint-action demonstrations.
- On physical robot deployment, R2BC reliably outperformed centralized behavior cloning, achieving **3.25** to **5.9** times better performance across Navigation and Block Pusher tasks when trained with human demonstrations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the online learning paradigm and decentralized policy structure were critical for R2BC's robust performance, with offline or centralized variants showing reduced efficacy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The online interaction, where non-demonstrating agents act autonomously, exposes the human demonstrator to varied states, including suboptimal teammate behaviors, allowing for the teaching of robust corrective and cooperative actions.
> - Online policy updates during demonstration collection are essential for effective multi-agent learning, promoting iterative improvement and resilience to distribution shifts.
> - Fully decentralized policy execution and demonstration collection can yield performance parity or even superiority compared to centralized approaches, enabling more practical deployment in real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Imitation Learning (IL), specifically focusing on its application to multi-agent systems (MAS), commonly referred to as Multi-Agent Imitation Learning (MAIL). While IL has demonstrated success in single-robot contexts, its extension to MAS presents challenges related to cooperation, coordination, and partial observability, which are not directly addressed by simply scaling single-agent techniques.

Previous MAIL approaches often rely on strong assumptions regarding the availability of demonstrations. These methods typically require coordinated and synchronous demonstrations where an expert simultaneously provides actions for all agents in a joint action space. Such assumptions render these techniques impractical for real-world deployment, as a single human operator faces significant cognitive burden and physical limitations in teleoperating multiple robots concurrently to achieve complex, coordinated tasks. Efforts in multi-robot teleoperation have explored manual switching between robots or employing teams of human operators, but these also present scalability and complexity issues. Some methods utilize learned models to reduce control dimensionality, but these are often task-specific and lack generalization. Other works consider fleet imitation learning, where multiple supervisors manage independent single-agent IL instances, which differs from scenarios requiring inter-robot observation and coordination.

This paper addresses a fundamental gap in the MAIL landscape: the absence of a practical method for a single human to provide feasible demonstrations for a team of interacting robots. It specifically targets scenarios where demonstrations must be provided to one agent at a time, relaxing the "unrealistic assumption" of joint-action demonstrations prevalent in much of the existing MAIL literature, which often uses pre-trained reinforcement learning policies or planners to generate expert data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to extend imitation learning to multi-agent systems under the realistic constraint that human operators can only feasibly provide demonstrations to one agent at a time. The authors articulate this as a novel research question: "How can we extend imitation learning to multi-agent systems when humans can only feasibly provide demonstrations to one agent at a time?"

The motivation for addressing this problem stems from the practical limitations of deploying multi-agent imitation learning in real-world scenarios. Current MAIL methods typically assume access to "noisily-optimal demonstrations" in the joint action space, meaning an expert provides correct actions for all agents simultaneously. This assumption is deemed unrealistic and often implausible for human operators due to several factors:
- **Cognitive Burden**: Monitoring and controlling multiple interacting agents simultaneously significantly increases the cognitive load on a human operator, impacting the quality and consistency of demonstrations.
- **Underactuated Control**: Teleoperation interfaces are often not designed to control the high degrees of freedom inherent in complex multi-robot systems without specialized, task-specific control schemes.
- **Deployment Feasibility**: The need for synchronized, centralized demonstrations often necessitates access to sophisticated pre-trained policies or planners, which may either obviate the need for IL or be difficult to obtain for novel, "in-the-wild" tasks.

To overcome these barriers, the authors aim to develop a method that allows a single human demonstrator to iteratively teach cooperative multi-agent behavior by focusing on one agent at a time, while the other agents act autonomously based on their current learned policies. This approach is motivated by the goal of making multi-agent imitation learning more accessible and practical for real-world deployment, particularly in applications such as search and rescue, disaster response, surveillance, swarm robotics, and warehouse automation, where coordinated multi-robot teams are beneficial. The specific contributions of this work are to:
1. Introduce and formally define the problem setting of multi-agent imitation learning from single-agent demonstrations.
2. Propose Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC) as a novel technique to address this problem, eliminating the requirement for coordinated joint-action demonstrations.
3. Empirically evaluate R2BC in simulation, demonstrating its performance parity or superiority compared to oracle behavior cloning methods that rely on privileged centralized demonstrations.
4. Deploy R2BC on physical robot tasks using real human demonstrations, showcasing its effectiveness in real-world scenarios and comparing it against centralized behavior cloning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The multi-agent learning problem is framed as a Markov (Stochastic) Game, characterized by a set of agents I, a state space S, joint observation space O, and joint action space A. The environment dynamics T govern transitions between states based on joint actions. The focus is on cooperative tasks with shared rewards.

Standard Imitation Learning aims to learn a joint policy π: S → A from expert trajectories (sequences of states and joint actions). This involves minimizing a loss function (e.g., squared error) between the learned policy's actions and the expert's actions in demonstrated states.

The key departure in this work is the assumption that a single human cannot provide joint actions. Instead, the human provides "single-agent demonstrations" for agent *i*, which are sequences of local observations *o<sub>i,t</sub>* and individual actions *a<sub>i,t</sub>*. The objective is to train multi-agent policies that achieve collective performance using only *N* sets of single-agent demonstrations, {D<sub>1</sub>, D<sub>2</sub>..., D<sub>N</sub>}.

#### 3.2. Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC)

R2BC is an online imitation learning algorithm designed for sequential, single-agent demonstrations. The core idea is that a human demonstrator cycles through agents in a round-robin fashion. For each agent *i*, the human teleoperates *i* to perform the task, while all other agents *j ≠ i* execute their current learned policies *π<sub>j</sub>*. This process collects individual demonstration data *(o<sub>i,t</sub>, a<sub>i,t</sub>)* into agent *i*'s buffer D<sub>i</sub>.

The algorithm proceeds as follows:
1. **Initialization**: Initialize *N* individual agent policies {*π<sub>1</sub>..., π<sub>N</sub>*} and their respective demonstration buffers {D<sub>1</sub>..., D<sub>N</sub>}.
2. **Round-Robin Demonstration Collection**:
 - For a specified number of iterations, the process cycles through each agent *i* from 1 to *N*.
 - For each *i*, the environment is reset.
 - During an episode, the human expert provides action *a<sub>i,t</sub>* for agent *i* based on its observation *o<sub>i,t</sub>*.
 - Simultaneously, all other agents *j ≠ i* compute their actions *a<sub>j,t</sub>* using their current learned policies *π<sub>j</sub>(o<sub>j,t</sub>)*.
 - A joint action *a<sub>t</sub>* (concatenation of all individual actions) is executed in the environment.
 - The state-action pair *(o<sub>i,t</sub>, a<sub>i,t</sub>)* for the demonstrated agent *i* is appended to its buffer D<sub>i</sub>.
3. **Policy Update**: At regular intervals (every *k* demonstrations), all policies *π<sub>i</sub>* are updated. Each *π<sub>i</sub>* is trained independently using standard Behavior Cloning (BC) loss (mean squared error) on its corresponding demonstration dataset D<sub>i</sub>.

R2BC incorporates two key extensions to traditional centralized behavior cloning:
1. **Full Decentralization**: Both policy execution and demonstration collection are decentralized. Each agent maintains its own policy and learns from its specific demonstrations, promoting realistic deployment with partial observability and limited communication.
2. **Online Learning**: Demonstrations are provided online, allowing agents to iteratively improve their policies while interacting with other agents operating autonomously. This exposes the demonstrator to varied scenarios, including instances where non-demonstrating agents exhibit suboptimal behavior, enabling the expert to teach corrective actions and foster resilience to distribution shift.

#### 3.3. Experimental Design

**Simulated Experiments**:
- **Environment**: Vectorized Multi-Agent Simulator (VMAS).
- **Tasks**: Four cooperative multi-agent tasks: Navigation (3 agents), Balance (3 agents), Buzz Wire (2 agents, coupled dynamics), and Transport (3 agents, package pushing).
- **Baselines**:
 - **Joint Behavior Cloning (JBC)**: An "oracle" baseline, training a policy from centralized, synchronous, joint-action demonstrations. These demonstrations are generated by a pre-trained multi-agent reinforcement learning policy (MAPPO), representing an unrealistic "super-human" expert.
 - **DAgger**: An oracle online IL method where a joint-action expert provides corrective labels to on-policy rollouts.
 - **DART**: An oracle online IL method where noise is injected into the demonstrator’s teleoperation to vary state distribution and allow for online corrections.
- **Metrics**: Normalized average environment reward (0.0 for random policy, 1.0 for oracle expert). Covariate shift reduction measured by train-test loss gap.
- **Training**: MSE loss, learning rate 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, minibatch size 256.

**Simulated Ablation Studies**:
- **Decentralized JBC**: Uses offline oracle joint-action demonstrations but trains *N* independent decentralized policies based on local observations.
- **Offline R2BC (No-Op Agents)**: Human demonstrates to one agent, others do not act (no-op). Policies are updated offline after all demonstrations are collected.
- **Offline R2BC (Random Agents)**: Human demonstrates to one agent, others take random actions. Policies are updated offline.
- **Centralized R2BC**: Maintains a single centralized network for joint actions, but during training, the loss is masked to update only the output corresponding to the demonstrated agent.

**Physical Robot Deployment**:
- **Hardware**: Three HeRo+ robots, (differential drive mobile robots) with 2D action space (forward/backward and angular velocity).
- **State Tracking**: Centralized Robot Operating System (ROS) server uses a Realsense D435i camera and AruCo markers.
- **Tasks**:
 - **Navigation**: Physical version of the simulated task (agents navigate to goals, avoid collisions).
 - **Block Pusher**: Physical variation of the simulated Transport task (agents push an elongated block to a goal).
- **Human Demonstration Collection**: Two authors provided 240 demonstrations for each task in a customized simulated environment (matching HeRo+ kinematics and action space) using Xbox360 joysticks (R2BC: single controller per agent; JBC: two controllers for joint control).
- **Evaluation**: Cumulative inverse distance (Navigation, 90s horizon), cumulative package distance (Block Pusher, 30s horizon).
- **Sim2Real Transfer Mitigation**: Evaluation included scenarios with 0, 1, or 2 human supervisor interventions (3 seconds of manual control to correct robot behavior).
- **Statistical Analysis**: Welch two-sample t-test for one-tailed significance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Simulated Experiments

- **Comparison to Joint Action Behavior Cloning (JBC)**: R2BC consistently matched or surpassed the performance of the JBC oracle baseline across all four simulated tasks (Navigation, Balance, Buzz Wire, Transport) (Figure 2). This outcome was unexpected, as JBC had access to "super-human" coordinated demonstrations from a trained multi-agent RL policy. The authors hypothesize that JBC's data, consisting solely of near-optimal behaviors, lacks examples for error correction or recovery from out-of-distribution states. In contrast, R2BC benefits from the initial unpredictable actions of non-demonstrating agents, creating varied states where the demonstrator can teach corrective cooperative behaviors.
- **Comparison to Online Imitation Learning Baselines (DAgger, DART)**: R2BC achieved performance comparable to both privileged online baselines, DAgger and DART (Figure 2). This suggests that R2BC's online paradigm, where non-demonstrating agents introduce observation variance, effectively reduces compounding errors in a manner similar to the explicit noise injection or corrective labeling mechanisms of DART and DAgger, respectively. Empirical evidence (Figure 3) demonstrated that R2BC exhibited a similar reduction in the train-test loss gap, indicative of improved generalization and reduced covariate shift, compared to these oracle online methods, and significantly better than JBC.

#### 4.2. Simulated Ablation Studies

The ablation studies demonstrated the importance of both decentralization and online learning for R2BC's efficacy (Figure 4).

- **Decentralized JBC vs. Centralized JBC**: Decentralized JBC, where policies are independent and learn from local observations based on oracle joint demonstrations, performed on par or better than centralized JBC. This suggested that decentralizing policies can offer performance benefits, particularly in tasks like Balance and Transport.
- **Offline R2BC Variants (No-Op Agents, Random Agents)**: These offline approaches, where policies are not updated during the demonstration phase, were unreliable and showed high variance across tasks. This underscored the necessity of online policy updates, where agents learn iteratively in the presence of existing, evolving teammates, to achieve robust performance.
- **Centralized R2BC**: A centralized network for joint actions, with masked loss for the demonstrated agent, performed slightly worse or comparably to the decentralized R2BC. This result indicated that full decentralization of policies contributes to better or at least equivalent performance across tasks.

The conclusion from these ablations was that the full R2BC framework, encompassing both decentralized policies and online learning during demonstration collection, is essential for achieving robust multi-agent learning from single-agent demonstrations.

#### 4.3. Physical Robot Deployment

- **Navigation Task**: R2BC performed an average of 3.25 times better than JBC without supervisor interventions. With one intervention, R2BC outperformed JBC by 3.92 times (p = 0.0094). With two interventions, R2BC maintained a 3.36 times advantage (p < 0.005) (Figure 6). Initial zero-shot performance showed high variance for both methods, but R2BC consistently showed a higher mean reward.
- **Block Pusher Task**: R2BC performed 5.9 times better than JBC without supervisor interventions (p = 0.0126). This advantage persisted with one intervention (5.84 times better, p = 0.0107) and two interventions (3.16 times better, p = 0.0239) (Figure 6).

These real-world deployment results, utilizing human-provided demonstrations, confirmed that R2BC reliably outperformed traditional centralized behavior cloning. While sim-to-real distribution shift was present, leading to occasional local minima, supervisor interventions effectively corrected these issues. Notably, brief interventions had a more significant positive impact on R2BC's performance compared to JBC, suggesting that R2BC policies are more amenable to human correction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a practical solution to a significant challenge in the deployment of multi-agent robotic systems: the collection of feasible human demonstrations for complex cooperative tasks. By introducing Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC), the paper offers a method that aligns with human capabilities, allowing a single operator to teach multi-robot teams without requiring synchronous, joint-action control, which is often an unrealistic demand of prior multi-agent imitation learning (MAIL) approaches.

The primary significance of R2BC lies in its potential to:
- **Bridge the Gap in MAIL**: It addresses a critical bottleneck by relaxing the strong assumptions about demonstration data typically made in MAIL, thereby making imitation learning more accessible and scalable for multi-robot applications.
- **Enhance Real-World Deployability**: By enabling training from single-agent demonstrations, R2BC facilitates the transfer of multi-robot skills from human experts to robotic teams in practical settings, where simultaneous multi-robot teleoperation is impractical. This expands the applicability of IL to domains such as search and rescue, disaster response, surveillance, and logistics, where human supervisors can teach complex coordinated behaviors to robot teams more intuitively.
- **Improve Learning Robustness**: The online learning aspect of R2BC, where non-demonstrating agents act autonomously, exposes the expert to varied state distributions, including suboptimal behaviors from teammates. This potentially leads to more robust policies that can handle errors and distribution shifts, which is a known challenge in imitation learning.
- **Outperform Oracle Baselines**: The empirical finding that R2BC can match or even surpass behavior cloning methods trained on "super-human" oracle demonstrations is noteworthy. This suggests that the practical, human-centric approach of R2BC may offer inherent advantages in learning robust cooperative strategies.
- **Validate Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The successful deployment on physical robots using human demonstrations, even with sim-to-real transfer challenges, demonstrates R2BC's practical viability and its ability to learn policies that generalize to the physical world. The enhanced impact of supervisor interventions on R2BC further indicates that the learned policies are interpretable and easily steerable by humans.

Future work could focus on evaluating the cognitive burden on human demonstrators through user studies with non-experts, which would further establish R2BC's user-friendliness and overall practicality. Developing theoretical guarantees for convergence and covariate shift reduction would also provide a deeper understanding of the method's underlying mechanisms and reinforce the empirical observations.

Overall, R2BC represents a step towards making multi-agent imitation learning a more practical and widely applicable paradigm for human-robot collaboration, fostering the development and deployment of intelligent, cooperative multi-robot systems.
