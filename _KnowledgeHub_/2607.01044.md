# Robots Ask the Way: Communication-Enabled Social Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sapienza University of Rome introduce Communication-enabled Social Navigation (CommNav), allowing robots to proactively ask human residents for information to locate a target individual in multi-human environments. This approach enhances search efficiency and safety, demonstrating robustness to natural, colloquial human language instructions.

## Problem

- Existing social navigation methods are reactive, focusing on collision avoidance, and lack mechanisms for proactively gathering information from other agents.
- Robots struggle with inefficient exhaustive searches when tasked with finding a specific individual in complex, multi-human environments.
- Current state-of-the-art social navigation approaches do not leverage human knowledge of recent spatial events or the collaborative potential in human-robot cohabitation.

## Method

- The paper introduces **Communication-enabled Social Navigation (CommNav)** as a task where a robot actively queries non-target humans for egocentric cues about a target individual's location and movements.
- A dedicated **Communication Module (COMM)** is integrated into a ==DDPPO== navigation model, processing either structured data or natural language cues to estimate the target's location, functioning as an additional sensor input.
- The COMM module is ==pre-trained== on a large dataset of communication instances to effectively interpret sparse and intermittent human-provided information, allowing the policy to incorporate hints without continuous updates.

## Results

- CommNav (COMM) achieved an Episode Success (ES) of **0.24**, an **8 percentage-point** improvement in Finding Success (S = **0.78**) and a **10 percentage-point** improvement in ES over baseline DDPPO with interaction (ES = **0.14**).
- The system showed robustness to natural human language, with COMM_L(Human) achieving an Episode Success of **0.23 ± 0.01**, statistically similar to the **0.20 ± 0.01** achieved by the LLM-generated COMM_L model.
- CommNav variants consistently outperformed baselines (DDPPO and SDA) in three-human environments, particularly at half speed, achieving an ES of **0.29** compared to DDPPO's **0.21** and SDA's **0.24**, with reduced collision rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit, query-based communication with non-target human residents significantly enhances a robot's ability to locate a target in complex, multi-human environments, moving beyond purely reactive navigation.
> - Navigation policies trained on structured communication data exhibit robust performance when exposed to natural, colloquial, and often imprecise human language instructions, maintaining similar episode success rates.
> - The pre-training of the communication module effectively addresses the challenge of sparse interaction signals inherent in human-robot communication, allowing the robot to make informed decisions with infrequent cues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI has seen significant advancements, particularly in robotic navigation, fueled by rich 3D indoor datasets and advanced simulators like Habitat, iGibson, and AI2-THOR. Traditional embodied navigation research has primarily focused on reactive collision avoidance, motion planning, and adapting to dynamic, partially unknown environments. While these methods allow robots to navigate shared spaces, they typically treat humans as dynamic obstacles or rely on implicit social cues. Current state-of-the-art social navigation approaches, such as those implemented in Habitat 3.0 and SDA, often leverage privileged information about scene and agent elements to forecast human trajectories and enhance navigation, but they lack mechanisms for proactive information gathering through direct human-robot communication.

Prior work has explored the use of language in cooperative navigation, demonstrating that dialogue can improve performance in long-horizon tasks and that intermittent language instructions can boost task completion. Some research has investigated communication as an implicit or explicit channel to convey robot intent or explored natural gestures to guide embodied agents. However, these studies have generally not focused on explicit, information-seeking communication where the robot actively queries non-target humans for sparse egocentric cues about another person and integrates this information into a mapless, multi-human search strategy.

This research introduces "Communication-enabled Social Navigation" (CommNav), positioning itself as an extension to existing social navigation paradigms. It addresses a critical limitation by enabling robotic agents to actively seek assistance from human residents to locate target individuals. By integrating communication as a core mechanism, CommNav moves beyond reactive collision avoidance to proactive information acquisition, aiming to enhance efficiency and cooperation in dynamic, multi-agent environments. The work also tackles the practical challenge of transitioning from structured communication data to natural language, aligning with efforts to make human-robot interaction more intuitive and robust in real-world settings. This places the paper at the intersection of embodied navigation, multi-agent systems, human-robot interaction, and natural language processing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and evaluate Communication-enabled Social Navigation (CommNav), a novel task that allows robotic agents to proactively gather information from human residents to locate a specific target individual in a multi-agent environment.

The motivations for pursuing this objective are multi-faceted:

- **Limitation of Current Social Navigation Methods:** Existing social navigation methods predominantly focus on reactive collision avoidance and adapting trajectories based on observed human behavior. They lack mechanisms to proactively acquire task-relevant information from other agents. This reactive paradigm can lead to inefficient exhaustive searches when a robot needs to find a specific individual in a shared space, such as a home, office, or care facility. For example, a robot tasked with delivering an item would, under traditional methods, resort to a time-consuming room-by-room search.
- **Human Analogy and Efficiency:** Humans naturally utilize communication to obtain spatial cues and directions from others. This ability significantly reduces exploration time and improves navigation efficiency in complex or unfamiliar environments. The research aims to imbue robots with a similar capability, enabling them to leverage the collective knowledge of human residents.
- **Leveraging Human Knowledge:** Current state-of-the-art social navigation methods cannot exploit human knowledge of recent spatial events or the collaborative potential inherent in human-robot cohabitation. Integrating communication allows robots to tap into this valuable source of information, such as recent sightings, locations, or movements of the target individual.
- **Addressing Challenges of Communication Integration:** Incorporating communication into embodied navigation presents several technical challenges. These include dealing with sparse and unpredictable interaction signals, handling heterogeneous types of cues (e.g., precise coordinates vs. vague directions), and ensuring scalability as the number of agents in the environment grows.
- **Bridging Structured Data and Natural Language:** A significant practical motivation is to bridge the gap between idealized structured data and the inherent approximation and incompleteness of real human communication. The authors aim to investigate whether navigation policies trained on structured communication can effectively generalize to and perform robustly with natural, colloquial human language instructions. This is crucial for real-world deployability.
- **Extension to Multi-Agent Environments:** The work specifically targets multi-human environments, acknowledging that the task of identifying and assisting a target among multiple residents is more complex than single-human navigation.

By addressing these points, the research aims to demonstrate that explicit human-robot communication can substantially enhance multi-person navigation performance, improve search efficiency, and create a more cooperative human-robot ecosystem.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Communication-enabled Social Navigation (CommNav) and evaluates it by extending the Habitat 3.0 simulator to create Habitat 3.0c, a communication-enabled variant for multi-human environments.

#### 3.1. CommNav Task Formalization

CommNav is formalized as a multi-agent navigation problem where a robot actively seeks assistance from human residents to locate a target individual. Instead of relying solely on visual exploration, the robot can opportunistically approach any resident, ask for information (e.g., "Have you seen them?"), and receive cues about the target's recent sightings, location, and movement. The decision to approach a bystander is learned end-to-end by the navigation policy.

#### 3.2. Content of Communication Messages (S)

When the robot is within communication range and oriented towards a human, it receives a message encoded as a state vector **S** comprising five components:
- **x_h** (have seen): A boolean indicating if the speaker has seen the target.
- **x_t** (when): A scalar representing steps since the target was last seen.
- **x_l** (location): The last known 3D location of the target agent, relative to the speaking agent.
- **x_d** (direction): A 3D point representing the direction of the target human, relative to the speaking agent.
- **x_p** (path): 100 3D point coordinates of the speaker’s past trajectory, relative to the speaking agent.
All coordinates are egocentric, relative to the speaking agent's current position. Message content updates dynamically based on whether the target is in sight or has been previously encountered.

#### 3.3. Natural Language Generation (L)

To bridge the gap between structured data and natural human interaction, the QWEN3-8B language model is used to translate each structured state vector **S** into a short, natural language utterance **L**. The generated instructions avoid place names, focusing on relative directions (e.g., "behind me") to ensure the policy learns spatial reasoning from social cues.

#### 3.4. RL with Human-Robot Communication Architecture

The core of the approach integrates a dedicated Communication Module (COMM) into a state-of-the-art DDPPO navigation model, which is typically based on a ResNet architecture for processing visual observations.
- **Parallel Processing:** A baseline ResNet encoder processes raw RGB-depth observations. In parallel, the COMM module interprets the intermittent human-provided inputs (**S** or **L**).
- **COMM Output:** The COMM module translates these cues into an estimated target location, ˆx_target, which is treated as an additional sensor input.
- **Navigation Policy Network:** This network fuses the visual features from the ResNet encoder and the ˆx_target estimate from COMM to generate robot actions (linear and angular velocity commands).
- **Fallback Mechanism:** When human input is unavailable (due to sparse interaction signals), the COMM module provides a placeholder, allowing the agent to rely on its visual policy and maintain autonomy.

#### 3.5. Policy Training

The navigation policy is trained using the DDPPO reinforcement learning framework with success-based and efficiency-weighted rewards, tailored for CommNav. Initial training occurs without communication to ensure the policy learns robust navigation from visual inputs alone, after which communication is introduced.

#### 3.6. Communication Module (COMM) Pre-training

The COMM module is pre-trained on a proxy task to effectively interpret infrequent human-provided information.
- **Dataset:** A dataset of 2.4 million communication instances of **S** is collected over 60 million simulation steps of baseline training. Each instance includes **S** and the ground-truth target location **x_target**.
- **Structured Path:** For structured inputs, the trajectory **x_p** is embedded by a Trajectory Encoder (a Spatio-Temporal MLP). This embedding is then concatenated with [x_h, x_t, x_l, x_d] and fed into a Multilayer Regressor, which outputs the estimated target location ˆx_target.
- **Language Path (COMM_L):** For natural language inputs, 7,000 samples from the collected instances are converted into natural language reports using QWEN3-8B. A BERT encoder then processes these textual inputs, and COMM_L is trained to regress **x_target** using these language cues alone. Both paths learn the same regression objective but from different modalities.
This pre-training allows the policy to incorporate communicative hints about the target’s location without requiring continuous updates, integrating human input efficiently.

#### 3.7. Habitat 3.0c Simulator

- **Multi-Human Extension:** Habitat 3.0c extends Habitat 3.0 to support multiple human agents, where the robot must identify and assist a target among several individuals.
- **Communication Trigger:** Communication is triggered when the robot is within range and facing a human.
- **Realistic Interaction:** To encourage realistic interactions, Habitat 3.0c integrates Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance (ORCA) and introduces a probability (p = 0.25) that humans ignore the robot for an entire episode, forcing the robot to learn dynamic yielding.
- **Robot-Agnostic Inputs:** The robot’s policy takes egocentric depth and a humanoid detector as input, making the approach generalizable to different robot platforms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CommNav involved comparisons against state-of-the-art social navigation baselines (DDPPO and SDA) in Habitat 3.0c, an extended multi-human communication-enabled simulator. Metrics included Finding Success (S), average steps to find (S_steps), path efficiency (SPS), Following Rate (F), collision rates (CR, CR_T), and Episode Success (ES).

#### 4.1. CommNav Performance with Structured Data

- **Increased Task Difficulty:** Transitioning from single-human (Habitat 3.0) to multi-human (Habitat 3.0c) environments significantly decreased Episode Success (ES) for baselines (e.g., DDPPO dropped from 0.40 to 0.14), highlighting the increased complexity.
- **Superior Performance with Communication:** Integrating the COMM module into DDPPO (COMM) yielded substantial improvements. COMM achieved an Episode Success (ES) of 0.24, an 8 percentage-point improvement in Finding Success (S = 0.78) and a 10 percentage-point improvement in ES over DDPPO with interaction (ES = 0.14).
- **Efficiency:** COMM achieved a lower S_steps (572) compared to baselines (608–618), indicating improved search efficiency despite the overhead of querying. This suggests the policy learns to query selectively.
- **Collision Avoidance:** COMM achieved a lower overall collision rate (CR = 0.51) than DDPPO w/ Interaction (0.68) and other baselines. The communication task, requiring close proximity to non-target humans, provided dense interaction-based supervision for avoidance. CR_T (collision with target) was also minimized to 0.23, demonstrating safe target following.

#### 4.2. Natural Language Communication and Human Study

- **LLM-Generated Language (COMM_L):** Training with LLM-generated language instructions (COMM_L) maintained high Finding Success (S = 0.78) and improved S_steps (546) compared to COMM, but showed a modest degradation in episode-level metrics (ES dropped from 0.24 to 0.20, CR increased, Following decreased). This indicates that the added noise and underspecification of generated language slightly reduced reliability.
- **Colloquial Human Language (COMM_L(Human)):** A human study involving 20 participants revealed that human-provided instructions were simpler and less detailed than LLM-generated ones. Semantic similarity was high (0.83 cosine similarity), but lexical overlap was lower (0.50 BERTScore F1).
- **Robustness to Human Language:** Despite the less precise nature of colloquial human inputs, COMM_L(Human) achieved an Episode Success (ES) of 0.23 ± 0.01, which was statistically similar to and even slightly higher than the 0.20 ± 0.01 ES achieved by the LLM-generated COMM_L model. The localization error analysis (Fig. 4) showed similar error distributions for both COMM_L and COMM_L(Human), suggesting that the policy’s robustness is due to its sensitivity to downstream factors rather than intermediate regression quality.

#### 4.3. Impact of Communication Components (Ablation Study)

- The communication module demonstrated broad robustness to partial information.
- The most significant drop in ES occurred when the **x_h** (have seen) component was removed (0.24 to 0.19), indicating its crucial role in gating the availability of encounter-related cues.
- Removing **x_t** (when) or the full speaker trajectory **x_p** also reduced ES, suggesting that temporal recency and the bystander's path are important for the policy to assess the actionability of a cue. This aligns with human communication patterns where such cues are often expressed approximately.

#### 4.4. Scalability to Multiple Human Agents

- **Three-Human Environment:** When evaluated with three humanoid agents, both COMM variants consistently outperformed DDPPO and SDA.
- **Full Speed:** At full speed, COMM achieved higher S (0.67 vs. 0.61–0.62) and ES (0.12 vs. 0.07–0.08), with reduced S_steps.
- **Half Speed:** At half speed, the performance gap widened. COMM achieved an ES of 0.29, significantly higher than DDPPO (0.21) and SDA (0.24), and exhibited markedly lower collision rates (CR = 0.42 vs. 0.49–0.53). This suggests that communication-driven guidance scales effectively to denser settings and becomes more advantageous as navigation conditions become safer.

#### 4.5. Longer Training Ablation (Appendix)

- Additional experiments confirmed that the performance improvements of COMM are primarily due to its specialized design and the pre-training of the communication module, rather than merely extended training time for the navigation policy. Fine-tuning with a frozen COMM module proved to be substantially more effective and efficient than training COMM from scratch.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Significance

This research marks a notable advancement in embodied AI by formally introducing Communication-enabled Social Navigation (CommNav) and demonstrating the explicit integration of human-robot communication into navigation policies.
- **Proactive Information Acquisition:** The work establishes that proactive, query-based communication with non-target human residents substantially enhances a robot's ability to locate a target in complex, multi-human environments, moving beyond reactive collision avoidance paradigms.
- **Robustness to Natural Language:** A key finding is the robust performance of the navigation policy when exposed to natural, colloquial, and often imprecise human language instructions. The model's episode success was statistically indistinguishable from that achieved with highly precise, structured data, suggesting a practical path towards grounding these policies in real-world human interaction.
- **Addressing Sparse Signals:** The design of the COMM module, particularly its pre-training on a communication pretext task, effectively addresses the challenge of sparse and intermittent interaction signals inherent in human-robot communication.
- **Scalability:** The experiments demonstrate that communication-enabled navigation policies scale effectively to environments with multiple human agents, providing increasing advantages as navigation conditions become less saturated.
- **Simulator Extension:** The creation of Habitat 3.0c provides a valuable tool for future research in communication-enabled multi-human embodied AI tasks.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The findings of this paper have several potential impacts on the development and deployment of autonomous robots:
- **Enhanced Robotic Assistants:** The ability for robots to ask for and interpret directions from humans could lead to more efficient and capable robotic assistants in various real-world settings such as households, offices, hospitals, and care facilities. Robots could perform tasks like delivery, retrieval, or guiding individuals more effectively by leveraging human intelligence.
- **More Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** By demonstrating robustness to colloquial language, the research moves towards more natural and intuitive human-robot interfaces, reducing the need for humans to learn specific commands or adhere to precise communication protocols.
- **New Research Avenues:** CommNav opens new avenues for research in human-robot interaction, particularly in areas like multi-turn dialogue management, complex spatial reasoning from natural language, and the development of more sophisticated social grounding mechanisms for embodied agents.
- **Improved Navigation in Crowded Spaces:** For applications in dense or dynamic environments, communicative guidance could allow robots to navigate more safely and efficiently by obtaining timely information about crowd movements or target locations, potentially reducing collision rates.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to future research directions:
- **Simulation Environment:** The current evaluation is conducted in simulation with noiseless sensing and oracle-level person segmentation and identification. Real-world deployment would require robust practical modules for these capabilities.
- **Single-Turn Interaction:** The focus on single-turn communication isolates the impact of social grounding. Future work could extend this framework to multi-turn clarification cycles and more complex dialogue management.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The paper raises ethical concerns regarding surveillance when socially aware navigation supports vulnerable users. Real-world deployment will necessitate strong safeguards, including explicit consent mechanisms, privacy-preserving data handling, and clear policy constraints on communication.
