# Generalizable Reasoning through Compositional Energy Minimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harvard University and the University of Barcelona developed a framework for generalizable reasoning that learns modular energy functions for subproblems and combines them, utilizing Parallel Energy Minimization to solve complex tasks. This approach demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, often outperforming state-of-the-art methods in benchmarks like N-Queens, 3-SAT, Graph Coloring, and Crosswords.

## Problem

- Machine learning models, especially end-to-end approaches, exhibit poor generalization to structurally more complex or out-of-distribution reasoning problems.
- Combinatorial optimization problems often require extensive, diverse datasets for effective training, limiting scalability and robustness.
- Current AI methods lack the compositional reasoning abilities seen in human intelligence, struggling to apply learned rules to novel, harder tasks.

## Method

- Formulates reasoning as minimizing a neural ==Energy-Based Model (EBM)== trained using a denoising diffusion objective and an additional contrastive loss.
- Decomposes complex problems into subproblems, learning ==modular EBMs== for each, and then optimizes the sum of these EBMs for a complete solution.
- Employs ==Parallel Energy Minimization (PEM)==, an algorithm that uses multiple parallel particles, importance sampling, resampling, and noise injection to effectively navigate and optimize complex energy landscapes.

## Results

- Achieved **97 out of 100** correct solutions for the 8-Queens problem, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art combinatorial optimization methods and generalizing to N-Queens for N=7-10.
- Outperformed neural SAT solvers on 3-SAT, solving **91 out of 100** instances for 20 variables/91 clauses, and **43 out of 100** for 50 variables/218 clauses.
- Demonstrated superior performance in Graph Coloring over GNN-based and RL-guided methods across various graph types, notably improving generalization to larger and denser graphs.
- Showed competitive results on Crosswords, reaching an **80.4%** letter success rate, comparable to Tree-of-Thought LLM strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning modular energy functions for subproblems allows for compositional generalization to significantly harder and larger problem instances beyond training data.
> - Reasoning can be effectively framed as an energy minimization problem, where increasing computational effort during inference (e.g., via more PEM particles) directly improves solution quality for complex tasks.
> - Parallel Energy Minimization (PEM) provides a robust strategy for optimizing over high-dimensional, non-convex energy landscapes, successfully avoiding local minima and enhancing solution discovery.
