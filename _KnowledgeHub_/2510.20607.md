# Generalizable Reasoning through Compositional Energy Minimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harvard University and the University of Barcelona developed a framework for generalizable reasoning that learns modular energy functions for subproblems and combines them, utilizing Parallel Energy Minimization to solve complex tasks. This approach demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, often outperforming state-of-the-art methods in benchmarks like N-Queens, 3-SAT, Graph Coloring, and Crosswords.

## Problem

- Machine learning models, especially end-to-end approaches, exhibit poor generalization to structurally more complex or out-of-distribution reasoning problems.
- Combinatorial optimization problems often require extensive, diverse datasets for effective training, limiting scalability and robustness.
- Current AI methods lack the compositional reasoning abilities seen in human intelligence, struggling to apply learned rules to novel, harder tasks.

## Method

- Formulates reasoning as minimizing a neural ==Energy-Based Model (EBM)== trained using a denoising diffusion objective and an additional contrastive loss.
- Decomposes complex problems into subproblems, learning ==modular EBMs== for each, and then optimizes the sum of these EBMs for a complete solution.
- Employs ==Parallel Energy Minimization (PEM)==, an algorithm that uses multiple parallel particles, importance sampling, resampling, and noise injection to effectively navigate and optimize complex energy landscapes.

## Results

- Achieved **97 out of 100** correct solutions for the 8-Queens problem, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art combinatorial optimization methods and generalizing to N-Queens for N=7-10.
- Outperformed neural SAT solvers on 3-SAT, solving **91 out of 100** instances for 20 variables/91 clauses, and **43 out of 100** for 50 variables/218 clauses.
- Demonstrated superior performance in Graph Coloring over GNN-based and RL-guided methods across various graph types, notably improving generalization to larger and denser graphs.
- Showed competitive results on Crosswords, reaching an **80.4%** letter success rate, comparable to Tree-of-Thought LLM strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning modular energy functions for subproblems allows for compositional generalization to significantly harder and larger problem instances beyond training data.
> - Reasoning can be effectively framed as an energy minimization problem, where increasing computational effort during inference (e.g., via more PEM particles) directly improves solution quality for complex tasks.
> - Parallel Energy Minimization (PEM) provides a robust strategy for optimizing over high-dimensional, non-convex energy landscapes, successfully avoiding local minima and enhancing solution discovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses one of the most significant challenges in machine learning: **generalization in complex reasoning tasks**. Traditional machine learning models, particularly those trained end-to-end, excel at pattern recognition within their training distribution but often struggle when confronted with problems that are structurally more complex or fall outside this distribution. This limitation contrasts sharply with human intelligence, where learning basic rules and applying them compositionally allows for robust generalization to novel and harder problems.

The paper situates itself against several existing paradigms:

- **End-to-End Reasoning Models:** These models directly map inputs to solutions, learning statistical heuristics. While effective for similar problems, they fail to generalize compositionally, a common failing for combinatorial optimization (CO) and logical tasks.
- **Reasoning as Optimization:** Previous research has explored formulating reasoning as an optimization problem, integrating logical rules into neural networks (e.g., differentiable SAT solvers, theorem proving, probabilistic logic). However, many of these approaches are domain-specific or rely on strong assumptions, still often adopting an end-to-end learning strategy.
- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for Combinatorial Optimization:** GNNs are a standard tool in CO due to their ability to represent variable-constraint relations. However, they are known to struggle with out-of-distribution generalization and require extensive, diverse datasets, falling prey to the same limitations as other end-to-end approaches.
- **Iterative Computation/Refinement:** Some methods use neural networks to iteratively refine solutions. While this aligns with the optimization framework, the *compositional* aspect is often missing.
- **Energy-Based Models (EBMs) and Diffusion Models:** The authors explicitly build upon these, noting that prior EBM work primarily focused on learning probabilistic generative models over data. This paper shifts the focus of EBMs from pure generation to *optimization for reasoning*, introducing a novel application of these powerful models.

By proposing a **compositional strategy** that learns energy landscapes for *subproblems* and then combines them, the authors carve out a distinct niche. This approach directly tackles the generalization bottleneck by allowing the model to learn fundamental constraints (akin to human rules) and then flexibly compose them to solve problems of arbitrary scale and complexity. It directly aims to move beyond heuristic-based solutions towards more rule-based, extensible reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **generalizable reasoning framework** capable of solving complex problems that are substantially harder and more intricate than those observed during the training phase. The authors aim to bridge the gap between typical machine learning's pattern recognition capabilities and the compositional reasoning abilities characteristic of human cognition.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing the Generalization Deficit:** Existing end-to-end machine learning models struggle to generalize to novel conditions and problem distributions, especially in reasoning tasks where structural complexity increases. The authors highlight this as a fundamental limitation preventing AI from tackling real-world complex problems effectively.
- **Mimicking Human Reasoning:** Humans learn rules and constraints governing a problem and then apply them compositionally. They can also invest more cognitive effort (time) to search for solutions to harder tasks, rather than solely relying on pre-learned heuristics. The proposed approach aims to emulate this by learning modular energy functions and allowing for adjustable computational budgets during inference.
- **Casting Reasoning as an Optimization Problem:** By framing reasoning as minimizing an energy function, where valid solutions correspond to low-energy states, the authors seek to leverage robust optimization techniques. This formulation inherently supports the idea that harder problems can be solved by dedicating more computational resources to the optimization process.
- **Leveraging Energy-Based Models for Optimization:** While EBMs have been primarily used for generative tasks, the authors are motivated to adapt their power for complex reasoning by learning energy landscapes that encode the satisfiability of constraints, opening a new application domain for these models.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond "pattern matching" towards a system that can "reason by rules," allowing for greater flexibility, robustness, and scalability in solving challenging combinatorial and logical problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed "Generalizable Reasoning through Compositional Energy Minimization," involves several innovative components:

#### 3.1. Reasoning as Energy Minimization

The core idea is to define a neural Energy-Based Model (EBM), $E_\theta(x, y)$, which assigns lower energy to valid solutions $y$ for a given problem input $x$. Finding a solution $\hat{y}$ then becomes an optimization problem: $\hat{y} = \arg\min_y E_\theta(x, y)$, typically solved via gradient descent.

#### 3.2. Diffusion Energy-Based Models for Training

Training EBMs effectively can be challenging. Instead of backpropagating through many optimization steps, the authors adopt a denoising diffusion training objective, similar to. This objective trains the gradient of the EBM to match a noise distribution at each timestep. Specifically, given a ground-truth label $y$ and a noisy version $y^* = \sqrt{1-\sigma_t}y + \sigma_t\epsilon$, the mean squared error (MSE) loss is $L_{MSE}(\theta) = E_{y,\epsilon} [\|\epsilon + \sigma_t \nabla_y E_\theta(y^*, t)\|^2]$. This allows the model to learn an energy gradient that transforms noisy inputs into target solutions iteratively.

#### 3.3. Shaping the Energy Landscape

To ensure that ground-truth solutions are indeed minima and to enhance landscape regions not fully covered by the diffusion objective, an additional **contrastive loss** ($L_{CL}(\theta)$) is introduced, following. This loss guides the energy function by comparing noise-corrupted positive samples (true solutions) and negative samples (invalid solutions), ensuring positive samples have lower energy.

#### 3.4. Compositional Reasoning

To achieve generalization to more complex problems, the authors propose a **compositional approach**. A complex problem $x$ is decomposed into simpler subproblems $x = \{x_1, \ldots, x_N\}$. The model learns individual EBMs, $E^k_\theta(x_k, y_k)$, for each subproblem (e.g., satisfying a single clause in 3-SAT, or coloring a single edge in graph coloring). A complete solution for the original problem is then found by optimizing the *sum* of these individual energy functions: $\hat{y} = \arg\min_y \sum_{k=1}^N E^k_\theta(x_k, y_k)$. This modularity allows the model to tackle problems with an arbitrary number of constraints, effectively generalizing beyond training sizes.

#### 3.5. Parallel Energy Minimization (PEM) for Improved Sampling

Optimizing over a composed energy landscape, which often contains numerous local minima, is difficult. Inspired by Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC), the authors introduce **Parallel Energy Minimization (PEM)**. This algorithm maintains a set of $P$ parallel "particles" that are optimized over $T$ steps. At each step:
1. **Importance Evaluation:** Particles are weighted based on their energy values (lower energy = higher weight).
2. **Selection:** Particles are resampled based on these weights, effectively discarding those stuck in high-energy local minima.
3. **Resampling:** A predefined amount of Gaussian noise is added to the resampled particles to encourage exploration.
4. **Optimization:** Particles are updated using the gradient of the energy function.
This parallel strategy enhances exploration and helps avoid premature convergence to local minima, leading to better quality samples.

#### 3.6. Refinement of the Energy Landscape

Composing multiple energy objectives can introduce inaccuracies. To mitigate this, a **refinement strategy** is employed. The composed energy function (the sum of subproblem EBMs) is further refined using the same diffusion training objective but applied to full ground-truth solutions of the larger, composed problem. This fine-tuning step helps align the minima of the combined energy landscape with valid global solutions, improving robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the efficacy of "Generalizable Reasoning through Compositional Energy Minimization" across a diverse suite of challenging reasoning problems:

- **N-Queens Problem:**
 - The model, trained on a single instance of 8-Queens (learning to place a single queen per row/column/diagonal), significantly **outperformed state-of-the-art combinatorial optimization methods** like LWD, GFlowNets, DIFUSCO, and Fast T2T. It achieved 97 out of 100 correct solutions for the 8-queens problem, whereas the best baseline managed only 41.
 - **Generalization:** It successfully generalized to larger N-Queens problems (7-10 queens) by adjusting the number of particles in PEM.
 - **Scalability:** Increasing the number of parallel particles in PEM directly correlated with a higher number of correct solutions and better average queen placement, showcasing its adaptive computational budget.
 - **Qualitative:** Visualizations showed that correct solutions were assigned low energy, and incorrect solutions (violating constraints) had higher energy. PEM generated better quality samples than reverse diffusion alone.
 - **Ablation:** PEM substantially outperformed traditional MCMC samplers (ULA, MALA, UHMC, HMC), and the combination of diffusion and contrastive losses was crucial for optimal performance. The method also generated a higher diversity of unique solutions compared to deterministic approaches like DeepT.

- **3-SAT Problem:**
 - Trained on individual 3-SAT clauses, the model generalized to entire SAT instances with many variables and clauses.
 - It **significantly outperformed neural SAT solvers** (NeuroSAT, NSNet) and other combinatorial optimization models. For a similar distribution (20 variables, 91 clauses), it solved 91 out of 100 instances, compared to NSNet's 58. For a larger distribution (50 variables, 218 clauses), it solved 43 instances, against NSNet's 37.
 - **Qualitative:** Unsatisfied clauses were assigned higher energy, while satisfied clauses received lower energy, confirming the energy function's accurate representation of constraint satisfaction.
 - **Scalability:** Performance improved with an increased number of particles.
 - **Ablation:** PEM again proved superior to other samplers, and the combined diffusion and contrastive losses, along with a fine-tuning step, were essential for robust generalization.

- **Graph Coloring Problem:**
 - Trained on valid colorings for individual edges, the approach generalized to coloring entire graphs using a specified number of colors.
 - It **outperformed canonical Graph Neural Networks** (GCN, GAT), specialized GNN-based graph coloring models (GNN-GCP), and even RL-guided methods (XLVIN) across various random graph distributions (Erdos-Renyi, Holme-Kim, Regular Expander) and denser graphs (Paley, Complete graphs, COLOR benchmark).
 - **Generalization:** Notably, while GNN-based methods struggled with increasing graph size, the proposed approach maintained strong performance, demonstrating better generalization to larger and denser graphs (e.g., for the 'homer' graph with 556 nodes, it had 69 conflicting edges vs. GNN-GCP's 667). Comparisons against LLMs (Gemini, Deepseek, Qwen) also showed superiority, especially for larger graphs where LLMs struggled.
 - **Qualitative:** Conflicting edges in a graph were assigned higher energy, accurately reflecting constraint violations.
 - **Scalability:** Increasing particles and timesteps consistently led to a reduction in the number of conflicting edges, yielding more optimal solutions.

- **Crosswords:**
 - Trained on single word generation from hints, the model composed these for a 5x5 crossword grid.
 - It achieved performance **competitive with state-of-the-art LLM inference strategies** like Tree-of-Thought (ToT), achieving a higher letter success rate (80.4% vs. ToT's 78.0%) and a slightly lower word success rate (50.5% vs. ToT's 60.0%). It significantly surpassed standard Input-Output (IO) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting.
 - **Qualitative:** The parallel particles explored different solutions, ultimately converging to valid crossword solutions.

Overall, the main findings consistently demonstrate that compositional energy minimization, combined with PEM, offers a powerful and highly generalizable approach to complex reasoning problems, often surpassing specialized state-of-the-art methods across diverse domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Generalizable Reasoning through Compositional Energy Minimization" represents a significant step forward in the pursuit of more capable and generalizable artificial intelligence. Its primary contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Novel Paradigm for Reasoning Generalization:** The paper introduces a compelling shift from traditional end-to-end, pattern-recognition-based machine learning to a more **compositional and rule-based approach** for reasoning. By learning modular energy functions for subproblems and combining them, the framework offers a principled way to generalize to problems far more complex than those seen during training, a core challenge in AI. This aligns more closely with human cognitive processes for complex problem-solving.
- **Advancement in Energy-Based Models (EBMs):** It successfully re-contextualizes EBMs from primarily generative tasks to **optimization for complex reasoning**. The proposed combination of diffusion and contrastive losses provides a robust training strategy for learning effective energy landscapes that encode problem constraints.
- **Introduction of Parallel Energy Minimization (PEM):** The development of PEM is a crucial methodological innovation. It effectively addresses the challenge of optimizing over highly complex, non-convex energy landscapes formed by the composition of multiple objectives, which are prone to local minima. By maintaining and intelligently resampling parallel particles with adaptive noise, PEM ensures better exploration and convergence to high-quality solutions. Its ability to adapt the computational budget (number of particles) to problem difficulty is also a practical advantage.
- **Empirical Versatility and State-of-the-Art Performance:** The extensive empirical evaluation across diverse, well-known NP-complete and complex reasoning tasks (N-Queens, 3-SAT, Graph Coloring, Crosswords) demonstrates the framework's broad applicability and superior performance compared to specialized and general-purpose baselines. This broad validation highlights the method's potential as a general-purpose reasoning engine.
- **Potential for More Robust and Interpretable AI:** By learning fundamental constraints (the "rules" of a subproblem) rather than opaque end-to-end mappings, the resulting models could offer greater interpretability in how solutions are derived. The modular nature also implies greater robustness as changes in problem structure might only require recompiling existing sub-EBMs rather than retraining an entire monolithic model.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge certain limitations, which also point to avenues for future research:
- **Gaussian Assumption:** The current method assumes a Gaussian distribution for noise and incremental updates. Exploring non-Gaussian objectives and initializations could extend its applicability to different types of solution spaces.
- **Graph Coloring Optimality:** While performing well, there is still room for improvement in achieving optimal solutions for graph coloring, suggesting that further research into EBM training strategies could yield even more accurate energy landscapes.

In conclusion, this paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of machine reasoning, offering a generalizable, compositional, and empirically validated approach that pushes the boundaries of what machine learning models can achieve in solving complex problems. The emphasis on learning underlying rules and combining them provides a promising direction for developing more intelligent and adaptive AI systems.
