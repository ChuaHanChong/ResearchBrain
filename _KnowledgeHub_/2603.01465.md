# Non-Markovian Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation via Keyframe Chaining

> [!summary] Summary
> Keyframe-Chaining VLA tackles non-Markovian long-horizon manipulation by distilling continuous observations into a sparse, event-triggered set of semantic keyframes via a two-stage metric-learning selector, then conditioning a flow-matching policy on these keyframes plus the current frame, reaching 92.0% success versus 57.0% for the best dense-history baseline.

## Problem

- Standard VLA policies condition only on the current or a short dense history window, implicitly assuming Markovian state sufficiency that collapses under state aliasing.
- Extending context windows to capture distant history hits quadratic attention cost, making real-time long-horizon control computationally prohibitive.
- Existing benchmarks like LIBERO, RLBench, and CALVIN remain predominantly Markovian, failing to penalize myopic policies that ignore non-Markovian dependencies.

## Method

- **Keyframe Selection Module** (KSM): a two-stage Task-Modulated architecture that first learns a discriminative embedding via ==triplet-margin metric learning==, then detects semantic transitions with a ==FiLM-modulated query network==.
- **Greedy Temporal Smoothing**: an inference-time stabilization mechanism that caches provisional keyframes and commits them to the history buffer only after scores stay below threshold across a validation window.
- **Keyframe-Chaining Policy**: integrates retrieved sparse keyframes with the current observation as interleaved tokens into a GR00T-N1.5 backbone via context-aware prompting, conditioning a ==Flow Matching== action head.

## Results

- On a new four-task ManiSkill Non-Markovian benchmark, the method achieves **92.0%** average success rate versus **57.0%** for the best fixed-stride GR00T-N1.5 baseline and **15.5-16.0%** for dense-window baselines.
- KSM diagnostics show the two-stage training achieves **97.5%** F1 versus **85.1%** without metric pre-training and **83.4%** for joint end-to-end training, with FNR dropping from **20.4%** to **2.5%**.
- Real-world AgileX Piper trials over 20 episodes per task yield **48.75%** average success rate and **75.3%** completion rate, versus **6.3%**/**43.1%** for GR00T-N1.5 and **3.8%**/**32.6%** for Diffusion Policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fixed-stride sampling cannot generalize across tasks with distinct temporal dynamics; long-horizon memory needs event-driven, semantic-relevance-based retrieval, not just longer windows.
> - Decoupling metric-learning pretraining from query-matching training is critical; joint end-to-end training collapses recall on transient, hard-to-disentangle semantic events.
> - Sparse, discriminative history outperforms dense, noisy windows, suggesting the bottleneck in long-horizon manipulation is semantic relevance of context, not raw memory capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and pervasive challenge in modern robot learning: enabling Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to effectively handle long-horizon, non-Markovian tasks.

**The Rise and Limitations of Current VLA Models:**
The field has witnessed remarkable advancements with VLA models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, GR00T), which integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation. These models have shown impressive capabilities in various control tasks. However, as the paper points out, they remain "shackled by a ‘dense observation’ paradigm." This means they primarily rely on immediate, short, and dense histories of observations, implicitly assuming a Markovian property where the current state is sufficient to determine optimal actions.

**The Non-Markovian Challenge and State Aliasing:**
In reality, many complex manipulation tasks are non-Markovian. Optimal actions often depend on specific past events or states that are no longer visible in the immediate observation—a phenomenon known as "state aliasing." For instance, knowing if an ingredient has been added to a dish requires memory, as the visual state might not change significantly. Current VLA models, with their limited temporal receptive fields, are "myopic" and ill-equipped for such tasks.

**Existing Solutions and Their Shortcomings:**
Researchers have explored several strategies to extend memory in robot policies, but each has limitations:
- **Extending Context Windows:** While theoretically sound, this approach quickly becomes computationally prohibitive due to the quadratic complexity of attention mechanisms in transformer-based models, hindering real-time control.
- **Token Compression (e.g., ContextVLA, HAMLET):** These methods compress observations into compact clusters. However, they often achieve insufficient compression ratios to cover full-episode histories, leading to the loss of critical early cues.
- **Hierarchical Planners (e.g., MemER):** These models use VLMs to generate textual subgoals. While logically robust, they introduce a bottleneck for fine-grained spatial information and incur high inference overhead due to reliance on natural language.
- **Explicit Visual Retention/Memory Banks (e.g., MemoryVLA, TraceVLA, SAM2Act, HiF-VLA):** These approaches construct memory banks or track physical dynamics. However, they can suffer from information attenuation (MemoryVLA) or lack the capacity to abstract high-level task semantics (TraceVLA, SAM2Act, HiF-VLA).
- **Traditional Video Keyframe Selection (e.g., AKS, frame-voyager):** While useful in computer vision for long-context video understanding, these often rely on heavy multimodal encoders or iterative VLM queries, making them too computationally expensive and slow for real-time robotic control. Offline summarization algorithms also conflict with the streaming nature of robotic control.

**The Niche for Keyframe Chaining:**
This paper carves a distinct niche by proposing a *lightweight, real-time, event-driven* mechanism for capturing *sparse semantic history*. Instead of dense, compressed, or text-based memories, it focuses on explicitly selecting and chaining *visual keyframes* that mark critical semantic transitions. This approach aims to provide the VLA policy with a global perspective of the task structure, enabling reasoning over events separated by vast temporal distances without incurring the computational overhead of dense attention or the information loss of over-compression. It directly addresses the "dense observation" paradigm's shortcomings by offering a "sparse, semantic-aware input formulation," thereby pushing the boundaries of VLA generalization to truly long-horizon, non-Markovian robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to robustly generalize to complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks that are characterized by non-Markovian dependencies. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aimed at overcoming critical limitations of existing VLA paradigms:

- **Overcome Reliance on Immediate Observations:** Current VLA models heavily depend on short, dense observation windows. The authors aim to move beyond this "myopic" paradigm, which inherently fails when critical task information is not present in the immediate sensory input.
- **Address Non-Markovian Dependencies and State Aliasing:** The core motivation is to allow robots to remember and utilize specific past states that are crucial for current decision-making, even when the visual scene is ambiguous (state aliasing). This includes scenarios where an optimal action depends on a sequence of events rather than just the current observation.
- **Mitigate Computational Bottlenecks:** While extending the context window could theoretically help, its quadratic computational complexity makes it impractical for real-time robot control. The paper aims to find an efficient way to provide long-term context without hitting this "computational wall."
- **Preserve Fine-Grained Spatial and Temporal Information:** Unlike hierarchical planners that abstract information into natural language, the goal is to retain fine-grained visual information relevant to manipulation while still providing long-horizon context.
- **Bridge the Evaluation Gap in Robot Learning:** The authors highlight a significant lack of benchmarks specifically designed for long-horizon, non-Markovian tasks. Existing benchmarks often have a "Markovian bias," failing to adequately challenge memory-dependent policies. A key motivation is to introduce a rigorous evaluation suite for the community.
- **Develop a Lightweight and Real-Time Solution:** The motivation is to create a system that can operate in closed-loop robotic control scenarios, which demand low latency and efficient processing. This necessitates a lightweight mechanism for historical context retrieval.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose the **Keyframe-Chaining VLA** framework, which seeks to decouple temporal abstraction from action generation, efficiently distill continuous observations into a sparse semantic history, and ground the policy in task topology rather than just instantaneous states.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Keyframe-Chaining VLA framework is designed around two synergistic modules: a lightweight Keyframe Selection Module (KSM) and a Keyframe-Conditioned Action Policy, supported by a novel benchmark suite.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The core idea is to transform the VLA input stream from dense, continuous observations into a sparse, semantic-aware sequence of keyframes and the current observation. This allows the policy to condition on essential historical information without the computational burden of processing redundant frames.

#### 3.2. Keyframe Selection Module (KSM)

The KSM is crucial for distilling continuous visual streams into a "Sparse Semantic History" composed of critical semantic keyframes. It employs a two-stage, Task-Modulated architecture:

- **Stage I: Unified Multi-Task Metric Learning:**
 - **Visual Encoder:** A ResNet-18 (Eθ) is used to map input images into a discriminative feature representation.
 - **Triplet Margin Loss:** This loss function (Ltriplet = max(0, d(Ia, Ip) - d(Ia, In) + m)) is employed to optimize the encoder.
 - **Anchor Selection (Ia):** A ground-truth keyframe is chosen, defined by its task, phase, and episode.
 - **Positive Sampling (Ip):** A frame from a *different episode* but the *same phase* as the anchor is selected. This forces the encoder to learn representations that are invariant to specific episode variations within a phase.
 - **Balanced Negative Sampling (In):** Three types of negatives are sampled with equal probability to ensure robust discrimination:
 - *Temporal Neighbors:* Frames close in time to the anchor within the same episode, to enforce fine-grained discrimination against visually similar, but semantically irrelevant, frames.
 - *Intra-task Phase Negatives:* Keyframes from the same task but a different phase, to capture unique semantic progression within a task.
 - *Inter-task Negatives:* Keyframes from an entirely different task, ensuring global task discriminability.
 - This stage creates a latent space where semantically relevant keyframes are clustered, and irrelevant frames are pushed away.

- **Stage II: Task-Modulated Query Matching Training:**
 - **Frozen Encoder:** The visual encoder from Stage I is frozen.
 - **Local Temporal Context:** A sliding window of features (Hvis) is aggregated using self-attention to incorporate immediate context.
 - **Dynamic Logic Query (qlogic):** This is the "Task-Modulated" component. Learnable embeddings for task identities (e_task) and generic phase concepts (e_phase) are used. The natural language instruction (l) determines the task ID, which is then projected into affine parameters [γ, β] via a generator (gϕ). These parameters then modulate the shared phase embedding (e_phase) using FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) to create a task-specific query.
 - **Matching Score (st):** The query (qlogic) interacts with the aggregated visual features (Hvis) via cross-attention to compute a matching score (st).
 - **Keyframe Detection:** If st exceeds a confidence threshold (τconf), the current observation is identified as a milestone, added to the sparse history buffer, and the phase pointer is updated.
 - **Balanced Training Protocol:** This stage uses a phase-guided balanced sampling strategy with binary labels: positives (ground-truth keyframe and its immediate neighbors) and negatives (randomly sampled in-trajectory frames, and phase-mismatched pairs).

- **Inference and Greedy Smoothing:**
 - To prevent spurious detections, a greedy temporal smoothing mechanism is used. When st > τconf, a provisional keyframe is cached, and a temporal validation window begins. If a stronger candidate appears within the window, it replaces the provisional keyframe, resetting the timer. A keyframe is finalized only if the score remains below τconf for the entire window duration, ensuring stability and accuracy.

#### 3.3. Keyframe-Chaining Policy Architecture

- **Backbone:** The authors leverage GR00T-N1.5, a state-of-the-art generalist VLA policy, as the backbone due to its transformer-based architecture's inherent ability to handle variable-length token sequences.
- **Input Stream:** The policy receives a non-uniform input sequence (I = {o_k1..., o_kn, o_t}) comprising the retrieved historical keyframes and the current observation.
- **Context-Aware Prompting:** A structured textual system prompt is used to explicitly instruct the model on the chronological sequence of multi-view image pairs, guiding its reasoning over temporal relations and task progression.
- **Action Generation:** The semantically aligned input sequence (I) is fed into a Flow Matching action head (DiT) to generate continuous robot actions.

#### 3.4. ManiSkill Benchmark Suite for Long-Horizon Semantic Reasoning

Recognizing the lack of suitable non-Markovian benchmarks, the authors developed and open-sourced a specialized suite built upon the ManiSkill simulator. It features four novel tasks with "Hard Non-Markovian Constraints":
- **Spatial Reconfiguration:** Reconstructing a stack after disassembly, requiring memory of the initial order.
- **Temporal Sequencing:** Performing pick-and-place in a strict color order, where intermediate states are aliased.
- **Counting & Latency:** Pushing a cube only after two light flashes with randomized intervals, requiring state persistence over static periods.
- **Identity Tracking:** Picking a specific (originally central) block after a visually identical set of blocks has been shuffled, demanding object constancy and event recall.

#### 3.5. Real-World Experiments

The approach is validated on an AgileX Piper robotic arm, using 50 expert demonstrations per task to adapt the policy. This demonstrates its practical applicability in noisy, physical environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate the superior performance and robustness of the Keyframe-Chaining VLA framework across both simulated and real-world long-horizon, non-Markovian tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation: Memory Dependence Benchmark

- **Overall Performance:** Keyframe-Chaining VLA achieved a remarkable **92.0% mean success rate** on the custom ManiSkill benchmark, significantly outperforming all baselines. The strongest baseline managed only 57.0%.
- **Failure of Short-Term Baselines:** Policies relying on short, dense observation windows (e.g., Diffusion Policy, π0, GR00T-N1.5 with short-term history, N_h ≤ 3, I = 1) consistently failed, averaging below 30% success. This underscores their fundamental inability to handle long-horizon dependencies and state aliasing.
- **Limitations of Long-Term Fixed-Stride Baselines:** GR00T-N1.5 with long-term fixed-stride sampling (N_h = 3, I > 1) showed an inherent trade-off. While some stride lengths performed better on specific tasks (e.g., I=40 for Spatial Reconfiguration, 60% success; I=20 for Counting, 74% success), no single fixed stride generalized well across all tasks. Coarse strides missed rapid events (e.g., light flashes in Counting), while fine strides failed to cover the entire necessary history for other tasks (e.g., Spatial Reconfiguration). This highlights the inflexibility of fixed-interval sampling for diverse temporal dynamics.
- **Superiority of Event-Driven Retrieval:** In contrast, the Keyframe-Chaining VLA, by explicitly selecting semantically significant frames, achieved consistently high performance, including 100% success rates in Identity Tracking and Counting & Latency tasks. This validates the hypothesis that semantic relevance of retrieved context is more critical than just memory capacity.

#### 4.2. KSM Evaluation and Ablations

- **Efficacy of Two-Stage KSM:** The proposed two-stage KSM design demonstrated near-perfect performance with an **F1-score of 97.5%**. This indicates high precision and recall in detecting critical semantic keyframes.
- **Necessity of Metric Pre-training:** Ablation studies showed that the "Joint End-to-End" training paradigm for KSM suffered from a high False Negative Rate (20.4%), particularly in the challenging Counting task (Recall=40.0%). This confirms that the explicit metric learning (Stage I) is crucial for building a discriminative embedding space that allows the model to disentangle transient semantic events from background noise.
- **Impact of Prompt Refinement:** Context-aware prompting proved essential, particularly for complex tasks like Spatial Reconfiguration, where its absence led to a significant performance drop (from 70% to 56%). This highlights the importance of guiding the VLA model to correctly interpret the structured input sequence.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Decisive Performance Margin:** In real-world trials using an AgileX Piper robotic arm, the Keyframe-Chaining VLA achieved a **48.75% mean success rate** and **75.3% mean completion rate**.
- **Outperforming Baselines:** This significantly surpassed the performance of baselines (DP: 3.8% SR / 32.6% CR; GR00T: 6.3% SR / 43.1% CR).
- **Robustness in Physical Environments:** The results demonstrate the framework's ability to maintain semantic consistency and achieve superior stage-wise progress over long horizons even amidst real-world noise and perceptual disturbances, validating its practical applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Non-Markovian Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation via Keyframe Chaining" paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of robot learning, with the potential to profoundly impact the development of more intelligent and capable robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Fundamental Limitation in Robotics

The most significant impact lies in tackling the pervasive "Markovian bias" in current robot learning, which hinders the generalization of robots to real-world, complex, multi-stage tasks. By enabling VLA models to effectively handle non-Markovian dependencies and state aliasing, this work pushes robots closer to exhibiting genuine long-term memory and reasoning capabilities, moving beyond reactive behaviors.

#### 5.2. Enhancing Efficiency and Scalability of VLA Models

The proposed Keyframe-Chaining VLA framework offers a computationally efficient alternative to existing methods. By replacing dense, redundant observation streams with a sparse, semantically meaningful history, it circumvents the quadratic computational complexity of long-context attention. This efficiency is crucial for enabling real-time, closed-loop control on physical robots, a practical necessity for deployment.

#### 5.3. Enabling More Complex and Adaptive Robot Behaviors

The ability to recall and utilize critical past events allows robots to perform tasks that require:
- **Sequential Logic:** Executing steps in a specific order based on prior completions.
- **Object Constancy:** Tracking objects even when they become visually identical or are occluded.
- **Persistent State Maintenance:** Remembering internal states during visually static or ambiguous periods.
- **Reconfiguration and Planning:** Adapting to changes based on an understanding of the initial state or intermediate transformations.
These capabilities are foundational for advanced robotic applications in manufacturing, service robotics, and even human-robot collaboration, where understanding a task's entire history is often vital.

#### 5.4. Contributing a New Benchmark for Research Advancement

The introduction of the Long-Horizon Memory-dependence Benchmark built on ManiSkill is a substantial contribution to the research community. By providing a standardized, challenging suite of non-Markovian tasks, the authors fill a critical evaluation gap. This benchmark will be invaluable for fostering future research in memory-based robot learning, allowing researchers to rigorously compare and develop new algorithms that truly address temporal reasoning.

#### 5.5. Demonstrated Real-World Applicability

The successful deployment and superior performance in real-world experiments on an AgileX Piper robotic arm underscore the practical viability and robustness of the Keyframe-Chaining VLA. This moves the research beyond simulation, demonstrating its potential for immediate impact in physical robotic systems.

#### 5.6. Future Directions and Roadmap

The paper also constructively points towards future research avenues, such as compressing raw visual keyframes into more compact latent abstractions or discrete semantic tokens. This indicates a clear roadmap for further optimizing memory representation and scalability, promising even more efficient and powerful long-horizon control in the future.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the capabilities of VLA models in robot manipulation by providing an elegant, efficient, and robust solution for handling non-Markovian dependencies. Its dual contributions—a novel methodological framework and a critical new benchmark—are poised to accelerate the development of truly intelligent, memory-aware generalist robots.
