# WAM-Flow: Parallel Coarse-to-Fine Motion Planning via Discrete Flow Matching for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> WAM-Flow develops an end-to-end autonomous driving system using discrete flow matching, achieving a PDMS of 90.3 on NAVSIM-v1 and the lowest average collision rate of 0.23% on nuScenes. The system provides flexible coarse-to-fine planning, enabling a 4.67x inference speedup for rapid approximate plans while maintaining high fidelity.

## Problem

- VLA models for autonomous driving struggle to simultaneously balance expressive reasoning, high-fidelity control, and robust closed-loop performance.
- Autoregressive decoding in VLA models suffers from sequential bottlenecks, slow inference speeds, and accumulated exposure bias errors, limiting real-time application.
- Applying discrete flow matching to VLA faces challenges including data/compute intensity, weak encoding of continuous numerical values in token embeddings, and lack of explicit safety enforcement in likelihood-based training.

## Method

- Formulates ego-trajectory planning as a ==discrete flow matching== problem over a structured token space, supporting parallel denoising and bidirectional refinement for tunable compute-accuracy trade-offs.
- Introduces a metric-aligned ==numerical tokenizer== with a triplet-margin ranking loss to ensure that latent space distances between embeddings accurately reflect underlying scalar differences for continuous motion data.
- Integrates simulator-guided reinforcement learning (GRPO) with a composite reward function to explicitly optimize for safety (no-collision, drivable area compliance) and performance (ego progress, comfort) beyond supervised imitation.

## Results

- Achieved the highest PDMS of 90.3 on NAVSIM-v1 and a superior EPDMS of 84.7 on NAVSIM-v2, outperforming existing autoregressive and diffusion-based VLA models.
- Set a new state-of-the-art with the lowest average collision rate (**0.23%**) among all evaluated VLA methods on the nuScenes dataset, including a **0.00%** collision rate at the 1-second horizon.
- Demonstrated a tunable compute-accuracy trade-off: 1-step denoising achieved competitive performance with a **4.67x** inference speedup (**0.09s**) over strong baselines, while 5-step refinement reached peak performance (90.3 PDMS) matching baseline latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) serves as an effective and efficient new paradigm for VLA models in autonomous driving, enabling parallel generation and coarse-to-fine refinement of trajectories.
> - Preserving metric relationships of continuous values through a specialized tokenizer is crucial for achieving high-precision numerical regression and stable refinement in discrete token-based planning.
> - Combining supervised learning with an online simulator-guided reinforcement learning objective, such as GRPO, effectively aligns the policy with critical safety and performance criteria, leading to more robust closed-loop driving behaviors.
