# WAM-Flow: Parallel Coarse-to-Fine Motion Planning via Discrete Flow Matching for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> WAM-Flow develops an end-to-end autonomous driving system using discrete flow matching, achieving a PDMS of 90.3 on NAVSIM-v1 and the lowest average collision rate of 0.23% on nuScenes. The system provides flexible coarse-to-fine planning, enabling a 4.67x inference speedup for rapid approximate plans while maintaining high fidelity.

## Problem

- VLA models for autonomous driving struggle to simultaneously balance expressive reasoning, high-fidelity control, and robust closed-loop performance.
- Autoregressive decoding in VLA models suffers from sequential bottlenecks, slow inference speeds, and accumulated exposure bias errors, limiting real-time application.
- Applying discrete flow matching to VLA faces challenges including data/compute intensity, weak encoding of continuous numerical values in token embeddings, and lack of explicit safety enforcement in likelihood-based training.

## Method

- Formulates ego-trajectory planning as a ==discrete flow matching== problem over a structured token space, supporting parallel denoising and bidirectional refinement for tunable compute-accuracy trade-offs.
- Introduces a metric-aligned ==numerical tokenizer== with a triplet-margin ranking loss to ensure that latent space distances between embeddings accurately reflect underlying scalar differences for continuous motion data.
- Integrates simulator-guided reinforcement learning (GRPO) with a composite reward function to explicitly optimize for safety (no-collision, drivable area compliance) and performance (ego progress, comfort) beyond supervised imitation.

## Results

- Achieved the highest PDMS of 90.3 on NAVSIM-v1 and a superior EPDMS of 84.7 on NAVSIM-v2, outperforming existing autoregressive and diffusion-based VLA models.
- Set a new state-of-the-art with the lowest average collision rate (**0.23%**) among all evaluated VLA methods on the nuScenes dataset, including a **0.00%** collision rate at the 1-second horizon.
- Demonstrated a tunable compute-accuracy trade-off: 1-step denoising achieved competitive performance with a **4.67x** inference speedup (**0.09s**) over strong baselines, while 5-step refinement reached peak performance (90.3 PDMS) matching baseline latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) serves as an effective and efficient new paradigm for VLA models in autonomous driving, enabling parallel generation and coarse-to-fine refinement of trajectories.
> - Preserving metric relationships of continuous values through a specialized tokenizer is crucial for achieving high-precision numerical regression and stable refinement in discrete token-based planning.
> - Combining supervised learning with an online simulator-guided reinforcement learning objective, such as GRPO, effectively aligns the policy with critical safety and performance criteria, leading to more robust closed-loop driving behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of end-to-end autonomous driving has seen rapid advancements, primarily driven by the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to directly map raw sensor inputs (like camera images) and high-level natural language instructions to precise ego-vehicle motion plans. The research landscape is broadly categorized into two paradigms:

- **Dual-system methods:** These approaches typically use autoregressive (AR) Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as high-level reasoning modules to extract driving intent or scene summaries. The outputs from these VLMs then feed into separate downstream motion planning networks, often employing diffusion-based iterative optimization to generate smooth trajectories. Examples include methods that combine VLMs with diffusion planners.
- **Single-system approaches:** These models unify reasoning and planning within a single VLM by reformulating trajectory or action prediction as a text generation problem. EMMA and DrivingGPT are prominent examples, where actions are generated sequentially in a linguistic space.

Existing AR models, while strong in causal reasoning, suffer from **sequential decoding bottlenecks** and **accumulated exposure bias errors**, leading to slow inference speeds and potential instability in closed-loop control. Diffusion-based methods address some of these limitations by enabling parallel sampling for denoising, but often lack explicit reasoning interpretability.

**WAM-Flow introduces discrete flow matching (DFM) as a novel alternative paradigm for VLA policies in end-to-end autonomous driving.** Discrete flow matching, an emerging area in generative modeling, models probability transport over discrete token spaces via a continuous-time Markov chain. Unlike autoregressive decoders, DFM supports fully parallel denoising and bidirectional refinement during generation. This allows for a **coarse-to-fine planning capability**, where initial coarse motion hypotheses can be rapidly generated and then refined through additional denoising steps, offering a tunable compute–accuracy trade-off. This flexibility is highly advantageous for autonomous driving, where different scenarios demand varying levels of planning precision and computational urgency.

By demonstrating the effectiveness of DFM in autonomous driving, WAM-Flow carves out a new research direction, positioning itself as a promising approach that potentially combines the benefits of parallel generation (like diffusion models) with a structured, controllable generation process (unlike purely sequential AR models), while explicitly addressing the metric-alignment of continuous control signals. The paper also integrates **reinforcement learning (RL) with simulator guidance (GRPO)**, building on recent trends in using RL to align large models with safety and performance objectives in driving, further differentiating it from purely supervised methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of WAM-Flow is to develop a VLA model for end-to-end autonomous driving that overcomes the limitations of current autoregressive and diffusion-based approaches, specifically by leveraging discrete flow matching. The motivation stems from three critical challenges:

1. **Balancing expressive reasoning, high-fidelity control, and robust closed-loop performance:** Existing VLA models struggle to simultaneously excel in these three aspects. Autoregressive models provide reasoning but are slow for high-fidelity control, while diffusion models offer high-fidelity control but often lack explicit interpretability. WAM-Flow aims for a policy representation that inherently supports all three.
2. **Addressing the limitations of autoregressive decoding:** Autoregressive models generate trajectories token by token, leading to sequential bottlenecks, slow inference, and exposure bias errors. The paper seeks a paradigm that enables **parallel, bidirectional denoising and coarse-to-fine refinement**, allowing for flexible trade-offs between computational efficiency and prediction fidelity. This is particularly crucial for autonomous driving, where latency and accuracy are highly context-dependent.
3. **Tackling the specific challenges of applying discrete flow matching (DFM) to VLA for autonomous driving:**
 - **Data and compute intensity of DFM training:** Training DFM from scratch is resource-intensive, and existing general-purpose VLMs lack sufficient road-scene competence. The objective is to devise an effective **multi-stage adaptation strategy** to transfer knowledge from pre-trained VLMs and enhance domain-specific understanding.
 - **Weak encoding of metric relationships by standard text token embeddings:** Continuous numerical values (e.g., position, velocity) are critical for high-precision motion planning, but standard text tokenizers do not preserve their metric structure, hindering accurate regression. A key objective is to develop a **metric-aligned numerical tokenizer** that faithfully reflects underlying scalar differences in the latent space.
 - **Lack of explicit safety, ego-progress, and comfort enforcement in likelihood-based training:** Supervised training focuses on mimicking expert trajectories but doesn't guarantee desirable closed-loop behaviors. The motivation is to integrate a **simulator-guided reinforcement learning objective (GRPO)** that explicitly optimizes for these critical driving objectives while retaining parallel generation capabilities.

In essence, WAM-Flow aims to establish discrete flow matching as a new, promising paradigm for end-to-end autonomous driving, delivering a reliable and scalable system with tunable compute-accuracy trade-offs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WAM-Flow formulates ego-trajectory planning as a **discrete flow matching (DFM) problem over a structured token space**. The methodology consists of several interconnected components:

- **Problem Formulation:** The task is framed as conditional sequence generation, mapping multimodal inputs (front-view image, natural-language navigation, ego-vehicle states) to a discrete token sequence representing an 8-waypoint future trajectory spanning 4 seconds. The core idea is that a flow network learns to transport a simple prior distribution in the discrete token space to the expert trajectory distribution.

- **Metric-Aligned Numerical Tokenizer:** To handle the continuous nature of motion planning data (position, heading, velocity, acceleration), the authors introduce a specialized tokenizer.
 - Continuous scalars are discretized into a uniform codebook over a range (e.g., [-100, 100] with 0.01 resolution, yielding 20,001 tokens).
 - Each scalar token is mapped to an L2-normalized embedding.
 - A **triplet-margin ranking loss** ($L_{num}$) is applied during training. This loss ensures that the Euclidean distances between embeddings in the latent space are monotonic with respect to the underlying scalar differences. This creates a "numerically coherent token space" crucial for stable coarse-to-fine refinement.

- **Geometry-Aware Discrete Flow Matching Objective:**
 - The model learns to predict a velocity field that transports a simple base distribution to the complex data distribution of expert trajectories.
 - A conditional probability path, inspired by Gibbs distributions, is designed, where the probability of a token is inversely proportional to its "distance" from the target token, using a learned distance metric $d(x, x_1)$. This distance metric is a weighted sum of coordinate-wise dissimilarities, incorporating the tokenizer-induced distances for numerical values.
 - The model is trained to approximate the true posterior distribution of the target token ($p_{1|t}(x_1|x)$) by minimizing a conditional flow matching cross-entropy loss ($L_{CE}$). This formulation directly supports parallel token transitions during generation.

- **Model Architecture:**
 - WAM-Flow adapts a **Janus-1.5B multimodal backbone**, a pre-trained VLM, for VLA planning.
 - Images are processed by SigLIP and aligned to the Janus text-token space via an MLP.
 - The Janus tokenizer is extended with the 20,001 numerically grounded tokens from the metric-aligned tokenizer for input ego-state and output waypoint coordinates.
 - The decoder's text head is expanded to this enlarged vocabulary and trained with the DFM objective.

- **Simulator-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - To overcome the limitation of supervised training (not explicitly enforcing safety, comfort, and progress), an online GRPO reinforcement learning stage is introduced.
 - **Reward Design:** A composite reward function $R(\tau)$ is designed, decomposing NAVSIM's PDMS metrics into multiplicative safety penalties (No-Collision, Drivable Area Compliance) and an additive weighted average of performance objectives (Ego Progress, Time-to-Collision, Comfort). The multiplicative safety term ensures strict constraint satisfaction.
 - **GRPO Objective:** For a given scene, multiple candidate trajectories are sampled in parallel from the current policy. A group baseline (average reward of the group) is used to reduce variance. The GRPO objective maximizes a clipped importance-sampled reward while applying a KL regularization term to prevent large policy shifts from the supervised reference, thereby stabilizing training and preserving parallel generation.

- **Multi-Stage Training Curriculum:**
 1. **Numerical Embedding Training:** Initialize numerical embeddings randomly, freeze VLA backbone. Train numerical embeddings and language-model head on nuPlan dataset using $L_{CE}$ and $L_{num}$ for 4 epochs.
 2. **VQA Pre-training:** Enhance road-scene perception. Train the VLA (with the new embeddings) using $L_{CE}$ for 3 epochs on a large-scale VQA dataset (general multimodal VQA + driving-specific VQA).
 3. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Fine-tune the VLA backbone on the nuPlan dataset with $L_{CE}$ for 2 epochs.
 4. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Online GRPO with simulator feedback (NAVSIM dataset) for 0.5 epoch, maximizing $L_{GRPO}$ with KL regularization towards the SFT policy.

- **Inference:** Utilizes Euler discretization over the time interval. Initial tokens are sampled uniformly. At each step, a target token is drawn from the model's posterior, and a transition rate is computed. Tokens either transition to a new state (sampled proportionally to rates) or remain unchanged, based on a random variable and the transition rate. This parallel denoising process is repeated for a tunable number of steps (n), yielding the final trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WAM-Flow demonstrates strong performance across multiple benchmarks and provides valuable insights into the utility of discrete flow matching for autonomous driving.

- **Superior Closed-Loop Performance on NAVSIM-v1:** WAM-Flow achieves the highest PDMS of **90.3**, outperforming both autoregressive (e.g., AutoVLA 89.1, RecogDrive 89.6) and diffusion-based VLA models. It shows superior performance in critical safety metrics: **No-Collision (99.2%)** and **Drivable Area Compliance (98.3%)**. This is particularly notable as WAM-Flow uses only a single front-view camera, while many baselines rely on multi-view setups or LiDAR.
- **Leading Performance on NAVSIM-v2:** On the more comprehensive NAVSIM-v2 benchmark, WAM-Flow achieves a superior overall **EPDMS of 84.7**. It also leads in specific sub-metrics such as No-Collision (98.5%), Driving Direction Compliance (99.5%), and Lane Keeping (97.4%), highlighting its robustness in diverse and dynamic scenarios.
- **State-of-the-Art Collision Rate on nuScenes:** Under the UniAD metrics, WAM-Flow sets a new state-of-the-art with the **lowest average collision rate (0.23%)** among all evaluated VLA methods on the nuScenes dataset. It also achieves a perfect 0.00% collision rate at the 1-second horizon, demonstrating superior short-term safety.
- **Tunable Compute-Accuracy Trade-off (Coarse-to-Fine Planning):** A key finding is the flexibility of WAM-Flow's coarse-to-fine sampling.
 - With **1-step denoising**, it achieves competitive performance (89.1 PDMS) with a significant **inference speedup of 4.67× over RecogDrive** (0.09s vs 0.42s). This allows for rapid approximate plans in straightforward scenarios.
 - With **5-step refinement**, it reaches its peak performance (90.3 PDMS) while matching RecogDrive's latency. This demonstrates that more complex situations benefit from higher-precision refinement, without incurring excessive computational cost compared to strong baselines.
- **Impact of Proposed Components (Ablation Studies):**
 - The **metric-aligned numerical tokenizer** significantly improves PDMS by 4.9 points over standard text tokenization and an additional 2.3 points with metric-aligned embeddings, underscoring its necessity for precise numerical regression.
 - **Large-scale VQA pretraining** provides a substantial gain of 3.3 points in PDMS, highlighting the importance of domain-specific cross-modal adaptation for road-scene competence.
 - **Simulator-guided GRPO** contributes significantly, achieving the highest PDMS of 90.3, validating the critical role of aligning the policy with explicit safety and performance objectives through reinforcement learning.
 - Optimal **GRPO group size (3)** and balanced **reward weighting (5:5:2 for EP:TTC:Comfort)** are crucial for maximizing performance, demonstrating the sensitivity and importance of proper RL setup.

These results collectively establish discrete flow matching as an effective and efficient paradigm for end-to-end autonomous driving, offering superior safety, performance, and flexibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WAM-Flow's introduction of discrete flow matching (DFM) as a paradigm for end-to-end autonomous driving represents a significant advancement with several potential impacts:

- **New Paradigm for Autonomous Driving VLAs:** This work establishes DFM as a viable and highly competitive alternative to existing autoregressive and diffusion-based VLA models. Its ability to perform fully parallel, bidirectional denoising opens new avenues for designing more efficient and flexible autonomous driving systems.
- **Flexible Compute-Accuracy Trade-off:** The inherent coarse-to-fine refinement capability of WAM-Flow is a critical advantage. This allows autonomous systems to adapt their planning precision and computational load based on real-time driving complexity and available resources. In simple scenarios, rapid, coarse plans can be generated quickly, while complex or safety-critical situations can trigger more detailed, multi-step refinement, optimizing for both latency and safety. This tunable design is vital for real-world deployment.
- **Enhanced Safety and Performance:** By integrating a metric-aligned numerical tokenizer and simulator-guided GRPO with a composite reward function, WAM-Flow achieves superior scores in safety-critical metrics (No-Collision, Drivable Area Compliance) and overall driving performance (PDMS, EPDMS). This structured approach to continuous control and safety alignment can lead to more reliable and trustworthy autonomous driving systems.
- **Improved Efficiency:** The parallel nature of DFM inference provides significant speedups compared to autoregressive models, which are bottlenecked by sequential decoding. This efficiency is crucial for real-time decision-making in autonomous vehicles, allowing for quicker responses and better anticipation.
- **Bridging Continuous and Discrete Spaces:** The metric-aligned numerical tokenizer is an innovative solution for effectively representing continuous control signals within a discrete token space. This method addresses a fundamental challenge in VLA models where high-precision numerical regression is required, opening doors for better integration of continuous control with large language models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** WAM-Flow's success paves the way for further research into DFM for robotics and control. Future work can explore extending the framework to variable-horizon planning, incorporating more diverse multimodal sensor inputs (LiDAR, radar, etc.) for enhanced perception and robustness, and developing more generalizable world models to reduce reliance on specific simulators. Ultimately, real-world deployment and rigorous testing will be essential to validate its performance under actual driving conditions, moving this promising paradigm from simulation to practical application.

In conclusion, WAM-Flow offers a robust, efficient, and flexible approach to autonomous driving, leveraging the power of discrete flow matching to achieve superior safety and performance while offering a desirable compute-accuracy trade-off.
