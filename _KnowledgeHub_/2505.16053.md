# Learning from Algorithm Feedback: One-Shot SAT Solver Guidance with GNNs

> [!summary] Summary
> The RLAF paradigm enables a Graph Neural Network (GNN) to learn one-shot guidance for SAT solvers by optimizing directly on computational cost. This approach accelerates established SAT solvers by up to 69% on certain problem types, generalizing to larger instances and outperforming supervised learning methods.

## Problem

- Hand-crafted branching heuristics in SAT solvers are rigid, require extensive expert knowledge, and often do not adapt well across diverse problem instances.
- Existing machine learning approaches either rely on costly expert-defined labels (supervised learning) or incur prohibitive computational overhead due to per-decision inference (reinforcement learning).
- Suboptimal heuristic design limits the full efficiency potential of complete search algorithms for NP-complete problems like SAT.

## Method

- Propose the Reinforcement Learning from ==Algorithm Feedback== (**RLAF**) paradigm, directly optimizing a GNN policy to minimize SAT solver computational cost.
- Design a "one-shot" GNN that generates a full set of variable weights and polarities for a SAT instance in a single forward pass before solver execution.
- Integrate these learned weights and polarities into existing solver branching heuristics (e.g., VSIDS, look-ahead) as a multiplicative factor and direct polarity choice.

## Results

- RLAF-guided Glucose solver achieved mean runtime reductions of up to **69%** on satisfiable 3SAT(400) problems and consistently accelerated solvers across 3-coloring and cryptographic instances.
- The approach consistently outperformed supervised learning baselines for guiding SAT solvers, demonstrating the efficacy of direct optimization on solver cost.
- Learned policies generalized from relatively "easy" training instances to significantly harder and larger problems, with GNN inference time being negligible compared to solver runtime.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct optimization on solver performance via RLAF can yield more effective guidance than supervised learning based on proxy labels.
> - A single GNN inference at the beginning of the solver run is sufficient to provide effective, global guidance and overcome the computational bottleneck of per-decision deep learning.
> - Policies trained on simpler problem instances generalize effectively to larger and more complex problems, demonstrating the transferability of learned strategies.
