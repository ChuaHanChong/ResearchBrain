# Efficient and Reliable Teleoperation through Real-to-Sim-to-Real Shared Autonomy

> [!summary] Summary
> Residual Copilot is a real-to-sim-to-real shared-autonomy framework for contact-rich teleoperation. Under five minutes of real teleoperation data fits a non-parametric k-nearest-neighbor human surrogate, which drives PPO training of a residual copilot in Isaac Lab that adds corrective deltas to operator commands. Deployed zero-shot, it raises success and cuts completion time on NIST-board gear meshing, nut threading, and peg insertion.

## Problem

- Expert-prior shared autonomy needs a competent autonomous policy first, shifting the burden onto the very problem teleoperation was meant to sidestep.
- Copilot-learning methods instead demand extensive human-in-the-loop training or large datasets for an accurate human surrogate, and rarely reach real-world contact-rich deployment.
- Parametric behavioral-cloning pilots are data-hungry and brittle under copilot-induced distribution shift, while abstract noisy or laggy pilots generalize poorly to real operators.

## Method

- **kNN Pilot**: ==non-parametric surrogate== retrieving demonstrated commands under a weighted translation, rotation, gripper distance, with ==action chunking== and smooth gated perturbations for coherence under shift.
- **Residual Copilot**: ==Gaussian MLP policy== trained by ==PPO== on sparse success plus shaping rewards, emitting scaled deltas composed additively for translation and gripper, multiplicatively for rotation.
- ==Real-to-sim-to-real calibration== tunes PID gains, admittance stiffness, damping, inertia, friction and center of mass to real trajectories, then randomizes controller and pose-estimation error for transfer.

## Results

- Simulation cross-pilot evaluation: the copilot reaches **1.00** gear, **0.99** peg, **0.81** nut task progression under the kNN pilot versus **0.86**, **0.46**, **0.02** for Residual BC.
- User study with **sixteen** participants: nut threading success improves by up to **30%**, completion times fall, NASA-TLX workload drops and satisfaction rises over direct teleoperation.
- Downstream diffusion policy trained on copilot data hits **19/20** grasps and **9/20** insertions under matched successes, against **6/20** and **0/20** for teleoperation data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Retrieval-based surrogates beat parametric ones in low-data regimes: staying inside empirical support keeps the human model coherent when the assistant shifts the distribution.
> - Residual corrections split roles cleanly: humans keep sequencing and intent, the policy handles millimeter alignment and contact, so assistance transfers across operator skill levels.
> - Assistance improves data, not just execution; consistent trajectories train better imitation policies at equal success counts. Caution: user co-adaptation under assistance stays unmodeled.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Teleoperation plays a role in complex robotic manipulation tasks and is a method for collecting demonstration data to train visuomotor policies. However, for tasks requiring fine-grained, contact-rich, and high-precision actions, teleoperation can be slow, prone to errors, and unreliable, even for experienced operators. This limitation is attributed to a mismatch between human capabilities and the demands of continuous, low-level control through teleoperation interfaces, which often suffer from limited viewpoints, latency, and the embodiment gap. Such challenges make millimeter-scale alignment and contact regulation difficult for direct human control.

Shared autonomy has emerged as an approach to mitigate these issues by integrating human intent with automated assistance. Prior work in shared autonomy broadly falls into two categories:
1. **Expert-prior shared autonomy:** These methods blend human commands with an autonomous prior, such as a pre-trained policy or planner. A limitation of this approach is its reliance on the availability of a competent autonomous policy, which itself can be a significant research challenge. Furthermore, blending human input with an autonomous policy does not inherently guarantee that the resulting behavior remains on the intended motion manifold.
2. **Copilot learning:** This category involves learning assistance directly as a copilot, conditioned on both the pilot command and the system state, often using reinforcement learning or residual control formulations. While these methods do not presuppose an expert autonomous prior and aim to preserve human intent, they frequently require extensive human-in-the-loop training or large datasets to accurately model human behavior, posing challenges for practical deployment in complex real-world scenarios. Parametric behavioral cloning (BC) pilots, in particular, can be data-intensive and brittle under distribution shifts.

This work contributes to the copilot learning paradigm by addressing the "pilot model bottleneck." Rather than assuming an expert autonomous policy or requiring extensive data for a parametric human model, the proposed framework introduces a lightweight, non-parametric k-nearest-neighbor (kNN) human surrogate. This surrogate is constructed from a minimal amount of real-world teleoperation data. By doing so, the research aims to enable stable simulation-based training of a residual copilot policy that can effectively transfer to real-world, fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation tasks. The approach extends previous work in residual shared autonomy, which primarily focused on simulation, to demonstrate real-world performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a shared autonomy framework that enhances the efficiency and reliability of teleoperation for fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is supported by several specific objectives and motivations:

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Improve Teleoperation Performance:** The researchers aim to demonstrate that their system can increase task success rates and reduce execution times for human operators, including both novices and experienced users, in tasks that demand high precision and delicate contact handling, such as nut threading, gear meshing, and peg insertion.
2. **Develop a Data-Efficient Human Surrogate:** A critical objective is to create a model of human behavior that can effectively drive simulation-based copilot training. This model must be lightweight, non-parametric, and capable of being accurately constructed from a very limited amount of real-world teleoperation data (specifically, less than five minutes). The surrogate must also maintain coherence under the distribution shifts that arise during the copilot's exploratory training.
3. **Enable Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The framework is designed to allow a residual copilot policy to be trained entirely within simulation, leveraging model-free reinforcement learning, and subsequently deployed directly into the physical world with effective performance. This necessitates careful digitalization of the task environment and modeling of system dynamics, as well as the human surrogate itself.
4. **Preserve Human Intent:** The proposed residual formulation aims to ensure that the automated assistance provides low-level corrective behaviors that augment human commands rather than overriding them. This allows the human operator to maintain a sense of high-level control and intent.
5. **Enhance Demonstration Quality for Imitation Learning:** An additional objective is to investigate whether teleoperation performed with the assistance of the residual copilot yields higher-quality, more consistent demonstration data. Such data could subsequently improve the performance of downstream imitation learning policies, making data collection for autonomous system training more efficient.

**Motivation:**

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in robotic manipulation and existing solutions:

- **Limitations of Direct Teleoperation:** For tasks requiring high precision in dynamic environments or delicate contact, human operators often struggle. This is due to factors such as restricted visual feedback, communication latency, and the physical mismatch (embodiment gap) between human and robot kinematics/dynamics. These limitations lead to slow, error-prone, and unreliable task execution, particularly for millimeter-scale alignment and force control.
- **Bottleneck in Data Collection for Autonomy:** The difficulty of performing high-quality teleoperation directly translates into a bottleneck for collecting robust and diverse demonstration data. Such data is foundational for training modern visuomotor policies, making improved teleoperation a prerequisite for scalable robotic learning.
- **Deficiencies in Current Shared Autonomy Approaches:**
 - Approaches relying on *expert priors* for assistance are often impractical because acquiring a competent autonomous policy is frequently as complex as solving the original task.
 - Existing *copilot learning* methods typically require either extensive human-in-the-loop training, which is costly and time-consuming, or large datasets to learn a parametric model of human behavior. These parametric models can be brittle and prone to failure when faced with slight deviations or novel scenarios, limiting their real-world applicability.
- **Need for Preserving Human Intent:** A shared autonomy system should assist without completely taking over or drastically altering the human's intended motion. Learning *residual* corrective behaviors is a natural way to achieve this, allowing the human to focus on strategy while the copilot handles low-level robustness.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for a practical, data-efficient, and robust shared autonomy solution that can bridge the gap between human capabilities and robotic precision in challenging manipulation tasks, while also facilitating the collection of high-quality data for autonomous learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a "real-to-sim-to-real" shared autonomy framework, designed to leverage the scalability of simulation for training a residual copilot policy that assists human teleoperation in real-world, contact-rich tasks.

**Problem Definition and Residual Control:**
The problem is formalized as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The full state includes the environment state (`s_t`) and the human pilot's unobservable latent goal (`g`). The human pilot provides a goal-implicit control command (`a_h_t`) comprising a target task-space end-effector pose and a gripper command. The copilot, observing the environment state and human input (`o_t = (s_t, a_h_t)`), learns a residual policy (`π_r`) to predict a normalized corrective action (`a_res_t`). The final executed action (`a_t`) is a composition of the human's base action and the scaled residual action (`a_t = a_h_t ⊕ αa_res_t`), where `⊕` denotes additive composition for translation/gripper and multiplicative for rotation. The residual policy is parameterized as a Gaussian policy with an MLP backbone and optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a model-free reinforcement learning algorithm. The reward function is decomposed into general objectives (`R_general`) and a task-specific sparse success signal (`R_success`).

**Human Surrogate Model (kNN Pilot):**
To facilitate scalable training in simulation without requiring human-in-the-loop interaction, a human surrogate is employed. The proposed **kNN Pilot** is a non-parametric k-nearest-neighbor model. It is constructed from less than five minutes of real-world teleoperation data, which may include unsuccessful demonstrations.
- **Rationale:** The kNN approach is chosen because it avoids extrapolation beyond the empirical data support, which contributes to stable behavior under the moderate distribution shifts induced by copilot exploration. It is also designed to be data-efficient.
- **Action Retrieval:** For a given state, the kNN Pilot retrieves the `k` nearest demonstrated commands based on a weighted distance metric that considers translational, rotational (quaternion geodesic distance), and gripper components of end-effector commands. A temperature-scaled softmax over negative distances is used to sample one of these neighbors.
- **Robustness Mechanisms:**
 1. **Action Chunking:** To preserve temporal consistency and prevent erratic switching between neighbors, the surrogate outputs a short sequence (chunk) of consecutive demonstrated commands from the selected neighbor.
 2. **Smooth Local Perturbations:** To expand coverage and improve robustness under distribution shifts, localized, temporally correlated noise is injected into the kNN-retrieved commands. A Bernoulli gate controls the activation frequency and the smooth rise/decay of these noisy phases.

**Admittance Control:**
To enable safe and compliant interactions crucial for contact-rich tasks, commands are executed with task-space admittance control. This mechanism models the robot end-effector's response to external forces using a virtual spring-mass-damper system, allowing it to yield under contact while tracking goal commands. The robot's built-in FT-sensor admittance mode is utilized.

**Real-to-Sim-to-Real Pipeline:**
The methodology integrates real-world data collection, simulation-based training, and real-world deployment.
- **Real-to-Sim Stage:**
 1. **Human Surrogate Construction:** The kNN Pilot is directly fit using a small set of real teleoperation demonstrations.
 2. **Environment Digitalization and Calibration:** The real-world task environment is replicated in simulation. Crucially, low-level control parameters (e.g., joint-space PID gains, task-space admittance gains) and physical parameters affecting contact dynamics (e.g., friction coefficients, object center of mass) are calibrated to match observed real-world trajectories, enhancing simulation fidelity.
- **Sim-to-Real Stage:**
 1. **Domain Randomization (DMR):** To improve robustness and facilitate transfer to the real world, domain randomization is applied during simulation training. This involves randomizing controller parameters to account for potential discrepancies between simulation and hardware, randomizing object pose observations to model real-world pose estimation errors, and randomizing initial configurations to promote generalization. Additionally, the human surrogate itself is randomized by injecting temporally correlated residual noise to enhance robustness.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted using a UFactory xArm7 robotic manipulator controlled via GELLO for teleoperation. A wrist-mounted force-torque sensor enabled admittance control, and an Intel RealSense D455 camera with FoundationPose was used for object pose estimation. The simulation environment leveraged NVIDIA Isaac Lab. The tasks included three fine-grained, contact-rich assembly operations: Gear Meshing, Nut Threading, and Peg Insertion. A user study involving 16 participants (novice and experienced) was performed in the real world, comparing direct teleoperation with copilot-assisted teleoperation, followed by subjective workload (NASA-TLX) and user satisfaction questionnaires.

**Baselines for Comparison:**
To evaluate the proposed Residual Copilot, several baselines were implemented:
- **Pilot Baselines:**
 - **BC Pilot:** A behavioral surrogate trained via imitation learning (Diffusion Policy) on augmented real teleoperation data.
 - **Expert Pilot:** A Diffusion Policy trained on a large dataset (2000 successful rollouts) generated by the Residual Copilot itself, serving as a near-optimal reference.
 - **Laggy Pilot / Noisy Pilot:** Abstract pilots designed to simulate common human teleoperation imperfections.
- **Copilot Baselines:**
 - **GD Copilots (Guided Diffusion):** Two variants of diffusion-based copilots (GD BC and GD Expert) that denoise actions conditioned on pilot commands, differing in their training data optimality.
 - **Residual BC:** A residual reinforcement learning copilot trained to assist the BC Pilot, which differs from the proposed method only in the choice of human surrogate model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from real-world user studies and simulation experiments, addressing the effectiveness of the Residual Copilot, the performance of the kNN human surrogate, and the quality of data for downstream imitation learning.

#### 4.1. Copilot Performance (Q1: Can our Residual Copilot improve human operator performance?)

- **Objective Task Performance:** The Residual Copilot consistently improved teleoperation performance across three fine-grained, contact-rich assembly tasks: Gear Meshing, Nut Threading, and Peg Insertion.
 - **Increased Success Rates:** The system yielded higher task success rates compared to direct teleoperation. For example, in Nut Threading, it improved success by up to 30%, largely by stabilizing orientation and amplifying rotational commands, which are critical for maintaining thread engagement. In Gear Meshing, it facilitated precise insertion and subtle rotational corrections, reducing alignment errors. For Peg Insertion, it stabilized the end-effector at pre-grasp poses, enhancing grasp reliability.
 - **Improved Efficiency:** The Residual Copilot generally reduced task completion times. This reduction was attributed to fewer failed attempts, less need for corrective motions, and streamlined recovery behaviors, as illustrated qualitatively in task overlays.
- **Subjective User Experience:** Participant feedback supported the objective performance gains.
 - **Reduced Workload:** Operators reported lower workload across multiple NASA-TLX subscales, including mental, physical, and temporal demand, effort, and frustration.
 - **Increased Satisfaction:** Participants indicated higher overall satisfaction with the copilot-assisted method, rating it higher for aspects like "correct intent," "assist helpfully," "would reuse," "safety," "trust," and "ease of use."
- **Conclusion:** The Residual Copilot demonstrated improved task outcomes, reduced cognitive and physical burden for operators, and maintained an intuitive division of control, validating its effectiveness for fine-grained manipulation.

#### 4.2. Human Surrogate Analysis (Q2: Does our kNN Pilot outperform prior human surrogates and baselines?)

- **Real-World Comparison (Residual Copilot vs. Residual BC):** When compared to Residual BC (which uses a parametric behavioral cloning pilot) in the real-world gear meshing task, the Residual Copilot (using the kNN Pilot) achieved better objective performance (success rate and completion time) and received higher subjective ratings, particularly regarding "correct intent." This suggests that the kNN Pilot, by staying within empirical data support, enabled more stable exploration and better generalization, leading to more reliable and intent-preserving corrections.
- **Simulation-Based Cross-Pilot Generalization (Table I):** Extensive simulation experiments evaluated copilots trained with different pilot models against various evaluation pilots, assessing robustness to pilot mismatch.
 - **Robustness of Residual Copilot (Ours):** The Residual Copilot achieved the highest in-distribution performance and exhibited the smallest performance degradation when evaluated with unseen pilot models (e.g., Laggy, Noisy, Expert, BC Pilots). This indicates its robustness to distribution shifts and varied human behaviors.
 - **Limitations of Residual BC:** Residual BC consistently showed lower task progression under suboptimal evaluation pilots, implying it struggled to generalize when the training pilot differed from the deployment pilot, potentially due to brittle extrapolation beyond its training distribution.
 - **Sensitivity of GD Copilots:** Guided Diffusion (GD) Copilots were highly sensitive to the pilot model. The GD Expert, for instance, sometimes performed worse than 'No Copilot' even with the Expert Pilot, suggesting that test-time guidance could steer actions away from task-optimal regions if the underlying pilot distribution was suboptimal. This highlights the challenge of reconciling action optimality with human intent preservation in such methods.
- **Conclusion:** The lightweight kNN Pilot proved more robust and generalizable than parametric behavioral cloning pilots and guided diffusion approaches, especially under distribution shifts common in shared autonomy.

#### 4.3. Data Quality Comparison (Q3: Does copilot-assisted data lead to improved downstream imitation policy performance?)

- **Experiment:** Vision-based Diffusion Policies (DPs) were trained on demonstration data collected under Residual Copilot assistance versus direct teleoperation for gear meshing, under two conditions: matched total attempts and matched successful trials.
- **Results:**
 - **Matched Attempts:** With the same number of teleoperation trials, the DP trained on copilot-assisted data achieved substantially higher grasp and insertion success rates (e.g., 18/20 grasp, 11/20 insertion) compared to the DP trained on direct teleoperation data (7/20 grasp, 1/20 insertion). This suggests that copilot assistance makes data collection more efficient by yielding more successful and learnable trajectories within a given effort budget.
 - **Matched Successes:** Even when both datasets contained the exact same number of successful demonstrations, the DP trained on copilot-assisted data outperformed the DP trained on direct teleoperation data (19/20 grasp, 9/20 insertion vs. 6/20 grasp, 0/20 insertion).
- **Conclusion:** This finding indicates a qualitative difference in the collected data. Copilot-assisted demonstrations are more consistent in alignment, contact timing, and approach strategies, while still reflecting human intent. This consistency reduces trajectory variability, making the action distribution easier for imitation learning policies to model and generalize from, thereby improving the learnability and consistency of downstream policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a real-to-sim-to-real shared autonomy framework that offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on robotics research and applications.

**1. Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:**
The framework directly addresses the limitations of human teleoperation in fine-grained, contact-rich tasks. By providing effective low-level assistance, the system makes complex manipulation more reliable and efficient for human operators. This improvement can reduce operator workload, decrease frustration, and increase overall satisfaction, which is crucial for long-duration or high-stakes teleoperation scenarios (e.g., remote inspection, hazardous environment manipulation, or surgical robotics).

**2. Increased Accessibility for Teleoperation:**
The demonstrated improvement in task success rates for novice operators indicates that the system can lower the skill barrier for performing intricate robotic tasks. This could broaden the accessibility of advanced robotic systems, enabling a wider range of users to effectively control robots for complex applications without extensive prior training.

**3. Efficient and High-Quality Data Collection for Autonomous Learning:**
A notable impact is the ability of the Residual Copilot to facilitate the collection of higher-quality demonstrations for imitation learning. The consistency and structured nature of copilot-assisted trajectories lead to more robust and generalizable downstream autonomous policies, even when controlling for the number of successful demonstrations. This can accelerate the development and deployment of fully autonomous systems by streamlining the data acquisition process, reducing the need for extensive data augmentation, and ultimately lowering the cost of training.

**4. Robustness and Generalizability:**
The lightweight kNN human surrogate, trained from minimal real data, provides a robust model for simulation-based copilot training. This non-parametric approach, combined with domain randomization and a residual control formulation, contributes to the copilot's ability to generalize across different human pilot behaviors and transfer effectively from simulation to real-world environments. Its capacity to handle variations in human skill level, reaction delays, and execution errors enhances the system's practical applicability.

**5. Practicality and Integrability:**
The residual formulation is designed to be largely agnostic to the specific skill level of the teleoperator and can be integrated with existing teleoperation interfaces without requiring modifications to human control strategies. This ease of integration makes the proposed shared autonomy system a practical tool for immediate adoption in various teleoperation settings, serving both assistive execution and high-quality data collection purposes.

**6. Future Research Directions:**
The identified limitation regarding potential human adaptation and emergent strategies when a copilot is present highlights an important area for future research. Investigating explicit modeling of co-adaptation or online updates to the human surrogate could further enhance the system's intelligence and adaptability, leading to more sophisticated and truly collaborative human-robot interaction.

In summary, this work offers a valuable framework for advancing shared autonomy, making robotic manipulation more accessible, reliable, and efficient. Its contribution to improving data quality for imitation learning holds particular significance for accelerating the development of future autonomous robotic systems.
