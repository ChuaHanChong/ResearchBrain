# Learning Terrain-Aware Whole-Body Control for Perceptive Legged Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The TA-WBC framework provides a unified reinforcement learning approach for terrain-aware whole-body control in legged manipulators. It integrates proactive perception and precise manipulation through a hybrid exteroception encoder and a novel foot-contact plane-based end-effector sampling method, achieving robust loco-manipulation and successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in complex environments.

## Problem

- Legged manipulators often lack proactive terrain adaptation, leading to unexpected contacts and base pose perturbations that compromise end-effector tracking accuracy during traversal of complex terrains.
- Existing end-effector goal sampling methods are typically restricted to flat terrains, causing instability in manipulation tasks as the robot's base pose continuously varies on non-planar surfaces.
- Training a single unified policy that effectively integrates both expansive whole-body coordination and multi-terrain adaptability is challenging, risking 'catastrophic forgetting' or necessitating complex high-level decision modules.

## Method

- A unified, end-to-end ==reinforcement learning== framework, TA-WBC, is developed for perceptive legged loco-manipulation over various terrains, combining proprioceptive and exteroceptive observations.
- A hybrid exteroceptive encoder is introduced, utilizing a foot-centric, multi-ring sampling strategy for elevation maps to provide higher resolution around critical foot contact areas and decouple input from the base coordinate frame.
- A novel spherical end-effector goal sampling method, based on the Foot Contact Plane (FCP), is employed to provide stable manipulation targets independent of base fluctuations and enable seamless transitions in cross-terrain loco-manipulation.
- A dual-policy distillation module is integrated into a two-stage learning framework, allowing the unified policy to inherit robust locomotion reflexes and precise manipulation skills while preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- In simulations, TA-WBC achieved robust, collision-free locomotion (**0.56%** unexpected contact rate, max terrain level **9.00**) and expanded reachable workspace (**0.825** m³) on complex terrains by proactively adjusting base pitch, outperforming baseline methods.
- The framework demonstrated end-effector tracking error of **0.025** m and reachable workspace of **0.884** m³ on flat ground, comparable to proprioception-only whole-body control, while significantly surpassing decoupled baselines.
- The trained policy was successfully deployed on a real Unitree B2 robot via zero-shot transfer, executing long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., climbing a five-step staircase at **0.8** m/s, picking/throwing objects) 5 times consecutively without falls or stumbles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating structured exteroceptive perception, such as the foot-centric multi-ring elevation map, allows legged manipulators to proactively adapt their posture and footholds, significantly reducing unexpected contacts and improving manipulation stability on varied terrains.
> - Decoupling end-effector goal sampling from the robot's fluctuating base pose by using a Foot Contact Plane (FCP) as a dynamic reference frame is crucial for achieving stable and continuous manipulation targets across diverse and uneven environments.
> - Policy distillation is an effective strategy to integrate multiple complex skills (e.g., expansive whole-body motion and robust terrain traversal) into a single unified policy, mitigating catastrophic forgetting and enabling comprehensive control without needing high-level switching modules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of mobile manipulation systems has been progressing, transitioning from structured industrial environments to more complex human-centric settings. This transition necessitates advanced capabilities for robots to operate in diverse and unstructured environments. Traditional mobile manipulators, often based on wheeled platforms, are primarily restricted to planar or flat surfaces. This limitation constrains their utility in scenarios such as disaster response, construction sites, or multi-level residential areas, where varied terrain is common.

Legged manipulators, particularly quadruped robots equipped with robotic arms, are emerging as a solution due to their inherent ability to traverse unstructured terrains and leverage their floating base redundancy to extend their operational workspace. For instance, they can adjust their body posture (e.g., bending front legs or elevating the front body) to interact with objects at various heights or locations.

However, achieving coordinated whole-body control (WBC) for these systems presents significant challenges. These include managing complex whole-body dynamics, high degrees of freedom (DoF), and the intricate coupling between locomotion and high-accuracy manipulation tasks. Historically, model-based methods like Model Predictive Control (MPC) and hierarchical Quadratic Programming (QP) have been employed for WBC. While these methods can be stable in structured settings, they are often computationally intensive and susceptible to unmodeled disturbances, limiting their robustness in unstructured environments.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has recently gained prominence as an alternative for developing robust legged-manipulation controllers. RL-based approaches can offer superior disturbance rejection and enhanced whole-body coordination without requiring explicit dynamic modeling, facilitating transfer from simulation to real-world deployment.

Despite advancements in RL-based WBC, a significant gap exists: most current methods primarily assume flat, quasi-horizontal terrain and rely predominantly on proprioceptive feedback. They often treat terrain irregularities as unmodeled disturbances, addressed reactively rather than proactively. While perceptive cross-terrain loco-manipulation has been explored for humanoid robots, it remains particularly challenging for quadrupedal manipulators due to their non-anthropomorphic morphology and lack of direct human demonstrations for learning.

This paper addresses three specific challenges within this landscape:
1. **Reactive Terrain Adaptation:** Blind robots tend to make unexpected contact with obstacles, leading to sudden base pose perturbations that compromise end-effector tracking accuracy during traversal of terrains like stairs or steps.
2. **Limited End-Effector Sampling:** Existing training methods for end-effector goal sampling are often restricted to flat terrains, which can lead to instability in cross-terrain loco-manipulation due to continuous variations in base pose and height.
3. **Unified Policy Training Difficulty:** Training a single policy that effectively integrates both whole-body coordination and multi-terrain adaptability is complex and can lead to "catastrophic forgetting," where learning one skill degrades the other, or necessitates complex high-level decision modules for policy switching.

This work contributes to the advancement of legged manipulators by developing a unified, terrain-aware whole-body control framework that explicitly incorporates exteroceptive perception and robust learning strategies to overcome these limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a terrain-aware whole-body control (TA-WBC) framework for legged manipulators that enables effective loco-manipulation across diverse and challenging terrains. The motivation stems from several critical limitations observed in existing whole-body controllers for legged manipulators:

1. **Lack of Terrain Perception:** Many current whole-body controllers predominantly rely on proprioception, meaning they perceive internal robot states but lack critical exteroception for understanding the external terrain topology. This absence of environmental awareness hinders their ability to proactively adapt to varying terrain conditions and navigate complex environments effectively.
 - **Problem:** When traversing irregular terrains like stairs or steps, a robot without terrain perception must first physically collide with obstacles to sense them. This reactive approach inevitably causes sudden perturbations in the robot's floating base pose, which can severely compromise the accuracy of onboard end-effector tracking crucial for manipulation tasks. The goal is to enable proactive adaptation and collision avoidance.

2. **Inadequate End-Effector Goal Sampling for Varied Terrains:** Existing reinforcement learning methods for legged loco-manipulation often assume flat terrain, sampling end-effector goals in a coordinate system where the origin's height relative to the ground remains invariant.
 - **Problem:** This approach is ineffective when the robot traverses non-planar terrain, as continuous variations in base height and pose (especially pitch) introduce instability in end-effector tracking. The objective is to develop a sampling method that decouples the manipulation target from base fluctuations, ensuring stable and continuous end-effector goal tracking across diverse terrains.

3. **Difficulty in Unified Policy Training:** Integrating expansive whole-body motion with robust terrain adaptability into a single, unified control policy is a significant challenge.
 - **Problem:** Decoupling perceptive locomotion and blind whole-body control into separate policies can lead to complex high-level decision-making or manual policy switching, which is undesirable for autonomous operation. Training a single policy risks "catastrophic forgetting," where the robot might lose whole-body coordination abilities after learning terrain traversal, or vice-versa. The motivation is to develop a unified controller that simultaneously possesses both capabilities without degradation of either.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Introduce a novel RL-based unified policy for whole-body loco-manipulation tailored for various terrains.
- Develop a hybrid exteroception encoder to extract terrain features for proactive posture and foothold adaptation.
- Propose an end-effector sampling method based on the foot contact plane to ensure stable manipulation targets independent of base fluctuations.
- Implement a dual-policy distillation module to integrate expansive whole-body motion with terrain adaptability within a single policy, preventing catastrophic forgetting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **TA-WBC (Terrain-Aware Whole-Body Control)** framework is presented as a unified, end-to-end reinforcement learning solution for perceptive legged loco-manipulation over various terrains. The framework's overview is depicted in Fig. 2.

**A. Problem Definition**

1. **State Space Design:** The robot's state (`o_t`) comprises two main components:
 - **Proprioceptive Observation (`o_p_t`):** Includes base states (roll, pitch, angular velocities), 18-DoF joint positions and velocities, the last action, current commands (desired base linear/angular velocities, desired end-effector pose in arm frame), foot contact states, gait timing reference, and an inferred 20-dimensional latent vector (`hat_z_p_t`) representing environmental dynamics predicted from proprioceptive history.
 - **Exteroceptive Observation (`o_e_t`):** An elevation map (50x4 grid) around the robot, constructed from point cloud perception, with points sampled around each foot.
 The complete observation also incorporates a history horizon of `H=10` past proprioceptive observations.

2. **Action Space Design:** The controller simultaneously controls both the legged robot and its mounted arm.
 - For the leg joints, the RL policy directly outputs desired joint positions (`q_leg_d_t`) for all 12 leg joints, which are then converted to torque commands by a PD controller.
 - For the arm joints, desired arm joint positions (`q_arm_d_t`) are provided by an Inverse Kinematics (IK) method. The policy is trained to adjust the robot's legs to achieve an appropriate base pose that facilitates the end-effector's reach to its target.

**B. Policy Learning**

The TA-WBC policy (`π`) receives proprioceptive observations, an exteroceptive latent vector (`z_e_t`), and the inferred environmental dynamics (`hat_z_p_t`). During training, two pre-trained policies supervise TA-WBC to concurrently achieve robust cross-terrain locomotion and expansive whole-body motion within a single unified policy.

1. **Regularized Online Adaptation (ROA):** This framework is used to achieve partially observable whole-body control. It consists of:
 - A privileged encoder (`ϕ_priv`) that extracts a physics latent vector (`z_p_t`) from privileged information (e.g., actual friction, external payload, motor strength).
 - An adaptation encoder (`ϕ_adapt`) trained to implicitly infer latent environmental dynamics (`hat_z_p_t`) from a history of proprioceptive observations (`h_t`) by imitating `z_p_t`.
 The physics latent vector `z_p_t` is regularized to maintain closeness to `hat_z_p_t`.

2. **Hybrid Exteroceptive Encoder:** To address the limitations of conventional base-centric height maps (spatial misalignment, insufficient resolution around feet), a novel foot-centric, multi-ring sampling strategy is employed (Fig. 3).
 - For each of the four feet, 50 terrain height samples are collected in 5 concentric rings centered at the foot's vertical projection. The rings have varying radii (0.07m to 0.42m) and contain a varying number of points (6 to 14), with `θ=0` aligned with the robot's forward heading. This non-uniform density provides higher resolution around critical foot contact areas and decouples input from the base coordinate frame.
 - The encoder has a hierarchical structure: 1D convolutional layers process each concentric ring independently, followed by flattening and concatenation of local features for each foot. An MLP then produces a compact latent vector (`z_e_k`) for each foot. The final exteroceptive latent vector `z_e` is a concatenation of these four foot-specific latent vectors. This design exploits the circular sampling pattern and quadrupedal symmetry, enhancing training efficiency and convergence.

3. **End-Effector Goal Sampling:** To enable seamless end-effector goal transitions in cross-terrain loco-manipulation, a novel spherical sampling method based on the **Foot Contact Plane (FCP)** is introduced (Fig. 4).
 - The FCP is determined by fitting a least-squares plane to the four foot contact points, updated only upon detection of valid foot-ground contacts.
 - A ground projection frame (G) is constructed: its origin (`p_G`) is the base position projected onto the FCP, and its axes are derived from the FCP's normal vector and the base's projected x-axis on the plane.
 - The sampling frame (S) has its origin (`p_c`) at a fixed offset from the origin of frame G, with identical axes.
 - The end-effector goal (`p_end`) for each time interval is uniformly sampled within a sphere of 0.8m radius centered at the origin of frame S. The command (`p_cmd_t`) is then linearly interpolated between the current end-effector state and `p_end`.
 This method generates distance-invariant pitch and yaw angles relative to the valid foot contact plane, which, combined with collision checks, provides smooth and adaptive 6D end-effector pose sampling, improving training efficiency.

4. **Policy Distillation:** A two-stage learning framework integrated with dual-policy distillation is employed to prevent catastrophic forgetting and ensure both whole-body coordination and perceptive traversal capabilities.
 - **Stage 1:** A perceptive whole-body policy (`π_s`) is trained for loco-manipulation on simple, quasi-level terrain. Concurrently, a terrain-aware cross-terrain locomotion policy (`π_l`) is trained with randomly sampled arm joint configurations.
 - **Stage 2:** The unified policy (`π`) is trained using PPO under the supervision of both `π_s` and `π_l`. A terrain-conditioned distillation loss (`L_distill`) is added to the standard PPO objective. This loss uses Kullback-Leibler divergence to guide `π` towards the action distributions of `π_s` when on quasi-level planes or receiving standing commands, and towards `π_l` otherwise. This process allows `π` to inherit precise manipulation skills from `π_s` and robust locomotion reflexes from `π_l`.

5. **Arm Control:** Damped least squares is used to solve the inverse kinematics for the robot arm in the world frame. This method is chosen for its enhanced numerical stability near singularities compared to standard pseudoinverse methods, simplifying the learning task by avoiding increased goal command dimensions and additional reward functions associated with keypoint-based orientation tracking.

6. **Reward Design and Domain Randomization:** The reward function (`r_t`) is a weighted sum of components, including command tracking rewards for base velocities and end-effector pose, base stability rewards (height, pitch, hip), gait pattern rewards, and regularization terms for smooth motions and joint limits (Table I). For sim-to-real transfer, domain randomization is applied to physical parameters such as mass, friction, and actuator strength.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the TA-WBC framework was evaluated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments, comparing it against several baselines using defined metrics: linear/yaw rate tracking error (E_v, E_yaw), end-effector position tracking error (E_ee), reachable workspace (V_w), unexpected contact rate (r_c), and maximum available terrain level (l_ter).

**A. Simulation Results**

1. **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Simple Terrain (Quasi-horizontal plane):** TA-WBC was compared against "Blind Locomotion and IK (BL+IK)" (proprioception-based locomotion with decoupled IK) and "Blind Whole-Body Control (Blind WBC)" (proprioception-only whole-body control).
 - TA-WBC demonstrated performance comparable to Blind WBC on flat ground, achieving a reachable workspace (V_w) of 0.884 m³ and an end-effector tracking error (E_ee) of 0.025 m (Table II(a)).
 - It significantly outperformed the decoupled BL+IK baseline in V_w (0.884 m³ vs. 0.710 m³) and E_ee (0.025 m vs. 0.076 m), attributed to the coordinated motion of leg joints. Slight increases in E_v and E_yaw for TA-WBC were observed, but considered negligible compared to the gains in manipulation capabilities.

 - **Complex Terrain (Slopes, Steps, and Stairs):** TA-WBC was compared against BL+IK and "Perceptive Locomotion and IK (PL+IK)" (perceptive locomotion with decoupled IK).
 - BL+IK exhibited the highest unexpected contact rate (r_c) at 18.78% and largest E_ee at 0.316 m, indicating a failure to proactively adapt to terrain (Table II(b), Fig. 5(a)).
 - PL+IK improved terrain anticipation, reducing r_c to 0.97% and E_ee to 0.155 m, achieving an l_ter of 9.00. However, it maintained a relatively fixed base pitch during traversal, which limited its reachable workspace (V_w) to 0.683 m³ (Fig. 5(b)).
 - TA-WBC achieved robust, collision-free locomotion (r_c of 0.56%, l_ter of 9.00), and notably expanded the reachable workspace (V_w of 0.825 m³) by enabling active base pitch adjustments while traversing slopes and stairs (Fig. 5(c), (d)). Its E_ee was 0.042 m.

2. **Ablation Study:** Ablation experiments validated the contribution of individual modules (Table III).
 - Removing all exteroceptive input (a pure proprioceptive WBC) resulted in the poorest performance across all metrics (r_c of 24.37%, l_ter of 5.57), highlighting the necessity of terrain perception.
 - Replacing the foot-centric multi-ring sampling with a base-centric grip sampling (b) or using a flat MLP instead of the hybrid encoder architecture (c) led to slower training convergence, decreased l_ter, and increased r_c (2.57% and 4.20% respectively), validating the proposed perception encoding strategy.
 - Removal of the policy distillation module (d) led to reduced V_w (0.742 m³ vs. 0.825 m³ for TA-WBC) and increased E_ee (0.161 m vs. 0.042 m), confirming its role in preventing gradient interference and maintaining combined capabilities.

**B. Real-World Deployment**

The trained TA-WBC policy was directly deployed on a Unitree B2 quadruped robot with a Unitree Z1 arm via zero-shot transfer, utilizing onboard depth camera and LiDAR for elevation mapping. High-level commands were provided via VR teleoperation.

1. **Standing Whole-Body Motion:**
 - When an end-effector target was very low (e.g., reaching the ground), the decoupled policy (BL+IK) failed to reach it due to arm workspace limitations (Fig. 6(a)).
 - TA-WBC enabled the robot to adjust its base pitch to grasp objects below 10 cm on flat ground (Fig. 6(b)).
 - During a static task on stairs, where hind feet were on different steps resulting in significant roll and pitch base poses, TA-WBC allowed the end-effector to reach the ground with dynamic pitch variations exceeding 25 degrees, demonstrating robustness in challenging base configurations (Fig. 6(d)).

2. **Long-Horizon Loco-Manipulation:**
 - A continuous task involving picking up a bottle on a flat plane, climbing a five-step staircase (17 cm height per step), throwing the bottle into a bin on a platform, turning around, descending the stairs, and returning to the start, was successfully executed (Fig. 7).
 - The robot traversed stairs at speeds up to 0.8 m/s without unexpected foot-terrain collisions, demonstrating flexibility and safety.
 - Repeated tests with varied starting zones, grasping targets, and velocity commands showed that TA-WBC successfully completed the entire long-horizon task 5 times consecutively without falls or stumbles, verifying its robustness and adaptability in complex real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

**Significance:**
This research introduces a unified and terrain-aware whole-body control framework (TA-WBC) that addresses a critical gap in legged loco-manipulation: the integration of robust terrain perception with expansive whole-body coordination within a single, end-to-end learning-based controller. The work demonstrates that by leveraging a hybrid exteroception encoder, a novel foot-contact plane based end-effector sampling method, and a dual-policy distillation module, a legged manipulator can achieve proactive terrain adaptation and precise manipulation simultaneously, overcoming the limitations of reactive control, constrained workspace, and catastrophic forgetting observed in prior approaches. The zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities further validate the robustness and practical applicability of the learned policy.

**Potential Impact:**
The development of TA-WBC has several potential impacts:

1. **Expanded Application Domains:** This framework could enable legged manipulators to operate autonomously and effectively in a broader range of complex, unstructured, and human-centric environments. These include disaster relief scenarios, construction sites, multi-level residential areas, or outdoor inspection tasks where both mobile locomotion over challenging terrains and precise manipulation capabilities are required.
2. **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Capabilities:** By allowing robots to proactively adapt to terrain and coordinate their entire body, the framework significantly enhances their autonomy. This moves beyond merely traversing obstacles to actively leveraging the robot's full kinematic redundancy to achieve manipulation goals, such as reaching objects at extreme heights or low-lying positions while maintaining stability on uneven ground.
3. **Improved Robustness and Safety:** The reduction in unexpected foot-terrain collisions and improved end-effector tracking accuracy directly translates to safer and more reliable robot operation. This is crucial for deployment in environments where stability is paramount and collisions could lead to damage or task failure.
4. **Foundation for Higher-Level Control:** The robust, low-level, unified controller provided by TA-WBC lays a strong foundation for future research in sophisticated high-level policies. These high-level policies could then focus on task planning, semantic understanding, and complex decision-making, while relying on TA-WBC to execute the low-level loco-manipulation commands reliably across diverse terrains, paving the way for fully autonomous legged manipulators.
5. **Advancements in Reinforcement Learning for Robotics:** The methodological contributions, particularly the hybrid exteroception encoder for structured perceptive input and the dual-policy distillation for multi-skill integration, offer insights for designing more efficient and robust RL-based control architectures for complex robotic systems.
