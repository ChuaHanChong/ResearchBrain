# Learning Terrain-Aware Whole-Body Control for Perceptive Legged Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The TA-WBC framework provides a unified reinforcement learning approach for terrain-aware whole-body control in legged manipulators. It integrates proactive perception and precise manipulation through a hybrid exteroception encoder and a novel foot-contact plane-based end-effector sampling method, achieving robust loco-manipulation and successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in complex environments.

## Problem

- Legged manipulators often lack proactive terrain adaptation, leading to unexpected contacts and base pose perturbations that compromise end-effector tracking accuracy during traversal of complex terrains.
- Existing end-effector goal sampling methods are typically restricted to flat terrains, causing instability in manipulation tasks as the robot's base pose continuously varies on non-planar surfaces.
- Training a single unified policy that effectively integrates both expansive whole-body coordination and multi-terrain adaptability is challenging, risking 'catastrophic forgetting' or necessitating complex high-level decision modules.

## Method

- A unified, end-to-end ==reinforcement learning== framework, TA-WBC, is developed for perceptive legged loco-manipulation over various terrains, combining proprioceptive and exteroceptive observations.
- A hybrid exteroceptive encoder is introduced, utilizing a foot-centric, multi-ring sampling strategy for elevation maps to provide higher resolution around critical foot contact areas and decouple input from the base coordinate frame.
- A novel spherical end-effector goal sampling method, based on the Foot Contact Plane (FCP), is employed to provide stable manipulation targets independent of base fluctuations and enable seamless transitions in cross-terrain loco-manipulation.
- A dual-policy distillation module is integrated into a two-stage learning framework, allowing the unified policy to inherit robust locomotion reflexes and precise manipulation skills while preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- In simulations, TA-WBC achieved robust, collision-free locomotion (**0.56%** unexpected contact rate, max terrain level **9.00**) and expanded reachable workspace (**0.825** m³) on complex terrains by proactively adjusting base pitch, outperforming baseline methods.
- The framework demonstrated end-effector tracking error of **0.025** m and reachable workspace of **0.884** m³ on flat ground, comparable to proprioception-only whole-body control, while significantly surpassing decoupled baselines.
- The trained policy was successfully deployed on a real Unitree B2 robot via zero-shot transfer, executing long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., climbing a five-step staircase at **0.8** m/s, picking/throwing objects) 5 times consecutively without falls or stumbles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating structured exteroceptive perception, such as the foot-centric multi-ring elevation map, allows legged manipulators to proactively adapt their posture and footholds, significantly reducing unexpected contacts and improving manipulation stability on varied terrains.
> - Decoupling end-effector goal sampling from the robot's fluctuating base pose by using a Foot Contact Plane (FCP) as a dynamic reference frame is crucial for achieving stable and continuous manipulation targets across diverse and uneven environments.
> - Policy distillation is an effective strategy to integrate multiple complex skills (e.g., expansive whole-body motion and robust terrain traversal) into a single unified policy, mitigating catastrophic forgetting and enabling comprehensive control without needing high-level switching modules.
