# GCNT: Graph-Based Transformer Policies for Morphology-Agnostic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Graph-Based Transformer Policies (GCNT) enables universal control for robots with diverse morphologies by explicitly decomposing morphological information extraction and inter-limb communication. Researchers from Jilin University and Changchun University of Science and Technology demonstrate that GCNT achieves leading performance on standard benchmarks and robust zero-shot generalization to unseen robot configurations and physical variations.

## Problem

- Traditional Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) requires training a separate policy network from scratch for each distinct robot morphology, leading to high resource expenditure and incompatibility with variable state/action spaces.
- Prior morphology-agnostic DRL methods suffer from limitations such as information degradation in multi-hop message passing (GNNs) or neglect of crucial morphological information (pure Transformers).
- Existing methods for integrating morphology into Transformers (e.g., traversal-based approaches) can lead to indexing inconsistencies, hindering effective policy learning.

## Method

- **GCNT** modularizes robots, representing them as ==graphs==, and decomposes the control problem into distinct phases of ==morphological information extraction== and ==direct inter-limb communication==.
- It employs an improved ==Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)== and a ==Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL)== module to extract comprehensive local and global morphological information from the robot's graph representation.
- A ==Transformer== module facilitates direct communication among all robot limbs, integrating ==learnable physical distance embeddings== to enhance attention scores and leverage extracted morphological features for action generation.

## Results

- GCNT demonstrated superior sample efficiency and achieved the highest average performance across all environments in both SMPENV and UNIMAL benchmarks, outperforming seven baseline methods.
- The framework exhibited strong **zero-shot generalization**, achieving leading scores in **5 out of 7** unseen robot morphologies and demonstrating robust control under various kinematics and dynamics variations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each architectural component (GCN, WL, distance embedding) positively contributes to performance, and visualization of learned embeddings showed GCNT extracts consistent structural information for similar limb functions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly separating morphological information extraction from inter-limb communication via specialized modules (GCN, WL) and a Transformer leads to more effective and robust universal control policies.
> - Combining both local (GCN) and global (WL) structural information effectively overcomes the limitations of prior methods that either lost information or failed to leverage morphology adequately.
> - Incorporating learnable physical distance embeddings directly into the multi-head self-attention mechanism significantly improves coordination between distant robot limbs, leading to better overall performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has demonstrated capabilities in continuous control tasks for mobile robots. However, traditional DRL approaches typically necessitate training a specific policy network from scratch for each distinct robot configuration. This paradigm leads to substantial time and resource expenditures, becoming impractical when managing multiple robots or systems with variable morphologies.

The present work operates within the emerging sub-field of morphology-agnostic reinforcement learning, also referred to as inhomogeneous multi-task RL or modular RL. This area seeks to develop universal control networks that enable a single policy to govern robots with diverse physical configurations. The motivation for this research direction is multi-faceted: it can enhance robot robustness and resilience (e.g., allowing operation despite limb damage), enable optimization of robot configurations for specific environments without retraining, serve as a pre-training mechanism for fine-tuning new morphologies, and facilitate the development of large-scale models in robotics by providing unified representations.

A core challenge in morphology-agnostic RL is addressing the variable dimensions of state and action spaces across different robot morphologies. Early attempts to address this issue adopted modular robot representations, treating each robot as a collection of identical modules. Initial policy networks operating on these modular representations frequently employed message-passing mechanisms, often based on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), such as NerveNet, Dynamic Graph Networks (DGN), and Shared Modularization Policy (SMP). These methods faced limitations, particularly in information capacity, where critical information could degrade over multiple communication hops, hindering coordination between distant modules.

Subsequently, research shifted towards Transformer-based architectures, exemplified by AMORPHEUS. Transformers offer a direct communication mechanism among robot modules via multi-head self-attention, mitigating the information loss observed in multi-hop message passing. However, purely Transformer-based approaches often did not explicitly incorporate or leverage the robot's morphological information, which has been identified as a significant factor for effective universal control.

More recent endeavors have focused on integrating morphological information into Transformer architectures. Methods such as Structure-aware Transformer (SWAT), MetaMorph, ModuMorph, and GET-Zero have explored ways to encode structural biases or positional embeddings within the Transformer framework. Despite these advancements, challenges persist in both the efficient extraction of comprehensive morphological information and ensuring its effective utilization for communication among robot modules. This work, GCNT, positions itself as an advancement in this third stage, aiming to overcome the limitations of previous methods by proposing a novel architecture that explicitly decomposes the problem into distinct processes of morphological information extraction and direct node communication, thereby seeking to more effectively leverage both graphical structure and attention mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a universal controller capable of generating resilient locomotion behaviors for robots with diverse morphologies, including those not encountered during training (zero-shot generalization). This overarching goal is motivated by several identified shortcomings in existing morphology-agnostic reinforcement learning approaches.

The core motivation stems from the inherent difficulty traditional DRL faces when confronted with robots of varying configurations. Since different robots possess different numbers of joints and sensors, their state and action spaces often vary in dimension. Standard MLP-based policy networks require fixed input and output dimensions, rendering them incompatible with such variability. While modularization, where robots are represented as combinations of identical modules, offers a pathway to standardize input/output dimensions for individual modules, the critical challenge then becomes how to effectively coordinate these discretized modules to achieve coherent overall robot actions.

The authors identify two specific limitations in prior work addressing this coordination problem:
1. **Limited Capacity of Message Passing Mechanisms:** Analogous to the vanishing gradient problem in Recurrent Neural Networks, multi-hop message passing mechanisms (e.g., those used in some GNN-based methods) can lead to significant attenuation or loss of original information. This compromises effective communication and coordination, especially between physically distant robot limbs (e.g., a robot's hand and foot).
2. **Neglect of Morphological Information in Pure Transformer Architectures:** While Transformer-based models address direct communication, many early implementations did not explicitly leverage the robot's intrinsic morphological structure. This structural information has been empirically demonstrated to be crucial for developing robust universal controllers. Furthermore, existing methods that attempt to incorporate morphology, such as traversal-based approaches, may suffer from indexing inconsistencies, leading to suboptimal or hindering effects on policy learning.

To address these limitations, the key objectives and motivations for proposing GCNT are:
- **Decomposition of the Control Problem:** To introduce a novel decomposition of the morphology-agnostic control problem into two distinct but complementary processes: efficient morphological information extraction and robust inter-node communication.
- **Effective Morphological Information Extraction:** To design a mechanism, combining an improved Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) module and a Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) module, capable of thoroughly extracting both local and global morphological information from the robot's graph representation. This aims to overcome issues like indexing inconsistency encountered in traversal-based methods and capture comprehensive structural insights. The GCN is specifically motivated by its ability to aggregate neighboring information, while the WL module is intended to capture overall graph structure.
- **Direct and Informed Node Communication:** To integrate a Transformer module that facilitates direct communication among all robot limbs, ensuring that the extracted morphological information is fully utilized. The motivation here is to avoid information degradation and allow each node to assess the importance of information from other nodes, enhanced by considering physical distances between limbs.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** To develop a policy network that demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization capabilities to unseen robot morphologies and variations in kinematics and dynamics, thereby improving the robustness and adaptability of robotic systems in diverse and unstructured environments.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to overcome the identified weaknesses in the current landscape of morphology-agnostic reinforcement learning, leading to more capable and versatile robot control policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GCNT (Graph-Based Transformer Policies) architecture is designed to enable morphology-agnostic reinforcement learning by modularizing robots and leveraging graph representations. The approach decomposes the control problem into distinct phases of morphological information extraction and limb communication.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Robots are modularized, with each limb and its associated joint treated as a module. These modules are assumed to have identical state space and action space dimensions. A robot with $K$ limbs has a state $s_t = \{s^1_t..., s^K_t\}$ and produces actions $a_t = \{a^1_t..., a^K_t\}$, where $s^i_t$ is the local state and $a^i_t$ is the local action for limb $i$.
The robot's morphology is represented as an undirected graph $G = \langle V, E \rangle$, where nodes $v_i \in V$ correspond to limbs, and edges $(v_i, v_j) \in E$ indicate direct connections. This graph is formalized via an adjacency matrix $A$. The objective is to train a universal policy network $\pi_\theta$ that maximizes the expected cumulative discounted return across a set of $N$ agents with different morphologies.

#### 3.2. GCNT Network Architecture

The GCNT architecture comprises five main modules that process various inputs for each robot morphology:

- **Limb Observation Module:** This module processes the local observation for each limb. Inputs include motion state information (e.g., relative position to torso, linear and angular velocities, joint angle) and hardware attribute information (e.g., mass, shape, range of motion, gear ratio). All observations are passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to map them to a higher-dimensional feature space for subsequent processing.

- **GCN Module (Improved Graph Convolutional Network):** This module is designed to extract local morphological information. It uses the normalized graph Laplacian for feature aggregation, essentially updating each node's features based on its own and its neighbors' features.
 - **Input:** One-hot encoded representations of limb types serve as the initial node attributes $X$. The adjacency matrix $A$ (with added identity matrix $I_K$ for self-loops) is used for graph connectivity.
 - **Improvements:** To enhance performance and prevent gradient vanishing, the authors introduce additional linear layers and residual connections within each GCN block. The computation for a layer $l$ is modified to $H^{(l+1)} = \sigma(\tilde{D}^{-1/2} \tilde{A} \tilde{D}^{-1/2} H^{(l)} W^{(l)}_1) W^{(l)}_2 + H^{(l)}$, where $W^{(l)}_1$ and $W^{(l)}_2$ are trainable weight matrices, $\sigma$ is an activation function, and $H^{(l)}$ is the feature matrix.

- **Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) Module:** This module complements the GCN by capturing the overall, global morphological information of the robot.
 - **Mechanism:** The Weisfeiler-Lehman algorithm is applied, using the type of each limb as the initial "color" for each node. The algorithm iteratively updates node labels based on their own label and sorted neighbor labels, then hashes this combination. After a fixed number of iterations, a vector representing the occurrence times of each unique "color string" throughout the process is generated. This vector is concatenated with the local morphological information from the GCN module for each node, aiming to provide a global structural context.

- **Learnable Distance Embedding Module:** This module aims to incorporate the influence of physical distances between limbs into the communication process.
 - **Distance Calculation:** The Floyd algorithm is used to compute the shortest path distances $D_{i,j}$ between all pairs of nodes (limbs) in the robot's graph.
 - **Embedding:** These distance values $D_{i,j}$ are passed through a learnable mapping function $g_\phi(\cdot)$ to produce a vector $R_{i,j}$. Each element of $R_{i,j}$ corresponds to a head in the multi-head attention mechanism of the Transformer.
 - **Integration:** In the Transformer, $R_{i,j}$ is added to the attention score $\alpha_{i,j}^{(h)}$ (for head $h$) before the Softmax function, allowing the Transformer to account for inter-limb distances during communication.

- **Transformer Module:** This module is responsible for enabling direct communication among limbs and aggregating all processed information.
 - **Input:** The high-dimensional feature vectors from the limb observation module, combined with the morphological information (concatenation of GCN and WL module outputs), are fed into the Transformer.
 - **Mechanism:** It utilizes a multi-head self-attention mechanism, where the learnable distance embeddings are incorporated into the attention score calculation (Equation 5 in the paper). This allows each node to directly communicate with all other nodes, weighing their importance based on attention scores that are influenced by distance.
 - **Output:** The processed features from the Transformer are passed through a final linear layer to generate the local actions for each limb. Residual connections spanning the entire network, connecting initial limb observations to the Transformer's output, are also employed to prevent feature forgetting.

#### 3.3. Optimization Method

GCNT employs an actor-critic architecture, a standard practice for continuous control tasks. The actor and critic networks share the same GCNT architecture.
- For the SMPENV benchmark, the TD3 (Twin Delayed DDPG) algorithm is utilized.
- For the UNIMAL benchmark, the PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) algorithm is applied, consistent with previous work in these respective benchmarks.
During training, multiple agents (robots with different morphologies) interact with their environments, collecting samples in separate replay buffers (for TD3) or shared buffers (for PPO). Training updates are then performed sequentially or in epochs, aiming to maximize the objective function defined as the expected cumulative return across all agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of GCNT was evaluated across eight distinct scenarios within two standard benchmarks, SMPENV and UNIMAL, and compared against seven baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Training Results in SMPENV

In SMPENV, GCNT was compared with SMP (message passing), AMORPHEUS (Transformer-based), and SWAT (structure-aware Transformer). Experiments were conducted on four environments: Walker++, Cheetah++, Humanoid++, and CWHH++ (a complex cross-domain environment).
- **Superior Performance:** GCNT consistently demonstrated the highest sample efficiency and achieved the best average performance across all four test environments.
- **Limitations of Baselines:** SMP exhibited the lowest performance, attributed to severe information loss during multi-hop message transmission, hindering coordination between distant limbs. AMORPHEUS, a pure Transformer, performed well on simpler configurations (e.g., Walker++) but struggled with more complex robot morphologies due to its lack of morphological information utilization. SWAT, while incorporating morphological information, occasionally performed worse than AMORPHEUS (e.g., in Walker++), suggesting that its traversal-based morphology extraction could sometimes be detrimental to policy learning.
- **Effective Morphology Extraction:** GCNT's consistent outperformance over SWAT indicated a more effective and beneficial extraction and utilization of morphological information in all tested scenarios.

#### 4.2. Training Results in UNIMAL

In UNIMAL, GCNT was compared with MetaMorph, MetaMorph*, ModuMorph, SR-fair, and SR-top (an approximate upper bound). Experiments were conducted on Flat Terrain (FT), Incline, Mixed Terrain (MT), and Obstacles, involving control of 100 robots with different morphologies.
- **Leading Performance:** GCNT achieved leading performance across all four UNIMAL environments, exhibiting higher sample efficiency than SR-fair.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** While ModuMorph showed similar performance, GCNT achieved comparable results with a significantly lower parameter count (2-3 times fewer). The authors also demonstrated in an appendix that GCNT augmented with a hypernetwork (similar to ModuMorph's approach) could surpass ModuMorph's performance, suggesting the core GCNT mechanism's robustness.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Generalization to Unseen Morphologies

Experiments in SMPENV evaluated GCNT's ability to generalize to morphologies not included in the training set without fine-tuning.
- **Strong Generalization:** GCNT achieved the highest average scores in 5 out of 7 unseen test morphologies (e.g., walker 6 main, humanoid 7 left leg).
- **Scaling with Complexity:** GCNT's generalization performance improved as the number of limbs increased, indicating that its method of extracting and utilizing morphological information is particularly effective for more complex robot configurations. Its performance was less dominant in simpler configurations with fewer limbs (e.g., walker 3 main, cheetah 3 balanced).

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Generalization to Kinematics and Dynamics Variations

In UNIMAL, GCNT was tested on 400 new robots where the topological structure remained the same as the training set, but attributes like armature, density, damping, gear, module shape, and joint angle were varied.
- **Robustness to Attribute Changes:** GCNT demonstrated the strongest generalization ability to both kinematics and dynamics variations among all tested methods, indicating its policy's resilience to changes in physical parameters.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the Humanoid++ environment (chosen for its complexity) investigated the contribution of GCNT's individual modules: GCN, Weisfeiler-Lehman, and learnable distance embedding.
- **Module Contributions:** Removing any single module resulted in a performance drop, confirming that each component contributes positively to the overall performance. Even with modules removed, the ablated GCNT variants still outperformed the pure Transformer baseline (AMORPHEUS), indicating the foundational strength of the overall architecture.

#### 4.6. Morphological Information Embedding

To assess the effectiveness of morphological information extraction, t-SNE was used to visualize the 128-dimensional structural vectors learned by GCNT and SWAT in the CWHH++ environment.
- **Consistent Representation:** GCNT mapped limbs with similar functions (e.g., different types of legs or arms) closely together in the 2D embedding space, irrespective of the specific robot configuration they belonged to. This indicated that GCNT's graph-based and WL-based methods effectively extract consistent structural information.
- **Inconsistency in Baselines:** In contrast, SWAT's traversal-based method resulted in scattered embeddings for similar limbs across different robot formations, suggesting an inability to consistently extract structural information, which likely explained its inferior performance in locomotion and generalization tasks.

#### 4.7. Behavior Analysis

Visualization of the movement trajectory of a humanoid 2d 9 full robot in the CWHH++ environment revealed behavioral differences:
- **GCNT's Coordinated Locomotion:** GCNT achieved the best locomotion performance, characterized by effective, symmetric, and stable movements, indicating superior coordination derived from effective morphological information use.
- **Baseline Behaviors:** SWAT showed smaller stride lengths and slower motion due to insufficient morphological information extraction. AMORPHEUS exhibited a jumping behavior, failing to alternate legs, because it did not utilize morphological information. SMP's movements were unsteady and asymmetric, attributed to information loss in message passing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work, GCNT, presents a novel architectural approach to morphology-agnostic reinforcement learning, offering several contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotics.

**Advancement in Universal Robot Control:** The core significance lies in GCNT's ability to effectively combine graph convolutional networks for robust morphological information extraction with Transformer networks for direct and informed inter-limb communication. This decomposition and integration strategy addresses known limitations in prior work, such as the information degradation in multi-hop message passing and the neglect of structural context in pure Transformer models. The improved GCN with residual connections and the complementary Weisfeiler-Lehman module for global morphology contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of the robot's physical structure.

**Enhanced Robot Robustness and Resilience:** By enabling a single policy network to control robots with varying and even damaged morphologies, GCNT directly contributes to more robust and resilient robotic systems. For instance, a robot designed with this technology could potentially continue to operate or adapt its locomotion strategy even after losing a limb, without requiring extensive retraining. This capability is critical for applications in unpredictable or hazardous environments, such as field exploration or disaster response.

**Reduced Development and Training Costs:** The paradigm of training a universal controller for multiple robot configurations significantly reduces the engineering effort and computational resources associated with robot deployment. Instead of developing and training a unique policy for each robot variant or task, a single GCNT model can be learned and then applied to a wide range of similar or novel morphologies. This streamlines the development process and makes advanced robotic control more accessible.

**Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization of GCNT to unseen robot morphologies and variations in kinematics and dynamics is a key practical implication. This means robots can be deployed in environments with unforeseen physical characteristics or with minor design variations without the need for additional training. This level of adaptability is crucial for real-world scenarios where precise environmental modeling or exhaustive pre-training for every possible robot configuration is infeasible.

**New Research Directions in Robot Learning:** The method's novel decomposition of the control problem into distinct morphological information extraction and node communication modules offers a new conceptual framework. This approach could inspire future research to further refine each module or explore alternative mechanisms for these sub-problems, potentially leading to even more sophisticated and adaptive robot control systems. The ability to provide a unified representation for robots of different morphologies also paves the way for scaling up robot learning models, similar to the advancements seen in large language models and vision transformers.

In summary, GCNT contributes to the practical utility of DRL in robotics by addressing fundamental challenges in morphology-agnostic control. Its capacity for robust performance, efficient generalization, and reduced training overhead positions it as a significant step towards more autonomous, adaptable, and versatile robotic agents.
