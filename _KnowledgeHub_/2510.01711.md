# Contrastive Representation Regularization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST, UC Berkeley, and RLWRLD introduced Robot State-aware Contrastive Loss (RS-CL), a method that explicitly aligns Vision-Language Model (VLM) representations with proprioceptive states to enhance robot manipulation. This approach yielded a 69.7% success rate on RoboCasa-Kitchen and improved real-robot task success by 13.3%, achieving these gains with computational efficiency and strong generalization.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack inherent sensitivity to control-relevant robotic signals (e.g., proprioceptive states) as they are trained on general visual data.
- This often leads to VLM representations in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models being dominated by visual appearance rather than factors crucial for precise robot control.
- Existing solutions to bridge this representational gap often require extensive additional datasets, multi-stage training, or introduce significant computational overhead.

## Method

- Developed Robot State-aware Contrastive Loss (RS-CL), an auxiliary objective that uses continuous proprioceptive distances as soft contrastive supervision to regularize VLM representations.
- Integrated a learnable summarization token and a lightweight projector to create compact VLM embeddings for efficient contrastive learning within existing VLA pipelines.
- Introduced "view cutoff" as a representation-level augmentation to generate diverse contrastive pairs efficiently without additional VLM forward passes, improving robustness to viewpoint variations.

## Results

- Achieved a new state-of-the-art success rate of **69.7%** on the RoboCasa-Kitchen benchmark and improved real-robot manipulation task success rates by **13.3%** (from **45.0%** to **58.3%**).
- Outperformed Time-Contrastive Networks (TCN) with a higher success rate (**53.0%** vs. **50.0%**) and significantly lower computational cost (**2.91** x 10^12 FLOPs vs. **7.53** x 10^12 FLOPs).
- Consistently improved performance when training VLA models from scratch on various VLM backbones, in low-data regimes, and maintained strong generalization across visual and physical variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly aligning VLM representations with robot proprioceptive states through a contrastive objective significantly improves the control-relevance of the learned features for action prediction.
> - Using continuous distances between proprioceptive states as soft labels for contrastive learning provides a more stable and effective signal for representation alignment than action-based cues.
> - Efficient, representation-level augmentations, such as randomly masking view features, can create effective contrastive pairs without incurring high computational costs or requiring architectural changes.
