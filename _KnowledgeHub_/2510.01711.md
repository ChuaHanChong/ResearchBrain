# Contrastive Representation Regularization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST, UC Berkeley, and RLWRLD introduced Robot State-aware Contrastive Loss (RS-CL), a method that explicitly aligns Vision-Language Model (VLM) representations with proprioceptive states to enhance robot manipulation. This approach yielded a 69.7% success rate on RoboCasa-Kitchen and improved real-robot task success by 13.3%, achieving these gains with computational efficiency and strong generalization.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack inherent sensitivity to control-relevant robotic signals (e.g., proprioceptive states) as they are trained on general visual data.
- This often leads to VLM representations in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models being dominated by visual appearance rather than factors crucial for precise robot control.
- Existing solutions to bridge this representational gap often require extensive additional datasets, multi-stage training, or introduce significant computational overhead.

## Method

- Developed Robot State-aware Contrastive Loss (RS-CL), an auxiliary objective that uses continuous proprioceptive distances as soft contrastive supervision to regularize VLM representations.
- Integrated a learnable summarization token and a lightweight projector to create compact VLM embeddings for efficient contrastive learning within existing VLA pipelines.
- Introduced "view cutoff" as a representation-level augmentation to generate diverse contrastive pairs efficiently without additional VLM forward passes, improving robustness to viewpoint variations.

## Results

- Achieved a new state-of-the-art success rate of **69.7%** on the RoboCasa-Kitchen benchmark and improved real-robot manipulation task success rates by **13.3%** (from **45.0%** to **58.3%**).
- Outperformed Time-Contrastive Networks (TCN) with a higher success rate (**53.0%** vs. **50.0%**) and significantly lower computational cost (**2.91** x 10^12 FLOPs vs. **7.53** x 10^12 FLOPs).
- Consistently improved performance when training VLA models from scratch on various VLM backbones, in low-data regimes, and maintained strong generalization across visual and physical variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly aligning VLM representations with robot proprioceptive states through a contrastive objective significantly improves the control-relevance of the learned features for action prediction.
> - Using continuous distances between proprioceptive states as soft labels for contrastive learning provides a more stable and effective signal for representation alignment than action-based cues.
> - Efficient, representation-level augmentations, such as randomly masking view features, can create effective contrastive pairs without incurring high computational costs or requiring architectural changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent framework in robot manipulation, capitalizing on the rich visual and semantic representations derived from pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models typically utilize a generative action decoder conditioned on VLM-generated representations, trained with an action prediction loss based on ground-truth action sequences.

However, VLMs are primarily trained on large-scale visual instruction datasets and are not inherently exposed to modalities specific to robotics, such as low-level control actions or proprioceptive information. Consequently, VLM representations, when directly applied or minimally fine-tuned, often prioritize general visual appearance over control-relevant robotic signals. This can lead to suboptimal performance in VLA models, as the VLM may not effectively capture the nuances required for precise robot control.

Prior research has addressed this by training the VLM backbone alongside the action decoder. Strategies include direct VLM updates via action prediction gradients, introducing auxiliary objectives with curated instruction datasets, or learning intermediate subtasks and discretized actions. Other approaches involve further training VLMs on embodied reasoning, spatial grounding tasks, or autoregressive prediction of discretized actions before fine-tuning for continuous action prediction. While these methods aim to bridge the representational gap, they often necessitate additional training stages, carefully curated datasets, or significant computational resources.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing an efficient and compatible method to refine VLM representations directly for action prediction within existing VLA training pipelines. It introduces a contrastive learning objective that explicitly guides VLM representations to capture robotic signals, specifically proprioceptive states, without requiring extensive additional datasets or complex multi-stage training. This approach seeks to provide a lightweight regularization that enhances control-relevant representation learning in a single, end-to-end training stage, thereby improving the accuracy and robustness of VLA models in robot manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robot manipulation by improving the quality of their underlying Vision-Language Model (VLM) representations. The authors identify a specific limitation: while pre-trained VLMs provide rich visual and semantic grounding, their representations are often suboptimal for robotic tasks because they lack inherent sensitivity to robotic signals such as low-level control actions and proprioceptive information. This deficiency leads to VLM embeddings that are primarily dominated by visual appearance cues rather than factors critical for robot control, such as the robot's current pose or the precise actions required to complete a task.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that VLM representations, when visualized, tend to cluster based on environmental visual features (e.g., scene layout, large background objects) even when different episodes execute the same task. This misalignment suggests that the representations do not effectively capture control-relevant factors or the progression of a task. The authors hypothesize that explicitly aligning VLM representations with the robot's physical state will directly improve the action prediction capabilities of VLA models.

To address this, the key objectives are:
1. **Introduce a novel representation regularization method:** Develop a mechanism, termed Robot State-aware Contrastive Loss (RS-CL), that leverages continuous proprioceptive distances as soft contrastive supervision. This aims to restructure the VLM embedding space to better reflect control-relevant robotic signals.
2. **Ensure efficiency and compatibility:** Design RS-CL to operate directly at the representation level, complementing the original action prediction objective. This seeks to enable single-stage, end-to-end representation learning for VLA models without introducing substantial computational overhead or requiring modifications to existing training pipelines.
3. **Enhance control-relevant representation learning:** Guide the VLM to learn representations that are not only semantically rich but also deeply grounded in the robot’s physical state, thereby leading to more accurate action prediction.
4. **Validate broad applicability:** Demonstrate the effectiveness of RS-CL across various training scenarios, including fine-tuning state-of-the-art VLA models, training VLA models from scratch on different VLM backbones, and evaluating performance on both simulation benchmarks and real-robot manipulation tasks.

By explicitly guiding VLM representations to incorporate robotic signals, particularly proprioceptive states, the authors aim to bridge the gap between general-purpose VLM capabilities and the specific demands of accurate and precise robot control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Robot State-aware Contrastive Loss (RS-CL), is introduced as an auxiliary objective to regularize the Vision-Language Model (VLM) representation space within a standard Vision-Language-Action (VLA) training framework.

#### 3.1. Vision-Language-Action Model Framework

The VLA framework processes multimodal inputs, including observation images from multiple views ($O^V_t$), a task instruction ($c$), and the robot’s proprioceptive state ($q$), to predict a future action chunk ($A_t$). The architecture employs a pre-trained VLM to encode $[O^V_t, c]$ into a hidden representation. A lightweight adapter module ($f_\phi$) further processes the VLM output ($h = f_\phi(\text{VLM}(O^V_t, c))$). This processed representation ($h$) then conditions an action decoder ($D_\theta$), which generates $A_t$ using the current robot state ($q$). For the action decoder, the DiT architecture is adopted, trained with a flow-matching objective ($L_{FM}$) to predict interpolated action chunks. Following established practices, the VLM backbone is frozen during training, with only the adapter module ($f_\phi$) and action decoder ($D_\theta$) being updated.

#### 3.2. Robot State-aware Contrastive Loss (RS-CL)

RS-CL is designed to explicitly align VLM representations with the robot’s proprioceptive states. It comprises three main components:

- **Amortizing VLM Embeddings for Representation Learning:**
 To manage the computational complexity of applying contrastive learning to long VLM output sequences, a learnable summarization token ($u$) is introduced. This token is appended to the VLM output and processed by the adapter ($f_\phi$). The output corresponding to this token ($w$) is then projected by a lightweight projector ($g_\psi$) into a compact representative embedding ($z = g_\psi(w)$), which is used for contrastive learning. The original embedding ($h$) continues to serve as the conditioning input for the action decoder.

- **Incorporating Robot States into Contrastive Learning:**
 The core of RS-CL is a weighted variant of the InfoNCE loss. This objective uses the continuous distances between proprioceptive states to assign soft weights ($w_{ij}$) to similarity scores. Conceptually, embeddings associated with more similar proprioceptive states are pulled closer together in the representation space. The soft weights $w_{ij}$ are calculated based on the Euclidean distance between proprioceptive states $q_i$ and $q_j$, using an exponential function with a temperature parameter ($\beta$) to control the sharpness of the weighting: $w_{ij} = e^{-\|q_i - q_j\|^2 / \beta} / \sum_k e^{-\|q_i - q_k\|^2 / \beta}$. The overall RS-CL objective is defined as: $L_{RS-CL}(\phi, \psi) = - \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i,j=1}^B w_{ij} \log \frac{e^{\text{sim}(z_i, \tilde{z}_j)/\tau}}{\sum_{k=1}^B e^{\text{sim}(z_i, \tilde{z}_k)/\tau}}$, where $\tilde{z}$ denotes an augmented batch, $\text{sim}$ is cosine similarity, and $\tau$ is a temperature.

- **Representation Augmentation for Contrastive Pairs (View Cutoff):**
 To generate diverse contrastive pairs efficiently without additional VLM forward passes, the method introduces "view cutoff." This representation-level augmentation randomly selects a single view index from the multiple camera observations and masks out its corresponding feature slice from the VLM output $\text{VLM}(O^V_t, c)$. This operation creates an augmented representation ($\tilde{z}$) for contrastive learning, maintaining semantic content while introducing controlled variation.

The complete training objective combines the flow-matching loss ($L_{FM}$) with the RS-CL loss: $L = L_{FM} + \lambda L_{RS-CL}$, where $\lambda$ is a weighting coefficient. All parameters (action decoder $\theta$, adapter $\phi$, and projector $\psi$) are jointly optimized end-to-end.

#### 3.3. Implementation and Training Details

The baseline VLA framework adopted is GR00T N1.5. A 2-layer MLP with a hidden dimension of 2048 and a projection dimension of 128 implements the projection head ($g_\psi$). The weighting coefficient $\lambda$ for $L_{RS-CL}$ is initialized to 1.0 and decayed to 0 using a cosine schedule, emphasizing representation refinement early in training. Proprioceptive states primarily include end-effector position (x, y, z, 6D rotation, and gripper state), normalized to [-1, 1]. In real-robot experiments, absolute joint positions are also explored.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RS-CL was conducted across simulation benchmarks and real-robot experiments, in both fine-tuning and from-scratch training scenarios.

#### 4.1. Fine-tuning Experiments

- **Simulation Performance:**
 - On the **RoboCasa-Kitchen** benchmark, RS-CL achieved a success rate of 69.7%, establishing a new state-of-the-art performance among the evaluated VLA baselines. It consistently outperformed the GR00T N1.5 baseline across varying dataset sizes (30, 100, and 300 demonstrations). Notably, RS-CL yielded substantial gains in pick-and-place tasks, with success rates improving from 30.3% to 41.5% (+11.2%) with 30 demonstrations, attributed to its ability to produce more accurate actions for tasks requiring precise positioning.
 - On the **LIBERO** benchmark, RS-CL also demonstrated improved performance, achieving an average success rate of 96.4% compared to the GR00T N1.5 baseline of 95.7%, confirming its robustness across different benchmarks.
 - A comparative analysis with **Time-Contrastive Networks (TCN)** showed that RS-CL provided a higher success rate (53.0% vs. 50.0% for multi-view TCN and 50.3% for single-view TCN) with a significantly smaller increase in computational cost (2.91 x 10^12 FLOPs vs. 7.53 x 10^12 FLOPs). This efficiency was attributed to the view-cutoff augmentation operating at the representation level.

- **Real-Robot Performance:**
 - RS-CL led to consistent performance improvements on challenging real-robot manipulation tasks using a 7-DoF manipulator. Overall success rates improved from 45.0% to 58.3% (+13.3%) across pick-and-place and close-lid tasks.
 - For the close-lid task, RS-CL enhanced both partial success (lifting) and full success (accurate closing) even under partial occlusion, suggesting improved precise positioning and robustness to viewpoint changes due to proprioceptive supervision and view-cutoff augmentation.
 - Generalization experiments indicated that RS-CL maintained and in some cases led to stronger generalization performance across visual appearance, physical property variations, language following, and lighting conditions.

#### 4.2. From-Scratch Experiments

- **Diverse VLM Backbones:** RS-CL consistently improved success rates when training VLA models from scratch on various pre-trained VLM backbones, including Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and SigLIP2. This demonstrated the generalizability of the representation regularization beyond a specific VLM.
- **Trainable Capacity:** On SigLIP2, RS-CL showed larger improvements when the VLM backbone was unfrozen, suggesting that it can benefit from increased trainable parameters.
- **Comparison to VLM Training Strategies:** RS-CL achieved higher success rates than VLMs that were further trained on robotics datasets (e.g., RoboBrain, VeBrain, Cosmos-Reason1, NORA). Moreover, it further enhanced the benefits of these adapted models when combined. This indicated that RS-CL effectively bridges the gap between general-purpose VLM representations and action prediction requirements, potentially more so than robotics-specific VLM pre-training alone. Even for GR00T N1.5 VLM, RS-CL provided additional gains.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analyses

- **Soft-label Supervision Target:** Using "current state distance" as the soft-label target in RS-CL yielded the highest average success rate (69.7%), outperforming vanilla InfoNCE (67.3%) and distances based on "next action sequence" (66.7%) or "next single action" (66.8%). This suggests that proprioceptive state provides a more stable and effective signal for representation alignment than action-based cues, which can be multimodal.
- **Representation Augmentation Strategy:** The proposed "view cutoff" augmentation achieved the highest success rate (69.7%) compared to no augmentation (65.3%) and other representation-level cutoff operations like token cutoff (66.3%), feature cutoff (67.5%), or span cutoff (67.3%). This highlights the particular benefit of simulating viewpoint variation for robust representation learning in multi-view robotic contexts.
- **Quantitative Analysis of Representation Alignment:**
 - **KNN task-phase classification accuracy:** RS-CL improved task-phase classification accuracy from 20.3% (baseline) to 22.9% for representations of identical tasks across diverse scenes, moving closer to the performance of ground-truth proprioceptive states (25.6%). This indicates that RS-CL helps the conditioning representation encode state observations more reliably and robustly to visual changes.
 - **CKNNA (Centered Kernel Normalized Nearest Neighbors Alignment):** RS-CL increased the representation similarity between learned embeddings and proprioceptive features, signifying a more structured embedding space that captures control-relevant signals.
- **Detailed Visualizations:** Visualizations showed that while pre-trained VLM representations clustered primarily by visual appearance, RS-CL aligned task progression trajectories across visually distinct environments, indicating a more robot-state-centric organization of the embedding space.
- **Adaptability:** RS-CL demonstrated effectiveness when applied to a different VLA architecture, π0-FAST (an autoregressive VLA model), indicating its broad applicability beyond flow-matching based models.
- **Proprioceptive State Configurations:** RS-CL provided consistent improvements across different proprioceptive state definitions (end-effector pose, joint position) and in higher-dimensional, more complex state spaces from DexMimicGen, showing its versatility.
- **Task-wise Analysis:** RS-CL yielded the largest gains on pick-and-place tasks and twisting/insertion tasks, which require precise control, while showing smaller improvements on open-and-close tasks that have more constrained action spaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning, particularly in the domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The introduction of Robot State-aware Contrastive Loss (RS-CL) addresses a fundamental challenge: the inherent insensitivity of general-purpose Vision-Language Model (VLM) representations to specific robotic signals, such as proprioceptive states and low-level control actions.

The **significance** of this work can be summarized as follows:
- **Enhanced Representation Quality:** RS-CL provides a mechanism to explicitly align VLM representations with physically grounded robotic signals. This results in representations that are not only semantically rich but also deeply relevant to precise action prediction, improving the efficacy of VLA models in complex manipulation tasks.
- **Efficiency and Compatibility:** Unlike many prior approaches that require additional training stages, curated large-scale datasets, or substantial architectural modifications, RS-CL is designed to be lightweight and fully compatible with existing VLA training pipelines. The proposed view cutoff augmentation, operating at the representation level, contributes to this efficiency by minimizing additional computational overhead.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The empirical results demonstrate that RS-CL consistently improves the performance of state-of-the-art VLA models, achieving new benchmarks on RoboCasa-Kitchen and showing substantial gains on challenging real-robot manipulation tasks. This suggests a practical and effective method for improving robotic control.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The method's effectiveness is validated across diverse training scenarios, including fine-tuning and from-scratch training with various pre-trained VLM backbones, different proprioceptive state configurations, and even different action modeling paradigms (flow-matching vs. autoregressive). This indicates its broad applicability and robustness.
- **Directly Addressing a Core Limitation:** By focusing on refining the conditioning representations, the work directly tackles the problem of VLM embeddings being dominated by visual appearance rather than control-relevant factors, thereby bridging a critical gap for practical robot deployment.

The **potential impact** of this work is substantial for advancing robot capabilities:
- **More Precise and Reliable Robot Control:** The improvements in tasks requiring precise positioning, such as pick-and-place and close-lid tasks, indicate that RS-CL can lead to more accurate and reliable robot manipulation, which is crucial for deployment in unstructured environments.
- **Reduced Data Requirements:** The significant gains observed in low-data regimes (e.g., 30 demonstrations for pick-and-place tasks) suggest that RS-CL could enable more effective robot learning with fewer expert demonstrations, reducing the cost and effort of data collection.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work encourages further exploration into integrating other physically grounded sensor signals, such as object pose or tactile feedback, into VLM representations. This could pave the way for more versatile and nuanced robot control in highly interactive or contact-rich tasks.
- **Broader Adoption of VLMs in Robotics:** By making VLM representations more amenable to robotic control in an efficient manner, RS-CL could facilitate the broader adoption of powerful vision-language models across various robotic platforms and applications, including humanoid robots and dexterous manipulation.

In summary, this research offers a principled, effective, and computationally efficient method to enhance the core representational capabilities of VLA models, driving progress towards more capable and autonomous robotic systems.
