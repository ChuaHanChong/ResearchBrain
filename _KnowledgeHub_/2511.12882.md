# Towards High-Consistency Embodied World Model with Multi-View Trajectory Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Midea Group, Tongji University, and East China Normal University introduce MTV-World, an embodied world model that enhances visuomotor prediction and physical interaction consistency by utilizing multi-view trajectory videos as explicit control signals and object-aware priors. The model outperforms baselines on robot manipulation tasks, achieving a Jaccard Index of 54.9 for interaction accuracy on a dual-arm dataset and demonstrating robust generalization.

## Problem

- Existing embodied world models struggle to accurately translate low-level robot actions into precise movements within predicted visual frames, resulting in unrealistic arm trajectories.
- Inaccurate robot arm motion leads to inconsistent simulations of object-arm interactions, undermining the reliability of world models for physical dynamics.
- A lack of objective evaluation metrics that quantitatively assess robotic motion precision and object interaction accuracy beyond perceptual quality (e.g., FID, FVD).

## Method

- Introduces ==trajectory videos== as explicit, visually grounded control signals, synthesized by projecting 3D robot end-effector poses onto 2D image planes across multiple camera views.
- Incorporates an ==object mask== from the initial frame as a ==foreground prior== to explicitly model the interaction between robotic arms and manipulated objects.
- Develops **MTV-World**, a ==Diffusion Transformer==-based ==multi-view architecture== that integrates reference images, text prompts, multi-view video sequences, and the synthesized trajectory videos for robust prediction.

## Results

- MTV-World achieved superior performance across both perceptual and interaction metrics, with a Jaccard Index (J) of **54.9** (View 1) and **45.0** (View 2) on a self-collected dual-arm dataset, significantly outperforming baselines (e.g., Ctrl-World's J of **35.3** for View 1).
- The model consistently outperformed baselines on the RealSource-World dataset, demonstrating generalization capability by maintaining high consistency and accurate object-arm interactions (e.g., J of **41.8** vs. Ctrl-World's **26.4** on the Magazines task).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the trajectory representation, multi-view architecture, and object mask prior individually contribute to improved performance, and MTV-World demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization to novel camera views, manipulation tasks, and objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using explicit multi-view trajectory videos as control signals provides clearer guidance for the world model, significantly improving the precision of predicted robot arm movements and the physical consistency of object interactions.
> - A multi-view architectural design effectively mitigates spatial information loss that occurs when projecting 3D actions to 2D, enhancing overall geometric and physical consistency.
> - Integrating an object mask as a foreground prior facilitates the modeling of contact-aware motion dynamics, leading to more accurate and reliable robot-object interaction predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper contributes to the field of embodied intelligence, specifically focusing on the development of world models. World models are predictive simulators that forecast future environmental states based on current observations and planned actions. These models are considered foundational for embodied intelligence, enabling robots to understand, predict, and interact with their physical environments. They have applications as data engines for generating diverse visual experiences to enhance policy learning, and as policy evaluators, offering a safe environment for refining decision-making strategies.

Existing research in world models has explored various approaches, including open-world video generation, navigation command conditioning, and incorporating physics-informed supervision. Embodied world models extend this by focusing on prediction and interaction within physical environments via visual observations and actions. This area has seen the development of hierarchical simulation frameworks, multi-level action-conditioning mechanisms, latent feature alignment for long-horizon prediction, large-scale video-action modeling, and unified vision-language-action (VLA) and world simulation frameworks. Some approaches also involve 4D embodied video datasets with depth and surface normals for physical consistency, and the integration of reinforcement learning for verifiable rewards.

However, a recurring limitation in current embodied world models is their difficulty in accurately translating low-level actions (e.g., joint positions) into precise robotic movements within predicted visual frames. This often results in unrealistic arm trajectories and, consequently, an inability to reliably simulate object-arm interactions that are consistent with real-world physical dynamics. This paper addresses these specific challenges by proposing a method that enhances the precision of visuomotor prediction and the physical consistency of object interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an embodied world model, named MTV-World, that can achieve precise visuomotor prediction and ensure high consistency with real-world physical interactions. The motivation stems from two significant limitations identified in existing embodied world models:

1. **Imprecise Translation of Low-Level Actions:** Current models frequently struggle to accurately map low-level robotic actions, such as joint positions, into corresponding precise movements in predicted video frames. This often leads to generated arm trajectories that appear unstable or unrealistic, diverging from how robots would move in the physical world. This imprecision undermines the utility of world models as accurate predictive simulators.

2. **Inconsistent Object-Arm Interactions:** As a direct consequence of the first challenge, when the predicted robot arm motion itself is inaccurate, the model cannot reliably simulate how the arm would interact with objects in the environment. This results in inconsistencies between the model's predictions and actual physical dynamics, making the generated simulations less trustworthy for tasks like policy evaluation or data generation. For instance, a model might predict a successful grasp when, in reality, the arm misses the object, or vice-versa.

To address these limitations, the authors propose moving beyond direct use of low-level actions for control. They are motivated to introduce a more explicit and visually grounded control signal: multi-view trajectory videos. This approach aims to provide the model with clearer guidance on robotic movements, thereby improving both the precision of the predicted arm trajectories and the accuracy of object-arm interactions. Furthermore, recognizing that projecting 3D actions to 2D images inherently causes spatial information loss, the researchers are motivated to incorporate a multi-view framework and object-aware priors to compensate for this loss and enhance geometric and physical consistency.

Finally, the paper is also motivated to establish a systematic and objective evaluation methodology. Existing metrics, such as FID and FVD, primarily assess perceptual quality but do not directly quantify the accuracy of robotic motion or object interaction. Therefore, a key objective is to develop an auto-evaluation pipeline that measures both robotic motion precision and object interaction accuracy, formulated as an object location matching problem using a robust metric like the Jaccard Index.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed MTV-World model addresses the identified challenges through a multi-faceted approach involving novel trajectory and object representations, a multi-view model architecture, and a dedicated evaluation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The world model `W` is designed to simulate the physical environment by predicting future observations `(o_{t+1}..., o_{t+T})` given a current observation `o_t = [I_t^1..., I_t^V, q_t]` (multi-view images `I_t^v` and robot pose `q_t`), a language instruction `l`, and a sequence of actions `A_t = [a_{t+1}..., a_{t+T}]` generated by a policy `π`.

#### 3.2. Trajectory Representation

To provide explicit motion control signals and overcome issues with low-level actions, MTV-World utilizes "trajectory videos." The generation process involves three steps:
1. **3D Pose Transformation:** Raw action sequences (robot joint positions `q_t`) generated by a vision-language-action (VLA) model are first converted into Cartesian-space end-effector poses. This is achieved using the forward kinematics (FK) model of a 6-DoF robot, which computes the 3D position `p_w = (x_w, y_w, z_w)` of each point in the robot's base (world) coordinate system.
2. **2D Image Projection:** The 3D world coordinates `p_w` are transformed into the camera coordinate system `p_c` using calibrated camera extrinsic parameters (rotation matrix `R` and translation vector `t`). These camera coordinates `p_c = (x_c, y_c, z_c)` are then projected onto the 2D image plane to obtain pixel coordinates `(u, v)` using the pinhole camera model, which incorporates the camera's intrinsic parameters (focal lengths `f_x, f_y` and principal point `c_x, c_y`).
3. **Trajectory Video Synthesis:** For each frame, the projected 2D pixel coordinates of the end-effector are rendered as fading trails of glowing points. These points visualize the continuity of motion over time. This process is performed for each camera view, producing `V` synchronized trajectory videos `X_traj`, which serve as intuitive control signals for the model.

#### 3.3. Object Representation

To explicitly model the interaction between robotic arms and manipulated objects, the model incorporates an object mask from the initial frame as a foreground prior. The masks are obtained automatically through a pipeline:
1. **Textual Description Generation:** A Vision Language Model (VLM) is prompted in a visual question answering (VQA) manner to generate textual descriptions for each object in the input image.
2. **Segmentation Mask Generation:** These textual descriptions are then fed into a Referring Video Object Segmentation (RVOS) model, which generates segmentation masks for all objects across the entire video.
3. **Initial Frame Mask Prior:** For the world model, only the object mask extracted from the initial frame is used as a foreground prior. This single-frame mask is duplicated across all frames to construct a reference mask sequence for the entire video.

#### 3.4. Model Architecture

The MTV-World architecture is based on a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) framework, designed for multi-view video synthesis conditioned on control signals.
1. **Reference Image and Text Encoding:** The first frame from each of the `V` synchronized camera views (`{I_v}`), along with a text prompt `l`, serve as scene priors. Each reference image `I_v` is encoded by a pre-trained CLIP image encoder to extract global semantic features `F_v`. The text prompt `l` is tokenized and embedded by a umT5 text encoder, yielding contextual tokens `F_text`. These features (`{F_1..., F_V, F_text}`) are then concatenated to form a unified multimodal context, which interacts with the latent representations via cross-attention within the DiT.
2. **Video and Trajectory Encoding:** Both the multi-view video sequences `X_video` (initial frame plus `T` future frames) and the multi-view trajectory control videos `X_traj` are encoded into compact latent representations using a shared variational autoencoder (VAE) encoder.
3. **Multi-View Latent Integration:** Each reference image `I_v` is also processed by the same VAE encoder to obtain a reference latent `r_v`. This `r_v` is passed through a lightweight adapter `ϕ(·)` to align its channel dimension, resulting in `˜r_v`. For each view `v`, the processed reference latent `˜r_v` is concatenated with its corresponding encoded video latent sequence `X_v` along the temporal dimension. This forms a unified multi-view latent set `X` where each row corresponds to a camera view and includes its reference latent.
4. **Diffusion Process and Decoding:** This unified latent set `X` is then processed by the Diffusion Transformer layers. The DiT generates an output sequence `ˆX`. To reconstruct the predicted video, the initial reference tokens are removed, and only the frame-wise outputs (`X_kept`) corresponding to the future `T` frames across all views are retained. These retained latent representations are then decoded into pixel space using the shared VAE decoder `Dec(·)`, yielding multi-view video predictions `{X_pred_v}`.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Pipeline

The paper introduces an automated evaluation pipeline to quantitatively assess both robotic motion precision and object interaction accuracy, as existing metrics like FID and FVD primarily measure perceptual quality.
1. **Object Location Matching:** The core idea is that if a generated video accurately simulates robot manipulation and object interaction, the predicted object positions should closely match those in the ground-truth video. This is formulated as a mask-matching problem.
2. **Automated Mask Generation:** Similar to the object representation step, a VLM (Doubao Thinking Vision) is used in a VQA manner to generate text descriptions for objects in both generated and ground-truth videos. These descriptions are then used by an RVOS model (SAMWISE) to produce object segmentation masks across the entire video.
3. **Jaccard Index (J) Calculation:** For each frame `t`, the Jaccard Index `J_t` is computed between the predicted object mask `M_t_pred` and the ground-truth object mask `M_t_gt`. The overall video-level Jaccard Index `J` is obtained by averaging `J_t` across all frames `T`. This metric quantifies the spatial consistency of object locations and manipulation accuracy. The pipeline is fully automated, providing an end-to-end quantitative assessment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MTV-World was conducted on a self-collected dual-arm robotic manipulation dataset and the RealSource-World dataset, comparing it against established baselines and through extensive ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Dataset and Task Setup

- **Self-collected Dataset:** Comprises 1,492 videos across 15 real-world dual-arm manipulation tasks (e.g., Bussing Table, Stack Blocks Two), with both successful and failed trials (8:2 ratio). It uses an ALOHA-style dual-arm robot platform (YAM) with two 6-DoF manipulators and two RealSense D457 cameras (View 1: lower, View 2: higher).
- **RealSource-World Dataset:** Used for generalization, selecting three single-view tasks (Magazines, Pen Holder, TV Cabinet) with 600 videos.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) for perceptual quality and temporal consistency, respectively. Jaccard Index (J) for spatial interaction consistency and manipulation accuracy, as defined by the proposed evaluation pipeline.

#### 4.2. Baseline Comparison

MTV-World was compared with three baselines: World-Eval (vector-based action encoding), Ctrl-World (3D-projection-based action processing), and Fast-Token (token-based action compression).

- **Self-collected Dataset (Table 1):** MTV-World demonstrated superior performance across all metrics and views. For View 1, it achieved FID of 22.0, FVD of 42.8, and J of 54.9. For View 2, it achieved FID of 17.5, FVD of 65.8, and J of 45.0. These results represent significant improvements over baselines, indicating that explicit multi-view trajectory control videos lead to more precise and physically grounded robotic behaviors compared to implicit or compressed action representations. For example, Ctrl-World achieved J scores of 35.3 (View 1) and 22.2 (View 2), while MTV-World's scores were 54.9 and 45.0 respectively.

- **RealSource-World Dataset (Table 2):** MTV-World consistently outperformed Ctrl-World and Fast-Token across the three selected tasks (Magazines, Pen Holder, TV Cabinet). For instance, on the Magazines task, MTV-World achieved FID 26.2, FVD 206.6, and J 41.8, while Ctrl-World achieved FID 30.0, FVD 438.3, and J 26.4. This confirms the generalization capability of the trajectory-video control mechanism to maintain high consistency and accurate object-arm interactions on a different, large-scale dataset.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 3)

Ablation experiments systematically validated the contributions of MTV-World's components. The results are reported using the Jaccard Index (J) per task and averaged.

- **Trajectory Representation:** Comparisons with baselines (World-Eval, Ctrl-World, Fast-Token) confirmed that MTV-World's explicit trajectory video control consistently yielded higher J scores across all tasks and views, demonstrating its effectiveness in guiding motion and interaction generation.
- **Multi-View vs. Single-View:** A Single-View variant showed that while it performed comparably in the simpler low-view scenario (J of 51.3 averaged), its performance significantly dropped in the more complex high-view scenario (J of 38.0 averaged), lagging behind the Multi-View MTV-World (J of 45.0 averaged for high view). This indicates that the multi-view architecture enhances geometric consistency and captures object interactions more effectively from complementary perspectives.
- **Zero-Shot Ability:** MTV-World exhibited robust zero-shot generalization to novel camera views, manipulation tasks, and objects not seen during training or testing. The zero-shot model achieved competitive average J scores of 49.4 for View 1 and 39.0 for View 2, demonstrating its ability to generalize to unseen conditions.
- **Object Mask:** The inclusion of the object mask as a foreground prior improved J scores across both views (average J of 54.9 for View 1 and 45.0 for View 2, compared to 54.8 and 42.6 without the mask). This suggests that the object mask is effective in facilitating the modeling of contact-aware motion dynamics and accurate interaction between the robot arm and manipulated objects.

#### 4.4. Visualization Results

- **Self-collected Dataset (Figure 3):** Qualitative comparisons demonstrated MTV-World's ability to predict physically consistent outcomes. In a "Stack Blocks" task, MTV-World correctly predicted the block being stacked, unlike World-Eval which misplaced it. In a "Place Bread Plate" failure case, MTV-World accurately reproduced the failed trajectory where the bread was not placed, whereas World-Eval incorrectly showed a successful placement.
- **RealSource-World Dataset (Figure 4):** For the "Pen Holder" task, MTV-World's predictions closely aligned with the ground truth, successfully completing the task without robotic arm movement inconsistencies or interaction failures, which were observed in Ctrl-World and Fast-Token.
- **Multi-View Trajectory Videos (Figure 5):** Visualizations for tasks like "Shake Bottle" and "Collect Toy" showed that the glowing trajectory points formed paths consistent with the intended robot actions, clearly depicting spatio-temporal motion prediction and manipulation dynamics across different views.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MTV-World, a novel embodied world model that addresses key limitations in existing models by enhancing the precision of visuomotor prediction and the consistency of physical interactions. The significance of this work lies in several areas:

1. **Improved Physical Consistency and Precision:** By employing multi-view trajectory videos as explicit control signals, MTV-World demonstrates a method for bridging the gap between low-level robot actions and realistic, high-fidelity physical interactions in predicted visual environments. This is a critical step towards creating world models that can reliably simulate complex robotic manipulation tasks. The multi-view framework further mitigates spatial information loss, contributing to enhanced geometric consistency.

2. **Enhanced Action Representation:** The proposed trajectory video representation offers an alternative to conventional low-level action inputs or latent action encodings. Its explicit, visually grounded nature provides clearer guidance to the world model, leading to more accurate robotic arm movements and object manipulations. This could influence future designs of control interfaces for embodied AI systems.

3. **Systematic Evaluation Methodology:** The development of an automated evaluation pipeline, which leverages multimodal large models and referring video object segmentation to measure interaction accuracy via the Jaccard Index, is a notable contribution. This pipeline provides a more objective and task-relevant metric beyond traditional perceptual quality measures (FID, FVD), enabling a more comprehensive assessment of world model capabilities in embodied contexts. This methodology could become a standard for evaluating the functional performance of embodied world models.

4. **Robust Generalization:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization capabilities to novel views, tasks, and objects suggest that MTV-World has the potential to operate effectively in diverse and unseen environments without extensive retraining. This is crucial for deploying embodied AI systems in dynamic real-world settings.

The potential impact of MTV-World is substantial. As a more precise and physically consistent predictive simulator, it could:
- **Accelerate Policy Learning:** Generate high-fidelity synthetic data, reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection and enabling faster training of robotic policies.
- **Improve Policy Evaluation:** Provide a more reliable and safe environment for evaluating and refining robot control policies, reducing the risk of damage or unsafe behaviors during real-world experimentation.
- **Enable Complex Robotic Applications:** Facilitate the development of robots capable of performing more intricate and coordinated multi-arm manipulation tasks, especially in scenarios requiring high precision and robust interaction with objects.
- **Advance Embodied AI Research:** Serve as a foundation for future research in embodied intelligence, particularly in areas requiring accurate physical reasoning, planning, and long-horizon prediction.
