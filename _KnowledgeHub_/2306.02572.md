# Introduction to Latent Variable Energy-Based Models: A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> A conceptual framework for autonomous machine intelligence is presented, built upon latent variable energy-based models and Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). This framework aims to enable self-supervised learning of world models that handle multimodality and uncertainty, supporting hierarchical planning.

## Problem

- Current supervised learning (SL) requires extensive, costly labeled datasets and struggles with generalization beyond training distributions.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) demands numerous trial-and-error episodes, making it impractical for many real-world applications where mistakes are costly.
- Existing AI systems lack the flexibility, common sense reasoning, and generalization abilities that characterize human intelligence.

## Method

- Proposing a conceptual framework centered on ==energy-based== models (EBMs) with latent variables to define compatibility between variables, supporting multimodal predictions and uncertainty.
- Introducing Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) that learn representations of inputs and outputs in a common embedding space, trained via self-supervised learning.
- Developing Hierarchical JEPAs (H-JEPAs) to enable planning and prediction at multiple temporal or abstraction levels, akin to human hierarchical planning.

## Results

- The framework enables learning rich world models directly from unlabeled data, mirroring human observational learning processes.
- It supports multimodal predictions and structured representation of uncertainty, allowing AI systems to account for multiple plausible outcomes.
- The approach facilitates hierarchical planning, allowing systems to break down complex long-term goals into subgoals and engage in 'mental imagination' to evaluate action sequences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning is critical for autonomous intelligence, allowing systems to learn rich world models from unlabeled data by predicting parts of inputs from other parts.
> - Energy-based models inherently handle multimodal outputs and provide a measure of uncertainty through their energy landscape, unlike deterministic models.
> - Latent variables within energy-based models enable the representation of multiple plausible futures or interpretations, facilitating reasoning under ambiguity and efficient representation of multimodal distributions.
