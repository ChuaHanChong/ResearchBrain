# Cosmos-Drive-Dreams: Scalable Synthetic Driving Data Generation with World Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Cosmos-Drive-Dreams is a synthetic data generation pipeline built on Cosmos-Drive, a suite of driving-specialized world foundation models post-trained from NVIDIA Cosmos-1, that renders HDMap/LiDAR-conditioned videos, rewrites prompts for scenario diversity, expands single-view to multi-view, and filters outputs via VLM rejection sampling, yielding synthetic data that measurably improves 3D lane detection, 3D object detection, and driving policy learning, especially on rare and safety-critical scenarios.

## Problem

- Physics-based simulators offer controllable scenario authoring but lack photorealism and require heavy manual engineering to cover long-tail edge cases like snowy nights or multi-agent collisions.
- Reconstruction-based renderers (NeRF, Gaussian splatting) achieve high fidelity but stay tied to the original captured viewpoints and cannot synthesize entirely novel scenes or modify weather and lighting attributes.
- Prior generative driving-video models struggle to produce rare safety-critical events with fidelity since they inherit the same real-world training distribution where such events are underrepresented.

## Method

- **Cosmos-Drive** model suite post-trains ==Cosmos-1 WFM== into four specialized models: a ==ControlNet== for HDMap/LiDAR-conditioned precise layout control, a single-to-multi-view expansion model, an in-the-wild video annotation model, and a LiDAR generation model with sensor-accurate range-map representation.
- The **Cosmos-Drive-Dreams** pipeline chains these models with an ==LLM-based prompt rewriter== (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) that diversifies weather and time-of-day text conditions while preserving scene layout and content.
- A ==VLM-based rejection sampling== module (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) performs automated visual-quality filtering to discard artifacted or implausible generated clips before they enter the synthetic training set.

## Results

- On Waymo Open and RDS-HQ 3D lane detection, Cosmos-Drive-Dreams beats an Albumentations augmentation baseline, gaining **+10.4%** F1 on rainy and **+9.4%** on foggy splits, and **+6.4%** on the overall test set.
- In 3D object detection, augmenting 1K real clips with generated data yields a **55.0%** relative LET-AP gain, matching the benefit of a **9.3x** increase in real training clips, with consistent gains across monocular, multi-view, and LiDAR-based detectors.
- For policy learning, synthetic augmentation lowers minADE across all real-clip scales (**30k-135k**), and targeted VRU/left-turn synthetic data improves those corner-case metrics using synthetic clips at under **2%** of the training set without degrading overall driving performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Post-training a large pretrained world foundation model for a narrow domain lets synthetic data generation inherit broad physical priors while still achieving domain-specific controllability and fidelity.
> - Small, targeted injections of synthetic data addressing a specific underrepresented scenario type can move corner-case metrics substantially more efficiently than proportionally scaling generic real-world data collection.
> - Automated VLM-based rejection sampling offers a scalable, human-free substitute for manual quality control, suggesting generative data pipelines can self-curate rather than rely on labor-intensive filtering.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and reliable autonomous vehicles is profoundly data-driven, yet real-world data collection and annotation present immense challenges. These challenges include the high cost and time commitment, and more critically, the difficulty in capturing "long-tail" or "corner-case" scenarios (e.g., unusual weather, rare pedestrian behaviors, near-collisions) that are vital for training safe and generalized AV systems. Models trained solely on common real-world data often fail to generalize to these critical, but infrequent, events.

Synthetic data generation (SDG) has emerged as a promising solution. The paper categorizes existing SDG approaches into three main types:
- **Physics-based simulators (e.g., CARLA, AirSim):** These offer high control and customizability by crafting 3D digital scenes using graphics engines. However, they often lack photorealism, require extensive manual engineering to scale, and struggle to generate diverse long-tail scenarios with high fidelity.
- **Reconstruction-based rendering (e.g., NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting based methods like UniSim):** These convert real-world driving logs into renderable neural scenes, offering high photorealism and closed-loop simulation capabilities. Their limitation is that outputs are heavily tied to original observations, making it difficult to create entirely *new* scenes or modify scene attributes (like weather or lighting) beyond what was observed. They are "non-generative" in the sense of creating novel content.
- **Generation-based simulators (e.g., Drive-WM, MagicDrive, GAIA):** These leverage advances in generative AI, particularly diffusion models, to synthesize realistic driving scenes. While powerful, they can be limited by the underlying training data distribution, struggling to produce rare scenarios with high fidelity if these are underrepresented in real-world datasets. GAIA-2's attempt to scale training data (14,000 hours) shows the effort required to improve generative coverage.

`Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` positions itself as a significant advancement within the **generation-based simulators** category, explicitly addressing the limitations of existing approaches. By building on NVIDIA's `Cosmos-1 World Foundation Model`—a large-scale generalist generative video model pre-trained on "tens of millions of physically grounded video clips"—and then *post-training* it on 20,000 hours of "carefully balanced driving footage" (NVIDIA's proprietary Real Driving Scene, RDS dataset), this work aims to bridge the gap. It combines the strong generative capabilities of a foundation model with domain-specific knowledge and fine-grained control, allowing for the creation of *novel* and *diverse* scenarios (including extreme weather, specific layouts, and challenging events) that are both photorealistic and spatiotemporally consistent. The integration of advanced features like multi-view generation, in-the-wild video annotation for HDMap and LiDAR, and LLM/VLM-based quality control further distinguishes it, moving towards a more complete and automated synthetic data generation pipeline for AVs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` research are:

1. **Address Data Scarcity and Cost in AV Development:** Collecting and annotating real-world data for autonomous vehicle (AV) systems is prohibitively time-consuming and costly. This applies to basic driving scenarios, but the problem is exacerbated for rare or "long-tail" events.
2. **Capture Rare and Safety-Critical Edge Cases:** It is especially challenging to collect sufficient real-world data for rare edge cases (e.g., sudden pedestrian crossings, erratic vehicle behavior, extreme weather, unusual road layouts). These scenarios, however, are critical for training robust and reliable AV perception and driving policy models, as models trained solely on naturally occurring data often fail to generalize to such corner cases.
3. **Facilitate Downstream AV Tasks:** The ultimate goal is to generate challenging scenarios that can directly enhance the training and testing of downstream AV tasks, including 3D perception (e.g., 3D lane detection, 3D object detection) and driving policy learning, thereby improving the reliability and safety of autonomous systems.
4. **Leverage World Foundation Models for Domain Specialization:** To achieve scalability and high fidelity, the project aims to demonstrate the utility of specializing a powerful generalist "world foundation model" (NVIDIA Cosmos-1) for the driving domain. This approach leverages broad world knowledge while tailoring it to the specific geometric and dynamic constraints of driving.
5. **Enable Controllable, High-Fidelity, Multi-View, and Spatiotemporally Consistent Generation:** Beyond just generating images, the objective is to produce *videos* that maintain consistency across time and multiple camera views, can be precisely controlled by structured inputs (like HDMaps and LiDAR), and are photorealistic.
6. **Provide a Scalable and Automated Pipeline:** The research seeks to develop a pipeline that can significantly scale the quantity and diversity of driving datasets with minimal manual intervention, incorporating automated quality control mechanisms (e.g., rejection sampling).
7. **Open-Source Contribution:** A key motivation is to open-source the pipeline toolkit, dataset, and model weights through NVIDIA’s Cosmos platform, fostering broader research and development in the AV community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` pipeline (Figure 2) is a sophisticated synthetic data generation (SDG) system built upon `Cosmos-Drive`, a suite of models specialized from NVIDIA's generalist `Cosmos-1` world foundation model. The overall approach follows a "data flywheel" concept, where synthetic data continuously enhances AV models.

#### 3.1. Cosmos-Drive Model Suite for AV (The Core Generative Engine)

`Cosmos-Drive` consists of several post-trained models from the `Cosmos-1 World Foundation Model (WFM)`, which is a collection of generalist WFMs for Physical AI. The post-training utilizes large-scale, high-quality driving datasets like `Real Driving Scene (RDS)` (3.6M 20-second 6-view clips) and `Real Driving Scene HQ (RDS-HQ)` (750 hours of 30-FPS 6-view clips with detailed annotations).

The specialized `Cosmos-Drive` models include:

- **`Cosmos-Transfer1-7B-Sample-AV` (Precise Layout Control):** This model generates single-view driving videos conditioned on structured inputs like HDMap videos (containing lane lines, road boundaries, 3D cuboids for objects, traffic lights, etc.) and optional LiDAR depth. It uses a ControlNet architecture on top of `Cosmos-7B-Sample-AV` (a `Cosmos-1` text-to-video model fine-tuned for driving). This allows for precise, geometry-aware control over scene layouts and ego-car trajectories, crucial for simulating specific and rare scenarios (e.g., emergency vehicle encounters, sharp turns).
- **`Cosmos-7B-Single2Multiview-Sample-AV` (Single-View to Multi-View Expansion):** This model extends a generated single-view (e.g., frontal) video into multiple consistent camera views (up to six, like front, rear, sides). It is fine-tuned from `Cosmos-7B-Multiview-Sample-AV` (another `Cosmos-1` model post-trained for multi-view driving videos). The architecture allows flexible conditioning on one view to generate others while maintaining spatiotemporal consistency.
- **`Cosmos-7B-Annotate-Sample-AV` (In-the-Wild Video Annotation Model):** A novel component that addresses data accessibility. It takes in-the-wild (unlabeled) single-view RGB videos and predicts corresponding HDMap and LiDAR depth information. This enables `re-simulation` of scenarios from public videos, effectively generating structured labels where none existed, reducing reliance on costly sensor setups and manual annotation. The model adapts `Cosmos-7B-Sample-AV` by replacing text tokens with trainable context embeddings for each output modality.
- **`Cosmos-7B-LiDAR-GEN-Sample-AV` (LiDAR Generation Model):** This model synthesizes high-quality LiDAR point cloud data, represented as range maps, conditioned on either HDMap inputs or RGB images. It includes careful LiDAR preprocessing (motion compensation, elevation/azimuth angle profiles) and a fine-tuned LiDAR tokenizer to handle the unique modality characteristics. This enhances the completeness of the neural simulator for physical AI.

#### 3.2. Cosmos-Drive-Dreams Pipeline Steps (The SDG Workflow)

The `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` pipeline orchestrates these models to generate high-quality synthetic datasets:

1. **Structured Label/Video Input (Step ➊):** The pipeline starts with either existing structured labels (HDMaps, 3D cuboids, ego trajectories) from datasets like RDS-HQ and Waymo Open, or it can generate these labels from "in-the-wild" RGB videos using `Cosmos-7B-Annotate-Sample-AV`. This generates pixel-aligned HDMap condition videos.
2. **Prompt Rewriting and Single-View Generation (Step ➋):**
 - An **LLM-based Prompt Rewriter (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct)** automatically modifies an initial video caption to create diverse textual descriptions. This allows generating variations of the same scene with different environmental contexts (e.g., "rainy," "snowy," "golden-hour," "nighttime") while preserving the core content and spatial layout, ensuring consistency with the HDMap condition.
 - The `Cosmos-Transfer1-7B-Sample-AV` model then synthesizes high-quality single-view videos using the HDMap condition and the diverse text prompts.
3. **Multi-View Expansion (Step ➌):** The generated single-view videos are expanded into a consistent multi-view format (e.g., front, rear, side views) using the `Cosmos-7B-Single2Multiview-Sample-AV` model. This is critical for AV applications that rely on a holistic understanding of the vehicle's surroundings.
4. **Rejection Sampling (Step ➍):** To ensure visual realism and consistency, a **Vision-Language Model (VLM) filter (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct)** performs automated rejection sampling. This VLM evaluates short video clips for visual anomalies (e.g., object disappearance, shape distortion, rendering glitches) and discards low-quality or unrealistic outputs, providing a human-free quality control mechanism.

The output is a high-fidelity, diverse synthetic driving dataset with corresponding structured labels, suitable for direct use in training downstream AV tasks. The authors applied this pipeline to generate 7x synthetic clips for each real clip from RDS-HQ and Waymo Open datasets, across various conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper evaluates the effectiveness of `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` generated data across three critical downstream autonomous driving tasks: 3D lane detection, 3D object detection, and driving policy learning. The key findings consistently demonstrate performance improvements, especially for challenging "long-tail" scenarios.

#### 4.1. 3D Lane Detection

- **Dataset:** RDS-HQ (various sizes, 2k-20k clips) and Waymo Open Dataset.
- **Model:** LATR, a transformer-based monocular 3D lane detector.
- **Comparison:** `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` (SDG) vs. baseline (real data only) vs. Albumentations (pixel-level augmentation).
- **Results:**
 - `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` significantly improved F1-score and category accuracy over both baselines.
 - **Corner Cases:** Most notable gains were observed in extreme weather conditions. For RDS-HQ (2k clips), SDG achieved F1-score improvements of **10.4% on rainy** and **9.4% on foggy** test splits, which are underrepresented in training data.
 - **Generalization:** A 6.4% gain on the overall test set demonstrated broad augmentation capability.
 - **Data Efficiency:** SDG provided consistent improvements even when augmenting large real-world datasets (e.g., +2.4% on F1-score for foggy conditions with 20k real clips), suggesting continued benefits even with substantial real data. The performance gap between models with and without SDG narrowed with more real data, indicating SDG's effectiveness in lower-data regimes but also the potential for further scaling of SDG.

#### 4.2. 3D Object Detection

- **Dataset:** RDS-HQ (2k, 20k, 52k for HDMap labels) and Waymo Open Dataset.
- **Model:** BEVFormer, a temporal, transformer-based 3D object detector.
- **Settings:**
 - **Setting A (SDG augments real clips):** Consistent with lane detection, `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` improved LET-AP metrics across all dataset scales and conditions (monocular and multi-view). Significant gains were seen in rainy and foggy scenes.
 - **Setting B (SDG from disjoint HDMap labels):** SDG data generated from auto-labeled RDS-HQ significantly boosted performance on human-labeled RDS-HQ-HL, demonstrating transferability from synthetic labels to realistic driving clips.
- **Data Efficiency:** Adding `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` SDG data to 1K real RDS-HQ-HL clips yielded a performance improvement (+14.5% in LET-AP) comparable to a 9.3x increase in *real* data (Fig. 16, Left). Performance correlated positively with the amount of SDG data added.

#### 4.3. LiDAR-based 3D Object Detection

- **Dataset:** RDS-HQ (1k, 2k).
- **Model:** Transfusion-LiDAR, a 3D object detector.
- **Results:** The addition of SDG LiDAR data (150k frames generated with the specialized LiDAR model) generally improved detection performance (mAP) for different real data subsets, particularly enhancing recall for larger and more distant objects.

#### 4.4. Policy Learning

- **Dataset:** RDS-HQ (30k-135k clips) for training, RDS-Bench[Policy] for evaluation.
- **Model:** A transformer-based policy model.
- **Results:**
 - Adding synthetic data consistently improved trajectory prediction accuracy (minADE metric) for any given amount of real-world clips. The effect was particularly strong for smaller training set sizes.
 - Even the largest real dataset (135k clips) benefited from SDG, suggesting that SDG's added diversity helps reduce overfitting and boosts generalization.
 - **Data Efficiency:** SDG significantly lowered the demand for real-world data to achieve a target performance. For example, achieving minADE = 1.35 required 60k real clips, but only 50k real clips with 푅푠2푟 = 0.5 SDG, or 39k clips with 푅푠2푟 = 3.0 SDG.
 - **Targeted Improvements:** Small amounts of targeted SDG data (e.g., for vulnerable road users or sharp left turns) significantly improved performance in these corner cases without negatively impacting general driving performance, demonstrating SDG's ability to address specific weaknesses.

Overall, the findings strongly support that `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` effectively mitigates the long-tail distribution problem and enhances generalization in diverse downstream AV tasks by providing high-fidelity, diverse, and controllable synthetic data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` pipeline represents a significant step forward in scalable synthetic data generation for autonomous driving, with profound implications for the development and deployment of AV technology.

1. **Addressing the Long-Tail Problem:** The most critical impact is its demonstrated ability to effectively generate and augment training data for rare and challenging "corner-case" scenarios. This directly tackles a major bottleneck in AV development: the difficulty and cost of acquiring sufficient real-world data for these safety-critical situations. By improving model performance on extreme weather, nighttime, and unusual object interactions, `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` contributes to building more robust and safer AV systems.
2. **Scalability and Cost Reduction:** By leveraging powerful generative foundation models and automating key steps (prompt rewriting, multi-view expansion, quality control), the pipeline offers a highly scalable solution for producing vast quantities of diverse data. This has the potential to significantly reduce the reliance on expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection and manual annotation efforts. The ability to automatically annotate in-the-wild videos for generating HDMap and LiDAR further enhances data accessibility.
3. **Enhanced Model Generalization and Robustness:** The experimental results across 3D lane detection, 3D object detection, and policy learning consistently show that augmenting training datasets with `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` data leads to improved performance, particularly under challenging conditions. This indicates better generalization capabilities for AV models, making them more reliable in diverse real-world environments.
4. **Flexible and Controllable Data Generation:** The modular design of `Cosmos-Drive` allows for fine-grained control over scene layouts, ego-vehicle trajectories, weather conditions, and object placement. This flexibility enables AV developers to specifically target and generate data for scenarios that are currently underserved or problematic for their models, facilitating rapid iteration and improvement.
5. **Paving the Way for Neural Simulators:** This work advances the concept of "neural simulators" for Physical AI. By generating multi-modal data (RGB, HDMap, LiDAR) with spatiotemporal consistency and precise control, `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` moves closer to providing a comprehensive, high-fidelity neural simulation environment that can complement or even replace aspects of traditional physics-based simulators.
6. **Open-Source Contribution:** NVIDIA's decision to open-source the `Cosmos-Drive-Dreams` pipeline toolkit, a subset of the RDS-HQ dataset, and the synthetic dataset itself, along with model weights, is a substantial contribution to the research community. This lowers the barrier to entry for other researchers and companies to experiment with and build upon these advanced generative capabilities, potentially accelerating innovations in AV and other Physical AI domains.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge a primary limitation: the reliance on computationally heavy diffusion models makes large-scale data generation "time- and resource-intensive." Future work will focus on optimizing these aspects to further enhance the scalability and efficiency of the pipeline. Additionally, while the paper demonstrates strong results, continuous research will be needed to enhance the diversity, photorealism, and physical accuracy for even more complex and rare scenarios, and to rigorously validate the safety implications of training on synthetic data for real-world deployment.
