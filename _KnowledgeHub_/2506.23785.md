# Visual Textualization for Image Prompted Object Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> VisTex-OVLM is a novel image-prompted object detection method that projects visual exemplars into the text feature space of Object-level Vision-Language Models (OVLMs). This approach enhances detection capabilities for rare or novel categories without altering the OVLM's original architecture, thereby preserving its pre-trained object-text alignment and generalization.

## Problem

- OVLMs demonstrate suboptimal performance on rare or underrepresented objects not sufficiently covered in their vast pre-training datasets.
- Text prompts for object detection can suffer from granularity limitations, semantic bias, or omissions, making fine-grained object distinction challenging.
- Existing few-shot methods for OVLMs often disrupt the models' pre-trained object-text alignment and generalization capabilities when adapting to new data.

## Method

- A ==visual textualization== process is introduced to convert visual information from support images into a textualized feature format compatible with OVLM text prompts.
- ==Multi-scale Textualizing Blocks (MSTB)== extract and map multi-scale visual features from image-engineered support images into the OVLM's text feature space, using a parameter-sharing strategy for efficiency.
- Textualized visual tokens are consolidated via non-parametric ==Multi-Stage Fusion (MSF)== and concatenated directly with original text prompts, then fed into an unmodified and frozen pre-trained OVLM.

## Results

- VisTex-GLIP achieved state-of-the-art performance on PASCAL VOC (e.g., **74.8** AP50 on Split 1, 10-shot) and MSCOCO few-shot object detection benchmarks, outperforming prior VLM-based methods.
- The method demonstrated superior detection for novel categories across open-set scenarios including LVIS MiniVal, 11 ODinW35 datasets, and 5 challenging medical datasets (MoNu, CCRCC, ConSep, LIDC, Deeplesion).
- Ablation studies confirmed that multi-scale textualization, parameter-sharing in MSTB, and non-parametric multi-stage fusion are critical components that synergistically enhance performance and maintain OVLM integrity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Projecting visual semantics from few-shot exemplars directly into the text feature space enables seamless integration with text-prompt-exclusive OVLMs without architectural modifications.
> - Employing background blur in image prompt engineering effectively highlights target objects, and multi-scale visual textualization leverages diverse feature representations for robust object learning.
> - Strictly preserving the pre-trained OVLM architecture and its object-text alignment is crucial for maintaining strong generalization capabilities across both base and novel categories, preventing performance degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), object detection, and few-shot/open-set learning. It addresses critical limitations of current state-of-the-art Object-level Vision-Language Models (OVLMs) like GLIP and GroundingDINO, which have achieved remarkable success in zero-shot object detection (ZSOD) by leveraging vast amounts of image-text pre-training data.

**Key aspects of the landscape:**
- **Evolution of VLMs for Object Detection:** Initially, general VLMs like CLIP demonstrated strong image-level alignment but required significant adaptation (fine-tuning or additional modules) for object-level tasks. OVLMs, such as GLIP and GroundingDINO, emerged to bridge this gap by incorporating object detection and phrase grounding data during pre-training, leading to superior object-text alignment and better performance in downstream detection tasks.
- **Limitations of Zero-Shot Object Detection (ZSOD):** Despite their advancements, ZSOD with OVLMs faces inherent challenges. Objects in real-world downstream tasks are often underrepresented in pre-training data, leading to suboptimal performance on "rare" categories. Additionally, text prompts, while powerful, can lack sufficient granularity to distinguish fine-grained objects or may suffer from semantic bias and omissions.
- **Few-Shot Object Detection (FSOD) and Generalization:** To address ZSOD limitations, few-shot object detection (FSOD) methods emerged, typically by leveraging a small number of visual exemplars (support images). Traditional FSOD methods often rely on meta-learning or complex inter-class relationship modeling. More recently, VLM-based FSOD methods have explored fine-tuning or adding processing structures to incorporate few-shot visual information. However, a significant drawback of these approaches, as highlighted by the authors' empirical analysis, is the risk of "disrupting the OVLM's original object-text alignment and harming its generalization capabilities." This disruption leads to a degradation of performance on existing (base) categories, a problem in generalized few-shot object detection (GFSOD) scenarios.
- **Emergence of Image Prompting:** Image prompting is a novel paradigm that uses images as supplementary semantic guidance alongside text prompts. Existing image prompting methods for detection, like OWL-ViT, are often restricted by pre-defined architectures or image-object pre-training. MQ-Det, specifically for OVLMs, attempts to integrate visual exemplars by modulating text prompts through added cross-attention modules. However, the authors argue that MQ-Det only re-weights existing text tokens without fully incorporating new visual information, and its added structures still compromise the pre-trained object-text alignment.
- **The Gap VisTex-OVLM Fills:** VisTex-OVLM directly addresses the unfulfilled need for an image-prompting method that can introduce rich visual semantics from few-shot exemplars into text-prompt-exclusive OVLMs *without altering their original architecture or disrupting their pre-trained object-text alignment*. By projecting visual information into the *text feature space*, it provides a seamless and architecture-preserving integration, thereby enhancing detection capabilities for rare and novel categories while maintaining generalization on known ones.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this paper is to develop a novel image-prompted object detection method that enhances Object-level Vision-Language Models' (OVLMs) ability to detect rare or novel categories, particularly those that are challenging to describe textually or are underrepresented in their pre-training data. This enhancement must be achieved *without disrupting the OVLMs' pre-trained object-text alignment and generalization capabilities*.

The motivation stems from several intrinsic limitations of current OVLM-based zero-shot object detection (ZSOD) and existing few-shot object detection (FSOD) methods:

- **Underrepresentation of Objects:** Many objects encountered in real-world downstream tasks are rare or absent in the vast pre-training datasets of OVLMs. This leads to suboptimal zero-shot transfer performance, as the models lack sufficient prior knowledge about these categories.
- **Text Prompt Granularity and Ambiguity:** Natural language text prompts often lack the precision or detail required to uniquely describe fine-grained objects. This can cause semantic bias, omissions, or make it difficult for OVLMs to distinguish between visually similar objects that share similar textual descriptions.
- **Degradation of Pre-trained Alignment:** Existing VLM-based FSOD or image-prompting methods typically involve fine-tuning the model weights or introducing new architectural components (e.g., cross-attention modules in MQ-Det). The authors empirically demonstrate (Fig. 1) that such modifications, especially with limited target-domain data, can significantly skew the object-text similarity distribution in the source domain, thereby degrading the crucial pre-trained object-text alignment and harming the model's overall generalization ability.
- **Lack of Native Image Prompting Support in OVLMs:** Most OVLMs are designed to be text-prompt-exclusive, lacking a native architectural mechanism to directly incorporate visual information from exemplar images into their prompting process. This necessitates specialized solutions.

VisTex-OVLM aims to overcome these issues by introducing a "visual textualization" process that effectively projects visual exemplars into the text feature space. This allows the combined visual and textual prompts to guide the *unmodified* pre-trained OVLM, leveraging its inherent capabilities without compromising its established knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **VisTex-OVLM** introduces a novel "visual textualization" process to enable image-prompted object detection in OVLMs without altering their pre-trained architecture or object-text alignment. The core idea is to transform visual information from support images into a format that can be directly concatenated with text prompts and fed into an unmodified OVLM.

The methodology consists of three main components:

1. **Image Prompt Engineering:**
 - To maximize the utility of few-shot annotations and isolate the target object from background noise, the authors employ an "image prompt engineering" technique.
 - Specifically, for a given one-shot support image with a target bounding box, regions *outside* this bounding box are subjected to background blur. This technique ensures that the visual exemplar primarily focuses on the object of interest, guiding the model more effectively.

2. **Visual Textualization with Multi-scale Textualizing Blocks (MSTB):**
 - This is the central mechanism for projecting visual information into the text feature space.
 - The frozen visual encoder of the pre-trained OVLM is used to extract multi-scale visual features ($R_i^S$) from the prompt-engineered support image. These multi-scale features capture objects of varying sizes and levels of detail.
 - **Multi-scale Textualizing Blocks (MSTBs)** are designed to process these features. An MSTB consists of:
 - A series of 3x3 convolutions with stride 2 for progressive downsampling, particularly for larger-scale features, to extract the most effective visual information.
 - An MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) that maps the processed visual features to the dimension of the OVLM's text feature space ($d_T$).
 - **Parameter-Sharing Strategy:** To enhance training efficiency on the base dataset and leverage shared knowledge across different feature scales, a single set of convolutions and an MLP are shared across all scales (except the smallest, which is directly fed). This strategy facilitates learning mapping functions that project diverse scale features into a unified text feature space. The output of this step for each stage `i` is a textualized visual feature $f_{P_i}^S$.

3. **Multi-Stage Fusion (MSF):**
 - The textualized visual features ($f_{P_i}^S$) generated by MSTBs from different stages of the visual encoder are then consolidated into a single textualized visual token ($f_P^S$).
 - The MSF strategy employs *non-parametric operations* (e.g., max pooling, as indicated in ablation studies) to fuse these features. This is crucial for preserving computational resources and, importantly, avoiding the introduction of new learnable parameters that could disrupt the OVLM's pre-trained alignment.
 - This synergistic combination of multi-scale textualizing and multi-stage fusion optimally leverages the OVLM's strong object-text alignment and object-level feature extraction capabilities to produce a semantically rich textualized visual token.

**Direct Support Image Prompting:**
- The final textualized visual token ($f_P^S$) for each support image is concatenated directly with the original text prompt tokens (processed by BERT).
- This combined textual-visual prompt is then fed into the *unmodified and frozen* pre-trained OVLM (e.g., GLIP or GroundingDINO). This direct embedding of visual semantics into the text prompt stream, without altering the OVLM's internal architecture or weights, is the core novelty that preserves the model's pre-trained object-text alignment and generalization.
- For K-shot tasks, textualized tokens from multiple support images are concatenated independently, ensuring that each shot's unique information is preserved.
- During training, only the MSTB is trainable, using the OVLM's pre-training loss functions on base categories. Once trained, MSTB can directly textualize novel class data without further fine-tuning, making the system highly adaptable and user-friendly for open-vocabulary detection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VisTex-OVLM demonstrates superior performance across a range of challenging benchmarks, validating its core hypothesis of enhancing OVLM capabilities without compromising pre-trained alignment.

#### 4.1. Performance in Open-Set Scenarios

- VisTex-OVLM significantly outperforms or matches other methods in open-set scenarios, including LVIS MiniVal and 16 unseen datasets (11 from ODinW35 and 5 medical datasets like MoNu, CCRCC, ConSep, LIDC, Deeplesion).
- Specifically, VisTex-DINO shows strong gains (e.g., LVIS MiniVal AP 42.8 vs. GroundingDINO-ZS 36.1, and substantial improvements across ODinW35 and medical datasets).
- VisTex-GLIP also achieves competitive results (e.g., LVIS MiniVal AP 50.7 vs. GLIP-ZS 37.3), often surpassing GLIP-FF (fully fine-tuned) which introduces new textual knowledge but potentially disrupts alignment.
- The strong performance on medical datasets, which represent significant domain shifts and rare categories, highlights the method's robustness and broad applicability, consistent with the interdisciplinary background of the authors.

#### 4.2. Comparison on Standard Few-Shot Object Detection (FSOD) Benchmarks

- **PASCAL VOC:** VisTex-GLIP achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on all three PASCAL VOC Novel Splits across various K-shot settings (1, 2, 3, 5, 10 shots). For example, on Split 1 (10-shot), VisTex-GLIP achieves 74.8 AP50, significantly outperforming previous SOTA VLM-based methods like MTL-FSOD (72.2) and MQ-Det (65.1), as well as non-VLM methods. VisTex-DINO also shows competitive improvements over its baselines.
- **MSCOCO:** VisTex-GLIP and VisTex-DINO achieve SOTA results on the MSCOCO FSOD benchmark, particularly for higher shot numbers. VisTex-GLIP reaches 53.6 mAP at 30 shots, surpassing MTL-FSOD (25.2) and MQ-Det (48.1). This demonstrates its effectiveness in integrating visual exemplars for enhanced detection performance.

#### 4.3. Generalized Few-Shot Object Detection (GFSOD) Performance

- VisTex-GLIP demonstrates strong performance in GFSOD settings on MSCOCO, maintaining robust detection for both base and novel categories. At 30 shots, it achieves 48.9 AP, 47.2 bAP (base AP), and 53.6 nAP (novel AP), showcasing its ability to avoid overfitting and knowledge forgetting on base classes while significantly boosting performance on novel ones. This highlights the success of preserving the OVLM's generalization capabilities.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Prompting Mode and Components (Tab. 5):**
 - Using visual support images alongside text prompts (VisTex-GLIP) significantly improves performance compared to text-only (GLIP-ZS) or methods that disrupt alignment (GLIP-FF, GLIP-MaPLe, MQ-Det).
 - Image prompts *alone* (without text prompts) show reduced performance, emphasizing the crucial role of text prompts in providing essential semantic guidance.
 - The combined use of MSTB sharing and multi-stage fusion (MSF) yields the best mAP, validating the synergistic effect of these components.
- **Multi-scale Textualization and MSTB Sharing (Tab. 11):** Using multi-scale features from the vision encoder consistently outperforms single-layer features. The parameter-sharing strategy in MSTB further improves textualization effectiveness and reduces parameters.
- **Multi-Stage Fusion (MSF) (Tab. 12, 14):** Fusing features from all 8 stages (1→8) of GLIP-L yields the best performance. Incorporating information from lower layers is crucial, as features from later, more abstract stages alone (e.g., stage 8) lead to significant performance drops. Max pooling is identified as the most effective non-parametric fusion method.
- **Shot Fusion Mode (Tab. 13):** Concatenating textualized tokens from different K-shot support images maintains individual shot information and leads to the best results, enabling the model to better grasp the support sample distribution.
- **Image Prompt Engineering (Tab. 15):** The "BG blur" technique for preprocessing support images consistently performs best, effectively highlighting the target object while retaining some background context, and avoiding information loss.

#### 4.5. Visualizations (Fig. 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8)

- Output visualizations (Fig. 3, 5, 6, 8) qualitatively demonstrate VisTex-GLIP's superior ability to detect novel objects with fewer false positives and false negatives compared to other methods, especially in diverse and challenging domains like medical images.
- Attention heatmaps (Fig. 4) provide a crucial insight: VisTex-GLIP exhibits stronger target focus and reduced background noise compared to methods like GLIP-FF, GLIP-MaPLe, and MQ-Det, directly supporting the claim of preserving object-text alignment and effective visual information integration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VisTex-OVLM presents a significant advancement in the field of open-vocabulary and few-shot object detection by addressing a fundamental challenge: how to effectively introduce new visual knowledge into powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models without compromising their carefully learned internal representations and generalization abilities.

The key significance and potential impact are:

- **Preservation of OVLM Integrity:** The most impactful contribution is the method's ability to enhance detection capabilities for novel categories while rigorously preserving the original architecture and object-text alignment of existing OVLMs. This is a paradigm shift from fine-tuning or architectural modification, which often leads to "catastrophic forgetting" or performance degradation on base classes. This ensures that the benefits of massive pre-training are retained, leading to more robust and generalized models.
- **Enhanced Few-Shot and Open-Set Detection:** By effectively "textualizing" visual exemplars, VisTex-OVLM significantly boosts performance on rare, novel, and even domain-shifted categories. This is crucial for real-world applications where datasets are often long-tailed, and new objects frequently appear. The SOTA results on PASCAL VOC, MSCOCO, LVIS, and especially challenging medical datasets, underscore its practical utility.
- **Broad Applicability and User-Friendliness:** VisTex-OVLM is shown to be compatible with common OVLMs like GLIP and GroundingDINO, indicating its generalizability across different foundation models. Furthermore, its design allows for the MSTB to be trained once on base categories, after which it can directly textualize novel class data for prompting without further fine-tuning on these novel classes. This "zero-shot training" for novel categories makes the method highly user-friendly and practical for real-time open-vocabulary detection scenarios (e.g., on mobile devices).
- **Unlocking New Prompting Paradigms:** The concept of "visual textualization" opens up new avenues for prompt engineering in VLMs. By demonstrating a robust way to integrate visual information directly into the language modality, it could inspire future research into more sophisticated multimodal prompting techniques that blend various types of prompts (text, image, audio, etc.) in a harmonious and architecture-preserving manner.
- **Applications in Specialized Domains:** The strong performance on medical datasets suggests a significant impact on areas like medical imaging analysis, where annotated data for specific conditions or rare diseases is scarce. Being able to quickly adapt a general-purpose object detector to specialized medical tasks with just a few examples, without extensive re-training or domain-specific fine-tuning, is invaluable.
- **Towards More General AI Systems:** By improving the ability of VLMs to detect objects in an open-ended manner, even those unseen during pre-training, VisTex-OVLM contributes to the development of more adaptable and general-purpose AI systems that can better perceive and understand the world around them, even in novel situations.

In summary, VisTex-OVLM offers an elegant and effective solution to a long-standing problem in VLM-based object detection, marking a significant step towards truly adaptable and generalized object recognition systems.
