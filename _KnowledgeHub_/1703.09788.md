# Towards Automatic Learning of Procedures from Web Instructional Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> ProcNets segment long, unconstrained instructional videos into category-independent procedure steps using only visual evidence. Frame features from ResNet are encoded by a bi-directional LSTM, an anchor-offset temporal convolution proposes segment candidates, and a segment-level LSTM with beam search picks the final sequence and its length. On the new 2000-video YouCook2 dataset it reaches 50.6 Jaccard and 37.0 mIoU, beating summarization and action-proposal baselines.

## Problem

- Procedure learning methods assume subtitles or language are concurrently available, yet machine-generated ASR text is inaccurate and absent in many real deployment scenarios.
- Prior procedure discovery fixes the number of steps per task, ignoring process variation where different instances of one recipe need different step counts.
- Event proposal and action labeling optimize recall over many overlapping proposals or frame-level transitions, capturing neither long-range segment dependencies nor background activity between steps.

## Method

- **YouCook2** dataset: 2000 untrimmed YouTube cooking videos, 89 recipes, 176 hours, each step given temporal boundaries plus an imperative English sentence, double-annotated for consensus.
- Procedure segment proposal module: 16 explicit anchors per frame through temporal convolution over bi-LSTM context-aware features, emitting a procedureness score plus center and length offsets regressed with smooth-l1.
- Sequential prediction module: an LSTM fed Proposal Vector, Location Embedding one-hot over candidates, and mean-pooled Segment Content, decoding segments by beam search until an end token. Together the modules form **ProcNets**.

## Results

- ProcNets-LSTM reaches **50.6** Jaccard and **37.0** mIoU on test, versus **45.2/32.2** for vsLSTM, **45.6/26.7** for SCNN-prop, and **40.1/35.1** for uniform segmentation.
- Ablations show Location Embedding matters most, removing it drops Jaccard **8.7%** and mIoU **5.1%** relatively; removing Proposal Vector costs **6%** Jaccard, segment visual features least.
- With 10 proposals per video at IoU 0.5, ProcNets-NMS attains **37.1** recall and **30.4** precision, over **9%** and **7%** above vsLSTM and SCNN-prop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling dependencies at segment level rather than frame level is more tractable for recurrent models and lets the network decide how many steps to emit.
> - A uniform-partition baseline is surprisingly strong for instructional video, since procedures span whole clips; report it or overstate gains.
> - Halving and swapping video order collapses performance, evidence the model genuinely encodes global temporal structure rather than local appearance cues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of action understanding in videos is an area of ongoing research, encompassing tasks such as action recognition, detection, and labeling. Prior work in these domains has largely concentrated on instantaneous or short-term actions within short or highly structured videos. Examples include recognizing specific actions (e.g., "walking") or detecting their occurrences.

However, a gap exists in the understanding of complex, multi-step tasks, which the authors define as "procedures." These procedures involve sequences of actions over longer durations, often found in unconstrained instructional videos (e.g., cooking tutorials, tire changing guides). Current methodologies for learning such procedures frequently rely on auxiliary information like action labels or video subtitles, even during evaluation. This dependency on non-visual cues limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where such information might be absent or inaccurate, such as with automatically generated subtitles.

This paper addresses this limitation by focusing on learning the structure of procedures solely from visual evidence in long, unconstrained videos. It introduces the problem of "procedure segmentation," which aims to identify and temporally localize semantically meaningful, category-independent segments that constitute a complete procedure. This distinguishes it from related problems:
- **Event Proposal**: While event proposals also localize category-independent temporal events, they prioritize recall of a large number of potential events, which can be loosely coupled and overlap. Procedure segmentation, in contrast, seeks to identify a sequence of necessary, closely-coupled, and largely non-overlapping segments that represent a coherent human-consensus procedure.
- **Existing Procedure Learning**: Previous efforts in procedure learning often assume the concurrent availability of linguistic input (e.g., subtitles) or a fixed, known number of steps for a given procedure. These assumptions are restrictive, as real-world scenarios may lack accurate text, and procedures of the same type can vary in their number of steps.

By developing a method that operates purely on visual information and collecting a dataset without reliance on such restrictive assumptions, this work aims to advance video understanding beyond atomic actions towards a more holistic comprehension of complex, real-world tasks. This research contributes to enabling artificial agents to learn skills and language through observation, which is a broader goal in fields like robotics and artificial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to ascertain whether the human-consensus structure of a procedure can be automatically learned from a large collection of long, unconstrained videos using only visual evidence. This overarching question motivates several specific goals:

1. **Introduce and Formalize Procedure Segmentation**: The paper aims to define and formally introduce the problem of "procedure segmentation." This involves segmenting an untrimmed video containing a complex task into a sequence of category-independent procedure segments, each characterized by its temporal start and end boundaries. Each segment is intended to be conceptually compact, representing a distinct step in the procedure as understood by human observers.

2. **Overcome Reliance on Non-Visual Cues**: A primary motivation is to address the limitations of existing methods that heavily depend on action labels, video subtitles, or pre-defined sequences of steps. The authors seek to develop a system that functions without these explicit textual or structural inputs, relying purely on visual information for procedure understanding. This is crucial for enabling learning in environments where such cues are unavailable or unreliable.

3. **Develop a Large-Scale, Appropriately Annotated Dataset**: The researchers identified a critical lack of suitable large-scale datasets for the procedure segmentation problem. Existing instructional video datasets either have limited video counts, lack diversity in content, or do not provide specific annotations for procedure segment boundaries or descriptions without relying on concurrent language. Therefore, a key objective was to collect and distribute a new dataset that provides temporal boundary annotations for procedure segments and imperative English sentence descriptions, reflecting human consensus on procedure structure. This dataset would then facilitate the development and evaluation of purely vision-based procedure segmentation models.

4. **Propose an Automatic Procedure Segmentation Method**: Given the limitations of existing approaches and the introduction of the new problem, the paper aims to propose an end-to-end model capable of automatically segmenting videos into procedure segments without prior knowledge of the procedure type or the number of steps involved. This model should be robust to the unconstrained nature of web videos, handling challenges such as camera motion, zooms, and scene changes. The model is designed to learn segment-level dependencies rather than just frame-level or pre-defined transitions, reflecting the sequential nature of procedures.

5. **Evaluate and Demonstrate Effectiveness**: A final objective is to rigorously evaluate the proposed model against competitive baselines using standard metrics. The evaluation aims to demonstrate the effectiveness of the model in accurately localizing procedure segments and inferring the underlying temporal structure of procedures, thereby contributing to the broader goal of agent learning from observational data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves the creation of a new dataset and the development of a novel deep learning architecture, ProcNets, for procedure segmentation.

### 4. YouCook2 Dataset Collection and Annotation

Recognizing the absence of a suitable large-scale dataset, the authors collected "YouCook2," a novel cooking video dataset specifically designed for procedure segmentation.
- **Scale and Diversity**: YouCook2 comprises 2000 untrimmed videos, totaling 176 hours in length, distributed across 89 distinct recipes. These recipes cover four major cuisine locales (Africa, Americas, Asia, Europe), ensuring a variety of cooking styles, methods, ingredients, and kitchen environments. Videos were sourced from YouTube, inherently presenting challenges like fast camera motion, zooms, and defocusing.
- **Annotation Process**: Each video was annotated with temporal boundaries (start and end timestamps) for each procedure segment and described with imperative English sentences (e.g., "Grill the tomatoes in a pan"). Annotators had access to audio and subtitles but were instructed to create their own descriptions and temporal segmentations to capture human consensus on how a procedure is structured. To ensure this consensus, two annotators were used for each video: one for the primary annotation effort and another for verification. The paper states that only temporal boundary annotations are used in the current work, while recipe descriptions are made available for future research.
- **Dataset Statistics**: Videos contain an average of 7.7 segments, with durations ranging from 3 to 16 segments. The average video length is 5.27 minutes, and the average segment duration is 19.6 seconds. The dataset vocabulary for descriptions consists of approximately 2600 words.
- **Data Splits**: The dataset was randomly divided into training (67%), validation (23%), and testing (10%) sets, with the splits maintained across recipes to ensure consistent representation. The test set also includes unseen recipes from other datasets to assess generalization.

### 5. Procedure Segmentation Networks (ProcNets)

ProcNets is an end-to-end model designed to segment untrimmed videos into a sequence of procedure segments without relying on recipe categories or segment descriptions during training. It consists of three primary modules:

- **Context-Aware Video Encoding**:
 - **Input**: Frame-wise CNN features extracted using ResNet-34, pretrained on ImageNet and MSCOCO Caption. Each video is represented as a sequence of L sampled frames, each with a 512-dimensional feature vector.
 - **Processing**: These ResNet features are fed into a bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) network. The Bi-LSTM processes the sequence of features in both forward and backward directions, capturing temporal context.
 - **Output**: The outputs from the forward and backward LSTMs are concatenated with the original ResNet feature for each frame, and the dimension is reduced to match the ResNet feature size. These are termed "context-aware features."

- **Procedure Segment Proposal**:
 - **Concept**: Inspired by spatial object detection methods like Faster R-CNN, this module generates candidate procedure segments.
 - **Anchors**: A set of K explicit anchors is defined, each with a specific length (l_k, ranging from 3 to 123 frames) and centers that cover all frames of the video.
 - **Prediction**: A temporal convolution operation is applied to the context-aware features. For each anchor at each frame location, the convolution outputs three values: a proposal score (indicating the likelihood of being a procedure segment), and two offsets (center and length offsets) to refine the anchor's boundaries. A sigmoid function is applied to the score, and Tanh functions to the offsets.
 - **Loss Function (Proposal)**: This module is trained with two components:
 1. **Classification Loss**: Binary cross-entropy loss classifies proposals as either a procedure segment or not. Positive samples are proposals with an Intersection over Union (IoU) of at least 0.8 with any ground-truth segment, while negative samples have IoU less than 0.2.
 2. **Regression Loss**: Smooth L1-loss is applied to positive samples to regress the predicted center and length offsets towards the ground-truth offsets, expressed relative to the anchor's properties.

- **Sequential Prediction**:
 - **Purpose**: This module learns segment-level temporal dependencies among the proposed candidates and dynamically determines the final sequence and number of procedure segments.
 - **Model**: A Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network is employed for sequence modeling.
 - **LSTM Input**: At each time step `t`, the LSTM receives a concatenation of three types of information:
 1. **Proposal Vector (S)**: A fixed vector representing max-pooled proposal scores from the proposal module. This encodes the confidence and coarse locations of all likely segment candidates.
 2. **Location Embedding (B_t)**: A vector representing the location of the previous (or current for `t=0`) segment. During training, this is a trainable embedding of a one-hot vector corresponding to the ground-truth segment's nearest proposal candidate. During inference, it's derived from the previously generated segment.
 3. **Segment Content (C_t)**: The visual features of the previous (or current for `t=0`) segment. This is obtained by mean-pooling the video's ResNet features within the segment's temporal boundaries, followed by a fully-connected layer to reduce dimensionality.
 - **Output**: The LSTM's softmax output provides the likelihood of each proposal being the *next* segment in the sequence.
 - **Loss Function (Sequential Prediction)**: Cross-entropy loss is applied to maximize the likelihood of the ground-truth segment sequence.
 - **Inference**: A beam search algorithm (or greedy selection) is used to sample a sequence of segment indices. The process terminates when a special `<end>` token is predicted.

### 6. Overall Loss Function

The total loss for ProcNets is a weighted sum of the three loss components:
L = L_classification + α_regression * L_regression + α_sequential * L_sequential.
Empirically, all discount factors (α) were set to 1.

### 7. Data Preprocessing and Training Details

Videos are uniformly down-sampled to 500 frames. To augment data, each video is sampled 10 times with temporal shifts. ResNet-34 features pretrained on ImageNet and MSCOCO Caption are used. The model uses Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 4 × 10^-5. CNN fine-tuning is disabled to expedite training.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The experiments benchmarked ProcNets against several baselines on the YouCook2 dataset, utilizing Jaccard and mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) metrics for procedure segmentation, as well as recall, precision, and F1 score for proposal localization accuracy.

### 9. Procedure Segmentation Performance

The proposed ProcNets-LSTM model demonstrated superior performance over all baselines.
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - On the test set, ProcNets-LSTM achieved a Jaccard score of 50.6% and an mIoU of 37.0%.
 - The next best performer was ProcNets-NMS (a variant replacing sequential prediction with Non-Maximal Suppression) with Jaccard 47.6% and mIoU 33.9%.
 - Other baselines like vsLSTM (Video Summarization LSTM) and SCNN-prop (Segment CNN for proposals) yielded Jaccard scores of 45.2% and 45.6% respectively, and mIoU scores of 32.2% and 26.7%. A Uniform baseline achieved Jaccard 40.1% and mIoU 35.1%.
- **Effectiveness of Segment-Level Modeling**: The results indicate that modeling segment-level temporal dependencies, as done by ProcNets-LSTM, is more effective for procedure segmentation than frame-level dependency modeling (vsLSTM) or mere segment proposal without sequential context (SCNN-prop, ProcNets-NMS).

### 10. Ablation Study on Sequential Prediction Module Input

An ablation study investigated the contribution of the three input components to the sequential prediction LSTM: Proposal Vector, Location Embedding, and Segment Content.
- **Impact of Proposal Vector**: Removing the Proposal Vector resulted in the largest performance drop (Jaccard dropped from 50.6% to 47.6%), indicating its significant role in informing the model about candidate segment confidence and location.
- **Impact of Location Embedding**: Omitting the Location Embedding led to a notable decrease (Jaccard dropped to 46.2%, mIoU to 35.1%), underscoring its importance for learning the temporal structure and relative positioning of segments within a procedure.
- **Impact of Segment Content**: Removing the Segment Content had the least impact on performance (Jaccard 49.0%, mIoU 36.4%), suggesting that while visual features are foundational, their direct input to the sequential prediction module is less critical than the structural (location) and confidence (proposal score) information for learning segment dependencies.

### 11. Proposal Localization Accuracy

For evaluating the quality of segment proposals, ProcNets-NMS was compared with baselines by fixing the number of proposed segments to 10 per video.
- ProcNets-NMS achieved a recall of 37.1%, precision of 30.4%, and an F1 score of 33.4% (at an IoU threshold of 0.5).
- These scores were higher than those of SCNN-prop (Recall 28.2%, Precision 23.2%, F1 25.4%) and vsLSTM (Recall 22.1%, Precision 24.1%, F1 23.0%), demonstrating the effectiveness of ProcNets' proposal mechanism in accurately localizing segments.

### 12. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples from the YouCook2 test set illustrated that ProcNets can accurately localize procedure segments and predict their appropriate lengths. The model also demonstrated an ability to adapt the number of proposed segments to individual videos, proposing fewer segments at the beginning and end where less cooking activity typically occurs. In some cases, the model identified semantically meaningful steps (e.g., cutting bread) that were not explicitly annotated in the ground truth, suggesting its capacity for rich understanding.

### 13. Analysis of Temporal Structure Learning

To assess the model's ability to learn temporal structure, a perturbation experiment was conducted. Videos were split into two halves (V_a and V_b) and then reordered as V_b V_a.
- **Performance Degradation**: Running the pre-trained ProcNets on these permuted videos resulted in a performance drop of over 50%.
- **Segment Prediction Behavior**: For 10%-20% of the permuted videos, segments were predicted only at the beginning of V_b.
- **Interpretation**: This significant degradation and altered prediction behavior suggest that ProcNets effectively learns and relies on the natural temporal dependencies within a procedure. When these dependencies are violated by permutation, the model's ability to segment correctly is severely hampered, indicating that it does not merely detect independent events but rather infers a coherent sequential structure.

### 14. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of computer vision and artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of video understanding and agent learning.

**Introduction of a Novel Problem:** The paper introduces and formally addresses the problem of "category-independent procedure segmentation" in unconstrained, untrimmed videos. This problem bridges the gap between low-level action recognition and high-level task comprehension, moving towards a more complete understanding of how complex human activities unfold. By defining procedure segments as semantically meaningful, conceptually compact steps reflecting human consensus, the work sets a new direction for structured video analysis.

**Creation of a Unique, Large-Scale Dataset:** The collection and distribution of YouCook2 is a substantial contribution. It is the first large-scale dataset for procedure segmentation that includes both temporal boundary annotations and imperative English sentence descriptions, without relying on concurrent language inputs. Its diversity in recipes, cooking styles, and unconstrained video characteristics makes it a robust benchmark for developing and evaluating models that learn from visual evidence alone. This dataset addresses a critical data scarcity, enabling future research in procedure learning, video summarization, and dense video captioning.

**Development of an Innovative Model (ProcNets):** The proposed ProcNets architecture, particularly its segment-level recurrent network, represents an advancement in video sequence modeling. Unlike previous methods that often assume fixed action sets, concurrent language, or rely on frame-level dependencies, ProcNets learns dynamic segment-level temporal dependencies. This allows the model to predict a variable number of procedure segments and infer the sequential structure directly from visual input, making it more adaptable to the variability of real-world procedures.

**Advancement in Automated Agent Learning:** The ability of ProcNets to automatically segment procedures based solely on visual evidence has direct implications for developing intelligent agents (embodied or software). Such agents could potentially learn complex manipulation skills or language by observing demonstrations, without explicit programming or textual instructions. This moves towards enabling robots to autonomously acquire knowledge from unstructured human demonstrations available on platforms like YouTube.

**Potential for Downstream Applications:** The output procedure segments generated by ProcNets can serve as a foundational preprocessing step for various other tasks, enhancing their capabilities:
- **Dense Video Captioning**: Providing well-defined temporal boundaries for procedure steps can significantly improve the quality and detail of generated video descriptions.
- **Event Parsing**: The identified procedure segments offer a structured representation of video content, facilitating more accurate and nuanced event parsing.
- **Weakly Supervised Segmentation**: The methodology could be extended to scenarios with weak audio/subtitle signals, using the learned visual structure to refine or generate more precise annotations.

In summary, this research not only introduces a novel and challenging problem but also provides a comprehensive solution through a dedicated dataset and a robust deep learning model. Its findings underscore the feasibility of visually-driven procedure understanding and lay groundwork for future advancements in intelligent systems capable of learning from observation.
