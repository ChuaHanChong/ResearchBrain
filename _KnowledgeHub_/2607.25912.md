# SAM3D-Guided Object-Centric Representation Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SAM3D-VLA enriches Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with object-centric 3D knowledge by distilling priors from a SAM3D teacher into RGB-based backbones during training. This approach yielded a 99.1% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and improved real-world manipulation success from 21.3% to 44.3% under occlusion, without requiring additional 3D inputs during robot inference.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on 2D visual observations, leading to insufficient fine-grained 3D understanding of objects.
- This limitation hampers VLA model performance in scenarios involving occlusion, significant object pose/scale variations, or precise spatial interaction.
- Current methods for 3D integration often require additional 3D sensors at inference time or modify the core VLA architecture, increasing deployment complexity.

## Method

- Introduces **SAM3D-VLA**, an ==object-centric 3D representation alignment framework== built upon a 	extunderscore 	extunderscore  VLA architecture.
- ==Distills object-level 3D priors== from a frozen SAM3D teacher model into the VLA's intermediate visual features via a ==masked, normalized MSE alignment loss== during training.
- Leverages ==open-vocabulary object detection and segmentation== to identify and mask task-relevant objects for 3D feature extraction.

## Results

- Attained a **99.1%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing all baseline VLA methods.
- Showcased a **71.6%** 5-step success rate on the CALVIN benchmark, demonstrating robust performance on long-horizon sequential manipulation.
- Increased real-world task success rates from **21.3%** to **44.3%** under challenging occlusion conditions, using a Piper-X robotic arm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric 3D knowledge can be effectively distilled into 2D-based VLA models, enhancing their understanding of object shape, pose, and spatial layout.
> - This distillation can occur entirely during training, eliminating the need for additional 3D inputs or modules during the robot's inference time.
> - Providing subtask-aware 3D supervision allows VLA models to maintain focus on the correct target object across multi-stage, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant direction in general robot manipulation, aiming to enable robots to understand high-level language instructions and perform diverse tasks. These models commonly adapt pre-trained vision-language models to predict robot actions, primarily relying on 2D visual observations (RGB images). While effective in many scenarios, this reliance on 2D input often leads to a deficiency in fine-grained 3D understanding of target objects, especially under conditions such as occlusion, significant pose variations, scale changes, or when precise spatial interaction is required.

Previous research efforts to enhance VLA models with spatial or 3D information generally fall into several categories:
1. **Explicit 3D Observation Integration:** Some methods directly incorporate explicit 3D observations like depth maps, point clouds, or RGB-D inputs into the policy (e.g.,). While these approaches demonstrate the value of 3D structure, they often necessitate additional sensor inputs at inference time and may alter the original input-output interface of the VLA model. Furthermore, they frequently focus on global scene-level spatial representations rather than object-centric details.
2. **Implicit Spatial Information through Architectural Design:** Other works introduce spatial information via specific architectural designs, customized action representations, or representation-level spatial supervision (e.g., SpatialVLA, Spatial Forcing). These methods aim to imbue spatial awareness but might still involve modifications to the core VLA architecture or require 3D foundation models during inference.
3. **2D Visual Grounding:** Visual grounding techniques improve attention to target regions through cropped inputs, bounding box predictions, or gaze reconstruction (e.g.,). However, the supervision for these methods primarily remains within the 2D image space and typically does not explicitly provide information regarding the target object's 3D shape or spatial layout.

This paper distinguishes itself by addressing the limitation of 2D vision in VLA models by injecting *object-centric 3D knowledge* into the underlying visual representations. Critically, it achieves this **without requiring any additional 3D inputs or modules during the robot's inference time**. The work positions itself as a method that distills rich 3D priors from a specialized 3D teacher model (SAM3D) into the intermediate representations of an RGB-based VLA backbone during training. This approach aims to bridge the gap between the perceptual capabilities of 3D-aware models and the practical inference requirements of 2D-based VLA systems, offering a solution that enhances performance in complex manipulation tasks while maintaining a streamlined deployment pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that while Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models show potential for general robot manipulation, their widespread reliance on 2D RGB image backbones inherently limits their ability to robustly handle tasks requiring fine-grained 3D understanding. This limitation manifests particularly in scenarios characterized by:
- **Occlusion:** When parts of the target object are hidden from view.
- **Pose Variation:** When objects are encountered in diverse orientations.
- **Scale Changes:** When objects appear at different sizes in the visual field.
- **Precise Spatial Interaction:** Tasks demanding accurate placement, insertion, or contact, where the exact 3D geometry and spatial relationship of objects are critical.

Existing VLA models, learning primarily from action prediction on 2D visual data, often fail to internalize essential 3D properties such as an object's shape, precise pose, scale, and detailed spatial layout. This results in reduced performance and robustness in complex manipulation environments.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To enhance RGB-based VLA policies with object-centric 3D knowledge:** The primary goal is to imbue VLA models with a more comprehensive understanding of target objects' 3D characteristics, moving beyond simple 2D feature extraction. This implies enabling the policy to implicitly "know" about the shape, surface structure, and spatial arrangement of relevant objects.
2. **To preserve the original RGB-language-to-action inference pipeline:** A crucial design constraint is that any enhancement in 3D understanding should not introduce additional complexity or sensor requirements during deployment. This means the improved policy must operate solely on standard RGB images and language instructions at test time, without necessitating depth maps, point clouds, object masks, or specialized 3D modules. This objective is driven by practical considerations for real-world robotic deployment, where additional sensors or complex pipelines can increase computational cost, latency, and system fragility.
3. **To improve performance in challenging manipulation scenarios:** Specifically, the research aims to demonstrate consistent performance improvements in tasks involving long-horizon planning, generalization across different object configurations, and robustness in the presence of disturbances like occlusion or clutter. The ability to focus on and understand the 3D properties of relevant objects across sequential subtasks is a particular focus for long-horizon tasks.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to develop VLA models that are both perceptually more capable in 3D and practically deployable in real-world robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, SAM3D-VLA, introduces an object-centric 3D representation alignment framework designed to distill 3D priors into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models during training while maintaining a conventional 2D RGB and language-based inference pipeline. The core of the methodology is built upon the $\pi^0$ VLA architecture.

#### 3.1. Vision-Language-Action Model Foundation

SAM3D-VLA utilizes the $\pi^0$ model as its base VLA architecture. $\pi^0$ integrates a pre-trained vision-language backbone with a continuous action expert. The backbone consists of a SigLIP vision encoder and a Gemma language model. The model processes multi-view RGB images, a language instruction, and robot proprioceptive state ($o_t$) to predict a sequence of continuous actions ($A_t$) using a conditional flow matching objective ($L_{action}$). The VLM backbone generates hidden representations ($H_t$) from various Transformer layers, which then condition the action expert.

#### 3.2. SAM3D-guided Object-centric Feature Alignment

The primary mechanism for injecting 3D knowledge is a representation alignment process during training:
- **Frozen SAM3D Teacher:** SAM3D is employed as a fixed (frozen) 3D teacher model. SAM3D is designed to extract dense 3D features from segmented objects within single images.
- **Object Localization and Segmentation:** During training, task-relevant objects are automatically identified for each training frame and camera view. This involves:
 - **Subtask Decomposition:** For long-horizon tasks, high-level instructions are first decomposed into sequential subtasks (e.g., using a large language model like GPT-4o).
 - **Object Grounding:** Open-vocabulary object detection models such as Grounding DINO and YOLO are used to localize the target object specified by the current subtask.
 - **Mask Generation:** The Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM2) generates a precise binary mask for the localized target object.
- **3D Feature Extraction:** The original RGB image and its corresponding object mask are fed into the frozen SAM3D teacher. SAM3D extracts dense object-centric 3D features ($T_t$), which encapsulate object-level geometric priors (e.g., shape, surface structure, spatial layout). For multi-view observations, each view is processed independently, and its features are later aggregated.
- **Feature Alignment Process:**
 - **Spatial Resampling:** The raw SAM3D teacher features ($T_t$) have a different token resolution than the student $\pi^0$ visual tokens. $T_t$ is reshaped into a 2D feature grid ($T_{2D,t}$), and then bilinearly interpolated to match the spatial resolution ($H_S \times W_S$) of the student's visual token grid. This produces spatially aligned teacher tokens ($\bar{T}_t$).
 - **Student Feature Projection:** Intermediate visual features ($S_t$) are extracted from a selected Transformer layer of the $\pi^0$ VLM backbone. Since the dimensionality of $S_t$ typically differs from $D_T$ (SAM3D feature dimension), a lightweight projection layer ($P_\phi$) maps $S_t$ into the SAM3D feature space, resulting in projected student features ($\hat{T}_t$).
 - **Masked Normalized MSE Alignment Loss:** An alignment loss ($L_{align}$) is computed between the projected student features ($\hat{T}_t$) and the resampled SAM3D teacher features ($\bar{T}_t$). To ensure the supervision focuses on relevant areas, a token-level mask ($m_t$) is derived from the object mask and applied, restricting the loss computation to target-object tokens. Both $\hat{T}_t$ and $\bar{T}_t$ are $\ell_2$-normalized before computing the Mean Squared Error (MSE), promoting directional feature alignment and robustness to scale discrepancies.
 - The total training objective becomes a weighted sum: $L = L_{action} + \alpha L_{align}$, where $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter controlling the strength of 3D supervision.
- **Inference Pipeline:** Crucially, during inference, all components related to the 3D teacher (object detection, segmentation, SAM3D, and the alignment loss) are entirely removed. The trained VLA policy functions solely on RGB images and language instructions, preserving the original $\pi^0$ inference paradigm.

#### 3.3. Frozen-representation Probing Adapter

To quantitatively assess whether the VLA model effectively encodes 3D information, a probing experiment is conducted. After training the VLA policy, its entire backbone is frozen. A lightweight two-layer MLP probe ($P_\omega$) is then trained on top of the same intermediate visual features ($S_t$) used for alignment. This probe attempts to predict the spatially resampled SAM3D target features ($\bar{T}_t$). A lower probing loss (Equation 5) indicates that the learned VLA features are more predictive of the SAM3D priors, confirming successful distillation of 3D object-centric information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SAM3D-VLA was evaluated through extensive experiments across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments on LIBERO

The method was tested on the LIBERO benchmark, which includes LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Long, designed to assess generalization across various task components.
- **Overall Performance:** SAM3D-VLA achieved an average success rate of 99.1%, outperforming all other state-of-the-art methods, including strong 2D VLA baselines like $\pi^0$ (94.2%), UniVLA (95.2%), and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), as well as explicit and implicit 3D VLA methods (Table 1).
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** A notable improvement was observed on LIBERO-Long tasks, where SAM3D-VLA achieved 98.4% success. These tasks involve sequential subtasks where the relevant target object may change. The subtask-aware processing, which provides stage-specific SAM3D supervision, is attributed to this enhancement, enabling the policy to focus on the appropriate object at each stage.

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiments on CALVIN

The CALVIN benchmark involves long-horizon sequential manipulation tasks across different environments.
- **Long-Horizon Consistency:** SAM3D-VLA demonstrated the highest performance with an average completed length of 4.11 and a 5-step success rate of 71.6% (Table 2). This outcome surpasses ReconVLA (3.95 average length), UniVLA (3.80 average length), and other baselines. The consistent improvement across increasing task lengths indicates that the SAM3D-guided object-centric alignment helps the policy better encode 3D priors for sequential manipulation, leading to more robust long-horizon task completion.

#### 4.3. Real-world Experiments

The method was further validated on a Piper-X robotic arm platform, equipped with wrist and head cameras. Experiments were conducted on three manipulation scenarios: cooking, flower arrangement, and block stacking. These tasks cover a range of manipulation primitives including object selection, grasping, insertion, and placement.
- **Performance under Varied Conditions:** SAM3D-VLA improved the average success rate from 50.2% to 65.2% under standard (ST) conditions. Critically, under more challenging occlusion (OC) settings, which involved changed object positions, distractor objects, and partial occlusions, the average success rate increased from 21.3% to 44.3% (Table 3).
- **Long-Horizon Real-world Tasks:** Significant gains were observed in real-world long-horizon tasks such as cooking, flower arrangement, and stacking. These results reinforce the finding that subtask-aware SAM3D supervision assists the policy in maintaining focus on the correct target object throughout multi-stage tasks and effectively utilizing object-level shape and layout priors, thereby enhancing real-world robustness.

#### 4.4. Frozen-representation Probing

The probing experiments, mentioned as a verification mechanism, confirmed that the SAM3D object-centric 3D priors are more accurately recoverable from the learned VLA features after training with SAM3D-VLA. This result provides empirical evidence that the alignment process successfully distills 3D object knowledge into the VLA's internal representations.

In summary, the main findings consistently demonstrate that SAM3D-VLA achieves superior performance compared to existing VLA models across various simulation and real-world benchmarks, particularly in tasks requiring precise object manipulation and long-horizon planning. These improvements are attributed to the effective, training-time-only distillation of object-centric 3D knowledge into the VLA policy's intermediate representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SAM3D-VLA, an object-centric 3D representation alignment framework that addresses a notable limitation in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the lack of fine-grained 3D understanding from predominantly 2D visual inputs. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced VLA Robustness and Performance:** The consistent improvements observed across LIBERO, CALVIN, and real-world Piper-X tasks, particularly in long-horizon and object-centric generalization scenarios, indicate that injecting object-centric 3D priors significantly enhances the manipulation capabilities of VLA models. This increased robustness is crucial for deploying robotic systems in diverse and unstructured environments.

2. **Practical Deployment Advantage:** A key innovation is the distillation of 3D knowledge *only during training*, allowing the VLA policy to maintain its original RGB-language-to-action inference pipeline. This means no additional depth sensors, point clouds, explicit masks, or dedicated 3D modules are required at test time. This design choice removes practical barriers related to computational overhead, sensor integration, and real-time processing, making the enhanced VLA models more amenable to real-world industrial and service robotics applications.

3. **Improved Long-Horizon Task Execution:** The subtask-aware object-centric supervision effectively guides the policy to focus on changing target objects across sequential manipulation stages. This leads to stronger long-horizon consistency and success rates, which is a critical capability for complex multi-step robotic tasks often found in manufacturing, logistics, and household automation.

4. **Foundation for Future VLA Research:** This work establishes a precedent for implicitly integrating rich perceptual information from advanced 3D models into VLA architectures without altering their deployment interface. It suggests a promising direction for future research in several areas:
 - **Advanced 3D Teachers:** Exploring multi-view or temporal 3D teachers could address limitations of single-image SAM3D under severe occlusion or for transparent objects.
 - **Generalizability Across VLA Backbones:** The alignment framework, although demonstrated with $\pi^0$, is conceptually generalizable and could be applied to other mainstream VLA architectures.
 - **Refined Object-Task Association:** Future work could focus on improving the automatic generation and association of subtask annotations and object masks to minimize noise in the supervisory signal.

5. **Addressing Key Bottlenecks in Robot Learning:** By explicitly identifying and addressing "Object-Centric 3D understanding" as a bottleneck, this research provides a targeted solution that complements broader efforts in global spatial awareness or 2D visual grounding. This contributes to a more holistic understanding of the perceptual requirements for generalist robot policies.

Despite acknowledged limitations, such as reliance on automated data annotation and evaluation on a specific robot platform, the SAM3D-VLA framework represents a significant step towards developing more capable, robust, and deployable VLA models for complex robotic manipulation.
