# Modality-Composable Diffusion Policy via Inference-Time Distribution-level Composition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) developed Modality-Composable Diffusion Policy (MCDP), a method for integrating multiple single-modality Diffusion Policies at inference time without additional training. This approach achieved up to 86% success rate in robot manipulation tasks by combining RGB- and point cloud-based policies, outperforming individual unimodal policies.

## Problem

- Existing Diffusion Policy (DP) frameworks primarily rely on a single visual modality, limiting their perception and understanding of complex, multimodal real-world scenes.
- Training generalized DPs on heterogeneous multimodal data incurs substantial data collection and computational costs due to the exponentially larger data requirements for joint distributions.
- Leveraging large vision-language models for multimodal understanding also demands considerable computational resources for training and fine-tuning.

## Method

- The **Modality-Composable** ==Diffusion Policy (MCDP)== leverages compositional principles from Energy-based Models and Compositional Diffusion Models to combine pre-trained, unimodal DPs at inference time.
- It sums the noise estimates from individual modality-specific DPs, each conditioned on its unique visual input (e.g., RGB images or point clouds), using a weighted approach.
- The method employs a Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)-free composition, enabling flexibility with existing independently trained DPs and ensuring sampling efficiency for real-time robot control.

## Results

- MCDP achieved a success rate of **0.86** in the "Empty Cup Place" task with a weight of **0.4** for the RGB policy, surpassing individual RGB (**0.42**) and point cloud (**0.62**) policies.
- Performance improvements were maximized when the better-performing unimodal DP was assigned a larger weight; for example, in "Dual Bottles Pick (Easy)", an RGB policy with **0.77** SR contributed to an MCDP SR of **0.85** when weighted at **0.8**.
- Visual analysis demonstrated MCDP's ability to mitigate errors, correcting directional deviation and excessive force in grasping tasks by integrating corrective information from a complementary modality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Composing diffusion scores from multiple unimodal policies at inference can capture a more generalized action distribution, reducing reliance on the limitations of any single modality.
> - The composed distribution adaptively shapes itself based on assigned weights, dynamically integrating essential features from each modality to enhance task execution.
> - Even with moderate individual accuracies, combining unimodal DPs can lead to superior overall performance, while low-accuracy constituent policies can negatively impact the joint distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has seen significant advancements in policy representation, particularly with the emergence of Diffusion Policy (DP). DP models leverage denoising diffusion probabilistic models to represent complex, multi-modal action distributions, offering advantages in training stability and flexibility for tasks such as manipulation and navigation. Existing DP frameworks, however, typically operate on a single visual modality, such as RGB images or point clouds. This unimodal reliance limits their ability to fully capture the nuances of real-world environments, potentially leading to suboptimal performance and reduced generalization capabilities.

Addressing the limitations of unimodal DPs often involves training generalized policies on heterogeneous multimodal data. However, this approach incurs substantial data collection and computational costs. Learning joint distributions over multiple variables requires exponentially more samples than learning single-variable distributions. Similarly, leveraging large vision-language models for multimodal understanding, while powerful, also demands considerable computational resources for training.

This research positions itself within the context of compositional generative models, which offer a paradigm for combining "expertized" models at inference time to construct a more robust generative system. This concept has been explored in various domains, including visual generation, language generation, and trajectory composition, notably with Energy-based Models (EBMs) and more recently with compositional diffusion models. By drawing inspiration from these compositional principles, this work proposes a method to enhance Diffusion Policies for robotics without the need for additional, expensive multimodal training. It aims to bridge the gap between the efficacy of single-modality DPs and the demand for more generalized, multimodal robotic capabilities by providing a cost-effective composition strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy, adaptability, and generalization potential of Diffusion Policies (DPs) in robot learning by effectively integrating information from multiple visual modalities. This is to be achieved without incurring the substantial computational and data acquisition costs associated with training new, complex multimodal DP models from scratch.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current robot learning paradigms utilizing Diffusion Policies:

- **Unimodal Limitation:** Existing Diffusion Policy frameworks predominantly rely on a single visual input modality, such as RGB images or point clouds. This narrow scope prevents these policies from fully perceiving and understanding the intricate complexities of real-world scenes, which are inherently multimodal. Consequently, this limitation can lead to reduced accuracy and suboptimal decision-making in diverse and dynamic environments.
- **High Costs of Multimodal Training:** While training a single, generalized DP capable of processing heterogeneous multimodal data would conceptually improve performance, the practical implementation is cost-prohibitive. Collecting extensive multimodal datasets for robust training is resource-intensive. Furthermore, learning joint distributions across multiple variables necessitates an exponentially larger number of data samples compared to learning distributions on single variables, exacerbating data-related expenses.
- **Computational Burden of Large Models:** Alternative approaches, such as adapting vision-language models to create vision-language-action models for multimodal understanding, also face the challenge of substantial computational resources required for their training and fine-tuning. This restricts their accessibility and scalability for many robotic applications.
- **Leveraging Existing Assets:** The existence of pre-trained, single-modality DPs represents a valuable but underutilized asset. There is a motivation to develop a method that can efficiently combine the individual strengths of these expertized models, thereby maximizing their utility without re-training or significant modification.
- **Inspiration from Compositional Models:** The success of compositional diffusion models and Energy-Based Models (EBMs) in other generative tasks suggests that combining diffusion scores or energy functions from different expert models at inference time can yield a more powerful and generalized system. This provides a clear motivational path for applying similar compositional principles to Diffusion Policies in a modality-specific context.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to provide a practical and efficient solution for developing more capable robotic policies that can leverage diverse sensory information without demanding a complete overhaul of existing training paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the **Modality-Composable Diffusion Policy (MCDP)**, which leverages compositional principles to combine the distributional scores of multiple pre-trained Diffusion Policies (DPs), each specialized for a single visual modality, during the inference phase. This approach aims to create a more expressive and capable policy without requiring additional training.

**Theoretical Foundations:**

1. **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM):** The work is grounded in DDPMs, which are generative models that iteratively denoise a random noise distribution to produce samples. The process is governed by a Langevin dynamics-based update rule (Eq. 1), where a neural network $\epsilon_{\theta}$ estimates the noise to be removed from a noisy trajectory $\tau_t$ at timestep $t$.

2. **Energy-based Models (EBMs) with Composition:** EBMs define a probability distribution via an unnormalized energy function, $p_{\theta}(\tau) \propto e^{-E_{\theta}(\tau)}$. Their sampling update rule is functionally similar to DDPMs, relying on the gradient of the energy function (Eq. 2). Crucially, compositional EBMs combine multiple independent energy functions by multiplying their respective probability distributions, which is equivalent to summing their energy functions: $p_{product}(\tau) \propto e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{n} E_{i,\theta}(\tau)}$ (Eq. 3). This leads to a combined Langevin dynamics update rule where the gradients of individual energy functions are summed (Eq. 4).

3. **Compositional Diffusion Models (CDM):** Inspired by compositional EBMs, CDMs extend this principle to model trajectory distributions conditioned on multiple concepts. The joint probability distribution of a trajectory $\tau$ conditioned on a set of concepts $\{c_1, \dots, c_n\}$ can be expressed as a product of terms involving the conditional probabilities $p(c_i|\tau)$ (Eq. 5, 6). The score function of this composed distribution is then derived as a sum of individual score functions (Eq. 7). This leads to a generalized noise estimation formula for CDMs (Eq. 8), which can be seen as an extension of classifier-free guidance (CFG).

**Modality-Composable Diffusion Policy (MCDP):**

Building upon the CDM framework, MCDP specifically applies these compositional principles to combine DPs conditioned on different *single visual modalities* (e.g., RGB images, point clouds).

- **Joint Probability Distribution:** The joint probability distribution for the action trajectory conditioned on multiple modalities is formulated as a product of individual modality-conditioned probability distributions (Eq. 9).
- **Inference-Time Composition Strategy:** Unlike standard CFG which often involves training with unconditional and conditional components, MCDP proposes a simplified, CFG-free composition at inference time. The estimated noise for the combined policy, $\hat{\epsilon}_{M^*}$, is computed as a weighted sum of the noise estimates from individual pre-trained DPs, each conditioned on its specific modality $c_i$ (i.e., $M_i$):
 $\hat{\epsilon}_{M^*}(\tau_t, t, c) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i (\epsilon_{\theta}(\tau_t, t, c_i))$, where $\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i = 1$ (Eq. 10).
- **Reasons for CFG-Free Composition:**
 - **Policy Flexibility:** This approach allows for the composition of policies trained independently or derived from existing open-source models (e.g., DP3, Octo), most of which do not incorporate CFG training. This enhances the applicability of MCDP.
 - **Sampling Efficiency:** CFG-based sampling typically requires double computation (for both unconditional and conditional scores), which can significantly slow down the inference process. For real-time robot control, this efficiency loss is critical, making the CFG-free approach more practical.
- **Sampling Process (Algorithm 1):** The algorithm outlines the iterative denoising process. It starts with a noise trajectory, and at each denoising step, it:
 1. Obtains noise estimates ($\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \dots$) from each pre-trained policy ($\pi_1, \pi_2, \dots$) using its respective input modality ($M_1, M_2, \dots$).
 2. Composes these noise estimates using the assigned weights ($w_1, w_2, \dots$) to get a combined noise estimate ($\epsilon_{M^*}$).
 3. Applies the denoising update rule using the composed noise estimate to refine the trajectory.
 This process is repeated for a specified number of denoising steps until a final action trajectory is generated.

**Experimental Setup:**

- **Dataset:** Experiments were conducted using the RoboTwin dataset, an early version of a dual-arm robot benchmark.
- **Component Policies:** The unimodal DPs used for composition were:
 - `DP_img`: A Diffusion Policy based on RGB images (corresponding to the original DP by Chi et al., 2023).
 - `DP_pcd`: A Diffusion Policy based on point cloud data (corresponding to DP3 by Ze et al., 2024, without point cloud color).
- **Scheduler:** The default DDIM scheduler for `DP_pcd` with 10 inference steps was replaced with a DDPM scheduler with 100 inference steps to align with the `DP_img` scheduler.
- **Evaluation:** The performance of MCDP was evaluated across various robotic manipulation tasks, analyzing the influence of different weight configurations ($w_1$ for `DP_img` and $1-w_1$ for `DP_pcd`) on the success rate. Visualizations were also used to analyze sample distributions and execution advantages.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the RoboTwin dataset aimed to understand how different weight configurations impact MCDP performance and to explain the advantages of the composed policy.

**Influence of Weight Configurations on MCDP Performance (Table 1):**

The evaluation of MCDP across multiple tasks revealed several findings regarding the interaction of unimodal policy performance and weight assignment:

1. **When Both Unimodal DPs Demonstrate Moderate Accuracy:** In scenarios where both `DP_img` and `DP_pcd` exhibit moderate, non-negligible success rates (above 30%), MCDP often achieves higher accuracy than either unimodal DP individually, provided appropriate weight configurations are selected. For example, in the "Empty Cup Place" task, `DP_img` achieved a success rate (SR) of 0.42, and `DP_pcd` achieved 0.62. MCDP, however, peaked at an SR of 0.86 with `w_1` (weight for `DP_img`) = 0.4. This outcome suggests that the composition of diffusion scores can capture a more generalized distribution, reducing the policy's reliance on the limitations of a single modality.

2. **When One Unimodal DP Exhibits Significantly Lower Accuracy:** If one of the constituent DPs has a substantially lower accuracy, MCDP generally struggles to surpass the performance of the better-performing unimodal DP. For instance, in the "Pick Apple Messy" task, `DP_pcd` achieved an SR of 0.26, while `DP_img` achieved 0.05. MCDP's peak performance was 0.25 (with `w_1` = 0.1), which fell short of `DP_pcd`'s individual performance. This indicates that low-accuracy scores from a weaker modality can negatively impact the joint distribution, diminishing the overall effectiveness of the composed policy.

3. **Maximizing Improvement with Stronger Unimodal DP:** Building on the observation of performance gains, the experiments suggest that the improvement from MCDP is maximized when the better-performing unimodal DP is assigned a larger weight in the composition. An example is the "Dual Bottles Pick (Easy)" task, where `DP_img` achieved an SR of 0.77, while `DP_pcd` achieved 0.36. MCDP reached its highest SR of 0.85 when `w_1` was 0.8, effectively leveraging the stronger `DP_img`. This provides a practical guideline: allocating higher weights to policies that individually perform better can optimize the composed policy's effectiveness.

**Analysis of MCDP’s Advantage Based on Visualization (Figure 4):**

Visual analysis of MCDP provided insights into how the composition mechanism contributes to improved performance:

1. **Sample Distribution Adaptation (Figure 4a):** Visualizations of generated sample distributions under different weight configurations demonstrated that the joint distribution dynamically adapts. As the weight `w_1` for `DP_img` increases, the composed distribution exhibits characteristics leaning towards those of `DP_img`. Conversely, larger `w_2` for `DP_pcd` shifts the distribution towards `DP_pcd`. This adaptive shaping of the composed distribution by weight tuning enables it to integrate and highlight essential features from each modality, which contributes to more successful policy execution.

2. **Error Mitigation in Grasping (Figure 4b):** A specific example from a grasping task illustrated MCDP's ability to mitigate errors. A unimodal `DP_img` policy exhibited directional deviation and excessive force during execution, leading to task failure. MCDP, by integrating the corrective trajectory information from `DP_pcd`, was able to adjust both the direction and the applied force. This combined action resolved the deviation and resulted in successful grasping, demonstrating that MCDP can effectively leverage the strengths of one modality to compensate for the weaknesses of another.

These findings collectively highlight MCDP's capability to improve performance and adaptability in robotic tasks by strategically combining unimodal policies, while also offering empirical guidance for weight configuration based on the individual performance characteristics of the constituent DPs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Modality-Composable Diffusion Policy (MCDP) framework presents several significant contributions to the field of robot learning, with notable potential impacts:

**Significance:**

- **Cost-Effective Multimodal Integration:** The primary significance of MCDP lies in its ability to combine different visual modalities for robot control *at inference time* without requiring additional training. This directly addresses the substantial computational and data acquisition costs typically associated with training complex, generalized multimodal Diffusion Policies from scratch. By reusing pre-trained, unimodal DPs, the framework offers an efficient pathway to multimodal capabilities.
- **Enhanced Adaptability and Performance:** The empirical results demonstrate that MCDP can achieve superior performance and adaptability compared to individual unimodal DPs, especially when the constituent policies possess moderate to high individual accuracy. This improvement stems from the capacity of the composed distribution to capture a more generalized and robust representation of desired actions.
- **Empirical Guidance for Policy Composition:** The extensive evaluation of weight configurations across diverse scenarios provides valuable empirical insights and practical guidelines for selecting optimal weighting strategies. This is crucial for practitioners deploying compositional policies, as it informs how to effectively balance the influence of different modalities based on their individual performance characteristics.
- **Leveraging Existing Models:** MCDP offers a method to derive greater utility from existing single-modality Diffusion Policies. This capability could prolong the lifespan and broaden the applicability of previously developed or open-sourced DP models, fostering a more modular and reusable approach to robot learning research.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Development of Generalizable Policies:** The compositional approach of MCDP holds the potential to facilitate the development of more generalizable robot learning policies. By dynamically combining modalities, robots could become more robust to partial sensor failures or varying environmental conditions, leading to policies that are effective across different tasks, environments, and even robot embodiments.
- **Improved Robustness and Reliability:** The ability to mitigate errors from one modality by leveraging the strengths of another, as demonstrated in the grasping visualization, can lead to more robust and reliable robotic systems. This is particularly critical in safety-sensitive applications where consistent performance under diverse conditions is paramount.
- **Reduced Barriers to Entry for Multimodal Robotics:** By offering an efficient way to integrate multiple sensor streams, MCDP could lower the technical and resource barriers for researchers and developers to explore multimodal robot control. Instead of needing massive datasets and computational power for every new multimodal policy, existing single-modality models can be combined and tuned.
- **Foundation for Future Compositional Research:** This work provides a promising direction for future research into compositional robot learning. The insights gained from modality-level composition could be extended to composition across different skills, tasks, or even different robot platforms, fostering a paradigm of modular and adaptive robot intelligence.
