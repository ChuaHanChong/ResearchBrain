# Modality-Composable Diffusion Policy via Inference-Time Distribution-level Composition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) developed Modality-Composable Diffusion Policy (MCDP), a method for integrating multiple single-modality Diffusion Policies at inference time without additional training. This approach achieved up to 86% success rate in robot manipulation tasks by combining RGB- and point cloud-based policies, outperforming individual unimodal policies.

## Problem

- Existing Diffusion Policy (DP) frameworks primarily rely on a single visual modality, limiting their perception and understanding of complex, multimodal real-world scenes.
- Training generalized DPs on heterogeneous multimodal data incurs substantial data collection and computational costs due to the exponentially larger data requirements for joint distributions.
- Leveraging large vision-language models for multimodal understanding also demands considerable computational resources for training and fine-tuning.

## Method

- The **Modality-Composable** ==Diffusion Policy (MCDP)== leverages compositional principles from Energy-based Models and Compositional Diffusion Models to combine pre-trained, unimodal DPs at inference time.
- It sums the noise estimates from individual modality-specific DPs, each conditioned on its unique visual input (e.g., RGB images or point clouds), using a weighted approach.
- The method employs a Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)-free composition, enabling flexibility with existing independently trained DPs and ensuring sampling efficiency for real-time robot control.

## Results

- MCDP achieved a success rate of **0.86** in the "Empty Cup Place" task with a weight of **0.4** for the RGB policy, surpassing individual RGB (**0.42**) and point cloud (**0.62**) policies.
- Performance improvements were maximized when the better-performing unimodal DP was assigned a larger weight; for example, in "Dual Bottles Pick (Easy)", an RGB policy with **0.77** SR contributed to an MCDP SR of **0.85** when weighted at **0.8**.
- Visual analysis demonstrated MCDP's ability to mitigate errors, correcting directional deviation and excessive force in grasping tasks by integrating corrective information from a complementary modality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Composing diffusion scores from multiple unimodal policies at inference can capture a more generalized action distribution, reducing reliance on the limitations of any single modality.
> - The composed distribution adaptively shapes itself based on assigned weights, dynamically integrating essential features from each modality to enhance task execution.
> - Even with moderate individual accuracies, combining unimodal DPs can lead to superior overall performance, while low-accuracy constituent policies can negatively impact the joint distribution.
