# SpatialScore: Towards Comprehensive Evaluation for Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI Laboratory introduce SpatialScore, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) spatial intelligence, and propose SpatialCorpus for supervised fine-tuning and SpatialAgent for a training-free, tool-augmented enhancement. The work reveals significant performance gaps in MLLMs' 3D understanding, demonstrating that these methods can improve capabilities across various spatial reasoning tasks, with SpatialAgent achieving up to +8.33% accuracy on the benchmark.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit fragmented and limited capabilities in spatial intelligence, struggling with nuanced understanding of physical space.
- Existing MLLM benchmarks for spatial reasoning are over-simplistic, focusing on superficial queries rather than rigorous visual geometry perception (e.g., camera pose, metric measurements).
- Current evaluations have a narrow scope, relying on single-modality inputs or isolated skills, failing to provide a holistic assessment of spatial intelligence for real-world applications like embodied AI.

## Method

- Introduced ==SpatialScore==, a comprehensive benchmark with **5,025** manually verified samples, covering **30** diverse spatial reasoning tasks across various data types, modalities, and question formats.
- Developed ==SpatialCorpus==, a large-scale training resource with **331K** multimodal QA pairs for supervised fine-tuning of MLLMs, curated using automated 3D data repurposing and synthetic data generation.
- Proposed ==SpatialAgent==, a training-free, multi-agent framework that leverages an off-the-shelf MLLM core augmented with **12** specialized spatial perception tools, operating under Plan-Execute and ReAct paradigms.

## Results

- State-of-the-art proprietary MLLMs like Gemini-3-Pro achieved **60.12%** on SpatialScore, while leading open-source models scored **56.63%**, both substantially below human performance (**86.60%**).
- Supervised fine-tuning of Qwen3-VL models on SpatialCorpus resulted in notable overall accuracy improvements, with the 4B model gaining **+10.47%** and the 8B model **+9.23%**.
- SpatialAgent provided significant training-free enhancements, with the ReAct paradigm boosting Qwen3-VL 4B/8B by **+7.78%** and **+8.33%** respectively, showing consistent gains across most tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial gap persists between state-of-the-art MLLMs and human-level spatial intelligence, particularly in complex visual geometry and real-world 3D perception tasks.
> - Larger MLLMs generally exhibit stronger spatial reasoning, but existing spatial-specific fine-tuning datasets show limited efficacy, underscoring the need for more comprehensive evaluation and training data.
> - Both data-driven fine-tuning (SpatialCorpus) and training-free, tool-augmented agent-based approaches (SpatialAgent) are effective strategies for enhancing MLLMs' spatial intelligence, each with distinct advantages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across various domains, including general semantic question answering and mathematical reasoning. However, the development of MLLMs' spatial intelligence, which involves both spatial perception and understanding, remains an area with fragmented progress. This gap is notable given the importance of human-like spatial reasoning for real-world applications such as embodied AI and autonomous navigation.

Traditional computer vision has developed robust tools and mathematical foundations for spatial perception, often leveraging geometric optimization and feed-forward neural networks. While these advances have revitalized vision-only paradigms, they frequently lack tight language integration and unified evaluation protocols. Recent studies have initiated explorations into integrating semantic understanding with spatial perception within MLLMs. However, existing benchmarks in this nascent field typically suffer from limitations, including a focus on over-simplistic tasks (e.g., object presence or coarse position relations), neglecting rigorous visual geometry perception (e.g., camera pose, dynamics), and a narrow evaluation scope. Prior assessments are often fragmented, relying on superficial queries (e.g., Yes/No judgments), single-modality inputs (e.g., static images), or isolated skills (e.g., size estimation). Consequently, they have not provided a holistic measurement of spatial intelligence.

This work addresses these limitations by aiming to systematically investigate the extent to which existing MLLMs possess comprehensive spatial intelligence. It seeks to provide a thorough evaluation framework and complementary solutions to advance MLLMs towards more sophisticated spatial understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a holistic assessment of the spatial understanding capabilities of modern MLLMs and to propose data-driven and agent-based solutions to enhance these capabilities. The motivation stems from the observed fragmentation and limited scope of existing evaluations for spatial intelligence in MLLMs, despite the critical role of such abilities in real-world applications.

The paper outlines four specific contributions that serve as its key objectives:

1. **Introduce SpatialScore:** The researchers aimed to develop a comprehensive and diverse benchmark for multimodal spatial intelligence. This benchmark was designed to cover multiple visual data types (real-world, simulation, AI-generated content), various input modalities (images and videos), and diverse question-answering formats (judgment, multi-choice, and open-ended QA). The goal was to include approximately 5,000 manually verified samples spanning 30 distinct spatial reasoning tasks, thereby creating a more rigorous and complete evaluation tool than previously available.
2. **Extensively Evaluate MLLMs:** A core objective was to utilize the newly developed SpatialScore benchmark to conduct a wide-ranging evaluation of 49 representative MLLMs. This evaluation aimed to identify persistent challenges in spatial intelligence and to quantify the performance gap between current models and human-level spatial reasoning.
3. **Construct SpatialCorpus:** To address the observed performance gaps and advance model capabilities through a data-driven approach, the researchers aimed to build a large-scale training resource. This resource, named SpatialCorpus, was intended to contain 331,000 multimodal question-answering samples, designed to support supervised fine-tuning on spatial reasoning tasks and improve the performance of existing models.
4. **Develop SpatialAgent:** Complementing the data-driven strategy, another objective was to develop a training-free paradigm for enhancing spatial reasoning. This involved creating SpatialAgent, a multi-agent system equipped with 12 specialized spatial perception tools. SpatialAgent was designed to support both Plan-Execute and ReAct reasoning frameworks, enabling substantial gains in spatial reasoning without requiring additional model training.

Through these contributions, the work aims to establish a solid foundation for guiding the development of MLLMs towards human-level spatial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves a multi-pronged approach encompassing the development of a new benchmark (SpatialScore), a large-scale training dataset (SpatialCorpus), and an agentic framework (SpatialAgent) for enhancing MLLMs' spatial intelligence.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as a question-answering paradigm. Given a textual question *q* and a visual input *v* (image, multi-frame sequence, or video), an MLLM Φ produces a free-text response *r*. For enhancement, two pathways are explored: (i) supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on SpatialCorpus, resulting in an updated model ˆΦ; and (ii) an agentic framework, SpatialAgent (*A*), which augments a pretrained MLLM (Φ) at inference time by coordinating external spatial tools *T* = {*t*₁..., *t*ₙ}.

#### 3.2. SpatialScore Benchmark Construction

SpatialScore is designed as a comprehensive and diverse benchmark. Its construction involved two main stages:

- **3D Data Repurposing:** To address the scarcity of QA data on 3D visual geometry perception, a scalable pipeline was developed. It leverages precise 3D annotations (e.g., depth, 3D bounding boxes) from multiple 3D detection and reconstruction datasets, including ScanNet++, Omni3D, WildRGB-D, PointOdyssey, and CA-1M. From 500 sampled scenes, open-ended QA pairs were generated by combining predefined question templates with LLM-based rewriting (DeepSeek-v3) to enrich linguistic variety. A subset of these was converted into judgment (Yes/No) and multi-choice formats. Distractors for multi-choice questions were generated using three strategies: random sampling of same-category annotations, small perturbations of ground-truth values, and LLM-synthesized plausible distractors. This process yielded 2.3K high-quality QA samples.
- **Data Integration & Curation:** SpatialScore integrated spatially relevant evaluation samples from 23 existing datasets, covering cognitive psychology, 2D/3D spatial relations, distance reasoning, and general QA benchmarks. Combining these with the repurposed 3D data resulted in 63,857 candidate samples. A strong LLM (GPT-OSS-120B) filtered out questions answerable without visual information, reducing the pool to 40,238. Finally, five volunteers manually verified 8,793 valid samples based on criteria of correctness, relevance to spatial reasoning, uniqueness, and category balance. The final SpatialScore benchmark comprises 5,025 manually verified, approximately balanced samples, spanning 30 distinct tasks grouped into 10 categories (e.g., mental animation, depth estimation, camera pose & motion, object localization). It covers real-world, simulated, and AI-generated content, with single-image, multi-frame sequence, and video inputs, and judgment, multi-choice, and open-ended QA formats.

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-tuning with SpatialCorpus

As a data-driven approach, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) aims to enhance MLLMs' spatial understanding by training on domain-specific data.

- **SpatialCorpus Construction:** An automated pipeline was developed to create SpatialCorpus. It repurposed 3D annotations from the training sets of ScanNet++, Omni3D, WildRGB-D, and PointOdyssey (excluding CA-1M). Rule-based templates were used for question diversity to manage costs. To bolster mental animation, synthetic data from simulators (spatial maps, 2D/3D rotation sequences) were incorporated. SpatialCorpus contains approximately 331K multimodal QA pairs across 16 tasks in 7 categories.
- **Fine-tuning Process:** The Qwen3-VL series of models were fine-tuned on SpatialCorpus using standard cross-entropy loss. The visual encoder was kept fixed, while the MLP projecting visual features and the LLM parameters were jointly optimized.

#### 3.4. SpatialAgent Multi-Agent System

SpatialAgent offers a training-free alternative by augmenting off-the-shelf MLLMs with a multi-agent system coordinating external spatial tools. It operates under two reasoning paradigms:

- **Plan-Execute (PE) Paradigm:** This paradigm involves three components: a planner (Φ_plan), an executor (Φ_exec), and a summarizer (Φ_sum). The planner generates a structured, multi-step plan for tool invocation. The executor sequentially executes this plan, obtaining tool outputs. The summarizer then processes these outputs and the original inputs to produce the final response. This approach is structured and efficient but may lack flexibility in complex scenarios.
- **ReAct Paradigm:** This paradigm utilizes an observer (Φ_obs), an executor (Φ_exec), and a summarizer (Φ_sum), alongside a memory module. The observer iteratively generates actions based on the current context and interaction history. The executor performs the action, and the results are added to memory. This interleaved reasoning-and-action cycle continues until a "Terminate" action is triggered, after which the summarizer synthesizes all accumulated evidence for the final response. ReAct offers greater flexibility through dynamic planning but is less efficient due to its iterative nature.
- **Toolbox:** SpatialAgent integrates 12 specialized, open-source spatial perception tools, categorized as:
 - **General Perception:** Tools for object counting (Rex-Omni), localization (Rex-Omni), segmentation (SAM2), and 3D object detection (DetAny3D).
 - **Motion & Transformation:** Tools for dense optical flow estimation (RAFT), camera extrinsics (VGGT), and feature matching/homography estimation (SIFT).
 - **Pose & Geometry:** Tools for camera intrinsics/extrinsics (VGGT), metric depth estimation (Depth-Anything-V2), 3D object orientation (OrientAnything), and 3D scene reconstruction/distance measurement (MapAnything).
 - **Auxiliary Tools:** General-purpose utilities like "Terminate" for concluding reasoning and "SelfThink" for enhancing MLLM core reasoning via prompt engineering.

All components within both paradigms are guided by meticulously designed prompts to ensure proper instruction following and tool orchestration. Exception handling mechanisms are in place to address malformed outputs or infinite loops, with a fallback to direct MLLM reasoning if agent failures persist.

#### 3.5. Experimental Settings

Evaluation metrics include accuracy for judgment/multi-choice questions and Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) for numerical open-ended questions. Scoring for complex open-ended QAs combines parsing functions and LLM-based scoring. Experiments are conducted on Nvidia A100 GPUs with fixed random seeds and temperature to ensure reproducibility. Specific sampling strategies are applied for video inputs across different models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations of 49 MLLMs on the SpatialScore benchmark, alongside quantitative and qualitative analyses of the proposed data-driven and agent-based solutions.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Representative MLLMs on SpatialScore

A comprehensive evaluation of 49 MLLMs on SpatialScore revealed several key observations:
- **Overall Performance:** Gemini-3-Pro achieved the highest overall score at 60.12%, while Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B led among open-source models with 56.63%. Despite these performances, a substantial gap of 26.48 percentage points persists between state-of-the-art models and human-level spatial understanding (86.60%).
- **Model Scale vs. Performance:** Generally, larger models within families (e.g., InternVL and Qwen-VL series) exhibited stronger performance, suggesting a correlation between intrinsic reasoning ability and spatial intelligence.
- **Limitations of Existing Fine-tuning:** Most models specifically fine-tuned for spatial understanding (e.g., SpaceThinker, SpatialReasoner) showed only marginal gains, and some even underperformed their base models. This indicates that current fine-tuning strategies and datasets for spatial understanding are often insufficient for broad generalization across the diverse and challenging tasks in SpatialScore, with VST being a notable exception demonstrating significant improvements.
- **Limitations of Current Models:** While models achieved near-human performance on certain tasks like mental animation and object localization, they struggled markedly with view reasoning, camera pose, motion analysis, and realistic 3D perception. This discrepancy highlights a pronounced deficiency in handling complex 3D visual geometry.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results of Proposed Approaches

The study investigated the impact of SpatialCorpus-based supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and SpatialAgent-based reasoning on the Qwen3-VL series models.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning with SpatialCorpus:** Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-4B and Qwen3-VL-8B on SpatialCorpus resulted in notable overall accuracy improvements of +10.47 and +9.23 percentage points, respectively. These gains were primarily concentrated on tasks that were already well-represented or optimized in the training data, such as mental animation and camera analysis. However, tasks with limited data scalability (e.g., view reasoning) sometimes suffered from catastrophic forgetting, suggesting that purely data-driven approaches may have inherent limitations in achieving holistic improvements across all types of spatial reasoning.
- **SpatialAgent Performance:** SpatialAgent, operating in a training-free manner, also delivered improvements. Using the Plan-Execute paradigm, it achieved gains of +6.41 (Qwen3-VL-4B) and +7.27 (Qwen3-VL-8B) in overall accuracy. The ReAct paradigm further improved performance, yielding gains of +7.78 (Qwen3-VL-4B) and +8.33 (Qwen3-VL-8B). SpatialAgent consistently enhanced performance across a broader range of tasks, demonstrating its robustness and practicality without incurring additional training costs.
- **Subset Analysis:** On the SpatialScore-Repurpose subset (newly constructed 3D annotation data), SFT with SpatialCorpus showed substantial improvements (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B from 54.53% to 76.29%), indicating strong alignment with the training data distribution. SpatialAgent also showed considerable gains on this subset (Qwen3-VL-8B from 54.53% to 67.51%) and provided consistent improvements on the SpatialScore-OpenSource subset (data from existing benchmarks), demonstrating its generalizability across diverse data sources.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative analyses illustrated SpatialAgent's structured and interpretable reasoning process. The agent effectively decomposes complex tasks and dynamically invokes appropriate tools, leading to more accurate solutions than direct MLLM responses. While SpatialAgent generally performed well, occasional failures were observed due to suboptimal tool execution or misinterpretation of intermediate results, highlighting areas for future refinement in MLLM capabilities and toolbox design. The ReAct paradigm exhibited a significantly lower failure rate (0.02%) compared to Plan-Execute (2.25%-8.24%), attributed to its iterative reasoning and error-correction capabilities.

#### 4.4. Computational Efficiency

While SpatialAgent achieved performance gains, it introduced increased latency. For Qwen3-VL-8B, direct inference averaged 0.9 seconds per sample, whereas Plan-Execute required approximately 5.4 seconds, and ReAct averaged 9.3 seconds, depending on input length, interaction turns, and tool invocations. The tools within SpatialAgent's toolbox are lightweight and can be offloaded to the CPU when idle, allowing the entire reasoning process to execute on a single Nvidia A100 GPU.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides several contributions to the field of multimodal large language models and spatial intelligence.

Firstly, the introduction of **SpatialScore** establishes a comprehensive and diverse benchmark for evaluating spatial understanding. By integrating newly repurposed 3D data with meticulously curated samples from 23 existing datasets, and covering a wide array of visual data types, input modalities, and question-answering formats across 30 distinct tasks, SpatialScore addresses the limitations of fragmented and narrow previous evaluations. This benchmark serves as a rigorous testbed, offering a standardized platform for assessing and comparing the spatial reasoning capabilities of current and future MLLMs. Its public release is expected to foster systematic progress in the field.

Secondly, the extensive evaluation of 49 representative MLLMs on SpatialScore quantifies the current state of the art and clearly **reveals persistent challenges and a substantial gap between modern models and human-level spatial intelligence**. The identified deficiencies, particularly in complex visual geometry perception, camera pose and motion analysis, and real-world 3D understanding, provide clear directions for future research and development in MLLM architectures and training methodologies. The observation that existing spatial-specific fine-tuning often yields only marginal gains further underscores the difficulty and complexity of true spatial intelligence.

Thirdly, the development of **SpatialCorpus** offers a large-scale, high-quality training resource designed for supervised fine-tuning. With 331,000 multimodal QA samples, it provides a scalable data-driven pathway to enhance MLLMs' spatial reasoning abilities. The demonstrated improvements in models fine-tuned on SpatialCorpus validate its utility as a foundational dataset for targeted capability development, particularly in areas where data-driven optimization is effective.

Finally, the proposal of **SpatialAgent**, a novel multi-agent framework, introduces a training-free paradigm for improving spatial intelligence. By orchestrating 12 specialized, open-source spatial perception tools under both Plan-Execute and ReAct reasoning paradigms, SpatialAgent enables pretrained MLLMs to perform complex spatial reasoning tasks more effectively. Its ability to achieve notable performance gains without additional model training offers a practical and robust solution that preserves general capabilities while enhancing specialized skills. This agentic approach highlights the potential of combining MLLMs with external tools for interpretable and adaptable reasoning, suggesting a promising direction for developing more reliable and intelligent systems for real-world scenarios.

Collectively, these contributions are expected to serve as a foundation for advancing MLLMs towards more robust and human-like spatial intelligence, which is critical for the development of applications in areas such as embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous navigation. The public release of all data, code, and models further facilitates open research and collaboration within the scientific community.
