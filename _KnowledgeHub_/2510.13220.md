# EvoTest: Evolutionary Test-Time Learning for Self-Improving Agentic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, EvoTest, enables AI agents to continuously learn and improve during deployment by holistically evolving their entire configuration, including policy prompts, structured memory, hyperparameters, and tool-use routines. Evaluated on the newly introduced Jericho Test-Time Learning (J-TTL) benchmark, EvoTest consistently surpassed both gradient-free and gradient-based adaptation methods in complex interactive environments.

## Problem

- Current AI agents often operate with fixed policies, limiting their ability to adapt and learn complex skills on-the-fly in novel, dynamic environments.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks specifically designed to measure and drive progress in rapid, in-session agent improvement (test-time learning).
- Existing adaptation methods struggle with characteristics of complex tasks, such as sparse rewards, long-range planning, data scarcity, or are limited to single-channel modifications.

## Method

- Introduces the Jericho ==Test-Time Learning== (J-TTL) benchmark, an evaluation setup where agents repeatedly play the same complex, text-based adventure game to demonstrate progressive performance improvement within a single session.
- Proposes **EvoTest**, an evolutionary test-time learning framework with a two-agent design (Actor and Evolver) that operates on a fixed, non-trainable backbone LLM.
- EvoTest performs 'whole-system evolution' by iteratively generating and selecting revised agent configurations, encompassing changes to policy prompts, structured memory, hyperparameters, and tool-use routines, using narrative feedback from full episode transcripts.

## Results

- EvoTest achieved the highest average AUC scores (**0.47**/**0.50**) across all six games on the J-TTL benchmark, substantially outperforming gradient-free baselines like EvoPrompt (**0.34**/**0.36**) and Reflexion (**0.32**/**0.34**).
- EvoTest demonstrated significantly higher average AUC (**0.47**/**0.50**) compared to the gradient-based online reinforcement learning baseline, GRPO (**0.30**), highlighting its data efficiency in test-time learning scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of prompt evolution and UCB selection for performance and stability, with EvoTest operating efficiently (20-30 seconds per update) compared to costly RL fine-tuning (5-10 minutes per pass on 4 H100 GPUs).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time learning is demonstrably effective for improving agent performance on complex tasks, with learning-based methods consistently outperforming static agents.
> - Holistic, whole-system evolution across multiple agent components (prompt, memory, tools, hyperparameters) effectively addresses complex performance bottlenecks that single-channel adaptation methods cannot resolve.
> - Leveraging rich, narrative episode transcripts for semantic credit assignment offers a more data-efficient and robust learning mechanism than relying on sparse scalar rewards for gradient-based methods, particularly in sparse-reward environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a recognized limitation in current AI agents: their capacity for "on-the-fly" learning and self-improvement in novel, dynamic environments. Existing AI agents often operate with fixed policies, resembling systems that can execute predefined instructions but struggle to reform their internal processes based on new experiences. This characteristic restricts their reliability and practical utility in diverse, real-world scenarios.

The field has explored various approaches to enhance agent adaptability. One prominent direction involves **reflection-based methods**, exemplified by Reflexion, where agents verbally analyze past failures and append these insights to their prompts for subsequent attempts. Another area focuses on **advanced memory systems**, such as MemGPT and MemoryBank, which equip agents with structured mechanisms to manage and recall information over extended interactions. Additionally, **automated prompt optimization methods** (e.g., APE, OPRO, TextGrad, Promptbreeder, EvoPrompt) aim to iteratively refine the textual instructions guiding agent behavior through generative or evolutionary algorithms. Other research explores **uncertainty-aware planning** (e.g., Uncertainty of Thoughts) or **weight-update methods** like online fine-tuning and reinforcement learning (RL) to enable policy adaptation.

Despite these advancements, a gap exists in standardized testbeds specifically designed to measure and drive progress in rapid, in-session agent improvement. Current evaluation setups often concentrate on single-episode performance or generalization across different tasks, rather than an agent's ability to learn and improve within repeated attempts at the same complex task.

This work positions itself within this landscape by:
1. **Introducing a novel benchmark** (J-TTL) specifically tailored for test-time learning, addressing the identified gap in evaluation frameworks.
2. **Proposing a new evolutionary framework** (EvoTest) that extends beyond single-channel adaptation (like prompt-only evolution or memory-only updates) by enabling whole-system evolution. This aims to holistically optimize multiple aspects of an agent's configuration, including its policy prompt, structured memory, hyperparameters, and tool-use routines, in a gradient-free manner. The work seeks to provide a more data-efficient and practical alternative to gradient-based online learning methods, particularly in scenarios characterized by data scarcity and sparse rewards.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper identifies a fundamental limitation of current AI agents: their inability to learn complex skills during deployment (at test time). This limitation leads to agents exhibiting fixed policies that struggle to adapt to novel situations, a behavior characterized by the authors as "clever but clueless." This challenge significantly restricts their practical application in dynamic settings where continuous learning from experience is crucial.

The research is motivated by two primary objectives:

1. **Systematically Measure Test-Time Learning Capacity:**
 - **Motivation:** Progress in developing self-improving AI agents has been hindered by a lack of standardized evaluation frameworks specifically designed to measure an agent's ability for rapid, in-session improvement. Existing benchmarks often focus on single-episode performance or generalization, not iterative learning on a persistent task.
 - **Objective:** To introduce the **Jericho Test-Time Learning (J-TTL) benchmark**. This benchmark is designed as an evaluation setup where an agent repeatedly plays the same complex, text-based adventure game for several consecutive episodes. The core task is for the agent to demonstrate progressive performance improvement from one episode to the next, using only the experience gathered within that single session. The benchmark aims to provide a clear, quantitative measure of an agent's on-the-fly learning capabilities.

2. **Develop an Effective Framework for Rapid, Holistic Test-Time Adaptation:**
 - **Motivation:** Analysis on the J-TTL benchmark reveals that existing adaptation methods, including reflection-based approaches, memory systems, and gradient-based online fine-tuning or reinforcement learning (RL), exhibit significant limitations. These methods struggle with characteristics of complex text-adventure games such as complex puzzles, long-range planning, sparse rewards (where many critical actions yield no points), and irreversible consequences. Online fine-tuning and RL are particularly noted as being too slow and data-inefficient for the rapid learning demands of the test-time setting, often failing to learn effectively from sparse or noisy signals. Single-channel adaptation methods are also unable to resolve complex performance bottlenecks that stem from interactions between different agent components.
 - **Objective:** To propose **EvoTest**, an evolutionary test-time learning framework. EvoTest is designed to improve an agent's performance without relying on fine-tuning or gradient-based updates. Instead, it aims to evolve the entire agentic system after each episode. This involves generating a revised configuration that can encompass changes to the agent's guiding prompt, memory, hyperparameters, and tool-use routines. The motivation is to achieve a more comprehensive and data-efficient form of learning by leveraging rich, narrative feedback from full episode transcripts rather than solely relying on sparse scalar rewards.

In summary, the paper aims to establish a benchmark for a critical but underserved area of AI agent research and to provide a novel, practical, and effective algorithmic solution to address the challenges revealed by this benchmark.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Jericho Test-Time Learning (J-TTL) benchmark and proposes the EvoTest framework to address its challenges.

#### 3.1. The Jericho Test-Time Learning (J-TTL) Benchmark

The J-TTL benchmark is designed to measure an agent's capacity for on-the-fly learning through repeated attempts at the same task.

- **Environment:** The benchmark utilizes Interactive Fiction (IF) games from the Jericho suite, which are text-based simulation environments. These games are characterized by complex puzzles, long-range planning requirements, sparse rewards, and irreversible consequences.
- **Interaction Protocol:** In each step, an agent receives a textual observation (state), issues a natural-language command (action), and obtains a numerical score change (reward).
- **Episodic Structure:** An agent plays the same game for `K` consecutive episodes. After each episode, the environment is reset to its identical initial state, ensuring that any performance improvement is solely attributable to the agent's internal learning process. Each episode is capped by a step limit (`T`).
- **Goals:**
 - **Episodic Goal:** Maximize the final score within a single playthrough.
 - **Learning Goal:** Progressively increase the final score from one episode to the next, using only the experience gathered within that session.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Learning Curve:** A plot of the final return `R(e)` against the episode index `e` to visualize learning progress.
 - **Area Under the Curve (AUC):** A normalized quantitative metric (`P(K, e=1) R(e) / (K * Rmax)`) measuring the total achieved score against the maximum possible score over the session, providing a value between 0 and 1.

#### 3.2. The EvoTest Framework

EvoTest is an evolutionary test-time learning framework designed for rapid, holistic adaptation without fine-tuning model weights. It operates on a fixed, non-trainable backbone Large Language Model (LLM).

- **Architecture:** EvoTest decouples acting from adaptation using a two-agent design: an Actor Agent and an Evolver Agent.
 - **Actor Agent:** Plays a full episode using a given agent configuration. It interacts with the environment by querying the backbone LLM, conditioning its actions on the current observation and information retrieved from its memory as dictated by its tool-use routines.
 - **Evolver Agent:** After each episode, this agent analyzes the full trajectory transcript and the parent configuration. It then performs "whole-system evolution" by generating a set of new candidate "child" configurations for the next episode.

- **Agentic Configuration (χ):** The learnable components of an agent are instantiated as a holistic configuration `χ = (p, M, h, u)`, comprising:
 - **Policy Prompt (`p`):** A system prompt guiding the backbone LLM with high-level strategies, heuristics, and behavioral guardrails.
 - **Deployment-time Memory (`M`):** A structured, queryable database updated by the Evolver Agent. It stores prior experiences, categorized into:
 - **Success Memory:** Logs state-action pairs that led to score increases.
 - **Failure Memory:** Records patterns associated with stalls or negative outcomes (e.g., repetitive action loops).
 - **Hyperparameters (`h`):** Parameters controlling the LLM's inference and the agent's decision-making (e.g., temperature, exploration strength, stopping criteria).
 - **Tool-Use Routines (`u`):** Active components executed at each decision-making step:
 - **Memory Interaction Logic:** Rules for querying memory (`M`). If a match is found, the routine can inject proven actions into the LLM's prompt.
 - **State Abstraction Logic:** An evolvable Python function (`extract_state`) that processes verbose game history into a concise summary of milestones, included in the prompt for efficient situational awareness.

- **The Two-Agent Learning Loop (Act-Evolve Cycle):**
 1. **Acting Phase:** The Actor Agent, using its current configuration `χ(e)`, plays episode `e`, generating trajectory `τ(e)` and final return `R(e)`.
 2. **Evolution Phase:** The Evolver Agent receives `τ(e)` and `χ(e)`. It uses an LLM to analyze the transcript and propose improvements to the configuration. Evolutionary operators include:
 - **Prompt Mutation:** Rewriting `p(e)` to incorporate new strategies or prevent observed failures.
 - **Memory Update:** Programmatically parsing `τ(e)` to identify and log important events (score increases, non-productive loops) into `M(e+1)`.
 - **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Adjusting `h(e)` (e.g., increasing temperature after repetitive loops).
 - **Tool-Use Refinement:** Modifying `u(e)` (e.g., strengthening memory consultation rules or refining the `extract_state` function).
 This process generates a set of candidate child configurations `C(e+1)`.
 3. **Selection Phase:** The next configuration `χ(e+1)` is selected from a pool comprising the parent `χ(e)` and its new children `C(e+1)`. This selection is guided by the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) algorithm, balancing exploitation of well-performing configurations (`μ(˜χ)`) with exploration of less-tried ones (`β * sqrt(log N / (1 + n(˜χ)))`). UCB helps prevent the system from getting stuck on suboptimal evolutionary paths and maintains stable learning.

EvoTest's core mechanism is to transform episode narratives into multi-faceted improvements, enabling a deeper form of learning than methods limited to a single aspect of agent configuration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments address three research questions (RQ1-RQ3) on the J-TTL benchmark, comparing EvoTest against various baselines using different LLM backbones (google/gemini-2.5-flash, anthropic/claude-4-sonnet-20250522 for actor; openai/o3-2025-04-16 for evolver/optimizer).

- **RQ1: Test-Time Learning Effectiveness:**
 - Results indicate that test-time learning is effective. All learning-based methods achieved higher average AUC scores than the non-learning Static baseline across all games. Learning curves for these methods consistently showed upward trends, in contrast to the flat performance of the Static agent. This suggests that iterative learning from experience, even over a few attempts, can improve agent performance on complex tasks.

- **RQ2: EvoTest vs. Existing Adaptation Methods (Gradient-Free):**
 - EvoTest consistently achieved the highest AUC scores across all six games for both backbone LLMs (average AUC of 0.47/0.50), demonstrating substantial improvement over other gradient-free baselines. For example, it outperformed EvoPrompt (0.34/0.36) and Reflexion (0.32/0.34).
 - The paper attributes EvoTest's superior performance to its holistic, whole-system evolution. Unlike methods that only modify prompts, memories, or single hyperparameters, EvoTest concurrently optimizes the prompt, memory-access routines, and decision hyperparameters. This multi-faceted adaptation allows it to address complex performance bottlenecks that single-channel approaches cannot resolve. Learning curves also indicated a steeper rate of improvement, suggesting more efficient learning.

- **RQ3: Evolutionary Adaptation vs. RL (Gradient-Based):**
 - EvoTest demonstrated a significantly higher average AUC (0.47/0.50) compared to the gradient-based RL baseline, GRPO (online) (0.30). This indicates EvoTest's effectiveness in test-time learning, particularly in addressing data scarcity.
 - The paper explains this by highlighting EvoTest's ability to leverage the entire episode transcript as a rich, narrative feedback signal for semantic credit assignment, rather than relying on sparse scalar rewards for gradient updates. This narrative analysis allows the Evolver Agent to identify causal chains of failures and successes and apply explicit, targeted, and structural edits to the agent's configuration, which is presented as a more data-efficient mechanism for learning from single experiences in sparse-reward environments.

**Model Analysis:**

- **Ablation on Key Components:**
 - Removing any component from EvoTest resulted in performance degradation. The largest drop in AUC occurred when prompt evolution was removed, indicating its primary role in strategic adaptation.
 - Removing UCB selection led to significant performance instability and catastrophic drops, demonstrating UCB's role in preventing the agent from over-committing to high-risk mutations and ensuring a more stable learning trajectory.
- **Efficiency Analysis:**
 - EvoTest and other gradient-free methods conducted an update step in approximately 20-30 seconds (1 LLM API call), which is practical for test-time learning.
 - In contrast, weight-update methods like online RL required 5-10 minutes per fine-tuning pass on 4 H100 GPUs, indicating high computational cost and hardware demands that are less suitable for rapid test-time adaptation.
- **Impact of Evolver Agent's LLM:**
 - The performance of EvoTest correlated positively with the quality of the LLM used for the Evolver Agent, with more powerful models yielding higher AUC scores. However, even with smaller Evolver LLMs, performance remained substantially above the Static baseline, indicating the robustness of the framework.
- **Ablation on Prompt Evolution Structure:**
 - Using a simple, generic prompt mutation instruction for the Evolver Agent significantly underperformed EvoTest's full structured evolution, indicating that the detailed, multi-part master prompt and sophisticated analysis of the Evolver are crucial for performance gains.
- **Fairer Comparison with Fine-Tuning (Qwen3-32B Backbone):**
 - When the Actor LLM was standardized to qwen/qwen3-32b for all methods, EvoTest (average AUC 0.35 with Qwen Evolver, 0.40 with OpenAI Evolver) still outperformed the strongest weight-update baseline, GRPO (0.31). This reinforces EvoTest's data efficiency and effectiveness independent of proprietary actor models.
- **Scalability with More Capable Models:**
 - Experiments with next-generation LLMs (e.g., gpt-5.1, gemini-3-pro-preview) showed that EvoTest yielded disproportionately larger performance gains compared to static agents as backbone model capabilities improved, suggesting the framework scales effectively with underlying model advancements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of artificial intelligence by addressing the challenge of on-the-fly learning in autonomous agents.

**Key Contributions:**

- **J-TTL Benchmark:** The introduction of the Jericho Test-Time Learning (J-TTL) benchmark provides a standardized and challenging environment for systematically measuring an agent's capacity for rapid, in-session self-improvement. This benchmark fills a gap in existing evaluation frameworks, which often do not adequately assess iterative learning on persistent tasks.
- **EvoTest Framework:** The proposed EvoTest framework offers a novel, gradient-free approach to test-time learning. By evolving the entire agentic configuration—including the policy prompt, structured memory, hyperparameters, and tool-use routines—EvoTest enables holistic adaptation. This multi-faceted evolution, driven by rich narrative feedback from episode transcripts, allows for more comprehensive strategic refinement compared to methods that focus on single components.
- **Empirical Performance and Efficiency:** The experimental results demonstrate that EvoTest consistently and substantially outperforms a range of strong baselines, including memory-based, reflection-based, automated prompt optimization, and gradient-based online fine-tuning methods. Its superior performance, particularly in data-scarce and sparse-reward environments, highlights its data efficiency and practicality for test-time learning scenarios. The computational efficiency of EvoTest, requiring only LLM forward passes rather than costly and hardware-intensive gradient updates, positions it as a more viable solution for deployment-time adaptation.

**Potential Impact:**

The development of self-improving agentic systems capable of learning and adapting quickly from experience holds implications for various AI applications. Agents equipped with test-time learning capabilities could exhibit enhanced robustness and reliability in dynamic and unpredictable environments, such as robotics, interactive systems, and complex decision-making processes. By shifting the paradigm from relying on sparse scalar rewards to leveraging rich narrative feedback for credit assignment, EvoTest offers a direction for developing more human-like learning mechanisms in AI.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The paper acknowledges several limitations, including:
- **Bounded by Pre-trained Capabilities:** As a gradient-free method, EvoTest cannot instill fundamentally new, low-level reasoning patterns outside the backbone LLM's pre-trained knowledge.
- **Task-Specific Overfitting:** The learned strategies may be specific to the current task instance and might not generalize to minor task variations.
- **Dependency on Evolver Agent:** The effectiveness of the framework is correlated with the reasoning capabilities of the Evolver LLM, potentially requiring access to powerful (and costly) models.
- **Search Space Complexity:** The current UCB-based evolutionary strategy might be prone to local optima in complex search spaces.

Future research could explore hybrid approaches combining rapid EvoTest-style adaptation with slower, background fine-tuning to enhance core competencies. Investigating how the symbolic strategies evolved by EvoTest could be abstracted and generalized into a library of reusable skills represents another important direction. Additionally, exploring more sophisticated evolutionary algorithms to navigate the complex strategic landscape could further enhance the framework's capabilities.
