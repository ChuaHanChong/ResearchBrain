# EvoTest: Evolutionary Test-Time Learning for Self-Improving Agentic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, EvoTest, enables AI agents to continuously learn and improve during deployment by holistically evolving their entire configuration, including policy prompts, structured memory, hyperparameters, and tool-use routines. Evaluated on the newly introduced Jericho Test-Time Learning (J-TTL) benchmark, EvoTest consistently surpassed both gradient-free and gradient-based adaptation methods in complex interactive environments.

## Problem

- Current AI agents often operate with fixed policies, limiting their ability to adapt and learn complex skills on-the-fly in novel, dynamic environments.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks specifically designed to measure and drive progress in rapid, in-session agent improvement (test-time learning).
- Existing adaptation methods struggle with characteristics of complex tasks, such as sparse rewards, long-range planning, data scarcity, or are limited to single-channel modifications.

## Method

- Introduces the Jericho ==Test-Time Learning== (J-TTL) benchmark, an evaluation setup where agents repeatedly play the same complex, text-based adventure game to demonstrate progressive performance improvement within a single session.
- Proposes **EvoTest**, an evolutionary test-time learning framework with a two-agent design (Actor and Evolver) that operates on a fixed, non-trainable backbone LLM.
- EvoTest performs 'whole-system evolution' by iteratively generating and selecting revised agent configurations, encompassing changes to policy prompts, structured memory, hyperparameters, and tool-use routines, using narrative feedback from full episode transcripts.

## Results

- EvoTest achieved the highest average AUC scores (**0.47**/**0.50**) across all six games on the J-TTL benchmark, substantially outperforming gradient-free baselines like EvoPrompt (**0.34**/**0.36**) and Reflexion (**0.32**/**0.34**).
- EvoTest demonstrated significantly higher average AUC (**0.47**/**0.50**) compared to the gradient-based online reinforcement learning baseline, GRPO (**0.30**), highlighting its data efficiency in test-time learning scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of prompt evolution and UCB selection for performance and stability, with EvoTest operating efficiently (20-30 seconds per update) compared to costly RL fine-tuning (5-10 minutes per pass on 4 H100 GPUs).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time learning is demonstrably effective for improving agent performance on complex tasks, with learning-based methods consistently outperforming static agents.
> - Holistic, whole-system evolution across multiple agent components (prompt, memory, tools, hyperparameters) effectively addresses complex performance bottlenecks that single-channel adaptation methods cannot resolve.
> - Leveraging rich, narrative episode transcripts for semantic credit assignment offers a more data-efficient and robust learning mechanism than relying on sparse scalar rewards for gradient-based methods, particularly in sparse-reward environments.
