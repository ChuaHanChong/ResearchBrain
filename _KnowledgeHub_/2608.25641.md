# Leveraging Inter-object Affordances for Efficient Planning in Contact-rich Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> UTAMP-2 extends Unified TAMP with symbolic abstractions of inter-object affordances: supportiveness, supportability, part-to-part support, graspability by member, liftability, and slidability. These enrich PDDL preconditions for pick, place, and a new slide action, so physical constraints are resolved during task planning rather than motion planning. On kitchen-organization tasks it reaches 100% success (90% with VLM-detected affordances) and plans one to two orders of magnitude faster than baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional TAMP encodes constraints over simplified object models, treating everything as light manageable cuboids, ignoring shape, size, and material properties critical for contact-rich execution.
- Original Unified TAMP abstracts motion constraints but omits physical properties, so it yields infeasible actions like lifting heavy objects or placing items on round surfaces.
- LLM/VLM planners lack the reasoning to enforce all inter-object physical constraints, and pushing constraint reasoning into sub-symbolic motion planning inflates planning time.

## Method

- **UTAMP-2** defines part-based ==affordance predicates== (isSupportive, isSupportable, isP2PSupportive, canGraspFrom, canLift, canSlide) over compound objects and their member parts, covering wrap-, edge-, and handle-grasping.
- Extends ==U-TAMP=='s StableGrasp and StablePlace constraint sets with new StablePick and StableSlide sets, adding workspace-engulfment preconditions and a slide-support action for unliftable objects.
- Builds a ==PDDL== domain solved by ==Fast Downward==; a ==VLM== reads a scene image, extracts object attributes, and a parser converts them into affordance literals for the problem file.

## Results

- Ground-truth affordance variant reached **100%** success and the VLM-detected variant **90%**, while original U-TAMP and the SayCan-style VLM planner both fell below **50%** (VLM **39%**).
- Planning time dropped by roughly **two orders of magnitude** versus both U-TAMP and the VLM planner, and grew far more slowly as scenes scaled from **2 to 5** objects.
- On sliding-required configurations, success was **1.0** and **0.81** for the two variants at **0.17s** and **0.20s** planning time, versus **0.41** and **66.9s** for the VLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pushing physical constraints into symbolic preconditions does double duty: it guarantees validity and prunes heuristic search, so richer domains can plan faster, not slower.
> - Language models are better used as affordance detectors than as planners; 0.98 precision/recall still cost 10 points of success because stacking is brittle to single errors.
> - Hand-authored affordance rules (small cannot support large, round surfaces unsuitable, large implies heavy) generalize broadly but remain scenario-specific simplifications, not learned physics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) has been a significant area of development for long-horizon planning in robotic manipulation. Established TAMP approaches primarily focus on sequencing actions and defining geometric and kinematic constraints. However, a limitation of these methods is their reliance on simplified object models, which frequently overlook crucial physical properties such as shape, size, and material. This simplification hinders the precise definition of physical interaction constraints and the full exploitation of objects' affordances, thereby restricting the applicability of TAMP in realistic, contact-rich environments. Furthermore, traditional TAMP often involves extensive sub-symbolic reasoning during motion planning, which can lead to increased planning times and reduced success rates.

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) has introduced new avenues for TAMP research. These models are leveraged for their common-sense knowledge and reasoning capabilities, with applications ranging from extracting affordances and generating symbolic goal descriptions to performing task planning and determining constraints. Approaches like SayCan have attempted to integrate LMs with affordance detectors to account for physical constraints in plan generation. However, despite improvements in simple manipulation tasks, these VLM-based methods often lack the detailed understanding of physical interaction constraints and inter-object affordances necessary for reliable long-horizon planning in complex, real-world settings.

Other related work has explored incorporating affordance information into TAMP, using visual perception or LLMs to estimate affordances and constraints. However, many of these approaches do not explicitly build robust symbolic abstractions of affordances that can be directly integrated into task planning for generating physically feasible plans.

This paper builds upon previous work, particularly the Unified TAMP (U-TAMP) framework, which introduced object-centric abstractions of motion constraints to minimize sub-symbolic reasoning. While U-TAMP effectively unifies motion planning with task planning by considering basic motion constraints at the symbolic level, it did not explicitly incorporate physical properties or inter-object affordances. This research positions itself by addressing this gap, extending U-TAMP to account for heterogeneous object properties and their resulting inter-object affordances, thereby aiming to enhance planning efficiency and success rates in realistic, contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) approaches when applied to real-world, contact-rich robotic tasks. Current TAMP methods often assume simplified object models, neglecting critical physical properties such as object shape, size, and material. This oversight prevents the comprehensive definition of physical interaction constraints, leading to plans that may be geometrically feasible but physically unstable or impossible to execute in realistic environments. For instance, traditional planners might attempt to stack a heavy object on a fragile one, grasp an oversized object, or place an object on a non-supportive, rounded surface, resulting in execution failures.

The authors propose that these physical properties define "inter-object affordances"—the capabilities of objects to interact with each other in specific ways (e.g., one object supporting another, or a robot grasping a specific part of an object). Integrating these affordances into the planning process is crucial for generating truly feasible and efficient plans.

The specific objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Account for physical properties to define part-based interaction constraints:** The research aims to develop a method that systematically considers physical attributes (shape, size, material) of objects to define detailed physical interaction constraints between their parts. These constraints are conceptualized as inter-object affordances, characterizing how different parts of objects can interact (e.g., for grasping or support).

2. **Build robust symbolic abstractions of affordances:** The objective is to translate these physical interaction constraints into robust symbolic abstractions related to grasping, supporting, lifting, and sliding. These abstractions are then integrated into the definitions of common robotic actions, such as picking, placement, and sliding, at the symbolic planning level. This approach is intended to enable the task planner to reason about physical feasibility early in the planning process, reducing the need for extensive sub-symbolic reasoning during motion planning.

3. **Define a PDDL-based planning domain incorporating inter-object affordances:** A key goal is to create a Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL)-based planning domain that explicitly encodes these inter-object affordances and physical constraints. By enriching the planning domain with this information, the authors intend to leverage the efficiency of AI symbolic planners to generate long-horizon plans that are inherently physically feasible, even for tasks involving objects with heterogeneous properties.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to significantly improve planning success rates and reduce planning computation times in contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks, particularly when compared to existing TAMP methods that either use simplified object models or rely on less structured common-sense reasoning from large language models. The selected application scenario, kitchen table organization involving various household objects, serves as a testbed for demonstrating the efficacy of this approach in a realistic setting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology extends the Unified TAMP (U-TAMP) framework by integrating explicit representations of inter-object affordances, derived from object physical properties, into its PDDL-based planning domain. This enhancement is designed to enable the task planner to generate physically feasible plans for contact-rich tasks involving objects with diverse characteristics.

**Affordance Definitions:**
The approach defines specific inter-object affordances based on the physical attributes of objects and their parts. These definitions are presented as predicates for integration into the PDDL domain:

- **Supportiveness:** A part of an object (`?part1` of `?obj1`) is `isSupportive` if it is a flat surface or can effectively function as one, suitable for supporting other objects. For instance, the top of a cutting board is supportive, while the rounded top of a salt shaker is not.
- **Supportability:** A part of an object (`?part1` of `?obj1`) is `isSupportable` if the object can be stably placed on that part onto a flat surface. Rounded or thin parts, like the edges of a knife, are typically not supportable.
- **Inter-object Supportiveness:** The predicate `isP2PSupportive ?part2 ?obj2 ?part1 ?obj1` denotes that a specific part (`?part2`) of `?obj2` can stably support `?obj1` placed on its part (`?part1`), considering the shapes, sizes, and materials of both objects. For example, a tray's top surface can support a mug placed on its bottom.
- **Inter-object Graspability:** The predicate `canGraspFrom ?obj1 ?o1-mem ?o1m-hp ?o1m-hf1 ?o1m-hf2` indicates that an object (`?obj1`) can be stably grasped by a specific member (`?o1-mem`, e.g., a handle) with the gripper palm (`?o1m-hp`) and fingers (`?o1m-hf1`, `?o1m-hf2`) interacting with designated parts of the member. This differentiates between various grasping types (wrap-grasping, edge-grasping, handle-grasping) and considers an object's bulkiness or shape, such as a frying pan body being too large for grasping.
- **Lifting:** The predicate `canLift ?obj1 ?o1-mem` signifies that `?obj1` is sufficiently light to be lifted by the robot when grasped via its member `?o1-mem` in at least one stable configuration. This accounts for object weight in relation to robot payload.
- **Sliding:** The predicate `canSlide ?obj1 ?obj2 ?o1-o2 ?o2-o1` indicates that `?obj1` can be stably slid across `?o2-o1` of `?obj2` while resting on its `?o1-o2` part. This is applicable for heavy objects that cannot be lifted but can be manipulated by sliding.

**Action Definitions:**
The study defines three core actions, extending or adapting those from the original U-TAMP:
1. **`pick-support`:** For picking an object with the gripper. This action incorporates `StableGrasp` and `StablePick` constraints. `StableGrasp` checks for a valid grasping configuration of an object's member within the robot's workspace using `canGraspFrom`. `StablePick` extends this by adding `canLift`, ensuring the object is liftable.
2. **`place-support`:** For placing an object on a surface. This action includes `StablePlace` constraints, which require the placed object to be stably supported by the target surface. It verifies `isP2PSupportive` between the interacting parts of the placed and supporting objects, ensuring physical stability.
3. **`slide-support`:** For sliding an object across a surface. This new action requires `StableGrasp` but *not* `StablePick` (implying the object cannot be lifted). It includes `StableSlide` constraints, which check `canSlide` and require an empty space adjacent to the object on the support surface for movement.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted in the CoppeliaSim simulator, featuring a Franka Emika Panda robot with a two-finger gripper on a kitchen table. The task involves organizing kitchenware (salt shaker, glass mug, cutting board, frying pan, wooden tray) by stacking them on a tray. Objects possess diverse physical properties defining their affordances (e.g., a heavy, unliftable tray that is slidable; a salt shaker with a non-supportive rounded top).

**Comparison Approaches:**
The proposed method (`UTAMP-2`) is evaluated against three baselines:
- **UTAMP-1:** The original U-TAMP approach, which uses object-centric predicates for motion constraints but lacks inter-object affordances and physical attributes.
- **UTAMP-2-GT:** The proposed U-TAMP extension with hand-crafted (ground truth) inter-object affordances. This serves as an upper bound for performance with perfect affordance detection.
- **UTAMP-2-VLM:** The proposed U-TAMP extension where inter-object affordances are detected by a Vision-Language Model (OpenAI's ChatGPT o4-mini) from scene images and then translated into PDDL.
- **VLM:** A VLM-based planner (proxy of SayCan) that uses the OpenAI ChatGPT o4-mini model for end-to-end task planning, implicitly reasoning about affordances from common-sense knowledge.

**Evaluation Metrics and Scenarios:**
- **Success Rate:** Proportion of initial configurations leading to successful execution in simulation, with plans validated against physical constraints.
- **Planning Time:** Computation time from plan initiation to reception.
Experiments are conducted across two scenarios:
1. Tasks where all objects are within the robot's reachable workspace, requiring only `pick-support` and `place-support` actions. This allows comparison with UTAMP-1.
2. Tasks where the wooden tray is outside the reachable workspace, necessitating the `slide-support` action. This compares `UTAMP-2` and `VLM` only.
Scene complexity is varied by using 2 to 5 manipulable objects. Fast Downward is used for PDDL-based planning, and RRT-Connect for motion planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in simulated kitchen table organization scenarios, comparing the proposed `UTAMP-2` approach (with both ground-truth and VLM-detected affordances) against the original `UTAMP-1` and a VLM-based planner. The evaluation focused on planning success rates and computation times across varying scene complexities.

**Overall Performance (Pick and Place Actions Only):**
- **Success Rate:**
 - `UTAMP-2-GT` achieved the highest success rate at 100%. This indicates that when inter-object affordances and physical constraints are accurately provided to the planner, it can consistently generate feasible plans.
 - `UTAMP-2-VLM` achieved a 90% success rate. The VLM used for affordance detection in this approach demonstrated high precision, recall, and F1 scores (all averaging 0.98) for individual affordance detection. The slight drop from 100% to 90% in overall task success was attributed to the task's sensitivity to even a few inconsistently detected supporting affordances, which could render entire stacking sequences unfeasible.
 - `UTAMP-1` (the original U-TAMP) and the `VLM` (end-to-end VLM planner) exhibited substantially lower success rates, both falling below 50% (VLM at 39%). This indicates that without explicit consideration of physical properties and inter-object affordances, plans generated by these methods frequently result in physically unfeasible actions or unstable configurations.

- **Planning Time:**
 - `UTAMP-2` variants (both GT and VLM-assisted) demonstrated significantly faster planning times, outperforming other methods by one to two orders of magnitude on a logarithmic scale. This suggests that the rich symbolic abstractions of inter-object physical constraints effectively guide the heuristic search in task planning, leading to quicker plan generation.
 - `UTAMP-1` showed longer planning times than `UTAMP-2` variants, implying that its less expressive action preconditions necessitated more extensive search.
 - The `VLM` approach exhibited the slowest planning times and the highest stochasticity, characterized by the largest variance. This was attributed to the inherent latency and response time of the VLM, particularly when operating with high reasoning effort.

**Performance with Increasing Scene Complexity (Per-object-count Results for Pick and Place):**
- As the number of manipulable objects increased (from 2 to 5), `UTAMP-2` variants largely maintained their high success rates.
- Conversely, the success rates of `UTAMP-1` and `VLM` significantly decreased with increasing scene complexity. They struggled to generate successful plans, especially with 5 objects, often failing to identify a valid stacking order or feasible actions.
- Planning time increased for all methods with higher scene complexity. However, `UTAMP-2` variants showed a more moderate increase, avoiding a full order of magnitude jump. `UTAMP-1`, in particular, experienced a substantial increase in planning time for larger numbers of objects, reinforcing the conclusion that explicit inter-object physical constraints contribute to more efficient heuristic search.

**Performance in Configurations Requiring Sliding Actions:**
- In scenarios where the wooden tray was initially outside the reachable workspace, necessitating a `slide-support` action, `UTAMP-2-GT` maintained a 100% success rate.
- `UTAMP-2-VLM` achieved an 81% success rate, again indicating that VLM-based affordance detection, while largely accurate, can introduce inconsistencies that impact overall task success.
- The `VLM` planner achieved a 41% success rate in these more complex scenarios, consistent with its performance in pick-and-place tasks.
- Planning times showed similar trends, with `UTAMP-2` variants being significantly faster than the `VLM` approach (e.g., 0.17s vs. 66.9s on average for GT vs. VLM).

**Analysis of Failures:**
- For `UTAMP-2-VLM`, failures typically occurred not because plans violated symbolic constraints, but because the VLM's inconsistent detection of object attributes led to the planner being unable to find a feasible plan in the first place, often due to missing necessary supporting affordances for stacking.
- For the `VLM` planner, failures were primarily due to violations of fundamental support constraints (e.g., placing objects inside a frying pan when they should be on top, or placing an object on an already occupied surface despite explicit instructions in the prompt). Violations of picking constraints (e.g., two consecutive picking actions) and incorrect stacking orders due to a lack of deep reasoning about inter-object affordances were also observed.

In summary, the findings indicate that explicit symbolic encoding of inter-object affordances and physical constraints within a PDDL-based TAMP framework, as implemented in `UTAMP-2`, leads to substantial improvements in both planning success rates and efficiency compared to methods that lack such detailed representations or rely solely on implicit common-sense reasoning from VLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates that explicitly leveraging inter-object affordances through rich symbolic abstractions can significantly enhance the efficiency and success rates of robotic Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) in contact-rich manipulation tasks. The proposed `UTAMP-2` method addresses critical limitations of traditional TAMP, which often struggle with realistic objects due to simplified models that disregard physical properties.

The primary significance of this work lies in its contribution to bridging the gap between high-level task planning and low-level motion constraints by integrating detailed physical interaction rules directly into the symbolic planning domain. By defining object-centric predicates for grasping, supporting, lifting, and sliding that account for heterogeneous object shapes, sizes, and materials, the approach allows task planners to reason about physical feasibility at an early stage. This minimizes the need for computationally expensive sub-symbolic reasoning during motion planning, which is a common bottleneck in conventional TAMP systems.

The experimental results highlight several key impacts:
- **Increased Planning Reliability:** `UTAMP-2-GT` achieved a 100% success rate in complex kitchen organization tasks, demonstrating that with accurate affordance information, the method can reliably generate physically feasible plans. This contrasts with traditional `UTAMP-1` and VLM-based planners, which exhibited significantly lower success rates, particularly as scene complexity increased.
- **Enhanced Planning Efficiency:** The `UTAMP-2` variants achieved planning times one to two orders of magnitude faster than baseline methods. This efficiency gain is crucial for real-time robotic applications, where rapid response is often required. The reduction in planning time is a direct consequence of the enriched planning domain, which guides the AI planner's heuristic search more effectively.
- **Integration with Vision-Language Models:** The `UTAMP-2-VLM` approach shows a viable pathway for incorporating perception-based affordance detection from VLMs into a robust symbolic planning framework. While VLM-based detection still introduces some inconsistencies that impact overall success, the high precision and recall of individual affordance detection suggest that VLMs can serve as valuable tools for grounding symbolic knowledge, even if further robustness improvements are needed for mission-critical tasks.
- **Robustness in Contact-Rich Scenarios:** The ability to handle diverse objects (e.g., heavy, unliftable trays that require sliding; delicate glasses; various grasping points) and define nuanced interaction rules positions this method for applications in various real-world scenarios, from manufacturing and logistics to domestic assistance, where contact-rich manipulation is prevalent.

The findings also provide insight into the current limitations of end-to-end VLM-based planning for robotics. While VLMs possess common-sense reasoning, they frequently fail to enforce strict physical constraints or understand the precise implications of interactions, leading to infeasible plans. This research suggests that a hybrid approach, where VLMs assist in perception and symbol grounding while a formal symbolic planner handles constraint satisfaction, may be more effective for complex, safety-critical robotic tasks.

In conclusion, this work offers a significant step towards more autonomous and capable robotic systems by enabling them to plan and execute tasks with a deeper understanding of the physical world. While the approach relies on certain simplifying assumptions regarding affordance assignment, it lays a foundation for future extensions to more complex actions and broader environments, potentially impacting the development of advanced robotic manipulation capabilities across various industries.
