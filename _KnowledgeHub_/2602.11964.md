# Gaia2: Benchmarking LLM Agents on Dynamic and Asynchronous Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meta SuperIntelligence Labs developed GAIA2, a benchmark and open-source ARE framework, to evaluate large language model agents in dynamic, asynchronous, and multi-agent environments. Empirical evaluation revealed that state-of-the-art models, including GPT-5 (high) at 42.1% pass@1 and Kimi-K2 at 20.1% pass@1 among open-source models, face significant challenges with temporal reasoning, noise robustness, and ambiguity resolution.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for LLM agents do not adequately evaluate performance in dynamic, asynchronous, and complex real-world environments.
- There is a lack of comprehensive, verifiable frameworks to assess capabilities like temporal reasoning, noise robustness, ambiguity resolution, and multi-agent collaboration.
- Current Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods struggle due to insufficient fine-grained reward signals for complex, multi-step tasks.

## Method

- An open-source ==Agents Research Environments== (ARE) framework was developed to create asynchronous, event-driven environments for LLM agent evaluation.
- The GAIA2 benchmark was constructed using ARE, comprising 1,120 scenarios that test critical capabilities in dynamic settings, including temporal reasoning and multi-agent interaction.
- A novel action-level verifier was integrated to provide precise, verifiable rewards for each agent action, enabling scalable Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

## Results

- GPT-5 (high) achieved an overall pass@1 score of **42.1%**, while Kimi-K2 led open-source models with **20.1%** pass@1, demonstrating current performance ceilings.
- Models struggled with complex capabilities such as ambiguity resolution, adaptability, and time-sensitive tasks; for instance, GPT-5 (high) scored **0.0%** on time-sensitive tasks with default inference but **34.4%** when latency was eliminated.
- Agent2Agent experiments indicated that weaker models benefited from increased collaboration, and heterogeneous teams (strong main agent, weaker app-agents) improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art LLM agents exhibit trade-offs, with no single model demonstrating superior performance across all capabilities like temporal reasoning, adaptability, and noise robustness.
> - Inference latency significantly impacts performance on time-sensitive tasks, where faster models achieved higher scores even with potentially weaker overall reasoning.
> - Multi-agent collaboration can benefit weaker models but may not yield cost-normalized gains for stronger agents, indicating that efficient task decomposition and delegation are crucial.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous large language model (LLM) agents capable of performing complex, multi-step tasks in dynamic environments is a rapidly evolving frontier in AI. This paper addresses a critical gap in the current research landscape: the lack of comprehensive benchmarks that can truly evaluate LLM agents in realistic, asynchronous settings.

**Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** The paper effectively contextualizes its contribution by outlining the shortcomings of prior work. Many existing benchmarks, such as ALFWorld (Shridhar et al., 2021), WebShop (Yao et al., 2023a), WebArena (Zhou etal., 2024), WorkArena (Drouin et al., 2024), AppWorld (Trivedi et al., 2024), ToolSandbox (Lu et al., 2024), GAIA (Mialon et al., 2023), SWE-bench (Jimenez et al., 2024), and BrowseComp (Wei et al., 2025), primarily fall into one of two categories:
- **Static setups:** These often evaluate only final outcomes, lacking insight into intermediate steps or the process an agent takes.
- **Synchronous environments:** In these, the environment only changes in direct response to an agent's actions. This design fails to capture crucial real-world challenges like handling asynchronous events, operating under temporal constraints, or adapting to noise and uncertainty that evolve independently of the agent.

**Emerging Dynamic and Multi-Agent Benchmarks:** The paper acknowledges other efforts that have started incorporating temporal dynamics and multi-agent interaction, including VendingBench (Backlund & Petersson, 2025), τ-Bench and τ²-Bench (Yao et al., 2024; Barres et al., 2025), MultiAgentBench (Zhu et al., 2025), and MCP-based benchmarks (Wang et al., 2025). While these are steps in the right direction, Gaia2 distinguishes itself by **unifying** several critical dimensions—asynchronous execution, temporal reasoning, noise robustness, ambiguity resolution, and multi-agent collaboration—under a single, verifiable evaluation framework.

**Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** A key trend in improving LLM agents at scale is RLVR, which offers a more reliable alternative to preference-based methods (OpenAI, 2024b; DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025; Mistral-AI et al., 2025; MoonshotAI et al., 2025). However, RLVR inherently requires robust, fine-grained reward signals. Existing verification strategies, such as exact match for final outputs (GAIA) or milestone-based checks (ToolSandbox), often lack the precision needed for granular credit assignment in complex, dynamic tasks. Gaia2's introduction of a write-action verifier directly addresses this need, making the benchmark highly relevant for training future RLVR-based agent systems.

By introducing both the ARE framework and the Gaia2 benchmark, this work aims to provide a more realistic, comprehensive, and actionable evaluation paradigm that moves beyond the limitations of previous efforts, directly bridging the gap between current academic benchmarks and the demands of real-world agent deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind Gaia2 stems from the growing disparity between how LLM agents are typically evaluated and the complexities they face in real-world deployments. Current benchmarks fail to capture critical challenges, thereby hindering the development of truly robust and adaptable agents.

The paper articulates three main contributions, which serve as its core objectives:

1. **ARE Framework (Agents Research Environments):** The primary objective is to release a general-purpose, open-source platform for building asynchronous, event-driven benchmarks. This framework is designed to support scalable evaluation and efficient data generation for Reinforcement Learning (RL), enabling researchers to create dynamic environments that mirror real-world scenarios. This aims to provide the community with a flexible, foundational infrastructure for developing and testing next-generation agent systems.

2. **Gaia2 Benchmark:** The paper aims to introduce Gaia2 as the first benchmark that unifies several critical agent capabilities under a single, verifiable evaluation framework. Specifically, it targets:
 - **Asynchronous execution:** Environments evolve independently of agent actions.
 - **Temporal reasoning:** Agents must operate under time constraints and maintain temporal awareness.
 - **Noise robustness:** Agents must adapt to noisy and dynamic events.
 - **Ambiguity resolution:** Agents must identify and resolve unclear or contradictory task instructions.
 - **Multi-agent collaboration:** Agents must coordinate with other agents to accomplish tasks.
 Crucially, Gaia2 is designed to be "directly usable for RLVR," meaning its verification mechanism provides fine-grained, action-level rewards essential for training.

3. **Empirical Study:** A significant objective is to conduct a comprehensive empirical study by evaluating leading proprietary and open-source models on Gaia2. This is intended to expose fundamental trade-offs between reasoning strength, efficiency, robustness, and cost, providing insights into the current state-of-the-art and charting open directions for future research in practical agent systems. The study also aims to highlight specific failure modes and challenges in closing the "sim2real" gap.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more demanding and realistic testing ground for LLM agents, one that fosters the development of agents capable of handling the dynamism and unpredictability of real-world interactions, while simultaneously providing the tools necessary for training them effectively with RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research is two-fold: first, the development of the foundational **Agents Research Environments (ARE)** platform, and second, the construction of the **Gaia2 benchmark** on top of ARE, coupled with a robust **verifier** and an **empirical evaluation**.

#### 3.1. Agents Research Environments (ARE) Framework

ARE is introduced as a general-purpose platform designed for building, running, and analyzing LLM agents in dynamic, asynchronous environments.

- **Core Concepts:** ARE is built on five core abstractions:
 - **Apps:** Stateful APIs (e.g., email, messaging, calendar) exposing "read" (no state change) and "write" (state-changing) tools.
 - **Environments:** Collections of apps, a time manager, and governing rules, capable of hosting one or more agents.
 - **Events:** Everything that happens in the environment (tool calls, state changes, scheduled updates). Events are fully logged, timestamped, and organized into Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) for dependencies. Special event types include Agent/User/Env, Conditional, Validation, and Oracle events.
 - **Notifications:** A configurable observability layer that selects which events are pushed to an agent's context, allowing for study of proactive/reactive behavior under varying visibility.
 - **Scenarios:** Dynamic trajectories specifying an initial state, a DAG of events (including user requests, intermediate events), and a verification method.
- **Asynchronicity and Time:** A critical feature is that ARE environments run asynchronously from the agent. Model generation time directly consumes simulated time, meaning external events can occur during an agent's reasoning. This enables evaluation of temporal awareness and responsiveness, impossible in synchronous settings.
- **Mobile Environment:** As an instantiation of ARE, the "Mobile" environment features twelve common consumer apps (e.g., Messages, Emails, Calendar, Contacts) with 101 associated tools. "Universes" are complete instances of this environment, populated with synthetic but coherent data, seeded with personas, and containing 400K-800K tokens of content, suitable for long-context tasks.
- **Agent Orchestration:** A model-agnostic ReAct-like scaffold (Yao et al., 2023b) is used, where the agent outputs one tool call per step in JSON. This orchestration includes pre-step (injecting notifications) and post-step (checking termination) hooks. An ablation study confirmed this sequential scaffold does not artificially bottleneck performance compared to Parallel Tool Calling.

#### 3.2. Gaia2 Benchmark Design

Gaia2 builds on ARE's abstractions, comprising 1,120 human-annotated scenarios across 10 distinct Mobile universes.

- **Capabilities Evaluated:** Gaia2 categorizes scenarios into seven capabilities:
 - **Core Capabilities:** Execution (chaining write actions), Search (chaining read actions), Ambiguity (identifying impossible/contradictory tasks and seeking clarification), Adaptability (revising plans due to environmental changes), and Time (executing actions under temporal constraints).
 - **Augmentations:** Noise (stress-testing robustness with tool anomalies and irrelevant events) and Agent2Agent (evaluating collaboration by replacing apps with "app-agents" that the main agent must communicate with).
- **Scenario Design and Annotation Protocol:** Scenarios are created using an ARE annotation interface, where annotators design DAGs of write actions and environment events as ground truth. Scenarios are designed to stress one capability primarily, while acknowledging inherent compositionality. A rigorous validation process (multiple annotators, consistency checks, automated guardrails, difficulty calibration) ensures quality and verifiability.

#### 3.3. ARE Verifier

A cornerstone of Gaia2 is its novel action-level verifier, crucial for fine-grained evaluation and RLVR.

- **Mechanism:** The verifier evaluates agent trajectories against a *minimal oracle sequence* of write actions. Read actions are not penalized. Evaluation covers four dimensions:
 - **Consistency:** Matching tool names/counts, argument checks (exact for rigid fields, LLM rubrics for flexible text fields).
 - **Causality:** Ensuring agent actions respect the dependency DAG of oracle actions.
 - **Timing:** Verifying actions fall within specified tolerance windows relative to oracle timing.
 - **Completeness:** Success requires matching all oracle write actions.
- **Performance:** The verifier achieves 0.98 agreement and 0.99 precision with 0.95 recall on 450 hand-labeled validation trajectories, significantly outperforming LLM-only baselines.
- **Robustness:** The verifier design is robust against "judge-hacking" (e.g., agents embedding complex code in messages to trick LLM judges) through task-agnostic style checks.

#### 3.4. Experiments

- **Setup:** State-of-the-art proprietary (GPT-5, Claude-4 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Pro) and open-source models (Kimi-K2, Llama-4 Maverick, GPT-OSS 120B, Qwen3 235B, Grok-4, GPT-4o, Llama 3.3 70B Instruct) were evaluated using the same ReAct-style scaffold. Scenarios were run three times (pass@1 metric), with full context length, temperature 0.5, and 16K token generation limits per turn.
- **Termination Conditions:** Scenarios ended on task completion, context overflow, 200 steps, or timeout.
- **Time-Sensitive Evaluation:** Gaia2-Time was evaluated in two modes: default (inference consumes simulated time) and "instant" (zero generation latency) to isolate the impact of inference speed.
- **Multi-Agent Evaluation:** Agent2Agent scenarios explored homogeneous and heterogeneous teams by varying the underlying models for main and app-agents.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that Gaia2 provides a realistic, robust, and extensible framework for evaluating LLM agents in dynamic environments, with a verification system directly supporting advanced RL training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation on Gaia2 provided critical insights into the capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art LLM agents.

- **Overall Performance and Model Dominance:** No single model dominates across all capabilities, highlighting inherent trade-offs. GPT-5 (high) achieved the strongest overall score of 42.1% pass@1, maintaining an 8-point lead over Claude-4 Sonnet. Kimi-K2 emerged as the leader among open-source models with 20.1% pass@1. This "no dominance" finding underscores the multi-faceted nature of agent intelligence in dynamic environments.

- **Capability-Specific Strengths and Weaknesses:**
 - **Execution and Search:** These were the "easiest" splits, consistent with the saturation observed in prior, simpler benchmarks. Models generally performed better here.
 - **Ambiguity and Adaptability:** These remained highly challenging. Only Claude-4 Sonnet and GPT-5 (high) showed robust performance, indicating that most models struggle with recognizing and resolving unclear instructions or dynamically adjusting to environmental changes.
 - **Time:** This split effectively differentiated frontier models. Gemini 2.5 Pro and Claude-4 Sonnet achieved the highest scores (7.3% and 8.1% respectively), primarily due to their efficiency and lower latency. GPT-5 (high), despite its strong reasoning, scored 0.0% in default mode, indicating a significant challenge with time-sensitive tasks due to longer inference times. In "instant" mode (zero generation latency), GPT-5 (high) jumped to 34.4%, and Sonnet to 26.7%, confirming that inference speed is a critical factor for temporal tasks.
 - **Noise Robustness:** Most models scored below 20%, indicating a general lack of robustness to perturbations like random tool execution failures or irrelevant events, even for top models like GPT-5 (high) which reached 35.4%.
 - **Agent2Agent Collaboration:** Weaker models (e.g., Llama 4 Maverick) showed improved pass@k and reduced tool-call errors with increased collaboration (higher agent-to-agent ratio `r`). However, stronger models like Claude-4 Sonnet did not see similar cost-normalized performance gains, suggesting that coordination overhead might offset benefits if decomposition is not optimal or if models are already highly capable in single-agent planning. Heterogeneous teams, where a strong main agent plans for weaker app-agents, showed performance boosts, highlighting the potential for strategic delegation.

- **Cost-Performance-Time Trade-offs:** The evaluation revealed clear trade-offs. GPT-5's reasoning variants demonstrated direct scaling: higher test-time compute (and thus cost) yielded better performance but also longer solution times. Claude-4 Sonnet offered competitive accuracy at a higher cost than some GPT-5 variants but with significantly faster execution. These findings emphasize the inadequacy of purely capability-based metrics and advocate for cost-normalized evaluation to reflect real-world deployment constraints.

- **Performance Drivers:**
 - Performance correlated positively with the number of tool calls (indicating exploration) and output tokens (indicating reasoning verbosity).
 - Claude-4 Sonnet and Kimi-K2 were identified as outliers, achieving high performance with relatively fewer output tokens, suggesting exceptional efficiency.
 - "Thinking" variants (e.g., Qwen3-235B Thinking, Claude-4 Sonnet Thinking) generated more tokens per step but took fewer overall steps, leading to higher pass@1 and lower cost per solved scenario, demonstrating a trade-off between verbosity and efficiency.

In summary, Gaia2 highlights that while LLMs excel at basic instruction following and information retrieval, they struggle significantly with nuanced aspects of real-world interaction, such as temporal constraints, dynamic adaptation, noise, and complex multi-agent coordination. The benchmark effectively exposes current limitations and necessary directions for future agent development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the ARE framework and Gaia2 benchmark carries profound significance and potential impact for the advancement of LLM agents and the broader AI research community.

1. **Revolutionizing LLM Agent Evaluation:** Gaia2 addresses a critical void in current evaluation methodologies by providing a benchmark for truly dynamic, asynchronous, and complex real-world agent interactions. By moving beyond static or synchronous evaluations, it pushes the field towards assessing agents on capabilities essential for practical deployment, such as temporal awareness, robustness to noise, ambiguity resolution, and multi-agent collaboration. This more realistic evaluation paradigm will drive the development of agents that are genuinely more adaptable and reliable in real-world settings.

2. **Enabling Scalable Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** The novel action-level verifier within ARE is a game-changer for RLVR. By offering fine-grained, reproducible credit assignment for every state-changing action, it provides the precise reward signals necessary for training LLM agents at scale. This directly addresses one of the major bottlenecks in developing highly capable agents through RL, paving the way for more efficient and effective learning algorithms that can close the performance gap between current models and human-level task completion.

3. **Fostering Community-Driven Research through Open-Source Infrastructure:** The release of ARE as an open-source, general-purpose platform is a substantial contribution. It empowers the wider AI research community to not only use Gaia2 but also to build, extend, and standardize new benchmarks and develop novel agent systems. This open infrastructure can accelerate research, reduce fragmentation across different agent environments, and foster collaborative innovation in agent design and evaluation. The modular nature of ARE (apps, events, notifications, scenarios) ensures its extensibility beyond consumer mobile environments to other domains.

4. **Exposing Fundamental Challenges and Guiding Future Research:** The empirical study provides invaluable insights by clearly exposing the current limitations of frontier LLMs. The finding that no single model dominates across all capabilities, coupled with specific weaknesses in time-sensitive tasks, noise robustness, and ambiguity resolution, highlights critical areas for future research. This informs the development of next-generation agent architectures that might incorporate:
 - **Adaptive Compute Strategies:** The "inverse scaling" observed in time-sensitive tasks suggests the need for agents that can dynamically adjust their reasoning depth and speed based on task requirements and temporal constraints (e.g., using lightweight reasoning for routine tasks and deeper deliberation only when necessary).
 - **Improved Temporal Planning:** Current models struggle with complex temporal reasoning and acting within deadlines, pointing to a need for better integration of temporal awareness into agent planning.
 - **Robustness Mechanisms:** The low scores in noise scenarios emphasize the necessity for agents to be more resilient to real-world imperfections like API errors and environmental distractors.

5. **Advancing Multi-Agent Systems:** The Agent2Agent experiments offer early insights into the complexities of LLM agent collaboration. The results suggest that effective task decomposition and the strategic use of heterogeneous teams (e.g., strong main agents delegating to specialized sub-agents) can be pathways to building more efficient and robust multi-agent systems. This opens a new "compute scaling axis" for task automation, where specialized sub-agents can handle sub-goals, potentially optimizing resource utilization.

6. **Addressing the "Sim2Real" Gap:** By introducing a benchmark that more closely mimics the unpredictable and dynamic nature of real-world environments, Gaia2 directly tackles the "sim2real" gap for LLM agents. The challenges revealed push the field towards developing agents that are not just intelligent in controlled settings but are truly capable of navigating and performing in the messy reality of practical applications.

In conclusion, Gaia2 is not merely a new benchmark; it's a foundational step towards building truly capable and practical LLM agents. By providing both a challenging evaluation and the open infrastructure to conduct such evaluations, it sets a new standard for research and development in this critical area of AI.
