# Principled RL for Diffusion LLMs Emerges from a Sequence-Level Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> ELBO-based Sequence-level Policy Optimization (ESPO), developed by researchers from Renmin University, Stanford, Lambda, Inc., and Tsinghua University, provides a principled reinforcement learning framework for diffusion large language models (dLLMs). It effectively fine-tunes dLLMs by treating entire sequences as actions and employing a stabilized Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), achieving 20-60+ absolute point improvements on planning tasks and consistent gains across mathematics and coding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms designed for autoregressive (AR) models fundamentally mismatch the non-autoregressive generation process of diffusion LLMs (dLLMs), as token-level conditional probabilities are ill-defined or intractable.
- Prior attempts to apply RL to dLLMs relied on unstable heuristic approximations for token-level likelihoods or computationally prohibitive trajectory-level optimization, hindering large-scale and stable training.
- This incompatibility prevents dLLMs from fully leveraging RL for aligning with preferences and enhancing performance on complex tasks requiring global consistency.

## Method

- **ESPO** redefines the RL action space to be ==sequence-level==, treating the generation of an entire text sequence as a single, atomic action, directly aligning with dLLMs' holistic generation process.
- It utilizes the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) as a tractable proxy for the intractable sequence log-likelihood, applying a per-token normalization to the importance ratio to ensure numerical stability.
- The framework incorporates a robust quadratic (k2) estimator for the KL-divergence regularization term and employs antithetic and coupled sampling for effective variance reduction during training.

## Results

- ESPO achieved substantial performance gains on planning tasks, with **20**-40 absolute point improvements on Countdown and over **60** points on Sudoku compared to existing baselines, validating its efficacy for tasks requiring global coherence.
- The framework delivered consistent positive gains on mathematics benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH) and coding tasks (HumanEval, MBPP), demonstrating its broad applicability in enhancing dLLM capabilities across various domains.
- Ablation studies empirically confirmed that the sequence-level action space with ELBO approximation and the k2 KL-divergence estimator are critical for stable and superior performance, outperforming unstable token-level and alternative KL estimator approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A sequence-level perspective is crucial for applying RL to dLLMs, as token-level decompositions of the ELBO introduce inconsistency and instability into the learning process.
> - Stabilization techniques, particularly per-token normalization of importance ratios and the use of a stable quadratic KL-divergence estimator (k2), are essential for principled and efficient policy optimization in dLLM-RL.
> - The computational overhead of increasing Monte Carlo samples for ELBO estimation in dLLMs is moderate, as the denoising generation phase dominates the overall training FLOPs, making the approach scalable.
