# Principled RL for Diffusion LLMs Emerges from a Sequence-Level Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> ELBO-based Sequence-level Policy Optimization (ESPO), developed by researchers from Renmin University, Stanford, Lambda, Inc., and Tsinghua University, provides a principled reinforcement learning framework for diffusion large language models (dLLMs). It effectively fine-tunes dLLMs by treating entire sequences as actions and employing a stabilized Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), achieving 20-60+ absolute point improvements on planning tasks and consistent gains across mathematics and coding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms designed for autoregressive (AR) models fundamentally mismatch the non-autoregressive generation process of diffusion LLMs (dLLMs), as token-level conditional probabilities are ill-defined or intractable.
- Prior attempts to apply RL to dLLMs relied on unstable heuristic approximations for token-level likelihoods or computationally prohibitive trajectory-level optimization, hindering large-scale and stable training.
- This incompatibility prevents dLLMs from fully leveraging RL for aligning with preferences and enhancing performance on complex tasks requiring global consistency.

## Method

- **ESPO** redefines the RL action space to be ==sequence-level==, treating the generation of an entire text sequence as a single, atomic action, directly aligning with dLLMs' holistic generation process.
- It utilizes the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) as a tractable proxy for the intractable sequence log-likelihood, applying a per-token normalization to the importance ratio to ensure numerical stability.
- The framework incorporates a robust quadratic (k2) estimator for the KL-divergence regularization term and employs antithetic and coupled sampling for effective variance reduction during training.

## Results

- ESPO achieved substantial performance gains on planning tasks, with **20**-40 absolute point improvements on Countdown and over **60** points on Sudoku compared to existing baselines, validating its efficacy for tasks requiring global coherence.
- The framework delivered consistent positive gains on mathematics benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH) and coding tasks (HumanEval, MBPP), demonstrating its broad applicability in enhancing dLLM capabilities across various domains.
- Ablation studies empirically confirmed that the sequence-level action space with ELBO approximation and the k2 KL-divergence estimator are critical for stable and superior performance, outperforming unstable token-level and alternative KL estimator approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A sequence-level perspective is crucial for applying RL to dLLMs, as token-level decompositions of the ELBO introduce inconsistency and instability into the learning process.
> - Stabilization techniques, particularly per-token normalization of importance ratios and the use of a stable quadratic KL-divergence estimator (k2), are essential for principled and efficient policy optimization in dLLM-RL.
> - The computational overhead of increasing Monte Carlo samples for ELBO estimation in dLLMs is moderate, as the denoising generation phase dominates the overall training FLOPs, making the approach scalable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the cutting edge of large language models (LLMs) and reinforcement learning (RL), specifically addressing a critical challenge in the nascent field of diffusion large language models (dLLMs).

**Context of LLMs:** The landscape of LLMs has been predominantly shaped by autoregressive (AR) models (e.g., GPT series), which generate text sequentially, token by token. While highly successful, AR models have inherent limitations in parallel generation and sometimes struggle with long-context global consistency due to their left-to-right dependency.
Diffusion models, traditionally successful in image generation, have recently emerged as a powerful alternative for language generation (dLLMs). These models frame text generation as an iterative denoising process, offering advantages in non-autoregressive generation, long-context handling, multimodal applications, and potentially faster inference.

**Role of Reinforcement Learning in LLMs:** Reinforcement Learning, particularly methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), has proven instrumental in "post-training" AR LLMs. It aligns models with human preferences, enhances reasoning capabilities, and improves performance on complex tasks like math, coding, and planning.

**The Gap and Challenge:** The core challenge identified by the authors is the fundamental incompatibility between mainstream RL algorithms (which are largely designed for AR models) and dLLMs. AR-based RL algorithms like GRPO rely on token-level conditional probabilities and importance ratios (e.g., $\pi_\theta(y_k | x, y_{<k}) / \pi_{\theta_{old}}(y_k | x, y_{<k})$). However, dLLMs generate sequences non-autoregressively, meaning these token-level conditionals are either ill-defined or computationally prohibitive to obtain.

**Existing Approaches and Their Limitations:** Prior attempts to bridge this gap in dLLMs have resorted to heuristic approximations:
- **Mean-field surrogates (e.g., d1)**: These approximate token likelihoods by ignoring context, leading to inaccuracy.
- **Token-level ELBO contributions (e.g., UniGRPO, Coupled-GRPO)**: These decompose the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) into token-level contributions. While better aligned with dLLMs than mean-field, the ELBO is a sequence-level lower bound, and its individual token components lack a formal probabilistic interpretation as conditional likelihoods, introducing "unknown inconsistency" when used in token-level RL objectives.
- **Trajectory-level formulations (e.g., LLadou)**: These treat each denoising step as an action, optimizing over multi-step trajectories. While theoretically sound, they are computationally heavy, requiring sequential network evaluations for each timestep, making them infeasible for large-scale training.

This work steps into this gap by proposing a *principled* approach that fundamentally re-evaluates how RL should be applied to dLLMs, moving beyond heuristics and computational bottlenecks. It argues that the issue is not finding a *better token-level proxy*, but rather that the *token-level decomposition itself is ill-suited* for dLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a principled and empirically effective reinforcement learning framework specifically designed for diffusion large language models (dLLMs), thereby overcoming the fundamental challenges posed by their non-autoregressive generation mechanism.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Addressing the Fundamental Mismatch**: The primary motivation stems from the inherent incompatibility between traditional, token-level autoregressive (AR) RL objectives and the holistic, non-autoregressive sequence generation process of dLLMs. Mainstream RL algorithms for language models assume a left-to-right factorization of sequence likelihoods, which dLLMs do not provide.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Heuristic Proxies**: Existing attempts to adapt RL to dLLMs have relied on heuristic approximations for token-level likelihoods (e.g., mean-field approximations, token-level ELBO contributions). These proxies introduce inaccuracies, inconsistencies, and instability, limiting the effectiveness of RL in improving dLLMs. The authors aim to move beyond these ad-hoc solutions to a more theoretically sound approach.
3. **Enabling Stable and Efficient Large-Scale Training**: Previous trajectory-level RL formulations for dLLMs, while potentially principled, are computationally prohibitive due to the sequential nature of denoising steps. A key motivation is to design an RL framework that is both principled and computationally efficient, enabling stable training for large-scale dLLMs.
4. **Unlocking the Full Potential of dLLMs through RL**: Given the proven success of RL in enhancing AR LLMs for complex reasoning, coding, and planning tasks, the authors are motivated to unlock similar gains for dLLMs. They hypothesize that a properly formulated RL objective could significantly boost dLLM performance, especially on tasks requiring global consistency, where dLLMs' holistic generation might offer advantages.
5. **Establishing a New Paradigm for dLLM-RL**: Ultimately, the work aims to establish sequence-level optimization as the correct and most effective paradigm for applying RL to dLLMs, providing a robust foundation for future research in this area.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **ELBO-based Sequence-level Policy Optimization (ESPO)**, a novel RL framework specifically tailored for dLLMs, built upon a sequence-level perspective.

The core components of ESPO are:

1. **Sequence-Level Action Space**: Instead of treating each token generation as an independent action, ESPO conceptualizes the generation of an entire sequence ($y$) as a single, atomic action. This fundamental shift directly aligns the RL framework with the non-autoregressive nature of dLLMs, which produce a full sequence through iterative denoising. This bypasses the ill-defined token-level conditional probabilities.

2. **ELBO as a Tractable Sequence Log-Likelihood Proxy**: The exact log-likelihood ($\log \pi_\theta(y|x)$) for dLLMs is generally intractable. ESPO leverages the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), denoted as $L_\theta(y|x)$, as a tractable and empirically effective proxy for this intractable sequence log-likelihood. The ELBO provides a mathematically rigorous lower bound for the joint sequence log-likelihood. The authors specifically use a lower-variance variant of the ELBO (Eq. 3), which involves a discrete number of masked tokens ($l$).

3. **Stabilized Importance Ratio**: A direct application of the sequence-level importance ratio using the raw ELBO difference (i.e., $\exp(L_\theta(y^{(i)}|x) - L_{\theta_{old}}(y^{(i)}|x))$) leads to severe numerical instability due to the ELBO's magnitude scaling with sequence length. To counter this, ESPO incorporates a crucial stabilization technique:
 - **Per-token normalization of the log-ratio**: The log-ratio is normalized by the sequence length $L$, transforming the unstable raw difference into a stable, per-token scale: $\exp \left( \frac{1}{L} (L_\theta(y^{(i)}|x) - L_{\theta_{old}}(y^{(i)}|x)) \right)$. This allows for effective training even with long sequences.

4. **Robust KL-Divergence Estimation**: The GRPO objective includes a KL-divergence term ($D_{KL}(\pi_\theta, \pi_{ref})$) to regularize policy updates. The authors identify that common KL estimators like k3 (which involves exponentials of ELBO differences) and k1 (which has a zero gradient) are unstable or ineffective in the sequence-level dLLM context. ESPO addresses this by adopting the **k2 estimator**:
 - **Quadratic KL estimator (k2)**: The k2 estimator, defined as $\frac{1}{2} (L_\theta(y^{(i)}|x) - L_{ref}(y^{(i)}|x))^2$, is a simple quadratic function of the ELBO difference. This polynomial form avoids exponential terms, ensuring stable and well-behaved gradient signals, which is empirically shown to be critical for stable learning.

5. **Variance Reduction Techniques**: To further stabilize training, ESPO integrates two standard variance reduction techniques:
 - **Antithetic Sampling through Mask Sharing**: When computing ELBO differences (e.g., for the importance ratio or KL term), the same sampled timesteps and masked positions are shared between the Monte Carlo estimators of the current and old/reference policies. This introduces negative correlation, reducing variance.
 - **Coupled Sampling**: This involves constructing a complementary mask for each sampled mask, ensuring every token contributes to the learning signal and further stabilizing the objective.

**Ablation Studies for Validation**: The methodological choices are rigorously validated through extensive ablation studies:
- **Action Space and Likelihood Approximation**: Comparison of four variants (Token-level + Mean-field, Token-level + ELBO, Sequence-level + Mean-field, Sequence-level + ELBO) clearly demonstrates the superiority and stability of the Sequence-level + ELBO approach.
- **KL-Divergence Estimator**: Ablation comparing k1, k2, and k3 estimators empirically confirms k2 as the only choice that enables stable and efficient learning, with k1 collapsing and k3 stagnating due to instability.

In summary, ESPO fundamentally re-thinks RL for dLLMs by adopting a sequence-level perspective and leveraging the ELBO as a principled likelihood proxy, augmented with critical stabilization techniques and variance reduction, leading to a robust and effective framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on various benchmarks demonstrate the significant effectiveness and stability of ESPO.

**Ablation Studies (Validation of Design Choices):**
1. **Action Space and Likelihood Approximation (Sudoku Task, Fig. 1):**
 - **Mean-field is a poor proxy**: Both token-level and sequence-level approaches using mean-field likelihoods failed to learn effectively, confirming its fundamental misalignment with dLLM's conditional denoising.
 - **Token-level ELBO is unstable**: While initially promising, the token-level ELBO approach suffered from high instability and eventual collapse, validating the authors' hypothesis that breaking the ELBO's integrity across tokens introduces inconsistency.
 - **Sequence-level ELBO is superior**: ESPO (Sequence-level + ELBO) achieved the fastest, most stable learning, converging to the highest reward, strongly validating the core design of a sequence-level action space with a holistic ELBO proxy.
2. **KL-Divergence Estimator (Sudoku Task, Fig. 2 & 6):**
 - **k1 estimator (zero gradient)**: Showed initial progress but then collapsed catastrophically, with uncontrolled gradient norms and negative KL estimates, indicating a complete loss of constraint.
 - **k3 estimator (unbiased for reverse-KL gradient, unstable exponential term)**: Stagnated at low rewards and exhibited extreme, infrequent spikes in KL estimates and gradient norms, confirming its instability for sequence-level optimization.
 - **k2 estimator (stable quadratic form)**: Enabled stable and efficient learning, consistently improving and converging to the highest reward, with smooth and well-behaved KL estimates and gradient norms. This empirically confirms k2 as the most robust choice.
 - **Ablation with token-level baseline (Table 5)**: Applying the k2 estimator to the token-level baseline (d1) yielded comparable low performance to its original k3 version, confirming that the KL estimator alone does not account for ESPO's superior performance; the sequence-level approach is key.

**Benchmark Results (Tables 1 & 2):**
ESPO was evaluated on two dLLM backbones (LLaDA-8B-Instruct and Dream-7B-Instruct) across three categories of tasks: mathematics (GSM8K, MATH), coding (HumanEval, MBPP, with EvalPlus variants), and planning (Countdown, Sudoku).

1. **Dominant Performance on Planning Tasks**: ESPO achieved the most dramatic improvements on planning tasks, which require global consistency:
 - **Countdown**: Outperformed strongest baselines by **20-40 absolute points** (e.g., LLaDA: +60.9 to +63.3 points over base LLaDA model, Dream: +47.4 to +60.3 points).
 - **Sudoku**: Showed gains of **over 60 points** (e.g., LLaDA: +67.9 to +74.5 points over base LLaDA, Dream: +57.3 to +70.2 points).
 - This strongly validates the hypothesis that a sequence-level objective is superior for tasks requiring holistic coherence that token-level methods struggle to capture.

2. **Consistent Gains on Mathematics and Coding**:
 - **Mathematics (GSM8K, MATH)**: Achieved modest but consistent positive gains, surpassing all previous token-level dLLM-RL methods averaged over sequence lengths. For LLaDA, improvements ranged from +1.6 to +8.7 points, and for Dream, +1.0 to +3.8 points.
 - **Coding (HumanEval, MBPP)**: Consistently enhanced performance, averaging +2.3 to +7.6 points over the base LLaDA model, and even achieving competitive results compared to LLaDA-1.5 (a larger model trained on a private dataset).
 - These gains, despite performance ceilings from the base models' pre-existing knowledge, demonstrate the broad effectiveness of ESPO even on knowledge-intensive tasks.

**Training Dynamics (Fig. 3, 7, 8):**
- **Planning Tasks (Countdown, Sudoku)**: ESPO showed superior and faster convergence to near-optimal policies compared to d1 and wd1, which often stagnated at low rewards. The Sudoku dynamics revealed a sharp phase transition with a spike in gradient norm and KL divergence, indicating successful discovery of logical rules and a significant policy shift.
- **Mathematics Tasks (MATH, GSM8K)**: Training rewards for all methods were stable, but ESPO's test set performance suggests better generalization, indicating it avoids overfitting more effectively than baselines.

**Training Efficiency (Table 3):**
- Analysis of FLOPs showed that increasing the number of Monte Carlo (MC) samples for ELBO estimation only moderately increases total training cost (e.g., 47% increase from 1 to 4 MC samples), because the generation phase (K denoising steps) dominates computation. This is a significant advantage over methods where training time scales linearly with MC samples or denoising steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "PRINCIPLED RL FOR DIFFUSION LLMS EMERGES FROM A SEQUENCE-LEVEL PERSPECTIVE" carries significant implications for the development and application of advanced large language models, particularly in the realm of diffusion models.

1. **Pioneering Principled RL for dLLMs**: This paper provides the first principled and empirically validated framework for applying reinforcement learning to diffusion large language models. By identifying and directly addressing the fundamental incompatibility between autoregressive RL objectives and non-autoregressive dLLMs, ESPO moves beyond the heuristic approximations that characterized previous attempts. This establishes a robust theoretical and practical foundation for dLLM post-training.

2. **Unlocking dLLM Potential**: The dramatic performance gains, especially on planning and reasoning tasks requiring global consistency (e.g., 20-60+ points on Countdown and Sudoku), highlight that dLLMs, when properly fine-tuned with RL, can excel in domains where their holistic generation process offers distinct advantages. This makes dLLMs more competitive and versatile, potentially broadening their adoption for complex problem-solving.

3. **Improved Model Alignment and Control**: RL is crucial for aligning LLMs with specific objectives and user preferences. ESPO's ability to effectively apply RL to dLLMs means these models can now be more precisely steered towards desired behaviors, generating outputs that are not just fluent but also factually accurate, logically coherent, or task-compliant. This is particularly important for critical applications in coding, scientific reasoning, and autonomous planning.

4. **Advancement in RL Theory and Practice**: The insights into the instability of token-level ELBO approximations and the critical role of robust KL-divergence estimators (like k2) extend beyond dLLMs. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of policy optimization in challenging, non-autoregressive, and high-variance environments, which could inform RL research in other generative modeling contexts.

5. **Computational Efficiency for Scalability**: The analysis demonstrating that the generation phase dominates dLLM training costs, and that increasing Monte Carlo samples for ELBO estimation only moderately increases total FLOPs, is crucial for large-scale applications. This understanding allows researchers and practitioners to optimize training pipelines more effectively, making RL fine-tuning of dLLMs more feasible and accessible for larger models.

6. **Future Research Directions**: ESPO opens several avenues for future research. It provides a strong baseline for exploring more advanced RL techniques (e.g., different reward models, off-policy methods, multi-objective optimization) within the dLLM paradigm. It could also inspire new architectural designs for dLLMs that are even more amenable to sequence-level RL or lead to hybrid models combining the strengths of AR and diffusion approaches.

In conclusion, ESPO represents a significant step forward in the field of LLMs, providing a principled and highly effective method for post-training diffusion language models. Its impact will likely be seen in the development of more capable, robust, and controllable dLLMs across a wide range of challenging applications.
