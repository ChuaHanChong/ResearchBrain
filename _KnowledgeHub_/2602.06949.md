# DreamDojo: A Generalist Robot World Model from Large-Scale Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamDojo introduces a foundation world model that learns to simulate diverse, dexterous robot tasks by training on an unprecedented 44,711 hours of egocentric human video data. The model achieves real-time inference at 10.81 FPS and demonstrates a strong correlation (Pearson r=0.995) between simulated and real-world policy evaluation success rates.

## Problem

- Developing generalist robots is hindered by the difficulty of creating world models that can robustly simulate diverse, real-world action outcomes.
- Existing robot world models are limited by expensive and scarce robot-specific data, high-dimensional continuous action spaces, and a lack of explicit action labels in readily available human videos.
- Current video world models struggle to generalize to unseen objects, environments, or predict the consequences of counterfactual actions.

## Method

- Curated **DreamDojo**-HV, a large-scale dataset of 44,711 hours of egocentric human videos, to provide diverse physical interaction data.
- Introduced ==self-supervised continuous latent actions== to extract causal information from unlabeled human videos, making them usable for action-conditioned world modeling.
- Developed a foundation world model architecture incorporating relative actions, chunked action injection, and a ==temporal consistency loss==, followed by a ==distillation pipeline== for real-time inference.

## Results

- DreamDojo showed consistent improvements in simulation quality and generalization across novel evaluation sets by scaling human video data, outperforming baselines in human preference evaluations on out-of-distribution scenarios.
- A distillation pipeline enabled the model to run at **10.81 FPS** on a single H**100** GPU, achieving a nearly **4x** speedup with robust long-horizon consistency over 1-minute rollouts.
- The model proved effective for downstream robot tasks, correlating with real-world success rates (Pearson r=**0.995**) for policy evaluation and improving model-based planning success by up to **17**% (**2x**) for a fruit packing task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging an unprecedented scale of egocentric human video data for pretraining allows world models to learn diverse physics and generalize to open-world robot tasks, overcoming robot data scarcity.
> - Continuous latent actions derived in a self-supervised manner can effectively serve as embodiment-agnostic proxy actions, enabling the transfer of causal interaction knowledge from unlabeled human videos to robot control.
> - Architectural enhancements like relative actions and chunked action injection, combined with a temporal consistency loss and distillation, are essential for achieving precise action controllability and real-time inference in robot world models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

DreamDojo addresses a critical bottleneck in the development of generalist robots: the ability to robustly simulate the outcomes of actions in diverse, real-world environments. This capability, known as "world modeling," is widely recognized as a cornerstone for advanced AI agents, enabling planning, policy evaluation, and efficient learning.

Historically, world models have seen success in controlled environments like games (e.g., Atari) or autonomous driving simulations, where data collection is relatively straightforward, and action spaces are often discrete or lower-dimensional. However, extending these models to **dexterous robotics tasks** in the "open world" presents significant challenges:
- **Limited Robot Data:** Collecting diverse, high-quality robot interaction data is expensive and labor-intensive, leading to narrow data distributions that struggle to generalize to novel objects or environments.
- **High-Dimensional and Contact-Rich Actions:** Robot manipulation involves complex, continuous action spaces (e.g., joint torques, end-effector poses) and intricate contact physics, which are difficult to model accurately.
- **Lack of Action Labels:** While passive video data is abundant, explicit action labels (e.g., precise joint movements) for real-world interactions are scarce, hindering the learning of action-conditioned dynamics.
- **Generalization Gap:** Existing video world models often fail to predict the consequences of "counterfactual" actions or generalize to entirely unseen scenarios, limiting their utility beyond observed setups.

DreamDojo positions itself at the forefront of tackling these challenges by proposing a paradigm shift: leveraging **large-scale egocentric human videos** for pretraining. The core insight is that the underlying physics governing human interactions with objects is largely consistent with robot interactions, allowing for effective knowledge transfer despite the "embodiment gap." This contrasts with previous works that primarily rely on limited robot teleoperation data.

The work builds upon recent advancements in **video generation** and **foundation models**, particularly diffusion models (e.g., Cosmos-Predict2.5, WAN2.2 tokenizer). By adapting these powerful generative architectures, DreamDojo aims to create a "foundation world model" – a large, general-purpose model pre-trained on massive, diverse data, capable of adapting to specific downstream tasks with minimal fine-tuning. The introduction of "continuous latent actions" as a self-supervised proxy for actions is a key innovation for overcoming the data scarcity of explicit action labels, aligning with other research exploring latent action representations (e.g., AdaWorld, DexWM).

Furthermore, DreamDojo's focus on **real-time inference** through a distillation pipeline addresses the practical limitations of slow generative models, making it suitable for online applications like live teleoperation and model-based planning, areas where simulation speed is paramount. This positions DreamDojo as a crucial step towards truly generalist, embodied AI agents that can learn and operate effectively in complex, dynamic real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of DreamDojo are multifaceted, aiming to address critical gaps in the field of generalist robotics:

1. **Develop a Generalist Robot World Model:** The overarching goal is to create a "foundation world model" capable of understanding and simulating diverse interactions across a wide range of objects and environments for dexterous robot tasks. This contrasts with specialized models often confined to specific tasks or limited operational domains.
2. **Overcome Data Scarcity and Distribution Limitations in Robot Learning:** Traditional robot data collection is costly and yields narrow datasets, which restrict world models' ability to generalize. DreamDojo aims to bypass this by demonstrating that abundant, diverse human video data can effectively "pretrain" a world model, significantly expanding its understanding of physical interactions.
3. **Enable Effective Knowledge Transfer from Unlabeled Human Videos:** A major challenge with human videos is the lack of explicit, fine-grained action labels suitable for robot control. A key objective is to introduce a method – continuous latent actions – that can extract semantically meaningful "proxy actions" in a self-supervised manner, ensuring causality between observations and actions is learned, thus making unlabeled videos valuable for action-conditioned world simulation.
4. **Achieve Precise Action Controllability for High-Dimensional Robot Actions:** Existing video world models often struggle with the high-dimensional, continuous action spaces and contact-rich nature of robot manipulation. DreamDojo aims to develop architectural and training enhancements (e.g., relative actions, chunked action injection, temporal consistency loss) to improve the model's ability to follow precise robot action commands.
5. **Facilitate Real-time, Long-Horizon Simulation:** For practical applications like live teleoperation and online planning, the world model must operate at real-time speeds and maintain consistency over extended periods. An objective is to develop a distillation pipeline that can accelerate the model's inference speed and improve its long-horizon temporal stability, transforming a powerful but slow generative model into a practical, interactive tool.
6. **Unlock Practical Downstream Applications for Robot Learning:** Ultimately, DreamDojo is motivated by the desire to accelerate robot policy development. By providing a reliable, generalizable, and real-time world model, it seeks to enable applications such as:
 - **Live Teleoperation:** Allowing humans to directly control virtual robots in real-time, aiding in data collection and intuitive interaction.
 - **Policy Evaluation:** Serving as a cost-effective and scalable simulator for testing and comparing robot policies without requiring extensive real-world deployment.
 - **Model-based Planning:** Allowing robots to predict the outcomes of potential actions and plan optimal sequences to achieve goals, especially in unforeseen situations.

In essence, DreamDojo's motivation lies in pushing the boundaries of what robot world models can achieve, transforming them from limited in-distribution simulators into versatile, general-purpose tools that learn from the vastness of human experience to drive the next generation of intelligent robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamDojo's methodology is structured around three core phases: pretraining on human videos, post-training on target robot data, and distillation for real-time performance. It innovatively combines large-scale data curation, self-supervised action representation learning, and architectural enhancements.

#### 3.1. DreamDojo-HV Dataset: Scaling and Diversity

The foundation of DreamDojo is its expansive and diverse dataset, a deliberate departure from limited robot-only datasets. The authors curate a mixture of three egocentric human video datasets for pretraining:
- **In-lab:** A controlled, in-house dataset collected with precise hand pose tracking (Manus gloves, Vive Ultimate Tracker), enabling direct retargeting to robot actions. Used for validating core designs and introducing new objects/verbs.
- **EgoDex (Hoque et al., 2025):** A public dexterous human manipulation dataset with high-precision 3D hand/finger poses captured by Apple Vision Pro, enriching object variety.
- **DreamDojo-HV:** The centerpiece, a large-scale, in-house dataset collected via crowdsourcing. It comprises **43,827 hours** of egocentric videos, encompassing a vast spectrum of loco-manipulation skills and highly diverse environments (household, industrial, retail, etc.). Each episode is annotated with text descriptions.

Collectively, this data mixture totals **44,711 hours**, making it the largest human interaction dataset for world model pretraining, with over 9,869 unique scenes, 6,015 unique tasks, and 43,237 unique objects. This unprecedented scale and diversity are crucial for learning robust physics and dynamics that generalize to open-world scenarios.

#### 3.2. DreamDojo Foundation World Model Architecture

DreamDojo is built upon the **Cosmos-Predict2.5** model (Ali et al., 2025), a latent video diffusion model. The core architecture processes video latents (from WAN2.2 tokenizer) and uses text and conditional frame inputs. For DreamDojo, the authors introduce two key improvements to enhance action controllability, especially for high-dimensional robot actions:
- **Relative Actions:** Instead of absolute robot joint poses, actions are represented as *relative changes* from the pose at the beginning of each latent frame (every 4 timesteps). This reduces modeling complexity by focusing on changes, making generalization to continuous and compositional actions more effective.
- **Chunked Action Injection:** To address causality confusion, actions are not injected as a global condition. Instead, 4 consecutive actions (a "chunk") are sent to their corresponding latent frame. This strong temporal prior improves learning efficiency and controllability by explicitly linking actions to their immediate visual consequences.

#### 3.3. Pretraining from Human Videos with Latent Actions

The critical step to leverage the vast, *unlabeled* human video data is the introduction of **continuous latent actions** as unified proxy actions.
- **Latent Action Model:** A VAE-based spatiotemporal Transformer (Bruce et al., 2024) is trained in a self-supervised manner. Its encoder takes two consecutive frames (ft, ft+1) and compresses the action information into a low-dimensional embedding (â_t) via an information bottleneck. The decoder uses this embedding and ft to reconstruct ft+1. This compact embedding captures critical motions between frames and is shown to transfer across embodiments (human to robot).
- **Action Conditioning:** During pretraining, these extracted latent actions are projected and injected into the world model's DiT blocks alongside timestep embeddings, allowing the model to learn action-conditioned video prediction even from unlabeled human data.
- **Training Objective:** The standard flow matching loss (from Cosmos-Predict2.5) is augmented with a **temporal consistency loss**. This additional term minimizes the prediction error of *temporal transitions* between predicted velocities, providing a more direct signal for learning object dynamics and action following, leading to improved object completeness and reduced artifacts. The final loss is `L_final = L_flow + λ L_temporal`.

#### 3.4. Post-Training on Target Robots

After pretraining on human videos, DreamDojo is adapted to specific robot embodiments (e.g., GR-1, AgiBot) with a small, in-distribution target robot dataset.
- The action conditioning layer (action MLP) is reinitialized and fine-tuned, along with all other pretrained weights. This allows the model to learn the specific action space of the target robot while retaining the rich physical knowledge acquired during human video pretraining.

#### 3.5. Distillation for Real-time Performance

To enable real-time, autoregressive inference, a distillation pipeline based on **Self Forcing** (Huang et al., 2025) is employed:
- **Teacher-Student Setup:** The pretrained DreamDojo acts as the "teacher" (bidirectional attention, many denoising steps), and a "student" model (same architecture, but with causal attention and fewer denoising steps, e.g., 4) is trained.
- **Warmup Stage:** The student is trained to regress to ODE solutions generated by the teacher, with its context provided by teacher-generated latents.
- **Distillation Stage:** The student's context is constructed from its *own* previously generated latents (autoregressive inference simulation). A distribution matching loss (KL divergence based) is used, with a gradient estimated from real (teacher) and fake (student) distributions. To improve long-horizon consistency, the student generates more frames (N' > N) than the teacher's horizon, with loss computed on a sliding window.

This distillation process significantly boosts inference speed (from ~2.72 FPS to ~10.81 FPS) while maintaining predictive quality and enhancing context consistency, crucial for live applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DreamDojo's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating its capabilities in data leverage, generalization, and practical applications.

#### 4.1. Effects of Different Action Conditions (Latent Actions)

- **Superiority of Latent Actions:** Experiments comparing different pretraining strategies show that introducing continuous latent actions significantly improves simulation quality (higher PSNR, SSIM, lower LPIPS) on novel In-lab and EgoDex evaluation sets, narrowing the gap to ideal scenarios with ground-truth action labels (retargeted actions or MANO poses). Action-free pretraining yields only marginal benefits, while latent action conditioning provides robust knowledge transfer of causality and physics from unlabeled human videos.

#### 4.2. Effects of Different Data Mixtures (Scale and Diversity)

- **Consistent Improvement with Data Scale:** Ablation studies on data mixtures demonstrate that progressively adding more human datasets (In-lab + EgoDex, then + DreamDojo-HV) consistently enhances model performance across all evaluation benchmarks, including In-lab Eval, EgoDex Eval, DreamDojo-HV Eval (unseen object interactions), and Counterfactual Eval (counterfactual actions). This highlights the direct benefits of increased data scale and diversity for learning physics and action controllability.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Unseen Scenarios (Human Preference Evaluation)

- **Strong OOD Generalization:** Human preference evaluations on EgoDex-novel Eval and DreamDojo-HV-novel Eval (scenarios with edited backgrounds to mimic OOD environments) show that both DreamDojo-2B and DreamDojo-14B significantly outperform the Cosmos-Predict2.5 baseline in terms of "physics correctness" and "action following." The larger DreamDojo-14B model exhibits the most competitive performance, confirming the model's ability to generalize to entirely unseen environmental changes.

#### 4.4. Ablations of Design Choices (Architecture and Loss)

- **Impact of Architectural and Loss Designs:** Experiments on GR-1 validation and Counterfactual Eval sets demonstrate the cumulative benefits of DreamDojo's specific design choices:
 - **Relative Actions** and **Chunked Action Injection** significantly improve simulation quality (e.g., higher PSNR, SSIM, lower LPIPS), underscoring their importance for precise action controllability.
 - The proposed **Temporal Consistency Loss** further enhances performance across both expert demonstrations and counterfactual trajectories, improving action following and object modeling.

#### 4.5. Benefits of Distillation for Real-Time Interaction

- **Real-time Performance:** The distillation pipeline successfully converts the DreamDojo-2B teacher model into a student model capable of running at **10.81 FPS** on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU, a nearly 4x speedup over the teacher's 2.72 FPS.
- **Long-Horizon Consistency:** The student model maintains performance close to that of the teacher over 1-minute long-horizon rollouts, with only minor degradation. Its autoregressive causal prediction, coupled with a larger context window (12 frames vs. 1 for the teacher), provides superior robustness to occlusions and camera shifts, enhancing long-term consistency.
- **Preserved Generalization:** Distillation from a human-video-pretrained teacher (DreamDojo) leads to significantly better generalization in the student model compared to distillation from an unpretrained teacher, indicating that the benefits of pretraining are preserved.

#### 4.6. Downstream Applications

DreamDojo's utility is demonstrated across several critical applications:
- **Policy Evaluation:** For an AgiBot fruit packing task, DreamDojo's simulated success rates show a **strong linear correlation (Pearson r=0.995)** with real-world success rates and maintain a **highly consistent ranking (MMRV=0.003)** across different policy checkpoints. This establishes DreamDojo as a reliable and cost-effective simulator for evaluating robot policies.
- **Model-based Planning:** By enabling the model to predict future outcomes of action proposals, DreamDojo facilitates online planning. It demonstrates significant improvements (up to a **17% / 2x increase in success rate**) for a fruit packing task, allowing robots to adaptively select optimal actions, especially for policy groups with higher performance variance.
- **Live Teleoperation:** The distilled DreamDojo-2B model successfully enables real-time teleoperation of a virtual G1 robot using a PICO VR controller, providing an interactive and intuitive control interface.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamDojo represents a significant leap forward in the field of embodied AI and robotics, particularly in the domain of world modeling. Its core significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering a Generalist Robot World Model from Human Videos:** DreamDojo introduces the first foundation world model specifically for *open-world dexterous robot tasks* that effectively leverages *large-scale human egocentric videos*. This shifts the paradigm from costly, robot-specific data collection to harnessing the vast, naturally occurring data of human interactions, opening a scalable path toward generalist robot capabilities.
2. **Bridging the Embodiment Gap with Latent Actions:** The novel use of self-supervised continuous latent actions successfully addresses the challenge of transferring interaction knowledge from human videos (without explicit action labels) to robot control. This approach provides a unified, embodiment-agnostic representation of actions, unlocking the potential of internet-scale video for robot learning.
3. **Enabling Scalable and Realistic Robot Simulation:** By acquiring a comprehensive understanding of physics and diverse interactions from 44k hours of human videos, DreamDojo dramatically improves the realism and generalizability of robot simulations. This allows for accurate predictions in out-of-distribution scenarios and for counterfactual actions, which is crucial for robust robot development.
4. **Accelerating Robot Policy Development through Practical Applications:** The ability to simulate reliably and in real-time offers direct, tangible benefits for robotics:
 - **Faster Iteration:** Developers can rapidly evaluate and refine robot policies in a high-fidelity simulator, significantly reducing the reliance on slow and expensive real-world testing.
 - **Enhanced Safety:** Risky or exploratory behaviors can be tested in simulation first, improving the safety of real-world robot deployment.
 - **Robust Control:** Model-based planning capabilities allow robots to anticipate outcomes and adjust actions dynamically, leading to more resilient and intelligent control in complex environments.
 - **Intuitive Interaction:** Real-time teleoperation provides a natural interface for human-robot collaboration and data generation.
5. **Setting a New Benchmark for World Model Architectures:** The architectural innovations, such as relative action representation, chunked action injection, and the temporal consistency loss, contribute to a more efficient and precise learning of action-conditioned dynamics, demonstrating pathways for building more controllable generative models.
6. **Advancing the Frontier of Foundational Models in Robotics:** DreamDojo's success reinforces the concept of foundation models in embodied AI, showing that large-scale pretraining on diverse, human-centric data can yield highly capable and adaptable models for robot control.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also highlight areas for future impact:
- **Uncommon Actions and Nuanced Failures:** DreamDojo currently struggles with simulating highly uncommon actions (e.g., slapping, fast waving) and precisely generating nuanced failure modes. Future work could focus on broadening action distribution coverage, potentially through policy rollouts or incorporating more stochastic data.
- **Absolute Success Rate Discrepancy:** The simulated success rates are often higher than real-world counterparts, indicating a need for more accurate modeling of real-world imperfections and failure conditions.
- **Inference Speed and Engineering Optimizations:** While distillation significantly improves speed, further engineering optimizations could push real-time performance even higher.
- **Single-View Limitation:** The current model does not naturally support multi-view simulation, which is critical for state-of-the-art robot policies.
- **Knowledge Retention in Fine-tuning:** Deeper exploration of fine-tuning strategies (e.g., LoRA, parameter merging) is needed to optimally retain pretrained knowledge when adapting to target embodiments.

Despite these limitations, DreamDojo makes a profound contribution by demonstrating a viable and scalable path toward general-purpose robot world models. Its ability to learn diverse physics and precise control from human experience, combined with real-time interactive capabilities, paves the way for a new generation of intelligent and adaptable robots.
