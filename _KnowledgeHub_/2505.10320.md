# J1: Incentivizing Thinking in LLM-as-a-Judge via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> FAIR at Meta developed J1, an online reinforcement learning framework that explicitly optimizes chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs acting as judges for other LLM responses. J1 models achieve state-of-the-art performance across five diverse benchmarks using only synthetic data, demonstrating robust generalizability and effectively mitigating positional bias.

## Problem

- Traditional LLM evaluation methods struggle with the complexity and diversity of LLM outputs, creating a bottleneck in AI development.
- Existing LLM-as-a-Judge methods primarily rely on prompting or offline fine-tuning, which do not directly optimize the reasoning process and often depend on costly human preference data.
- Challenges include data scarcity, the need for generalizability across verifiable and subjective tasks, and issues like positional bias in pairwise evaluations.

## Method

- J1 introduces an online Reinforcement Learning approach using ==GRPO== to directly optimize the ==chain-of-thought== reasoning process for LLM judges, incentivizing detailed thinking.
- It converts all judgment tasks into a unified, verifiable format by generating synthetic preference data, eliminating the need for human annotation.
- The framework employs ==multitask training==, combining pairwise comparisons and pointwise scoring (where pointwise judges are trained via distant supervision from pairwise data), and integrates consistency-based rewards to mitigate positional bias.

## Results

- J1 models, particularly J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask, achieved state-of-the-art performance across all **five** evaluated benchmarks (PPE, RewardBench, JudgeBench, RM-Bench, FollowBenchEval).
- J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask outperformed strong generative reward models like EvalPlanner (**+6.8%**) and DeepSeek-GRM-27B (**+17%**), and even surpassed the 671B DeepSeek-R1 and closed models like OpenAI's o1-mini and o3 on some benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that including both response orderings in training and applying verdict consistency rewards significantly improved position-consistent accuracy and reduced verdict flips in pairwise evaluations, while the multitask formulation offered superior performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly optimizing chain-of-thought reasoning in LLM judges via online RL with verifiable rewards substantially improves evaluation quality and robustness.
> - High-performing, generalist LLM judges can be trained solely on large-scale synthetic preference data, significantly reducing reliance on expensive human annotations.
> - A multitask approach, synergistically combining pairwise and pointwise evaluation paradigms and addressing positional bias through consistency rewards, yields superior and more reliable performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of large language models has brought into sharp focus the challenge of evaluating their performance effectively. Traditional evaluation methods, often relying on fixed benchmarks or human annotation, struggle to keep pace with the complexity and diversity of LLM outputs. This bottleneck in evaluation quality hinders further progress in AI development.

**LLM-as-a-Judge** models have emerged as a promising solution. Instead of human evaluators, powerful LLMs are leveraged to judge the quality of other LLMs' responses. A crucial aspect of these LLM judges is their ability to perform **chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning** – generating explicit, step-by-step thoughts before arriving at a judgment. This reasoning makes the evaluation process more transparent and often more accurate, mimicking how human experts approach complex tasks.

Initially, CoT reasoning in LLM judges was invoked primarily through sophisticated prompting techniques (Zheng et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2024; Saha et al., 2024). Subsequent research explored iterative fine-tuning and direct preference optimization (DPO) methods to enhance these reasoning steps (Mahan et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024d; Yu et al., 2025a; Saha et al., 2025). However, these methods primarily rely on offline training with pre-collected preference data.

"J1: Incentivizing Thinking in LLM-as-a-Judge via Reinforcement Learning" directly addresses the need for more effective optimization of the reasoning process. It positions itself as a significant advancement by moving towards **online Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, which allows for direct optimization of evaluation quality based on verifiable rewards. This approach draws inspiration from recent successes in improving LLM reasoning through RL with verifiable rewards (Guo et al., 2025a; Team et al., 2025; OpenAI, 2025).

Furthermore, the paper tackles several persistent challenges in the LLM evaluation landscape:
- **Data Scarcity:** Reliance on costly human annotations limits the scale and diversity of training data for judges. J1 aims to overcome this by using exclusively synthetic data.
- **Generalizability:** Creating a single judge capable of handling both verifiable (e.g., math) and subjective, non-verifiable (e.g., creative writing) tasks is a major goal.
- **Positional Bias:** A known issue in pairwise evaluations, where the order of responses can influence the judgment, is explicitly addressed.
- **Flexibility:** The ability to perform both pointwise (scoring a single response) and pairwise (comparing two responses) evaluations within a unified framework is highly desirable.

By introducing an RL-based framework to explicitly incentivize and optimize "thinking" in LLM judges, J1 aims to set a new state-of-the-art in LLM evaluation, contributing to more reliable and efficient development of future AI models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of J1 is to develop a robust and high-performing LLM-as-a-Judge framework that effectively "thinks" – generating detailed chain-of-thought reasoning – before making evaluative decisions. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and translated into key aspects of the J1 framework:

1. **Optimize Reasoning for Evaluation:** The primary motivation is to move beyond mere prompting or offline fine-tuning of LLM judges and directly optimize their chain-of-thought reasoning process. The authors hypothesize that online Reinforcement Learning (RL) can more effectively teach LLMs to think critically about evaluation tasks, leading to better judgment quality.

2. **Achieve Unified Verifiable Training:** A significant challenge is training a generalist judge across diverse domains, especially when many tasks (like judging creative writing) are subjective and typically non-verifiable. The objective is to convert *all* judgment tasks, regardless of their inherent verifiability, into a unified format where verifiable rewards can be assigned. This enables a single, scalable training recipe for a generalist judge using RL.

3. **Leverage Synthetic Data for Scalability:** Human annotation for preference data is expensive and time-consuming, limiting the scale of training. J1 is motivated to rely solely on synthetically generated preference pairs, eliminating the need for costly human feedback and enabling large-scale, diverse training.

4. **Mitigate Positional Bias and Enhance Consistency:** Positional bias is a well-known problem in pairwise LLM evaluation, where the order of responses can sway the judge's decision. An objective of J1 is to explicitly address this bias through consistency-based rewards and by exploring novel training formulations like pointwise judges derived from pairwise supervision.

5. **Develop a Multitask and Flexible Judge:** The ideal LLM judge should be versatile, capable of performing both pairwise comparisons and pointwise scoring. The goal is to unify these paradigms into a single multitask model that benefits from both comparative context (pairwise) and inherent consistency (pointwise).

6. **Attain State-of-the-Art Performance:** Ultimately, the motivation is to demonstrate that this RL-based, thinking-judge framework can achieve superior performance compared to existing LLM judges and reward models across a wide range of benchmarks, even when using smaller model sizes and less training data than some baselines. This would validate the efficacy of J1's novel training approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The J1 framework introduces an online Reinforcement Learning approach to train LLMs as judges, emphasizing chain-of-thought reasoning. It consists of three primary methodological pillars: Unified Verifiable Training, Reasoning-Optimized Training, and Multitask & Bias-Aware Judging.

#### 3.1. Unified Verifiable Training

The cornerstone of J1's approach is converting all judgment tasks, irrespective of their intrinsic verifiability, into a format amenable to RL with verifiable rewards.

- **Synthetic Data Generation:** J1 leverages a dataset of 22K synthetic preference pairs, comprising 17K from WildChat (user prompts) and 5K from MATH (mathematical problems). This removes reliance on expensive human annotations.
 - For **WildChat**, rejected responses are created by first generating a "noisy" variant of the original instruction and then producing a good response to this noisy instruction, making it a poor response to the original instruction. This creates a verifiable "better/worse" pair.
 - For **MATH**, rejected responses are LLM generations that do not lead to the gold answer.
 - This strategy effectively transforms subjective evaluation tasks into verifiable ones (i.e., predicting the objectively "better" response as defined by the generation process), allowing the use of RL.
- **Bias Mitigation in Data:** To combat positional bias in pairwise evaluations, training data is augmented to include both orderings of response pairs ((x, a, b) and (x, b, a)). Training batches are constructed to be "position-agnostic," processing both orderings within the same batch.

#### 3.2. Reward Modeling

J1 employs a simple yet effective rule-based reward system to optimize for judgment accuracy and consistency during RL training.

- **Verdict Correctness:** The primary reward is binary: +1 for a correct verdict (correctly identifying the preferred response), 0 otherwise.
- **Verdict Consistency:** To explicitly address positional bias, a consistency reward is introduced. A +1 reward is granted only if the model produces the *correct* verdict for *both* input orderings of a given response pair ((x, a, b) and (x, b, a)). This encourages the model to be robust to response order.
- Format-based rewards (e.g., enforcing "<think>" tags) were explored but found to offer no significant performance benefit, suggesting that the primary rewards sufficiently guide the model.

#### 3.3. J1 Formulations and Training

J1 utilizes GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), an online reinforcement learning algorithm, to jointly optimize the generation of thought tokens and final judgments. Several judge formulations are explored:

- **Pairwise J1 with Verdict (PaV):** This is the primary formulation, where the judge takes an instruction (x) and two responses (a, b), generates intermediate thought tokens (t), and outputs a final verdict (y) indicating the preferred response. The reward is based on the correctness of this verdict.
- **Pairwise J1 with Scores (PaS):** Instead of a binary verdict, this variant generates real-valued scores (s_a, s_b) for responses a and b, respectively. The verdict is implicitly derived from the higher score. Rewards are binary, based on whether predicted scores align with the gold verdict.
- **Pairwise J1 with Scores&Verdict (PaVS):** This formulation generates both scores and a direct verdict. The scores are treated as latent variables aiding the verdict, and the reward is computed solely based on the verdict.
- **Pointwise J1 (PoS):** This novel variant takes a single instruction (x) and response (a) as input and outputs a quality score (s). Crucially, J1 trains this pointwise judge via *distant supervision* from the same pairwise training data used for other variants. Each preference pair (chosen/rejected) is split into two samples, and the model is rewarded if its individual scores for the chosen and rejected responses are consistent with the original pairwise gold verdict (i.e., chosen > rejected). This allows for an inherently position-consistent judge without direct pointwise human labels.
- **Multitask Pairwise & Pointwise J1 (MT):** This is the culmination of the formulations, jointly training a single model on both pairwise and pointwise data. This aims to combine the strengths of both evaluation paradigms (comparative context from pairwise, inherent consistency from pointwise) into a unified, generalist judge.

**Training Details:**
- J1 models are trained on top of Llama-3.1-Instruct and Qwen3 base models, at scales of 8B, 32B, and 70B.
- The policy actor generates 5 rollouts per prompt with ancestral sampling (temperature 1.0).
- Hyperparameters include specific learning rates and a KL coefficient (0.01 for 8B, 0 for 70B to encourage exploration).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

J1 demonstrates compelling performance and novel insights into LLM-as-a-Judge models, particularly through its RL-based training and multitask approach.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance Across Benchmarks

- **Overall Superiority:** J1 models, especially J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask, achieve state-of-the-art performance across all five evaluated benchmarks: PPE (Preference Proxy Evaluations), RewardBench, JudgeBench, RM-Bench, and FollowBenchEval.
- **Outperformance of Strong Baselines:**
 - **Generative Reward Models:** J1-Qwen3-32B-MultiTask significantly outperforms EvalPlanner (trained on the same data but with DPO) by 6.8% and DeepSeek-GRM-27B by 17% (the latter trained on significantly more data).
 - **Open-weight Reasoning Models:** J1-Llama models consistently outperform DeepSeek-R1-Distilled-Llama across all benchmarks. Impressively, J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask also surpasses the much larger 671B DeepSeek-R1 and even closed models like OpenAI's o1-mini and o3 on some benchmarks, despite being a 32B model trained *only* on synthetic data.
 - **Scalar Reward Models:** J1-Llama-8B is competitive with or outperforms larger scalar RMs like Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B.
- **Generalizability:** J1 exhibits strong performance across diverse tasks (verifiable and non-verifiable) and different categories (chat, reasoning, safety, math), suggesting it functions as a robust generalist judge.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Multitask and Bias-Aware Design

- **Multitask Advantage:** The J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask model, which combines pairwise and pointwise training, emerges as the best performer, outperforming separately trained pairwise and pointwise judges. This highlights the synergy between leveraging comparative context (pairwise) and inherent consistency (pointwise).
- **Bias Mitigation:**
 - When measured by *position-consistent accuracy* (correct verdict for both response orderings), Pointwise-J1 models generally outperform Pairwise-J1 models, confirming the inherent consistency advantage of pointwise evaluation.
 - However, Pairwise-J1 models often achieve higher overall accuracy on a random ordering, benefiting from direct comparison.
 - Ablations show that including both response orderings in training data and applying verdict consistency rewards significantly improves the position-consistent accuracy of Pairwise-J1 and reduces verdict flips.

#### 4.3. Score Distribution and Test-time Scaling

- **Score Distribution:** The multitask J1 model, when used in a pairwise setting, exhibits a sparser score distribution and a larger difference between scores of chosen and rejected responses compared to its pointwise usage. This is attributed to the direct comparative training in the pairwise setup.
- **Test-time Scaling:** Employing self-consistency (majority vote over multiple verdicts) or averaging multiple pointwise scores at test time further enhances J1's performance, increasing position-consistent accuracy and reducing tie rates for both pairwise and pointwise models across different scales.

#### 4.4. Ablations and Qualitative Analysis

- **Reward Schemes:** The simplest reward scheme (positive binary reward for correct verdicts) performs best; adding negative rewards or format rewards does not yield noticeable improvements.
- **Seed Prompts:** J1 is robust to variations in the "thinking" seed prompt, performing comparably with both a generic thinking prompt and an "EvalPlanner-style" prompt that includes explicit planning steps. The simpler prompt can even elicit richer reasoning.
- **Qualitative Insights:** Analysis of J1's thought traces reveals sophisticated evaluation strategies:
 - **Dynamic Criteria Generation:** The model outlines evaluation criteria specific to the prompt.
 - **Reference Answer Creation:** J1 often self-generates a reference answer (especially for verifiable tasks) to use as a baseline.
 - **Iterative Self-Correction:** It critically re-evaluates initial assessments.
 - **Detailed Feedback:** J1 provides precise feedback on why a response is good or bad, often pointing out specific errors (e.g., calculation mistakes in math problems).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The J1 framework represents a significant step forward in the field of LLM evaluation, with far-reaching implications:

1. **Elevated Evaluation Quality:** By directly incentivizing and optimizing chain-of-thought reasoning through online RL, J1 produces LLM judges that are more accurate, robust, and capable of nuanced evaluations. This directly addresses the "bottleneck" in AI progress caused by insufficient evaluation capabilities.

2. **Reduced Reliance on Human Annotation:** The successful demonstration of training state-of-the-art LLM judges solely on synthetic data is a critical achievement. It dramatically reduces the cost and time associated with acquiring human preference labels, making scalable and continuous improvement of evaluation models more feasible. This has broad implications for the development cycle of future LLMs.

3. **Enhanced LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** J1's ability to develop systematic evaluation strategies, including self-generated reference answers, iterative self-correction, and detailed feedback, showcases the power of RL in cultivating advanced reasoning in LLMs. This suggests that similar RL techniques could be applied to improve reasoning in LLMs for other complex tasks beyond judging.

4. **Open Science Contribution:** Built upon open-weight Llama and Qwen models, J1 contributes to the broader open-source AI community. The detailed methodology and reproducibility statement encourage further research and development in this area, fostering collaborative advancement in LLM evaluation.

5. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The ability of J1 to excel across both verifiable and non-verifiable tasks, as well as its effectiveness across different model scales (8B to 70B), highlights its generalist nature and robustness. This makes J1 a versatile tool applicable at various stages of LLM development, from pre-training and fine-tuning to final benchmark evaluation.

6. **Advanced Bias Mitigation:** J1's methodical approach to positional bias, through consistency rewards and the introduction of a pointwise judge trained on pairwise supervision, offers practical solutions to a long-standing challenge. The multitask formulation providing the best of both worlds (comparative context and inherent consistency) is particularly impactful for building balanced and fair evaluators.

In conclusion, J1 offers a powerful and efficient paradigm for training "thinking" LLM judges. Its innovative use of RL with verifiable rewards on synthetic data, coupled with its focus on bias mitigation and multitask capabilities, not only establishes new benchmarks for evaluation quality but also provides a blueprint for future advancements in LLM self-improvement and assessment.
