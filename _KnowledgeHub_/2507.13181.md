# Spectral Bellman Method: Unifying Representation and Exploration in RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Technion, Google Research, Google DeepMind, and Nvidia Research developed the Spectral Bellman Method (SBM), a framework that learns state-action representations inherently aligned with Bellman dynamics to unify value estimation and exploration in reinforcement learning. When integrated with DQN, SBM achieved an average Human Normalized Score of 2.23 on the Atari ALE benchmark, outperforming vanilla DQN (1.62), and notably improved performance on hard-exploration Atari games.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning approaches often treat representation learning and exploration as separate problems, limiting their synergistic potential.
- Directly learning state-action representations that minimize the Inherent Bellman Error (IBE) for Bellman compatibility typically involves computationally intractable min-max-min optimizations with general function approximators.
- Many representation learning methods in RL are not explicitly optimized for Bellman updates, which can lead to suboptimal Q-function estimation and less effective uncertainty-guided exploration.

## Method

- Introduced the **Spectral Bellman Method** (SBM), which leverages ==spectral properties== of the Bellman operator under a zero-IBE condition to derive a tractable objective for learning Bellman-aligned state-action representations.
- Formulated an SBM Loss that performs alternating gradient-based updates on features and Q-function parameters, incorporating moving average covariance matrices and an orthogonality regularizer to ensure stability and structural properties.
- Integrated the learned SBM representations with Thompson Sampling, using the feature covariance to sample exploratory Q-function parameters and guide efficient data collection, particularly in challenging exploration environments.

## Results

- SBM, integrated with DQN and Thompson Sampling, achieved a Human Normalized Score (HNS) of 2.23 on the full Atari ALE benchmark (vs. 1.62 for vanilla DQN) and **0.45** on the hard-exploration Atari games subset (vs. **0.24** for vanilla DQN).
- Augmenting the advanced R2D2 agent with SBM and Thompson Sampling resulted in an HNS of 3.53 on Atari ALE (vs. 3.21 for baseline R2D2) and **0.61** on hard-exploration games (vs. **0.40** for baseline R2D2).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the SBM's spectral objective yielded better features (2.23 HNS) compared to a naive Bellman Mean Squared Error loss (1.82 HNS), demonstrating the benefits of its robust regularization and alternating optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fundamental spectral relationship exists where, under the zero-IBE condition, the transformation of value functions by the Bellman operator is inherently linked to the covariance structure of the features.
> - This spectral connection enables the formulation of a tractable, gradient-based learning objective that approximates low-IBE representation learning, bypassing the computational complexity of direct IBE minimization.
> - Learning representations across a distribution of value functions, rather than solely for the current policy, fosters more robust feature learning that significantly enhances structured exploration capabilities.
