# Spectral Bellman Method: Unifying Representation and Exploration in RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Technion, Google Research, Google DeepMind, and Nvidia Research developed the Spectral Bellman Method (SBM), a framework that learns state-action representations inherently aligned with Bellman dynamics to unify value estimation and exploration in reinforcement learning. When integrated with DQN, SBM achieved an average Human Normalized Score of 2.23 on the Atari ALE benchmark, outperforming vanilla DQN (1.62), and notably improved performance on hard-exploration Atari games.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning approaches often treat representation learning and exploration as separate problems, limiting their synergistic potential.
- Directly learning state-action representations that minimize the Inherent Bellman Error (IBE) for Bellman compatibility typically involves computationally intractable min-max-min optimizations with general function approximators.
- Many representation learning methods in RL are not explicitly optimized for Bellman updates, which can lead to suboptimal Q-function estimation and less effective uncertainty-guided exploration.

## Method

- Introduced the **Spectral Bellman Method** (SBM), which leverages ==spectral properties== of the Bellman operator under a zero-IBE condition to derive a tractable objective for learning Bellman-aligned state-action representations.
- Formulated an SBM Loss that performs alternating gradient-based updates on features and Q-function parameters, incorporating moving average covariance matrices and an orthogonality regularizer to ensure stability and structural properties.
- Integrated the learned SBM representations with Thompson Sampling, using the feature covariance to sample exploratory Q-function parameters and guide efficient data collection, particularly in challenging exploration environments.

## Results

- SBM, integrated with DQN and Thompson Sampling, achieved a Human Normalized Score (HNS) of 2.23 on the full Atari ALE benchmark (vs. 1.62 for vanilla DQN) and **0.45** on the hard-exploration Atari games subset (vs. **0.24** for vanilla DQN).
- Augmenting the advanced R2D2 agent with SBM and Thompson Sampling resulted in an HNS of 3.53 on Atari ALE (vs. 3.21 for baseline R2D2) and **0.61** on hard-exploration games (vs. **0.40** for baseline R2D2).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the SBM's spectral objective yielded better features (2.23 HNS) compared to a naive Bellman Mean Squared Error loss (1.82 HNS), demonstrating the benefits of its robust regularization and alternating optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fundamental spectral relationship exists where, under the zero-IBE condition, the transformation of value functions by the Bellman operator is inherently linked to the covariance structure of the features.
> - This spectral connection enables the formulation of a tractable, gradient-based learning objective that approximates low-IBE representation learning, bypassing the computational complexity of direct IBE minimization.
> - Learning representations across a distribution of value functions, rather than solely for the current policy, fosters more robust feature learning that significantly enhances structured exploration capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) faces challenges in two interconnected areas: learning effective state-action representations and conducting efficient exploration, particularly in complex and high-dimensional environments. Existing research often addresses these as separate problems. Representation learning in RL has explored various auxiliary objectives, such as contrastive learning (e.g., Laskin et al., 2020), autoencoders (e.g., Kingma et al., 2019), and Laplace-based methods like Successor Features (e.g., Mahadevan, 2005; Barreto et al., 2017). While these methods can yield generalizable features, some are not explicitly optimized for Bellman compatibility or task-specific performance in value-based RL. Directly learning linear representations for the optimal Q-function can also be sample-inefficient under model misspecification (e.g., Du et al., 2019).

Another line of research focuses on the Inherent Bellman Error (IBE) (Zanette et al., 2020a), which quantifies how well a function space can represent Bellman updates. A low IBE is desirable as it indicates that the function space is nearly closed under the Bellman operator, generalizing concepts from Linear MDPs (Jin et al., 2020). However, directly discovering features that satisfy a low-IBE condition, particularly with general function approximators, typically involves complex and computationally intensive min-max-min optimizations (e.g., Modi et al., 2024), impeding practical application.

Exploration strategies in RL range from count-based methods (e.g., Bellemare et al., 2016) and intrinsic motivation (e.g., Pathak et al., 2017) to uncertainty-based approaches like Thompson Sampling (TS) (e.g., Osband et al., 2013). TS is particularly effective in settings with linear function approximation or low-IBE representations (e.g., Azizzadenesheli et al., 2018; Zanette et al., 2020b), as it leverages uncertainty in value estimates to guide data collection.

This work contributes to the intersection of these areas by proposing a framework that directly unifies representation learning and exploration. It aims to learn representations that are inherently Bellman-aligned, making them suitable for value estimation and providing a structure for efficient uncertainty-guided exploration. The method differs from prior IBE-based approaches by offering a tractable objective and integrates with existing value-based RL algorithms like DQN and R2D2, building on the theoretical underpinnings of low-IBE while addressing practical implementation challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to introduce a novel framework, the Spectral Bellman Method (SBM), for learning state-action representations that inherently unify efficient value estimation and strategic exploration in reinforcement learning. This objective is motivated by several key challenges in current RL practices:

1. **Misalignment of Representations with RL Task Objectives**: Many existing representation learning methods are derived from model-learning or auxiliary tasks, which may not directly align with the fundamental structure of Bellman updates central to value-based RL. This misalignment can lead to suboptimal performance in learning Q-functions and subsequent policy optimization. The authors aim to derive representations directly from the Bellman structure.

2. **Tractability of Low-IBE Representation Learning**: The Inherent Bellman Error (IBE) quantifies the suitability of a feature space for value-based RL by measuring how well Bellman backups can be represented within that space. A zero-IBE condition implies that the function space is closed under the Bellman operator, which is theoretically desirable for learning and exploration. However, directly optimizing for a low-IBE condition, especially with general function approximators, typically involves computationally intractable min-max-min optimization problems (Modi et al., 2024). A key motivation is to make low-IBE representation learning tractable for practical applications.

3. **Synergistic Integration of Representation Learning and Exploration**: While effective representations are crucial for accurate value estimation, they also play a role in guiding exploration by capturing uncertainty. Many approaches treat representation learning and exploration as separate problems. The authors are motivated to develop a framework where features not only support accurate value estimation but also naturally facilitate structured exploration strategies, thereby achieving a deeper synergy between these two aspects of RL.

The core insight driving SBM is a fundamental spectral relationship: under the zero-IBE condition, the transformation of a distribution of value functions by the Bellman operator is intrinsically linked to the covariance structure of the features themselves. This connection suggests that features aligned with Bellman updates will possess a covariance structure that can be exploited for structured exploration. By leveraging this insight, the work aims to derive a new, theoretically-grounded objective for learning state-action features that capture this Bellman-aligned covariance, avoiding the complex optimization of direct IBE minimization. This objective is designed to enable structured exploration by aligning feature covariance with Bellman dynamics, thus improving performance in hard-exploration and long-horizon tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Spectral Bellman Method (SBM) is introduced as a framework to learn low-IBE representations and integrate them with exploration. The methodology is structured around leveraging spectral properties of the Bellman operator under the zero-IBE condition, formulating a tractable learning objective, and integrating it with Thompson Sampling for exploration.

#### 3.1. Background: Zero-IBE Condition and Exploration

The work considers a standard Markov Decision Process (MDP) and approximates the optimal Q-function Q∗(s,a) linearly with features ϕ(s,a) ∈ R^d as Qθ(s,a) = ϕ(s,a)^⊤θ. The Inherent Bellman Error (IBE), Iϕ, quantifies the minimum error in representing Bellman updates within the feature space Qϕ. A zero-IBE condition (Iϕ=0) signifies that the function space Qϕ is closed under the Bellman operator T. This condition generalizes Linear MDPs and is desirable because it guarantees the representability of Bellman backups. For exploration, a representation with low IBE facilitates efficient uncertainty reduction. The paper focuses on Thompson Sampling (TS), where exploration noise is added to the least-squares Q-function parameter estimate, often sampled from N(ˆθ_LS, σ_expΣ⁻¹), where Σ is the feature covariance matrix.

#### 3.2. Challenges with Direct IBE Minimization

Directly minimizing the IBE leads to complex min-max-min optimization problems. A simpler, but still challenging, approach involves minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between TQθ(s,a) and its representation in the learned feature space, ϕ(s,a)⊤˜θ(θ), across distributions of state-action pairs (ρ) and Q-function parameters (ν). This MSE objective (Equation 1) is difficult due to the non-linearity of T and does not inherently promote desirable structural properties in ϕ and ˜θ.

#### 3.3. Bellman Spectral Decomposition Under Zero IBE

To address these challenges, SBM leverages a spectral analysis of the Bellman operator. Under the zero-IBE condition, when the Bellman operator transforms a distribution of Q-functions, the resulting (weighted) feature matrix and post-Bellman parameter matrix correspond to the singular vectors of this transformation.
The approach introduces augmented feature and parameter mappings: ¯ϕ(s,a) := √ρ(s,a)ϕ(s,a) and ¯θ(θ) := ˜θ(θ)√ν(θ). It defines two covariance matrices: Λ₁ = Eρ[ϕ(s,a)ϕ(s,a)⊤] and Λ₂ = Eν[˜θ(θ)˜θ(θ)⊤]. A key insight (Theorem 1, Appendix A) states that under zero IBE and orthogonality assumptions, these covariance matrices are diagonal, and their values are related to the singular values of the augmented Bellman operator. This spectral relationship motivates an iterative approach analogous to the power iteration method for singular value decomposition. Proposition 1 (Section 3.2) provides identities that resemble power iteration update rules.

#### 3.4. Practical SBM Learning Objective

Algorithm 1 outlines a conceptual power iteration method, but directly solving linear equations at each step is prohibitive. To make this practical, the SBM Loss is formulated (Section 3.3). This loss consists of two main components, L₁(ϕ) and L₂(˜θ), and an orthogonality regularizer L_orth:
- **L₁(ϕ)** updates ϕ to align with Bellman-transformed Q-values using the current parameter covariance Λ₂ and parameters ˜θ.
- **L₂(˜θ)** updates ˜θ to best represent Bellman-transformed Q-values given current features ϕ and their covariance Λ₁.
- **L_orth** (Appendix E.1) enforces orthogonality on the features and parameters, which is a condition derived from the spectral decomposition theorem.

Proposition 2 (Section 3.3, Appendix C) formally demonstrates that minimizing the SBM Loss is equivalent to performing the updates of the power iteration algorithm. This allows for optimization using gradient-based methods, and in practice, an unconstrained, relaxed version of the SBM Loss is used.

**Comparison to Bellman MSE**: SBM Loss is distinct from the naive MSE objective. It enables alternating optimization, uses robust moving average covariance matrices (Λ₁, Λ₂) for regularization, and includes an explicit orthogonality regularizer, in contrast to the noisy, single-sample estimates in MSE.

#### 3.5. Efficient Exploration with Thompson Sampling

The learned SBM representation is then utilized for efficient exploration through Thompson Sampling. Before each rollout, exploratory Q-function parameters ˆθ_TS are sampled from a Gaussian distribution N(ˆθ_LS, σ_expΣ⁻¹), where ˆθ_LS is the least-squares estimate of the optimal parameter and Σ is the feature covariance matrix (Equation 5). This process guides data collection towards reducing uncertainty in the learned feature space.

#### 3.6. Q-Learning with SBM (Algorithm 2)

Algorithm 2 details the practical integration of SBM with Q-learning. It follows an iterative loop comprising three phases:
1. **Data Collection with TS Exploration**: An exploratory policy πˆθ_TS is generated using ˆθ_TS sampled from the posterior, and transitions are added to a replay buffer D. The covariance matrix Σ for TS is derived from the current features ϕ.
2. **Policy Optimization**: Standard Q-learning updates refine the Q-function parameters ˆθ by minimizing the Bellman error using mini-batches from D (Equation 6).
3. **Representation Learning**: The feature mapping ϕ is updated by minimizing the SBM Loss. Crucially, the distribution over parameters ν(θ) for SBM is centered around the newly updated Q-parameters (ν(θ) = N(ˆθ, σ²_repI)), focusing feature learning on parameters relevant to the current policy. The state-action distribution ρ(s,a) is implicitly defined by sampling from the replay buffer.

#### 3.7. Extensions and Implementation Details

The SBM framework extends to h-step Bellman operators (T^h). Proposition 3 (Appendix D.1) demonstrates that a low one-step IBE implies a low h-step IBE, allowing SBM Loss to use multi-step targets such as Retrace(λ) (Munos et al., 2016) in place of TQθ. Practical implementation details include using a residual network for ˜θ(θ), exponential moving averages for Λ₁ and Λ₂ for stability, norm clipping for feature outputs, and specific network architectures for DQN and R2D2 backbones (Appendix E.2).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate the effectiveness of integrating SBM into deep RL algorithms, specifically DQN and R2D2, on the Atari game suite. Performance is measured using Human Normalized Score (HNS) across 55 games, including a subset of hard-exploration games (Atari Explore).

#### 4.1. DQN Comparison

- **SBM vs. Vanilla DQN**: SBM significantly outperformed vanilla DQN in aggregated HNS across the full Atari ALE benchmark and, notably, on the Atari Explore subset. SBM combined with Thompson Sampling (TS) achieved an average HNS of 2.23 (DQN: 1.62) and 0.45 (DQN: 0.24) on Atari ALE and Atari Explore, respectively.
- **Impact of TS**: The combination of SBM representation learning with TS exploration yielded substantial performance gains, especially in hard-exploration tasks, suggesting that the learned representations effectively facilitate structured exploration.
- **Specific Game Performance**: SBM underperformed compared to DQN in some precision-control games (e.g., Breakout). The authors suggest this is a characteristic side-effect of optimistic exploration strategies like TS in environments with "narrow" optimal paths or instant-death conditions, where high-variance actions can lead to termination.

#### 4.2. R2D2 Comparison

- **SBM vs. Baseline R2D2**: Augmenting the R2D2 agent with SBM representation learning, using a Retrace operator target for multi-step Bellman updates, resulted in improved performance over the baseline R2D2. SBM+R2D2 with TS achieved an average HNS of 3.53 (R2D2: 3.21) on Atari ALE and 0.61 (R2D2: 0.40) on Atari Explore.
- **Significant Improvement on Hard Exploration**: The most notable improvement for R2D2 was observed when combined with TS, particularly on the Atari Explore subset, indicating the benefit of SBM's unified approach in challenging environments.

#### 4.3. Online PVN Comparison

- SBM with TS demonstrated better performance than Online Proto-Value Networks (PVN) (Farebrother et al., 2023), especially on the Atari Explore subset. Online PVN, adapted to the online learning setup, showed improvement over vanilla DQN, but SBM's approach of learning reward-dependent features aligned with the optimal Bellman operator appeared more effective for maximizing online rewards compared to PVN's reward-agnostic successor measure representations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study using a DQN backbone provided further insights (Table 2):
- **SBM vs. Naive MSE**: SBM-learned features yielded significantly better results (2.23 HNS on Atari ALE) compared to features learned by minimizing a naive MSE loss (1.82 HNS). This supports the advantage of the spectral objective over direct Bellman error minimization, emphasizing the robust regularization and alternating optimization aspects of SBM.
- **Distribution over Value Functions**: Limiting representation learning to only the current policy (effectively standard DQN objective with TS) showed degradation (1.73 HNS) compared to SBM or even the full Naive MSE loss. This highlights the importance of learning representations over a distribution of value functions (via ν(θ)) rather than solely fitting the current policy.
- **Orthogonality Regularization**: Performance without the orthogonality regularizer was slightly lower (2.11 HNS vs. 2.23 HNS for full SBM), suggesting that while beneficial for theoretical alignment, strict orthogonality is not the primary driver of SBM's performance gains, but contributes positively.

#### 4.5. Computational Overhead

The computational overhead of SBM was reported as approximately 20% increase in compute time per 1 million environment steps, mainly from training the ˜θ network and computing the inverse covariance for Thompson Sampling. The core representation learning cost was noted to be similar to vanilla baselines, and the Q-learning phase remained efficient.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Spectral Bellman Method (SBM) presents a theoretically-motivated framework that has demonstrated empirical improvements in deep reinforcement learning, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Unified Representation Learning and Exploration**: The primary significance of SBM lies in its approach to explicitly unify representation learning with exploration. By deriving representations from the Inherent Bellman Error (IBE) condition and linking Bellman dynamics to feature covariance, SBM provides features that are inherently suited for both accurate value estimation and uncertainty-driven exploration. This addresses a long-standing challenge in RL, where these two aspects are often tackled separately.

2. **Tractable Low-IBE Learning**: SBM offers a practical and computationally tractable method for learning representations that satisfy or approximate the desirable low-IBE condition. Traditional direct minimization of IBE involves complex and often intractable optimization problems. By leveraging a spectral relationship and power iteration-like updates, SBM provides a gradient-based objective that avoids these complexities, making IBE-based model-free representation learning more feasible for real-world applications.

3. **Improved Performance in Challenging Environments**: The experimental results on Atari, particularly on hard-exploration games, demonstrate consistent and substantial performance improvements over strong baselines (DQN and R2D2). This suggests that the Bellman-aligned features learned by SBM, when combined with Thompson Sampling, enable more effective data gathering and policy optimization in environments with sparse rewards or long-horizon dependencies.

4. **Compatibility and Extensibility**: SBM is designed as a modification to existing value-based RL algorithms, rather than a standalone agent. Its successful integration with both DQN (a basic deep Q-learning algorithm) and R2D2 (an advanced, recurrent, multi-step agent) highlights its versatility. The framework naturally extends to multi-step Bellman operators, allowing it to leverage techniques like Retrace, which are critical for high-performing modern RL agents. This compatibility implies that SBM can potentially enhance a wide range of current and future value-based RL architectures.

5. **Principled Feature Learning**: Unlike some auxiliary-task-based representation learning methods that may generate task-agnostic features or features not directly optimized for Bellman consistency, SBM's objective is directly derived from the structural implications of Bellman updates. This provides a more principled path toward learning representations that are structurally sound and directly relevant to the core objective of value-based RL.

6. **Future Research Directions**: The work opens avenues for further research, including strengthening the theoretical analysis (e.g., convergence guarantees under non-zero IBE or with function approximation), developing more adaptive parameter sampling strategies (ν(θ)), and extending the framework to other RL paradigms, such as distributional RL or actor-critic methods. Addressing identified limitations, such as sensitivity to the parameter distribution hyperparameter (σ²_rep) and broader empirical validation across different domains (e.g., continuous control), represents valuable next steps.

In summary, SBM contributes to the development of more efficient, stable, and exploratory RL agents by explicitly optimizing features for Bellman consistency across a distribution of value functions, thereby unifying two critical components of effective reinforcement learning.
