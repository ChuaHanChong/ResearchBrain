# SMPLer-X: Scaling Up Expressive Human Pose and Shape Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> SMPLer-X is a generalist foundation model for expressive human pose and shape estimation (body, hands, face as SMPL-X) built from a minimalist ViT backbone, a hand/face ROI-cropping neck, and per-part regression heads, trained on up to 4.5M instances from 32 benchmarked datasets. Scaling data and model size cuts mean primary error from over 110 mm to 63.1 mm, with state-of-the-art results on seven benchmarks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art expressive pose methods train on a confined handful of datasets, bottlenecking accuracy across varied scenarios and blocking generalization to unseen capture environments.
- Mainstream whole-body pipelines crop hands and face into separate multi-stage networks, causing inconsistent articulation and implausible wrist rotations under occlusion, truncation, and blur.
- Prior multi-dataset human-centric studies cover only 2D tasks, skeleton keypoints, or body-only SMPL, and never combine more than ten datasets for expressive estimation.

## Method

- Systematic 32-dataset EHPS benchmark: train one ==ViT-S== model per dataset, rank all by ==mean primary error (MPE)==, a new metric averaging primary errors across AGORA, UBody, EgoBody, 3DPW, EHF.
- Minimalist **SMPLer-X** architecture: ==ViTPose==-pretrained ==ViT== backbone (S/B/L/H), a neck predicting hand and face boxes for ==ROI feature cropping==, and separate body, hand, face regression heads.
- Leakage-free data-scaling recipe: rank datasets on target training sets, take top 5/10/20/32 with balanced up/down-sampling to fixed totals, then ==finetune== the generalist into per-benchmark specialists.

## Results

- Joint data and model scaling drops MPE from **110.8 mm** (ViT-S, 5 datasets) to **63.1 mm** (ViT-H, 32 datasets); gains taper beyond ViT-L.
- Finetuned SMPLer-X-L20 sets AGORA-test records at **107.2 mm** NMVE (**11.0%** over prior best), plus **57.4 mm** UBody PVE, **63.6 mm** EgoBody PVE, and **62.4 mm** EHF PVE without finetuning.
- Unseen-domain transfer: SMPLer-X-L10 hits **76.9 mm** PVE on ARCTIC and L20 **77.7 mm** on DNA-Rendering, versus OSX's **102.6** and **105.7 mm**; ViT-B runs **33.1 FPS**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dataset size stops predicting value past roughly 100K instances; diversity of scenes, appearance, and camera views buys more generalization than raw instance count.
> - Synthetic and pseudo-labelled SMPL-X data transfer surprisingly well to real benchmarks, while body-only SMPL labels transfer poorly, annotation parameter space matters more than annotation source.
> - Rank candidate datasets on your own target training set, then finetune a generalist: a leakage-free recipe for cheap adaptation of any foundation model to new scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Expressive Human Pose and Shape Estimation (EHPS) is a field dedicated to capturing the motion and form of the entire human body, including hands and face, from monocular images or videos. This task typically utilizes parametric models such as SMPL-X to represent complex human geometry and articulation. EHPS has applications across various industries, including animation, gaming, and fashion.

Recent years have seen the development of numerous datasets designed to cover diverse capture environments, pose distributions, body visibility, and camera views. Despite this proliferation of data, state-of-the-art EHPS methods have generally relied on a limited subset of these datasets for training. This dependency often results in models that perform well within their specific training domains but exhibit reduced generalization capabilities when encountering varied or unseen scenarios. Prior research has explored multi-dataset training for related human-centric tasks, such as 2D pose estimation or body-only 3D pose and shape estimation (e.g., HumanBench, UniHCP, work by Sárándi et al., and Pang et al.). However, these efforts have not fully extended to the comprehensive EHPS task, which simultaneously involves the expressive modeling of the body, hands, and face.

The current landscape for EHPS also involves a transition from complex multi-stage pipelines, which typically detect and crop hands and face patches for separate processing, to more streamlined one-stage frameworks. Methods like OSX have demonstrated the potential of Vision Transformer (ViT)-based backbones for this task, offering a more concise approach. However, even these advanced methods have not systematically explored the impact of large-scale, diverse data combinations or extensive model scaling.

This work addresses these limitations by systematically investigating the impact of data and model scaling within the EHPS domain. It aims to establish whether a "generalist foundation model," trained on a significantly larger and more diverse collection of datasets and utilizing larger model architectures, can overcome the generalization bottleneck observed in previous methods. By focusing on a minimalist architecture, the authors intend to decouple the scaling investigation from complex algorithmic innovations, thereby providing a foundational understanding and a robust baseline for future EHPS research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the feasibility and effectiveness of scaling up Expressive Human Pose and Shape Estimation (EHPS) towards creating a generalist foundation model, termed SMPLer-X. This model is conceptualized to be robust and universally applicable across a wide array of human body, hand, and face motion capture scenarios.

The key motivations and specific objectives guiding this work are:

1. **Address Generalization Limitations:** Current EHPS methods exhibit a significant dependency on a confined set of training datasets, leading to limitations in performance across varied scenarios and poor generalization to unseen environments. The primary motivation is to overcome this bottleneck by exploring comprehensive data resources.

2. **Establish a Systematic EHPS Benchmark:** To gain a deeper understanding of existing data resources, the research aims to establish the first systematic benchmark for EHPS. This involves evaluating the performance of models trained on individual datasets across multiple established benchmarks to identify inconsistencies, domain gaps, and factors contributing to generalizability.

3. **Investigate Data Scaling:** A core objective is to systematically explore the impact of training with a large and diverse collection of EHPS datasets. This includes:
 - Benchmarking 32 diverse EHPS datasets to understand their individual contributions to generalization.
 - Capitalizing on insights from this benchmarking to optimize training schemes and select datasets that significantly enhance EHPS capabilities.
 - Evaluating the effect of increasing the number of training instances (up to 4.5 million) from multiple data sources.

4. **Investigate Model Scaling:** The study aims to understand the scaling laws of model sizes in EHPS. This involves:
 - Leveraging Vision Transformers (ViT) of varying sizes (up to ViT-Huge) as backbones to examine their impact on performance.
 - Designing a minimalist and scalable model architecture (SMPLer-X) that is efficient for large-scale training and decoupled from specific algorithmic complexities, thus serving as a clean foundation for future research.

5. **Develop a Generalist Foundation Model:** The overarching goal is to build SMPLer-X, a foundation model trained with massive data and large model parameters, which demonstrates strong, balanced performance across diverse test benchmarks and exhibits excellent transferability to unseen environments.

6. **Enable Specialist Models through Finetuning:** The research aims to validate strategies for finetuning the generalist foundation model into domain-specific specialist models. This would allow for further performance boosts on particular benchmarks and offer a cost-effective adaptation mechanism for specific applications.

7. **Provide Critical Guidance for Dataset Collection:** Through extensive analysis of dataset attributes, the work seeks to yield practical insights and tips for future dataset collection efforts, specifically concerning factors that contribute to model generalizability.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to shift the community norm from limited-dataset training to a more comprehensive, large-scale approach, thereby advancing the robustness and applicability of EHPS models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research adopts a systematic approach to investigate data and model scaling for Expressive Human Pose and Shape Estimation (EHPS). The methodology can be segmented into preliminary definitions, dataset benchmarking, model architecture design, training of generalist foundation models, and finetuning for specialist applications.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

- **Parametric Model:** The study exclusively uses the SMPL-X parametric human model, which represents the human body, hands, and face geometries. The objective is to estimate pose parameters ($\theta \in R^{55 \times 3}$) encompassing body, hands, eyes, and jaw poses; joint body, hand, and face shape parameters ($\beta \in R^{10}$); and facial expression parameters ($\psi \in R^{10}$).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Standard EHPS metrics are employed, including Per-Vertex Error (PVE), Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE), and their Procrustes-aligned versions (PA-PVE, PA-MPJPE). For the AGORA leaderboard, Normalized Mean Vertex Error (NMVE) and Normalized Mean Joint Error (NMJE) are used. The authors introduce Mean Primary Error (MPE) as a consolidated metric, averaging MPJPE for 3DPW and PVE for AGORA, UBody, EgoBody, and EHF, to gauge overall generalizability. All errors are reported in millimeters (mm).

#### 3.2. Overview of Data Sources and Benchmarking EHPS Datasets

- **Dataset Types:** The study analyzes 32 EHPS datasets, categorized into:
 - **Motion Capture Datasets:** Typically collected in studio environments using optical or vision-based multi-view systems (e.g., Human3.6M, ARCTIC). These offer high-quality 3D annotations but have physical constraints.
 - **Pseudo-Annotated Datasets:** Re-annotate existing 2D image datasets with parametric human annotations (e.g., MSCOCO, MPII). These leverage 2D diversity, with pseudo-3D annotations proving effective despite potential noise.
 - **Synthetic Datasets:** Produced with rendering engines (e.g., AGORA, BEDLAM). These provide accurate 3D annotations and scalability, but may suffer from a synthetic-to-real domain gap.
- **Evaluation Datasets:** Five key datasets (AGORA, UBody, EgoBody, 3DPW, EHF) are selected for comprehensive evaluation, chosen for their accurate SMPL/SMPL-X annotations, representativeness, wide usage (or recency), and clearly defined test sets. These datasets collectively provide broad coverage of the EHPS data landscape, as visualized through UMAP dimension reduction of various features (image, global orientation, body pose).
- **Individual Dataset Benchmarking:** A standardized SMPLer-X model with a ViT-S backbone is trained on the training set of each of the 32 datasets. Performance is then evaluated on the validation/test sets of the five key evaluation benchmarks (excluding in-domain results for MPE calculation). This process yields a ranking (Table 1) and insights into performance across diverse domains. A separate benchmark based on training sets of key evaluation benchmarks is used for subsequent data selection (Table 22).
- **Analysis of Dataset Attributes:** The authors analyze the correlation between dataset attributes and generalizability, including:
 - **Number of Training Instances:** Investigating if "more" data consistently leads to better performance.
 - **Scene Type:** Categorizing datasets as in-the-wild, indoor, or studio.
 - **Real/Synthetic Appearance:** Assessing the transferability of synthetic data.
 - **Annotation Type:** Comparing the efficacy of accurate vs. pseudo SMPL-X annotations and SMPL annotations.

#### 3.3. Model Architectures: SMPLer-X

- **Minimalist Design:** SMPLer-X is designed with a minimalist architecture to ensure scalability and efficiency for large-scale training and to serve as a clean foundation for research, decoupled from complex algorithmic designs. It consists of three main parts:
 - **Backbone:** Employs Vision Transformer (ViT) variants (ViT-Small, Base, Large, Huge) for scalable image feature extraction. The input image is cropped by a whole-body bounding box, resized, tokenized into patches, and positional encoding is added.
 - **Neck:** Predicts face and hand bounding boxes from the processed image tokens. These bounding boxes are used by a Region of Interest (ROI) module to crop features from the feature map, which are then reorganized, undergo transposed convolution, and fed into hand and face heads.
 - **Heads:** Regression heads are used to estimate parameters for each body part. A positional module and a regressor module are used for hand and body heads, while the face head includes only a regressor module, following OSX. SMPLer-X does not rely on third-party detectors, cross-part feature interaction modules, or heavy decoders.

#### 3.4. Training the Generalist Foundation Models

- **Data Selection:** Datasets are chosen based on their ranking from the training-set-based benchmark (Table 22), prioritizing higher-ranked datasets. Four data amounts are used for training: 5, 10, 20, and 32 datasets, corresponding to total lengths of 0.75M, 1.5M, 3.0M, and 4.5M instances.
- **Balanced Sampling:** To prevent larger datasets from dominating, a balanced sampling strategy is adopted, where all selected datasets are uniformly upsampled or downsampled to the same length, contributing equally to the total designated length. An ablation study showed sampling strategy did not significantly impact performance.
- **Data Preprocessing:** Datasets are standardized into the HumanData format. For gendered SMPL-X annotations, a small adapter network is trained to convert them to neutral SMPL-X parameters. For datasets with only SMPL annotations, only global orientation and body pose are supervised, as direct transferability to SMPL-X is limited.
- **Model Scaling:** Training is conducted with four ViT backbone sizes: Small (S), Base (B), Large (L), and Huge (H), pretrained by ViTPose.
- **Training Configuration:** Training is performed on 16 V100 GPUs with a total batch size of 512 (256 for ViT-Huge) for 10 epochs. The Adam optimizer is used with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule (initial 1e-5, min 1e-6).

#### 3.5. Finetuning the Specialists

- **Strategy:** Foundation models are finetuned to become domain-specific specialists. The finetuning strategy involves selecting five high-ranking datasets (based on the target training set's benchmark) to finetune the model for 5 epochs. This is done to emulate real-world scenarios where test data is unavailable.
- **Finetuning Scope:** Finetuning experiments are primarily conducted on the ViT-L backbone model. Ablation studies evaluate finetuning the full network versus only specific parts (e.g., neck and heads) to assess efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides insights into data and model scaling for EHPS, culminating in the development of the SMPLer-X foundation model.

#### 4.1. Benchmarking EHPS Datasets

- **Domain Gaps and Inconsistencies:** The initial benchmarking revealed that models trained on individual datasets typically perform well within their specific domain but exhibit substantial performance degradation when evaluated on different domains. For instance, a model trained on AGORA (ranked 1st on AGORA validation) ranked 24th on EHF, indicating significant domain gaps and the challenging, diverse nature of the EHPS landscape. This finding underscores the necessity of data scaling for robust and transferable models.
- **Impact of Dataset Attributes:**
 - **Number of Instances:** Datasets with approximately 100K instances were observed to be effective. Merely increasing the number of instances beyond this threshold did not guarantee proportionate performance improvements; factors such as diversity and quality became more critical.
 - **Scene Type:** In-the-wild datasets generally led to higher-ranked models, followed by indoor datasets with varied scenes. Studio datasets typically ranked lower in overall generalizability. This suggests that diversifying indoor scenes can be a viable alternative if in-the-wild collection is not feasible.
 - **Real/Synthetic Appearance:** Contemporary synthetic datasets demonstrated strong performance and transferability to real-world scenarios. Analysis revealed that synthetic data often included a substantial number of in-the-wild-like images, overlapping with real-world distributions and offering coverage for various scenarios.
 - **Annotation Type:** Accurate or pseudo-SMPL-X annotations were crucial for high performance. Datasets with SMPL-X annotations, even pseudo-labeled ones (e.g., InstaVariety), frequently ranked among the top performers. In contrast, SMPL annotations were less effective for SMPL-X estimation.

#### 4.2. Generalist Foundation Models (SMPLer-X)

- **Effectiveness of Data Scaling:** Increasing the number of diverse training datasets from 5 to 32 (total instances from 0.75M to 4.5M) consistently improved the mean primary error (MPE) across all ViT backbone sizes. For example, SMPLer-X-S32 (trained on 32 datasets) achieved an MPE of 82.6 mm, compared to SMPLer-X-S5 (5 datasets) at 110.8 mm. This demonstrates that more diverse datasets contribute significantly to generalizability.
- **Effectiveness of Model Scaling:** Larger Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones generally led to better performance. Scaling from ViT-Small (S) to ViT-Huge (H) systematically reduced MPE for a given number of training datasets. For example, with 32 datasets, SMPLer-X-H32 achieved an MPE of 63.1 mm, outperforming SMPLer-X-S32 at 82.6 mm. However, diminishing returns were observed; while ViT-Huge has more than twice the parameters of ViT-Large, the performance gain was not always proportional.
- **Superiority over Single-Dataset Training:** The generalist SMPLer-X foundation models consistently outperformed models trained on individual datasets (as shown in Table 1 vs. Table 2), demonstrating improved robustness and balanced results across various scenarios without requiring specific training for each use case.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The SMPLer-X foundation models achieved state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks. For instance, SMPLer-X-H32 achieved PVEs of 69.5 mm (AGORA), 59.5 mm (EgoBody), 54.5 mm (UBody), and 56.8 mm (EHF) without finetuning, and 75.0 mm MPJPE on 3DPW.
- **High Transferability:** SMPLer-X demonstrated strong transferability to unseen datasets not used during training, such as ARCTIC and DNA-Rendering-HiRes. Even without finetuning, these models significantly outperformed existing state-of-the-art methods that employed conventional data sampling strategies, indicating rich knowledge acquisition.
- **Inference Speed:** SMPLer-X models generally exhibited higher inference speeds (up to 36.2 FPS for ViT-S) compared to existing SOTA methods like OSX (12.2 FPS), attributing this efficiency to its minimalist architecture. Smaller versions achieved real-time performance.

#### 4.3. Specialist Models via Finetuning

- **Significant Performance Boost:** Finetuning the generalist foundation models on specific target training sets consistently led to substantial performance enhancements. For example, finetuning SMPLer-X-L20 on the AGORA training set resulted in a new record of 107.2 mm NMVE on the AGORA leaderboard, an 11.0% improvement. Similar improvements were observed on EgoBody, UBody, and EHF.
- **Efficient Finetuning:** An ablation study on finetuning strategies showed that finetuning only the neck and head modules of the foundation model could achieve comparable, and sometimes even slightly better, performance than finetuning the entire network. This approach required significantly fewer learnable parameters (e.g., 31M vs. 662M for ViT-H32), suggesting that the backbone, once extensively pretrained, becomes highly generalizable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of Expressive Human Pose and Shape Estimation (EHPS) and establishes a foundation for future advancements.

**Significance:**

1. **First Systematic EHPS Dataset Benchmark:** The work provides the first systematic and comprehensive benchmark of 32 EHPS datasets, offering critical guidance for understanding dataset characteristics and their contributions to model generalization. This benchmark helps in reassessing the utility of existing datasets and informs strategic data selection for large-scale training.
2. **Validation of Data and Model Scaling:** The study empirically validates the efficacy of both data scaling (using up to 4.5 million instances from diverse sources) and model scaling (employing Vision Transformers up to ViT-Huge) for developing robust and transferable EHPS models. This demonstrates that moving beyond limited-dataset training is crucial for achieving high performance across varied scenarios.
3. **Introduction of SMPLer-X as a Generalist Foundation Model:** The development of SMPLer-X, a generalist foundation model with a minimalist architecture, serves as a strong baseline for future EHPS research. By decoupling the model's architecture from complex algorithmic designs, it facilitates further investigation into data and parameter scaling, providing a clean foundation for algorithmic explorations.
4. **Effective Benchmarking-Finetuning Paradigm:** The proposed paradigm, which involves benchmarking datasets to guide the selection of relevant data for finetuning a foundation model, offers a practical and efficient strategy for adapting generalist models to specific scenarios. This enables the creation of potent, domain-specific specialist models with relatively low adaptation costs.
5. **Insights for Future Data Collection:** The detailed analysis of dataset attributes (e.g., instance count, scene type, real/synthetic nature, annotation quality) provides valuable insights for the design and collection of future EHPS datasets, emphasizing diversity, quality, and the importance of SMPL-X annotations.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Advancing EHPS Capabilities:** SMPLer-X's consistent state-of-the-art performance across numerous benchmarks, combined with its strong transferability to unseen environments, represents an advancement in EHPS capabilities. This can lead to more accurate and reliable human motion capture for a wider range of real-world applications.
2. **Enabling New Applications:** Improved generalization and robustness can expand the utility of EHPS in industries such as animation (for more realistic character movements), gaming (for enhanced player avatar control and interaction), fashion (for virtual try-ons and garment design), and virtual reality/augmented reality (for more immersive human-computer interaction).
3. **Reduced Development Costs:** The generalist foundation model, with its efficient finetuning capabilities, can reduce the development cost and time for creating EHPS solutions tailored to specific use cases, as developers can adapt a powerful pre-trained model rather than training from scratch.
4. **Catalyst for Research:** SMPLer-X can serve as a plug-and-play component or a benchmark for researchers exploring novel EHPS algorithms, data augmentation techniques, or new parametric human models. The insights into data characteristics can guide subsequent dataset creation efforts.

**Limitations:**

The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
1. **Evaluation Scope:** While comprehensive, the five benchmark datasets used might not fully represent the entirety of real-world data distributions.
2. **Architectural Exploration:** Due to the prohibitive computational cost associated with training foundation models, the study's investigation into various model architectures was limited, focusing primarily on ViT backbones.

**Potential Negative Societal Impact:**

The authors recognize the potential for negative societal impact associated with powerful EHPS models, such as their misuse for unwarranted surveillance or privacy violation. This highlights the ongoing ethical considerations in the development and deployment of human-centric AI technologies.
