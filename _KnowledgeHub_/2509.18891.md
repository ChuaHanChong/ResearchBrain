# Attack for Defense: Adversarial Agents for Point Prompt Optimization Empowering Segment Anything Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Taiyuan University of Technology and Nanjing University Of Aeronautics And Astronautics developed Point Prompt Defender (PPD), an adversarial reinforcement learning framework that autonomously optimizes point prompts for the Segment Anything Model (SAM). PPD, by simulating an "attack-for-defense" scenario, consistently improved SAM's segmentation performance across natural and medical image datasets, achieving up to 27.8% mDSC improvement on PASCAL VOC with suboptimal initial prompts and outperforming prior one-shot methods.

## Problem

- SAM's segmentation performance is highly contingent on the quality of manual or heuristically crafted input prompts, limiting its scalability and automation.
- Existing automatic prompt generation methods (e.g., supervised fine-tuning, geometric engineering, prior reinforcement learning) either lack generalization across domains, require task-specific supervision, or struggle with adaptive reasoning and diverse initial prompt configurations.
- A need exists for a robust and generalizable method to refine SAM's point prompts that is task-agnostic and does not require additional training or adaptation for new downstream tasks.

## Method

- Introduces **Point Prompt Defender** (PPD), an ==adversarial reinforcement learning== framework that models prompt optimization as a competitive game between an attack agent and a defense agent.
- Constructs a task-agnostic environment using a dual-space heterogeneous graph representation of image patches, incorporating DINOv2 features for semantic affinity and physical distances for spatial affinity.
- Trains two Deep Q-Network (DQN) agents: an attacker that seeks to degrade SAM's segmentation performance by activating prompts, and a defender that aims to recover or improve it by deactivating prompts, both agents learn based on direct feedback from SAM's Dice coefficient.

## Results

- The attack agent significantly degraded SAM's performance, with mDSC dropping by **44.3%** on PASCAL VOC; subsequently, the PPD defense agent successfully recovered mDSC by **39.3%**.
- PPD, when starting with suboptimal initial prompts, improved SAM's mDSC by **27.8%** on PASCAL VOC, **9.9%** on ISIC, and **23.4%** on Kvasir without retraining.
- PPD-FM (PPD integrated with feature matching initialization) achieved superior segmentation performance against other one-shot SAM-based methods, scoring **69.1%** mDSC on PASCAL VOC, **76.3%** on ISIC, and **54.8%** on Kvasir.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial training through an "attack-for-defense" paradigm enhances the robustness and generalization capabilities of the prompt optimization policy by exposing the defense agent to diverse and challenging prompt configurations.
> - Integrating direct segmentation feedback from SAM's output (Dice coefficient) into the reinforcement learning reward function allows for adaptive and context-aware prompt refinement.
> - Utilizing a task-agnostic environment, constructed with universal visual features (DINOv2) and dual-space graph representations, enables the trained defense agent to operate as a plug-and-play solution across various domains without retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional image segmentation has predominantly relied on architectural engineering, involving the design and training of specialized network structures like U-Net on task-specific datasets. While this approach has yielded performance gains, it often struggles with generalization to unseen domains, susceptibility to local optima, limited task transferability, and high costs associated with architecture design and tuning, particularly in scenarios with limited labels or shifting data distributions.

Recent advancements have seen the emergence of pretrained foundation models (PFMs), which have expanded the capabilities of computer vision systems by achieving robust segmentation across diverse scenarios. Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) such as DINOv2 learn generic and transferable visual representations from raw image data, enabling strong generalization and zero-shot performance. Among these, the Segment Anything Model (SAM) has been established as a prominent framework for general-purpose segmentation. SAM leverages large-scale pretraining on diverse mask–image–prompt triplets and a prompt-driven interaction design to decouple inference from task-specific supervision, offering adaptability and training-free deployment.

Despite its strengths, SAM's performance remains contingent on the quality of manual prompt input, which limits its scalability in fully automated pipelines or applications where human interaction is impractical. This dependency has led to efforts in automatic or learned prompt generation. One line of research focuses on fine-tuning prompt encoders to adapt SAM to downstream tasks; however, these methods typically require additional task-specific supervision, which constrains their scalability and generalization to unseen domains. Another approach, geometric prompt engineering (e.g., Matcher, GBMSeg), generates prompts heuristically based on spatial or semantic priors. While these methods improve automation, they treat prompts as static inputs, lacking the adaptive reasoning capabilities required for varying segmentation contexts. A reinforcement learning framework, Point Prompt Optimizer (PPO), introduced iterative prompt refinement based on inter-prompt relations. However, PPO optimizes prompts in isolation from SAM's segmentation outputs and suffers from limited diversity in its training environments, leading to learned policies that are sensitive to the quality and distribution of initial prompts and often fail to generalize across domains.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing an adversarial reinforcement learning framework that dynamically optimizes point prompts. By introducing a competitive attack-for-defense paradigm, the work aims to enable robust and generalized prompt refinement that directly integrates feedback from SAM's segmentation output, thereby overcoming the constraints of static, supervised, or isolated reinforcement learning-based prompt generation strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalized method for automatically optimizing point prompts for the Segment Anything Model (SAM), thereby enhancing its segmentation performance without requiring task-specific retraining.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current approaches to leveraging SAM:
- **Reliance on Suboptimal Prompts:** SAM's performance is critically dependent on the quality of input prompts. Existing methods often rely on heuristic or manually crafted prompts, which are not scalable, lack generalization across diverse tasks and domains, and can be impractical or costly in automated systems.
- **Limitations of Supervised Prompt Generation:** Approaches that fine-tune prompt encoders for SAM often require additional task-specific supervision. This dependency restricts their ability to generalize to new, unseen domains or tasks, as they are tailored to specific datasets or annotations.
- **Static Nature of Geometric Prompt Engineering:** Geometric methods that generate prompts based on spatial or semantic priors treat prompts as static inputs. They lack the capacity to adaptively reason about the utility of a prompt in real-time, based on SAM's segmentation feedback or the evolving context of the image. This limits their effectiveness in dynamic or complex scenarios.
- **Deficiencies in Prior Reinforcement Learning Approaches:** Previous reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks, such as Point Prompt Optimizer (PPO), have attempted to iteratively refine prompt locations. However, these methods typically optimize prompts in isolation, without directly coordinating with SAM's segmentation feedback during training. Furthermore, they have shown sensitivity to the quality and distribution of initial prompts and often fail to generalize due to limited diversity in their training environments. This indicates a need for an RL framework that can learn more robust and transferable prompt optimization strategies.
- **Need for a Generalizable and Plug-and-Play Solution:** There is a demand for a task-agnostic solution that can enhance SAM's performance across various domains (e.g., natural images, medical images) without the need for model retraining or adaptation. Such a solution would significantly improve the flexibility and deployment ease of SAM in diverse applications.

To address these motivations, the authors propose Point Prompt Defender (PPD), which utilizes an adversarial reinforcement learning framework to dynamically explore the segmentation landscape. By modeling prompt optimization as a competitive game between an "attacker" (degrading SAM performance) and a "defender" (restoring SAM performance), the framework aims to learn a robust and generalizable policy for prompt refinement that is directly guided by SAM's segmentation feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Point Prompt Defender (PPD) framework employs an adversarial reinforcement learning approach with an attack-for-defense paradigm to optimize point prompts for SAM. The methodology involves constructing a task-agnostic environment, training two adversarial agents (attacker and defender) using Deep Q-Networks (DQN), and deploying only the defense agent for inference.

**A. Environment Construction**
To facilitate structured prompt optimization via reinforcement learning, a task-agnostic environment is constructed as a dual-space heterogeneous graph.
- **Image Patch Representation:** An input image `X` is divided into non-overlapping patches `x = {x1..., xn}`. Each patch `xi` is represented by a feature vector `fi` extracted using the DINOv2 image encoder.
- **Dual-space Distance Matrices:**
 - **Feature Distance Matrix (Mf):** Computed by pairwise Euclidean distances between the DINOv2 feature vectors of patches: `Mf(i, j) = ||fi - fj||`. This encodes semantic affinities.
 - **Physical Distance Matrix (Mp):** Computed by Euclidean distances between the geometric centers of patches: `Mp(i, j) = ||xi - xj||`. This encodes spatial affinities.
 - These two matrices define the edge attributes in a dual-space graph `G = (V, Ef, Ep)`, where `V` are the nodes (image patches), and `Ef` and `Ep` are feature-based and physical-based edge sets, respectively.
- **Ideal Prompt Initialization:** The environment is initialized with an "ideal prompt scheme" derived from the ground-truth segmentation mask of each training image. Positive prompts are uniformly sampled inside the mask, and negative prompts are sampled outside. These ideal prompts form the initial node set in the graph, providing a semantically informative starting state for the adversarial learning process.

**B. Adversarial Agent Training Process**
PPD optimizes the spatial distribution of point prompts through a two-stage adversarial interaction between an attack agent and a defense agent within the constructed dual-space graph environment.
- **Training Episode Initialization:** Each episode starts with the ideal prompt configuration.
- **Attacker Agent (Attack Phase):**
 - **Goal:** Maximize the degradation of SAM's segmentation performance.
 - **Action:** At each time step `t`, the attack agent selects an action `a_atk_t` from the set of inactive prompts to activate a subset of prompts. These activated prompts are added to the current prompt configuration.
 - **SAM Interaction:** The modified prompt set is fed into SAM, which generates a segmentation mask `M_hat_t`.
 - **Reward Function:** The attacker's reward `r_atk_t` is defined as the negative change in the Dice coefficient: `-(Dice(M_hat_t, M) - Dice(M_hat_t-1, M))`, where `M` is the ground truth. A decrease in Dice coefficient (worse segmentation) yields a higher reward for the attacker, incentivizing maximal degradation.
- **Defender Agent (Defense Phase):**
 - **Goal:** Recover SAM's segmentation performance by counteracting the attacker's actions.
 - **Action:** The defense agent receives the prompt configuration modified by the attacker. It selects an action `a_def_t` from the set of active prompts to deactivate a subset of them.
 - **SAM Interaction:** The refined prompt set is inputted into SAM for a new segmentation prediction `M_hat_t`.
 - **Reward Function:** The defender's reward `r_def_t` is defined as the positive change in the Dice coefficient: `Dice(M_hat_t, M) - Dice(M_hat_t-1, M)`. An increase in Dice coefficient (better segmentation) yields a higher reward for the defender, incentivizing performance recovery.
- **Training Details:** Both agents are trained alternately using Deep Q-Networks (DQN). Each agent maintains a Q-network to approximate the action-value function, trained by minimizing the temporal difference loss. The discount factor `γ` balances immediate and future rewards, and target networks are periodically updated for stability. An `ϵ-greedy` strategy is employed for action selection.

**C. Agent Inference for Prompt Optimization**
During inference, the environment is constructed using task-agnostic features from DINOv2. Only the trained defense agent is deployed. It refines arbitrary initial prompt sets by identifying and suppressing low-quality or harmful prompts. This design enables a task-agnostic, plug-and-play enhancement of SAM's segmentation performance without requiring any additional retraining or adaptation to new downstream tasks or domains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluated PPD's task-agnostic capabilities, its robustness against adversarial attacks, its ability to optimize initial prompts, and its comparative performance against other SAM-based one-shot methods. The PPD framework was trained on 1000 images from the FSS-1000 dataset and evaluated on unseen datasets: PASCAL VOC (natural scenes) and ISIC, Kvasir (medical scenes).

**A. Ablation Study**
- **Defense Against Adversarial Attacks:**
 - The attack agent demonstrated its effectiveness in degrading SAM's performance. For instance, on PASCAL VOC, the mean Dice coefficient (mDSC) dropped by 44.3% and mean IoU (mIoU) by 47.9% when the attack agent perturbed ideal prompts. Similar degradations were observed on ISIC (mDSC: -30.9%, mIoU: -30.4%) and Kvasir (mDSC: -23.3%, mIoU: -25.0%).
 - The defense agent effectively restored segmentation quality. Following the attack, the defender improved mDSC on PASCAL VOC by 39.3%, on ISIC by 25.1%, and on Kvasir by 16.7%. These recoveries brought the performance close to that achieved with ideal prompts. Qualitative results visually confirmed this degradation by the attacker and subsequent recovery by the defender.
- **Optimization of Initial Prompts:**
 - When starting with noisy or suboptimal initial prompts generated by a training-free feature matching strategy, SAM's performance was limited (e.g., PASCAL VOC: 41.3 mDSC, 34.8 mIoU; ISIC: 66.4 mDSC, 55.0 mIoU; Kvasir: 31.4 mDSC, 21.5 mIoU).
 - The PPD defense agent, without retraining, significantly refined these prompts, leading to consistent performance improvements across all datasets. For example, mDSC improved by 27.8% on PASCAL VOC, 9.9% on ISIC, and 23.4% on Kvasir. Qualitative results indicated that PPD effectively removed disruptive points, leading to more accurate segmentations.

**B. Comparison with SAM-based One-shot Methods**
The PPD framework, integrated with a feature matching prompt initialization (PPD-FM), was compared against several other one-shot SAM-based segmentation methods (PerSAM, PerSAM-F, Matcher, VRP-SAM, GBMSeg, FM-PPO).
- PPD-FM consistently achieved superior segmentation performance across natural and medical image datasets without any task-specific retraining.
 - On PASCAL VOC, PPD-FM achieved 69.1% mDSC and 60.3% mIoU.
 - On ISIC, it achieved 76.3% mDSC and 64.2% mIoU.
 - On Kvasir, it achieved 54.8% mDSC and 44.9% mIoU.
- The advantage of PPD-FM was particularly notable on medical datasets (ISIC, Kvasir), where methods like VRP-SAM, which rely on prompt encoders primarily designed for natural images, struggled significantly (e.g., VRP-SAM scored 4.6% mDSC on ISIC and 0.0% on Kvasir).
- PPD-FM also outperformed FM-PPO, a previous reinforcement learning-based prompt optimizer, across all datasets (e.g., PPD-FM achieved 69.1% mDSC on PASCAL VOC compared to FM-PPO's 62.1%). This indicates that the adversarial dual-agent framework in PPD enhances robustness and generalization by learning to refine prompts under diverse and challenging conditions simulated by the attacker, addressing the sensitivity of prior RL methods to initial prompt quality.
- Qualitative results showed that PPD-FM produced more accurate boundaries and suppressed irrelevant regions compared to other methods, even when starting with suboptimal initial prompts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Point Prompt Defender (PPD), a novel framework that addresses several critical limitations in the application of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for diverse segmentation tasks. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** PPD introduces an "attack-for-defense" adversarial reinforcement learning paradigm, which trains a defense agent to effectively counteract prompts designed to degrade SAM's performance. This adversarial training strengthens the defense agent's ability to refine prompts under challenging conditions, leading to policies that generalize well across varied prompt configurations and unseen domains (both natural and medical images) without requiring task-specific retraining. This significantly improves SAM's robustness to noisy or suboptimal initial prompts.
- **Automation and Scalability:** By automatically optimizing point prompts based on direct segmentation feedback, PPD reduces the reliance on manual or heuristically crafted prompts. This enables SAM to be deployed more effectively in fully automated pipelines, enhancing its scalability in scenarios where human interaction is impractical or costly.
- **Task-Agnostic and Plug-and-Play Solution:** The framework's design, leveraging DINOv2 features for environment construction and training only the defense agent for inference, renders it task-agnostic and plug-and-play. It can refine arbitrary coarse prompt sets to enhance SAM's segmentation performance across diverse tasks and domains without further fine-tuning or adaptation. This flexibility reduces deployment overhead and broadens SAM's applicability.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Prior Methods:** PPD directly addresses the shortcomings of previous prompt engineering strategies. Unlike methods requiring task-specific supervision, PPD operates in a training-free inference mode. It also moves beyond static prompt generation by adaptively reasoning about prompt utility through reinforcement learning, and it overcomes the sensitivity and limited generalization of earlier RL approaches (like PPO) by integrating SAM's direct segmentation feedback within an adversarial learning setup.
- **Potential for Diverse Applications:** The demonstrated effectiveness across both natural and medical image datasets suggests broad applicability. In medical imaging, where precise segmentation is crucial and manual annotation is time-consuming, PPD could significantly aid in automated diagnosis, surgical planning, and quantitative analysis. In other domains, it could enhance efficiency in tasks like industrial inspection, autonomous driving, and content creation by improving segmentation accuracy from minimal input.
- **Future Research Direction:** The identified limitation regarding extremely poor initializations suggests avenues for future work, such as integrating more advanced and adaptive prompt generation strategies (e.g., text-guided or multimodal references) with PPD to further improve its performance in "cold-start" scenarios.

Overall, PPD provides a robust, flexible, and interpretable framework for prompt optimization, offering a new perspective on adaptively enhancing the utility of prompt-based vision models like SAM.
