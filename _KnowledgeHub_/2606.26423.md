# CoStream: Composing Simple Behaviors for Generalizable Complex Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CoStream enables robots to perform long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks with sub-millimeter precision and zero-shot generalization by composing simple, independent behaviors. The framework achieved near-perfect success rates on challenging high-precision assembly tasks and significantly outperformed baselines on everyday manipulation, leveraging real-time vision, language, tactile, and force feedback.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation struggles to achieve both high precision for contact-rich tasks and broad generalization to novel scenarios without extensive retraining.
- Classical modular pipelines offer precision but require costly, task-specific engineering, while monolithic end-to-end policies often lack precision and robustness for fine-grained contact.
- Existing robotic control paradigms limit adaptability and robustness by assuming capabilities must be deployed as rigid pipelines or indivisible, monolithic systems.

## Method

- **CoStream** introduces a ==hierarchical framework== that divides complex tasks into ==stage compilation== and ==per-stage action composition==, integrating foundation models and diverse sensing.
- It orchestrates ==three independent behaviors== (semantic for task-frame anchoring, predictive for nominal motion, reactive for tactile/force residuals) that operate in parallel at their natural rates.
- A ==multi-rate SE(3) action composer== fuses outputs from these behaviors into a single end-effector pose command, executed by a ==compliant controller== that manages contact forces and recovery rules.

## Results

- Achieved near-perfect success rates (**14/15** or **15/15**) on high-precision assembly tasks (Drill, RAM, CPU, GPU insertion) where baseline methods (VoxPoser, π 0.5 ) consistently failed (**0/15**).
- Outperformed monolithic VLA policies on everyday manipulation tasks, for example, achieving **14/15** successes for 'Clothes → Box' compared to **3/15** by the baseline.
- Demonstrated robustness to human-induced perturbations by autonomously detecting and correcting for in-gripper object slip during insertion tasks, showing real-time adaptation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the reactive behavior, as removing tactile and force feedback caused success rates to plummet from **15/15** to **3/15** on the Drill Insertion task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex, precise robotic manipulation can effectively emerge from the composition of simpler, independent, sensor-grounded behaviors rather than relying on monolithic systems.
> - Structured integration of diverse multimodal sensing—vision, language, tactile, and force—is crucial for real-time adaptation and robustness in contact-rich scenarios.
> - A shared SE(3) task-space interface enables modularity, allowing behaviors to contribute at their appropriate rates, enhancing robustness to perturbations and facilitating zero-shot task transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation aims to enable robots to perform complex tasks in diverse environments. A significant challenge lies in achieving both high precision, particularly for contact-rich operations with tight clearances, and broad generalization, allowing robots to adapt to new tasks without extensive retraining. Current approaches in robotic manipulation typically fall into two main paradigms, both of which exhibit limitations in addressing these combined requirements.

The first paradigm involves classical, modular pipelines, which explicitly decouple components such as perception, state estimation, planning, and control. These pipelines are capable of achieving high-precision control through carefully engineered, task-specific interfaces. However, their rigidity necessitates bespoke engineering and manual redesign of perception and state estimation modules when adapting to novel tasks, making them costly and inflexible.

The second paradigm employs monolithic end-to-end policies, such as vision-language-action (VLA) models. These models attempt to learn directly from multimodal observations to actions, aiming to encapsulate semantic reasoning and high-frequency contact dynamics within a single network. While such approaches offer potential for improved generalization by absorbing web-scale knowledge, they typically suffer from a lack of precision on complex, out-of-distribution tasks. Their data-hungry nature requires extensive human demonstrations for generalization to each new scenario, making them less suitable for fine-grained, contact-rich interactions unless retrained with new data.

Both of these dominant paradigms share an underlying assumption: once a manipulation capability is acquired, it must be deployed either as a rigid pipeline or as an indivisible, monolithic system. This implicit assumption limits their adaptability and robustness.

This research introduces CoStream, a framework that challenges this assumption by drawing inspiration from behavior-based robotics, which posits that complex behaviors can emerge from the composition of simpler, independent sensory behaviors. CoStream aims to bridge the gap between the high precision offered by classical methods and the generalization aspirations of end-to-end learning. It specifically targets contact-rich manipulation, an area where current foundation models often struggle to integrate real-time tactile and force feedback effectively, often relying primarily on vision or predictive simulation. By proposing a compositional approach, CoStream seeks to provide a more flexible and robust solution for complex manipulation tasks, avoiding the extensive manual engineering of traditional task and motion planning (TAMP) and the burdensome retraining requirements of monolithic end-to-end policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic manipulation framework capable of executing long-horizon, contact-rich complex manipulation tasks with both sub-millimeter precision and out-of-the-box generalization to novel scenarios. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, driven by the limitations of existing robotic control paradigms.

The key motivations for developing CoStream stem from the observed shortcomings of current approaches:
1. **Addressing the Precision-Generalization Dichotomy**: Existing robotic systems struggle to simultaneously achieve the millimeter-level precision required for tasks like inserting a GPU into a PCIe slot, and the ability to generalize that capability to entirely new tasks without significant redesign or retraining. Classical pipelines offer precision but lack generalization, while monolithic end-to-end policies offer potential for generalization but often lack the necessary precision and robustness for contact-rich interactions. CoStream is motivated to overcome this fundamental trade-off.
2. **Enabling Compositional Intelligence**: The authors are motivated by the hypothesis that complex manipulation capabilities can naturally emerge from the composition of simple, independent behaviors, rather than requiring a single, complex, or monolithic control system. This approach aims to simplify the development of individual components, making it easier to integrate diverse sensor modalities and specialized algorithms.
3. **Integrating Diverse Sensing Modalities Effectively**: Tasks requiring contact, such as assembly, often depend on real-time tactile and force feedback, which vision-centric foundation models typically lack or inadequately incorporate. The motivation is to create a framework that natively orchestrates and leverages diverse sensing modalities (vision, language, tactile, force) by assigning them to specific, composable behaviors.
4. **Enhancing Robustness to Perturbations**: In real-world scenarios, robots inevitably encounter unexpected deviations or perturbations (e.g., object slip, minor misalignments). A key motivation is to enable robots to robustly recover from such real-time perturbations during execution, which current open-loop or purely vision-based systems often fail to do.
5. **Achieving Zero-Shot Task Transfer**: The goal is to develop a system that can transfer across various manipulation tasks—from delicate handling to high-force assembly—without requiring task-specific policy retraining or extensive redesign of behavior modules. This emphasizes the aim for broad applicability and efficiency.
6. **Establishing a Standardized Interface**: By proposing a common SE(3) task-space interface, the motivation is to facilitate the modularity and composability of different behaviors, allowing each to contribute its reliable component (a frame, a motion, or a correction) at its own natural rate.

In essence, CoStream is motivated by the need for a more adaptable, precise, and robust robotic manipulation system that can effectively handle the complexities of real-world contact-rich tasks through a principled compositional approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoStream is designed as a hierarchical framework that divides long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation into two primary levels: stage compilation and per-stage action composition. This structure integrates foundation models and diverse sensing modalities into a set of composable core behaviors, all operating through a shared SE(3) interface.

#### 3.1. Scene Understanding and Stage Compilation

The process begins with an **open-vocabulary scene parser**. This module processes free-form language instructions (L) and RGB-D observations to generate a `Scene Summary`. The `Scene Summary` encapsulates critical information such as object poses, grasp frames, calibrated task frames, point clouds, affordances, symbolic predicates, and the robot's state. The underlying perception stack utilizes components like FoundationStereo for dense scene point clouds, M2T2 for 6-DoF grasp prediction, Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 for object detection and language grounding, and SAM 3 for per-object mask generation.

Following the `Scene Summary` generation, a **contact-aware policy compiler** converts the task into a sequence of typed stages. Each stage `k` is defined by two specifications:
- **Stage Specification (S_k)**: Contains the relational objective, reference frames, execution template, guard conditions, and recovery rules necessary to instantiate the active behaviors for that stage.
- **Composition Specification (C_k)**: Details how the outputs of different behaviors should be combined, including the composition frame, axis ownership, residual bounds, and fallback behavior. These specifications configure pre-defined behavior templates and controller profiles, rather than generating continuous control parameters or thresholds directly.

#### 3.2. Per-Stage Parallel Action Generation

At runtime, for each active stage, CoStream orchestrates three independent behaviors that run at their respective natural rates and contribute distinct components to a shared SE(3) interface:

- **Semantic Behavior (Task-Frame Anchor)**: This behavior produces `W_T_k_I`, a single anchor transform from the world frame (W) to a stage-specific task frame (I). This anchor is updated once per stage or upon reobservation. It grounds the relational objective and reference frames specified in `S_k` by minimizing a weighted SE(3) alignment objective. Its inputs can include LLM/VLM outputs, keypoints, calibrated fixtures, templates, or geometric constraints. The anchor establishes a task frame, not a trajectory, which facilitates the decomposition of motion and compliance along its axes, contributing to SE(3)-equivariance.

- **Predictive Behavior (Nominal Motion)**: This behavior generates `I_T_k_traj(t)`, a continuous nominal motion expressed in the task frame (I). It employs a video world model, conditioned on current visual observations (`I_t`) and a step-specific instruction (`L_step`), to sample multiple rollouts. A VLM critic then evaluates and selects the rollout (`V*`) that best satisfies semantic and safety constraints. A 3D keypoint tracker subsequently lifts `V*` to an object-centric reference relative to `W_T_k_I`, forming the nominal motion prior. This behavior focuses on providing a reasonable motion plan, not precise contact control.

- **Reactive Behavior (Tactile Residual and Force Loop)**: Operating at a high frequency (25 Hz), this behavior contributes a tactile residual to the composer and manages a force control loop. It also flags contact events such as slip, excessive force, or loss of progress for a runtime supervisor.
 - **Tactile Residual**: Addresses in-hand slip using a GelSight Mini tactile sensor. It tracks the object's pose relative to its nominal in-hand pose using NormalFlow, a per-frame inverse-compositional estimator. The computed rigid-body transform `ΔT_k_tac(t)` compensates for this measured sliding online, ensuring that semantic and predictive targets remain valid relative to the grasped object.
 - **Force Loop**: Implemented by the compliant controller, this loop regulates contact forces using the robot's estimated end-effector wrench. Depending on the stage, it can apply Cartesian impedance (a virtual spring) to maintain a target force during sustained contact (e.g., wiping) or position-based admittance to bound contact force during insertion, mitigating jamming or damage.

#### 3.3. Multi-Rate SE(3) Action Composer

At each control tick, the action composer fuses the outputs from these behaviors into a single end-effector pose command, `W_T_k_cmd(t)`. The `W_T_k_I` anchor is latched for the active stage, `I_T_k_traj(t)` is interpolated, and the latest `ΔT_k_tac(t)` from the tactile sensor is used. The composition follows two steps:
1. The nominal motion is anchored in the world frame: `W_T_k_nom(t) = W_T_k_I * I_T_k_traj(t)`.
2. The tactile residual is applied via right-multiplication: `W_T_k_cmd(t) = W_T_k_nom(t) * ΔT_k_tac(t)`.
The use of right-multiplication ensures that `ΔT_k_tac(t)`, expressed in the composition frame `F_k`, effectively cancels in-hand sliding. This real-time recomputation from latest inputs minimizes drift. If a behavior component is missing or stale, a per-stage fallback (`H_k`) is applied, allowing graceful degradation.

#### 3.4. Controller Compiler and Compliant Execution

The `W_T_k_cmd(t)` from the composer is then executed by a low-level compliant controller. The **controller compiler** translates `S_k` and `C_k` into robot-level parameters, including Cartesian stiffness and damping, impedance/admittance axis selection, force/torque and residual bounds, and guard/recovery rules. These parameters are instantiated from a library of calibrated profiles, ensuring that the VLM does not directly generate control gains but rather selects appropriate predefined profiles. In free space, the controller uses high stiffness. During contact, stiffness is lowered, or admittance control is engaged on axes marked compliant in `C_k`, with force limits enforced by wrench estimates. Runtime guards monitor progress and force, triggering stage advancement, recovery, reobservation, or replanning as needed.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

The experimental setup consists of two Franka Emika Panda robot arms. One arm is equipped with a GelSight Mini tactile sensor for precise manipulation, while the other uses a standard parallel-jaw gripper. External visual perception is provided by calibrated RealSense D415 cameras.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CoStream involved real-world experiments on a variety of long-horizon manipulation tasks, categorized into high-precision assembly and everyday manipulation. The results consistently demonstrated CoStream's capabilities relative to established baselines and in isolation through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Success Rates

(Table 1 in the paper)
- **High-Precision Assembly Tasks**: CoStream achieved high success rates across all four tested tasks: Drill Insertion (15/15), RAM Insertion (14/15), CPU Placement (14/15), and GPU Insertion (15/15). In contrast, both baseline methods, VoxPoser and π0.5, consistently failed to complete these tasks (0/15 successes for both on all assembly tasks). This indicates CoStream's ability to handle tight clearances and contact-rich scenarios effectively.
- **Everyday Manipulation Tasks**: CoStream also demonstrated superior performance on everyday tasks compared to π0.5. It achieved 5/15 for "Turn on the Lamp" (vs. 0/15 for π0.5), 8/15 for "Wipe the Whiteboard" (vs. 0/15 for π0.5), 13/15 for "Cup → Plate" (vs. 4/15 for π0.5), and 14/15 for "Clothes → Box" (vs. 3/15 for π0.5). The improvements were particularly notable in object transfer tasks.

#### 4.2. Failure Analysis of Baselines

The paper provides insights into why the baselines failed:
- **VoxPoser**: This modular vision-language planning pipeline typically failed before making contact. Its reliance on affordance maps generated from a single initial visual snapshot meant it could not adapt to real-time deviations in geometry or contact. Once a trajectory was planned, any misalignment led to the part jamming against the target.
- **π0.5**: This monolithic VLA policy failed during contact. It mapped observations directly to actions and was unable to differentiate genuine contact events from perceptual ambiguities. This resulted in high-frequency, unstable motions upon contact, preventing successful alignment and insertion.

#### 4.3. Failure Analysis of CoStream

The two instances of failure for CoStream were analyzed:
- The single CPU placement failure occurred when the chip rotated beyond the tactile contact patch, preventing the reactive behavior from registering and correcting the deviation.
- The single RAM insertion failure happened when the downward force briefly exceeded the compiled force-limit envelope during the locking stage.
These failures occurred at the operational boundaries of the reactive behavior's sensing or compliance, rather than indicating a fundamental flaw in the composition mechanism.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Rollouts

(Figure 4 in the paper)
- **Drill Insertion**: CoStream demonstrated successful, compliant adaptation that kept the drill bit centered within a 0.5 mm clearance, preventing jamming.
- **Computer Assembly Sequence (CPU, RAM, GPU)**: The system performed a continuous assembly sequence without any retraining between subtasks. It adapted its behavior and control loops from delicate, force-sensitive CPU handling to high-force RAM locking purely by updating object-centric constraints, demonstrating zero-shot task transfer across diverse physical requirements.

#### 4.5. Robustness to Human Perturbation

(Figure 5 in the paper)
CoStream's robustness was tested by manually rotating the held object within the gripper during insertion, simulating an unexpected slip. The reactive behavior successfully detected these human-induced deviations from the reference normal map and autonomously reoriented the end-effector to realign the object. This capability was demonstrated across RAM, CPU, and GPU insertion tasks, confirming the framework's ability to recover from real-time external disturbances.

#### 4.6. Mechanism Ablation Study

(Table 2 in the paper)
An ablation study on the Drill Insertion task revealed the critical role of the reactive behavior. When the reactive behavior was removed (disabling tactile and force feedback), the success rate plummeted from 15/15 to 3/15. This outcome underscores that the reactive component is essential for rejecting angular misalignments and maintaining sub-millimeter precision during contact-rich operations.

Overall, the results indicate that CoStream provides a robust and generalizable approach to complex, contact-rich robotic manipulation, outperforming existing paradigms in these challenging scenarios and demonstrating effective integration of multimodal sensing for real-time adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoStream represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a novel approach to achieving both high precision and broad generalization in complex, contact-rich tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Addressing the Precision-Generalization Gap

The primary impact of CoStream is its capacity to bridge the long-standing gap between the high precision typically offered by classical, task-specific pipelines and the broad generalization aspired to by monolithic end-to-end learning policies. By successfully demonstrating near-perfect performance in sub-millimeter clearance assembly tasks while also transferring to everyday manipulation, CoStream offers a paradigm that overcomes the limitations of both traditional and modern approaches.

#### 5.2. Validation of Behavior Composition for Robotics

CoStream validates the principle that complex robotic behaviors can emerge from the composition of simpler, independent, sensor-grounded behaviors. This contrasts with the prevailing assumption in much of current robotics research that complex tasks necessitate complex, monolithic control systems. This finding could inspire further research into modular, compositional architectures for robotic intelligence.

#### 5.3. Effective Integration of Multimodal Sensing

The framework showcases an effective method for orchestrating diverse sensing modalities—vision, language, tactile, and force feedback—into a unified control system. Each behavior leverages the modality most appropriate for its role, operating at its natural rate. This structured integration, particularly the real-time utilization of tactile and force feedback for contact-rich interactions, is a notable advancement over vision-centric foundation models that often struggle with the intricacies of physical contact.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability

CoStream's reactive behavior, coupled with its compliant control, provides inherent robustness to unexpected perturbations and real-time deviations. The demonstrated ability to recover from simulated in-gripper slip is critical for deploying robots in unstructured or dynamic environments where perfect initial conditions cannot be guaranteed. This enhances the reliability and safety of robotic systems.

#### 5.5. Zero-Shot Generalization and Task Transfer

The ability of CoStream to perform a continuous computer assembly sequence (CPU, RAM, GPU) without retraining, by merely updating object-centric constraints, highlights its potential for zero-shot generalization. This indicates that the framework can adapt to diverse contact regimes and task requirements using a fixed set of behaviors and control loops, significantly reducing the engineering effort and data requirements typically associated with new tasks.

#### 5.6. Implications for Future Robot Control Architectures

CoStream suggests a promising direction for future robot control systems, moving away from purely monolithic or rigid pipelined designs towards more flexible, composable architectures. This could lead to more interpretable, auditable, and maintainable robot policies, where individual behaviors can be developed, tested, and improved independently.

#### 5.7. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its strengths, CoStream has acknowledged limitations that point to important avenues for future research. Its reliance on the quality of upstream perception (pose estimates, visual prediction) means errors propagate. The current focus on structured rigid-object manipulation with calibrated perception and explicit stage specifications suggests future work could involve extending the framework to deformable/articulated objects, dexterous in-hand manipulation, and settings with ambiguous goals. Furthermore, the reactive behavior's limits concerning tactile coverage and end-effector wrench estimation offer opportunities for improvement. The use of proprietary models in the perception stack also highlights a challenge for full reproducibility and offline use. Addressing these limitations could further broaden the applicability and impact of compositional behavior frameworks in robotics.
