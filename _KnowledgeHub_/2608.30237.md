# Motus2: A Self-Evolving General World Model for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Motus2 is a self-evolving general world model for dexterous manipulation in which one shared-parameter video-action transformer exposes three interfaces: a policy proposing action chunks, an action-conditioned simulator predicting their visual consequences, and a value evaluator scoring task progress. Trained on 130K hours of egocentric human video plus robot data, it reaches 84% average real-robot success versus 51% without robot-domain mid-training.

## Problem

- Existing world models bolt an action head onto a simulator without coupling prediction and evaluation into a closed decision-and-learning loop for policy improvement.
- Imitation from curated action-supervised demonstrations gives no notion of whether an action is good, and embodiment-aligned robot data is expensive to collect at scale.
- Egocentric human priors transfer poorly: contact events like slip and grasp formation are visually ambiguous, and hands occlude objects, breaking single-observation Markov assumptions.

## Method

- An ==action-first attention mask== over ==chunk-autoregressive blocks== factorizes one shared backbone into policy, action-conditioned simulator, and read-only value query, so action tokens never see their own future video.
- ==Trajectory-dependent loss gates== route only successful demonstrations to action supervision, sending failed and suboptimal runs to dynamics and ==relative-progress value learning==; ==DiffusionNFT== converts branch values into ==flow-matching== policy updates.
- A staged curriculum scales monocular to stereo egocentric pretraining then robot mid-training, plus a lightweight ==tactile expert== refining sub-chunks from cached KV and predicting future force.

## Results

- On five real dexterous tasks, mid-trained Motus2 averages **84%** success over 20 rollouts each, versus **51%** for egocentric-pretrain-only SFT and **0%** for both the VLA baseline and WAN-SFT.
- Best-of-N planning adds **2.5 points** over the **65.0%** base policy and DiffusionNFT MBRL adds **7.5 points**; combined they reach **75.0%** average success across two tasks.
- Global autoregression beats hybrid memory on occlusion probes (**78%** versus **52%** in simulation, **57.5%** versus **25%** real), and tactile feedback lifts contact-rich tasks from **60.0%** to **72.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy, simulator, and evaluator can be conditional query modes of one parameter set; the value interface, not the action head, is what enables self-improvement.
> - Failure data is not waste: gating losses per trajectory lets bad runs teach dynamics and value while never contaminating the imitation target.
> - Full-history autoregression beat compressed memory tokens here, suggesting context compression still discards task-critical evidence despite its bounded-cost advantage.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

General embodied agents aim to integrate perception, prediction, action, evaluation, and improvement into a unified system. World models have emerged as a promising approach for this goal by learning environmental dynamics and predicting future states. However, existing world models frequently treat action generation as a separate output head appended to a simulator, often lacking a closed decision-and-learning loop for continuous policy improvement. Contemporary robot foundation models are predominantly trained on curated, action-supervised datasets. This approach is resource-intensive for data collection and does not inherently provide a mechanism for the policy to discern desirable from undesirable actions or to improve from its own interaction outcomes.

Motus2 positions itself within the field of General World Models (GWMs), which seek to unify the understanding of the current world, imagination of action-conditioned futures, and action grounded by external feedback. Previous work, such as Motus, began to unify policy (world-action model) and simulator (action-conditioned world model) interfaces into a single shared model. Motus2 extends this foundation by explicitly integrating a third interface: an evaluator, realized as a value model, which assesses predicted outcomes to provide signals for policy improvement. This integration addresses a core challenge in model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL): how to couple action decision-making, world simulation, and value estimation within a unified model to leverage heterogeneous interaction data (e.g., expert demonstrations, successful trajectories, suboptimal executions, and failures) for comprehensive policy, dynamics, and value learning.

The work also addresses challenges in dexterous manipulation, which requires coordinating high degrees-of-freedom hands, reasoning about discontinuous contact dynamics, and managing partial observability due to self-occlusion. It builds upon research in scalable data sources, particularly egocentric human data (monocular and stereo), for learning interaction priors. The paper acknowledges the difficulty of transferring these priors to robot execution, motivating the need for robust robot-domain adaptation and incorporating modalities like tactile feedback to disambiguate contact-critical events. By proposing a self-evolving GWM that integrates these capabilities through model and data scaling, Motus2 aims to advance the development of more capable and autonomous dexterous manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of Motus2 is to develop a self-evolving General World Model for dexterous manipulation. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Enabling Self-Evolution and Policy Improvement:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing robot policies, primarily trained via supervised imitation, lack an inherent mechanism to improve from their own experiences or to distinguish between beneficial and detrimental actions. Collecting sufficiently diverse and high-quality robot demonstrations for all possible scenarios is prohibitively expensive and often leads to static policies.
 - **Objective:** To formulate a system where the model can propose candidate actions, predict their consequences, evaluate the desirability of these predicted outcomes, and use this feedback to refine its own policy in a closed-loop manner.

2. **Unifying Core Control and Prediction Interfaces:**
 - **Motivation:** Many existing world models treat action generation as an auxiliary output, separate from the core simulation or evaluation logic. A truly general embodied agent, however, should integrate the capabilities of action proposal, consequence prediction, and outcome evaluation causally within a single, coherent system.
 - **Objective:** To integrate a policy (world-action model), a simulator (action-conditioned world model), and an evaluator (value model) into a *single model with shared parameters*. This aims to leverage shared representations for perception, imagination, and decision-making, allowing the model to determine *what action to attempt*, *what will happen under that action*, and *whether the predicted outcome is desirable*. A key challenge is to ensure proper information flow without exposing future observations to action prediction during policy inference.

3. **Leveraging Scalable Data for Dexterous Manipulation:**
 - **Motivation:** Dexterous manipulation policies require extensive interaction priors. Egocentric human data offers a scalable source of diverse manipulation behaviors and hand-object interactions. However, transferring these human-centric priors to robot embodiments is challenging due to domain gaps, ambiguities in vision (e.g., contact), and partial observability.
 - **Objective:** To develop a hierarchical data-scaling curriculum that progresses from large-scale monocular egocentric data to synchronized stereo egocentric data, followed by targeted robot-domain adaptation using robot trajectories and human-robot alignment data. This aims to progressively ground human priors into effective robot execution.

4. **Differentiated Learning from Heterogeneous Interactions:**
 - **Motivation:** Not all interaction data is equally valuable for every learning objective. Expert demonstrations are suitable for action imitation, while failed or suboptimal interactions contain valuable information about environmental dynamics and task-progress assessment.
 - **Objective:** To design a supervision routing mechanism that selectively applies loss functions based on the type of trajectory. Curated successful demonstrations are used for action learning, while failed and suboptimal interactions primarily contribute to dynamics modeling and value learning, thereby preventing the policy from imitating undesirable behaviors.

5. **Addressing Specific Challenges in Dexterous Control:**
 - **Motivation:** Dexterous manipulation often involves long-horizon tasks with partial observability where relevant information may be occluded or appear much later. Additionally, contact-critical events (e.g., grasping, slipping) are often ambiguous from visual data alone.
 - **Objective:** To investigate and implement advanced temporal context mechanisms beyond a simple sliding window (e.g., global autoregression, hybrid memory) to manage long-horizon partial observability. Additionally, to incorporate tactile feedback through a lightweight expert system for contact-aware control and prediction, enhancing physical grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Motus2 is designed as a self-evolving general world model for dexterous manipulation, built upon a unified architecture that scales both model capabilities and data utilization.

#### 3.1. General World Model Architecture

The core of Motus2 is a single neural network with shared parameters, conceptually derived from UniDiffuser-style joint video-action modeling. This model exposes three distinct control interfaces:
1. **Policy (World-Action Model, WAM):** Generates executable action chunks ($A_t$) based on the current context ($c_t$), which includes language instructions, current robot proprioception, and an encoded visual history.
2. **Simulator (Action-Conditioned World Model, AC-WM):** Predicts future visual observations ($Z_t$) given the current context and a proposed action chunk.
3. **Evaluator (Value Model, VM):** Assesses the predicted outcomes ($Z_t$) by estimating their task progress ($Y_t$), conditioned on the context, proposed actions, and predicted visual consequences.

These interfaces are mathematically represented by a factored density: $p_θ(A_t, Z_t, Y_t | c_t) = π_θ(A_t | c_t) \cdot p_{wm_θ}(Z_t | c_t, A_t) \cdot p_{vm_θ}(Y_t | c_t, A_t, Z_t)$. This factorization ensures a causal dependency: action prediction occurs first, then future video prediction based on actions, and finally value estimation based on both.

#### 3.2. Training Curriculum and Supervision Routing

The training of Motus2 follows a staged curriculum:
- **Pre-training (Human Data):**
 - **Stage 1 (Video-only):** Initializes the visual backbone from a foundation video diffusion model (Wan 2.2-TI2V-5B). It trains the video pathway using conditional flow matching on a large corpus of low- and high-resolution monocular egocentric videos. Action and value components are inactive.
 - **Stage 2 (Joint Video-Action):** Continues from Stage 1, introducing synchronized stereo egocentric data. It jointly trains left-view, right-view, and action tokens, with bidirectional visibility within action chunks.
- **Mid-training (Robot-Domain Adaptation):** This crucial stage grounds human priors into the robot domain. It introduces two key mechanisms:
 - **Action-First Mask:** Replaces the bidirectional mask with an action-first mask. This mask ensures that action tokens cannot access future video or value tokens from their own chunk, while future video tokens can read actions, and value queries can read both. This enforces the causal dependency required for control and planning.
 - **Trajectory-Dependent Supervision Routing:** To leverage diverse interaction data effectively, a loss gating mechanism is employed.
 - **Policy Mode:** Jointly supervises action and future video; *only curated successful robot trajectories* activate action supervision.
 - **Simulation Mode:** Supervises only action-conditioned future prediction, using recorded actions as clean conditioning variables rather than imitation targets. This mode processes failed and suboptimal trajectories to learn dynamics.
 - **Evaluation Mode:** Supervises only the value readout, keeping actions and video clean. This mode processes failed and suboptimal trajectories, along with task-irrelevant interactions, to learn outcome assessment.
 This selective routing ensures that the model learns from failures and suboptimal behaviors for dynamics and value modeling without learning to reproduce them in its policy.
- **Post-training:** Specializes the mid-trained checkpoint for target-robot control, with extensions for MBRL, long-history context, and tactile feedback.

#### 3.3. Value-Guided Closed-Loop Self-Evolution

The unified policy-simulator-evaluator chain enables self-evolution through value-guided mechanisms:
- **Progress-Based Value Learning:** The evaluator is trained using a relative-progress formulation. Successful trajectory segments are assigned positive rewards (e.g., $ \frac{\Delta t}{T-t} $), while failed or task-irrelevant segments receive negative rewards (e.g., $ -\frac{\Delta t}{T-t} $). These values are discretized into categorical targets for training.
- **Model-Based Planning (Test-time):** At inference, the model proposes N candidate action chunks using the policy, simulates their visual consequences using the simulator, and evaluates their task progress using the evaluator. The chunk with the highest predicted value is selected and executed (Best-of-N planning) in a receding-horizon fashion.
- **Policy Optimization (MBRL):** Evaluator scores are fed back to improve the action pathway using DiffusionNFT. This method converts scalar branch scores into a flow-matching policy update. High-value candidates guide the policy toward those actions, while low-value candidates guide it away. This process occurs asynchronously, with separate pipelines for rollout, value scoring, reference construction, and policy optimization to maximize throughput.

#### 3.4. Working Memory Mechanisms

To address long-horizon partial observability, Motus2 implements three context mechanisms:
- **Sliding Window (Default):** A fixed-length window of recent observations is maintained in a KV cache, with older frames evicted. Temporal RoPE is window-relative.
- **Global Autoregression:** This variant retains all preceding clean visual latents from the entire episode, using episode-level temporal coordinates. This leads to attention costs that grow with episode length.
- **Hybrid Working Memory:** Inspired by MemoryWAM, this mechanism retains initial anchor frames and a recent observation window at full resolution, while representing older intermediate observations using persistent memory tokens.
These long-history variants are trained using variable-length episode packing to efficiently handle diverse sequence lengths.

#### 3.5. Tactile Expert

A lightweight tactile expert is integrated to provide contact-aware control, especially for ambiguous vision-only scenarios.
- **Architecture:** A 30-layer transformer that reuses the backbone’s intermediate action chunk and detached layer-wise KV cache. It self-attends over tactile and intermediate-action tokens and reads cached K/V states from the corresponding backbone layer.
- **Tactile-conditioned Action Refinement:** The backbone first denoises an action chunk to an intermediate noise level ($\sigma_c$). Immediately before each short sub-chunk is executed, the tactile expert uses the latest tactile window to refine the action, performing the final denoising step.
- **Tactile Prediction:** The expert also predicts future force signals following an action sub-chunk, using a flow-matching objective. This prediction is used during training to supervise how contact evolves.
The tactile expert processes force-torque vectors and, for some hands, deformation images from finger pads. Its losses update only the expert-specific parameters, not the shared video backbone or evaluator.

#### 3.6. Robot Platforms and Data Collection

The system is instantiated on fully biomimetic platforms featuring stereo vision, dual arms, dual dexterous hands (Wuji and Sharpa Wave), and tactile sensing. A large-scale egocentric corpus of approximately 130K hours (monocular and stereo) is used for pre-training, complemented by robot-specific trajectories and human-robot alignment data for mid-training. The data undergoes pose standardization, quality-aware temporal segmentation (BoxTrim), and vision-language annotation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to address four research questions regarding egocentric human pretraining, model-based policy improvement, long-horizon context mechanisms, and tactile feedback.

#### 4.1. Egocentric Human Pretraining and Robot Control (RQ1)

- **Comparison:** The study compared policies initialized from different stages: `π_0.5` (a contemporary baseline without egocentric pretraining), WAN-SFT (SFT only, no egocentric pretraining), Pretrain-SFT (egocentric pretraining followed by SFT), and Motus2 (Midtrain-SFT) (egocentric pretraining, robot-domain mid-training, and SFT). All methods used matched target-task SFT data and evaluation protocols across five dexterous manipulation tasks: Place Ball, Multi-Finger, Attach Eraser, Screw Bulb, and Put Phone.
- **Results:**
 - `π_0.5` and WAN-SFT achieved 0% average success rate (SR).
 - Pretrain-SFT achieved 51% average SR, demonstrating the benefit of egocentric human pretraining.
 - Motus2 (Midtrain-SFT) further improved the average SR to 84%, indicating a 33-point gain from subsequent robot-domain mid-training. Specific task performance for Motus2 (Midtrain-SFT) included 100% on Place Ball and Attach Eraser, 90% on Screw Bulb, 70% on Multi-Finger, and 60% on Put Phone.
- **Conclusion:** Egocentric human pretraining, combined with robot-domain mid-training, significantly enhances downstream robot control performance.

#### 4.2. Scaling Laws of Stereo Egocentric Human Data

- **Methodology:** The paper investigated the scaling trend for synchronized stereo egocentric data by training models on nested subsets (2k, 4k, 10k, 20k raw recording hours). Held-out human-action prediction error was used as the generalization metric.
- **Results:** The optimal validation error decreased monotonically as the pretraining corpus size increased. A log-linear relationship was observed: $L_{val}^*(D) \approx 0.101 - 0.005 \ln D$.
- **Conclusion:** The scaling trends previously identified for monocular egocentric data extend to stereo observations within the measured range, suggesting continued benefits from increasing stereo egocentric data volume.

#### 4.3. Model-Based Policy Improvement and Test-Time Planning (RQ2)

- **Comparison:** A factorial design evaluated four variants starting from the same target-robot checkpoint: Motus2 (baseline), Motus2 + Planning (Best-of-N), Motus2 + MBRL (DiffusionNFT), and Motus2 + MBRL + Planning. Experiments were conducted on Put Phone and Multi-Finger tasks.
- **Results:**
 - Motus2 achieved a 65.0% average SR.
 - Motus2 + Planning showed a 2.5-point gain, reaching 67.5% average SR.
 - Motus2 + MBRL achieved a 7.5-point gain, reaching 72.5% average SR.
 - Combining both, Motus2 + MBRL + Planning, yielded a total 10.0-point gain, achieving 75.0% average SR.
- **Conclusion:** Both model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) through policy optimization and test-time planning (Best-of-N candidate selection) independently contribute to improved success rates, and their combination provides additive benefits. Qualitative analysis of value trajectories indicated that the value model successfully tracked task progress, rising during successful execution and decreasing upon failures.

#### 4.4. Long-Horizon Context Mechanisms (RQ3)

- **Comparison:** The study compared global autoregression (retaining full history) and hybrid working memory (MemoryWAM-style) on long-horizon memory probes: Find Square and Press Button. Evaluations were performed in both simulation and on a real robot.
- **Results:**
 - **Simulation:** Global autoregression achieved 78% average SR (84% on Find Square, 72% on Press Button), while hybrid memory achieved 52% average SR (64% on Find Square, 40% on Press Button).
 - **Real Robot:** Global autoregression achieved 57.5% average SR (65% on Find Square, 50% on Press Button), outperforming hybrid memory at 25.0% average SR (30% on Find Square, 20% on Press Button).
- **Conclusion:** Global autoregression, which retains the complete observation history, demonstrated superior performance over hybrid working memory for tasks requiring long-horizon context in both simulation and real-robot environments.

#### 4.5. Effect of Tactile Feedback (RQ4)

- **Comparison:** Two variants were compared: one without the tactile expert (`w/o Tactile`) and one with the complete tactile refinement and prediction objective (`w/ Tactile`). Both started from the same mid-trained checkpoint and used the same post-training data. Tasks included Pull Out the Paper Cup and Tear Paper, both involving contact-sensitive manipulation.
- **Results:**
 - `w/o Tactile` achieved 60.0% average SR (65% on Pull Out Paper Cup, 55% on Tear Paper).
 - `w/ Tactile` achieved 72.5% average SR (75% on Pull Out Paper Cup, 70% on Tear Paper), representing a 12.5-point gain.
- **Conclusion:** The lightweight tactile expert improves performance in contact-rich and fine-grained manipulation tasks, indicating the benefit of tactile feedback for physical grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Motus2 presents a comprehensive framework for developing self-evolving general world models in dexterous manipulation, demonstrating a unified approach to core robotic intelligence functions.

**Significance:**
- **Unified General World Model:** A key contribution is the demonstration that policy, simulator, and evaluator interfaces can be realized as conditional functions of a *single shared-parameter video-action model*. This departs from approaches that treat these as separate systems, promoting architectural parsimony and potentially enabling more coherent internal representations.
- **Closed-Loop Self-Evolution:** The integration of value-guided model-based reinforcement learning within this unified framework establishes a concrete path towards robotic systems that can continuously improve their policies by predicting consequences and evaluating outcomes, moving beyond the limitations of purely imitation-based learning.
- **Hierarchical Data Scaling:** The research validates a hierarchical egocentric human-data scaling strategy, from large-scale monocular and stereo human data to robot-domain adaptation. This provides a methodology for effectively leveraging abundant human interaction data to bootstrap complex robot skills.
- **Intelligent Data Utilization:** Motus2 effectively uses heterogeneous interaction data. It distinguishes between curated expert demonstrations for action learning and failed or suboptimal interactions for dynamics and value learning, thereby extracting maximum information from diverse experiences without teaching undesirable behaviors.
- **Enhanced Dexterity:** The demonstrated benefits of explicit long-history context mechanisms (global autoregression) and contact-sensitive tactile feedback underscore critical components for robust dexterous manipulation in complex, partially observable, and contact-rich scenarios.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Autonomous Robots:** The self-evolving capability could lead to more autonomous robotic systems that can adapt to novel situations and recover from errors without constant human intervention, increasing their utility in unstructured environments.
- **Foundation for Generalist Robots:** The unified GWM architecture could serve as a foundational component for generalist robots, capable of performing a wide array of tasks by integrating diverse sensory inputs and decision-making processes.
- **Reduced Data Requirements for Robot Learning:** By effectively transferring priors from large-scale egocentric human data, Motus2's data scaling approach could mitigate the intensive and costly data collection often associated with training robot policies from scratch.
- **Advancements in Multimodal Control:** The successful integration of visual, proprioceptive, language, and tactile signals within a generative and decision-making framework pushes the boundaries of multimodal control for dexterous robots.

**Limitations identified by the authors:**
- **Scalability of Tactile Supervision:** The primary limitation is the difficulty in scaling wearable tactile supervision across different robot embodiments due to significant morphological gaps between human and current dexterous robot hands. This leads to challenges in glove patterning, signal noise (e.g., from internal fabric contact), and limits the direct transferability of human tactile data to robots.
- **Morphological Gap:** The geometric differences between human hands and even biomimetic robot hands restrict the reliability of cross-embodiment tactile learning and data transfer.

**Future Work suggested by the authors:**
- **Tactile Hardware and Data:** Advances in wearable tactile materials and fabrication could enable scalable, whole-hand tactile sensing for multimodal egocentric human data collection.
- **Anthropomorphic Hand Design:** Continued progress in designing robot hands that are more morphologically similar to human hands could narrow the embodiment gap, facilitating more direct cross-embodiment learning.
- **Model Scaling:** Stronger video foundation models and unified multimodal models could provide richer generative priors, longer-horizon predictive dynamics, and more faithful multimodal simulation, serving as a basis for even more capable GWMs that unify all signals (visual, language, action, tactile, value) for continuous improvement.
