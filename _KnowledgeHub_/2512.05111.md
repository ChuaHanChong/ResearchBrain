# ARM-Thinker: Reinforcing Multimodal Generative Reward Models with Agentic Tool Use and Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Lab and collaborating institutions introduced ARM-Thinker, a framework that integrates agentic tool use and visual reasoning into multimodal generative reward models. This approach allows models to actively verify information, leading to improved reliability, interpretability, and an average performance gain of 16.2% on reward modeling benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional multimodal reward models often hallucinate or lack fine-grained visual grounding, leading to unreliable judgments.
- Existing reward models cannot actively use external tools for verification (e.g., image cropping, document retrieval), limiting their ability to handle complex, evidence-grounded tasks.
- The absence of benchmarks specifically designed to evaluate multi-step, tool-augmented reasoning in reward models hinders progress in this area.

## Method

- Developed an agentic reward model with a "==think-act-verify==" loop that dynamically invokes multimodal tools for evidence gathering.
- Integrated various tools, including instruction-following checks, image cropping/zooming for fine-grained visual inspection, and document retrieval for long contexts.
- Employed a multi-stage training approach with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a unique reward design to encourage both tool use and accurate judgment.

## Results

- ARM-Thinker-**7B** achieved an average improvement of **16.2%** on reward modeling benchmarks, outperforming the baseline and matching or exceeding proprietary models like GPT-4o.
- The model demonstrated strong emergent tool-use capabilities, with a **9.6%** average improvement on visual tool-use benchmarks without explicit tool-use demonstrations.
- Training led to generalized improvements in multimodal reasoning, showing an average gain of **4.2%** across benchmarks like MMMU and MathVista, with specific gains of **10.9%** on WeMath.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equipping reward models with agentic tool-use capabilities enables them to actively seek and verify evidence, significantly enhancing judgment reliability and interpretability.
> - An adaptive, multi-stage reinforcement learning strategy with distinct reward functions is critical for effectively training agentic reward models that balance tool exploration and task accuracy.
> - The process of training for evidence-grounded verification tasks can also lead to emergent improvements in general multimodal reasoning abilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of ARM-Thinker is situated at the crucial intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within AI: Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), Reward Models (RMs), tool-augmented AI agents, and multimodal reasoning.

**a. The Role of Reward Models in AI Alignment:**
Reward models are fundamental to aligning powerful generative AI systems, especially LLMs and LVLMs, with human preferences and desired behaviors. They provide a scalar score or preference ranking that guides reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or other preference-based optimization techniques. Historically, reward models have struggled with accurately evaluating complex, open-ended, and fine-grained tasks due to limitations such as hallucination, weak visual grounding, and an inability to perform active verification. This often leads to rewarding fluent but factually incorrect or unsupported responses.

**b. Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
The paper identifies two main categories of existing approaches:
- **Rule-based verifiers:** These are brittle, struggle with paraphrasing, cannot assign partial credit, and are impractical for subjective or complex ground truths.
- **Model-based generative reward models:** These typically operate in a single pass without actively seeking evidence. This leads to hallucinated rationales, biases (e.g., position/length biases), and a lack of means to retrieve or verify cited content. They optimize for broad coverage via scoring rather than evidence-grounded reasoning, lacking a "think-act-verify" loop.

**c. Emergence of Tool-Augmented AI Agents:**
A significant trend in recent AI research is the integration of external tools into LLMs and LVLMs, transforming them into "agents" capable of interacting with environments, executing code, retrieving information, or manipulating images. This "thinking with images" paradigm, where models dynamically invoke tools for image zooming, cropping, or external knowledge retrieval, has shown promise in enhancing reasoning and grounding. However, integrating these capabilities effectively into *reward models* has been largely unexplored, as existing multimodal RMs primarily focus on improving accuracy through better training data or long-horizon reasoning *without* tool-calling abilities. The intuition has been that current RM tasks are too simple, allowing RMs to rely on improved perception and reasoning alone.

**d. ARM-Thinker's Position:**
ARM-Thinker directly addresses these limitations by introducing an "agentic" paradigm to multimodal reward modeling. It transforms a passive reward scorer into an active, interactive agent that can autonomously invoke tools (like image cropping, document retrieval, or instruction verification) to gather verifiable evidence before making a judgment. This capability is novel for reward models, filling a critical gap in the research landscape. By allowing RMs to "think, act, and verify," ARM-Thinker aims to make their judgments more reliable, interpretable, and evidence-grounded, especially for complex multimodal tasks that demand fine-grained perception and multi-step reasoning across diverse data formats (e.g., long documents, high-resolution images). This work pushes the frontier by demonstrating that agentic capabilities are not just for generative AI but are also crucial for robust evaluative AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of ARM-Thinker is to develop an **Agentic multimodal Reward Model** that can autonomously invoke external tools to ground its judgments in verifiable evidence, thereby overcoming critical limitations of existing reward models.

The motivation for this research stems from several key challenges in current multimodal AI systems:

1. **Hallucination and Weak Visual Grounding:** Traditional reward models, operating in a single pass, often generate fluent but factually incorrect rationales or fail to adequately ground their judgments in fine-grained visual details. This reduces their reliability, particularly for tasks where precise factual verification is essential.
2. **Inability to Use Tools for Verification:** Existing reward models lack the capability to interact with external tools to actively retrieve, localize, or verify information. This passive nature prevents them from cross-referencing multi-page evidence, inspecting fine visual details through cropping/zooming, or validating reasoning claims, which are crucial for complex multimodal reasoning tasks.
3. **Increasing Complexity of Multimodal Tasks:** Modern AI tasks are becoming more cross-modal, open-ended, and fine-grained (e.g., long-document QA, multi-step instruction following). Evaluating correctness in such scenarios requires sophisticated semantic understanding and explicit grounding in verifiable evidence, moving beyond simple string matching. Judgments must assess both reasoning quality and factual support across diverse modalities and contexts.
4. **Agentic Evaluation as a Planning Problem:** Robust evaluation of multimodal tasks is inherently a planning problem. A judge model needs to decide *when* to think, *which tool* to call, *how to parameterize it*, and *how to integrate* intermediate results into a coherent, causal chain without succumbing to hallucinations. Current reward models are not designed for this deliberative, interactive process.
5. **Lack of Specialized Benchmarks:** Existing reward model benchmarks primarily rely on static QA pairs and cannot adequately assess the intermediate steps of tool use and evidence gathering, making it difficult to evaluate agentic reward models effectively.

In response to these challenges, ARM-Thinker aims to:
- **Enable active, verifiable judgment:** Equip reward models with an explicit "think–act–verify" loop, allowing them to plan reasoning steps, invoke multimodal tools, and issue evidence-grounded scalar scores with interpretable rationales.
- **Enhance reliability and interpretability:** Ensure that judgments are conditional on accessible evidence, rather than mere surface fluency, thereby making the reward signals more trustworthy and their underlying reasoning transparent.
- **Develop a scalable training paradigm:** Design a data-generation pipeline and a multi-stage reinforcement learning approach (GRPO) that can efficiently train agentic reward models with scarce labels, constructing discriminative preference pairs anchored by verifiable checks.
- **Introduce a new evaluation benchmark:** Create ARMBench-VL, a benchmark specifically designed to assess multi-step, evidence-grounded reasoning and tool-calling behaviors in multimodal reward models.

By achieving these objectives, ARM-Thinker seeks to significantly enhance the accuracy, trustworthiness, and interpretability of reward models, making them more capable of handling the demands of next-generation multimodal AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ARM-Thinker’s methodology is built around an agentic reasoning reward model that leverages a "think-act-verify" loop and a multi-stage reinforcement learning pipeline.

**a. Multimodal Tools Integrated Agent Loop:**
The core of ARM-Thinker is its agent loop architecture, which deviates from traditional passive reward models. It operates as an active agent, dynamically invoking tools, refining its understanding, and verifying outputs before rendering a judgment.
- **Think-Act-Observe Cycle:** Inspired by ReAct, each reasoning trajectory consists of multiple cycles:
 - **Thought:** An intermediate reasoning or planning step enclosed in `<think>...</think>`.
 - **Action:** Either an external tool invocation wrapped in `<tool_call>...</tool_call>` or termination of reasoning with a final answer using `<answer>...</answer>`.
 - **Observation:** The result returned by the environment after a tool invocation (text and/or image), wrapped in `<tool_response>...</tool_response>`.
 These observations are used by the agent to refine subsequent thoughts, enabling iterative refinement.
- **Multimodal Tools:** ARM-Thinker integrates three categories of tools:
 - **Instruction-Following Check Tools:** 19 textual validators verifying compliance with linguistic or structural constraints (e.g., word count, keyword usage).
 - **Image Crop and Zoom-in Tools:** For fine-grained visual inspection, allowing spatial focusing on specific image regions.
 - **Document Retrieval Tools:** `doc_page_retrieval_by_query` and `doc_page_retrieval_by_index` for retrieving relevant pages from long documents.
- **Indexed Memory Map:** A lightweight memory map maintains original and intermediate multimodal artifacts (short textual responses, tool-produced image crops) for retrieval and context during multi-turn reasoning.

**b. Data Gathering for ARM-Thinker Training:**
To train ARM-Thinker, a scalable data-generation pipeline is employed:
- **Preference Data Generation:**
 - Uses LLaVA-Critic for general multimodal QA reward supervision.
 - Supplements with agentic task data: DeepEyes (Image Crop/Zoom-in), MM-IFEngine (Instruction-Following Check), and MP-DocVQA (Document Retrieval).
 - GPT-4o-mini is used to generate semantically related but flawed negative responses (`r-`) for given ground-truth responses (`r+`), creating preference pairs `(q, I, r+, r-)` where `r+` is preferred over `r-`. Overly similar pairs are removed to ensure clear contrast.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) & Cold Start Generation:**
 - **Difficulty Filtration:** Trivial samples (100% accuracy by base model in five rollouts) are removed to focus on challenging instances.
 - **Trajectory Generation:** Stronger LVLMs generate multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories augmented with explicit tool invocations on the remaining data.
 - **Trajectory Filtering:** Trajectories are filtered based on format, accuracy, and successful tool calls.
 - **SFT & Cold Start:** The Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model is fine-tuned using LLaVA-Critic data for fundamental reward capability and the filtered agentic CoT data for "cold start" initialization, teaching structured reasoning and correct tool-use behaviors.

**c. Multi-Stage Training with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
ARM-Thinker utilizes a two-stage GRPO training approach with distinct reward functions to progressively guide the policy.
- **Rollout a Group of Trajectories:** For each multimodal query-image pair `(q, I)`, the model generates a group of `n` trajectories, each a complete reasoning trace with tool interactions.
- **Reward Design:**
 - **Stage 1: Tool Call Encouragement (R_tool):** The objective is to encourage active tool usage and stabilize early exploration.
 `R_tool = R_f + R_try * I(tool_calls > 0)`
 Where `R_f` enforces correct output format and `R_try` assigns a positive signal if a reasonable attempt to call a tool is made.
 - **Stage 2: Accuracy Refinement (R_acc):** After learning tool invocation, the focus shifts to factual correctness and verifiable tool efficacy.
 `R_acc = { R_f + R_try, if R_a = 0 and tool_calls > 0;`
 ` { R_f + R_a, if R_a > 0 and succ_tool_calls = 0;`
 ` { R_f + R_a + R_succ, if R_a > 0 and succ_tool_calls > 0.`
 Here, `R_a` evaluates factual correctness of the final answer, and `R_succ` provides additional credit when tool usage directly contributes to a correct prediction. `R_f` and `R_try` continue to regulate format and encourage exploration. This hierarchical design stabilizes training and provides interpretable reward signals.

By combining an active agent loop with diverse multimodal tools, a robust data generation process, and a carefully designed multi-stage reinforcement learning strategy, ARM-Thinker aims to learn effective, evidence-grounded judgment capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ARM-Thinker-7B demonstrates consistent and substantial improvements across various benchmarks, highlighting the effectiveness of its agentic approach to multimodal reward modeling.

**a. Performance on Reward Model Benchmarks:**
ARM-Thinker-7B achieves significant gains over its base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and is highly competitive with, or superior to, other state-of-the-art models, including proprietary ones like GPT-4o.
- **Overall Improvement:** An average improvement of **+16.2%** across reward modeling benchmarks.
- **VL-RewardBench:** Achieves 67.8% accuracy, a **+17.7%** gain over the baseline, demonstrating superior hallucination detection, reasoning evaluation, and general judgment.
- **RewardBench-2 (text-only):** Yields a **+12.5%** gain (59.6% accuracy), indicating improved reward capability even in a text-only context.
- **ARMBench-VL (Proposed Benchmark):** Scores 64.6% accuracy, an impressive **+18.5%** over the baseline, with balanced gains across fine-grained perception (FG), instruction following (IF), and document understanding (Doc) tasks. This benchmark specifically assesses agentic verification and tool-calling behaviors.
- **Comparison with GPT-4o:** ARM-Thinker-7B surpasses GPT-4o's average accuracy (64.9%) on these benchmarks, demonstrating its efficacy against powerful proprietary models.

**b. Performance on Tool-Use (Think-with-Images) Benchmarks:**
ARM-Thinker-7B shows strong capabilities in tool-assisted visual reasoning, particularly with image manipulation tools.
- **Overall Improvement:** An average improvement of **+9.6%** over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline across visual tool-use benchmarks (V*Bench, HRBench-4K/8K, MME-RW).
- **Overall Accuracy:** Achieves 76.5% overall accuracy, matching or exceeding specialized models like Mini-o3, which are explicitly trained with tool-use supervision.
- **Generalization:** A notable finding is that ARM-Thinker-7B acquires tool-calling abilities emergently through reward-based optimization, without explicit tool-use demonstrations in its training data, demonstrating strong generalization to tool-use scenarios.

**c. Performance on General Multimodal Reasoning Benchmarks:**
The training for verification tasks also translates into improved general reasoning abilities.
- **Overall Improvement:** Achieves consistent improvements over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, with an average gain of **+4.2%** across benchmarks like MMMU, MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, WeMath, and LogicVista.
- **Specific Gains:** Notable gains of **+10.9%** on WeMath and **+8.7%** on LogicVista, suggesting that judging response quality requires careful logical analysis and error detection, which is implicitly enhanced by ARM-Thinker's approach.

**d. Ablation Studies:**
- **Tool Use vs. No Tool Use:** The baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B shows performance degradation when tools are enabled without explicit supervision, indicating it doesn't know *how* or *when* to use them. In contrast, ARM-Thinker-7B maintains strong performance without tools (comparable to the baseline) and yields substantial additional improvements when tools are enabled. This confirms that ARM-Thinker effectively learns both the necessity and effective use of tools.
- **Different Reward Function Designs:**
 - **"Only Acc & Fmt Reward"** (no tool awareness) resulted in minimal tool use and performance plateaus, highlighting the indispensability of tool utilization.
 - **"Fixed Tool Reward"** (constant bonus for tool invocation) led to unchecked tool calls and only marginal accuracy gains, demonstrating that simply maximizing tool calls is ineffective and can lead to overuse without utility.
 - **ARM-Thinker Reward (ours):** This adaptive, context-dependent reward successfully balanced accuracy maximization with appropriate tool utilization. It achieved the highest final accuracy while learning a disciplined tool-use policy, with tool-call frequency stabilizing and even contracting slightly after initial exploration. This indicates the model learned to invoke tools based on contextual utility rather than a naive bonus.

In summary, ARM-Thinker significantly advances the capability of multimodal reward models by endowing them with agentic tool-use and verifiable reasoning, leading to more accurate, interpretable, and generalized judgments across diverse tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ARM-Thinker framework represents a significant step forward in the development of robust and trustworthy multimodal AI systems, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

**a. Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness of AI Judgments:**
By transforming reward models from passive scorers into active, evidence-grounded agents, ARM-Thinker drastically improves the reliability and interpretability of AI judgments. This capability is crucial for high-stakes applications where factual accuracy and verifiable reasoning are paramount, such as scientific research analysis, medical diagnosis support, legal document review, and critical infrastructure monitoring. Humans can now better understand *why* an AI model made a particular judgment, fostering greater trust in AI systems.

**b. Bridging the Gap between Passive Scoring and Active Reasoning:**
ARM-Thinker moves beyond the limitations of existing reward models that often hallucinate or lack deep visual grounding. Its "think-act-verify" loop allows the model to actively seek and verify evidence using external tools, aligning its internal reasoning processes more closely with human-like analytical approaches. This paradigm shift can pave the way for more sophisticated evaluative AI that can autonomously debug, refine, and justify its assessments.

**c. Advancement in Multimodal AI Alignment:**
The work contributes directly to the challenge of aligning LVLMs with complex human preferences. By providing more accurate and verifiable reward signals, ARM-Thinker can improve the efficacy of reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), leading to generative models that are better grounded, less prone to hallucination, and more capable of following nuanced instructions across modalities. This is critical as generative AI models become more powerful and ubiquitous.

**d. Novel Benchmarking for Agentic AI:**
The introduction of ARMBench-VL is a crucial contribution to the field. It addresses the inadequacy of existing benchmarks in evaluating agentic, evidence-grounded reasoning and tool-calling behaviors in reward models. This new benchmark provides a standardized platform for future research, enabling fair and comprehensive assessment of advanced multimodal evaluators and accelerating progress in this area.

**e. Scalable Training of Agentic Capabilities:**
The proposed multi-stage GRPO training pipeline, coupled with the scalable data-generation strategy, demonstrates a practical and label-efficient method for endowing models with complex agentic behaviors without requiring extensive, hand-crafted tool-use demonstrations. This implies that agentic capabilities can be learned emergently through appropriate reward shaping, making the approach more adaptable and scalable to a wider array of tools and tasks.

**f. Generalization to Broader AI Capabilities:**
The observed improvements in general multimodal mathematical and logical reasoning benchmarks suggest that training for rigorous, verifiable judgment can enhance a model's foundational reasoning abilities. This indicates a positive synergy where improving evaluative intelligence also strengthens generative and problem-solving capacities, pointing towards more holistically intelligent AI systems.

**g. Future Directions:**
The authors plan to extend ARM-Thinker to a broader set of tools and expand its applicability. This could include:
- Integrating more diverse and complex tools (e.g., code execution, web search, advanced data analysis).
- Extending the framework to dynamic multimodal data such as video and spatio-temporal reasoning, building on related work in holistic video understanding and complex object segmentation.
- Developing more autonomous systems capable of full-scale computer interaction, moving beyond specific API calls to general computer use agents that learn and evolve through experience.

In conclusion, ARM-Thinker establishes a compelling case for the necessity and effectiveness of agentic capabilities in reward models. It not only addresses critical shortcomings in current multimodal evaluation but also lays robust groundwork for the development of more intelligent, reliable, and interpretable AI systems that can actively reason, verify, and learn from evidence across diverse modalities.
