# iCub3 Avatar System: Enabling Remote Fully-Immersive Embodiment of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The iCub3 Avatar System integrates the iCub3 humanoid robot with a comprehensive teleoperation framework, enabling fully immersive remote embodiment through multi-modal control and sensory feedback. The system successfully demonstrated verbal, non-verbal, manipulation, and locomotion capabilities in real-world deployments and secured second place in the ANA Avatar XPrize semifinals.

## Problem

- Existing avatar systems for bipedal humanoids lacked comprehensive integration of full-body retargeting, detailed sensory feedback, and emotional expression for long-distance operation.
- Challenges in developing robust and dynamically capable humanoid platforms capable of stable bipedal locomotion while being teleoperated.
- The growing demand for advanced avatar technologies to enable human presence and complex operations in distant, potentially hazardous, or inaccessible real-world locations.

## Method

- Developed the iCub3 humanoid robot, a 54-DoF platform optimized for locomotion and physical interaction, featuring enhanced mechanics, actuation, and sensor suite compared to previous iCub versions.
- Implemented a three-layered robot control architecture (==Trajectory Optimization==, Simplified Model Control, Whole-Body QP Control) to ensure stable bipedal locomotion and task execution while integrating operator inputs.
- Designed a multi-modal operator system using wearable devices (VR headset, haptic gloves, omnidirectional treadmill, custom haptic suit) for comprehensive input and provided multi-sensory feedback (visual, auditory, haptic, weight perception) to the operator.

## Results

- Demonstrated successful remote embodiment over distances up to 300 km with low latency (<25 ms), allowing the robot to perform social interactions, verbal communication, and delicate manipulation in public venues like the Biennale di Venezia.
- Achieved 95 out of 100 points and ranked second overall among 38 teams in the ANA Avatar XPrize Semifinals, showcasing high accuracy in puzzle manipulation (approx. 5 cm Z-error, 0.5s retargeting lag) and successful weight/texture perception.
- Identified critical areas for improvement during the ANA Avatar XPrize Finals, including challenges with Wi-Fi connectivity, operator cognitive load, depth perception, and the robot's dexterity for heavy-duty tasks, providing a clear roadmap for future avatar system development.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comprehensive multi-modal sensory feedback (visual, auditory, haptic, weight, touch) is crucial for a strong sense of presence and effective remote interaction in a humanoid avatar system.
> - An integrated approach combining a robust humanoid platform with versatile teleoperation and teleperception interfaces can enable complex, real-world tasks for remote embodiment.
> - Autonomous balance control is essential for bipedal humanoids, sometimes requiring a trade-off between strict retargeting performance and robot stability, especially under external disturbances or high cognitive load on the operator.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of avatar technologies is situated within the broader context of telexistence and immersive virtual realities. The increasing need for remote operation capabilities, highlighted by events such as the COVID-19 pandemic, and the growth of digital ecosystems, underscore the relevance of systems that allow humans to inhabit remote real locations or virtual environments. Initiatives like the ANA Avatar XPrize further catalyze research in this area, focusing on real-time human presence transfer to remote locations.

Traditional physical avatar systems often comprise a robot with locomotion capabilities, an operator system for retargeting and tele-perception, and a communication layer. While various robotic platforms have been utilized, including multi-legged or wheeled robots, humanoid robots offer advantages in human-robot interaction due to their human-likeness, which can enhance acceptance, social performance, and clarity of intent. Humanoids are also capable of complex movements in confined spaces. However, their bipedal nature introduces challenges related to stability, which often necessitates autonomous balance control by the robot.

Previous research in humanoid teleoperation has explored aspects such as manipulation, locomotion, and sensory feedback. Some systems focus on reproducing operator movements for social engagement with small humanoids, while others retarget full-size humanoid motions, sometimes incorporating lower-body or full-body exoskeletons for balance control or haptic feedback. However, fully comprehensive systems that integrate full-body retargeting (including locomotion intent), detailed sensory feedback (visual, auditory, haptic, weight, touch), and emotional expression (facial control) are less common, especially for bipedal humanoids operating over long distances.

This work contributes to the landscape by presenting an integrated avatar system that leverages a robust, purpose-built humanoid robot (iCub3) and a versatile teleoperation framework. It aims to advance the state of the art by enabling fully immersive embodiment of a bipedal humanoid robot, encompassing a wide array of human interaction modalities and comprehensive sensory feedback, and validating this integration in complex, real-world, and competitive scenarios. Unlike many systems that use wheeled or seated robots, this system emphasizes bipedal locomotion with a lightweight operator setup.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of the iCub3 Avatar System are twofold:

1. **To present the iCub3 humanoid robot as an advanced robotic avatar platform:** The paper introduces iCub3 as the latest iteration in the iCub series, incorporating significant design improvements over fifteen years of development. The motivation here is to showcase a humanoid platform optimized for locomotion and physical interaction tasks, suitable for serving as a highly capable physical avatar. This objective addresses the need for robust and dynamically capable humanoid platforms for remote embodiment.

2. **To present a versatile avatar system enabling humans to embody humanoid robots comprehensively:** The core objective is to develop a generic avatar system that facilitates full human embodiment of humanoid robots. This system aims to transmit the operator's capabilities to the robot, encompassing:
 - **Locomotion:** Allowing the operator to control the robot's movement and navigation in a remote environment.
 - **Manipulation:** Enabling precise control over the robot's hands and arms for object interaction.
 - **Voice and Face Expressions:** Facilitating verbal and non-verbal communication, including emotional cues, through the robot.
 - **Comprehensive Sensory Feedback:** Providing the operator with rich sensory information from the remote environment, including visual (first-person perspective), auditory, haptic (touch and force), and proprioceptive cues (e.g., perceived weight).

The overarching motivation for this work stems from the increasing demand for advanced avatar technologies for remote operations. This includes enabling human presence in distant, potentially hazardous, or inaccessible real-world locations (e.g., post-disaster scenarios, remote exploration) and facilitating richer interactions within emerging digital and virtual ecosystems. The ANA Avatar XPrize competition serves as a significant external motivation and a challenging testbed for developing and validating such systems, pushing the boundaries of real-time human presence transfer. The paper explicitly states its contribution to technologies and methods that empower humans to embody physical humanoid robot avatars for real-time operations in remote locations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The iCub3 Avatar System is structured with two main interfaces: the teleoperation interface and the teleperception interface, connected via a physical network.

#### 3.1. The Avatar: iCub3 Robot

The iCub3 is a 125 cm tall, 52 kg humanoid robot with 54 degrees of freedom.

- **Mechanics:** Its structure is aluminum alloy, with plastic covers. It features 4 joints in the head, 3 in the neck, 7 in each arm, 9 in each hand (tendon-driven, controlling thumb, index, middle separately, ring/pinkie jointly), 3 in the torso, and 6 in each leg. Feet have two sections for ankle assembly.
- **Actuation:** Uses DC motors for eyes, eyelids, neck, wrists, and hands (1/100 Harmonic Drive gearbox). Brushless motors drive the torso, arms, and legs (1/100 Harmonic Drive, except hip/ankle roll with 1/160 gearbox). Motors vary in rated power and torque.
- **Power, Connectivity, Computation, and Electronics:** Powered by an external supplier or a 600 Wh battery. Connects via Ethernet or 5GHz Wi-Fi. Features an 11th-gen Intel Core i7 PC in the head (Ubuntu, 16GB RAM), communicating with distributed 32-bit Arm Cortex microcontrollers (Ethernet Motor Supervisor (EMS) boards for three-phase motors, MC4Plus boards for DC motors) via an Ethernet bus. An NVIDIA Jetson Xavier NX Module processes camera images.
- **Sensors:** Equipped with 8 six-axis force/torque (F/T) sensors (F/T-45 at shoulders and feet, F/T-58 in thighs), integrated IMUs, and artificial skin on upper arms and hands. Two Basler daA3840-30mc cameras in the head (4K resolution, 30 fps, adjustable vergence/version/tilt) with eyelids, microphones, a speaker, and LEDs for facial expressions. Joint positions are measured by 18-bit absolute magnetic encoders.
- **Comparison to Classical iCub:** iCub3 is taller, heavier, and features more powerful leg motors. Torso and shoulder joints are serial direct mechanisms, offering greater range of motion and robustness compared to tendon-driven mechanisms in older iCubs. It has a larger battery, a more powerful head PC with GPU, an additional Intel Realsense D435i depth camera, higher resolution eye cameras, and F/T-58 sensors.

#### 3.2. Robot Control

A three-layered control architecture is employed:

1. **Trajectory Optimization:** Computes contact locations and timings, modeling the robot as a unicycle for walking pattern generation. It can plan desired footsteps and CoM/ZMP trajectories for bipedal locomotion using cubic spline interpolation and the algebraic Divergent Component of Motion generator. A modified unicycle model allows for side motions.
2. **Simplified Model Control:** Uses the Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM) to find feasible CoM trajectories, generating desired CoM velocities to stabilize the CoM and ZMP.
3. **Whole-Body Quadratic Programming (QP) Control:** Generates joint position references. It solves a hierarchical optimization problem (QP) to minimize various task errors (e.g., torso orientation, joint positions) while ensuring high-priority tasks like foot pose tracking and CoM tracking. Joint regularization tasks integrate desired joint positions from the retargeting system. A gain-scheduling technique smoothly transitions weights between walking and standing modes.

#### 3.3. Communication Layer

The system uses **YARP** (Yet Another Robot Platform) as middleware for peer-to-peer communication between different applications running on networked PCs. For remote operation, **OpenVPN** is used to connect the robot and operator sub-networks, with a noted latency ranging from 5ms (local) to several hundred milliseconds (poor internet).

#### 3.4. The Operator System

The operator uses a suite of wearable devices:

- **HTC VIVE family:** Pro Eye headset (for visual/auditory feedback and gaze/eye openness tracking), facial tracker (for facial expressions), and trackers (for position/orientation).
- **SenseGlove DK1 haptic gloves:** For finger motion tracking and haptic feedback (vibrations, passive force).
- **Cyberith Virtualizer Elite 2 omnidirectional treadmill:** For locomotion input via sliding motion and direction sensing.
- **IIT custom-developed iFeel system:** Sensorized haptic suit (for body touch feedback) and shoes (for ground interaction force measurement).

#### 3.5. Retargeting Interfaces

These interfaces process operator inputs to control the robot.

- **Manipulation Interfaces:** A multi-modal sensor-fusion algorithm combines data from HTC VIVE, SenseGlove, and iFeel nodes.
 - **iFeel only:** Headset tracks head motion; body motion controlled by iFeel node orientation/velocity (70 Hz).
 - **iFeel plus trackers:** Adds VIVE trackers for Cartesian hand position (90 Hz), with iFeel nodes regulating internal movements.
 - Operator gaze and eye openness (from VIVE headset/facial tracker) control robot eyelids and gaze. SenseGlove translates finger motions to robot finger references.
 - A constrained inverse kinematics algorithm (Dynamical Inverse Kinematics) maps wearable sensor data to robot joint velocities, ensuring joint limits and filtering disturbances.
- **Locomotion Interfaces:**
 - **Virtualizer:** Operator walks by sliding on the treadmill, generating walking direction and speed references for the robot's planning layer.
 - **iFeel Walking:** A more portable solution using VIVE trackers on feet/waist and iFeel shoes (with F/T-45 sensors) for intention detection (forward/backward, sideways, rotation by foot movement) and triggering robot motion by stepping in place. Stepping frequency modulates speed. It provides greater freedom but can disorient novice operators.
- **Voice and Face Expressions Interfaces:** VIVE headset microphone for verbal communication, VIVE facial tracker for replaying operator facial expressions via robot LEDs.

#### 3.6. Feedback Interfaces

These convey robot sensor measurements to the operator.

- **Visual Feedback:** Robot camera images displayed in the VIVE headset.
- **Auditory Feedback:** Robot microphone audio played on the headset's headphones.
- **Haptic Feedback:**
 - **SenseGlove:** Vibration motors and brakes in fingertips/back of hand provide touch and passive force feedback.
 - **iFeel haptic nodes:** Reproduce touch on robot arms via vibrations from the robot's sensorized skin. They also modulate vibrations based on the vertical force exerted on robot arms (weight feedback).

#### 3.7. Logging Systems

Two logging systems are used:
- **Online Monitoring:** Openmct framework displays live robot data (e.g., battery status, communication delay) via a browser.
- **Offline Processing:** Robot data and walking controller data saved periodically in .mat files for detailed analysis using a robot-log-visualizer tool for 3D representation and synchronized camera streams.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The iCub3 Avatar System was validated through multiple real-world deployments and participation in the ANA Avatar XPrize competition, demonstrating its capabilities under various requirements and objectives.

#### 4.1. Remote Teleoperation: iCub3 explores the Biennale di Venezia (Nov 2021)

- **Setup:** Operator in Genoa, iCub3 robot in Venice (approx. 290 km apart), connected via standard fiber optic internet.
- **Objective:** Enable verbal, non-verbal, and physical interaction with a remote recipient.
- **Findings:** The system demonstrated effective remote embodiment. Network latency was consistently below 25 ms, which did not affect operator experience or robot stability. The operator successfully navigated the venue using robot locomotion, engaged in proficient verbal interaction using visual and auditory feedback, and conveyed non-verbal cues through facial expressions (eyelid control, smiling) and body language. Touch feedback was crucial for delicate manipulation of installations and for perceiving physical contact (e.g., a recipient touching the robot's arm from outside its field of view), enabling the operator to react and turn towards the recipient. The interaction culminated in a shared hug, illustrating the emotional implications of the system. Numerical data was not collected for this early demonstration.

#### 4.2. Remote Teleoperation: iCub3 on the stage of the “We Make Future” Festival (June 2022)

- **Setup:** Operator in Genoa, iCub3 robot in Rimini (approx. 300 km apart), similar network delay. Demonstrated live to an audience of approximately 2000 spectators.
- **Objective:** Perform a physical collaboration task with a remote recipient and engage the public.
- **Findings:** The system, enhanced with VIVE trackers and iFeel haptic nodes for improved Cartesian control and weight feedback, enabled the robot to engage with the audience and perform a collaborative task. The robot successfully walked while carrying a 0.5 kg box. Center of mass (CoM) tracking data showed the robot's autonomous balance control, prioritizing stability over retargeting performance when external disturbances (like carrying weight) occurred. Force/torque (F/T) sensors on the arms measured the vertical forces, indicating the box's weight transfer from the recipient to the robot and back.

#### 4.3. ANA Avatar XPrize Semifinals (March 2022)

- **Setup:** IIT labs in Genoa, two novice XPrize judges as operators (30-minute training).
- **Objective:** Maximize sense of presence and shared situational awareness, precise end-effector control, and assess system capabilities (perception, gaze, gestures, haptics, manipulation, grasping, mobility).
- **Overall Score:** iCub3 achieved 95 out of 100 points, ranking second overall among 38 teams.
 - **Puzzle task:** The robot manipulated puzzle pieces with high accuracy using VIVE trackers and fine finger control. Cartesian error plots showed approximately 5 cm error in the z-direction and a 0.5-second retargeting lag, which was attributed to the balancing controller's intervention. Operators compensated for these with visual feedback and slow movements.
 - **Weight task:** The operator successfully detected the weight of a 1 kg vase using both robot hands, aided by iFeel haptic nodes for haptic feedback and a numerical weight display in the headset. F/T sensors in the arms provided force measurements, though they sometimes overestimated the weight due to internal forces.
 - **Texture task:** The robot's sensorized skin on the fingers, combined with haptic glove vibrations, enabled the operator to perceive the embossed texture of the vase. This feedback was tailored for light touches. No numerical data was logged for this task.
 - **Locomotion task:** The robot navigated a designated area using the Cyberith Virtualizer, turning in place and walking forward. CoM tracking data demonstrated the balancing controller's performance during locomotion.

#### 4.4. ANA Avatar XPrize Finals (Nov 2022)

- **Setup:** Long Beach Convention Center, Los Angeles; XPrize judge as operator (45-minute training). Competition focused on heavy-duty tasks on an "exploration of another planet" themed course.
- **Outcome:** The robot collided with a door pillar, fell, and the trial was terminated (ranked 14th out of 17 teams). Wi-Fi connectivity issues (bandwidth < 100-150 MB/sec) and lack of logging data were noted.
 - **Switch task:** Due to the iCub3 hands' design for light objects, a plastic cylinder attached to the forearm was used to activate a switch requiring 30 N of force (internal resistance was reduced by organizers).
 - **Locomotion tasks:** The iFeel walking solution was adopted for its portability and ability to command sideways motion, replacing the bulky Virtualizer. The walking controller frequency was increased to 500 Hz for robustness. iCub3 was the only bipedal robot to complete tasks. The collision during a door passage was attributed to the operator underestimating robot dimensions and the rigid arm position controller leading to destabilizing forces upon impact.
 - **Bottles task:** Involved single-hand weight estimation of canisters using F/T sensors and iFeel haptic nodes, with numeric weight displayed in the headset.
 - **Drill task:** Required significant modifications due to the drill's weight (2.5 kg) and trigger force (15 N). New gearboxes, a longer middle finger, and tying the index and middle fingers together were implemented to enable drill activation.
 - **Texture task:** Identifying a rough textured rock was approached by contacting the rocks with the sensorized palm. A neural network (scaled AlexNet variant) trained on tactile sensor activations classified textures (plain vs. rough) with 78% accuracy, triggering corresponding vibration patterns on the operator's hand.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotics and human-robot interaction by introducing a comprehensive and versatile avatar system built around the iCub3 humanoid robot.

### 6. Significance

- **Advanced Humanoid Platform:** The iCub3 robot represents an evolution in humanoid design, showcasing increased robustness, height, and power compared to its predecessors, optimized for locomotion and physical interaction tasks crucial for avatar applications.
- **Integrated Embodiment System:** The system demonstrates a high level of integration, enabling full-body embodiment of a bipedal humanoid, encompassing locomotion, manipulation, verbal, and non-verbal communication, alongside multi-modal sensory feedback (visual, auditory, haptic, weight, touch). This addresses the gap in fully fledged bipedal humanoid avatar systems.
- **Real-world Validation:** The extensive validation in remote, real-world scenarios (Biennale, We Make Future) and a high-stakes international competition (ANA Avatar XPrize) provides empirical evidence of the system's capabilities and limitations under varying conditions, including significant distances and public engagement. Its second-place finish in the XPrize semifinals underscores its performance.
- **Design Insights and Recommendations:** The paper provides valuable practical insights and design recommendations derived from the development and testing process. These insights, covering trade-offs in operator effort, avatar design, modularity, use of off-the-shelf vs. custom technologies, and team management, are crucial for future research in humanoid avatar systems.
- **Open-source Ethos:** The mention of open-source code for logging systems (openmct, robometry, robot-log-visualizer) supports reproducibility and collaborative development in the robotics community.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enabling Remote Presence and Work:** The system has the potential to enable humans to perform complex tasks, interact with environments, and engage socially in remote locations, which could be beneficial in fields such as tele-exploration, remote assistance, hazardous environment operations, and cultural preservation (as demonstrated by the Biennale visit).
- **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction:** The emphasis on non-verbal communication (facial expressions, body language) and comprehensive feedback can lead to more natural and effective human-robot and human-avatar-human interactions, fostering a stronger sense of presence and social connection.
- **Guidance for Future Avatar Development:** The detailed discussion of shortcomings identified during the XPrize finals (cognitive load, depth perception, communication reliability, robot stability, hand dexterity) provides a clear roadmap for future research and engineering efforts in avatar systems. Addressing these challenges will be critical for achieving more robust, intuitive, and versatile avatar platforms.
- **Technological Convergence:** The system's "organic ensemble" approach, where operator and robot technologies share similarities at logical, hardware, and software levels (e.g., F/T sensors in robot feet and operator shoes, similar inverse kinematics), highlights a trend towards deeper integration and co-design of human-robot systems.

In summary, the iCub3 Avatar System represents a robust and well-validated platform that pushes the boundaries of remote humanoid embodiment. Its comprehensive approach and the lessons learned from real-world deployments provide a foundational contribution to the ongoing development of advanced avatar technologies.
