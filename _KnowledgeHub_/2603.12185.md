# ComFree-Sim: A GPU-Parallelized Analytical Contact Physics Engine for Scalable Contact-Rich Robotics Simulation and Control

> [!summary] Summary
> ComFree-Sim, a GPU-parallelized analytical contact physics engine, accelerates contact-rich robotics simulation by employing a complementarity-free contact model. The engine achieved up to 3x faster simulation speed and nearly 2x throughput compared to state-of-the-art GPU simulators, enabling 2.4x faster real-time Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control and a 27 percentage point increase in closed-loop success rates on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Contact resolution in existing physics simulators constitutes a computational bottleneck, often scaling superlinearly with the number of contacts due to iterative solvers.
- Current GPU-accelerated simulators struggle with scalability in dense contact scenarios, hindering large-scale data generation and parallelized sampling for control.
- Achieving real-time performance for contact-rich robotic tasks like dexterous manipulation is challenging due to high simulation latency.

## Method

- Developed **ComFree-Sim**, a GPU-parallelized analytical contact physics engine built on a ==complementarity-free (ComFree) contact modeling== paradigm.
- Implemented a unified ==6D frictional contact model==, extending to include tangential, torsional, and rolling friction, with analytical computation of contact forces.
- Leveraged GPU parallelism using the ==Warp framework== for efficient, decoupled contact resolution across contact pairs and polyhedral cone facets, integrated with a ==MuJoCo-compatible interface==.

## Results

- ComFree-Sim achieved approximately **3x faster simulation speed** and demonstrated near-linear scaling of step-time with contact count, with nearly **2x throughput** for parallel simulations compared to MJWarp.
- Demonstrated comparable or lower penetration depths than MJWarp and accurately captured torsional and rolling friction phenomena.
- Enabled real-time **MPPI** control on physical hardware, showing an average **2.4x speedup** in compute time and a **+27 percentage point** increase in closed-loop success rates for dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Analytical, complementarity-free contact modeling can offer comparable physical fidelity and numerical stability to iterative solvers with superior computational efficiency.
> - The decoupled nature of analytical contact resolution is highly amenable to GPU parallelization, enabling near-linear runtime scaling with contact count.
> - Employing a MuJoCo-compatible interface facilitates the seamless integration of high-performance physics backends into existing robotics simulation and control pipelines.
