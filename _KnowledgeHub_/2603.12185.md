# ComFree-Sim: A GPU-Parallelized Analytical Contact Physics Engine for Scalable Contact-Rich Robotics Simulation and Control

> [!summary] Summary
> ComFree-Sim, a GPU-parallelized analytical contact physics engine, accelerates contact-rich robotics simulation by employing a complementarity-free contact model. The engine achieved up to 3x faster simulation speed and nearly 2x throughput compared to state-of-the-art GPU simulators, enabling 2.4x faster real-time Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control and a 27 percentage point increase in closed-loop success rates on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Contact resolution in existing physics simulators constitutes a computational bottleneck, often scaling superlinearly with the number of contacts due to iterative solvers.
- Current GPU-accelerated simulators struggle with scalability in dense contact scenarios, hindering large-scale data generation and parallelized sampling for control.
- Achieving real-time performance for contact-rich robotic tasks like dexterous manipulation is challenging due to high simulation latency.

## Method

- Developed **ComFree-Sim**, a GPU-parallelized analytical contact physics engine built on a ==complementarity-free (ComFree) contact modeling== paradigm.
- Implemented a unified ==6D frictional contact model==, extending to include tangential, torsional, and rolling friction, with analytical computation of contact forces.
- Leveraged GPU parallelism using the ==Warp framework== for efficient, decoupled contact resolution across contact pairs and polyhedral cone facets, integrated with a ==MuJoCo-compatible interface==.

## Results

- ComFree-Sim achieved approximately **3x faster simulation speed** and demonstrated near-linear scaling of step-time with contact count, with nearly **2x throughput** for parallel simulations compared to MJWarp.
- Demonstrated comparable or lower penetration depths than MJWarp and accurately captured torsional and rolling friction phenomena.
- Enabled real-time **MPPI** control on physical hardware, showing an average **2.4x speedup** in compute time and a **+27 percentage point** increase in closed-loop success rates for dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Analytical, complementarity-free contact modeling can offer comparable physical fidelity and numerical stability to iterative solvers with superior computational efficiency.
> - The decoupled nature of analytical contact resolution is highly amenable to GPU parallelization, enabling near-linear runtime scaling with contact count.
> - Employing a MuJoCo-compatible interface facilitates the seamless integration of high-performance physics backends into existing robotics simulation and control pipelines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Scalable physics simulation is a foundational component in contemporary robotics research, supporting various applications such as large-scale data generation for policy training, parallelized sampling for model predictive control (MPC), and the development of end-to-end differentiable pipelines for system design. Over recent years, advancements in GPU-accelerated simulators and system-level platforms have substantially increased parallel simulation throughput, which has facilitated the training and evaluation of increasingly intricate robotic tasks.

Despite these advancements, contact-rich manipulation and locomotion tasks continue to present a significant bottleneck in simulation due: the computational expense of resolving intermittent, unilateral, and frictional contacts. A typical rigid-body simulation cycle involves collision detection, contact resolution, and time integration. Among these stages, contact resolution, responsible for computing contact impulses and post-contact velocities, exerts the most significant influence on physical fidelity, numerical stability, and overall runtime.

Current mainstream approaches to contact resolution typically enforce non-penetration and Coulomb friction through complementarity constraints or constrained optimization formulations. These methods generally necessitate per-step iterative solutions, whose computational cost often scales superlinearly with the number of contacts. This scaling characteristic becomes a prohibitive factor for applications requiring batched simulations, differentiable simulations, or real-time online planning. Even advanced GPU-parallelized physics engines, such as MuJoCo Warp (MJWarp), can experience notable increases in step-time within dense contact scenarios, which complicates their deployment in real-time systems.

This paper positions ComFree-Sim as an effort to address this specific computational bottleneck. By introducing a GPU-parallelized analytical contact physics engine built upon a complementarity-free contact modeling paradigm, the work aims to offer a computationally efficient alternative to existing iterative, optimization-based contact solvers. The design of ComFree-Sim, which leverages GPU parallelism and analytical solutions, is intended to provide a more scalable and predictable runtime performance, particularly in contact-dense environments. This approach is intended to enhance the practicality of high-frequency, contact-rich rollouts that are critical for modern learning and control algorithms, potentially serving as an accelerated backend for established robotics simulation platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a GPU-parallelized analytical contact physics engine, named ComFree-Sim, that can efficiently and accurately simulate contact-rich robotic tasks. This development is specifically aimed at overcoming the prevalent computational bottleneck associated with contact resolution in existing physics simulators.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and needs within the field of robotics simulation and control:

1. **Addressing the Contact Resolution Bottleneck:** Mainstream physics engines, which enforce non-penetration and Coulomb friction using complementarity constraints or constrained optimization, rely on iterative solvers. The computational cost of these solvers typically grows superlinearly (often quadratically) with the number of contacts. This scaling is a significant impediment for scenarios involving a high density of contacts, thereby limiting the practical application of these simulators in complex robotic tasks. The paper identifies this superlinear scaling as a fundamental difficulty that impacts the efficiency of batched, differentiable simulation, and online planning.

2. **Enabling Scalable Simulation for Modern Robotics:** Modern robotics applications, including large-scale data generation for policy training, parallelized sampling for Model Predictive Control (MPC), and end-to-end differentiable pipelines, necessitate highly scalable physics simulation infrastructure. Existing GPU-accelerated simulators, despite their advancements, often exhibit substantial step-time growth in dense contact scenes, which hinders real-time deployment and large-scale experimentation. ComFree-Sim aims to provide a solution that scales more favorably with contact count, thereby enabling these advanced robotics applications.

3. **Improving Real-Time Performance for Control:** Sampling-based predictive control methods often require numerous simulation rollouts within a short time frame to achieve real-time performance. The computational overhead of contact resolution in existing simulators limits real-time results to contact-sparse or low-frequency control settings. A key motivation for ComFree-Sim is to reduce rollout latency, thus enabling high-frequency control in contact-rich tasks such as dexterous manipulation, which require rapid and numerous evaluations of potential robot actions.

4. **Maintaining Physical Fidelity and Stability:** While seeking to improve computational efficiency, the development of ComFree-Sim is also motivated by the need to preserve physical realism and numerical stability. The objective is to demonstrate that a lightweight analytical formulation can maintain comparable physical fidelity to more computationally intensive, iterative solvers, without introducing significant degradation in contact behaviors or stability issues that could compromise its utility for accurate model-based control and learning.

5. **Providing a Practical and Integrable Solution:** The research is motivated by the desire to create a competitive backend that can serve as a drop-in replacement for existing widely-used simulators (e.g., MuJoCo and its accelerated variants). By providing a MuJoCo-compatible interface and implementing the engine in Warp, the authors aim to facilitate its adoption within the robotics community, enabling researchers and practitioners to leverage its benefits without extensive modifications to their existing simulation and control pipelines.

In summary, the core objectives are to overcome the iterative solver bottleneck by developing a GPU-parallelized analytical contact engine that offers near-linear runtime scaling, high throughput, and robust physical fidelity, thereby making advanced contact-rich robotics simulation and control more practical and scalable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ComFree-Sim is developed as a GPU-parallelized analytical contact physics engine, built upon a complementarity-free contact modeling paradigm. The methodology focuses on computing contact impulses in closed form, thereby avoiding the iterative solvers characteristic of traditional complementarity-based approaches.

**A. Generic Complementarity-Free Contact Modeling**
The core of ComFree-Sim is the complementarity-free (ComFree) contact modeling approach, adapted from prior work. This method resolves contact impulses analytically by employing a prediction-correction principle operating within the dual cone of Coulomb friction.

1. **System Dynamics:** The dynamics of multi-joint robot systems (robots, objects, environments) are represented by the equation:
 $M(q) dv + c(q, v) dt = τ dt + J(q)^⊤ λ dt$
 where $q$ and $v$ are joint position and velocity, $M(q)$ is the joint-space inertia, $dv$ is the velocity increment, $c(q, v)$ are bias forces, $τ$ is the applied generalized force, $J(q)$ is the contact Jacobian, and $λ$ represents the step-averaged contact wrench (force/torque).

2. **Prediction Phase:** The process begins by predicting the system velocity ($v^+_{smooth}$) solely under non-contact (smooth) forces $τ$ and $c$:
 $v^+_{smooth}:= v + M^{-1}(τ - c) dt$

3. **Unified 6D Frictional Contact Model:** To encompass a broad range of frictional behaviors, ComFree-Sim extends the model to a 6D formulation that includes tangential (sliding) friction, torsional friction (about the contact normal), and rolling friction (in the tangential plane).
 - **Primal Friction Cone Constraints:** These constraints define the limits for the normal force ($λ_n$), tangential friction impulse ($λ_t$), torsional friction moment ($m_{tor}$), and rolling friction moment ($m_{roll}$):
 $\|λ_t\| ≤ μ_t λ_n$, $|m_{tor}| ≤ μ_{tor} λ_n$, $\|m_{rol}\| ≤ μ_{rol} λ_n$
 where $μ_*$ are friction coefficients, and $μ_{tor}$, $μ_{roll}$ have units of length.
 - **Dual Cone Constraints:** These are defined in the constraint-velocity space. Relative linear and angular velocities ($v_c, ω_c$) are decomposed into normal velocity ($v^n_c$), tangential slip velocity ($v^t_c$), torsional angular velocity ($ω^tor_c$), and rolling angular velocity ($ω^rol_c$). The dual-cone constraints are:
 $μ_s \|J_s v\| ≤ J_n v + ϕ/dt$, for $s ∈ \{t, tor, rol\}$, where $ϕ$ is the signed gap distance.

4. **Polyhedral Approximation:** Each quadratic dual-cone constraint is approximated by linear polyhedral constraints to facilitate computation. This involves selecting a set of unit directions spanning the relevant subspace for each friction type, yielding linearized inequalities: $\tilde{J}_s v + \tilde{ϕ}/dt ≥ 0$.

5. **Analytical Contact Force Computation:** The contact force/torque ($λ_s$) is computed analytically as:
 $λ_s = (K(q)(\tilde{J}_s v^+_{smooth} dt + \tilde{ϕ}) - D(q)(\tilde{J}_s v^+_{smooth}))_+$
 where $(\cdot)_+$ denotes element-wise $\max(x, 0)$, and $K(q)$, $D(q)$ are stiffness and damping gains in the dual-cone space. This calculation essentially measures the violation of dual-cone constraints under the predicted smooth velocity and applies a stabilizing impulse.

6. **Velocity Correction:** The final post-contact velocity ($v^+$) is obtained by correcting the smooth predicted velocity with the accumulated contact impulses:
 $v^+ = v^+_{smooth} + M^{-1} \sum_{s∈\{t,tor,rol\}} \tilde{J}^⊤_s λ_s dt$

**B. Dual Cone Impedance Heuristics**
To manage the computational cost of evaluating $K(q)$ and $D(q)$, and to provide a practical tuning interface, ComFree-Sim introduces a heuristic parameterization.
- **Approximation:** Instead of the exact inverse inertia approximation, $K(q) dt + D(q) ≈ \frac{1}{dt} (\tilde{J}M^{-1}\tilde{J}^⊤)^{-1}$, simpler user-set global parameters are used.
- **Heuristic Parameters:** $K(q) = k_{user} M(ϕ)/dt$ and $D(q) = d_{user} M(ϕ)/dt$, where $k_{user}$ and $d_{user}$ are global impedance parameters.
- **Constraint-Space Impedance:** $M(ϕ)$ is a constraint-space impedance that adapts to the signed distance $ϕ$ between contacting bodies, using a gap-dependent scaling factor $r(|ϕ|)$ similar to MuJoCo's backend solver settings, ensuring a MuJoCo-compatible interface.

**C. GPU Implementation for Contact Resolution**
A core aspect of the methodology is the GPU parallelization, enabled by the decoupled nature of the analytical contact resolution. Contact resolution is independent across contact pairs and separable across polyhedral cone facets, which maps directly to GPU kernels. The implementation uses the Warp programming framework and involves four main kernel launches:
1. **Smooth Prediction:** Computes the smooth predicted velocity in parallel across degrees of freedom (DoFs).
2. **Per-Contact, Per-Face Dual-Cone Solve:** In parallel for each contact, friction type, and polyhedral face, it computes the Jacobian terms, measures dual-cone violation, and calculates the analytical impulse.
3. **Accumulate Generalized Impulse:** Accumulates the computed impulses from all contacts, friction types, and faces.
4. **Velocity Correction:** Applies the accumulated impulse to the smooth predicted velocity to obtain the final post-contact velocity, again in parallel over DoFs.

**D. Integration**
ComFree-Sim is designed to be a drop-in backend alternative to MJWarp, implemented in Warp and exposed through a MuJoCo-compatible interface. This allows for seamless integration into existing simulation and control pipelines that currently utilize MuJoCo or MJWarp.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ComFree-Sim against MJWarp, a state-of-the-art GPU simulator, covered physical fidelity, numerical stability, and computational efficiency. Both simulators used the same collision detection and time integration to isolate the contact resolution backend.

**A. Penetration Depth**
- **Test:** A collision-rich drop test involving five 5x5 arrays of convex primitives (cubes, cylinders, ellipsoids, capsules, spheres) dropped onto a flat box.
- **Results:** With appropriate tuning of its user-defined impedance parameters ($k_{user}$, $d_{user}$), ComFree-Sim achieved penetration depths comparable to or lower than MJWarp. For instance, with $k_{user}=0.5$ and $d_{user}=0.005$, ComFree-Sim showed a mean penetration of 0.9 ± 1.5 mm, compared to MJWarp's 1.7 ± 4.9 mm. Penetration was observed to increase with $k_{user}$ and decrease with $d_{user}$.

**B. Torsional and Rolling Friction Modelling**
- **Torsional Friction Test:** A sphere in contact with a plane, constrained to rotate about the z-axis. Measured angular velocity decay.
- **Rolling Friction Test:** A cylinder rolling freely on a plane. Measured decay of its center of mass (COM) velocity.
- **Results:** Both tests demonstrated that ComFree-Sim accurately captures torsional and rolling dissipation. The observed monotone decay trends of angular and linear velocities, respectively, and their consistent response to varying friction coefficients confirmed the model's physical fidelity for these friction types.

**C. Simulation Stability**
- **Test:** A free-fall-and-sliding test where a cube was dropped onto a plane with initial linear and angular velocities under gravity and friction. Tracked horizontal speed and vertical position over time.
- **Results:** ComFree-Sim exhibited consistent, monotone decay of horizontal velocity, indicating that kinetic energy was dissipated without spurious drift or growth. The simulation remained stable across a range of impedance settings ($k_{user}$, $d_{user}$) and timesteps (e.g., stable even at $dt=0.02s$). It was noted that ComFree-Sim generally benefits from smaller timesteps than MJWarp, with $dt=0.002s$ being used in most benchmarks.

**D. Runtime Performance w.r.t. Contact Numbers**
- **Test:** 512 parallel environments, each with three 2x2 arrays of convex primitives stacked, with small initial velocity perturbations to introduce stochasticity. Measured wall-clock time per full simulation step (excluding rendering) as a function of the total number of contacts.
- **Results:** ComFree-Sim achieved approximately 3x faster simulation speed and demonstrated near-linear scaling of step-time with contact count. In contrast, MJWarp showed nonlinear (often superlinear) step-time growth and higher variance at comparable contact counts.

**E. Throughput for Parallel Simulation**
- **Test:** Benchmarked simulation throughput against the number of parallel environments (256 to 4096) in an Allegro hand in-hand cube grasping scene.
- **Results:** ComFree-Sim achieved nearly 2x the throughput of MJWarp, demonstrating its advantage for large-scale sampling-based control and robot learning.

**F. Real-Time MPC for Real-World Dexterous Manipulation**
- **Setup:** ComFree-Sim was deployed as the predictive model in a real-time Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control loop for multi-fingered in-hand object reorientation on a physical LEAP Hand. Compared against MJWarp with identical MPC settings.
- **Results:**
 - **Accuracy:** ComFree-Sim achieved manipulation accuracy comparable to MJWarp. While MJWarp sometimes yielded slightly lower raw error on specific tasks (e.g., Duck-Yaw), ComFree-Sim matched or improved performance on others (e.g., Cube-Roll/Pitch).
 - **Speedup:** ComFree-Sim reduced per-step MPPI compute time for all object-task pairs, demonstrating an average speedup of approximately 2.4x (median 2.2x). This resulted in sub-30 ms MPPI step times (13.9–28.2 ms).
 - **Success Rate:** The faster rollouts directly translated into consistently higher closed-loop success rates, with an average increase of 27 percentage points across eight benchmarks. This enabled control frequencies of 35–72 Hz on hardware.

**G. Dynamic-Aware Retargeting for Agile Locomotion**
- **Setup:** ComFree-Sim was used for the optimization phase of model-based, dynamics-aware motion retargeting (SPIDER framework) for Unitree G1 robot motions (e.g., Cartwheels, Martial Arts, Dance, Getup, Pushbox). The full trajectory rollout was performed using MuJoCo-CPU, allowing evaluation of the sim-to-sim gap.
- **Results:**
 - **Tracking Performance:** ComFree-Sim achieved performance comparable to MJWarp on humanoid tracking tasks, with minor differences in position, quaternion, and joint errors. For the Pushbox task, object pose differences were also comparable.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** ComFree-Sim consistently achieved faster MPPI optimization times, except for the PushBox task, demonstrating better computational efficiency.
 - **Sim-to-Sim Gap:** The results indicated a manageable sim-to-sim gap between ComFree-Sim and the MuJoCo contact backend, despite MuJoCo's mature optimization-based solver being a strong baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ComFree-Sim presents a significant contribution to the field of robotics simulation and control, primarily by addressing the computational bottlenecks associated with contact resolution in contact-rich environments. Its development and evaluation demonstrate several key areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The primary significance of ComFree-Sim lies in its ability to achieve near-linear runtime scaling with the number of contacts, a notable improvement over the superlinear scaling often observed in traditional iterative solvers. This efficiency, coupled with 2-3 times higher throughput in dense contact scenes, makes it a powerful tool for accelerating simulation-based robotics research. This directly impacts the feasibility of large-scale data generation for policy training and parallelized sampling for model predictive control, which are often limited by simulation speed.

2. **Enabling Advanced Real-Time Control:** By substantially reducing per-step simulation latency, ComFree-Sim facilitates higher control frequencies (e.g., 35-72 Hz for MPPI-based control) on physical hardware. This enables more agile, responsive, and robust control for complex, contact-rich tasks like dexterous manipulation, where rapid feedback and numerous predictive rollouts are critical. The demonstrated increase in closed-loop success rates in real-world manipulation tasks highlights its practical utility for hardware deployment.

3. **Advancing Robot Learning and Policy Training:** The increased throughput for parallel simulation provided by ComFree-Sim is directly beneficial for robot learning. Training complex policies, especially for contact-rich behaviors, often requires vast amounts of simulated experience. A faster, more scalable simulation backend can significantly reduce the time and computational resources required for such training, potentially accelerating the development of more capable and intelligent robotic systems.

4. **Strengthening Model-Based Control and Planning:** ComFree-Sim offers a computationally lightweight backend for model-based control and planning algorithms, such as MPPI and dynamics-aware retargeting. Its efficiency allows for more frequent and extensive exploration of action spaces within a given planning horizon, enhancing the performance and practicality of these advanced control strategies.

5. **Practical Integration and Accessibility:** The implementation of ComFree-Sim in Warp and its exposure through a MuJoCo-compatible interface enhance its practical impact. This design choice facilitates easy integration into existing workflows and platforms that rely on MuJoCo, reducing the barrier to adoption for researchers and developers. It offers a drop-in alternative that leverages GPU acceleration without requiring a complete overhaul of existing pipelines.

6. **Validation of Analytical Contact Modeling:** The work validates the effectiveness of complementarity-free contact modeling in practical, high-performance simulation scenarios. By demonstrating comparable physical fidelity and stability to state-of-the-art iterative solvers, it suggests that analytical approaches can offer a competitive and more efficient paradigm for contact physics computation, potentially inspiring further research into analytical and lightweight modeling techniques.

In conclusion, ComFree-Sim represents a step forward in addressing one of the most persistent challenges in robotics simulation. Its focus on computational efficiency, coupled with demonstrated fidelity and real-world applicability, positions it as a valuable tool that can accelerate research in robot learning, advanced control, and physically-plausible motion planning, ultimately contributing to the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems.
