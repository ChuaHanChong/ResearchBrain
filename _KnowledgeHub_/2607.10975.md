# Real-Time Rulebook-Aware Nonlinear MPC for Autonomous Driving with Priority-Biased Tiered Slacks

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a real-time Nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC), W-SQP, that manages a priority-ordered rulebook for autonomous driving through tiered slack penalties, enabling auditable decision-making. The authors also propose and validate a log-independent evaluation protocol that accurately assesses driving quality by decoupling it from imitation of human trajectories.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving motion planners need to adhere to a priority-ordered rulebook (e.g., safety, regulatory, comfort, efficiency) in real time while navigating complex environments.
- Existing planning approaches often lack transparency or auditability regarding which rules are relaxed during conflicts, hindering debugging and safety justification.
- Standard evaluation benchmarks for autonomous driving can unfairly penalize competent non-imitative planners by conflating adherence to a logged human trajectory with overall driving quality.

## Method

- Developed **W-SQP**, a weighted soft-constrained quadratic-penalty ==NMPC== that compiles nine driving rule families into a single online ==nonlinear program==.
- **W-SQP** utilizes strongly separated ==quadratic slack penalties== across four priority tiers (Safety > Regulatory > Comfort > Efficiency) to enable real-time, auditable trade-offs among conflicting objectives.
- Proposed a ==log-independent evaluation protocol== that uses ==closed-loop rollouts== and categorizes metrics into log-coupled (measuring imitation) and log-independent (measuring intrinsic driving quality like safety and regulatory compliance).

## Results

- W-SQP achieved **93.6%** compliance on log-independent (safety/regulatory) rules, comparable to expert replay (**93.7%**) and outperforming non-imitative baselines by approximately **2 percentage points**.
- The tiered slack mechanism proved effective, with ablation studies showing significant performance degradation without priority-based weighting, and stronger ordering monotonically improving compliance.
- The aggregate compliance deficit of W-SQP relative to expert replay was almost entirely attributed to log-coupled rules (**-10.1 pp**), with a negligible systematic deficit on log-independent safety/regulatory rules (**-0.04 pp**), confirming the unfair penalty of imitation-based metrics.
- W-SQP demonstrated real-time capability, with a median solve time of **28.4 ms** and an observed maximum of **104 ms** under a **90 ms** CPU-time limit, exhibiting anytime properties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly tiered slack penalties within an NMPC framework allow for real-time, auditable resolution of complex, prioritized rulebook conflicts in autonomous driving.
> - The observed aggregate performance deficits of non-imitative autonomous driving controllers in benchmarks are predominantly due to their divergence from logged human trajectories, rather than fundamental failures in safety or regulatory compliance.
> - A clear separation of evaluation metrics into log-coupled and log-independent categories is essential for fair and accurate assessment of diverse autonomous driving policies, preventing a 'proxy failure' where imitation is mistaken for driving quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper addresses challenges at the intersection of motion planning, control, and evaluation for autonomous driving systems. Traditional autonomous vehicle motion planners aim to ensure collision avoidance and goal achievement. However, the authors identify a broader requirement for planners to navigate complex environments while simultaneously adhering to a priority-ordered rulebook (e.g., safety, regulatory compliance, comfort, efficiency) in real time. Furthermore, these decisions must be auditable, allowing engineers to trace relaxed rules during conflicts.

Existing approaches in this domain include:
- **Lexicographic schemes and cascaded strict-priority quadratic programs:** These can achieve high rates in whole-body control but face increased latency and warm-start disruption when applied to nonconvex driving NMPC due to sequential solves and tier-freezing.
- **Learned planners:** While scalable, they often lack transparency regarding per-rule decision-making, which hinders auditability.
- **Conventional penalty stacks:** These do not explicitly reveal how conflicts among competing objectives are resolved.
- **Formal methods (e.g., temporal logic, monitors, repair) and safety filters (e.g., Control Barrier Functions - CBF, Responsibility-Sensitive Safety - RSS):** These primarily focus on enforcing safety feasibility but may not integrate a comprehensive, priority-ordered rulebook into a single planning framework that allows for auditable trade-offs.
- **Real-time constrained MPC:** Some collocation-based schemes embed safety constraints, but this work aims for a broader rulebook compilation.

A key challenge in evaluating these planners, particularly non-imitative ones, is the common use of closed-loop benchmarks that may conflate adherence to a logged human trajectory with overall driving quality. This can unfairly penalize planners that develop valid, but divergent, trajectories.

This work positions itself by proposing a planning formulation that aims to make the resolution of competing priorities explicit and auditable, while remaining computationally viable for real-time control. Simultaneously, it introduces an evaluation methodology designed to measure driving quality independently of resemblance to a single recorded trajectory, thereby addressing a form of "proxy failure" in current benchmarking practices.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations for this research stem from the practical requirements and recognized shortcomings in the development and evaluation of autonomous driving motion planners.

The primary objectives include:

- **Real-time Rulebook Resolution:** To develop a motion planner capable of resolving conflicts among a priority-ordered rulebook (safety > regulatory > comfort > efficiency) at the vehicle's control rate (10 Hz). This addresses the routine nature of such conflicts in driving, where trade-offs, like briefly crossing a lane to pass an obstacle or early braking for comfort, can impact other rules.
- **Auditability of Decisions:** To ensure that after any conflict or objective trade-off, an engineer can identify which specific rule was relaxed and quantify the extent of that relaxation. This is crucial for debugging, performance analysis, and meeting safety and regulatory justification requirements in autonomous systems.
- **Computational Tractability:** To achieve the above objectives within the strict computational constraints of real-time autonomous driving, specifically targeting control-rate operation without compromising decision quality.
- **Fair Evaluation of Non-Imitative Controllers:** To develop an evaluation methodology that accurately assesses driving quality without penalizing planners for choosing valid trajectories that deviate from a recorded human log. This is motivated by the observation that aggregating log-alignment terms with physical and regulatory rules can bias evaluation against planners that do not strictly imitate human driving, thus conflating "drives like the recorded human" with "drives well."

These objectives are motivated by two identified gaps in the current research landscape:
1. **A Planning Formulation Gap:** A need for a planning formulation that explicitly handles competing priorities in an auditable manner, while being computationally feasible for continuous operation at the control rate. Existing methods either struggle with real-time nonconvex NMPC (lexicographic) or lack transparency (learned planners, standard penalty stacks).
2. **An Evaluation Methodology Gap:** A need for an evaluation framework that measures intrinsic driving quality (safety, regulatory compliance) without structurally rewarding resemblance to a single historical trajectory. This is highlighted as a specific instance of Goodhart's law, where a proxy (log alignment) becomes the target, potentially obscuring genuine performance.

By addressing these two gaps, the authors aim to advance the development of practical, transparent, and fairly evaluated autonomous driving systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around the development of W-SQP, a weighted soft-constrained quadratic-penalty Nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC), and an accompanying log-independent evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. The W-SQP Controller

W-SQP is a receding-horizon NMPC that compiles nine driving rule families into a single online nonlinear program (NLP) using CasADi for symbolic differentiation and IPOPT for solving.

- **Model and Decision Variables:**
 - The ego vehicle follows a kinematic-bicycle model with state $x_k = [p_x_k, p_y_k, \psi_k, v_k]^\top$ (position, yaw, speed) and input $u_k = [a_k, \delta_k]^\top$ (longitudinal acceleration, steering).
 - Dynamics are discretized using fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration at $\Delta t = 0.1 s$.
 - The NMPC optimizes over $N = 30$ steps (3.0 s) with decision variables $X = \{x_0, \ldots, x_N\}$, $U = \{u_0, \ldots, u_{N-1}\}$, and $S$ (slacks).
 - The initial state $x_0$ is fixed to the current executed ego state.
- **Objective Function:**
 - The total cost $J = J_{base} + J_{dyn} + J_{slack}$ consists of three parts.
 - $J_{base}$: Tracks a low-weight reference ($x_{ref}$), penalizes control effort, and input rate. The reference $x_{ref}$ is the expert log, but its low weight (relative to slack penalties) ensures W-SQP is non-imitative.
 - $J_{dyn}$: Shapes the dynamics to discourage aggressive transients, penalizing jerk and lateral acceleration.
 - $J_{slack}$: Encodes priority through squared, weight-separated penalties for four tiers: $J_{slack} = \sum_{j=1}^{4} \rho_j \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (s_k^{(j)})^2$, with $\rho_1 \gg \rho_2 \gg \rho_3 \gg \rho_4$. The tiers are ordered as Safety ($\rho_1=10^8$) > Regulatory ($\rho_2=10^5$) > Comfort ($\rho_3=10^2$) > Efficiency ($\rho_4=1$). This quadratic (L2) penalty is chosen for numerical robustness, despite lacking the exact constraint satisfaction property of L1 penalties.
- **Tiered Soft Constraints:**
 - Nine rule families are mapped to one of the four tiers.
 - Each rule surrogate $g_i(x_k, u_k) \le 0$ is relaxed by a single shared nonnegative slack per tier per step: $g_i(x_k, u_k) \le s_k^{(\pi(i))}$, where $\pi(i)$ is the tier mapping for rule $i$. This design uses only $4N = 120$ slacks, contributing to tractability.
 - Actuation bounds (acceleration, steering, speed) are treated as hard physical constraints and are never relaxed. Collision avoidance and road boundary, while high-priority, are Tier-1 soft constraints.
- **Constraint Surrogates:**
 - **Tier 1 (Safety):** Collision avoidance (squared distance to tracked objects) and road boundary (signed distance to road edge).
 - **Tier 2 (Regulatory):** Speed limit, traffic light stop (clamped proximity weight makes this non-smooth), and route adherence (L2 distance to reference path).
 - **Tier 3 (Comfort):** Deceleration, jerk, and lateral acceleration limits.
 - **Tier 4 (Efficiency):** Headway (using center-to-center distance to constant-velocity predicted agents).
 - Acknowledge surrogate-evaluator mismatch and rules without explicit surrogates in the NLP.
- **Real-time Implementation and Auditability:**
 - The controller replans at 10 Hz from its executed state.
 - A 90 ms CPU-time limit is imposed for IPOPT, returning the best iterate found within the limit (anytime capability).
 - The first control output from a potentially non-converged iterate is projected through the vehicle dynamics to ensure a model-consistent next pose.
 - Normalized per-rule residuals are recorded at each cycle for auditability.
- **Computational Analysis:**
 - The transcribed NLP has 304 decision variables, 818 constraints, and a sparse Jacobian (0.96% density), which IPOPT/MUMPS exploits.
 - Median solve time was 28.4 ms, with a 95th percentile of 230 ms and maximum of 5.74 s without a limit. With the 90 ms limit, max solve time was 104 ms, and 11.9% of solves hit the limit.
 - An open-loop braking fallback handles solver failures (0.58% of solves).
 - Sensitivity analysis showed graceful degradation, with log-independent compliance remaining near parity even at a 30 ms limit (where 55% of solves hit the limit).

#### 3.2. Log-Independent Evaluation Protocol

The paper introduces a protocol to separate driving quality from resemblance to a recorded human trajectory.

- **Closed-loop Rollouts:** Scenarios from the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) are used in Waymax, with ego policy interacting with reactive Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) non-ego agents. Metrics are evaluated solely on executed states.
- **Compliance Metrics:** Each rule $r$ has a violation predicate $\phi_r$ and applicability indicator $\alpha_r$, yielding a per-rule compliance $c_r(\pi, \omega)$.
- **Taxonomy by Referenced State:**
 - **Log-coupled metrics ($R_{log}$):** Predicates reference the recorded log ($ \xi^{\star}_{\omega}$), e.g., path tracking and route adherence. These measure imitation.
 - **Log-independent metrics ($R_{ind}$):** Predicates depend only on the physically realized executed driving ($D^{\pi}_{\omega}$), e.g., collision, speed limit, drivable surface. These subdivide into map-defined, interaction-defined, and controller-defined.
- **Log-Coupling Confound:**
 - **Proposition 1 (Expert-replay dominance):** Expert replay achieves 100% compliance on log-coupled rules by definition, regardless of physical driving quality.
 - **Corollary 1 (Aggregate imitation premium):** Any aggregate gap between a planner and expert replay decomposes into a non-negative imitation premium (from log-coupled rules) and a genuine driving-quality difference (from log-independent rules).
 - **Proposition 2 (Non-identifiability of driving quality):** Log-coupled metrics can differentiate between policies of identical log-independent driving quality.
- **Imitation Invariance:** A metric is imitation-invariant if its value is unchanged when the reference log is replaced by any other dynamically feasible, rule-satisfying trajectory, while the executed driving is fixed. Log-independent metrics satisfy this.
- **Statistical Methodology:** Wilcoxon signed-rank test (primary), paired t-test (secondary), Benjamini–Hochberg correction for false-discovery rate control, and Cohen’s d for effect size.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Environment:** Waymax simulator with WOMD scenarios, 10 Hz, IDM non-ego agents, ground-truth state.
- **Scenario Set:** 150 WOMD scenarios selected by a complexity-ranked scanner, favoring interaction-rich scenes.
- **Baselines:** A lightweight reactive controller (pure-pursuit + IDM) and a stronger sampling-based planner (PDM-Closed–style), both non-imitative.
- **Falsification Controls:** Exogenous interaction density (measured from logs), log-informed prediction ablation, threshold sensitivity sweep, and a divergence sweep (blending W-SQP's pose towards the log).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of W-SQP was conducted in closed-loop simulation across 150 WOMD scenarios, comparing it against expert replay and two non-imitative baselines, utilizing the proposed log-independent evaluation protocol.

- **Control Performance Against Baselines:**
 - On log-independent (safety/regulatory) rules, W-SQP achieved 93.6% compliance, which was comparable to expert replay (93.7%) and led both non-imitative baselines (PP+IDM at 91.8% and PDM-Closed at 91.5%) by approximately 2 percentage points (pp). This suggests W-SQP's safety and regulatory behavior closely matches that of human drivers among non-imitative controllers.
 - On log-coupled (imitation) rules, W-SQP scored 89.9%, significantly higher than PDM-Closed (67.9%) and PP+IDM (33.0%). However, this higher score is partly attributed to W-SQP's soft route corridor and low-weight tracking reference, which provide route intent.
 - The comparison across planners illustrated a narrow spread (approx. 2 pp) in driving quality (log-independent compliance) versus a large spread (approx. 57 pp) in imitation (log-coupled compliance), with the expert replay always achieving 100% on imitation.
- **Effectiveness of Tiered Slack Mechanism:**
 - An ablation study where all slack weights were set to their geometric mean (no tiering) resulted in a log-independent deficit that increased from -0.04 pp to -0.91 pp and a log-coupled deficit that almost doubled from -10.1 pp to -19.8 pp.
 - Sweeping the priority ratio between adjacent tiers while maintaining constant total stiffness showed that stronger ordering monotonically improved both log-independent and log-coupled deficits, confirming the importance of the priority structure.
 - Fleet-level audit records showed that safety/regulatory surrogates were relaxed infrequently (e.g., collision 2.5%, road-edge 4.9%), while the low-priority route surrogate absorbed most relaxation (approx. 17%), consistent with the tiered design.
- **Compliance Gap is Log-Coupled:**
 - The primary finding is that the apparent aggregate compliance deficit of W-SQP relative to expert replay is almost entirely attributable to log-coupled rules.
 - The mean paired difference for the two log-coupled rules was -10.1 pp.
 - Conversely, the mean paired difference for the sixteen log-independent safety/regulatory rules was -0.04 pp, with a 95% bootstrap confidence interval of [-1.07, +0.52] pp, indicating no systematic group-level deficit. These confidence intervals did not overlap with those for log-coupled rules.
 - The control-defined rules showed an intermediate deficit of -0.75 pp.
 - This confirms that W-SQP is penalized for not imitating the recorded human trajectory, rather than for systematic deficiencies in safety or regulatory compliance.
- **Per-Rule Analysis:**
 - The largest negative deltas were for path tracking (-10.7 pp) and route adherence (-9.5 pp), both log-coupled rules.
 - Small, localized regressions were observed in log-independent rules, specifically non-traversable surface (-2.9 pp), speed limit (-2.3 pp), and drivable surface (-2.1 pp). These deficits primarily occurred in high-divergence scenarios and averaged 0.0 pp in low-divergence scenarios, suggesting they represent recovery transients in challenging situations rather than broad safety regressions.
- **Fairness of Aggregate Score:**
 - The standard aggregate score (including log-coupled rules) recorded W-SQP as losing 44 out of 150 scenarios and winning 32.
 - Under the log-independent score, the verdict was balanced (30 losses, 34 wins), with 14 scenarios flipped out of the "loss" category.
 - This demonstrates that the aggregate score can misleadingly penalize competent non-imitative planners.
- **Falsification Controls:**
 - **Divergence:** As W-SQP's executed pose was increasingly blended toward the logged pose (reducing divergence), the log-coupled deficit smoothly decreased towards zero, while log-independent compliance remained at parity.
 - **Interaction Density:** The log-coupled deficit did not grow with increasing exogenous interaction density; instead, it showed a positive correlation, suggesting that dense traffic funnels the ego onto paths similar to the human's, thus reducing divergence.
 - **Velocity Predictor:** Replacing W-SQP’s constant-velocity agent prediction with a log-informed horizon-average velocity showed minimal impact on the log-coupled difference and no change for log-independent rules, indicating robustness to this specific prediction model.
 - **Generality Across Planners:** The log-coupled ≫ log-independent pattern reproduced on both reactive PP+IDM and sampling-based PDM-Closed-style baselines, confirming the deficit is a metric property rather than specific to W-SQP.
 - **Threshold Artifact:** Sweeping deviation thresholds for log-coupled rules did not eliminate the substantial deficit, indicating it is not a threshold-specific artifact.
 - **Scenario Selection:** The effect persisted and even sharpened in a uniformly random sample of WOMD scenarios, suggesting complexity selection did not inflate the deficit.
- **Real-Time Performance:**
 - Median wall-clock solve time on a workstation was 28.4 ms.
 - With a 90 ms CPU-time limit, the maximum observed wall-clock time was 104 ms, and 11.9% of solves hit the limit.
 - Dynamics projection ensured model-consistent executed poses even for non-converged iterates. This establishes anytime-capable operation on the tested platform.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the development and evaluation of autonomous driving systems in several key areas:

- **Enhanced Auditability and Transparency in Motion Planning:** W-SQP introduces a method for managing complex, priority-ordered rulebooks within a single NMPC framework, providing a clear audit trail. The logging of normalized per-rule residuals offers transparency into the controller's decision-making process, allowing engineers to identify which rules were relaxed and by how much in post-analysis. This addresses a critical need for explainability and justification in safety-critical autonomous systems.
- **Real-time Decision-Making with Priority Biasing:** The tiered-slack approach, with its strongly separated penalty weights, provides a mechanism to bias constraint violations towards lower-priority rules (comfort, efficiency) while maintaining hard actuation bounds. Its computational performance (median 28 ms solve time, 104 ms max under a 90 ms limit) demonstrates its potential for real-time operation in autonomous vehicles, characterizing it as an anytime-capable controller prototype.
- **Fairer Evaluation of Non-Imitative Controllers:** The proposed log-independent evaluation protocol and its empirical validation have significant implications for benchmarking autonomous driving planners. By separating log-coupled metrics (which measure resemblance to human driving) from log-independent metrics (which measure intrinsic driving quality like safety and regulatory compliance), the protocol enables a more accurate assessment of controller performance. This can prevent competent, but non-imitative, planners from being unfairly penalized in aggregate scores, thus fostering innovation in planning approaches that may diverge from human driving while remaining safe and compliant.
- **Reinterpretation of Benchmarking Results:** The findings suggest that a portion of performance degradation observed in benchmarks, particularly when using more reactive non-ego agents, might be attributable to planners diverging from an un-followable human log rather than actual safety compromises. This re-contextualizes existing and future benchmarking outcomes, advocating for a clearer distinction between imitation and quality.
- **Foundation for Future Safety Mechanisms:** While W-SQP does not provide formal safety guarantees, its structured approach to rule handling, combined with auditability, can serve as a component in a broader safety architecture. The explicit tuning knobs for priority bias offer a practical avenue for balancing objectives, which could be integrated with certified feasibility or reachability mechanisms in future work to achieve formal safety claims.

The limitations acknowledged by the authors, such as reliance on a single evaluator, the IDM reactive-agent model, lack of sensing/actuation errors, and the need for embedded profiling, indicate clear directions for future research. However, the core contributions in auditable, real-time, priority-biased planning and a principled evaluation methodology represent advancements in the field of autonomous driving.
