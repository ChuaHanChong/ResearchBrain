# V-Rubrics: Visual Faithfulness via Rubric-Based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> V-Rubrics treats visual faithfulness as a credit-assignment problem in vision-language model post-training. It decomposes reference answers into atomic rubric items scored along Visual Faithfulness, Reasoning Consistency and Instruction Following, then feeds item-level, prefix-localized partial credit into GRPO. Trained from a Qwen3-VL-8B SFT checkpoint on a new 50,248-example annotated set, it beats both that SFT baseline and answer-only GRPO, adding 1.79 Overall Avg points.

## Problem

- Scalar outcome, preference or binary correctness rewards in multimodal RL say only whether an answer is acceptable, never which visual claims are actually grounded.
- Hallucination diagnostics and multimodal judges assess faithfulness post hoc; captioning and instruction-following metrics miss unsupported details and supply no optimizable training signal.
- Text-side rubric RL extends rewards beyond verifiable domains but leaves criteria ungrounded in image evidence and collapsed into a single sequence-level score.

## Method

- **V-Rubrics 50K**: 50,248 examples from 17 grounded sources, rule-filtered, difficulty-stratified by eight rejection-sampling rollouts, then annotated by Gemini-3-Pro into 352,938 atomic VF/RC/IF criteria with importance weights.
- ==Rubric reward==: an LLM verifier binary-scores each self-contained criterion; weight-normalized partial credit blends with a binary answer reward through a mixing coefficient, and PITFALL items veto credit.
- ==Component-wise prefix-localized GRPO advantage==: every rubric item is standardized separately across the rollout group and masked to the response prefix ending at its aligned evidence span.

## Results

- Rubric GRPO reaches **68.04** Overall Avg on general and knowledge benchmarks versus **66.25** for answer-only GRPO and **64.93** for SFT; knowledge average rises **59.35** to **61.88**.
- On visual math, chart and logic it scores **62.45** Overall Avg, **4.00** points above SFT (about **6.8%** relative), with WeMath **86.29** and DynaMath-Worst **42.32**.
- Ablation: sequence-level rubric aggregation gives **67.74** against component-wise prefix credit at **68.04**; scalar GRPO still wins on MathVerse V/O and CharXiv reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward granularity, not reward magnitude, is the lever: making the unit of supervision a grounded proposition changes where the gradient actually lands.
> - Rubric gains concentrate on tasks whose final answer rests on fragile intermediate subclaims; broad capability benchmarks show little or no benefit.
> - Auto-generated rubrics inherit generator and judge bias, and prefix alignment is fuzzy-matched, so item credit is practical local feedback, not exact token supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This research addresses a recognized challenge in the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs): the tendency to generate fluent linguistic responses that are not consistently grounded in the visual evidence provided by an image. Prior work has highlighted instances of object hallucination, multimodal faithfulness evaluation challenges, and reasoning failures in VLMs, particularly when dealing with complex visual data like charts, documents, and diagrams. These studies indicate that VLMs can produce seemingly plausible but factually unsupported details, which conventional metrics for captioning or instruction-following often fail to detect.

Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) alignment pipelines, commonly used for post-training large language models, typically rely on scalar rewards such as preference scores, binary correctness labels, or holistic judge evaluations. While effective for tasks with clear, verifiable outcomes, these scalar rewards are considered insufficient for multimodal reasoning where responses can contain a mix of accurate observations, invalid inferences, and formatting issues. A single outcome score cannot pinpoint the specific visual claim that is ungrounded, the exact reasoning step that failed, or the instruction constraint that was violated.

This work integrates with the emerging area of rubric-based RL, which has demonstrated that instance-specific criteria can extend the utility of reward learning beyond strictly verifiable domains in text-only contexts. The paper extends this concept to VLMs by focusing on developing *visually grounded* criteria. It aims to bridge the gap between diagnostic evaluation of VLM faithfulness and actual training mechanisms, proposing a method to convert fine-grained visual faithfulness criteria into direct supervision signals for VLM optimization. This distinguishes it from other multimodal RL approaches that often still rely on holistic response-level rewards, and from pure evaluation methods by leveraging rubrics for active model training.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a fundamental limitation observed in contemporary Vision-Language Models (VLMs): their responses, while linguistically fluent, frequently exhibit insufficient grounding in the accompanying visual evidence. This can manifest as the mention of unsupported objects, incorrect chart values, or erroneous intermediate inferences, any of which can undermine the overall reliability of an otherwise plausible answer. The paper identifies this issue as a "credit-assignment failure" within multimodal post-training frameworks.

The current approaches to VLM post-training, particularly those involving reinforcement learning (RL), predominantly utilize scalar outcome rewards. These rewards typically provide a single signal indicating whether an entire generated answer is acceptable or not. However, such a coarse-grained signal fails to provide specific feedback on the internal components of a VLM's response. It does not differentiate between correctly grounded visual facts, valid reasoning steps, or accurately followed instructions from those that are unsupported or erroneous. Consequently, a VLM might receive a low reward for an answer that is mostly correct but contains a single ungrounded assertion, or a high reward for a lucky guess. This lack of granularity hinders the model's ability to learn precisely where its visual understanding or reasoning processes succeed or fail.

Against this background, the key objectives of this research are multi-faceted:

1. **Formulate Visual Faithfulness as a Fine-Grained Credit-Assignment Problem**: The core objective is to redefine visual faithfulness not merely as an evaluation metric but as an actionable training problem. This involves decomposing complex VLM responses into atomic, independently scorable propositions.
2. **Introduce a Structured Framework for Reward Generation**: The paper aims to develop a system, termed Visual Rubrics-Based Reinforcement Learning (V-Rubrics RL), that can systematically decompose reference responses into atomic rubric items. These items are categorized by capability dimensions: Visual Faithfulness (VF), Reasoning Consistency (RC), and Instruction Following (IF). This decomposition is intended to provide structured partial credit for grounded content and targeted penalties for unsupported or inconsistent claims.
3. **Construct a Visually Grounded Training Dataset (V-Rubrics 50K)**: A significant objective is the creation of a large-scale training dataset of 50,248 examples. This dataset is designed to pair images, instructions, and reference responses with these atomic VF/RC/IF criteria, complete with importance weights. The goal is to provide rich, structured supervision signals directly relevant to visual grounding during post-training.
4. **Develop a Reinforcement Learning Training Method for Fine-Grained Rewards**: The research seeks to adapt existing RL algorithms, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to effectively utilize these fine-grained rubric-based rewards. Crucially, the objective is not to collapse rubric judgments into a single sequence-level score but to preserve item-level reward components and, where possible, localize their advantages to corresponding response prefixes.
5. **Demonstrate Efficacy and Targeted Improvement**: A key objective is to empirically validate that this rubric-based GRPO approach improves VLM performance. The expectation is that the gains will be most prominent on tasks that specifically demand grounded intermediate reasoning, rather than merely broad VLM capabilities.
6. **Advocate for Visual Grounding as a Training Interface**: Beyond immediate performance gains, the motivation is to establish visual grounding as a reusable and structured training interface for future VLM post-training and alignment efforts.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of VLMs, enabling them to produce answers that are not only fluent but also robustly supported by visual evidence and logical reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The research introduces Visual Rubrics-Based Reinforcement Learning (V-Rubrics RL), a framework designed to convert fine-grained visual rubrics into item-level scores and prefix-localized token advantages for VLM training. The methodology is structured into five main components: problem setup, SFT initialization, V-Rubrics 50K construction, rubric design principles, and rubric-based RL training.

**Problem Setup:**
The framework defines an input as an image-instruction pair (x = (v, q)). A VLM policy (π_θ) generates a response (a). Instead of treating a reference response (y) as a single target, it is decomposed into a set of rubric items (R(x)). Each item (r_j) includes a self-contained criterion, a dimension (d_j ∈ {VF, RC, IF} for Visual Faithfulness, Reasoning Consistency, Instruction Following), an importance label (c_j), and a numeric weight (w_j). This setup allows for granular credit assignment.

**SFT Initialization and Pipeline:**
The process begins with the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model. This backbone is fine-tuned on the public OpenMMReasoner-SFT-874K corpus, adapting OpenMMReasoner's cold-start data recipe. The resulting policy, π_SFT, serves as the fixed supervised checkpoint. For subsequent Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) runs, two independent parameter copies are created: π_θ(0) initializes the trainable actor, and π_ref is a frozen KL reference policy, both initialized from π_SFT.

**V-Rubrics 50K Construction:**
This dataset is a central component, comprising 50,248 examples derived from 17 visually grounded sources, including diagram reasoning, chart understanding, document VQA, mathematical visual reasoning, counting, educational QA, and general visual reasoning datasets.
1. **Normalization and Filtering**: Examples from diverse sources are normalized into a common image–instruction–reference format. Deterministic rule-based filters are applied to retain valid, non-trivial, high-quality content and remove duplicates.
2. **Difficulty Assignment**: The fine-tuned π_SFT generates eight rejection-sampling rollouts for each filtered example. The empirical success rate (k/8) provides a model-relative signal of example difficulty, categorized as hard, medium, or simple. This leads to a fixed training set mixture of 18,121 hard (36.1%), 25,306 medium (50.4%), and 6,821 simple (13.6%) examples.
3. **Rubric Annotation**: Gemini-3-Pro annotates every selected example using a structured multimodal prompt and annotation protocol. This protocol converts the image, instruction, and reference response into atomic VF/RC/IF criteria with explicit importance weights and a shared JSON output schema.

**Rubric Design Principles:**
Four principles guide rubric design:
1. **Visually Grounded**: VF items refer to verifiable image evidence (objects, attributes, relations, counts, text, chart values).
2. **Self-Contained**: Each criterion explicitly states its target, allowing independent scoring by a verifier without external context.
3. **Coverage**: The item set evaluates intermediate visual facts and reasoning steps, not just the final answer.
4. **Importance**: Central criteria receive greater weight, categorized as ESSENTIAL, IMPORTANT, OPTIONAL, or PITFALL.

**Reward Design and RL Training:**
1. **Rubric-Based Reward**: For a generated response (a), an LLM rubric verifier (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B) assigns a binary satisfaction score (s_j(a;x) ∈ {0, 1}) to each criterion. A normalized, importance-weighted partial credit (R_rub) is calculated from positive criteria. This is blended with a binary final-answer reward (R_ans) using a parameter α (set to 0.5), along with a standard binary format reward. Pitfall violations act as a semantic veto.
2. **Rubrics-Guided RL Training**: The training utilizes Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
 - **Group-Relative Standardization**: For each score component (u), its group-relative standardization (z_u) is computed by normalizing against the mean and standard deviation of scores across a group of rollouts.
 - **Component-wise, Prefix-Localized Credit**: Unlike sequence-level GRPO which assigns a single standardized reward to the entire response, this method uses a more granular approach. The standardized answer advantage (α * z_R_ans) is broadcast across the full response. Each available rubric advantage ((1−α) * ρ_j * z_s_j) is standardized separately and contributes only through its prefix mask (M_j,t). This mask is determined by aligning the verifier's supporting evidence sentence to the response, localizing the advantage to the corresponding prefix (t ≤ t_j,end). Unlocalized items contribute sequence-wide, item-factorized credit.

**Evaluation**:
Models are evaluated using VLMEvalKit on 10 benchmark families covering general VLM ability, knowledge-oriented reasoning, visual math, chart reasoning, and logic (e.g., MMBench, MMMU, MathVista, LogicVista, CharXiv). Standard accuracy and unweighted averages are reported. The LLM judge (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B) also performs answer equivalence and rubric verification for training rewards.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments demonstrate that the proposed rubric-based reinforcement learning approach consistently improves the performance of Vision-Language Models, particularly on tasks requiring visually grounded reasoning. The findings are summarized as follows:

**Overall Performance Improvement:**
The rubric-based GRPO method ("+ GRPO w/ rubrics (Ours)") generally outperformed both the supervised fine-tuned (SFT) baseline and the answer-only GRPO baseline ("+ GRPO"). This indicates that integrating fine-grained, visually grounded rubrics into the RL training signal is effective for enhancing VLM capabilities.

**Targeted Gains on Knowledge and Academic Reasoning Benchmarks (Table 1):**
- On general VLM and knowledge-oriented benchmarks, rubric-based GRPO showed an improvement of 1.79 points in "Overall Avg." (combining MMBench-Dev and three knowledge metrics) compared to answer-level GRPO.
- The gains were primarily concentrated on the MMMU and MMMU-Pro benchmarks, which emphasize multi-step academic reasoning and complex knowledge application. For instance, on MMMU-Val, rubric-based GRPO achieved 70.56, surpassing SFT (66.78) and answer-only GRPO (68.00).
- In contrast, the gains on MMBench-Dev (a measure of broad VLM capability) were marginal, with scalar GRPO sometimes showing slightly higher performance. This suggests that rubrics contribute most significantly where a task requires more than simple recognition or direct instruction following, involving deeper reasoning.

**Enhanced Performance on Visual Math, Chart, and Logic Tasks (Table 2):**
- The impact of rubric-based GRPO was more pronounced on visual math, chart, and logic reasoning tasks. It improved the "Overall Avg." (across five visual-math and two chart/logic metrics) by 4.00 points over the SFT baseline and maintained a slight lead over answer-level GRPO.
- The strongest improvements were observed on metrics that depend on grounded intermediate perception and multi-step inference, such as MathVision, DynaMath, WeMath, and LogicVista. For example, on MathVision-test, rubric-based GRPO achieved 58.88, compared to SFT (55.46) and answer-only GRPO (56.71). Similarly, on WeMath-Loose, it reached 86.29, outperforming SFT (77.43) and answer-only GRPO (84.86).
- Conversely, answer-level GRPO remained marginally better on MathVerse V/O and CharXiv reasoning, indicating that the benefits of rubric-based training are not uniform across all benchmarks but are concentrated where the rubric items can provide useful partial credit for specific reasoning components.

**Ablation Study on Reward and Credit Assignment (Table 3):**
An ablation study confirmed the importance of the specific reward design:
- Answer-only GRPO achieved an "Overall Avg." of 66.25.
- Aggregating rubric rewards into a scalar sequence-level signal, combined with the answer reward, improved performance to 67.74, a gain of 1.49 points.
- The full rubric-based approach, which uses component-wise standardization and prefix-localized credit, further improved the "Overall Avg." to 68.04. This additional 0.30-point difference over scalar rubric aggregation suggests that retaining item-level granularity and localizing credit within the response contributes to optimizing VLM behavior.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
Qualitative examples illustrated how rubric training leads to more accurate and faithful reasoning. For instance, in one example, the answer-only checkpoint made an unsupported inference regarding age to birth year, while the rubric-trained model correctly preserved the intermediate reasoning step. In another case, the answer-only model misread a graph location, whereas the rubric-trained model traced the visible segments before providing an accurate answer. These examples demonstrate that rubrics effectively enforce intermediate visual and logical claims, which scalar rewards might overlook, thereby improving the model's grounding.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes several contributions that have implications for the development and understanding of Vision-Language Models.

Firstly, the work addresses a fundamental limitation of current VLMs: their propensity for visual hallucinations and ungrounded reasoning, even when producing fluent textual responses. By reframing visual faithfulness as a fine-grained credit-assignment problem, the paper moves beyond mere diagnostic evaluation of these issues towards a trainable solution. This could lead to the development of more reliable and trustworthy VLMs for applications where factual accuracy and grounding in visual evidence are critical, such as scientific analysis, medical diagnostics, or legal document review.

Secondly, the introduction of Visual Rubrics-Based Reinforcement Learning (V-Rubrics RL) provides a novel methodological framework for VLM post-training. The decomposition of reference responses into atomic Visual Faithfulness (VF), Reasoning Consistency (RC), and Instruction Following (IF) criteria, coupled with prefix-localized credit assignment, offers a granular approach to reward shaping. This departs from conventional scalar reward systems, demonstrating that *how* feedback is structured and delivered to a model can significantly influence its learning outcomes, particularly in complex multimodal reasoning tasks. This granular feedback mechanism could reduce the occurrence of subtle errors that are often missed by holistic evaluation metrics.

Thirdly, the creation of V-Rubrics 50K is a tangible resource for the research community. This dataset, comprising over 50,000 examples with detailed, machine-generated rubric annotations across 17 diverse visually grounded sources, provides a scalable and structured form of supervision. This dataset can facilitate future research into fine-grained multimodal alignment, reward model development, and the training of more robust reasoning agents.

The empirical results, showing consistent improvements over SFT and answer-only GRPO baselines, especially on reasoning-heavy visual tasks, underscore the practical efficacy of the V-Rubrics approach. The findings suggest that future VLM post-training paradigms should consider visual grounding not just as an evaluation diagnostic, but as an explicit, structured interface for guiding model optimization. This could influence the design of VLM architectures and training objectives to inherently prioritize visual fidelity and logical consistency.

The transparency in discussing limitations, such as the reliance on automatically generated rubrics, the approximate nature of prefix localization, and potential judge-model biases, is also noteworthy. This encourages further research into refining rubric quality, exploring human-in-the-loop validation, and testing the generalizability of these methods across different model families and sensitive domains. Overall, this work advances the field by proposing a structured, trainable approach to enhance the visual faithfulness and reasoning consistency of VLMs, paving the way for more dependable multimodal AI systems.
