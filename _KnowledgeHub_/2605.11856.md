# UniVLR: Unifying Text and Vision in Visual Latent Reasoning for Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> UniVLR unifies textual reasoning and auxiliary visual evidence within a single visual channel, enabling multimodal large language models to reason using compact visual latent tokens. This framework, from a collaboration including the University of Science and Technology of China and Tsinghua University, reduces generated reasoning tokens by 15.2x on average and boosts performance on visual reasoning benchmarks, for instance, raising the V* score from 77.4 to 82.7 and HRBench4K from 69.0 to 73.3.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal LLMs for visual reasoning often rely on tool-based approaches, which are rigid, suffer from latency, and have stability issues with externally generated auxiliary images.
- Interleaved visual latent reasoning methods, while internalizing some visual deliberation, still require extensive explicit textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT), reducing efficiency and increasing token generation costs.
- The fragmented nature of interleaved text and visual tokens can lead to auxiliary images not being fully integrated into the reasoning process, resulting in sparse attention and underutilization of visual evidence.

## Method

- A ==Unified Visual Canvas Rendering== component transforms explicit textual reasoning traces and auxiliary images into a single, cohesive visual representation.
- A ==two-stage Unified Visual Latent Alignment== strategy teaches the **UniVLR** MLLM to autoregressively generate ==continuous visual latent tokens==, first grounding visual latents and then unifying text-vision representations.
- During inference, the model operates in a ==continuous autoregressive mode==, reasoning entirely through these compact unified visual latent tokens and directly decoding the final answer, bypassing explicit textual CoT.

## Results

- UniVLR achieved an average **15.2x reduction** in generated reasoning tokens during inference compared to interleaved methods, leading to notable inference time reductions, e.g., **33.8%** on HRBench8K.
- The framework improved performance on various benchmarks, with the V* score increasing from **77.4 to 82.7** and HRBench4K from **69.0 to 73.3**, surpassing both textual reasoning baselines and other visual latent reasoning methods.
- Causal perturbation experiments demonstrated that manipulating the latent hidden states directly impacted task accuracy, confirming that these latent tokens effectively encode task-relevant visual reasoning information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating textual reasoning and visual evidence into a single visual latent channel significantly reduces the number of generated tokens and improves inference efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.
> - The model's latent tokens are deeply visually grounded, carrying essential task-relevant reasoning information that, when perturbed, consistently impacts performance.
> - A carefully designed two-stage training curriculum, along with aspect-aware 2D pooling for target latent generation, is essential for robust and stable alignment of the continuous visual latent representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are increasingly being developed to perform complex reasoning tasks that involve visual information, moving beyond mere image understanding to "thinking with images." This capability is crucial for applications such as geometric problem-solving, chart question answering, high-resolution visual comprehension, and embodied planning, where models must repeatedly interact with visual data.

The current research landscape for MLLMs in visual reasoning can be broadly categorized into two main paradigms:

1. **Tool-based Visual Reasoning:** This paradigm augments reasoning chains with externally generated auxiliary images (ee.g., crops, annotations, sketches) and interleaves them with textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT). While effective, this approach is limited by the rigidity of predefined tools, the latency associated with external calls, and the potential instability of generated auxiliary images, which can hinder flexible and continuous visual reasoning. Examples include PixelReasoner and DeepEyes.

2. **Visual Latent Reasoning:** As an alternative, this paradigm involves MLLMs autoregressively generating latent visual tokens in the visual embedding space, enabling a form of internal visual reasoning without explicit tool calls. This aims to overcome the bottlenecks of tool-based methods. Despite progress, most existing visual latent reasoning methods, such as LVR, Monet, SkiLa, and CoVT, adopt an interleaved design. In this design, explicit textual CoT and latent visual tokens are generated alternately. This interleaved approach presents two limitations:
 - **Limited Efficiency:** It still requires full explicit text CoT segments between visual latent steps, which does not substantially reduce the overall generation cost compared to standard text-based CoT.
 - **Fragmented Reasoning:** Auxiliary images may not fully participate in the reasoning process because the model can rely on nearby text tokens rather than consistently attending to earlier auxiliary images. Analysis has indicated that subsequent CoT tokens exhibit sparse and diffuse attention to auxiliary images in this interleaved setting.

UniVLR addresses the limitations of the interleaved design in visual latent reasoning by proposing a unified approach. It departs from the notion that explicit text CoT must be an independent reasoning path. Instead, UniVLR treats textual reasoning and auxiliary visual evidence as a shared visual workspace, rendering reasoning traces and auxiliary images into a single visual representation. This unification aims to enhance both the efficiency and the integration of visual information in MLLM reasoning, positioning UniVLR as an advancement towards a more cohesive paradigm for visual thinking in MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce UniVLR, a unified visual latent reasoning framework that integrates textual reasoning and auxiliary visual evidence into a single visual workspace. This framework aims to enable MLLMs to perform efficient visual thinking by reasoning solely through compact visual latent tokens, without relying on explicit textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) at inference time.

The motivation for this approach stems from several observations and identified limitations of existing methods:

1. **Inefficiency of Interleaved Visual Latent Reasoning:** Current visual latent reasoning methods, while attempting to internalize visual deliberation, still largely employ an interleaved design. This involves alternately generating explicit text CoT and latent visual tokens. This design limits efficiency gains because the model is still required to produce verbose textual CoT segments, which does not substantially reduce the overall token generation cost. A key motivation for latent reasoning is to shorten reasoning trajectories, and the interleaved approach falls short in this regard.

2. **Fragmented Visual Information Integration:** In the interleaved paradigm, explicit text tokens and latent visual tokens exist in heterogeneous forms within a long sequence. This can lead to fragmented reasoning where auxiliary images may not be fully integrated. The model might preferentially rely on nearby text tokens, resulting in sparse and diffuse attention to earlier auxiliary images. This suggests that auxiliary images are not consistently and actively participating in subsequent reasoning steps.

3. **Cognitive Basis for Unified Visual Workspace:** The paper draws motivation from cognitive science, observing that humans often organize notes, annotations, and diagrams within a shared perceptual workspace during complex reasoning. This suggests that switching between separate text and image channels is not always the most natural or efficient mode of cognition.

4. **Leveraging MLLM Capabilities for Rendered Text:** Modern MLLMs, due to extensive multimodal pretraining, have developed robust OCR and layout understanding abilities. This makes rendered text a readily interpretable visual signal. Therefore, intermediate textual reasoning does not necessarily need to remain as explicit text tokens but can be rendered into images, spatially composed with auxiliary images, and processed through a unified visual pathway.

Based on these motivations, UniVLR aims to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To render each textual reasoning step into an image and spatially concatenate it with the corresponding auxiliary image, creating a "unified image" that jointly carries reasoning process and visual evidence.
- To learn to compress this unified representation into compact visual latent tokens.
- To enable the model to reason entirely through these unified visual latents at inference time, directly decoding only the final answer, thereby avoiding external tool calls and verbose text reasoning.
- To demonstrate that this unified design leads to denser and more focused attention over auxiliary images, indicating more active participation of visual evidence in reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniVLR proposes a unified visual latent reasoning framework that processes both textual reasoning and auxiliary visual evidence within a single visual latent channel. The methodology consists of three main components: Unified Visual Canvas Rendering, a two-stage Unified Visual Latent Alignment procedure, and Continuous Autoregressive Inference.

#### 3.1. Unified Visual Canvas Rendering

This component is responsible for transforming heterogeneous reasoning traces into a unified visual representation.
- **Unified Canvas Construction:** For each training instance, a textual reasoning trace $R = \{r_1, \ldots, r_L\}$ and auxiliary images $U = \{u_1, \ldots, u_M\}$ are input to a rendering function $\Phi(\cdot)$. This function composes them into a unified visual canvas $c = \Phi(R, U)$. The canvas visually integrates textual reasoning with auxiliary evidence, allowing both to be processed via the vision pathway.
- **Adaptive Rendering:** The $\Phi(\cdot)$ function employs lightweight adaptive strategies to ensure the canvas is compact and readable. For text, it adjusts font size, line wrapping, and spacing based on length. For images, it resizes according to aspect ratios and can highlight salient regions with visual cues (e.g., boxes, arrows). Appendix B.5 provides detailed algorithms (Vertical Layout, Compact Left-Right Layout, Fixed-Canvas Adaptive Wrap) for these rendering strategies, demonstrating their dynamic adjustment of canvas dimensions and component layouts.
- **Target Visual Embeddings:** The vision encoder $E_{vis}$ of the base MLLM extracts a 2D visual feature map $F \in \mathbb{R}^{H_f \times W_f \times d}$ from the unified canvas $c$. To obtain $K$ fixed-length latent supervision targets while preserving spatial structure, aspect-aware 2D pooling $\Pi_{h^\star, w^\star}$ is applied. This pooling identifies an optimal grid $(h^\star, w^\star)$ that minimizes aspect-ratio distortion, resulting in the target latent sequence $\tilde{Z} = [\tilde{z}_1, \ldots, \tilde{z}_K] \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$.

#### 3.2. Unified Visual Latent Alignment

This section describes the training process where the model learns to autoregressively generate continuous visual latent tokens by aligning them with the constructed visual supervision targets. A two-stage alignment strategy is used for stability and semantic unification.
- **Stage I: Visual Latent Grounding:** This initial stage establishes a continuous reasoning interface between the MLLM and its visual embedding space. Auxiliary visual reasoning images are used as latent alignment targets. The model is trained to generate $K$ continuous latent tokens and then decode the final answer.
- **Stage II: Text–Vision Unified Alignment:** Starting from the Stage I checkpoint, the training targets are switched to the unified canvas targets, which combine rendered textual reasoning and auxiliary visual evidence. Training on these targets teaches the model to represent both explicit reasoning traces and visual evidence through a single latent visual channel.
- **Latent Alignment Objective:** Both stages use an autoregressive latent training format: $S = [X, \text{<|univlr\_start|>}, \tilde{z}_1, \ldots, \tilde{z}_K, \text{<|univlr\_end|>}, A]$, where $X$ is the input, $A$ is the answer, and $\tilde{z}_k$ are target latent embeddings. The model predicts $\hat{z}_k$ from its hidden states. The alignment loss $L_{align}$ combines normalized regression loss and cosine similarity: $L_{align} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \left[ \frac{1}{d} \| \text{LN}(\hat{z}_k) - \text{LN}(\tilde{z}_k) \|_2^2 + (1 - \text{cos}(\text{LN}(\hat{z}_k), \text{LN}(\tilde{z}_k))) \right]$.
- **Total Objective and Teacher Forcing:** The final objective $L = L_{CE} + \lambda_{align} L_{align}$ combines latent alignment with standard language modeling. $L_{CE}$ applies to discrete tokens ($\text{<|univlr\_start|>}$, $\text{<|univlr\_end|>}$, and $A$). Latent teacher forcing is employed during training, where the target latent embedding is fed as the next latent input to stabilize continuous autoregressive learning.

#### 3.3. Continuous Autoregressive Inference

After training, UniVLR performs inference without the rendering function $\Phi(\cdot)$ or the external vision encoder.
- **Latent Reasoning Mode:** When the model generates $\text{<|univlr\_start|>}$, it switches from discrete token generation to continuous latent reasoning. At each step $k$, a latent token $\hat{z}_k$ is predicted from the previous hidden state and directly fed back as the input embedding for the next step ($e_k = \hat{z}_k$). This recurrence continues for a predefined latent budget of $K$ steps.
- **Answer Decoding:** After $K$ latent steps, the model emits $\text{<|univlr\_end|>}$ and reverts to the discrete vocabulary space to decode the final answer $A$. This results in a compact continuous reasoning phase followed by a brief natural-language answer.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

UniVLR is instantiated on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. The vision encoder and patch-merger are frozen to preserve visual priors. The LLM backbone and a lightweight MLP-based latent reasoning head are fine-tuned. The teacher forcing latent budget is $K_{train}=24$, and the inference budget is $K_{infer}=12$. A custom dataset, UniVLR-SFT-140K, is constructed from Visual-CoT and rigorously filtered Zebra-CoT samples, organized for the two-stage curriculum. Rigorous data filtering (lower-bound, upper-bound, aspect-ratio) ensures high-quality visual supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on various perception and visual reasoning benchmarks, including V\*, HRBench (4K and 8K), and MME-RealWorld-Lite, provide detailed insights into UniVLR's performance, efficiency, and underlying mechanisms.

- **Performance and Efficiency (RQ1):**
 - UniVLR demonstrates improved performance compared to textual reasoning baselines (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B), with notable gains across all evaluated benchmarks (e.g., V\* score improved from 77.4 to 82.7, HRBench4K from 69.0 to 73.3).
 - It achieves the highest overall scores among visual latent reasoning methods (LVR, Monet, SkiLa, CoVT), reaching an average overall score of 68.9.
 - A significant finding is the substantial reduction in generated reasoning tokens (15.2x fewer tokens on average) while maintaining or exceeding accuracy. UniVLR uses only 12 latent tokens and no explicit intermediate text during inference, in contrast to interleaved methods that generate 190-270 tokens.
 - Additional efficiency evaluations confirm that UniVLR reduces average per-sample inference time (e.g., 33.8% reduction on HRBench8K) and maintains a compact GPU memory footprint, outperforming several baselines in terms of speed.

- **Unified Latent Representation Analysis (RQ2):**
 - Analysis of hidden-state distributions indicates that UniVLR's latent tokens align more closely with visual-token representations than textual-token representations. This suggests the model indeed utilizes a visually grounded latent channel.
 - Causal perturbation analysis reveals that manipulating latent hidden states (zeroing, adding Gaussian noise, repeating states) consistently reduces accuracy, demonstrating that these latent tokens carry task-relevant reasoning information and are not merely placeholders.

- **Effect of Latent Token Number (RQ3):**
 - Introducing a small number of latent tokens significantly improves performance compared to direct answering (K=0).
 - Performance typically peaks at a moderate latent budget (e.g., K=12 for inference) and can decline with an excessively large number of tokens, suggesting a potential for representation drift.
 - Stage-II training contributes to more stable latent scaling, resulting in higher performance and a wider stable plateau across different inference-time latent budgets.

- **Ablation Study (RQ4):**
 - **Reasoning Formulation and Curriculum (RFC):** A two-stage training curriculum (Stage I for visual latent grounding, Stage II for text-vision unified alignment) is critical; single-stage mixed training leads to a performance drop. The Vertical Layout rendering strategy (Algorithm 1) proved most effective, outperforming Left-Right and Fixed Wrap layouts. Rigorous data filtering (from Zebra-CoT) is essential to prevent noisy supervision and ensure the latent tokens encode relevant visual semantics.
 - **Representation Alignment (REPA):** Preserving 2D spatial topology through aspect-aware 2D pooling is fundamental for reasoning performance. A lightweight MLP projection head optimally balances visual feature alignment and autoregressive stability. Aligning latent tokens to middle-layer hidden states yielded better performance than front or final layers, suggesting that middle layers retain richer visual and spatial information.
 - **Impact of $\lambda_{align}$ (Appendix D.1):** A latent alignment loss weight of $\lambda_{align}=0.1$ provided the best accuracy-stability trade-off. Higher values generally led to performance degradation, suggesting that excessive alignment pressure can reduce latent state flexibility.
 - **Latent Teacher Forcing (Appendix D.3):** Full latent teacher forcing during training was found to be more effective than scheduled sampling. Scheduled sampling (half-replay) led to performance degradation, indicating that stabilizing the high-dimensional continuous latent manifold with clean supervision is more beneficial than directly reducing exposure bias in this context.

- **Generalization on Diverse Multimodal Tasks (Appendix D.2):**
 - UniVLR demonstrates competitive generalization capabilities across various multimodal tasks, including TextVQA, MME\_translation, POPE (object hallucination), and WeMath\_loose, without severe catastrophic forgetting.
 - It showed improvements on TextVQA and POPE, suggesting enhanced perceptual grounding, better extraction of visual textual evidence, and reduced object hallucination due to its visually-anchored latent channel.
 - Performance on MME\_translation remained perfect, and on WeMath\_loose, while slightly below the base model, it was competitive with other visual latent reasoning baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniVLR represents an advancement in visual latent reasoning for MLLMs by proposing a unified framework that fundamentally alters how models process textual reasoning and auxiliary visual evidence. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Visual Latent Reasoning:** UniVLR introduces a new paradigm where explicit textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is no longer maintained as a separate inference-time channel. Instead, both reasoning semantics and visual evidence are integrated into a single, unified visual representation. This moves visual latent reasoning toward a more cohesive and natural form of multimodal thinking, inspired by human cognitive processes.

- **Enhanced Efficiency:** The framework significantly improves the efficiency of MLLMs in visual reasoning tasks. By compressing reasoning traces and auxiliary images into compact visual latent tokens, UniVLR substantially reduces the number of generated tokens (e.g., 15.2x reduction) and associated inference time and memory usage. This efficiency gain is critical for deploying MLLMs in latency-sensitive, perception-heavy applications.

- **Improved Reasoning Integration:** The unified visual canvas encourages denser and more focused attention to auxiliary images, suggesting that visual evidence actively participates in the reasoning process rather than being marginally referenced. This leads to more robust and accurate multimodal comprehension.

- **Strong Performance:** UniVLR achieves competitive or superior performance on a diverse set of real-world perception and visual reasoning benchmarks, outperforming prior visual latent reasoning methods that rely on interleaved text-latent designs. This demonstrates the effectiveness of the unified approach in complex tasks.

- **Robustness and Generality:** The consistent performance gains observed across different base MLLMs, such as Qwen2.5-VL-7B, highlight the robustness and generality of the UniVLR framework. This suggests its applicability to a broader range of MLLM architectures.

- **Mitigation of Hallucination:** The improved performance on tasks like POPE indicates that grounding reasoning states in a unified visual channel can encourage models to rely more strongly on perceptual evidence, potentially reducing object hallucination and improving factual accuracy in multimodal contexts.

**Potential Applications:** The advancements offered by UniVLR hold promise for applications requiring efficient and deeply integrated visual reasoning, including:
- High-resolution document analysis and understanding.
- Complex chart and diagram interpretation.
- Guided visual search and spatial reasoning.
- Embodied AI and robotic planning, where continuous visual deliberation is essential.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges limitations, such as dependence on the vision encoder's OCR capabilities, reduced direct interpretability of latent tokens compared to explicit CoT, and the use of a fixed latent-token budget. It also notes that UniVLR is not intended to replace tool-based reasoning for tasks requiring exact measurements or executable visual manipulations. These considerations highlight areas for future research, including developing complementary interpretability tools and dynamic latent budgeting mechanisms, to ensure that efficiency gains do not compromise accountability and safety in real-world deployments.
