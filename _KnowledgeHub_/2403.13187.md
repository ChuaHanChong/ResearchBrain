# Evolutionary Optimization of Model Merging Recipes

> [!summary] Summary
> Sakana AI researchers developed Evolutionary Model Merge, an automated framework utilizing evolutionary algorithms to optimize the combination of pre-trained large language and vision-language models. This method produced EvoLLM-JP, achieving 55.2% on Japanese math reasoning and outperforming 70B-parameter models on general Japanese benchmarks, alongside EvoVLM-JP, which improved culturally-aware visual question answering for Japanese.

## Problem

- Current model merging techniques heavily rely on human intuition and trial-and-error, making recipe discovery unsystematic and limiting scalability.
- Training large foundation models from scratch is prohibitively expensive and resource-intensive.
- Systematically combining knowledge from models trained on disparate domains to achieve novel, emergent capabilities is challenging.

## Method

- Utilizes ==evolutionary algorithms, specifically CMA-ES==, to automate the discovery of optimal model merging "recipes."
- Explores the ==Parameter Space (PS)== to optimize layer-wise weight merging coefficients using an enhanced TIES-Merging with DARE.
- Navigates the ==Data Flow Space (DFS)== by evolving sequences of layers from different models, incorporating a learnable scaling matrix to manage distribution shifts.
- Allows for hybrid approaches combining both parameter and data flow space optimizations.

## Results

- EvoLLM-JP achieved **55.2%** accuracy on MGSM-JA (Japanese Math), a substantial improvement over source models (below **30%**) and surpassed a 70B-parameter model on general Japanese benchmarks (**70.5%** vs **68.3%**).
- EvoVLM-JP demonstrated enhanced cultural awareness and improved performance on Japanese visual question answering, achieving **5**1.2%** ROUGE-L** on a new culturally-specific benchmark, compared to **41.1%** for the baseline.
- The evolutionary approach consistently outperformed unoptimized merging methods and specialized LoRA fine-tuning, avoiding catastrophic forgetting while achieving higher task-specific performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolutionary algorithms effectively automate the search for optimal model merging recipes across vast configuration spaces.
> - Optimizing for a specific cross-domain task can lead to surprising generalization and performance improvements on unrelated, broader benchmarks.
> - A learnable scaling matrix is critical in Data Flow Space merging to mitigate distribution shifts between layers from different base models.
> - Evolutionary merging can integrate disparate knowledge domains, leading to emergent capabilities that are difficult to achieve with conventional methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas within artificial intelligence, primarily large language models (LLMs), model merging, and evolutionary algorithms.

**Model Merging:** The landscape of LLM development has been largely dominated by the creation and training of increasingly larger models, demanding immense computational resources and data. Model merging has recently emerged as a highly cost-effective and promising alternative. Instead of training new models from scratch, it combines existing pre-trained models, often fine-tuned for specific tasks, into a single, more versatile architecture. The paper highlights that the HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard is now "dominated by merged models," signaling a significant paradigm shift towards leveraging the collective intelligence of the open-source community.

Traditional model merging techniques, such as "model soup" (simple weight averaging), Task Arithmetic (combining "task vectors" representing learned differences), TIES-Merging, and DARE (which addresses parameter interference and sparsification), have made significant strides. However, these methods often rely heavily on "human intuition and domain knowledge" for selecting models and "merging recipes," which the authors describe as a "black art or alchemy." This dependency limits the full potential and scalability of model merging given the vast diversity of available open models and benchmarks. The paper also references "Frankenmerging," a technique that involves stacking layers from different models, but notes its current reliance on trial-and-error, making it difficult to systematically optimize.

**Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) and Neural Architecture Search (NAS):** The work draws a direct parallel to Neural Architecture Search (NAS), a field where EAs have been successfully applied to discover novel neural network architectures. However, a key distinction is emphasized: unlike traditional NAS, which requires training each candidate architecture from scratch—a computationally prohibitive process for large models—this work *composes* *existing* pre-trained layers or models. This bypasses the costly inner-loop training, making the approach significantly more compute-efficient. Concepts like Weight Agnostic Neural Networks (WANNs) and Hypernetworks are cited as related ideas where network structures or weights can be explored or generated without extensive gradient-based training.

**Open-source AI Development:** The paper explicitly frames model merging as analogous to "traditional open-source software development," where components are continually improved and combined by a community of developers, researchers, and hobbyists. This collaborative ecosystem, exemplified by tools like `mergekit`, has democratized foundation model development. This work aims to further empower this community by automating the most challenging aspect of model merging: recipe discovery.

In essence, this research seeks to bridge the gap between the efficiency and accessibility of model merging and the systematic, exploratory power of evolutionary algorithms. By automating the discovery of complex merging strategies, it aims to unlock new levels of performance and cross-domain capabilities that are difficult for human experts to find, ultimately contributing to a more efficient and collaborative AI development paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind this research stem from the acknowledged limitations of current large language model (LLM) development and existing model merging practices:

1. **Automate Model Merging Recipe Discovery:** The primary motivation is to move beyond the current "black art" or "alchemy" of model merging, which heavily relies on human intuition, domain knowledge, and trial-and-error. The authors aim to introduce a systematic, automated approach using evolutionary algorithms to discover effective model combinations and merging recipes.

2. **Enhance Cost-Effectiveness and Resource Efficiency:** Developing powerful LLMs from scratch demands substantial computational resources and extensive training data. Model merging offers a cost-effective alternative by combining existing pre-trained models. This work seeks to maximize this advantage by efficiently harnessing the "collective intelligence" of diverse open-source models, thereby reducing the need for costly additional training.

3. **Facilitate Cross-Domain Merging and Emergent Capabilities:** A key objective is to demonstrate the ability to merge models from disparate domains (e.g., a non-English LLM with a Math LLM, or a non-English LLM with a Vision-Language Model). The goal is to show that such cross-domain merging, when optimized evolutionarily, can yield novel and unintuitive capabilities that might exceed what conventional human design strategies could achieve.

4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization:** The authors are motivated to produce highly capable merged models that not only excel on specific target tasks (like Japanese Math reasoning) but also exhibit surprising generalization to a wide array of other, unoptimized benchmarks. They aim to contribute these state-of-the-art models back to the open-source community.

5. **Introduce a New Paradigm for Foundation Model Development:** Ultimately, the research aims to challenge the conventional, gradient-based, and resource-intensive paradigm of foundation model development. By demonstrating that evolutionary methods can produce competitive and even superior models without requiring extensive training, the authors intend to pave the way for exploring alternative, potentially more efficient, approaches to AI model creation.

In summary, the authors seek to democratize access to advanced AI capabilities by making model composition more systematic, efficient, and capable of discovering unforeseen synergies between existing models, particularly for specialized and cross-cultural applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, named "Evolutionary Model Merge," is to employ evolutionary algorithms to automatically discover optimal "recipes" for combining foundation models. The approach systematically explores two orthogonal configuration spaces: the Parameter Space (PS) and the Data Flow Space (DFS), and also allows for their integration.

#### 3.1. Merging in the Parameter Space (PS)

- **Goal:** To combine the *weights* of multiple foundational models that share the *same neural network architecture* into a unified model, aiming for performance superior to individual components.
- **Mechanism:** The method builds upon existing state-of-the-art weight merging techniques, specifically enhancing **TIES-Merging** with **DARE**. This allows for more granular, layer-wise control over parameter merging.
- **Optimization Target:** The evolutionary algorithm optimizes specific merging configuration parameters, including sparsification and weight mixing coefficients, for each layer (including input/output embeddings).
- **Evolutionary Algorithm:** **CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy)**, implemented in Optuna, is used. This algorithm is well-suited for high-dimensional, non-convex optimization problems.
- **Fitness Function:** Performance metrics on a chosen task (e.g., accuracy for math problems, ROUGE score for VQA) guide the evolutionary search.

#### 3.2. Merging in the Data Flow Space (DFS)

- **Goal:** To optimize the *inference path* of tokens through a combination of layers from *different models*, while keeping the original layer weights intact. This is akin to an advanced "Frankenmerging" approach.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of blending weights, DFS merging selects a sequence of layers from a collection of diverse models. For a sequence of `T` inference steps, the method searches for which layer from which model `L(t)i,j` should be used at each step `t`.
- **Search Space Management:** To manage the astronomically large search space (M+1)^T, where M is the total number of layers across models, the method simplifies it. It conceptually lays out all layers in sequential order (model A layers, then model B layers, repeated `r` times). An indicator array `I` (of size `M x r`) determines whether a specific layer is included or excluded, reducing the search space to 2^T.
- **Addressing Distribution Shift:** A critical innovation is the inclusion of a learnable scaling matrix `W` (or a hypernetwork to parameterize `W`). This matrix scales inputs as they transition from a layer in one model to a layer in another, mitigating distribution shifts that would otherwise degrade performance. `W` is co-optimized with the indicator array `I` by the evolutionary algorithm.
- **Constraints:** Initial efforts limited to serial connections and non-adaptive configurations. For LLM experiments, the tokenizer and input/output embeddings of one base model (Model A) were fixed, and its initial/final transformer layers defined the start/end of the inference path.

#### 3.3. Merging in Both Spaces (Hybrid Approach)

- **Concept:** The PS and DFS merging approaches are orthogonal and can be combined.
- **Implementation:** A PS-merged model can be generated first and then included in the pool of source models for a subsequent DFS merging step.
- **Multi-objective Optimization:** The framework can be extended with multi-objective genetic algorithms (e.g., NSGA-II) for scenarios where multiple objectives need to be balanced.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup Details

- **Japanese Math LLM:**
 - **Source Models:** Three 7B-parameter models fine-tuned from Mistral-7B-v0.1: `shisa-gamma-7b-v1` (Japanese general LLM), `WizardMath-7B-V1.1` and `Abel-7B-002` (English Math LLMs).
 - **Datasets:** MGSM-JA (test set) for final evaluation, and a custom Japanese-translated subset of GSM8k (different samples) for evolutionary optimization to prevent overfitting.
 - **Evaluation:** Zero-shot pass@1 accuracy for MGSM-JA (numerical value + Japanese reasoning text), and Japanese Language Model Evaluation Harness (JP-LMEH) for general Japanese proficiency.
 - **Optimization:** CMA-ES for 1000 trials (PS) or 100 generations (DFS) with specific hyperparameters.
- **Japanese VLM:**
 - **Source Models:** `shisa-gamma-7b-v1` (Japanese LLM) and the LLM component of `LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B` (English VLM). Both 7B-parameter Mistral-based models.
 - **Datasets:** `JA-VG-VQA-500` and a newly created `JA-VLM-Bench-In-the-Wild` (42 images, 50 questions, Japanese cultural content) for evaluation. Another subset of Japanese Visual Genome VQA for optimization.
 - **Evaluation:** ROUGE-L with Japanese language detection to penalize non-Japanese responses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results demonstrating the effectiveness of evolutionary optimization for model merging across different domains and modalities.

#### 4.1. Japanese Math LLM (EvoLLM-JP)

- **Significant Performance Uplift:** On the MGSM-JA benchmark (math ability in Japanese), source models (Japanese general LLM and English Math LLMs) scored below 30.0% accuracy. The PS-merged model (Model 4) achieved 52.0%, and the hybrid PS+DFS model (Model 6) reached 55.2%. This represents a substantial improvement in combining disparate knowledge.
- **Emergent Capabilities:** The merged models were able to successfully solve math problems that stumped their individual source models, indicating a genuine integration and emergence of cross-domain reasoning (Japanese language + Math).
- **Surprising Generalization:** Beyond the target math task, the merged models (especially PS-merged) achieved remarkably high scores on the general Japanese Language Model Evaluation Harness (JP-LMEH), with an average of 70.5% for the PS model and 66.2% for the hybrid. This *surpassed* previous state-of-the-art 70B-parameter Japanese LLMs (e.g., Japanese StableLM 70B at 68.3%) despite having significantly fewer parameters (7B to 10B). This suggests that optimizing for a specific cross-domain task can lead to broad improvements.
- **Analysis of PS Merging:** The CMA-ES optimization revealed that all three source models contributed significantly, with the sum of weights approaching 2, indicating that amplification (rather than simple interpolation) was effective. The Japanese LLM's contribution showed a pronounced "density," possibly due to its extensive pre-training on Japanese text.
- **Analysis of DFS Merging:**
 - The learnable scaling matrix `W` was crucial, with ablation studies showing a performance decline exceeding 20% without it. This highlights its role in mitigating distribution shifts between layers from different models.
 - DFS merging was found to identify and "skip" or "remove" redundant/harmful layers (e.g., layer #30 from MetaMath-13B-V1.0), leading to performance gains.
 - DFS also showed an ability to "add" missing capabilities by incorporating layers from other models to compensate for deficiencies in a primary model (e.g., shifting output token distribution towards Japanese or improving problem understanding).
 - The order of source models in DFS merging significantly impacts performance.

#### 4.2. Japanese Vision-Language Model (EvoVLM-JP)

- **Culturally-Aware VLM:** The evolved VLM demonstrated enhanced proficiency in handling culturally-specific content. On the new `JA-VLM-Bench-In-the-Wild` dataset, EvoVLM-JP achieved 51.2% ROUGE-L (PS) and 47.6% (PS+DFS), outperforming the source LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B (41.1%) and the Japanese Stable VLM (40.5%).
- **Improved Japanese VQA:** Performance on `JA-VG-VQA-500` also improved, indicating successful integration of Japanese language capabilities into the VLM.
- **Qualitative Results:** Case studies showcased EvoVLM-JP's ability to correctly identify Japanese cultural elements (e.g., Koi-nobori) and linguistic nuances (calling a green traffic light "blue" in Japanese, which is culturally accurate), outperforming baselines that either lacked the cultural knowledge or struggled with consistent Japanese output.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Baselines

- **Unoptimized Merging:** Evolutionary Model Merge significantly outperformed unoptimized merging methods like TIES-Merge, DARE-TIES (with default parameters), and Frankenmerging. Unoptimized Frankenmerging often failed completely (0.0% accuracy on MGSM-JA).
- **Fine-tuning:** Evolutionary merging also outperformed traditional fine-tuning on the specialized task, achieving higher MGSM-JA scores (e.g., 50%+ vs. 43.2% for LoRA fine-tuning). Crucially, fine-tuning often led to significant decreases in general language proficiency (JP-LMEH), suggesting catastrophic forgetting, whereas evolutionary merging maintained or even improved it. The authors also argue that direct comparison with fine-tuning on *all* source datasets is impractical due to data unavailability and immense computational cost.

#### 4.4. Robustness and Scalability

- **Manual Model Selection:** The PS merging approach demonstrated robustness to the inclusion of "irrelevant" models (e.g., German or Chinese LLMs) in the source pool, with performance degradation remaining limited even with 8 additional models.
- **Scaling to Larger Models:** The method showed consistent improvements when applied to 13B-parameter models (Llama-2-13b fine-tunes), indicating its scalability beyond 7B models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Evolutionary Optimization of Model Merging Recipes" paper represents a significant contribution to the field of AI, particularly in the realm of foundation model development. Its implications are far-reaching, addressing challenges of cost, resource accessibility, and the current limitations of human-driven model design.

#### 5.1. A New Paradigm for Foundation Model Development

The most profound impact of this work is its proposal of a novel paradigm for creating new AI models. By leveraging evolutionary techniques to compose existing pre-trained models, it challenges the conventional, often prohibitively expensive, approach of training models from scratch using gradient-based optimization. This paradigm shift offers a computationally efficient alternative for achieving high-performance models.

#### 5.2. Democratization and Accessibility of AI

The ability to combine existing open-source models without extensive additional training significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing highly capable AI systems. This democratizes access to advanced AI, enabling smaller institutions, researchers in resource-constrained environments, and even hobbyists to create specialized and robust models. This is particularly impactful for languages and domains that may not attract the massive investment seen in mainstream English-centric AI development.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Cost-Effectiveness and Resource Efficiency

Foundation models are notoriously expensive to train, requiring massive compute and energy. This research demonstrates that by intelligently merging and repurposing existing models—which have already absorbed considerable resources during their initial training—new capabilities can be unlocked with comparatively minimal additional computational cost. This has positive implications for the environmental footprint of AI development.

#### 5.4. Discovery of Emergent and Cross-Domain Capabilities

The paper effectively showcases the method's ability to discover "novel and unintuitive ways" to merge models, leading to emergent capabilities that transcend the sum of their parts. The creation of a Japanese LLM with strong Math reasoning, and a culturally-aware Japanese VLM, exemplifies this. The surprising generalization of the Japanese Math LLM to broad Japanese language benchmarks, despite not being optimized for them, further highlights this potential for unexpected and valuable synergies.

#### 5.5. Contribution to Open Science

Sakana AI's commitment to open-sourcing the developed state-of-the-art models (EvoLLM-JP and EvoVLM-JP) is a vital contribution. This provides concrete, high-performing examples of evolutionary model merging, enabling other researchers to build upon their work and fostering further innovation in the open-source community. The subsequent implementation of evolutionary model merging in popular open-source tools like MergeKit and Optuna Hub demonstrates its practical utility and broad adoption.

#### 5.6. Future Directions and Broader Impact

The authors themselves suggest several exciting future directions:
- **Beyond LLMs and VLMs:** The successful application to image diffusion models (EvoSDXL, EvoUkiyoe) after the preprint's release underscores the method's versatility across different AI modalities.
- **Automated Source Model Selection:** Extending evolution to select the "ingredients" (source models) from a vast population would further automate the process.
- **Swarm Intelligence:** Exploring the creation of diverse "swarms" of foundation models, each with niche capabilities, that can collectively self-improve through interaction.
- **Strategic Prototyping:** For large institutions or governments, this approach offers a cheaper and faster way to develop proof-of-concept prototype models before committing to the immense capital and resources required for training custom models from scratch.

#### 5.7. Limitations and Ethical Considerations

The paper acknowledges important limitations:
- **Inherited Limitations:** Merged models inherit biases and limitations from their source models, potentially leading to illogical or factually flawed outputs.
- **Lack of Alignment/Instruction Tuning:** The current method does not encompass instruction fine-tuning or alignment techniques, which are crucial for ensuring models produce safe, helpful, and ethical responses in real-world applications.
- **Interpretability:** The increased complexity of merged models, especially those from DFS, might reduce interpretability, making it harder to understand *why* a particular combination works.

Despite these limitations, the paper positions this work as a "proof of concept" for a fundamentally new direction in AI development. The ethical considerations around unexpected behaviors and biases in complex AI systems remain a critical area for continued research and rigorous evaluation, especially for mission-critical or widely deployed applications.

In conclusion, "Evolutionary Optimization of Model Merging Recipes" introduces a powerful and efficient methodology that could fundamentally alter how foundation models are developed. By synergistically combining the power of evolutionary algorithms with the cost-effectiveness of model merging, it opens up new avenues for innovation, accessibility, and the discovery of novel AI capabilities.
