# AREX: Towards a Recursively Self-Improving Agent for Deep Research

> [!summary] Summary
> AREX, a recursively self-improving agent framework from BAAI, introduces a novel bi-level architecture that leverages the asymmetry between discovery and verification to systematically refine provisional answers in deep research tasks. The framework, incorporating an autonomous context-updating mechanism, demonstrates strong performance across diverse benchmarks, often outperforming comparable baselines and achieving competitive results with much larger models.

## Problem

- Deep research tasks require agents to identify answers that simultaneously satisfy multiple complex constraints, often from extensive, disparate information.
- Existing deep research agents struggle with systematic progress over long horizons, encountering persistent early errors, redundant exploration, or premature acceptance of partial solutions.
- Managing the growing interaction history in long-horizon research is challenging, potentially leading to unwieldy contexts or the accidental discarding of crucial evidence.

## Method

- **AREX** employs a hierarchical, bi-level ==Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)== process, alternating between an inner research loop (evidence gathering) and an outer self-improvement loop (verifying and refining provisional answers).
- It implements an ==Autonomous Context Updating (ACU)== mechanism, allowing the agent to learn when and how to compress interaction history into a concise "improvement state" that preserves verified findings and unresolved constraints.
- A multi-stage training framework is utilized, combining ==progressive multi-round capability training==, ==key-step focused supervision==, and ==step-aware reinforcement learning== to teach heterogeneous research skills.

## Results

- AREX-Base (**10B** activated parameters) achieved strong performance across six benchmarks (e.g., BrowseComp, GAIA, DeepSearchQA), outperforming comparable-scale baselines and competing with substantially larger frontier models.
- AREX-Turbo, a more compact **4B** model, demonstrated notable capabilities by outperforming Qwen3.5-35B on five of the six evaluation benchmarks.
- Ablation studies showed that the Autonomous Context Updating (ACU) improved accuracy by **11.8 points** in the inner loop, and the full Recursive Self-Improvement framework provided a **22.9-point** gain over a baseline without either component on BrowseComp.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging the 'discovery-verification asymmetry' by using verification as a dynamic control signal, rather than a final filter, enables systematic and recursive refinement of research trajectories.
> - An autonomously learned context-updating mechanism is critical for maintaining a compact, decision-relevant research state and sustaining effective reasoning over long, complex interactions.
> - Targeted supervision on decision-critical, often underlearned, steps within long trajectories significantly improves the agent's ability to acquire complex, heterogeneous capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep research tasks require agents to identify answers that simultaneously satisfy multiple complex constraints, often involving extensive information retrieval and integration from various sources. Current approaches to this challenge typically involve extending a single search trajectory with additional reasoning, tool interactions, or context. While these methods can broaden exploration, they may face limitations such as the persistence of early errors, redundant exploration of exhausted directions, or premature acceptance of partially valid solutions. The core difficulty lies in systematically identifying unresolved constraints and using that diagnosis to formulate targeted subsequent research problems.

This work addresses a perceived discovery–verification asymmetry in deep research. Discovering a complete answer that satisfies all constraints is often resource-intensive due to navigating large and sparsely informative search spaces. In contrast, verifying a candidate solution can frequently be decomposed into a series of simpler, constraint-wise checks. Existing research has leveraged verification for ranking completed candidate trajectories or refining decisions within an ongoing trajectory. AREX differentiates itself by positioning verification as a dynamic control signal that defines the transition between distinct research rounds. It transforms a provisional answer into a partially verified state, which then informs and guides subsequent targeted refinement, thereby enabling a recursive self-improvement process rather than merely a final filtering mechanism or local action critic. Furthermore, the work addresses the challenge of managing a growing interaction history in long-horizon research by proposing an autonomous context-update mechanism, moving beyond fixed heuristics or generic summarization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient agent capable of performing "deep research," characterized by the need to find answers that jointly satisfy multiple, often coupled, constraints. The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties faced by existing deep research agents, which, despite advanced reasoning and tool-use capabilities, often struggle with systematic progress over long horizons.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives include:
- **Addressing Discovery–Verification Asymmetry:** The authors observe that discovering a comprehensive, constraint-satisfying answer is costly, while verifying components of a candidate answer is often more tractable. This fundamental asymmetry is identified as a critical opportunity for improving research efficiency. The objective is to leverage this asymmetry to enable a more structured and systematic research process.
- **Enabling Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** Building upon the discovery–verification asymmetry, the work aims to introduce a recursive self-improvement paradigm. Instead of simply searching longer, the agent should continuously refine its current understanding by verifying intermediate results and using these partially verified states to guide targeted refinement. This involves alternating between evidence gathering and provisional answer construction, and a subsequent audit phase that identifies unresolved claims for follow-up.
- **Sustaining RSI over Long Horizons:** Long-duration research generates extensive interaction histories, which can become unwieldy, distracting future reasoning, or inadvertently discarding crucial evidence if truncated indiscriminately. A key objective is to develop a mechanism for autonomously managing this context, compressing growing interaction history into a concise "improvement state" that preserves verified evidence and unresolved constraints without relying on external models. This context management is intended to be aligned with the agent's evolving beliefs and future actions.
- **Developing a Multi-Stage Training Framework:** Deep research capabilities are heterogeneous, encompassing tool invocation, web navigation, evidence acquisition, complex reasoning, context tracking, and answer synthesis. A motivation is to devise a training methodology that progressively teaches these diverse skills and addresses the sparse credit-assignment problem inherent in long-horizon tasks. This involves leveraging verified synthetic tasks, high-quality teacher trajectories, agentic mid-training, and reinforcement learning, with a particular focus on accentuating key decision points during training.
- **Demonstrating Performance and Efficiency:** The ultimate objective is to instantiate this framework in both dense and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models and evaluate their performance against comparable baselines and larger models across a range of deep search, wide search, multi-constraint information seeking, and reasoning benchmarks. This aims to demonstrate that the RSI approach offers an effective pathway to capable and efficient deep research agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AREX implements a hierarchical, bi-level recursive self-improvement (RSI) process, comprising an inner research loop and an outer self-improvement loop. This framework operates on backbone models such as Qwen3.5-4B (for AREX-Turbo) and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (for AREX-Base).

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

Given an input query, AREX first derives an initial research objective. The **inner research loop** then executes research actions, integrates evidence, and updates the current answer until the objective is sufficiently addressed. It outputs a provisional answer, supporting evidence, and an answer-level confidence score. The **outer self-improvement loop** evaluates this provisional result. If the confidence score meets a predefined threshold, the answer is accepted. Otherwise, the outer loop assesses the recoverability of the current research trajectory. If recoverable, useful findings are preserved, and a targeted objective is formulated for refinement. If not, the investigation is restarted from the original problem.

#### 3.2. Inner Research Loop

This loop incrementally constructs an answer. At each step, it analyzes the current research objective, invokes external tools (e.g., search, browse), integrates observations, and determines the next research step. The interaction history, `h^(k)_t`, serves as the model’s working research state. The research policy `π_θ` generates intermediate analyses and actions based on the original problem, current objective, and accumulated trajectory.
- **Autonomous Context Updating (ACU):** To manage the accumulating interaction history, AREX learns to autonomously invoke a dedicated `update_context` tool. This tool compresses `h^(k)_t` into a refreshed research state `z^(k)_t`. This state preserves verified findings and citations, current candidates, unresolved constraints, validity concerns, rejected candidates, and the next-step plan, removing redundant observations and obsolete plans. The model decides when to invoke `update_context` (e.g., after resolving a subproblem or changing its plan), allowing subsequent steps to operate on this compact, decision-relevant context.
- **Structured Answer Externalization:** Once the inner loop determines the objective is sufficiently investigated, it invokes a `finish` interface. This produces a structured result `r^(k) = (y^(k), E^(k), s^(k))`, containing the provisional answer, supporting evidence with document identifiers, and an answer-level confidence score `s^(k)` (0-100). This structured output is designed for evaluation by the outer loop.

#### 3.3. Outer Self-Improvement Loop

This loop receives the structured result `r^(k)` and the terminal effective context from the inner loop. Its decision logic is based on the confidence score `s^(k)`:
- **Accept:** If `s^(k)` meets or exceeds a confidence threshold `τ`, the answer is accepted.
- **Refine:** If `s^(k)` is below `τ` but the trajectory is deemed recoverable, the outer loop preserves reliable findings (`P^(k)`) and identifies issues (`I^(k)`) that require further investigation. These issues are converted into a targeted objective `q^(k+1)` for the next recursive round, which is initialized from a refreshed state incorporating preserved information.
- **Restart:** If `s^(k)` is below `τ` and the trajectory is determined to be too noisy, misleading, or uninformative, the accumulated trajectory is discarded, and the next recursive round is initialized solely from the original problem.
This process is bounded by a maximum number of recursive rounds.

#### 3.4. Training Data Construction

The training involves a specialized dataset comprising challenging research tasks and high-quality multi-step investigation trajectories.
- **Recursive Research Task Synthesis:** Problems are constructed to require iterative information gathering, multi-source evidence integration, intermediate hypothesis verification, and adaptive research planning. Tasks are generated from real-world sources (web, scientific literature, knowledge bases) using human-defined templates. A target solution `y` and associated verifiable constraints `C(y)` are extracted, which are then transformed into indirect queries `x` requiring multi-hop reasoning. Tasks are automatically verified for correctness, uniqueness, evidence availability, and difficulty, filtering out ambiguous or trivially solvable problems.
- **Teacher Trajectory Collection and Quality Control:** Strong teacher models interact with the same tools and environment as AREX to generate trajectories `τ_i`. These trajectories record actions, tool calls, observations, intermediate analyses, and structured answers. A rigorous quality-control process filters trajectories based on: meaningful iterative investigation, coherent research-state maintenance, adaptation to new evidence, valid tool interactions, evidence-grounded answers, and a sufficiently high answer-level confidence score.

#### 3.5. Training Pipeline

AREX employs a multi-stage training framework to acquire heterogeneous capabilities.
- **Multi-stage Agentic Mid-training:**
 - **Progressive Multi-round Capability Training:** Initially, the model is trained on browse-intensive, multi-round trajectories to establish fundamental tool-use, web navigation, and iterative research behaviors. Subsequently, it is trained on expert-level reasoning data to strengthen long-form thinking, hypothesis comparison, and problem-solving.
 - **Key-step Focused Mixed-Capability Consolidation:** This stage combines capability-expanding tasks (e.g., academic paper research) with selective replay of *key steps* from browse-intensive trajectories. Key steps are identified using rule-based detectors for semantically meaningful events, such as the first tool invocation providing answer-relevant evidence, rejection of incorrect hypotheses, or context-update invocations that preserve verified evidence. Training loss is concentrated on these key steps to address observed underfitting of critical decisions.
- **Step-aware Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Step-aware Group Policy Optimization:** Instead of sequence-level rewards, this method uses a turn-level policy optimization. It computes a length-normalized step-level policy ratio `ρ_i,j(θ)` and applies hierarchical averaging over steps within trajectories and then over trajectories in the rollout group.
 - **Step Reward Shaping:** Outcome rewards are augmented with step-level reward shaping, using the key-step annotations from mid-training as auxiliary bonuses for successful trajectories. This emphasizes decision-critical steps while maintaining final-answer correctness as the primary optimization objective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

AREX models, AREX-Base (122B-A10B Mixture-of-Experts, 10B activated parameters) and AREX-Turbo (4B dense model), were evaluated on six benchmarks: BrowseComp, GAIA, xbench-2510, DeepSearchQA, WideSearch, and Humanity’s Last Exam (HLE) with tools.
- **AREX-Base** consistently achieved strong performance, outperforming comparable-scale baselines and remaining competitive with models utilizing substantially more activated parameters, including larger Qwen3.5 variants, MiroThinker-H1, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi-K2.6, and frontier proprietary systems like Gemini-3.1-Pro on specific benchmarks. For example, AREX-Base achieved the highest reported score on WideSearch-en and competitive results on BrowseComp and DeepSearchQA.
- **AREX-Turbo**, a more compact 4B model, also demonstrated notable capabilities, outperforming Qwen3.5-35B on five of the six benchmarks.
These results indicate that the recursive self-improvement framework enables robust and transferable research capabilities across diverse search, reasoning, and tool-use scenarios at varying model scales.

#### 4.2. Inference Framework Analysis

An analysis of AREX's inference-time behavior focused on Autonomous Context Updating (ACU) within the inner loop and the outer self-improvement loop.
- **Context-Update Behavior**: AREX invoked `update_context` in 80.3% of BrowseComp cases, typically proactively at a mean active-context size of 25,721 tokens, well below the 128K token limit. The primary triggers for updates included revising the search strategy (66.9%) and rejecting candidates (13.6%). The updated context preserved crucial information such as unresolved constraints (95.5%), next-step plans (96.4%), verified findings (72.1%), and rejected candidates (81.5%). This demonstrates ACU's role as a mechanism for trajectory consolidation and research-state refreshing.
- **Recursive Self-Improvement**: Ablation studies on BrowseComp demonstrated the impact of ACU and the outer self-improvement loop. Enabling ACU in the inner loop alone improved accuracy by 11.8 points (from 59.6% to 71.4%) in a single-round setting. The outer self-improvement loop provided further gains: 10.2 points without ACU (from 59.6% to 69.8%) and 11.1 points with ACU (from 71.4% to 82.5%). The full AREX system achieved 82.5% accuracy, representing a 22.9-point gain over the configuration without either component.
- **Answer-Level Confidence Score**: The confidence scores produced by the `finish` tool showed good calibration. For correct final outputs, 89.3% (without ACU) and 95.9% (with ACU) concentrated in the 90-100 confidence bin. Conversely, a substantial portion of incorrect outputs, 61.0% (without ACU) and 55.2% (with ACU), fell below a confidence score of 60. This separation supports the confidence-based decision procedure of the outer loop.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Controlled ablations on BrowseComp investigated the impact of training components.
- **Progressive Multi-round Capability Training**: Replacing the progressive training (sequential acquisition of browse-intensive then reasoning-intensive capabilities) with direct mixed training reduced accuracy from 82.5% to 77.5%. This suggests that phased acquisition of heterogeneous capabilities is more effective than simultaneous learning.
- **Key-step Focused Supervision**: After full-trajectory mid-training, an analysis of step-level loss revealed that annotated key steps (evidence discovery, path rejection/redirection, key context-update) consistently exhibited higher average losses (19-29% higher) compared to ordinary steps. Replacing key-step focused supervision with random-step replay, while maintaining equivalent budgets, resulted in the largest performance degradation, reducing accuracy from 82.5% to 74.1%. This finding indicates that targeted supervision on underlearned, decision-critical steps is more impactful than general replay.
- **Step-aware Reinforcement Learning**: Replacing the step-aware RL objective with standard Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) lowered accuracy from 82.5% to 79.4%. This 3.1-point gain suggests that step-aware policy optimization further refines the research policy after mid-training.

These ablations collectively demonstrate that AREX's performance is supported by the synergistic combination of its progressive multi-round capability training, key-step focused supervision, and step-aware reinforcement learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a framework for recursively self-improving agents, AREX, which represents a conceptual shift in how large language models approach deep research tasks. By explicitly leveraging the discovery–verification asymmetry, AREX transforms the verification process from a final filter into an active control signal that guides successive rounds of research. This allows the agent to systematically refine provisional answers, preserve verified progress, and target unresolved constraints.

The development of an autonomous context-update tool is significant for enabling long-horizon research. This learned mechanism allows the agent to maintain a compact, decision-relevant research state without relying on external models or fixed heuristics, addressing a key challenge in extended agentic interactions.

The multi-stage training framework, incorporating verified synthetic tasks, high-quality trajectories, and particularly the key-step focused supervision and step-aware reinforcement learning, demonstrates an effective methodology for training agents with complex, heterogeneous capabilities. The finding that decision-critical steps are often underlearned by conventional full-trajectory training and that targeted intervention improves performance has implications for agent training beyond this specific application.

Empirically, AREX's consistent performance gains over comparable-scale baselines and its competitiveness with models of substantially greater activated parameters across a diverse set of benchmarks validate the effectiveness of its design principles. This suggests a pathway toward developing more capable and efficient deep research agents, potentially reducing the computational resources required for complex information-seeking and reasoning tasks.

Future research directions suggested by this work include investigating more generalized and autonomous mechanisms for estimating the utility of individual steps and assigning fine-grained training signals. The principles demonstrated by AREX—verification-guided state refinement and step-aware optimization—offer a promising foundation for building more reliable and robust long-horizon research agents.
