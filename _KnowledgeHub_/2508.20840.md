# Learning Primitive Embodied World Models: Towards Scalable Robotic Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Primitive Embodied World Models (PEWM) proposes a paradigm shift in robotic learning, focusing on generating short, semantically atomic primitive actions rather than long-horizon trajectories. This approach demonstrates improved zero-shot task success on RLBench and real-world manipulation, while achieving real-time inference at 12 FPS and significantly reduced VRAM usage for physically consistent video generation.

## Problem

- Video-generation-based world models for robotic learning struggle with scalability due to high-dimensional, sparse embodied data, hindering high-fidelity and long-horizon video prediction.
- Existing approaches face challenges in achieving fine-grained alignment between linguistic concepts and low-level actions, often overlooking effective data design and suffering from data scarcity.
- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limitations in long-horizon generalization and domain transfer, constrained by data and computational demands.

## Method

- The Primitive Embodied World Models (PEWM) framework focuses on modeling short, semantically atomic action units (primitives) to simplify the learning task and improve efficiency.
- A primitive-centric dataset (D_prim) is constructed using spatially and temporally enhanced multi-camera observations, smart AI-assisted annotation, and full-arm shooting.
- PEWM leverages a dual-level compositional generalization: intra-primitive via fine-tuned diffusion models for high-fidelity video generation and inter-primitive through a VLM planner for sequencing primitives into complex tasks.

## Results

- PEWM achieved up to 93% success rate on RLBench tasks like 'close box' and demonstrated robust real-world performance, outperforming OpenVLA zero-shot on tasks such as 'pick up cup' (16/20 task success vs. 0/20).
- The framework exhibited strong primitive-level compositional generalization, achieving 80% success on unseen (predicate, object) combinations like 'pick jar' and 70% on 'open cup'.
- Significant efficiency gains were observed, with 75x faster inference, 6-7x lower VRAM usage (11 GB), and real-time 12 FPS future frame prediction, while also producing superior video quality and physical consistency (EPiCS score 11.45/13).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The space of fundamental primitive motions is significantly smaller and more manageable than the vast space of overall embodied data, suggesting a primitive-level modeling approach is more scalable.
> - Decomposing complex tasks into short, semantically atomic primitives drastically reduces video generation complexity, leading to more precise linguistic-visual alignment and increased data efficiency.
> - A three-stage training strategy combining simulated and real-world data, coupled with causal distillation, is crucial for achieving physically consistent video generation and real-time performance.
