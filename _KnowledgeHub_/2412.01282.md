# Align-KD: Distilling Cross-Modal Alignment Knowledge for Mobile Vision-Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Peking University and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab introduces Align-KD, a knowledge distillation strategy that specifically transfers cross-modal alignment knowledge from larger Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to smaller mobile VLMs. The approach significantly boosts the performance of a MobileVLM V2 1.7B model by up to 2.0 average points across six benchmarks, enabling more capable VLM deployment on edge devices.

## Problem

- Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are excessively large and computationally intensive, restricting their deployment on resource-constrained edge devices like mobile phones.
- Scaling down VLM architectures often leads to a severe trade-off, where performance significantly degrades as model size is reduced further.
- Existing knowledge distillation (KD) techniques for large models primarily focus on single-modal LLMs or neglect the crucial aspect of cross-modal alignment specific to VLMs.

## Method

- The method identifies the ==first layer of the VLM's LLM component== as the key location for cross-modal alignment by analyzing feature transformations.
- It proposes distilling cross-modal alignment knowledge through three specific loss components: ==first-layer text-query-vision attention==, vision enhancement based on text's focusing, and ==Reverse Kullback-Leibler Divergence== for output distribution.
- Align-KD integrates these novel distillation losses with a standard supervised learning loss, training a smaller student model (MobileVLM V2 1.7B) from a larger teacher (MobileVLM V2 7B).

## Results

- Align-KD improved the MobileVLM V2 1.7B model's average performance by **1.4** points on the 'Long' dataset and **2.0** points on the 'Short' dataset across six VLM benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that distilling the first layer's text-query-vision attention (
L
A
1,t−v
) was the most impactful component, outperforming other attention distillation strategies.
- The proposed method maintains acceptable computational overhead, requiring 296-676 GPU hours and 22.3-30.7 GB/device memory, making it feasible for practical training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-modal alignment, crucial for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), primarily occurs at the first layer of the Large Language Model (LLM) component.
> - Explicitly distilling the first layer's text-query-vision attention is highly effective and efficient for transferring critical cross-modal alignment knowledge to smaller VLM students.
> - Targeted knowledge distillation can significantly mitigate performance degradation in resource-limited training scenarios, allowing smaller VLMs to achieve comparable or superior performance to larger baselines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated advanced understanding and reasoning capabilities across multimodal tasks, representing a significant advancement in artificial intelligence. Concurrently, there is an increasing demand for deploying capable AI models directly on mobile and edge devices, driven by applications such as AI assistants. However, large VLMs are computationally intensive and possess extensive parameter counts, making their deployment on resource-constrained devices challenging.

Current efforts to address this challenge typically involve simplifying model structures or employing compression techniques like quantization and pruning. While such methods can reduce model size, they often introduce a trade-off with performance, especially when models are scaled down significantly. The MobileVLM family of models, including MobileVLM V1 and V2, represents a notable contribution in this area, specifically designed for mobile deployment. MobileVLM V2 1.7B, for instance, has demonstrated competitive performance against larger 7B models. Nevertheless, further reduction in model size typically results in more pronounced performance degradation, indicating a need for alternative enhancement strategies.

Knowledge Distillation (KD) is a technique used to transfer knowledge from a larger, more powerful "teacher" model to a smaller, more efficient "student" model, thereby improving the student's capabilities without increasing its size or requiring additional data. While KD has been widely applied, existing large model distillation techniques primarily focus on single-modal Large Language Models (LLMs) or utilize teacher models to generate synthetic training data for students. In the context of VLMs, earlier distillation efforts often pertained to pre-large model architectures and focused on aligning vision proposals, which are not characteristic of modern VLM designs. Some approaches apply Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss across transformer layers, but this may not be suitable for VLMs with diverse layer structures.

A critical gap identified by this research is the lack of specific consideration for distilling *cross-modal alignment knowledge* in VLMs. This alignment, which enables VLMs to integrate and reason across visual and textual inputs effectively, is fundamental to their operation. This paper introduces Align-KD to specifically address this gap, positioning it as a method to enhance mobile VLMs by explicitly transferring cross-modal alignment knowledge from larger teacher models, thereby improving performance in resource-limited scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from the inherent conflict between the powerful capabilities of modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the strict resource constraints of mobile and edge devices. While VLMs offer advanced multimodal understanding, their substantial size and computational demands render direct deployment on mobile platforms difficult. Existing methods for adapting VLMs to mobile environments, such as structural simplification, often lead to a significant compromise in performance, particularly as models become smaller.

The authors note that Knowledge Distillation (KD) is a promising avenue for improving smaller models without increasing their size. However, they identify a critical limitation in existing KD approaches: most are designed for single-modal Large Language Models (LLMs) or do not adequately address the unique challenge of distilling *cross-modal alignment knowledge* in VLMs. This alignment, which involves integrating information from disparate visual and textual modalities into a unified feature space, is considered fundamental for VLM comprehension and reasoning. Without effective cross-modal alignment, even robust LLM components within VLMs may struggle to process multimodal inputs accurately.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the research are:

1. **Develop a dedicated knowledge distillation method for mobile VLMs:** The primary objective is to propose "Align-KD," a novel KD strategy specifically tailored for the architectural and functional characteristics of VLMs, particularly those designed for mobile deployment.
2. **Focus on distilling cross-modal alignment knowledge:** This involves guiding the student model to learn how to effectively match and integrate information between visual and textual inputs. The authors hypothesize that this alignment primarily occurs in the shallow layers of the VLM's language model component and within the vision projector.
3. **Enhance vision token projection based on text focus:** A specific aim is to enable the student model to learn how to project vision tokens into the text embedding space by considering the varying importance or "focus" of different text prompts on specific visual elements.
4. **Achieve stable and universal performance improvement:** The method is intended to yield consistent performance gains across multiple VLM benchmarks for a mobile-scale student model (specifically MobileVLM V2 1.7B) when distilled from a larger teacher model (MobileVLM V2 7B).
5. **Ensure lightweight design and broad applicability:** The proposed Align-KD method is designed to be computationally efficient and not reliant on highly specific VLM architectural details, promoting its generalizability across various resource-limited scenarios and different VLM designs.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to facilitate the more widespread and effective deployment of powerful VLM capabilities on edge devices, expanding their application scope in real-world, resource-constrained environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Align-KD centers on distilling cross-modal alignment knowledge from a larger teacher VLM to a smaller student VLM. The approach is structured around three main components: attention distillation at the first Transformer layer, vision token enhancement based on text focus, and output distribution matching. The student model used is MobileVLM V2 1.7B, and the teacher model is MobileVLM V2 7B.

#### 3.1. Exploration of Alignment Location

The authors first investigate where cross-modal alignment predominantly occurs within VLMs. They hypothesize that, similar to observations in LLMs where initial and final layers handle input/output projection and dramatic feature changes, the first layer of the LLM component in a VLM plays a crucial role in mapping and aligning features from different modalities into a shared high-dimensional space.
To support this, they conduct experiments on MobileVLM V2 models of varying sizes (1.7B, 3B, 7B) by analyzing feature changes across Transformer layers:
- **Cosine similarity of adjacent layers:** Measures the change in feature space. Results indicate higher similarity in middle layers and more dramatic changes in the first and last layers.
- **Cosine similarity and Euclidean distance between vision and text tokens:** Examines the convergence of features from different modalities. The results show significant shifts in these metrics within the first and last layers, reinforcing the idea that these layers are critical for modality mapping and alignment.

#### 3.2. Align-KD Components

The Align-KD method integrates three distinct loss functions:

1. **First Layer Text-Query-Vision Attention Distillation (L_A_1,t-v):**
 - **Rationale:** The attention mechanism, particularly the text-query-vision attention (A_t-v) in the Transformer's first layer, is identified as a direct indicator of how text inputs perceive and interact with visual inputs. This interaction is key to cross-modal alignment.
 - **Mechanism:** The student model is guided to mimic the teacher's text-query-vision attention matrix from its first Transformer layer (A_T_1,t-v). This distillation is limited to the A_t-v part, rather than the entire attention matrix or multiple layers.
 - **Implementation:** An $1 \times 1$ convolution projector ($P_{attn}$) is used to align the dimensions of the teacher's attention matrix with the student's. A Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is then applied: $L_{A_{1,t-v}} = MSE(P_{attn}(A^T_{1,t-v}), A^S_{1,t-v})$.
 - **Advantages:** This approach is presented as lightweight and efficient due to its focus on a specific, critical part of the model. It also offers flexibility, as it does not depend on the specific depth or detailed architecture of the VLM.

2. **Vision Enhancement Based on Text's Focusing (L_V):**
 - **Rationale:** The vision projector in VLMs performs an initial, rough alignment by downsampling vision embeddings. However, this process often lacks explicit context from the text modality. The teacher's text-query-vision attention (A_T_1,t-v) provides valuable information about which vision tokens are most relevant to a given text prompt.
 - **Mechanism:** This component leverages the teacher's first-layer cross-modal attention to dynamically enhance the student's vision token embeddings.
 - **Sub-components:**
 - **Focused Vision Token Distillation ($L_{V-focus}$):** Vision tokens with high attention scores (indicating text focus) are identified by summing the teacher's $A^T_{1,t-v}$ along the text dimension. The top-K most attended vision tokens ($Idx_K$) are selected. Knowledge is then injected from the teacher's corresponding vision embeddings to the student's using an MSE loss with an $1 \times 1$ convolution projector ($P_V$): $L_{V-focus} = MSE(P_V(Emb^T_{Idx_K}), Emb^S_{Idx_K})$.
 - **General Vision Token Distillation ($L_{V-all}$):** To ensure comprehensive knowledge transfer and prevent neglecting less-focused tokens, a general MSE loss is also applied to all vision tokens: $L_{V-all} = MSE(P_V(Emb^T), Emb^S)$.
 - **Combined Loss:** The two sub-components are combined with a weighting factor $\lambda$ (set to 0.1): $L_V = L_{V-all} + \lambda L_{V-focus}$.

3. **Reverse Kullback-Leibler Divergence (RKLD) on Outputs (L_RKLD):**
 - **Rationale:** Following previous work in LLM distillation, RKLD is used to guide the student model to learn the output distribution of the teacher. This is considered beneficial for "mean-seeking" learning, which is more robust than "mode-seeking" learning, especially given the diverse nature of VLM inputs (images and varied text prompts).
 - **Mechanism:** RKLD is calculated between the predicted output distributions of the student ($p_S$) and teacher ($p_T$): $L_{RKLD} = p_S \log(\frac{p_S}{p_T})$.

#### 3.3. Overall Knowledge Distillation Strategy

The total loss function for training the student model is a combination of the original supervised loss ($L_{Sup}$) and the three proposed distillation losses:
$L = L_{Sup} + L_{A_{1,t-v}} + L_V + L_{RKLD}$.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- **Training Stages:** The process follows MobileVLM V2's multi-step training strategy, including a pre-training phase on ShareGPT4V-PT (1.2 million image-text pairs) and a multi-task finetuning phase on various VQA and conversational datasets (COCO, SBU, Visual Dialog, ShareGPT4V, SQA, IConQA, TextVQA, VSR, VIGC).
- **Hardware and Efficiency:** Training is performed on 8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs, employing gradient accumulation (global batch size of 256 for pre-training, 128 for finetuning) and the ZeRO2 strategy for memory efficiency.
- **Frozen Components:** The vision encoder and tokenizer are frozen during training, with only the rest of the network being updated.
- **Hyperparameters:** Maximum learning rates are set for the projector and other components, with a cosine schedule. The $\lambda$ for $L_V$ is 0.1, and top-K for focused vision tokens is 16.

#### 3.5. Data and Dataset Reforming

To evaluate the method under different resource constraints, two subsets of the training data are created based on maximum prompt length:
- **Short:** Maximum prompt length of 512 embedded tokens.
- **Long:** Maximum prompt length of 2048 embedded tokens.
Overlong data points are dropped (not truncated), leading to slight differences in data volume between the subsets. This allows for testing Align-KD's effectiveness in scenarios with limited computational resources and varying input complexities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed findings regarding the effectiveness, components, and computational aspects of the Align-KD method.

#### 4.1. Overall Effectiveness of Align-KD (Table 1, Figure 1)

Align-KD consistently improved the performance of the MobileVLM V2 1.7B student model across six diverse benchmarks (MME, GQA, TextVQA, POPE, MMBench-dev, SQA).
- **Long Dataset Training:** When trained with the "Long" subdataset (maximum prompt length 2048 tokens), the MobileVLM V2 1.7B model with Align-KD achieved an average score of 65.1, representing an improvement of 1.4 points compared to the baseline MobileVLM V2 1.7B model (63.7) without distillation. Notable improvements included 3.2 points on SQA and 1.6 points on MMBench-dev.
- **Short Dataset Training (Resource-Limited Scenario):** Under the "Short" subdataset (maximum prompt length 512 tokens), Align-KD yielded an even more substantial average improvement of 2.0 points, moving from a baseline of 62.4 to 64.4. Specific gains included 3.8 points on GQA and 1.2 points on POPE (a benchmark for hallucination testing).
The results indicate that Align-KD enables the student model, even when trained with resource-limited data (short prompts), to achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding the baseline model trained with more extensive data (long prompts without Align-KD).

#### 4.2. Ablation Study of Align-KD Components (Table 3)

The ablation study, conducted on the "Short" subdataset, assessed the contribution of each Align-KD component:
- **Reverse Kullback-Leibler Divergence (L_RKLD):** Provided an average score of 63.0, a modest improvement over the 62.4 baseline, suggesting some effectiveness in output distribution matching.
- **First Layer Text-Query-Vision Attention Distillation (L_A_1,t-v):** Showed a more balanced and notable improvement, raising the average score to 63.6. This highlights the importance of distilling cross-modal attention.
- **General Vision Token Distillation (L_V-all):** When added, further increased the average score to 63.8.
- **Focused Vision Token Distillation (L_V-focus) combined with L_V-all:** The full $L_V$ component, which adaptively enhances vision tokens, achieved the highest average score of 64.4. This indicates that dynamically emphasizing important vision tokens contributes significantly to overall performance.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study of Attention Distillation Strategy (Table 4)

Further ablation experiments explored different attention distillation strategies:
- **A_1,v-v (Vision-Query-Vision Attention KD):** Distilling only vision-query-vision attention from the first layer resulted in a severe performance drop, yielding an average score of 43.7. This suggests that this form of self-attention is not conducive to initial cross-modal alignment.
- **A_1,t-t (Text-Query-Text Attention KD):** Distilling only text-query-text attention showed better performance than A_1,v-v, with an average score of 63.2, but was slightly lower than the proposed A_1,t-v strategy (64.4). This is attributed to the stability provided by the fixed text tokenizer.
- **A_1,all (Full First Layer Attention KD):** Distilling the entire first layer's attention resulted in an average score of 63.5, which was lower than the A_1,t-v only strategy (64.4). This suggests that the inclusion of A_1,v-v (and potentially A_1,t-t) negatively impacts the overall distillation when combined with the crucial A_1,t-v component.
- **+A_last,all (Adding Last Layer Full Attention KD):** Incorporating distillation of the last layer's full attention alongside Align-KD led to a performance decrease (average 64.1) compared to Align-KD alone. This implies that deep feature distillation might overlap with the RKLD component or introduce unnecessary complexity without significant benefit.

#### 4.4. Computational Cost (Table 5)

The paper provides a comparison of training time and memory workload for Align-KD:
- Training MobileVLM V2 1.7B with Align-KD on the "Short" subdataset required 296 GPU hours and a maximum of 22.3 GB of memory per device.
- On the "Long" subdataset, training with Align-KD consumed 676 GPU hours and 30.7 GB of memory per device.
While Align-KD introduces additional computational overhead compared to vanilla training, the memory loads are deemed acceptable for commercial (e.g., RTX 3090) and enterprise (e.g., NVIDIA V100) GPUs, considering the universal performance improvements achieved.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Align-KD, a knowledge distillation method designed to enhance mobile Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with a specific focus on distilling cross-modal alignment knowledge. This approach contributes to the field by addressing a previously identified gap in large model distillation techniques for VLMs.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to **enhance the capabilities of smaller, mobile-grade VLMs** without increasing their parameter count or requiring additional raw data. By enabling a 1.7B parameter model to learn rich knowledge from a 7B parameter teacher, Align-KD facilitates the deployment of more capable multimodal AI on resource-constrained edge devices, such as smartphones and robots. This has direct implications for expanding the application scope of advanced AI, making powerful VLM functionalities accessible in offline or low-bandwidth environments.

A key contribution is the **novel focus on cross-modal alignment knowledge distillation**. The research empirically demonstrates that targeting the text-query-vision attention in the first Transformer layer, along with text-focused vision token enhancement, is effective for improving VLM performance. This contrasts with prior distillation methods that often overlooked this crucial aspect of multimodal integration or applied less targeted approaches, such as distilling entire attention matrices or focusing solely on single-modal features. The ablation studies provide evidence that this specific targeting is more beneficial than other attention distillation strategies, leading to a more stable and effective knowledge transfer.

Furthermore, the **lightweight design and generalizability** of Align-KD enhance its potential impact. By focusing on critical components and avoiding reliance on specific VLM architectural details or deep layer-wise distillation, the method is presented as adaptable to various VLM architectures. This flexibility could facilitate its adoption across different mobile VLM frameworks. The demonstration of consistent performance gains across diverse benchmarks, including those evaluating general reasoning and hallucination resistance (e.g., POPE), indicates the robustness of the learned knowledge.

The findings also provide **insights into the internal workings of VLMs**, particularly regarding where cross-modal alignment processes predominantly occur. The empirical evidence suggesting the importance of the first Transformer layer in feature projection and alignment could guide future architectural designs and optimization strategies for VLMs.

While the method incurs additional computational overhead during training compared to standard VLM training, the authors argue that the improved performance and acceptable memory footprint for modern GPUs make it a viable strategy. Overall, Align-KD represents a step towards bridging the gap between the computational demands of advanced VLMs and the practical constraints of mobile deployment, thereby contributing to the broader development of ubiquitous and capable artificial intelligence.
