# TriDi: Trilateral Diffusion of 3D Humans, Objects, and Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> TriDi is the first joint probabilistic diffusion model over 3D Human pose, Object pose, and Interaction (contact-text) that generates any of the seven conditional/joint distributions with one transformer, unifying prior one-way HOI methods and beating specialized baselines on BEHAVE and GRAB.

## Problem

- Prior HOI methods model only one-way posteriors, either human given object or object given human, each needing a separately tailored architecture and training procedure.
- Interaction is represented either as contact maps, expressive but hard for users to control, or as text, controllable but lacking spatial reasoning capability, never both jointly.
- No prior human-object interaction method addresses right-handed statistical bias in interaction datasets, limiting generalization and diversity of generated poses.

## Method

- **TriDi**'s ==trilateral diffusion== process extends ==UniDiffuser== to three modalities (Human, Object, Interaction), letting one ==transformer== denoise all three jointly via ==token-wise self-attention== across 2^3-1=7 conditioning modes.
- ==Contact-Text latent representation==: two encoders (contact map, ==CLIP== text embedding) trained with autoencoding, cross-modal, and latent-similarity losses into a shared space decodable back to contact maps.
- ==Classifier-based reconstruction guidance== perturbs denoising predictions each step using a supervising function that penalizes human-object vertex distances where the predicted contact map is active, enforcing physical contact.

## Results

- On BEHAVE and GRAB, TriDi improves 1-NNA, Coverage, and MMD over specialized one-way baselines (GNet, Object Pop-up+cVAE) by up to **47%**, despite handling all seven modes with one model.
- Geometrical consistency metrics show TriDi reduces MPJPE to **20.8/15.3cm** and object center error to **15.3/5.0cm** on BEHAVE/GRAB, outperforming NN, ObjPOP, and GNet baselines.
- A **40**-response user study preferred TriDi outputs over baselines in about **89%** of cases and rated them on par with ground truth in about **52%** of cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling the full joint distribution of interacting entities rather than one fixed conditional direction can improve every marginal task simultaneously, not just add flexibility.
> - Fusing complementary interaction representations, spatial contact maps and interpretable text, into one shared latent space lets a single control signal serve both precision and usability.
> - Simple symmetry-based data augmentation (left-right mirroring) can meaningfully counter handedness bias in small interaction datasets, a fix largely overlooked by prior HOI work.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of 3D human-object interaction (HOI) modeling is a significant area within computer vision, with implications for virtual reality, augmented reality, content creation, and synthetic data generation. Prior research in this domain has largely focused on modeling HOI in a unidirectional manner. For example, some methods generate plausible human poses and interactions conditioned on a given 3D object, while others predict object poses or types based on a human pose. These approaches often require tailored models, specialized design choices, and distinct training procedures for each specific conditioning scenario (e.g., P(Human|Object) or P(Object|Human)).

More granular existing works address aspects such as dynamic motions of humans interacting with objects, focusing on hand-object interactions, or reconstructing interactions from external data like images or videos. Contact modeling, a crucial component for realistic interactions, has also been explored through various representations like contact maps or distances, sometimes augmented by textual descriptions. Emerging areas include compositional shape generation involving humans and objects, and modeling interactions among multiple humans and objects.

The current work identifies a gap in this landscape: the absence of a unified, joint probabilistic model that simultaneously considers the human (H), object (O), and interaction (I) modalities. Existing methods, by focusing on individual conditional distributions, are noted as impractical and difficult to scale due to their specialized nature. This paper aims to address this limitation by proposing a single, compact architecture capable of modeling the joint distribution P(H, O, I), thereby encompassing all possible conditional distributions within a unified framework. This represents a shift from the paradigm of developing discrete models for each specific conditional generation task.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **TriDi**, a unified probabilistic model capable of capturing the joint distribution of 3D human pose, object pose, and human-object interaction (P(H, O, I)). This model aims to address the limitations of existing approaches that predominantly operate in a one-way direction, requiring separate and specialized models for each conditioning scenario.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations and requirements:

- **Versatility and Flexibility:** Human-object interactions are inherently interdependent. Different objects afford different human poses, and vice-versa. Moreover, multiple interactions are possible between a given human and an object, each entailing specific contact patterns. A comprehensive model should capture this interplay in a flexible manner, allowing for generation or prediction regardless of which modalities are provided as input conditions. This flexibility is crucial for numerous applications.
- **Addressing the "One-Way" Limitation:** Current methods typically model either human interaction given an object (e.g., P(H|O)) or object pose given a human (e.g., P(O|H)). This necessitates developing, training, and maintaining multiple distinct models for different conditioning cases, which is inefficient, impractical, and challenging to scale. The authors seek to replace this fragmented paradigm with a single architecture that can handle all conditional and unconditional combinations.
- **Broadening Application Scope:** A unified joint model offers a wider range of applications. This includes:
 - Generating humans that naturally interact with a specified object.
 - Generating objects that fit a given human pose.
 - Unconditional generation of plausible human-object interaction scenes.
 - Extending annotations of existing 3D human-contact datasets by automatically generating compatible objects.
 - Serving content creation pipelines (e.g., in animation, game development), AR/VR environments, ergonomics studies, and manufacturing design where realistic HOI is critical.
- **Enhanced User Control for Interaction:** Interaction (I) is a complex modality. While textual descriptions offer intuitive control, they may lack the spatial precision of contact maps. Conversely, contact maps are expressive but challenging for users to directly manipulate. The authors are motivated to create a novel interaction representation that combines the intuitiveness of text with the expressiveness of contact maps, allowing for detailed guidance of the generation process.
- **Improving Data Utilization:** Acknowledging the variability and potential biases in HOI datasets (e.g., prevalence of right-handed interactions), the research aims to incorporate data augmentation strategies (like left-right symmetry mirroring) to improve the model's generalization capabilities and address data scarcity.

By achieving these objectives, TriDi aims to provide a robust and versatile tool for generating and understanding 3D human-object interactions, surpassing the capabilities of specialized, single-direction approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TriDi is designed as a unified, transformer-based architecture that models the joint distribution of 3D human (H), object (O), and interaction (I) modalities through a novel three-way diffusion process.

**Core Diffusion Formulation:**
The method extends the UniDiffuser paradigm, which typically handles two modalities, to a three-modality setting. It employs a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) framework. The model TriDiψ is trained to recover the original, unnoised samples (H0, O0, I0) from their noised versions (HtH, OtO, ItI) at various diffusion timesteps (tH, tO, tI). The training objective minimizes the L2 distance between the model's prediction and the ground truth, conditioned on the object class CO. The flexibility to sample from seven possible distributions (joint and all conditional combinations, e.g., P(H, O, I), P(H, I|O), P(O|H, I), etc.) is achieved by manipulating the timesteps: setting a timestep to 0 denotes a conditioned modality, while setting it to T (the final diffusion step) indicates a modality to be predicted.

**Modalities Representations:**
1. **Human (H):** The human is represented using the SMPL+H body model, which includes pose parameters (θH ∈ R^(51×3)), shape parameters (βH ∈ R^10), and a 6-DoF global pose (gH ∈ R^9). A decimated version of the SMPL mesh (V_H ∈ R^690 vertices) is used to reduce computational load while retaining detail for full mesh recovery. Rotations are converted to a 6D representation before being input to the network.
2. **Object (O):** The canonical geometry of the object is provided as input conditioning. It is represented by PointNeXt features (fO ∈ R^1024) and a one-hot class encoding (yO). The model's task for the object modality is to diffuse its 6-DoF global pose (gO ∈ R^9).
3. **Interaction (I):** A novel representation for interaction is introduced, combining textual descriptions and contact maps into a shared, compact latent space (zI ∈ R^128). This is achieved by simultaneously training two encoders: EϕI for contact maps (ϕI ∈ {0, 1}^690, a binary map on human vertices) and ET_I for CLIP embeddings of textual descriptions (T_I). A decoder DϕI maps the latent space (zI) back to a contact map (ϕI). The training objective for this module combines Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) losses for autoencoding contact maps and encoding text-to-contact maps, along with an L2 loss to ensure similarity in the latent space between contact-derived and text-derived embeddings. This unified latent representation allows for intuitive text-based control while providing detailed spatial guidance through contact information.

**Network Architecture:**
TriDi is implemented as a Transformer architecture. An initial embedding layer maps the input modalities (human parameters, object pose, interaction latent, and object class features) into a common token space. Token-level self-attention within the Transformer is then utilized to capture fine-grained conditional relationships among the three modalities.

**Guidance Mechanism:**
To ensure that generated interactions adhere to physical contact constraints (avoiding floating objects or interpenetration), TriDi incorporates a classifier-based guidance mechanism during the denoising process. This guidance perturbs the model's prediction at each diffusion step based on feedback from a supervising function F. The function F quantifies the discrepancy between the predicted contact map (derived from the interaction latent ˆzI) and the actual distances between the predicted human and object meshes. Specifically, F sums the product of the predicted contact map values and the minimum distances from human vertices to the closest object vertices. The reconstruction guidance formulation (from) is applied to modify the predicted (ˆH, ˆO, ˆI) tuple directly at each denoising step, guided by the gradient of F, with a specific weight λ. This mechanism is typically applied during the later stages of the denoising process.

**Training Details:**
- **Datasets:** The model is primarily trained on a combined dataset of BEHAVE and GRAB, following established train-test splits. Scalability is also explored by extending training to InterCap and OMOMO datasets.
- **Contact-Text Labeling:** For training, contact maps (ϕI) are automatically generated by thresholding human-object distances. Text descriptions (T_I) are composed dynamically using predefined templates that incorporate detected contacting body parts and the object's class, making the text encoder robust to diverse inputs.
- **Data Augmentation:** To address data biases (e.g., right-handed interactions) and increase data variability, all training samples are randomly mirrored through the ZY plane, effectively doubling the dataset.
- **Loss Functions:** The overall training loss for TriDi is a weighted sum of L1/L2 losses applied to the human (pose, shape, global pose, vertex positions), object (global pose, vertex positions), and interaction (latent representation, human-object distances) modalities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates TriDi's capabilities as a unified model for 3D human, object, and interaction generation, surpassing specialized baselines in several aspects.

**1. Unified Joint Modeling:**
TriDi is presented as the first model to formulate and realize the joint probabilistic distribution P(H, O, I). This single network can operate in seven distinct modes, encompassing the joint distribution and all possible conditional combinations (e.g., P(H, I|O), P(O, I|H), P(H, O|I), P(H|O, I), P(O|H, I), P(I|H, O), and P(H, O, I)). This approach consolidates the capabilities of prior works, which typically address only specific one-way conditional generation tasks.

**2. Quality of Generated Distribution (Quantitative):**
Evaluations on the BEHAVE and GRAB datasets against specialized one-way baselines (GNet for H,I|O; ObjPOP+cVAE for O,I|H) show TriDi's superior performance:
- **Coverage (COV):** TriDi consistently achieves higher COV scores, indicating that its generated samples cover a larger portion of the ground truth data distribution. Improvements of up to 47% were reported.
- **Minimum Matching Distance (MMD):** TriDi yields lower MMD values, signifying better alignment between the generated and ground truth distributions.
- **1-Nearest Neighbor Accuracy (1-NNA):** The 1-NNA scores for TriDi are closer to the optimal 50%, suggesting that the model generates non-trivial and diverse samples, effectively distinguishing between generated and real data.
- **Comparison with Single-Mode Variants:** TriDi performs on par with or better than its single-mode variants (s-TriDi-HI for H,I|O and s-TriDi-OI for O,I|H), suggesting that joint training across all modalities benefits the overall generalization and performance of the model. Similar trends were observed for H,O|I when compared to s-TriDi-HO.

**3. Geometrical Consistency of Generation (Quantitative):**
In terms of spatial accuracy and realism:
- **Human Pose Error (MPJPE, MPJPE-PA):** TriDi produces significantly lower Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (MPJPE-PA) for human predictions compared to baselines, indicating more accurate human poses.
- **Object Pose Error (E_v2v, E_c):** The model achieves lower vertex-to-vertex (E_v2v) and object center (E_c) errors for object predictions. Notably, for GRAB, TriDi showed drastic improvement in object center and orientation even over the regressive ObjPOP.
- **Contact Accuracy (Acc_cont):** TriDi demonstrates high contact accuracy, with predicted contact being on par with or better than contacts inferred directly from the generated meshes. This suggests the network effectively learns and utilizes the interaction (I) modality for plausible physical contact.
- **Penetration Analysis:** SDF-based penetration metrics (Min. D., C%, PD, Psc) show TriDi's generated samples have minimal interpenetration and maintain realistic contact, closely matching ground truth values and outperforming baselines which exhibited more "floaters" or penetrations.

**4. Qualitative Results:**
Visual comparisons illustrate that TriDi's generations are better aligned with the provided conditions, demonstrating reduced interpenetration, preservation of fine-grained details for smaller objects, and greater diversity in the poses of limbs not constrained by contact. A user study (detailed in supplementary material) indicated that TriDi's output was preferred over baselines in approximately 89% of cases and was comparable to ground truth samples in about 52% of cases.

**5. Ablation Studies:**
Ablations confirmed the contribution of key components:
- **Augmentation (NoAug):** Removing the symmetry augmentation led to a decrease in 1-NNA, suggesting a less diverse generated distribution.
- **Interaction Modality (H, O):** Training without explicit I modality diffusion (modeling only H, O) resulted in slightly reduced geometrical consistency.
- **Guidance (NoGuide):** While guidance showed a minor impact on distribution metrics, it played a crucial role in improving geometrical consistency and ensuring plausible contacts.

**6. Applications and Generalization:**
TriDi's versatility enables several practical applications:
- **Scene Population:** The model can populate 3D scenes by generating humans, objects, or both, based on initial conditions (e.g., placing a human or object in a scene and generating the complement).
- **Interaction Reconstruction:** TriDi can indirectly reconstruct objects given human and contact annotations derived from images (e.g., from the DAMON dataset), demonstrating generalization to unseen data distributions.
- **Sequences with Keyframing:** The model can generate keyframes of interactions, which can then be interpolated to produce dynamic sequences.
- **Generalization to Unseen Geometry:** TriDi shows capability in generating realistic interactions for objects with novel geometries, provided their functionality aligns with learned patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TriDi model represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D human-object interaction modeling by introducing the first joint probabilistic model for human, object, and interaction (P(H, O, I)). This unification has several implications and potential impacts:

- **Paradigm Shift in HOI Modeling:** By providing a single architecture capable of operating in seven different modes (joint and all conditional distributions), TriDi simplifies the complex landscape of HOI generation. It effectively renders prior specialized, one-way models as specific use cases of a more general framework, reducing the need for separate models, architectures, and training procedures for each task. This unified approach can streamline research and development in HOI.
- **Enhanced Generative Capabilities:** The model's ability to reason about the interdependencies between human pose, object properties, and interaction details simultaneously leads to more coherent and plausible generations. Quantitative results demonstrate superior performance against specialized baselines in terms of distribution quality (diversity and coverage) and geometrical consistency (pose and contact accuracy). This suggests TriDi produces more realistic and varied HOI samples.
- **Novel Interaction Representation:** The innovative Contact-Text interaction representation is a notable contribution. It effectively bridges the gap between the intuitive control offered by text descriptions and the detailed spatial guidance provided by contact maps. This hybrid approach enhances user control and enables the model to generate more precise and physically plausible interactions, while being robust to diverse textual inputs.
- **Broadening Application Horizons:** TriDi's versatility unlocks new possibilities and improves existing workflows in various domains:
 - **Content Creation:** Facilitates the generation of realistic 3D animations and virtual environments for film, gaming, and design.
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** Can be used to create large-scale, diverse synthetic datasets of HOI, which are valuable for training other computer vision models, especially when real-world data collection is challenging or expensive.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality:** Enables more natural and interactive experiences by generating plausible object responses to human actions or vice versa.
 - **Ergonomics and Product Design:** Can simulate human interactions with prototypes, aiding in the design of user-friendly products and environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a foundation for more complex HOI modeling. Future avenues include scaling to multi-human and multi-object interactions for realistic social scenarios, incorporating richer scene conditioning, and developing methods less sensitive to data distribution biases for objects with novel functionalities.

In summary, TriDi represents a step toward a more holistic understanding and generation of 3D human-object interactions, offering a powerful, versatile, and high-performing tool for research and applied fields.
