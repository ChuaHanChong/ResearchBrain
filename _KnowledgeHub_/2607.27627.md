# Arm2Air: Cross-Embodiment Skeleton Transfer for 3D Relay Formation

> [!summary] Summary
> Arm2Air is a method that transfers obstacle-avoidance geometric skeletons from robot arms to guide 3D UAV relay formation in cluttered urban environments. It reduces median end-to-end planning runtime by 64.9% and improves bottleneck capacity by 65.0% compared to traditional 3D planning methods.

## Problem

- Deploying robust UAV relay networks in complex urban 3D environments is challenging due to numerous obstacles, communication range limits, and the need to optimize for wireless link quality.
- Traditional 3D UAV motion planning incurs high computational costs, often two orders of magnitude slower than 2D planning, hindering rapid deployment in dynamic scenarios.
- Ensuring both geometric collision avoidance and robust communication performance (e.g., bottleneck capacity, end-to-end delay) simultaneously forms a complex, coupled optimization problem.

## Method

- Proposes '**Arm2Air**,' a ==cross-embodiment skeleton transfer== method that leverages the structural resemblance between a serial robot arm (source domain) and a UAV relay backbone (target domain).
- A ==transformer-based== platform predicts target-domain relay coordinates, conditioned on obstacle-aware geometric skeletons extracted and aligned from robot arm motions.
- Employs ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== for parameter-efficient fine-tuning on limited target-domain data, followed by a ==communication-aware refinement== stage that optimizes for bottleneck capacity and minimizes various penalties.

## Results

- Arm2Air reduced median end-to-end planning runtime by **64.9%** (13.930 seconds) and produced a **5.2%** shorter chain length (72.719 m) compared to conventional 3D planners.
- It improved communication performance by increasing bottleneck capacity by **65.0%** (3.897 Mbps) and reducing end-to-end delay by **12.8%** (1994.44 ms) against the strongest baselines.
- The method enabled accurate, data-efficient adaptation, reducing relay-position Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) by **53.6%** while updating only **0.134 million parameters** with limited target-domain data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ordered, obstacle-aware geometric skeletons can be effectively transferred as structural priors between heterogeneous embodied systems (e.g., robot arms to UAVs) to guide complex 3D planning tasks.
> - Abstracting complex 3D UAV relay placement into a skeleton transfer problem significantly reduces computational burden and data requirements compared to direct 3D search methods.
> - Combining a skeleton-conditioned prediction model with a communication-aware refinement optimization is crucial for achieving high-quality, robust UAV relay backbones that meet both geometric and communication performance criteria.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the fields of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) systems, wireless communication networks, motion planning, and robot learning, specifically addressing challenges in deploying UAV relay networks in complex three-dimensional (3D) environments.

Traditional UAV motion planning in 3D is recognized as computationally complex. Existing approaches often involve direct search for collision-free paths or control policies within the UAV's operational domain. The paper notes that generalized obstacle-avoidance motion planning is fundamentally hard, and sampling-based planners like RRT and PRM face limitations in solution quality and computational cost, particularly as environment complexity increases.

Regarding UAV wireless relay networks, prior research focuses on jointly optimizing 3D placement, trajectory, line-of-sight (LoS) channels, energy constraints, and quality-of-service (QoS) requirements. These are often non-convex optimization problems, and a geometrically collision-free path does not inherently guarantee a robust communication backbone. The challenge lies in simultaneously satisfying geometric constraints (obstacle avoidance, range, altitude) and communication performance metrics (bottleneck capacity, end-to-end delay).

In the broader context of robot learning, cross-embodiment transfer learning has been explored for transferring control policies, locomotion, or manipulation skills between robots with different kinematic structures or degrees of freedom. This typically involves identifying common structural knowledge despite physical differences.

"Arm2Air" distinguishes itself by proposing a representation-level approach that leverages knowledge transfer. Instead of performing a direct 3D search from scratch for UAV relay placement, it draws an analogy between the serial-chain structure of a robot arm and a UAV relay backbone. The work contributes by extending cross-embodiment learning to the domain of communication-constrained UAV relay networks, focusing on transferring ordered *geometric skeletons* as structural priors rather than low-level control commands or dynamics. This aims to reduce the computational burden and data requirements associated with planning in highly cluttered 3D urban environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and effective method for forming robust UAV relay backbones in challenging urban 3D environments. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and specific aims:

1. **Addressing the Difficulty of Urban Relay Placement**: UAV relay networks are crucial for restoring or establishing communication connectivity in disaster zones or areas with damaged infrastructure. However, deploying these networks in urban settings is complex due to the need to jointly consider LoS blockage, communication range limitations, altitude constraints, and the presence of numerous 3D obstacles (buildings). This makes the problem inherently harder than simple shortest-path planning.

2. **Mitigating High Computational Cost of 3D Planning**: The paper highlights that direct 3D planning for UAVs incurs significantly higher computational costs compared to its 2D counterpart, sometimes by up to two orders of magnitude. This computational scaling behavior necessitates a more efficient approach, especially for rapid deployment scenarios. The motivation is to avoid repetitive and expensive direct 3D searches.

3. **Ensuring Communication-Aware Placement**: A geometrically collision-free path for UAVs does not automatically translate into an effective communication backbone. The performance of a relay network is limited by its bottleneck capacity (weakest hop) and accumulated end-to-end delay. Therefore, the placement must explicitly consider and optimize for wireless link quality metrics in addition to geometric constraints.

4. **Leveraging Structural Similarities Across Embodiments**: The core motivation for the proposed transfer learning approach stems from the observation that a UAV relay backbone, which forms a serial communication chain from a gateway to a target, shares a structural resemblance with a serial robotic manipulator. Both involve an ordered chain of nodes/joints reconfiguring around obstacles to achieve a goal. This structural commonality, despite differences in dynamics and control, is hypothesized to allow for knowledge transfer.

5. **Improving Data and Parameter Efficiency**: Developing effective planning models for complex 3D environments typically requires substantial amounts of training data. The research aims to achieve accurate relay placement with limited target-domain data by leveraging pre-existing knowledge from a source domain and employing parameter-efficient adaptation techniques.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives include:
- To formally formulate urban UAV relay backbone formation as a coupled problem involving 3D path planning and communication constraints.
- To propose a representation-level knowledge transfer method that leverages ordered obstacle-aware structures from a heterogeneous source embodiment (robot arms) to initialize UAV relay placement.
- To design a skeleton-conditioned, transformer-based transfer platform capable of predicting target-domain relay coordinates, incorporating Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for efficient fine-tuning, and integrating a communication-aware refinement stage.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Arm2Air" methodology is designed to transfer obstacle-avoidance geometric skeletons from robot arms to UAV relay backbone formation, comprising four main stages: scenario representation, source geometric skeleton transfer, skeleton-conditioned relay prediction, and communication-aware refinement.

#### 3.1. Relay Scenario Representation

A relay scenario is defined by a tuple `E = (g, t, B, Ω, N)`, where `g` is the ground control station (gateway), `t` is the mission UAV (target), `B` is the set of building obstacles, `Ω` is the operable 3D workspace, and `N` is the number of intermediate relay UAVs. A placement is an ordered chain `X = [x_0, ..., x_{N+1}]`, with `x_0 = g` and `x_{N+1} = t`. Each consecutive pair forms a wireless hop. Hop validity depends on LoS (`ℓ_i = 1`) and distance (`d_i ≤ r_max`). The communication capacity `c_i` for a valid hop follows a distance-based Shannon model, considering bandwidth, transmit power, noise, interference, and path-loss exponent. Blocked hops have zero capacity. The backbone throughput is limited by the bottleneck capacity `C_bot(X) = min_i c_i`, and end-to-end delay `D_e2e(X)` is also considered.

#### 3.2. Source Geometric Skeleton Transfer

This stage extracts a structural prior from a robot arm, which serves as the source domain.
- **Source Data Generation**: Robot-arm motions are generated using a pretrained Neural MP (Motion Planner) model.
- **Skeleton Extraction**: These motions are converted into ordered sequences of 3D joint positions, `P = [p_0, ..., p_{N+1}]`, through forward kinematics. This represents the geometric skeleton from the arm's base `p_0` to its end-effector `p_{N+1}`.
- **Normalization and Alignment**: The extracted skeleton `P` is normalized by its base-to-tip span. It is then aligned to the UAV gateway-target axis using a rotation matrix `R_E`, a scaling factor `S_E` (to match the city map scale), and a vertical mapping function `h_E` (to fit into the low-altitude corridor of the UAV domain). This aligned chain, `X_0`, serves as the initial structural prior that already incorporates obstacle-avoidance geometry.

#### 3.3. Skeleton-Conditioned Relay Prediction

A transformer-based transfer platform is used to predict target-domain relay coordinates conditioned on the aligned source skeleton.
- **Transformer Architecture**: The platform takes four types of tokens as input: a global scene token, obstacle point-cloud tokens representing the environment, source-skeleton tokens (`ˆp_i`), and `N` learned relay query tokens (each with type and positional embeddings).
- **Encoding and Prediction**: A self-attention encoder mixes these concatenated tokens. The outputs from the relay query tokens are then passed through a bounded head that maps normalized coordinates to the `Ω` workspace, yielding the predicted target-domain relay chain `˜X = [˜x_1, ..., ˜x_N]`.
- **Training Stages**:
 - **Source Pretraining**: The transformer platform is initially pretrained using ordered robot-arm skeletons (derived from Neural MP motions) as teacher placements. This phase trains the model to learn the general concept of an ordered, obstacle-aware chain.
 - **Target Adaptation with LoRA**: To adapt the pretrained model to the specific constraints of the UAV domain with limited data, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is employed. The pretrained backbone of the transformer is frozen. Low-rank adapters (matrices `A` and `B`) are inserted into the feed-forward layers. Only these adapters, the relay query embeddings, and the output head are updated during fine-tuning. This approach significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters, allowing the platform to learn feasible teacher placements (reflecting LoS, distance, altitude, and capacity constraints) from limited target data while being conditioned on the aligned source skeleton.

#### 3.4. Communication-Aware Refinement

The predicted chain `˜X` is not directly used as the final solution but serves as an initialization for a subsequent optimization step.
- **Objective Function**: Arm2Air minimizes a multi-objective function `J(X)` over the workspace `Ω`. This function aims to maximize bottleneck capacity (by minimizing `-C_bot(X)`) and minimize various penalties: LoS blockage (`Φ_los`), excessive hop distances, altitude violations, safety distance violations between relays, deviation from the aligned source prior (`∥x_i - x_0i∥^2`), workspace boundary violations (`Φ_bnd`), and end-to-end delay (`D_e2e(X)`).
- **Feasibility-First Strategy**: The refinement process initializes candidate chains from the transfer-platform prediction (`˜X`), the aligned source skeleton (`X_0`), interpolations between them, and other geometrically diverse samples. Hard constraints (e.g., LoS, minimum rate) are evaluated before the continuous objective, ensuring that only feasible placements are considered or prioritized.
- **Topology Search**: If the initial solution lacks sufficient communication quality, a restricted topology search is performed in the vicinity of the transferred skeleton to explore alternative LoS corridors. This hierarchical procedure focuses the search near obstacle-aware geometries while maintaining exploration capabilities. The optimization determines the final backbone `X* = arg min_{X∈Ω} J(X)`.

#### 3.5. Backbone Feasibility and Metrics

A chain is considered feasible if all hops maintain LoS and are within range, each hop capacity meets a minimum rate, and all relays satisfy safety distance, altitude corridor, and workspace bounds. Key metrics reported include bottleneck capacity (`C_bot`), end-to-end delay (`D_e2e`), and mean relay displacement (`¯M`), which quantifies the average 3D displacement of relays from the aligned source chain to the final placement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Arm2Air involved several experiments, demonstrating its performance in planning runtime, communication quality, movement efficiency, and data efficiency.

#### 4.1. Cost of Three-Dimensional Search

An initial experiment quantified the computational burden of 3D planning compared to 2D planning. On paired layouts with identical horizontal obstacle geometry, increasing map scale from 1× to 20× showed that 3D planning incurred significantly higher runtime. At 20× scale, A*, D*, and Dijkstra were 34.8, 79.9, and 116.5 times slower, respectively, than their 2D counterparts. The number of explored states also increased substantially (10.2x to 37.8x). This finding validated the motivation for a transfer-based initialization method to bypass the high cost of direct 3D search.

#### 4.2. Planning Performance

Evaluated on nine held-out high-clutter 3D urban maps, Arm2Air was compared against conventional planners (A*, D*, Dijkstra, RRT) that generate collision-free trajectories and then undergo communication-aware refinement.
- **Runtime**: Arm2Air reduced median end-to-end planning runtime by **64.9%** (13.930 seconds) relative to RRT (39.741 seconds), which was the fastest conventional planner.
- **Path Length**: It produced a **5.2% shorter chain** (72.719 m) than A* (76.743 m), which achieved the shortest baseline path length.
- **Communication Metrics**: Compared to the strongest baseline for each metric, Arm2Air increased bottleneck capacity (`C_min`) by **65.0%** (3.897 Mbps) and reduced end-to-end delay by **12.8%** (1994.44 ms).
These results indicate that the transferred skeleton reduces search cost and provides an effective initialization for serial relay formation.

#### 4.3. Communication Performance

Arm2Air was compared with IMPC-MD and AO Placement on a separate holdout set of 30 dense urban maps with high obstruction.
- **Compared with IMPC-MD**: Arm2Air demonstrated superior performance across all communication metrics. It increased bottleneck capacity (`C_min`) by **32.6%** (3.772 Mbps vs 2.844 Mbps), which was statistically significant (p = 0.006). It reduced capacity variance by **74.7%**, reduced maximum hop distance (`D_max`) by **13.2%** (statistically significant, p = 0.006), and reduced hop-distance variance by **75.2%**. Mean relay displacement (`Move`) was also reduced by **16.9%**.
- **Compared with AO Placement**: Arm2Air increased `C_min` by **83.1%**, reduced capacity variance by **99.9%**, reduced `D_max` by **27.1%**, and reduced hop-distance variance by **99.1%**. These four improvements were statistically significant (p ≤ 0.002). Mean relay displacement was reduced by **6.8%**.
The findings suggest that Arm2Air constructs more robust weakest links and distributes relay UAVs more evenly throughout the communication chain.

#### 4.4. Movement Efficiency

The mean relay displacement (`Move`) from the aligned source chain to the final placement was evaluated. Arm2Air required 14.639 m of adjustment, compared to 17.612 m for IMPC-MD and 15.714 m for AO Placement. This represents mean reductions of 16.9% and 6.8% respectively. While the movement difference was not statistically significant, the lower mean adjustment accompanied significant gains in communication quality, supporting the transferred skeleton as a geometrically informed prior.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study: Data-Efficient Target Adaptation

This study investigated the effectiveness of source pretraining with limited target-domain data. Using only three target-domain training maps, Arm2Air was compared against training from scratch (Scratch) and full fine-tuning.
- **RMSE Reduction**: Arm2Air reduced relay-position Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) by **53.6%** (3.092 m) relative to Scratch (6.669 m).
- **Parameter Efficiency**: Arm2Air achieved a 4.6% lower mean RMSE than Full Fine-tuning (3.241 m) while updating only **0.134 million parameters**, compared to 1.383 million for both Scratch and Full Fine-tuning.
These results demonstrate that the transferred representation enables accurate and parameter-efficient adaptation with minimal target-domain data.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study: Input and Training-Stage Ablation

An ablation study on the direct transformer output (before refinement) highlighted the contribution of each component:
- **No Source Prior**: Removing the aligned skeleton tokens increased RMSE by **17.4%**, indicating the source prior acts as a beneficial geometric bias.
- **No PCD (Point Cloud Data)**: Removing obstacle point-cloud tokens increased RMSE by **155.7%**, demonstrating the model's reliance on direct obstacle observation.
- **No LoRA**: Training without LoRA-based adaptation increased RMSE by **134.7%**.
- **No Communication FT (Fine-Tuning)**: Using the pre-communication LoRA checkpoint increased RMSE by **151.0%**.
These results confirm that the combined components—scene observations, the source skeleton as a prior, and target-domain adaptation—are crucial for accurate relay placement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Arm2Air research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic communication and motion planning, particularly for UAV relay networks.

**Computational and Data Efficiency**: The primary significance lies in demonstrating a method for computationally and data-efficient UAV relay placement in complex 3D urban environments. By leveraging cross-embodiment skeleton transfer, Arm2Air substantially reduces the planning runtime compared to traditional direct 3D search methods. Furthermore, its use of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) enables effective model adaptation with considerably fewer target-domain training examples and trainable parameters, addressing a critical challenge in scenarios where obtaining extensive real-world training data is difficult or costly.

**Improved Communication Performance and Robustness**: The results indicate that Arm2Air not only finds solutions faster but also generates higher-quality relay backbones. The method consistently achieved higher bottleneck capacity and lower end-to-end delay, alongside more balanced hop distances and capacities across the relay chain. This improved robustness and quality of communication links are crucial for reliable data transmission in critical applications such as disaster response or surveillance.

**Broader Applicability of Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: The work suggests a broader principle for transferring ordered structural priors across heterogeneous embodied tasks. By abstracting the problem to a geometric skeleton rather than low-level dynamics or control commands, Arm2Air opens avenues for applying similar transfer learning paradigms to other structured robotic problems.

**Potential Impact**:
- **Disaster Response and Emergency Communications**: Enables faster and more reliable deployment of aerial communication infrastructure in areas affected by disasters, where existing communication networks are compromised.
- **Temporary Network Deployment**: Facilitates rapid establishment of communication links for events, temporary surveillance, or military operations in complex terrains.
- **Advancements in Robot Learning**: Provides a concrete example of successful cross-embodiment transfer, potentially inspiring new approaches for complex multi-robot coordination, formation control, and sensor network deployment in challenging environments.
- **Reduced Development Costs**: The data and parameter efficiency could lower the barriers to entry for developing and deploying sophisticated UAV systems, as less bespoke training data and computational resources may be required.

**Future Directions**: The paper identifies several areas for future work, including examining the transferability under online point clouds, dynamic obstacles, channel uncertainty, and physical multi-UAV deployment. These extensions would further enhance the real-world applicability and robustness of the Arm2Air framework, moving towards more dynamic and uncertain operational environments.
