# PRIME: Physically-consistent Robotic Inertial and Motion Estimation for Legged and Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The PRIME framework simultaneously estimates robot motion, contact forces, and uncertain inertial parameters for legged and humanoid robots by integrating differentiable contact dynamics into an optimization-based approach. This enables the reconstruction of physically consistent trajectories from noisy sensor data, enhancing model accuracy for downstream control and learning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing state estimation methods for legged and humanoid robots often produce kinematically consistent but dynamically inconsistent motions, especially in contact-rich environments.
- Crucial information such as contact forces, precise contact timing, and dynamically consistent inertial parameters are typically unobserved, hindering accurate model-based control and data-driven learning.
- System identification is often decoupled from state estimation and struggles with unknown external forces or numerical stability issues when incorporating contact constraints.

## Method

- **PRIME** formulates the problem as a ==Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation==, jointly optimizing for state trajectories, contact impulses, and inertial parameters using a ==Log-Cholesky parameterization== for physical consistency.
- It incorporates ==differentiable contact dynamics==, including smoothed complementarity conditions and an Anitescu-style friction model, to transform complex contact constraints into a tractable, unconstrained convex optimization problem.
- The estimation is performed using Feasibility-Driven Differential Dynamic Programming (FDDP), leveraging analytic gradients of the smoothed contact dynamics for efficiency and robustness.

## Results

- PRIME accurately reconstructed contact sequences and identified inertial parameters for a hopper in simulation, converging reliably even with biased initial models.
- On quadrupedal and humanoid robots (Unitree Go2 and G1), the framework demonstrated accurate identification of payload masses (e.g., **4.8 kg** on Go2 for a **4.6 kg** payload) and produced dynamically consistent trajectories, even from noisy hardware sensor data.
- The method achieved improved contact force reconstruction compared to baselines, successfully inferring complex multi-point contact patterns and heel-to-toe rolling for humanoid locomotion, solving problems within **200-400 seconds** for **10-15 second** horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling differentiable contact dynamics allows for robust optimization across diverse and frequent contact transitions, providing well-behaved gradients for parameter estimation.
> - Jointly estimating motion, contact forces, and inertial parameters within a single framework ensures full physical consistency, unlike decoupled approaches.
> - Utilizing Log-Cholesky parameterization for inertial properties guarantees their physical feasibility and provides smooth derivatives for gradient-based optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The accurate estimation of robot motion, particularly for legged and humanoid robots operating in contact-rich environments, is a fundamental challenge in robotics. Existing state estimation pipelines, such as those relying on Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs) or external motion capture systems, are predominantly kinematics-centric. They typically recover only the robot's configuration and velocity, leaving crucial information like contact forces, precise contact timing, and dynamically consistent inertial parameters unobserved. This limitation often results in reconstructed motions that violate rigid-body dynamics, especially during interactions involving intermittent contacts, impacts, or frictional forces. Such kinematic inconsistencies can degrade the performance of downstream tasks like motion planning, feedback control, and various data-driven learning approaches including imitation learning and the development of robot foundation models, which rely on high-fidelity, physically consistent data.

Previous work in state estimation has incorporated dynamics-related factors, such as momentum terms or contact constraints, to infer ground-reaction forces or torso disturbances. However, these methods often treat contact in a decoupled manner, without enforcing full physical consistency across the entire system dynamics. Similarly, system identification, traditionally a separate process, often struggles with unknown external forces or requires specialized force/torque sensors. While some recent efforts have begun to unify state estimation and system identification within a single framework to leverage physical consistency, they have faced challenges. These include reliance on decoupled contact measurements or numerical difficulties arising from non-smooth complementarity constraints inherent in rigid body contact models, often limiting applications to simpler scenarios (e.g., planar systems, single-contact events).

This research positions PRIME as an advancement that addresses these limitations by integrating differentiable contact dynamics within a joint motion and parameter estimation framework. It aims to achieve physically consistent reconstructions for complex, multi-contact scenarios, an area where existing methods have shown limitations in robustness and applicability. The explicit modeling of smoothed contact dynamics and the joint estimation of all relevant variables (kinematics, contact forces, and inertial parameters) distinguish this work from approaches that either decouple these tasks or struggle with the numerical stability of contact constraints in high-dimensional systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for PRIME stems from the observed discrepancies between kinematically derived robot motions and the underlying physical reality, particularly for legged and humanoid robots engaging in contact-rich interactions. These robots navigate and manipulate objects by establishing intermittent contacts with their environment. While contemporary sensing technologies can provide accurate kinematic data (e.g., joint positions, floating-base poses), they typically do not directly measure contact forces, the precise timing of contact events, or the robot's specific inertial properties. This gap leads to several challenges:

- **Dynamical Inconsistency:** Kinematic reconstructions often inherently violate rigid-body dynamics, particularly during phases of locomotion or manipulation involving impacts, stance transitions, or complex frictional interactions. This is because the forces and moments acting on the robot are not fully accounted for or are implicitly absorbed into measurement noise.
- **Hindrance to Downstream Applications:** Such inconsistencies in recovered robot states or data directly impede the effectiveness of crucial robotic applications. Planning and control algorithms require accurate dynamic models to generate feasible and stable motions. Furthermore, data-driven methods, including imitation learning, vision-language-action models, and emerging robot foundation models, depend on high-quality, physically grounded data to learn robust and generalizable behaviors. Data that is dynamically inconsistent can lead to suboptimal policies or models that do not generalize well to real-world deployment.
- **Limitations in System Identification:** Existing system identification methods, when decoupled from state estimation or contact dynamics, often struggle with accurately characterizing inertial parameters, especially in the presence of unknown or unmodeled contact forces.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of the PRIME framework are:

- **Accurate and Dynamically Consistent Motion Estimation:** To refine raw kinematic measurements and actuator commands into a trajectory that is fully consistent with rigid-body dynamics and contact physics. This involves reconstructing not just the robot's configuration and velocity, but also the unobserved contact forces and contact timing.
- **Joint Estimation of Inertial Parameters:** To simultaneously identify physically consistent inertial parameters of the robot's links. This addresses situations where nominal robot models may not perfectly match the physical hardware (e.g., due to payloads or manufacturing variations), providing a calibrated model for improved control and simulation.
- **Robust Handling of Contact Dynamics:** To incorporate differentiable contact dynamics, including smoothed complementarity constraints and an Anitescu-style friction model, to formulate a smooth optimization problem. This is intended to ensure tractability and robustness across versatile and frequent contact transitions, avoiding numerical issues associated with discontinuous contact models.
- **Evaluation on Complex Systems:** To evaluate the proposed framework on real-world contact-rich locomotion tasks involving diverse legged platforms, specifically quadrupedal robots and humanoid robots, demonstrating its applicability and performance in high-dimensional and dynamic scenarios.

By achieving these objectives, PRIME aims to provide a comprehensive and physically grounded reconstruction of robot behavior, addressing a critical need for reliable state estimation and model calibration in advanced robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRIME formulates the motion and parameter estimation problem as a Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation task, which is solved as a Parameter Full-Information Estimation (PFIE) problem using an optimization-based approach. The core of the methodology lies in its detailed modeling of robot dynamics, contact interactions, inertial parameters, and sensor measurements, coupled with a robust optimization scheme.

**Robot Dynamics Modeling:**
The robot's state is defined by generalized coordinates $q$ (floating-base pose and joint positions) and generalized velocities $v$ (base twist and joint velocities). The continuous-time dynamics are approximated using a discrete-time semi-implicit Euler integration scheme. The equations of motion incorporate the inertia matrix $M(q)$, the bias vector $h(q, v)$ (Coriolis, centrifugal, gravity), the input matrix $B$ mapping joint torques $u$, and generalized forces arising from contact $J_i(q)^T f_i$. Contact forces $f_i$ are associated with contact Jacobians $J_i(q)$, with collisions modeled between rigid bodies at points and planes. A rigid contact model with anisotropic friction is employed, with contact constraints enforced via smoothed complementarity conditions using a log-barrier relaxation.

**Inertia Parameterization:**
The Lagrangian dynamics are linear with respect to a set of inertial parameters $\pi$ (mass, first mass moment, and rotational inertia components for each link). To ensure physical consistency, the pseudo-inertia matrix, constructed from these parameters, is required to be positive definite. For unconstrained optimization, PRIME utilizes a Log-Cholesky inertial parameterization, $\theta$, which provides a smooth mapping from $\theta$ to the standard inertial parameters $\pi$ and allows for closed-form Jacobian derivatives.

**Process Modeling with Contact Dynamics:**
The paper emphasizes that physical consistency in time-stepping contact dynamics couples the next-step configuration, velocity, and contact impulses.
- **Complementarity Condition:** This condition expresses that the signed distance between bodies and the normal contact impulse must be non-negative, and a non-zero normal force only occurs when bodies are in contact.
- **Maximum Dissipation:** This principle selects tangential friction forces that maximize kinetic energy dissipation.
- **MPCC and Convex Optimization:** These conditions collectively form a Mathematical Program with Complementarity Constraints (MPCC). PRIME reformulates this MPCC as a convex Second-Order Cone Program (SOCP) using Anitescu's relaxation. While this relaxation might introduce some nonphysical behaviors (e.g., spurious lift-off), it is deemed acceptable for locomotion where contacts are typically firm. Friction is encoded using both impulse cones and their dual velocity cones.
- **Smoothing of Contact Dynamics:** A critical aspect is the log-barrier relaxation of the SOCP, which transforms it into an unconstrained convex optimization problem. This smoothing provides well-behaved and informative gradients around contact transitions, enabling the use of gradient-based optimization methods. A customized Newton solver is used for efficient computation, warm-started with a feasibility initializer.
- **Approximation of Latent Impulses:** The contact impulses are recovered from the first-order optimality conditions of the smoothed problem, effectively providing impulse estimates from the estimated trajectory.
The overall process dynamics are represented as $x^+ \leftarrow F(x, u, \theta) \oplus \delta_x$, where $F$ denotes the smoothed contact dynamics, and $\delta_x$ is a zero-mean Gaussian process disturbance.

**Measurement Modeling:**
PRIME integrates a multi-sensor measurement model $y = \text{Meas}(x, u) \oplus \delta_y$, where $\delta_y$ represents zero-mean Gaussian measurement noise. This model fuses heterogeneous sensor modalities, including joint positions and velocities from encoders, floating-base measurements from motion capture systems, and actuator torque measurements. Notably, direct contact force or torque measurements are not required; these are inferred through the contact dynamics model.

**Optimization-Based Estimation (PFIE):**
The problem is formulated as minimizing a cost function consisting of:
- A regularization term for the initial inertial parameter estimate.
- A prior term for the initial state of the trajectory.
- Sums of squared errors for process noise (dynamics consistency) over the horizon.
- Sums of squared errors for measurement noise (sensor consistency) over the horizon.
The objective is to jointly estimate the state trajectory $x_{[0,T]}$, the noise variables $\delta_{[0,T]}$, and the inertial parameters $\theta$. The Log-Cholesky parameterization ensures that the inertial parameters remain physically feasible without explicit constraints.

**Optimal Estimation via Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP):**
Both the FIE (Full Information Estimation) and PFIE problems are solved using Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP). DDP works by recursively solving a Bellman equation, approximating the Q-function locally with a second-order expansion. This yields optimal uncertainty and state/parameter perturbations via backward and forward passes.

**Multiple Shooting Differential Dynamic Programming (FDDP):**
To enhance robustness, especially with numerically sensitive high-fidelity contact models, PRIME employs Feasibility-Driven Differential Dynamic Programming (FDDP). Unlike classical DDP, FDDP can handle dynamics infeasibility during early iterations, progressively reducing these defects, which is beneficial for problems involving contact dynamics.

**Analytic Gradient of Contact Dynamics:**
A key enabler for the efficiency of (F)DDP is the use of analytic gradients. Since the smoothed contact dynamics are expressed as an unconstrained convex optimization problem, their derivatives with respect to state and parameters can be obtained analytically through sensitivity analysis and the implicit function theorem. These analytic gradients are essential for accurate and fast computation within the DDP framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PRIME was conducted across various legged platforms in both simulation and hardware, demonstrating its capability to jointly estimate robot motion and uncertain inertial properties.

**Hopper Experiments (Simulation):**
PRIME was validated on a hopping robot in a custom MATLAB simulator using a 2.5-second horizon. The results indicated that the method effectively filtered noise-corrupted kinematic and actuator measurements. It successfully reconstructed the contact sequence and accurately identified inertial parameters, converging reliably toward ground-truth values even when initialized with biased inertial models.

**Quadrupedal Robots (Unitree Go2) (Simulation & Hardware):**
Experiments with the Unitree Go2 quadruped focused on jointly estimating motion and identifying uncertain torso inertia, often due to added payloads.
- **Simulation:** Two scenarios were tested: adding a 3 kg payload to the torso and shifting the torso's center of mass (COM) downward by 0.1 m. PRIME accurately reflected these imposed inertial changes in its estimates. The reconstructed trajectories and contact forces closely matched the ground truth.
- **Hardware:** With a 4.6 kg payload attached, PRIME estimated an additional mass of approximately 4.8 kg and a downward shift of the torso COM, consistent with the physical setup. The reconstructed state trajectory maintained consistency with real experiments, even when raw motion-capture kinematics exhibited orientation bias and jitter, which are dynamically inconsistent. PRIME provided high-quality, contact-consistent reconstructions despite these measurement artifacts.
- **Efficiency:** For a 10-second horizon (1000 samples), PRIME converged within 200 seconds on a standard PC, indicating computational efficiency for offline processing.

**Humanoid Robots (Unitree G1) (Simulation & Hardware):**
Experiments on the Unitree G1 humanoid involved diverse locomotion behaviors like walking, running, and dancing, using a 15-second horizon (1500 samples).
- **Inertial Identification:** While ground-truth inertial parameters were unavailable, PRIME identified an additional mass of approximately 3.4 kg, aligning with a known discrepancy between the nominal model mass and the scale-measured physical robot mass (attributed to the battery). This identification also preserved a similar COM location and appropriately increased rotational inertia.
- **Force Reconstruction:** With system identification enabled, PRIME achieved a lower overall cost and produced contact force estimates that more closely matched ground-truth data obtained from a force plate, demonstrating improved accuracy over estimation without identification. It successfully reconstructed smooth planar contact transitions, including heel-to-toe rolling and complex contact patterns during dancing, using a four-point contact model. This level of detailed contact force reconstruction for humanoid motions was noted as a novel demonstration.
- **Efficiency:** PRIME solved each humanoid problem in approximately 400 seconds on the same PC.

**Baseline Comparison (Supplementary Material):**
A comparison with a contact-constrained estimator (which infers contact flags kinematically and models contact as a single mid-foot wrench) in G1 simulation showed that PRIME provided substantially better contact-force reconstruction and a lower optimization cost. This highlights the benefit of PRIME's explicit modeling of contact dynamics and joint inference of contact status.

**Smoothed Contact Dynamics Analysis (Supplementary Material):**
Analysis of the smoothing parameter ($\kappa$) demonstrated that a moderate value (e.g., $\kappa = 500$) promotes optimization convergence by providing informative gradients, while still maintaining physical consistency and tolerating noise.

**Computation Profiling (Supplementary Material):**
A single quadruped simulation rollout with differentiation took approximately 80 microseconds on average, suggesting the potential for future real-time implementations in a receding-horizon (MHE) framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PRIME framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic state estimation and system identification, particularly for contact-rich legged and humanoid robots. By explicitly integrating differentiable contact dynamics and simultaneously estimating motion, contact forces, and inertial parameters, this work addresses a long-standing challenge of achieving physically consistent reconstructions from noisy and incomplete sensor data.

The primary significance of PRIME lies in its ability to:
- **Enhance State Estimation Quality:** It provides dynamically consistent motion trajectories, which are more reliable than purely kinematic reconstructions, especially in complex, multi-contact scenarios where existing methods often fail to capture true physical behavior.
- **Improve System Calibration:** By accurately identifying uncertain inertial parameters from observed motion, PRIME offers a mechanism to calibrate robot models to match physical hardware. This calibrated information is directly beneficial for improving the performance of model-based control strategies and motion planning algorithms, which rely on precise robot dynamics.
- **Reconstruct Latent Information:** The framework effectively infers unobserved contact forces and contact timings, offering a detailed understanding of robot-environment interactions without requiring specialized force sensors. This capability is crucial for analyzing complex behaviors and for developing more sophisticated interaction control.

The potential impact of PRIME extends to several key areas:
- **Robust Robot Control and Planning:** With more accurate state estimates and calibrated dynamic models, robots can execute more stable, agile, and robust motions, particularly in dynamic and uncertain environments. This can lead to advancements in tasks like rough-terrain locomotion, dynamic manipulation, and human-robot interaction where accurate contact reasoning is paramount.
- **High-Quality Data for Machine Learning:** PRIME transforms raw robot logs into "force- and contact-annotated motion reconstructions." This process enriches real-robot datasets with physically grounded supervision, providing high-fidelity training data for data-driven learning applications. This includes, but is not limited to, large-scale behavior modeling, robot foundation models, and vision-language-action models, where the quality and physical consistency of training data are critical for generalization and robustness.
- **Generalization to Other Robotic Tasks:** While validated on locomotion, the underlying formulation of PRIME is applicable to other contact-rich behaviors, such as robotic manipulation or loco-manipulation. This suggests its potential to address similar challenges of intermittent and unobserved interactions in diverse robotic domains.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research into the observability and identifiability of motion, contact forces, and inertial parameters under various sensory limitations and task structures. Furthermore, the demonstrated computational efficiency of single-step operations suggests the potential for developing real-time receding-horizon (MHE) formulations of PRIME, which would enable online, adaptive state and parameter estimation in deployed systems.
