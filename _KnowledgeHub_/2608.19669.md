# Scaffolding Minds: Optimizing Latent Visual Target Representations for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Scaffolding Minds improves latent visual chain-of-thought for VLMs by learning a dedicated scaffolding encoder that produces reasoning-optimized latent targets (replacing frozen off-the-shelf vision features), paired with an RL sampler that learns Gaussian mean and variance over residual latent actions for exploration. Gains: +9.5% (up to +19% at 32x32) on FrozenLake, +5.2% average across nine visual reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- SFT-stage latent targets come from a frozen off-the-shelf vision encoder tuned for generic perception, not the downstream reasoning task, yielding suboptimal, misaligned latent supervision.
- Existing RL methods for latent reasoning either optimize only discrete text tokens, giving no credit assignment to latent actions at all.
- Where latent RL exists (VLPO), it applies deterministic fixed-variance Gaussian regularization that constrains drift but never samples alternative latent trajectories for exploration.

## Method

- ==Scaffolding encoder==: a trainable copy of the VLM vision encoder plus ==cross-attention pooling==, trained end-to-end through the frozen base VLM's task loss to map a helper image into an optimized latent target z*.
- ==Generation-phase distillation==: the VLM learns to predict its own latent block from input alone (no helper image), matched to the frozen scaffolding target via ==L2 loss== plus answer cross-entropy, with latents produced in one forward pass, not autoregressively.
- ==Scaffolding RL==: two MLP heads predict input-adaptive mean and variance of a ==Gaussian== over residual latent adjustments on the generated latent prior, sampled during rollout so reward can directly optimize latent exploration.

## Results

- On FrozenLake (Levels 8-32), Scaffolding Encoder+RL reaches **75.0%** average accuracy, beating strongest latent baseline VaLR (65.5%) by **+9.5%** and widening to **+19%** at Level 32 (55.0% vs baseline collapse).
- Across nine visual-centric benchmarks (V*, BLINK, MMVP, MMStar, CVBench, HRBench-4K/8K, MME-RealWorld-Lite, Jigsaw), average accuracy hits **73.9%**, a **+5.2%** gain over strongest prior latent method and **+6.2%** over thinking-with-images systems.
- Ablations: switching Stage 1 objective from L2-regression-to-frozen-features to task-loss-driven attention pooling plus tuned vision encoder contributes **+14.3%** combined; Scaffolding RL yields **+3.0%** versus VLPO's unstable +1.8% and text-only GRPO's +0.6%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Target-representation quality dominates latent reasoning gains more than the training objective alone; tuning the encoder that produces the latent target matters more than swapping the regression loss.
> - Deterministic regularization toward a fixed latent output is not equivalent to a sampling policy; without an explicit stochastic action distribution, RL cannot explore alternative latent trajectories.
> - A weak Stage 1 target caps Stage 2 RL gains regardless of RL method quality, so investing in target optimization before RL exploration is the higher-leverage design choice.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses advancements in multimodal reasoning within Vision-Language Models (VLMs), a field that has seen increasing capabilities through various methods. Traditional approaches often rely on textual chain-of-thought (CoT) to guide reasoning. While effective in some contexts, these text-centric methods can lead to a loss of important visual details necessary for complex reasoning tasks. This limitation has motivated the development of latent visual reasoning methods.

Latent visual reasoning aims to bridge the gap by integrating continuous latent visual tokens directly into the reasoning chain, enabling models to process information in both textual and visual modalities. This paradigm typically involves a two-stage training process:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Stage:** A helper image, often an annotated crop, is encoded into latent tokens using an off-the-shelf vision encoder. These tokens serve as a target to teach the VLM visual chain-of-thought.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Stage:** The generated latent tokens are further refined using reward feedback to improve task performance.

The present work identifies two specific limitations within this established two-stage latent reasoning framework. First, the SFT stage's reliance on general-purpose, off-the-shelf vision encoders can produce suboptimal latent representations that are not explicitly aligned with the downstream reasoning task. Such representations may retain irrelevant perceptual information while failing to emphasize critical intermediate visual evidence. Second, existing RL methods for latent reasoning often address the latent component only through deterministic regularization or by solely optimizing text tokens. This approach restricts the exploration of alternative latent trajectories, which can limit performance gains or lead to instability in reward optimization.

"Scaffolding Minds" positions itself within this landscape by proposing modifications to both stages of the latent visual reasoning paradigm, aiming to optimize the latent visual target representations and enhance exploration capabilities in the latent space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve multimodal reasoning capabilities in VLMs by addressing two identified limitations in the existing two-stage latent visual reasoning paradigm.

The motivation for these improvements stems from the following detailed points:

**Motivation for SFT Stage Improvement: Optimizing Latent Target Representations**
- **Problem:** In the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage of conventional latent reasoning, the latent target for the VLM's generated visual tokens is derived by encoding a helper image using a frozen, off-the-shelf vision encoder. Examples of such encoders include SigLIP, which are typically trained for general-purpose visual representation tasks.
- **Limitation:** A general-purpose vision encoder is not inherently optimized for the specific downstream reasoning objective. Consequently, the latent features it produces from the helper image may:
 - Preserve perceptual content that is irrelevant to the particular reasoning task.
 - Fail to adequately emphasize the intermediate visual evidence crucial for effective task-specific reasoning.
- **Resulting Constraint:** When the VLM is supervised to match these suboptimal latent targets, its capacity to learn and utilize truly useful reasoning information is limited. This means the model is guided toward a convenient but ultimately less effective target representation, hindering its potential even if the VLM itself is highly trainable.
- **Objective of Scaffolding Minds:** To overcome this, the authors propose to learn a *dedicated scaffolding encoder*. This encoder is designed to encode the helper image into latent targets ($z^*$) that are explicitly optimized for the reasoning task. The optimization occurs end-to-end, driven by the downstream task loss, ensuring the latent representation is highly relevant to the problem at hand.

**Motivation for RL Stage Improvement: Enabling Latent Space Exploration**
- **Problem:** Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods applied to latent reasoning either restrict policy optimization solely to discrete text tokens (e.g., GRPO), providing no direct likelihood or credit assignment for continuous latent actions, or incorporate latent embeddings through deterministic regularization (e.g., Visual-Latent Policy Optimization, VLPO). In VLPO, a fixed-variance Gaussian formulation is used to penalize drift from a reference latent output, but it does not involve sampling.
- **Limitation:** Without sampling alternative latent actions for exploration, reward optimization cannot effectively discover more useful or efficient latent reasoning trajectories. Deterministic regularization merely constrains how far a latent representation drifts but does not facilitate the active search for better continuous latent actions. This can lead to small, unstable, or suboptimal performance gains in the RL stage, as observed in prior work.
- **Resulting Constraint:** The inability to directly explore the continuous latent action space limits the VLM's capacity to adapt and refine its visual reasoning process based on reward signals.
- **Objective of Scaffolding Minds:** To address this, the authors introduce "Scaffolding RL." This approach replaces deterministic latent regularization with an *explicit Gaussian sampler over residual latent actions*. Crucially, the mean ($\mu$) and variance ($\sigma^2$) of this Gaussian sampler are learned and made *input-adaptive*, conditioned on the VLM's generated latent prior. This allows the policy to sample actions during rollout, actively exploring alternative latent reasoning trajectories and directly optimizing these continuous actions through reward feedback.

By addressing these two limitations, Scaffolding Minds aims to provide a more effective and robust framework for multimodal reasoning, enabling VLMs to "think" more proficiently in both text and vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Scaffolding Minds is structured as a two-stage framework designed to address the aforementioned limitations in latent visual reasoning. The approach optimizes latent target representations and enhances exploration in the latent space.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: SFT via Scaffolding Encoder

The first stage focuses on learning an optimized latent target representation and training the VLM to generate its own latent block without needing the helper image at inference time. This stage is divided into two phases:

##### 3.1.1. Scaffolding Phase

- **Goal:** To learn a task-optimized latent target $z^*$ from a helper image $h$, replacing the use of frozen, general-purpose vision encoder features.
- **Scaffolding Encoder Architecture:** The scaffolding encoder, denoted $g^*_\psi$, is implemented as a trainable copy of the VLM's vision encoder followed by a cross-attention pooling module. This module compresses the visual tokens from the helper image $h$ into $K$ continuous latent tokens, $z^* = g^*_\psi(h) \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$.
- **Training Process:** During this phase, the *base VLM is kept frozen*. Only the parameters $\psi$ of the scaffolding encoder are updated. The generated $z^*$ tokens are directly inserted into the VLM at the designated latent positions. The training objective is the standard token-level cross-entropy loss ($L_{CE}$) on the ground-truth answer tokens $y$, given the input $x$ and the scaffolding encoder's $z^*$:
 $L_{scaffolding}(\psi) = L_{CE}(y | x, z^*)$.
- **Rationale:** This process trains the scaffolding encoder to produce a $z^*$ that contains precisely the reasoning-sensitive features required by the (frozen) VLM to generate the correct answer. This direct optimization ensures the latent target is tailored to the downstream task, rather than being a generic visual representation.

##### 3.1.2. Generation Phase

- **Goal:** To fine-tune the VLM to generate its own latent block $z$ from the input $x$ alone, approximating the optimized target $z^*$ learned in the Scaffolding Phase. The helper image $h$ and the scaffolding encoder $g^*_\psi$ are not used during inference.
- **VLM Fine-tuning:** In this phase, the *scaffolding encoder $g^*_\psi$ is frozen*. The parameters $\theta$ of the base VLM are fine-tuned. The VLM generates $K$ latent tokens $z$ from learnable embeddings that pass through its transformer layers, using $x$ as context. This generation occurs in a single forward pass, avoiding autoregressive generation of latent tokens to speed up inference. The answer text $y$ is then generated autoregressively from $x$ and $z$.
- **Training Objective:** The VLM is trained with a combined loss function:
 $L_{generation}(\theta) = \lambda_{latent} \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K ||z_k - z^*_k||_2^2 + \lambda_{task} L_{CE}(y | x, z)$.
 - The first term is an $L_2$ matching loss, encouraging the VLM's generated latent tokens $z$ to align with the optimized target $z^*$.
 - The second term is a cross-entropy loss, ensuring the VLM can still correctly generate the answer $y$ using its own predicted latent block $z$.
 - $\lambda_{latent}$ and $\lambda_{task}$ are hyperparameters balancing these two objectives.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Scaffolding RL

The second stage introduces a reinforcement learning approach to refine the latent generation process, enabling active exploration in the continuous latent space.

- **Problem with Prior RL:** Existing methods either apply RL only to discrete text tokens or use deterministic, fixed-variance Gaussian regularization for latent tokens, which limits exploration.
- **Scaffolding RL Approach:**
 - **Base Latent Action:** The latent prior $z_\theta = f_\theta(x, c_{pre})$ (where $c_{pre}$ is optional preceding text reasoning) generated by the VLM from Stage 1 is treated as a base action.
 - **Learned Residual Adjustments:** The model learns adaptive, reward-guided adjustments $\Delta_z^*$ to this base action.
 - **Gaussian Sampler:** Instead of deterministic output, a Gaussian policy is defined over residual latent actions: $\Delta_z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu(z_\theta), \sigma(z_\theta)^2)$.
 - **Input-Adaptive Mean and Variance:** Two lightweight MLP heads are added on top of the shared VLM hidden states to learn the mean $\mu(z_\theta)$ and variance $\sigma(z_\theta)$ dynamically. This allows the model to adapt its exploration strategy: wider search when the prior is uncertain, and safer exploitation when it is likely optimal. The mean head is zero-initialized, centering initial exploration on the prior, while the variance head starts with small positive values for stable initial exploration.
 - **Perturbed Latent Block:** The sampled residual adjustment $\Delta_z$ is added to the base latent action, yielding $z_\theta + \Delta_z$, which is then used in the VLM's forward pass.
 - **RL Objective:** A GRPO-style clipped objective is used, which combines terms for both latent and text components. This objective sums a block-level latent importance ratio ($\rho_{lat}$) for the sampled latent action and a token-level text importance ratio ($\rho_{text}$) for the discrete answer trajectory. It also includes a KL divergence penalty against the fixed Stage 1 reference policy.
 - **Trainability:** During Stage 2, both the VLM's latent generation component ($f_\theta$) and the two MLP heads for $\mu$ and $\sigma$ remain trainable, allowing for direct optimization of continuous latent actions through reward feedback.

By integrating these two stages, Scaffolding Minds aims to create a VLM that generates latent representations optimized for specific reasoning tasks and can actively explore and refine these representations through reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Scaffolding Minds across two distinct settings: spatial planning (FrozenLake) and a suite of visual-centric reasoning benchmarks. The results demonstrate consistent performance improvements over baseline and prior latent reasoning methods.

#### 4.1. FrozenLake Spatial Planning

The FrozenLake benchmark involves multi-step spatial planning on grid-based environments of varying complexity (8x8 to 32x32).
- **Overall Performance:** Scaffolding Encoder+RL achieved an average accuracy of 75.0%, representing a +23.0% improvement over the SFT baseline (52.0%).
- **Against Latent Reasoning Baselines:** The method improved over the strongest prior latent reasoning approach (VaLR) by +9.5% on average. This gain is attributed to the learned latent target space being more suitable for decision-relevant reasoning compared to targets derived from general-purpose vision features.
- **Against Image-Generation Methods:** Scaffolding Minds outperformed image-generation methods like Diff Thinker by +10.7% on average. This suggests that explicit generation of intermediate images is not necessary for strong visual reasoning, provided the latent space effectively captures the reasoning structure.
- **Scaling with Difficulty:** The performance advantage of Scaffolding Minds increased with the complexity of the maze. For the most challenging 32x32 grids, the gain over the strongest latent baseline (VaLR) widened to +19% (55.0% vs. 36.0%), indicating more graceful degradation as task difficulty increases.

#### 4.2. Visual-Centric Reasoning Benchmarks

The method was evaluated on nine visual-centric reasoning benchmarks (V*, BLINK, MMVP, MMStar, CV-Bench, HRBench-4K, HRBench-8K, MME-RealWorld-Lite, and Jigsaw) to assess its generalization capabilities.
- **Overall Performance:** Scaffolding Encoder+RL achieved an average accuracy of 73.9%, showing an average increase of +10.4% over the Vanilla SFT baseline (63.5%) and +12.8% over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model (61.1%).
- **Against Prior Latent Reasoning Methods:** It improved the average score by +5.2% compared to the strongest prior latent reasoning method (SkiLa, 73.9% vs. 67.7%). These gains were consistent, particularly on benchmarks requiring spatial discrimination and fine-grained visual comparison.
- **Against Thinking-with-Images Methods:** The method improved over the strongest prior average from thinking-with-images style methods (DeepEyes) by +6.2% (73.9% vs. 67.5%).
- **Specific Task Performance:** Notable improvements were observed on MMVP (+20.7%), MME-RealWorld-Lite (+17.6%), and CVBench (+13.9%) relative to the base model, tasks that emphasize spatial and fine-grained visual distinctions. The method also achieved the best results on CVBench, HRBench-4K, and HRBench-8K, indicating effectiveness for tasks requiring precise regional evidence and high-resolution understanding.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Key Contributions

- **SFT Visual Target Ablation:**
 - Switching from $L_2$ regression to a cross-attention pooling objective with downstream task loss yielded a +4.3% accuracy increase.
 - Additionally tuning the vision encoder (the Scaffolding Encoder) contributed a further +10.0% accuracy gain.
 - This indicates that optimizing the latent target through task-specific training, rather than merely changing the objective or using fixed features, is the dominant factor in performance improvement for Stage 1.
- **RL Method Comparison:**
 - "No Sampling on Latent" (text-only GRPO) showed a marginal +0.6% gain.
 - VLPO (deterministic Gaussian regularization) achieved +1.8% but exhibited fluctuations.
 - Scaffolding RL (learned Gaussian adjustment with sampling) provided the largest and most stable gain of +3.0%. This highlights the importance of sampling learned adjustments for effective, reward-driven exploration in latent space.
- **Complementarity of Stages 1 and 2:** The results demonstrated that while Scaffolding RL can refine targets (yielding +5.2% gain without the scaffolding encoder), it cannot fully compensate for a weak initial latent target. A strong scaffolding encoder in Stage 1 provides a high-accuracy starting point, enabling Stage 2 RL to achieve its full potential.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Ablations

- **Number of Latent Tokens ($K$):** $K=4$ was identified as the optimal setting for FrozenLake, balancing expressiveness for multi-step spatial plans with learnability and avoiding redundancy.
- **Helper Image Choice:** The scaffolding encoder was shown to better utilize richer, non-natural helper images (e.g., value-function heatmaps), leading to a +12.0% gain compared to an off-the-shelf encoder. This demonstrates its ability to learn from task-specific helper representations more effectively.

#### 4.5. Inference Efficiency

Scaffolding Minds demonstrated high inference efficiency. It incurred a +4.6% overhead (272 ms per query) compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model (260 ms). This overhead is comparable to other latent reasoning methods (typically +2% to +8%) but significantly lower than image-generation methods (e.g., +250%), while achieving stronger reasoning gains.

#### 4.6. Model Scale and LoRA Fine-tuning

The benefits of Scaffolding Minds were maintained across different model scales and fine-tuning strategies. Similar proportional gains were observed with a smaller Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone and with parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning on the 7B model, though full fine-tuning at 7B remained the highest performing configuration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Scaffolding Minds" framework contributes to the field of multimodal reasoning by addressing limitations in existing latent visual reasoning paradigms. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Latent Visual Reasoning:** The work demonstrates that directly optimizing the latent visual target representation for a given reasoning task, rather than relying on generic vision features from off-the-shelf encoders, yields substantial improvements in reasoning accuracy. This highlights a critical area for future development in VLM architectures.
- **Enhanced Exploration in Latent Space:** By introducing a learned, input-adaptive Gaussian policy for sampling residual latent actions, the method enables active, reward-driven exploration of continuous latent reasoning trajectories. This is a methodological advancement over prior deterministic or text-only RL approaches, offering a more effective mechanism for VLM refinement.
- **Improved Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** The consistent and notable performance gains on both structured spatial planning tasks (FrozenLake) and a wide range of visual-centric reasoning benchmarks indicate the generalizability and robustness of the proposed framework. The particular improvements on tasks requiring fine-grained visual distinctions and high-resolution understanding suggest its utility in scenarios where visual precision is paramount.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Achieving significant reasoning improvements with only a minimal increase in inference-time overhead (less than 5% over the base VLM) is an important practical advantage. This makes Scaffolding Minds a viable approach for real-world applications where efficient multi-step visual reasoning is required, contrasting with more computationally intensive image-generation methods.
- **Complementary Design:** The finding that the scaffolding encoder (Stage 1) and Scaffolding RL (Stage 2) are complementary emphasizes that both a well-optimized initial latent target and subsequent active exploration are important for maximizing VLM reasoning capabilities. This suggests that holistic framework design, addressing multiple stages of the reasoning pipeline, can lead to more robust systems.

**Potential Impact:**
The principles introduced by Scaffolding Minds could influence the design of future VLMs, prompting a focus on task-specific latent representation learning and adaptive exploration strategies. This may lead to the development of more intelligent and efficient multimodal AI agents capable of handling complex visual-linguistic tasks with greater accuracy and less computational burden.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- **Reliance on Helper Images:** The current framework requires intermediate helper images during training. Future research could explore extending the framework to scenarios where such helper images are unavailable or expensive to acquire, potentially through automatic helper image generation or other self-supervised mechanisms.
- **Helper Image Design:** While value-function heatmaps were effective for FrozenLake, the paper notes that an exhaustive search for optimal helper image designs was not performed. Automatically proposing suitable helper images for novel domains remains an open research question, which could further unlock the potential of the scaffolding encoder.
