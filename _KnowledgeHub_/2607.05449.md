# Geometry-Aware Infrastructure-Anchored Denoiser for UWB Sensing and Work-Zone Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> GAIA introduces a UWB range denoising framework that integrates geometric awareness into the learning process for reconstructing work-zone boundaries. This approach improves overall distance MSE by 18.4% and increases polygon IoU by 15.5% on real-world UWB data compared to existing learning-based methods, enabling more accurate and spatially coherent perception of dynamic road environments.

## Problem

- Existing methods for dynamic work-zone perception (e.g., LiDAR, vision-based, trajectory-based) are often costly, susceptible to environmental factors like occlusion, or treat work-zone boundaries as an implicit byproduct, hindering direct geometric reconstruction.
- UWB radio ranging, while low-cost, is prone to significant errors from non-line-of-sight (NLOS) propagation, burst noise, and long-tail errors in outdoor work zones, compromising its reliability for geometric tasks.
- Previous UWB range denoising approaches primarily focus on signal-level accuracy (e.g., mean range error) and do not explicitly model or optimize for the geometric consistency crucial for accurate work-zone boundary reconstruction.

## Method

- **GAIA** infers a ==latent anchor layout== from temporal multi-anchor UWB observations and integrates this inferred geometry as an explicit spatial prior into the ==range denoising== process.
- The framework uses a ==Gated Fusion module== to adaptively combine learned range corrections from a ==temporal refinement network== with geometry-consistent distances derived from the inferred latent layout.
- Training employs a ==multi-component loss function== that supervises predicted distances and inferred layouts while regularizing for ==geometry consistency== at both the range and anchor levels.

## Results

- GAIA reduced the overall range Mean Squared Error (MSE) to **0.1414**, an **18.4%** improvement, and increased polygon Intersection-over-Union (IoU) by **15.5%** to **0.2390** compared to the strongest learning-based baseline on a real-world UWB dataset.
- The framework demonstrated superior robustness in a real-data-calibrated stress-test simulator, achieving a **50.8%** reduction in overall distance MSE and an improved polygon IoU under challenging NLOS and error conditions.
- A zero-shot robustness study confirmed GAIA's ability to maintain higher mean IoU across various stressors (e.g., increased noise, outlier rates, fewer anchors), consistently outperforming baselines even without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and incorporating a latent anchor layout derived from temporal UWB observations as a spatial prior significantly enhances the geometric consistency of work-zone boundary reconstruction.
> - A Gated Fusion mechanism effectively balances learned residual corrections with deterministically computed geometry-consistent distances, providing robustness against various UWB measurement errors.
> - Jointly optimizing range denoising with geometry-aware loss components (layout supervision, geometry consistency, anchor-level geometry) is crucial for achieving spatially coherent and accurate work-zone boundary estimates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses challenges in perceiving dynamic road environments, specifically work zones, and integrates advancements in Ultra-wideband (UWB) sensing with geometry-aware machine learning.

Current approaches to work-zone perception often involve methods such as:
- **LiDAR-based mobile mapping systems:** These systems can recover detailed roadway geometry but require specialized, calibrated equipment and may not support continuous adaptation to rapidly changing conditions.
- **Vision-based pipelines:** These detect temporary traffic control devices and infer scene extent. However, they are susceptible to occlusion, adverse lighting conditions, and rely on extensive annotated data.
- **Trajectory-based crowdsourcing:** These methods infer drivable regions from accumulated vehicle paths but can lag behind abrupt layout changes and may degrade with sparse observations.
- **High-definition (HD) map update literature:** This field highlights the difficulties and costs associated with maintaining accurate geometric representations of dynamic road environments.

A common limitation among these existing approaches is their primary optimization for element-level detection or localization, treating the drivable work-zone boundary as an implicit byproduct rather than a direct geometric reconstruction objective. This indirect formulation can obscure the inherent geometric structure of the problem and limit the ability to reason about boundary-level errors.

UWB radio ranging offers a complementary sensing modality. Its fine time-of-flight resolution enables accurate distance measurement at a modest cost, and UWB-enabled roadside units (RSUs) can provide multi-anchor geometric constraints to a vehicle-mounted tag. This vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) setup provides a direct signal path to the work-zone boundary. However, UWB ranging is susceptible to non-line-of-sight (NLOS) propagation, multipath reflections, and environmental interference, which introduce bias and instability.

Recent work has explored learning-based UWB range correction to mitigate NLOS-induced bias. These methods typically frame the problem as range denoising, with the objective of recovering accurate anchor-wise distances. Work-zone geometry reconstruction often remains a secondary, downstream outcome. This creates a gap because reconstruction quality is disproportionately affected by errors on *geometrically critical* anchors, a factor not fully captured by average ranging error minimization alone. Furthermore, real outdoor UWB data collection is costly and may not cover all severe NLOS, burst-noise, and long-tail error cases, necessitating evaluation strategies that combine real-world data with controlled simulations.

This paper positions itself by proposing a UWB denoising framework that explicitly integrates geometric awareness into the learning process, moving beyond signal-level accuracy to prioritize boundary-consistent work-zone reconstruction. It addresses the limitations of existing perception methods by offering a low-cost, direct geometric sensing modality for dynamic work zones and enhances learning-based UWB denoising by orienting it toward a direct geometric objective. The evaluation strategy emphasizes real-world data while utilizing a calibrated simulator for stress testing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve accurate perception of work-zone geometry, which is recognized as a foundational requirement for intelligent transportation systems, enabling compliant routing, hazard buffering, and safe passage. The paper identifies UWB sensing as a low-cost and infrastructure-aided path toward this goal.

The motivation for developing the GAIA framework stems from several identified challenges and limitations:

1. **UWB Ranging Challenges in Work Zones:** Outdoor work zones present a dynamic and challenging environment for UWB sensing. Measurements are frequently affected by:
 - **Non-line-of-sight (NLOS) propagation:** Obstacles common in work zones block direct signal paths.
 - **Burst noise:** Sudden, transient errors that can significantly disrupt measurements.
 - **Long-tail errors:** Infrequent but large errors that can severely distort spatial reconstruction.
 These factors introduce significant bias and instability, undermining the reliability of UWB measurements for geometric tasks.

2. **Limitations of Existing Range Denoising Approaches:** Prior learning-based methods for UWB range correction primarily formulate the problem as signal-level range denoising. Their objectives are typically defined by mean range error or LOS/NLOS classification accuracy. This approach does not explicitly model or optimize for the geometric structure needed for reliable boundary reconstruction. The paper highlights that work-zone boundary reconstruction is inherently geometry-sensitive; errors on geometrically critical anchors can severely distort the reconstructed boundary, even if the average ranging error is low. Conversely, moderate errors on non-critical anchors may have a limited impact on the overall boundary structure. Consequently, minimizing average ranging error alone is considered insufficient for reliable boundary reconstruction.

3. **Need for Geometry-Aware Modeling:** The geometric nature of work-zone boundaries necessitates a direct approach to geometric consistency. Existing methods, by treating the boundary as an implicit byproduct of object detection or localization, miss opportunities to reason about and optimize for boundary-level errors directly. The authors argue that a framework is needed that links range correction to spatially coherent work-zone geometry estimation.

4. **Data Collection Challenges:** Real outdoor work-zone UWB data are expensive to collect and may not encompass the full spectrum of severe NLOS, burst-noise, and long-tail error cases that can occur. This motivates the need for a robust evaluation strategy that centers on real-world data while supplementing it with controlled stress-test simulations calibrated to real-data error characteristics.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the GAIA framework are:
- To formulate UWB work-zone mapping as *boundary-oriented range denoising*, where supervised range prediction is regularized by latent layout and geometry-consistency objectives.
- To design a geometry-aware denoising framework that infers a latent anchor layout from temporal multi-anchor UWB observations and feeds this inferred geometry back into the denoising process as an explicit spatial prior. This joint enforcement of temporal consistency and spatially coherent boundary reconstruction is intended.
- To validate the framework primarily on real-world outdoor UWB measurements, complemented by a real-data-calibrated stress-test simulator for examining robustness under challenging conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GAIA (Geometry-Aware Infrastructure-Anchored denoiser) is a learned UWB range-denoising framework designed for work-zone geometry reconstruction. Its core principle is to infer a latent geometric representation from temporal multi-anchor UWB observations and integrate this inferred geometry back into the denoising process as an explicit spatial prior.

The framework processes input sequences through six interconnected modules:

1. **Masked Observation Construction:**
 - **Input:** Raw UWB distance measurements ($d_{obs}^{seq}$) and a validity mask ($m_{seq}$).
 - **Process:** The validity mask is applied element-wise to the raw measurements to obtain $d_{obs}^{use}$, ensuring that only valid (non-padded) anchor measurements contribute to subsequent computations.

2. **PoseMLP Base Module:**
 - **Input:** Masked observations ($d_{obs}^{use}$) and the vehicle location sequence ($p_{seq}$).
 - **Process:** This module is a pretrained, pose-aware denoising network (based on PoseMLP from prior work). It is kept frozen during GAIA's training to provide a stable, per-step initialization reflecting coarse anchor-layout information. For each time step, it predicts an initial per-anchor distance estimate ($b_{val}$).

3. **Temporal Refinement Module:**
 - **Input:** Masked observations ($d_{obs}^{use}$), the base prediction ($b_{val}$), validity mask ($m_{seq}$), and vehicle location sequence ($p_{seq}$).
 - **Process:** These inputs are concatenated along the feature dimension. The resulting fused sequential feature is then processed by a bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) followed by a LayerNorm layer. The bidirectional GRU captures temporal dependencies in both forward and backward directions, which is intended to handle temporally correlated noise. The output is a latent temporal feature ($h$) summarizing ranging dynamics, motion context, and cross-anchor interactions.

4. **Layout Head Module:**
 - **Input:** The latent temporal feature ($h$).
 - **Process:** The temporal feature $h$ is aggregated along the time dimension using a mean operator to produce a global representation ($h_{mean}$). This is fed into a three-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) with ReLU activations, which predicts an initial latent anchor layout ($l_{init}$). An anchor-level validity mask ($m_{anchor}$), derived from $m_{seq}$, is applied to $l_{init}$ to yield the final latent layout ($l$). This module explicitly introduces spatial reasoning by inferring anchor positions from temporal features, thereby encouraging consistency between predicted distances and the underlying spatial structure.

5. **GeoDist Module:**
 - **Input:** The predicted latent anchor layout ($l$) and the vehicle location sequence ($p_{seq}$).
 - **Process:** This is a deterministic module with no learnable parameters. It computes geometry-consistent distances ($l_d$) between the vehicle positions and each predicted anchor in the layout using Euclidean distance. This module projects the inferred anchor layout back into the ranging space, providing a physically grounded reference signal.

6. **Prediction Head and Gated Fusion Modules:**
 - **Prediction Head Input:** The temporal feature ($h$), base prediction ($b_{val}$), masked observation ($d_{obs}^{use}$), and geometry-derived distance ($l_d$).
 - **Prediction Head Process:** These inputs are concatenated and passed through a three-layer MLP. This module outputs a residual correction term ($\Delta$) and a gating logit ($z_{gate}$). The residual term refines the frozen PoseMLP prediction. A sigmoid activation applied to $z_{gate}$ produces a gating signal ($g$). The refined base estimate ($d_{refined}$) is computed by adding $\Delta$ to $b_{val}$ and enforcing non-negativity.
 - **Gated Fusion Process:** The Gated Fusion module deterministically combines the residual-corrected prediction ($d_{refined}$) and the geometry-derived distance ($l_d$) using the gating signal $g$: $pred = (g \odot d_{refined} + (1 - g) \odot l_d) \odot m_{seq}$. This adaptive fusion balances learned corrections with geometry-consistent estimates, producing the final denoised distance predictions ($pred$).

**Anchor Reconstruction for Evaluation:**
To assess geometric consistency, anchor layouts ($\hat{a}_i$) are reconstructed from the final denoised distances ($pred$) and the known vehicle trajectory ($p_{seq}$). This is achieved by solving a weighted nonlinear least-squares problem, where LOS measurements are assigned a higher weight (1) than NLOS measurements (0.2). This step provides a geometry-level interpretation of the predicted distances.

**Training Strategy and Loss Functions:**
GAIA is trained using a multi-component loss function:

1. **Masked Distance Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{mask}$):** This is the primary supervision, calculated as a weighted masked Huber loss between the denoised prediction ($pred$) and the ground-truth distances ($d_{gt}^{seq}$). The weighting tensor incorporates the validity mask and optional NLOS mask, allowing for differential emphasis on LOS and NLOS samples.
2. **Layout Supervision Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{layout}$):** A mean squared error (MSE) loss between the predicted latent layout ($l$) and the ground-truth anchor positions ($A_{gt}$). This loss encourages the model to infer a geometrically consistent anchor configuration.
3. **Geometry Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{cons}$):** An MSE loss between the final predicted distances ($pred$) and the geometry-derived distances ($l_d$). This term encourages agreement between the learned denoising output and the distances implied by the inferred spatial structure.
4. **Anchor-level Geometry Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{anchor}$):** An optional term based on differentiable multilateration. Anchor positions are recovered from temporally subsampled predicted distances and vehicle trajectories. The recovered positions are then compared against the ground-truth anchor layout ($A_{gt}$) using an MSE term, combined with the residual of the geometric fitting process. This acts as a downstream geometric constraint.

The total objective function is a weighted sum of these four terms, where $\mathcal{L}_{mask}$ is the primary term and the others serve as auxiliary geometric regularization. The PoseMLP Base module remains frozen during this end-to-end optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GAIA involved experiments on a real-world outdoor UWB dataset and a supplementary real-data-calibrated stress-test simulator.

#### 4.1. Main Results on the Real-World UWB Dataset

- **Overall Performance:** GAIA achieved the lowest overall range Mean Squared Error (MSE) and the highest polygon Intersection-over-Union (IoU) among the evaluated methods (Kalman, MLP, PoseKalman, PoseMLP).
- **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - GAIA obtained an overall distance MSE of 0.1414, which represents an 18.4% reduction compared to PoseMLP (0.1733), the strongest learning-based baseline.
 - GAIA achieved a polygon IoU of 0.2390, a 15.5% increase over PoseMLP (0.2069).
 - It also exhibited the lowest MSE for both Line-of-Sight (LOS) (0.1098) and Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) (0.1529) conditions, suggesting improved range denoising across different propagation regimes.
- **Geometric Metrics:** While PoseMLP recorded slightly lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for anchor position (2.1703 vs. GAIA's 2.2493) and Hausdorff distance (3.2905 vs. GAIA's 3.7241), GAIA's superior polygon IoU indicates improved overall boundary agreement and a better reconstructed work-zone shape.

#### 4.2. Per-Episode Distribution Analysis on Real UWB Data

- Empirical Cumulative Distribution Functions (CDFs) for polygon IoU showed GAIA consistently outperforming baselines across most of the range, suggesting that its IoU advantage is not attributable to a few high-performing episodes.
- Box plots indicated that GAIA exhibited a higher median IoU and a more concentrated spread across episodes, supporting the consistency of its boundary overlap improvement.

#### 4.3. Supplementary Stress-Test Simulation Results

- **Robustness under Severe Conditions:** In the calibrated stress-test simulator, designed to expose models to stronger NLOS corruption, long-tail errors, and diverse geometry variations, GAIA consistently demonstrated superior performance across all metrics.
- **Quantitative Improvements in Simulation:**
 - GAIA's overall distance MSE was 0.3821, a 50.8% reduction compared to PoseMLP (0.7773).
 - The MAE anchor position was reduced to 19.7536 from PoseMLP's 25.6580.
 - Polygon IoU increased from 0.3531 (PoseMLP) to 0.4286.
- These results suggest that the geometry-aware modeling in GAIA is particularly effective under challenging ranging conditions.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Stressors (Zero-Shot)

- A zero-shot robustness study, where various stressors (additive range noise, outlier rate, NLOS bias amplification, number of anchors) were applied to observations without retraining, showed GAIA maintaining the highest mean IoU across the full range of each stressor.
- GAIA degraded gradually under increasing stress. For example, IoU fell from 0.429 (base) to 0.376 with 4m of added range noise.
- It retained a positive mean IoU margin over the best baseline in all cases, although this margin significantly narrowed when only four anchors were available, a condition where geometric constraints become severely sparse for all methods.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Impact of Geometric Components:** Removing the Layout Head module or the GeoDist module resulted in degraded polygon IoU and Hausdorff distance, confirming the importance of explicit latent layout estimation and geometry-consistent distance modeling for coherent work-zone structure.
- **PoseMLP Base Contribution:** The variant where the PoseMLP Base was unfrozen and jointly trained achieved a higher polygon IoU (0.3799) than other ablations, but still performed below the full GAIA model. This indicates that the geometry-aware components (Layout Head and GeoDist) are primary drivers of geometric reconstruction improvement, with the PoseMLP Base serving mainly as a stable initialization.

#### 4.6. Simulator Statistical Check

- A statistical comparison confirmed that the calibrated simulator's ranging-error distribution, including mean, p95, p99, outlier rate, and burst characteristics, more closely matched the real outdoor UWB dataset compared to a naive simulator. This validated its use as a supplementary stress-test environment.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Analysis

- Visualizations of reconstructed anchor layouts demonstrated that GAIA produced layouts closely aligned with the ground-truth work-zone geometry, exhibiting high spatial consistency across anchors.
- In contrast, baseline methods frequently yielded distorted layouts with significant deviations from true anchor positions, resulting in poor geometric alignment and structural errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GAIA framework addresses a fundamental challenge in intelligent transportation systems by focusing on the accurate, real-time perception of dynamic work-zone geometry. This work offers several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Work-Zone Safety and Operations:** By providing more accurate and geometrically consistent work-zone boundary reconstructions, GAIA can contribute to improved safety for both autonomous vehicles and human drivers. This facilitates compliant routing, effective hazard buffering, and safer passage through dynamic construction areas, potentially reducing accidents and fatalities.

2. **Advancement in UWB Sensing for V2I Applications:** The research demonstrates a methodological shift in UWB range denoising, moving beyond signal-level accuracy to prioritize downstream geometric consistency. This geometry-aware approach leverages the structured information from multi-anchor UWB deployments more effectively, unlocking new potentials for UWB in V2I infrastructure sensing and spatial awareness.

3. **Cost-Effective Infrastructure-Aided Perception:** UWB sensing offers a low-cost alternative to more expensive technologies like LiDAR for continuous geometric mapping. GAIA's ability to extract accurate and coherent work-zone geometry from UWB data enhances the feasibility of deploying infrastructure-aided perception systems for dynamic road environments at scale. This could lead to more frequent and less costly updates of HD maps in such volatile areas.

4. **Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The evaluation on both real-world outdoor UWB data and a real-data-calibrated stress-test simulator indicates that GAIA maintains performance and robustness under complex propagation conditions, including severe NLOS, burst noise, and long-tail errors. This makes the approach suitable for real-world deployment where environmental variability is common.

5. **Foundational Framework for Geometric Learning:** The explicit modeling of latent anchor layouts and the integration of geometry-consistent distance projection provide a template for designing other perception systems where raw sensor data is noisy but underlies a known geometric structure. The framework's ability to balance learned corrections with deterministic geometric constraints offers a generalizable paradigm.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The study highlights the importance of geometry-aware objectives and comprehensive evaluation strategies. It also implicitly suggests directions for future work, such as exploring causal temporal modules for strictly real-time applications and analyzing GAIA's performance with noisier vehicle pose inputs from other onboard positioning modules.

In summary, GAIA represents a contribution to infrastructure-aided work-zone reconstruction by coupling UWB range correction with a direct geometric objective. Its demonstrated ability to produce more spatially coherent work-zone boundaries from noisy UWB measurements has implications for enhancing the safety and operational efficiency of intelligent transportation systems.
