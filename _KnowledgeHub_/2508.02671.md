# Raw Data Matters: Enhancing Prompt Tuning by Internal Augmentation on Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> AugPT enhances Vision-Language Model prompt tuning by leveraging internal data augmentation and a self-corrective filtering mechanism. The method improves base-to-new generalization, achieving an average harmonic mean of 83.97% across 11 datasets, and demonstrates robust cross-dataset transfer while reducing reliance on external data or knowledge.

## Problem

- Prompt tuning performance is highly sensitive to the limited availability of labeled image-text pairs in downstream tasks, especially in few-shot settings.
- Existing data amplification strategies for prompt tuning rely on external knowledge or additional unlabeled data, incurring high costs and limiting generality.
- Naive self-supervised augmentation techniques can introduce noisy samples, potentially compromising critical visual features and model performance.

## Method

- ==AugPT (Augmentation-driven Prompt Tuning)== introduces a self-contained distillation-based framework that enhances prompt tuning solely through internal augmentation of the existing raw dataset.
- An ==Adaptive Self-supervised Augmentation (ASA)== module with dynamic amplitude sampling generates diverse visual views from raw images, adapting to various data distributions.
- A ==Consensus-based Filtering Gate (CFG)== utilizes the pre-tuned teacher model's logit-level consistency to identify and filter out noisy augmented samples, ensuring data quality.

## Results

- AugPT achieved an average harmonic mean of **83.97%** across 11 recognition datasets, surpassing its PromptKD backbone (**83.41%**) and all other external knowledge-driven baselines in base-to-new generalization.
- The method demonstrated enhanced performance in few-shot unlabeled image settings, showing more pronounced gains on data-scarce datasets like EuroSAT.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Adaptive Self-supervised Augmentation (ASA) and Consensus-based Filtering Gate (CFG) modules positively contribute to the overall performance, validating their necessity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality data amplification for prompt tuning can be achieved internally from the raw dataset, eliminating the need for external knowledge or additional unlabeled images.
> - The logit-level consistency of pre-tuned prompt tuning models provides an effective self-corrective mechanism to filter noisy augmented data and ensure semantic alignment.
> - Dynamic amplitude sampling in data augmentation improves adaptability across diverse datasets and generalizes better than fixed-amplitude strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exemplified by models like CLIP (Radford et al. 2021). VLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in cross-modal understanding but are computationally expensive to fine-tune entirely for downstream tasks. To address this, **parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)** techniques, particularly **prompt tuning**, have emerged as a dominant paradigm. Prompt tuning involves freezing the VLM's original parameters and introducing a small set of learnable "prompt" vectors that guide the pre-trained model towards specific task distributions.

A significant challenge in prompt tuning, especially in real-world scenarios, is the **scarcity of labeled image-text pairs** for target downstream tasks, often necessitating few-shot learning settings. To mitigate this data limitation, existing strategies typically resort to **data amplification**. These strategies predominantly fall into two categories:
1. **External Knowledge Integration:** This involves leveraging external resources like Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate additional text descriptions or attributes (e.g., HPT, ArGue, LLaMP), or utilizing pre-structured knowledge bases (e.g., KAPT). While effective, these methods incur **high costs** for data collection and processing, and their reliance on task-specific external knowledge can **limit generality and transferability**.
2. **Distillation-based Methods with External Unlabeled Data:** More recent approaches, like PromptKD (Li et al. 2024a) and CasPL (Wu et al. 2024), employ knowledge distillation. A teacher model (fine-tuned on few-shot labeled data) guides a student model using a *larger pool of unlabeled images*. However, these methods still depend on the **expansion of unlabeled image libraries**, which also entails extra data collection costs, and often do not fully exploit the latent features within the *already available* raw images.

"Raw Data Matters" directly addresses these limitations by introducing **AugPT (Augmentation-driven Prompt Tuning)**. Instead of seeking external knowledge or additional unlabeled data, AugPT proposes a **self-contained distillation-based approach** that enhances prompt tuning solely by **internal augmentation of the existing raw dataset**. This represents a critical shift in data-level optimization for prompt tuning, moving from external dependency to maximizing the utility of internal, readily available information. The work explicitly tackles the problem of noisy samples introduced by naive augmentation techniques through a novel filtering mechanism, further refining the quality of internally generated data. By focusing on efficient and self-sufficient data exploitation, AugPT positions itself as a more practical, cost-effective, and generalizable solution within the prompt tuning landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of AugPT are clearly articulated in the paper:

**Motivation:**
1. **Data Sensitivity of Prompt Tuning:** The authors empirically demonstrate that prompt tuning performance is highly sensitive to the scale of training data (Fig. 1a). Intuitively, more relevant data leads to better fine-tuning and generalization.
2. **Limitations of Existing Data Amplification:** Current data amplification strategies for prompt tuning are hindered by their reliance on:
 - **External Knowledge:** Large Language Models or pre-structured knowledge bases demand significant costs for data collection and processing.
 - **External Unlabeled Data:** Even state-of-the-art distillation-based methods still require expanding unlabeled image libraries, incurring additional data collection overhead.
 - **Inadequate Feature Exploitation:** Existing methods generally overlook the deeper utilization of features present *within the raw image modality itself*.
3. **Noise in Self-supervised Augmentation:** Traditional self-supervised augmentation techniques, while simple, often lack error correction mechanisms, leading to the introduction of "noisy samples" that may discard critical visual features (Fig. 1b).
4. **Underutilized Model Property:** The authors identify a previously "overlooked property of pre-tuned prompt tuning models: logit-level consistency reflects semantic similarity across images." This insight forms the basis for a self-corrective mechanism.

1. **Develop a Self-Contained Distillation-Based Prompt Tuning Approach:** The core objective is to propose AugPT, a novel method that enhances prompt tuning by utilizing *only internal augmentation* on the *raw dataset*, thereby eliminating the need for external knowledge or additional data.
2. **Better Exploit Known Features:** AugPT aims to more thoroughly exploit the latent features within the existing raw images by generating diverse and semantically consistent augmented views.
3. **Introduce Adaptive Self-supervised Augmentation:** To diversify feature representations from unlabeled images, AugPT seeks to develop an augmentation module that is adaptive to various data distributions and parameter-efficient.
4. **Incorporate a Novel Filtering Mechanism:** To ensure the quality of augmented data and prevent the introduction of noise, a "Consensus Test" based gating mechanism will be designed, leveraging the consistency property of pre-tuned prompt tuning models.
5. **Achieve Superior Performance and Generalization:** Ultimately, AugPT aims to simultaneously enhance model performance and generalization capabilities across various downstream tasks and datasets, setting new state-of-the-art (SOTA) benchmarks, particularly in low-data regimes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AugPT operates as a self-contained distillation-based prompt tuning framework, building upon an existing backbone model (specifically, PromptKD, a SOTA distillation-based approach). Its methodology is structured into three interdependent modules: Adaptive Self-supervised Augmentation (ASA), Consensus-based Filtering Gate (CFG), and Optimized Prompt Distillation (OPD).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of Prompt Tuning and Distillation

The paper first establishes the foundation by explaining standard CLIP-based prompt tuning. This involves freezing the VLM's encoders (image `f(·)` and text `g(·)`) and introducing learnable text prompts `P_t` and optional image prompts `P_v`. These prompts are optimized using a cross-entropy loss to align image and text embeddings.

It then outlines distillation-based prompt tuning, which forms AugPT's backbone. Here, a robust **teacher model** (e.g., PromptSRC with ViT-L/14 encoders) is first fine-tuned on few-shot labeled data. A **student model** (e.g., ViT-B/16 with learnable student image prompts `P_stu_v` and a projection layer `Proj(·)`) is then trained using unlabeled images `I'`. The student learns by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the teacher's predicted logits for `I'` and its own predicted logits, encouraging the student's distribution to align with the teacher's. Crucially, existing distillation methods largely rely on *additional* unlabeled images beyond the initial labeled few-shot set.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Self-supervised Augmentation (ASA)

This module addresses the need for richer feature representations from existing raw unlabeled images `I'`.
- **Internal Data Augmentation:** Instead of external data, AugPT performs internal data augmentation to generate diverse views of each raw image, aiming to cover more visual details.
- **Parameter-Free Strategy:** To maintain efficiency and avoid additional learnable layers, AugPT adopts a parameter-free, self-supervised augmentation strategy.
- **Modified RandAugment:** Building on RandAugment (Cubuk et al. 2020), for each raw image `I'`, AugPT performs `N` augmentations. Each augmentation involves `S` steps, where in each step `s`, an operation `γ^(s)` is sampled uniformly from a pre-defined set `Y` of 16 common transformation policies (e.g., Rotate, ShearX, Color, etc.).
- **Dynamic Amplitude Sampling:** A key innovation is the **dynamic sampling of augmentation amplitude `A^(s)`** from a uniform distribution `U(0, A_max)`. Unlike the original RandAugment which uses a pre-searched, fixed amplitude, this dynamic approach allows the augmentation operations to better adapt to the disparate feature distributions of various datasets, improving generalization.
- **Augmented Set:** This process generates a set `D(I')` containing the original raw image `I'` and its `N` augmented variants.

#### 3.3. Consensus-based Filtering Gate (CFG)

This module addresses the potential for noisy samples introduced by self-supervised augmentation.
- **Leveraging Logit Consistency:** AugPT observes that for pre-tuned prompt tuning models, logit-level consistency (i.e., multiple views yielding the same top-1 predicted category) reflects semantic similarity. This is explained by the deterministic mapping of the frozen teacher model from input image to embedding space, where consistent top-1 predictions imply embeddings within the same decision boundary region.
- **Online Inference with Teacher Model:** The previously fine-tuned teacher model (with `P_tea_v` and `P_tea_t`) is reused to perform online inference on all members `I'_j` in `D(I')`, producing similarity logits `ℓ_j` for each image against all `c` candidate classes.
- **Top-1 Consensus Test:**
 1. For each `I'_j`, the top-1 predicted category index `θ_j` is determined (argmax of `ℓ_j`).
 2. A sequence `u` of these top-1 predictions is formed.
 3. The **consensus category `θ*`** is identified as the category with the highest frequency count in `u`.
- **Filtering Mechanism:** Only images `I'_j` from `D(I')` whose top-1 predicted category `θ_j` aligns with the consensus `θ*` are accepted into a refined subset `I_acc`. Crucially, even the *raw image `I'` itself* is subjected to this filter, allowing AugPT to discard potentially noisy raw samples or those where augmentation diverged significantly. This acts as a self-corrective mechanism.

#### 3.4. Optimized Prompt Distillation (OPD)

This final module integrates the filtered augmented data into the distillation process.
- **Teacher Logit Sampling:** For each accepted image `ˆI_p` in `I_acc`, the corresponding teacher logits `ˆℓ_tea_p` (which have `θ_p = θ*`) are sampled.
- **Student Model Optimization:** A student model (identical to the backbone, with CLIP-ViT-B/16 encoder and learnable image prompts `P_stu_v`) is then fine-tuned.
- **KL Divergence:** The student's learnable prompts `P_stu_v` and the MLP-based projection layer `Proj(·)` are optimized by minimizing the KL divergence between the teacher logits `ˆℓ_tea_p` and the student logits `ˆℓ_stu_p` (obtained by `sim[g_tea(P_tea_t), Proj(f_stu(P_stu_v, ˆI_p))]`). This process aligns the student's visual features, extracted from the high-quality augmented views, with the teacher's text embeddings.

In summary, AugPT cleverly reuses the capabilities of a pre-tuned teacher model for two purposes: to effectively filter noisy augmented samples and to guide the student model's learning, all while relying exclusively on internal augmentation of existing raw data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive experiments across 11 diverse datasets to validate AugPT's effectiveness in downstream tasks, particularly focusing on base-to-new generalization and cross-dataset transfer.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** 11 recognition datasets including ImageNet, Caltech101, OxfordPets, StanfordCars, Flowers102, Food101, FGVCAircraft, SUN397, DTD, EuroSAT, and UCF101. These datasets represent a wide range of domain distributions.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set of 7 representative data-driven prompt tuning models, including:
 - Foundational methods: CoOp, PromptSRC.
 - External knowledge-based methods: KAPT (structured knowledge), HPT (LLM-generated descriptive texts), ArGue (LLM-generated attributes), LLaMP (LLM-guided fine-tuning).
 - State-of-the-art distillation-based method (AugPT's backbone): PromptKD (Li et al. 2024a).
 - Additional baselines like zero-shot CLIP, CoCoOp, KgCoOp, MaPLe, TCP, MMRL, MAO, CasPL, and KDPL are included in the supplementary materials.
- **Metrics:** Base-class accuracy, New-class generalization accuracy, and their Harmonic Mean (H).
- **Teacher/Student Models:** PromptSRC with ViT-L/14 encoders for the teacher, and ViT-B/16 for the student, consistent with PromptKD.
- **Ablation Studies:** Conducted to evaluate the contribution of individual AugPT components (ASA, CFG), the impact of different augmentation strategies, and the influence of hyperparameters (number of augmented images N, step size S, filtering gate operator, projection layer).

#### 4.2. Key Findings

1. **Superior Base-to-New Generalization (Full Dataset):** (Table 1)
 - AugPT consistently *surpasses all external knowledge-driven baselines* across all 11 datasets in both base-class accuracy and new-class generalization.
 - Crucially, AugPT *outperforms its SOTA distillation-based backbone, PromptKD*, in average harmonic mean (H) (83.97% vs. 83.41%), base accuracy (87.31% vs. 86.91%), and new accuracy (80.87% vs. 80.17%). This demonstrates that simply exploiting *internal raw data* can yield better results than methods relying on external information or basic distillation with unlabeled data.

2. **Enhanced Performance in Few-shot Unlabeled Image Settings:** (Table 2)
 - In more challenging scenarios with limited unlabeled images (8-shot and 16-shot per class), AugPT still significantly outperforms PromptKD in harmonic mean performance.
 - The performance gains are *more pronounced* on datasets with extremely limited images (e.g., EuroSAT), highlighting AugPT's practical value in data-scarce environments. This suggests that AugPT's strategy of comprehensively exploiting internal features is especially effective when data is scarce.

3. **Robust Cross-Dataset Transfer:** (Table 3)
 - When fine-tuned on ImageNet (source) and transferred to 10 other datasets (target) in an unsupervised setting, AugPT achieves performance no lower than PromptKD across most datasets. This demonstrates strong robustness and reliable generalization in fully unsupervised target domains, suggesting that internal augmentation does not amplify biases from the teacher model, even with distributional shifts.

4. **Effectiveness of Proposed Components (Ablation Studies):**
 - **ASA and CFG Contributions:** (Table 4) Both the Adaptive Self-supervised Augmentation (ASA) and the Consensus-based Filtering Gate (CFG) contribute positively to performance. ASA provides a significant initial boost, and CFG further refines results by effectively filtering noisy samples, validating the necessity and efficacy of both modules.
 - **Augmentation Strategy:** (Table 5) AugPT's dynamic amplitude RandAugment (ASA) consistently outperforms fixed-amplitude RandAugment, simple Random Resized Crop, and Fixmatch, indicating its superior generalization across diverse data distributions.
 - **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** (Fig. 3a, 3b) Optimal performance is achieved at `N=5` augmented views and `S=2` augmentation steps. Performance declines with excessive augmentation, likely due to redundant computations or noise introduction.
 - **Consensus Test Stringency:** (Fig. 3c) The Top-1 Consensus Test proves optimal. Stricter Top-K consensus leads to performance degradation, suggesting that over-filtering too many augmented views compromises the completeness of input visual features.
 - **Projection Layer:** (Table 13) Consistent with the PromptKD backbone, a 2-layer MLP for the projection layer yields the best results, balancing underfitting and overfitting.

5. **Adaptability and Computational Efficiency:** (Tables 14, 15 & Fig. 6)
 - **Adaptability:** AugPT's strategy shows adaptability by improving the performance of UPL (another distillation-based prompt tuning method), suggesting it can be a plug-and-play enhancement for similar frameworks.
 - **Computational Cost:** While AugPT incurs a moderate increase in GPU memory usage and tuning FPS compared to PromptKD (due to parallel processing of augmented views), it introduces no additional learnable parameters. Its fine-tuning FPS is significantly faster than LLM-guided models, and inference speed remains comparable to its backbone (PromptKD), demonstrating its parameter efficiency and practical viability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AugPT makes several significant contributions to the field of Vision-Language Models and parameter-efficient fine-tuning:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Data Amplification:** The most notable significance lies in demonstrating that high-quality data amplification for prompt tuning can be achieved *internally*, solely from the raw dataset, without recourse to external knowledge bases, LLMs, or additional unlabeled data. This fundamentally shifts the resource requirements for enhancing VLM adaptation.
2. **Cost-Effectiveness and Generality:** By eliminating the need for external data collection and processing, AugPT offers a highly cost-effective solution. Its independence from task-specific external knowledge also enhances its generality and transferability across diverse domains, making it a more practical choice for real-world applications.
3. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** AugPT consistently achieves new state-of-the-art results in base-to-new generalization and demonstrates strong performance in cross-dataset transfer tasks. Its particular effectiveness in few-shot, data-scarce regimes addresses a critical challenge in VLM deployment, enabling robust adaptation even with limited resources.
4. **Innovative Self-Correction Mechanism:** The Consensus-based Filtering Gate is a novel contribution that allows the model to autonomously identify and filter out noisy augmented samples. This self-corrective mechanism, leveraging an overlooked property of pre-tuned models, is an elegant solution to a common problem in data augmentation without requiring external supervision.
5. **Parameter Efficiency:** AugPT introduces no additional learnable parameters, maintaining the lightweight nature desirable in PEFT methods. While there is a moderate increase in computational cost during fine-tuning due to parallel processing of augmented views, the inference speed remains competitive with, or superior to, existing advanced prompt tuning methods, especially those reliant on LLMs.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated VLM Adoption:** AugPT can significantly lower the barrier to entry for adapting powerful VLMs to niche or proprietary downstream tasks where large datasets or external knowledge are unavailable or prohibitively expensive to acquire.
- **Improved Efficiency in Research and Development:** Researchers and practitioners can achieve better VLM performance with existing data, reducing the need for costly data acquisition campaigns and accelerating development cycles.
- **Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** The demonstrated effectiveness in few-shot settings and robust cross-dataset generalization makes AugPT highly valuable for real-world applications where data distributions can be diverse and labeled data scarce.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
1. **Coupling with Distillation-Based Prompt Tuning:** AugPT's current design is tightly integrated with distillation-based prompt tuning, limiting its immediate adaptability to other prompt tuning paradigms.
2. **Focus on Recognition Tasks:** The current work primarily focuses on improving performance for recognition-related tasks, leaving its applicability to other types of tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation, object detection) unexplored.
3. **Moderate Computational Cost:** The parallel processing of augmented views introduces a moderate increase in computational cost during fine-tuning, which could be a concern for highly resource-constrained environments.

Future work includes extending AugPT to support a broader range of backbone models and downstream tasks, and considering improvements to its computational efficiency. Overall, "Raw Data Matters" presents a highly impactful and practical approach to enhancing VLM adaptation, emphasizing the untapped potential within existing raw data.
