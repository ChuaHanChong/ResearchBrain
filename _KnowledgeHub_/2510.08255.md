# Opponent Shaping in LLM Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> This research investigates opponent shaping in LLM agents, proposing ShapeLLM, a model-free opponent shaping algorithm adapted for transformer architectures. The study demonstrates that LLM agents can strategically influence co-players' learning dynamics in both competitive and cooperative game-theoretic environments, for example, achieving an average reward of 3.96 in the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma compared to 0.10 for the shaped opponent, while baseline naive agents averaged 1 each.

## Problem

- Understanding the strategic behavior of Large Language Model (LLM) agents in multi-agent systems is essential for their effective and safe deployment.
- Existing multi-agent LLM research often treats LLM agents as static entities, overlooking dynamic strategic interactions where agents adapt to one another.
- Traditional opponent shaping (OS) algorithms from Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) are not directly applicable to transformer-based LLMs due to architectural differences and requirements for higher-order derivatives or specific memory structures.

## Method

- Introduces ShapeLLM, a novel model-free ==opponent shaping== algorithm designed for transformer architectures that integrates both intra-episode history and inter-episode context into structured natural language prompts.
- LLM agents, based on `gemma-2-2b-it`, are fine-tuned using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in repeated 2x2 matrix game environments.
- The shaper agent's policy parameters are updated less frequently (only after completing an entire trial, spanning multiple episodes of opponent learning) to account for and influence the opponent's learning dynamics.

## Results

- In competitive games like the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, shapers successfully exploited naive opponents, achieving an average reward of 3.96 compared to the opponent's **0.10** (baseline was 1 for each naive agent).
- In cooperative settings such as the Cooperative Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma and Iterated Stag Hunt, shapers consistently guided interactions towards mutual cooperation and Pareto-optimal equilibria, leading to improved collective welfare.
- ShapeLLM demonstrated robustness against opponents initialized with varying propensities for specific actions and to different prompt formulations, with ablation studies confirming that observing opponent learning dynamics across episodes is key to shaping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM agents, when augmented with ShapeLLM, possess the capability to actively influence the learning trajectories and behaviors of other LLM agents solely through interaction.
> - Opponent shaping in LLMs can be leveraged for both exploiting naive opponents in competitive scenarios and fostering coordination and prosocial behavior in cooperative games.
> - Encoding opponent learning dynamics across episodes as contextual information in natural language prompts is crucial for effective shaping, distinguishing it from simply observing within-episode history.
