# Reasoning by Superposition: A Theoretical Perspective on Chain of Continuous Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, UCSD, and Meta AI theoretically demonstrate that Chain-of-Continuous-Thought allows Large Language Models to solve directed graph reachability in significantly fewer steps (D steps vs. O(n^2) for discrete CoT) by forming "superposition states" that encode multiple search frontiers simultaneously. This efficiency and the emergent parallel search behavior were confirmed empirically with a lightweight transformer achieving near-perfect accuracy.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) with traditional discrete Chain-of-Thought (CoT) exhibit limitations in scalability and efficiency for complex reasoning tasks, often requiring numerous sequential steps.
- Solving fundamental graph reasoning problems, such as directed graph reachability, using constant-depth transformers with discrete CoT is known to require O(n^2) decoding steps.
- A formal theoretical understanding of the mechanisms behind the observed empirical performance gains of continuous Chain-of-Thought (COCONUT) and its ability to store "superposition states" was lacking.

## Method

- The authors constructed a rigorous ==proof-by-construction==, designing a minimal ==two-layer autoregressive transformer== capable of solving directed graph reachability using ==continuous thought vectors==.
- A key element of the construction involves an =="attention chooser" mechanism== enabling attention heads to selectively process specific relative positions, facilitating flexible, length-independent information flow.
- Experimental validation was conducted by training a **2-layer** GPT-2 style decoder on graph reasoning tasks from the ==ProsQA dataset== and empirically analyzing its internal ==attention patterns== and ==continuous thought representations==.

## Results

- A **2-layer** transformer with D steps of continuous CoT (where D is graph diameter) was theoretically proven to solve directed graph reachability, offering a drastic improvement over the **O(n^2)** steps required for discrete CoT.
- Empirical evaluations demonstrated that a lightweight **2-layer** COCONUT model achieved near-perfect accuracy on the ProsQA graph reachability task, outperforming both **2-layer** and larger **12-layer** discrete CoT models.
- Analysis of attention maps and latent space similarity confirmed that continuous thought vectors effectively encode reachable, frontier, and optimal nodes in superposition, aligning with theoretical predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous thought vectors within transformers can function as "superposition states," simultaneously encoding multiple candidate search frontiers, enabling parallel exploration in reasoning tasks.
> - This parallel processing mechanism allows for a substantial reduction in the number of reasoning steps needed for tasks like graph reachability, significantly improving efficiency over discrete CoT.
> - The superpositional representations and parallel search strategy can emerge automatically during gradient-based training, even with intermediate supervision focused solely on optimal paths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and evolving area in artificial intelligence: understanding and enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). LLMs have achieved remarkable success across various applications, notably in complex reasoning tasks when guided by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting techniques (Wei et al., 2022). CoT involves generating intermediate "thinking tokens" before producing a final answer, transforming single-step inference into a multi-step reasoning process.

However, despite these advancements, LLMs still face significant hurdles in tasks requiring sophisticated reasoning and planning, especially as problem scales increase (Kambhampati, 2024; Valmeekam et al., 2024). A key limitation of traditional, *discrete* CoT is its sequential nature, where each thought token is explicitly sampled. Existing theoretical works have shown that while discrete CoT boosts LLM capabilities, it often requires a large number of steps or specific architectural configurations. For instance, solving directed graph reachability with constant-depth transformers and discrete CoT is known to require O(n^2) decoding steps, where 'n' is the number of vertices (Merrill and Sabharwal, 2023a).

This paper builds directly upon recent empirical work on *continuous* Chain-of-Thought (COCONUT) (Hao et al., 2024), which demonstrated that using continuous latent thoughts for reasoning can yield significant performance improvements on tasks like directed graph reachability and mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K). Critically, COCONUT empirically suggested that continuous latent thoughts might simultaneously store multiple candidate search frontiers – a phenomenon reminiscent of "superposition states."

The present work fills a crucial theoretical gap by providing a formal understanding of *why* continuous CoT achieves superior performance. While previous theoretical studies on transformer expressivity (e.g., Yun et al., 2019; Bhattamishra et al., 2020a,b; Pérez et al., 2021) and the benefits of discrete CoT (e.g., Liu et al., 2022; Feng et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024) exist, they largely focus on discrete tokens or require problem-specific positional encodings. Theoretical studies specifically on *continuous* CoT have been rare, with a recent complementary work by Gozeten et al. (2025) exploring superposition in arithmetic domains, but potentially requiring exponentially large embedding dimensions.

By proving that a lightweight transformer architecture with continuous CoT can solve graph reachability with vastly fewer steps (D steps, where D is the graph diameter, compared to O(n^2) for discrete CoT) and by demonstrating the mechanism of "superposition states," this paper establishes a novel theoretical foundation for a more powerful paradigm of LLM reasoning. It directly challenges the limitations of sequential discrete reasoning and opens up new avenues for designing and training more efficient and capable LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **provide a rigorous theoretical understanding of the mechanism underlying Chain-of-Continuous-Thought (COCONUT)**, specifically explaining why it empirically outperforms its discrete counterparts in complex reasoning tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several more specific motivations:

1. **Demystify Continuous CoT:** The empirical success of COCONUT (Hao et al., 2024) hinted at novel reasoning mechanisms, particularly the ability to store multiple search frontiers simultaneously. The paper aims to formally prove and characterize this hypothesized "superposition state" behavior.
2. **Benchmark Against a Fundamental Problem:** To provide a clear and quantifiable theoretical comparison, the authors focus on the **directed graph reachability problem**. This problem is chosen because it is fundamental in computer science, abstractable to many real-world applications (e.g., knowledge graphs, planning), and has established theoretical benchmarks for transformer expressivity with discrete CoT.
3. **Demonstrate Efficiency Gains:** The research seeks to theoretically establish that continuous CoT can solve graph reachability with significantly greater efficiency (fewer reasoning steps) than the best-known results for constant-depth transformers utilizing discrete CoT. Specifically, to show that *D* continuous steps (where D is graph diameter) are sufficient, as opposed to *O(n^2)* discrete steps (where n is the number of vertices).
4. **Practicality and Generalizability:** Unlike some theoretical constructions that rely on highly specialized positional encodings, a key motivation is to show that the proposed mechanism works with **widely-used positional encodings** in practice, such as sinusoidal positional encoding and Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE).
5. **Alignment with Training Dynamics:** The authors aim to empirically verify that the theoretically constructed superpositional reasoning mechanism can indeed **automatically emerge during gradient-based training** without explicit supervision designed to guide the model towards parallel exploration.

In essence, the paper seeks to elevate continuous CoT from an empirically observed phenomenon to a theoretically grounded reasoning paradigm, elucidating its efficiency and unique parallel processing capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper combines rigorous theoretical proof-by-construction with extensive empirical validation.

**A. Theoretical Construction (Proof by Construction):**
The core of the theoretical approach is to construct a minimal yet powerful transformer architecture—a **two-layer autoregressive transformer**—that can solve the directed graph reachability problem using continuous thoughts.

1. **Problem Formulation:** The problem is defined as determining if a path exists from a given root node `r` to one of two candidate destination nodes (`c1`, `c2`) in a directed graph, guaranteeing exactly one is reachable. The input format specifies how graph edges, candidates, and the root are encoded as a sequence of tokens.
2. **Chain of Continuous Thought (COCONUT):** In contrast to discrete CoT, the transformer directly appends its output embedding from the previous step as the input embedding for the next continuous thought step, bypassing discrete token sampling.
3. **Building Blocks – Attention Chooser:** A crucial element of the construction is the "attention chooser" (Lemma 1/3). This mechanism allows an attention head to selectively attend to a specific relative position (e.g., `i-ℓ`) if the current token at position `i` is of a certain type (e.g., `<x>`), or to an "attention sink" (e.g., the first token) otherwise. This dynamic attention routing is essential for flexible, length-independent processing. The proof leverages properties of sinusoidal positional encodings (and later extended to RoPE).
4. **Two-Layer Transformer Mechanism (Inductive Proof):**
- **Layer 0 (Input Processing):** Initial input embeddings are enriched with positional encodings.
- **Layer 1 (Information Extraction and Buffering):** This layer utilizes multiple "attention chooser" heads. For each edge token `<e>`, specific heads are configured to attend to its preceding source node (`s_i`) and target node (`t_i`). The value matrices then copy the embeddings of these source and target nodes into designated "buffer spaces" within the edge token's embedding. This effectively associates each edge token with its constituent nodes. Similarly, the `<R>` (reasoning) token's buffer stores candidate nodes, and the `<A>` (answer) token interacts with the last continuous thought. The subsequent MLP layer acts as a filter, removing noise and normalizing the buffered information.
- **Layer 2 (Superposition State Update/Expansion):**
 - **Attention:** This layer's attention mechanism is designed to take the current continuous thought `[t_c]` (which is a superposition of reachable nodes `V_c`) and find all edge tokens whose source node `s_j` is present in this superposition. It then collects the corresponding target nodes `t_j` (stored in the edge token's buffer space). This effectively performs a breadth-first search (BFS) step in parallel.
 - **MLP:** The MLP then aggregates these collected target nodes with the existing reachable nodes from `V_c`, normalizes their weights, and filters out noise. The output `[t_{c+1}]` is then proven to be a normalized superposition of `V_{c+1}` (all nodes reachable in `c+1` steps).
5. **Final Prediction:** After `C` steps of continuous thought (where `C` is greater than or equal to the graph diameter `D`), the final continuous thought `[t_C]` contains a superposition of all reachable nodes. The answer token `<A>` queries this state, and a simple linear readout layer `W_O` can measure the presence of `c_1` vs. `c_2` to make the final prediction.

**B. Experimental Validation:**
To empirically verify the theoretical construction, the authors perform experiments on a subset of the ProsQA dataset (Hao et al., 2024), which features graph reasoning questions requiring 3-4 hops.

1. **Model and Training:** A 2-layer GPT-2 style decoder is trained from scratch using AdamW. A multi-stage training strategy is adopted, where the model learns to generate `i` continuous thoughts before predicting the `i`-th node in the given Chain-of-Thought solution.
2. **Baselines:** Performance is compared against discrete CoT (2-layer and a larger 12-layer model), and a "No CoT" baseline.
3. **Latent Reasoning Visualization:**
- **Attention Maps:** Visual inspection of Layer 1 attention patterns confirms that edge tokens indeed attend to and copy their source and target nodes. Layer 2 attention analysis quantifies attention scores to "Reachable," "Not Reachable," "Frontier," and "Optimal" edges, showing concentration on the expansion frontier.
- **Continuous Thought Representation:** Inner products between continuous thoughts `[t_i]` and individual node embeddings `u_v` are computed. This reveals whether `[t_i]` encodes reachable, frontier, and optimal nodes with higher similarity, thereby validating the superposition hypothesis.
4. **Exploration Priority Analysis:** An additional experiment with "COCONUT-BFS" supervision (where intermediate supervision tokens are uniformly drawn from frontier nodes, not just the optimal path) is conducted to investigate if the observed bias towards optimal paths is an artifact of the training curriculum.

This dual approach of theoretical rigor and empirical evidence strongly supports the paper's claims about the mechanism and effectiveness of continuous Chain-of-Thought.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a compelling set of theoretical and empirical findings that collectively illuminate the power of continuous Chain-of-Thought (COCONUT):

1. **Theoretical Efficiency Superiority:** The most significant theoretical finding is that a **two-layer transformer with *D* steps of continuous CoT can solve the directed graph reachability problem**, where *D* is the diameter of the graph. This is a dramatic improvement over the best-known result for constant-depth transformers with discrete CoT, which requires *O(n^2)* decoding steps (where *n* is the number of vertices). Since *D < n* (and often *D* is logarithmic or a small constant in practice), continuous CoT offers a substantially more efficient theoretical path for this fundamental reasoning task.
2. **Superposition as the Core Mechanism:** The theoretical construction rigorously proves that each continuous thought vector `[t_c]` acts as a **"superposition state," encoding multiple search frontiers simultaneously**. Specifically, `[t_c]` is shown to be a normalized superposition of all vertices `V_c` reachable from the root within `c` steps. This parallel encoding allows the model to perform implicit Breadth-First Search (BFS) in each step, overcoming the sequential sampling limitation of discrete tokens.
3. **Alignment with Practical Positional Encodings:** The theoretical construction is demonstrated to work with **widely-used positional encodings** such as sinusoidal and Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), increasing its practical relevance.
4. **Empirical Validation of Superposition:**
- **High Accuracy:** A lightweight 2-layer COCONUT model trained from scratch achieves **near-perfect accuracy** on the ProsQA graph reachability task. This significantly outperforms both a 2-layer and a much larger 12-layer discrete CoT model, confirming the efficiency gains predicted by the theory.
- **Attention Pattern Confirmation:** Empirical analysis of attention maps aligns with the theoretical design:
 - Layer 1 attention heads are observed to correctly identify and **copy source and target nodes into the buffer spaces** of the respective edge tokens.
 - Layer 2 attention shows a clear concentration on **"Reachable" and "Frontier" edges**, indicating that the continuous thought actively expands the set of currently reachable nodes in a BFS-like manner.
- **Latent Representation Insight:** The inner product analysis between continuous thoughts and node embeddings reveals that nodes within the current "reachable set" (especially "Frontier" and "Optimal" nodes) exhibit **markedly higher similarity scores** to the continuous thought vector. This empirically validates that the continuous thought indeed encodes multiple plausible search frontiers in superposition.
5. **Emergent Behavior of Parallel Search:** Remarkably, the superpositional representation and parallel search strategy are found to **automatically emerge during training**, even when the model is supervised only with optimal paths during intermediate stages. This suggests that the model intrinsically learns to explore multiple paths implicitly, rather than requiring explicit multi-path supervision. While optimal paths receive slightly higher attention, non-optimal frontier nodes are still prioritized over non-frontier nodes, indicating an implicit BFS.

These findings strongly support the hypothesis that continuous latent thoughts enable a fundamentally different, more parallel, and efficient form of reasoning in LLMs, largely due to their ability to maintain superposition states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper makes highly significant contributions to the theoretical understanding and practical development of Large Language Models, with several potential impacts:

1. **Foundational Understanding of LLM Reasoning:** The work provides a crucial theoretical bedrock for the empirical success of continuous Chain-of-Thought (COCONUT). By formalizing the concept of "superposition states" and demonstrating *how* a transformer can leverage them for parallel computation, it offers a deeper mechanistic understanding of advanced LLM reasoning capabilities. This moves beyond treating LLMs as black boxes for complex tasks.
2. **Establishing Expressivity Separation:** The proof of superior efficiency for continuous CoT (D steps vs. O(n^2) for discrete CoT in graph reachability) establishes a strong theoretical separation in the expressive power between continuous and discrete thought processes in transformers. This is a critical finding that could motivate a paradigm shift in how complex reasoning tasks are approached within LLMs.
3. **Guiding Architectural Design:** The detailed construction of a 2-layer transformer capable of solving graph reachability efficiently provides concrete architectural insights. It demonstrates that powerful reasoning does not necessarily require extremely deep models if the information flow and internal representations are designed optimally, potentially leading to more lightweight yet highly capable reasoning architectures.
4. **Unlocking Parallel Reasoning in LLMs:** The concept of LLMs maintaining "superposition states" that encode multiple possibilities simultaneously is analogous to quantum computation in its efficiency implications. This offers a novel way to conceptualize and implement parallel exploration and problem-solving within LLMs, which traditionally operate on sequential tokens. This could lead to breakthroughs in areas requiring extensive search, such as planning, theorem proving, and scientific discovery.
5. **Implications for LLM Training Paradigms:** The observation that superpositional representations automatically emerge during training, even with supervision focused solely on optimal paths, is profoundly impactful. It suggests that LLMs might inherently gravitate towards efficient, parallel search strategies when trained on reasoning tasks, potentially simplifying the curriculum design for complex reasoning. This finding warrants further investigation into the inductive biases of LLMs.
6. **Broader Applicability to Complex Reasoning:** Graph reachability is a fundamental problem underpinning many real-world challenges, from knowledge graph traversal to logistical planning. The principles elucidated here for graph reachability could be extended to other, more generalized complex reasoning problems, planning problems, and even multi-agent coordination, where maintaining and evaluating multiple possibilities concurrently is crucial.
7. **Stimulating Future Research:** The paper explicitly outlines exciting future directions, including deriving lower bounds for discrete CoT to formally prove a *strict* separation, further exploring the emergence of parallel search behavior from training dynamics, and extending the benefits of continuous reasoning to even broader settings. These open questions indicate a vibrant and promising research agenda ignited by this work.

In conclusion, "Reasoning by Superposition" provides a landmark theoretical explanation for the enhanced reasoning capabilities observed with continuous Chain-of-Thought. Its findings not only advance our fundamental understanding of LLMs but also offer concrete pathways for developing more efficient, powerful, and generalizable AI systems for complex problem-solving.
