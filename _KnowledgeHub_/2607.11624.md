# SKooP: Symmetric Koopman Predictions for Faster and More Generalizable Legged Robot Locomotion with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SKooP presents a framework for legged robot locomotion, integrating controlled Koopman embedding with morphological symmetry priors within a reinforcement learning architecture. This approach enables faster policy convergence and improves generalization to unseen tasks, demonstrating an 8.49% Symmetry Index and higher overall success rates on bipedal tasks compared to non-symmetric baselines, while also yielding more stable and symmetric gaits.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning for complex legged robots suffers from low sample efficiency and limited generalization due to nonlinear dynamics and high dimensionality.
- Current model-free RL often converges to suboptimal policies, while traditional model-based control requires significant expert knowledge and manual tuning.
- Prior Koopman-RL applications were typically limited to low-dimensional systems and lacked direct integration of Koopman predictions into online policy learning for complex robots.

## Method

- **SKooP** combines ==morphological symmetries== and a controlled Koopman autoencoder (**ecDAE**) within an online ==actor-critic== RL framework, training the Koopman model concurrently with the policy.
- The **ecDAE** learns a linear representation of system dynamics in a ==lifted latent space==, using ==multi-step predictions== over a horizon H.
- Koopman-predicted latent states are supplied as ==privileged observations== to the critic network, while actor, critic, encoder, and decoder networks are constrained with ==G-equivariant/invariant== properties to leverage symmetries.

## Results

- SKooP achieved an **8.49% Symmetry Index** and higher overall success rates (**82.77%** on trained, **77.70%** on mirrored task) on the "push door" task, outperforming symmetric baselines and exhibiting lower variance across seeds.
- Symmetry-constrained methods, including SKooP, showed steeper initial convergence rates and higher mean episode returns, with SKooP demonstrating the fastest convergence on the challenging "push door" task.
- The learned symmetric Koopman model (ecDAE) consistently achieved lower state prediction errors, maintaining almost identical accuracy on both trained and mirrored tasks, whereas non-symmetric models performed approximately **50% worse** on mirrored tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating both morphological symmetry priors and Koopman dynamics predictions synergistically within an online RL framework leads to more efficient and generalizable learning for high-dimensional robots.
> - Providing the critic with Koopman-predicted latent states offers smoother and more informative features, simplifying value function learning and resulting in higher-quality policies.
> - Online, concurrent training of the Koopman model with the RL policy ensures the learned dynamics representation is continuously refined and relevant to the agent's evolving behavior, avoiding pre-training mismatches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown promise in robot control, particularly with advancements in simulation fidelity. However, for complex systems like legged robots with nonlinear dynamics, learning effective policies remains challenging. Model-free RL methods typically suffer from low sample efficiency, limited generalization, and convergence to suboptimal policies due to their reliance on extensive trial and error. Conversely, traditional model-based control methods exploit known physics but often demand significant expert knowledge and manual tuning.

A growing area of research focuses on integrating physics priors into RL to combine the strengths of both approaches. This paper specifically targets two such priors: symmetry information and linearized dynamics based on Koopman theory.

Symmetry priors have been incorporated into deep learning and robot learning, primarily through data augmentation or by enforcing equivariant constraints on neural network architectures. These methods have demonstrated improvements in sample efficiency and policy generalizability, though sometimes at the cost of performance.

Koopman theory posits that any nonlinear system’s dynamics can be represented linearly in an infinite-dimensional function space. Data-driven approximations of the Koopman operator, often via autoencoders, have been used to characterize system dynamics or for optimal control through methods like Model Predictive Control (MPC). More recently, Koopman theory has been explored in RL to facilitate optimal control learning or to provide linear mappings for value functions. However, previous applications of Koopman-RL often focused on well-defined, low-dimensional benchmark systems, with limited validation on high-dimensional robots with complex dynamics. Furthermore, many robotic control approaches leveraging Koopman operators have not directly injected Koopman predictions into the policy learning process itself.

Some prior work has explored the intersection of Koopman theory and symmetries, such as using Koopman linearization to enforce symmetries in offline RL or employing equivariant Koopman autoencoders for dynamics analysis. However, these efforts have not typically extended to the control of complex robots or the direct enhancement of online RL policy learning.

This work, SKooP, aims to bridge these gaps by developing a unified framework that combines both controlled Koopman embedding and morphological symmetry priors. Its objective is to enable faster initial policy convergence, improve learned behavior quality, and enhance generalization specifically for high-dimensional legged robots, by integrating Koopman predictions as privileged information within an actor-critic RL architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of current reinforcement learning approaches when applied to complex, high-dimensional systems such as legged robots. These limitations include poor sample efficiency, a lack of generalization to novel scenarios, and the tendency to converge to suboptimal control policies. The paper posits that incorporating domain-specific physics priors can mitigate these issues.

The key motivations are twofold:
1. **Exploiting Morphological Symmetries:** Legged robots inherently possess symmetries (e.g., C2 symmetry over the sagittal plane). Leveraging these symmetries during the learning process can reduce the amount of data required for training and inherently lead to more robust and generalizable policies. By guiding the learning algorithm with these fundamental structural properties, the search space for optimal policies can be constrained effectively.
2. **Utilizing Koopman Theory for Predictive Dynamics:** Koopman theory provides a mechanism to transform complex nonlinear system dynamics into a simpler, globally linear representation within a lifted function space. This linear representation offers predictive capabilities that can be highly beneficial for an RL agent, which often struggles with predicting future states and values in complex environments.

The combination of these two priors is motivated by the observation that Koopman theory can facilitate the identification and exploitation of symmetries within the system dynamics. Integrating them synergistically is hypothesized to yield a more powerful and efficient learning framework than using either prior in isolation.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the SKooP approach are:
- To **develop a unified learning approach** that concurrently combines the advantages of a controlled Koopman embedding and morphological symmetry priors within an online RL framework.
- To **learn a Koopman model of the system dynamics alongside the policy**. This online, concurrent training strategy ensures that the learned lifting function (autoencoder) is continuously refined on a state space relevant to the agent's evolving behavior, avoiding potential mismatches from pre-trained models.
- To **utilize Koopman predictions as privileged observations for the critic network**. By feeding the critic with information about future latent states, the goal is to provide smoother, more informative features, thereby simplifying the critic's task of learning a precise value function and enabling the learning of higher-quality motions.
- To **incorporate group symmetries into the actor, critic, encoder, and decoder networks**. This architectural constraint aims to produce a highly equivariant policy that is inherently more sample-efficient and generalizable to mirrored or novel symmetric tasks.
- To **validate the approach through in-depth analysis on high-dimensional legged robots** performing challenging bipedal locomotion tasks, moving beyond the low-dimensional benchmarks commonly used in prior Koopman-RL research.
- To **conduct detailed ablation studies** to quantify the specific contributions of symmetry constraints and Koopman predictions to the agent's performance, convergence characteristics, and learned behaviors.
- To **demonstrate the transferability of learned policies** to different simulation environments (sim-to-sim transfer) and to unseen, mirrored tasks, thereby showcasing the robustness and generalization capabilities of SKooP.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SKooP (Symmetric Koopman Predictions) approach integrates morphological symmetries and a controlled Koopman autoencoder within an actor-critic reinforcement learning framework. The system operates online, training a Koopman dynamics model concurrently with the RL agent.

**A. Equivariant Networks**
SKooP imposes symmetry priors on its neural networks. Following the properties of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), the actor network is designed to be G-equivariant, meaning that applying a group transformation ($g \triangleright x$) to the state input results in the corresponding group transformation ($g \triangleright \pi(x)$) of the action output. Conversely, the critic network is G-invariant, ensuring its output remains unchanged under group transformations of the state ($V_\xi(g \triangleright x) = V_\xi(x)$).
These symmetry constraints are extended to the Koopman autoencoder: both the encoder ($f_\phi$) and decoder ($f^{-1}_\phi$) are constrained to be G-equivariant. This ensures that the learned latent space respects the system's symmetries. These constraints are implemented using Equivariant Multilayer Perceptrons (EMLPs), which lead to a block-diagonal structure for the Koopman linear dynamics matrices (A and B) by decomposing the function space into isotypic subspaces.

**B. Koopman Autoencoder with Symmetries (ecDAE)**
A key component is the Equivariant Controlled Dynamics Auto-Encoder (ecDAE), which learns a lifting function ($f_\phi$) to map the robot's state ($x_k$) to a higher-dimensional latent Koopman state ($z_k$) where the dynamics are approximately linear: $z_{k+1} = Az_k + Bu_k$. The model also learns a reconstruction function ($f^{-1}_\phi$) to map back from the latent space to the original state space.
For predictive capabilities, the ecDAE is trained for multi-step predictions over a horizon $H$. The predicted latent state $z_{k+H}$ is given by a linear combination involving $A^H$, $f_\phi(x_k)$, and a sum of control inputs $u_{k+i}$. The ecDAE is trained using three loss functions:
- **State Reconstruction Loss ($L_{sr}$):** Minimizes the difference between the input state and its reconstruction ($||x_k - f^{-1}_\phi(f_\phi(x_k))||_2^2$).
- **Latent State Prediction Loss ($L_{lp}$):** Minimizes the difference between the actual future latent state and the Koopman model's prediction of it ($||f_\phi(x_{k+H}) - K_H f_\phi(x_k)||_2^2$).
- **State Prediction Loss ($L_{sp}$):** Minimizes the difference between the actual future state and the decoded Koopman prediction of it ($||x_{k+H} - f^{-1}_\phi(K_H f_\phi(x_k))||_2^2$).

**C. Latent State Privileged Observations**
In the SKooP framework, the actor-critic architecture is asymmetric. The actor, for deployment efficiency, receives only the current state $x_k$. However, the critic receives a "privileged observation" in the form of the one-step Koopman-predicted latent state $z_{k+1} = Af_\phi(x_k) + Bu_k$. This prediction, derived from the ecDAE, is intended to provide the critic with smoother and more informative features about the system's anticipated future, thereby simplifying the task of learning the value function and enhancing the quality of learned policies.

**D. Online DAE Training**
To ensure the learned Koopman model is relevant to the agent's exploration, the ecDAE is trained concurrently with the RL policy. This avoids costly pre-training and adaptation issues. A separate Prioritized Experience Replay (PER) buffer stores state-action-next state transitions $(x_k, u_k, x_{k+1})$ for ecDAE training. This buffer prioritizes data points that yielded higher autoencoder loss, focusing learning on more challenging or less-understood transitions. Initial data is gathered through random actions, and then for each RL training step (using Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO, in this work), a corresponding ecDAE training step is executed.

**E. Experimental Setup**
The approach was validated using vanilla PPO as the base RL algorithm. Experiments were conducted on the Cyberdog 2 quadruped robot, performing three challenging bipedal locomotion tasks: "stand dance," "walk slope," and "push door." The tasks require the robot to transition to and maintain an upright, two-legged stance. Training utilized the massively parallel Isaac Gym simulator, with sim-to-sim transfer validation performed in MuJoCo for robustness assessment. The ecDAE was configured with a prediction horizon of $H=5$, and the latent state $z$ had a dimensionality three times that of the observation state $x$. An ablation study was performed comparing SKooP against PPO, PPOeqic (symmetric actor/critic without autoencoder), and several variants of SKooP with specific components removed (SKooP-NoSym-NoPred, SKooP-NoSym, SKooP-NoPred).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SKooP covered policy symmetry, training performance, properties of the learned Koopman model, and sim-to-sim transferability.

**A. Policy Symmetry Evaluation**
1. **Success Rate (SR) and Symmetry Index (SI):** On the "push door" task, non-symmetric approaches (PPO, SKooP-NoSym-NoPred, SKooP-NoSym) achieved high SR on the trained (right-opening) task but failed to generalize to the mirrored (left-opening) task, evidenced by a high Symmetry Index (SI) often near 200%. In contrast, symmetric approaches (PPOeqic, SKooP-NoPred, SKooP) showed significant generalization. SKooP demonstrated the highest overall SR (82.77% for right-opening, 77.70% for left-opening) and the lowest SI (8.49%), outperforming PPOeqic and SKooP-NoPred, and exhibiting lower variance across seeds.
2. **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Performance:** When tested with OOD initial states, SKooP consistently maintained strong generalization and low SI, significantly outperforming PPOeqic in both SR and SI under these conditions.
3. **Joint Trajectory Analysis:** For the "stand dance" task, SKooP produced the most symmetric rear leg joint trajectories, indicating enhanced stability. This was quantified by a 96.7% reduction in mean phase Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and a 42.3% reduction in amplitude difference (∆θ_diff) compared to SKooP-NoSym. Non-symmetric methods exhibited noticeable asymmetries in joint movements.

**B. Training Performance**
Across the three bipedal locomotion tasks, SKooP, SKooP-NoPred, and PPOeqic (all incorporating symmetries) exhibited steeper initial convergence rates compared to methods without symmetry priors. SKooP achieved higher mean episode returns than PPO and PPOeqic. On the challenging "push door" task, SKooP significantly outperformed PPO and PPOeqic in terms of final reward values and also demonstrated faster convergence than SKooP-NoPred. A slight initial slowdown for SKooP compared to -NoPred variants was noted, attributed to the Koopman prediction introducing more error when the autoencoder is in its early, less converged training phase.

**C. Stability and Controllability of the Koopman Model**
1. **Eigenvalues:** The ecDAE (symmetry-constrained) models consistently learned larger maximum eigenvalue magnitudes for the Koopman matrix A. In two out of three tasks ("walk slope" and "push door"), the ecDAE learned an eigenvalue close to 1.0, suggesting the identification of an invariant mode that helps represent the system's dynamics across symmetry groups. The "stand dance" task, which does not involve significant base displacement, did not exhibit this invariant mode.
2. **Controllability:** The ecDAE consistently showed a larger condition number (κ(C)) and a lower rank of the controllability matrix (rank(C)) compared to the non-symmetric cDAE. This implies that the ecDAE learns a more compact latent representation by recognizing and effectively ignoring redundant control directions dictated by the system's symmetries.
3. **Autoencoder Loss Comparison:** For the "push door" task, the ecDAE demonstrated lower state reconstruction ($L_{sr}$) and state prediction ($L_{sp}$) errors compared to the cDAE. Crucially, the ecDAE maintained almost identical state prediction accuracy on both the trained (right-opening) and mirrored (left-opening) tasks, whereas the cDAE's state prediction error on the mirrored task was approximately 50% worse. Ablation of the invariant mode in the ecDAE significantly increased prediction errors, confirming its importance.

**D. Sim-to-Sim Transfer**
To assess robustness, SKooP policies trained in Isaac Gym for the "stand dance" task were transferred to MuJoCo under various perturbed conditions (e.g., different friction, added mass, velocity pushes, rotor inertia). The policies demonstrated robustness, with no task failures observed. While linear velocity tracking (v_xy MSE) remained similar across simulators and conditions, angular velocity (ω_z MSE) tracking performance experienced degradation in MuJoCo, indicating potential discrepancies in how angular dynamics are modeled between the simulators or an area for further policy refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SKooP framework represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic locomotion control through reinforcement learning, particularly for complex legged robots. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Efficiency and Generalization:** SKooP demonstrably reduces the convergence time of RL algorithms and improves the quality of learned rewards. By effectively embedding morphological symmetries and utilizing Koopman predictions, the approach significantly boosts policy generalization to unseen, mirrored tasks and out-of-distribution initial states. This addresses critical limitations of traditional RL, making the training of complex robotic behaviors more data-efficient and robust.
2. **Higher Quality and More Stable Locomotion:** The integration of symmetry priors leads to learned policies that produce qualitatively better motions, characterized by more stable and symmetric gaits. This is crucial for real-world deployment where consistent, predictable, and robust robot behavior is paramount.
3. **Advancement in Koopman Theory Application for Robotics:** This research successfully translates Koopman theory from theoretical benchmarks to practical, high-dimensional legged robot control. The novel approach of feeding Koopman-predicted latent states directly to the critic provides a tangible mechanism for leveraging the linear dynamics in a lifted space to inform and accelerate policy learning, an area previously underexplored for robotic control.
4. **Improved Model Interpretability and Robustness:** The analysis of the learned Koopman models highlights how symmetry constraints guide the autoencoder to discover more compact and robust representations of the system dynamics, including invariant modes. This deeper understanding of the system's inherent structure can inform future model-based design and control strategies.
5. **Modularity and Adaptability:** SKooP's modular design, being compatible with existing actor-critic algorithms like PPO, suggests its broad applicability across various RL methodologies. The demonstrated robustness to perturbations and promising sim-to-sim transferability indicate its potential for real-world deployment, provided that remaining simulator discrepancies, such as angular velocity tracking, are addressed.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a robust foundation for further exploration into the synergistic integration of physics-informed models (like Koopman operators and symmetries) with data-driven reinforcement learning. Future research can build upon SKooP to investigate alternative Koopman learning strategies, transferability to diverse robotic platforms and tasks, and the critical challenge of bridging the sim-to-real gap more effectively through learned, symmetric, and predictive dynamic models.

In conclusion, SKooP contributes to making RL-based control for complex legged robots more efficient, generalizable, and robust, which are essential steps toward their wider adoption in varied and unpredictable environments.
