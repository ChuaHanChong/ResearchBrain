# OK-Robot: What Really Matters in Integrating Open-Knowledge Models for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> OK-Robot, a collaboration between New York University and AI at Meta, demonstrates an integrated system for open-vocabulary mobile manipulation by intelligently combining existing open-knowledge models. The framework achieved a 58.5% success rate in diverse, unseen real-world home environments for pick-and-drop tasks, representing a significant performance increase over prior methods without environment-specific training.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with robustly performing open-ended tasks in complex, unseen real-world environments due to brittleness and lack of generalization.
- Integrating state-of-the-art perception (VLMs) and manipulation models into a cohesive, high-performing system for tasks like pick-and-drop remains challenging.
- Prior approaches in open-vocabulary mobile manipulation (OVMM) show low success rates (e.g., 33% in challenges), indicating compounded inaccuracies across components.

## Method

- Developed **OK-Robot**, a modular framework integrating off-the-shelf ==open-knowledge models== (==VLMs== for perception, pre-trained grasping models like ==AnyGrasp==) and robotic primitives.
- Employs a ==zero-shot== approach, requiring no environment-specific training or fine-tuning, only an initial environment scan to create a static, object-centric ==semantic memory (VoxelMap)==.
- Combines ==open-vocabulary object navigation==, ==open-vocabulary grasping==, and a ==dropping heuristic== in a linear ==state-machine== (using ==OWL-ViT==, ==SAM==, and ==CLIP==), deploying on a mobile manipulator like the Hello Robot: Stretch.

## Results

- OK-Robot achieved a **58.5%** success rate for full pick-and-drop tasks across **171 trials** in 10 diverse, unseen real-world home environments, representing a nearly **1.8x** performance increase over prior work.
- The system's success rate increased to **82.4%** in cleaner, decluttered environments, demonstrating the impact of environmental complexity on performance.
- Ablation studies showed that VoxelMap consistently outperformed alternative semantic memory modules (e.g., CLIP-Fields) and AnyGrasp provided superior grasping performance over other tested methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current open-knowledge Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and pre-trained grasping models are effective for zero-shot object identification and manipulation in real-world settings.
> - Intelligent, systems-first integration of existing components with careful heuristics (e.g., for navigation path, grasp selection) is crucial for real-world robustness, rather than solely developing new fundamental models.
> - Systematic analysis of failure modes highlights critical limitations in VLM semantic retrieval, grasping pose generation, and robot hardware capabilities, guiding future research.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The overarching goal of robotics research is to create general-purpose robots capable of operating effectively in complex, unstructured environments, such as human homes. Despite remarkable progress in individual subfields like computer vision, natural language processing, and robotic control primitives (navigation, grasping), a significant gap persists in the ability of integrated robotic systems to perform robustly in unseen, real-world scenarios. Current state-of-the-art systems often suffer from brittleness, lack of generalization, and are typically "closed" – meaning they are tailored to specific environments or object sets, requiring extensive retraining or fine-tuning for new deployments.

This paper explicitly addresses this perplexing disparity. While large vision models demonstrate impressive capabilities in semantic understanding and connecting visual representations to language, and robotic skills like navigation and grasping are increasingly mature, combining them into a cohesive system for open-ended tasks proves challenging. The authors highlight this difficulty by citing the low success rate (33%) of the winning solution in the NeurIPS 2023 Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM) Challenge, a task that requires robots to pick up arbitrary objects and move them to specified locations in novel environments. The core problem, as identified, is not a single limitation but rather the compounding of inaccuracies and limitations from different components, such as the quality of language queries, the reachability of navigation targets, and the reliability of grasping models.

OK-Robot positions itself as a "systems-first approach" to bridge this gap. Instead of developing new fundamental models, it focuses on the intelligent and nuanced integration of existing "Open Knowledge" models – those trained on large, publicly available datasets and capable of zero-shot generalization – with established robotic primitives. This approach aims to demonstrate what is truly achievable with current off-the-shelf technologies when carefully combined, without requiring any environment-specific training or fine-tuning. The work builds upon recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP, OWL-ViT, and Segment Anything Model (SAM), and leverages pre-trained grasping models such as AnyGrasp, which have shown strong capabilities in their respective domains. By demonstrating an integrated system that significantly outperforms prior work in real-world OVMM tasks, OK-Robot contributes to the growing body of literature on embodied AI, pushing towards more adaptable and generally capable robotic agents for domestic applications. It implicitly argues that the bottleneck is no longer solely in the individual capabilities of perception or manipulation models, but in their synergistic and robust integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a novel, open-knowledge based robotics framework, named **OK-Robot**, designed to perform open-ended pick-and-drop operations in previously unseen, unstructured home environments. The central driving motivation is to demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of integrating existing state-of-the-art vision-language models (VLMs) and robotic primitives (navigation and grasping) without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning for new environments. This "zero-shot" deployment capability is crucial for creating truly general-purpose home robots.

More specifically, the paper sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

- **Develop an Integrated Framework:** To combine distinct, off-the-shelf components—VLMs for object detection and localization, a navigation module for mobility, and a grasping module for manipulation—into a cohesive, functional system for mobile manipulation.
- **Achieve Zero-Shot Generalization:** To enable the robot to operate in completely novel home environments immediately after a brief, initial "scan," without any prior exposure or task-specific data collection in that environment.
- **Evaluate Real-World Performance:** To rigorously test OK-Robot's performance in a diverse set of 10 real-world home environments, conducting a large number of pick-and-drop experiments (171 trials) to gather empirical data on its success rates and identify practical challenges.
- **Identify Critical Integration Nuances:** To understand "what really matters" when combining open-knowledge models with robotic modules. This involves analyzing how different component choices impact overall system performance and what heuristics or design decisions are crucial for robustness in real-world settings.
- **Analyze Failure Modes:** To systematically categorize and understand the root causes of failures, providing insights into the limitations of current open-knowledge models and robotic hardware when deployed in complex home environments. This objective is crucial for guiding future research efforts.
- **Release Code and Data:** To contribute to the open-science community by sharing the code and robot videos, encouraging reproducibility and further investigation into open-knowledge robotics.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the recognized brittleness and limited generalization of existing robotic systems, particularly in unstructured domestic settings where data collection for specialized training is scarce. By adopting a systems-first approach with pre-trained, "open-knowledge" models, the authors aim to bypass the need for massive, robot-specific datasets and instead leverage the vast knowledge embedded in foundation models. This not only offers a pathway to more adaptable robots but also provides valuable insights into the compounding nature of errors in multi-stage robotic tasks, thereby highlighting key areas for future innovation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OK-Robot employs a modular, systems-first approach, integrating several state-of-the-art open-knowledge models and robotic primitives into a linear state-machine to perform "pick up A (from B) and drop it on/in C" tasks. The system is deployed on a Hello Robot: Stretch mobile manipulator, known for its portability and suitability for indoor human environments. Crucially, the system requires no training or fine-tuning in the target environment, relying entirely on zero-shot generalization from pre-trained models.

The methodology can be broken down into three primary subsystems and a deployment protocol.

#### 3.1. Open-Home, Open-Vocabulary Object Navigation

This module allows the robot to map a home and navigate to objects specified by natural language queries.
- **Scanning the Home:** A manual pre-mapping phase using an iPhone (Record3D app) captures a sequence of posed RGB-D images of the environment. This takes less than a minute per room.
- **Detecting Objects:** On each frame of the scan, the open-vocabulary object detector OWL-ViT is applied. Its bounding box outputs are refined into object masks using Segment Anything (SAM). A large set of Scannet200 labels serves as object queries for detection.
- **Object-Centric Semantic Memory (VoxelMap):** The object masks from each frame are back-projected into real-world coordinates to create a point cloud, with each point associated with a CLIP semantic vector. This point cloud is then voxelized at a 5 cm resolution, and each voxel stores a detector-confidence weighted average of CLIP embeddings. This VoxelMap serves as a static, object-centric semantic memory of the environment.
- **Querying the Memory:** Given a language query, it is converted into a CLIP language embedding. The VoxelMap is then queried to find the voxel (and thus the 3D location) with the highest dot product similarity to the query embedding. The system also supports "A on B" queries by finding A-points closest to B-points.
- **Navigating to Objects:** Once a 3D target location is identified, the robot plans a path. This involves balancing three objectives: proximity to the object, maintaining a small buffer for gripper movement, and collision avoidance. Three score functions (s1 for object proximity, s2 for gripper space, s3 for obstacle avoidance) are combined with weights (s = s1 + 8s2 + 8s3) to find the optimal robot position. Path planning utilizes a 2D, 10cmx10cm obstacle grid derived from the VoxelMap, employing an A* algorithm with `s3` as a heuristic to generate Voronoi-like paths.

#### 3.2. Open-Vocabulary Grasping in the Real World

Once the robot navigates to the object's vicinity, it attempts to grasp it.
- **Grasp Perception:** The robot's head camera captures an RGB-D image focused on the object's 3D location. A pre-trained grasping model, AnyGrasp, is used to generate a multitude of collision-free grasp proposals for a parallel-jaw gripper, along with a "graspness score" for each.
- **Filtering Grasps:** LangSam is employed to segment the captured image based on the language query, providing an object mask. Grasp points falling within this mask are considered. A heuristic `S - (θ/4 / 10)` (where S is graspness score and θ is angle between grasp normal and floor normal) is used to select the best grasp, prioritizing horizontal grasps for better robustness against minor calibration errors.
- **Grasp Execution:** A simple pre-grasp approach is used, where the gripper approaches the target point in progressively smaller motions. After closing the gripper, the arm lifts, retracts, and rotates the wrist to tuck the object securely, maintaining the robot's footprint for subsequent navigation.

#### 3.3. Dropping Heuristic

After picking an object, the robot navigates to the drop location using the same navigation methods as for picking.
- **Drop Point Identification:** LangSam segments the point cloud from the robot's head camera for the drop location query.
- **Drop Height Calculation:** The segmented point cloud is aligned to the robot's coordinates. A median (x,y) point is found, and a drop height `z_max` is calculated as 0.2 meters above the maximum z-coordinate within a certain radius of the median, to avoid collisions.
- **Execution:** The gripper moves above the calculated drop point and opens to release the object.

#### 3.4. Deployment in Homes Protocol

The integrated system is deployed "zero-shot" in novel home environments. A new room is scanned (under a minute), processed into a VoxelMap (under five minutes), and the robot can then immediately begin tasks. For experiments, 10-20 manipulable objects are arbitrarily chosen per scene, and GPT-4V is used to generate consistent language queries, minimizing experimenter bias. Navigation failures are filtered out, and remaining pick-and-drop tasks are executed sequentially without resets. The authors also performed ablation studies on alternative navigation (CLIP-Fields, USA-Net) and grasping (Open Graspness, Contact-GraspNet, Top-Down grasp) components in controlled lab settings to justify their choices.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OK-Robot's evaluation in 10 diverse, unseen real-world home environments yielded significant results, demonstrating a new state-of-the-art in zero-shot Open Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM).

- **Overall Performance:** OK-Robot achieved a **58.5% success rate** in completing full pick-and-drop tasks across 171 trials in cluttered, novel home environments. This represents a substantial improvement, with "nearly 1.8x the performance of prior work" (likely referring to the HomeRobot baseline in the NeurIPS OVMM Challenge, which had a 33% success rate). When operating in cleaner, decluttered environments, the success rate further increased to **82.4%**.

- **Key Observations from Experiments:**
 - **Effectiveness of Pre-trained VLMs for Navigation:** The study confirmed that current open-vocabulary VLMs like CLIP and OWL-ViT are highly effective for identifying arbitrary objects and enabling zero-shot navigation to them in real-world settings.
 - **Direct Applicability of Pre-trained Grasping Models:** Specialized robot models like AnyGrasp, pre-trained on large datasets, can be applied out-of-the-box for open-vocabulary grasping without additional training or fine-tuning, contributing significantly to manipulation capabilities.
 - **Crucial Role of Component Integration and Heuristics:** A simple state-machine model combining these pre-trained components without training proved effective. Furthermore, using specific heuristics to compensate for the robot's physical limitations (e.g., preference for horizontal grasps, weighted navigation scores) was critical for achieving higher success rates in real-world deployment.
 - **Remaining Challenges:** Despite the advancements, the results highlighted that significant improvements are still needed in the underlying VLMs, robot manipulation models, and robot morphology to achieve robust, high-performance general-purpose robotics.

- **Ablation Study Results:**
 - **Semantic Memory Modules:** In comparative tests, VoxelMap (used in OK-Robot) consistently outperformed alternative semantic memory modules like CLIP-Fields and USA-Net for navigation, demonstrating higher average performance and significantly lower variance, indicating greater reliability.
 - **Grasping Modules:** AnyGrasp demonstrated superior performance, achieving nearly 50% better success rates compared to the next best candidate, HomeRobot's Top-Down grasp heuristic. Interestingly, the simple heuristic-based Top-Down grasp outperformed more complex learning-based methods like Open Graspness (AnyGrasp's open-source baseline) and Contact-GraspNet, underscoring the inherent difficulty in building truly general-purpose grasping models that transfer well to diverse real-world conditions.

- **Impact of Environment Factors (Clutter, Ambiguity, Affordance):** The experiments, conducted across different "cleanup levels" (none, low, high clutter/ambiguity), provided clear insights into the impact of environmental complexity on performance:
 - **Navigation Failure:** Decreased from 15% in cluttered environments to 4% in clean environments, indicating that ambiguous language queries and multiple visually similar objects (common in cluttered homes) are significant challenges.
 - **Manipulation Failure:** Decreased from 25% in cluttered environments to 13% in clean ones, showing that physical clutter around objects significantly impedes grasping success.
 - **Placing Failure:** Remained relatively constant (around 4%), as the drop module is less affected by object ambiguity or clutter once the drop location is reached.

- **Understanding Failure Modes (Long-Tail Analysis):** The detailed analysis of failures revealed the most frequent causes:
 1. **Semantic Memory Retrieval (9.3%):** Primarily due to ambiguous natural language queries or multiple visually/semantically similar objects, leading the VLM to misidentify the target.
 2. **Difficult Grasp Pose (8.0%):** The pre-trained grasping model sometimes proposed unreliable or unrealistic grasps, or grasps that were infeasible given the robot's joint limits or reach. Failures also occurred when grasping flat objects or due to collisions during an un-planned grasp trajectory.
 3. **Robot Hardware Limitations (7.5%):** Issues such as payload limits (e.g., inability to lift heavy objects), reach limits (objects too high or far), occasional sensor miscalibration affecting hand-eye coordination, and difficulties with base wheel mobility on varied surfaces (carpet to floor).

The successful reproduction of OK-Robot in two larger, more complex homes outside the initial experiment set (Pittsburgh, PA, and Fremont, CA) further validates its robust generalization capabilities to truly novel environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OK-Robot project represents a significant step forward in the field of general-purpose mobile manipulation, particularly for unstructured home environments. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that a substantial level of autonomous functionality can be achieved by thoughtfully integrating existing, "open-knowledge" pre-trained models, without the need for environment-specific data collection or fine-tuning. This "zero-shot" capability directly addresses the long-standing challenge of deploying robots in novel, unpredictable settings.

The key impacts and contributions of this work are:

- **New State-of-the-Art in Zero-Shot OVMM:** By achieving a 58.5% success rate in diverse, cluttered real homes (and 82.4% in cleaner ones), OK-Robot sets a new benchmark for open-vocabulary mobile manipulation. This empirical validation in real-world conditions, rather than simulations or highly controlled lab settings, is a critical step towards practical robot deployment.
- **Validation of Modular, Open-Knowledge Architectures:** The success of OK-Robot underscores the power of modular system design. It demonstrates that by leveraging the robust, generalizable representations learned by large vision-language models and specialized manipulation models, complex robot tasks can be achieved even with imperfect individual components. This approach significantly reduces the data burden often associated with end-to-end robot learning.
- **Detailed Insights into Real-World Challenges:** The comprehensive analysis of failure modes—attributing errors to semantic ambiguity, grasping limitations, and hardware constraints—provides invaluable guidance for future research. This granular understanding helps the community prioritize efforts, whether it's improving VLM grounding, developing more versatile grasp planning algorithms, or engineering more robust and dexterous robot hardware.
- **Guidance for Future Research Directions:** The limitations section explicitly calls out several open problems: the need for live, dynamic semantic memory and obstacle maps (as current ones are static), the development of grasp *plans* (trajectories) instead of just poses, enhanced interactivity between the robot and user for disambiguation, robust failure detection and recovery mechanisms, and the importance of more capable and reliable robot hardware. These insights directly inform the research agenda for the next generation of general-purpose robots.
- **Promotion of Open Science:** The release of code and robot videos is a crucial contribution that enables reproducibility and encourages further investigation. By providing an open platform, OK-Robot fosters collaborative research and accelerates progress in open-knowledge robotics, allowing other researchers to build upon and extend this work.

In essence, OK-Robot not only advances the technical capabilities of mobile manipulation but also provides a pragmatic roadmap for future development. It shifts the focus from solely developing bigger, more complex models to emphasizing the intelligent integration and robust deployment of existing capabilities, thereby bringing the dream of general-purpose, home-based robots closer to reality.
