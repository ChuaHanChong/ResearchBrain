# Not All Tokens and Heads Are Equally Important: Dual-Level Attention Intervention for Hallucination Mitigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed VisFlow, a training-free, dual-level attention intervention framework that modulates attention patterns during inference to mitigate visual hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). This method significantly improved factual grounding on benchmarks like POPE and CHAIR, reducing hallucination rates while maintaining decoding efficiency comparable to standard beam search.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate visual hallucinations, producing descriptions inconsistent with or inaccurately reflecting visual input.
- Existing hallucination mitigation strategies are often computationally expensive (e.g., fine-tuning, contrastive decoding) or introduce latency (e.g., auxiliary modules).
- LVLMs suffer from insufficient visual attention, often focusing on uninformative visual tokens, and an over-reliance on linguistic priors and generic system prompts.

## Method

- ==VisFlow== introduces a dual-level attention intervention framework applied at inference time, directly modulating attention patterns within the LVLM decoder.
- ==Token-level Attention Intervention (TAI)== enhances attention to visual salient tokens and attenuates attention to visual sink tokens, correcting RoPE-induced positional biases.
- ==Head-level Attention Intervention (HAI)== identifies and suppresses over-attention in system-dominant and text-dominant attention heads, reducing the influence of linguistic priors.

## Results

- VisFlow achieved state-of-the-art performance on the POPE benchmark, improving the Adversarial F1 score for LLaVA-1.5 from **73.54** to **84.35**, significantly outperforming contrastive decoding methods.
- On the CHAIR benchmark, VisFlow reduced sentence-level (CHAIR_s) hallucinations for LLaVA-1.5 from **6.8** to **3.8** and instance-level (CHAIR_i) hallucinations from **20.0** to **15.0**.
- The framework maintained decoding speed comparable to standard beam search, demonstrating significantly higher efficiency (Tokens Per Second) compared to other decoding-based hallucination mitigation methods requiring multiple forward passes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Not all tokens and attention heads are equally important; targeted intervention at these fine-grained levels can more effectively and efficiently mitigate hallucinations.
> - Analysis of information flow reveals sparse visual attention, layer-wise decay of visual information, and RoPE-induced positional biases contribute to hallucinations.
> - Specific attention heads within the LVLM decoder specialize in visual processing versus relying on language priors, allowing for targeted modulation.
