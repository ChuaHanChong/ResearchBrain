# VisRAG 2.0: Evidence-Guided Multi-Image Reasoning in Visual Retrieval-Augmented Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The EVisRAG framework enhances visual retrieval-augmented generation by guiding Vision-Language Models through explicit observe, record, reason, and answer phases for multi-image reasoning. It achieves substantial performance gains, with an average +19% accuracy and +27% F1 score over its 7B parameter backbone, across five visual question answering benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Visual RAG systems struggle to effectively integrate and reason over evidence distributed across multiple retrieved images.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often exhibit weak grounding, leading to hallucinations and erroneous conclusions despite access to relevant visual information.
- Current VLM training strategies suffer from intertwined perception and reasoning reward signals, complicating credit assignment and training stability in multi-step visual tasks.

## Method

- ==EVisRAG== introduces an ==evidence-guided reasoning paradigm== that explicitly sequences visual processing into 'observe,' 'record evidence' from each image, 'reason,' and 'answer' steps.
- The framework employs a novel ==Reward-Scoped Group Relative Policy Optimization (RS-GRPO)== algorithm, which assigns fine-grained, distinct rewards (format, perception, derivation) to specific token spans within the generated output.
- A two-stage training strategy involves initial supervised fine-tuning followed by RS-GRPO to jointly optimize both visual perception and logical reasoning capabilities.

## Results

- EVisRAG achieves an average of **+19%** accuracy and **+27%** F1 score improvement over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone across five VQA benchmarks, outperforming larger **32B** models and other state-of-the-art methods.
- Ablation studies confirm that the evidence-guided reasoning paradigm, dedicated perception reward, and Reward-Scoped Group Relative Policy Optimization (RS-GRPO) are critical components for the framework's superior performance.
- The framework demonstrates improved visual attention, focusing more precisely on relevant image regions, and exhibits superior robustness to varying evidence densities and a controlled reduction in hallucinations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An explicit, structured approach to perceiving and recording evidence from each image before reasoning significantly improves a VLM's ability to synthesize information from multiple visual sources.
> - Assigning distinct reward signals to specific components of the generated output (reward scoping) is crucial for sharpening credit assignment, stabilizing reinforcement learning, and effectively training complex, multi-stage visual reasoning tasks.
> - Enhanced fine-grained visual attention, guided by dedicated perception rewards, is fundamental for robust multi-image reasoning and effectively mitigating hallucination in VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses critical limitations within the rapidly evolving fields of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly when applied to complex visual data.

**Evolution from RAG to Visual RAG (VRAG):**
Traditional RAG systems (Lewis et al., 2020) augment Large Language Models (LLMs) with external textual knowledge bases to reduce hallucinations and improve factual grounding. However, a significant portion of real-world knowledge is inherently non-textual, residing in images, tables, and complex document layouts. Initial attempts to integrate this visual information often involved preprocessing steps like optical character recognition (OCR) or image captioning, which inevitably discard crucial visual and spatial cues (Zhang et al., 2024b).

This limitation led to the emergence of **Visual RAG (VRAG)** (Yu et al., 2025; Faysse et al., 2024), which retrieves document page snapshots directly, allowing VLMs to read evidence from images. Recent advancements in VRAG systems have begun to integrate reinforcement learning (RL) to guide the retrieval and reasoning process, enabling models to derive answers directly from pixel-level information rather than linearized text (Peng et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025).

**Challenges in Current VRAG and VLRMs:**
Despite these advances, existing VRAG systems often struggle with modality-specific challenges:
- **Cross-image grounding:** Reliably perceiving and integrating evidence distributed across multiple retrieved images. Many systems transplant text-based RAG practices to vision without fully accounting for visual complexities.
- **Layout-aware reading and region-level attention:** Failing to accurately focus on and interpret specific regions within images.
- **Weak grounding and hallucinations:** leading to erroneous conclusions despite access to relevant visual information.
- **Architectural complexity:** Some approaches introduce auxiliary agents or perception-oriented actions (Wang et al., 2025b; a), which improve visual detail attention but complicate end-to-end training and increase computational costs.
- **Ineffective reward signals:** Current VLRM training strategies often mix perception and reasoning rewards, blurring credit assignment and causing interference, especially in multi-image scenarios.

**EVisRAG's Position:**
EVisRAG (proposed in VisRAG 2.0) positions itself as a solution to these challenges by focusing on **evidence-guided multi-image reasoning** with precise visual perception. It builds upon the foundation of VRAG and VLRMs but introduces a novel framework and training paradigm to explicitly address the complexities of integrating evidence from multiple visual sources. By enhancing perceptual grounding and streamlining the reasoning process through a structured, detective-like approach, EVisRAG aims to push the boundaries of reliable, grounded visual question answering and document understanding. It seeks to overcome the limitations of previous methods that either oversimplify visual information or introduce excessive architectural complexity, thereby offering a more effective and adaptable RAG framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind EVisRAG stems from the observed shortcomings in existing Visual Retrieval-Augmented Generation (VRAG) systems, particularly their inability to effectively process and synthesize information from multiple images to inform reasoning.

The **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **Enhance Reliable Visual Perception:** To enable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to reliably perceive and integrate evidence across multiple retrieved images, moving beyond single-image reasoning capabilities. This includes improving layout-aware reading and region-level attention.
2. **Reduce Hallucinations and Improve Grounding:** To mitigate the tendency of VLMs to hallucinate or draw erroneous conclusions by ensuring that answers are robustly grounded in the actual visual evidence provided.
3. **Develop an End-to-End Evidence-Guided Reasoning Framework:** To design a cohesive, sequential framework that guides VLMs through observing visual content, explicitly recording pertinent evidence from each image, and then reasoning based *only* on this collected evidence. This mimics a "real detective's" process of gathering and synthesizing clues.
4. **Optimize VLM Training for Multi-Image Reasoning:** To introduce a novel reinforcement learning optimization strategy that effectively trains VLMs for both fine-grained visual perception and logical reasoning, with a clear separation of reward signals to avoid interference. This aims to improve credit assignment during learning.

In essence, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between VLMs' impressive general capabilities and their often-unstable performance in multi-image, evidence-intensive visual question answering tasks. They seek to create a VRAG system that is not only powerful but also precise, robust, and transparent in how it leverages visual knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EVisRAG proposes an end-to-end framework built on an evidence-guided reasoning paradigm, optimized by a novel reinforcement learning algorithm.

#### 3.1. The EVisRAG Framework Overview

The EVisRAG framework processes an initial query `q` and a corpus `D` of document-page snapshots to produce a final answer `a` through a step-by-step reasoning process. This process is explicitly structured into sequential phases:

1. **Information Gathering:**
 - Given a query `q` and a document corpus `D`, EVisRAG first retrieves a set of top-k relevant document pages, `D_R = {d_i}`, using a retrieval paradigm like VisRAG (Yu et al., 2025). This ensures the model has access to potentially relevant visual context. In experiments, top-3 images are used for evaluation, and top-1 to top-5 for training data augmentation.

2. **Visual Perception Phase:** This phase comprises two sub-steps to systematically extract information:
 - **Observe:** EVisRAG sequentially observes each retrieved image `d_i` in `D_R` and generates a coarse, page-aware description, `r_observe`. This initial observation helps the VLM understand the overall content of each image.
 - **Record Evidence:** Conditioned on the query `q` and the `r_observe` sequence, EVisRAG then records specific evidence from each page by generating per-image evidence sequences, `r^(i)_evidence`. This step is crucial for fine-grained grounding; it requires the model to identify and summarize question-relevant information within each image. If an image contains no relevant information, the model is expected to explicitly state "no relevant information."

3. **Answer Reasoning Phase:** After perception, EVisRAG acts like a "detective" to synthesize the gathered information:
 - **Reason:** Based on the query `q` and the `r_perception` (comprising `r_observe` and `r_evidence`), the model formulates a coherent reasoning trajectory, `r_reason`. This involves distilling leads, forming hypotheses across pages, cross-checking contradictions, and structuring the logical steps to arrive at an answer.
 - **Answer:** Finally, conditioned on `q`, `r_perception`, and `r_reason`, EVisRAG produces the final answer sequence, `r_answer`. If the retrieved context is insufficient to answer the question, the model is guided to output "insufficient to answer."

#### 3.2. Optimizing VLMs with Reward-Scoped Group Relative Policy Optimization (RS-GRPO)

To effectively train EVisRAG, the authors employ a two-stage training strategy:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as a Cold Start:** The model is initially fine-tuned using a supervised approach to acquire basic abilities and generate high-quality "golden reasoning trajectories" from larger VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B) as ground truth. This process involves collecting 60,000 SFT samples.
2. **Reward-Scoped Group Relative Policy Optimization (RS-GRPO):** This is the core innovation for training. RS-GRPO extends the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm by introducing "Reward Scopes" to jointly optimize visual perception and reasoning abilities.

 - **Fine-Grained Rewards:** Three distinct reward channels are used:
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** Enforces adherence to the evidence-guided reasoning paradigm by ensuring the model generates outputs in the correct sequence of `<observe>`, `<evidence>`, `<think>`, and `<answer>` tags. This provides stable supervision for the intermediate steps.
 - **Perception Reward (R_perception):** Assesses whether question-relevant regions are correctly localized and summarized for each image. It uses an F1 score for relevant evidence and awards points for correctly identifying "no relevant information" in irrelevant images. This directly encourages accurate visual grounding.
 - **Derivation Reward (R_derivation):** Evaluates the correctness of the final answer based on the gold truth, or rewards "insufficient to answer" if the context is truly inadequate. This ensures the reasoning leads to factually correct conclusions.

 - **Reward Scopes for Credit Assignment:** The key idea is to route these rewards to specific token spans (`reward scopes`) within the generated output sequence:
 - `R_perception` supervises tokens in the `observe` (`T_o`) and `record evidence` (`T_e`) scopes.
 - `R_derivation` supervises tokens in the `reason` (`T_r`) and `answer` (`T_a`) scopes.
 - `R_format` applies to all tokens, ensuring structural adherence.
 This sharpens credit assignment, reduces interference between different learning objectives, and stabilizes training.

 - **Token-Level Advantage Calculation:** For each token `t` in a sampled output `i`, an aggregated token reward `¯R^i_t` is calculated as the mean of its in-scope reward channels. This is then used to compute a group-normalized token-level advantage `ˆA^i_t` across a group of sampled outputs, similar to standard GRPO, but with the added layer of reward scoping.

 - **Objective Function:** The model is optimized by minimizing a specific RS-GRPO objective function (Equation 10), which incorporates these token-level advantages. This objective enhances exploration diversity and training stability, leading to more robust learning of both perceptual and reasoning skills.

#### 3.3. Data and Baselines

- **Datasets:** Evaluated on five VQA tasks: ChartQA, InfographicsVQA, MP-DocVQA, SlideVQA, and ViDoseek, covering diverse document types and multi-document scenarios.
- **Context Sufficiency:** Questions are categorized by "sufficient context" or "insufficient context" based on retrieved images, allowing for nuanced evaluation of both reasoning and hallucination avoidance.
- **Baselines:** Compared against:
 - General VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/32B, MiMo-VL-7B-RL)
 - RL-trained VLRMs (Vision-R1, MM-Eureka, Ocean-R1, ThinkLite-VL, OpenVLThinker)
 - Other VRAG methods (R1-Router, MMSearch-R1, VRAG-RL)
- All baselines use VisRAG-Ret for retrieval.

This methodology provides a structured, evidence-driven approach to multi-image reasoning, complemented by a finely-tuned reinforcement learning strategy that directly addresses the complex interplay of perception and reasoning in visual multimodal tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate the superior performance and robustness of EVisRAG across various visual question answering benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Substantial Gains:** EVisRAG consistently outperforms all baselines across five VQA benchmarks (ChartQA, InfoVQA, DocVQA, SlideVQA, ViDoSeek). On average, it achieves a +19% improvement in accuracy and a +27% improvement in F1 score over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone model.
- **Outperforming Strong Baselines:** EVisRAG also significantly surpasses other state-of-the-art RL-trained VLRMs, including OpenVLThinker, by an average of +9.74% accuracy and +10.73% F1 score.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** EVisRAG, with a 7B parameter model, even outperforms considerably larger 32B parameter models, highlighting its efficiency and the effectiveness of its training approach.
- **Joint Optimization:** The results confirm that EVisRAG's ability to jointly improve perception and reasoning capabilities via RS-GRPO leads to a more effective and adaptable RAG framework.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation studies validate the contributions of each core component of EVisRAG:
- **Evidence-Guided Reasoning Paradigm:** Training with a standard "think-then-answer" approach (w/o Perception) yielded only modest gains compared to EVisRAG, demonstrating the necessity of the explicit observe-record-reason structure.
- **Perception Reward:** Removing the dedicated perception reward (w/o Perception Reward) resulted in a performance drop, underscoring its role in enhancing the visual perception ability of VLMs.
- **Reward-Scoped GRPO (RS-GRPO):** When all rewards were uniformly aggregated and applied to every token (w/o RS-GRPO), performance decreased. This confirms that RS-GRPO's mechanism of applying rewards directly within their designated scopes is crucial for sharpening credit assignment, stabilizing optimization, and achieving peak performance.

#### 4.3. Evaluating Perceptual Ability Through Visual Attention

- **Quantifiable Perception:** EVisRAG achieves the highest reasoning accuracy alongside the highest "visual evidence attention ratio" (percentage of attention mass within human-annotated evidence boxes). This quantitative finding directly links improved attention to higher answer accuracy.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Case studies (e.g., identifying Thailand as a rubber exporter) show that EVisRAG focuses its attention much more precisely on task-critical visual regions (e.g., the top bar in a chart), while baselines often diffuse attention across less relevant areas. This "detective-like" focus on relevant cues is a key differentiator.

#### 4.4. Visual Evidence Density Comparison

- **Robustness to Noise:** EVisRAG demonstrates superior generalizability and robustness in multi-image scenarios, particularly under varying evidence densities. As the number of retrieved images increases, the overall evidence density often decreases (more noise, fewer relevant tokens per image). EVisRAG consistently outperforms baselines (Qwen-7B, OpenVLThinker) across all density levels.
- **Hallucination Resistance:** On the DocVQA dataset, EVisRAG maintains stable performance even as evidence density decreases, indicating a strong ability to resist hallucination effects in noisy multi-document contexts.

#### 4.5. Impact of Training on Model Performance (Hallucination Control)

- **Reduced Hallucination:** Before training, the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B exhibited a strong hallucination tendency, producing incorrect responses even with correctly retrieved context. After training, EVisRAG shows a significant increase in correct generations in correctly retrieved contexts and a controlled reduction in incorrect generations in "insufficient context" scenarios.
- **Evidence-Sensitive Reasoning:** The trained model exhibits strengthened evidence-sensitive reasoning, meaning it is more faithful to the retrieved content and less prone to generating unsupported answers, even when the retrieved context is imperfect.

In summary, the results conclusively show that EVisRAG, powered by its evidence-guided paradigm and RS-GRPO, establishes a new state-of-the-art in multi-image visual retrieval-augmented generation. It achieves this by simultaneously enhancing precise visual perception, improving reasoning grounding, and effectively controlling hallucinations, even with a relatively smaller model size.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EVisRAG framework, as presented in VisRAG 2.0, marks a significant step forward in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for tasks involving complex visual information and multiple image inputs. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Multi-Image Visual Reasoning:** EVisRAG directly addresses a major limitation of current VRAG systems by enabling VLMs to reliably perceive and integrate evidence across multiple images. This capability is crucial for understanding multi-page documents, infographics, and slide decks, where information is often distributed and requires cross-referencing.
2. **Enhanced Perceptual Grounding:** By introducing an explicit "observe-then-localize" process and a dedicated perception reward, EVisRAG significantly improves the VLM's ability to focus on and extract question-relevant details from visual content. This fine-grained perceptual alignment makes the reasoning process more reliable and less prone to misinterpretations.
3. **Reduced Hallucinations and Increased Trustworthiness:** The framework’s emphasis on evidence-guided reasoning, coupled with its ability to correctly identify "insufficient information," substantially reduces hallucinations. This leads to more trustworthy and factually grounded AI outputs, which is critical for real-world applications where accuracy and reliability are paramount.
4. **Novel and Robust Reinforcement Learning Strategy:** Reward-Scoped Group Relative Policy Optimization (RS-GRPO) is a key methodological contribution. By assigning fine-grained rewards to specific token spans, it solves the credit assignment problem in complex multi-step reasoning, leading to more stable and effective training. This approach could be adopted and adapted for training other complex chain-of-thought or multi-stage reasoning models in both visual and textual domains.
5. **Improved Model Efficiency and Scalability:** Demonstrating that a 7B parameter model (EVisRAG) can outperform much larger 32B models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) highlights the efficiency of the proposed paradigm and training method. This suggests that EVisRAG can achieve state-of-the-art performance without requiring disproportionately large models, making advanced multimodal reasoning more accessible and deployable.
6. **Broader Applicability:** The structured reasoning paradigm and robust training approach are generalizable. They can inform the development of next-generation AI systems that need to process and reason over diverse and complex multimodal data, including scientific papers, legal documents, medical images, and educational materials.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** EVisRAG opens several avenues for future research, such as exploring more dynamic retrieval mechanisms, incorporating interactive human feedback into the evidence collection phase, or extending the reward-scoped optimization to even more complex reasoning tasks with longer and more diverse chains of thought.

In conclusion, EVisRAG represents a significant advancement in building more intelligent, reliable, and interpretable visual retrieval-augmented generation systems. Its practical performance gains, coupled with its innovative methodological contributions, position it as a foundational work for future developments in multimodal AI.
