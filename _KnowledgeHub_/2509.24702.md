# Enhancing Physical Plausibility in Video Generation by Reasoning the Implausibility

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Western Australia and the University of Sydney developed a training-free framework to enhance the physical plausibility of videos generated by diffusion models. The method employs large language models to reason about implausible physical scenarios and then uses a novel synchronized decoupled guidance strategy during inference to steer video generation towards physically consistent outcomes, yielding improved adherence to physical laws.

## Problem

- Current text-to-video diffusion models frequently generate content that violates fundamental physical laws and commonsense, despite advancements in visual fidelity.
- User-provided prompts often lack explicit physical details, making it challenging for generative models to infer and adhere to complex physical behaviors.
- Conventional negative prompting is ineffective at suppressing subtle physical implausibilities due to lagged suppression and accumulated bias in the denoising trajectory.

## Method

- A Physics-Aware Reasoning (PAR) pipeline uses a large language model to analyze user prompts and construct targeted counterfactual prompts that describe physically implausible yet visually coherent behaviors.
- A Synchronized Decoupled Guidance (SDG) strategy was developed to overcome limitations of traditional negative prompting by incorporating Synchronized Directional Normalization (SDN) and Trajectory-Decoupled Denoising (TDD).
- The entire framework operates at inference time without requiring any re-training or ==fine-tuning== of the base text-to-==video diffusion== models.

## Results

- The framework consistently improved physics-related scores on both the PhyGenBench and VideoPhy benchmarks, with the Wan2.1-**14B** model achieving a **0.10** increase in PhyGenBench's Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA) score.
- Qualitative comparisons demonstrated enhanced physical plausibility, including more natural object dynamics, realistic material interactions, and smoother temporal evolutions in generated videos.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Physics-Aware Reasoning (PAR) module and the individual components of Synchronized Decoupled Guidance (SDN and TDD) are critical and complementary for achieving performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit reasoning about physical implausibility, enabled by LLMs, can effectively guide generative processes toward more physically consistent outcomes during inference.
> - Counterfactual prompts, when precisely structured and targeted to contradict specific physical laws, provide a more potent signal for suppressing undesired behaviors than generic negative prompts.
> - Addressing the limitations of conventional negative prompting through synchronized, direction-focused corrections and decoupled latent trajectories is crucial for consistently preventing the emergence of physically implausible content.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models, such as those employing Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), have enabled the generation of visually realistic video sequences across a diverse range of visual concepts and textual prompts. Models like Open-Sora, Cosmos, CogVideoX, and Wan2.1 have demonstrated significant progress in fidelity, temporal consistency, and adherence to user prompts. Many of these models leverage large-scale text-to-image backbones and augment them with temporal layers, benefiting from massive video-text datasets. Proprietary systems like Sora and Google DeepMind's Veo series have further showcased capabilities in generating long, high-fidelity videos with complex dynamics.

Despite these achievements in photorealism and temporal coherence, a persistent challenge in current video generative models is their frequent failure to adhere to fundamental physical laws and commonsense. Generated videos often depict physically implausible motions, such as objects defying gravity, fluids exhibiting unnatural behavior, or phase transitions occurring incorrectly. This limitation is significant because if generative video models are to function as general-purpose "world simulators," they must reliably respect physical principles, not solely aesthetic qualities.

The research community has recognized this gap, leading to the development of benchmarks specifically designed to evaluate physical plausibility. Examples include PhyGenBench, which assesses alignment with 27 physical laws across four domains, and VideoPhy, which uses human judgments to evaluate semantic adherence and physical commonsense. These evaluations consistently indicate that state-of-the-art models frequently violate physical commonsense, and that simply scaling models or refining prompts does not fully resolve the issue.

Existing efforts to address physical implausibility often involve integrating explicit physical constraints or training models with physics-aware data. Some approaches parse objects from static images to simulate rigid-body dynamics or employ differentiable physics simulations during training. Other methods, like PhyT2V, use large language and vision-language models for iterative prompt refinement, while DiffPhy, VideoREPA, and WISA incorporate structured physical signals or augmented training data requiring additional training or fine-tuning.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a training-free framework that addresses physical implausibility at inference time. It positions itself as a complementary approach to methods requiring re-training, offering a plug-and-play solution that enhances physical fidelity by explicitly reasoning about implausibility rather than implicitly learning it through data alone. The paper focuses on overcoming the limitations of conventional negative prompting when applied to subtle physical violations, thus establishing a new paradigm for physics-aware video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the physical plausibility of videos generated by diffusion models without requiring additional training or fine-tuning of the underlying generative model. This is achieved by explicitly reasoning about physically implausible scenarios and guiding the generative process away from such outcomes during inference.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed limitations and emerging needs in the field of video generation:

- **Inherent Limitations of Implicit Physical Reasoning:** Current text-to-video diffusion models learn physical reasoning implicitly from large-scale text-video datasets. This implicit learning process is resource-intensive, challenging to scale effectively, and often insufficient. Despite significant progress in visual fidelity, these models frequently produce motions that violate fundamental physical laws, leading to videos that are visually realistic but physically inconsistent.
- **Underspecification of User Prompts:** User-provided prompts typically describe surface-level visual content but often lack explicit details about the underlying physical processes, entities, interactions, or expected causal evolution. This underspecification makes it difficult for generative models to infer and adhere to complex physical behaviors without additional guidance. For instance, a prompt describing "water vapor contacting a cool glass surface" might omit the expected condensation process.
- **Need for General-Purpose World Simulators:** For video generative models to evolve into reliable "general-purpose world simulators," they must consistently respect physical commonsense. The current inability of models to adhere to basic physics undermines their utility in applications requiring accurate simulation or predictive capabilities.
- **Gaps in Existing Negative Prompting:** While negative prompting is a technique commonly used in diffusion models to suppress undesired semantic attributes, the authors identify two critical limitations when attempting to suppress physical implausibility through this naive application:
 - **Lagged Suppression Effect:** In the early stages of denoising, when the latent space is close to isotropic Gaussian noise, the discrepancy vector between positive and negative prompt conditions is small. This means negative prompting has minimal influence on the coarse, low-frequency structures that define global layout. Only in later denoising steps, when these structures are largely established, does the suppression become appreciable, acting as a retroactive corrector rather than a proactive preventer of implausible content.
 - **Cumulative Trajectory Bias:** The denoiser's predictions for both positive and negative prompts are typically conditioned on the *same* latent trajectory. This shared trajectory is predominantly shaped by the positive prompt in early steps, causing an accumulated bias. Consequently, the negative prompt is forced to operate on latents that have already inherited undesirable physical biases, limiting its ability to fully eliminate "locked-in" implausible structures.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to provide a practical, efficient, and effective method for improving the physical fidelity of generated videos, thereby pushing video generation models closer to the goal of physically coherent world simulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is structured around two key components: (i) a **Physics-Aware Reasoning (PAR)** pipeline for constructing targeted counterfactual prompts, and (ii) a novel **Synchronized Decoupled Guidance (SDG)** strategy designed to leverage these prompts effectively during the video generation process. The entire framework operates at inference time and requires no re-training or fine-tuning of the base diffusion model.

#### 3.1. Physics-Aware Reasoning (PAR) for Counterfactual Construction

The PAR pipeline utilizes a large language model (LLM) to systematically generate counterfactual prompts that encode physically implausible yet visually plausible behaviors. This pipeline addresses the underspecification of typical user prompts and ensures that counterfactuals are targeted and structured.

1. **Physics Reasoning:** Given a user prompt (e.g., "A tennis ball is gently placed on the surface of a bucket filled with water"), the LLM first performs a structured analysis. It identifies relevant attributes, including:
 - **Entities:** (e.g., tennis ball, bucket, water)
 - **Interactions:** (e.g., buoyancy between ball and water)
 - **Environmental Conditions:** (e.g., open air)
 - **Temporal Evolution:** (e.g., the ball would float)
 This step enriches the prompt with necessary physical context and provides a description of how the event would typically unfold according to physical laws.
2. **Counterfactual Construction:** Based on the detailed physical reasoning, the LLM then synthesizes a counterfactual prompt. This counterfactual is designed to:
 - Preserve the same entities and scene context as the original prompt.
 - Deliberately violate the governing physical law or expected causal chain identified in the reasoning step.
 - Remain visually plausible, meaning the described violation is conceptually understandable even if physically impossible (e.g., "A tennis ball... but instead of floating, it immediately sinks straight to the bottom without resistance, as if its density were far greater than that of water.").

The necessity for PAR lies in ensuring that counterfactuals are *targeted* and *structured*. Without explicit physics reasoning, a generic approach might generate irrelevant or unintended physical violations (e.g., "vapor instantly freezes into solid ice upon contact" instead of violating condensation). PAR ensures the counterfactual directly contradicts the intended physical principle while maintaining visual coherence, providing an effective signal for subsequent guidance.

#### 3.2. Synchronized Decoupled Guidance (SDG)

SDG is a novel guidance mechanism proposed to overcome the limitations of conventional negative prompting (specifically, lagged suppression and cumulative trajectory bias) when enforcing physical plausibility.

##### 3.2.1. Gaps in Existing Negative Prompt Guidance (Classifier-Free Guidance based Negative Prompting)

The paper first identifies two fundamental issues with applying standard negative prompting (which interpolates between positive, negative, and often unconditional noise estimates) to physical plausibility:

1. **Lagged Suppression Effect:** In early denoising steps, when the latent representation `x_t` is highly noisy, the discrepancy (`Δ_t`) between the conditional noise predictions for positive (`p_+`) and negative (`p_-`) prompts is small. This means the negative prompt's suppressive effect is minimal when the global, low-frequency structures (like object placement and scene layout) are being formed by the positive prompt. The suppression only becomes significant in later steps, acting as a reactive correction rather than a proactive prevention.
2. **Cumulative Trajectory Bias:** In traditional negative prompting, both the positive and negative noise predictions are conditioned on the *same* evolving latent state `x_t`. This `x_t` is primarily shaped by the positive prompt's influence from earlier steps, leading to an accumulated bias in the latent trajectory. Consequently, the negative prompt's ability to suppress undesired physical phenomena is constrained because it's trying to correct content already "locked in" by the positive prompt's earlier conditioning.

##### 3.2.2. SDG Designs to Overcome Gaps

To directly address these limitations, SDG integrates two complementary designs:

1. **Synchronized Directional Normalization (SDN):** To mitigate the lagged suppression effect, SDG normalizes the directional difference between the noise estimates of the positive and counterfactual prompts. Instead of relying on the raw magnitude of `Δ_t` (which is small early on), SDN focuses on its direction. The guidance applies a consistent, direction-focused correction `λ * (Δ_t / ||Δ_t||)` from the earliest denoising steps. This ensures the counterfactual prompt's suppressive influence is "temporally synchronized" with the positive prompt's constructive effect, proactively preventing implausible structures from emerging.
2. **Trajectory-Decoupled Denoising (TDD):** To address cumulative trajectory bias, SDG decouples the conditioning paths. Instead of a single latent trajectory `x_t`, two separate latents are evolved in parallel: `x_+t` for the user prompt `p_+` and `x_-t` for the counterfactual prompt `p_-`. This means the counterfactual branch is not biased by the accumulated physical errors introduced by the user prompt's conditioning on a shared trajectory.

##### 3.2.3. Overall SDG Mechanism

By integrating both SDN and TDD, SDG transforms the guidance from a late-stage, biased corrector into an early-stage, unbiased preventer. The final adjusted noise prediction `ˆϵ_+` for the positive branch is calculated by taking the conditional noise estimate for `x_+t` (which includes standard classifier-free guidance for `p_+`) and adding a normalized, direction-aware contrastive term derived from the decoupled `ϵ_+` and `ϵ_-` noise predictions. This ensures that suppression is both non-delayed and unbiased, maximizing the utility of the reasoning-based physical counterfactuals as proactive constraints against implausible content throughout the denoising process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed training-free framework in enhancing physical plausibility in video generation.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons

The method was evaluated on two open-source text-to-video diffusion models: CogVideoX-5B and Wan2.1-14B, using two established benchmarks: PhyGenBench and VideoPhy.

- **Overall Performance:** The addition of the training-free Synchronized Decoupled Guidance (SDG) framework consistently improved physics-related scores on both backbones. Gains were modest for CogVideoX-5B but more pronounced for Wan2.1-14B, with PhyGenBench's Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA) score increasing from 0.40 to 0.50. On VideoPhy, improvements in Semantic Adherence (SA) and Physical Commonsense (PC) were also observed.
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art:** The SDG-enhanced variants were competitive with or stronger than several earlier base models on VideoPhy and PhyGenBench. Notably, the approach achieved comparable or superior performance to physics-aware models that require additional training or bespoke modules (e.g., PhyT2V, DiffPhy, VideoREPA, WISA), despite SDG being an inference-time-only strategy.
- **Domain-Specific Improvements:** When evaluated across different physical domains on PhyGenBench (mechanics, optics, thermal, and material interactions), the method showed improvements in PCA scores across all four categories. For Wan2.1-14B, particularly significant increases were observed in thermal (0.36 → 0.51) and mechanics (0.36 → 0.47) domains, indicating generalizability across diverse physical phenomena.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparisons

Qualitative results further illustrate the enhanced physical plausibility:

- **Mechanics (Bouncing Ball):** The baseline Wan2.1-14B generated a tennis ball motion inconsistent with gravity, showing limited deformation and abrupt transitions. The proposed method produced a more natural trajectory, visible compression upon impact, and a smoother rebound, aligning with expected elastic behavior.
- **Material Interaction (Highlighter on Cardboard):** CogVideoX-5B struggled to capture the proper interaction, with strokes appearing flat and disconnected. The proposed method generated strokes that adhered naturally to the surface, with ink blending seamlessly into the cardboard texture.
- **Other Examples (Appendix):** Additional qualitative comparisons demonstrated improved fluid-object interactions (e.g., spoon in water with realistic refraction), smoother material transformations (e.g., butter melting with rising vapor), and more faithful object dynamics (e.g., fire propagating gradually on hay).
- **GPT-4o Evaluations:** GPT-4o assessments invoked via PhyGenBench's automatic evaluator generally rated videos generated by the proposed method as more physically plausible than those from baseline models.
- **Comparison with Negative Prompting (NP):** The research explicitly compared the proposed approach with naive negative prompting within classifier-free guidance. It found that NP yielded only limited improvements, while SDG effectively mitigated the shortcomings, demonstrating the specific advantages of its synchronized and decoupled designs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on PhyGenBench to ascertain the individual contributions of the Physics-Aware Reasoning (PAR) module and the two designs within Synchronized Decoupled Guidance (SDG): Synchronized Directional Normalization (SDN) and Trajectory-Decoupled Denoising (TDD).

- **Overall Framework:** The full version of the framework achieved the highest average PCA score (0.50 for Wan2.1-14B).
- **Impact of SDG:** Removing the entire SDG module (i.e., both SDN and TDD) led to a significant performance drop (average PCA of 0.43), indicating the critical role of the guidance mechanism.
- **Individual SDG Components:** Removing either SDN or TDD individually resulted in noticeable performance degradation (average PCA of 0.47–0.48). This confirmed that both SDN (addressing lagged suppression) and TDD (addressing cumulative trajectory bias) make complementary and crucial contributions to suppressing implausible content.
- **Impact of PAR:** Replacing the LLM-generated structured counterfactual prompts (PAR) with the default generic negative prompt used in the baseline model reduced performance (average PCA of 0.47). While still better than the baseline, this confirmed that PAR provides more targeted and physics-aware counterfactuals, which are essential for SDG's effectiveness. Qualitative ablation showed that without PAR, counterfactuals were generic and introduced irrelevant violations, whereas with PAR, they targeted specific, meaningful physical law violations.

#### 4.4. Limitation

The research identifies that despite overall improvements, chemical reaction scenarios remain challenging. Even when the PAR pipeline generates accurate counterfactual prompts, the video generation model may still struggle to render complex chemical processes realistically, often exhibiting unnatural color changes or implausible redox dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a training-free framework for enhancing physical plausibility in diffusion-based video generation, establishing a novel paradigm with several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Novel Training-Free Paradigm:** The work presents a plug-and-play, inference-time-only method that significantly improves physical fidelity without requiring any re-training or fine-tuning of existing diffusion models. This offers a practical and resource-efficient solution, allowing broad applicability to a wide range of pre-trained video generation models. This is particularly valuable given the high computational cost of training large-scale generative models.
- **Explicit Reasoning for Plausibility:** By introducing a Physics-Aware Reasoning (PAR) pipeline, the framework moves beyond implicit learning of physical laws. It demonstrates that explicitly reasoning about physical implausibility through structured counterfactual prompts can effectively guide generative processes. This highlights the potential of integrating large language models (LLMs) to inject high-level physical intelligence into purely data-driven generative models.
- **Overcoming Negative Prompting Limitations:** The Synchronized Decoupled Guidance (SDG) strategy directly addresses fundamental limitations of conventional negative prompting, namely the lagged suppression effect and cumulative trajectory bias. By proposing synchronized directional normalization and trajectory-decoupled denoising, the research provides a robust mechanism to ensure that undesirable content is suppressed immediately and consistently throughout the entire denoising process, a significant advancement for controllable generation.
- **Advancing World Simulation:** The ability to generate physically plausible videos is a critical step towards developing more accurate and reliable "world simulators." These simulators have broad potential applications in fields such as robotics, virtual reality, scientific research, and complex system design, where adherence to physical laws is paramount for realistic interaction and prediction.
- **Improved Generative Model Control and Reliability:** The framework enhances the trustworthiness and utility of video generative models. By reducing physical inconsistencies, the generated content becomes more reliable for practical applications and provides a more faithful representation of real-world phenomena, fostering greater user confidence.
- **Complementary Approach to Existing Methods:** This work complements existing physics-aware approaches that rely on additional training or specialized datasets. It provides an alternative or supplementary strategy for scenarios where re-training is not feasible or desired, and it can potentially be combined with future training-based methods for even greater impact.

In summary, the research contributes a principled and effective methodology for embedding physical commonsense into video generation. It demonstrates that a combination of structured reasoning and carefully designed inference-time guidance can substantially elevate the physical fidelity of generated videos, paving the way for more intelligent and physically grounded generative AI systems.
