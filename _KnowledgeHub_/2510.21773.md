# Real-Time QP Solvers: A Concise Review and Practical Guide Towards Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This review compiles existing benchmarking data on Quadratic Programming (QP) solvers for legged robots, providing practical guidance for selection in Model Predictive Control and Whole-Body Control based on problem type, hardware, and energy efficiency. It demonstrates ARM-based platforms' high Solve Frequency per Watt for embedded real-time optimization.

## Problem

- Legged robots require real-time optimization with stringent latency, energy, and computational constraints on embedded hardware for modules like state estimation, motion planning, and dynamic control.
- Selecting appropriate Quadratic Programming (QP) solvers is complex due to diverse QP problem structures (e.g., sparse long-horizon MPC vs. small, dense high-frequency WBC) and a wide array of available algorithmic approaches.
- Previous solver comparisons often prioritize solve time and numerical accuracy, overlooking energy efficiency, which is a critical factor for battery-powered robotic systems.

## Method

- Systematically reviewed and classified Quadratic Programming (**QP**) solver families, including Active-Set, Interior-Point, Operator-Splitting, and Augmented Lagrangian methods, along with code generation tools and conic solvers.
- Consolidated and analyzed publicly available benchmarking data from resources such as `qpbenchmark` and `qpmad_benchmark`, as well as a detailed study evaluating six solvers on ==MPC== and WBC problems.
- Evaluated solver performance based on solve time, numerical accuracy, feasibility, and the energy-normalized Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW) across different hardware platforms (desktop x86, embedded x86, embedded ARM).

## Results

- For sparse MPC QPs, HPIPM (structured IPM) consistently showed the lowest solve times, while OSQP (operator-splitting) provided robust performance at moderate accuracy targets.
- For small, dense WBC QPs, Eiquadprog and qpmad (active-set methods) achieved sub-millisecond solve times, with qpmad demonstrating up to a twofold speedup over Eiquadprog due to optimized overhead.
- The NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (ARM-based) platform achieved the highest SFPW across various solver-problem combinations, indicating its efficiency for onboard real-time optimization under strict energy budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single QP solver universally dominates all scenarios; optimal selection depends on a synergistic alignment of solver family with the QP's structure, required accuracy, hardware characteristics, and energy constraints.
> - The energy-normalized Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW) metric is crucial for battery-powered robots, with ARM-based embedded platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX) demonstrating the highest SFPW.
> - Warm-starting capabilities are vital for repetitive real-time control, and specialized code generation tools offer highly optimized, predictable timing for fixed-structure QPs suitable for hard real-time embedded deployment.
