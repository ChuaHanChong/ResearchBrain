# Real-Time QP Solvers: A Concise Review and Practical Guide Towards Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This review compiles existing benchmarking data on Quadratic Programming (QP) solvers for legged robots, providing practical guidance for selection in Model Predictive Control and Whole-Body Control based on problem type, hardware, and energy efficiency. It demonstrates ARM-based platforms' high Solve Frequency per Watt for embedded real-time optimization.

## Problem

- Legged robots require real-time optimization with stringent latency, energy, and computational constraints on embedded hardware for modules like state estimation, motion planning, and dynamic control.
- Selecting appropriate Quadratic Programming (QP) solvers is complex due to diverse QP problem structures (e.g., sparse long-horizon MPC vs. small, dense high-frequency WBC) and a wide array of available algorithmic approaches.
- Previous solver comparisons often prioritize solve time and numerical accuracy, overlooking energy efficiency, which is a critical factor for battery-powered robotic systems.

## Method

- Systematically reviewed and classified Quadratic Programming (**QP**) solver families, including Active-Set, Interior-Point, Operator-Splitting, and Augmented Lagrangian methods, along with code generation tools and conic solvers.
- Consolidated and analyzed publicly available benchmarking data from resources such as `qpbenchmark` and `qpmad_benchmark`, as well as a detailed study evaluating six solvers on ==MPC== and WBC problems.
- Evaluated solver performance based on solve time, numerical accuracy, feasibility, and the energy-normalized Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW) across different hardware platforms (desktop x86, embedded x86, embedded ARM).

## Results

- For sparse MPC QPs, HPIPM (structured IPM) consistently showed the lowest solve times, while OSQP (operator-splitting) provided robust performance at moderate accuracy targets.
- For small, dense WBC QPs, Eiquadprog and qpmad (active-set methods) achieved sub-millisecond solve times, with qpmad demonstrating up to a twofold speedup over Eiquadprog due to optimized overhead.
- The NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (ARM-based) platform achieved the highest SFPW across various solver-problem combinations, indicating its efficiency for onboard real-time optimization under strict energy budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single QP solver universally dominates all scenarios; optimal selection depends on a synergistic alignment of solver family with the QP's structure, required accuracy, hardware characteristics, and energy constraints.
> - The energy-normalized Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW) metric is crucial for battery-powered robots, with ARM-based embedded platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX) demonstrating the highest SFPW.
> - Warm-starting capabilities are vital for repetitive real-time control, and specialized code generation tools offer highly optimized, predictable timing for fixed-structure QPs suitable for hard real-time embedded deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Real-time optimization is a fundamental component in the control architectures of advanced robotic systems, particularly legged robots. This encompasses various modules such as state estimation, motion planning, and dynamic control. Quadratic Programming (QP) provides a framework for formulating control objectives and constraints in a convex, computationally efficient, and theoretically grounded manner. Consequently, QP is extensively utilized in legged and humanoid robot control stacks, where operations are subject to stringent latency, energy, and computational resource constraints on embedded platforms.

Within standard legged robot control, QP manifests in two primary contexts:
1. **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** MPC typically computes contact forces or center-of-mass trajectories over a finite horizon. These problems often result in sparse QPs with optimal-control-specific structures.
2. **Whole-Body Control (WBC):** WBC translates high-level commands into joint torques or velocities, subject to various constraints including actuation limits, friction cones, and contact conditions. This generally leads to smaller to medium-sized dense QPs that require solving at high frequencies (100–1000 Hz).

Prior research efforts have highlighted that the selection of an appropriate QP solver is dependent on the specific scenario and the structure of the problem. For instance, structure-exploiting interior-point solvers are often favored for sparse MPC QPs, while operator-splitting solvers are considered for large generic sparse QPs where moderate accuracy is sufficient. For dense WBC QPs, active-set solvers have historically been strong baselines. The energy cost of optimization, especially for battery-powered robots, has also emerged as a critical metric, leading to the development of energy-normalized metrics such as Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW) to facilitate comparisons across diverse hardware architectures. This paper contributes to this landscape by systematically reviewing solver families and consolidating existing benchmarking data to offer practical guidance for solver selection in legged robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:
1. To review and classify Quadratic Programming (QP) solver families that are relevant to real-time control applications in robotics. This includes summarizing representative solvers from both open-source and commercial ecosystems.
2. To consolidate and analyze publicly available benchmarking evidence, drawing from resources such as `qpbenchmark` and `qpmad_benchmark`. The aim is to translate these performance results into practical, actionable guidance for selecting QP solvers within Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Whole-Body Control (WBC) pipelines for legged robots.

The motivation for this work stems from the critical role of QP in modern legged robot control and the demanding operational environments of these systems. Legged robots operate under tight timing budgets, limited energy availability, and constrained computational resources, particularly on embedded hardware. The efficiency, reliability, numerical robustness, and warm-starting capabilities of QP solvers directly influence the feasibility and performance of closed-loop control. Given the diversity of QP problems arising in robotics (e.g., sparse long-horizon MPC vs. dense high-frequency WBC) and the array of available solver algorithms, there is a need for a unified understanding of solver characteristics and a practical guide for informed selection. The paper specifically highlights that previous comparisons often prioritize solve time and numerical accuracy, while overlooking energy efficiency, a crucial factor for battery-powered robotic systems. By incorporating energy-normalized metrics and offering scenario-specific recommendations, the research aims to support the development of agile and autonomous legged systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper employs a comprehensive approach that combines a theoretical review of QP formulations and solver algorithms with an analysis of existing empirical benchmarking data.

#### 3.1. Quadratic Programming Formulation

The report begins by formulating the standard convex QP problem. It details how nonlinear programs (NLPs), particularly in the context of Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP), can be approximated locally by QPs. The standard convex QP is expressed as:
`min (1/2) * x^T * H * x + q^T * x`
`s.t. A * x = b`
`C * x <= u`
where `x` is the decision variable, `H` is the Hessian matrix, `q` is the linear term vector, and `A*x=b` and `C*x<=u` represent equality and inequality constraints, respectively. For problems solely with equality constraints, the solution can be derived from the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) system.

#### 3.2. QP Solver Classification and Algorithmic Approaches

The paper classifies QP solvers into four main algorithmic families commonly used in robotics, also discussing code generation tools and conic solvers:

- **Active-Set Methods:** These methods iteratively identify the set of active inequality constraints and solve equality-constrained subproblems. They are effective for small-to-medium dense QPs where the active set changes slowly, allowing for strong warm-starting. Representative solvers include qpOASES, Eiquadprog, qpmad, and DAQP.
- **Interior-Point Methods (IPMs):** IPMs approach the feasible region from the interior by introducing a barrier term for inequality constraints. Structured IPMs, such as HPIPM, exploit optimal control problem (OCP) sparsity common in MPC. Sparse robotics-oriented IPMs include qpSWIFT. Proximal IPM variants like PIQP target ill-conditioned sparse QPs while aiming for embedded-friendly implementations.
- **Operator-Splitting Methods (e.g., ADMM):** These solvers introduce slack variables for inequalities and typically decompose the problem into smaller, easier-to-solve subproblems. They aim for predictable per-iteration costs and effective warm-starting. OSQP is a widely used example in this category.
- **Augmented Lagrangian/Proximal Methods:** These methods minimize a penalized Lagrangian, often exhibiting robustness in contact-rich scenarios. ProxQP is a representative solver in robotics that utilizes this approach.

Additionally, the paper discusses:
- **Code Generation Tools:** For fixed-structure QPs where dimensions and sparsity patterns are constant, tools like CVXGEN, CVXPYgen, and FORCESPRO can generate specialized C solvers. These are optimized for tight footprints and predictable timing, suitable for embedded deployment.
- **Conic Solvers:** When problems involve conic constraints beyond standard QP inequalities or require certificates, conic IPMs such as Clarabel offer a general convex optimization route, although they may introduce overhead compared to QP-specialized solvers.

#### 3.3. Benchmarking and Performance Evaluation Framework

The paper does not present new primary benchmarking results but consolidates and synthesizes findings from existing, publicly available benchmarks, specifically referencing:
- `qpbenchmark`: This suite includes convex QPs derived from linear MPC tasks (e.g., cart-pole balancing, humanoid walking) and evaluates metrics such as solve time, residuals, and duality gaps across various solvers and tolerances.
- `qpmad_benchmark`: This benchmark focuses on evaluating dense QP solvers (qpmad, qpOASES, Eiquadprog) commonly used for condensed MPC and inverse-dynamics formulations.
- Stark et al.: A detailed benchmarking study that evaluated six solvers (HPIPM, PROXQP, OSQP, qpOASES, Eiquadprog, qpSWIFT) on MPC and WBC problems. This study assessed both dense and sparse QP formulations across different hardware platforms (desktop x86 CPU, LattePanda Alpha x86 embedded, NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX ARM) using solve time, feasibility metrics, and the energy-normalized **Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW)**.

The paper uses unified comparison tables (Table 1 and Table 2 in the original text) to summarize the characteristics, advantages, limitations, and best-suited scenarios for various solver families and representative solvers. These tables consider attributes like warm-starting, accuracy, early termination support, real-time suitability, and support for dual-gap or iteration-based termination.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The analysis of consolidated benchmarking evidence yielded several key findings regarding the performance and suitability of QP solvers for real-time control in legged robotics:

#### 4.1. Solver Performance Across Problem Types and Hardware

- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):**
 - For **sparse QP formulations** arising from long-horizon optimal control problems (OCP), **HPIPM** (a structure-exploiting Interior-Point Method) consistently demonstrated the lowest solve times. Its ability to leverage structured sparsity made it a strong candidate for embedded real-time MPC, with performance scaling well with prediction horizon.
 - **OSQP** (an Operator-Splitting solver) provided robust and consistent solve times, particularly at moderate accuracy targets, and required minimal tuning. However, it typically required more iterations to achieve high-precision solutions compared to IPMs.
 - **qpOASES** (an Active-Set solver) performed well on smaller-horizon MPC problems but became less competitive as problem size increased, likely due to the complexities of active-set changes in larger QPs.
- **Whole-Body Control (WBC):**
 - For **small, dense QPs** typical in WBC and inverse dynamics, **Eiquadprog** (a classical active-set method) achieved sub-millisecond solve times across all tested hardware platforms, making it suitable for high-frequency torque control.
 - **PROXQP** (an Augmented Lagrangian approach) also exhibited reliable performance in WBC tasks, demonstrating stable solve times on both desktop and embedded hardware. It completed all test cases, indicating favorable numerical robustness in practice, even if not explicitly benchmarked for ill-conditioning.
 - **qpSWIFT** (a sparse primal-dual IPM designed for robotics) generally underperformed relative to other solvers in this dense WBC setting and showed weaker numerical robustness.

#### 4.2. Energy Efficiency and Hardware Interaction

- The **NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (ARM-based)** embedded platform achieved the highest **Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW)** across experiments. This was particularly evident when paired with OSQP for MPC and with Eiquadprog or PROXQP for WBC tasks. This highlights the potential of ARM-based hardware for onboard real-time optimization under strict energy constraints. The SFPW metric provided a fairer basis for comparing performance across heterogeneous CPU architectures.

#### 4.3. Specialized Solver Performance

- **qpmad** (a lightweight C++ dual active-set solver) consistently outperformed Eiquadprog on small- to medium-sized dense QPs, achieving up to a twofold speedup. Its efficiency is attributed to optimized memory access, low overhead, and minimal dependencies, making it attractive for high-frequency control.
- **DAQP** (a dual active-set solver) is relevant for fully condensed MPC QPs with fixed structure, supporting aggressive warm-starting and efficient recursive LDL updates. It is not intended for large, generic sparse problems.
- **PIQP** (a proximal interior-point solver) targets sparse and dense QPs with an emphasis on ill-conditioning. It combines a proximal mechanism with an IPM core, offering numerical stability and allocation-free updates for embedded targets.
- **Code Generation Tools** (FORCESPRO, CVXGEN, CVXPYgen) are compelling for hard real-time deployment when problem structure and dimensions are fixed. They generate highly optimized C code, prioritizing predictable timing and small footprints at the expense of flexibility if the problem formulation changes.
- **Clarabel** (a conic IPM) broadens the modeling scope to include conic constraints beyond standard QPs. While providing a principled convex optimization route, its generality can introduce overhead compared to QP-specialized solvers in tight real-time loops.

#### 4.4. General Observations

- **No single solver universally dominates** all scenarios. Optimal performance arises from a synergistic alignment of solver family with the QP's structure, required accuracy, hardware characteristics, and energy constraints.
- **Warm-starting** capabilities are crucial for repetitive real-time control tasks, with active-set methods benefiting significantly when the active set remains stable, and operator-splitting methods leveraging factorization caching.
- **Robustness** to ill-conditioning and infeasibility is a practical consideration, with solvers like ProxQP and PIQP specifically addressing these challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in this paper contribute to the advancement of real-time control for legged robotic systems by providing a structured and evidence-based guide for Quadratic Programming (QP) solver selection.

The significance and potential impact include:

- **Informed Decision-Making for Robotics Engineers:** The consolidated review and practical guidance offer clear recommendations for choosing QP solvers based on specific application requirements (e.g., MPC vs. WBC), problem characteristics (sparse vs. dense, small vs. large horizon), and hardware platforms (embedded ARM vs. x86). This reduces the trial-and-error process for system developers and aids in optimizing control architectures.
- **Improved Legged Robot Performance:** By facilitating the selection of solvers that balance speed, accuracy, robustness, and energy efficiency, the paper supports the development of more agile, autonomous, and energy-efficient legged robots. This can lead to enhanced dynamic locomotion, manipulation capabilities, and overall operational endurance.
- **Emphasis on Energy Efficiency:** The inclusion and analysis of the Solve Frequency per Watt (SFPW) metric highlight the importance of energy consumption in real-time optimization, particularly for battery-powered robots. This promotes a more holistic approach to solver evaluation, driving the adoption of energy-efficient solutions and hardware.
- **Guidance for Embedded Systems Design:** The evaluation across different embedded platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX) provides valuable insights for hardware selection and optimization strategies in resource-constrained environments, underscoring the potential of ARM-based platforms.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies emerging trends such as ill-conditioned, conic, and code-generated QP deployments, as well as future work including GPU-based solvers, distributed MPC integration, and end-to-end evaluation of jitter and energy metrics. This provides a roadmap for further research and development in the field of real-time optimization for robotics.
- **Resource for the Robotics Community:** By consolidating information on various solver families, their algorithmic structures, computational characteristics, and practical trade-offs in an accessible format, the paper serves as a valuable reference for both researchers and practitioners in robotics.

In summary, this work provides a practical bridge between the theoretical understanding of QP solvers and their real-world application in the demanding context of legged robotics, directly impacting the design and performance of future autonomous systems.
