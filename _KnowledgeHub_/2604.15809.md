# Aligning What Vision-Language Models See and Perceive with Adaptive Information Flow

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST and POSTECH introduce Adaptive Information Flow (AIF), a training-free method that enhances Vision-Language Model perception by dynamically modulating the causal mask during inference. AIF selectively blocks interaction between irrelevant visual tokens and text tokens based on visual token entropy, leading to performance improvements on benchmarks like V* (+7.9%), RealWorldQA (+4.9%), TextVQA (+2.1%), and reducing hallucination on POPE (+3.3%) for LLaVA-1.5-7B, with minimal computational cost.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often exhibit a "misalignment between seeing and perception," where they visually attend to relevant image regions but still produce incorrect answers.
- Text tokens in VLMs frequently disperse cross-attention too broadly, drawing noisy visual information from irrelevant background regions during decoding.
- Existing methods to address VLM limitations typically require extensive model retraining, architectural modifications, or are limited to post-hoc output corrections.

## Method

- Developed **AIF** (==Adaptive Information Flow==), a training-free, inference-time method that dynamically modulates the internal information flow within VLMs by selectively altering the ==causal mask==.
- Quantifies ==token dynamics== (how visual tokens attend to text tokens across layers) using ==entropy== to identify and prioritize important visual tokens, which exhibit less random activation patterns.
- Blocks interactions between high-entropy (irrelevant) visual tokens and text tokens within the causal mask, centralizing the VLM's cross-modal attention on critical image regions.

## Results

- AIF improved general VQA performance for LLaVA-1.5-7B on V* (**+7.9%**), RealWorldQA (**+4.9%**), and MMStar (**+6.4%**), with similar gains for Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- Enhanced OCR understanding on TextVQA (**+2.1%** for LLaVA-1.5-7B) and SeedBench2-Plus (**+3.6%**), and improved visual grounding on RefCOCO/+/g (average **+2.3%** for Qwen2.5-VL-7B).
- Reduced object hallucination on POPE for LLaVA-1.5-7B (**+3.3%**) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (**+1.7%**), demonstrating increased VLM reliability with minimal additional inference cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The observed misalignment in VLMs stems from suboptimal information flow, where text tokens incorporate distracting visual information from irrelevant image parts.
> - Visual tokens corresponding to important object regions display distinct and less random activation patterns across VLM layers compared to irrelevant background tokens, which can be quantified by entropy.
> - Modulating the internal information flow by adaptively altering the causal mask during inference can significantly improve VLM perceptual accuracy and mitigate issues like hallucination without retraining the model.
