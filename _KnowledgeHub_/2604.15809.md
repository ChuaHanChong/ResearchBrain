# Aligning What Vision-Language Models See and Perceive with Adaptive Information Flow

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST and POSTECH introduce Adaptive Information Flow (AIF), a training-free method that enhances Vision-Language Model perception by dynamically modulating the causal mask during inference. AIF selectively blocks interaction between irrelevant visual tokens and text tokens based on visual token entropy, leading to performance improvements on benchmarks like V* (+7.9%), RealWorldQA (+4.9%), TextVQA (+2.1%), and reducing hallucination on POPE (+3.3%) for LLaVA-1.5-7B, with minimal computational cost.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often exhibit a "misalignment between seeing and perception," where they visually attend to relevant image regions but still produce incorrect answers.
- Text tokens in VLMs frequently disperse cross-attention too broadly, drawing noisy visual information from irrelevant background regions during decoding.
- Existing methods to address VLM limitations typically require extensive model retraining, architectural modifications, or are limited to post-hoc output corrections.

## Method

- Developed **AIF** (==Adaptive Information Flow==), a training-free, inference-time method that dynamically modulates the internal information flow within VLMs by selectively altering the ==causal mask==.
- Quantifies ==token dynamics== (how visual tokens attend to text tokens across layers) using ==entropy== to identify and prioritize important visual tokens, which exhibit less random activation patterns.
- Blocks interactions between high-entropy (irrelevant) visual tokens and text tokens within the causal mask, centralizing the VLM's cross-modal attention on critical image regions.

## Results

- AIF improved general VQA performance for LLaVA-1.5-7B on V* (**+7.9%**), RealWorldQA (**+4.9%**), and MMStar (**+6.4%**), with similar gains for Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- Enhanced OCR understanding on TextVQA (**+2.1%** for LLaVA-1.5-7B) and SeedBench2-Plus (**+3.6%**), and improved visual grounding on RefCOCO/+/g (average **+2.3%** for Qwen2.5-VL-7B).
- Reduced object hallucination on POPE for LLaVA-1.5-7B (**+3.3%**) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (**+1.7%**), demonstrating increased VLM reliability with minimal additional inference cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The observed misalignment in VLMs stems from suboptimal information flow, where text tokens incorporate distracting visual information from irrelevant image parts.
> - Visual tokens corresponding to important object regions display distinct and less random activation patterns across VLM layers compared to irrelevant background tokens, which can be quantified by entropy.
> - Modulating the internal information flow by adaptively altering the causal mask during inference can significantly improve VLM perceptual accuracy and mitigate issues like hallucination without retraining the model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across a range of tasks, including visual recognition, document parsing, and visual grounding. These models integrate visual and linguistic information to perform complex multimodal understanding and reasoning. However, a documented challenge in VLM research is the "misalignment between seeing and perception." This refers to instances where a VLM may visually attend to the correct or relevant image region in response to a query but subsequently fail to produce the correct answer. This phenomenon suggests that merely "seeing" the right region does not guarantee accurate "perception" or reasoning for generating the correct output.

Prior research has attempted to address this misalignment through several avenues:
- **Training-free methods:** Approaches like visual cropping aim to enhance fine-grained perception by magnifying question-relevant regions. While effective in some scenarios, these methods often incur increased inference time and may show limited improvement for tasks requiring relational reasoning or counting.
- **Training-based methods:** Strengthening the interaction between image and text often involves re-training the model, which typically requires substantial computational resources and large datasets.
- **Information flow analysis:** Existing work in this area primarily focuses on diagnosing and interpreting how visual and linguistic information propagates through VLM layers, identifying critical fusion points, or improving efficiency through token pruning. These analyses are often descriptive or lead to post-hoc output corrections rather than dynamically altering internal processing pathways during inference.
- **Causal Mask modifications:** The standard left-to-right causal masking, inherited from language models, can restrict cross-modal interaction. Some studies propose architectural, positional, or training-time modifications to the causal mask to enhance interaction.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a training-free, inference-time method that directly modulates the internal information flow within VLMs by selectively altering the causal mask. This approach differs from previous efforts by focusing on adaptive, pathway-level control based on the dynamics of visual tokens, without requiring model retraining, architectural changes, or significant computational overhead. It positions itself as an alternative to computationally intensive retraining or limited post-hoc corrections, offering a dynamic regulation of how modalities interact within the model during inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the perception capability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by addressing the observed misalignment between what VLMs visually attend to ("seeing") and the accuracy of their generated answers ("perception").

The motivation for this work stems from a specific observation:
1. **Problem Identification:** The authors identify that despite VLMs often focusing their visual attention on relevant image regions in response to a question, they frequently produce incorrect answers. This suggests a disconnect where the visual information, even if initially directed correctly, is not effectively utilized or is corrupted during subsequent processing.
2. **Hypothesized Cause (Suboptimal Information Flow):** The paper posits that this misalignment can be attributed to suboptimal information flow within the VLM during the decoding process. Specifically, text tokens in the language model may distribute their cross-attention too broadly, including to irrelevant visual tokens in the background or other distracting regions. This dispersion of attention is hypothesized to introduce noisy visual information into the textual decoding pathway, thereby distracting the model and leading to incorrect answers. An example provided in the paper (Fig. 1a) illustrates instruction tokens spreading attention across many background visual tokens, leading to a spatially dispersed attention pattern rather than concentrating on the object region crucial for the correct answer.
3. **Proposed Solution Principle (Information Flow Modulation):** Based on this hypothesis, the core motivation is to demonstrate that actively modulating this information flow during inference can improve a VLM's perceptual accuracy. The underlying idea is that text tokens should primarily associate with, and derive information from, only the most important visual tokens, while suppressing interference from irrelevant visual regions.
4. **Specific Objective: Training-Free Adaptive Modulation:** The detailed objective is to develop a training-free method capable of identifying these "important" visual tokens and then selectively blocking the interaction between "irrelevant" visual tokens and text tokens via causal mask modulation during inference. This aims to re-route an effective information pathway towards critical image regions, thereby improving answer accuracy without the need for extensive retraining or architectural modifications. The paper highlights the observation that visual tokens corresponding to object regions exhibit distinct activation patterns across different decoding layers compared to irrelevant regions, suggesting that these "token dynamics" could serve as a basis for identifying importance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Adaptive Information Flow (AIF) modulation, aims to improve VLM perception by dynamically controlling cross-modal information flow during inference. This is achieved without model retraining, architectural changes, or auxiliary modules.

The approach involves the following steps:

1. **Preliminary: VLM Workflow and Causal Mask:**
 - VLMs process an input image and text prompt by first converting them into visual tokens and text tokens. These tokens are concatenated and passed through a language model, which generates answers autoregressively.
 - During language decoding, visual and text tokens interact via an attention mechanism, constrained by a causal mask. A standard causal mask (Fig. 2a) strictly limits information flow, preventing tokens from attending to future tokens in the sequence. The authors state that modulating this causal mask can directly reshape cross-modal information flow and influence model performance.

2. **Quantifying Token Dynamics:**
 - To understand the interaction between visual and text tokens, the authors investigate "token dynamics," which refers to how visual tokens attend to text tokens across different layers of the language model.
 - The token dynamics for a visual token *v_i* are defined as a set *D_{v_i} = {d^l_{v_i}}* from *l=1* to *L*, where *d^l_{v_i}* is the maximum attention value between *v_i* and any text token in layer *l*. *L* is the total number of LLM layers.
 - The average value in *D_{v_i}* is denoted as *μ_{v_i} = (1/L) Σ d^l_{v_i}*.
 - Observation: Visual tokens corresponding to object regions often exhibit distinct, high activation patterns in specific LLM layers, while irrelevant regions show more irregular activation patterns (Fig. 1b, Fig. 4). This difference in dynamics forms the basis for measuring importance.

3. **Measuring the Importance of Visual Tokens using Entropy:**
 - Building on the observation that important and irrelevant visual tokens have distinct dynamics (specifically, randomness), the authors propose using entropy as a measure of a visual token's importance.
 - The entropy for each visual token *v_i* is computed based on its token dynamics as:
 - *Ent_{v_i} = Σ_{l=1 to L} [- (d^l_{v_i} / (L ⋅ μ_{v_i})) ⋅ log(d^l_{v_i} / (L ⋅ μ_{v_i}))]*
 - A high *Ent_{v_i}* value indicates high randomness in the token's activation pattern across layers, signifying lower importance. Consequently, tokens with higher entropy values are considered less important and are candidates for masking.

4. **Adaptive Information Flow Modulation:**
 - **Pipeline Overview (Fig. 6):**
 1. Input image and user prompt are encoded into visual and text tokens.
 2. A one-step language decoding pass is performed to collect internal attention statistics, specifically the token dynamics (*D_{v_i}*) for each visual token.
 3. Based on *D_{v_i}*, the token dynamics-based entropy (*Ent_{v_i}*) is computed for all visual tokens, creating a token entropy map.
 4. **Adaptive Masking Strategy:** The goal is to select a subset of visual tokens to mask such that the spatial distribution of visual-to-textual attention becomes more centralized on important regions. This is framed as an optimal thresholding problem.
 - The initial attention distribution of all visual tokens is quantified using an entropy measure *S₀ = Σ_{i=1 to M} [- (μ_{v_i} / Σ_k μ_{v_k}) ⋅ log(μ_{v_i} / Σ_k μ_{v_k})]*
 - A set of candidate mask ratios (e.g., 0.1 to 0.9, in 0.1 intervals) is considered. For each ratio, visual tokens are sorted by their *Ent_{v_i}* values (highest entropy first), and the top percentage are selected for masking.
 - For each candidate mask ratio, the attention distribution of the *retained* visual tokens is re-quantified (implicitly, by calculating a similar entropy score for the unmasked tokens).
 - The mask ratio that yields an entropy value "most distant from *S₀*" (implying a significant shift towards a less random, more centralized distribution of attention among the remaining tokens) is selected.
 5. **Causal Mask Modulation:** The interactions between the chosen masked visual tokens and *text tokens* are blocked in the causal mask. This is implemented by setting the corresponding entries in the causal mask to *−∞* (Fig. 2b). Crucially, the information flow *within visual tokens* remains unchanged, preserving all image content.
 6. The modulated causal mask is then fed into the language model for the final reasoning and answer generation.
 - **Computational Cost:** The entire process requires one additional LLM decoding step to gather token dynamics (roughly equivalent to generating one text token) and a computationally efficient causal mask generation step (milliseconds). This results in an inference cost similar to one token generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study extensively evaluates the proposed Adaptive Information Flow (AIF) method across a diverse set of tasks and VLM architectures, primarily LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

**Initial Investigations and Insights (Section 3):**
- **Impact of Visual Tokens:** Analysis revealed that only a subset of visual tokens significantly impacts model output. Visual tokens with low average attention (μ_vi) values had minimal effect on performance, even when 90% were masked. Conversely, masking visual tokens with high μ_vi values led to substantial performance degradation (e.g., over 50% relative drop on TextVQA with 10% masking). This indicates that high-μ_vi tokens are crucial.
- **Effectiveness of Information Flow Modulation:** Manual (oracle) modulation of information flow, where specific irrelevant visual tokens were masked, was shown to improve results across datasets (RealWorldQA, TextVQA, CountBench). This suggested that focusing the VLM's attention by suppressing distractions can enhance performance (Table 1, Fig. 5).

**Performance Across Various Benchmarks:**

- **General Visual Question Answering (Table 2):**
 - AIF significantly improved LLaVA-1.5-7B performance on V* (+7.9%), RealWorldQA (+4.9%), and MMStar (+6.4%).
 - Qwen2.5-VL-7B also showed substantial gains on V* (+6.3%), RealWorldQA (+6.0%), and MMStar (+7.0%).
 - These results suggest AIF enhances general multimodal reasoning capabilities.
- **OCR Understanding (Table 2):**
 - Consistent improvements were observed for LLaVA-1.5-7B on TextVQA (+2.1%) and SeedBench2-Plus (+3.6%).
 - Qwen2.5-VL-7B also improved on TextVQA (+1.1%) and SeedBench2-Plus (+6.1%).
 - This indicates AIF's effectiveness in fine-grained tasks requiring precise perception of small objects or text.
- **Grounding and Counting (Table 3, Table 2):**
 - **Visual Grounding:** AIF applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B notably improved grounding results on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, with an average overall improvement of 2.3% over the baseline. The method also outperformed Grounding-DINO-L, a specialized visual grounding model, demonstrating enhanced precise object localization.
 - **Counting:** On the CountBench dataset, AIF achieved an improvement of approximately 2-3% over the baselines.
- **Hallucination Mitigation (Table 4):**
 - The method improved accuracy on the POPE (object hallucination) dataset for both LLaVA-1.5-7B (+3.3%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (+1.7%). This suggests that by allowing VLMs to focus on related regions, information flow modulation can help alleviate object hallucinations.

**Comparisons with Additional Methods (Table 5):**
- AIF was compared against a training-free method (ViCrop) and a training-based method (CCA) using LLaVA-1.5-7B as the base model.
- AIF outperformed both ViCrop and CCA on MMStar, RealWorldQA, and POPE, achieving superior performance without requiring model retraining.

**Ablation Studies (Section 5.3):**
- **Quantitative Justification of Token Entropy (Table 6):** Evaluation on RefCOCO showed that selecting the top 20% low-entropy tokens (i.e., important tokens according to AIF) resulted in significantly higher recall of object tokens across small, medium, and large object sizes compared to selecting high-attention tokens (e.g., +20.9% for small objects). This supports token entropy as a more reliable indicator of token importance.
- **Masked Visual Tokens (Fig. 7b):** Visualizations indicated that the masked tokens predominantly corresponded to background or irrelevant objects, allowing the model to focus on pertinent regions.
- **Attention Distribution After Modulation (Fig. 7c, 7d):** After AIF, the text-to-image attention, which was initially dispersed, became more centralized on the object regions relevant to the question, leading to correct answers.
- **Masking Strategy (Table 7):** The proposed adaptive masking strategy outperformed both random token masking and masking low-entropy tokens (which degraded performance, as low-entropy tokens are deemed important).
- **Comparison with Future-Aware Causal Mask (Table 8):** Alternative causal mask designs (visual-to-visual and visual-to-textual) showed performance deterioration, suggesting that broadly altering the causal mask can have negative impacts. AIF's selective modulation proved more effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the understanding and improvement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by identifying and addressing a specific limitation related to suboptimal information flow. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Insight into VLM Limitations:** The paper rigorously demonstrates that the "misalignment between seeing and perception" in VLMs is directly linked to suboptimal information flow, where text tokens distribute attention to irrelevant visual regions. This insight highlights that the quality and focus of cross-modal interaction, rather than merely its presence or extent, are crucial for accurate VLM outputs.
- **Introduction of a Training-Free, Adaptive Solution:** The Adaptive Information Flow (AIF) method provides an effective, training-free approach to mitigate this issue. By adaptively modulating the causal mask during inference, the method steers the VLM's attention towards important visual tokens while suppressing noise from irrelevant areas. This contrasts with existing methods that require extensive retraining, architectural modifications, or are limited to post-hoc corrections.
- **Broad Applicability and Performance Gains:** AIF demonstrates consistent and substantial performance improvements across a wide array of VLM tasks, including general visual question answering, optical character recognition, visual grounding, object counting, and hallucination mitigation. Its effectiveness is shown on multiple representative VLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2.5-VL), underscoring its generality and robustness.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A significant impact is the achievement of these performance gains with minimal additional computational cost, approximately equivalent to generating one extra token during inference. This makes AIF a practical and scalable solution for deploying more accurate VLMs without demanding additional training resources or significantly slowing down inference.
- **Pathway-Level Control:** The approach offers a novel form of pathway-level control over cross-modal interaction. Unlike methods that focus on data augmentation, architectural enhancements, or output re-ranking, AIF directly intervenes in the internal attention mechanisms to optimize the flow of information. This paradigm may inspire future research into dynamic, context-aware internal control mechanisms for VLMs.
- **Challenging Assumptions:** This work implicitly challenges the assumption that maximizing attention or providing more visual tokens is always beneficial. Instead, it suggests that *selective* attention and *filtered* information flow can lead to more grounded and reliable VLM reasoning.

In summary, this research offers a practical, efficient, and theoretically grounded method for enhancing VLM perception. By providing a mechanism for adaptive information flow modulation at inference time, it contributes to the development of more accurate, robust, and less hallucinatory multimodal AI systems, potentially sparking new directions in VLM architectural design and internal mechanism optimization.
