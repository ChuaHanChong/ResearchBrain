# From Verifiable Dot to Reward Chain: Harnessing Verifiable Reference-based Rewards for Reinforcement Learning of Open-ended Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Technologies Co.,Ltd developed Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reference-Based Rewards (RLVRR), a framework that leverages "reward chains" derived from reference responses to align large language models for open-ended generation tasks. RLVRR consistently outperforms SFT, DPO, and reward model-based approaches, while enabling unified training for both reasoning and open-ended tasks with significantly reduced data and computational costs.

## Problem

- Existing LLM alignment methods like RLHF and DPO are highly expensive due to reliance on human preference data and are prone to reward hacking.
- Traditional Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is limited to tasks with unambiguous ground truth or a single, verifiable outcome, making it inapplicable to open-ended generation.
- Simple metrics like BLEU used as rewards for open-ended tasks often fail to capture nuanced content and stylistic properties, leading to misaligned and noisy signals.

## Method

- The **RLVRR** framework introduces a "==Reward Chain==" concept, which extracts an ordered sequence of verifiable linguistic signals from high-quality reference responses rather than relying on a single verifiable dot.
- Rewards are decomposed into two components: a content reward derived from hierarchically extracted keywords and their Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) alignment, and a style reward computed by executing LLM-generated verifiable Python functions.
- An off-the-shelf LLM (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) is used offline to generate these verifiable content keywords and style code checks, and the final policy is optimized using GRPO with the aggregated rule-based reward signal.

## Results

- RLVRR consistently outperforms SFT (even with **10x** more data), DPO, and various RM/GRM-based approaches across over 10 benchmarks, showing improvements of +2.3 to +2.7 points over RM.
- The framework unifies training for both structured reasoning and open-ended generation tasks; a model trained on a mix of **10K** math and open-ended data matches specialized performance and surpasses INSTRUCT models trained on millions of samples.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of content reward and the superiority of LCS for alignment and hierarchical keyword extraction; the data construction cost for **10K** samples is approximately $21.36, with negligible overhead during RL training (**0.71%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extending the verifiable reward paradigm to open-ended generation is achievable by decomposing rewards into a structured "reward chain" of content and style elements derived from reference responses.
> - LLM-generated verifiers are cost-effective for offline data construction and provide precise, rule-based feedback, circumventing the need for expensive human preference data and learned reward models during RL training.
> - Using Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) to evaluate content keyword alignment effectively captures both presence and sequential order, which is crucial for preventing reward hacking and improving semantic fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment: extending the benefits of verifiable reinforcement learning to open-ended generation tasks. The broader landscape of LLM alignment is dominated by techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

**Current Landscape and Gaps:**
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** RLHF, leveraging human preference data to train a reward model, has been highly successful in aligning LLMs with human values and instructions. However, it suffers from significant drawbacks:
 - **High Cost:** Obtaining large-scale, high-quality human preference annotations is extremely expensive and time-consuming.
 - **Reward Hacking:** Reward models are prone to overfitting superficial artifacts and spurious correlations, leading to undesirable model behaviors that exploit the reward function rather than achieving true alignment.
 - **Training Instability:** RL optimization with learned reward models can be brittle and difficult to stabilize.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** DPO emerged as an offline alternative to RLHF, directly optimizing the policy from preference data without explicitly training a separate reward model. While more stable than RLHF in some aspects, DPO still relies on the same costly preference data and shares some susceptibility to its limitations.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** A more recent paradigm, RLVR, has shown immense promise in *reasoning tasks* such as mathematics and code generation. RLVR leverages deterministic, rule-based verifiers (e.g., math solvers, code compilers) to check the correctness of a final answer—the "verifiable dot." This approach is highly efficient and reliable because the reward signal is unambiguous and not learned, effectively guiding exploration towards correct reasoning paths. However, a major limitation of traditional RLVR is its applicability: it explicitly requires an unambiguous ground truth or a single, verifiable outcome, which is inherently absent in open-ended generation tasks.
- **Simple Metrics as Rewards:** Some attempts have been made to use simpler metrics like BLEU as reward signals for open-ended tasks (e.g., Chang et al., 2025). However, as noted in the paper, n-gram precision metrics like BLEU often fail to capture the nuanced content and stylistic properties desired by humans, leading to misaligned and noisy reward signals.

**RLVRR's Contribution to the Landscape:**
RLVRR directly addresses the critical gap between the efficiency and reliability of RLVR for reasoning tasks and the complex, subjective nature of open-ended generation. It positions itself as a method that **extends the verifiable reward paradigm to open-ended generation**, overcoming the limitations of traditional RLVR while providing a more robust and cost-effective alternative to RLHF/DPO. By extracting "verifiable reference-based rewards" in the form of a "reward chain" (decomposed into content and style), RLVRR bridges the gap, offering a principled approach to LLM alignment that combines the exploratory strength of RL with the efficiency and verifiability inherent in rule-based systems. This work therefore represents a significant step towards creating more scalable, general-purpose LLM alignment techniques that can effectively handle the diverse requirements of various tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for introducing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reference-Based Rewards (RLVRR) stems from the inherent limitations of existing reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms when applied to the complex domain of open-ended text generation.

**Primary Motivations:**

1. **Bridging the Gap in Verifiable Rewards:** While Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has proven highly effective for tasks with unambiguous ground truth (like math problem-solving or code generation, where a "verifiable dot" signifies correctness), it inherently struggles with open-ended generation. In such tasks, there is no single, deterministic "correct" answer, making the traditional "verifiable dot" concept inapplicable. The primary motivation is to extend the efficiency and reliability of verifiable rewards to this challenging domain.
2. **Mitigating Reward Hacking in RLHF:** Current state-of-the-art methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) rely on learned reward models. A significant problem with these models is their susceptibility to "reward hacking," where the LLM learns to exploit superficial patterns in the reward signal rather than genuinely improving its generation quality. This leads to models that appear to perform well according to the reward model but generate misaligned or poor-quality text in practice. RLVRR aims to provide a more robust, rule-based reward signal that is less prone to such exploitation.
3. **Reducing High Annotation Costs:** Training effective reward models for RLHF requires vast amounts of expensive human preference data. This cost is a major barrier to widespread adoption and customization of LLM alignment. RLVRR seeks to dramatically reduce this cost by deriving reward signals from readily available, high-quality reference responses rather than expensive human comparisons.
4. **Improving Efficiency and Stability of RL Training:** Learned reward models can be computationally intensive to load and query during RL training, and their training itself can be unstable. RLVRR's approach of using lightweight, rule-based verifiers promises to offer a more efficient and stable training process.
5. **Achieving Better Generalization:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often leads to models that memorize training data but struggle to generalize to unseen inputs (exposure bias). The paper hypothesizes that by reinforcing "what matters" (key semantic elements and stylistic properties) rather than token-level mimicry, RLVRR can lead to better generalization.

**Key Objectives of RLVRR:**

1. **Develop a "Reward Chain" for Open-Ended Generation:** Instead of a single "verifiable dot," RLVRR aims to extract an *ordered sequence of verifiable linguistic signals* from high-quality reference responses. This "reward chain" provides more granular and structured supervision for open-ended tasks.
2. **Decompose Rewards into Content and Style:** To comprehensively evaluate open-ended generations, the framework seeks to decompose the reward signal into two dimensions:
 - **Content Reward:** Focusing on the presence and order of deterministic core concepts (e.g., keywords, key entities) derived from references.
 - **Style Reward:** Evaluating adherence to stylistic properties (e.g., length, format, markdown usage) through LLM-based, verifiable Python checks.
3. **Combine RL's Exploration with SFT-like Guidance:** The objective is to harness the exploratory power of RL while injecting the efficiency and reliability of supervised signals at a more granular, token-level (or segment-level) through the reward chain, leading to stable learning.
4. **Demonstrate Scalability and Robustness:** Prove that RLVRR can operate effectively across various model scales, generalize to diverse tasks (both open-ended and structured reasoning), and be computationally economical for both data construction and RL training.
5. **Preserve Output Diversity:** Acknowledge and address the potential concern that reference-based rewards might limit output diversity, ensuring the method enhances quality without sacrificing variety.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RLVRR framework proposes a novel approach to generate reliable and low-cost rewards for open-ended generation by leveraging a "Reward Chain" extracted from reference responses. This effectively extends the verifiable reward paradigm beyond tasks with a single, unambiguous correct answer.

**Problem Formulation:**
The core of RLVRR adheres to a standard RL objective, aiming to train a policy $\pi_{\theta}$ that maximizes a reward function $r_{\phi}(x, y)$ for a generated response $y$ given an instruction $x$. Critically, unlike RLHF which learns $r_{\phi}$ via a reward model, RLVRR *derives* $r_{\phi}$ directly from rule-based, verifiable linguistic signals grounded in a reference answer $z$. The total reward $r_{\phi}$ is an aggregation (simple averaging in experiments) of two main components: content reward ($r_c$) and style reward ($r_s$).

**A. Data Construction (Offline Phase):**
An off-the-shelf LLM (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) is used to generate the verifiable components for both content and style rewards. This process is performed offline and is designed to be highly cost-efficient.

1. **Verifiable Keywords for Content Reward:**
 - **Two-Level Hierarchical Extraction:** This method ensures comprehensive and precise keyword coverage.
 - **Level 1: Key Point Identification:** The LLM first identifies a set of "essential key points" ($p_m$) that an AI assistant *must* address when answering the question. This provides a high-level structure for content evaluation.
 - **Level 2: Keyword Extraction:** For each identified key point ($p_m$), the LLM then extracts a set of "keywords" ($K_m$) (each typically fewer than three words) directly from the *reference answer*. These keywords encode the core facts, concepts, or entities necessary to assess correctness and relevance.
 - This hierarchical approach allows for flexibility in phrasing while anchoring the reward to deterministic core concepts.

2. **Verifiable Code for Style Reward:**
 - To quantify stylistic alignment, the LLM generates a set of verifiable Python functions, denoted as $CodeEval_n(\cdot)$. Each function is designed to assess whether the generated rollout adheres to specific stylistic properties derived from the reference answer. These properties can include:
 - Overall answer length (within a reasonable range).
 - Use of markdown formatting (e.g., headers, bullet points, bold text).
 - Structural elements like paragraph count or the presence of specific delimiters.
 - Additionally, the LLM assigns a numerical weight ($w_n$) to each $CodeEval_n(\cdot)$ function, reflecting its relative importance in assessing the overall style.

**B. Reward Calculation (During RL Training):**

1. **Content Reward ($r_c$):**
 - During RL training, for a given rollout $y$, the content reward assesses its fidelity to the reference $z$ based on the extracted keywords.
 - For each key point $p_m$, the method extracts matched keyword sequences ($K_m^y$ from $y$ and $K_m^z$ from $z$) using regular expression matching. Crucially, these sequences preserve both the *frequency* and *sequential order* of matched keywords.
 - **Longest Common Subsequence (LCS):** The semantic coherence between $y$ and $z$ for each key point $p_m$ is computed using the LCS metric between $K_m^y$ and $K_m^z$. LCS is chosen because it inherently captures keyword ordering and repetition, making it suitable for evaluating structural and semantic fidelity.
 - The alignment score for $p_m$ is the normalized LCS length. The overall content reward $r_c(x, y, z)$ is then the mean alignment score across all key points.
 - **Multiple References:** To enhance robustness, the framework supports multiple reference answers $\{z_i\}$. In this case, for each key point, the maximum alignment score across all references is chosen.

2. **Style Reward ($r_s$):**
 - The style reward $r_s(x, y, z)$ is computed by executing each of the generated $CodeEval_n(y)$ Python functions on the rollout $y$. Each function returns a binary result (True/False or 1/0).
 - The results are aggregated as a weighted sum, where each $CodeEval_n(y)$ output is multiplied by its assigned weight $w_n$.

**C. Reinforcement Learning Optimization:**
The policy is then optimized using an RL algorithm, specifically GRPO (Generalized Policy Optimization), which is a common choice for LLM alignment. The calculated $r_{\phi}$ directly serves as the reward signal, guiding the policy's exploration and generation process. This direct, rule-based reward signal eliminates the need for a separate, learned reward model during training, contributing to efficiency and stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across a diverse set of over 10 benchmarks, utilizing Qwen and Llama models, demonstrate the substantial advantages and broad applicability of RLVRR.

1. **Superior Performance in Open-Ended Generation:**
 - RLVRR consistently and significantly **outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**, even when SFT is trained with ten times more data (100K vs. 10K samples). This highlights RLVRR's ability to extract more potent and generalizable signals from limited reference data.
 - It **surpasses various alternative reward strategies**, including random rewards, BLEU, advanced learned Reward Models (RMs), Generative Reward Models (GRMs), and RLPR (a verifier-free RL method). For instance, RLVRR improves over RM-based approaches by +2.3 to +2.7 points on different Qwen2.5 models.
 - RLVRR also exhibits **stronger performance compared to Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**, a widely adopted alignment method, further validating its effectiveness.
 - The benefits of RLVRR are **robust across different model scales** (Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3.1-8B) and initializations, demonstrating its general applicability.

2. **Unification of Structured Reasoning and Open-Ended Generation:**
 - The paper demonstrates that RLVRR can be effectively integrated into the broader RLVR framework, unifying the training for both closed-form reasoning (e.g., mathematics) and open-ended generation tasks.
 - While RLVR trained solely on math data excels in math but generalizes poorly to open-ended tasks, RLVRR trained on open-ended data not only performs strongly there but also improves mathematical reasoning.
 - Crucially, **unified training on mixed data** (e.g., 5k math + 5k open-ended) achieves the best balance, matching the performance of specialized RLVR for math and dedicated RLVRR for open-ended tasks. This mixed-data model even **surpasses INSTRUCT models** trained on millions of samples, despite using only 10K RL training instances, showcasing exceptional data efficiency.
 - RLVRR also shows **better compatibility with reasoning tasks compared to RM**, highlighting its robustness across domains.

3. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Effectiveness:**
 - **Content Reward is Critical:** Removing the content reward leads to a severe performance degradation (13.0 points average drop), emphasizing its essential role.
 - **LCS Superiority:** Replacing LCS with naive "direct matching" results in catastrophic failure, as LCS's ability to capture keyword ordering and repetition is crucial for preventing reward hacking.
 - **Hierarchical Keyword Extraction:** The two-level (key points then keywords) extraction process significantly improves performance (0.9 points), outperforming single-step or rule-based (random, TF-IDF) keyword selection methods by 3.7–4.1 points. This validates the LLM-driven structured extraction.
 - **Style Reward's Importance:** The absence of style reward reduces performance by 2.8 points, and using unweighted style components drops performance by 1.2 points, confirming the value of learned stylistic guidance.
 - **Reference LLM Robustness:** RLVRR's performance holds even when using a less powerful open-source LLM (Llama3-70B-Instruct) for generating verifiable components instead of GPT-4o-mini, indicating practical flexibility.

4. **Enhanced Generalization and Diversity:**
 - **"Learning What Matters":** Compared to SFT, RLVRR exhibits more stable BLEU scores and higher semantic similarity on development sets, while SFT shows higher BLEU on the training set but poorer generalization. This suggests RLVRR learns to preserve "key semantic elements" and allows for "flexible phrasing," leading to better generalization and avoiding SFT's token-level memorization and exposure bias.
 - **Self-Data Distillation Benefit:** Distilling SFT models using trajectories generated by RLVRR significantly outperforms standard SFT (2.5-point gain), demonstrating the high quality of RLVRR's supervision signals.
 - **Diversity Preservation:** Despite using rule-based verifiers, RLVRR maintains output diversity, achieving comparable Self-BLEU scores to RM and INSTRUCT models. It enhances quality without sacrificing variety.

5. **Cost-Effectiveness and Training Stability:**
 - **Minimal Overhead:** RLVRR introduces a negligible computational overhead during RL training (only 0.71% compared to random rewards), making it highly efficient. This contrasts sharply with RM (8.28% overhead) and GRM (6.05% overhead), which require loading auxiliary models.
 - **Low Data Construction Cost:** The total estimated API cost for generating verifiable components for 10K samples using GPT-4o-mini is a mere $21.36, making the data synthesis phase highly economical.
 - **Stable Training Dynamics:** RLVRR achieves a stable and substantial increase in reward during training, with response lengths stabilizing after an initial exploratory phase. This indicates robust learning and effective mitigation of reward hacking, unlike RMs which tend to favor longer responses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RLVRR represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model alignment, with profound implications and potential impacts across research and practical applications.

1. **Democratizing Advanced LLM Alignment:** One of the most significant impacts of RLVRR is its potential to democratize access to advanced LLM alignment techniques. By offering a robust, low-cost, and reliable alternative to RLHF and DPO, RLVRR dramatically reduces the financial and computational barriers associated with human annotation and complex reward model training. This enables a broader range of researchers, developers, and organizations, including those with limited resources, to fine-tune and align LLMs for specific tasks and domains.

2. **Extending the Verifiable Reward Paradigm:** This work successfully extends the highly effective paradigm of verifiable reinforcement learning from narrowly defined reasoning tasks to the much broader and more challenging domain of open-ended generation. This is a crucial conceptual leap, allowing the benefits of deterministic, rule-based feedback—precision, stability, and resistance to hacking—to be applied where subjective quality and diverse correct answers are the norm.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** RLVRR's focus on structured, reference-based verification for both content and style leads to models that are more robust against reward hacking and generalize more effectively. By reinforcing the "semantic essence" and "stylistic properties" rather than token-level mimicry, the method fosters a deeper understanding of desired output characteristics. This is vital for deploying LLMs in real-world scenarios where unseen prompts and diverse requirements are common.

4. **Unified LLM Alignment Framework:** The demonstrated ability of RLVRR to seamlessly integrate with traditional RLVR for mathematical reasoning, facilitating unified training for both structured and open-ended tasks, is a powerful contribution. This paves the way for a more holistic and coherent approach to aligning general-purpose LLMs, where a single training pipeline can address various types of tasks and desired behaviors. This unification simplifies the LLM development lifecycle and reduces the complexity of managing multiple specialized alignment strategies.

5. **Practical Efficiency and Scalability:** The negligible computational overhead during RL training and the extremely low cost of data construction make RLVRR highly practical for large-scale deployment. In an era where LLM training and alignment costs are soaring, an efficient and scalable method like RLVRR is invaluable for rapidly iterating on model improvements and adapting LLMs to specific industry needs without prohibitive expenses.

6. **Principled Path for Future Research:** RLVRR establishes a principled and efficient path towards broader verifiable reinforcement learning. Its modular design (separate content and style rewards, LLM-generated verifiers) offers clear avenues for future research, such as exploring more sophisticated methods for reference extraction, dynamic weighting of reward components, or adapting the framework to multimodal generation tasks. It encourages a shift from subjective, hard-to-interpret reward models to transparent, verifiable, and explainable reward mechanisms.

In essence, RLVRR addresses a core limitation of current LLM alignment, making verifiable RL a viable and superior option for open-ended generation. Its combination of performance, cost-effectiveness, and robustness positions it as a foundational contribution with significant potential to shape the future of general-purpose LLM alignment.
