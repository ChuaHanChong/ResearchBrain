# M$^3$CoT: A Novel Benchmark for Multi-Domain Multi-step Multi-modal Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology, Central South University, and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed M3CoT, a multi-domain, multi-step, multi-modal Chain-of-Thought benchmark. The benchmark reveals a substantial performance gap between state-of-the-art VLLMs and human accuracy, with GPT-4V achieving 62.60% compared to human performance of 91.17%, and its design rigorously enforces visual necessity and complex multi-step reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) benchmarks often contained samples where visual information was not strictly necessary for solving the problem, allowing models to rely solely on text.
- Current MCoT tasks predominantly involved single-step visual reasoning, failing to capture the complexity of real-world scenarios that demand multiple, integrated visual and textual reasoning steps.
- Important domains like commonsense reasoning and mathematics were largely underrepresented or absent in existing multi-modal benchmarks, limiting comprehensive evaluation of VLLMs' generalization abilities.

## Method

- The **M3CoT** benchmark was constructed by refining existing datasets and augmenting them with new multi-modal data in mathematics and commonsense domains.
- A meticulous ==multi-stage process==, including ==automatic filtering== and extensive ==human annotation==, ensured that each sample genuinely required visual input and involved at least two distinct ==multi-modal reasoning steps==.
- Mathematics problems were augmented by programmatically converting formulas to images, and commonsense questions were generated using LLMs prompted to require insights from multiple visual clues.

## Results

- M3CoT, a new multi-domain, multi-step, multi-modal benchmark, comprises **11,459 samples** with an average of **10.9 reasoning steps** per rationale, significantly higher than prior benchmarks (e.g., ScienceQA's **2.5**).
- State-of-the-art VLLMs, including GPT-4V, achieved **62.60%** accuracy on M3CoT, revealing a substantial gap compared to human performance of **91.17%**.
- Fine-tuning VLLMs on the M3CoT training set notably improved their performance, with some fine-tuned models outperforming larger zero-shot counterparts, indicating the benchmark's utility for model improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting benefits Vision Large Language Models (VLLMs) only if they have parameters exceeding 10 billion, indicating CoT as an emergent ability tied to model scale.
> - Performance of VLLMs on multi-step multi-modal reasoning drastically declines as the number of required reasoning steps increases, suggesting this is a challenging Out-of-Distribution problem for current models.
> - Effective In-Context Learning (ICL) for multi-modal tasks relies heavily on high-quality, genuinely interleaved image-text examples, with insufficient quality or interleaving potentially degrading performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated at the forefront of research into multi-modal reasoning, specifically within the paradigm of Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable reasoning abilities through CoT, where models generate intermediate steps to solve complex problems. This success initially emerged in purely textual domains (e.g., Wei et al., 2022a,b).

The natural progression has been to extend these CoT capabilities to multi-modal contexts, giving rise to Multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT). MCoT requires models to leverage information from both textual and visual modalities to perform step-by-step reasoning. Early foundational work, such as the introduction of the ScienceQA benchmark (Lu et al., 2022a), laid the groundwork for MCoT research. Subsequent efforts have focused on enhancing MCoT performance through two-stage approaches (Zhang et al., 2023c) and distilling high-quality rationales from larger LLMs (Wang et al., 2023a), with some even reporting performance surpassing human benchmarks on existing datasets.

However, this paper critically challenges the perceived "solved" status of MCoT. The authors argue that current MCoT benchmarks, despite leading to impressive reported accuracies, are fundamentally too simplistic and suffer from three major drawbacks:
1. **Absence of Visual Modal Reasoning:** Many "multi-modal" samples can be solved solely based on textual context, without genuinely requiring visual information (as illustrated in Figure 1a).
2. **Single-Step Visual Modal Reasoning:** Existing benchmarks primarily involve single-step visual reasoning, failing to capture the complexity of real-world scenarios that demand multiple, integrated visual and textual reasoning steps (Figure 1b vs. 1c). Figure 2a and 5 vividly illustrate this, showing that previous benchmarks like ScienceQA, MMMU, and VCR have a very low proportion of multi-step MCoT data (e.g., ScienceQA ~8%, MMMU ~8%, VCR ~4%, compared to M3CoT's 100%).
3. **Domain Missing:** Critical domains like commonsense reasoning and mathematics, which are crucial for comprehensive evaluation of multi-modal capabilities, are largely absent from current MCoT benchmarks (Figure 2b).

M3CoT directly addresses these limitations. By introducing a benchmark specifically designed for *multi-domain, multi-step, multi-modal* CoT, this work aims to provide a more rigorous and realistic evaluation environment. It pushes the research frontier by exposing the true limitations of current Vision Large Language Models (VLLMs) in complex reasoning tasks, thereby guiding the development of more capable and robust multi-modal AI systems. This positions M3CoT as a crucial resource for reassessing existing progress and inspiring the next generation of MCoT models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to introduce **M3CoT (Multi-Domain Multi-step Multi-modal Chain-of-Thought)**, a novel and more challenging benchmark designed to accurately evaluate and advance the capabilities of multi-modal reasoning models.

The motivation behind developing M3CoT stems from the critical assessment that current MCoT benchmarks fail to genuinely reflect the complexities of real-world multi-modal reasoning. Specifically, the authors identify and aim to address three core deficiencies in existing datasets:

1. **To Ensure Genuine Multi-modal Reasoning:** Current benchmarks often contain samples where visual information is not strictly necessary, allowing models to infer answers solely from text. M3CoT aims to create a dataset where visual input is indispensable for reaching the correct conclusion.
2. **To Promote Multi-Step Reasoning:** The majority of existing MCoT tasks involve only single-step visual reasoning. Real-world scenarios frequently require intricate, multi-step inference that integrates information across modalities sequentially. M3CoT is designed to demand at least two distinct multi-modal reasoning steps, pushing models beyond superficial understanding.
3. **To Broaden Domain Coverage:** The authors note a significant lack of diversity in domains within existing MCoT benchmarks, particularly the underrepresentation of commonsense and mathematics. By incorporating these critical domains, M3CoT seeks to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of VLLMs' generalization abilities and domain-specific reasoning.

In essence, the key motivation is to provide a "pioneering foundation" that exposes the current limitations of VLLMs, which may appear highly proficient on simpler benchmarks, and to serve as a valuable resource for driving breakthroughs in complex multi-domain, multi-step, multi-modal chain-of-thought research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction of the M3CoT benchmark involved a meticulous and multi-stage process, as detailed in Figure 3 of the paper. This workflow aimed to systematically address the identified weaknesses of prior MCoT datasets.

#### 3.1. Dataset Foundation and Initial Filtering

M3CoT is primarily built upon existing datasets, including ScienceQA (Lu et al., 2022b), MATH (Hendrycks et al., 2021), Sherlock (Hessel et al., 2022), and augmented with data from TabMWP (Lu et al., 2022c) and KiloGram (Ji et al., 2022). The process began by refining these raw datasets:

##### 3.1.1. Absence of Visual Modal Reasoning Sample Removal

- **Automatic Removal:** Initially, samples without associated images were filtered out to ensure all remaining data *potentially* required visual input.
- **Manual Annotation:** To ensure *actual* visual necessity, expert annotators were employed. They first reviewed textual contexts (questions, options, rationales, answers) with images masked. If a question could be answered solely from text, it was removed. Images were then revealed for re-verification where visual information was deemed potentially necessary. This step alone removed approximately 30% of multi-modal samples from ScienceQA, highlighting the superficiality of existing benchmarks.

##### 3.1.2. Multi-step Multi-modal Sample Construction

- **Automatic Sample Removal:** To focus on complexity, samples with overly simplistic rationales (fewer than two reasoning steps) were automatically filtered. This reduced manual annotation burden for clearly single-step tasks.
- **Multi-step Sample Manual Annotation:** Human experts meticulously evaluated remaining samples. The primary task was to determine if multi-step reasoning was required and, crucially, if these multiple steps necessitated the integration of both textual and visual modalities to arrive at the solution. This process ensured that M3CoT truly captured complex, interleaved multi-modal reasoning.

#### 3.2. Multi-modal CoT Domain Augmentation

To address the "domain missing" challenge, M3CoT significantly augmented its coverage of mathematics and commonsense.

- **Mathematics Domain Augmentation:**
 - **Source:** Primarily MATH (Hendrycks et al., 2021), a text-only mathematical reasoning dataset.
 - **Option Generation:** GPT-3.5-turbo was prompted to generate four plausible multiple-choice options (one correct, three distractors) for each text-based mathematical question.
 - **Image Creation:** Geometric and formulaic codes from the MATH dataset were programmatically converted into PNG images. These images were then spliced with textual content using HTML frameworks to create multi-modal samples.
- **Commonsense Domain Augmentation:**
 - **Source:** Sherlock (Hessel et al., 2022), which provides visual clues but lacks specific questions, options, or answers.
 - **Content Generation:** LLMs were used to generate questions, options, and answers based on the visual clues. A crucial design aspect was the prompting strategy, which *enforced* the LLM to generate questions requiring insights from *multiple* visual clues, thereby ensuring multi-step multi-modal reasoning.
- **Additional Augmentation:** For topics in ScienceQA that became sparse after filtering, data was synthesized using similar LLM-guided methods or integrated from other open-source datasets like TabMWP and KiloGram. Geographical images were also manually synthesized.

#### 3.3. Quality Assurance

Rigorous quality control measures were implemented to ensure the reliability and integrity of the M3CoT dataset:
- **Onboarding Test:** All annotators underwent a mandatory test (100 samples) and had to achieve at least 80% accuracy, validated by three experts, before participating in the main annotation tasks.
- **Human Annotation & Rationale Rewriting:** Annotators reviewed and refined rationales for accuracy and coherence. GPT-3.5-turbo was initially used for rationale rewriting to improve quality before human annotation, followed by expert human review.
- **Human Recheck:** All annotated data was rechecked twice by annotators to confirm adherence to multi-step multi-modal reasoning criteria and logical coherence. A high Kappa coefficient of 0.85 indicated strong inter-annotator agreement.
- **Image Redundancy Removal:** Samples with identical questions and over 99% grayscale image similarity were removed to ensure diversity.

#### 3.4. Problem Formalization

The paper formally defines multi-step multi-modal CoT as a scenario involving an image (I), a question (Q), context (C), and options (O). The model must generate a step-wise rationale R_m = {S_1..., S_m}, where each step S_i is determined by P(S_i | I, T) (P(S_i | T, R_{i-1}) for text-only steps or P(S_i | I, T, R_{i-1}) for multi-modal steps). Crucially, multi-step and multi-modal reasoning is defined by requiring at least two steps ( |S| ≥ 2 ) that explicitly depend on multi-modal interaction. This formalization underpins the benchmark's design principles.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on M3CoT provided several critical insights into the current state of Vision Large Language Models (VLLMs) in complex multi-modal reasoning.

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Complexity

- M3CoT comprises 11,459 samples, divided into train (7,863), validation (1,108), and test (2,358) sets.
- It covers three primary domains (science knowledge, mathematics, and commonsense), 17 topics, and 263 categories, significantly enhancing domain diversity (Figure 6).
- The average rationale length in M3CoT is 294, substantially higher than ScienceQA's 48, indicating increased reasoning complexity.
- Crucially, M3CoT features an average of 10.9 reasoning steps per rationale, compared to 2.5 for ScienceQA, 3.0 for OKVQA, and 1.0 for MMMU/VCR (Figure 5). This confirms its multi-step nature.
- Image diversity (Figure 4a) and semantic coverage (Figure 4b) are also enhanced, demonstrating broader and richer visual content.

#### 4.2. VLLM Performance on M3CoT (Zero-Shot CoT)

- **Significant Gap with Human Performance:** All tested VLLMs, including state-of-the-art models like GPT-4V, exhibit a substantial performance gap compared to human accuracy. GPT-4V achieves 62.60% accuracy with CoT prompting, while human performance is 91.17%. This clearly indicates that M3CoT poses a genuine challenge that current models struggle to overcome.
- **Disparity Between Closed and Open-Source Models:** GPT-4V significantly outperforms open-source VLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-V1.5-13B, CogVLM-17B, InstructBLIP-13B) by at least 7.98% on the total accuracy, highlighting the advanced capabilities of proprietary models, likely due to larger scale, higher quality data, and more sophisticated training.
- **Emergent CoT Abilities in Larger Models:** The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategy (e.g., "Let's think step-by-step!") only benefits VLLMs with parameters over 10 billion (≥13B). Smaller models (e.g., InstructBLIP-7B, LLaVA-V1.5-7B, Kosmos-2-2B) either show marginal improvement or even performance degradation with CoT, suggesting that CoT is an emergent ability tied to model scale.

#### 4.3. Analysis of VLLM Failures on M3CoT

The authors conducted detailed analyses to understand why VLLMs struggle:
- **Multi-step MCoT is Significantly More Challenging:** Performance on multi-step MCoT samples in M3CoT is at least 29.06% lower than on single-step MCoT samples from ScienceQA (Figure 7a). As the number of reasoning steps increases, model performance drastically declines (Figure 7b). The minimal semantic overlap in rationales between datasets (Figure 7c) further suggests that multi-step MCoT is an Out-of-Distribution (OOD) problem for models trained on simpler data.
- **Rationale Quality is Crucial:** A strong correlation was observed between the multi-dimensional quality of generated rationales (e.g., semantic coverage, knowledge usage, step diversity, informativeness) and the final accuracy (Figure 8). Models that produced higher-quality rationales achieved better performance, indicating that improving the coherence and accuracy of intermediate reasoning steps is vital.
- **Increased Multi-modal Interaction is Needed:** There's a positive correlation between the number of multi-modal interaction steps in a reasoning process and performance (Figure 9). This implies that tasks requiring deeper and more frequent integration of visual and textual information pose a greater challenge, and current models are limited in this aspect.

#### 4.4. Exploration Beyond Zero-shot CoT

The paper further explored alternative strategies:
- **Multi-modal Tool Usage:** Integrating external multi-modal tools with text-modal LLMs (e.g., HuggingGPT, VisualChatGPT) performed significantly worse than GPT-4V (by 28.21%) and even below random for some cases (Table 2). This failure is attributed to the LLMs' inability to effectively "see" and leverage visual information during the tool *planning* phase, leading to incorrect tool selection and usage (Figures 15, 16).
- **In-Context Learning (ICL):**
 - **Text-only examples:** ICL with text-only in-domain examples failed to significantly boost multi-modal reasoning performance (Figure 10a), contrary to its success in purely textual CoT.
 - **Image-text interleaving examples:** For models not explicitly trained on interleaved image-text data (e.g., LLaVA-13B), ICL with such examples *degraded* performance with more shots (Figure 10b). Even OpenFlamingo, trained on interleaved data, showed a slight decline. Only GPT-4V, extensively trained on high-quality interleaved data, improved with more shots, though still lagging behind its direct CoT performance. This highlights the critical need for high-quality, truly interleaved multi-modal examples for effective ICL.
- **Fine-tuning:**
 - Fine-tuning traditional Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and VLLMs on the M3CoT training set significantly improved performance (Table 2). Fine-tuned VLMs (lowest 44.85%) often outperformed zero-shot open-source VLLMs (highest 38.86%) and even surpassed Gemini's zero-shot accuracy (47.50%).
 - Fine-tuning VLLMs (models with more parameters) proved generally more effective, reinforcing the importance of model scale for complex tasks on M3CoT.

**Key Takeaways from Exploration:**
1. Effective tool planning requires both visual and textual information.
2. High-quality multi-modal interleaving is essential for successful ICL in M3CoT.
3. Fine-tuning is a more promising strategy for improving performance on multi-step MCoT tasks compared to vanilla in-context learning, tool usage, or simple prompting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of M3CoT represents a significant contribution to the field of multi-modal AI, particularly in the domain of complex reasoning. Its primary significance lies in its direct and effective challenge to the prevailing optimism surrounding the capabilities of current Vision Large Language Models (VLLMs) on multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) tasks.

#### 5.1. Reassessment of Current Progress

By systematically exposing the limitations of existing MCoT benchmarks—namely, the absence of true visual reasoning, the prevalence of single-step reasoning, and the narrowness of domain coverage—M3CoT forces a critical reassessment of how "solved" the MCoT problem truly is. The stark performance gap between VLLMs (even GPT-4V) and human accuracy on M3CoT demonstrates that current models are far from mastering complex, multi-domain, multi-step multi-modal reasoning, despite their impressive scores on simpler datasets. This re-evaluation is crucial for setting realistic expectations and guiding future research.

#### 5.2. Paving the Way for Advanced MCoT Research

M3CoT is the first benchmark to rigorously address the multi-domain, multi-step, and multi-modal CoT scenario simultaneously. This pioneering effort provides a much-needed, challenging testbed that reflects real-world reasoning complexities. It pushes the boundaries beyond simple VQA or single-step inferences, compelling researchers to develop more sophisticated models capable of:
- **Deeper Multi-modal Interaction:** Models will need to genuinely integrate information from both modalities at multiple stages of reasoning, moving beyond superficial recognition or relying solely on textual cues.
- **Robust Multi-step Inference:** The higher number of reasoning steps demands models that can maintain coherence, track information, and make logical connections over extended reasoning chains.
- **Broad Generalization:** The inclusion of diverse domains like commonsense and mathematics will push models to generalize their multi-modal reasoning abilities across vastly different knowledge areas, rather than specializing in narrow domains.

#### 5.3. Concrete Guidance for Future Development

The detailed experimental analysis and "takeaways" derived from M3CoT provide clear, actionable insights for future research directions. Specifically, the findings suggest that:
- Improving the intrinsic **quality of generated rationales** is paramount for enhancing overall reasoning performance.
- Developing VLLMs that can perform **more complex and frequent multi-modal interactions** within their reasoning processes is essential.
- Current **tool-usage frameworks** for LLMs need significant enhancement to incorporate visual understanding during planning.
- The effectiveness of **In-Context Learning (ICL)** for MCoT relies heavily on the quality and *interleaved nature* of multi-modal examples, indicating a need for better data and architectural design for ICL in this domain.
- **Fine-tuning** appears to be a highly promising avenue for substantially boosting VLLM performance on complex multi-step MCoT, even enabling smaller models to outperform larger zero-shot counterparts.

#### 5.4. Facilitation of Open Science

The authors' commitment to making all data and code publicly available ensures that M3CoT can serve as a widely accessible and valuable resource. This fosters reproducibility, encourages independent verification, and accelerates collaborative research efforts in the multi-modal reasoning community.

#### 5.5. Acknowledged Limitations and Ethical Considerations

The authors transparently discuss the limitations, including potential human subjectivity in annotation, the single-language nature of the dataset (English), and the future emphasis on open-source models due to the volatility of proprietary ones. They also clearly outline ethical considerations regarding data sourcing, participant recruitment (informed consent, fair compensation), and efforts to mitigate biases. This responsible approach adds to the credibility and impact of the work.

In conclusion, M3CoT is more than just a new dataset; it's a recalibration of the research challenge in multi-modal reasoning. By setting a higher bar for "solved," it creates a clear roadmap for developing truly intelligent VLLMs capable of intricate, human-like reasoning across diverse visual and linguistic contexts.
