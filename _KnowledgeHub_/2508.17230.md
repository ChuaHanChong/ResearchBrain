# 4D Visual Pre-training for Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> 4D Visual Pre-training (FVP) improves 3D visual representations for robot learning through a conditional diffusion model that predicts future 3D point clouds. This approach achieves absolute performance gains of up to 55% in real-world robotic tasks and enhances generalization capabilities in vision-language-action models.

## Problem

- Robot learning struggles with generalizable 3D visual representations, as most pre-training relies on 2D data which fails to capture the physical world's three-dimensional nature.
- Current 2D pre-trained policies are often sensitive to camera viewpoints and struggle with complex spatial reasoning in robotics.
- Existing 3D pre-training methods for robotics, while addressing geometry, often overlook the crucial temporal dynamics of robot interactions and are hindered by scarce large-scale 3D datasets.

## Method

- Introduces **4D Visual Pre-training (FVP)**, a self-supervised framework designed to learn spatio-temporal 3D visual representations via ==next-point-cloud prediction==.
- Utilizes a ==conditional diffusion model== to predict the next 3D point cloud, conditioned on the latent representation of a historical 3D observation, thereby capturing dynamic environmental changes.
- FVP serves as a ==plug-in pre-training module== for existing 3D visual encoders and imitation learning policies, allowing fine-tuning for downstream tasks and improved feature learning.

## Results

- Achieved average success rate increases of **16.9%** (in-domain) and **24.7%** (out-of-domain) in 12 simulation benchmarks (Adroit, MetaWorld) using the DP3 policy.
- Demonstrated absolute performance gains ranging from **15%** to **55%** across 12 real-world robotic manipulation tasks on various robot platforms, outperforming other 3D/4D and 2D pre-training methods.
- Enhanced Vision-Language-Action models (e.g., RDT-1B) with improved spatial understanding (**14/20** success for placing objects at corners), knowledge transfer (**16/20** for new object pickup), and capability in long-horizon tasks (**3/20** for complex multi-step tasks).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating temporal dynamics with 3D observations (4D) through future state prediction significantly enhances generalizable visual representations for robot learning.
> - Conditional diffusion models can effectively capture complex spatio-temporal distributions from robot trajectories, enabling more accurate predictions of future 3D states.
> - The learned spatio-temporal 3D representations boost the performance and generalization of various robot learning approaches, including existing 3D imitation learning policies and large Vision-Language-Action models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is increasingly reliant on generalizable visual representations to enable robots to perform diverse tasks efficiently and robustly. Traditionally, much of this progress has come from pre-training models on vast 2D image datasets (e.g., ImageNet, Ego4D), which are readily available on the web. However, a significant limitation of 2D representations is their inherent inability to fully capture the three-dimensional nature of the physical world, which is crucial for precise robotic manipulation. This often leads to policies that are sensitive to camera viewpoints and struggle with complex spatial reasoning.

Recognizing this gap, there has been a growing interest in utilizing 3D visual inputs, such as point clouds, for robot learning. Point clouds provide explicit geometric information, offering a more direct understanding of object shapes and spatial relationships. Prior works in 3D representation learning for robotics have explored methods like contrastive learning (e.g., STRL, C2P) or masked signal modeling (e.g., PointMAE), where models learn by distinguishing similar vs. dissimilar point clouds or by reconstructing masked portions of a point cloud. While these methods have shown promise, they often lack the temporal reasoning capabilities essential for dynamic robotic tasks.

Concurrently, diffusion models have emerged as powerful generative models, excelling in tasks like image and point cloud generation due to their ability to model complex data distributions through an iterative denoising process. Their application has recently expanded to various robotics domains, including reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and motion planning.

The "4D Visual Pre-training (FVP)" framework positions itself at the intersection of these trends. It addresses the critical need for universal 3D pre-training by proposing a novel self-supervised objective that leverages the temporal dimension (hence "4D") of robotic trajectories. Unlike previous 3D pre-training methods that focus on static point cloud properties, FVP explicitly trains a model to predict the *next* 3D point cloud based on historical 3D observations and robot actions. By framing this as a conditional diffusion problem, FVP imbues the learned representations with a deep understanding of spatio-temporal dynamics, which is particularly vital for continuous robotic manipulation. This approach directly contrasts with 2D pre-training methods by embracing the 3D nature of robot perception and goes beyond static 3D representation learning by incorporating temporal context. Thus, FVP aims to provide a more robust, generalizable, and physically grounded visual representation suitable for real-world robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, general visual pre-training framework that can significantly enhance 3D visual representations for real-world robot learning tasks. The authors explicitly state their goal is "a general visual pre-training framework that could improve all 3D representations."

This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap in Robot Perception:** While general visual representations from web-scale 2D image datasets have advanced robot learning, they inherently neglect the 3D nature of the physical world. This limitation often results in visuomotor policies that are less robust to changes in camera viewpoint or challenging 3D spatial interactions. The authors are motivated to move beyond 2D and leverage the explicit geometric information provided by 3D point clouds.
2. **Addressing 3D Data Scarcity:** Unlike the vast availability of 2D images, large-scale, diverse 3D point cloud datasets for robotics are scarce. This scarcity makes it challenging to train universal 3D representations from scratch. FVP seeks an alternative approach: a self-supervised pre-training method that can be applied to existing, albeit smaller, robot trajectory datasets to improve various 3D encoders, rather than relying on a single, massive 3D dataset.
3. **Enhancing Temporal Understanding:** Robotic tasks are inherently dynamic, requiring an understanding of how the environment evolves over time in response to robot actions. Previous 3D pre-training methods often treat point clouds as static observations. FVP's core motivation is to capture this crucial spatio-temporal information by training models to predict future states, thus enabling the visual model to better capture the motion characteristics and dynamic behaviors critical for robotic manipulation.
4. **Improving Data Efficiency and Generalization:** By learning effective spatio-temporal 3D representations, the framework aims to make downstream robot learning (e.g., imitation learning) more data-efficient and policies more generalizable across different tasks, objects, and even robot platforms. The ultimate goal is to enable state-of-the-art performance in real-world manipulation.
5. **Leveraging Diffusion Models' Expressiveness:** The recent success of diffusion models in generative tasks motivates their application to learn complex data distributions, specifically the distribution of future 3D point clouds. The authors aim to harness the denoising capabilities of diffusion models to refine predicted point clouds, thereby yielding more accurate and robust visual features.

In essence, FVP is motivated by the need for robust, generalizable, and spatio-temporally aware 3D visual representations for robotics, offering a practical pre-training solution that works with available robot trajectory data and enhances existing 3D policy learning methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "4D Visual Pre-training (FVP)" framework proposes a novel self-supervised learning objective: **next-point-cloud-prediction** using a **conditional diffusion model**. This approach leverages temporal sequences of 3D point clouds ("4D") to learn robust visual representations for robotic manipulation.

**A. Core Framework Overview (FVP Pipeline):**

FVP operates as a general plug-in pre-training module that can enhance various 3D imitation learning methods. The process involves three main stages: demonstration collection, 3D visual representation extraction, and future point cloud generation.

1. **Demonstration Collection:** The framework utilizes recorded robot trajectories, consisting of sequences of observation-action pairs (x_t = (o_t, a_t)). Each observation o_t is a 3D point cloud at time *t*, and a_t represents the robot's joint position or action at that time. These demonstrations can be either "in-domain" (specific to the target task) or "out-of-domain" (from larger, publicly available robot datasets like Robomind).

2. **Extracting 3D Visual Representations:**
 - FVP employs a standard 3D visual encoder to process the point cloud from a *previous* frame, o_{t-1}.
 - Crucially, FVP supports various general 3D encoders, demonstrating its versatility. Examples include PointNet++, Point Transformer, and the DP3 Encoder.
 - This encoder transforms the input point cloud into a latent visual representation, *z* ∈ R^(N × C_v), where N is the number of points and C_v are the feature dimensions. This latent representation *z* serves as the *condition* for the diffusion model.

3. **Generating Future Point Cloud with a Conditional Diffusion Model:**
 - The core of FVP is a conditional diffusion probabilistic model responsible for predicting the *next* frame's point cloud, o_t, conditioned on the latent representation *z* from o_{t-1}.
 - **Diffusion Model Revisited:** The paper first provides a brief overview of denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs). These models learn to reverse a gradual noise-adding process (diffusion process) that transforms an original data sample (o_t) into pure Gaussian noise. The reverse process involves iteratively denoising the noisy data to reconstruct the original sample. The training objective is to learn a neural network, ϵ_θ, that predicts the noise added at each step.
 - **Conditional Prediction:** In FVP, the diffusion model (ϵ_θ) is modified to be *conditional*. Instead of simply denoising a random Gaussian noise into an arbitrary point cloud, it denoises it into the *specific next point cloud* (o_t) guided by the visual representation *z* from o_{t-1}.
 - Specifically, the latent representation *z* is projected onto the noisy point cloud of the current frame, o_t^T (where T is the number of noise steps). The input to the diffusion model becomes a concatenated feature vector [o_t^T, z] ∈ R^(N × (C_v + 3)).
 - The network ϵ_θ: R^(N × (C_v + 3)) → R^(N × 3) is then trained to predict the noise ϵ.
 - The optimization objective is an L2 loss between the predicted noise and the ground truth noise: L = E_{ϵ∼N(0,I)} [∥ϵ - ϵ_θ(o_t^+, T)∥²].
 - During inference, a random 3D Gaussian noise is iteratively denoised, conditioned on *z* from the preceding observation, to generate the predicted next point cloud.

**B. "4D" Aspect:**
The "4D" in FVP signifies the explicit incorporation of **time** into the learning of **3D** visual representations. By training the model to predict the *next* 3D point cloud from a *history* of 3D point clouds, FVP encourages the visual encoder to learn spatio-temporal dynamics and motion characteristics inherent in robot trajectories, which is crucial for understanding how the environment changes.

**C. Downstream Robotic Tasks:**
Once the 3D visual representations are pre-trained using FVP, they are applied to downstream real-world robotic manipulation tasks.
- The pre-trained visual encoder from FVP directly replaces the original visual representation module within existing 3D visuomotor policies (e.g., RISE, DP3).
- During the training of these policies on expert demonstrations, the pre-trained visual representations and the policy backbone are fine-tuned end-to-end. This allows the policy to leverage the rich spatio-temporal features learned during FVP pre-training.

This methodology provides a flexible and powerful way to impart dynamic 3D understanding into robot learning systems, enhancing their performance and generalization capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The FVP framework was rigorously evaluated across a comprehensive set of simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing 3D visual representations.

**A. Efficacy in Simulation Tasks:**
- **Benchmarks:** FVP was tested on 12 simulation tasks from the Adroit and MetaWorld benchmarks, utilizing the DP3 policy.
- **In-domain Pre-training:** When pre-trained on in-domain datasets (data specific to the task), FVP boosted the average success rate on these benchmarks by **16.9%**.
- **Out-of-domain Pre-training:** Using out-of-domain datasets (e.g., sum of all tasks in a benchmark), FVP pre-trained DP3 achieved an even more significant average improvement of **24.7%** in task success rates, particularly in challenging tasks like "Hand Insert" and "Pick Out of Hole Hand Insert Disassemble."
- **Comparison to 2D Methods:** FVP pre-trained DP3 significantly outperformed 2D imitation learning methods (e.g., Diffusion Policy with MVP or R3M pre-training) even when those 2D methods were pre-trained on much larger datasets (e.g., >300M images), highlighting the advantage of 3D representations.
- **Encoder Generalization:** The benefits of FVP were consistent across various 3D encoders, including DP3 Encoder, PointNet++, and Point Transformer, indicating its general applicability.

**B. Efficacy in Real-World Tasks:**
- **Diverse Robotic Platforms:** FVP was validated on 12 real-world tasks across a range of robots: UR5 single-arm with a gripper, UR5 single-arm with a 16-DoF Leap Hand, AgileX dual-arm robot, and TianGong humanoid robot.
- **Significant Performance Boost:** FVP demonstrated substantial absolute improvements ranging from **15% to 55%** when applied to state-of-the-art 3D imitation learning methods like DP3 and RISE. RISE pre-trained with FVP achieved state-of-the-art performance, surpassing both 2D (e.g., ACT, Diffusion Policy) and other 3D single-task imitation learning methods.
- **Dexterous Hand Tasks:** FVP notably improved success rates in complex dexterous hand tasks (e.g., PickPlace, FlipCup, Assembly, ArtiManip), attributing this to FVP's ability to model complex motion trajectories by incorporating time frames.
- **Comparison to Other 3D Pre-training:** FVP consistently outperformed other 3D/4D pre-training methods (e.g., PointMAE, STRL, C2P) on real-world tasks, whether using in-domain or out-of-domain (Robomind) datasets.
- **Comparison to 2D Large Models:** FVP pre-training for DP3 proved more effective in real-world tasks than large 2D pre-trained models like R3M, MVP, and MAE (Soup-1M+100 DoH), even when those were fine-tuned with the same policy model.

**C. Enhancement of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:**
- **RDT-1B Integration:** FVP was applied to the RDT-1B VLA model, which typically relies on 2D images. By incorporating a FVP pre-trained 3D point cloud encoder, the RDT-1B model showed improved performance.
- **Improved Task Success:** FVP pre-trained 3D encoders significantly enhanced RDT-1B's success rates on various real-world tasks (e.g., PickSquare, PlaceBottle, PutBox, StackBowl, WipePlate) compared to 2D-only inputs or 3D inputs without FVP pre-training.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:**
 - **Spatial Understanding:** FVP pre-training improved RDT-1B's spatial perception (e.g., placing apples at four corners), showing a success rate of 14/20 compared to 8/20 (2D) and 11/20 (3D without FVP).
 - **Knowledge Transfer:** The model exhibited better task transferability (e.g., picking up an apple after learning to pick a banana), achieving 16/20 with FVP vs. 10/20 (2D) and 14/20 (3D without FVP).
 - **Language Understanding:** While less pronounced, FVP slightly enhanced robustness in language understanding (e.g., "pour water from left/right bottle").
 - **Long-Horizon Tasks:** FVP significantly improved performance on complex multi-step long-horizon tasks (e.g., placing apple, wiping table, pouring water), achieving 3/20 success vs. 0/20 (2D) and 2/20 (3D without FVP).

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **Importance of Historical Frames:** Removing historical frame information (i.e., using only the current frame's point cloud) led to a notable decrease in success rates (e.g., from 20/20 to 15/20 for PickSquare), confirming the critical role of temporal context (the "4D" aspect).
- **Impact of Number of Historical Frames:** Using a single historical frame as input yielded the best performance (e.g., 20/20 for PickSquare), with performance gradually decreasing as more historical frames were included (e.g., 15/20 for 4 frames), suggesting that a recent history is most impactful, and excessive history might introduce noise or irrelevant information.
- **Importance of Fine-tuning:** Freezing the pre-trained visual encoder during downstream policy training resulted in lower success rates, indicating that fine-tuning is necessary to adapt the pre-trained features to specific task dynamics and overcome potential domain gaps.

Overall, the findings robustly support FVP as a highly effective pre-training framework that significantly boosts the performance, robustness, and generalization capabilities of 3D visual representations for a wide array of robotic manipulation tasks, from simple pick-and-place to complex bimanual and humanoid operations, and even large VLA models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "4D Visual Pre-training (FVP)" framework presents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, with substantial potential impact on the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

**A. Addressing a Critical Gap in 3D Representation Learning:**
FVP directly addresses the long-standing challenge of learning generalizable 3D visual representations for robotics. While the importance of 3D information is recognized, the scarcity of large-scale 3D datasets has hampered progress. FVP offers a practical solution by providing a self-supervised pre-training method that can leverage existing robot trajectory data, rather than requiring massive, universally curated 3D datasets. This makes advanced 3D robot learning more accessible and scalable.

**B. Enabling More Robust and Generalizable Robotic Policies:**
By explicitly incorporating temporal dynamics into 3D visual representation learning—the "4D" aspect of next-point-cloud prediction via diffusion models—FVP equips robotic policies with a deeper understanding of how the environment evolves and how actions influence future states. This leads to:
- **Enhanced Performance:** Demonstrating significant performance boosts (up to 55% absolute improvement in real-world tasks, 28% overall average) across diverse manipulation tasks, robot platforms (single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid), and policy learning methods (DP3, RISE).
- **Improved Robustness:** The spatio-temporal features learned by FVP allow policies to be more robust to variations in the environment and complex motion trajectories, as evidenced in dexterous hand tasks.
- **Better Generalization:** FVP-pretrained models exhibit stronger generalization capabilities, performing well even with out-of-domain pre-training data and enhancing the transferability of knowledge to unseen tasks and scenarios.

**C. Advancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:**
The successful integration of FVP into large VLA models like RDT-1B is particularly impactful. It demonstrates that incorporating pre-trained 3D visual inputs can significantly augment the capabilities of these multimodal models, leading to:
- **Superior Spatial Understanding:** Robots can better perceive and interact with the 3D world when guided by FVP's representations.
- **Improved Task Comprehension:** Enhanced understanding of both the physical environment and linguistic instructions for complex tasks.
- **Better Long-Horizon Planning:** The ability to tackle multi-step, long-horizon tasks more effectively, which is a key challenge in advanced robotics.

**D. Versatility and Compatibility:**
FVP's design as a "plug-in" module means it can readily integrate with and improve various existing 3D encoders and imitation learning frameworks. This versatility maximizes its potential impact by allowing researchers and practitioners to leverage their preferred architectures while benefiting from FVP's pre-training advantages.

**E. Paving the Way for Future Research:**
The work highlights the critical role of spatio-temporal 3D understanding for truly intelligent robotic behavior. It encourages further exploration into novel self-supervised objectives that capture dynamic environmental interactions. The identified limitation regarding the lack of comprehensive open-source 3D robot datasets (e.g., missing camera parameters or depth data in Open-X-Embodiment) also points to a crucial area for future community effort in data collection and standardization.

In conclusion, FVP represents a powerful step forward in robot learning, offering a practical and highly effective method for learning generalizable 3D visual representations. Its ability to boost performance across a wide range of tasks and robot types, and to enhance large VLA models, positions it as a significant contribution towards enabling more capable, autonomous, and general-purpose robots in real-world environments.
