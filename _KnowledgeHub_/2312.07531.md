# WHAM: Reconstructing World-grounded Humans with Accurate 3D Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> WHAM reconstructs accurate 3D human pose and shape (HPS) and global motion from monocular video, operating efficiently within a consistent world coordinate system. It achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on challenging benchmarks while addressing issues like dynamic camera motion and foot sliding on diverse terrains.

## Problem

- Existing 3D HPS methods often produce temporally inconsistent or less accurate results, particularly video-based approaches.
- Global 3D motion estimation struggles with outputs relative to the camera, foot sliding artifacts, high computational costs, and disentangling human from dynamic camera motion.
- A lack of efficient, accurate, and robust solutions for world-grounded human motion estimation from monocular video for online applications.

## Method

- **WHAM** is an online, ==regression-based framework== that integrates motion context from ==2D keypoints==, dense visual cues from image features, and ==camera angular velocity==.
- It employs a ==two-stage training scheme==, pretraining on synthetically generated ==AMASS== data with dynamic virtual cameras and robust contact labels, followed by fine-tuning on real video datasets.
- A ==contact-aware trajectory refinement== mechanism is introduced to minimize foot sliding and accurately track motion on non-flat surfaces by leveraging ==foot-ground contact probabilities==.

## Results

- WHAM (ViT) achieves state-of-the-art per-frame 3D HPS accuracy, e.g., **35.9 mm** PA-MPJPE on 3DPW, outperforming previous image-based and video-based methods.
- It significantly improves global trajectory estimation, showing substantially lower Root Translation Error (e.g., **4.6%** with DROID-SLAM) and minimal foot sliding (**4.4 mm** FS error) on EMDB 2.
- Ablation studies confirm that feature integration, motion lifting pretraining, neural initialization, camera angular velocity input, and contact-aware trajectory refinement are all critical for its high performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective fusion of sparse 2D keypoint motion context and dense visual features from video frames significantly improves per-frame 3D HPS accuracy and temporal smoothness.
> - Leveraging camera angular velocity, obtained from SLAM or gyroscopes, is crucial for disentangling human motion from dynamic camera motion, enabling accurate global trajectory estimation.
> - A contact-aware trajectory refinement process based on foot velocity (rather than height) robustly mitigates foot sliding on diverse, non-flat terrains, while pretraining the 2D-to-3D lifting task on synthetic AMASS data projected onto dynamically moving cameras establishes a strong, camera-agnostic motion prior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D human pose and shape (HPS) estimation from monocular video has seen rapid advancements, yet several persistent limitations hinder its widespread adoption in real-world applications. WHAM (World-grounded Humans with Accurate Motion) positions itself as a direct response to these limitations, aiming to push the state-of-the-art significantly.

Historically, 3D HPS methods can be broadly categorized into image-based and video-based approaches. **Image-based methods** (e.g., HMR, SPIN, CLIFF, HMR2.0) focus on reconstructing 3D pose and shape from single images. While these have achieved remarkable accuracy, they often struggle with temporal consistency when applied frame-by-frame to videos, leading to jittery or implausible motions. Their strength lies in leveraging large datasets, often augmented with pseudo-ground truth 3D labels.

**Video-based methods** (e.g., VIBE, TCMR, MPS-Net, GLoT) explicitly attempt to exploit temporal information using recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or transformer architectures. Counter-intuitively, the paper highlights a significant limitation: existing video-based methods are often *less accurate* than the best single-frame methods in terms of per-frame pose and shape, potentially due to the scarcity of large-scale video datasets with ground-truth 3D annotations. WHAM aims to overcome this by effectively fusing motion context from 2D keypoint sequences (learned from vast mocap data like AMASS) with dense visual cues from real video frames.

A critical challenge addressed by WHAM is **global 3D motion estimation**. Most HPS methods reconstruct the human body in *camera coordinates*, meaning the output is relative to the camera's viewpoint. For applications requiring interaction with the environment (e.g., AR/VR, robotics), understanding human motion in a *consistent global coordinate system* is paramount. Prior work on global motion estimation often faces several issues:
1. **Foot sliding:** Methods assuming a flat ground plane or lacking robust contact modeling often produce unnatural foot sliding.
2. **Computational expense:** Highly accurate methods (e.g., SLAHMR, PACE) rely on optimization pipelines that are computationally intensive and unsuitable for real-time or online applications.
3. **Camera motion entanglement:** Decoupling human motion from dynamic camera motion is inherently ambiguous. Some approaches use off-the-shelf SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) for camera pose or other sensors (IMUs, RGB-D), but the latter are often intrusive or require specialized hardware. Regression-based methods (e.g., TRACE) are faster but tend to lack temporal consistency and accuracy.

WHAM directly tackles these limitations by introducing a novel, efficient, and accurate regression-based framework that performs HPS estimation directly in global coordinates. It innovates by:
- Learning from extensive mocap data (AMASS) for robust 3D motion priors.
- Fusing sparse 2D keypoint context with rich visual information from video pixels.
- Leveraging camera angular velocity (from SLAM or gyroscopes) to disentangle human and camera motion.
- Introducing a contact-aware trajectory refinement mechanism to eliminate foot sliding and enable generalization to non-flat terrains.

By addressing these core challenges, WHAM seeks to bridge the gap between accurate 3D human reconstruction, temporal consistency, global localization, and computational efficiency, thereby setting a new benchmark for markerless motion capture in the wild.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of WHAM is to accurately and efficiently estimate the 3D pose and shape of a person from monocular video within a consistent global coordinate system. This ambitious objective is driven by several specific motivations and aims to overcome critical limitations in the existing research landscape:

1. **Global Coordinate System Estimation:** A primary motivation is to move beyond estimations in camera coordinates. Many applications in AR/VR, robotics, and biomechanics require understanding human interaction within a real-world environment, necessitating a global, world-grounded representation of human motion.
2. **Computational Efficiency for Online Applications:** Current accurate methods often rely on computationally expensive optimization pipelines, rendering them impractical for real-time or online use. WHAM aims to develop an efficient, regression-based solution that can operate at high frame rates.
3. **Enhanced Accuracy and Temporal Smoothness:** Counter-intuitively, existing video-based methods have often been less accurate than the best single-frame approaches. WHAM seeks to reverse this trend by designing a system that leverages temporal information effectively to achieve both high per-frame accuracy and temporal smoothness, without over-smoothing realistic motions.
4. **Robustness to Dynamic Cameras and Diverse Terrains:** Accurately estimating global human trajectories from videos captured by moving cameras is a significant challenge due to the entanglement of human and camera motion. Furthermore, prior work often assumes flat ground planes, leading to implausible foot sliding on uneven surfaces. WHAM aims to handle arbitrary camera movements and accurately capture motion on diverse terrains (e.g., climbing stairs) by integrating camera angular velocity and a contact-aware refinement mechanism.
5. **Realistic Foot-Ground Contact:** Foot sliding is a common artifact in many 3D human motion reconstructions. A key objective is to produce natural-looking motion with realistic foot-ground contact, which is crucial for visual plausibility and physical accuracy.
6. **Leveraging Motion and Visual Context:** The paper is motivated by the idea that both the temporal context of human motion (learnable from motion capture data) and dense visual information from video frames (image cues) are crucial. WHAM aims to effectively integrate these two types of information, which existing methods often fail to combine optimally.

By addressing these objectives, WHAM seeks to make markerless human motion capture widely available and practical for demanding applications in various domains, from interactive entertainment to scientific analysis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WHAM introduces a novel, online, regression-based framework designed to reconstruct accurate 3D human motion in a global coordinate system from monocular video. Its methodology hinges on a multi-component neural network architecture and a two-stage training scheme that effectively integrates motion context, visual cues, camera dynamics, and foot-ground contact information.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture Overview

The system takes a raw video sequence as input. It first extracts 2D keypoints using a pre-trained detector (ViTPose) and static image features using a pre-trained image encoder. These form the basis for several interconnected modules:

1. **Uni-directional Motion Encoder (E_M) and Decoder (D_M):**
 - **Motion Encoder (E_M):** An RNN-based encoder processes sequences of 2D keypoints to extract `ϕ_m`, a motion context feature. Keypoints are normalized, and bounding box center/scale information is concatenated for robust representation. The uni-directional nature makes it suitable for online inference.
 - **Motion Decoder (D_M):** Another RNN-based decoder, taking the enhanced motion feature `ˆϕ_m` (described below), predicts the SMPL parameters (`θ`, `β`), weak-perspective camera translation (`c`), and crucial `p`, the foot-ground contact probability. It also predicts intermediate 3D keypoints (`x_3D`) to guide `ϕ_m` in representing 3D structure, a cascaded approach that aids learning. Neural Initialization is used for RNN hidden states, predicting them from the initial frame's pose instead of zero-padding, to ensure consistent learning objectives across frames.

2. **Motion and Visual Feature Integrator (F_I):**
 - This critical module addresses the sparsity of 2D keypoints. It combines the `ϕ_m` (motion context from 2D keypoints) with `ϕ_i` (dense visual context from image features extracted by a pre-trained image encoder).
 - `F_I` is a neural network that uses a simple residual connection: `ˆϕ_m = ϕ_m + F_I(concat(ϕ_m, ϕ_i))`. This fusion enriches the motion features with visual details, resolving ambiguities inherent in lifting sparse 2D keypoints to 3D meshes.

3. **Global Trajectory Decoder (D_T):**
 - This dedicated RNN decoder estimates the initial global root orientation (`Γ_0`) and root velocity (`v_0`).
 - To disentangle human motion from camera motion, the camera's angular velocity (`ω`) is concatenated with `ϕ_m` as an input. `ω` can be obtained from an off-the-shelf SLAM algorithm (e.g., DPVO, DROID-SLAM) or directly from a camera's gyroscope. `D_T` recursively predicts the global orientation, factoring in camera dynamics.

4. **Contact Aware Trajectory Refinement (R_T):**
 - To mitigate foot sliding and enable accurate tracking on non-flat surfaces, `WHAM` uses `p` (foot-ground contact probability) from `D_M`.
 - First, an egocentric root velocity `˜v` is adjusted to minimize foot sliding, based on the averaged velocity of toes and heels when `p` exceeds a threshold.
 - A trajectory refining network (`R_T`) then updates the root orientation (`Γ`) and velocity (`v`) using the history of `ϕ_m`, initial `Γ_0`, and `˜v`. This addresses noise that might arise from inaccurate contact or pose estimation.
 - Finally, the global translation (`τ`) is computed by integrating the refined velocities over time (roll-out operation).

#### 3.2. Training Strategy

WHAM employs a robust two-stage training strategy:

1. **Pretraining on AMASS:**
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** A vast dataset of synthetic 2D keypoint sequences and ground-truth SMPL parameters is generated from AMASS. Crucially, unlike prior work, this involves projecting 3D keypoints onto *dynamic* virtual cameras with varied rotational and translational motions. This approach ensures the learned motion representation is camera-agnostic and robust to real-world camera movements.
 - **Contact Label Generation:** Ground truth contact labels are derived from foot velocities (of heel and toe vertices), rather than height, to ensure generalizability to non-flat surfaces like stairs.
 - **Training Objective:** This stage trains `E_M`, `D_M`, and `D_T` to effectively lift 2D keypoint sequences to 3D poses and initial global trajectories, building a strong motion prior.

2. **Fine-tuning on Video Datasets:**
 - **Datasets:** The pre-trained network is fine-tuned on diverse real video datasets, including 3DPW, Human3.6M, MPI-INF-3DHP, and InstaVariety. Optionally, the large synthetic BEDLAM dataset can be added.
 - **Joint Training:** The entire network, including the newly introduced `F_I` and `R_T`, is trained. The pre-trained modules (E_M, D_M, D_T) are fine-tuned with a smaller learning rate, while `F_I` is trained from scratch.
 - **Image Encoder:** A pre-trained, fixed-weight image encoder (e.g., from SPIN, CLIFF, or HMR2.0) is used to extract visual features, allowing WHAM to leverage robust image-based representations.
 - **Supervision:** WHAM is supervised using a comprehensive set of loss functions for SMPL parameters, vertices, 3D keypoints, 2D reprojection (full-perspective or weak-perspective), cascaded 3D keypoint consistency, root orientation, root velocity, contact probability, camera angular velocity, camera pose, and a foot sliding loss based on foot velocity during contact.

This holistic methodology, from data synthesis and network architecture to training, is designed to simultaneously achieve accuracy, efficiency, and robustness in world-grounded 3D human motion estimation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WHAM demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple challenging benchmarks, achieving significant improvements in both 3D human pose and shape (HPS) accuracy and global trajectory estimation.

#### 4.1. 3D Human Motion Recovery (Per-frame and Smoothness)

- **Superior Per-frame Accuracy:** WHAM (especially the version using a Vision Transformer backbone, WHAM (ViT)) consistently outperforms all existing image-based and video-based state-of-the-art methods across all per-frame metrics (MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, and PVE) on three distinct in-the-wild datasets: 3DPW, RICH, and EMDB. For instance, on 3DPW, WHAM (ViT) achieves a PA-MPJPE of 35.9 mm, significantly lower than the best previous method ReFit (40.5 mm). This result is particularly noteworthy as it disproves the prevailing notion that video-based methods are less accurate than single-frame approaches.
- **Enhanced Inter-frame Smoothness:** WHAM exhibits significantly lower acceleration error compared to state-of-the-art per-frame methods (e.g., HMR2.0, ReFit), indicating that it reconstructs smooth and plausible 3D human motion over time without sacrificing accuracy. While its acceleration error is comparable to other temporal methods (e.g., TCMR, GLoT), WHAM avoids their tendency to over-smooth, as qualitatively shown in Figure 4 where WHAM accurately captures intricate joint movements like knee flexion during stair ascent, unlike methods that over-smooth and miss such details.
- **Impact of Image Encoder Backbone:** The choice of the image encoder backbone influences performance, with transformer-based architectures (ViT) yielding the highest accuracy, followed by HRNet (WHAM (HR)) and ResNet (WHAM (Res)).
- **BEDLAM Dataset Impact:** While training with BEDLAM further improves accuracy for WHAM, the relative improvement is smaller compared to other methods, suggesting WHAM's primary training strategy is already highly effective.

#### 4.2. 3D Global Trajectory Recovery

- **Unprecedented Global Accuracy:** WHAM significantly outperforms all existing methods for global motion estimation on the EMDB 2 dataset. This includes both optimization-based approaches like SLAHMR and regression-based methods like TRACE, as well as a strong baseline combining SLAM (DPVO) with an HPS regressor (HMR2.0).
- **Reduced Trajectory Error and Jitter:** WHAM achieves substantially lower WA-MPJPE_100, W-MPJPE_100, and Root Translation Error (RTE) than all competitors. For example, WHAM (w/ DROID) achieves an RTE of 4.6%, drastically lower than SLAHMR's 10.2% and TRACE's 17.7%. It also produces significantly less jitter compared to TRACE.
- **Minimal Foot Sliding:** WHAM demonstrates exceptional performance in reducing foot sliding (FS), achieving an FS error of 4.4 mm, which is substantially better than SLAHMR (14.5 mm) and TRACE (370.7 mm). This indicates highly plausible and physically consistent ground contact.
- **Agnostic to Angular Velocity Source:** WHAM performs consistently well whether using angular velocity from SLAM methods (DPVO, DROID-SLAM) or ground-truth gyroscope data, highlighting its robustness to the source of camera motion information.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Feature Integration is Crucial:** The feature integrator (F_I) is vital, as its removal (`w/o F_I`) leads to a notable decrease in both HPS accuracy and global trajectory estimation. This validates the effectiveness of fusing sparse motion context with dense visual context.
- **Lifting Pretraining is Fundamental:** Pretraining on the 2D-to-3D lifting task using AMASS is foundational; removing it (`w/o lifting`) results in a severe performance degradation across all metrics.
- **Neural Initialization (NI) Matters:** Removing Neural Initialization (`w/o NI`) impairs MPJPE, confirming its role in stabilizing RNN training.
- **Camera Angular Velocity (ω) is Essential for Global Motion:** Decoupling camera and human motion is critical. Removing camera angular velocity input (`w/o ω`) significantly increases the overall trajectory error (RTE) while maintaining similar per-frame pose accuracy, underscoring its importance for world-grounded estimation.
- **Contact-Aware Trajectory Refinement Improves Global Motion and Foot Sliding:** Disabling the trajectory refinement (`w/o traj. ref.`) increases global trajectory error and foot sliding, despite slightly reducing jitter. This confirms its targeted contribution to accuracy and foot-ground contact realism.

In summary, WHAM not only sets new accuracy benchmarks for 3D HPS and global trajectory estimation but also achieves this efficiently and with robust solutions to critical issues like foot sliding and dynamic camera motion, as systematically demonstrated through comprehensive quantitative and qualitative analyses, supported by detailed ablation studies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WHAM represents a significant leap forward in the field of 3D human pose and shape (HPS) estimation from monocular video, addressing several long-standing limitations and opening up new avenues for real-world applications.

1. **Bridging Key Gaps in HPS Research:**
 - **Global, World-grounded Motion:** WHAM effectively shifts the paradigm from camera-centric to world-centric human motion reconstruction. This is crucial for applications requiring understanding of human-environment interaction, where the global position and movement of the human relative to the scene are essential.
 - **Efficiency Meets Accuracy:** Historically, accurate 3D HPS methods have been computationally expensive, while efficient methods often sacrificed accuracy. WHAM breaks this trade-off by achieving state-of-the-art accuracy with an online, regression-based framework that runs significantly faster than prior optimization-based approaches. This combination makes it suitable for real-time applications.
 - **Overcoming Video-based Limitations:** By outperforming both image-based and other video-based methods in per-frame accuracy while maintaining temporal smoothness, WHAM addresses the counter-intuitive problem where video context previously failed to yield superior accuracy. This validates a novel approach to combining motion and visual cues effectively.

2. **Robustness to Real-world Challenges:**
 - **Dynamic Cameras:** The ability to accurately estimate human motion despite an arbitrarily moving camera is a major advancement, making WHAM highly practical for in-the-wild video capture.
 - **Elimination of Foot Sliding on Diverse Terrains:** The novel contact-aware trajectory refinement mechanism is a critical contribution. By preventing foot sliding and enabling accurate tracking on non-planar surfaces (like stairs), WHAM produces far more plausible and physically realistic motion, enhancing the quality of reconstruction significantly.

3. **Unified and Comprehensive Approach:**
 - WHAM's methodology is a sophisticated integration of motion context (learned from mocap data via 2D keypoints), dense visual context (from image features), external camera dynamics (angular velocity from SLAM/gyroscope), and learned contact information. This holistic approach captures a broader spectrum of cues than previous methods, leading to its superior performance.
 - The two-stage training scheme, leveraging vast synthetic data for motion priors and then fine-tuning on real video with visual context, is a robust strategy for tackling data scarcity and ambiguity.

4. **Broad Potential Impact and Applications:** The capabilities of WHAM have far-reaching implications across various domains:
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Enables more realistic and immersive interactions between virtual environments and real-world human users.
 - **Gaming:** Allows for highly realistic character animation from live video, potentially simplifying game development workflows.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** Provides robots with accurate real-time understanding of human motion in shared spaces, enhancing safety and collaboration.
 - **Sports Analysis:** Offers precise biomechanical insights from standard video footage, without the need for intrusive markers or specialized equipment.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Improves pedestrian and human activity recognition, contributing to safer and more intelligent autonomous systems.
 - **Film and Animation:** Streamlines the process of motion capture for digital characters.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** WHAM serves as a strong baseline and opens several promising avenues for future work, as highlighted by the authors:
 - Integrating more comprehensive 3D scene information from SLAM (intrinsics, extrinsics, 3D scene structure) to enforce consistency between the human and the environment.
 - Initializing optimization-based post-processing (akin to bundle adjustment) for even higher accuracy by jointly optimizing camera, scene, and human motion.
 - Developing real-time, phone-based implementations to democratize high-quality motion capture.
 - Extending contact estimation beyond feet to other body parts and addressing human-object interactions.

In conclusion, WHAM's combination of accuracy, efficiency, and robustness in world-grounded human motion estimation from monocular video marks a substantial advancement, poised to drive the next generation of markerless motion capture applications and inspire further innovation in the field.
