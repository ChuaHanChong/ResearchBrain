# GenVidBench: A 6-Million Benchmark for AI-Generated Video Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces GenVidBench, a 6.78-million video benchmark for AI-generated video detection, featuring challenging cross-source and cross-generator evaluation settings and rich semantic annotations. This dataset proved more difficult for state-of-the-art models, with DeMamba achieving the highest accuracy of 85.47% in the most challenging configuration.

## Problem

- The rapid advancement of AI video generation blurs the line between real and synthetic content, raising concerns about misinformation and digital trust.
- Existing AI-generated video detection datasets are limited in scale, diversity, and lack challenging evaluation settings, such as cross-source or cross-generator scenarios.
- Current detection models struggle to generalize, often overfitting to specific generator artifacts and failing to perform robustly on unseen generative sources.

## Method

- Developed **GenVidBench**, a large-scale dataset comprising 6.78 million videos from two distinct real video sources and 11 state-of-the-art AI video generators.
- Implemented a rigorous dataset design with explicit ==cross-source and cross-generator evaluation settings== to promote the development of more generalizable detection models.
- Provided rich ==semantic annotations== (object categories, actions, locations) for videos, enabling fine-grained analysis and scenario-specific detection research.

## Results

- GenVidBench demonstrated increased difficulty compared to existing datasets; for instance, SlowFast's accuracy dropped from **82.55%** on NeuralTextures to **70.06%** on GenVidBench.
- In the primary cross-source and cross-generator task on GenVidBench-6M, DeMamba achieved the highest Top-1 accuracy (**85.47%**), F1 score (**90.27**), and AUROC (**99.28**).
- Analysis revealed that Sora and SVD-generated videos were exceptionally challenging for detection models, while CogVideo outputs were generally easier to identify, reflecting varying degrees of generative quality and realism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current AI-generated video detection models exhibit substantial performance degradation when confronted with unseen generative models or different real video sources, underscoring a significant generalization gap.
> - Transformer-based architectures generally outperform CNNs in AI-generated video detection, with DeMamba demonstrating superior effectiveness in the challenging cross-evaluation tasks.
> - The detectability of AI-generated videos varies considerably among generators, with outputs from models like Sora and SVD proving particularly challenging to distinguish from real content.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) in generating realistic video content has created a new challenge in distinguishing synthetic videos from authentic ones. This development has significant implications, including the potential for widespread misinformation, damage to personal and corporate reputations, and the escalation of cybersecurity threats. Consequently, the development of robust AI-generated video detection systems has become a critical area of research.

Historically, research in detecting manipulated media has focused on AI-generated images (e.g., GenImage, WildFake, ArtiFact, DiffusionDB) and deepfake videos (e.g., DFDC, Celeb-DF, FaceForensics++). Deepfake datasets primarily concentrate on facial manipulations using techniques like GANs and VAEs, often lacking diversity in general video content beyond faces.

In the more specific domain of AI-generated video detection, several datasets have emerged, including Generated Video Dataset (GVD), GenVideo, Generated Video Forensics (GVF), and GenVidDet. However, these existing datasets present limitations:
- **Scale and Scope**: Datasets like GVD (11k videos) and GVF (2.8k videos) are relatively small. While GenVideo (2.27M) and GenVidDet (2.66M) offer a larger scale, they still fall short of the volume necessary for training highly generalized models.
- **Lack of Ancillary Data**: Many existing datasets (GVD, GenVideo, GenVidDet) do not provide the original prompts or images used for video generation, nor do they include semantic labels, which limits their utility for fine-grained analysis.
- **Limited Challenge Settings**: Crucially, most prior datasets do not incorporate challenging setups like cross-source or cross-generator evaluation. This means models trained on these datasets might perform well by learning artifacts specific to certain generators or content styles present in the training set, rather than developing generalizable detection capabilities. GVF includes video pairs and semantic labels but lacks a cross-source setting and is small in scale.

The work presented in this paper directly addresses these limitations by introducing GenVidBench, a dataset designed to be significantly larger, more diverse, and more challenging than its predecessors. It aims to provide a benchmark that can drive the development of more robust and generalized AI-generated video detectors, aligning with the increasing sophistication of video generation technologies like Sora, MuseV, and Kling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to introduce GenVidBench, a novel, large-scale, and challenging dataset specifically designed to advance the field of AI-generated video detection. The motivation for creating GenVidBench is multifaceted, stemming from the rapid evolution of video generation technology and the societal challenges it presents.

The primary motivations and specific objectives are as follows:

- **Addressing the Blurring Line Between Real and Synthetic Videos**: Recent breakthroughs in AI video generation models, exemplified by technologies such as Sora, have led to a substantial increase in the realism and quality of AI-generated videos. This has made it increasingly difficult for both human observers and automated systems to reliably distinguish between real and synthetic content. The paper highlights this as an urgent issue requiring advanced detection capabilities.
- **Mitigating Societal Risks from Misinformation**: The ability to generate highly realistic, yet entirely fabricated, videos poses considerable risks. These include the accelerated dissemination of false information, potential damage to the reputations of individuals and organizations, and increased vulnerabilities in cybersecurity. Effective detection mechanisms are crucial to counter these threats.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Quality Limitations**: The development of high-performance AI-generated video detectors has been impeded by the absence of adequately large-scale, high-quality, and diverse datasets. Existing datasets often lack the volume, variety of generators, or specific challenging characteristics needed to train and rigorously evaluate robust detection models.
- **Promoting Generalizability in Detection Models**: Many current detection models tend to overfit to specific artifacts present in the training data, particularly when trained and tested on videos from the same generator. This limits their effectiveness when encountering videos from new or unknown generative sources. GenVidBench aims to mitigate this by explicitly designing for:
 - **Large-scale video collection**: To provide statistical robustness and improve the generalization ability of models across diverse scenarios. GenVidBench contains 6.78 million videos, making it the largest dataset in this domain.
 - **Cross-Source and Cross-Generator Diversity**: To compel detectors to learn fundamental distinguishing features rather than generator-specific quirks. The dataset ensures that training and test sets are derived from different real video sources and generated by distinct sets of AI models, preventing models from relying on content or known generator attributes.
 - **Inclusion of State-of-the-Art Generators**: By incorporating videos from 11 advanced AI video generators (including Sora, MuseV, Mora, Kling), the dataset ensures that it represents the cutting edge of video generation quality, posing a realistic challenge for detection models.
- **Enabling Fine-Grained Analysis and Scenario-Specific Detection**: The provision of rich semantic annotations (object categories, actions, and locations) for videos is a key objective. This allows researchers to:
 - Analyze detection performance under specific conditions (e.g., identifying videos of 'people' performing 'active engagement' in 'natural landscapes').
 - Identify "hard cases" where detection models struggle, which can inform future research directions.
 - Develop and evaluate detectors tailored to specific application scenarios or content types.
- **Establishing a Comprehensive Benchmark**: By providing a meticulously constructed dataset alongside extensive experimental results from state-of-the-art video classification models, the paper aims to establish a solid foundation and benchmark for the research community, facilitating efficient development and evaluation of AI-generated video detection technologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing GenVidBench focuses on creating a large-scale, diverse, and challenging dataset for AI-generated video detection through a structured collection and annotation process.

**Dataset Scale and Versions:**
GenVidBench comprises a total of 6,784,490 video samples, making it the largest dataset of its kind. To accommodate varying computational resources, a lightweight version, GenVidBench-143k, consisting of 143,400 meticulously sampled videos, is also provided. This subset maintains representativeness and diversity for faster model iteration.

**Video Sources and Generation:**
The dataset integrates videos from two distinct real video sources and 11 state-of-the-art AI video generators.
- **Real Video Sources:**
 - Vript: Used as a real video source for the training set's paired videos.
 - HD-VG-130M: Used as a real video source for the test set's paired videos.
- **AI Video Generators:** The 11 generators are divided into training and test sets to facilitate cross-generator evaluation.
 - **Training Set Generators (Pair 1)**: Pika, VideoCrafterV2 (VCV2), Modelscope (MS), T2V-Zero (T2VZ), and OpenSora. These are primarily Text-to-Video (T2V) or Image-to-Video (I2V) models.
 - **Test Set Generators (Pair 2)**: MuseV, SVD, Mora, CogVideo, Sora, and Kling. These also include both T2V and I2V capabilities.

**Dataset Construction with Challenging Characteristics:**
The dataset construction incorporates several design principles to enhance its challenge and utility for generalization:

1. **Paired Video Generation for Content Alignment**:
 - **Pair 1 (Training)**: Videos from Pika, VCV2, MS, and T2VZ are generated using *uniform text prompts* sourced from the VidProM dataset. These generated videos are paired with real videos from Vript, ensuring semantic content similarity.
 - **Pair 2 (Testing)**: Videos from MuseV and SVD are generated by extracting *frames (images)* from the HD-VG-130M dataset, serving as Image-to-Video (I2V) generation. Mora and CogVideo videos are generated using *text prompts* extracted from the HD-VG-130M dataset, serving as Text-to-Video (T2V) generation. These generated videos are paired with real videos from HD-VG-130M. This content alignment ensures that detectors cannot simply differentiate videos based on content variations, forcing them to learn subtle generative artifacts.

2. **Cross-Source Setting**: The entire first video pair (Vript-based real and its corresponding generations) is allocated to the training set. The entire second video pair (HD-VG-130M-based real and its corresponding generations) is allocated to the test set. This design evaluates the detector's ability to generalize to real videos and synthetic videos derived from an entirely different real-world source than those seen during training.

3. **Cross-Generator Setting**: The set of AI generators used for the training pair (Pika, VCV2, MS, T2VZ, OpenSora) is completely distinct from the set of generators used for the test pair (MuseV, SVD, Mora, CogVideo, Sora, Kling). This prevents models from learning generator-specific fingerprints, compelling them to identify more universal indicators of AI generation.

4. **Diverse Video Attributes**: GenVidBench includes a wide range of video attributes:
 - **Types**: Real and AI-generated.
 - **Tasks**: Text-to-Video (T2V) and Image-to-Video (I2V).
 - **Resolutions**: From 256x256 (e.g., Modelscope, OpenSora) to 1920x1080 (e.g., Sora).
 - **Frame Rates**: From 4 FPS (e.g., CogVideo, T2V-Zero) to 30 FPS (e.g., Vript, HD-VG-130M, Sora, Kling). This heterogeneity aims for comprehensive coverage of diverse content.

**Semantic Annotations:**
A portion of the dataset is enriched with semantic content labels, categorized along three dimensions:
1. **Object Categories**: Describing the main subjects in the video (e.g., People, Animals, Buildings, Natures).
2. **Actions**: Reflecting the temporal attributes (e.g., Static Postures, Holding Objects, Active Engagement).
3. **Locations**: Indicating the scenario complexity (e.g., Natural Landscapes, Cities and Towns, Residential and Indoor Places).
These labels are generated by sampling prompts, utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs) to extract subjects, and then aggregating them into abstract categories (no more than ten options per dimension) to form a classification tree. This allows for scenario-specific analysis and focused research on particular types of content.

**Experimental Evaluation:**
The paper evaluates the performance of 11 state-of-the-art video classification models on GenVidBench. These include CNN-based models (I3D, SlowFast, TPN, TIN, TRN, TSM, X3D), Transformer-based models (TimeSformer, UniFormerV2, VideoSwin, MViTv2-S/B), and a Mamba-based model (DeMamba).
- **Input**: Models process 8 frames sampled from each video, resized to 224x224 pixels.
- **Training**: Default learning rates from MMAction2 are used, along with data augmentation techniques like random flipping and cropping.
- **Tasks**: Experiments include:
 - Cross-validation to assess intra-generator vs. inter-generator performance.
 - Comparison of same-source vs. cross-source generation effects.
 - The primary cross-source and cross-generator detection task on GenVidBench-6M.
 - A scenario-specific task focusing on the 'Plants' category to demonstrate the utility of semantic labels.
- **Metrics**: Top-1 accuracy, F1 score, and Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (AUROC) are used for evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of GenVidBench yielded several key findings, underscoring the dataset's challenging nature and providing insights into the performance of current AI-generated video detection models.

**1. Challenging Nature of GenVidBench:**
- **Cross-Generator Difficulty**: Initial experiments using VideoSwin-Tiny demonstrated that models achieve high accuracy (above 97.40%, some exceeding 99.90%) when trained and tested on videos from the *same generator*. However, performance significantly degrades when testing on videos generated by an *unseen generator*. For instance, a model trained on Pika-generated videos showed an accuracy of 54.66% when tested on SVD-generated videos. This confirms that current models struggle to generalize across different generative models.
- **Cross-Source Difficulty**: Testing models on videos from *different generation sources* (e.g., distinct underlying real video datasets) consistently resulted in lower average accuracies compared to testing on videos from the same source. For example, for models in "Pair1" (Pika, VCV2, ModelScope, T2V-Zero), the mean accuracy was 61.81% for same-source testing but dropped to 56.71% for cross-source testing. Similar trends were observed for "Pair2" (MuseV, SVD, CogVideo, Mora), with mean accuracies of 67.79% and 61.27%, respectively. This indicates a strong dependency of detectors on the generation source.
- **Comparison with Existing Datasets**: When benchmarked against other deepfake and AI-generated video datasets, GenVidBench proved to be more challenging. SlowFast, I3D, and F3Net models consistently achieved lower performance on GenVidBench compared to datasets like NeuralTextures, GVF, and GenVideo. For instance, SlowFast's accuracy dropped from 82.55% on NeuralTextures to 70.06% on GenVidBench, highlighting the increased difficulty presented by the proposed dataset.

**2. Performance of State-of-the-Art Detection Models (Cross-Source and Cross-Generator Task on GenVidBench-6M):**
- **Top Performers**: DeMamba achieved the highest Top-1 accuracy (85.47%), F1 score (90.27), and AUROC (99.28), demonstrating its effectiveness in this challenging setting. MViTv2-S followed with a Top-1 accuracy of 80.45%.
- **Model Architectures**: Transformer-based models generally outperformed CNN-based models, with TSM being the best-performing CNN at 73.88% Top-1 accuracy. This suggests that architectures capable of capturing long-range dependencies and complex patterns are better suited for this task.
- **Detectability of Real vs. Fake**: Real videos were generally easier to distinguish from AI-generated ones, with most classification accuracies exceeding 95.04%.
- **Generator-Specific Difficulty**:
 - **Sora-generated videos** were identified as particularly challenging to classify correctly, likely reflecting their superior generation quality. However, it is noted that the sample size for Sora in the dataset is small (51 videos), which could influence this observation.
 - **CogVideo-generated videos** were generally easier to detect, possibly due to limited temporal continuity or more discernible artifacts. DeMamba, for example, achieved 98.66% accuracy on CogVideo.
 - **SVD-generated videos** also posed a significant challenge for detection models, with models like I3D and SlowFast showing particularly low accuracy on this subset (e.g., I3D 12.04%, SlowFast 29.80% for SVD).

**3. Hard Case Analysis (VideoSwin-Tiny):**
- A detailed analysis of hard cases (generated videos with very low assigned detection probability) revealed considerable variation across generators.
- **SVD** was found to generate the most challenging videos for the detector, suggesting its output quality makes it harder to distinguish from real content.
- **CogVideo**, conversely, produced almost no severely ambiguous cases, aligning with its generally higher detectability.
- The mean probability assigned to hard cases across categories was approximately 0.297, with SVD having the highest mean (0.739) and CogVideo the lowest (0.017), indicating the relative difficulty of detecting their outputs.

**4. Scenario-Specific Task (Plants Class):**
- Evaluating models specifically on the 'Plants' content category demonstrated that performance can differ significantly from overall dataset performance.
- TimeSformer achieved the highest Top-1 accuracy for this class (75.09%), while VideoSwin, a strong performer overall, showed a lower accuracy of 52.86%.
- Within the 'Plants' class, SVD again exhibited the lowest classification accuracy, implying that its generated videos of plants are particularly realistic or difficult to detect. This highlights the utility of GenVidBench's semantic labels for fine-grained analysis of detection performance in specific contexts.

In summary, the results consistently indicate that GenVidBench is a challenging dataset, primarily due to its cross-source and cross-generator design. While certain advanced models show promise, there remains substantial room for improvement in developing truly robust and generalizable AI-generated video detectors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GenVidBench introduces a significant advancement in the field of AI-generated video detection, offering substantial significance and potential impact:

**1. Advancement of AI-Generated Video Detection Research:**
The most direct impact is the provision of a comprehensive, large-scale, and challenging benchmark dataset. By surpassing existing datasets in scale (6.78 million videos) and diversity, GenVidBench facilitates more robust training and rigorous evaluation of detection models. It moves beyond simpler detection scenarios by incorporating state-of-the-art generative models and deliberately constructing challenging cross-source and cross-generator tasks. This will likely accelerate research by providing a standardized platform for comparing new methodologies and identifying their strengths and weaknesses.

**2. Development of More Generalizable Detectors:**
The dataset's unique design, particularly the cross-source and cross-generator settings, compels researchers to develop detectors that learn intrinsic properties distinguishing real from synthetic videos, rather than relying on generator-specific artifacts or content cues. This is crucial for real-world applications where the source and generator of a manipulated video are typically unknown. The benchmark encourages the development of models that are more resilient to evolving generation techniques and novel unseen generators.

**3. Enhanced Understanding of Detection Challenges:**
The detailed experimental results, including the analysis of hard cases and scenario-specific performance, provide valuable insights into the current limitations of detection models. Identifying which generators produce the most challenging videos (e.g., Sora, SVD) and which content categories are particularly difficult (e.g., 'Plants' for SVD) can guide future research efforts toward specific problem areas, such as improving temporal consistency detection or artifact analysis in complex scenes.

**4. Support for Scenario-Specific Applications:**
The inclusion of rich semantic annotations (object categories, actions, locations) enables researchers to tailor detection models for specific application scenarios. For instance, a model could be developed and evaluated specifically for detecting generated videos depicting "people" in "active engagement" in "residential places." This allows for specialized detectors that might be more effective in targeted use cases, potentially improving accuracy where it is most critical.

**5. Contribution to Countering Misinformation and Malicious Use:**
By fostering the development of more accurate and generalized AI-generated video detectors, GenVidBench indirectly contributes to societal efforts to combat misinformation, propaganda, and other malicious uses of synthetic media. As detection capabilities improve, it becomes harder for bad actors to leverage AI-generated videos for harmful purposes, thereby bolstering digital trust and security.

**6. Future Research Directions:**
The benchmark results, which show that even state-of-the-art models exhibit significant performance gaps in cross-generator and cross-source tasks, highlight the need for further research in areas such as domain adaptation, robust feature learning, and novel architectural designs. The availability of semantic labels also opens avenues for exploring how content attributes influence detectability, potentially leading to more interpretable and explainable detection models.

In conclusion, GenVidBench is positioned to become a foundational resource for the AI community, providing the necessary data scale and complexity to push the boundaries of AI-generated video detection and address the pressing societal implications of advanced video synthesis technologies.
