# Truncated Proximal Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Truncated Proximal Policy Optimization (T-PPO) significantly enhances the training efficiency of large language models for complex reasoning tasks by enabling progressive policy updates on incomplete trajectories. The method achieved up to 2.5x higher training efficiency, leading to a 60% reduction in wall-clock time while securing a 62 pass@1 score on the AIME 2024 benchmark.

## Problem

- Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a standard for fine-tuning LLMs, suffers from computational inefficiency due to its on-policy nature, requiring new, lengthy trajectory collection for each policy update, especially for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
- The 'barrel effect' in distributed PPO training leads to low GPU utilization, as the system waits for the longest generated response in a batch to complete, leaving other resources idle.
- Achieving high reasoning performance in LLMs typically demands generation of very long sequences, exacerbating the PPO efficiency problem and increasing training costs.

## Method

- Introduces ==Extended Generalized Advantage Estimation (EGAE)==, which allows the calculation of advantage estimates on truncated trajectories by approximating the value of future, ungenerated states, enabling progressive policy updates.
- Implements a ==token filtering strategy with successive batching==: generation is truncated by a window length, and completed sequences are dynamically replaced with new prompts to maintain constant batch size and maximize GPU throughput.
- Employs a ==dual optimization mechanism== where the policy model is updated on currently generated tokens using EGAE, while the value model is updated using Monte Carlo returns only from fully completed trajectories to ensure unbiased value predictions.

## Results

- T-PPO achieved a **62** pass@1 score on the AIME 2024 benchmark, outperforming existing baselines like DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B (**47**), DAPO (**50**), GePPO (**50**), PPO-EWMA (**52**), and slightly exceeding VAPO (**60**).
- It demonstrated up to **2.5x** higher training efficiency, resulting in a **60%** reduction in wall-clock time compared to prior state-of-the-art synchronization algorithms.
- Hardware utilization significantly improved, reaching **249** operations/byte in policy rollout on an Nvidia H800 GPU, compared to PPO's **84** operations/byte, indicating better compute resource utilization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy optimization for large language models (LLMs) can be effectively performed on truncated, incomplete reasoning trajectories, significantly reducing idle compute time.
> - Dynamic batch management and successive batching, where new prompts fill slots left by completed sequences, substantially improve GPU utilization during LLM generation.
> - Decoupling policy updates (using Extended Generalized Advantage Estimation on current tokens) from value updates (using Monte Carlo on completed trajectories) allows for both efficient training and stable, unbiased value predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically addressing a critical bottleneck in the training of reasoning-oriented LLMs: **computational inefficiency**. Recent advancements in LLMs, exemplified by models like OpenAI’s o1, DeepSeek-R1, and QwQ, have showcased remarkable reasoning capabilities, particularly when employing extended Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. These CoT capabilities are largely attributed to the application of deep RL techniques, with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) being the predominant algorithm for fine-tuning LLMs to generate structured, logical reasoning steps.

However, PPO's inherent on-policy nature presents significant challenges to training efficiency. It requires new data collection (rollouts) for each policy update, which becomes prohibitively expensive when LLMs generate long CoT trajectories. The "barrel effect" in distributed computing, where overall training speed is limited by the slowest component (in this case, the longest generated response in a batch), further exacerbates this issue, leading to underutilization of expensive hardware like GPUs.

The broader research landscape has seen various attempts to mitigate PPO's inefficiency. One major thrust involves developing **off-policy PPO variants** (e.g., GePPO, Off-Policy PPO, PPO-EWMA), which aim to reuse old trajectories to improve sample efficiency. While these methods can be more training-efficient, they often suffer from high variance in policy gradient estimators, leading to unstable training and potentially degraded performance. Another approach, seen in models like KIMI K1.5, involves using partial rollouts or reusing chunks of previous trajectories, focusing on data reuse to avoid full regeneration costs.

T-PPO directly addresses these challenges by proposing an **enhanced on-policy framework** that significantly boosts efficiency without sacrificing performance or stability. Instead of relying on off-policy data, T-PPO innovates within the on-policy paradigm by:
1. **Enabling progressive policy updates:** By allowing policy optimization from *incomplete* trajectories, T-PPO reduces the idle time of GPUs that are waiting for the longest sequences in a batch to complete. This is a crucial distinction from traditional PPO where an entire batch must complete before an update.
2. **Optimizing hardware utilization:** The "truncated rollout strategy" and "successive batching" directly tackle the low hardware utilization problem, making the training process less dependent on the maximal response length and more dynamic.

This work contributes to the active research area of making LLM training more scalable and cost-effective, which is vital for developing and deploying advanced AI systems. It pushes the boundaries of on-policy RL for LLMs by introducing a novel way to manage long sequence generation, offering a practical solution to a major computational hurdle.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly improve the training efficiency of Large Language Models (LLMs) that specialize in reasoning tasks, particularly those generating long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) responses**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing PPO's Inefficiency for Long Sequences:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is the de facto standard for fine-tuning LLMs with reinforcement learning. However, its on-policy nature mandates that new data (trajectories) be collected for each policy update. When LLMs generate extensive CoT responses—which are crucial for complex reasoning tasks like mathematical problem-solving—these data collection phases become exceedingly long and computationally expensive, leading to protracted training durations.
- **Mitigating Low Hardware Utilization:** A significant practical challenge in training LLMs with RL, especially with varying response lengths in a batch, is the "barrel effect." The system often waits for the longest sequence in a batch to complete its generation, leaving GPUs underutilized while other shorter sequences are idle. This leads to inefficient use of expensive computational resources. The authors explicitly state that T-PPO aims to mitigate this "inherent drawback of fully synchronized long-generation procedures, where resources often sit idle during the waiting periods for complete rollouts."
- **Maintaining Performance and Stability:** While improving efficiency, a critical underlying motivation is to do so *without sacrificing* the strong reasoning capabilities or the training stability that PPO offers. Many off-policy variants, while efficient, struggle with high variance and unstable training, which can degrade final model performance. T-PPO aims to achieve efficiency while maintaining or even enhancing reasoning performance and robust convergence.
- **Facilitating Practical Deployment:** By reducing training costs and time, the research seeks to make advanced reasoning LLMs more accessible and practical for real-world deployment. Lower training overhead allows for faster iteration, more extensive experimentation, and ultimately, the development of more specialized and capable expert models for various professional domains.

In essence, the authors are motivated to solve the practical problem of scaling RL training for complex LLM behaviors by fundamentally rethinking how policy updates and data collection occur when dealing with variable and extended sequence lengths, thereby maximizing hardware throughput and accelerating the entire training process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

T-PPO introduces a novel approach to Proximal Policy Optimization designed to enhance training efficiency for LLMs generating long reasoning chains. Its methodology hinges on three core components: Extended Generalized Advantage Estimation (EGAE), a token filtering strategy, and an optimized dual-optimization mechanism.

#### 3.1. Extended Generalized Advantage Estimation (EGAE)

The cornerstone of T-PPO's ability to handle incomplete trajectories is EGAE. Traditionally, Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) calculates an advantage estimate ($\hat{A}_t$) using a discounted sum of TD residuals ($\delta_t$) over a complete trajectory, balancing bias and variance. T-PPO extends this by allowing advantage computation even when a trajectory is **truncated** at a specified "window length" ($l$) rather than waiting for the entire response to complete.

The key innovation here is a heuristic assumption: for a truncated trajectory $\tau = (s_0, a_0..., s_{l-1}, a_{l-1})$, the value of the next, ungenerated state is approximated as equal to the value of the last generated state, i.e., $V(s_l) = V(s_{l-1})$. Under this assumption, the GAE formula (Equation 2 in the paper) remains applicable, simply replacing the total trajectory length $T$ with the truncation length $l$. This allows for "progressive policy updates" during the response generation process itself, decoupling policy updates from the completion of full responses and significantly improving computational resource utilization. The authors argue that as TD residuals are heavily discounted, truncating the trajectory has a negligible effect on the actions in the front portion of the response.

#### 3.2. Token Filtering Strategy (Successive Batching)

This strategy directly addresses the problem of GPU underutilization caused by varying response lengths in a batch. Instead of waiting for all sequences in a batch to complete, T-PPO employs a **successive batching** mechanism:
- **Truncated Rollouts:** Generation is truncated by a predefined "window length" ($l$).
- **Dynamic Batch Management:** If some sequences within a batch reach an end-of-sequence token (i.e., complete their generation) before the window length is reached, they are removed from the current batch. New prompts are then inserted into their place for the subsequent training step. Incomplete sequences continue generating from where they left off in the next iteration. This ensures the total batch size remains constant, maximizing GPU throughput.
- **Selective Optimization:** The strategy also dictates which tokens are used for optimizing the policy and value models:
 - **Policy Model Optimization:** The policy model is trained on response tokens generated in the *current training step*, regardless of whether the sequence is complete or not. This leverages the EGAE. Additionally, the authors note that some of the latest generated tokens of *unfinished* sequences may be excluded from policy training if their advantage estimates are deemed too high variance.
 - **Value Model Optimization:** Crucially, the value model is trained using a **Monte Carlo approach** (TD(1)), which relies on *actual observed returns* from *fully completed* trajectories. This means value updates are deferred until a sequence is entirely generated. This decision is made to "maintain strictly unbiased state-value predictions," deliberately avoiding the approximation bias introduced by GAE's temporal difference components for the value function.

This dual optimization strategy allows for independent, yet simultaneous, improvement of both policy and value models, with the token filtering mechanism ensuring that computational resources are efficiently utilized by continually feeding the GPUs with active generation tasks.

#### 3.3. Implementation and Training Efficiency Analysis

Algorithm 1 outlines the complete T-PPO procedure:
1. Initialize policy and value networks.
2. In each step, collect a batch of prompts (combining unfinished samples from the previous step with new ones).
3. Update the `old` policy.
4. Run the `old` policy to collect trajectories up to the `window length` ($l$).
5. Compute rewards for *finished* trajectories.
6. Calculate advantage estimates ($\hat{A}_{i,t}$) for *each policy token* using EGAE (Equation 6).
7. In mini-batches, update the policy model by maximizing the clipped T-PPO objective (Equation 1) using the policy tokens and their EGAE-derived advantages.
8. Update the value function via gradient descent (Equation 5) using the value tokens (from *finished* trajectories).

The authors analyze the efficiency gains: if $L$ is the actual maximum response length and $l$ is the window length, then the generation and training time savings are approximately $L/l$ times. This "end-to-end training efficiency ratio" directly translates into faster training for the same number of steps. The method operates on-policy, like vanilla PPO, but achieves significant efficiency by breaking down the synchronized generation barrier and allowing for continuous policy improvements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that T-PPO achieves substantial improvements in training efficiency for reasoning LLMs without compromising, and in some cases enhancing, their performance.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen-2.5-Base-32B.
- **Benchmark:** American Invitational Mathematics Examination (AIME 2024), chosen for its requirement of long chains-of-thought.
- **Training Data:** DAPO-Math-17K, comprising past AIME problems and artificially constructed math problems.
- **Reward Function:** A sparse, verification-based reward (R(x,y) = 1 if the final answer is correct, 0 otherwise) using Math-Verify.
- **Hyperparameters:** Standard PPO-like settings (AdamW optimizer, specific learning rates for policy and critic, batch size 512, GAE $\lambda=0.95$, $\gamma=1.0$, clipping parameters $\epsilon_{low/high}$ and $\xi_{low/high}$). Crucially, maximum response length was 24k, while T-PPO's window length was 8k (implying $k=3$ potential speedup factor). KL divergence was removed from the loss to encourage exploration, given the distribution shift between the base model and reasoning task.
- **Baselines:** Vanilla PPO, DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, DAPO, VAPO, GePPO, and PPO-EWMA.

#### 4.2. Main Results (Performance)

- **AIME 2024 Scores:** T-PPO achieved a **62 pass@1 score** on the AIME 2024 benchmark (specifically, 61.88 avg@32).
- **Outperformance:** This score surpasses most existing competitors, including DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B (47), DAPO (50), GePPO (50), and PPO-EWMA (52). It also slightly outperforms VAPO (60), which was previously a state-of-the-art asynchronous PPO algorithm.
- **Convergence:** The results indicate T-PPO's robust convergence behavior, attributed to its sample-efficient learning mechanism.

#### 4.3. Training Efficiency

- **Wall-Clock Time Reduction:** T-PPO achieved up to **2.5x higher training efficiency**, leading to a **60% reduction in wall-clock time** compared to the previous state-of-the-art synchronization algorithms (Figure 1 and Table 1).
- **RL Iteration Breakdown (Figure 4):** While T-PPO and PPO-EWMA showed comparable per-step wall-clock time, T-PPO demonstrated significantly *fewer convergence steps* (6720 steps for T-PPO vs. 11200 steps for PPO-EWMA). This translates to much lower *total run time* for T-PPO to converge compared to both vanilla PPO and asynchronous PPO variants.
- **Hardware Utilization (Roofline Analysis - Figure 5):** A Roofline analysis on an Nvidia H800 GPU revealed that T-PPO achieves a computational intensity of **249 operations/byte** in policy rollout, substantially higher than PPO’s 84 operations/byte. This signifies that T-PPO better utilizes compute resources by reducing memory-bound bottlenecks during the generation stage, positioning it closer to the arithmetic peak.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics (Response Length - Figure 6)

- **Stable and Enhanced Reasoning Capacity:** The analysis of generated response length during training showed an initial increase, a temporary decline, and then a recovery, ultimately stabilizing at a length that surpasses vanilla PPO. This non-monotonic pattern suggests the model refines its reasoning strategy. The final increased response length indicates that T-PPO preserves, and potentially enhances, the model's capacity for generating longer, more complex chains of thought, which is critical for complex reasoning tasks.

In summary, T-PPO successfully demonstrates its effectiveness by achieving competitive (and often superior) performance on a challenging reasoning benchmark while drastically cutting down training time and improving hardware utilization, making it a highly practical method for training large-scale reasoning LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Truncated Proximal Policy Optimization (T-PPO) represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) training, particularly for models designed to perform complex reasoning tasks. Its impact can be assessed across several dimensions:

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Scalability Bottleneck

The most immediate and profound impact of T-PPO is its ability to tackle the inherent inefficiency of PPO when training LLMs that generate long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) sequences. The substantial reduction in training time (up to 2.5x speedup, 60% wall-clock time reduction) directly addresses a major practical bottleneck. This efficiency gain means that:
- **Faster Iteration Cycles:** Researchers and developers can train and fine-tune reasoning LLMs much more quickly, accelerating research and development cycles.
- **Reduced Computational Costs:** Less training time translates directly to lower GPU hours, making advanced LLM training more affordable and accessible for institutions and companies with finite resources.
- **Maximized Hardware Utilization:** By mitigating GPU idle times through its successive batching and truncated rollout strategy, T-PPO ensures that expensive computational resources are used more effectively, yielding better return on investment for high-end AI hardware.

#### 5.2. Enabling More Capable and Specialized LLMs

The ability to efficiently train LLMs for long-chain reasoning is crucial for unlocking their full potential in complex domains. By making this process more tractable, T-PPO facilitates the development of:
- **Superior Reasoning Models:** The experimental results show that T-PPO not only speeds up training but also achieves competitive, and often superior, performance on challenging benchmarks like AIME. This suggests that the method allows models to effectively learn and leverage extended reasoning capabilities.
- **Domain-Specific Expert Models:** The authors explicitly state their hope that the method can "bring more inspiration for delving into specialized expert models for professional domains." Reduced training costs make it more feasible to fine-tune LLMs for specific, highly specialized tasks requiring deep reasoning, such as legal analysis, medical diagnosis, or scientific discovery.

#### 5.3. Advancing On-Policy Reinforcement Learning for LLMs

While off-policy RL methods have gained attention for their sample efficiency, they often grapple with stability issues and high variance. T-PPO demonstrates that significant efficiency gains can still be achieved within an on-policy framework by intelligently managing data collection and policy updates. The introduction of Extended Generalized Advantage Estimation (EGAE) is a notable algorithmic contribution, showing how advantage estimation can be robustly performed on incomplete trajectories, which has broader implications for continuous or long-horizon tasks in RL beyond just LLMs.

#### 5.4. Practical Guidance and Future Research

The detailed experimental analysis, including training dynamics and Roofline analysis, provides valuable empirical insights into the practical aspects of large-scale RL training. This practical guidance can inform future research into efficient training methodologies. The paper encourages further exploration of truncated or more advanced RL methods, opening new avenues for research into dynamic truncation strategies, adaptive window lengths, or even hybrid on-policy/off-policy approaches that leverage T-PPO's strengths.

In conclusion, T-PPO offers a principled and highly effective solution to a critical challenge in scaling LLM training. By significantly boosting efficiency while preserving or enhancing performance, it lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying sophisticated reasoning AI, thus accelerating progress in the field and expanding the practical applicability of cutting-edge LLMs.
