# Truncated Proximal Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Truncated Proximal Policy Optimization (T-PPO) significantly enhances the training efficiency of large language models for complex reasoning tasks by enabling progressive policy updates on incomplete trajectories. The method achieved up to 2.5x higher training efficiency, leading to a 60% reduction in wall-clock time while securing a 62 pass@1 score on the AIME 2024 benchmark.

## Problem

- Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a standard for fine-tuning LLMs, suffers from computational inefficiency due to its on-policy nature, requiring new, lengthy trajectory collection for each policy update, especially for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
- The 'barrel effect' in distributed PPO training leads to low GPU utilization, as the system waits for the longest generated response in a batch to complete, leaving other resources idle.
- Achieving high reasoning performance in LLMs typically demands generation of very long sequences, exacerbating the PPO efficiency problem and increasing training costs.

## Method

- Introduces ==Extended Generalized Advantage Estimation (EGAE)==, which allows the calculation of advantage estimates on truncated trajectories by approximating the value of future, ungenerated states, enabling progressive policy updates.
- Implements a ==token filtering strategy with successive batching==: generation is truncated by a window length, and completed sequences are dynamically replaced with new prompts to maintain constant batch size and maximize GPU throughput.
- Employs a ==dual optimization mechanism== where the policy model is updated on currently generated tokens using EGAE, while the value model is updated using Monte Carlo returns only from fully completed trajectories to ensure unbiased value predictions.

## Results

- T-PPO achieved a **62** pass@1 score on the AIME 2024 benchmark, outperforming existing baselines like DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B (**47**), DAPO (**50**), GePPO (**50**), PPO-EWMA (**52**), and slightly exceeding VAPO (**60**).
- It demonstrated up to **2.5x** higher training efficiency, resulting in a **60%** reduction in wall-clock time compared to prior state-of-the-art synchronization algorithms.
- Hardware utilization significantly improved, reaching **249** operations/byte in policy rollout on an Nvidia H800 GPU, compared to PPO's **84** operations/byte, indicating better compute resource utilization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy optimization for large language models (LLMs) can be effectively performed on truncated, incomplete reasoning trajectories, significantly reducing idle compute time.
> - Dynamic batch management and successive batching, where new prompts fill slots left by completed sequences, substantially improve GPU utilization during LLM generation.
> - Decoupling policy updates (using Extended Generalized Advantage Estimation on current tokens) from value updates (using Monte Carlo on completed trajectories) allows for both efficient training and stable, unbiased value predictions.
