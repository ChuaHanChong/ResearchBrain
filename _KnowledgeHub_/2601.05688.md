# SketchVL: Policy Optimization via Fine-Grained Credit Assignment for Chart Understanding and More

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xi'an Jiaotong University and Lenovo Research developed SketchVL, a multimodal large language model, and a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, FinePO, to address coarse credit assignment in multi-step visual reasoning. The approach yielded substantial performance improvements, with SketchVL-3B outperforming its base model by 15.32 percentage points on ChartQA and 3.76 points on ChartBench, enhancing chart understanding while preserving general multimodal capabilities.

## Problem

- Automated chart understanding demands precise, multi-step visual reasoning, making it challenging for AI models.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) use coarse, outcome-only feedback, which struggles with multi-step reasoning by failing to distinguish between correct and incorrect intermediate steps.
- This coarse credit assignment introduces noise into the learning signal, punishing sound intermediate logic or rewarding flawed steps, thereby hindering effective policy optimization for compositional reasoning.

## Method

- The authors introduce **SketchVL**, an MLLM that performs ==iterative reasoning== by explicitly rendering visual markers onto an image within a "Reasoning on Image" (RoI) paradigm, forming a visible reasoning trajectory.
- They propose FinePO, a novel RL algorithm that performs fine-grained credit assignment by redistributing coarse trajectory-level advantages to individual steps using a Fine-grained Process Reward Model (FinePRM).
- A FinePRM is trained using a cross-modal distillation pipeline to provide scalar scores for each reasoning step, evaluating its intent and action based on visual changes before and after the action.

## Results

- SketchVL-**7B** consistently outperformed its base model, Qwen2.5VL-**7B**, on chart-expert datasets, while SketchVL-**3B** demonstrated gains of 15.32 percentage points on ChartQA and 3.76 points on ChartBench over Qwen2.5VL-**3B**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the substantial performance boost from FinePO's fine-grained credit assignment and the critical role of KL action regularization in fostering diverse and robust reasoning strategies.
- SketchVL achieved competitive or superior results on general-purpose multimodal benchmarks such as MathVista and MMStar, indicating that the approach enhances specialized task performance while preserving generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained credit assignment, by leveraging explicit step-by-step visual reasoning, provides a more precise and stable learning signal for MLLMs on complex, compositional tasks like chart understanding.
> - The FinePRM effectively assesses the quality of individual reasoning actions, enabling a granular feedback mechanism that significantly improves policy optimization over traditional coarse reward methods.
> - KL divergence-based action regularization is crucial for preventing "action bias" (over-reliance on easy-to-score actions) and encouraging diverse, robust reasoning strategies, leading to more generalizable models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Automated chart understanding represents a significant challenge in the field of artificial intelligence due to the high-density visual information and complex visual reasoning required. Charts are ubiquitous in various forms of documentation, making their automated interpretation valuable for document analysis. Recent advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), such as Qwen2.5VL, Gemma3, GPT-5, and Gemini-2.5, have shown improvements in this area. Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a method to enhance MLLMs' multimodal understanding, as demonstrated by approaches like Vision-R1 and VLM-R1.

However, a critical limitation in current RL practices for MLLMs, particularly for tasks requiring multi-step reasoning, is the coarse nature of credit assignment. Existing RL methods typically provide outcome-only feedback, computing a single advantage from the final result and applying it uniformly across all tokens in a generated trajectory. This approach can be problematic for tasks with step-dependent structures, like chart analysis, where an incorrect final answer might still contain correct intermediate reasoning steps, or a correct final answer might mask flawed intermediate logic. Such coarse feedback can introduce noise into the learning signal, hindering effective policy optimization.

This paper addresses the problem of coarse credit assignment within the context of MLLMs performing visual reasoning tasks, specifically chart understanding. It builds upon the "Reasoning on Image" (RoI) paradigm, where models externalize their intermediate reasoning steps by drawing visual markers on the image. This paradigm provides an explicit, step-by-step decomposition of the reasoning process, which is foundational for enabling fine-grained credit assignment. By proposing a novel RL algorithm, FinePO, the research aims to refine how learning signals are delivered to MLLMs, making them more precise and stable for compositional reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) capable of advanced, multi-step visual reasoning, particularly in chart understanding, by overcoming the limitations of coarse credit assignment in traditional reinforcement learning (RL).

The motivation stems from several observations:

- **Complexity of Chart Understanding:** Charts are dense visual carriers of complex data. Understanding them requires precise and sequential visual reasoning, involving steps such as localizing legends and axes, reading values, aligning categories, comparing trends, and synthesizing conclusions. Any minor error at an intermediate step (e.g., imprecise cropping, misreading a tick mark, or mismatched legend) can lead to an incorrect final interpretation.
- **Limitations of Coarse RL Feedback:** Most existing RL practices for MLLMs apply a single, global reward based on the final outcome of a generated response. This "outcome-only" feedback mechanism, while effective for some tasks, struggles with multi-step reasoning processes. When an MLLM generates a sequence of actions, a uniform reward or penalty across the entire sequence fails to distinguish between correct and incorrect individual reasoning steps. This can lead to:
 - **Punishing sound intermediate logic:** Correct steps within an overall incorrect trajectory might be unfairly penalized.
 - **Rewarding flawed steps:** Incorrect or suboptimal steps might be inadvertently reinforced if they happen to contribute to a globally correct final answer.
 Such noise in the learning signal limits the effectiveness of RL in improving complex, compositional reasoning abilities.
- **Need for Fine-Grained Credit Assignment:** To address the step-dependency and compositional nature of tasks like chart analysis, there is a need for a mechanism to assess and reinforce each intermediate step along the reasoning chain. Providing sharper, lower-noise learning signals by precisely rewarding correct sub-decisions and penalizing missteps is hypothesized to better match the task's intrinsic structure.
- **Leveraging the Reasoning on Image (RoI) Paradigm:** The RoI paradigm, where models explicitly externalize their reasoning steps as visual markers on an image, offers a structured framework. This explicit, visible trajectory of marking actions provides the necessary intermediate states and actions to enable step-level credit assignment.

To achieve these objectives, the authors introduce:

- **SketchVL:** A novel MLLM that operates within the RoI paradigm, drawing its intermediate reasoning steps as markers on the image and feeding the annotated image back to itself, forming a multi-step reasoning process.
- **FinePO:** A new RL algorithm specifically designed for fine-grained credit assignment within each reasoning trajectory. FinePO leverages a Fine-grained Process Reward Model (FinePRM) to evaluate the quality of individual drawing actions, thereby redistributing coarse trajectory-level advantages to individual steps.
- **Associated Datasets:** The creation of large-scale, multimodal datasets for training both the SketchVL model (for cold-start and RL phases) and the FinePRM.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on SketchVL, a multimodal interactive reasoning model, optimized by a novel reinforcement learning algorithm called FinePO.

#### 3.1. SketchVL Architecture and Training Paradigm

SketchVL is designed as an MLLM that performs iterative reasoning by explicitly rendering visual markers onto an image. This annotated image is then fed back into the model to guide subsequent decisions, forming a visible reasoning trajectory.
The training of SketchVL follows a two-stage "ColdStart-RL" methodology:
1. **Cold Start Phase:** The model is supervised to acquire foundational localization capabilities and robust reasoning patterns for "reasoning-on-image" tasks. This phase utilizes a 50K dataset constructed from parts of the ChartSketcher data pipeline and visual-to-text annotations from EvoChart, GQA, and ChartQA-Train.
2. **FinePO RL Phase:** Following the cold start, the model's complex reasoning abilities are further optimized using the FinePO algorithm. This phase uses a collected 9K mixed-domain data points.

#### 3.2. FinePO Algorithm for Fine-Grained Credit Assignment

FinePO addresses the coarse reward problem by operating on reasoning trajectories decomposed into discrete, visual marking actions. Each step includes a textual 'intent' and a corresponding 'action' (rendering a visual marker). The algorithm involves two main phases:

1. **Cross-trajectory Advantage Computation:**
 - For a given prompt, *k* candidate responses (trajectories) are generated.
 - Each response *y_i* is assigned a terminal reward *R(y_i)* based on its overall correctness.
 - The advantage *A(y_i)* for each response is calculated by subtracting the mean reward of the comparison group from the individual response's reward: *A(y_i) = R(y_i) - (1/k) Σ R(y_j)*. This provides a coarse, high-level signal indicating whether a response is better or worse than average within its group.

2. **Intra-trajectory Credit Assignment:**
 - This phase redistributes the coarse advantage *A(y_i)* among individual steps *s_j* within a trajectory using a **Fine-grained Process Reward Model (FinePRM)**, denoted as *P*.
 - **FinePRM Scoring:** The FinePRM provides a scalar score *p_j* for each reasoning step *s_j* based on its intent, action, and the visual change between the image before (*img_j-1*) and after (*img_j*) the action: *p_j = P(intent_j, action_j, img_j-1, img_j)*.
 - **KL Divergence Constraint:** To counteract potential bias towards easy-to-score action types, a KL divergence constraint is introduced. An offset *O_clipped(a_j)* is calculated based on the KL divergence between the current policy's action distribution *P_k(a_j)* and a pre-computed prior action distribution *Q(a_j)* from the training set. This offset is then added to the process score to yield a regularized score *p'_j*: *p'_j = p_j + O_clipped(a_j)*.
 - **Relative Deviation Calculation:** For a response *y_i*, a length-weighted average score *p̄* of all steps is computed: *p̄ = (Σ L_j ⋅ p'_j) / (Σ L_j)*, where *L_j* is the token length of step *s_j*. The deviation for each step is *∆_j = p'_j - p̄*.
 - **Advantage Redistribution:** The coarse advantage *A(y_i)* is redistributed to each step *s_j* as *A'(s_j) = A(y_i) + α ⋅ k ⋅ ∆_j*. Here, *α* is a hyperparameter controlling adjustment intensity, and *k* is a dynamic scaling factor proportional to *|A(y_i)|* and inversely proportional to the maximum deviation *max(0, ∆_j)*. This ensures the fine-grained signal is grounded in the overall performance.
 - **Conservative Clipping:** To maintain stability, the final step-level advantage *A(s_j)* is clipped. For globally superior responses (*A(y_i) > 0*), *A(s_j)* is clipped between 0 and *β ⋅ A(y_i)*. For suboptimal responses (*A(y_i) ≤ 0*), it is clipped between *β ⋅ A(y_i)* and 0. This prevents excessive positive advantage for bad steps or negative advantage for good steps.

#### 3.3. FinePRM Architecture and Data Collection

The FinePRM is an MLLM-based reward model trained to provide process-level scores.
- **Architecture:** It takes as input the textual 'intent' and 'action', along with the image pair before and after the action (*img_j-1*, *img_j*). It acts as an assessor, comparing the visual modification and evaluating whether the change is a precise execution of the intent and action. The task is framed as a four-way classification: Excellent, Acceptable, Poor, or Unacceptable, which are mapped to scalar values [4.0, 3.0, 2.0, 1.0].
- **Training Data Collection:** A cross-modal distillation pipeline is used to generate a large-scale dataset of 'intent-action' pairs.
 1. **Visual-to-Text Annotation:** SAM (for segmentation) and PaddleOCR (for text recognition) are used to partition images into object-centric patches. A leading open-source MLLM (Qwen2.5VL-72B-Instruct) then generates 'self-attributes' (intrinsic properties) and 'interactive attributes' (relationships with surroundings) for each highlighted object within these patches. This reduces the MLLM's cognitive load.
 2. **Text-to-Image Distillation:** The structured annotations are used to prompt an LLM (Qwen-NEXT-80B-A3B) to distill 'intent-action' pairs. A rendering engine applies the 'action' to produce 'before' and 'after' image states.
 - **Direct Generation:** Simple, single-step 'intent-action' pairs are generated.
 - **Trajectory-based Simulation:** A powerful LLM answers question-answering pairs, simulating multi-step reasoning trajectories, from which individual steps are harvested.
 - **Noise Injection:** Noise is systematically introduced to the 'action' component of ground-truth steps to create negative samples, ensuring diversity and a clear distinction between correct and incorrect execution.
 The final FinePRM training data consists of 473K samples with a deliberate 2:4:3:1 ratio for Excellent, Acceptable, Poor, and Unacceptable labels, respectively, to focus learning on critical decision boundaries.

#### 3.4. Experimental Settings

- **Data Construction:**
 - Cold Start: 50K synthetic QA pairs from EvoChart, GQA, ChartQA-Train metadata, distilled using Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507.
 - FinePRM: 473K SFT samples from EvoChart, GQA, ChartQA-Train, OpenImages, using SAM, PaddleOCR, Qwen2.5VL-72B-Instruct, and Qwen-NEXT-80B-A3B. Five action types (Line, Point, Rectangle, Circle, Text) with specific balancing and label ratios.
 - RL Data: 9K prompts from ChartQA-Train-Augmented, ChartQA-Train-Human, Vision-R1-RL, ChartBench-Train, and VisualCoT-Train.
- **Evaluation:** Benchmarks included chart-expert datasets (EvoChart-QA, ChartQA, ChartQA-Pro, ChartBench, PlotQA) and general-purpose datasets (MMStar, MathVista). DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B was used as an evaluation discriminator with 9-vote majority for correctness, applying specific evaluation rules (e.g., 5% relative error for numeric values).
- **Training:** Two SketchVL versions were trained (7B and 3B backbone on Qwen2.5VL-7B/3B-Instruct). FinePRM was trained on Qwen2.5VL-7B-Instruct. Training durations were 4 epochs for FinePRM, 2 for cold start, and 1 for FinePO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of SketchVL and the FinePO algorithm across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison

- **Superiority over Base Models:** SketchVL-7B consistently outperformed its base model, Qwen2.5VL-7B, across most chart-expert datasets. The performance gains were more substantial for SketchVL-3B, which showed notable improvements over Qwen2.5VL-3B, with gains of 15.32 percentage points on ChartQA and 3.76 points on ChartBench. This suggests that the FinePO training method is effective, and the smaller model might be more compelled to learn the intended reasoning process from the fine-grained signals due to less capacity to "hack" the reward model.
- **Comparison with Other Reasoning Models:** SketchVL-3B surpassed VLM-R1 on most benchmarks. SketchVL also significantly outperformed ChartSketcher, another interactive reasoning model, specifically its 2B version.
- **Generalization:** SketchVL maintained strong general-purpose multimodal capabilities. SketchVL-7B achieved competitive or superior results on non-chart-centric datasets like MathVista and MMStar, indicating that FinePO enhances specialized task performance while preserving generalization.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

Ablation studies using SketchVL-3B revealed the contributions of FinePO's components:
- **Value of Fine-Grained Credit Assignment:**
 - The full SketchVL model showed consistent performance gains (e.g., 2.08-point improvement on ChartQA) compared to a baseline using naive GRPO (which applies coarse trajectory-level rewards).
 - Replacing the FinePRM with random scores ('w/o FinePRM (random* scores)') resulted in a performance drop, confirming the FinePRM provides a meaningful and necessary evaluation signal. These results validate the efficacy of FinePO's fine-grained credit assignment.
- **Role of KL Action Regularization:** The model without KL action regularization performed comparably to the full model in terms of final task accuracy. However, further analysis (Figure 4 and 3) indicated that without KL regularization, the model exhibits severe "Action Bias", heavily favoring a few easy-to-score action types, particularly in the larger 7B model. The KL constraint prevents this collapse, encouraging diverse and balanced action use, leading to a more robust reasoning strategy and higher-quality intermediate steps.
- **Impact of RL and Generalization:** The RL phase provided a substantial performance boost over the cold-start-only model, and this improvement extended to datasets not included in RL training (e.g., PlotQA). This demonstrates FinePO's ability to enhance generalization.
- **Importance of the RoI Paradigm:** Removing the model's ability to draw on the image ('w/o Sketch (zero GRPO)') resulted in a drastic performance collapse across all benchmarks, underscoring the fundamental role of the iterative, visual reasoning process for these complex tasks.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Reasoning Process

- **FinePRM Scores:** Full SketchVL models consistently achieved the highest average FinePRM process scores across all test sets, including datasets not used in RL training (e.g., PlotQA). This indicates that FinePO effectively aligns the model's step-level behavior with the FinePRM's quality assessment, fostering intrinsic and generalizable reasoning capabilities.
- **Action Type Distribution (Figure 4):** KL action regularization was shown to be crucial in preventing action bias. Without it, the model (especially SketchVL-7B) showed a strong preference for certain action types, which could indicate "reward hacking." The KL constraint forced the model to develop a more diverse and robust action strategy.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics Analysis (Figure 8)

- **Stability:** The full SketchVL-3B model exhibited significantly higher training stability, characterized by lower gradient norms and controlled KL divergence, compared to baselines. Models trained with naive GRPO or without sketching showed drastic spikes in gradient norm and unstable KL divergence, confirming the noise introduced by coarse rewards.
- **Reward Value:** The final converged reward of the full SketchVL model was slightly lower than baselines. This is attributed to FinePO's strict constraints (KL Action Regularization, FinePRM alignment) which prevent "reward hacking." These constraints, while reducing absolute reward, compel the model to learn a more rigorous and generalizable reasoning logic, leading to better real-world performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of multimodal AI by addressing a fundamental challenge in applying reinforcement learning to complex, multi-step reasoning tasks.

- **Advancement in RL for MLLMs:** The introduction of FinePO offers a novel approach to the credit assignment problem, moving beyond coarse, trajectory-level rewards to fine-grained, step-level feedback. This method provides a more precise and stable learning signal for MLLMs, which is critical for tasks requiring compositional reasoning.
- **Improved Visual Reasoning Capabilities:** SketchVL, optimized with FinePO, demonstrates enhanced performance in chart understanding and maintains strong general-purpose multimodal capabilities. This suggests that the approach can lead to more robust and accurate MLLMs for visually intensive tasks.
- **Contribution of Datasets and Methodology:** The work introduces two large-scale datasets: a 473K multimodal dataset for training the FinePRM and a 50K dataset for SketchVL's cold-start phase. The cross-modal distillation pipeline for FinePRM data generation is a methodological contribution for constructing high-quality process reward models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The concept of explicit visual reasoning (RoI paradigm) combined with fine-grained credit assignment offers a promising direction for training MLLMs that can "think" more deliberatively and transparently. This could be extended to other domains requiring complex visual interaction and decision-making, such as robot manipulation or medical image analysis.
- **Practical Implications:** Improved automated chart understanding can have practical impacts in various sectors, including business intelligence, scientific research, and financial analysis, by enabling more accurate and efficient extraction of insights from visual data.

The paper also acknowledges limitations, such as the absence of a standardized benchmark for quantitatively evaluating process reward models and the inherent challenge of constructing an unbiased FinePRM. These points highlight areas for future research to further refine and validate fine-grained credit assignment methodologies.
