# Make Geometry Matter for Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoSR is a framework that makes geometry tokens from pretrained 3D foundation models actually count for vision-language spatial reasoning. It masks 2D vision tokens during training to suppress appearance shortcuts, and gates geometry-versus-vision contributions per token and channel at fusion time. On VSI-Bench it averages 51.9 and on DSR-Bench 66.1, beating prior geometry-injection methods in both static and dynamic settings.

## Problem

- Prior geometry-injection methods fuse 3D tokens naively and fine-tune as usual, leaving vision-language models free to answer from 2D appearance shortcuts instead of geometry.
- Injected geometry gives only marginal gains in static scenes and can actively hurt dynamic video reasoning, where naive fusion underperforms removing the geometry branch entirely.
- Earlier 3D-aware multimodal models need depth sensors, point clouds, or multi-stage reconstruction, which is noisy and does not scale to monocular in-the-wild video.

## Method

- **Geometry-Unleashing Masking** zeroes a fraction of 2D vision tokens during training, sampled randomly for static scenes, so the backbone must consult the geometry stream.
- For dynamic video, ==TopK masking== ranks geometry tokens by ==cross-attention== relevance from text-conditioned bottleneck queries, then masks the co-located vision tokens after spatial interpolation.
- **Geometry-Guided Fusion** learns a ==sigmoid gate== per token and channel over layer-normalized vision and geometry features, blending them instead of uniform addition or concatenation.

## Results

- On VSI-Bench static reasoning GeoSR averages **51.9**, above VG-LLM at **50.7** and Spatial-MLLM at **48.4**, with gains concentrated in relative distance and route planning.
- On DSR-Bench dynamic reasoning it reaches **66.1** average versus **58.9** for GSM and **38.4** for VG-LLM, leading all thirteen subtask types.
- Ablations confirm both parts: dropping masking costs **1.2** points dynamic, and the no-geometry variant at **64.0** beats naive fusion at **62.8**, reproducing the harm claim.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adding a stronger modality is not enough; if an easier shortcut modality remains available, the network keeps using it and ignores the new signal.
> - Modality dropout during training plus a learned per-token gate at inference is cheap: 0.07B extra parameters and 0.01s added latency over the baseline.
> - Aggressive masking works best, 0.8 ratio at 0.5 probability; too little leaves shortcuts intact, too much strips context the model still needs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen substantial advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in image and video understanding, largely due to large-scale training paradigms. These models demonstrate proficiency in tasks such as object recognition, event detection, and general semantic comprehension. However, a significant limitation persists in their ability to perform complex spatial reasoning. Spatial reasoning encompasses understanding the 3D relationships between objects, their relative positions, directions, distances, and how these relationships evolve over time in both static and dynamic scenes. This capability is crucial for numerous real-world applications, including robotics, autonomous navigation, and advanced video analysis.

Previous attempts to augment VLMs with spatial reasoning capabilities have explored several avenues. Initial approaches often relied on explicit 3D inputs, such as depth maps, point clouds, or pre-built 3D scene graphs. While effective in certain contexts, these methods frequently require specialized sensors or multi-stage 3D reconstruction pipelines, which can introduce noise, limit scalability, and are not universally applicable to common scenarios where only monocular images or videos are available.

A more recent and scalable paradigm involves extracting implicit geometric features from monocular videos using pretrained 3D foundation models. These features are then injected into VLMs as "geometry tokens" to complement the standard 2D vision tokens. This approach aims to provide VLMs with the necessary 3D structural information without requiring explicit 3D inputs. Existing methods in this line typically fuse these geometry tokens with visual tokens and fine-tune the combined model on spatial reasoning datasets. Examples include VG-LLM and Spatial-MLLM, which aim to improve spatial understanding under viewpoint changes, and GSM, which focuses on dynamic 4D reasoning.

This paper identifies a critical overlooked aspect within this geometry-token injection paradigm: despite the intuitive promise of integrating pretrained geometric priors, VLMs often fail to effectively utilize these injected geometry tokens. The authors observe that under conventional naive token fusion and standard fine-tuning, models tend to default to appearance-driven shortcuts derived from 2D visual cues, treating geometry tokens as dispensable auxiliary signals. This underutilization can result in limited performance gains or, in some dynamic scenarios, even negative impacts on spatial reasoning accuracy. The current work, GeoSR, directly addresses this identified gap by proposing a framework specifically designed to compel VLMs to actively and reasonably integrate geometric information, thereby making geometry "matter" for spatial reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named GeoSR (Geometry for Spatial Reasoning), that effectively encourages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to utilize geometric information for spatial reasoning tasks in both static scenes and dynamic videos. The motivation stems from a specific observed limitation in existing geometry-aware VLM architectures.

The authors identified that while many advanced VLMs are capable of general image and video understanding, they exhibit brittleness when confronted with questions demanding precise spatial reasoning. Such questions involve understanding object locations, orientations, distances, directions, and their temporal evolution in 3D space, especially under varying viewpoints, occlusions, and motion.

To address this deficiency, recent research has focused on injecting geometric cues into VLMs, often by extracting implicit 3D features (geometry tokens) from monocular images or videos using pretrained 3D foundation models. These geometry tokens are then typically fused with 2D vision tokens. However, the authors' empirical observations revealed a counterintuitive and reproducible phenomenon: this "naive token fusion followed by standard fine-tuning" approach frequently leads to the underutilization of geometry tokens. For static scenes, performance gains were often marginal. More notably, in dynamic scenarios, the injection of geometry tokens could sometimes even result in a performance *decrease* compared to models without geometry injection.

This observation indicated that VLMs, when presented with both 2D visual cues and 3D geometric information, tend to primarily rely on appearance-driven shortcuts within the 2D visual tokens. They often treat the geometric information as a secondary or dispensable signal rather than actionable evidence for spatial reasoning. Furthermore, indiscriminately mixing geometric and appearance information without proper guidance could dilute valuable geometric cues, allowing the model to avoid consulting geometry even when it is critical.

Therefore, the core motivation behind GeoSR is the principle that it is insufficient to merely *provide* geometry tokens; the VLM must be *compelled* to effectively *rely* on geometric information when spatial reasoning tasks demand it. GeoSR aims to overcome this issue by introducing mechanisms that actively encourage and guide the VLM to integrate and reason with the injected geometry tokens in a more effective and reasonable manner. The framework seeks to unleash the potential of these geometric cues, transforming them from underutilized auxiliary signals into integral components of the spatial reasoning process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoSR proposes a novel framework built upon the existing geometry-aware VLM architecture, which typically augments a VLM with a pretrained geometry branch and a fusion module. The goal is to make geometry tokens actionable, rather than ignorable, for spatial reasoning.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Geometry-Aware Framework

The baseline framework consists of a VLM, a prompt tokenizer, a visual tokenizer, and a geometric tokenizer. Input images/video frames ($I_t$) are processed by the visual tokenizer to produce 2D vision tokens ($F_V$) and by a pretrained 3D model (geometric tokenizer) to generate geometry tokens ($F_G$). A text prompt ($Q$) is encoded into text tokens ($F_P$). These token streams are then combined via a fusion module ($F(\cdot)$) to produce fused tokens ($F$), which are fed into the VLM backbone along with $F_P$ to generate an answer.
Two common fusion mechanisms are identified as baselines:
- **Additive Fusion:** Predominantly used in static spatial reasoning, this involves aligning geometry tokens ($F_G$) via an MLP and reshaping, then adding them directly to vision tokens ($F_V$): $F = F_V + \text{MLP}(\text{Reshape}(F_G))$.
- **QFormer Fusion:** Often employed for dynamic spatial reasoning, this more sophisticated module uses learnable bottleneck tokens ($B$) to summarize text prompt intent through cross-attention with $F_P$. These bottleneck tokens then query geometry tokens ($F_G$) via another cross-attention to produce question-relevant geometry evidence ($Z_G$). This compact $Z_G$ is then concatenated with vision tokens: $F = [F_V, \text{MLP}(Z_G)]$.

#### 3.2. GeoSR's Key Components

GeoSR introduces two primary strategies to address the underutilization of geometry tokens:

##### 3.2.1. Geometry-Unleashing Masking

This component aims to suppress 2D appearance-driven shortcuts during training, thereby forcing the model to consult geometry tokens for spatial reasoning. The masking process applies a binary mask ($m$) to vision tokens ($F_V$) element-wise: $\tilde{F}_V = m \odot F_V$, where $m_j=0$ if masking is enabled ($\delta=1$) and token $j$ is in the mask set ($M$). $\delta$ is sampled from a Bernoulli distribution with probability $\beta$.

- **For Static Spatial Reasoning:** A simple MAE-style random masking is applied. The mask set ($M$) of size $K$ (where $K = \lceil \gamma \cdot (\text{total vision token positions}) \rceil$ and $\gamma$ is the masking ratio) is uniformly sampled from the vision token positions.
- **For Dynamic Spatial Reasoning:** Masking is guided by question-relevant geometric evidence.
 1. The QFormer mechanism (as in preliminary QFormer Fusion) is used to obtain $Z_G$ and the attention weights ($A$) from querying geometry tokens ($F_G$) with bottleneck tokens ($F_B$).
 2. Relevance scores ($s$) for each geometry token are derived by averaging attention probabilities across heads and bottleneck tokens, then normalizing them.
 3. The mask set ($M$) is determined by selecting the TopK geometry tokens with the highest relevance scores (based on masking ratio $\gamma$).
 4. To address potential resolution mismatches between geometry tokens ($F_G$) and vision tokens ($F_V$), 2D spatial interpolation is applied to align the masked regions from geometry token resolution to vision token resolution. This ensures the mask on $F_V$ corresponds to critical geometric areas identified from $F_G$.

##### 3.2.2. Geometry-Guided Fusion

This module adaptively controls the contribution of geometric evidence to each fused token, ensuring geometry dominates when it is informative and necessary. It operates on the masked vision tokens ($\tilde{F}_V$) and prepared geometry features ($\tilde{F}_G$).

- **Geometry Feature Preparation ($\tilde{F}_G$):**
 - **For Static Spatial Reasoning:** $\tilde{F}_G$ is obtained by applying an MLP and reshaping to the original geometry tokens ($F_G$), similar to additive fusion.
 - **For Dynamic Spatial Reasoning:** The compact geometry evidence ($Z_G$) from the QFormer is first redistributed back to full spatiotemporal geometry tokens ($\hat{F}'_G$) aligned with the vision tokens using cross-attention, where $\hat{F}'_G$ acts as the query and $Z_G$ as key/value. This produces the fine-grained geometry feature map $\tilde{F}_G$. This retrieval step emphasizes dynamics condensed in $Z_G$.
- **Adaptive Gating:** A token- and channel-wise gate ($\alpha$) is learned using a sigmoid function: $\alpha = \sigma(W_g [V \parallel G] + b_g)$, where $V = \text{LN}_v(\tilde{F}_V)$ and $G = \text{LN}_g(\tilde{F}_G)$ are layer-normalized masked vision and geometry features, respectively.
- **Final Fusion:** The fused token $F$ is generated by adaptively mixing $V$ and $G$: $F = \alpha \odot V + (1 - \alpha) \odot G$. For dynamic spatial reasoning, the overall dynamic geometry information ($Z_G$) is also appended to $F$ before feeding into the VLM backbone: $[F, Z_G]$.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **VLM Backbone:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B is used for both static and dynamic reasoning.
- **Geometric Models:** VGGT for static (Qwen2.5-VL-7B as base) and $\pi^3$ for dynamic (Qwen2.5-VL-7B as base).
- **Training:** Tokenizers and 3D geometry models are frozen. The fusion module and VLM backbone are fine-tuned.
 - **Static:** Masking ratio $\gamma=0.8$, enable probability $\beta=0.5$. Trained for 1 epoch with Adam optimizer, batch size 64, learning rate $1 \times 10^{-5}$ (linear warmup, cosine decay).
 - **Dynamic:** Bottleneck length $L_B=32$, masking ratio $\gamma=0.8$, enable probability $\beta=0.5$. Trained for 1 epoch with Adam, batch size 32, learning rate $2 \times 10^{-7}$ (linear warmup).
- **Inference:** Masking is disabled, and full vision tokens are fed into the fusion module.
- **Hardware:** All experiments performed on 4x H200 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across static and dynamic spatial reasoning benchmarks, supported by quantitative results and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

GeoSR consistently establishes new state-of-the-art performance on both static and dynamic spatial reasoning benchmarks. This indicates that the proposed strategies for encouraging and guiding the use of geometry tokens are effective in enhancing the spatial reasoning capabilities of VLMs.

#### 4.2. Static Spatial Reasoning (VSI-Bench)

On the VSI-Bench dataset, which focuses on rigid scenes with viewpoint and visibility changes, GeoSR achieved an average accuracy of **51.9%**. This represents an improvement over a range of baselines:
- **Proprietary API Models:** GeoSR surpassed strong commercial models like GPT-4o (47.8%), Gemini-1.5 Flash (45.7%), and Gemini-1.5 Pro (48.8%).
- **General Video Understanding Models:** GeoSR significantly outperformed open-source general video models such as Qwen2.5-VL-7B (33.0%), LLaVA-Video-7B (35.6%), and even larger models like InternVL3-78B (48.4%).
- **Existing Spatial Reasoning Models:** GeoSR also demonstrated superiority over specialized spatial reasoning models that incorporate geometry priors, including Spatial-MLLM (48.4%) and VG-LLM (50.7%). The gains were observed across numerical questions (e.g., Object Counting, Absolute Distance) and multiple-choice questions (e.g., Relative Distance, Route Planning). This demonstrates that for static scenes, geometric information, when properly utilized, contributes to a more stable and robust spatial understanding.

#### 4.3. Dynamic Spatial Reasoning (DSR-Bench)

On the DSR-Bench dataset, which features in-the-wild videos with evolving spatial relations due to object and camera motion, GeoSR achieved an average accuracy of **66.1%**. This improvement is more pronounced compared to static reasoning and represents a substantial advancement:
- **Proprietary API Models:** GeoSR notably surpassed proprietary models like GPT-4o (26.4%), GPT-5 (30.8%), Gemini-2.5-Flash (24.9%), and Gemini-2.5-Pro (31.7%), highlighting the difficulty of dynamic spatial reasoning for general VLMs without specific geometric handling.
- **General Video Understanding Models:** Open-source video models, including Qwen2.5-VL-7B (23.5%) and LLaVA-Video-7B (25.9%), showed significantly lower performance, indicating their brittleness in dynamic spatial contexts.
- **Existing Spatial Reasoning Models:** GeoSR notably outperformed prior state-of-the-art spatial reasoning models that attempted to integrate geometry for dynamic scenes. For example, it significantly improved upon VG-LLM (38.4%) and GSM (58.9%), which previously represented strong baselines in this domain. GeoSR achieved the highest scores on almost all subtask types, including various absolute, relative, and non-template questions, suggesting that its approach to unleashing and guiding geometry is particularly beneficial when spatiotemporal consistency is critical.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the individual and combined contributions of GeoSR's two core components: Geometry-Unleashing Masking (Geo. Mask.) and Geometry-Guided Fusion (Geo. Fus.).
- **Impact of Geo. Fus.:** Replacing Geo. Fus. with naive original fusion (Ori. Fus.) led to performance reductions on both benchmarks (e.g., average drops from 51.9% to 50.0% on VSI-Bench and 66.1% to 64.7% on DSR-Bench), indicating that naive fusion is less effective in leveraging geometry.
- **Impact of Geo. Mask.:** Removing Geo. Mask. while retaining Geo. Fus. also resulted in noticeable performance drops (e.g., 50.9% on VSI-Bench and 64.9% on DSR-Bench compared to GeoSR's 51.9% and 66.1% respectively). This indicates that even with adaptive fusion, models can still underutilize geometry without mechanisms to suppress appearance shortcuts.
- **Combined Effect:** Removing both Geo. Fus. and Ori. Fus. (i.e., Geo. Mask. only) further degraded results (49.6% on VSI-Bench, 58.1% on DSR-Bench), suggesting masking alone is insufficient without a robust geometry routing mechanism.
- **Negative Impact of Naive Geometry Injection:** The "no-geometry" baseline (f) (49.8% on VSI-Bench, 64.0% on DSR-Bench) demonstrated that simple geometry token injection (e) (50.2% on VSI-Bench, 62.8% on DSR-Bench) could sometimes perform worse than having no geometry branch at all, especially on the dynamic benchmark. This confirms the paper's initial observation regarding the detrimental effects of uncontrolled geometry fusion, particularly in dynamic settings where appearance cues are less stable.
These ablations collectively support the claim that Geo. Mask. makes geometry *effective* by weakening appearance shortcuts, and Geo. Fus. makes geometry usage *reasonable* through adaptive routing. The improvements were more pronounced in dynamic scenarios, where motion and occlusion make appearance cues less reliable.

#### 4.5. Hyperparameter Analysis

Analysis of masking hyperparameters on DSR-Bench revealed that a masking ratio ($\gamma$) of 0.8 and an enable probability ($\beta$) of 0.5 struck a balance between suppressing shortcuts and preserving sufficient context, yielding optimal performance.

#### 4.6. Computational Overhead

GeoSR demonstrated negligible increases in runtime, model size, and peak memory compared to geometry-aware baselines. For dynamic reasoning, GeoSR had a runtime of 0.41s, model size of 9.23B parameters, and peak memory of 18.95GB, compared to the baseline's 0.40s, 9.16B, and 18.81GB respectively. This indicates that the proposed architectural enhancements are computationally efficient.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental challenge in the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs): the effective utilization of geometric information for spatial reasoning. Despite the increasing availability and integration of 3D geometric priors into VLMs, prior work has often struggled with underutilization of these cues, sometimes even leading to performance degradation. GeoSR's core contribution lies in demonstrating that geometry tokens are not automatically treated as actionable evidence by VLMs and proposing specific mechanisms to rectify this.

The significance of GeoSR stems from several factors:

1. **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning Capabilities:** By compelling and guiding VLMs to actively reason with geometry tokens, GeoSR significantly improves performance on complex spatial reasoning tasks across both static and dynamic environments. The consistent establishment of new state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like VSI-Bench and DSR-Bench validates its effectiveness. This indicates a more robust understanding of 3D relationships, distances, directions, and their temporal evolution, which are critical for comprehensive scene comprehension.

2. **Addressing a Critical Failure Mode:** The paper's identification and empirical validation of the "underutilization of geometry tokens under naive fusion" phenomenon represent a crucial insight. This finding highlights that merely injecting more information is insufficient; how that information is integrated and prioritized by the model is paramount. GeoSR provides a principled solution to this architectural oversight.

3. **Novel Integration Mechanisms:** The proposed Geometry-Unleashing Masking and Geometry-Guided Fusion components offer novel and effective strategies for multimodal information integration. Geometry-Unleashing Masking acts as a training-time intervention to reduce reliance on superficial 2D visual cues, forcing deeper geometric reasoning. Geometry-Guided Fusion introduces an adaptive, fine-grained control mechanism, allowing geometric information to contribute more substantially when contextually relevant. These components could inspire similar adaptive fusion techniques for other multimodal integration challenges.

4. **Practical Efficiency:** The negligible computational overhead introduced by GeoSR, in terms of runtime, model size, and memory footprint, underscores its practical applicability. This efficiency suggests that the benefits of improved spatial reasoning can be realized without incurring substantial additional computational costs, making it a viable enhancement for deploying VLMs in real-world scenarios.

5. **Broadened Applicability of VLMs:** Improved spatial reasoning capabilities directly impact a wide array of applications. In **robotics** and **autonomous driving**, precise 3D understanding of surroundings, object interactions, and navigation paths is essential for safe and effective operation. For **augmented/virtual reality**, consistent spatial understanding is vital for realistic content placement and user interaction. In **video surveillance** and **sports analytics**, accurate tracking of objects and their movements, along with understanding complex spatiotemporal relationships, can lead to more insightful analyses.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also implicitly impacts future research by emphasizing the importance of designing sophisticated fusion and training strategies for multimodal models. It suggests that simply scaling models or injecting more data/modalities may not yield optimal results without corresponding advancements in how these diverse information streams are processed and integrated. The acknowledged limitations regarding dataset quality and annotation ambiguity also open avenues for developing more robust and geometrically precise benchmarks.

In summary, GeoSR represents a significant step forward in enhancing the spatial reasoning abilities of Vision-Language Models. By offering a computationally efficient and architecturally sound approach to making geometric information genuinely "matter," this work contributes to the development of more intelligent, robust, and spatially aware AI systems.
