# VideoVLA: Video Generators Can Be Generalizable Robot Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Microsoft Research Asia, Xi'an Jiaotong University, and Fudan University developed VideoVLA, a robot manipulator that repurposes large pre-trained video generation models. This system jointly predicts future video states and corresponding actions, achieving enhanced generalization capabilities for novel objects and skills in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often built on understanding-oriented foundation models, exhibit limited true generalization to unseen tasks, novel objects, or entirely unfamiliar environments.
- Deploying robots in diverse, unpredictable open-world environments requires significantly more robust generalization than existing systems can provide.
- The dominant paradigm in VLA research has not fully exploited the inherent physical understanding and predictive capabilities present in large-scale generative models.

## Method

- VideoVLA transforms a large pre-trained video generation model (CogVideoX) into a generalizable robotic manipulator by adapting its Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone.
- The system is designed to jointly predict both a sequence of robot actions and a corresponding video clip depicting the anticipated future visual outcomes.
- It uses a unified multi-modal input sequence, concatenating language instruction tokens, the current visual observation's latent, and targets (future frame latents and action chunk) for joint denoising within a DDPM framework.

## Results

- VideoVLA achieved the highest average performance across 12 tasks in simulation (SIMPLER environment) and real-world pick, stack, and place tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines.
- The model demonstrated superior generalization to novel objects, achieving a 65.2% average success rate on 10 unseen objects in simulation and successfully handling all 12 novel objects in real-world pick-and-place tasks.
- It exhibited robust cross-embodiment skill transfer, outperforming the second-best model by 28.2 percentage points on average for 8 novel skills in simulation and achieving the highest success rates across all 6 evaluated novel skills in real-world scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A strong positive correlation exists between the quality of the robot's predicted visual imagination and the reliability of its corresponding action predictions, suggesting imagination as an implicit indicator of action accuracy.
> - Leveraging the physical plausibility and generalization capabilities embedded in large video generation models, trained on massive real-world videos, can effectively enhance robotic planning and execution.
> - Jointly modeling video (future visual outcomes) and action modalities within a single generative framework leads to a powerful and consistent perception-action loop for generalizable robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "VideoVLA" positions itself at the forefront of robot manipulation research, specifically addressing the critical challenge of **generalization** in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The broader landscape of robot manipulation is striving towards artificial general intelligence (AGI), where robots can perform a wide array of tasks in diverse, unpredictable, and open-world environments.

Prior to VideoVLA, the dominant paradigm in VLA models (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, CogACT, π0) has largely focused on leveraging **pre-trained understanding-oriented models**. These models—including large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and pure vision models (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2)—are excellent at interpreting complex visual scenes and following natural language instructions. They have significantly reduced the need for extensive task-specific robotic data and enabled knowledge transfer from general-purpose models. However, despite their successes, these approaches have demonstrated **limited true generalization** to unseen tasks, novel objects, or entirely unfamiliar environments. They primarily excel in in-domain scenarios or those with minor variations from their training distribution.

VideoVLA proposes a **paradigm shift** by exploring the potential of adapting **large pre-trained video generation models** as the foundation for VLA manipulators. This is a crucial departure from the understanding-oriented approaches. The motivation for this shift stems from the remarkable generalization capabilities and physical plausibility observed in recent video generation models (e.g., CogVideoX, Stable Video Diffusion, HunyuanVideo, Wan). These generative models, trained on massive real-world videos, inherently learn complex physical dynamics and can anticipate future world states, which are fundamental requirements for robust robotic planning and execution. The authors argue that the ability of video generators to handle novel text and visual conditions naturally aligns with the robot manipulator's need to deal with unseen instructions and observations.

While some existing works have incorporated video generation into robot manipulation, they typically do so in a **modular fashion**. For instance, some approaches use video predictions as a planning component to extract actions via inverse dynamics models, optical flow, or goal-frame conditioning. VideoVLA distinguishes itself by introducing a **unified, end-to-end VLA model** directly adapted from a large pre-trained video generation model. This model **jointly predicts both the video (future visual outcomes) and action modalities** within a single large transformer architecture. This integrated dual-prediction strategy, along with fully exploiting the capabilities of large-scale pre-trained video generators, sets it apart from earlier efforts like UVA and VPP, which are described as initial steps toward this paradigm but differ in architectural details, generalization scope, and explicit correlation analysis.

In essence, VideoVLA challenges the conventional wisdom in VLA research by demonstrating that the immense knowledge of physical world dynamics embedded within large-scale video generators, usually leveraged for content creation, can be effectively repurposed and fine-tuned for generalizable robot control, offering a promising new avenue towards more robust and adaptive robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "VideoVLA" is to **explore the potential of transforming large video generation models into generalizable robotic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) manipulators.** This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Addressing the Generalization Gap in Robot Manipulation:** The primary motivation stems from the observed limitations in generalization capabilities of current VLA models. While existing VLA systems, built upon pre-trained vision-language understanding models, have made strides in perception and instruction following, they still struggle significantly when confronted with novel tasks, unfamiliar objects, or unseen environments. True generalization is deemed essential for deploying robots in the unpredictable, variable conditions of the open world and is a critical step towards achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
2. **Leveraging the Strengths of Video Generation Models:** The authors are keenly motivated by the recent advancements in large video generation models. These models have demonstrated:
 - **Remarkable generalization:** They can synthesize high-quality, coherent video content when conditioned on novel textual or visual inputs, suggesting an inherent ability to adapt to new situations.
 - **Outstanding physical plausibility:** Their ability to generate realistic videos implies a deep understanding of physical dynamics, learned from massive real-world video datasets. This understanding is crucial for a robot manipulator to reason about the physical consequences of its actions.
 - **Inherent planning capability:** By predicting future world states based on given instructions, video generators exhibit a form of visual planning, a capability vital for robots to anticipate and organize their interactions with the environment.
3. **Bridging Video Generation and Robotic Manipulation:** A key objective is to seamlessly adapt these powerful video generation capabilities to the domain of robotic manipulation. This involves enabling video generation models to not only produce plausible future visuals but also to simultaneously generate instruction-following actions that can be directly executed by a robot.
4. **Establishing Consistency Between Imagination and Execution:** To effectively transfer the strong generalization abilities of video generation models to the action domain, the work aims to ensure consistency between the robot's actual execution of generated actions and the visual imagination represented in the generated videos. The hypothesis is that a strong correlation between high-quality imagined futures and reliable action predictions is key to robust task success.
5. **Pioneering a New Paradigm:** The paper aims to demonstrate the feasibility and potential of using pre-trained *generative* video models as a foundation for robot learning, contrasting with the dominant reliance on pre-trained *understanding-oriented* models in previous VLA research. This objective seeks to inspire a paradigm shift in how foundational models are leveraged for robotics.

In summary, VideoVLA's objectives are to overcome the generalization bottleneck in robot manipulation by tapping into the inherent understanding of physical dynamics and planning capabilities embedded in large video generation models, thereby proposing a novel, integrated approach to VLA systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoVLA proposes a simple yet effective approach to transforming a large video generation model into a generalizable robotic manipulator. Its methodology is centered around a multi-modal Diffusion Transformer (DiT) that jointly models video, language, and action modalities.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Given a **text instruction** \(T\) describing a task and the **current visual observation** \(O\) (an image), VideoVLA aims to jointly predict:
1. An **action chunk** \(A = \{a_i \in \mathbb{R}^7\}_{i=1}^K\): A sequence of \(K\) actions, where each \(a_i\) is a 7-dimensional vector. This vector encodes wrist rotation (3 dimensions), wrist translation (3 dimensions), and gripper state (1 binary dimension: 0 for closed, 1 for open).
2. A **video clip** \(F = \{F_j\}_{j=1}^N\): A sequence of \(N\) frames depicting the anticipated future visual content resulting from executing the action chunk \(A\). For computational efficiency, the model predicts latent representations of these frames rather than raw pixels.
The process is iterative: after executing an action chunk, a new observation is obtained, and the model predicts the next chunk until the task is complete.

#### 3.2. Overview

VideoVLA comprises two main components:
1. **Encoders:** These convert the raw input modalities (language instruction, video clip) into token or latent representations suitable for the transformer.
2. **Diffusion Transformer (DiT) Backbone:** This core component, conditioned on language tokens and the current visual observation's latent, jointly predicts future frame latents and the corresponding action sequence.

#### 3.3. Data Preprocessing

To enhance computational efficiency, VideoVLA processes inputs in latent spaces:

- **Text Encoder:** A pre-trained T5 model is used to convert each language instruction \(T\) into a fixed-length sequence of 226 tokens, denoted as \(T'\).
- **Video Encoder:** The 3D-causal VAE encoder from CogVideoX is adopted. It transforms a video clip \(F\) (N frames) into a sequence of video latents \(V = \{V_j \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w}\}_{j=1}^n\). The causal design ensures that the first latent, \(V_1\), encodes only the first frame \(F_1\), which corresponds to the current visual observation \(O\). During inference, only \(O\) is encoded to obtain \(V_1\), while during training, the entire video clip \(F\) is used to obtain both \(V_1\) and the future frame latents \(\{V_j\}_{j=2}^n\).

#### 3.4. Unified Future Modeling

The core of VideoVLA's approach lies in its "Unified Future Modeling," implemented via a multi-modal Diffusion Transformer:

- **Architecture:** It utilizes a DiT-style architecture, which is a powerful backbone known for its scalability in generative tasks.
- **Multi-modal Input Sequence:** The conditioned inputs (\(T'\) and \(V_1\)) and the target variables (future frame latents \(\{V_j\}_{j=2}^n\) and action chunk \(A\)) are concatenated into a single, unified token sequence.
 - For visual latents, spatial dimensions are flattened into a 1D sequence (raster order).
 - Actions are represented directly as 7-D vectors; no tokenization is applied to the action modality itself.
- **Diffusion Process:** The model adopts the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) framework. Gaussian noise is added to both the future frame latents \(\{V_j\}_{j=2}^n\) and the action chunk \(A\).
- **Training Objective:** The DiT backbone is trained to **jointly denoise** these noisy versions of future video latents and actions using DDPM's diffusion loss. The noise timestep embedding is incorporated via adaptive LayerNorm, a standard practice in DiT.
- **Initialization:** Crucially, the DiT backbone is initialized using the pre-trained CogVideoX model. This leverages the extensive knowledge of physical dynamics and visual generation capabilities already acquired by the large-scale video generator.
- **Conditions:** The language tokens \(T'\) and the latent of the current visual observation \(V_1\) serve as conditions for the joint prediction process.
- **Optional Video Decoder:** An optional video decoder, inverse to the VAE encoder, can be used to convert the denoised future frame latents back into raw visual frames for visualization of the "imagined future."

This methodology highlights a departure from prior VLA models by treating video generation and action prediction as tightly coupled tasks within a single, powerful generative framework, rather than relying on separate perception and action modules. The joint denoising objective enforces consistency between predicted actions and their visual consequences, a core tenet of the VideoVLA approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VideoVLA demonstrates compelling results across various evaluation metrics, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines, particularly in generalization tasks. The key findings are:

1. **Strong Correlation Between Visual Imagination and Action Reliability:** A critical insight from the research is the discovery of a strong positive correlation between the quality of the predicted visual imaginations and the reliability of the corresponding action predictions. Experiments show that when VideoVLA generates high-quality, physically plausible future videos that align closely with actual environmental outcomes, the associated predicted actions consistently lead to higher task success rates (Figure 3, Table 10). This implies that visual imagination serves as an implicit indicator of action accuracy and underscores the importance of jointly predicting future visual dynamics alongside actions.

2. **Superior In-Domain Performance:**
 - **Simulation (SIMPLER environment):** VideoVLA achieves the highest average performance across the WidowX robot (Visual Matching) and Google robot (Variant Aggregation) benchmarks and boasts the highest overall average performance across all 12 evaluated tasks compared to baselines like RT-1-X, RT-2-X, Octo, OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, π0, and CogACT (Table 1).
 - **Real-World (Realman robot):** After fine-tuning on a collected dataset, VideoVLA achieves the highest average success rate across real-world "pick," "stack," and "place" tasks, even with novel distractor objects, demonstrating strong performance beyond simulation (Table 4).

3. **Robust Generalization to Novel Objects:**
 - **Simulation:** When tested on 10 novel objects (from YCB and GSO datasets) unseen during the Google robot's training, VideoVLA achieves the highest average success rate (65.2%), outperforming all baselines and showing superior performance on 8 out of 10 objects (Table 2).
 - **Real-World:** In real-world "pick and place" tasks with 12 novel objects, VideoVLA successfully handles all of them, with success rates ranging from 16.7% to 83.3%, significantly outperforming baselines that often achieved 0% success on multiple novel objects (Table 5).

4. **Effective Generalization to New Skills (Cross-Embodiment Transfer):**
 - **Simulation:** VideoVLA demonstrates remarkable ability to transfer skills learned by the WidowX robot to the Google robot, even when these skills were entirely absent from the Google robot's training data. It outperforms the second-best model (CogACT) by a substantial 28.2 percentage points on average across 8 novel skills (Table 3).
 - **Real-World:** Similar cross-embodiment skill transfer is observed with the Realman robot, which successfully performs skills learned by the WidowX robot (e.g., "Topple," "Wipe") that were novel to the Realman embodiment. VideoVLA achieves the highest success rates across all 6 evaluated novel skills (Table 6).

5. **Validation of Core Design Choices through Ablation Studies:**
 - **Backbone Choice:** Using a higher-quality pre-trained video generator (CogVideoX-5B) significantly improves task success rates compared to a lower-quality one (OpenSora-1.1) or training from scratch, confirming the importance of powerful generative pre-training (Table 7).
 - **Time Horizon:** Increasing the number of predicted future frames (from 13 to 49) consistently improves performance, suggesting that a longer temporal horizon allows for better action anticipation (Table 8).
 - **Dual-Prediction Strategy:** Ablations reveal that removing the video denoising loss or predicting actions without generating future videos leads to a substantial performance decline across all tasks, definitively demonstrating the critical role of the dual prediction strategy for robust execution and strong generalization (Table 9).
 - **Causal Masking and Asynchronous Schedules (Appendix):** The default bidirectional attention and synchronous diffusion schedules for actions and video significantly outperform causal masking or asynchronous training/inference strategies, indicating that continuous cross-modal information exchange is beneficial (Tables 13, 14).

In conclusion, VideoVLA's experiments consistently validate its core hypothesis: leveraging large pre-trained video generators with a dual-prediction strategy leads to superior generalization and in-domain performance in robot manipulation, driven by a strong interplay between visual imagination and action prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoVLA represents a significant advancement in robot manipulation research, particularly in its novel approach to achieving generalization, which has profound implications for the future of robotics and artificial general intelligence (AGI).

1. **Paradigm Shift in Robot Learning:** The most profound impact of VideoVLA lies in its pioneering paradigm shift. Traditionally, VLA models have relied on large pre-trained **understanding-oriented** foundation models (e.g., for vision, language, or vision-language perception). VideoVLA, however, demonstrates the feasibility and superior efficacy of leveraging large pre-trained **generative** video models as the backbone for robotic manipulators. This changes the fundamental assumption about which types of foundational AI models are most beneficial for embodied AI, opening up a vast new area of research.

2. **Unlocking Robust Generalization:** The work successfully tackles one of the most persistent challenges in robot manipulation: generalization. By demonstrating strong performance on novel objects and unprecedented cross-embodiment skill transfer in both simulation and real-world settings, VideoVLA brings robots closer to operating effectively in open-world, unpredictable environments. This capability is critical for moving beyond constrained lab settings to practical, real-world deployments across various industries and applications.

3. **Implicit Planning through Visual Imagination:** The discovery of a strong correlation between high-quality visual imagination and reliable action predictions is a key insight. It suggests that the act of generating plausible future visual states serves as an **implicit planning mechanism**. This internal "thinking" or "foresight" capability, inherent to powerful generative models, allows the robot to reason about the consequences of its actions, leading to more robust and accurate execution. This moves away from explicit planning modules and integrates planning more deeply within the perception-action loop.

4. **Scalable Foundation for General-Purpose Robotics:** By demonstrating that video generation models can be adapted into generalizable robot manipulators, VideoVLA positions these generative models as a scalable foundation for general-purpose robotics. As video generation models continue to improve in fidelity, consistency, and understanding of physics, the robot manipulation systems built upon them are expected to exhibit increasingly robust generalization, accelerating progress towards AGI.

5. **Broader Impact on AI Research:** Beyond robotics, this work has implications for the broader field of AI. It showcases how different modalities and tasks (video generation and action prediction) can be synergistically integrated within a single diffusion transformer framework, leading to emergent capabilities. This cross-pollination of techniques and insights between generative AI and embodied AI can inspire new architectures and training strategies for other multi-modal AI problems.

6. **Addressing Limitations and Future Directions:** While revolutionary, the paper acknowledges a key limitation: inference speed. The current setup, running at approximately 3 Hz for real-world deployment, might be restrictive for certain tasks. However, the authors clearly outline promising future directions, such as training smaller, robot-oriented video generators, employing one-step denoising techniques, or model distillation. These proposed solutions indicate a clear path for practical application, ensuring that the current proof-of-concept can evolve into a deployable technology.

In conclusion, VideoVLA not only provides a highly effective solution for robotic generalization but also redefines the architectural foundation for future VLA systems. By harnessing the generative power of video models, it offers a compelling vision for robots that can truly imagine, plan, and adapt, marking a significant step towards more intelligent and versatile autonomous agents.
