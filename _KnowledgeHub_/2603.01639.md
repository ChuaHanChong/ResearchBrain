# Learning to Draft: Adaptive Speculative Decoding with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Learning to Draft (LTD) presents an adaptive speculative decoding framework that uses two co-adaptive reinforcement learning policies to dynamically adjust draft depth and verification size, aiming to maximize large language model inference throughput. This approach delivers a 2.24x to 4.32x speedup over auto-regressive decoding, outperforming the Eagle3 baseline by up to 36.4% on models like Qwen3-32B, and demonstrates robustness in high-temperature sampling.

## Problem

- Large language models suffer from high computational cost and latency during inference, bottlenecking their use in real-time applications.
- Existing speculative decoding methods often rely on static, one-size-fits-all parameters for draft tree generation and verification, leading to suboptimal efficiency across diverse contexts.
- Current dynamic speculative approaches typically optimize proxy metrics like "acceptance length" without fully considering the actual time costs (throughput) and the interdependencies between drafting and verification phases.

## Method

- The problem of ==speculative decoding== optimization is framed as a Reinforcement Learning (RL) environment, directly optimizing for throughput (accepted tokens per unit of total time).
- Two co-adaptive policies are introduced: a Depth Policy (π_D) to decide when to stop drafting, and a Size Policy (π_V) to determine the optimal number of candidate tokens for parallel verification.
- Policies are jointly trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) through a two-stage iterative co-adaptation process, ensuring synergistic and stable learning.

## Results

- Achieved overall speedup ratios ranging from **2.24x** to **4.32x** compared to vanilla auto-regressive decoding.
- Improved throughput by up to **36.4%** over the state-of-the-art Eagle3 baseline on Qwen3-**32B**, with consistent gains across other models (e.g., **9.5%** on Deepseek-Distilled-Llama-**8B**).
- Demonstrated robustness with an approximate **5%** throughput gain at a sampling temperature of 1.0 and outperformed Eagle3 on 54 out of 57 MMLU subtasks, showcasing strong generalization across domains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly optimizing for throughput, rather than proxy metrics like acceptance length, is crucial for achieving true LLM inference acceleration as it balances token gains with their associated time costs.
> - The drafting and verification stages of speculative decoding are interdependent, requiring co-adaptive policies that learn to dynamically adjust to each other's actions for global optimization.
> - Reinforcement learning provides an effective mechanism for learning time-aware and context-adaptive strategies for fine-grained control over speculative decoding parameters, overcoming static heuristics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement and widespread adoption of Large Language Models (LLMs) have brought their remarkable capabilities to the forefront of AI research. However, a significant challenge persists: the high computational cost and latency associated with LLM inference, especially for complex, multi-step reasoning tasks. This latency is a critical bottleneck for deploying LLMs in real-time, interactive applications. Consequently, accelerating LLM inference has become a vibrant and crucial area of research.

Within this landscape, **speculative decoding** has emerged as one of the most effective and principled techniques. Speculative decoding accelerates inference by employing a smaller, faster "draft model" to generate a sequence of candidate tokens. These candidates are then verified in parallel by the larger, more accurate "target model." A key advantage of this approach is its ability to preserve the output distribution of the target model, ensuring that the accelerated output is of the same quality as traditional auto-regressive decoding.

The evolution of speculative decoding has seen several stages:
- **Early methods** focused on simple chain-structured drafts, where tokens were generated sequentially (e.g., Stern et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2023; Leviathan et al., 2023).
- **Recent advancements** introduced **tree-structured drafts** (e.g., SpecInfer by Miao et al., 2023; Eagle and Eagle3 by Li et al., 2024, 2025a). These methods significantly improve efficiency by allowing the parallel verification of multiple candidate sequences, leading to a higher number of accepted tokens (acceptance length) per draft-and-verify cycle.

Despite these advances, a common limitation of many state-of-the-art tree-based methods, such as Eagle3, is their reliance on a **static, one-size-fits-all strategy** for defining draft tree parameters (e.g., depth and width). These static configurations are typically found through manual tuning and do not adapt to the dynamic complexities of different input contexts or generation stages.

Recognizing this limitation, a burgeoning area of research has focused on **adaptive speculative decoding**. These dynamic methods aim to adjust the speculative strategy on-the-fly, typically by controlling:
- **Draft time:** Dynamically adjusting the generation length or draft depth (e.g., Brown et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024c; Huang et al., 2025a).
- **Verification time:** Selecting an optimal subset of candidate tokens for the target model (e.g., Zhong et al., 2024; Huo et al., 2025).

Most existing dynamic approaches, whether heuristic-based (using token probabilities, entropy) or model-based (using feed-forward networks on hidden states), primarily optimize for proxy metrics like **acceptance length** (the average number of tokens guessed correctly per verification step).

**This paper, "Learning to Draft (LTD)," critically identifies a significant gap:** maximizing acceptance length does *not* necessarily translate to maximizing overall generation speed. This is because these approaches often neglect the true computational cost (time spent) of the drafting and verification processes themselves. Furthermore, existing methods frequently treat the drafting and verification phases in isolation, failing to account for their inherent interdependencies.

LTD directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel approach that:
1. **Directly optimizes for throughput**, which is the true measure of efficiency (accepted tokens per unit of *total time*).
2. **Co-optimizes** both the drafting and verification phases through a synergistic, adaptive framework.

By formulating the problem as a reinforcement learning environment and training co-adaptive policies, LTD aims to move beyond static heuristics and isolated optimizations, establishing a new paradigm for time-aware and context-adaptive speculative decoding. It builds upon the efficiency of tree-based methods like Eagle3 but injects dynamic intelligence to achieve superior overall inference acceleration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "Learning to Draft (LTD)" is to **maximize the throughput of large language model (LLM) inference** during speculative decoding. Throughput, in this context, is precisely defined as the number of accepted tokens divided by the total wall-clock time required for a complete draft-and-verify cycle.

This primary objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing LLM Latency for Real-world Applications:** Modern LLMs, while powerful, are computationally intensive. The significant latency they introduce during inference, particularly for complex reasoning or lengthy generations, severely limits their applicability in real-time or interactive systems. Accelerating inference without compromising output quality is therefore paramount for widespread adoption.

2. **Limitations of Existing Speculative Decoding Methods:**
 - **Static Strategies:** Current state-of-the-art speculative decoding methods, such as Eagle3, often rely on pre-defined, static parameters (e.g., fixed draft tree size or verification size). These static configurations are typically set through manual tuning or grid search and cannot adapt to the varying complexities of different input prompts, generation stages, or model behaviors. This "one-size-fits-all" approach inherently leads to suboptimal performance in diverse contexts.
 - **Proxy Metric Optimization:** Recent dynamic approaches attempt to adapt the speculative process. However, many focus on optimizing for proxy metrics like "acceptance length" (the number of correctly guessed tokens per cycle). The critical insight of this paper is that maximizing acceptance length does not automatically maximize overall inference speed. A very long draft tree might lead to more accepted tokens, but if its generation and verification consume disproportionately more time, the overall throughput can decrease, making the process slower rather than faster. The true objective should be to optimize the balance between acceptance length and the time cost.

3. **Interdependence of Drafting and Verification:** The authors argue that the drafting and verification phases of speculative decoding are inherently interdependent. Existing methods often treat these two phases in isolation, optimizing one without fully considering its impact on the other. For instance, if a draft model generates an excellent, long sequence but the verification stage is rigidly limited in its capacity, the drafting effort is wasted. Conversely, if the verification stage is very flexible but the drafting stage is timid, potential gains are missed. An optimal strategy requires synergistic coordination between these two stages.

4. **Direct Throughput Optimization as the True Goal:** The core motivation of LTD is to directly optimize for the ultimate goal: throughput. By explicitly considering both the accepted tokens and the total time cost (draft time + verification time) as a unified reward signal within a reinforcement learning framework, LTD aims to overcome the shortcomings of prior work and learn strategies that truly maximize decoding efficiency. This involves adaptively managing the trade-off between generating more candidate tokens and the time expenditure required for both generating and verifying them.

In essence, LTD is motivated by the need for a **time-aware, context-adaptive, and co-optimized** approach to speculative decoding that moves beyond heuristic-based or isolated optimizations to directly tackle the problem of maximizing LLM inference throughput.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Learning to Draft (LTD) addresses the limitations of prior speculative decoding methods by framing the problem within a novel Reinforcement Learning (RL) environment. This approach allows for direct optimization of throughput by training two co-adaptive policies that dynamically manage the drafting and verification processes.

#### 3.1. Core Framework: Reinforcement Learning Environment

The interaction between the small draft model and the large target model during speculative decoding is formulated as an RL environment. The goal of the RL agent is to learn optimal actions that maximize a direct throughput reward.

**Key Components of the RL Environment:**

- **Agent:** The agent comprises two distinct, co-adaptive policies:
 - **Depth Policy (π_D):** This policy controls the *draft cost* by deciding, after each sequential forward pass of the draft model, whether to **continue** expanding the draft tree (generate more candidate tokens) or **stop** drafting and proceed to verification. Its action space is binary: A_πD = {0, 1}.
 - **Size Policy (π_V):** This policy manages the *verification cost* by determining the optimal **verification size (V)**, which is the number of candidate tokens to select from the generated draft tree for parallel verification by the target model. Its action space is a discrete range, A_πV ∈.

- **State (s_t):** At each time step *t*, the policies observe a state vector s_t designed to be informative about the current drafting context and potential for acceptance.
 - For the **Size Policy (π_V)**, the state s_t includes:
 - Current draft depth (D).
 - Context length (L) of the input sequence.
 - Predicted log-probability scores (P_1..., P_Vall) for *all* candidate tokens in the generated draft tree (padded to a fixed size). These probabilities are strong indicators of potential acceptance.
 - For the **Depth Policy (π_D)**, which makes decisions more frequently, a minimal subset of the state is used to optimize decision latency:
 - Probabilities of tokens at the last layer of the draft tree.
 - Current draft depth (D).
 - Context length (L).
 Both policies are implemented as lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to ensure their decision-making adds negligible computational overhead.

- **Reward (R_t):** The most critical aspect of LTD's methodology is its direct optimization objective. The reward signal R_t for each draft-and-verify cycle is defined as the **throughput**:
 R_t = L_A / (T_draft + T_verify)
 Where L_A is the number of accepted tokens (acceptance length), T_draft is the time taken by the draft model, and T_verify is the time taken by the target model for verification. This reward function directly incentivizes the policies to find the optimal balance between generating more correct tokens and minimizing the total time spent.

- **Optimization Algorithm:** The policies are jointly optimized using the **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** algorithm (Schulman et al., 2017), a robust and widely used on-policy RL algorithm suitable for this continuous control problem.

#### 3.2. Training Procedure: Two-Stage Co-Adaptation

To ensure stable convergence and synergistic operation, LTD employs a two-stage training procedure:

1. **Initial Policy Training:** In this stage, each policy is trained independently while its counterpart operates under a fixed, heuristic-based strategy. The HumanEval dataset is used for training due to its diverse code snippets.
 - The **Size Policy (π_V)** is trained for 100k steps with draft depths randomly sampled from, allowing it to learn how to choose verification sizes across various draft tree structures.
 - The **Depth Policy (π_D)** is trained for 1M steps using a constant verification size of 60, mirroring the heuristic used in the Eagle3 baseline. This provides a stable environment for the depth policy to learn when to stop drafting.
 This phase establishes robust individual policies as a foundation.

2. **Iterative Optimizing for Policy Co-Adaptation:** To achieve optimal synergistic performance, the policies undergo an iterative co-adaptation process. This involves alternating optimization rounds:
 - First, the **size policy (π_V) parameters are frozen**, and the **depth policy (π_D) is optimized** in response to the dynamic verification sizes proposed by π_V.
 - Subsequently, the newly updated **depth policy (π_D) is frozen**, and the **size policy (π_V) is optimized** based on the dynamic draft depths determined by π_D.
 Empirically, the authors found that just two rounds of this iterative process were sufficient to achieve a highly synergistic strategy and substantial performance gains, allowing the policies to progressively adapt to each other's evolving behaviors.

#### 3.3. Foundation and Implementation Details

The LTD method is built upon the state-of-the-art tree-based speculative decoding framework, **Eagle3**. This allows LTD to leverage the efficiency of tree-structured drafts while replacing Eagle3's static heuristics with dynamic, context-aware decision-making policies. Standard PPO parameters and a linear learning rate schedule with warm-up are used. The policies are intentionally kept lightweight to minimize their own inference overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of "Learning to Draft (LTD)" across diverse LLMs and tasks demonstrates its consistent superiority in accelerating inference, particularly by directly optimizing throughput.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Greedy Decoding (Temperature=0)

LTD consistently outperforms all competing approaches, including the highly optimized state-of-the-art Eagle3 baseline and other dynamic speculative methods.
- **Significant Speedup:** LTD achieves speedup ratios ranging from 2.24× to 4.32× compared to vanilla auto-regressive decoding.
- **Outperforming Eagle3:**
 - Up to **36.4% improvement** over Eagle3 on Qwen3-32B. This substantial gain highlights LTD's effectiveness on larger models where efficiency is even more critical.
 - **9.5% improvement** on Deepseek-Distilled-Llama-8B.
 - **6.5% improvement** on Llama-3-8B.
 - **5% improvement** on Vicuna-1.3-13B.
 - **4% improvement** on Qwen3-14B.
The fact that LTD often achieves higher overall speedup even when its average acceptance length (τ) is not the longest (compared to a Grid Search-optimized Eagle3 baseline) is a crucial finding. This validates the core hypothesis that directly optimizing for *throughput* (accepted tokens / total time) is more effective than solely maximizing acceptance length, as it strategically balances potential token gains against their associated time costs.

#### 4.2. Robustness in High-Temperature Sampling (Temperature=1)

LTD demonstrates strong robustness in high-temperature scenarios, where most other dynamic speculative methods tend to degrade significantly.
- LTD maintains an approximate **5% throughput gain** even at a sampling temperature of 1.0, despite being trained exclusively at greedy decoding (Temperature=0). This indicates its ability to generalize across different sampling strategies and its inherent stability in more unpredictable generation environments.

#### 4.3. Granular Analysis of Draft and Verification Time

An analysis of the composition of total inference time reveals that LTD's speedup stems from improvements in *both* drafting and verification phases:
- **Reduced Draft Time:** The dynamic depth policy effectively curtails speculation in scenarios where confidence is low, saving computational resources that would otherwise be wasted on unlikely candidate tokens.
- **Reduced Verification Time:** LTD achieves a lower token verification load. Paradoxically, for models like Llama, Vicuna, and DeepSeek, this lower overhead corresponds to a *higher* average acceptance length. This suggests that LTD is not just reducing the size of draft trees but strategically allocating candidate tokens to contexts where they are most likely to be accepted, thereby optimizing the utility of each verified token. For Qwen series models, where LTD showed lower acceptance length than baselines, it still achieved significantly higher speedup due to disproportionately larger reductions in verification time.

#### 4.4. Generalization Across Diverse Domains (MMLU)

To assess cross-domain generalization, LTD was evaluated on the MMLU benchmark, consisting of 57 diverse subtasks.
- LTD outperformed Eagle3 on **54 out of 57 subtasks**, achieving an average speedup improvement of 5%.
- Significant gains (over 10%) were observed in varied categories such as Mathematics, Logical Fallacies, Human Aging, and Government Politics, confirming the broad applicability of the adaptive strategy.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies Confirm Component Effectiveness

- **Iterative Optimization:** The iterative, co-adaptive training process was shown to yield progressive improvements in both mean speedup and acceptance length, validating that policies learn to synergistically adapt to each other.
- **Individual Policy Contributions:** Both the depth and size policies were confirmed to contribute to overall acceleration. The depth policy was found to play a slightly more significant role, but a naive combination of policies performed suboptimally, emphasizing the critical role of the co-adaptive training scheme.
- **Reward Signal Efficacy:** Experiments confirmed that "throughput" is a superior reward signal compared to "acceptance length" or "time cost" alone. Policies trained solely on acceptance length incurred excessive verification time, while those on time cost generated too few tokens to leverage parallel verification.
- **Observation Space:** The chosen observation space (probabilities, draft depth, context length) provides an optimal balance between informativeness and computational overhead, outperforming richer but more computationally expensive features like entropy or full hidden states.

#### 4.6. Minimal Overhead of RL Policies

The computational overhead introduced by the lightweight MLP policies themselves is minimal, typically less than 1.5% of the total inference time for both Llama and Vicuna models. The training time for these policies is also efficient, taking less than 30 GPU hours for the entire process, which is a favorable trade-off for significant inference speedups.

#### 4.7. Compatibility with Other Frameworks

LTD's effectiveness was also validated on Griffin, another dynamic tree framework. LTD consistently achieved state-of-the-art speedup on Griffin, demonstrating its generalizability beyond the Eagle3 baseline.

In summary, LTD successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of optimizing speculative decoding by directly targeting throughput through a sophisticated, co-adaptive reinforcement learning framework. Its superior performance, robustness, and generalizability establish it as a leading method for accelerating LLM inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Learning to Draft (LTD)" presents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) inference acceleration, with far-reaching implications for both research and practical applications. Its significance can be understood through its methodological novelty and its tangible benefits.

#### 5.1. Methodological Contributions

- **Direct Throughput Optimization:** LTD's most significant methodological contribution is its shift from optimizing proxy metrics (like acceptance length) to directly optimizing for *throughput* (accepted tokens per unit of time). This re-framing recognizes the true objective of efficient inference and overcomes the limitations of prior work that often led to suboptimal resource allocation despite improved acceptance rates. This principle of direct, time-aware optimization could inspire similar approaches in other computationally constrained systems.
- **Co-adaptive Policy Framework:** The introduction of two co-adaptive policies—one for draft depth and one for verification size—is a novel and powerful design. By training these policies iteratively to adapt to each other, LTD captures the inherent interdependence between the drafting and verification phases. This collaborative learning paradigm allows for dynamic resource allocation that is synergistic and globally optimal, rather than locally optimized in isolation. This co-adaptive approach offers a valuable blueprint for optimizing other multi-stage, interdependent computational pipelines in AI.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Fine-grained Control:** The successful application of reinforcement learning, specifically PPO, to dynamically control granular aspects of speculative decoding (draft depth and verification size) showcases the power of RL for complex, real-time decision-making in LLM systems. It moves beyond heuristics or simple supervised learning models by allowing the system to learn optimal strategies through trial and error, driven by a direct performance signal.

#### 5.2. Practical Significance and Potential Impact

- **Enhanced LLM Inference Efficiency:** The most immediate impact of LTD is its ability to significantly accelerate LLM inference. Achieving speedup ratios of 2.24× to 4.32× and outperforming state-of-the-art methods by up to 36.4% directly translates to faster response times for LLM-powered applications. This is crucial for improving user experience in interactive scenarios like chatbots, real-time code generation assistants, virtual agents, and content creation tools.
- **Reduced Operational Costs:** More efficient inference means that fewer computational resources (GPUs, energy) are required to generate the same amount of output. This leads to substantial reductions in the operational costs of deploying and scaling LLM services, making them more economically viable for a wider range of businesses and organizations.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** LTD's demonstrated robustness in high-temperature sampling scenarios, where other dynamic methods often falter, is highly impactful. Many creative or open-ended generation tasks utilize higher temperatures to encourage diversity; LTD ensures that these applications can still benefit from significant acceleration. Furthermore, its strong performance across 57 MMLU subtasks and various LLM architectures (Llama, Vicuna, DeepSeek, Qwen) highlights its broad applicability and generalizability across diverse models and domains, making it a versatile solution.
- **Facilitating Complex Reasoning Tasks:** As LLMs are increasingly tasked with deliberate, multi-step reasoning, inference latency becomes a major bottleneck. By accelerating these processes, LTD can help unlock the full potential of LLMs for complex problem-solving, scientific discovery, and advanced analytics, where speed is critical for iterative exploration and rapid prototyping.
- **Broader Adoption of LLMs:** By making LLM inference faster, cheaper, and more reliable, LTD lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying LLM-based solutions. This can accelerate innovation and foster wider adoption of LLM technology across industries.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of LTD opens new avenues for research into dynamic optimization of LLM inference. This could include exploring other aspects of the inference pipeline through RL, developing more sophisticated co-adaptive agents, or extending these principles to other deep learning models beyond LLMs.

In conclusion, "Learning to Draft" represents a pivotal step in making LLMs more practical and accessible. By tackling the core challenge of inference latency with an intelligent, adaptive, and throughput-focused approach, LTD not only pushes the boundaries of speculative decoding but also lays foundational groundwork for future advancements in efficient AI deployment.
