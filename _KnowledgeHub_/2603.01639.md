# Learning to Draft: Adaptive Speculative Decoding with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Learning to Draft (LTD) presents an adaptive speculative decoding framework that uses two co-adaptive reinforcement learning policies to dynamically adjust draft depth and verification size, aiming to maximize large language model inference throughput. This approach delivers a 2.24x to 4.32x speedup over auto-regressive decoding, outperforming the Eagle3 baseline by up to 36.4% on models like Qwen3-32B, and demonstrates robustness in high-temperature sampling.

## Problem

- Large language models suffer from high computational cost and latency during inference, bottlenecking their use in real-time applications.
- Existing speculative decoding methods often rely on static, one-size-fits-all parameters for draft tree generation and verification, leading to suboptimal efficiency across diverse contexts.
- Current dynamic speculative approaches typically optimize proxy metrics like "acceptance length" without fully considering the actual time costs (throughput) and the interdependencies between drafting and verification phases.

## Method

- The problem of ==speculative decoding== optimization is framed as a Reinforcement Learning (RL) environment, directly optimizing for throughput (accepted tokens per unit of total time).
- Two co-adaptive policies are introduced: a Depth Policy (π_D) to decide when to stop drafting, and a Size Policy (π_V) to determine the optimal number of candidate tokens for parallel verification.
- Policies are jointly trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) through a two-stage iterative co-adaptation process, ensuring synergistic and stable learning.

## Results

- Achieved overall speedup ratios ranging from **2.24x** to **4.32x** compared to vanilla auto-regressive decoding.
- Improved throughput by up to **36.4%** over the state-of-the-art Eagle3 baseline on Qwen3-**32B**, with consistent gains across other models (e.g., **9.5%** on Deepseek-Distilled-Llama-**8B**).
- Demonstrated robustness with an approximate **5%** throughput gain at a sampling temperature of 1.0 and outperformed Eagle3 on 54 out of 57 MMLU subtasks, showcasing strong generalization across domains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly optimizing for throughput, rather than proxy metrics like acceptance length, is crucial for achieving true LLM inference acceleration as it balances token gains with their associated time costs.
> - The drafting and verification stages of speculative decoding are interdependent, requiring co-adaptive policies that learn to dynamically adjust to each other's actions for global optimization.
> - Reinforcement learning provides an effective mechanism for learning time-aware and context-adaptive strategies for fine-grained control over speculative decoding parameters, overcoming static heuristics.
