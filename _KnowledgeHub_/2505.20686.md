# Accelerating RL for LLM Reasoning with Optimal Advantage Regression

> [!summary] Summary
> Cornell University and Princeton University researchers develop A*-PO (Policy Optimization via Optimal Advantage Regression), a two-stage reinforcement learning framework that achieves up to 2× faster training and 30% lower memory usage compared to PPO and GRPO by eliminating explicit critic networks and requiring only single generations per prompt during online training, while maintaining competitive performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks like GSM8K and MATH through offline optimal value function estimation followed by simplified advantage regression.

## Problem

- Current RL methods for LLM fine-tuning (PPO, GRPO, REBEL) require computationally expensive multiple generations per prompt or explicit critic networks during online training
- High memory consumption from managing critic networks or concurrent generations limits scalability to larger models and longer contexts
- Complex algorithmic designs with clipping mechanisms and normalization heuristics increase implementation difficulty and hyperparameter sensitivity

## Method

- Two-stage approach: ==offline estimation of optimal value function V*== using reference policy samples, followed by online policy updates via ==advantage regression==
- Single generation per prompt during online training eliminates computational overhead of multiple concurrent generations
- Direct optimal advantage approximation removes need for explicit critic networks while simplifying algorithm design
- Optional filtering of unsolvable problems further reduces training time by focusing on learnable examples

## Results

- **2x** faster training time than PPO/GRPO across Qwen2.5 models (1.5B, 3B, 7B) on GSM8K and MATH datasets while achieving competitive accuracy
- Over **30%** reduction in peak memory usage compared to baselines, with smallest KL divergence to reference policy indicating better knowledge preservation
- Superior performance on long-context reasoning tasks (**16,384** tokens) and strong generalization to out-of-domain benchmarks like OlympiadBench and AMC 2023
- Ablation studies show performance plateaus at N=**8** offline samples with optional problem filtering reducing training time by **28%** while maintaining accuracy

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal value function can be accurately estimated offline using reference policy samples, decoupling expensive value estimation from online policy updates
> - Theoretical guarantees show near-optimal performance with polynomial sample complexity without requiring sophisticated exploration mechanisms
> - Bias in value function estimation is well-controlled and decreases with more offline samples, with 8 samples typically sufficient for good performance
> - Single-generation online updates combined with simplified regression loss achieve comparable or superior performance to complex multi-generation methods

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) using reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance their reasoning capabilities. This area has gained significant traction, especially with the success of techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

**Current Challenges Addressed:**
Traditional policy optimization methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Generative Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization (GRPO) are widely used, but the authors highlight two major bottlenecks when applied to LLMs, especially for complex reasoning tasks:
1. **High Computational Overhead:** These methods often require multiple generations per prompt during online training (e.g., GRPO, REBEL, RLOO) or maintain an explicit critic network to estimate value functions (e.g., PPO). This leads to substantial computational costs.
2. **High Memory Consumption:** Managing critic networks or handling multiple concurrent generations per prompt consumes significant GPU memory, limiting the scalability to larger LLMs and longer context lengths.

**A*-PO's Contribution to the Landscape:**
The proposed method, A*-PO (Policy Optimization via Optimal Advantage Regression), directly addresses these limitations. It seeks to develop a *simpler and more efficient* RL algorithm for long-context reasoning. It differentiates itself from existing methods by:
- **Eliminating Explicit Critic Networks:** Unlike PPO, A*-PO does not require a separate critic network for value estimation.
- **Single Generation per Prompt (Online):** It drastically reduces online computational and memory overhead by requiring only one response per prompt during the online RL training stage. This contrasts sharply with GRPO and REBEL, which require multiple online generations.
- **Direct Optimal Advantage Approximation:** Instead of estimating the advantage of the *current* policy, A*-PO directly approximates the *optimal* value function in an offline manner. This innovative approach simplifies the online update rule.
- **Simplified Algorithm Design:** A*-PO eschews common heuristics like clipping mechanisms (used in PPO) and response-wise reward normalization, resulting in a more streamlined algorithm.
- **Theoretical Guarantees without Sophisticated Exploration:** A significant theoretical contribution is proving near-optimal performance with polynomial sample complexity *without* explicit exploration strategies, given a base model with a non-zero probability of solving the problem. This contrasts with many modern RL algorithms that rely on complex exploration mechanisms to ensure coverage.

The work builds upon the emerging paradigm of RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards), where rule-based rewards (e.g., binary correctness for math problems) are used. It aligns with other recent efforts (OREO, DAPO, DQO, DRO) that also aim for single-response efficiency and log-ratio regression, but A*-PO distinguishes itself by removing the need for a critic, further enhancing efficiency. The paper also acknowledges its conceptual ties to imitation learning algorithms like AggreVaTe(d) by leveraging the idea of regressing an optimal advantage.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **accelerate reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning of large language models for complex reasoning tasks** by significantly reducing computational overhead and memory consumption.

The core motivations driving this work are:
1. **Addressing Computational Inefficiency:** State-of-the-art RL policy optimization methods (like PPO, GRPO, REBEL) are computationally expensive and memory-intensive. This is primarily due to:
 - The necessity for multiple generations per prompt during online training.
 - The reliance on explicit critic networks to estimate value functions or advantages of the current policy.
 These limitations hinder scaling LLM reasoning capabilities, especially for larger models and long-context problems.
2. **Developing Simpler and More Efficient Algorithms:** The authors explicitly raise the question: "Can we develop simpler and more efficient RL algorithms for long context reasoning?" This query encapsulates the desire for a method that is not only faster and uses less memory but also reduces algorithmic complexity.
3. **Improving Scalability for Complex Tasks:** Given the increasing size and context demands of modern LLMs and reasoning tasks, an efficient fine-tuning mechanism is crucial for pushing the boundaries of LLM performance on challenging problems.
4. **Enhancing Theoretical Understanding:** Beyond practical efficiency, the authors aim to provide robust theoretical guarantees, particularly demonstrating that effective policy optimization can be achieved *without* sophisticated exploration strategies under realistic conditions, thereby simplifying the theoretical underpinnings of such algorithms.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a practical, scalable, and theoretically sound solution for RL fine-tuning of LLMs that overcomes the efficiency challenges of existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

A*-PO (Policy Optimization via Optimal Advantage Regression) is a novel two-stage policy optimization framework designed for efficient LLM training.

**Core Idea:**
The central insight is to directly approximate the *optimal* value function ($V^\star$) in an *offline* manner using a reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$), thereby eliminating the need for costly online value estimation or multiple online generations. The online policy update then leverages this pre-computed $V^\star$ to estimate the optimal advantage function.

**Detailed Stages:**

1. **Stage 1: Offline Estimation of Optimal Value Function ($V^\star(x)$)**
 - **Objective:** To estimate $V^\star(x)$ for every prompt $x$ in the training set. The KL-regularized RL objective's optimal value function $V^\star(x)$ has a closed-form expression: $V^\star(x) = \beta \ln E_{y \sim \pi_{ref}(\cdot|x)} [\exp(r(x,y)/\beta)]$.
 - **Process:** For each prompt $x$, $N$ independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) responses ($y_1..., y_N$) are sampled from the *reference policy* ($\pi_{ref}$). The empirical estimate $\hat{V}^\star(x)$ is computed as $\hat{V}^\star(x) \leftarrow \beta \ln \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \exp(r(x,y_i)/\beta) \right)$.
 - **Efficiency:** This stage is performed entirely offline. It is highly parallelizable and can leverage fast inference libraries (like vLLM, DeepSpeed) without requiring gradient computations, making it very efficient.
 - **Note on Bias:** While $\hat{V}^\star(x)$ is a biased estimate due to the non-linearity of the $\ln$ function, the authors theoretically show that this bias is well-controlled and shrinks with increasing $N$, provided the reference policy has a non-zero probability of generating a correct solution.
 - **Optional Filtering:** The algorithm also allows for an optional step to eliminate training prompts for which all $N$ solutions are incorrect, further improving training efficiency by focusing on solvable problems.

2. **Stage 2: On-Policy Update with Optimal Advantage Regression**
 - **Objective:** To update the current policy ($\pi_t$) to maximize the expected reward, using the pre-computed $\hat{V}^\star(x)$.
 - **Process:** In each iteration $t$, a batch of training samples $D = \{(x,y)\}$ is collected, where $y$ is generated by the *current policy* $\pi_t(\cdot|x)$. The policy $\pi_t$ is then updated to $\pi_{t+1}$ by performing Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) on the following least-squares regression loss:
 $ \ell_t(\pi) := E_{x,y \sim \pi_t(\cdot|x)} \left[ \beta \ln \frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)} - (r(x,y) - \hat{V}^\star(x)) \right]^2 $
 The term $(r(x,y) - \hat{V}^\star(x))$ approximates the optimal advantage $A^\star(x,y) = r(x,y) - V^\star(x)$ in the bandit setting (where $Q(x,y)=r(x,y)$).
 - **Key Advantage:** Crucially, this online stage requires **only a single generation** ($y$) per prompt ($x$). This is the primary driver for the significant reduction in computational and memory overhead compared to baselines.
 - **Algorithmic Simplicity:** Unlike PPO or GRPO, A*-PO does not use clipping mechanisms to constrain policy deviation, nor does it require response-wise reward normalization. It directly aims to learn $\pi^\star$ by regressing on the optimal advantages.
 - **Hyperparameters:** The practical implementation uses two KL-regularization coefficients, $\beta_1$ for Stage 1 (larger, for smoother $V^\star$ estimation) and $\beta_2$ for Stage 2 (smaller, to relax KL constraint and optimize reward). The authors fix these values across experiments, suggesting robustness.

**Theoretical Underpinnings:**
The paper provides formal performance guarantees, demonstrating that A*-PO achieves near-optimal performance with polynomial sample complexity. A key theoretical finding is that explicit exploration mechanisms (like optimism in the face of uncertainty) are *not* required, provided the base model's probability of solving a math question is lower-bounded above zero. This is framed as a reduction to no-regret online learning.

**Overall Approach:** A*-PO cleverly decouples the expensive value function estimation from the online policy update, allowing for highly efficient training. By leveraging the fixed reference policy for value estimation, it simplifies the online learning problem to a regression task, leading to substantial practical benefits.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of A*-PO extensively covers a range of mathematical reasoning benchmarks and model sizes, demonstrating its practical efficiency and performance.

#### 4.1. Basic Reasoning on GSM8K

- **Setup:** Experiments conducted on the GSM8K dataset using Qwen2.5 models (1.5B, 3B, 7B) with full parameter training on 4 H100 GPUs. Baselines include PPO, GRPO, and REBEL. A rule-based binary reward function (+1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) was used.
- **Key Findings:**
 - **Comparable Accuracy:** All methods achieved similar test accuracy on GSM8K, indicating that A*-PO is effective in optimizing the KL-regularized RL objective.
 - **Faster Training Time:** A*-PO consistently achieved the fastest training time, being up to **2x faster** than GRPO and PPO. This includes the time for the offline Stage 1 data collection. REBEL had comparable training time but significantly higher memory usage.
 - **Lower Peak Memory Usage:** A*-PO showed the lowest peak memory usage, over **30% less** than baselines, particularly noticeable compared to PPO (due to critic) and REBEL (due to simultaneous two generations).
 - **Smaller KL Divergence:** A*-PO maintained the smallest KL divergence to the reference policy, suggesting better preservation of the base model's knowledge while optimizing for reward.

#### 4.2. Advanced Reasoning on MATH

- **Setup:** Evaluated on the more challenging MATH dataset (high school math competitions), using the same model sizes and hyperparameter settings. Performance was assessed on MATH500 (in-distribution), Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, and AMC 2023 (out-of-domain).
- **Key Findings:**
 - **Higher Accuracy and Generalization:** A*-PO consistently ranked as either the best or second-best method across various benchmarks and model sizes. For the 7B model on MATH500, A*-PO notably outperformed baselines. Crucially, it demonstrated strong generalization capabilities on out-of-domain benchmarks.
 - **Continued Efficiency:** A*-PO remained the fastest and most memory-efficient method, especially for larger models (e.g., 2x faster than PPO/GRPO for 7B). It also maintained the smallest KL divergence.

#### 4.3. Long-context Reasoning with DeepSeek-distilled Qwen

- **Setup:** Tested A*-PO's ability to handle long context lengths using a DeepSeek-R1 distilled Qwen-1.5B model with 16,384 context length, trained on a subset of the OpenR1-Cleaned dataset. REBEL was excluded due to out-of-memory errors in this setting. Evaluated on competition-level AIME and HMMT benchmarks (Avg@32 and Pass@32 metrics).
- **Key Findings:**
 - **Scalability to Long Context:** A*-PO effectively scaled to long-context reasoning scenarios, achieving the best average performance across the AIME and HMMT benchmarks.
 - **Continued Efficiency:** It remained the most efficient, requiring significantly less training time (49 hours) compared to PPO (88 hours) and GRPO (90 hours), demonstrating its suitability for computationally demanding long-context tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Number of Generations (N) for $V^\star$ Estimation (Stage 1):**
 - Increasing $N$ from 1 to 32 led to a consistent decrease in the squared regression loss, indicating more accurate $V^\star$ estimates.
 - Training reward and MATH500 performance improved monotonically with $N$, plateauing around $N=8$. This suggests that for these tasks, 8 samples are sufficient for accurate enough $V^\star$ estimation.
- **Filtering Out Hard Problems:**
 - Filtering problems where all $N$ sampled responses from $\pi_{ref}$ were incorrect significantly reduced Stage 2 training time by shrinking the effective training set size.
 - Performance on MATH500 remained closely aligned, with the filtered run (N=8) even achieving the highest accuracy while reducing training time by 28%. This highlights a practical optimization strategy inherent to A*-PO's design.
- **$\beta_1$ for $V^\star$ Estimation (Stage 1):**
 - $\beta_1$ controls the smoothness of $V^\star$ estimation (smaller $\beta_1 \to$ closer to Pass@N, larger $\beta_1 \to$ closer to $V_{\pi_{ref}}$).
 - Smaller $\beta_1$ values (down to 1/8) led to lower final training loss, improved training reward, and better MATH500 performance. Further reduction in $\beta_1$ could lead to overly optimistic $V^\star$ estimates that the model cannot achieve. The optimal value was found to be $\beta_1 = 1/2$.

**Qualitative Analysis (Appendix B):**
A*-PO trained models exhibited cognitive behaviors crucial for reasoning, such as:
- **Verification:** Systematically checking answers after generation.
- **Subgoal Setting:** Decomposing problems into manageable steps.
- **Backtracking:** Abandoning failing approaches and trying new ones.

**Theoretical Results (Section 5):**
- **Near-Optimal Performance:** The theory proves that A*-PO achieves near-optimal expected performance against $\pi^\star$ (the optimal KL-regularized RL policy) with polynomial complexity.
- **Only Realizability:** Unlike many policy optimization methods that require strong conditions like policy completeness, A*-PO relies on a much weaker realizability condition ($\pi^\star \in \Pi$).
- **No Exploration:** A key theoretical finding is that sophisticated exploration mechanisms are *not* required. As long as the base model ($\pi_{ref}$) has a non-zero probability of solving a problem, A*-PO can learn a near-optimal policy. This simplifies the algorithm and contrasts with prior work needing complex exploration strategies.
- **Last-Iterate Convergence (for Log-Linear Policies with OGD):** For log-linear policies using Online Gradient Descent, the theorem shows that the *final policy* (not just an average) converges to $\pi^\star$, which is more desirable in practice. It also boasts a dimension-free learning rate.

In summary, A*-PO provides a highly efficient and effective method for fine-tuning LLMs for reasoning, backed by strong empirical evidence across diverse tasks and model sizes, and supported by theoretical guarantees that simplify the understanding of its convergence properties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

A*-PO represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model fine-tuning, particularly for reasoning tasks, with substantial practical and theoretical implications:

1. **Enabling Scalable RL for LLMs:** The most immediate impact is the drastic reduction in computational overhead (up to 2x faster training time) and memory consumption (over 30% less peak memory usage). This directly addresses critical bottlenecks in applying RL to ever-larger LLMs and more complex, long-context reasoning problems. By making RL fine-tuning more accessible and affordable, A*-PO can accelerate research and development in LLM capabilities.

2. **Simplified Algorithm Design:** A*-PO's design is notably simpler than many existing methods. By eliminating the need for explicit critic networks, clipping mechanisms, and response-wise reward normalization, it reduces the complexity of implementation and hyperparameter tuning. This simplicity can foster wider adoption and make RL fine-tuning more robust and easier to manage for practitioners.

3. **Strong Theoretical Foundations without Complex Exploration:** The theoretical guarantee that A*-PO can achieve near-optimal performance with polynomial sample complexity *without explicit exploration* is highly significant. This challenges the common assumption in much of RL theory that complex exploration is always necessary. It suggests that for scenarios where a base model has a non-zero probability of success (which is realistic for LLMs on reasoning tasks after pre-training), simpler learning paradigms are sufficient. This finding simplifies the theoretical landscape and could inspire new, more efficient RL algorithms.

4. **Improved Performance and Generalization:** Empirically, A*-PO achieves competitive or superior performance across a range of mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including challenging competition-level datasets. Its strong generalization capabilities on out-of-domain tasks further highlight its robustness and effectiveness.

5. **New Research Directions:** The success of A*-PO in leveraging offline $V^\star$ estimation and single-generation online updates opens up new avenues for research in RL fine-tuning. This includes exploring more sophisticated offline data collection strategies, investigating alternative ways to approximate optimal advantages, and applying this two-stage paradigm to other LLM capabilities beyond reasoning (e.g., code generation, scientific discovery). The optional filtering of hard prompts also suggests ways to optimize training by focusing on "learnable" problems.

6. **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The paper effectively bridges the gap between theoretical guarantees and practical efficiency. By demonstrating how a theoretically sound approach can lead to tangible improvements in training speed and resource usage, it provides a strong foundation for future practical applications of RL in LLM development.

In conclusion, A*-PO is a compelling contribution that significantly pushes the frontier of efficient and scalable RL fine-tuning for LLMs, offering a simpler, faster, and more memory-efficient approach with robust theoretical backing. Its impact is likely to be felt across both academic research and industrial applications, making advanced LLM reasoning capabilities more attainable.
