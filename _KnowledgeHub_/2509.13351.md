# Teaching LLMs to Plan: Logical Chain-of-Thought Instruction Tuning for Symbolic Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> PDDL-INSTRUCT introduces an instruction tuning framework that enhances Large Language Models' symbolic planning capabilities by integrating explicit logical chain-of-thought reasoning. The approach leads to substantially improved plan validity and accuracy across multiple planning domains, demonstrating up to a 66% absolute improvement over baseline LLMs in plan accuracy.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often fail to perform rigorous, multi-step logical reasoning required for automated symbolic planning, leading to unreliable plan generation.
- Current LLM-based planning approaches lack intrinsic mechanisms for systematically verifying action preconditions, effects, and overall plan validity.
- Applying Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting alone has not been consistently sufficient to impart the logical rigor needed for robust formal planning in LLMs.

## Method

- Introduces ==PDDL-INSTRUCT==, a three-phase instruction tuning framework, to teach LLMs explicit ==logical Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning== for symbolic planning.
- Incorporates an iterative training loop that leverages an external ==PDDL plan validator (VAL)== to provide ground-truth feedback on action validity and state transitions.
- Utilizes a two-stage optimization process focusing on improving reasoning chain quality and end-task planning performance, with an emphasis on ==detailed error feedback==.

## Results

- PDDL-INSTRUCT improved plan accuracy by an average of **66%** absolute percentage points over baseline LLMs, achieving up to **94%** validity for Llama-3 and **91%** for GPT-4 in Blocksworld.
- Detailed feedback on logical errors consistently outperformed binary feedback, leading to up to a **15** percentage point increase in plan accuracy across domains.
- The approach demonstrated strong generalization and substantial improvements across diverse planning domains, including a **64-fold** accuracy increase for Llama-3 in the complex Mystery Blocksworld domain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating logical Chain-of-Thought reasoning into LLM instruction tuning, combined with external validation, is effective in teaching formal symbolic planning.
> - Providing detailed feedback on specific logical errors during training leads to more robust planning capabilities in LLMs compared to binary validity feedback.
> - The method demonstrates strong cross-domain generalization, substantially improving planning accuracy and robustness even in complex and diverse planning scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) with symbolic AI, particularly in the domain of automated planning, represents a critical and rapidly evolving area of research. LLMs have demonstrated remarkable success in diverse cognitive tasks, from natural language understanding and generation to mathematics and coding. However, their proficiency often diminishes when faced with tasks requiring rigorous, multi-step logical reasoning and structured decision-making, such as automated planning.

Automated planning, traditionally handled by symbolic AI systems using formal languages like the Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL), requires precise reasoning about state transitions, action preconditions, and effects. Previous research has highlighted that while LLMs can generate plausible text, they frequently struggle with the logical coherence and systematic verification essential for generating valid plans. Issues include producing unreliable plans, failing to account for action effects as problem complexity scales, and limited self-correction capabilities.

This paper addresses a significant gap by enhancing LLMs' ability to perform *structured symbolic planning* directly, rather than merely using LLMs to *generate* PDDL models for external solvers. Existing approaches often fall into several categories:
1. **LLMs generating executable code:** Models generate code to dictate planned behaviors, but still face challenges in long-term reliability and complex reasoning.
2. **LLMs with closed-loop feedback:** Models interact with an environment or receive feedback for self-refinement, but their internal reasoning often remains opaque and unreliable, especially with iterative feedback.
3. **Hybrid LLM-symbolic approaches:** LLMs generate PDDL models (domain and problem files) which are then solved by traditional symbolic planners. While promising, this often requires human intervention or external verifiers to ensure the accuracy of the LLM-generated PDDL, as LLMs struggle to synthesize such structured models reliably.
4. **Finetuning LLMs for planning:** General finetuning can improve planning capabilities but often leads to high computational costs and reduced transferability to new domains.
5. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting:** This technique has greatly improved LLM reasoning by eliciting explicit intermediate steps. However, some studies have questioned its suitability for planning, arguing that it might not be sufficient for the logical rigor required.

PDDL-INSTRUCT distinguishes itself by integrating CoT reasoning directly into the instruction tuning process, specifically focusing on logical inference steps for plan generation and *action applicability verification*. Instead of merely generating plans or PDDL code, it teaches LLMs to *reason* about the formal planning process itself—precondition satisfaction, effect application, and invariant preservation. This pushes beyond simple finetuning by structuring the learning process around logical justification and external verification feedback (using VAL, a PDDL validator). This approach effectively bridges neural network capabilities with symbolic reasoning principles, aiming to instill robust, verifiable planning capabilities directly within the LLM, thereby enhancing trust and reliability in LLM-generated plans. It proposes that CoT, when properly integrated with instruction tuning and external validation, *is* suitable for planning, directly countering earlier critiques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly enhance Large Language Models' (LLMs) ability to perform structured symbolic planning**, particularly within formal representations like the Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL).

The core motivations behind PDDL-INSTRUCT stem from observed limitations of current LLMs:
- **Inability to perform rigorous symbolic planning:** Despite their impressive general reasoning capabilities, LLMs struggle with the precise logical inference required for automated planning tasks, often producing unreliable or invalid plans.
- **Lack of systematic verification:** Current LLMs lack inherent mechanisms for systematically verifying action applicability, state transitions, and overall plan validity, leading to a critical gap in their use for reliable real-world sequential decision-making.
- **Need for logical Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in planning:** While CoT has improved reasoning in other domains, its direct application to formal planning and verification has been challenging. The authors aim to demonstrate that with appropriate instruction tuning, CoT can be effectively leveraged for logical planning.
- **Bridging the neural-symbolic gap:** The research seeks to foster more trustworthy AI planning systems by combining the expressive power of LLMs with the logical precision and verifiability of symbolic planning. This is crucial for applications in safety-critical domains where plan correctness is paramount.

To achieve this, the paper's key objectives are:
1. To develop a novel instruction tuning framework, PDDL-INSTRUCT, that teaches LLMs to reason about action applicability and plan validity using explicit logical inference steps.
2. To formalize the planning verification process into decomposable reasoning chains, allowing LLMs to systematically check preconditions, apply effects, and validate invariants.
3. To empirically demonstrate that this CoT reasoning-based instruction tuning approach significantly improves planning accuracy and robustness, generalizing across different planning domains.
4. To enable LLMs to not only generate syntactically correct plans but also to verify their logical validity and potentially self-correct through structured reflection guided by external feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PDDL-INSTRUCT framework consists of a three-phase approach designed to systematically teach LLMs symbolic planning capabilities through logical Chain-of-Thought (CoT) instruction tuning. It leverages a pre-trained LLM, a dataset of PDDL planning domains and problems with solutions, and a PDDL plan validator (VAL) for feedback.

**Phase 1: Initial Instruction Tuning**
In this foundational phase, a pre-trained LLM is trained on a dataset (`D_1`) of planning domains and problems. The instruction prompts explicitly guide the model to analyze plans by explaining *why* each action is valid, detailing precondition satisfaction and effect application. Crucially, this phase includes both correct and deliberately incorrect plans. For incorrect plans, the model is trained to identify and explain various errors (e.g., unsatisfied preconditions, incorrect effect application, frame axiom violations, or failure to reach the goal). This initial phase establishes a baseline understanding of planning logic and the ability to articulate logical justifications.

**Phase 2: CoT Instruction Tuning**
This is the core innovation of PDDL-INSTRUCT and involves a two-stage iterative process (highlighted in Figure 1). The LLM, already tuned in Phase 1, is further trained using a new dataset (`D_2`) and a feedback loop with VAL:

1. **CoT Generation:** The LLM generates step-by-step state-action-state sequences `⟨s_0, a_1, s_1⟩. . . , ⟨s_n-1, a_n, s_n⟩` for a given problem, explicitly detailing precondition checks, effect applications, and resulting state changes. This is the Chain-of-Thought output.
2. **External Verification (VAL):** The generated reasoning chains are passed through an external PDDL plan validator (VAL) which systematically checks the validity of each state transition. This provides ground-truth feedback, unlike self-reflection approaches where LLMs attempt to critique their own reasoning internally.
3. **Feedback Types:** Two types of feedback are explored:
 - **Binary Feedback:** Simply indicates whether an action is valid or invalid.
 - **Detailed Feedback:** Provides specific reasoning about errors, such as which preconditions failed or which effects were incorrectly applied. The hypothesis is that detailed feedback leads to more robust planning.
4. **Iterative Refinement:** The verification results guide further instruction tuning. The model learns from this feedback loop, which is limited by a hyperparameter `η` (number of iterations).

**Two-Stage Optimization Process for Phase 2:**
To optimize the model in Phase 2, a distinct two-stage process is employed:

- **Stage 1: Reasoning Chain Optimization:** This stage optimizes the model parameters (`θ_t`) to improve the quality of the generated reasoning chains. A `L_reasoning` loss function is used, which measures the discrepancy between the generated step-by-step transitions and ideal logical inference sequences. It explicitly penalizes logical errors like applying actions with unsatisfied preconditions or failing to properly propagate effects. The step-wise loss (`L_step`) combines a state distance function (`d_state`) to measure differences in states and a `L_feedback` component based on VAL's error types (precondition violation, incorrect effect, goal achievement failure).
- **Stage 2: End-Task Performance Optimization:** Building on the reasoning-improved parameters (`θ_r_t`), this stage optimizes for overall planning performance. A `L_final` loss function measures how well the final generated plans match expected outcomes. This loss incorporates a penalty for invalid plans and a binary cross-entropy term for the model's predicted plan validity against VAL's ground truth. This stage ensures that improvements in logical reasoning translate into accurate and valid plans.

**Phase 3: Evaluation Phase**
After both training phases, the instruction-tuned LLM is evaluated on new, unseen planning domains and problems from a test dataset (`D_test`). The model directly generates complete state-action-state sequences, which are then assessed for correctness using VAL. This assessment is purely for evaluation; no feedback is returned to the model during this phase.

The framework assumes planning domains conform to basic PDDL features (STRIPS-like) and avoids complex features like conditional effects or durative actions to simplify the reasoning chain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of PDDL-INSTRUCT was conducted using Llama-3-8B and GPT-4 foundation models on PlanBench, a standardized benchmark, across three distinct planning domains: Blocksworld, Mystery Blocksworld (a complex variant of Blocksworld), and Logistics. The primary metric was Plan Accuracy, defined as the percentage of valid plans achieving the specified goal, as verified by VAL.

**Overall Performance (RQ1): Logical CoT Instruction Tuning Improves Plan Validity**
The results conclusively demonstrated that PDDL-INSTRUCT significantly outperformed baseline models (unmodified LLMs), models after only Phase 1 tuning, and models with only Phase 2 CoT tuning.
- For **Llama-3** with detailed feedback and `η = 15`, PDDL-INSTRUCT achieved validity rates of 94% (Blocksworld), 64% (Mystery Blocksworld), and 79% (Logistics). This represented an average absolute improvement of 35% over basic instruction tuning and a substantial **66% absolute improvement over the baseline models**.
- For **GPT-4** with detailed feedback and `η = 15`, similar strong performance was observed: 91% (Blocksworld), 59% (Mystery Blocksworld), and 78% (Logistics). This translated to an average absolute improvement of 48% over basic instruction tuning and **61% absolute improvement over the baseline**.
These findings unequivocally support that logical CoT instruction tuning enhances plan accuracy by enabling LLMs to reason explicitly about preconditions, effects, and state transitions.

**Impact of Feedback Type (RQ2): Detailed Feedback is Superior**
Comparing binary versus detailed feedback, the study found that **detailed feedback consistently led to better planning performance** across all domains and models.
- For Llama-3 with `η = 15`, detailed feedback improved plan accuracy by 5 percentage points in Blocksworld, 15 points in Mystery Blocksworld, and 7 points in Logistics compared to binary feedback.
- This pattern confirmed the hypothesis that providing specific reasoning errors allows the model to develop more robust verification capabilities. The advantage of detailed feedback was particularly evident in Mystery Blocksworld, the most complex domain.
- Increasing the iteration limit from `η = 10` to `η = 15` also yielded consistent improvements, especially with detailed feedback (averaging 4.3 percentage points improvement) compared to binary feedback (averaging 3.3 percentage points), indicating more effective learning from granular error information.

**Cross-Domain Generalization (RQ3): Domain-General Enhancement**
Performance varied across domains, reflecting their inherent complexity: Blocksworld yielded the highest accuracy, followed by Logistics, with Mystery Blocksworld being the most challenging.
- Despite varying absolute performance, the *relative improvement* from PDDL-INSTRUCT was substantial across all three domains. This indicates that the logical reasoning framework is domain-general and transfers effectively to different planning scenarios.
- The most dramatic improvements were observed in domains where baseline performance was weakest. For instance, Llama-3's accuracy on Mystery Blocksworld surged from a mere 1% (baseline) to 64% with PDDL-INSTRUCT (detailed feedback, `η = 15`), representing a **64x improvement**. This highlights the particular value of explicit logical reasoning for complex problems where simple pattern matching is insufficient.

**Ablation Study (Supplementary Material):** An ablation study confirmed the contribution of each phase. Phase 1 significantly improved performance over baseline, and Phase 2 (CoT instruction tuning) further boosted accuracy, particularly with detailed feedback. The combination of both phases yielded the best results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PDDL-INSTRUCT framework represents a significant step forward in enabling Large Language Models to perform robust and verifiable symbolic planning. By explicitly teaching LLMs to reason through logical chain-of-thought and providing targeted feedback from an external validator, this work successfully bridges a critical gap between the general reasoning capabilities of LLMs and the logical precision required for automated planning.

**Significance:**
- **Enhanced Reliability:** The most important contribution is the substantial improvement in plan accuracy and validity. Achieving up to 94% accuracy, with a 66% absolute improvement over baselines, demonstrates that LLMs can be trained to generate logically sound plans, which is crucial for real-world applications.
- **Verifiable AI Behavior:** By decomposing the planning process into explicit, verifiable logical steps, the approach inherently supports more interpretable and trustworthy AI behavior. This contrasts with traditional LLM outputs that can be opaque or unfaithful in their reasoning.
- **Bridging Neural and Symbolic AI:** PDDL-INSTRUCT effectively combines the strengths of neural networks (general pattern recognition, language understanding) with the rigor of symbolic AI (formal logic, planning rules). This hybrid approach offers a promising direction for future AI development.
- **Improved Instruction Tuning:** The research highlights the power of detailed, specific feedback over binary feedback in instruction tuning, especially for complex logical tasks. This insight can inform the design of more effective training methodologies for LLMs across various domains.
- **Foundation for Self-Correction:** While currently relying on an external verifier, the framework lays the groundwork for developing more advanced self-verification capabilities within LLMs, potentially reducing or eliminating the dependence on external tools in the future.

**Potential Impact:**

**Positive Impacts:**
- **Autonomous Decision-Making:** The improved planning capabilities can lead to more reliable and efficient autonomous systems in domains such as healthcare robotics, autonomous vehicles, logistics, and disaster response. Agents equipped with PDDL-INSTRUCT could generate safer, more predictable plans.
- **Democratization of Planning:** By allowing LLMs to reason about formal planning, the framework could democratize access to sophisticated planning tools for non-expert users, enabling them to articulate complex goals in natural language and receive logically sound plans.
- **New Research Avenues:** This work opens new research directions, including optimizing instruction tuning data, expanding to more complex PDDL features, developing self-verification, and applying the logical reasoning framework to other sequential decision-making tasks like theorem proving or complex puzzle solving.
- **Integration with Existing Tools:** The authors suggest that PDDL-INSTRUCT, when combined with frameworks like LLM-Modulo (which integrates external tools with LLMs), could further enhance efficiency by reducing the need for feedback loops, making the planning process faster and more reliable.

**Broader Impacts and Risks:**
- **Safety-Critical Domains:** While beneficial, over-reliance on LLM-generated plans in safety-critical applications (e.g., medical robots, autonomous defense systems) could still pose risks if the model fails. The hybrid nature might also obscure error attribution, complicating accountability.
- **Misuse Potential:** Enhanced planning capabilities could be misused for strategic manipulation or complex malicious behaviors. As AI systems become more adept at planning, safeguards and ethical guidelines become increasingly important.
- **Computational Cost:** The instruction tuning, especially with iterative feedback and detailed CoT, requires significant computational resources (GPUs, training time), which might limit accessibility for smaller research groups.

To mitigate risks, the authors recommend incorporating external verification, robust human oversight, and usage safeguards in real-world deployments. Overall, PDDL-INSTRUCT offers a promising avenue for developing AI systems that are not only capable but also trustworthy and understandable in their decision-making processes.
