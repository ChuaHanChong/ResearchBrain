# Latent Intuitive Physics: Learning to Transfer Hidden Physics from A 3D Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed a latent intuitive physics framework that infers hidden fluid properties from a single 3D video and transfers this learned physics to simulate novel scenes. The probabilistic approach significantly outperforms existing methods in generalizing fluid dynamics to unseen geometries and boundary conditions, while also providing superior short-term and long-term particle prediction accuracy when physical parameters are unknown.

## Problem

- Most deep learning physics simulators require explicit knowledge of physical properties, which are often difficult to obtain or measure.
- Prior intuitive physics models struggle with inherent stochasticity and complexity of real-world phenomena, often requiring extensive finetuning for new properties or overfitting to observed scenes.
- A lack of effective methods to transfer learned physical properties from visual observations to generalize simulations in entirely novel scenarios.

## Method

- A **Latent Intuitive Physics** framework is proposed, comprising a ==probabilistic particle transition module==, a ==physical prior learner==, a ==particle-based posterior estimator==, and a ==neural renderer==.
- A ==three-stage training pipeline== is introduced: pretraining the probabilistic fluid simulator on particle data, inferring scene-specific ==visual posterior latent features== from a single 3D video, and adapting only the physical prior learner to these visual posteriors.
- Hidden physical properties are captured in a ==probabilistic latent space==, allowing for transfer to a physics engine for simulation in novel scenes without explicit parameter estimation.

## Results

- The model achieved superior performance in simulating novel scenes, exhibiting significantly lower average Euclidean distance (e.g., **34.54±0.55** on novel geometry) compared to baselines like CConv (**52.49**) and PAC-NeRF (**51.10**).
- Demonstrated strong performance in future prediction of observed dynamics and superior particle-level predictions when physical properties were inaccessible, with a long-term average Euclidean distance (¯d) of **38.37±0.860** compared to **90.21** for DPI-NET.
- Qualitative results showed more reasonable and coherent fluid dynamics simulations on novel scenes, avoiding the noisy or physically implausible predictions often generated by baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Probabilistic latent variables can effectively capture unobservable, complex, and potentially stochastic physical properties of fluids directly from visual observations.
> - A staged pretraining-inference-transfer scheme allows for robust generalization of learned physics to novel scene geometries and boundary conditions.
> - Adapting only the physical prior learner, rather than the entire transition model, helps to leverage pretraining knowledge and reduces overfitting on limited visual data, improving transferability.
