# On Pre-Training for Visuo-Motor Control: Revisiting a Learning-from-Scratch Baseline

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper re-evaluates the paradigm of leveraging pre-trained visual representations for robotic control, showing that a strong Learning-from-Scratch baseline, enhanced with data augmentation, frequently matches or surpasses state-of-the-art frozen pre-trained methods across diverse visuo-motor tasks. It highlights the critical role of robust baselines and context-specific effectiveness for pre-training in this domain.

## Problem

- The prevailing assumption that large-scale pre-trained visual representations consistently offer superior performance and sample efficiency for robotic control tasks.
- Prior comparisons often used weak or unoptimized Learning-from-Scratch (LfS) baselines, potentially leading to misleading conclusions about the benefits of pre-training.
- A need for a clear, rigorously benchmarked understanding of the actual advantages and limitations of pre-trained representations in visuo-motor control.

## Method

- Develop a strong **LfS (+aug)** (Learning-from-Scratch) baseline by incorporating robust ==random shift data augmentation== and shallow convolutional neural networks.
- Conduct a comprehensive comparison of this enhanced LfS baseline against three state-of-the-art ==frozen pre-trained visual representations== (PVR, MVP, R3M).
- Evaluate performance across diverse robotic control tasks (dexterous manipulation, locomotion), various algorithm classes (==Behavior Cloning==, PPO, DrQ-v2), and both simulated and real-world robot setups.

## Results

- The `LfS (+aug)` baseline **matched or surpassed** the performance of frozen **PVR**, **MVP**, and **R3M** on a majority of evaluated tasks across different algorithms and domains, including real-robot experiments.
- No single frozen pre-trained representation consistently performed best; their efficacy varied significantly based on the specific task domain and control algorithm.
- Finetuning pre-trained representations on small amounts of in-domain data with data augmentation achieved **superior performance** compared to both frozen pre-trained methods and the strong `LfS (+aug)` baseline on specific tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A strong Learning-from-Scratch baseline, particularly when enhanced with effective data augmentation, can be highly competitive with or outperform recent frozen pre-trained visual representations on current benchmarks.
> - The effectiveness of pre-trained visual representations is highly dependent on the specific task and algorithm, with no single pre-trained model demonstrating universal superiority.
> - Data augmentation is critical for visual robustness, which pre-trained models do not inherently provide; a 'real-to-sim gap' hindering direct transfer of real-world pre-trained representations can be mitigated by finetuning with in-domain data and augmentation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself as a critical re-evaluation within the rapidly evolving field of deep learning, specifically at the intersection of computer vision, reinforcement learning, and robotics. Large-scale pre-training has achieved phenomenal success in domains like computer vision (e.g., ImageNet pre-training) and natural language processing (e.g., BERT, GPT models), leading to significant improvements in data efficiency, generalization, and performance. Naturally, researchers have sought to extend this paradigm to visuo-motor control, where learning directly from high-dimensional visual inputs is notoriously sample-inefficient.

Recent works, such as PVR (Parisi et al., 2022), MVP (Xiao et al., 2022), and R3M (Nair et al., 2022), have proposed leveraging visual representations pre-trained on vast out-of-domain vision datasets (like ImageNet or human interaction videos) and then *freezing* these representations for downstream policy learning. These studies generally reported that such frozen pre-trained representations enhance sample efficiency and/or asymptotic performance compared to learning visual representations from scratch.

However, the authors of this paper contend that these comparisons might have been made against insufficiently strong Learning-from-Scratch (LfS) baselines. They point out a "rich body of work" on strategies to improve LfS methods, including self-supervised representation learning and carefully curated data augmentations. This paper directly addresses this gap by rigorously strengthening the LfS baseline. Its contribution to the broader landscape is not to dismiss pre-training entirely, but to ensure that the actual benefits and limitations of pre-trained representations in visuo-motor control are accurately understood and benchmarked. By establishing a robust LfS baseline, the paper forces the research community to a higher standard of comparison, preventing premature conclusions and guiding future efforts toward more genuinely impactful advances in pre-training for robotics. It highlights that the "unreasonable effectiveness" of pre-trained models might sometimes be attributable to a lack of optimized baselines rather than inherent superiority in all contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate the effectiveness of pre-training in visuo-motor control tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Revisit and Strengthen the Learning-from-Scratch (LfS) Baseline:** The core motivation stems from the hypothesis that prior LfS baselines used for comparison against pre-trained representations were potentially weak. The authors aimed to construct a simple yet powerful LfS baseline by incorporating established techniques like data augmentation (specifically random shift) and shallow ConvNet encoders, which have proven effective in related visual reinforcement learning literature.
2. **Gain a "Sharp Understanding" of Pre-training Advantages:** By comparing a strong LfS baseline with state-of-the-art frozen pre-trained representations (PVR, MVP, R3M), the authors sought to precisely quantify when and where pre-training truly offers advantages. This includes evaluating various metrics beyond just peak performance, such as sample efficiency, visual robustness, and computational cost.
3. **Accurately Benchmark Progress:** The authors explicitly state their desire to help "accurately benchmark progress" in the area of pre-training for control. A strong baseline is essential for measuring incremental improvements and ensuring that new methods genuinely advance the state of the art. Without it, reported gains might be misleading.
4. **Provide "Must-Know Insights" for Researchers:** The paper intends to offer practical guidance and key observations for practitioners and researchers working on pre-trained representations in visuo-motor control. This includes insights into the importance of data augmentation, the existence of a "real-to-sim gap," and the limitations of current benchmarks.
5. **Inform Future Research Directions:** Based on their empirical findings, the authors aim to provide concrete recommendations for future research. This includes suggesting improvements for benchmark design, strategies for handling domain gaps, and refining training recipes for both LfS and pre-trained approaches.

In essence, the paper is motivated by a desire for methodological rigor and a clear understanding of what contributes to successful visuo-motor control from visual observations, especially in the context of large-scale pre-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a rigorous and comprehensive methodology to ensure a fair and insightful comparison between Learning-from-Scratch (LfS) methods and approaches utilizing frozen pre-trained visual representations. Their approach is characterized by:

#### 3.1. Fair Comparison Framework

The study focuses on comparing representative methods from both paradigms:
- **LfS Baselines:** Two variants are introduced:
 - `LfS (no aug)`: A shallow ConvNet encoder, with architectures adopted from prior work specific to each algorithm class (PVR's encoder for BC, Yarats et al. for off-policy RL, Hansen et al. for on-policy RL). This serves as a baseline for the impact of augmentation.
 - `LfS (+aug)`: The same shallow ConvNet encoder, but crucially enhanced with **random shift data augmentation** (Kostrikov et al., 2021; Yarats et al., 2021). This is presented as the "strong" LfS baseline, representing state-of-the-art LfS practices in visual RL.
- **Frozen Pre-trained Visual Representations:** Three prominent, concurrently proposed state-of-the-art models are chosen:
 - **PVR (Parisi et al., 2022):** Based on MoCo-v2 with a ResNet50 backbone trained on ImageNet, fusing features from multiple layers. It used random crop, horizontal flip, gray-scale, and color jitter during pre-training.
 - **MVP (Xiao et al., 2022):** Utilizes a Masked Autoencoder (MAE) with a ViT-S backbone trained on a large human interaction (HOI) dataset. It applied random crop and horizontal flip during pre-training.
 - **R3M (Nair et al., 2022):** Employs a ResNet50 backbone pre-trained with a multi-loss objective (time-contrastive, video-language alignment, L1 regularization) on the Ego4D human interaction video dataset. It only used random crop during pre-training.
 - **Consistency:** All pre-trained models are used in their original, frozen formulations, without modification, to ensure evaluation under conditions favorable to their original proposals. Publicly available models and demonstrations were used where possible.

#### 3.2. Diverse Experimental Setup

To ensure broad generalizability of findings, experiments were conducted across a variety of settings:
- **Task Domains (4):**
 - **Adroit (Rajeswaran et al., 2018):** Dexterous manipulation (2 tasks: Pen, Relocate), evaluated with Behavior Cloning (BC).
 - **DMControl (Tassa et al., 2018):** Locomotion and manipulation (5 tasks: Finger Spin, Reacher Easy, Cheetah Run, Walker Stand, Walker Walk), evaluated with BC and Off-policy RL (DrQ-v2).
 - **PixMC (Xiao et al., 2022):** Robotic manipulation (8 tasks across Franka and Kuka robots), evaluated with On-policy RL (PPO).
 - **Real Robot Setup:** Two manipulation tasks (Reach, Pick) on an xArm 7 robot, evaluated with BC. This adds crucial real-world validation.
- **Algorithm Classes (3):**
 - **Imitation Learning (Behavior Cloning - BC):** Used for Adroit, DMControl, and Real Robot tasks.
 - **On-policy RL (PPO - Schulman et al., 2017):** Used for PixMC tasks.
 - **Off-policy RL (DrQ-v2 - Yarats et al., 2021):** Used for DMControl tasks.

#### 3.3. Comprehensive Evaluation Metrics

Beyond standard performance metrics (success rate, episode return), the study considers:
- **Sample Efficiency:** Performance as a function of training steps/data.
- **Asymptotic Performance:** Peak performance achieved.
- **Visual Robustness:** Evaluated using the DMControl Generalization Benchmark (Hansen & Wang, 2021), testing agents trained on original environments in visually altered test environments (randomized colors, dynamic video backgrounds).
- **Computational Cost:** Training wall-time (s/iteration) and inference speed (s/episode).
- **Data Efficiency:** Performance with varying numbers of demonstrations (10, 25, 100) for BC tasks.

#### 3.4. Additional Experiments and Controls

- **Finetuning Pre-trained Representations:** Conducted experiments where frozen representations were finetuned on in-domain demonstration data (Adroit BC), with and without data augmentation, to understand the impact of alleviating the domain gap.
- **Impact of Augmentation Choice:** Explored different strong augmentations (color jitter, random overlay) for LfS on robustness benchmarks.
- **Strict Adherence to Prior Setups:** The authors explicitly state that they adopt the experimental setups of PVR, MVP, and DrQ-v2 without modification, including hyperparameters. This meticulous approach ensures that pre-trained representations are evaluated under conditions for which they were originally optimized, bolstering the fairness of the comparison.
- **Code Availability:** The authors have made their code public, promoting reproducibility and further research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yields several pivotal findings that challenge existing perceptions and offer crucial insights into the landscape of pre-training for visuo-motor control:

1. **LfS (+aug) Competitiveness:** The most striking finding is that the carefully designed Learning-from-Scratch baseline, enhanced with random shift data augmentation (`LfS (+aug)`), is **surprisingly competitive with, and in many cases outperforms**, recent frozen pre-trained representations (PVR, MVP, R3M). This holds true across a wide array of algorithms (BC, PPO, DrQ-v2), task domains (Adroit, DMControl, PixMC, real robot), and metrics (Figure 1, Table 2). This directly challenges the assumption that pre-trained representations automatically provide a significant performance edge on current benchmarks.

2. **No Universal Best Pre-trained Representation (Yet):** The efficacy of frozen pre-trained representations is found to be both task-dependent and algorithm-dependent. No single pre-trained model (PVR, MVP, R3M) consistently emerges as the best across all settings. For instance, MVP performs well on PixMC (for which it was designed) but less so on others. This "no free lunch" observation contrasts with the consistent strong performance of `LfS (+aug)` across all settings (Figure 4).

3. **Critical Role of Data Augmentation for Robustness:** Data augmentation is identified as *critical* for the visual robustness of learned representations, whether they are learned from scratch or from pre-trained models. Pre-trained representations that did not use color jitter during their own pre-training (MVP, R3M) were not inherently more robust to visual changes than their LfS counterparts. However, strong augmentations (like color jitter or random overlay) significantly improve robustness for both LfS and pre-trained representations (e.g., PVR, which used jitter). Interestingly, applying strong augmentation *during policy learning* with already frozen pre-trained representations was found to be ineffective (Figure 6, Figure 7, Table 3).

4. **Benefits of Finetuning and the "Real-to-Sim Gap":** While the primary study focuses on *frozen* representations, finetuning experiments reveal an important nuance. When pre-trained representations are finetuned on a small amount of *in-domain data* and coupled with *data augmentation* during finetuning, they can achieve performance superior to both their frozen counterparts and even the strong `LfS (+aug)` baseline (Table 5). The authors attribute this to a substantial **"real-to-sim gap"**—a domain discrepancy between large-scale real-world pre-training datasets and predominantly simulated control benchmarks. They also note that ResNet-based models (PVR, R3M) were easier to finetune than ViT-based MVP, potentially due to known optimization challenges with Transformers.

5. **Data Efficiency Nuances:** The study investigated data efficiency by varying the number of demonstrations. It found that LfS methods generally benefit more from larger demonstration datasets (100 demonstrations), while frozen pre-trained representations show only marginal advantages in very low-data regimes (10 demonstrations). This suggests that in current benchmarks, policy learning, rather than visual representation learning, might be the more significant bottleneck when data is limited (Figure 8).

6. **Computational Cost Trade-offs:** Frozen pre-trained representations offer substantial reductions in training wall-time for Behavior Cloning tasks, primarily because visual features can be pre-embedded and cached. However, their larger backbones can lead to slower inference speeds compared to the smaller LfS models. In Reinforcement Learning, where learning and inference are interleaved, overall wall-times tend to be more balanced (Table 4). The cost of pre-training the representations themselves is considered a one-time cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper carries significant implications for the research and development of visuo-motor control systems, particularly concerning the role of large-scale pre-training.

1. **Elevating Baselining Standards:** The most immediate impact is the establishment of a robust and competitive Learning-from-Scratch baseline. This forces the community to re-evaluate claims of superiority for pre-trained models, ensuring that future comparisons are made against strong, well-engineered alternatives. This will prevent inflated performance metrics and promote more rigorous scientific progress.
2. **Re-calibrating the Value Proposition of Pre-training:** The findings suggest that while pre-training holds promise, its "unreasonable effectiveness" in visuo-motor control on current benchmarks may have been overstated. Researchers must now explicitly demonstrate that their pre-trained approaches offer *genuine* advantages beyond what a carefully implemented LfS method can achieve, especially if they aim for high performance. The current main benefit appears to be reduced training cost due to universality, not necessarily outright performance superiority.
3. **Highlighting the Criticality of Data Augmentation:** The paper unequivocally emphasizes data augmentation as an absolutely critical component for robust visual representation learning in control, regardless of whether a model is learned from scratch or finetuned from a pre-trained one. This provides clear practical guidance for practitioners and encourages further research into advanced augmentation techniques tailored for control tasks.
4. **Identifying and Quantifying the "Real-to-Sim Gap":** The observation of the "real-to-sim gap" as a significant impediment to the direct transfer of real-world pre-trained representations to simulated control tasks is a crucial insight. This gap persists even to some extent in real-robot evaluations. This finding is highly significant as it offers a concrete explanation for the observed performance discrepancies and points to clear directions for mitigation.
5. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The paper provides actionable recommendations for the field:
 - **Benchmark Design:** It advocates for the development of new benchmarks that feature greater visual complexity, variation, realism, and emphasize multi-task learning. Current benchmarks, often repurposed from state-based RL, do not sufficiently stress the visual generalization capabilities that pre-trained models are expected to provide.
 - **Pre-training Strategies:** It suggests two main approaches to address the domain gap: either pre-train on data that more closely matches the downstream task distribution (e.g., synthetic data or more diverse real robot data) or consistently finetune pre-trained representations on small amounts of in-domain data, crucially *with data augmentation*. This points towards a hybrid approach where pre-training provides general features, and finetuning adapts them to the specific task domain, analogous to current trends in large language models.
 - **Refining Training Recipes:** It calls for further research into advanced pre-processing and data augmentation training recipes specifically for visuo-motor control, suggesting that current practices are still relatively underexplored compared to computer vision.

In conclusion, this paper serves as a vital touchstone for the visuo-motor control community. It does not dismiss pre-training but rather elevates the scientific discourse by providing a more accurate understanding of its current capabilities and limitations. By advocating for stronger baselines and a deeper consideration of factors like domain gaps and data augmentation, it lays a more solid foundation for truly impactful advancements in the quest for intelligent robotic control from visual input.
