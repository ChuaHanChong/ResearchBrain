# R-Horizon: How Far Can Your Large Reasoning Model Really Go in Breadth and Depth?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and Meituan LongCat Team developed R-HORIZON, a method and benchmark to evaluate and enhance Large Reasoning Models' (LRMs) long-horizon reasoning capabilities. Their work demonstrates that state-of-the-art LRMs exhibit significant performance degradation on complex, interdependent multi-step tasks but can achieve substantial improvements through reinforcement learning with R-HORIZON-generated data.

## Problem

- Current benchmarks for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) primarily focus on isolated, single-horizon tasks, failing to reflect the complexity of real-world multi-step problems.
- Real-world AI applications, especially agentic systems, require sequential planning and problem-solving over extended periods where previous outputs critically influence subsequent steps.
- Existing evaluation methods do not adequately assess LRMs' ability to perform sustained, interdependent reasoning across a 'long reasoning horizon'.

## Method

- The ==R-HORIZON methodology== systematically transforms existing single-horizon problems into complex, multi-step scenarios with explicit dependencies, tailored for mathematical, code, and agentic tasks.
- A novel ==R-HORIZON benchmark== was constructed using these composed datasets, covering 6 domains, to rigorously evaluate LRMs' 'actual accuracy' compared to 'theoretical accuracy' on long-horizon tasks.
- ==Reinforcement Learning with Verified Rewards (RLVR)== and ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== were applied using R-HORIZON data, incorporating distinct `R_last` and `R_all` reward schemes to train models for multi-horizon reasoning.

## Results

- Across **25** mainstream LRMs, performance degraded significantly with increased reasoning horizon; DeepSeek-R1's AIME25 accuracy dropped from **87.3%** (1 query) to **24.6%** (5 queries).
- RLVR training with R-HORIZON data yielded substantial improvements, with R1-Qwen-7B showing a **+17.4 point** increase on AIME24 (2 queries) and a **+7.5 point** improvement on single-horizon tasks.
- Trained models demonstrated enhanced efficiency with shorter responses, learned to allocate their 'thinking budget' more appropriately, and engaged in more frequent and longer-range reflections.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Even advanced LRMs exhibit a limited 'effective reasoning length,' with performance degrading significantly as the number of interdependent queries increases.
> - LRMs struggle with 'thinking budget' allocation and localized reflection, often 'overthinking' early reasoning stages and failing to identify/rectify errors from earlier steps in a long chain.
> - Explicitly training models with R-HORIZON-composed, sequentially dependent data via RLVR can substantially improve both multi-horizon and single-horizon reasoning performance and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) has been a significant trend in the field of Artificial Intelligence, with models like OpenAI’s o1 (OpenAI et al., 2024) and DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) demonstrating remarkable abilities, particularly through "test-time scaling" and "long Chain-of-Thought (CoT)" prompting. These techniques enable models to generate extended reasoning trajectories, leading to improved performance on complex tasks such as mathematical reasoning, code generation, and agentic planning.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in the existing research landscape: current benchmarks (e.g., GSM8k, MATH500) and training paradigms predominantly focus on "single-horizon tasks" or isolated problems. In these settings, questions and answers are independent, and models are not explicitly evaluated on their ability to perform multi-step reasoning where problems are interdependent and span a "long reasoning horizon." Real-world AI applications, especially in agentic systems (Yao et al., 2024; Tao et al., 2025), demand sequential planning and problem-solving over extended periods, where previous outputs critically influence subsequent steps. The inability of current evaluation methods to assess this complex, multi-horizon reasoning capability leaves an incomplete picture of LRMs' true potential and limitations.

This paper directly addresses this gap. It builds upon prior work exploring the "effective reasoning length" of LRMs (Su et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025b), which has shown that beyond an optimal CoT length, performance can degrade. It also relates to the "overthinking" phenomenon (Chen et al., 2025), where models generate verbose reasoning with diminishing or even negative returns. Unlike previous attempts like REST (Pan et al., 2025) which concatenate independent problems, R-HORIZON specifically constructs *dependent* problems to expose the challenges of prolonged, interconnected reasoning.

By proposing a novel method for query composition and constructing a new benchmark, R-HORIZON provides a much-needed tool to systematically evaluate how far LRMs can "go in breadth and depth" when faced with tasks requiring sustained, logically linked problem-solving. Furthermore, by integrating this approach into reinforcement learning with verified rewards (RLVR), the work explores how models can be explicitly *trained* to overcome these long-horizon reasoning limitations, a departure from conventional RL that often focuses on single, isolated problems. Thus, R-HORIZON represents a crucial step towards developing and assessing AI agents capable of more human-like, multi-faceted reasoning in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to comprehensively evaluate and enhance the "long-horizon reasoning" capabilities of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), addressing a significant shortcoming in current evaluation and training paradigms.

The **primary motivation** stems from the observation that while LRMs have achieved impressive feats on immediate, single-horizon tasks through techniques like long Chain-of-Thought (CoT), these tasks fail to reflect the complexity of real-world scenarios. In practical applications, AI agents are often required to:
1. **Reason over an extended series of steps:** Real-world problems rarely resolve in a single, isolated query. They typically involve multiple sequential actions or logical deductions.
2. **Handle interdependent problems:** The solution or outcome of one sub-problem frequently serves as a critical input for subsequent problems.
3. **Plan and act over longer periods:** This necessitates maintaining context, managing a "thinking budget," and identifying errors across an entire reasoning trajectory.

Current evaluation benchmarks, however, largely consist of isolated problems, preventing a true assessment of a model's ability to understand and respond to such complex, multi-horizon scenarios. Similarly, conventional reinforcement learning (RL) often trains models on discrete, single problems, which does not foster the development of long-horizon reasoning skills.

To tackle this, the authors propose **R-HORIZON** with the following key objectives:
- **Stimulate Long-Horizon Reasoning:** Develop a method, through "query composition," to transform existing single-horizon tasks into complex, multi-step scenarios with explicit dependencies.
- **Construct a Long-Horizon Reasoning Benchmark:** Create a new benchmark using the R-HORIZON method, encompassing diverse domains like mathematics, code generation, and agentic tasks, to rigorously evaluate LRMs.
- **Identify Limitations of Advanced LRMs:** Through evaluation, pinpoint specific failure modes of even state-of-the-art LRMs when confronted with long-horizon reasoning. These include limited effective reasoning length, poor reflection scope, and inadequate thinking budget allocation.
- **Enhance Long-Horizon Reasoning through Reinforcement Learning:** Utilize R-HORIZON-generated data for reinforcement learning with verified rewards (RLVR) to explore if models can be effectively trained to overcome these identified limitations and improve their multi-horizon reasoning capabilities.
- **Offer a Scalable and Controllable Paradigm:** Position R-HORIZON as a practical, low-cost solution for both evaluating and improving LRMs' ability to handle complex, extended reasoning.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to provide a more complete understanding of LRMs' reasoning limits and offer a pathway for building more robust and truly intelligent AI systems capable of tackling intricate real-world problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The R-HORIZON methodology is designed to create complex, multi-step reasoning tasks by composing simpler, single-horizon problems. This approach is applied for both evaluating and training Large Reasoning Models (LRMs).

#### 3.1. R-HORIZON Datasets Construction

The core of R-HORIZON lies in its ability to generate "long-horizon" problems from existing "seed problems." The process differs slightly based on the task domain:

- **Mathematical Tasks (Sequentially Composed Concatenation):**
 - **Seed Problem Filtering:** From an initial dataset (D), problems are filtered (`D_seed`) based on two criteria:
 1. The question `q` must contain at least one integer (`|I(q)| > 0`).
 2. The answer `a` must be an integer (`a ∈ Z`).
 3. A model `M` then identifies "key variables" within the integers of `q` by verifying if removing an integer `m` makes the problem unsolvable. Each filtered problem is represented as a triple `(q, a, K(q))`.
 - **Expanded Problem Composition (Algorithm 1):** This algorithm constructs dependency chains. For `n` seed problems, it iteratively creates `n-1` modified problems (`q'_(i+1)`) where a placeholder variable `v_(i+1)` in `q'_(i+1)` is defined by a dependency function `f_i(a_i) = m_(i+1)`. This means the solution `a_i` from the previous problem `i` is required to solve problem `i+1`. The dependency constraint is explicitly prepended to the problem statement, ensuring sequential solving.

- **Code Tasks:** Similar to mathematical tasks, code problems from LiveCodeBench are used as seed questions. However, due to the nature of sandbox execution for answers, direct dependency relationships between problems and answers (as in math) are not explicitly added. Instead, a "directly composed concatenation format" is used (Appendix A), implying problems are chained without direct numerical output dependencies in the prompt but still requiring sequential reasoning or context.

- **Agentic Tasks (WebShaper):** For web search tasks, questions from WebShaper are decomposed into structured data (Target, Variables). Using a topological sort on a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of questions and variables, sub-questions and seed questions are derived. Claude-Sonnet-4 is used to extract variable values from web pages. Questions are categorized by the number of variables (1 to 5), creating a multi-round tool-calling scenario (Appendix A).

#### 3.2. R-HORIZON Benchmark

The constructed long-horizon datasets are then used to build a benchmark comprising 6 representative datasets across mathematics (MATH500, AIME24, AIME25, AMC23), code generation (LiveCodeBench), and agent applications (WebShaper).

- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Actual Accuracy (`Acc(Q)`):** An "all-or-nothing" scoring is used. A composed problem `Q` is considered correct only if *all* its sub-problems `(ˆa_i)` are correctly solved (`ˆa_i = a_i`).
 - **Theoretical Accuracy (`Acc_expected(Q)`):** This metric estimates the expected accuracy by multiplying the individual pass rates `(p_i)` of the atomic seed problems: `Π p_i`. This serves as a baseline to understand the performance degradation on composed tasks.
 - **Answer Extraction:** GPT-4.1 (or similar model-based extraction for diversity) is used to extract answers from model responses for mathematical and agentic tasks. Code tasks use sandbox execution.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning with R-HORIZON

To explore how models can be trained for long-horizon reasoning, R-HORIZON datasets are leveraged for Reinforcement Learning with Verified Rewards (RLVR).

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** This algorithm (Shao et al., 2024) is adopted as the optimization method. GRPO removes the need for a separate value function, a common component in PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), by calculating advantages based on relative rewards within a group of sampled responses. This is applied with a token-level policy gradient loss.
- **Reward Design:** Two distinct reward schemes are designed for multi-horizon training data:
 - `R_last`: Rewards `1` if only the final answer `(ˆa_n)` is correct, `0` otherwise.
 - `R_all`: Rewards `1` only if *all* intermediate answers `(ˆa_i)` are correct, `0` otherwise. This distinction allows investigation into how different feedback granularities influence long-horizon reasoning development.

This comprehensive methodology allows for both rigorous evaluation of existing LRMs' long-horizon capabilities and the development of targeted training strategies to overcome their identified limitations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The R-HORIZON study yielded several critical findings regarding the long-horizon reasoning capabilities of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) and the efficacy of R-HORIZON for training.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Results on R-HORIZON Benchmark

- **Significant Performance Degradation:** Across 25 mainstream LRMs, including highly advanced models like DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B-Thinking, performance degraded significantly as the number of composed queries (reasoning horizon) increased. For example, DeepSeek-R1's accuracy on AIME25 dropped from 87.3% (n=1) to 24.6% (n=5). This degradation was observed across all model sizes and task categories.
- **Model Size and Degradation:** Larger models exhibited less severe performance degradation compared to smaller models. R1-Qwen-7B, for instance, showed a sharper decline than R1-Qwen-32B on MATH500 (93.6% to 0% for 7B vs. 99.6% to 93.4% for 32B at n=16).
- **Consistent Degradation Trends:** The degradation pattern was consistent across tasks of varying difficulty and types (mathematics, code, agentic tasks). More challenging tasks (e.g., AIME25 vs. AIME24) resulted in steeper performance drops. Code tasks showed even more severe degradation.
- **Error Type Analysis (Figure 5):**
 - "Problem Reasoning Errors" (errors in solving individual sub-problems) increased rapidly with more composed problems.
 - "Early Stop" (premature termination of generation) was a frequent issue, especially as the number of problems grew, indicating models struggle to complete long sequences.
 - "Dependency Reasoning Errors" (errors in calculating or using dependencies) increased but remained a relatively smaller proportion of overall errors, suggesting the primary challenge is not just dependency calculation but overall sustained reasoning.
- **Limited Effective Reasoning Length (Figure 6):** The gap between actual and theoretical accuracy widened considerably with increasing query numbers, demonstrating that LRMs struggle to maintain performance as reasoning length extends. Each model exhibited an "effective reasoning boundary" beyond which performance sharply declined (e.g., 7B model error range at 4-6k tokens, 32B at 8-10k tokens).
- **Localized Reflection and Overthinking (Figure 7 & 8):**
 - While reflection frequency increased with more problems, "long-range reflection" (reflection beyond the current problem to identify prior errors) remained limited for most models, suggesting highly localized self-correction.
 - Models (including DeepSeek-R1) failed to appropriately allocate their "thinking budget" across multiple problems, tending to over-allocate tokens to early reasoning stages. This "overthinking" on initial problems led to reduced resources or attention for subsequent problems.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning with R-HORIZON Datasets

- **Significant Performance Improvement:** Training with R-HORIZON-composed data substantially improved performance on multi-horizon reasoning tasks. For example, R1-Qwen-7B trained with 2-query composed data showed a +17.4 point increase on AIME24 (n=2) compared to training with original single-query data.
- **Generalization to Single-Horizon Tasks:** Surprisingly, training with R-HORIZON data also improved performance on the original single-horizon tasks (+7.5 on AIME24), suggesting that learning long-horizon reasoning capabilities can generalize and enhance fundamental reasoning skills.
- **Improved Efficiency and Reduced Overthinking (Figure 9b, 9d):** Models trained with composed data generated shorter responses and learned to allocate their thinking budget more reasonably across multiple problems, alleviating the "overthinking phenomenon." This indicates more efficient reasoning.
- **Enhanced Long-Range Reflection (Figure 9c):** Training with composed problems encouraged models to engage in more frequent and longer-range reflections, demonstrating that R-HORIZON can foster better self-correction over extended reasoning trajectories.
- **Impact of Composed Query Number and Reward Schemes (Table 1):**
 - Increasing the number of composed problems in training data generally led to stronger capabilities in handling tasks requiring more reasoning steps.
 - The `R_all` reward function (requiring all intermediate steps to be correct) often outperformed `R_last` (only rewarding the final answer) for multi-problem scenarios, suggesting that granular feedback on intermediate steps is beneficial for long-horizon learning.
- **Ablation Studies (Appendix D):**
 - **Dependencies Matter:** Sequentially dependent problems proved significantly harder than simply concatenating independent problems, confirming the importance of R-HORIZON's dependency construction.
 - **Evaluation Metric Anomaly:** A divergence between `Acc_all` (all correct) and `Acc_last` (final correct) was observed, with models sometimes getting the final answer right despite previous errors. This suggests potential data contamination issues where models "know" the answer without true sequential reasoning.
 - **Difficulty Ordering:** More powerful models benefited from ordering problems from easy-to-hard, leading to better token allocation, while smaller models did not show significant gains.

In summary, the evaluation highlights fundamental limitations in how current LRMs approach and execute long-horizon, interdependent reasoning. Crucially, the reinforcement learning experiments demonstrate that R-HORIZON provides a powerful and effective paradigm to not only expose these weaknesses but also actively train models to overcome them, leading to more capable and efficient reasoning agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The R-HORIZON research makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the development and evaluation of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs).

**1. Novel and Comprehensive Evaluation Paradigm:** R-HORIZON introduces a critically needed benchmark for "long-horizon reasoning." By moving beyond isolated, single-step tasks to complex, multi-step, and interdependent problem sequences, it offers a more realistic assessment of LRMs' capabilities for real-world applications. This benchmark exposes limitations that were previously obscured by conventional evaluation metrics, providing a clearer picture of where current models fall short.

**2. Identification of Core LRM Limitations:** The findings rigorously quantify and categorize key weaknesses in state-of-the-art LRMs when faced with extended reasoning tasks:
 - **Limited Effective Reasoning Length:** Models' performance degrades significantly beyond a certain reasoning trajectory length.
 - **Suboptimal Thinking Budget Allocation:** LRMs struggle to distribute computational resources (tokens) effectively across multiple sequential problems, often "overthinking" early stages.
 - **Localized Reflection:** Models exhibit limited ability for long-range self-correction, failing to identify and rectify errors from earlier steps in a complex chain.
 These insights are crucial for guiding future research in model architecture, training methodologies, and prompting strategies to mitigate these specific issues.

**3. Scalable and Controllable Data Synthesis for Training:** Beyond evaluation, R-HORIZON provides a scalable, controllable, and low-cost paradigm for generating synthetic long-horizon reasoning data. This is vital because high-quality, long-horizon data is scarce and expensive to produce manually. The ability to automatically compose dependent problems opens new avenues for creating diverse and challenging training datasets.

**4. Enhanced LRM Capabilities through RLVR:** The demonstration that training with R-HORIZON-composed data, using reinforcement learning with verified rewards (RLVR), significantly improves performance on both multi-horizon *and* single-horizon tasks is a breakthrough. It suggests that explicitly teaching models to navigate complex dependencies and manage extended reasoning trajectories can foster more generalized and robust reasoning abilities. This has the potential to reshape LRM training, shifting focus towards learning robust sequential problem-solving rather than just isolated task mastery.

**5. Impact on AI Agent Development:** The paper's findings and proposed solutions are particularly impactful for the development of autonomous AI agents. Real-world agents operating in complex environments (e.g., robotics, code generation, scientific discovery, web-based assistants) must perform multi-step planning, adapt to changing conditions based on previous actions, and correct errors over long operational horizons. R-HORIZON provides the tools to both stress-test and enhance the reasoning core of such agents, paving the way for more capable, reliable, and intelligent AI systems.

**6. Future Research Direction:** R-HORIZON lays a strong foundation for future advances in:
 - **Complex Reasoning Data Synthesis:** Encouraging the development of more sophisticated methods for generating diverse, multi-modal, and truly long-horizon datasets.
 - **Model Architectures:** Inspiring new LRM architectures designed to better manage context, allocate attention, and perform long-range reflection efficiently.
 - **Advanced RL Techniques:** Driving innovation in RL algorithms tailored for sequential decision-making and reward attribution across extended reasoning chains.

In conclusion, R-HORIZON not only highlights critical limitations in current LRMs but also offers a pragmatic and effective framework for advancing their long-horizon reasoning capabilities. This work is a crucial step toward building more robust and human-like intelligent systems capable of tackling the inherent complexity of the real world.
