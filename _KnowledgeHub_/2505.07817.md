# Pixel Motion as Universal Representation for Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> LangToMo presents a dual-system framework for language-conditioned robot control, leveraging dense pixel motion as an embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation. The method learns from unlabelled video-caption data, achieving 71.3% success in real-world tasks and 57.7% in simulated environments, outperforming several existing approaches.

## Problem

- Developing robotic systems capable of understanding and executing open-ended natural language instructions across diverse environments and robot embodiments.
- The high cost and scarcity of collecting expert demonstrations with precise action labels, limiting scalable robot learning.
- The absence of a truly universal representation for robot actions that is agnostic to specific robot hardware, camera viewpoints, and task contexts.

## Method

- ==Developed LangToMo==, a dual-system vision-language-action framework using pixel motion forecasts as universal intermediate representations.
- System 2, a conditional diffusion model, is trained self-supervisedly on large video-caption datasets to generate text-conditioned pixel motion sequences from a single observation frame.
- System 1 maps the generated pixel motion into executable robot action vectors, either through a lightweight learned vision transformer or hand-crafted geometric methods, operating at a higher temporal frequency.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **71.3%** on real-world xArm manipulation tasks when finetuned with robot and human demonstrations, surpassing LLaRA (**65.0%**) and AVDC (**0%**).
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot transfer on xArm with a **33.8%** average success rate, outperforming baselines like GPT-4o (**18.8%**).
- Attained **57.7%** overall success on 11 MetaWorld simulated tasks using a learned System 1, outperforming strong baselines including Diffusion Policy (**24.1%**) and AVDC (**43.1%**) by **14.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense pixel motion (optical flow) serves as an effective, universal, and interpretable representation for robot actions, facilitating knowledge transfer across different manipulators and human-to-robot learning.
> - A hierarchical dual-system architecture efficiently decouples high-level motion planning from low-level action execution, allowing sparse invocation of expensive generative models and dense, real-time control.
> - Conditional diffusion models are crucial for accurately modeling the multi-modal output space when mapping abstract language instructions to concrete future motion sequences.
