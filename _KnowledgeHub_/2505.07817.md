# Pixel Motion as Universal Representation for Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> LangToMo presents a dual-system framework for language-conditioned robot control, leveraging dense pixel motion as an embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation. The method learns from unlabelled video-caption data, achieving 71.3% success in real-world tasks and 57.7% in simulated environments, outperforming several existing approaches.

## Problem

- Developing robotic systems capable of understanding and executing open-ended natural language instructions across diverse environments and robot embodiments.
- The high cost and scarcity of collecting expert demonstrations with precise action labels, limiting scalable robot learning.
- The absence of a truly universal representation for robot actions that is agnostic to specific robot hardware, camera viewpoints, and task contexts.

## Method

- ==Developed LangToMo==, a dual-system vision-language-action framework using pixel motion forecasts as universal intermediate representations.
- System 2, a conditional diffusion model, is trained self-supervisedly on large video-caption datasets to generate text-conditioned pixel motion sequences from a single observation frame.
- System 1 maps the generated pixel motion into executable robot action vectors, either through a lightweight learned vision transformer or hand-crafted geometric methods, operating at a higher temporal frequency.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **71.3%** on real-world xArm manipulation tasks when finetuned with robot and human demonstrations, surpassing LLaRA (**65.0%**) and AVDC (**0%**).
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot transfer on xArm with a **33.8%** average success rate, outperforming baselines like GPT-4o (**18.8%**).
- Attained **57.7%** overall success on 11 MetaWorld simulated tasks using a learned System 1, outperforming strong baselines including Diffusion Policy (**24.1%**) and AVDC (**43.1%**) by **14.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense pixel motion (optical flow) serves as an effective, universal, and interpretable representation for robot actions, facilitating knowledge transfer across different manipulators and human-to-robot learning.
> - A hierarchical dual-system architecture efficiently decouples high-level motion planning from low-level action execution, allowing sparse invocation of expensive generative models and dense, real-time control.
> - Conditional diffusion models are crucial for accurately modeling the multi-modal output space when mapping abstract language instructions to concrete future motion sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Pixel Motion as Universal Representation for Robot Control" addresses a critical challenge in modern robotics: enabling robots to understand and execute open-ended natural language instructions across diverse environments and embodiments. This work sits at the confluence of several active research areas:

- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Robotics:** A major trend is to leverage the vast knowledge embedded in large-scale vision-language models, often trained on internet-scale data, to improve robotic capabilities. Approaches like RT-2, PaLM-E, and Octo demonstrate how VLMs can translate high-level language commands into low-level robot actions. LangToMo contributes to this by proposing a novel intermediate representation (pixel motion) that facilitates this translation, particularly for learning from action-agnostic video data.
- **Universal Representations for Robot Control:** A key requirement for generalist robots is representations that are agnostic to specific robot embodiments, viewpoints, and tasks. Prior work has explored various universal representations, such as point tracks (e.g., Track2Act, Im2Flow2Act, ATM) or abstract latent spaces. LangToMo specifically advocates for **dense pixel motion** as a universal, interpretable, and motion-centric representation. This contrasts with methods that predict full RGB images (e.g., AVDC, UniPi) or sparse keypoint trajectories, arguing that dense pixel motion captures both manipulator and object movements more efficiently and without explicit segmentation or point track heuristics.
- **Learning from Videos without Action Labels:** A significant bottleneck in robot learning is the high cost of collecting expert demonstrations with precise action labels. This paper aligns with research efforts to leverage readily available video-caption data (e.g., YouTube videos) where action labels are absent. By extracting pixel motion in a weakly-supervised manner using off-the-shelf optical flow algorithms (like RAFT), LangToMo enables scalable training on such datasets, bypassing the need for explicit pixel-level or action trajectory annotations.
- **Hierarchical and Dual-System Control:** Inspired by dual-process theories of cognition, recent robotics frameworks emphasize hierarchical control, decoupling high-level planning from low-level execution. LangToMo's dual-system architecture (System 2 for high-level motion generation, System 1 for low-level action mapping) fits this paradigm, offering benefits in flexibility, scalability, and independent training of components. This echoes works like RT-H and π0 that propose similar hierarchical structures.
- **Diffusion Models in Robotics:** Diffusion models have proven highly effective in generating diverse and high-quality data. LangToMo leverages conditional diffusion models for generating pixel motion sequences, addressing the multi-modal nature of mapping language to future motion (i.e., multiple valid motions for a given instruction). This positions the work within the growing trend of using generative models for robot planning and control.

LangToMo differentiates itself by focusing on dense, 2D-structured pixel motion as the universal representation, arguing it offers better generalizability and data efficiency compared to RGB-based predictions or sparse point-track approaches, especially when learning from diverse, untagged video-caption data. The unique dual-system structure further enhances its capabilities for flexible and scalable robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the LangToMo framework are threefold:

1. **Develop Universal Robot Control Representations:** A core motivation is to find a representation for robot actions that is truly *universal*, meaning it is agnostic to the specific robot embodiment, camera viewpoint, and task context. The authors propose dense pixel motion as such a representation, arguing that it inherently captures motion patterns efficiently and is interpretable, making it suitable for guiding diverse robotic systems.
2. **Enable Scalable Learning from Unlabeled Video Data:** Current robot learning methods often rely on expensive expert demonstrations with action labels. The authors aim to bridge the gap between open-ended natural language instructions and robot actions by leveraging large, readily available video-caption datasets that lack explicit action labels. Their goal is to develop a framework that can learn language-conditioned motion representations through weak supervision, without requiring pixel-level or action trajectory annotations.
3. **Create a Flexible and Generalizable Robot Control Framework:** The authors seek to design a system that is robust, efficient, and can operate in both unsupervised (zero-shot) and supervised settings with minimal embodiment-specific training. The proposed dual-system architecture is motivated by this goal, allowing for a high-level, generalizable motion planner (System 2) and a low-level, embodiment-aware action executor (System 1) to operate at different temporal frequencies for optimized performance.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to move towards more generalist, language-conditioned robots that can learn from the vast amount of human video data available, without being tied to specific robot hardware or requiring burdensome human annotation efforts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LangToMo is structured as a **dual-system vision-language-action (VLA) framework** that utilizes pixel motion forecasts as its intermediate representations. This hierarchical architecture separates the high-level task of motion generation from the low-level task of action execution.

#### 3.1. System 2: Pixel Motion Forecast (High-Level Controller)

- **Objective:** To generate text-conditioned pixel motion sequences from a single observation frame. This acts as a high-level policy, operating at sparse temporal intervals (`k`).
- **Representation:** Pixel motion (optical flow) between frames is chosen as the universal representation. It is dense (capturing both manipulator and object movements), 2D-structured, and embodiment-agnostic. The motion `y_i,i+k` represents the pixel displacement from frame `x_i` to `x_i+k`.
- **Model:** A **conditional image diffusion model** based on a 2D U-Net architecture. Diffusion models are well-suited for modeling the multi-modal output space of language-to-motion mappings.
- **Inputs to Diffusion Model:**
 - Current RGB image (`x_i`).
 - Previous pixel motion (`y_i-k,i`), providing temporal context and relative motion information (represented as motion from `x_i` to `x_i-k`).
 - Language instruction (`c`), encoded into a fixed-size vector using a pre-trained Universal Sentence Encoder (USE) model.
- **Output of Diffusion Model:** Predicted pixel motion (`y_i,i+k`).
- **Training:**
 - **Self-Supervised Learning:** The diffusion model is trained on video-caption data without action labels. Ground-truth pixel motion `y_i,i+k` is computed using the RAFT algorithm (Teed & Deng, 2020) from pairs of video frames `x_i` and `x_i+k`.
 - **Architecture Modification:** The U-Net's input and output heads are modified to handle 7-channel inputs (3 for RGB, 2 for previous flow, 2 for noisy target flow) and 2-channel outputs (predicted flow).
 - **Conditioning:** The image and previous pixel motion conditions are concatenated to the input channels and are *not* subjected to the noise corruption schedule during training or inference. The textual embedding is provided via cross-attention.
 - **Data Filtering:** Frames with zero optical flow are filtered, and zero motion is introduced at video ends to signify task completion.
- **Inference:** Pixel motion is forecasted using a 25-step DDIM scheduler. For sequential generation, the model uses the current observation `x_i`, language `c`, and the previously calculated motion as "previous motion" for the subsequent step.

#### 3.2. System 1: Pixel Motion to Action Mapping (Low-Level Controller)

- **Objective:** To translate the generated pixel motion representations from System 2 into executable robot action vectors. This acts as a low-level, embodiment-aware controller, operating at dense temporal intervals (`j` within `k`).
- **Inputs:** Predicted pixel motion (`y_i,i+k`), initial state image (`x_i`), and current state image (`x_i+j`).
- **Variants:**
 - **Learned Mapping (LTM-S):** A lightweight vision transformer (ViT-T) is used as `F`. It takes the channel-concatenated inputs (`y_i,i+k`, `x_i`, `x_i+j`) and predicts action vectors. This network is trained using a limited amount of embodiment-specific expert demonstration data with ground-truth action trajectories.
 - **Hand-Crafted Mapping (LTM-H):** Leveraging the interpretable nature of pixel motion, `F` can be designed manually.
 - For simulated environments (MetaWorld): Follows Ko et al. (2023) using ground-truth segmentation and depth maps to convert pixel motions into 3D robot control directions.
 - For real-world environments (xArm Table Top): Assumes a single plane, maps predicted pixel motions for robot end-effector center points onto the plane using visual geometry (after camera calibration), and converts positions to action vectors (following Li et al., 2024).
- **Hierarchical Inference:** System 2 is invoked sparsely (e.g., every `k` frames) to generate a sequence of motions. System 1 then executes these motions at a higher frequency (e.g., every `j` frames, where `j < k`) until the next System 2 invocation or task completion. This balances computational cost with dense control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LangToMo was evaluated across a range of simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating strong performance and validating its core design principles.

#### 4.1. Real-World Environment (xArm Table Top)

- **Tasks:** Four styles of object manipulation tasks requiring visual grounding, semantic understanding, and distractor robustness.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** LTM-H (hand-crafted System 1) achieved an average success rate of **33.8%**, significantly outperforming baselines like RT-2 Style (0%), LLaRA (22.5%), AVDC (0%), and GPT-4o (18.8%). This highlights the effectiveness of large-scale pretraining on OpenX for generalizable motion generation.
- **Finetuning with Demonstrations:**
 - LTM-H benefited significantly from finetuning on robot (RD) and human (HD) demonstrations. With RD+HD, it reached **71.3% average success**, outperforming LLaRA (65.0%) and all AVDC variants.
 - Crucially, LangToMo successfully leveraged **human demonstrations (HD)**, with LTM-H (HD) achieving 36.3% and LTM-H (RD+HD) outperforming LTM-H (RD) by 2.5%. In contrast, AVDC, which predicts RGB images, failed completely when trained on mixed RD+HD data (0.0%). This supports the claim that pixel motion is a more embodiment-agnostic representation than RGB, as the kinematic patterns are more similar between human and robot motions than their visual appearances (quantified by lower KL divergence for pixel motion distributions).

#### 4.2. MetaWorld Simulated Environment (Sawyer Robot Arm)

- **Tasks:** 11 diverse manipulation tasks (e.g., door open/close, button press, faucet open) challenging 3D motions, contact-rich manipulation, and semantic understanding.
- **Performance:**
 - LTM-S (learned System 1) achieved an overall success rate of **57.7%**, and LTM-H (hand-crafted System 1) achieved **52.1%**.
 - Both variants outperformed strong baselines including Diffusion Policy (24.1%), UniPi (6.1%), Im2Flow2Act (2.0%), and ATM (52.0%).
 - Comparing to AVDC, LangToMo showed clear improvements: LTM-S surpassed AVDC (Default, 43.1%) by **+14.6%**. Even AVDC (PT), pretrained on the same OpenX subset as LangToMo, showed no benefit, further underscoring the advantage of pixel motion as a robust representation that bridges domain gaps more effectively than RGB-based predictions.

#### 4.3. CALVIN and iThor Evaluations (Appendix)

- **CALVIN (Long-Horizon Tasks):** LTM-S demonstrated competitive performance on long-horizon, zero-shot tasks, outperforming several recent works, especially in data-limited settings (10% ABC split) where it surpassed VPP and GR-1.
- **iThor (Ego-Motion Navigation):** Despite not being explicitly designed for ego-motion tasks, LTM-H showed slight improvements over AVDC (31.9% vs. 31.3%), indicating potential for generalization to navigation tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (MetaWorld)

- **System 2 Input Conditioning:** Removing visual, language, or previous flow inputs significantly degraded performance (e.g., removing language dropped success from 57.7% to 39.7%), confirming the critical role of each conditioning signal.
- **Diffusion vs. Autoencoder:** Replacing the diffusion model with a simpler autoencoder for direct regression led to a sharp drop (16.2%), validating the necessity of diffusion models for handling the multi-modal nature of motion prediction.
- **Hierarchical Design:** Running System 1 and System 2 at the same frequency (i.e., generating multiple actions per System 2 motion at once) reduced success rates (48.7% vs. 57.7%), justifying the hierarchical design with dense low-level control.
- **Role of System 2:** Bypassing intermediate motion representations and training a System 1 to directly map visual input to actions performed poorly (3.2%), emphasizing the indispensable role of the System 2 motion generation module.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LangToMo represents a significant advancement in the field of language-conditioned robot control, with several key points of significance and potential impact:

- **Universal and Interpretable Representation:** The core contribution is the establishment of dense pixel motion as a highly effective universal representation for robot actions. Its embodiment-agnostic nature allows for learning from diverse human and robot videos, facilitating knowledge transfer across different manipulators. The interpretability of pixel motion also offers insights into the robot's intended actions, which is valuable for debugging and trust.
- **Scalable Learning from Unlabeled Data:** By leveraging self-supervised training on large video-caption datasets, LangToMo significantly reduces the reliance on costly, action-labeled expert demonstrations. This unlocks the potential of vast amounts of freely available online video content for robot learning, accelerating the development of generalist robotic agents.
- **Flexible and Efficient Dual-System Architecture:** The hierarchical decoupling into a high-level motion generator (System 2) and a low-level action executor (System 1) offers substantial benefits. It enables independent training of components, allowing the expensive generative model to operate sparsely while the lightweight, deterministic controller provides dense, real-time control. This design contributes to both efficiency and robustness in real-world deployments.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Transferability:** The empirical results, particularly in zero-shot transfer and finetuning with diverse demonstrations (human and robot), highlight LangToMo's strong generalizability across different tasks and environments. The ability to learn from human demonstrations using an embodiment-agnostic representation is particularly impactful for rapidly deploying robots in new scenarios.
- **Bridge between Language, Vision, and Action:** LangToMo provides a robust and elegant solution for connecting abstract language commands with concrete robot actions, utilizing the visual domain's inherent motion cues. This framework serves as a promising blueprint for future research in embodied AI.

**Limitations identified by the authors:**

- **Cost of System 1 Mappings:** While System 2 is general, System 1 still requires embodiment-specific (hand-crafted or learned with limited supervision) action mappings, which can be costly for each new robot.
- **2D Motion Representation:** The current framework models motion using 2D pixel displacements, lacking explicit depth cues. Extending to 3D motion representations is a future direction for more complex manipulation tasks.
- **Computational Expense:** Diffusion models, despite sparse invocation for System 2, remain computationally expensive during inference, potentially limiting their use in resource-constrained or real-time sensitive applications.
- **Ego Motion:** The current training setup is limited to fixed-camera videos (no ego motion). Expanding System 2 training to include videos with ego motion is necessary to scale to a wider variety of real-world scenarios.

Despite these limitations, LangToMo lays a strong foundation for future research in generalist robot learning, emphasizing the power of motion-centric representations and hierarchical control.
