# Planning without Search: Refining Frontier LLMs with Offline Goal-Conditioned RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Planning with a Natural Language Critic (PNLC) enables large language model agents to perform complex, multi-turn strategic planning by training a lightweight, external goal-conditioned critic using offline reinforcement learning. This method efficiently guides LLM decision-making at inference time, achieving superior performance on tasks such as web shopping, social deduction, and persuasion, while significantly reducing computational costs compared to search-based approaches.

## Problem

- Frontier Large Language Models (LLMs) often lack robust long-horizon reasoning and strategic planning capabilities required for complex, multi-turn interactive tasks like negotiation or social games.
- Scaling multi-turn Reinforcement Learning (RL) to fine-tune LLM agents is computationally prohibitive, and most advanced LLMs are only accessible via inference APIs, preventing direct weight modification.
- Existing LLM planning methods, such as sophisticated prompting or costly inference-time search algorithms (e.g., MCTS), are either insufficient for data-driven strategic planning or too expensive for practical applications.

## Method

- Introducing Planning with a ==Natural Language Critic== (**PNLC**), a two-phase framework that bypasses direct LLM fine-tuning and inference-time search.
- Training a lightweight, external goal-conditioned value function (critic) offline from existing interaction data, predicting the likelihood of reaching specified future goals.
- Guiding LLM agent decision-making at inference time by generating hypothetical goals and using the critic to provide natural language feedback, enabling iterative self-refinement of the LLM's high-level 'thoughts'.

## Results

- PNLC achieved superior performance across diverse multi-turn interactive tasks, including a **48.0%** success rate on WebShop, a **47.0%** win rate on AvalonBench, and an average donation of $**0.87** in a persuasion task.
- The method demonstrated significant efficiency, reducing decision-making time by approximately **90%** compared to search-based baselines (e.g., 5 seconds for PNLC vs. 46 seconds for Agent Q on WebShop).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both goal-conditioned values and the inference-time self-refinement loop are critical, with their removal causing substantial performance degradation, underscoring their importance for effective LLM planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rich, natural language feedback derived from goal-conditioned values is essential for effective LLM reasoning and planning in complex environments, surpassing simple scalar rewards.
> - A lightweight, data-driven auxiliary critic, trained offline, can efficiently imbue frontier LLMs with advanced planning capabilities without the need for costly direct model fine-tuning or extensive online interaction.
> - The ability for LLM agents to iteratively self-refine their 'thoughts' based on the critic's evaluations of potential positive and negative outcomes is critical for robust strategic decision-making.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) have led to their impressive performance across a wide array of natural language understanding and generation tasks, from question answering to creative writing. However, their application to complex, multi-turn interactive tasks—such as negotiation, persuasion, tool use, and strategic games—still presents significant challenges. These tasks demand long-horizon reasoning, strategic planning, and the ability to adapt to dynamic environments and interlocutors, capabilities that go beyond simple text generation or single-turn responses.

Historically, reinforcement learning (RL) has been seen as a promising paradigm for equipping agents with such planning capabilities. RL fine-tuning methods, like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), have been successfully applied to align LLMs with human preferences and improve their dialogue abilities. However, scaling *multi-turn RL* to train LLM *agents* for complex interactions is fraught with difficulties. These include prohibitive computational costs, extensive memory requirements, and the fundamental challenge that many frontier LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3) are only accessible via inference APIs, preventing direct fine-tuning of their weights.

Due to these scalability limitations, the current state-of-the-art for enhancing LLM agents' reasoning and planning in interactive settings primarily relies on sophisticated prompting mechanisms during inference. Approaches like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), ReAct (Reasoning and Acting), and Reflexion enable LLMs to generate reasoning steps, take actions, and even self-reflect on past experiences. More advanced methods leverage inference-time search algorithms, such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), as seen in LATS, Strategist, and Agent Q, to explore potential future trajectories and guide decision-making. While effective, these search-based methods incur substantial inference costs, often requiring numerous LLM calls for simulations, making them impractical for time-sensitive or budget-constrained applications. Moreover, they rely on the LLM's inherent ability to simulate and evaluate futures, which may not always be data-driven or well-calibrated.

This paper, "Planning without Search: Refining Frontier LLMs with Offline Goal-Conditioned RL," directly addresses this critical gap. It proposes a novel approach that bridges the power of RL-driven planning with the scalability and accessibility of API-based frontier LLMs. By eschewing direct LLM fine-tuning and costly inference-time search, the authors introduce a method that is both efficient and effective. The work leverages advancements in *offline RL*, a subfield where Sergey Levine has made significant contributions, which focuses on learning policies or value functions from pre-collected, static datasets without requiring real-time interaction with the environment. This aspect is key to overcoming the sample efficiency and online training challenges of traditional RL.

In essence, this research carve out a new niche by proposing a lightweight, auxiliary *critic* trained offline that can enhance the reasoning capabilities of *any* LLM agent, including API-based frontier models, without modifying their internal weights or incurring high inference costs for search. This positions the work as a significant step forward in making advanced, RL-informed planning capabilities broadly accessible for LLM-powered agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable frontier Large Language Models (LLMs) to effectively engage in complex, multi-turn interactive tasks that demand sophisticated long-horizon reasoning and strategic planning. These tasks go beyond simple question-answering or text generation, requiring agents to anticipate future outcomes, adapt to dynamic environments, and make decisions that may not yield immediate rewards but contribute to long-term success.

The authors are motivated by several critical challenges inherent in developing such advanced LLM agents:

1. **Limitations of Current LLMs in Complex Interactions:** While LLMs excel at many reasoning and problem-solving tasks, they often struggle with the strategic depth required for intricate multi-turn interactions like goal-oriented dialogue, social games, and negotiation. These scenarios necessitate anticipatory behavior and adaptive strategies, which LLMs do not inherently possess without further guidance.
2. **Scalability Barriers of Reinforcement Learning (RL) Fine-tuning:** Reinforcement learning, in theory, offers a powerful framework for optimizing agent behavior in interactive settings. However, applying multi-turn RL fine-tuning directly to LLMs is computationally prohibitive. It demands vast computational resources, high memory, and extensive data samples, making it impractical for the largest "frontier" models (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3). Furthermore, these state-of-the-art models often only expose inference APIs, precluding direct modification of their weights through traditional RL fine-tuning.
3. **Inefficiencies of Existing LLM Reasoning and Planning Methods:** Current approaches to enhance LLM reasoning, such as sophisticated prompting (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, ReAct) or inference-time search (e.g., MCTS-based methods like LATS, Agent Q, Strategist), have their own limitations. While prompting can elicit better reasoning, it often falls short in truly data-driven, long-term strategic decision-making. Inference-time search methods, though powerful, incur significant computational overhead due to repeated LLM calls for simulating trajectories, making them too slow and expensive for many real-world applications. These methods also rely heavily on the base LLM's "intuition" for evaluation, which may not always be calibrated or accurate.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes "Planning with a Natural Language Critic" (PNLC), a novel approach designed to provide a scalable, efficient, and effective solution. The core motivation is to allow LLM agents to leverage the benefits of RL-informed planning without the drawbacks of direct LLM fine-tuning or costly inference-time search, thereby making advanced planning capabilities accessible for even the largest API-based models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Planning with a Natural Language Critic (PNLC)**, is a two-phase approach that integrates offline reinforcement learning with LLM agents to enhance their planning and reasoning capabilities in multi-turn interactive tasks. The central idea is to train a lightweight, external "natural language critic" (a goal-conditioned value function) from offline data, which then guides the LLM agent's decision-making at inference time without directly fine-tuning the LLM or performing expensive online search.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and RL Formalism

The work frames interactive tasks within a Markov Decision Process (MDP) or Partially Observable MDP (POMDP) where the state comprises the history of interaction. Following the ReAct agent framework, an agent's action is composite, consisting of a high-level **thought** (`a_tht`) (a reasoning step or strategy) followed by an **environment action** (`a_env`) (e.g., an utterance). PNLC focuses on learning to plan over these high-level "thoughts" to abstract away the complexity of low-level linguistic actions. The goal is to find a policy that maximizes expected discounted return, traditionally estimated via Q-functions.

#### 3.2. Offline Training a Goal-Conditioned Value Function

The first phase involves training a goal-conditioned value function, which serves as the natural language critic.

- **Data Requirement:** An offline dataset of task-specific trajectories (D) generated by any prior agent (even suboptimal ones). These trajectories consist of sequences of states, thoughts, and outcomes.
- **Goal-Conditioned Q-function:** The objective is to learn `Q(s, a_tht, g)`, which predicts the *likelihood of reaching a specified goal state `g`* after taking thought `a_tht` in state `s`. This differs from standard Q-functions that predict scalar rewards; here, `r(s, g)` is a binary indicator of whether state `s` *is* goal `g`.
- **Efficiency Simplifications:** To make the training process computationally efficient and scalable:
 1. **Summarizing Trajectories:** Full histories of interaction are summarized into compact, natural language descriptions using a cheaper LLM (e.g., GPT-3). These summaries are designed to capture all relevant information for decision-making.
 2. **Embedding Language Descriptions:** Instead of learning directly over natural language, these summaries and thoughts are converted into low-dimensional numerical embeddings using an LLM's embedding API (e.g., GPT-3 embeddings). This allows the learned value function to operate on a concise numerical representation.
 3. **Lightweight Architecture:** The `Q`-function itself is a small Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with two hidden layers, not a full Transformer. This makes it significantly faster and cheaper to train and evaluate compared to an LLM.
- **Learning Algorithm:** The Q-function and a corresponding state-value function `V(s, g)` are trained using a modification of the Implicit Q-learning (IQL) algorithm, adapted to condition on goals. The IQL loss functions are used to estimate `Q` and `V` values.

#### 3.3. Planning using a Natural Language Critic at Inference Time

The second phase describes how the trained goal-conditioned value function is used to guide an LLM agent's decision-making during interaction.

- **Initial Thought Generation:** The LLM agent (e.g., GPT-4) first proposes an initial high-level `thought` (`a_tht`) based on the current `state` (`s`).
- **Hypothetical Goal Generation:** A separate LLM is prompted to generate `n` diverse hypothetical future `goals` (`g_1..., g_n`), encompassing both *positive* and *negative* potential outcomes, given the current state and the agent's proposed thought. These goals describe how the situation might unfold.
- **Critic Evaluation:** Each of these hypothetical goals (`g_i`) is then passed, along with the current state and proposed thought (in their embedded forms), to the pre-trained, lightweight `Q`-function. The `Q`-function outputs a scalar likelihood `Q(s, a_tht, g_i)` for each goal.
- **Natural Language Value Creation:** The generated goals and their corresponding likelihoods are synthesized into a "natural language value." This value provides rich, interpretable feedback (e.g., "40% probability user feels assured, but 30% user distrusts"). This is significantly more informative than a single scalar reward.
- **Self-Refinement:** The LLM agent receives this natural language value as feedback. It is then optionally allowed to *refine* its original proposed thought, based on the insights provided by the critic regarding potential positive and negative outcomes. This iterative refinement process replaces the need for costly inference-time search. The authors find that `m=2` iterations (one additional round of refinement) are typically sufficient.
- **Action Generation:** After refinement, the final thought guides the generation of the actual environment action (e.g., the specific utterance in a dialogue).

**Key Innovations:** PNLC introduces several critical innovations:
1. **Goal-Conditioned Values:** Learning outcome probabilities for various future states rather than just scalar rewards, enabling richer feedback.
2. **High-Level Planning:** Operating and refining "thoughts" (strategies) instead of low-level "utterances," simplifying the planning space.
3. **Efficient Inference:** Eliminating the need for inference-time search by using a pre-trained critic and a self-refinement loop guided by natural language values, making it scalable to frontier LLMs via API calls.
4. **Positive and Negative Outcomes:** Explicitly considering both desirable and undesirable future occurrences in the critic's feedback for more balanced and effective refinement.

This modular design, separating the powerful LLM's generation and reasoning from a lightweight, data-driven RL critic, allows PNLC to leverage the best of both worlds: the broad capabilities of frontier LLMs and the planning prowess of reinforcement learning, all while maintaining efficiency and scalability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that PNLC consistently achieves superior performance across a diverse suite of multi-turn interactive LLM agent benchmarks, while maintaining significantly higher efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance Across Diverse Tasks

PNLC was evaluated on three distinct tasks:
- **Web Shopping (WebShop):** An environment requiring tool use and navigation to purchase products based on user specifications. PNLC achieved a score of 78.2 and a success rate (SR) of 48.0%, outperforming all baselines, including specialized methods like Agent Q (77.1 score, 48.0 SR for n=30, but much slower) and general search-based methods like LATS (74.9 score, 44.0 SR for n=30).
- **Social Deduction (AvalonBench):** A game where an LLM agent (playing Merlin) must guide good players while concealing its identity from evil players through dialogue. PNLC achieved a win rate of 47.0%, significantly surpassing the task-specific Strategist (42.0% for n=30, much slower) and general LLM reasoning methods.
- **Persuasion:** A goal-oriented dialogue task where an LLM agent must persuade a participant to donate to a charity. PNLC achieved the highest average donation amount of $0.87, outperforming GDP-Zero ($0.74 for n=30, much slower) and other dialogue-focused approaches.

#### 4.2. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability

A critical finding is that PNLC achieves its superior performance with significantly lower computational costs and inference budgets than search-based baselines:
- For example, on WebShop, PNLC's decision-making time was approximately 5 seconds, compared to 46 seconds for Agent Q (n=30).
- On Avalon, PNLC took around 6 seconds, versus 62 seconds for Strategist (n=30).
This demonstrates that PNLC effectively replaces the need for extensive inference-time search with a lightweight, pre-trained critic, making it practical for real-world applications and scalable to the largest frontier LLMs (like GPT-4), which are accessed via APIs.

#### 4.3. Comparison with RL Fine-tuning and LLM Reasoning Methods

- **RL Fine-tuning (e.g., ArCHer):** Surprisingly, multi-turn RL fine-tuning methods, even when trained on the same data, performed poorly (e.g., 19.0% win rate for ArCHer on Avalon, 0.36 avg. donation for persuasion). The authors attribute this to these methods relying on weaker LLMs (e.g., GPT-2) as the base agent and the inherent difficulty of effectively training end-to-end value-based RL for LLMs.
- **LLM Reasoning (e.g., ReAct, Reflexion, LATS, Self-Plan):** While more sophisticated than basic prompting, these methods were generally outperformed by PNLC. Simple prompting (ReAct) yielded low scores. Reflexion showed some improvement but was still significantly behind PNLC. Search-based methods (LATS) performed better, but their "fast" versions (n=5 simulations) were still noticeably worse than PNLC, highlighting the trade-off between performance and inference cost.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Insights

The ablation study confirmed the critical importance of PNLC's core components:
- **PNLC (w/o goals):** Removing goal-conditioning (i.e., learning a scalar Q-value predicting overall success) caused performance to drop significantly, matching simple prompting methods (e.g., 25.0% win rate on Avalon vs. 47.0% for full PNLC). This underscores that the rich, natural language feedback from *goal-conditioned values* is essential for effective LLM reasoning.
- **PNLC (w/o refinement):** Replacing the inference-time self-refinement loop with a traditional "best-of-n" selection also led to a substantial performance drop (e.g., 28.0% win rate on Avalon vs. 47.0% for full PNLC). This validates the hypothesis that allowing the LLM agent to actively refine its thoughts based on the critic's feedback (especially considering potential negative outcomes) is crucial.
- **ReAct+Replan:** An ablation where a frontier LLM directly infers goal scores without offline training. While better than basic ReAct, it performed noticeably worse than full PNLC (e.g., 22.0% win rate on Avalon vs. 47.0% for full PNLC). This indicates that even powerful LLMs cannot reliably provide calibrated and accurate scores for long-horizon goals purely from their inherent "intuition," making the data-driven offline training of the critic indispensable. Qualitatively, these LLMs often provided uncalibrated, overly optimistic scores, leading to less effective self-correction.

In summary, the main findings strongly support PNLC as a highly effective, efficient, and scalable method for endowing LLM agents with sophisticated planning capabilities in complex interactive environments, consistently outperforming both direct RL fine-tuning and current state-of-the-art prompting and search-based LLM reasoning techniques.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Planning without Search: Refining Frontier LLMs with Offline Goal-Conditioned RL" carries significant implications for the development of advanced AI agents, particularly those powered by Large Language Models (LLMs). Its potential impact spans both theoretical advancements and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Bridging the Scalability Gap for RL-enhanced LLMs

Perhaps the most significant contribution is demonstrating a viable pathway to imbue frontier LLM agents with long-term planning and reasoning capabilities through reinforcement learning, without incurring the prohibitive computational and memory costs typically associated with direct LLM fine-tuning or extensive inference-time search. By training a lightweight, auxiliary goal-conditioned value function *offline* and leveraging it as a natural language critic during inference, PNLC bypasses the limitations of API-based models, making RL's benefits accessible to the most powerful LLMs. This opens up new avenues for integrating sophisticated control and planning with the impressive generative and understanding abilities of large foundation models.

#### 5.2. Enabling More Capable and Adaptive LLM Agents

The consistent outperformance of PNLC across diverse interactive benchmarks (web shopping, social deduction, persuasion) highlights its ability to significantly enhance LLM agents' strategic thinking, adaptiveness, and long-horizon decision-making. Agents can now plan by effectively evaluating potential future outcomes, both positive and negative, leading to more robust and goal-aligned behavior in complex, multi-turn interactions. This improvement is crucial for developing truly intelligent and autonomous agents that can navigate real-world scenarios.

#### 5.3. Efficient and Interpretable Planning Mechanism

PNLC offers a more efficient alternative to search-based planning methods, which typically consume vast inference budgets. By replacing costly simulations with a pre-trained critic that provides summarized natural language values, the method drastically reduces the computational overhead during decision-making. Furthermore, the natural language output of the critic provides a high degree of interpretability, allowing developers and potentially even end-users to understand *why* an agent is considering certain actions or predicting particular outcomes. This transparency is valuable for debugging, trust-building, and ethical considerations.

#### 5.4. A Novel Paradigm for LLM-RL Integration

The modular architecture of PNLC—where a powerful, fixed LLM acts as the agent, complemented by a separate, data-driven RL critic—represents a novel paradigm for combining these two fields. It moves beyond direct fine-tuning of the LLM policy, suggesting that auxiliary, specialized modules trained with RL can effectively guide and refine the reasoning of general-purpose LLMs. This "critic-guided reasoning" approach could inspire future research into hybrid AI systems that leverage the strengths of different AI components.

#### 5.5. Broadening Applications of LLM Agents

The enhanced planning capabilities and efficiency offered by PNLC have the potential to unlock a wider range of applications for LLM agents. These could include:
- **Advanced Dialogue Systems:** More strategic and persuasive chatbots for customer service, education, or therapeutic applications.
- **Complex Game AI:** Developing more human-like and strategic AI opponents or teammates in sophisticated games.
- **Intelligent Assistants:** Agents capable of executing multi-step tasks that involve interaction with dynamic environments or human users, such as complex online tasks or robotic control.
- **Decision Support Systems:** Where LLMs can provide refined strategic recommendations based on learned likelihoods of various outcomes.

#### 5.6. Guiding Future Research Directions

The paper also provides valuable pointers for future research. The need for task-specific value functions suggests exploring generalist critics or methods for efficient transfer learning. The reliance on LLM intuition for generating hypothetical goals points to the importance of understanding and potentially improving the quality and calibration of LLM-generated predictions, especially for sensitive domains. Lastly, a deeper investigation into the optimal quality and diversity of offline data required for effective critic training will be crucial for broader adoption.

In conclusion, PNLC represents a significant leap forward in making sophisticated RL-driven planning practical and scalable for frontier LLM agents. Its innovative approach addresses core challenges in the field, promising more intelligent, efficient, and interpretable AI agents for a myriad of complex interactive tasks.
