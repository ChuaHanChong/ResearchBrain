# Random Policy Valuation is Enough for LLM Reasoning with Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Kuaishou Technology, and StepFun developed ROVER, a minimalist reinforcement learning algorithm for LLM math reasoning, which leverages uniform policy valuation to enhance solution quality and reasoning diversity. It consistently achieved higher pass@1 and pass@k scores across various benchmarks while reducing training instability compared to existing RL methods.

## Problem

- General-purpose RL algorithms (like PPO) are often applied to LLM reasoning tasks, leading to training instability, reliance on complex heuristics, and non-stationary learning targets.
- These existing methods frequently suffer from "diversity collapse" or entropy collapse, where LLMs converge on a few high-reward solutions, limiting their ability to explore multiple valid reasoning paths.
- A critical mismatch exists between the complex, stochastic MDPs for which general RL was designed and the simpler, deterministic, tree-structured MDPs underlying LLM math reasoning with binary terminal rewards.

## Method

- ROVER introduces a theoretical framework proving that optimal actions for LLM math reasoning's specialized MDPs can be derived by evaluating the ==Q-function of a fixed uniformly random policy==.
- It employs a ==softmax policy== over these uniform-policy Q-values to stochastically sample actions, which effectively balances solution quality with desired reasoning diversity.
- For practical LLM training, ROVER uses an ==intrinsic relative Q-function parameterization==, ==mean-centered rewards==, and broadcasts the reward signal to tokens, simplifying the optimization objective and avoiding separate value networks.

## Results

- ROVER consistently outperformed all RL baselines in pass@1 and pass@k metrics across various math benchmarks, achieving an average **+8.2** pass@1 and **+16.8** pass@256 improvement over the strongest baseline on competition-level tasks for Qwen3-8B-Base.
- It significantly preserved policy entropy and demonstrated superior diversity, finding considerably more distinct reasoning strategies per question (e.g., **17** vs. **3** for GRPO on a sample problem).
- The method exhibited stronger generalization capabilities on out-of-distribution tasks and enabled models to generate enhanced reflection behaviors and discover novel reasoning strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - In finite-horizon, deterministic, tree-structured MDPs with binary terminal rewards, the optimal policy can be recovered by merely evaluating the Q-function of a uniformly random policy.
> - Introducing stochasticity via softmax over uniform-policy Q-values can effectively maintain reasoning diversity without a catastrophic loss of optimal performance, enabling a trade-off between quality and exploration.
> - Leveraging the unique structural properties of LLM reasoning MDPs allows for a minimalist RL approach that is more stable, efficient, and performant than complex, general-purpose RL algorithms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving field of **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** for enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities. RLVR, exemplified by frameworks like PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) and its variants (e.g., GRPO), has become a prominent paradigm for post-training LLMs, especially in tasks like mathematical problem-solving where solutions can be programmatically verified (e.g., correct/incorrect answers).

However, the authors identify a critical mismatch: general-purpose RL algorithms like PPO, originally designed for complex, stochastic environments such as computer games and robotics, are often applied directly to LLM reasoning tasks. The paper argues that LLM math reasoning, despite its apparent complexity, exhibits a structurally simpler underlying Markov Decision Process (MDP). Specifically, it's characterized by:
- **Finite-horizon:** Reasoning sequences have a defined end.
- **Deterministic state transitions:** Each action leads to a predictable next state (token concatenation).
- **Tree-structured dynamics:** Reasoning paths form a tree, without cycles or multiple parents for a state.
- **Binary terminal rewards:** The final answer is either correct (1) or incorrect (0).

Current PPO/GPI (Generalized Policy Iteration) based methods suffer from training instability, requiring complex heuristics (clipping, KL regularization, data selection), and lead to **diversity collapse** (entropy collapse), where the model converges on a few high-reward solutions, limiting its ability to explore alternative correct reasoning paths. This work directly challenges the prevailing assumption that sophisticated, general-purpose RL is necessary or even optimal for this specific domain.

The paper aligns with a broader trend in AI research seeking to simplify complex models and algorithms when domain-specific insights allow. It draws parallels with empirical observations in discrete environments regarding the utility of uniform-policy values, but uniquely provides a theoretical foundation tailored for LLM reasoning. By proposing a minimalist approach (ROVER), it pushes back against the increasing complexity of RL pipelines for LLMs, aiming for a more elegant and robust solution. This work contributes to the foundational understanding of RL applied to LLMs, suggesting that specialized algorithms can yield superior results by leveraging domain-specific structural properties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a minimalist yet highly effective RLVR algorithm for LLM math reasoning that simultaneously enhances **solution quality** and **reasoning diversity**, while overcoming the inherent instability and complexity of existing methods.

The motivation stems from a critical observation: the MDP structure underlying LLM math reasoning with verifiable rewards is significantly simpler than the general-purpose control problems for which algorithms like PPO were originally designed. This structural simplification (deterministic, tree-structured, finite-horizon, binary terminal rewards) suggests that many sophisticated techniques in existing RL methods might be unnecessary or even detrimental.

Specifically, the authors pose the guiding question: "Is there a minimalist yet highly effective RLVR algorithm that maintains both quality and diversity under this specialized MDP structure?" They are motivated to:
1. **Simplify RLVR:** Bypass the generalized policy iteration (GPI) loop, which iteratively alternates between policy evaluation and improvement, leading to non-stationary learning targets and instability.
2. **Enhance Diversity:** Address the "diversity collapse" problem common in reward-maximizing RL, where models over-exploit high-reward paths and lose the ability to generate multiple valid solutions, which is crucial for robust reasoning and higher pass@k performance.
3. **Improve Performance:** Achieve superior results in terms of both single-shot accuracy (pass@1) and overall problem-solving potential (pass@k) compared to state-of-the-art, more complex baselines.
4. **Provide Theoretical Grounding:** Offer a theoretical analysis to justify the efficacy of their simplified approach, moving beyond mere empirical observation.

By leveraging the unique characteristics of LLM reasoning MDPs, the authors aim to build a more stable, efficient, and performant RL framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of ROVER's methodology lies in a surprising theoretical insight and its practical translation into an efficient algorithm.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundation: Random Policy Valuation

The authors first formalize LLM math reasoning as a specialized finite-horizon MDP with deterministic transitions, tree-structured state space, and binary terminal rewards. Under this specific structure, they prove a remarkable result (Theorem 3.1):
- **Theorem 3.1:** The optimal action can be recovered by simply evaluating the Q-function of a fixed uniformly random policy (π_u) and then selecting actions greedily based on these Q-values.
This means that instead of iteratively improving a policy and evaluating its changing value, one can evaluate the simplest possible policy (uniform random) *once* and still derive optimal actions. The Q-value of a uniform policy, Q_πu(s, a), is interpreted as the probability of successfully reaching a correct terminal state if one takes action `a` at state `s` and then acts uniformly randomly thereafter. A value of 0 indicates no correct continuation exists from (s,a), while higher values indicate more promising paths.

#### 3.2. Balancing Quality and Diversity: Softmax Selection

While greedy selection from uniform-policy Q-values guarantees optimality (quality), it sacrifices diversity, leading to mode collapse. To address this, ROVER proposes sampling actions stochastically using a softmax over the uniform-policy Q-values:
- π_s(a|s) = exp(Q_πu(s,a)/ρ) / Σ_a' exp(Q_πu(s,a')/ρ), where ρ is a temperature parameter.
- **Theorem 3.2:** This softmax policy maintains a guaranteed level of performance relative to the optimal policy, with the bound tightening as the temperature ρ decreases. This theoretically confirms that diversity can be introduced without a catastrophic loss of quality. The Q_πu(s,a) values effectively serve as a "value map" of the reasoning landscape, allowing the softmax to explore multiple high-value pathways proportional to their estimated success probabilities.

#### 3.3. Practical Implementation: Scaling to LLMs (ROVER Algorithm)

To make this framework practical and scalable for LLMs with vast state/action spaces and long horizons, ROVER introduces several key implementation techniques (Algorithm 1):
- **Q Parameterization:** Instead of training a separate value network, ROVER intrinsically links the Q-function to the LLM's parameters. It defines a *relative Q-function* as Q(s_t, a_t) = ρ * (log π_θ(a_t|s_t) - log π_θ_old(a_t|s_t)). This measures the improvement relative to a fixed behavioral policy (π_θ_old), stabilizing training by centering initial Q-values around zero and learning relative changes.
- **Low-Variance Reward Signal:** To obtain a stable and dense reward signal for learning Q-values, ROVER:
 - Samples `n` responses per prompt (e.g., 8) to reduce variance.
 - Applies **mean-centered rewards**: `~r(x, y_i) = r(x, y_i) - (1/n) Σ r(x, y_i)`. This is inspired by group reward normalization techniques (e.g., GRPO) but without standard deviation normalization.
 - **Broadcasts rewards**: The centered reward `~r` is applied to every token in the generated sequence, ensuring efficient credit assignment even for long reasoning chains.
- **Optimization Objective:** ROVER minimizes a squared error loss between the estimated Q-value (Q(a_t|s_t)) and a Bellman-like target `~r + (1/|V|) Σ_a' Q(a'|s')`, where the target's Q-values are stop-gradient-ed to ensure stability. This directly aims to make the current Q-function predict the (mean) future success probability.

This minimalist approach avoids the complex clipping, KL regularization, and off-policy corrections common in PPO/GRPO, simplifying implementation and tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ROVER demonstrates consistently superior performance and diversity across various LLM sizes and benchmarks, despite its simplified approach.

#### 4.1. Countdown Tasks (Qwen2.5-3B)

- **Superior Test Scores:** ROVER quickly surpasses all baselines (GRPO, GRPO variants with KL/clip-higher) after 400 training steps, achieving the highest ceiling performance.
- **Entropy Preservation:** ROVER's policy entropy decays gracefully and remains significantly higher than baselines, which either collapse or fluctuate erratically. This stable, high entropy encourages sustained exploration.
- **Enhanced Diversity & Quality:** ROVER achieves the highest scores on both quality and diversity metrics, finding more distinct solutions per question compared to baselines. For example, for a sample problem, GRPO found 3 distinct solutions, while ROVER found 17.

#### 4.2. Math Reasoning Tasks (Qwen3-4B-Base, Qwen3-8B-Base, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B)

- **Consistently Higher Pass@1:** ROVER consistently outperforms all RL baselines (PPO, GRPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++) in pass@1 metrics across challenging benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, HMMT25, OlympiadBench, AMC23, MATH500). For Qwen3-8B-Base, it shows an average +8.2 improvement on pass@1 over the strongest baseline on competition-level tasks (AIME, HMMT). On HMMT25, ROVER nearly doubles the performance of REINFORCE++.
- **Significant Pass@k Improvement:** ROVER shows sustained and significant performance gains as `k` increases (from 1 to 256), consistently surpassing all baselines and even the base model at large `k` values. This is a critical indicator of enhanced diversity. For Qwen3-8B-Base, it achieved +16.8 improvement on pass@256 over the best baseline on average for AIME, HMMT tasks. Baselines often saturate or even underperform the base model at higher `k` due to mode collapse.
- **Strong Generalization on O.O.D. Tasks:** ROVER demonstrates stronger generalization capabilities on the challenging math-unrelated GPQA-diamond benchmark, achieving the best performance, indicating that its improved reasoning is not confined to the training distribution.

#### 4.3. Diversity Analysis

- **Highest Diversity Metrics:** Quantified using "number of distinct strategies" (judged by Claude-3.5-Sonnet), ROVER shows relative improvements of +6.8% over GRPO and +17.6% over the average of all baselines. It consistently improves the Pareto front between quality and diversity. Additional metrics like utility and cosine distance also confirm superior diversity.

#### 4.4. Behavioral Analysis

- **Robust Test-Time Scaling (Maj@k):** ROVER's majority voting (maj@k) performance scales robustly, consistently improving upon the base model across all `k` values, demonstrating its ability to maintain a diverse distribution over valid reasoning paths. Baselines suffer from mode collapse, hindering maj@k gains.
- **Enhanced Reflection Behaviors:** ROVER-trained models generate a significantly higher proportion of "forking tokens" associated with rethinking and self-correction (e.g., 'wait', 'however'), indicating active reflection and strategy pivoting.
- **Discovered Novel Strategies:** In qualitative case studies, ROVER was observed to discover additional, novel reasoning strategies compared to base models and GRPO-trained models, demonstrating its potential to push reasoning boundaries (e.g., finding a solution based on the inclusion-exclusion principle versus just "stars and bars").

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Q' Term:** The expected Q-value of the successor state (Q') in the Bellman target is crucial. Removing it (β=0) causes entropy and response length collapse, leading to a sharp drop in pass@k. Overly dominant Q' (β=5.0) diminishes the reward signal. ROVER's performance is robust across a wide range (β=0.2 to 1.0), showing it's not overly sensitive to this exact scaling.
- **Temperature ρ:** Confirmed that ρ = 1 provides a robust balance. Higher ρ (e.g., 3) leads to under-exploitation and slower convergence, while lower ρ (e.g., 0.001) causes premature exploitation, entropy collapse, and instability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ROVER represents a significant step towards simplifying and improving reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning tasks. Its core contributions and potential impacts are multi-faceted:

1. **Fundamental Simplification of RLVR:** By proving that optimal actions can be derived from a fixed uniformly random policy's Q-values in deterministic, tree-structured MDPs with binary terminal rewards, the paper offers a profound theoretical simplification. This bypasses the complex, unstable generalized policy iteration (GPI) loop prevalent in PPO and its derivatives, aligning with the philosophical idea of "simplicity is the ultimate sophistication."
2. **Overcoming RL Limitations in LLMs:** ROVER directly addresses two major shortcomings of current RLVR methods: training instability (due to non-stationary targets and complex heuristics) and diversity collapse (due to reward maximization). By maintaining high entropy and encouraging exploration, it yields policies that are not only more accurate but also more robust and versatile.
3. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** The empirical results are compelling, demonstrating superior performance in both pass@1 (quality) and pass@k (diversity) metrics across a wide array of challenging math and out-of-distribution benchmarks. The ability to discover novel reasoning strategies and exhibit stronger reflection behaviors suggests a deeper, more human-like problem-solving capacity.
4. **Practicality and Scalability:** The minimalist design of ROVER, without relying on separate value networks or intricate heuristic tuning, makes it more practical to implement and scale. Its intrinsic Q-parameterization and low-variance reward signal contribute to stable and efficient training, which is crucial for large LLMs.
5. **Inspiring Future Research:** ROVER's success challenges the conventional wisdom that RL for LLMs must be complex. It opens new avenues for research into tailoring RL algorithms to the specific structural properties of LLM generation and reasoning tasks. This could lead to a re-evaluation of current RL practices in LLMs, potentially inspiring the development of other domain-specific, simplified yet powerful algorithms for various LLM applications.
6. **Broader Impact on LLM Development:** Models trained with ROVER show better generalization and test-time scalability (maj@k), implying that the enhanced diversity leads to more reliable and adaptable LLMs. This could accelerate the development of more capable and robust AI systems, particularly in domains requiring complex, verifiable reasoning.

While the current work is limited to math reasoning tasks and models up to 8B parameters, the authors rightly point out that the underlying principles could be extended to other RLVR applications (e.g., with tool calls, intermediate feedback) and larger models, acknowledging the need to bridge the gap between theoretical ideal and practical approximation further. Overall, ROVER offers a fresh perspective on RLVR for LLMs, demonstrating that a deep understanding of the problem structure can lead to surprisingly simple and effective solutions.
