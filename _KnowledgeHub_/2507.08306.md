# M2-Reasoning: Empowering MLLMs with Unified General and Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Inclusion AI, Ant Group developed M2-Reasoning-7B, a Multimodal Large Language Model capable of unified general and spatial reasoning, which achieved state-of-the-art performance across 8 diverse benchmarks, including an average score of 45.0 on general reasoning and 82.3 on image-based spatial reasoning.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with understanding dynamic spatial interactions, including motion, orientation, and physical interactions.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, logically coherent datasets specifically designed for spatial reasoning, especially dynamic interactions.
- Training MLLMs on diverse tasks like general and specialized spatial reasoning leads to conflicts due to data heterogeneity and varying reward signals.

## Method

- A ==multi-stage data construction pipeline== synthesizes high-quality general reasoning (168K CoT instances) and spatial reasoning data (26.2K RLVR instances, image- and video-based).
- A ==two-stage training framework== starts with cold-start ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== and proceeds to dynamic multi-task ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)==.
- The RLVR stage utilizes a GRPO variant with task-specific reward formulations, including ==Exponential Decay Numeric Matching (EDNM)== for continuous spatial answers, and dynamic hyper-parameter adjustments for stable training.

## Results

- M2-Reasoning-7B achieved state-of-the-art performance across **8** diverse benchmarks, demonstrating advancements in both general and spatial reasoning.
- For general reasoning, it secured an average score of **45.0**, outperforming prior base-scale MLLMs like InternVL3-8B (**41.4**) and WeThink-VL-7B (**44.3**), with specific strengths in MathVista (**75.0**) and DynaMath (**26.8**).
- In spatial reasoning, it scored **82.3** on CV-Bench (image-based) and **42.3** on VSI-Bench (video-based), establishing new SOTA records for inferring Room Size (**55.4**) and Relative Direction (**47.3**) in video-based tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-stage training paradigm of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) effectively enhances reasoning capabilities in MLLMs.
> - Explicitly synthesizing high-quality, logically coherent data, particularly for dynamic spatial interactions, is crucial for improving MLLM's spatial understanding.
> - Adaptive training strategies, including task-specific reward functions like Exponential Decay Numeric Matching (EDNM) and dynamic hyper-parameter adjustments, are necessary to unify and optimize diverse reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has seen transformative advancements driven by Large Language Models (LLMs), which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding, generating, and reasoning with human language. Building on this foundation, **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)** have emerged as a frontier of AI research. MLLMs integrate the sophisticated language faculties of LLMs with visual perception, enabling them to process and reason across diverse modalities, primarily text and images. This allows MLLMs to tackle complex vision-language tasks such as visual question answering, detailed image captioning, and object grounding.

A significant recent breakthrough in enhancing the reasoning abilities of both LLMs and MLLMs has been the adoption of a two-stage training process involving **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** followed by **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. This paradigm, exemplified by leading models like OpenAI's o1/o3 and Deepseek-R1, has proven highly effective in unlocking advanced cognitive capabilities.

However, despite these strides, a critical limitation persists in current MLLMs: their **struggle with dynamic spatial interactions**. While they may excel at general reasoning or static visual analysis, they often fall short in comprehending motion, orientation, and physical interactions within dynamic environments. This gap is particularly significant for real-world applications requiring nuanced understanding of space and movement, such as robotics, autonomous navigation, and advanced human-computer interaction.

"M2-Reasoning: Empowering MLLMs with Unified General and Spatial Reasoning" directly addresses this critical gap. By focusing on explicitly enhancing both general and, crucially, spatial reasoning capabilities, this work contributes to pushing the boundaries of MLLMs beyond static image understanding to more dynamic and interactive scenarios. The paper's approach of combining novel data generation specifically for spatial reasoning with advanced training strategies like task-specific rewards directly addresses the shortcomings of existing models. It aims to bridge the divide between robust language understanding and sophisticated visual-spatial comprehension, thereby moving closer to developing MLLMs capable of more human-like, comprehensive reasoning in complex, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that excels in both general and spatial reasoning capabilities**, specifically addressing the current deficiency of MLLMs in understanding dynamic spatial interactions.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key observations:
- **Advancements in MLLMs are impressive but incomplete:** While MLLMs have shown remarkable progress in various vision-language tasks, a significant "critical gap" remains in their ability to reason about the dynamic interplay of space and motion. This limitation hinders their application in real-world scenarios that inherently involve complex spatial understanding.
- **Need for robust spatial reasoning in practical applications:** Real-world applications, from robotics and autonomous systems to virtual reality and advanced interactive interfaces, demand models that can accurately comprehend and reason about objects' positions, movements, relationships, and sizes in dynamic environments. Current MLLMs often fail to provide this level of nuanced spatial understanding.
- **Leveraging successful training paradigms:** The authors recognize the success of the two-stage training paradigm (SFT + RLVR) in enhancing reasoning abilities in LLMs and aim to adapt and optimize this approach for MLLMs, particularly for spatial reasoning tasks where fine-grained perceptual abilities are crucial.
- **Scarcity of high-quality spatial reasoning data:** A major challenge in developing such models is the lack of high-quality, logically coherent datasets that specifically target spatial reasoning, especially dynamic interactions. This necessitates a robust data generation and curation strategy.
- **Mitigating training conflicts:** Training a single model on diverse tasks, including both general and highly specialized spatial reasoning, can lead to conflicts due to data heterogeneity and varying reward signals. A sophisticated training strategy is needed to harmonize these different learning objectives.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to push MLLMs beyond their current limits by endowing them with a unified and superior capacity for both abstract problem-solving (general reasoning) and detailed, dynamic environmental understanding (spatial reasoning), thereby enabling more meaningful real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The M2-Reasoning-7B model is developed through a synergistic combination of two core innovations: a novel data pipeline and an advanced dynamic multi-task training strategy.

#### 3.1. Data Construction Pipeline

To overcome the scarcity of high-quality reasoning data, the authors establish a multi-stage pipeline for data synthesis and curation, covering both general and spatial reasoning, and catering to both cold-start (SFT) and RLVR stages.

- **General Reasoning Data:**
 - **High-Quality Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Synthesis:** This pipeline generates 168K cold-start instances by leveraging public datasets and an open-source vision-language reasoning model (WeThink-VL-7B) to synthesize reasoning chains. A high-temperature generation strategy ensures diversity. Critical to this stage is a comprehensive quality screening, using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as an automated evaluator. This evaluation assesses not only answer accuracy but also three key dimensions of "Reasoning Quality": Structural Correctness (adaptive granularity), Cognitive Load Management (gradual concept building), and Verification Richness (checking intermediate results). Only high-quality reasoning chains are retained.
 - **Difficulty Scoring for RLVR Data:** For the 100K general reasoning RLVR samples, prompt difficulty is quantified by the accuracy of 10 generated responses from Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Prompts with 0% or 100% accuracy are excluded to ensure meaningful gradient signals, with difficulty defined as `1 - accuracy`.

- **Spatial Reasoning Data:**
 - **Spatial Data Synthesis Pipeline:** This pipeline generates 26.2K RLVR instances (18.7K image-based, 7.5K video-based) based on controlled spatial simulations and real-world images.
 - **Automated Data Annotation:** Preprocessing steps (depth estimation, instance segmentation) on real-world images, alongside built-in annotations for simulated data, derive fundamental modalities (depth maps, 3D bounding boxes) crucial for QA pairs.
 - **Question-Answer Generation:** Ten categories of tasks are designed (e.g., Object-Count, SpatialRelation, RoomSize, AppearanceOrder), each with a corresponding QA generation algorithm leveraging annotations. Question-specific filtering mechanisms remove ambiguous or invalid object-relation pairs (e.g., filtering objects with similar distances for RelativeDistance tasks). Video-based tasks specifically capture dynamic information.
 - **Data Quality Validation:** A hybrid quality control strategy combines model-based assessments (Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct) for difficulty, answer correctness, object completeness, and option discriminability, with subsequent human verification.
 - **Spatial Data Augmentation Pipeline:** Synthesized data undergoes secondary augmentation to enhance diversity:
 - **Question-Type Augmentation:** Multiple-choice questions are transformed into fill-in-the-blank or true/false formats.
 - **Instruction Augmentation:** Prompts are enriched with formatting requirements (e.g., units, response types) to encourage adherence.
 - **Distribution Augmentation:** Random offsets to option letters in both target answer and candidate options mitigate model reliance on specific positions.

The overall data configuration involves a large cold-start dataset (3.3M image-text, 2.9M text-only) for SFT, and a smaller, highly curated RLVR dataset (100K general, 26.2K spatial) for fine-tuning advanced reasoning.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Multi-Task Training Strategy

M2-Reasoning is built upon the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct language model and incorporates a native-resolution vision encoder. Its development follows a two-stage training framework:

- **Stage 1: Cold-start (SFT):** Supervised fine-tuning on the cold-start data activates latent reasoning capabilities and standardizes output format, leveraging high-quality, semantically coherent trajectory data from the data pipeline. This stage produces `M2-Reasoning-CI`.
- **Stage 2: Dynamic Multi-task RLVR:** Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards is applied to data with verifiable answers, using a variant of **GRPO (General Reinforcement Learning Policy Optimization)**.
 - **Reward Formulation:** The reward `R_i` for a completion `o_i` consists of `R_acc_i` (accuracy reward, rule-based and task-type specific) and `R_fmt_i` (format reward, checking for `<think>` and `<answer>` blocks).
 - **General Reasoning Reward:** `R_gen_i` is a binary indicator function (`I`) based on exact matching (option letter, numeric digit, extract string, formal math expression) using Math-Verify and custom algorithms.
 - **Spatial Reasoning Reward (Exponential Decay Numeric Matching - EDNM):** For numerical spatial answers (e.g., size, distance), a continuous reward function `R_EDNM(x) = γ · exp(−λ · |x - x_gt| / (|x_gt| + ϵ))` is used. This provides a smoother gradient signal, crucial for tasks where exact matches are difficult, encouraging gradual optimization. Hyperparameters are set to `γ=1` and `λ=2`.
 - **Dynamic Reasoning Incentivizing:**
 - **Curriculum Sampling:** Training data for the RLVR stage is pre-scored and filtered offline based on difficulty, then organized in increasing order, enabling a step-wise, difficulty-ascending training paradigm for stable and effective learning.
 - **Step-wise Dynamic Optimization:** This addresses data heterogeneity and varying difficulty:
 - **Data Sampling Balance:** Each training batch contains data from only one specific task, and training steps are uniformly distributed across all tasks, ensuring balanced resource allocation and preserving task-specific characteristics.
 - **Dynamic Hyper-parameter Adjustment:** Modifications to GRPO include:
 - **Dynamic Advantage Weighting (`ˆα`):** Samples of moderate difficulty (online accuracy reward ~0.5) are assigned higher weights (`ˆα = σ * mean(R_acc_i) * (1 - mean(R_acc_i))`) as they are considered more informative.
 - **Cosine-Annealed KL Penalty (`ˆβ`):** The KL penalty coefficient is dynamically adjusted using a cosine annealing strategy (`ˆβ = (β/2) * (1 + cos(π * T_cur / T_max))`) to stabilize training on easier data and encourage longer, more meaningful outputs for challenging examples, complementing curriculum sampling.

This comprehensive methodology, combining meticulous data engineering with a sophisticated, dynamically optimized training paradigm, aims to equip M2-Reasoning-7B with superior and unified reasoning abilities across diverse general and spatial tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

M2-Reasoning-7B demonstrates state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across a comprehensive suite of 8 diverse benchmarks, encompassing both general and spatial reasoning domains.

#### 4.1. General Reasoning Evaluation

Evaluated against six benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, DynaMath, WeMath, LogicVista) measuring mathematical and logical reasoning, M2-Reasoning-7B achieved an average score of **45.0**, setting a new SOTA among base-scale MLLMs.

- **Overall Performance:** M2-Reasoning-7B surpasses leading general models like InternVL3-8B (41.4) and specialized reasoning models such as WeThink-VL-7B (44.3). This represents a significant **+9.5 point improvement** over the base model.
- **Benchmark Specific Strengths:**
 - It secured the best results on **MathVista (75.0)** and **DynaMath (26.8)**.
 - It achieved competitive second-place rankings on **MathVision (31.5)** and **LogicVista (50.0)**.
- The progression from the base model (35.5) to M2-Reasoning-CI-7B (42.9) and finally M2-Reasoning-7B (45.0) clearly validates the effectiveness of the two-stage training strategy, particularly the SFT and RLVR stages with curated data.

#### 4.2. Spatial Reasoning Evaluation

M2-Reasoning-7B was evaluated on two challenging spatial reasoning benchmarks: CV-Bench (image-based) and VSI-Bench (video-based).

- **CV-Bench (Image-based Spatial Reasoning):**
 - M2-Reasoning-7B achieved an average score of **82.3**, slightly surpassing the previous leading base-scale model, InternVL3-8B (82.0).
 - It demonstrated exceptional strength in complex spatial configurations, securing top scores in **Relation (92.8), Depth (89.3), and Distance (84.3)**. This indicates superior ability in processing and reasoning about relational and depth-related visual cues.
 - While InternVL3-8B maintained a slight edge in object counting, M2-Reasoning-7B's overall strong performance highlights its advanced static spatial understanding.

- **VSI-Bench (Video-based Spatial Imagination):**
 - M2-Reasoning-7B achieved a highly competitive average score of **42.3**, surpassing InternVL3-8B (42.1) and being second only to the large-scale Gemini-1.5-pro (45.4). This is particularly noteworthy as M2-Reasoning-7B is a 7B-parameter model competing closely with significantly larger models.
 - Crucially, M2-Reasoning-7B established new SOTA records for inferring **Room Size (55.4)** and determining **Relative Direction (47.3)**.
 - It also secured the second-best performance in estimating Object Size and Absolute Distance.
 - This performance underscores the model's sophisticated capability to infer complex spatial attributes (like scale and orientation) from dynamic scenes, demonstrating its particular strengths in nuanced video-based spatial reasoning tasks.

In summary, the results consistently show that M2-Reasoning-7B, a base-scale MLLM, achieves new state-of-the-art performance across a diverse range of reasoning tasks, especially demonstrating significant advancements in both general and, critically, dynamic spatial reasoning. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed data construction and training methodologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of M2-Reasoning-7B represents a significant advancement in the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in bridging the critical gap of dynamic spatial reasoning. Its unified approach to both general and spatial tasks, coupled with state-of-the-art performance across 8 benchmarks, highlights several key areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Enhanced Real-World Applicability:** By improving MLLMs' ability to reason about dynamic spatial interactions, M2-Reasoning-7B makes a crucial step towards more robust and reliable real-world applications. This is vital for fields such as robotics (e.g., navigation, object manipulation in complex environments), autonomous vehicles (e.g., understanding relative positions and movements of other vehicles and pedestrians), augmented reality (e.g., accurately overlaying digital content onto physical spaces), and advanced simulation environments.
- **Foundation for More Capable AI Agents:** The ability to understand "motion, orientation, and physical interactions" in dynamic environments is fundamental for creating AI agents that can interact intelligently with the physical world. M2-Reasoning-7B provides a stronger perceptual and reasoning backbone for such agents, enabling them to make more informed decisions and engage in more complex tasks.
- **Innovation in Data Curation and Training:** The paper's detailed methodology for generating high-quality, logically coherent reasoning trajectories (both general CoT and detailed spatial simulations) offers a valuable blueprint for future research. The "Exponential Decay Numeric Matching (EDNM)" reward function for spatial tasks is particularly insightful, addressing the challenges of continuous numeric predictions in RL environments. These innovations can inspire new approaches to data synthesis and reward design in other multimodal reasoning domains.
- **Validation of RLVR for Multimodal Reasoning:** The continued success of the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm, specifically adapted with dynamic multi-task optimization and task-specific rewards, further cements its role as a powerful technique for unlocking advanced reasoning in MLLMs. The paper demonstrates how careful customization of RLVR can effectively address the complexities of multimodal learning.
- **Competitive Performance at Base Scale:** Achieving SOTA results with a 7B-parameter model against larger models (like Gemini-1.5-pro in some VSI-Bench metrics) is significant. It suggests that optimized data and training strategies can yield highly competitive performance without requiring exorbitantly large models, making powerful MLLMs more accessible and computationally efficient.

Despite its impressive achievements, the authors candidly acknowledge several limitations, which also serve as clear directions for future research:
- **Constrained Reasoning Depth:** M2-Reasoning-7B sometimes produces shorter reasoning chains compared to specialized language-only models, limiting its ability to solve highly complex, multi-step problems requiring deeper logical deliberation. Future work should focus on enhancing the model's capacity for extended, intricate reasoning.
- **Pathological Repetition:** The model occasionally exhibits repetitive outputs, indicating instability in the generation process. Addressing this "pathological repetition" is crucial for improving output coherence and quality.
- **Suboptimal Visual Perception:** While strong in reasoning, the model's visual perception is not infallible, showing weaknesses in fine-grained details, leading to imprecise descriptions or even visual hallucinations. Continuous improvement in the foundational visual encoder and its integration with the language model is necessary.

In conclusion, M2-Reasoning-7B significantly pushes the capabilities of MLLMs in general and spatial reasoning. Its methodological contributions, particularly in data engineering and dynamic RLVR, are valuable for the research community. While acknowledging its current limitations, this work lays a strong foundation for developing more capable, perceptive, and intelligently interactive AI systems for dynamic real-world environments.
