# ReKep: Spatio-Temporal Reasoning of Relational Keypoint Constraints for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ReKep enables robots to perform diverse, multi-stage manipulation tasks in unstructured environments by automatically generating visually-grounded relational keypoint constraints from language instructions using foundation models. The system demonstrates robust real-time execution and zero-shot generalization across various complex behaviors, outperforming baseline methods on real-world robot platforms.

## Problem

- Existing constraint formulations for robotic manipulation often lack versatility for diverse, complex tasks, including multi-stage, in-the-wild, and bimanual operations.
- Manually specifying constraints, object models, or annotating keypoints for every new task is time-consuming and does not scale.
- Generated constraints must be in a format that allows off-the-shelf optimization solvers to produce real-time robot actions for continuous, closed-loop control.

## Method

- Introduces **ReKep** (==Relational Keypoint Constraints==), defined as Python functions encoding spatial relationships between ==3D keypoints==, which are used as ==cost functions== for manipulation.
- Employs a ==hierarchical optimization procedure==, decomposing tasks into 'sub-goal' and 'path' problems, solved in real-time (10 Hz) using off-the-shelf ==numerical solvers==.
- Automates keypoint proposal using ==DINOv2== and ==SAM== from RGB-D inputs, and leverages ==GPT-4o== to generate the ReKep Python functions directly from free-form language instructions and visual prompts.

## Results

- Achieved a **44.3%** total success rate across seven diverse real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming a VLM-based baseline (VoxPoser, **10.0%**) and demonstrating robustness to disturbances.
- Successfully generalized to novel manipulation strategies for **8** unique garment categories, achieving a **73.8%** execution success rate when the VLM generated a feasible strategy.
- Identified point tracking inaccuracies (due to occlusions) as the primary source of system failures, while the optimization module proved relatively robust.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visually-grounded keypoint constraints, automatically generated by VLMs, offer a versatile and scalable representation for complex robotic manipulation tasks.
> - A hierarchical, receding-horizon optimization framework enables real-time, reactive control, allowing robots to adapt to disturbances and execute multi-stage behaviors.
> - Leveraging foundation models (DINOv2 for keypoints, GPT-4o for constraint generation) automates the traditionally manual process of constraint specification, significantly reducing engineering effort.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces a core challenge: enabling robots to perform diverse, complex tasks in unstructured, "in-the-wild" environments without extensive manual programming or task-specific data. Traditionally, manipulation tasks have been formulated using rigid-body transformations between robots and objects, which requires pre-defined object models and struggles with deformable objects or fine geometric details. Data-driven approaches, while more flexible, often demand prohibitively large and combinatorially growing datasets for various tasks and objects.

"ReKep" (Relational Keypoint Constraints) directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel, visually-grounded representation for robotic manipulation constraints. It positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly advancing areas:

- **Constraint-based Robotics and Task & Motion Planning (TAMP):** The paper builds upon the long-standing tradition of expressing robotic behaviors as constraints. It cites foundational works in TAMP and constrained optimization. ReKep extends these by making constraints visually grounded and automatically specifiable, moving away from manual, logic-based definitions. It particularly draws inspiration from "Sequence-of-Constraints MPC", adapting its hierarchical optimization for real-time reactive control.
- **Keypoint-based Representations for Manipulation:** Keypoints, or semantic 3D points on objects, have shown promise for their interpretability, efficiency, and generalization to object variations. However, previous keypoint-based methods often relied on manual annotation, limiting their scalability. ReKep's innovation lies in automating the specification of these keypoints and their relational constraints.
- **Foundation Models in Robotics (Large Vision Models (LVMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs)):** This is the most significant contemporary trend that ReKep leverages. The paper explicitly uses DINOv2 (a self-supervised LVM for fine-grained visual features) and GPT-4o (a powerful VLM for visual reasoning and code generation). This approach aligns with the broader push to leverage the extensive pre-training knowledge of foundation models for open-world robotic tasks, as seen in concurrent works like VoxPoser (by some of the same authors), RT-2, and others. ReKep distinguishes itself by using VLMs not just for high-level planning or action prediction, but for *generating executable constraint code* based on visual prompts, thereby enabling precise 6-12 DoF control without explicit 3D pose estimation by the VLM.
- **Embodied AI and Generalization:** The ultimate goal of such research is to create general-purpose robots capable of performing novel tasks in diverse settings. ReKep's emphasis on "in-the-wild," "multi-stage," "bimanual," and "reactive" behaviors, coupled with its zero-shot generalization capabilities (as demonstrated in the garment folding task and simulation experiments), directly contributes to the vision of more versatile and autonomous embodied AI systems.

By intelligently combining advancements in constraint optimization with the power of modern foundation models, ReKep offers a novel paradigm for robot control that promises to reduce the need for labor-intensive manual engineering and task-specific data collection, bridging the gap between high-level language instructions and low-level robot actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The authors clearly articulate three primary challenges in formulating constraints for robotic manipulation tasks, which serve as the core motivations for ReKep:

1. **Versatility to Diverse Tasks:** Existing constraint formulations often lack the flexibility to adapt to a wide array of complex manipulation scenarios, including those requiring multiple stages, operations in unstructured "in-the-wild" environments, bimanual coordination, and reactive behaviors to unexpected changes. The objective is to create a representation versatile enough to handle these complexities.
2. **Freedom from Manual Labeling/Specification:** Manually specifying constraints for every new task is time-consuming and unscalable. This includes defining geometric primitives, object models, or annotating keypoints. A key motivation is to automate this specification process, ideally by leveraging recent advances in AI models.
3. **Real-time Optimality by Off-the-Shelf Solvers:** The generated constraints must be in a format that allows off-the-shelf optimization solvers to produce robot actions in real-time, enabling continuous, closed-loop control and reactivity. This avoids the computational burden of learning-based control methods that might struggle with high-dimensional action spaces or require extensive re-training.

To address these, the key objectives of the paper are:

- To introduce **Relational Keypoint Constraints (ReKep)** as a visually-grounded representation for manipulation tasks.
- To demonstrate that ReKep, expressed as sequences of Python functions mapping 3D keypoints to a numerical cost, can be used within a **hierarchical optimization procedure** to solve for real-time robot actions (end-effector poses in SE(3)).
- To devise an **automated procedure** for generating ReKep from free-form language instructions and RGB-D observations by leveraging large vision models (LVMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), thereby circumventing manual specification.
- To validate the system's capabilities on **real-world robot platforms** for a large variety of multi-stage, in-the-wild, bimanual, and reactive manipulation tasks, all without task-specific data or explicit environment models.

In essence, the motivation is to create a scalable, flexible, and efficient framework for general-purpose robotic manipulation that integrates symbolic reasoning (constraints) with perceptual grounding (keypoints) and the broad knowledge of foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReKep's methodology involves a clever three-part pipeline: visually grounding semantic keypoints, automatically generating relational constraints using foundation models, and then solving for robot actions via a real-time hierarchical optimization.

#### 3.1. Relational Keypoint Constraints (ReKep) Definition

A single ReKep instance is defined as a Python function $f: \mathbb{R}^{K \times 3} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ that maps an array of $K$ 3D keypoints to a numerical cost. A cost $f(k) \le 0$ indicates the constraint is satisfied. These functions perform arithmetic operations (e.g., L2 distance, dot product) on keypoints, encoding desired spatial relations (e.g., alignment, distance, orientation) between robot parts, object parts, or other entities.
Manipulation tasks are decomposed into $N$ stages. For each stage $i$, two types of constraints are defined:
- **Sub-goal constraints ($C^{(i)}_{\text{sub-goal}}$):** Keypoint relations to be achieved at the *end* of stage $i$.
- **Path constraints ($C^{(i)}_{\text{path}}$):** Keypoint relations to be satisfied for *every state within* stage $i$.

#### 3.2. Manipulation as Constrained Optimization

The problem is formulated as a continuous mathematical program (Eq. 1) to obtain the overall discrete-time end-effector trajectory $e_{1:T}$ and stage transition timings $g_{1:N}$. The objective minimizes auxiliary costs (e.g., collision avoidance, reachability) subject to:
- Initial robot pose and temporal ordering of stages.
- Sub-goal constraints being satisfied at their respective stage timings ($f(k_{g_i}) \le 0$).
- Path constraints being satisfied throughout their respective stages ($f(k_t) \le 0$ for $t \in [g_{i-1}, g_i]$).
- A forward model $h(k_t, e_t)$ that predicts keypoint changes based on end-effector actions.

#### 3.3. Decomposition and Algorithmic Instantiation

To achieve real-time performance (approx. 10 Hz), a hierarchical, receding-horizon optimization strategy is employed (Algorithm 1):
- **Sub-Goal Problem (Eq. 2):** Solves for the immediate next sub-goal end-effector pose ($e_{g_i}$) for the current stage $i$. It minimizes auxiliary costs while satisfying $C^{(i)}_{\text{sub-goal}}$.
- **Path Problem (Eq. 3):** After obtaining $e_{g_i}$, it solves for a trajectory $e_{t:g_i}$ from the current pose to the sub-goal. It minimizes auxiliary costs (e.g., path length) while satisfying $C^{(i)}_{\text{path}}$ for all states along the path.
- **Solvers:** SciPy's Dual Annealing (global) and SLSQP (local) are used. Dual Annealing is used for the first iteration of a stage, then local optimization is used for subsequent iterations for speed.
- **Forward Model for Keypoints:** A rigidity assumption is made for "grasped keypoints" (those belonging to the currently manipulated object), meaning their relative positions to the end-effector remain fixed. Other keypoints are assumed static. Actual keypoint positions are updated at 20 Hz using visual input.
- **Backtracking:** If path constraints are violated during execution, the system backtracks to a previous stage where conditions can still be met, enabling reactive replanning.
- **Auxiliary Costs:** Include scene collision avoidance (using ESDF from nvblox), robot reachability (using IK residuals), pose regularization, solution consistency, and self-collision avoidance for bimanual setups.

#### 3.4. Keypoint Proposal and ReKep Generation (Automated Specification)

This is a critical automated component leveraging foundation models:
- **Keypoint Proposal:** Given an RGB-D image, DINOv2 extracts patch-wise visual features. Segment Anything Model (SAM) generates object masks. Within each mask, k-means clustering (on PCA-projected features) identifies fine-grained regions. Cluster centroids are projected to 3D world coordinates using calibrated RGB-D cameras to generate keypoint candidates. Filtering removes candidates outside the workspace or too close to each other.
- **ReKep Generation:** The RGB image overlaid with numerical keypoint marks, along with a free-form language instruction, is fed into GPT-4o. GPT-4o, through visual prompting, generates:
 - The number of required stages for the task.
 - The Python functions for $C^{(i)}_{\text{sub-goal}}$ and $C^{(i)}_{\text{path}}$ for each stage.
 Importantly, GPT-4o specifies *arithmetic operations* on keypoints (e.g., `norm(k-k)` for distance), allowing it to implicitly reason about 3D rotations by using multiple points and their relations, which are then explicitly solved by the numerical optimizer. This circumvents the need for VLMs to directly predict complex 3D transformations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper demonstrates ReKep's capabilities through extensive real-world experiments on two robot platforms and a variety of complex manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. ReKep for In-the-Wild and Bimanual Manipulation

- **Experimental Setup:** Tested on a wheeled single-arm platform and a stationary dual-arm platform. Seven diverse tasks were chosen to showcase multi-stage, in-the-wild, bimanual, and reactive behaviors: Pour Tea, Recycle Can, Stow Book, Tape Box, Fold Garment, Pack Shoes, and Collaborative Folding. Each task had 10 trials with randomized object poses.
- **Baselines:** Compared against VoxPoser (another VLM-based approach) and a human-annotated ReKep version (as an upper bound for the automated system's potential).
- **Success Rates (Table 1):** ReKep (Auto) generally outperformed VoxPoser, achieving a total success rate of 44.3% across tasks, compared to VoxPoser's 10.0%. The "Annotated" ReKep achieved 68.6%, indicating room for improvement in the automated generation process but validating the core ReKep concept.
- **Reactive Behaviors (Table 2):** When tasks were subjected to external disturbances, ReKep (Auto) maintained higher success rates (26.7%) compared to VoxPoser (6.7%), demonstrating its robustness and ability to react to changes due to its high-frequency closed-loop execution and backtracking mechanism.
- **Qualitative Observations:** ReKep successfully formulated temporal dependencies (e.g., align spout before pouring), leveraged commonsense (e.g., recycle cans), enabled bimanual coordination (folding sleeves simultaneously), and handled kinematically challenging scenarios (stowing books in confined spaces).

#### 4.2. Generalization in Manipulation Strategies

- **Experimental Setup:** Focused on the garment folding task with 8 unique categories of garments, each requiring different folding strategies. This tested the system's ability to generalize to novel manipulation strategies beyond simple object interactions.
- **Metrics:** "Strategy Success" (VLM generating feasible ReKep) and "Execution Success" (robot successfully executing generated ReKep).
- **Results (Figure 5):** The system achieved a 52.5% "Strategy Success" rate and a 73.8% "Execution Success" rate. This indicates that when the foundation models generate a correct strategy (keypoints and constraints), the robot system is quite capable of executing it. The distinct strategies generated for different garment types demonstrate the VLM's ability to infer varied and human-like folding approaches, leveraging implicit commonsense and geometric reasoning.

#### 4.3. System Error Breakdown (Figure 4)

An empirical analysis of failure cases revealed the primary error sources:
- **Point Tracker (Largest source):** Frequent and intermittent occlusions pose significant challenges for accurate 3D keypoint tracking, leading to misinterpretations by the optimization solver.
- **Keypoint Proposal:** The DINOv2 + SAM pipeline sometimes misses relevant keypoints or proposes redundant ones.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** GPT-4o occasionally refers to incorrect keypoints or generates suboptimal/incomplete constraint functions.
- **Optimization Module:** Contributed less to failures despite limited time budgets, suggesting its robustness in finding feasible solutions given good inputs.
- Other modules (segmentation, 3D reconstruction, low-level control) were relatively minor error sources.

#### 4.4. Additional Insights (Appendix)

- **Simulation Experiments (A.12):** ReKep (zero-shot) showed significantly better generalization to unseen poses (0.68 vs 0.31) and unseen objects (0.72 vs 0.14) compared to a monolithic learning-based policy trained on 100 expert demonstrations, highlighting its strength in novel scenarios.
- **Visual Feature Extractor Comparison (A.13):** DINOv2 was found to produce sharper, more semantically distinguishable features for keypoint proposal, and the objectness prior from SAM was crucial to avoid background keypoints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReKep represents a significant step towards more general, adaptable, and autonomous robotic manipulation. Its core significance and potential impact stem from several key innovations:

- **Bridging High-Level Semantics and Low-Level Control:** By translating free-form language instructions into executable, visually-grounded constraint functions, ReKep effectively bridges the gap between human intent and robot action. This is crucial for user-friendly and intuitive robot programming.
- **Scalability and Reduced Manual Effort:** Automating the generation of constraints via foundation models is a game-changer. It drastically reduces the need for manual engineering, task-specific data collection, and complex geometric modeling, making it feasible to deploy robots for a much wider range of tasks in diverse, unstructured environments ("in-the-wild" capabilities).
- **Robustness and Reactivity in Real-time:** The combination of a hierarchical optimization framework, high-frequency keypoint tracking, and a robust backtracking mechanism enables real-time, closed-loop execution. This allows the robot to react dynamically to external disturbances, unexpected changes, and even human collaboration, which is vital for real-world deployment.
- **Handling Complex Manipulation Tasks:** ReKep's ability to naturally express multi-stage tasks with spatio-temporal dependencies, perform bimanual coordination, and implicitly handle 3D rotations through keypoint arithmetic demonstrates its versatility for intricate manipulation scenarios that often challenge traditional methods. The garment folding example specifically highlights its potential for reasoning about novel manipulation strategies.
- **Inspiration for Future Robot Learning:** The modular nature of ReKep, especially the error breakdown, provides clear insights into current limitations (e.g., point tracking, VLM robustness in generating complex code). This allows future research to focus on improving specific components, rather than grappling with monolithic black-box systems. The finding that the optimization solver is relatively robust suggests that improving perception and VLM capabilities will yield significant dividends for overall system performance.

Despite its promising advancements, ReKep is not without limitations. The reliance on a simple rigidity-based forward model for keypoints, the susceptibility to inaccuracies in point tracking (especially with occlusions), and the current assumption of a fixed sequence of stages are areas for future improvement. Addressing these will further solidify ReKep's foundation and expand its applicability to even more dynamic and complex interaction scenarios, such as articulated object manipulation or long-horizon tasks requiring more dynamic replanning. Overall, ReKep lays a strong groundwork for the next generation of intelligent, general-purpose robotic manipulation systems.
