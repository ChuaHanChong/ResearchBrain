# TRIG: Trajectory-Rig Decoupled Metric Geometry Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> TRIG, a framework from Carizon and ShanghaiTech University, improves multi-camera geometry learning for autonomous driving by decoupling ego-trajectory from static camera-rig geometry. It achieves state-of-the-art results across five benchmarks in 3D reconstruction and metric depth, while also improving ego-pose estimation, for example, boosting KITTI's ego-pose AUC from 87.6 to 99.1.

## Problem

- Existing multi-camera visual geometry models entangle time-varying ego-motion with static camera-rig geometry, preventing explicit utilization of vehicle-side geometric priors.
- Metric scale inference often relies on visual appearance, leading to inherent scale ambiguity and requiring post-hoc scale recovery methods like Sim(3) alignment.
- Current methods under-exploit the structural constraints of synchronized rigid multi-camera rigs specific to autonomous driving.

## Method

- **TRIG** ==factorizes camera poses== into ego-trajectory and static camera-rig components, encoding them separately and combining through ==additive composition== to represent geometry.
- Utilizes ==Sparse Temporal–Spatial Attention (STSA)== with interleaved ==Rig Blocks== (cross-camera spatial) and ==Traj Blocks== (cross-frame temporal) for efficient multi-camera, multi-frame processing.
- Applies ==decoupled pose supervision== with distinct losses for camera-rig geometry and ego-motion, aligning the training objective with the pose factorization.

## Results

- Attained new state-of-the-art results in 3D reconstruction and metric depth estimation across **five** autonomous driving benchmarks, directly predicting metric scale.
- Improved 3D reconstruction performance (e.g., KITTI Accuracy **0.333**, Completeness **0.351**) and dense metric depth (e.g., KITTI Abs Rel **0.046**, δ1.25 **0.967**).
- Achieved the highest ego-pose accuracy, boosting AUC@30° on KITTI from **87.6** to **99.1**, demonstrating improved stability in pose estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The factorization of camera poses into ego-trajectory and static camera-rig geometry allows for principled, independent modeling of dynamic motion and static topology.
> - Integrating vehicle-side geometric priors directly in a decoupled manner enables intrinsic metric-scale predictions, overcoming scale ambiguity without post-processing.
> - Sparse Temporal-Spatial Attention (STSA) efficiently aggregates geometric cues from long multi-camera sequences by selectively applying global constraints, balancing reasoning with computation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-centric autonomous driving systems rely on accurate metric scene geometry and ego-motion estimation derived from synchronized multi-camera observations. Current visual geometry models, while effective in general pose estimation, depth prediction, and 3D reconstruction from image sequences, often encounter challenges when applied directly to rigid multi-camera driving systems. These challenges stem from two primary issues:
- **Entangled Pose Representations**: Existing models frequently encode camera poses as a unified representation that conflates time-varying ego-motion with static camera-rig geometry. This entanglement limits the explicit utilization of vehicle-side geometric priors and complicates the separate modeling of motion dynamics and camera topology. This often necessitates costly global reasoning or post-processing steps, such as Sim(3) alignment, to achieve consistent metric-scale reconstruction.
- **Scale Ambiguity**: Many current methods primarily infer metric scale from visual appearance, leading to inherent scale ambiguity and potentially compromised geometric accuracy. While some geometry-prior methods have emerged, they often treat images as independent observations or are designed for generic visual scenes, under-exploiting the structural constraints of synchronized rigid camera rigs.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that explicitly decouples ego-trajectory from camera-rig geometry. This approach allows for separate modeling and optimization of dynamic ego-motion and static multi-camera topology, thereby aiming to improve metric scale consistency, pose stability, and overall 3D reconstruction quality in autonomous driving contexts. The work builds upon foundational models like VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer), adapting its architecture and training methodologies to incorporate these decoupled geometric priors effectively and efficiently. It aims to provide a direct metric-coordinate geometry learning solution, circumventing the need for post-hoc scale recovery methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a metric-aware perception framework for multi-camera autonomous driving that can accurately recover metric scene geometry and ego-motion by effectively leveraging vehicle-side geometric priors. The motivation stems from the observed limitations of existing visual geometry models when applied to autonomous driving scenarios.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:
- **Addressing Entangled Pose Representations**: The core motivation is the recognition that in autonomous driving, a camera pose inherently comprises a time-varying ego trajectory and a static camera-rig configuration. Existing models, by entangling these components into a single representation, make it challenging to exploit their distinct characteristics. The objective is to factorize camera poses into these two components to allow for independent modeling of ego-motion dynamics and static camera topology, while still enabling their joint optimization. This is expected to enhance the utilization of geometric priors specific to the vehicle setup.
- **Improving Metric Scale Consistency and Accuracy**: Current image-driven methods often infer metric scale implicitly, leading to scale ambiguity and limiting geometric accuracy. A key objective is to enable direct metric-coordinate geometry learning by explicitly incorporating vehicle-side geometric priors. This aims to establish a deterministic scale reference, allowing predictions to be directly interpreted in metric units without requiring post-hoc scale alignment, such as Sim(3) alignment.
- **Enhancing Pose Stability and Global Reconstruction Quality**: The separation of dynamic and static pose components is motivated by the potential to improve the stability of ego-pose estimation and the quality of global 3D scene reconstruction. By providing structured geometric priors, the framework aims to achieve more robust and drift-resistant pose estimates, which are crucial for downstream autonomous driving applications like planning and online HD map construction.
- **Developing Scalable and Computationally Efficient Geometric Reasoning**: The design also seeks to address the computational overhead associated with global self-attention mechanisms commonly used in multi-view and multi-frame processing. The motivation is to devise an attention mechanism that can effectively aggregate geometric cues from long-horizon, synchronized multi-camera sequences while maintaining computational efficiency. This involves an asymmetric temporal-spatial design, continuously aggregating trajectory cues but sparsely injecting rig constraints.

In summary, the research aims to overcome the geometric entanglement and scale ambiguity prevalent in existing visual foundation models by introducing a principled framework that explicitly decouples and supervises ego-trajectory and camera-rig geometry, leading to more accurate, stable, and computationally efficient metric geometry learning for autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Trajectory-Rig Decoupled Metric Geometry Learning (TRIG), is designed for multi-camera geometry learning from temporal sequences of synchronized surround-view images. It builds upon VGGT as its foundational visual geometry model and integrates three core components: decoupled pose encoding, sparse temporal–spatial attention, and decoupled pose supervision.

**A. Overview of TRIG**
TRIG processes an input clip of synchronized multi-camera images `I = {I_c_t | t = 1, ..., T, c = 1, ..., C}`. Each camera pose `P_t_w←c` (pose of camera `c` in world coordinates at time `t`) is factorized into an ego-trajectory component `P_t_w←ego` (ego-vehicle pose in world coordinates at time `t`) and a static camera-rig component `P_c_ego←c` (fixed extrinsic transform from camera `c` to ego-vehicle coordinates). These factors are encoded into trajectory priors (`z_t_traj`) and rig priors (`z_c_rig`) using Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). These priors are then injected into image tokens extracted by a frozen DINO backbone. The framework alternates between Rig Blocks and Traj Blocks to model cross-camera spatial constraints and cross-frame temporal motion cues, respectively. The refined features are then fed into the VGGT DPT and camera heads for depth and camera prediction.

**B. Decoupled Pose Encoding**
The methodology for decoupled pose encoding involves:
- **Factorization**: The input pose sequence `P` is divided into a reference camera's shared trajectory (`P_traj`) and a camera rig extracted from the reference timestamp (`P_rig`).
- **Encoding**: The trajectory component `P_t,0` for each time `t` (from the reference camera 0) is encoded as `z_t_traj = MLP_traj(P_t,0)`. Similarly, each camera-specific rig configuration `P_0,c` (at reference time 0) is encoded as `z_c_rig = MLP_rig(P_0,c)`.
- **Additive Composition**: A geometry-aware pose embedding `z_t,c_geo = z_t_traj + z_c_rig` is formed by adding these components, separating dynamic ego-motion from static multi-camera geometry.
- **Residual Injection**: Instead of replacing original camera tokens, the decoupled pose representation is injected through residual adaptation. A lightweight adapter projects `z_t,c_geo` and adds it to the original camera token `z_t,c_cam` to form `˜z_t,c_cam`. These adapted camera tokens are inserted into each frame attention block during transformer refinement, ensuring progressive geometric conditioning.

**C. Sparse Temporal–Spatial Attention (STSA)**
STSA manages token interactions efficiently in multi-camera, multi-frame sequences:
- **Structured Attention Patterns**:
 - **Frame Attention**: Self-attention applied independently within each camera observation (image tokens + injected pose tokens), refining local information without cross-camera or cross-timestamp mixing.
 - **Global Attention**: Self-attention across all observations and tokens, enabling communication across all frames and cameras for cross-camera geometric consistency.
 - **Temporal Attention**: Self-attention applied along each camera stream (across time for a single camera), aggregating long-range temporal cues while preserving camera-specific viewing directions.
- **Rig and Trajectory Blocks**:
 - **Rig Block**: Combines frame attention followed by global attention (`GlobalAttn(FrameAttn(X))`). This block leverages rig-aware pose tokens to condition global interaction on the fixed camera layout, promoting cross-view geometric consistency.
 - **Traj Block**: Combines frame attention followed by temporal attention (`TempAttn(FrameAttn(X))`). This block constrains long-range aggregation to intra-camera observations, facilitating temporal reasoning under ego-motion while preserving view-specific geometry.
- **Sparse Temporal–Spatial Composition**: The transformer primarily uses Traj Blocks across all its 24 layers for continuous temporal modeling. Rig Blocks are sparsely interleaved at specific layers (`{0, 4, 11, 17, 23}`) to enable global multi-camera interaction. This composition strategy aims to balance geometric information flow with computational efficiency by avoiding dense global attention at every layer.

**D. Decoupled Pose Supervision**
To align the training objective with the trajectory-rig factorization, TRIG supervises relative poses, partitioning them into two groups:
- **Rig Supervision (`L_rig`)**: Constrains the relative geometry between different cameras at the same timestamp (`T_c_i_←c_j`). This primarily enforces the fixed rig layout and promotes cross-camera consistency.
- **Trajectory Supervision (`L_traj`)**: Models ego-motion along each camera stream by supervising relative transformations between different timestamps for the same camera (`T_t_i_←t_j`). This focuses on ego-vehicle motion across time, independent of camera extrinsics.
Both supervision types utilize a relative pose loss (combining translation regression and rotation geodesic error on SO(3)), similar to π3. Additionally, focal components in the predicted pose encoding are supervised, and all pose losses are applied at each refinement stage with stage-dependent weights. This decoupled supervision explicitly separates cross-camera rig constraints from temporal ego-motion constraints.

**E. Base Model and Training Details**
- **Backbone**: A ViT-L backbone pretrained with DINOv2 is used as a frozen image encoder.
- **Decoder**: Intermediate tokens from specific transformer blocks (4th, 11th, 17th, 23rd) are fed into a DPT decoder for upsampling, producing dense depth and point maps.
- **Training**: The model is trained for 40K iterations on five public autonomous driving datasets (NuScenes, Waymo, KITTI, DDAD, OpenScene) with input images resized to 518 × 294. AdamW optimizer is used with a base learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁴, OneCycleLR with cosine annealing, DeepSpeed ZeRO-1 optimization, and bfloat16 mixed precision.
- **Loss Function**: The full training objective combines a depth loss (`L_depth`), a point map loss (`L_pmap`), and the decoupled pose loss (`L_pose = L_traj + L_rig`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated TRIG's performance across five autonomous driving benchmarks (KITTI, NuScenes, Waymo, OpenScene, DDAD) in three primary tasks: 3D point map reconstruction, metric depth estimation, and ego-pose estimation.

**A. 3D Reconstruction and Metric Depth**
- **3D Reconstruction**: TRIG achieved a new state-of-the-art performance in world-coordinate 3D Accuracy and Completeness across all five benchmarks (Table I). It consistently outperformed existing general visual geometry models (e.g., CUT3R, VGGT) and driving-specific architectures (e.g., DVGT). For instance, on KITTI, TRIG's Accuracy was 0.333 and Completeness was 0.351, significantly lower (better) than DVGT's 0.846 and 1.468 respectively. Qualitatively, TRIG produced sharper geometries and cleaner object boundaries compared to DVGT. A notable finding is that TRIG directly predicts metric scale without requiring post-hoc Sim(3) alignment, unlike many prior models.
- **Metric Depth**: TRIG demonstrated strong performance in dense depth estimation (Table II), dominating in both Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel) and the inlier ratio (δ1.25). For example, on KITTI, TRIG achieved an Abs Rel of 0.046 and δ1.25 of 0.967, compared to DVGT's 0.136 and 0.849. While on Waymo, DVGT showed a marginally higher δ1.25, TRIG maintained a superior global Abs Rel, suggesting a robust global metric consensus.

**B. Ego-Pose Estimation**
- TRIG achieved the highest pose accuracy across KITTI, NuScenes, Waymo, and DDAD, as measured by AUC@30° (Table III). For example, on KITTI, TRIG improved the AUC from 87.6 (DVGT) to 99.1. It also showed improved stability compared to prior-guided models like OmniVGGT, which showed significant degradation in wide-baseline configurations (e.g., 38.5 AUC on NuScenes for OmniVGGT vs. 96.6 for TRIG). The decoupled supervision strategy, by isolating inter-frame temporal constraints from intra-frame spatial constraints, was identified as a contributor to this performance.

**C. Ablation Studies**
Ablation studies validated the contributions of TRIG's core architectural components:
- **Sparse Temporal–Spatial Attention (STSA)**: Removing Rig Blocks led to a substantial increase in reconstruction Accuracy error (from 0.411 to 0.881) and decreases in depth and pose accuracy (Table IV). Frame-only and Global-only attention variants also performed worse than the full STSA, indicating the importance of jointly modeling temporal and cross-view interactions. The specific sparse composition with Rig Blocks at designated layers (`{0, 4, 11, 17, 23}`) was found to be effective. Additionally, STSA demonstrated improved efficiency, achieving 1.43× and 1.72× speedups compared to VGGT-style attention on NuScenes (Table V).
- **Decoupled Prior Encoding**: Replacing TRIG's distinct trajectory and rig embeddings with the entangled OmniVGGT pose prior resulted in marked deterioration across all metrics, with point completeness worsening from 0.577 to 0.691 (Table IV). This supports the hypothesis that ego-motion and camera-rig calibration are heterogeneous information sources requiring separate modeling.
- **Decoupled Pose Supervision**: Replacing TRIG's decoupled pose supervision with VGGT's standard pose loss consistently degraded both pose accuracy and geometric quality (Tables IV and VI). The full model showed improved Pose AUC under all angular thresholds (e.g., from 76.33 to 86.92 at 5°), indicating stronger resistance to pose drift, alongside improvements in dense depth and reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of TRIG signifies a methodological advancement in vision-centric autonomous driving perception, particularly concerning metric geometry and ego-motion estimation from multi-camera systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Geometric Accuracy and Consistency**: By explicitly decoupling ego-trajectory and camera-rig geometry, TRIG provides a more robust framework for understanding the complex geometric relationships in multi-camera setups. This decoupling mitigates the issue of entangled pose representations in prior models, leading to more accurate 3D reconstruction, denser metric depth prediction, and more stable ego-pose estimation. This direct metric-scale prediction eliminates the need for post-hoc scale recovery, which simplifies the perception pipeline and reduces potential error accumulation.
- **Improved Reliability for Autonomous Driving Applications**: Accurate and drift-resistant metric poses are foundational for various downstream autonomous driving applications. TRIG's ability to deliver highly accurate and stable poses directly in a driving-oriented 3D coordinate system means that reconstructed scene data is more reliable for tasks such as planning, navigation, obstacle detection, and online HD map construction. The reduction in pose drift is critical for long-term operational stability of autonomous vehicles.
- **Computational Efficiency through Sparse Attention**: The Sparse Temporal–Spatial Attention (STSA) mechanism offers a scalable solution for processing long sequences from multiple cameras. By selectively applying global attention through Rig Blocks and focusing on temporal aggregation via Traj Blocks, the framework maintains geometric reasoning capability while significantly reducing the quadratic computational cost associated with dense global self-attention. This efficiency is important for real-time processing requirements in autonomous driving.
- **Principled Integration of Geometric Priors**: TRIG demonstrates a principled approach to integrating vehicle-side geometric priors. By treating ego-motion dynamics and static camera topology as distinct but interacting entities, the framework effectively leverages these intrinsic constraints without corrupting the underlying visual feature space, as sometimes observed with entangled prior injections in other models. This methodological clarity could inspire similar structured approaches in other multi-modal perception tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The success of TRIG in establishing new state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks validates the decoupled learning paradigm. This work lays a foundation for future research in several directions, including further optimization of sparse attention mechanisms, exploration of dynamic rig configurations (e.g., for deformable sensor mounts), or the integration of other sensor modalities (e.g., LiDAR, radar) within a similarly decoupled framework. The robust performance in challenging, dynamic driving environments suggests its potential for broader adoption in research and industrial autonomous driving systems.
