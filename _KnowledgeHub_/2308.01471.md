# Implicit Occupancy Flow Fields for Perception and Prediction in Self-Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> IMPLICITO is an implicit neural network for joint perception and prediction in self-driving that predicts occupancy probability and backward flow at any continuous spatio-temporal query point, using deformable-offset cross-attention over BEV features from LiDAR and HD maps, outperforming object-based and dense-grid baselines on urban and highway benchmarks.

## Problem

- Object-based pipelines threshold detections to a small object set, discarding low-confidence objects and losing information critical for safety.
- Object-based trajectory forecasts must stay compact since output size scales linearly with object count, limiting expressiveness of future distributions.
- Dense object-free occupancy-flow grids are computationally expensive and suffer limited receptive field from fully convolutional architectures.

## Method

- ==Two-stream convolutional encoder== with ResNet and FPN fuses voxelized LiDAR and rasterized HD map into a shared BEV feature map with spatial and temporal context.
- ==Implicit decoder== predicts K learned ==reference-point offsets== per query, deformable-convolution style, then aggregates their interpolated features via ==cross-attention== for global receptive field.
- End-to-end training on continuous randomly sampled ==spatio-temporal query points== with ==binary cross-entropy== occupancy loss plus backward-flow regression loss on occupied points.

## Results

- On Argoverse2, IMPLICITO reaches mAP **0.799** and Soft-IoU **0.480**, beating MP3 (**0.774**/**0.422**) and OccFlow (**0.675**/**0.356**); larger HwySim gains (mAP **0.597** vs MP3 **0.466**).
- Ablation isolating decoder shows IMPLICITO beats ConvNet and ConvNetFT decoders trained with same encoder on both datasets, e.g. HwySim Soft-IoU **0.415** vs **0.351**.
- Offset-count ablation shows K=1 outperforms K=0 substantially on HwySim (mAP **0.716** vs **0.649**), while K=4 gives no further gain, confirming the offset mechanism's receptive-field benefit.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit continuous-query representations let downstream planners spend compute only where needed instead of paying for a dense spatio-temporal grid.
> - A single learned attention offset already captures most of the receptive-field benefit; extra offsets mainly help rare multi-source occupancy cases like complex intersections.
> - End-to-end training without hand-crafted detection features consistently beats detection-then-predict pipelines, since thresholding limits information available downstream.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of self-driving vehicles (SDVs) relies critically on their ability to accurately perceive their environment and predict the future behavior of other traffic participants. Traditionally, autonomy stacks break this complex problem into a sequential pipeline: perception, motion forecasting, and motion planning. This paper addresses a fundamental challenge within this pipeline by proposing a novel, unified approach to perception and prediction.

The existing research landscape for perception and prediction in SDVs can be broadly categorized into two main paradigms:

1. **Object-based Approaches:** These methods first perform object detection and tracking to identify individual traffic participants (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians) and then forecast their future trajectories.
 - **Limitations:** This paradigm faces several inherent drawbacks. Firstly, it poses a safety concern because object detectors often need to threshold confidence scores to maintain efficiency, leading to a trade-off between precision and recall where objects below a certain confidence might be missed entirely. This "blindness" to undetected objects can result in unsafe situations. Secondly, predicting trajectories for a finite set of detected objects limits the expressiveness of future behavior distributions, often generating only a handful of sample trajectories or parametric distributions. This simplification can lead to information loss, particularly in complex, multi-modal scenarios. Thirdly, uncertainty from the perception stage is often not fully propagated to downstream prediction, further compromising safety.

2. **Object-free (Explicit Grid) Approaches:** More recently, approaches have emerged that model the entire scene by predicting dense occupancy and flow grids across a spatio-temporal volume, directly from raw sensor data. These methods avoid detection thresholding and can represent highly expressive, non-parametric distributions of future occupancy and motion.
 - **Limitations:** While addressing some issues of object-based methods, explicit grid-based approaches introduce new challenges. They are computationally expensive due to the high dimensionality of the output grid, which must be finely quantized to mitigate errors. A significant portion of this computation is often unnecessary, as downstream motion planners typically only need to query specific spatio-temporal points or regions of interest around candidate trajectories, not a dense, whole-scene grid. Furthermore, fully convolutional networks (FCNs) commonly used in these methods suffer from a limited effective receptive field, which can be problematic for predicting the behavior of fast-moving objects or interactions over long distances.

The authors position their work, **IMPLICITO**, as a novel solution that transcends the limitations of both paradigms. By leveraging an "implicit" representation, inspired by implicit geometric reconstruction techniques in computer graphics, they aim to achieve the benefits of object-free methods (expressive representations, no thresholding) while overcoming their computational inefficiencies and receptive field constraints. This represents a significant conceptual shift, moving away from explicit grid computations towards a more query-driven, on-demand prediction model, directly aligned with the needs of a motion planner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind IMPLICITO stems from the critical need for robust, efficient, and accurate perception and prediction systems in self-driving vehicles, particularly in bridging the gaps left by existing paradigms. The authors highlight several key pain points:

1. **Computational Inefficiency of Explicit Grid Methods:** Current object-free methods, while expressive, compute dense spatio-temporal grids across the entire scene. Much of this computation is redundant, as a motion planner only requires information for specific points or regions relevant to its candidate trajectories. This leads to wasted computational resources and memory.
2. **Safety Concerns with Object-based Methods:** Object-based systems inherently risk missing objects due to detection confidence thresholds, which directly impacts safety by rendering the planner "blind" to critical hazards. Their limited expressiveness for future trajectories also restricts the planner's ability to anticipate complex behaviors.
3. **Limited Receptive Fields in Fully Convolutional Networks:** Explicit grid prediction methods often rely on FCNs, which can have difficulty capturing long-range dependencies and predicting the behavior of fast-moving vehicles far into the future, crucial for highway driving.

To address these motivations, the authors set forth the following key objectives for IMPLICITO:

- **Unified Perception and Prediction:** To develop a single neural network capable of performing both perception (current occupancy) and future prediction (future occupancy and motion flow) directly from raw sensor data and HD maps.
- **Implicit Representation:** To implicitly represent occupancy probability and flow over time as a continuous function, allowing direct querying at arbitrary spatio-temporal locations. This fundamentally shifts from "predict everything" to "predict what's needed."
- **Computational Efficiency:** To avoid unnecessary computations by only calculating occupancy and flow at specific query points requested by the downstream motion planner, thereby optimizing resource utilization.
- **Extended Receptive Field:** To design an architecture that overcomes the limited receptive field of previous explicit occupancy prediction methods, particularly for dynamic and distant objects, through an efficient global attention mechanism.
- **Superior Performance and Safety:** To demonstrate that this implicit, queryable model outperforms existing state-of-the-art object-based and explicit object-free methods in terms of accuracy, calibration, and consistency, contributing to a safer and more robust SDV system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of IMPLICITO is an implicit neural network that directly predicts occupancy probability and backward flow vectors for continuous spatio-temporal query points.

#### 3.1. Task Parameterization

- **Inputs:** The model takes two primary inputs:
 - **Voxelized LiDAR (L):** A sequence of the most recent 5 LiDAR sweeps, voxelized into a 3D representation (Depth x Height x Width) following a multi-sweep BEV voxelization scheme.
 - **Rasterized HD Map (M):** Lane centerlines from the high-definition map, rasterized into a single-channel BEV image with the same spatial dimensions as the LiDAR features.
- **Outputs:** For any given spatio-temporal query point `q = (x, y, t)` (where `(x,y)` are BEV coordinates and `t` is future time relative to the current timestep `t̄`):
 - **Occupancy (o):** A probability `o(q) ∈` indicating the likelihood of an agent occupying that location at that time.
 - **Backward Flow (f):** A 2D vector `f(q) ∈ R^2` representing the motion of an agent at `(x, y)` at time `t` to its position at `t-1`. This backward flow formulation is chosen for its ability to capture multi-modal forward motions with a single vector per grid cell.

#### 3.2. Network Architecture

The IMPLICITO network `ψ` consists of an encoder and an implicit decoder:
- **Encoder:** This component processes the raw sensor and map data to extract rich contextual features.
 - It uses two convolutional stems for LiDAR and map raster respectively.
 - A ResNet processes the concatenated features, generating multi-resolution feature planes.
 - A lightweight Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) processes these planes, resulting in a Bird's-Eye View (BEV) feature map `Z ∈ R^(C × H/2 × W/2)`. This map `Z` contains spatial and temporal information about the scene, capturing geometry, semantics, and past motion.
- **Implicit Decoder:** This is the novel component designed to address the receptive field limitation and enable query-based prediction. For each query point `q = (x, y, t)`:
 - **Local Feature Extraction:** It first bilinearly interpolates `Z` at `(x, y)` to get a local feature vector `z_q`.
 - **Deformable Reference Point Prediction:** Inspired by deformable convolutions, the decoder predicts `K` *reference points* `r_k = q + ∆q_k`. The offsets `∆q_k` are learned by a fully connected ResNet-based architecture, which effectively tells the network "where to look" in the feature map for relevant information, given the query point.
 - **Aggregated Global Context:** Features `z_{r_k}` are then interpolated from `Z` at these `K` reference points. Cross-attention is employed to aggregate `z_q` and `{z_{r_k}}` into a single, aggregated feature vector `z`. This mechanism allows the model to efficiently gather global context from distant regions in `Z` that might be relevant to the query point, especially for fast-moving objects or long-range interactions (e.g., looking 90 meters back along a lane for an object's past evidence).
 - **Prediction Heads:** Finally, `z` and `z_q` are concatenated along with the original query point `q` and fed into another fully connected ResNet-based architecture with two linear heads to predict the occupancy logits and the flow vector.

#### 3.3. Training

- **Loss Function:** The model is trained by minimizing a combined loss `L = L_o + λ_f L_f`.
 - `L_o` is a binary cross-entropy loss for occupancy, comparing predicted occupancy `ô(q)` with ground truth `o(q)`.
 - `L_f` is an L2 error for flow, applied only to occupied query points (`o(q)=1`), comparing predicted flow `f̂(q)` with ground truth `f(q)`. The flow loss is downweighted by `λ_f = 0.1`.
- **Query Point Sampling:** Crucially, during training, `|Q|` query points are sampled *uniformly* from the continuous spatio-temporal volume `[0, H] × [0, W] × [0, T]`. This continuous sampling is fundamental to the implicit representation and allows the model to generalize beyond fixed grid resolutions.
- **Ground Truth Generation:** Occupancy labels are determined by checking intersection with annotated bounding boxes. Flow vectors are computed using finite differences between consecutive object box annotations, augmented with a CTRA motion model to fill incomplete tracks and maintain occupancy persistence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted on the Argoverse 2 Sensor (AV2, urban) and HighwaySim (HwySim, highway) datasets, rigorously compare IMPLICITO against state-of-the-art object-based and explicit object-free baselines using a comprehensive set of metrics (mAP, Soft-IoU, ECE, EPE, Flow Grounded mAP/Soft-IoU, and inference time).

#### 4.1. Benchmark Against State-of-the-Art

- **Superior Performance:** IMPLICITO consistently outperforms all baselines (MULTIPATH, LANEGCN, GORELA for object-based; OCCFLOW, MP3 for explicit object-free) across all occupancy and flow metrics on both urban (AV2) and highway (HwySim) settings. For example, on AV2, IMPLICITO achieves an mAP of 0.799 and Soft-IoU of 0.480, significantly higher than MP3 (0.774 mAP, 0.422 Soft-IoU) and OCCFLOW (0.675 mAP, 0.356 Soft-IoU). Similar improvements are observed on HwySim.
- **Object-free Advantage:** The results reinforce the general advantage of object-free approaches over object-based ones for occupancy-flow prediction. Object-based methods often underperform, exhibiting issues like missed detections and inconsistencies with map or other actors in qualitative results, as well as poor calibration (high ECE).
- **End-to-End Training:** Ablations show that end-to-end training of object-free methods, even with simpler convolutional decoders (CONVNET/CONVNETFT), significantly improves performance compared to those relying on pre-trained detection and tracking modules (OCCFLOW), highlighting the value of a unified system.

#### 4.2. Influence of Decoder Architecture and Attention Offsets

- **Effectiveness of Implicit Decoder:** IMPLICITO's implicit decoder significantly outperforms other end-to-end trained explicit decoders (CONVNET, CONVNETFT, MP3), demonstrating the efficacy of its design, particularly on HwySim where larger receptive fields are crucial.
- **Importance of Attention Offsets (K):** An ablation study on the number of attention offsets `K` revealed that:
 - `K=1` (one attention offset) provides a substantial performance boost over `K=0` (no attention offsets, relying only on local features), especially on HwySim, confirming that the attention mechanism successfully expands the effective receptive field.
 - `K=4` (four attention offsets) shows mixed results, performing similarly or slightly worse than `K=1` in some metrics. The authors hypothesize that in typical scenarios, one offset effectively guides the model to relevant past locations, and more offsets might introduce noise without providing significant additional benefit for current datasets.
- **Visualization Insights:** Visualizations of backward flow and attention offsets show that the offsets learn to "look backwards" along the lanes, strongly correlating with the direction of backward flow. This validates the intuition that the attention mechanism effectively helps the model retrieve historical evidence for future prediction.

#### 4.3. Inference Time Comparison

- **Efficiency for Planners:** For "planner-relevant" query point counts (e.g., up to ~20,000 queries, corresponding to a large number of candidate trajectories), IMPLICITO exhibits a significantly faster decoder inference time compared to explicit object-free methods like MP3 and OCCFLOW. This is because IMPLICITO avoids computing a dense, scene-wide grid, focusing computation only on queried points. The inference time remains constant until GPU saturation, after which it increases approximately linearly.

#### 4.4. Occupancy Prediction Over Time and Calibration

- **Sustained Accuracy:** IMPLICITO maintains higher mAP and Soft-IoU at later prediction timesteps compared to baselines, indicating its ability to predict farther into the future more accurately.
- **Continuous Prediction Advantage:** Unlike explicit models that suffer from interpolation artifacts when queried at off-grid timesteps, IMPLICITO's continuous prediction ability leads to smoother and more accurate occupancy curves over time.
- **Improved Calibration:** Reliability diagrams show that object-free methods generally, and IMPLICITO specifically, are much better calibrated (i.e., predicted probabilities align with actual accuracy) compared to object-based methods, which tend to be overconfident. This is crucial for downstream planning that relies on probabilistic cost assessments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

IMPLICITO represents a significant step forward in perception and prediction for self-driving vehicles, offering several key advancements and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Safety:** By eliminating the need for detection thresholds, IMPLICITO improves object recall, making the system less prone to missing critical agents. Its superior accuracy, better calibration of predictions, and ability to model expressive, non-parametric distributions of future occupancy contribute to a more robust and safer understanding of the environment, crucial for preventing collisions and enabling reliable decision-making in complex scenarios. The extended effective receptive field is particularly important for high-speed highway driving, allowing earlier detection of distant, fast-moving threats.

2. **Increased Computational Efficiency for Self-Driving Stacks:** The queryable, implicit representation fundamentally shifts the computational paradigm from "compute everything" to "compute only what is needed." This avoids the vast redundancies of dense grid-based predictions, leading to substantial savings in computational resources and memory. This efficiency is critical for real-time deployment in energy-constrained and latency-sensitive autonomous vehicles, directly benefiting the motion planner by providing information on-demand and focusing computation on relevant areas (e.g., around candidate trajectories).

3. **Unified and Simplistic Architecture:** IMPLICITO proposes a unified approach to perception and prediction within a single neural network, moving towards a more end-to-end trainable and integrated autonomy stack. This simplification can reduce the complexity of the overall system, potentially leading to easier maintenance, debugging, and further optimization.

4. **Foundation for Advanced Motion Planning:** By providing accurate, calibrated, and continuously queryable spatio-temporal occupancy and flow fields, IMPLICITO offers a richer and more precise input for motion planners. This enables planners to make more informed decisions, better assess collision probabilities, and navigate dynamic environments with greater confidence and comfort. The ability to query at any continuous spatio-temporal point is a distinct advantage for planners that might require very fine-grained temporal resolution.

5. **Generalizability Across Driving Environments:** The demonstrated superior performance in both challenging urban and high-speed highway settings highlights the robustness and generalizability of the IMPLICITO approach, making it suitable for a wide range of operational domains for autonomous vehicles.

The paper sets the stage for future work, particularly in directly assessing the impact of these improvements on the downstream task of motion planning and exploring more sophisticated sampling methods for training. Overall, IMPLICITO pushes the boundaries of perception and prediction by offering a more efficient, accurate, and safer paradigm that directly addresses the practical needs of autonomous driving systems.
