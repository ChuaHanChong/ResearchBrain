# Deep Imitation Learning of Sequential Fabric Smoothing From an Algorithmic Supervisor

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley and Honda Research Institute developed deep imitation learning policies for sequentially smoothing rumpled fabric on a flat surface using a single-armed robot. The approach, leveraging an algorithmic supervisor in a custom simulator and sim-to-real transfer techniques, demonstrated that combined RGBD input yielded the highest final fabric coverage on a dVRK surgical robot, particularly for challenging initial fabric configurations.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of fabric and other deformable objects is challenging due to their high-dimensional configuration spaces and complex, unpredictable dynamics.
- Existing robotic fabric manipulation methods often rely on simplified fabric states (e.g., suspended, initially flat) or require lifting the fabric entirely, limiting applicability for highly rumpled fabric on a surface.
- Learning effective sequential policies for fabric smoothing from arbitrary rumpled configurations to a flattened state with limited robot actions is difficult.

## Method

- Developed deep imitation learning policies trained on an open-source Finite Element Method (FEM) fabric simulator, utilizing an 'algorithmic supervisor' to generate optimal pick points and pull vectors.
- Employed domain randomization during simulation training and Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) to facilitate robust sim-to-real transfer of learned policies to a physical dVRK surgical robot.
- Systematically evaluated and compared the performance of policies trained with different visual input modalities: color (RGB), depth (D), and combined color-depth (RGBD).

## Results

- Algorithmic Oracle policies achieved approximately **95%** final coverage in simulation, demonstrating the upper bound of performance with full state knowledge.
- Learned policies using DAgger consistently improved final coverage performance by an average of **6.1%** over behavior cloning alone in simulation.
- On a physical dVRK robot, RGBD policies achieved the highest final coverage (e.g., **83.0%** on the hardest tier), outperforming RGB and Depth policies and demonstrating successful sim-to-real transfer for sequential fabric smoothing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An algorithmic supervisor within a custom simulator can efficiently generate large, high-quality datasets for imitation learning, reducing the need for extensive real-world robot demonstrations.
> - DAgger significantly improves the generalization performance of learned policies by iteratively adapting them to states encountered during their own execution, enhancing robustness for sim-to-real transfer.
> - Combined RGBD visual input provides superior performance in real-world fabric smoothing tasks compared to RGB-only or Depth-only, especially for complex and challenging initial fabric configurations.
