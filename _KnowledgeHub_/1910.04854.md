# Deep Imitation Learning of Sequential Fabric Smoothing From an Algorithmic Supervisor

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley and Honda Research Institute developed deep imitation learning policies for sequentially smoothing rumpled fabric on a flat surface using a single-armed robot. The approach, leveraging an algorithmic supervisor in a custom simulator and sim-to-real transfer techniques, demonstrated that combined RGBD input yielded the highest final fabric coverage on a dVRK surgical robot, particularly for challenging initial fabric configurations.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of fabric and other deformable objects is challenging due to their high-dimensional configuration spaces and complex, unpredictable dynamics.
- Existing robotic fabric manipulation methods often rely on simplified fabric states (e.g., suspended, initially flat) or require lifting the fabric entirely, limiting applicability for highly rumpled fabric on a surface.
- Learning effective sequential policies for fabric smoothing from arbitrary rumpled configurations to a flattened state with limited robot actions is difficult.

## Method

- Developed deep imitation learning policies trained on an open-source Finite Element Method (FEM) fabric simulator, utilizing an 'algorithmic supervisor' to generate optimal pick points and pull vectors.
- Employed domain randomization during simulation training and Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) to facilitate robust sim-to-real transfer of learned policies to a physical dVRK surgical robot.
- Systematically evaluated and compared the performance of policies trained with different visual input modalities: color (RGB), depth (D), and combined color-depth (RGBD).

## Results

- Algorithmic Oracle policies achieved approximately **95%** final coverage in simulation, demonstrating the upper bound of performance with full state knowledge.
- Learned policies using DAgger consistently improved final coverage performance by an average of **6.1%** over behavior cloning alone in simulation.
- On a physical dVRK robot, RGBD policies achieved the highest final coverage (e.g., **83.0%** on the hardest tier), outperforming RGB and Depth policies and demonstrating successful sim-to-real transfer for sequential fabric smoothing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An algorithmic supervisor within a custom simulator can efficiently generate large, high-quality datasets for imitation learning, reducing the need for extensive real-world robot demonstrations.
> - DAgger significantly improves the generalization performance of learned policies by iteratively adapting them to states encountered during their own execution, enhancing robustness for sim-to-real transfer.
> - Combined RGBD visual input provides superior performance in real-world fabric smoothing tasks compared to RGB-only or Depth-only, especially for complex and challenging initial fabric configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The robotic manipulation of fabric and other deformable objects presents significant challenges due to their high-dimensional configuration spaces and complex, often unpredictable dynamics. Existing research in robotic fabric manipulation has explored various approaches, including bilateral robot systems that leverage gravity to identify fabric corners for folding or positioning. Examples include methods that iteratively re-grasp the lowest hanging point to flatten fabric, or those that identify and tension corners for tasks like laundry folding. These methods often rely on the fabric being suspended in mid-air or initially having relatively flat configurations, which simplifies perception and manipulation.

More recent advancements have involved machine learning techniques, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), to address the complexities of deformable object manipulation. Some RL applications in fabric folding have assumed the fabric is already flat or begins gripped in mid-air. Model-based RL has been used to train robots for tasks like folding pants, often requiring extensive physical robot execution and video prediction models. In surgical robotics, RL has been applied to learn tensioning policies for cutting gauze. However, many of these approaches either start from semi-structured fabric states or involve lifting the fabric entirely.

This paper specifically addresses the problem of transforming fabric from highly rumpled and disordered configurations to a smooth state using a single-armed robot, without fully lifting the fabric off a surface. This scenario is relevant for larger fabrics or robots with limited motion ranges. The work distinguishes itself from prior fabric smoothing research that focused on explicit wrinkle detection and pulling perpendicular to wrinkles, or methods that pulled at fixed angles from identified corners. While previous work by some of the authors estimated only a pick point, this paper extends it by simultaneously learning both the pick point and the pull vector from visual input. Furthermore, by developing a custom simulator for data generation, the authors circumvent the need for extensive physical robot interaction during initial training, which is a common bottleneck for RL approaches. The use of deep imitation learning from an algorithmic supervisor and the systematic comparison of different visual modalities (RGB, Depth, RGBD) for this specific problem context represent a notable contribution to the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate deep imitation learning policies for sequentially smoothing rectangular fabric samples from rumpled initial configurations to a state of maximal coverage on a flat plane. The motivation stems from the broad range of applications for fabric manipulation, including senior care, dressing assistance, sewing, ironing, laundry folding, fabric upholstery manufacturing, and handling gauze in robotic surgery. The inherent difficulty of manipulating fabric due to its infinite-dimensional configuration space and complex dynamics makes direct control challenging, thus motivating a learning-based approach.

Specific objectives include:
- **Learning Sequential Policies:** To learn policies that, given visual observations (color, depth, or combined color-depth images), can estimate appropriate pick points and pull vectors to spread fabric and maximize its coverage. The policies are designed to execute a sequence of such actions.
- **Leveraging Algorithmic Supervision:** To generate training data efficiently by developing a fabric simulator coupled with an "algorithmic supervisor." This supervisor has access to complete fabric state information, enabling it to provide optimal actions for data generation, thereby avoiding the need for human demonstrations or extensive real-world robot trials during initial policy training.
- **Addressing Data Distribution Mismatch:** To mitigate the domain gap between simulated training data and real-world deployment by employing domain randomization during simulation training and utilizing Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) to adapt the policy to states encountered during its own execution.
- **Evaluating Sensor Modalities:** To systematically compare the performance of learned policies when using different visual input modalities: color (RGB), depth (D), or combined color-depth (RGBD) images, both in simulation and on a physical robot. This aims to identify the most effective sensing approach for fabric smoothing.
- **Developing a Reproducible Environment:** To contribute an open-source simulation environment and dataset with defined difficulty tiers for initial fabric states, facilitating future research and standardized evaluation in fabric smoothing.
- **Demonstrating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To validate the learned policies by transferring them from simulation to a physical da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK) surgical robot, thereby demonstrating the practical applicability of the approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves developing a fabric simulator, designing an action space, defining baseline policies, and employing deep imitation learning with domain randomization and DAgger for policy training, followed by physical robot experiments.

**A. Fabric and Robot Simulator:**
A Finite Element Method (FEM) fabric simulator, implemented in Python with Cython, forms the core of the data generation environment. The fabric is modeled as a 25x25 grid of point masses connected by three types of springs: structural, shear, and flexion. Point mass positions are updated using Verlet integration, incorporating gravitational forces, spring correction forces, and damping. Provot's constraints ensure spring lengths do not exceed 10% of their default, and fabric-fabric collisions are handled by adding separation forces. The simulator provides full fabric state ($\xi_t$), while image observations ($o_t$) are generated by rendering a triangular mesh of the fabric in Blender 2.79 (chosen for its headless rendering capabilities).

**B. Actions:**
An action at time *t* is a 4D vector: $\langle x_t, y_t, \Delta x_t, \Delta y_t \rangle$. $(x_t, y_t)$ represent the pick point on the fabric plane, and $(\Delta x_t, \Delta y_t)$ represent the pull vector relative to the pick point. The simulator executes actions through six stages:
1. **Grasp:** The gripper moves downwards at $(x_t, y_t)$, grasping points within a small sphere (radius 0.003 units) on the top layer of the fabric. These points become "pinned."
2. **Pull Up:** Pinned points' z-coordinates are adjusted upwards (dz = 0.0025 units/iteration) for 50 iterations, while x and y remain fixed.
3. **First Pause:** Pinned points remain fixed for 80 iterations, allowing non-pinned points to settle.
4. **Linear Pull to Target:** Pinned points' x and y coordinates are adjusted according to $(\Delta x_t, \Delta y_t)$ for a variable number of iterations based on pull length, with z fixed.
5. **Second Pause:** Pinned points remain fixed for 300 iterations for further settling.
6. **Drop:** Pinned points are "un-pinned" and allowed to fall, and the entire cloth settles for 1000 iterations.

**C. Starting State Distributions:**
Three difficulty tiers for initial fabric configurations are defined:
- **Tier 1 (High Coverage, ~78.3%):** Two short, random pulls from a flat fabric, leaving all corners visible. A third short pull if coverage remains above 90%.
- **Tier 2 (Medium Coverage, ~57.6%):** Fabric drops from mid-air, followed by one random grasp-and-pull, and then a second to cover a corner.
- **Tier 3 (Low Coverage, ~41.1%):** A single high pull from a flat fabric, dragged in a random direction and dropped, typically resulting in hidden corners.

**D. Baseline Policies:**
Five baseline policies are evaluated:
1. **Random:** Selects pick points and pull directions uniformly at random.
2. **Highest (Max z):** Grasps one of the five highest points on the fabric (to avoid sticking) and pulls it towards its corresponding target position if the fabric were flat.
3. **Wrinkle:** Approximates Sun et al. by finding the point of largest local height variance (the largest wrinkle), identifying a neighboring point with the next largest variance, and pulling perpendicular to the vector between them towards the edge of the fabric plane.
4. **Oracle:** Uses full state information ($\xi_t$) to identify the fabric corner furthest from its target position on the fabric plane and pulls it towards that target. If a corner is occluded, it grasps the point directly above it.
5. **Oracle-Expose:** An extension of the Oracle policy. If all corners are occluded or visible corners are near targets, it performs a revealing action on an occluded corner by pulling away from its target.

**E. Imitation Learning with DAgger:**
The Oracle policy (not Oracle-Expose) serves as the algorithmic supervisor.
- **Policy Architecture:** A neural network processes 100x100xC input images (C=1 for D, C=3 for RGB, C=4 for RGBD). It consists of four convolutional layers (32 filters, 3x3 kernel each), followed by four dense layers (256 units each), totaling approximately 3.4 million parameters. The output is a 4D vector, with a hyperbolic tangent activation mapping components to [-1, 1].
- **Training Procedure:** OpenAI baselines are used with 10 parallel simulator environments.
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC) Phase:** Initial training minimizes L2 error on an offline dataset of 2000 supervisor-generated episodes per tier (500 epochs).
 - **DAgger Phase:** After BC, the agent's policy is rolled out. For every 20 steps per parallel environment (200 new samples), the Oracle supervisor labels the encountered states. These new (observation, action) pairs are added to the dataset, and the policy is retrained for 240 minibatch updates (size 128). This continues until 50,000 total agent steps.
- **Domain Randomization:** Applied during training to enhance sim-to-real transfer:
 - **RGB:** Randomization of fabric colors, background plane shading, camera pose (Gaussian noise to position/orientation), and brightness (gamma correction).
 - **Depth:** Randomization of camera pose and darkening of images to match real depth sensor characteristics.
 - **Both:** Uniform pixel noise is added.

**F. Physical Experiments:**
- **Robot System:** A single arm of a da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK) surgical robot is used with a gripper (10mm width, 75° opening). A foam rubber fabric plane prevents gripper damage.
- **Camera:** A Zivid One Plus RGBD camera is mounted 0.9 meters above the workspace.
- **Fabric:** 5x5 inch blue microfiber towel, within the randomized color distribution.
- **Experiment Protocol:** 20 episodes per input modality (RGB, D, RGBD) per tier (180 total episodes). Fabric is manually reset to similar initial states across comparable episodes.
- **Image Processing Pipeline (Real Images):**
 1. **Depth only:** In-painting for missing values.
 2. **Both:** Cropped to 100x100.
 3. **Depth only:** Clipped to min/max depth, converted to 3 channels (triplicating values), scaled to, and histogram equalized.
 4. **Both:** Bilateral filtering and de-noising (OpenCV functions).
- **Robot Calibration:** Checkerboard-based calibration maps neural network output to robot frame. Bilinear interpolation for unknown positions.
- **Grasp Heuristic:** If the robot misses fabric (detected by structural similarity of images before/after action), the next pick point is adjusted closer to the fabric plane's center until contact is made.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Simulation Results for Baseline Policies:**
- **Oracle Policies:** Both Oracle and Oracle-Expose achieved the highest average final coverage (approximately 95%) across all three difficulty tiers, requiring the fewest actions (1.7-4.7 actions). This demonstrates the upper bound of performance with full state knowledge.
- **Wrinkle Policy:** Achieved the next highest coverage (91.3%, 87.0%, 73.6% for Tiers 1-3 respectively), but required substantially more actions per episode (5.4-8.9 actions) compared to the Oracle policies.
- **Highest (Max z) Policy:** Showed moderate performance, especially on Tier 1 (66.2% coverage), but significantly declined on harder tiers (36.3% on Tier 3).
- **Random Policy:** Performed lowest, with coverage around 20-25%.

**B. Simulation Experiments for Learned Policies:**
- **DAgger Improvement:** DAgger consistently improved final coverage performance by an average of 6.1% over behavior cloning alone across all experimental conditions (RGB, D, RGBD across three tiers), demonstrating its effectiveness in addressing generalization issues.
- **RGB vs. D vs. RGBD:**
 - **RGB Policies:** Generally attained higher coverage in simulation than D policies, with gains of 10.8% (Tier 1), 8.3% (Tier 2), and 10.9% (Tier 3) over their depth-only counterparts. This was attributed to high color contrast in simulated images. RGB policies outperformed the Wrinkle baseline across all tiers.
 - **RGBD Policies:** Performed similarly to RGB-based policies in simulation, indicating that depth information did not provide a significant additional benefit in the simulation environment where color cues were strong. RGBD policies also outperformed the Wrinkle baseline.
 - **Depth-only Policies:** Outperformed the Wrinkle policy on Tier 3 (80.3% vs. 73.6%) but generally lagged behind RGB and RGBD policies in simulation.

**C. Physical Experiment Results:**
180 physical experiments (20 episodes per modality/tier) demonstrated sim-to-real transfer capabilities.
- **Overall Performance:** All learned policies successfully improved fabric coverage over their initial states across all tiers. Average net coverage gains over starting coverage were 25.2% (RGB), 7.8% (D), and 33.4% (RGBD).
- **Tier 1 (Easier States):**
 - RGB policies achieved high final coverage (96.2%), reaching the 92% threshold in all 20 episodes.
 - RGBD policies also performed strongly (95.0% final coverage), reaching the threshold in all 20 episodes.
 - D policies showed lower and more variable performance (78.8% final coverage).
- **Tier 2 (Medium States):**
 - RGBD policies achieved the highest final coverage (91.3%), with strong maximum coverage (92.7%).
 - RGB policies performed well (87.7% final coverage, 92.7% max coverage).
 - D policies again showed lower performance (64.9% final coverage).
- **Tier 3 (Harder States):**
 - RGBD policies demonstrated the strongest performance (83.0% final coverage, 85.8% max coverage), significantly outperforming RGB and D policies.
 - RGB policies achieved 75.0% final coverage.
 - D policies performed lowest (63.2% final coverage).
- **Conclusion on Modalities:** On harder starting fabric configurations, combined RGBD policies yielded the highest coverage, suggesting depth sensing is a valuable addition to color information in real-world scenarios, particularly when color cues might be ambiguous or less distinct than in simulation.

**D. Failure Cases:**
- **Depth-only Policies:** Exhibited susceptibility to highly counter-productive actions, sometimes pulling near the center of an almost smooth fabric, which substantially decreased coverage. This was attributed to potential issues with uneven fabric texture in real depth images not being perfectly replicated in simulation, or ambiguity when multiple corners are equally far from targets.
- **RGB Policies (on novel colors):** When tested on yellow fabric (a color outside the domain randomization distribution), the RGB policy performed poorly, failing to pick at corners or pull in correct directions, resulting in decreased coverage. The depth-only policy, being color-invariant, performed better in this specific scenario.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates the feasibility and effectiveness of using deep imitation learning with an algorithmic supervisor for sequential fabric smoothing, particularly for complex, rumpled fabric states. The key contributions and their potential impact are:

- **Accessible Data Generation:** The development of an open-source FEM fabric simulator and an algorithmic supervisor provides an efficient method for generating large datasets without reliance on physical robot demonstrations or human supervision. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for training deformable object manipulation policies, as it bypasses the extensive data collection typically required in real-world robotic setups.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful transfer of policies trained purely in simulation (using domain randomization and DAgger) to a physical surgical robot (dVRK) highlights the robustness of the approach. This success suggests that learned policies can generalize to real-world fabric variations, camera noise, and robot imprecision, which is crucial for practical applications.
- **Value of Multimodal Sensing:** The systematic comparison of RGB, D, and RGBD inputs clarifies the utility of different sensor modalities. While RGB was strong in simulation and on easier physical tasks, the superior performance of RGBD policies on challenging physical fabric configurations indicates that depth information provides valuable complementary cues for deformable object manipulation in real-world settings, where color alone may be insufficient or unreliable.
- **Enabling Robotic Autonomy for Deformable Objects:** The ability to learn sequential fabric smoothing policies from visual input moves closer to enabling robots to autonomously handle deformable objects in diverse scenarios. This has significant potential impact across various domains:
 - **Healthcare:** Assisting in tasks like bed-making or handling surgical gauze, improving efficiency and potentially safety in medical settings.
 - **Manufacturing:** Automating processes in fabric upholstery, apparel, and textile industries, reducing manual labor and increasing production rates.
 - **Domestic Robotics:** Enhancing capabilities of household robots for tasks such as laundry folding or tidying.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The open-source simulator and dataset provide a standardized benchmark for future work in fabric manipulation. The identified failure modes and the comparative analysis of sensor modalities offer specific directions for improving perception and control strategies for deformable objects.

Future work outlined by the authors includes testing on different fabric shapes and configurations, exploring state-of-the-art deep reinforcement learning methods (e.g., model-based RL for improved sample efficiency), utilizing higher-fidelity simulators like ARCSim, and extending the methodology to broader tasks like folding and wrapping, as well as to other deformable objects like ropes and strings. These extensions could further broaden the applicability and impact of this research in the field of robotic manipulation.
