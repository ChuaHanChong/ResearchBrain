# Visual General Intelligence: A White Paper

> [!summary] Summary
> A CVPR 2026 workshop white paper asking whether intelligence can emerge from visual experience itself rather than from language. It assembles ten researcher position statements spanning generative video foundation models, algorithmic creativity, lifelong visual learning, spatial AI, embodied closed loops, physical structure recovery, and vision-native attention, and concludes that VGI is irreducibly plural and demands benchmarks beyond static per-task accuracy.

## Problem

- Computer vision remains fragmented into task-specific models for detection, depth, and reconstruction, never unified around a common framework the way language models unified NLP.
- In vision-language models, image features, instruction tuning, and reasoning are entangled, so capabilities arising from visual experience cannot be isolated from those supplied by the language model.
- Generative models judged on perceptual plausibility can render a convincing falling cup while representing nothing about mass, contact, friction, or articulation anywhere inside them.

## Method

- A Table 1 taxonomy consolidates ten invited positions into groupings: scaling and generation, creativity, continual learning, ==persistent spatial memory==, embodiment and active perception, multimodal efficiency, and scientific discovery.
- Kataoka's **convergence hypothesis** proposes three complementary objectives, ==sequential prediction==, ==open-ended generation==, and ==reconstruction==, as the route from vision foundation models to visual general intelligence.
- Wu and Wu's ==structure decomposition== (entities, intrinsics, extrinsics, relations, dynamics) plus the **Scene Language**, a hybrid representation pairing ==symbolic programs== with learned ==per-entity embeddings==.

## Results

- Veo 3, trained only for entertainment video, performs **eight tasks** zero-shot: edge detection, segmentation, keypoint localization, super-resolution, editing, style transfer, maze solving, and graph traversal.
- **Eight** subsequent works, including open-source video models, replicated and extended these emergent zero-shot capabilities, supporting the claim that video generators already act as visual foundation models.
- On controlled algorithmic-creativity tasks, teacherless and diffusion multi-token training produced more diverse and original solutions than conventional next-token learning; no numeric benchmark is reported.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative objectives resist shortcut learning: a classifier can predict cow from grass, but a generator is penalized unless object, lighting, shadow, and texture are all right.
> - Photorealism is not physics. Test a representation by whether it can be edited, simulated, intervened on, and verified, not by how plausible its renders look.
> - Vision lacks a token-like compression layer, so the question of visual units is inseparable from deciding when to look, where to look, and at what detail.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This white paper positions itself within the contemporary landscape of Artificial Intelligence, particularly in the context of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). It draws a direct parallel with the recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), such as the GPT series, which have demonstrated emergent capabilities and transfer to unseen tasks through aggressive scaling and autoregressive language modeling on web-scale text. The authors pose the fundamental question of whether similar forms of intelligence and capabilities can emerge from visual modalities like images, videos, and geometry.

Historically, the paper notes that intelligence has often been discussed as the ability to acquire representations, capture regularities, generalize, and connect representations to prediction or action. However, it challenges the notion that intelligence should be solely reduced to language, highlighting the evolutionary history of advanced visual systems in Earth's history predating complex human language. This perspective suggests that vision may be a foundational intelligent function for understanding world structure, rather than merely a sensory input.

The paper acknowledges the transformative shift in computer vision from hand-designed features to deep learning, and more recently, to self-supervised learning, image-text contrastive learning, generative models, 3D/4D reconstruction, and embodied AI. It recognizes the significant progress in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like CLIP, Flamingo, BLIP/BLIP-2, and LLaVA, which integrate visual and linguistic information. However, it distinguishes its focus by exploring intelligence emerging specifically from visual experience, questioning which capabilities originate from visual experience itself versus those emerging from language models or their interaction.

The work aims to broaden the scope of computer vision research in the AGI era by exploring "Visual General Intelligence (VGI)"—intelligence that arises from visual modalities and tasks. It encourages the community to consider principles, benchmarks, and learning paradigms specific to vision's role as a core driver of intelligence, rather than solely as a component subservient to language.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this white paper is to reconsider intelligence from a vision-centric perspective and examine whether intelligence emerging from visual experience and learning may offer a pathway toward AGI. The authors are motivated by the following detailed goals:

- **Explore Visual Intelligence as a Pathway to AGI:** The central motivation is to investigate the potential for vision to be a core driver of general intelligence, analogous to how language models have demonstrated broad capabilities. This involves questioning what forms of intelligence can emerge specifically from visual data (images, videos, geometry).
- **Clarify Principles for Computer Vision in the AGI Era:** Rather than providing a single definition for visual intelligence, the paper aims to delineate key principles that computer vision research should pursue. This includes identifying relevant visual input modalities, appropriate benchmarks for evaluation, effective learning paradigms, and understanding the intrinsic relationship between vision (when taken as the core) and other modalities, particularly language.
- **Investigate Emergent Abilities in Visual Models:** Drawing inspiration from the "emergent abilities" observed in scaled-up language models, a key motivation is to ascertain whether similar unexpected capabilities can arise when visual models are scaled and exposed to increasingly diverse visual experiences. This involves considering whether vision requires unique principles or if scaling alone might suffice.
- **Challenge Language-Centric Views of Intelligence:** The paper argues against reducing intelligence solely to language. It highlights the evolutionary precedence of vision over language, suggesting that the visual modality might constitute a foundational intelligent function for understanding the world's structure. This motivates a "vision-first" or "vision-only" exploration before integrating with language.
- **Stimulate Proactive Research Design:** The authors intend to encourage the computer vision community to move beyond incremental improvements and benchmark accuracy. They propose proactively designing what should be measured in the AGI era, including real-world applications, adaptation to unknown environments, and societal impact.
- **Synthesize Diverse Expert Perspectives:** The white paper's structure, which gathers contributions from various researchers with different standpoints and affiliations, is motivated by the belief that VGI is not yet a fully established concept. Bringing together diverse hypotheses rooted in individual expertise is intended to foster open, optimistic, and bold exploration of potential pathways from vision to general intelligence.
- **Establish Vision's Foundational Role:** The paper aims to broaden the perceived role of vision research in the AGI era by emphasizing its capacity to understand, predict, and generalize from dense, observable, and measurable visual structures, either independently or in interaction with language.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This white paper employs a discursive and conceptual methodology rather than presenting novel empirical research or a single unified model. Its approach is characterized by:

- **Synthesis of Expert Perspectives:** The core methodology involves bringing together diverse viewpoints from leading researchers in computer vision and related fields. Each contributor presents their understanding of Visual General Intelligence (VGI), key challenges, and proposed directions. This collective input forms the basis for the paper's overarching discussion.
- **Comparative Analysis with LLMs:** The paper frequently references the development trajectory and emergent properties of Large Language Models (LLMs). This comparison serves as a framework for exploring analogous scaling laws, learning objectives, and potential emergent capabilities within the visual domain.
- **Identification of Key Research Areas:** Through the individual perspectives, the paper implicitly outlines critical research areas for VGI. These include:
 - **Generative Modeling:** Emphasized by Geirhos, Ramanan, Du, Raghunathan, and Kataoka et al., advocating for large-scale generative models, particularly video models, due to their demanding training objectives that require deep world understanding.
 - **Creativity and Open-ended Generation:** Raghunathan focuses on designing evaluations and learning objectives for generating diverse and original solutions, moving beyond single-correct-answer tasks.
 - **Lifelong and Continual Learning:** Asano, Davison, Du, and Liu advocate for systems that learn autonomously and adapt continuously throughout their operational lifetime, maintaining persistent representations rather than remaining fixed after pre-training.
 - **Embodied Intelligence and Active Perception:** Du and Liu stress the importance of an agent's interaction with the physical world, where action guides perception and learning, allowing the system to actively seek informative experiences.
 - **Recovery of Physical Structure ("Code of the World"):** Shangzhe Wu & Jiajun Wu propose that VGI should aim to infer underlying physical properties (geometry, materials, dynamics, relations) rather than merely pixel-level appearance, enabling intervention, simulation, and verification.
 - **Spatial AI and Persistent Representations:** Davison highlights the need for rich, efficient, and adaptable spatial representations, evolving from SLAM research, and considering future hardware architectures.
 - **Multimodal Integration and Efficiency:** Ramanan emphasizes integrating vision with other sensory modalities (audio, tactile, proprioceptive) and advocates for computational efficiency in both training and inference.
 - **Scientific Discovery:** Fouhey discusses the application of visual intelligence to scientific problems, which often involve limited data, lack of conventional ground truth, and the need for validation against domain knowledge and sensor systematics.
 - **Vision-Native Abstraction:** Liu proposes that visual systems should learn their own optimal units of perception and attention, deciding *when*, *where*, and *how much* detail to observe based on task relevance.
 - **Convergent Learning Objectives:** Kataoka et al. synthesize a hypothesis that VGI may emerge from the convergence of sequential prediction, open-ended generation, and reconstruction.
- **Identification of Challenges:** The paper systematically identifies current limitations and open questions, such as the computational expense of visual learning, the difficulty in obtaining diverse visual data, the lack of symbolic units in vision comparable to language, and the fragmentation of current visual capabilities.
- **Discussion of Evaluation Paradigms:** It implicitly (through individual perspectives) and explicitly (in the summary) advocates for new evaluation metrics that go beyond traditional accuracy, including measures of coherence, diversity, originality, physical consistency, transferability, and the ability to adapt continually.
- **Conceptual Framing:** The paper's overall approach is to conceptually frame VGI as a broad capacity emerging from visual experience, not reducible to isolated tasks or modalities, and to articulate a research agenda for its exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a white paper, this document does not present empirical results in the traditional sense. Instead, its "findings" are the collective conclusions, hypotheses, and proposed research directions synthesized from the diverse perspectives of the contributing authors. These can be summarized as follows:

- **VGI Definition and Scope:** Visual General Intelligence (VGI) is conceptualized as the capacity to acquire usable knowledge of the world from visual experience and apply it to prediction, imagination, reconstruction, and new tasks. It emphasizes intelligence emerging directly from visual experience, going beyond stronger image classifiers or visual encoders attached to Large Language Models (LLMs). VGI concerns discovering and verifying world structure, maintaining and updating knowledge over time, predicting futures, imagining alternatives, reconstructing unobserved structure, and adapting behavior.
- **Central Role of Generative Learning:** A recurring theme is the belief that generative models, particularly video models trained on web-scale data, are foundational for VGI. This is because generating realistic and coherent visual data necessitates a deeper understanding of the world's underlying structure (e.g., objects, backgrounds, textures, lighting, shadows, physics) than mere recognition tasks.
- **Beyond Pixel Prediction to Physical Understanding:** The paper highlights that generating photorealistic pixels, while impressive, does not inherently guarantee an understanding of the physical world. VGI requires recovering the "code" of the physical world—entities, intrinsic properties (geometry, material), extrinsic properties (pose, illumination), relations, and dynamics. This structured understanding enables capabilities like editing, simulation, verification, and reliable action.
- **Importance of Continual and Lifelong Learning:** VGI systems should not be static models. They are envisioned as continuously adaptive learners that develop throughout their visual lifetime. This involves building persistent, revisable world models from unfolding visual experience, without relying on labels or complete retraining, and effectively integrating new evidence while retaining acquired knowledge.
- **Embodied Intelligence and Active Perception:** Visual intelligence is strongly linked to embodied interaction. Agents that can actively select viewpoints, intervene in the world, and obtain feedback on their actions can resolve perceptual ambiguities and improve their visual models. This forms a closed loop where vision guides action, and action refines visual understanding.
- **Multimodality and Efficiency:** While vision-centric, the importance of multimodal integration beyond language, including tactile, audio, and proprioceptive signals, is recognized, especially for robotics. Efficiency in training and inference is also identified as crucial, suggesting a re-incorporation of classic representations (e.g., recurrence, 3D, multi-scale processing) and exploring methods like reinforcement learning for data curation.
- **New Evaluation Paradigms:** Traditional accuracy on fixed benchmarks is deemed insufficient for VGI. New evaluation criteria are proposed, including:
 - **Creativity:** Coherence, structural diversity, originality, and utility of generated outputs.
 - **Physical Consistency:** Whether recovered structures support intervention and simulation according to physical laws.
 - **Persistent Knowledge:** The ability to maintain and update world models over long durations.
 - **Transfer and Adaptation:** Generalization to novel tasks and environments without extensive retraining.
 - **Active Observation:** The capacity to determine *when* to look, *where* to look, and *how much* detail is needed.
- **Vision-Native Abstraction:** The concept that visual systems should develop their own "units of intelligence" and abstraction hierarchies (e.g., object-centric, hierarchical representations) rather than relying on language's symbolic units or human-designed tasks. This implies a need for significantly more effective compute and diverse data processing for these abstractions to emerge.
- **Convergence of Learning Objectives:** A central hypothesis presented is that VGI may emerge from the convergence of three fundamental learning objectives: sequential prediction (anticipating what comes next), open-ended generation (imagining what could exist), and reconstruction (inferring hidden structure from incomplete observations).
- **The Immediate Milestone:** The paper concludes that while a clear path from VGI to AGI is still nascent, the more immediate and critical milestone is to demonstrate that *intelligence can emerge from visual experience itself*, evidenced by transferable capabilities, persistent world knowledge, creative reasoning, active information seeking, continual adaptation, spatial and physical consistency, and reliable action in unfamiliar situations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This white paper carries significant implications for the future direction of Artificial Intelligence research, particularly within the computer vision community. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

- **Reframing the Role of Computer Vision in AGI:** The paper challenges the prevailing view that complex reasoning and abstraction primarily reside in language models, with vision serving merely as an input channel. By advocating for "Visual General Intelligence," it elevates vision to a foundational role, proposing it as an independent or pre-linguistic pathway to general intelligence. This reorientation could inspire a paradigm shift in how visual systems are designed and evaluated.
- **Shaping a New Research Agenda:** The diverse perspectives and identified open questions collectively lay out a comprehensive and ambitious research agenda for VGI. This includes emphasizing large-scale generative models, the recovery of physical world structure, continual and embodied learning, multimodal integration, and computational efficiency. This agenda provides guiding principles for future research directions, funding initiatives, and interdisciplinary collaborations.
- **Driving Innovation in Model Architectures and Learning Paradigms:** The paper's call for systems that learn throughout their lifetime, actively observe, and recover underlying physical "code" will likely spur innovation in model architectures (e.g., stateful representations, hierarchical dynamic models) and learning paradigms (e.g., self-supervised learning, reinforcement learning for data curation, convergent objectives of prediction, generation, and reconstruction).
- **Developing Advanced Evaluation Metrics:** The critique of traditional accuracy metrics and the proposal for evaluating creativity, physical consistency, persistent knowledge, and active learning can lead to the development of more sophisticated benchmarks. These new metrics would better capture the "intelligence" of visual systems beyond narrow task performance, fostering research on real-world generalization and robustness.
- **Enabling More Capable Embodied AI and Robotics:** By emphasizing the closed loop between vision, action, and continuous adaptation, the paper highlights a critical pathway for developing more autonomous, robust, and generally intelligent robots. These systems could perceive, reason about, and act reliably in complex, open-ended physical environments, potentially revolutionizing areas like automation, personal assistance, and exploration.
- **Facilitating Scientific Discovery:** The discussion on applying visual intelligence to scientific data, particularly in domains with limited ground truth and complex systematics, opens new avenues for AI to accelerate scientific understanding. By developing systems that can interpret specialized instrument data, reconcile it with physical knowledge, and handle inherent sensor biases, AI could become a more powerful tool for scientific discovery.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Research:** The paper inherently encourages collaboration across computer vision, machine learning, robotics, cognitive science, and potentially even philosophy and neuroscience. Insights from biological vision, human cognition, and the computational structure of the brain are explicitly or implicitly referenced, suggesting a more holistic approach to understanding and building intelligence.
- **Addressing Computational Challenges:** By highlighting the computational expense and data requirements, the paper implicitly calls for research into more efficient algorithms, hardware co-design, and intelligent data curation strategies. This could drive advancements in resource-aware AI and sustainable AI development.

In summary, this white paper serves as a conceptual cornerstone, aiming to redirect a significant portion of AI research toward a vision-centric pathway to general intelligence. It provides a robust framework for envisioning, developing, and evaluating future AI systems that can learn, understand, and interact with the physical world through visual experience.
