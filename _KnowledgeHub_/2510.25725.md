# A Humanoid Visual-Tactile-Action Dataset for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Kwon et al. introduce the first humanoid visual-tactile-action dataset for manipulating deformable soft objects under diverse pressure conditions, collected using dexterous hands with 2,124 high-resolution tactile sensors. The work demonstrates that dense tactile signals provide superior discriminative power for task conditions compared to sparse representations, although current policy learning optimization methods face challenges in fully leveraging this high-dimensional data.

## Problem

- Existing robot learning datasets primarily focus on rigid objects, neglecting the complexities of contact-rich manipulation with deformable soft objects.
- Prior datasets lack adequate representation of diverse pressure conditions and often utilize basic robotic grippers without anthropomorphic hands or high-resolution, spatially distributed tactile sensing.
- Current multi-modal datasets for manipulation often lack action labels or humanoid embodiments, limiting their applicability to complex real-world tasks requiring sensitive interactive control.

## Method

![[2510.25725-method.png]]
*(a) Overview of the teleoperation system including the Apple Vision Pro and Unitree G1 humanoid. (b) Deformable soft objects (towel, sponge) and (c) rigid objects (rolling pin, box) used for comparative tactile analysis.*

- **HumanoidVTA** — a novel humanoid ==visual-tactile-action dataset== using a teleoperated robot equipped with ==Inspire Hands==, featuring **2,124 high-resolution tactile sensors** across fingers and palm.
- Collected multi-modal data (==proprioception==, ==egocentric vision==, ==dense tactile signals==) from the humanoid robot manipulating soft objects (towel, sponge) under distinct 'strong' and 'weak' pressure conditions across four tasks.
- Employed a state-of-the-art imitation learning baseline (==Action Chunking Transformer (ACT)==) to evaluate the utility of the dataset, comparing policy learning performance with dense versus sparse tactile inputs.

## Results

- Dense tactile signals, visualized through **t-SNE**, clearly separated distinct task conditions and pressure levels, whereas sparse signals failed to do so, highlighting the discriminative power of high-resolution sensing.
- Policy learning experiments using ACT showed stable training loss reduction, but a relatively **small MAE gap** between models trained with dense vs. sparse tactile signals, suggesting current optimization limitations.
- Real-world deployment demonstrated the robot's ability to adapt actions to task conditions, though tasks requiring precise contact control (e.g., sponge grasping) presented challenges and would benefit from richer data and extended training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Manipulation of soft objects induces dynamic, complex, and time-varying tactile patterns across diverse contact regions, contrasting with the stable patterns observed during rigid object interaction.
> - Dense tactile signals provide a significantly more discriminative representation for distinguishing different tasks and pressure conditions compared to low-resolution sparse tactile signals.
> - Effectively leveraging the high dimensionality and potential noise of dense tactile signals for policy learning remains a challenge for current optimization strategies, indicating a need for more efficient processing methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces fundamental challenges in enabling robots to perform complex tasks involving contact-rich interactions in real-world environments. Recent advancements in multi-modal robotics have shown that integrating diverse sensing modalities, such as vision and touch, can enhance manipulation performance by providing comprehensive real-world information and expanding control capabilities. This integration allows robots to execute more sensitive motions and achieve interactive control, particularly when interacting with humans or objects possessing complex shapes and varied tactile properties. To address the complexity of real-world scenarios, it is crucial to collect multi-modal data across various robotic embodiments and task domains to ensure that learned representations are generalizable.

Prior research on manipulation tasks has predominantly concentrated on rigid objects. In such contexts, simply detecting contact is often sufficient, leading to the adoption of simpler tactile sensors to reduce cost and system complexity. Consequently, many existing datasets and studies do not adequately capture the spatially distributed and volumetric nature of real contact interactions, nor do they account for the diverse pressure conditions required during complex manipulation tasks. Most previous datasets were collected using basic robotic grippers and primarily surface-level tactile data, which limits their applicability to contact-rich scenarios. Existing visual-tactile datasets, while leveraging high-resolution image-based tactile sensors, have largely focused on texture recognition without incorporating action labels, or they lack anthropomorphic hands and humanoid embodiments. While some recent efforts have introduced dense tactile collections or addressed anthropomorphic hands, they often still assume rigid settings and underrepresent the manipulation of soft objects.

This work directly addresses these identified gaps by focusing on contact-rich manipulation of deformable soft objects, a significantly more challenging task due to continuous shape changes during interaction. It emphasizes the necessity of rich multi-modal signals from humanoid robots to accurately reflect the intricacies of real-world manipulation. By collecting a dataset that specifically targets soft objects under controlled pressure using a humanoid robot with dexterous hands, this research contributes to bridging the gap between current robotic capabilities and the requirements for performing sensitive, complex tasks in dynamic environments. It aims to provide a resource that can motivate and support the development of models capable of effectively leveraging the complexity and diversity of dense tactile signals for advanced robotic intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the limitations of existing robot learning datasets, which have primarily focused on rigid objects and have not adequately represented the diversity of pressure conditions crucial for real-world contact-rich manipulation, especially with deformable soft objects. The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **To develop and present a novel humanoid visual-tactile-action dataset:** The core motivation is to introduce a dataset that fills a significant void in robot learning. Existing datasets often lack data from humanoid robots, ignore deformable soft objects, or do not capture the nuanced pressure conditions required for realistic manipulation. This dataset aims to be the first of its kind, integrating visual, tactile, and action data collected from a humanoid robot manipulating soft objects under diverse contact conditions.
- **To capture multi-modal interactions under varying pressure conditions:** The research is motivated by the understanding that real-world manipulation involves dynamic and varied pressure applications. Traditional tactile sensors or contact detection methods are insufficient for tasks involving deformable objects. Therefore, a key objective is to record rich multi-modal data (proprioception, egocentric vision, high-resolution tactile signals) from dexterous humanoid hands while explicitly controlling and varying the applied pressure (strong vs. weak conditions).
- **To highlight the importance of high-resolution dense tactile sensing for soft object manipulation:** The study aims to demonstrate that manipulating soft objects induces complex, time-varying tactile patterns that cannot be adequately captured by sparse or low-resolution tactile sensors. By comparing dense and sparse tactile representations, the researchers intend to empirically validate the necessity of high-resolution tactile data for effectively distinguishing and understanding different contact conditions and task characteristics.
- **To demonstrate the utility of the dataset for policy learning:** A practical objective is to show how the collected dataset can be leveraged to train robot manipulation policies. This involves using a state-of-the-art imitation learning baseline (Action Chunking Transformer, ACT) and evaluating its performance when trained with either high-resolution dense tactile signals or low-resolution sparse signals. This serves to investigate the impact of tactile sensing resolution on learning performance.
- **To introduce a neural network architecture for efficient fusion of dense tactile information:** As part of enabling effective policy learning, the work implicitly aims to develop or adapt a method for processing and integrating the high-dimensional, spatially structured dense tactile data (e.g., converting tactile signals to image-like representations for CNN processing) within a multi-modal learning framework.
- **To conduct an in-depth analysis of the dataset:** Beyond mere collection, a motivation is to analyze the characteristics of the dense tactile signals, including their temporal evolution, spatial distribution across the hand, and how these patterns vary with object properties (rigid vs. soft) and pressure conditions. This analysis aims to provide insights into the nature of contact-rich manipulation.
- **To motivate future research on advanced optimization strategies:** The paper identifies the challenge of effectively leveraging the complexity and diversity of dense tactile signals within current robot learning models. A core motivation is to inspire future research into more efficient signal processing and optimization strategies specifically designed to handle the high dimensionality and potential noise associated with dense tactile data, ultimately leading to improved robotic intelligence for soft object manipulation.

In essence, the research is motivated by the recognized gap in datasets and methods for enabling robots, particularly humanoids with dexterous hands, to perform skillful contact-rich manipulation of deformable objects in real-world scenarios. It seeks to provide a foundational dataset and initial analyses to drive progress in this complex domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the collection and analysis of a multi-modal dataset using a humanoid robot, followed by experimental validation of the dataset's utility for policy learning.

**A. Data Collection via Humanoid Teleoperation**

- **Experimental Setup:** The data was collected using a humanoid teleoperation setup, which extended the Unitree workflow. The robot was equipped with Inspire Hand (RH56-DFX) dexterous hands, featuring 1,062 high-resolution sensors on each hand, distributed across fingers and palm, totaling 2,124 tactile sensors.
 - **Sensing Modalities:** The system recorded several types of data:
 - **Proprioceptive Data:** Arm and finger joint positions.
 - **Egocentric Vision:** From a head-mounted camera, with a resolution of 848 × 480 pixels.
 - **Tactile Signals:** From the Inspire Hands.
 - **Third-Person View:** Provided by an Intel RealSense D435 camera positioned 1 meter to the robot’s left.
 - **External Pressure Measurement:** A piezo-resistive tactile carpet was used to measure external contact pressure and provide real-time pressure heatmaps, which assisted in controlling the applied force during teleoperation.
- **Experimental Objects and Conditions:**
 - **Objects:** Deformable soft objects (a towel and a sponge) were chosen for primary manipulation tasks. Rigid objects were also used for comparative analysis of tactile signal patterns.
 - **Pressure Conditions:** Tasks were designed with "strong" and "weak" pressure conditions to observe how tactile signal distribution changes with intensity.
 - **Tasks:** Four distinct tasks were defined: Towel Strong, Towel Weak, Sponge Strong, and Sponge Weak.
- **Teleoperation Protocol:** Three experimenters executed actions via teleoperation. The protocol involved grasping objects under the predefined strong or weak pressure conditions. The real-time feedback from the tactile carpet heatmap ensured consistent application of the intended pressure levels.
- **Dataset Statistics:** A total of 101.9k frames of motion data were collected. Each task comprised approximately 77–80 episodes, with each episode lasting 20–30 seconds.

**B. Dataset Overview and Preprocessing**

- **Tactile Signal Normalization:** Raw tactile signals, initially ranging from 0–4095, were normalized to a scale of 0–1 to standardize sensor responses and ensure consistency across conditions.

**C. Dataset Analysis**

The collected data underwent several analytical procedures:

- **Temporal Analysis of Tactile Signals:** Tactile signal changes over time were analyzed for both rigid and soft object manipulation. This involved observing the dynamic evolution of pressure distributions during soft object interaction versus the stable characteristics seen with rigid objects.
- **Dense vs. Sparse Tactile Representations:** To assess the advantage of high-resolution sensing, dense tactile signals (2,124 sensors) were converted into a sparse form (42 sensors, a 98% reduction), inspired by methods in prior work.
 - **t-SNE Embedding:** t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) was used to visualize the separability of different task conditions based on both dense and sparse tactile signals, evaluating how clearly each representation could distinguish pressure patterns.
- **Tactile Pattern Analysis under Different Conditions:** The research analyzed which hand regions were primarily utilized for each task. Tactile patterns from different hand patches were visualized at specific time steps to identify variations in responses between tasks (e.g., Towel vs. Sponge) and pressure conditions (Strong vs. Weak).

**D. Experiments for Policy Learning**

- **Baseline Model:** The Action Chunking Transformer (ACT), a state-of-the-art imitation learning baseline, was employed for policy learning experiments.
- **Tactile Information Integration:** Tactile signals obtained from each hand patch were converted into image-like representations. Features were extracted from these 2D tactile maps using a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), a method commonly adopted in prior studies for processing spatial patterns in pressure data. These image-based tactile features were then incorporated into the ACT image encoder alongside visual inputs.
- **Comparison Conditions:** Performance was compared between two input conditions:
 - **ACT-Dense:** Using high-resolution dense tactile signals.
 - **ACT-Sparse:** Using low-resolution sparse tactile signals.
- **Evaluation Metric:** The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) was used to quantify policy performance, measuring the average absolute difference between predicted and ground-truth actions across all timesteps and action dimensions.
- **Training Protocol:** Models were trained on the four manipulation tasks (Sponge Strong, Sponge Weak, Towel Strong, Towel Weak). The dataset was split into 80% for training and 20% for evaluation. Each model was trained for 100K steps, with evaluations conducted every 20K steps. Training was performed with three different random seeds to assess consistency.

**E. Real-world Deployment**

- Trained models were tested in real-world environments. The robot's initial hand pose for these tests was varied from the poses used during data collection, simulating a more generalized application scenario. Observations were made regarding the model's adaptation to task-specific conditions and the challenges encountered in tasks requiring precise contact control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the characteristics of the collected dataset and the performance of policy learning models using different tactile representations.

**A. Dataset Characteristics and Analysis**

- **Dynamic Tactile Signals for Soft Objects:** Analysis of tactile signals revealed significant differences between rigid and soft object manipulation. When interacting with soft objects, pressure appeared across diverse contact regions and its distribution evolved dynamically over time. In contrast, for rigid objects, the contact distribution remained constant and stable in both direction and range. This finding underscored the necessity of high-resolution dense tactile sensing to faithfully capture the time-varying contact patterns induced by soft objects.
- **Superiority of Dense Tactile Representation:** Visualizations using t-SNE embedding demonstrated that dense tactile signals clearly separated the distributions of each task and pressure condition. Samples from the same task formed compact clusters, indicating that the data collection protocol effectively captured tactile information reflecting pressure differences. Conversely, low-resolution sparse tactile signals failed to clearly distinguish between these tasks, suggesting that dense tactile signals provide a more discriminative representation of task characteristics.
- **Varied Hand Region Utilization:** The analysis of tactile patterns across different hand patches showed task-specific usage. For the Towel task, fewer tactile signals were observed under strong pressure conditions. In contrast, the Sponge task exhibited a greater variety of tactile responses under high pressure. These comparative experiments confirmed that soft objects induce more significant and complex changes during manipulation, and high-resolution, dense tactile signals are better suited for classifying and understanding this information.

**B. Policy Learning Experimental Results**

- **Stable Training Loss Reduction:** Across all tasks and for both ACT-Dense and ACT-Sparse models, the learning curves consistently showed a steady decrease in training loss values as training progressed, indicating that the models were effectively learning from the provided data.
- **Limited Test Loss Reduction and Small Performance Gap:** Despite the stable reduction in training loss, the test loss did not decrease as significantly. The performance gap (measured by MAE) between the ACT-Dense (using high-resolution dense tactile signals) and ACT-Sparse (using low-resolution sparse tactile signals) models was relatively small across all tasks.
- **Task-Specific Performance Fluctuations:**
 - **Towel Weak Task:** This task exhibited unstable fluctuations in test performance, which the authors attributed to potentially greater uncertainty in tactile signals under weak pressure conditions.
 - **Sponge Tasks:** For the Sponge tasks, the difference between strong and weak conditions was more pronounced. This distinction was evident in the test losses, where Sponge Strong and Sponge Weak showed a clearer performance gap, reflecting the considerable variation in tactile signals between these two conditions.
- **Discussion on Optimization Limitations:** The authors attributed the relatively small performance gap between dense and sparse models and the less significant reduction in test loss to limitations in the current optimization process. They posited that dense signals, due to their large dimensionality and potentially higher noise levels, introduce greater optimization difficulty. This suggests that effectively learning from dense tactile signals remains a challenge, requiring more efficient signal processing and optimization strategies.

**C. Real-world Deployment**

- **Adaptation to Task Conditions:** During real-world deployment, the humanoid robot demonstrated adaptation of its actions to the specific conditions of each task.
- **Challenges in Precise Control:** Tasks requiring precise contact control, such as sponge grasping, were observed to be more challenging. The authors suggested that these tasks would benefit from richer data coverage and extended training horizons to improve performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in addressing the complexities of contact-rich interactions with deformable objects.

- **Addressing a Critical Gap in Datasets:** The primary significance of this work lies in the introduction of the first known humanoid visual-tactile-action dataset specifically designed for manipulating soft objects under diverse pressure conditions. Prior datasets largely neglected the interaction with deformable objects, focused on rigid objects, or utilized simpler robotic grippers with less sophisticated tactile sensing. This dataset fills a crucial void, providing a unique resource for researchers to develop and test more advanced robotic manipulation policies.
- **Enabling Realistic Contact-Rich Manipulation:** By collecting high-resolution, dense multi-modal data from a humanoid robot with dexterous hands, the dataset offers a more realistic representation of human-like interaction with objects. This capability is essential for enabling robots to perform complex, nuanced tasks in unstructured environments, such as household chores, industrial assembly with flexible components, or even surgical procedures, where deformable objects and precise contact control are paramount.
- **Highlighting the Value of Dense Tactile Sensing:** The detailed analysis of tactile signals demonstrates that dense, high-resolution tactile data is critical for capturing the dynamic and varied pressure distributions that occur during soft object manipulation. This finding emphasizes the necessity for advanced tactile sensing capabilities in future robotic systems and moves beyond simpler binary contact detection.
- **Guiding Future Research on Optimization Strategies:** While the policy learning experiments showed that dense tactile signals offer richer information, they also revealed challenges in effectively leveraging this complexity with current optimization techniques. This outcome is significant as it identifies a clear and important direction for future research: the development of novel signal processing and optimization strategies specifically tailored to handle the high dimensionality and potential noise associated with dense tactile data. This guidance is crucial for advancing the field beyond data collection to practical application.
- **Facilitating Advanced Multi-modal Robot Learning:** The dataset's multi-modal nature (vision, tactile, proprioception, action) provides a rich foundation for developing more robust and intelligent robot learning models. It supports research into sophisticated fusion techniques that integrate diverse sensor inputs to enhance perception, decision-making, and control in complex manipulation tasks.
- **Promoting Generalizability:** By collecting data from a humanoid platform, the research contributes to the broader goal of developing learned representations that generalize across different robotic embodiments and task domains, thereby expanding the applicability of robotic research to a wider range of real-world problems.

In summary, this work provides a foundational dataset and insightful analysis that advances the understanding and capabilities required for humanoid robots to perform sophisticated contact-rich manipulation of soft objects. It not only offers a valuable resource but also clearly delineates critical future research avenues for optimizing learning from complex tactile data, thereby contributing significantly to the progression of robotic intelligence.
