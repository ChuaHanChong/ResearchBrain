# OmniVTA: Visuo-Tactile World Modeling for Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce OmniVTA, a visuo-tactile world-modeling framework designed for contact-rich robotic manipulation, and OmniViTac, a large-scale, diverse visuo-tactile-action dataset. This framework integrates self-supervised tactile representation learning, a predictive multimodal world model, an adaptive fusion policy, and a high-frequency reflexive controller, demonstrating superior performance and robustness in real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation, particularly for contact-rich tasks, is limited by relying primarily on visual information, which often lacks details on forces and subtle contact changes.
- Existing visuo-tactile-action datasets are typically small in scale and narrow in task coverage, hindering the development of generalizable representations and robust contact dynamics models.
- Current visuo-tactile methods often treat tactile signals passively, failing to explicitly model contact dynamics or enable high-frequency closed-loop control for rapid reactions to dynamic changes.

## Method

- Created **OmniViTac**, a large-scale, physics-grounded dataset comprising over **21,000** synchronized trajectories across **86** tasks and 100+ objects, covering six distinct visuo-tactile interaction patterns.
- Proposed **OmniVTA**, a ==hierarchical slow-fast manipulation framework== that includes a ==self-supervised TactileVAE== for tactile representation, a ==Visuo-Tactile World Model (VTWM)== for short-horizon multimodal prediction, and an ==Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion Policy (AFP)== for action generation.
- Implemented a ==Reflexive Latent Tactile Controller (RLTC)== that provides high-frequency (60 Hz) closed-loop correction by refining actions based on predicted and observed tactile signals, directly addressing rapid contact changes.

## Results

- **OmniVTA** achieved state-of-the-art performance across six real-robot contact-rich tasks (Wipe, Peel, Cut, Assembly, Grasp, Adjustment), outperforming existing baselines.
- The framework demonstrated superior generalization capabilities to unseen object positions and tool configurations, and exhibited stronger robustness to external disturbances compared to other approaches.
- The Reflexive Latent Tactile Controller (RLTC) significantly improved task success rates under perturbations (e.g., **60%** for Wipe, **63%** for Peel) and ensured safer interactions by preventing excessive contact forces (average tangential deformation **0.35**, max **0.72**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the temporal evolution of visual observations and tactile signals through a predictive world model (VTWM) is effective for anticipating contact dynamics and improving prediction accuracy.
> - A high-frequency reflexive tactile controller (RLTC) is crucial for robust, contact-rich manipulation, enabling rapid recovery from environmental perturbations and preventing excessive contact forces.
> - Adaptive fusion of visual and tactile information, guided by predicted contact likelihood and dynamic tactile features (Latent Tactile Differential), significantly enhances policy robustness and generalization by dynamically weighting modality contributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving physical contact, increasingly recognizes the limitations of relying solely on visual information. Contact-rich tasks, such as wiping, peeling, and assembly, necessitate accurate perception of forces, friction, and subtle contact state changes that are often not discernible from vision alone. This recognition has driven research into visuo-tactile manipulation, where tactile sensors are integrated to complement visual data.

Current research efforts in visuo-tactile manipulation face two primary limitations. First, existing datasets for visuo-tactile-action alignment are typically small in scale and narrow in task coverage, hindering the development of generalizable visuo-tactile representations and robust contact dynamics models. The scarcity of synchronized visual and high-frequency tactile data across diverse scenarios limits the ability to capture the broad spectrum of contact mechanisms present in real-world manipulation. Second, from a methodological perspective, many existing approaches incorporate tactile signals primarily as auxiliary observations for contact-state recognition or visual occlusion compensation. They often do not explicitly model the evolution of contact dynamics or enable high-frequency closed-loop control. Policies typically rely on action chunking executed in an open-loop manner, which limits rapid reaction to dynamic contact changes like slippage or misalignment.

This work contributes to the broader landscape by addressing these data and methodological gaps. It introduces a large-scale, diverse dataset, OmniViTac, which aims to provide a comprehensive benchmark for learning visuo-tactile representations and policies. Methodologically, it proposes OmniVTA, a framework inspired by human sensorimotor control, which emphasizes predictive modeling of contact evolution and rapid tactile feedback for closed-loop correction. This approach distinguishes itself from prior work by integrating tactile representation learning, predictive multimodal modeling, adaptive fusion, and a high-frequency reflexive controller, thereby moving beyond passive tactile observation towards active contact dynamics modeling and control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome persistent limitations in visuo-tactile robotic manipulation, specifically concerning data availability and methodological approaches to contact-rich tasks. The motivation stems from the observed disparity between human dexterity in such tasks and the current capabilities of robotic systems.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Diversity:** Existing public visuo-tactile manipulation datasets are identified as being limited in both the scale of fully aligned vision-tactile-action demonstrations and the diversity of contact-rich tasks. This restricts the ability of learning algorithms to develop generalizable visuo-tactile representations and comprehensive models of contact dynamics. The goal is to provide a large-scale dataset that covers a wide array of manipulation scenarios, objects, and physics-grounded interaction patterns to better reflect real-world complexity.
2. **Enhancing Methodological Approaches for Tactile Integration:** Current visuo-tactile manipulation methods are noted for their limitations in representation and control. Tactile signals are often treated as passive observations, primarily for state recognition or visual compensation, rather than actively modeling contact dynamics or enabling explicit closed-loop control. The reliance on open-loop action execution further restricts the ability of robots to react promptly to rapid contact changes, such as slippage, misalignment, or external disturbances. The motivation is to develop a framework that actively leverages tactile signals for predictive modeling of contact evolution and employs rapid, closed-loop tactile feedback for precise, robust control.
3. **Mimicking Human Sensorimotor Control:** The research is inspired by neuroscience findings on human dexterity, which suggest that robust contact-rich manipulation relies on tightly coupled multimodal perception, predictive internal models, and rapid tactile feedback control. The objective is to translate these biological principles into a robotic system that can form feedforward anticipations of contact evolution and exploit high-frequency tactile feedback for immediate, closed-loop corrections.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:

- To create **OmniViTac**, a large-scale visuo-tactile-action aligned dataset comprising over 21,000 trajectories across 86 tasks and 100+ objects, organized into six physics-grounded interaction patterns.
- To propose **OmniVTA**, a world-model-based visuo-tactile manipulation framework that integrates four modules: a self-supervised tactile encoder, a two-stream visuo-tactile world model for short-horizon contact prediction, a contact-aware fusion policy for action generation, and a high-frequency (60Hz) reflexive controller for closed-loop deviation correction.
- To demonstrate, through real-robot experiments, that OmniVTA outperforms existing methods, exhibits robustness to perturbations, and generalizes effectively to unseen objects and geometric configurations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper encompasses both data collection and model development. It introduces a large-scale dataset, OmniViTac, and a hierarchical slow-fast manipulation framework, OmniVTA, designed for contact-rich tasks.

#### 3.1. The OmniViTac Dataset

OmniViTac is a large-scale, visuo-tactile-action aligned dataset designed to support data-driven learning for contact-rich manipulation.

- **Scale and Diversity:** It comprises 21,879 synchronized trajectories across 86 tasks and over 100 objects in diverse real-world environments. Data streams include RGB-D observations, high-frequency tactile signals, and robot actions. The dataset significantly expands upon previous visuo-tactile-action datasets in terms of task coverage and data volume.
- **Task Taxonomy:** The dataset is organized around six physics-grounded visuo-tactile interaction patterns: Wiping, Peeling, Cutting, Grasping, Assembly, and In-hand Adjustment. This taxonomy categorizes behaviors based on dominant tactile features and contact mechanics.
- **Hardware Setup and Data Collection:**
 - **Dual-Embodiment:** Data is collected using a UFACTORY xArm-7 robotic manipulator (via kinesthetic teaching and GELLO teleoperation) and a TacUMI handheld device. Both platforms use identical parallel-jaw grippers to minimize embodiment-induced domain gaps.
 - **Sensor Suite:**
 - **Visual:** Wrist-view and third-person-view RGB-D observations (Intel RealSense D435/L515 cameras) at 30 Hz. TacUMI also includes a wide-angle RGB camera.
 - **Tactile:** Four types of tactile sensors are supported: Xense (Quark N1), Daimon (Tac-WL), Tac3D-A1, and GelSight Mini. The Xense sensor is primarily used for the majority of data collection due to its robustness. These sensors provide RGB tactile images and/or 3D displacement fields at frequencies up to 60 Hz.
 - **Proprioceptive:** Robot joint states (for xArm), end-effector poses, and continuous gripper aperture values are recorded.
 - **Data Processing:** A rigorous pipeline ensures data fidelity, including timestamp alignment (error below 10 ms), automated trimming of static frames, and human-in-the-loop verification.
- **Tactile Pattern Analysis:** t-SNE projections of high-dimensional tactile signals reveal structured, semantically separable latent clusters corresponding to the six interaction patterns, validating the taxonomy and indicating that OmniViTac captures distinct contact dynamics.
- **Dataset Statistics:** Quantitative analysis highlights pattern-specific distributions of contact area, force intensity, and effective contact ratio. For example, precision tasks like Assembly show low contact area/force, while power tasks like Cutting show higher force intensities.

#### 3.2. OmniVTA Framework

OmniVTA is a hierarchical slow-fast manipulation policy built upon a visuo-tactile world model. It explicitly decomposes manipulation into a slow policy (planning) and a fast reflexive controller (correction).

- **System Overview:**
 - **Slow Policy (4Hz):** Integrates the Visuo-Tactile World Model (VTWM) and Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion Policy (AFP) to generate long-horizon action chunks from low-frequency visual, high-frequency tactile, and proprioceptive inputs.
 - **Fast Policy (60Hz):** A Reflexive Latent Tactile Controller (RLTC) refines actions at a higher frequency based on predicted and observed tactile signals, providing closed-loop correction.
 - Final actions are a weighted sum of slow-planned and fast-refined outputs.

- **TactileVAE (Tactile Representation Learning):**
 - **Objective:** Extract low-dimensional, continuous, task-agnostic tactile representations from 3D marker displacement data.
 - **Encoder:** A spatio-temporal encoder with causal 3D convolutions compresses input tactile sequences into latent feature maps (z_t).
 - **Decoder:** An Implicit Neural Representation (INR) decoder reconstructs a continuous 3D deformation field conditioned on spatial coordinates and the latent feature map. This avoids pixel reconstruction and models continuous surface deformation.
 - **Training:** Uses a reconstruction loss (L2 distance) and a KL regularization term.

- **Visuo-Tactile World Model (VTWM):**
 - **Objective:** Model the temporal evolution of visual observations and tactile signals to predict short-horizon contact dynamics.
 - **Architecture:** A two-stream conditional generative framework, with each stream using a spatial-temporal diffusion transformer.
 - **Inputs:** Past 'c' frames of observation latents (visual latent z_v from SD-VAE, tactile latent z_t from TactileVAE).
 - **Multi-modal Observation Conditioner:** Encodes visual observations, tactile embeddings, and 2D projected actions into a shared conditioning vector, injected into both visual and tactile world models.
 - **Dynamic-aware Weighted Loss:** Modifies the diffusion objective with dynamic weight maps (w_dyn) based on local temporal differences in tactile signals and amplitude weight maps (w_amp) based on contact intensity. This emphasizes high-frequency changes and strong contact responses during training (L_VTWM = L_diffusion + λ1*L_dyn + λ2*L_amp).

- **Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion Policy (AFP):**
 - **Objective:** Adaptively balance visual and tactile information for stable action planning based on predicted contact states.
 - **Latent Tactile Differential (LTD) Encoder:** Constructs a tactile representation (f_t) by concatenating current tactile features (f_c_t), predicted tactile features (f_p_t) from VTWM, and their difference (f_p_t - f_c_t). This highlights potential contact events and interaction dynamics.
 - **Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion:** A contact probability prediction module (MLP with sigmoid) estimates future contact likelihood from f_t. A gating network, conditioned on the contact logit and f_t, outputs normalized modality weights (W_t, W_v) to modulate tactile and visual features (f_v from ResNet-18 encoder) before concatenation (f_vt = concat(W_v * f_v, W_t * ˜f_t)).
 - **Visuo-Tactile Diffusion Policy:** A conditional denoising diffusion model (CNN-based) generates a short-horizon action chunk (A_c) from the fused visuo-tactile representation (f_vt) and robot proprioception (s), optimized with the DDPM objective (L_act).
 - **Training:** Jointly optimized with action loss and contact prediction loss (L_AFP = L_act + λ_ct*L_bce).

- **Reflexive Latent Tactile Controller (RLTC):**
 - **Objective:** Provide high-frequency (60 Hz) closed-loop control to refine actions and react to rapid contact changes.
 - **Inputs:** Single-frame observed tactile feedback (encoded by TactileVAE), upsampled predicted tactile features from VTWM, and historical robot delta actions/gripper states.
 - **Output:** A single-step refined action (a_r) predicted by a three-layer MLP.
 - **Training:** Learns corrective actions from "recovery segments" in human-collected data (where the robot transitions from abnormal to valid tactile states) using an MSE loss (L_RLTC).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of OmniVTA was conducted in real-robot settings across six tactile interaction categories, with comprehensive ablation studies on its components.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Object Diversity:** OmniVTA achieved the best performance across all six tasks (Wipe, Peel, Cut, Assembly, Grasp, Adjustment) compared to baselines (DP, DP+tactile, KineDex, ForceMimic, RDP, OmniVTA w/o RLTC).
- **Generalization:**
 - **Position Generalization:** OmniVTA demonstrated stronger robustness to unseen object heights, with its closed-loop variant (OmniVTA) achieving 58% success for Wipe, 48% for Peel, 83% for Cut, 50% for Assembly, and 65% for Adjustment, significantly outperforming baselines.
 - **Tool Generalization:** For the 'Cut' task, OmniVTA maintained high performance (83%) even with an unseen knife, suggesting reliance on tactile feedback rather than memorized trajectories.
- **Perturbation Robustness:** OmniVTA consistently achieved the highest success rates under external disturbances (object displacement), with the Reflexive Latent Tactile Controller (RLTC) significantly improving performance (e.g., Wipe: 60%, Peel: 63%, Cut: 60%, Assembly: 40%). This demonstrates the controller's ability to quickly recover stable contact.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - The closed-loop controller in OmniVTA consistently improved performance over its open-loop variant (OmniVTA w/o RLTC).
 - OmniVTA's controller was noted for preventing excessive contact forces, unlike some reactive policies (e.g., RDP) which sometimes caused sensor damage. OmniVTA maintained an average tangential deformation of 0.35 (max 0.72), compared to RDP's 0.56 (max 1.1).

#### 4.2. Component Analysis

- **TactileVAE:**
 - **Reconstruction Accuracy:** Consistently achieved the best L2 distance (lower is better) and cosine similarity (higher is better) across all six tasks compared to PCA and PointNet-AE baselines, indicating effective modeling of tactile deformation.
 - **Latent Representation Quality:** t-SNE visualization of learned representations showed clear clustering for different force patterns (press, twist, slide) and cross-sensor generalizability, even with unseen objects and sensors. Ablation studies indicated the importance of the implicit decoder and local feature maps for discriminative embeddings and reconstruction accuracy.
- **Visuo-Tactile World Model:**
 - **Prediction Accuracy:** Outperformed baselines (UVA, exUMI, KineDex, ForceMimic) in both short-term (2nd latent step) and long-term (6th latent step, average over 24 frames) tactile prediction (lower L2, higher cosine similarity), demonstrating effective cross-modal dynamics modeling.
 - **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Joint Visual-Tactile Generation:** Jointly predicting future visual features alongside tactile features improved tactile prediction accuracy.
 - **Action Representation:** 2D projected actions yielded better generalization to unseen object positions than 3D absolute or relative actions, suggesting alignment with visual observations for motion intent.
 - **Dynamic Weighting:** The dynamic-aware weighted loss improved tactile prediction performance, enhancing the modeling of high-frequency tactile patterns.
 - **Perturbation Robustness:** The world model exhibited recovery capability under observation perturbations (e.g., contact break due to object displacement), demonstrating its ability to re-establish contact.
- **Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion Policy:**
 - **Effect of Predicted Tactile Signals:** Incorporating predicted tactile information consistently improved policy success rates (e.g., 50% for Wipe with 6-step prediction vs. 12% with no prediction), with longer prediction horizons yielding greater benefits.
 - **Tactile Representation:** The Latent Tactile Differential (LTD) Encoder achieved higher success rates than direct concatenation of current and predicted features (e.g., 57% vs. 50% for Wipe), indicating effective capture of dynamic relationships.
 - **Gating Fusion Mechanism:** The adaptive gating mechanism improved the average task success rate by approximately 7% compared to direct feature concatenation. Visualizations showed a strong correlation between modality weights and contact states: tactile weight increased with predicted contact probability.
 - **Dependency on Tactile Prediction Accuracy:** Policy performance was directly linked to the accuracy of tactile prediction; lower prediction accuracy resulted in significant drops in success rate, proving its critical role.
 - **Generated Visual Features:** Incorporating generated visual features did not yield significant performance gains and substantially increased inference frequency, leading to their exclusion from the final policy design.
- **Reflexive Latent Tactile Controller (RLTC):**
 - The RLTC enabled high-frequency (60 Hz) closed-loop control, leading to more stable action execution and improved task success rates.
 - Crucially, it facilitated rapid recovery from environmental perturbations and adaptively modulated action magnitudes to prevent excessive contact forces, as demonstrated in perturbation experiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses fundamental limitations in visuo-tactile manipulation, offering contributions with potential broad impact on the field of robotics.

First, the introduction of **OmniViTac**, a large-scale, diverse, and physics-grounded visuo-tactile-action dataset, is a significant contribution. By spanning 86 tasks and over 100 objects across six interaction patterns, and ensuring high-fidelity temporal alignment across multiple sensor modalities and embodiments, OmniViTac provides a robust benchmark for future research. This dataset's scale and structured organization can enable the learning of more generalizable visuo-tactile representations and predictive contact models, fostering advancements in data-driven robotic manipulation. Its public availability will likely accelerate research in learning robust contact behaviors.

Second, the proposed **OmniVTA** framework represents a methodological advancement by moving beyond passive tactile observation. By integrating self-supervised tactile representation learning, a predictive visuo-tactile world model, a contact-aware fusion policy, and a high-frequency reflexive controller, OmniVTA offers a unified approach to robust, contact-rich manipulation. The explicit modeling of contact dynamics and the closed-loop tactile control mechanism, inspired by human sensorimotor capabilities, address critical shortcomings of prior open-loop methods.

The experimental results validate the efficacy of OmniVTA, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across diverse contact-rich tasks. Its superior generalization capabilities to unseen objects and geometric configurations, coupled with robustness to external disturbances, highlight the practical value of this framework. The ability to react promptly and safely to dynamic contact events, while also preventing excessive forces on tactile sensors, is a crucial step towards deploying robots in complex, unstructured environments.

The findings regarding the importance of accurate tactile prediction, the effectiveness of the Latent Tactile Differential Encoder, and the adaptive nature of the gating fusion mechanism provide insights into effective multimodal integration for robotic control. The demonstration that high-frequency reflexive control significantly enhances task success and robustness, especially under perturbations, underscores the necessity of reactive mechanisms in contact-rich scenarios.

The potential impact of this work extends to developing more dexterous, adaptable, and safe robotic systems for a wide range of applications, including manufacturing, service robotics, and even surgical assistance, where precise contact control and robustness to environmental variations are paramount. By providing both a rich dataset and a validated framework, this research lays groundwork for more capable embodied AI systems that can effectively perceive and interact with the physical world through touch.
