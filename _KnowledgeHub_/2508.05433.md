# Multimodal LLM-assisted Evolutionary Search for Programmatic Control Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The Multimodal LLM-assisted Evolutionary Search (MLES) framework enables the direct synthesis of high-performing, transparent programmatic control policies by integrating Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with evolutionary computation and visual feedback-driven behavior analysis. This approach achieved performance comparable to DRL baselines like PPO on tasks such as Lunar Lander and Car Racing, while producing human-readable code and traceable policy evolution.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies, often represented as opaque neural networks, lack transparency, making them difficult to understand, verify, debug, and deploy in safety-critical applications.
- Existing methods for interpretable policies either rely on post-hoc explanations (limited for proactive design) or direct methods constrained by static grammars and limited search spaces.
- Previous LLM-assisted Evolutionary Search (LES) in RL primarily focused on indirectly shaping reward functions for opaque neural policies, rather than directly synthesizing transparent programmatic policies.

## Method

- The **MLES** framework combines Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with ==Evolutionary Computation== (EC) in a closed-loop system for end-to-end programmatic policy discovery.
- Policies are represented as executable Python code paired with natural language 'thought' rationales and enhanced with quantitative metrics and multimodal behavioral evidence (BE), primarily visual feedback.
- Evolutionary operators (exploration and multimodal modification) leverage BE and MLLMs to generate new policies by identifying behavioral shortcomings and proposing targeted revisions or parameter adjustments.

## Results

- MLES achieved training performance comparable to PPO on Lunar Lander (1.090 vs. 1.032) and Car Racing (98.070 vs. 99.212), while outperforming an LES variant without multimodal analysis (EoH) across both tasks.
- MLES demonstrated superior search efficiency, discovering high-performing policies faster than DRL baselines on Lunar Lander and with lower variance on Car Racing, consuming approximately 5,000 environment resets for Lunar Lander.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multimodal modification operators, which utilize behavioral evidence, were critical for performance, with their removal causing the most pronounced degradation (e.g., **42.24%** drop on Lunar Lander).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating visual feedback-driven behavioral evidence (BE) transforms LLM-driven policy generation from stochastic trial-and-error into a more diagnostic and targeted refinement process, significantly enhancing search efficiency and robustness.
> - The natural language 'thought' accompanying policy code is crucial for MLLMs to interpret policies and facilitates more effective reasoning and modification.
> - The performance and efficiency of MLES are directly linked to and scale with the capabilities of the underlying MLLMs, indicating its long-term viability and potential for future improvements.
