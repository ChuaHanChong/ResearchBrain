# Traceable Evidence Enhanced Visual Grounded Reasoning: Evaluation and Methodology

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces TreeBench, a diagnostic benchmark, and TreeVGR, a training paradigm, to advance visual grounded reasoning (VGR) in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). TreeVGR, a 7B parameter model, achieved an overall accuracy of 50.4% and an mIoU of 44.0% on the challenging TreeBench, demonstrating substantial improvements over its base model and competitive performance with larger LMMs.

## Problem

- Existing LMM evaluation benchmarks lack comprehensive assessment of advanced visual grounded reasoning (VGR), particularly regarding fine-grained localization, traceable evidence, and complex second-order reasoning.
- Prior VGR training methods primarily supervise only the final answer, neglecting intermediate region-of-interest generation, which limits transparency and interpretability of reasoning pathways.
- Current LMMs struggle with 'thinking with images' tasks, showing deficiencies in focused visual perception, managing subtle targets, and resolving complex spatial and relational queries under perspective transformations.

## Method

- A diagnostic benchmark, **TreeBench**, was developed with 405 high-quality ==VQA pairs==, emphasizing focused visual perception, traceable evidence via bounding box evaluation, and vision-centric second-order reasoning across 10 sub-tasks.
- A two-stage training paradigm, TreeVGR, was proposed, combining cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) with traceable evidence using dual Intersection-over-Union (IoU) rewards.
- The RL stage explicitly supervises the generation of bounding boxes during reasoning, guiding the model to generate accurate localizations and explainable reasoning pathways.

## Results

- TreeBench proved highly challenging, with state-of-the-art private models like OpenAI-o3-0416 achieving only **54.8%** accuracy and Qwen2.5-VL-**72B** scoring **42.2%**.
- TreeVGR-**7B** achieved an overall accuracy of **50.4%** and an mIoU of **44.0%** on TreeBench, marking a 13.4 percentage point improvement over its base Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**.
- TreeVGR-**7B** demonstrated state-of-the-art open-source performance on V* Bench (**91.1%**, +16.8 pp over base), HR-Bench (+5.0 pp), and MME-RealWorld-Lite (+12.6 pp), showing strong generalization to other VGR benchmarks and even general multimodal QA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate localization (mIoU) is critical for visual grounded reasoning, especially for first-order perception tasks, although complex reasoning tasks also require 'second-order cognitive capabilities'.
> - Explicitly providing ground-truth bounding boxes as hints consistently boosts LMM performance, indicating the importance of precise visual grounding for 'evidence-based reasoning'.
> - Traceable evidence, through a dual IoU reward system during reinforcement learning, is crucial for enhancing both the accuracy and interpretability of VGR, preventing models from enumerating excessive or imprecise localizations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has demonstrated advanced reasoning capabilities in text-based tasks. This has motivated research into extending similar reasoning to Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), particularly in vision-language tasks. Early LMMs integrated visual features into LLM backbones, with subsequent advancements focusing on efficiency (e.g., LLaVA) and handling high-resolution inputs (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT, InternVL-1.5, Qwen2-VL). A recent development is "visual grounded reasoning" (VGR), exemplified by models like OpenAI-o3, which dynamically reference visual regions to perform "thinking with images" through interleaved image-text reasoning pathways.

However, the authors identify a gap in the existing research landscape: a lack of comprehensive evaluation benchmarks specifically designed to assess these advanced visual grounded reasoning capabilities holistically. Previous benchmarks such as POPE, MMBench, SEED-Bench, and MMMU tend to overlook fine-grained localization and verifiable reasoning chains. While some benchmarks like V* Bench, MME-RealWorld, and HR-Bench partially address localization or high-resolution inputs, they often lack traceability of evidence, complex second-order reasoning, or focus on simple spatial queries, making them insufficient for evaluating the nuanced multi-step reasoning processes akin to human "thinking with images." Furthermore, existing LMM training methods for VGR often supervise only the final answer, neglecting the intermediate region-of-interest generation processes, which limits the transparency and interpretability of the reasoning.

This work contributes to the landscape by introducing a new benchmark, TreeBench, to address these evaluation deficiencies, and a new training paradigm, TreeVGR, to enhance the transparency and accuracy of visual grounded reasoning in LMMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:
1. **To develop a comprehensive diagnostic benchmark for visual grounded reasoning (VGR).** The motivation for TreeBench stems from the absence of a benchmark capable of holistically evaluating LMMs' "thinking with images" capabilities. Existing benchmarks are insufficient because they:
 - Overlook fine-grained visual perception, particularly for subtle targets in complex scenes.
 - Lack traceable evidence, making it difficult to verify intermediate reasoning steps.
 - Do not adequately test second-order reasoning, which involves complex object interactions, spatial hierarchies, and perspective transformations beyond simple object localization.
 - Are susceptible to language bias due to models' reliance on textual reasoning pathways.
 The authors aim to bridge this gap by creating a benchmark that emphasizes focused visual perception, traceable evidence via bounding box evaluation, and sophisticated vision-centric reasoning.

2. **To propose an effective training paradigm that enhances traceable evidence-enhanced visual grounded reasoning in LMMs.** The motivation for TreeVGR arises from the limitation of prior VGR training methods, which typically supervise only the final answer, neglecting the explicit localization process. This makes it challenging to quantify the contribution of "grounding-then-answering" frameworks and understand the actual reasoning pathways. The objective is to develop a training methodology that explicitly supervises bounding box generation during reasoning, thereby fostering accurate localizations and explainable reasoning pathways, and improving overall VGR performance.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for both better evaluation tools and more transparent, effective training methods to advance the development of LMMs capable of robust and interpretable visual grounded reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This work introduces TreeBench, a diagnostic benchmark, and TreeVGR, a training paradigm, to address the identified gaps in evaluating and enhancing visual grounded reasoning.

#### 3.1. TreeBench: Traceable Evidence Evaluation Benchmark

TreeBench is constructed around three foundational principles:
- **Focused Visual Perception:** Evaluates the ability to identify subtle targets in cluttered, real-world scenes using precise textual descriptions, requiring hierarchical scene understanding and discrimination of similar distractors.
- **Traceable Evidence:** Provides quantifiable evaluation of reasoning chains through bounding box evaluation of target instances, aiming for explainable and transparent assessment.
- **Vision-Centric Second-Order Reasoning Capabilities:** Focuses on complex interactions, spatial containment, and relative relationships with perspective transformation, moving beyond simple object localization.

**Dataset Construction:**
1. **Image Selection:** 1,000 high-quality images were sampled from SA-1B, prioritizing images with dense objects suitable for evaluating fine-grained details and complex scenes.
2. **First Round Quality Control:** Eight LMM experts manually evaluated the relevance and quality of images for specific categories, resulting in 647 suitable images.
3. **Automated Question Generation:** OpenAI-o3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro were used to generate three diverse questions, multiple-choice options, and answers per image for 10 sub-tasks.
4. **Second Round Quality Control:** Human experts reviewed all six model-generated questions per image, selecting the most coherent and relevant or manually constructing new ones if necessary, ensuring alignment with sub-tasks, avoiding triviality, and maintaining clarity.
5. **Difficulty Filtering:** Questions answered correctly by all four state-of-the-art LMMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL3-78B, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash) were filtered out to maintain high difficulty.
6. **Third Round Quality Control:** Independent human annotators cross-validated the accuracy and relevance of the final question-option-answer pairs.

**Task Definitions (10 Sub-tasks):**
TreeBench systematically evaluates 10 core competencies, categorized into "Perception" (first-order reasoning) and "Reasoning" (second-order reasoning).
- **Perception Tasks (149 questions):**
 1. **Attributes:** Identify specific visual properties (e.g., color, shape) requiring attention to fine details.
 2. **Material:** Distinguish material properties (e.g., texture, finish) through visual cues.
 3. **Physical State:** Assess structural integrity or positional states (e.g., open/closed) through visual analysis.
 4. **Object Retrieval:** Interpret complex linguistic and spatial descriptions to locate subtle targets.
 5. **OCR-Integrated Question-Answering:** Extract text-based questions/options from images, integrating OCR with NLU.
- **Reasoning Tasks (256 questions):**
 1. **Perspective Transform:** Perform viewpoint transformations and interpret spatial relations under perspective shifts.
 2. **Ordering:** Analyze linearly ordered arrangements of objects (e.g., left-to-right) and resolve ordinal relationships.
 3. **Contact and Occlusion:** Analyze physical interactions between multiple objects (e.g., direct contact, occlusion layers).
 4. **Spatial Containment:** Benchmark hierarchical spatial relationships (e.g., containment, surface attachment).
 5. **Comparison:** Compare attributes across multiple objects (e.g., distance, size) and resolve differences.

The final benchmark comprises 405 high-quality VQA pairs, with target instances occupying an average of 3.05% of the image area, indicating a focus on small objects.

#### 3.2. TreeVGR: Traceable Evidence for Visual Grounded Reasoning

TreeVGR is a two-stage training paradigm designed to enhance localization-driven visual reasoning without requiring replaying cropped images, improving efficiency.

##### 3.2.1. Cold-Start Initialization

This stage uses supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to establish VGR capabilities computationally efficiently.
- **Data Construction:** Based on VGR-158K, a dataset providing pseudo-chain-of-thought annotations with bounding boxes. Coordinates are converted to absolute values for compatibility with Qwen2.5-VL. Samples are filtered to retain those with multiple bounding boxes (35K samples) to prioritize complex reasoning. A reflective subset of 4.7K samples is created by injecting synthetic incorrect bounding boxes and a meta-cognitive prompt ("Wait, this box seems to be wrong") to train the model to detect and correct errors. The resulting dataset is TreeVGR-SFT-35K.
- **Optimization:** TreeVGR-7B-CI is initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Training is conducted with 8 GPUs using LLaMA-Factory, AdamW optimizer (learning rate 5e-6), global batch size 256, and a cosine learning rate decay schedule with a 0.1 warmup ratio.

##### 3.2.2. Reinforcement Learning with Traceable Evidence

This stage refines reasoning trajectories through explicit supervision of generated bounding boxes using reinforcement learning (RL).
- **Reward Design:** The total reward ($R$) comprises three parts:
 1. **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):** 0 or 1, based on the correctness of the final answer (exact-matching for multiple-choice, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct as an online reward model for open-ended).
 2. **Formatting Reward ($R_{format}$):** 0 or 1, ensuring the reasoning and answer are enclosed in `<think>`/`</think>` and `<answer>`/`</answer>` tags.
 3. **Dual Intersection-over-Union (IoU) Reward ($R_{IoU}$):** An average of a recall term ($R_R_{IoU}$) and a precision term ($R_P_{IoU}$), ranging from 0 to 1.
 - **Recall Term ($R_R_{IoU}$):** Ensures each ground-truth bounding box ($b_k$) is matched with at least one prediction ($\hat{b}_i$). It is the average of the maximum IoU between all predictions and each ground-truth.
 - **Precision Term ($R_P_{IoU}$):** Discourages "empty" predictions that do not match any ground-truths. It is the average of the maximum IoU between all ground-truths and each prediction.
- **Data Construction:** 30K hard samples from the V* training set are filtered using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Additionally, 7K multiple-choice counting problems are curated from the VisDrone dataset (ground-truth numbers 5-10). The combined dataset is TreeVGR-RL-37K.
- **Optimization:** TreeVGR-7B is initialized from TreeVGR-7B-CI. Training uses 8 GPUs for the model and another 8 GPUs for the Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct reward model (via vLLM). Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed, with 5 training epochs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings regarding the challenges posed by TreeBench and the performance improvements achieved by TreeVGR.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on TreeBench

- **Challenging Nature:** TreeBench is a demanding benchmark. State-of-the-art private models like OpenAI-o3-0416 achieve an overall accuracy of 54.8%, while Gemini-2.5-Pro-0605 scores 54.6%. Even the strong open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B scores 42.2%. This indicates a significant performance gap for current LMMs.
- **TreeVGR Performance:** TreeVGR-7B demonstrates an overall accuracy of 50.4% and an mIoU of 44.0% on TreeBench, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B (which scores 37.0% accuracy). This represents a 13.4 percentage point improvement over its base model. TreeVGR-7B achieves comparable performance to much larger models like InternVL3-78B (46.4% accuracy).
- **Category-wise Performance:** TreeVGR shows notable improvements across various categories, particularly in Perception tasks like "Attributes" (up by 11.7 pp) and "Object Retrieval" (up by 26.1 pp), and Reasoning tasks such as "Perspective Transformation" (up by 35.4 pp) and "Comparison" (up by 24.2 pp) compared to its base Qwen2.5-VL-7B. TreeVGR also achieves higher mIoU across categories, indicating improved localization accuracy.

#### 4.2. Traceable Evidence Validation

- **Necessity of Bounding Boxes:** When target instances were masked during inference on TreeBench (Table 5), all models experienced a significant performance drop (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B dropped by 5.2 pp, OpenAI-o3 dropped by 21.0 pp). This finding suggests that the bounding box annotations in TreeBench are critical for accurate VGR and are not merely decorative.
- **Benefits of Explicit Hints:** Providing ground-truth bounding boxes as explicit textual hints during inference (Table 6) led to consistent performance gains across all models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B improved by 6.7 pp, Gemini-2.5-Pro by 6.9 pp). This indicates that explicit visual grounding helps models align reasoning with specific visual content, promoting "evidence-based reasoning" over "heuristic reasoning."

#### 4.3. Correlation Analysis

- **Localization and Performance:** A positive correlation was observed between localization precision (mIoU) and overall performance for VGR models (Table 2).
- **Perception vs. Reasoning:** Analysis of IoU distributions (Figures 8-10) showed a strong correlation between precise localization and perception task performance. However, for reasoning tasks, the correlation was weaker, suggesting that successful reasoning requires "second-order cognitive capabilities" beyond mere accurate localization.

#### 4.4. Performance on High-Resolution and Other Benchmarks

- **V* Bench, HR-Bench, MME-RealWorld-Lite:** TreeVGR-7B achieved open-source state-of-the-art performance on V* Bench (91.1%), improving significantly over Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 16.8 pp. It also demonstrated substantial improvements on HR-Bench (5.0 pp overall) and MME-RealWorld-Lite (12.6 pp overall), particularly in Perception categories (e.g., Object Detection, Attribute Detection) and Reasoning categories (e.g., Object Counting, Attribute Description).
- **General Multimodal Benchmarks:** TreeVGR-7B showed improvements on vision-centric question-answering benchmarks like CV-Bench-2D (+2.8 pp), CV-Bench-3D (+5.0 pp), and MMVP (+8.6 pp), even surpassing larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B on MMVP. Modest improvements were observed on MMBench and POPE, while performance was stable on AI2D and ChartQA.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Table 8)

- **Cold-Start Initialization:** This stage was found to be beneficial, improving accuracy on VGR benchmarks like V* and MME-RealWorld-Lite, even for out-of-domain samples.
- **Traceable VGR vs. Untraceable:** Incorporating dual IoU rewards (traceable evidence) during RL led to substantial performance gains on TreeBench and MME-RealWorld-Lite compared to untraceable RL (without $R_{IoU}$). This improvement encompassed both overall accuracy and mIoU.
- **Precision Term ($R_P_{IoU}$):** The precision term was critical for preventing the model from enumerating excessive bounding boxes to maximize recall, thus avoiding repetitive and non-convergent responses (Figure 14).
- **Recall Term ($R_R_{IoU}$):** The recall term was important for achieving precise and complete localization; without it, models showed limited mIoU despite accuracy improvements.
- **Vanilla Text-Only RL:** Text-only RL was less effective than visual grounded reasoning with traceable evidence, indicating the importance of pre-answer contextual grounding and accurate spatial localization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents two principal contributions: TreeBench, a novel evaluation benchmark, and TreeVGR, an enhanced training methodology, which collectively advance the field of visual grounded reasoning (VGR).

### 6. Significance of TreeBench

TreeBench addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of LMMs' VGR capabilities. By integrating three principles—focused visual perception of subtle targets, traceable evidence through bounding box evaluation, and second-order reasoning—it provides a more rigorous and comprehensive assessment than previous benchmarks. Its focus on small objects in complex scenes and its multi-stage quality control ensure the high difficulty and relevance of its questions. The observed low performance of even advanced models like OpenAI-o3 on TreeBench (below 60% accuracy) underscores its ability to effectively challenge current LMMs, thereby highlighting areas for future research and development. This benchmark offers a standardized tool for diagnosing specific strengths and weaknesses of VGR models, enabling researchers to identify whether errors stem from visual grounding failures or higher-level reasoning deficits. Its traceable evidence mechanism promotes transparency and explainability in model evaluation, moving beyond simple answer accuracy.

### 7. Significance of TreeVGR

TreeVGR introduces an effective training paradigm that enhances VGR methods by explicitly supervising localization and reasoning jointly through reinforcement learning with dual IoU rewards. This approach moves beyond simply supervising final answers, instead guiding the model to generate accurate and interpretable reasoning pathways. The empirical results demonstrate that TreeVGR significantly improves performance across various VGR benchmarks (e.g., +16.8 on V* Bench, +12.6 on MME-RealWorld-Lite, +13.4 on TreeBench) and even general multimodal benchmarks. The ablation studies confirm the importance of traceable evidence, particularly the dual IoU reward components, in fostering both precision and recall in bounding box generation and preventing common failure modes like excessive enumeration.

**Potential Impact:**
The combined contributions of TreeBench and TreeVGR have several potential impacts:
- **Accelerated LMM Development:** TreeBench provides a robust and challenging benchmark that can drive the development of LMMs with more human-like "thinking with images" capabilities, pushing models to improve their fine-grained perception and complex reasoning.
- **Enhanced Explainability and Trustworthiness:** TreeVGR's emphasis on traceable evidence leads to more transparent and explainable reasoning processes in LMMs. This is crucial for building trust in AI systems, especially in applications where understanding *how* a model arrived at its conclusion is as important as the conclusion itself.
- **Improved Real-world Applications:** By enabling LMMs to perform more accurate and grounded visual reasoning, this work can lead to advancements in diverse real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, medical imaging analysis, and complex visual question answering, where precise object localization and relational understanding are critical.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** TreeBench identifies "perspective transform" and basic perception in complex scenes as major bottlenecks, suggesting critical areas for future research into 3D awareness and detailed visual world perception for LMMs. The two-stage training approach of TreeVGR provides a blueprint for further exploration of efficient and effective VGR training strategies, potentially inspiring work on unifying vision-centric and text-centric reasoning models.

While the current implementation of TreeVGR is based on a 7B parameter model and TreeBench contains 405 curated pairs, scaling these to larger models and more extensive datasets are acknowledged as future work. These initial findings establish a foundation for more robust, explainable, and contextually aware multimodal AI.
