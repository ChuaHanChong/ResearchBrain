# DreamTraj: Generating 6-DoF Object Trajectories by Reading Unrendered Video Diffusion Latents

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamTraj presents a new framework for predicting 6-DoF object trajectories solely from a single RGB image and a natural language instruction, leveraging latent motion signals extracted early from a frozen video diffusion model. This method surpasses baselines requiring privileged inputs in trajectory accuracy and achieves a 4.6x inference speedup over generate-then-extract pipelines, while also introducing the Move dataset of 5,038 high-quality trajectories.

## Problem

- Accurately predicting 6-DoF object trajectories for robotic manipulation from minimal inputs (single image, language instruction) is challenging and often requires expensive sensors or privileged information.
- Existing 'generate-then-extract' methods that use video diffusion models are computationally inefficient, requiring full video synthesis and cascaded perception modules.
- There is a lack of large-scale, high-quality datasets providing object-centric 6-DoF trajectories with fine-grained natural language instructions for egocentric manipulation.

## Method

- **DreamTraj** uses a 'Reader' to extract implicit object motion and scene context signals directly from an ==early denoising step (k=16)== of a frozen image-to-video diffusion model (Wan2.2), conditioned only on an initial RGB image and language instruction.
- A small (7.1M parameters) ==flow-matching Diffusion Transformer== then decodes these coarse latent signals into a 6-DoF trajectory sequence in a scale-free representation, with metric scale restored via ==monocular depth estimation==.
- The authors curated the 'Move' dataset, consisting of 5,038 human-inspected 6-DoF trajectories from real and generatively augmented video clips, each paired with fine-grained natural language descriptions.

## Results

- DreamTraj achieved superior performance over privileged baselines (EgoScaler and ObjectForesight) in 6-DoF trajectory prediction, e.g., an ADE of **3.04 cm** and **7.9°** rotation error on real data over 2.0s, outperforming EgoScaler's 7.52 cm and 28.6°.
- The framework demonstrated a **4.6x** faster inference speed (**76 seconds** for a 49-frame trajectory) compared to generate-then-extract pipelines that require full video generation and external perception modules.
- The 'Move' dataset contributes **5,038** high-quality, human-inspected 6-DoF object trajectories with fine-grained language instructions, addressing a critical data scarcity issue for language-conditioned robotic manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decodable object motion representations emerge in the internal, intermediate features of image-to-video diffusion models at early denoising steps, allowing direct trajectory prediction without full video synthesis.
> - Leveraging both object-specific attention maps (q·k tracks) for motion and anchor-pooled hidden states for scene context provides sufficient information for accurate 6-DoF trajectory estimation.
> - Training the trajectory decoder across both real recorded data and generatively augmented data, using a domain flag, helps bridge the feature gap and improves generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in embodied intelligence: the accurate anticipation of object trajectories during manipulation. This capability is considered fundamental for robotic planning, imitation learning, and action verification. Current research trends emphasize object-centric SE(3) (6-degrees-of-freedom, 6-DoF) trajectories as a compact and executable interface for robot control. However, obtaining precise 3D spatial trajectories with metric translation and rotation typically requires specialized sensors (e.g., depth sensors), CAD models, multi-view camera rigs, or marker-based setups, making large-scale annotation expensive compared to 2D data or coarse labels. The broader goal is to generate dynamic 3D object motion from minimal inputs, specifically a single 2D image and a natural language instruction.

Existing approaches to 6-DoF object trajectory prediction generally fall into two categories, both presenting limitations:

1. **Direct dynamics learning from interaction data:** These methods (e.g., ObjectForesight, EgoScaler) involve feed-forward or diffusion models regressing future object pose sequences from scene observations.
 - **Data limitations:** They often rely on datasets with either automatically pseudo-labeled trajectories (sacrificing quality for quantity) or manually curated but small datasets. Semantic annotations are frequently coarse (e.g., verb-noun tags), which is insufficient for learning fine-grained, language-conditioned object motion.
 - **Privileged inputs:** Many state-of-the-art predictors require additional inputs beyond a single RGB frame and instruction, such as multi-frame visual context, object CAD meshes, depth input, or the object's initial 6-DoF pose. These requirements restrict their applicability in unconstrained environments.

2. **Video generation followed by motion extraction (generate-then-extract pipelines):** This family of methods (e.g., RIGVid) utilizes large video generators to synthesize future videos and then employs a cascade of perception modules (e.g., segmentation, point tracking, depth estimation, pose fitting) to extract trajectories from the generated pixels.
 - **Computational cost:** Accessing the motion prior embedded in large video models through full video generation and subsequent pixel-space extraction incurs substantial inference costs, hindering real-time applications.

DreamTraj positions itself as a novel approach that seeks to overcome these limitations by addressing the supervision gap, eliminating the need for privileged inputs, and improving inference efficiency. It proposes to leverage the implicit motion knowledge within video diffusion models without requiring full video synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method capable of predicting an object's 6-DoF trajectory solely from a single RGB image and a natural language instruction. This objective is driven by several motivations arising from the limitations of existing methods:

1. **Addressing the Supervision Gap:** Current datasets for egocentric manipulation often lack high-quality, object-centric 6-DoF trajectories paired with fine-grained natural language instructions. Large-scale datasets typically offer only coarse verb-noun tags or noisy auto-mined 6-DoF data, which is insufficient for training models to understand and generate precise, language-conditioned object movements. The motivation is to create a new dataset, "Move," that provides this specific type of high-quality supervision.

2. **Minimizing Input Requirements:** Many existing 6-DoF trajectory predictors necessitate "privileged" inputs such as multi-frame video context, object CAD models, depth information, or the object's initial 6-DoF pose at inference time. These requirements severely limit their practical deployment in real-world scenarios where such information is often unavailable or difficult to obtain. The motivation is to reduce the input interface to its minimum: a single RGB image and a task instruction, making the system more broadly applicable.

3. **Improving Inference Efficiency:** Methods that leverage video generation models by synthesizing a complete future video and then applying a cascade of perception modules to extract trajectories are computationally intensive. The full denoising process of a video diffusion model combined with multiple external perception models leads to high inference costs, making these approaches slow. The motivation is to develop a more efficient mechanism to access the motion prior within video diffusion models, bypassing the need for explicit video generation and pixel-space extraction.

The central hypothesis motivating the DreamTraj approach is that a decodable, clip-specific object-motion representation emerges within the internal, intermediate features of an image-to-video diffusion model at an early denoising step, before a full video is synthesized. By "reading" this latent motion signal, the system could predict trajectories more efficiently and directly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamTraj is designed to predict a 6-DoF object trajectory over 49 future frames (approximately 2 seconds) in the camera frame of an initial RGB image (I0), given a language instruction (ℓ). The approach involves a frozen image-to-video diffusion backbone and a small, trainable "Reader."

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction (Move Dataset)

To address the supervision gap, the authors curated the Move dataset, comprising 5,038 object-centric egocentric 6-DoF object trajectories. The dataset construction followed a three-phase pipeline:
- **Selection:** 7,246 raw clips from six existing egocentric hand-object corpora (HOI4D, TACO, HOT3D, H2O, OakInk2, FPHA) were initially pooled, chosen for providing frame-wise 6-DoF poses of rigid manipulated objects. Clips were filtered to retain 2,975 instances representing meaningful, single, describable manipulations with clean, gap-free pose tracks and object groundability in the first frame.
- **Object-Centric Re-annotation:** The selected clips, originally with coarse action categories, were re-annotated. An AWQ-quantized Qwen3.5-27B model drafted object-centric labels (manipulated object, verb, manner of motion) from sampled frames, which were then human-corrected and used to refine action boundaries. This produced fine-grained language instructions.
- **Generative Augmentation:** To expand the dataset, the first frame and instruction of retained clips were fed into a Wan2.2 video generation model. Multiple plausible future videos were generated using fresh seeds. A trajectory extraction pipeline (detailed below) labeled these generated videos, and humans screened the recovered tracks, yielding an additional 2,063 generated trajectories. This augmentation increased coverage within existing object categories and verbs without widening the semantic scope.

#### 3.2. Trajectory Extraction Pipeline (for Generated Videos)

To obtain 6-DoF labels for generated videos, a pipeline similar to RigVid and ObjectForesight was employed, adapted for generated content:
- **Object Grounding:** GroundingDINO and SAM2 localized the object in the first frame.
- **Pose Tracking:** FoundationPose tracked the object's per-frame pose through render-and-compare, given an object mesh, metric depth, and camera geometry from SpatialTracker v2.
- **Adaptations for Generated Content:**
 - Metric depth was obtained from DA3, replacing relative-depth predictors to ensure consistent metric scale.
 - Object meshes were reconstructed by SAM 3D Objects, as CAD models were often unavailable or imprecise for the dataset's objects.

#### 3.3. DreamTraj Architecture and Process

DreamTraj's methodology consists of three stages:

1. **Reading Motion from a Frozen Video Prior:**
 - **Backbone:** A frozen Wan2.2 image-to-video diffusion transformer was used. It was conditioned on I0 and ℓ and allowed to denoise toward an imagined future.
 - **Early Readout:** Internal features were extracted at an early denoising step (step 16 of 40, corresponding to t ≈ 882 in Wan's schedule), identified as the point where the motion signal saturates. Stride-2 flow caching was applied to halve backbone forward computation.
 - **Complementary Signals:** Two types of signals were extracted:
 - **Object Motion:** Query-key (q·k) attention maps from a specific layer (block 28, head 32) were treated as an implicit point tracker. The object was grounded once in I0, and soft-matching against every patch of every frame (always against frame 0) yielded per-frame centroid (2D bearing), spread (relative depth cue), and in-plane rotation. These four scalars represented object geometry.
 - **Scene Context:** Hidden states from specific blocks (0, 19, 39) were mean-pooled over a 4x4 anchor grid and projected to 128 dimensions via PCA.
 - The Reader thus received a sequence of 13 vectors, each 132-dimensions (128 scene context + 4 object geometry).

2. **Flow-Matching Trajectory Reader:**
 - A small (7.1M parameters) holistic flow-matching DiT (Diffusion Transformer) model decoded the coarse motion signals into the final trajectory. This model denoises all 13 pose tokens simultaneously.
 - **Pose Representation:** The output was a sequence of relative 9-D pose tokens (y_t): (δu_t, δv_t) for normalized image coordinate displacement, s_t = log(z_t/z_0) for log-depth ratio, and r_t ∈ R^6 for continuous 6-D rotation representation. This parameterization is scale-free.
 - **Inputs to Reader:** The 132-D readout was projected to 13 context tokens (width 256) for cross-attention. The noisy trajectory (xτ) was the token stream being denoised. Clip-level conditioning (denoising time τ, SigLIP2 embedding of ℓ, and a domain flag d) was summed into an AdaLN-Zero modulation vector.
 - **Training and Inference:** The Reader was trained with conditional flow matching, including endpoint, SO(3)-geodesic, and smoothness terms. At inference, 20 Euler steps integrated the predicted velocity field.
 - **Metric Scale Restoration:** The 13 scale-free tokens were resampled to 49 frames. Metric units were restored using a monocular depth estimate of the input frame (e.g., Depth Anything V2), and camera intrinsics recovered the metric position.

3. **Training Across Two Feature Domains:**
 - To bridge the gap between features from generated videos (at deployment) and 6-DoF supervision from recorded videos, the Reader was trained on both domains with shared weights.
 - **Domain A (Real):** Recorded video features were obtained by noising a recorded video to the readout step and running one backbone forward, supervised by the true camera-frame poses.
 - **Domain B (Generated):** Backbone imagined a future from the first frame and instruction, supervised by its own generated video's motion (extracted via the pipeline in Sec. 4.2). This domain aligned training with deployment by using generated features.
 - A domain flag 'd' (Eq. 2) was used as a conditioning input to absorb domain differences. Training involved a warm-up on Domain B before mixing in Domain A, with noisier channels in Domain B being down-weighted.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the Move dataset, DreamTraj's performance, efficiency, and architectural choices:

#### 4.1. Move Dataset Contribution

- The Move dataset provides 5,038 human-inspected egocentric 6-DoF object trajectories, each paired with a fine-grained natural language instruction. This dataset directly addresses a recognized gap in high-quality supervision for language-conditioned object motion generation.
- The dataset comprises 2,975 real captured trajectories and 2,063 generated trajectories, sourced from various egocentric corpora, covering 14 object categories and 11 verbs.

#### 4.2. Trajectory Prediction Accuracy (Table 1)

- **Against EgoScaler:** DreamTraj consistently outperformed EgoScaler across all evaluated metrics (ADE, FDE, Rot, Rot-final) for both real and generated video evaluations. This occurred despite EgoScaler receiving privileged inputs such as depth and the object's initial 6-DoF pose. For example, on real capture data over a 2.0s window, DreamTraj achieved an ADE of 3.04 cm and a rotation error of 7.9°, compared to EgoScaler's 7.52 cm and 28.6°.
- **Against ObjectForesight:** DreamTraj also surpassed ObjectForesight in both translation and rotation accuracy, even when ObjectForesight was provided with privileged inputs like multi-frame video context, object CAD meshes, and three ground-truth context poses. For instance, on real capture data over a 1.17s horizon, DreamTraj recorded an ADE of 1.97 cm and a rotation error of 6.5°, while ObjectForesight achieved 2.71 cm and 7.8°. Performance benefits persisted when ObjectForesight was extended to the whole action.
- Qualitative comparisons further illustrated DreamTraj's capability to predict more accurate and coherent object movements.

#### 4.3. Efficiency (Table 2)

- DreamTraj demonstrated a substantial efficiency advantage over the generate-then-extract pipeline. It completed inference for a 49-frame trajectory in 76 seconds, which is 4.6 times faster than the 348 seconds required by the baseline pipeline (comprising video generation, VAE decoding, and a four-model perception stack). This speedup is attributed to the early readout from the diffusion backbone and avoidance of full video synthesis and external perception.

#### 4.4. Optimal Readout Configuration (Table 3)

- **Readout Step:** Experiments indicated that the optimal denoising step for reading motion signals was k=16 (out of 40). This step is located immediately after the backbone's transition from its high-noise to low-noise expert (t=900), where the implicit motion signal was found to saturate, and later steps provided minimal additional benefit.
- **Attention Head:** Block 28, head 32 of the Wan2.2 backbone was identified as providing the most informative query-key attention track for object motion, based on a permutation-controlled margin metric.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Table 4)

- **Pooled Hidden States:** Removing the anchor-pooled hidden states from the Reader's input degraded translation accuracy (ADE increased by 0.49 cm) and significantly reduced depth correlation, indicating their role in providing scene-level geometry necessary for metric displacement.
- **Query-Key Track:** Excluding the q·k attention track, which represents object-specific motion, resulted in the largest degradation in translation accuracy (ADE increased by 0.71 cm), confirming its importance for object positioning.
- **Instruction Conditioning:** Removing language instruction conditioning from the Reader primarily impacted rotation accuracy more than translation, suggesting that fine-grained language helps disambiguate various rotational possibilities for a given translational path.

#### 4.6. Generalization (Fig. 4, 8)

- DreamTraj demonstrated generalization capabilities by successfully predicting object trajectories in 80% of 50 unseen scenes and objects drawn from outside the training corpora, including video game frames and phone photographs. These qualitative results suggest the model's ability to extrapolate beyond its training distribution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, DreamTraj, offers several advancements with potential impacts on embodied intelligence and robotic manipulation:

- **Novel Trajectory Generation Paradigm:** DreamTraj introduces a new method for generating 6-DoF object trajectories by directly interpreting the internal representations of a frozen image-to-video diffusion model. This approach bypasses the conventional requirement of fully synthesizing a video and then extracting motion from pixels, representing a conceptual shift in leveraging generative models for motion forecasting.

- **Reduced Input Requirements and Broader Applicability:** By requiring only a single RGB image and a natural language instruction, DreamTraj significantly minimizes the input conditions compared to previous methods that rely on privileged information such as multi-frame video, depth maps, or object CAD models. This makes the system more robust and applicable in diverse, unconstrained real-world robotic environments where such privileged data is often unavailable or impractical to acquire.

- **Enhanced Efficiency for Robotic Applications:** The observed 4.6x speedup over generate-then-extract pipelines suggests that DreamTraj can enable more responsive and efficient robotic planning. Faster trajectory prediction is crucial for real-time control, allowing robots to anticipate object movements and react promptly, thereby closing the perception-action loop more effectively.

- **High-Quality Dataset for Language-Conditioned Motion:** The creation of the Move dataset, featuring 5,038 human-inspected, object-centric 6-DoF trajectories paired with fine-grained language instructions, addresses a critical data scarcity issue. This dataset provides the precise language-to-motion grounding necessary for training and evaluating advanced models in language-conditioned object manipulation.

- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** The ability of DreamTraj to accurately predict future object poses before an action occurs has implications for various embodied AI tasks. Robots could use these predictions for proactive grasp planning, learning object-level rather than pixel-level human demonstrations, and verifying whether an intended action will achieve its goal.

The proposed method represents a step toward more capable and autonomous robotic systems. A natural next step for this research involves deploying DreamTraj to directly drive a real manipulator using its predicted trajectories, which would further validate its practical utility.
