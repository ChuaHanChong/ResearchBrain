# LaVi: Efficient Large Vision-Language Models via Internal Feature Modulation

> [!summary] Summary
> LaVi, from researchers primarily at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, introduces a novel Feature Modulation Injection paradigm for large vision-language models (LVLMs) that significantly reduces computational overhead and memory usage. The approach achieves up to a 94.0% reduction in FLOPs and a 3.1x inference speedup while maintaining or improving performance on various image and video understanding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Integrating visual information into large language models (LLMs) often compromises their pre-trained linguistic knowledge or introduces excessive computational burden.
- Existing "architectural injection" methods like Flamingo disrupt the LLM's internal structure, potentially degrading its core language capabilities.
- "In-context injection" methods, such as LLaVA, lead to prohibitive computational costs (FLOPs, latency, memory) due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention with long visual token sequences.

## Method

- Introduces ==Feature Modulation Injection (FMI)==, a novel paradigm that directly modulates the Large Language Model's (LLM) internal hidden states with visual information.
- Develops ==Vision-Infused Layer Normalization (ViLN)== that dynamically adjusts the LLM's standard Layer Normalization parameters using vision-guided deltas (Δαv, Δβv).
- Employs a lightweight conditioning module, typically attention-based, to generate these deltas, with ==zero-initialization== to facilitate stable adaptation and preserve linguistic priors.

## Results

- Achieved up to a **94.0%** reduction in FLOPs, a **3.1x** inference speedup, and a **51.5%** memory reduction compared to state-of-the-art LLaVA models of similar scale.
- Outperformed or matched LLaVA baselines on 9 image understanding benchmarks (e.g., VQAv2, MMBench) with up to **19.4x** FLOPs reduction.
- Showed **6x** to **7x** reduction in FLOPs on 6 video understanding benchmarks (e.g., EgoSchema, MVBench) while maintaining or improving performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature Modulation Injection (FMI) significantly improves computational efficiency and convergence speed while better preserving the LLM's linguistic proficiency compared to prior integration methods.
> - Both multiplicative (scaling) and additive (shifting) modulations of Layer Normalization parameters based on visual input are crucial for effective vision-language integration.
> - Applying modulation to a moderate, uniformly distributed subset (e.g., 25%) of LLM layers provides optimal performance and scalability without compromising linguistic priors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has experienced rapid advancements, largely catalyzed by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs). LVLMs aim to enable AI systems to perceive and reason across both visual (images, videos) and linguistic modalities, leading to remarkable capabilities in tasks like visual question answering, image captioning, and multimodal dialogue. However, a fundamental challenge persists: the efficient and effective integration of visual information into LLMs without compromising their pre-trained linguistic knowledge or incurring prohibitive computational costs.

Existing approaches to multimodal integration generally fall into two categories:

- **Architectural Injection:** Methods like Flamingo augment the LLM's architecture by introducing new layers, such as cross-attention or feed-forward modules, to explicitly process visual features alongside linguistic ones. While offering direct fusion, this approach often disrupts the LLM's inherent structure, potentially degrading its carefully learned linguistic priors and making it less adept at language-only tasks.
- **In-context Injection:** Dominant approaches, such as the LLaVA series, integrate visual information by converting visual features into a sequence of "visual tokens" and concatenating them directly with the textual input. These visual tokens are then processed by the LLM's existing self-attention layers. While preserving the LLM's architectural integrity, this method introduces a significant computational bottleneck. The self-attention mechanism's quadratic complexity with respect to sequence length means that a large number of visual tokens (e.g., hundreds for a single image, thousands for high-resolution images or videos) leads to drastically increased FLOPs, latency, and memory consumption, hindering real-time applications.

LaVi positions itself as a novel solution that addresses the limitations of both architectural and in-context injection. It proposes a third paradigm: **Internal Feature Modulation Injection (FMI)**. This approach aims to achieve seamless and efficient vision-language fusion by modulating the internal hidden states of the LLM, specifically within the ubiquitous Layer Normalization (LN) mechanism. By avoiding new architectural layers and bypassing the direct concatenation of numerous visual tokens, LaVi seeks to preserve linguistic priors and ensure computational scalability, thereby contributing a new direction to the ongoing efforts to develop more efficient and practical LVLMs. This work directly responds to the community's demand for LVLMs that are not only capable but also deployable in resource-constrained environments or real-time scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind LaVi stems from a critical bottleneck in existing Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs): the inefficient visual-language integration. The authors identify that current state-of-the-art methods either compromise the foundational linguistic capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) or introduce severe computational burdens, particularly when dealing with long visual contexts from high-resolution images or videos.

Based on this analysis, the paper establishes two fundamental principles for an ideal visual-language integration strategy:

1. **Minimal Structural Interference:** The integration method should ensure the preservation of the LLM's meticulously pre-trained linguistic knowledge. This is crucial to maintain the LLM's coherence in text generation and its strong reasoning capabilities, which are essential for robust vision-grounded understanding. Existing architectural injection methods often fail this principle by introducing new layers that disrupt the LLM's internal processing flow.
2. **Computational Scalability:** The approach must mitigate the inefficiencies arising from the quadratic complexity inherent in self-attention mechanisms when processing extensive visual tokens. In-context injection methods, while preserving architectural integrity, significantly increase the input sequence length with visual tokens, leading to substantial increases in FLOPs, latency, and memory usage.

Guided by these principles, the **key objective** of this research is to propose and develop **LaVi (Language and Vision Integrator)**, a novel LVLM framework that enables seamless and efficient vision-language fusion. Specifically, LaVi aims to achieve this through a unique approach called **internal feature modulation** within the LLMs, which directly modulates linguistic hidden states based on visual input. This design is intended to ensure precise vision-language alignment, preserve the LLM’s linguistic priors, and drastically reduce computational costs compared to current dominant methods, ultimately establishing LaVi as a scalable and practical solution for real-time multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LaVi introduces a novel paradigm for visual-language integration called **Feature Modulation Injection (FMI)**, which deviates significantly from existing architectural and in-context injection methods. The core idea is to integrate visual information by directly modulating the internal hidden states of the Large Language Model (LLM) through a lightweight, adaptive transformation.

The central component of FMI is the **Vision-Infused Layer Normalization (ViLN)**. Layer Normalization (LN) is a ubiquitous component in modern LLMs, applying learnable affine transformations (scaling `α` and shifting `β`) to normalize features. LaVi proposes to make these affine parameters dynamically responsive to visual input. Specifically, ViLN modifies the standard LN operation as follows:

`ViLN(t, v) = (α + ∆α_v) ⊙ ˆt + (β + ∆β_v)`

Here, `t` represents the linguistic hidden state, `v` represents visual features, `ˆt` is the normalized linguistic feature, and `α` and `β` are the original learnable affine parameters of the LLM. The crucial additions are `∆α_v` and `∆β_v`, which are vision-guided modulation parameters. These "deltas" adaptively adjust the original scaling and shifting based on the visual context. This mechanism enables visual signals to modulate token-wise linguistic representations in a fine-grained and context-aware manner through both multiplicative (`∆α_v`) and additive (`∆β_v`) operations.

To generate these vision-conditioned deltas (`∆α_v`, `∆β_v`), LaVi employs a **conditioning module**, denoted as `Cond(t, v)`. This module is responsible for aggregating relevant visual context for each text token. The output of `Cond(t, v)` is then projected through a lightweight learnable mapping (a linear transformation followed by a Swish activation) to produce the `∆α_v` and `∆β_v` values. A critical design choice for this projection is **zero-initialization**, which ensures that the vision-conditioned deltas are initially zero. This means that at the beginning of training, the forward pass of the ViLN module exactly replicates the original LLM's behavior, thereby facilitating stable adaptation and robust preservation of linguistic priors.

The paper explores three alternative implementations for the `Cond(·)` function, each offering a different trade-off between computational efficiency and multimodal performance:

1. **MLP-based Conditioning (`Cond_mlp`):** Inspired by MLP-Mixer architectures, this approach concatenates the text token with visual features, then processes them through sequential MLPs designed for token-mixing (across tokens) and channel-mixing (across embedding dimensions). The vision-aware embedding for the text token is then extracted.
2. **Conv-based Conditioning (`Cond_conv`):** Drawing inspiration from ConvMixer, this treats the concatenated text and visual sequence as a 1-D signal. It applies a depth-wise convolution (for mixing information between text and visual features) followed by an activation and a point-wise convolution (for integrating features across dimensions).
3. **Attention-based Conditioning (`Cond_attn`):** This method uses a cross-attention mechanism where the text token acts as the query, and the visual tokens serve as keys and values. This directly aggregates relevant visual context to produce a vision-aware representation for each text token. This attention-based approach is adopted as the default due to its simplicity and effectiveness.

The overall LaVi architecture selectively replaces standard LN layers within the LLM with ViLN modules. In their experiments, the authors uniformly select 25% of the LLM's layers for this replacement.

**Scalable Visual Input Support:** LaVi's FMI paradigm inherently supports scalable visual inputs without significant architectural changes:

- **High-resolution Images:** A tiling strategy is adopted, where images are divided into non-overlapping tiles. Each tile is independently encoded by the vision encoder, and their resulting visual tokens are concatenated along the sequence dimension.
- **Videos:** For video inputs, `k` frames are uniformly sampled. Each frame is encoded, undergoes adaptive pooling, and the resulting frame features are concatenated sequentially. Shared temporal position encoding is applied to capture temporal dynamics.

**Training Process:** LaVi follows a two-stage training paradigm:
1. **Pre-training:** The conditioning module and the vision encoder are trained using publicly available image-text pairs (e.g., CC12M).
2. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The entire LaVi model, including the ViLN modules, is fine-tuned on multimodal instruction datasets (e.g., LLaVA-665K, LLaVA-760K, LLaVA-OneVision data) to align vision-language capabilities with user instructions. The vision encoder is unfrozen during SFT for better alignment.

The LLM backbones used in experiments include Vicuna-v1.5-7B and Qwen2-7B-Instruct, combined with vision encoders like CLIP ViT-L/336px and SigLIP ViT-SO400M/384px, allowing for fair comparisons with established LLaVA variants.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate LaVi's exceptional efficiency and strong performance across a wide range of image and video understanding benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Efficiency

LaVi achieves a remarkable balance between computational efficiency and benchmark accuracy, setting it apart from existing LVLMs.
- Compared to **LLaVA-OV-7B**, a state-of-the-art baseline of similar parameter scale, LaVi (Qwen2-7B version) shows:
 - A **94.0% reduction in FLOPs**.
 - A **3.1x improvement in inference speed**, with latency reduced from 612.5 ms to just 198.1 ms.
 - A **51.5% reduction in memory usage**.
- Remarkably, LaVi requires **fewer FLOPs than LLaVA-OV-0.5B** (a much smaller model) yet **surpasses its benchmark accuracy by +15.5 points**. This highlights the efficiency of the internal feature modulation strategy.

#### 4.2. Image Understanding Evaluation (Table 1)

On 9 image-based benchmarks (including VQAv2, GQA, TextVQA, MMBench, etc.), LaVi variants consistently outperform or match their LLaVA counterparts while achieving significant efficiency gains:
- **LaVi-Image (Vicuna-7B)** vs. LLaVA-v1.5: Achieves a **14.0x reduction in FLOPs** while improving average accuracy by **1.0%**.
- **LaVi-Image (HD) (Vicuna-7B)** vs. LLaVA-v1.6: Boasts a **19.4x reduction in FLOPs** with an average accuracy improvement of **0.5%**.
- **LaVi (Qwen2-7B)** vs. LLaVA-OV: Delivers a **16.8x reduction in FLOPs** and an average accuracy improvement of **0.5%**.
These results strongly support the efficiency of the Feature Modulation Injection (FMI) paradigm over current integration strategies.

#### 4.3. Video Understanding Evaluation (Table 2)

LaVi's efficiency benefits extend robustly to video understanding across 6 benchmarks (e.g., EgoSchema, MLVU, MVBench):
- LaVi demonstrates a **6x to 7x reduction in FLOPs** compared to baseline models with identical frame counts.
- The 32-frame version of LaVi requires FLOPs comparable to *half* of those needed by the 8-frame Video-LLaVA, while maintaining superior or matching performance across all tested frame configurations.
- This signifies excellent scalability for handling temporal visual data.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Section 4.3)

A detailed ablation study under identical settings (SigLIP ViT-SO400M + Qwen2-7B-Instruct) provides insights into LaVi's design choices:

- **Comparison of Integration Techniques (Table 4, Figure 4):**
 - FMI (especially attention-based) achieves the best balance of efficiency (lowest training time, FLOPs) and performance.
 - FMI converges faster during pre-training, requiring only 1/8th of the training time of in-context injection for comparable performance.
 - Crucially, FMI better preserves linguistic proficiency, demonstrating superior performance on language-only benchmarks (MMLU, MBPP, MATH) compared to architectural and in-context injection methods.
- **Effect of Modulation at Different Sublayers (Table 3):** Applying ViLN to both the self-attention and feed-forward sublayers of the LLM yields optimal multimodal performance. Modulating the self-attention sublayer appears more critical.
- **Effect of Modulation Parameters (Table 5):** Both `∆α_v` (scaling) and `∆β_v` (shifting) parameters contribute positively and comparably to overall performance, indicating that both multiplicative and additive modulations are important for effective visual information integration.
- **Effect of Modulation Frequency (Table 6):** Applying ViLN to **25%** of the LLM's layers yields the best average performance, suggesting that a moderate frequency is sufficient, and higher frequencies do not necessarily lead to further gains.
- **Effect of Modulation Location (Table 7):** **Uniformly distributing** ViLN across layers (e.g., every fourth layer if 25% are chosen) results in the most effective multimodal integration, outperforming concentration in shallow, middle, or deep layers.

#### 4.5. Visualization and Analysis (Section 4.4)

- **Preservation of Linguistic Capabilities (Figure 5):** LaVi exhibits minimal feature drift compared to the original base LLM on language-only tasks, visually confirming its ability to preserve linguistic priors—a key advantage over other integration methods.
- **Modulation Influence on Early Layers (Figure 6):** Visual features exert a stronger modulation influence (larger cosine distance of features before/after ViLN) with more variance in early LLM layers, while deeper layers show reduced and more stable influence. This suggests early layers establish cross-modal alignments, which are then refined in deeper layers.
- **Modulation Influence on Semantically Rich Tokens (Figure 7):** LaVi's modulation influences semantically rich tokens (nouns, verbs) more significantly than functional tokens (conjunctions, punctuation). This indicates a meaningful, context-aware integration of visual information into relevant linguistic concepts.
- **Superior Vision Sequence Scalability (Figures 8 & 9):** LaVi maintains significant FLOPs and memory reductions (92.0% FLOPs, 61.1% memory for 128 frames) compared to baselines as the number of frames (or visual tokens from high-res images) increases, confirming its exceptional computational scalability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LaVi represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by directly confronting the critical challenge of inefficient visual-language integration. Its introduction of the **Internal Feature Modulation Injection (FMI)** paradigm and the **Vision-Infused Layer Normalization (ViLN)** offers a novel and highly effective alternative to existing approaches.

The significance and potential impact of LaVi can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering a New Integration Paradigm:** LaVi moves beyond the limitations of "architectural injection" (which disrupts LLM integrity) and "in-context injection" (which incurs prohibitive computational costs). By embedding visual information directly into the LLM's internal feature transformations (specifically, layer normalization), it offers a more elegant and efficient way to fuse modalities without structural compromise or excessive context expansion. This could inspire future LVLM designs to explore similar internal modulation mechanisms.

2. **Unprecedented Efficiency Gains:** The dramatic reductions in FLOPs (up to 94%), inference latency (3.1x faster), and memory usage (halved) are game-changing. These improvements are crucial for deploying powerful LVLMs in real-world applications where computational resources are limited or real-time responses are required. Examples include:
 - **Edge AI devices:** Enabling sophisticated multimodal understanding on smartphones, drones, or smart cameras.
 - **Real-time interaction:** Powering intelligent assistants for video conferencing, live streaming analysis, or autonomous navigation.
 - **Large-scale deployment:** Reducing the energy consumption and carbon footprint of running large AI models in data centers.

3. **Preservation of Linguistic Prowess:** A key concern with integrating visual information into LLMs is the potential degradation of their pre-trained linguistic capabilities. LaVi's design, particularly the zero-initialization of modulation deltas and its superior performance on language-only benchmarks, demonstrates that it effectively preserves the LLM's rich linguistic priors. This ensures that LaVi can perform strong multimodal reasoning without sacrificing its ability to understand and generate coherent text.

4. **Enhanced Scalability to Complex Visual Data:** LaVi's ability to efficiently handle high-resolution images and long video sequences, with its FLOPs and memory usage scaling much more favorably than previous methods, makes it highly practical for complex vision tasks. This opens doors for applications in areas like:
 - **Medical imaging:** Analyzing high-resolution scans with textual queries.
 - **Surveillance and security:** Processing vast amounts of video data efficiently.
 - **Content creation and analysis:** Understanding and generating content from long-form videos.

5. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The exploration of different conditioning mechanisms (MLP, Conv, Attention) and the ablation studies on modulation frequency and location highlight the robustness of the FMI concept. The fact that a moderately frequent, uniformly distributed modulation is effective suggests a broadly applicable and tunable approach.

In conclusion, LaVi's contribution is not just an incremental improvement but a conceptual shift in how visual and linguistic modalities can be integrated within large models. By addressing the critical bottleneck of efficiency while maintaining or even enhancing performance and linguistic fidelity, LaVi paves the way for a new generation of more practical, scalable, and deployable multimodal AI systems. The planned release of code and models will undoubtedly accelerate further research and adoption of this promising paradigm.
