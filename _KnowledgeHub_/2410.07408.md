# Automated Creation of Digital Cousins for Robust Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed "digital cousins," virtual environments automatically generated from a single real-world image, enabling robust zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer. The Automated Creation of Digital Cousins (ACDC) pipeline leverages foundation models to produce diverse simulated scenes, leading to improved robot policy generalization compared to training on single digital twins.

## Problem

- Training robot policies for real-world deployment is unsafe, costly, and difficult to scale due to the inherent "sim-to-real gap."
- Existing solutions like broad domain randomization often lack quality or relevance, while high-fidelity digital twins are expensive, labor-intensive, and limit policy generalization to specific instances.
- Achieving sufficient environment diversity for robust and generalizable robot policy learning remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- Introduced "digital cousins" as virtual assets or scenes that preserve geometric and semantic affordances of real-world counterparts without requiring an exact, high-fidelity replica.
- Developed the Automated Creation of Digital Cousins (ACDC) pipeline to automatically generate interactive simulated scenes from a single real-world RGB image with zero human input.
- Utilized large language and vision foundation models (GPT-4, GroundedSAM-v2, DINOv2, Depth-Anything-v2, CLIP) for real-world object extraction, 3D reconstruction, semantic matching with virtual assets, and scene generation.

## Results

- The ACDC pipeline successfully generated high-quality digital cousin scenes, accurately reproducing object categories and models with high geometric fidelity.
- Policies trained on digital cousins exhibited significantly superior robustness in out-of-distribution (unseen) setups while maintaining comparable performance on original setups, outperforming digital twin policies.
- Digital cousins enabled robust zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer, achieving a 90% success rate for a door-opening task on a real-world cabinet, compared to 25% for a digital twin policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relaxing the requirement for an exact virtual replica enables fully automated generation of diverse, yet semantically relevant, training data that is critical for robustness.
> - A distribution of "digital cousins" provides a more effective and 'guided' form of domain randomization, leading to better policy generalization compared to arbitrary randomization or training on single digital twins.
> - Integrating cutting-edge foundation models facilitates an end-to-end, automated pipeline from a single real-world image to interactive simulated environments for robot policy learning.
