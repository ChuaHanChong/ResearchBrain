# Adaptive Chain-of-Focus Reasoning via Dynamic Visual Search and Zooming for Efficient VLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beijing Institute of Technology and BIGAI developed Adaptive Chain-of-Focus (Adaptive-CoF), a framework enabling Vision-Language Models to perform efficient multimodal reasoning by adaptively deciding when and where to zoom into image regions. This approach reduces unnecessary zoom-in operations by 75% and visual token consumption by 50% for equivalent accuracy, while establishing new state-of-the-art results on high-resolution and complex reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing "think-with-image" Vision-Language Models (VLMs) incur substantial computational costs due to proactive and frequent visual zooming, leading to excessive token generation.
- Current methods struggle to balance the need for fine-grained visual detail, crucial for complex tasks, with the computational efficiency required for practical VLM deployment.
- Interactive VLMs often lack an adaptive mechanism to dynamically determine whether and where to focus attention on image regions based on the specific query and current context.

## Method

- An ==Adaptive Chain-of-Focus (Adaptive-CoF)== framework allows the VLM to iteratively refine visual understanding by selectively invoking "zoom-in" operations on key regions only when assessed as necessary.
- The model is trained using a ==two-stage pipeline==: initial Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) on a custom MM-Adaptive-CoF dataset, which includes fine-grained reasoning trajectories.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a novel ==Adaptive Group-Aware Reward (AGAR)== is then applied to optimize the model for both accuracy and efficient, adaptive visual interaction.

## Results

- Adaptive-CoF achieved state-of-the-art performance on high-resolution benchmarks like V* Bench (**90.1**) and HR-Bench 4K, and the MME-RealWorld-Lite comprehensive reasoning benchmark, surpassing larger models.
- The framework demonstrated exceptional computational efficiency, reducing zoom-in operations by **75%** and visual token usage by **50%** to achieve comparable peak accuracy against existing baselines.
- Adaptive-CoF exhibited intelligent behavior by inversely correlating zoom-in operations with input image resolution, performing significantly fewer calls at higher resolutions when sufficient detail was already present.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can be trained to adaptively allocate visual processing resources, dynamically deciding whether to zoom or respond, leading to substantial efficiency gains over proactive approaches.
> - A meticulously constructed dataset, incorporating diverse zoom-in requirements and step-by-step reasoning trajectories, is essential for effectively supervising and shaping adaptive visual search behaviors in VLMs.
> - The design of an adaptive, group-aware reward mechanism in reinforcement learning is crucial for guiding the model to balance accuracy with computational efficiency by explicitly penalizing superfluous visual explorations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress, largely driven by the integration of visual encoders with large language models (LLMs). Early multimodal reasoning efforts primarily focused on processing an image holistically and then performing textual chain-of-thought reasoning within the LLM. While effective for general understanding, this approach often struggled with tasks requiring fine-grained visual details, especially when dealing with small objects in high-resolution images, as crucial information could be lost or compressed during the initial visual encoding.

A significant breakthrough came with the emergence of the "think-with-image" interactive paradigm, inspired by advancements in long reasoning in LLMs (e.g., OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1). Models like OpenAI’s o3 demonstrated the ability to actively explore visual information by interleaving textual thoughts with dynamic manipulations of image regions, such as zooming in on relevant areas to acquire fine-grained visual cues. Subsequent models like DeepEyes and Pixel Reasoner further leveraged Reinforcement Learning (RL) to guide this visual exploration process, enabling them to perceive details previously invisible and ground their reasoning in concrete visual evidence.

However, a critical limitation of these interactive approaches is their *proactive* nature. Constant or overly frequent zooming, while enhancing detail perception, incurs massive computational costs due to the generation of excessive visual tokens. This inefficiency can also be detrimental by pushing models beyond their context window limits or by distracting them with irrelevant details.

This paper positions **Adaptive Chain-of-Focus (Adaptive-CoF)** as a direct solution to this performance-efficiency trade-off. It addresses the core challenge of balancing the need for fine-grained visual information with the computational overhead of acquiring it. Unlike proactive zooming models, Adaptive-CoF aims to enable VLMs to *adaptively and dynamically decide* whether the current visual evidence is sufficient, and *when* to zoom in for essential details. This aligns with a broader trend in LLM research towards "adaptive reasoning," where models intelligently allocate computational resources based on task complexity, often by routing tasks to specialized models or activating different reasoning modes. Adaptive-CoF extends this concept to the multimodal domain, learning an internal, context-aware policy for visual exploration, rather than relying on external routers or heuristic rules. By doing so, it pushes the frontier of VLM capabilities towards more intelligent, efficient, and human-like visual reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, **Adaptive Chain-of-Focus (Adaptive-CoF)**, that empowers Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform efficient and accurate multimodal reasoning through adaptive visual search and zooming. The core idea is to enable VLMs to intelligently decide *when* and *where* to focus their attention on image regions, rather than engaging in indiscriminate or proactive visual exploration.

The motivation behind this objective stems from critical limitations observed in existing VLM paradigms:
- **Inefficiency of Proactive Zooming:** While "think-with-image" models have greatly improved fine-grained reasoning by allowing dynamic image manipulation (zooming and cropping), their tendency to perform these operations proactively and frequently leads to significant computational costs. Generating excessive visual tokens from unnecessary zoom-ins consumes substantial resources and can even impair the model's ability to maintain a global understanding of the image within long context windows.
- **Balancing Performance and Efficiency:** There is an inherent trade-off between the depth of visual exploration (which typically enhances accuracy on fine-grained tasks) and computational efficiency. Current methods often lean too heavily on one side, either sacrificing detail for speed (e.g., holistic processing) or sacrificing speed for detail (e.g., proactive zooming). The motivation is to strike an optimal balance, achieving high performance without incurring prohibitive computational overhead.
- **Lack of Adaptive Strategy:** Existing interactive VLMs often lack a sophisticated mechanism to assess the necessity of zooming. They might zoom in even when sufficient information is available at a lower resolution or when the query does not require such granular detail. The researchers are motivated to imbue VLMs with an "adaptive" capability, allowing them to dynamically assess the required level of visual detail based on both the input image and the specific query.
- **Enhancing Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** Ultimately, the goal is to develop more robust, general-purpose VLMs that can handle a wider range of visual reasoning tasks, from simple direct answers to complex multi-step explorations, while remaining computationally practical for real-world deployment.

By addressing these points, Adaptive-CoF aims to establish a new paradigm for VLMs that is both highly accurate and exceptionally efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Adaptive Chain-of-Focus (Adaptive-CoF) framework equips VLMs with adaptive multimodal reasoning capabilities through a two-stage training pipeline: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). The method builds upon the architecture of existing VLMs, specifically Qwen2.5-VL-7B, by enabling it to selectively invoke visual evidence through key region localization and zoom operations.

#### 3.1. Architecture and Formulation

Adaptive-CoF operates in multiple steps. At each step, the VLM (comprising a vision encoder, a projector, and an LLM) can either generate a textual response (if current visual evidence is sufficient) or identify key regions with bounding boxes for further inspection. If fine-grained analysis is needed, the model invokes a "zoom-in" operation on these bounding boxes. The visual tokens extracted from these zoomed regions are then appended to the model's context, allowing for joint reasoning over both textual and enhanced visual information. This process creates a "chain of focus" where the model iteratively refines its visual understanding. The framework leverages Qwen2.5-VL's inherent tool-calling capability for cropping and zooming, standardizing the interface for visual operations.

#### 3.2. MM-Adaptive-CoF Dataset Construction

A crucial aspect of Adaptive-CoF is the meticulously constructed MM-Adaptive-CoF dataset, designed for both SFT and RL stages. It is built upon high-resolution images from the SA_1B dataset.

- **Query-Response Generation:**
 - 360 images from SA_1B are upscaled to 4K.
 - GPT-4.1 generates four queries per image, filtered for consistency with Qwen-2.5-VL-72B to ensure high-quality QA pairs.
 - To capture adaptive reasoning, each image is resized to multiple resolutions (224px to 4K). Qwen-2.5-VL-72B is prompted (five times for consistency) to assess if a query can be answered directly at each resolution. This generates data with *dynamic zoom-in requirements* (same query, different needs at different resolutions) and *diverse zoom-in needs* (different queries, different needs on the same image).

- **MM-Adaptive-CoF Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Dataset:**
 - This dataset comprises 2,201 QA pairs requiring zoom-in and 2,294 pairs answerable directly.
 - **Visual Search Agent:** For zoom-in-required QA pairs, a sophisticated visual search agent generates step-by-step reasoning trajectories.
 - **Core Controller:** GPT-4.1-mini, leveraging its ability to directly perceive and analyze images for rational plan generation.
 - **Components (Instantiated by Qwen2.5-VL-7B):**
 - **Locate Tool:** Returns bounding boxes for specified objects.
 - **VLM Understanding Tool:** Performs VQA on an image and query.
 - **Adjust Bbox Tool:** Refines bounding boxes based on textual instructions (e.g., "expand leftward").
 - **Verifier Module:** Evaluates output correctness against ground truth, provides feedback, and determines if further exploration is needed.
 - **Process:** The agent engages in an iterative thought-tool execution loop. It identifies and refines bounding boxes, uses the VLM understanding tool, and receives immediate feedback from the verifier. This self-correction cycle generates faithful and interpretable reasoning traces, which are then summarized into coherent explanations by DeepSeek-V3. This process explicitly supervises the intermediate reasoning steps, crucial for training adaptive VLMs.
 - For direct-answer QA pairs, GPT-4.1 constructs appropriate reasoning chains.
 - **Dataset Analysis:** Statistics show the dataset is balanced across resolutions, captures realistic zoom-in requirements, and covers diverse reasoning depths (most requiring 1-2 turns, few requiring many).

- **MM-Adaptive-CoF Reinforcement Learning (RL) Dataset:**
 - Approximately 10,000 diverse query-response instances.
 - Includes 1,500 proprietary MM-Adaptive-CoF QA pairs, augmented with public datasets like VisDrone, ArxivQA, and ThinkLite-VL to enhance diversity and robustness.

#### 3.3. Training Pipeline

- **Stage 1: Cold-Start Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):**
 - The Qwen2.5-VL-7B model is fine-tuned on the MM-Adaptive-CoF SFT dataset.
 - The ViT backbone is frozen, and LoRA fine-tuning is applied to the projector (2-layer MLP) and the Qwen2.5-7B language model.
 - Training uses a standard cross-entropy loss objective, enabling the model to generate coherent reasoning traces and understand the expected format for both textual and multimodal reasoning (interleaving text, bounding box predictions, and zoom-in operations).

- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - The SFT-trained model is further optimized using the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm on the MM-Adaptive-CoF RL dataset.
 - **Adaptive Group-Aware Reward (AGAR):** This is a key innovation. Instead of a simple binary reward, AGAR is designed to encourage both high accuracy and adaptive efficiency.
 - It combines correctness ($c_i$) and format validity ($f_i$) indicators for each rollout response.
 - A **group-level signal ($g$)** is introduced, indicating if *any* correct direct answer exists within a sampled group of G reasoning trajectories.
 - The reward for a zoom-in path ($z_i$) is dynamically discounted by $(1 - \delta \cdot g)$ if an efficient solution (direct answer) exists in the group ($g=1$). This penalizes unnecessary zooming when a simpler solution is available.
 - A correct direct answer ($d_i$) always receives the maximum reward (1), promoting efficiency. Incorrect answers receive a small format bonus ($\gamma \cdot f_i$).
 - Crucially, visual tokens are masked out during GRPO loss computation, ensuring policy updates primarily focus on the textual reasoning and decision-making (when and where to zoom), rather than the external visual embeddings themselves.

This two-stage approach, particularly the data generation with the visual search agent and the adaptive group-aware reward in RL, is designed to imbue the VLM with the intelligence to adapt its visual exploration strategy dynamically, thereby achieving a superior balance of accuracy and efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Adaptive-CoF achieves superior performance and exceptional efficiency, establishing a new state-of-the-art (SOTA) for open-source VLMs in the 7B-8B parameter class.

#### 4.1. Performance Against General Models

- **High-Resolution Benchmarks (V⋆ Bench, HR-Bench):** Adaptive-CoF significantly outperforms its base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with an 18.9% gain on the V⋆ Bench. It also shows strong improvements on HR-Bench 4K and 8K, demonstrating superior capability in fine-grained perception and reasoning in high-resolution images.
- **Comprehensive Reasoning (MME-RealWorld-Lite):** Adaptive-CoF achieves an overall score of 50.9, outperforming all listed models, including much larger 72B variants of other VLMs. This is attributed to its enhanced fine-grained perception and reasoning.
- **Hallucination and Specialized Reasoning (POPE, TallyQA, SpatialScore):** Adaptive-CoF sets a new SOTA on the POPE benchmark for hallucination reduction (3.4% gain over baseline). It also shows strong improvements on TallyQA (6.4% gain in quantitative reasoning) and SpatialScore (5.4% gain in spatial reasoning), showcasing its robustness and specialized reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.2. Performance Against Visual Grounded Reasoning Models

- **High-Resolution Fine-Grained Benchmarks:** Adaptive-CoF achieves a SOTA score of 90.1 on V⋆ Bench and the overall SOTA on HR-Bench 4K, while remaining highly competitive on HR-Bench 8K.
- **Comprehensive Reasoning:** It shows robust generalization on MME-RealWorld-Lite, achieving a score very close to the SOTA and leading in several sub-categories, demonstrating its effectiveness across diverse tasks.

#### 4.3. Exceptional Computational Efficiency (Adaptive Zoom-in Analysis)

- **Reduced Zoom-in Operations:** A key finding is Adaptive-CoF's remarkable efficiency. Compared to DeepEyes on HR-Bench 4K, it reduces zoom-in operations by 77.8% and uses only about 5.4% of the visual tokens. On V⋆ Bench, zoom-in operations are reduced by 75%. This indicates that the adaptive strategy effectively avoids unnecessary computations.
- **Adaptive Behavior with Resolution:** The model's mean zoom-in operations are inversely correlated with image resolution. At lower resolutions (e.g., 224px), it performs more zoom-ins (around 2.5 calls) to acquire details. At higher resolutions (e.g., 2560px), it makes significantly fewer calls (about 0.5 calls), recognizing that sufficient detail is already present.
- **Marginal Improvement in Token Efficiency (MITE Score):** The MITE score quantifies the accuracy gain per 100 additional visual tokens. Adaptive-CoF shows exceptionally high MITE scores at resolutions above 1024px, peaking at 30.8. This means its accuracy improvements at high resolutions are achieved with minimal additional token cost.
- **Overall Token Savings:** Adaptive-CoF achieves the same level of peak accuracy as its baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) while consuming nearly 50% fewer visual tokens, demonstrating a substantial computational saving.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Importance of SFT:** The supervised fine-tuning (cold-start) stage provides a substantial performance boost, highlighting its criticality in establishing foundational Adaptive-CoF reasoning patterns and tool-calling comprehension.
- **Necessity of Adaptive Group-Aware Reward (AGAR):** An RL variant with a simpler correctness+format reward fails to encourage visual reasoning; the model tends to avoid zoom-in. This strongly validates that AGAR is essential for learning the adaptive strategy, preventing the collapse of adaptive interaction behaviors and ensuring effective decision-making regarding zoom-ins.
- **Validation of Visual Interaction:** A text-only CoT variant without zoom-in reasoning shows a clear performance drop, confirming that explicit visual interaction and adaptive search are necessary and cannot be fully substituted by purely textual reasoning.

In summary, Adaptive-CoF not only achieves leading accuracy across diverse and challenging benchmarks but also sets a new standard for computational efficiency in multimodal reasoning by intelligently deciding when and how to interact with visual data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Adaptive Chain-of-Focus (Adaptive-CoF) framework represents a significant advancement in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with profound implications for both research and real-world applications.

- **Resolution of the Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** The most immediate and significant impact of Adaptive-CoF is its successful resolution of the long-standing trade-off between performance and efficiency in "think-with-image" VLMs. By enabling models to adaptively and dynamically decide when to perform visual search and zooming, the framework dramatically reduces computational costs (e.g., 75% fewer zoom-in operations, 50% fewer tokens for equivalent peak accuracy) while maintaining or even surpassing state-of-the-art accuracy. This makes powerful, fine-grained visual reasoning more practical and accessible.

- **Enhanced and Reliable Multimodal Reasoning:** Adaptive-CoF allows VLMs to achieve a more thorough, accurate, and reliable visual understanding. Its ability to dynamically focus on critical regions ensures that fine-grained details, often missed by holistic processing or in low-resolution contexts, are effectively captured. This leads to superior performance on tasks requiring high-resolution perception, complex spatial reasoning, and quantitative analysis, while also reducing hallucination rates.

- **Increased Practicality and Deployment Potential:** The substantial efficiency gains mean that sophisticated VLM capabilities can be deployed in environments with limited computational resources, such as edge devices or mobile applications. Faster inference times also make these models more viable for real-time interactive systems, customer service, or visual assistance tools where prompt responses are crucial. This democratization of advanced VLM capabilities could unlock a new wave of applications.

- **Foundational for Future AI Agents:** The adaptive reasoning mechanism, where an AI agent intelligently queries and processes visual information based on context and need, aligns closely with the long-term goal of developing more capable and autonomous AI agents. This work lays a strong foundation for building more sophisticated multimodal agents that can interact with the world in a more human-like, resourceful, and efficient manner.

- **Improved Interpretability:** The "chain of focus" steps generated by Adaptive-CoF can offer greater interpretability into the model's reasoning process. By explicitly showing where and why the model chose to zoom in or shift its focus, users can gain a clearer understanding of how the VLM arrived at its conclusions, fostering trust and facilitating debugging.

- **Catalyst for Further Research:** The introduced MM-Adaptive-CoF dataset and the adaptive group-aware reward mechanism are valuable contributions that can spur further research into adaptive and efficient multimodal learning. The paper explicitly points to future work extending this mechanism to interactive settings like visual dialogue and multi-image reasoning across scenes, suggesting a fertile ground for subsequent innovation.

In essence, Adaptive-CoF marks a critical step towards creating truly intelligent VLMs that can judiciously allocate their cognitive (computational) resources, moving beyond brute-force processing to a more nuanced, adaptive, and efficient understanding of the visual world.
