# CLaD: Planning with Grounded Foresight via Cross-Modal Latent Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> CLaD introduces a framework for robotic manipulation that learns to decouple kinematic and semantic state transitions, predicting grounded future latent states through cross-modal latent dynamics. The approach achieves an average success rate of 94.7% on the LIBERO-LONG benchmark, matching or exceeding larger models while using significantly fewer parameters and demonstrating higher computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation planning struggles to effectively coordinate coupled changes in robot proprioceptive state (kinematics) and visual scene semantic state.
- Current planning methods that rely on explicit semantic reasoning (e.g., subgoal images, textual plans) are computationally expensive and lack real-time applicability.
- Latent space planning offers efficiency but often conflates semantic and kinematic information, leading to physically or logically inconsistent trajectories due to unaligned representations during rollout.

## Method

- **CLaD** proposes a two-stage framework: first, learning ==cross-modal latent dynamics== to predict grounded future latent states (foresights), and then using these foresights to condition a diffusion-based action policy.
- It uses modality-specific transition embeddings and an ==asymmetric cross-attention== mechanism, where proprioceptive transitions query semantic transitions, to model how kinematic and semantic states jointly evolve.
- Grounded latent foresights are learned through self-supervised objectives, including an EMA-based latent prediction loss and an auxiliary reconstruction loss, to ensure predictions are robust and anchored to observable reality.

## Results

- CLaD achieved an average success rate of **94.7%** on the 10 LIBERO-LONG tasks, outperforming models with significantly more parameters (e.g., OpenVLA 7B, "pi"0.5_{**0.5**}**0.5** 3.3B).
- The framework demonstrated high computational efficiency, utilizing 0.66B parameters, **4 GB** memory, and achieving an inference rate of **25 Hz**, making it faster and more resource-efficient than larger baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining both proprioceptive and semantic foresights, the auxiliary reconstruction loss for grounding, and the asymmetric cross-attention design are crucial for CLaD's high performance and consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling cross-modal transitions (how proprioceptive and semantic states co-evolve) under actions, rather than just aligning static observations, is essential for robust and consistent robotic planning.
> - An asymmetric cross-attention design, where proprioceptive transitions query semantic transitions, provides an effective inductive bias for interpreting visual changes through kinematic context.
> - Anchoring abstract latent future state predictions (foresights) to observable proprioceptive and semantic states via an auxiliary reconstruction loss is critical for preventing representation collapse and maintaining real-world grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation tasks inherently involve coupled changes in both the robot's proprioceptive state (kinematic transitions, e.g., joint movements) and the visual scene's semantic state (semantic transitions, e.g., object displacement). Effectively coordinating these modalities during planning is a key challenge in robot learning.

Existing approaches to robotic planning generally fall into two categories:
- **Planning with Semantic Reasoning:** These methods often involve generating computationally expensive semantic artifacts, such as subgoal images or textual reasoning steps. Examples include language models grounding actions (e.g., SayCan), chain-of-thought methods for task decomposition, and image or video generation for guiding policies (e.g., SuSIE, Seer). While effective, these methods incur significant computational overhead due to iterative semantic generation.
- **Planning in Latent Space:** These approaches operate in compact learned representations to achieve computational efficiency. This category includes world model-based planning (e.g., recurrent state-space models like Dreamer or implicit trajectory optimization like TD-MPC2) and planning as latent inference (e.g., LBP, which uses backward reasoning from a goal). While efficient, a limitation of these methods is that their latent representations often implicitly conflate semantic and kinematic information without explicit mechanisms to ensure consistent evolution across different modalities. This can lead to latent representations decoupling during rollout, potentially resulting in physically or logically inconsistent trajectories.

Furthermore, prior research on **Cross-Modal Representation Learning in Robotics** has primarily focused on aligning static observations across modalities at individual timesteps. For instance, methods align visual observations with language instructions through contrastive objectives or embed robot kinematic states and image observations into a shared space using pre-trained transformers. However, these methods typically do not explicitly model *how* semantic and kinematic states jointly *change* under actions, thus lacking mechanisms to ensure consistency between changes in the visual scene and changes in the robot's joint configurations.

CLaD positions itself to address these limitations by proposing a framework that explicitly models how proprioceptive and semantic states jointly evolve under actions. Unlike prior work, CLaD emphasizes learning correlations between *transitions* rather than static states, aiming to capture the causal coupling between co-occurring kinematic and semantic changes. It seeks to combine the efficiency of latent space planning with the robustness of grounded, cross-modal reasoning, thereby avoiding the computational expense of explicit semantic generation while ensuring modality consistency, a gap that previous latent planning and cross-modal representation methods have not fully addressed.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for CLaD arises from the recognition that robotic manipulation tasks involve inherently coupled kinematic and semantic changes. Existing planning paradigms struggle to effectively integrate these cross-modal transitions, leading to specific shortcomings:

1. **Computational Inefficiency of Semantic Artifact Generation:** Current methods that rely on explicit semantic reasoning, such as generating subgoal images or textual plans, are often computationally expensive. This iterative generation process can limit real-time applicability and scalability. CLaD aims to bypass this by operating entirely within a compact latent space, predicting future states implicitly rather than explicitly generating semantic artifacts.

2. **Lack of Cross-Modal Alignment in Latent Space Planning:** While latent space planning offers computational efficiency, existing methods often learn latent representations that do not explicitly constrain how different modalities evolve together. This implicit conflation can lead to a decoupling of proprioceptive and semantic representations during task execution, potentially generating trajectories that are physically inconsistent with the visual scene or logically inconsistent with the task goal. CLaD's objective is to introduce explicit mechanisms, specifically cross-modal latent dynamics, to ensure that the latent representations of semantic and kinematic states remain aligned and consistent throughout a transition.

3. **Need for Grounded Future State Prediction:** To facilitate effective robot control, predicted future states (or subgoals) must be robust and reliably anchored to observable quantities. Abstract latent predictions, if not properly grounded, can suffer from representation collapse or drift, losing their correspondence with the real-world environment. CLaD aims to predict "grounded latent foresights" through a self-supervised learning scheme combined with auxiliary reconstruction losses, ensuring that these predictions are both abstract and rooted in observable proprioceptive and semantic states.

4. **Parameter Efficiency for Large-Scale Tasks:** Many state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models achieve high performance but at the cost of billions of parameters, demanding substantial computational resources for training and inference. A key objective for CLaD is to achieve competitive performance on complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks with significantly fewer parameters, thereby demonstrating a more parameter-efficient and scalable approach to robot planning.

In summary, CLaD's key objectives are to:
- Develop a cross-modal dynamics model that learns how proprioceptive and semantic transitions jointly evolve under actions.
- Predict compact, grounded future latent states (latent foresights) from these shared cross-modal dynamics.
- Utilize these foresights to condition a diffusion policy for efficient and expressive low-level control.
- Demonstrate high performance on challenging long-horizon tasks while maintaining computational and parameter efficiency compared to larger models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLaD adopts a two-stage framework designed to first learn grounded foresight through cross-modal latent dynamics and then use this foresight to guide a diffusion-based policy for action generation.

**Stage 1: Learning Cross-modal Latent Dynamics and Grounded Latent Foresight**

This stage focuses on capturing how proprioceptive and semantic states co-evolve under actions and predicting future latent states (foresights).

1. **Transition Embedding:**
 - At each timestep $t$, a proprioceptive state $p_t$ (e.g., joint angles, velocities) and a semantic state $s_t$ (derived from visual observation $v_t$ and language instruction $l$ via a frozen pre-trained VLM with a FiLM layer) are encoded into high-dimensional representations.
 - These states are then used to extract *transition representations*. Specifically, modality-specific cross-attention modules compute $z_p$ and $z_s$. For proprioception, $z_p$ is derived from querying current proprioceptive state $p_t$ with past proprioceptive state $p_{t-\tau}$ and the action sequence $a_{t-\tau:t}$. A similar process occurs for semantics to produce $z_s$. This design enables the extraction of embeddings that compress heterogeneous transition structures and capture causal dependencies on past state-action pairs.
 - During training, action tokens in $a_{t-\tau:t}$ are stochastically replaced with a learnable token (masking ratio $r=0.3$), encouraging the model to infer transitions from state differences and enhancing robustness.

2. **Learning Cross-modal Dynamics:**
 - To capture the correlated transitions between modalities, CLaD employs an **asymmetric cross-attention** mechanism. Proprioceptive transition representations ($z_p$) query semantic transition representations ($z_s$) to produce a shared dynamics representation $z_{p \to s}$.
 - This design choice is based on the intuition that kinematic transitions (how the robot moves) provide a grounded context for interpreting scene-level semantic changes (how the environment changes).
 - The resulting $z_{p \to s}$ is then projected into a compact representation $z_{dyn}$ using a learnable pooling mechanism. $z_{dyn}$ represents the "Cross-modal Latent Dynamics."

3. **Learning Grounded Latent Foresight:**
 - From the learned cross-modal dynamics $z_{dyn}$, lightweight MLP decoders ($g_p, g_s$) predict future latent foresights for both modalities: $\hat{z}_{t+\tau}^p$ and $\hat{z}_{t+\tau}^s$. This ensures that predicted future states adhere to the learned cross-modal dynamics.
 - To prevent representation collapse and ensure the foresights are "grounded" in observable reality, CLaD utilizes two self-supervised learning objectives:
 - **EMA-based Latent Prediction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{latent}$):** This objective employs exponential moving average (EMA) target encoders to compute stable target embeddings ($\bar{z}_{t+\tau}^p, \bar{z}_{t+\tau}^s$) from actual future states $p_{t+\tau}$ and $s_{t+\tau}$. The loss is computed as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted foresights and the L2-normalized EMA targets, preventing magnitude collapse while preserving semantic angular relationships.
 - **Auxiliary Reconstruction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{recon}$):** Lightweight decoders ($h_p, h_s$) are used to reconstruct the raw proprioceptive state $p_{t+\tau}$ and the visual component of the semantic state $s_{t+\tau}^v$ from the predicted latent foresights $\hat{z}_{t+\tau}^p$ and $\hat{z}_{t+\tau}^s$, respectively. This $L_1$ loss explicitly anchors the latent representations to observable quantities, preventing the latent space from drifting into excessive abstraction.
 - The overall objective for Stage 1 is a combination of these two losses: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{latent} + \lambda_{recon}\mathcal{L}_{recon}$, where $\lambda_{recon}=0.1$.

**Stage 2: Diffusion Policy Guided by Latent Foresight**

This stage leverages the learned grounded latent foresights from Stage 1 to condition a diffusion policy for generating action sequences.

1. **Foresight-conditioned Diffusion Control:**
 - The predicted latent foresights $\hat{z}_{t+\tau}^p$ and $\hat{z}_{t+\tau}^s$ from the *frozen* CLaD model are retrieved.
 - Current proprioceptive ($p_t$) and semantic ($s_t^v, s_l$) observations are encoded.
 - Each foresight embedding is adaptively modulated with its corresponding current observation using FiLM layers, producing conditioned representations $g_p$ and $g_s$. This modulation anchors the predicted foresight to the current observational context.
 - A standard Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)-based Diffusion Policy is then trained to generate action sequences $a_{t:t+\tau}$. The policy's noise prediction network is conditioned on the current noisy action $a_k$, diffusion timestep $k$, and the modulated foresights $g_p, g_s$. This allows the foresights to act as implicit, learned latent subgoals, guiding the policy without explicit semantic generation.

The entire CLaD model, including the VLM (0.1B), CLaD dynamics (0.33B), and Policy (0.23B), totals 0.66B parameters. Stage 1 is trained for 25K steps (2 hours), and Stage 2 for 200K steps (20 hours) on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CLaD focused on its performance on long-horizon manipulation tasks, computational efficiency, and the efficacy of its architectural components through ablation studies, primarily utilizing the LIBERO-LONG benchmark.

**Performance on Long-Horizon Planning (LIBERO-LONG):**
CLaD achieved an average success rate of 94.7% on the 10 LIBERO-LONG tasks. This performance was competitive with, and in some cases surpassed, larger Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:
- CLaD (0.66B parameters) outperformed OpenVLA (7B parameters, 93.8% success rate) and π$_{0.5}$ (3.3B parameters, 93.2% success rate) while utilizing significantly fewer parameters.
- Among methods of comparable scale, CLaD demonstrated stronger performance, surpassing SuSIE (0.86B, 76.3% success) by 18.2 percentage points and Seer (0.32B, 87.7% success) by 6.8 percentage points.
- CLaD also exhibited more stable performance across the 10 tasks compared to some baselines. For instance, on Task 9 ("put both pots on stove"), which involves perceptual ambiguity and fine-grained alignment, CLaD achieved 81.3% success compared to LBP's 60.0%, suggesting the benefit of explicit cross-modal dynamics in resolving such ambiguities.

**Computational Efficiency:**
CLaD demonstrated computational advantages compared to large-scale VLA models:
- **Parameters:** CLaD used 0.66B parameters, considerably less than OpenVLA (7.0B) and π$_{0.5}$ (3.3B).
- **Memory:** CLaD required 4 GB of memory, which is less than OpenVLA (15 GB) and π$_{0.5}$ (19 GB).
- **Inference Time:** CLaD achieved an inference rate of 25 Hz, faster than OpenVLA (6 Hz) and π$_{0.5}$ (10 Hz), enabling real-time deployment.
- **Planning Latency:** CLaD's planning latency was 0.012 seconds, which is competitive with other latent planning methods like LBP (0.008s) while yielding a higher average success rate (94.7% vs 88.6%). This indicates a favorable trade-off between model capacity and performance within the latent planning paradigm.

**Ablation Studies:**

1. **Modality Contribution Analysis:**
 - Conditioning the diffusion policy with full cross-modal foresight (CLaD: 94.7%) yielded the highest success rate.
 - Conditioning with only semantic foresight (CLaD$_s$: 91.5%) improved performance over a policy without foresight (Policy only: 84.8%), indicating the value of semantic future states.
 - Conversely, conditioning with only proprioceptive foresight (CLaD$_p$: 50.4%) substantially degraded performance, falling significantly below the no-foresight baseline. This finding suggests that kinematic predictions are less effective without proper semantic grounding and can introduce misleading signals. The incremental gain from CLaD$_s$ to CLaD further supports the benefit of jointly modeling kinematic and visual transitions.

2. **Effect of $\mathcal{L}_{recon}$ in Grounding Latent Foresight:**
 - Removing the auxiliary reconstruction loss ($\mathcal{L}_{recon}$) resulted in an 8.6% performance degradation (86.1% average success rate compared to 94.7% with $\mathcal{L}_{recon}$).
 - UMAP visualizations of the latent dynamics $z_{dyn}$ showed that with $\mathcal{L}_{recon}$, embeddings formed distinct, task-specific clusters. Without $\mathcal{L}_{recon}$, these clusters became diffuse and overlapping, indicating a loss of semantic structure and grounding in the latent space. This demonstrates $\mathcal{L}_{recon}$'s critical role in preventing representation drift and anchoring latent foresights to observable quantities.

3. **Asymmetric Cross-Attention Design:**
 - Comparing different cross-attention configurations:
 - Symmetric self-attention performed the worst (86.7% success rate).
 - Semantics querying proprioceptive transitions achieved 93.8% success.
 - Proprioceptive transitions querying semantic ones (CLaD's design) achieved the highest success rate of 94.7%.
 - This result validates the inductive bias that directing kinematic context to attend over semantic transitions is a beneficial design for extracting task-relevant cross-modal dynamics, as it provides a more grounded basis for interpreting visual changes.

**Supplementary Findings:**

- **Generalizability & Scalability:** While excelling on LIBERO-LONG, CLaD showed sub-optimal performance on short-horizon generalization tasks (LIBERO-Spatial, Object, Goal) compared to large VLAs pre-trained on massive datasets. This suggests CLaD's strength lies in knowledge-independent planning through cross-modal dynamics modeling rather than extensive pre-trained world knowledge.
- **Visualization of Learned Foresight:** Integrated Gradients revealed that CLaD's predicted latent foresights attribute high importance to task-relevant objects in the visual input. For example, attention concentrated near target objects during grasping and shifted between the held object and the target location during placement, indicating spatially grounded predictions.
- **Effect of Action-Free Data:** Experiments with action-free data or heavy action masking during training resulted in reduced performance compared to the baseline, confirming that action conditioning is necessary for learning robust, deterministic, and grounded foresight in CLaD.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLaD framework demonstrates several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation:

1. **Parameter-Efficient High-Performance Planning:** CLaD achieves performance competitive with, and in some cases exceeding, large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on long-horizon manipulation tasks, but with significantly fewer parameters (0.66B vs. 3.3B or 7B). This indicates that explicit modeling of cross-modal dynamics and grounded latent foresight can lead to highly effective planning without requiring the enormous model capacities and extensive pre-training of existing large VLAs. This efficiency has direct implications for reducing computational resource requirements for training and deployment, making advanced robot learning more accessible and scalable for real-world applications.

2. **Robustness through Grounded Cross-Modal Dynamics:** The core insight of learning correlations between *transitions* rather than static states, coupled with the asymmetric cross-attention mechanism, provides a robust way to ensure consistency between robot kinematics and visual scene changes. This explicit alignment of proprioceptive and semantic state evolution helps prevent the decoupling of latent representations, which can plague other latent space planning methods. The grounding of latent foresights via EMA targets and auxiliary reconstruction losses further strengthens the reliability of predictions, anchoring them to observable reality and preventing abstract representations from drifting or collapsing. This contributes to more consistent and reliable robot behavior, particularly in complex, multi-step tasks.

3. **Novel Inductive Bias for Robot Perception:** The design choice of using proprioceptive transitions to query semantic ones in the asymmetric cross-attention provides a beneficial inductive bias. It allows the model to interpret scene-level visual changes through the robot's kinematic context, mirroring how a robot's interaction with the environment is perceived. This approach offers a structured way for a robot to "understand" how its actions causally affect the visual world, leading to more effective and context-aware planning.

4. **Advancement in Latent Space Planning:** CLaD pushes the boundaries of latent space planning by demonstrating that computational efficiency can be combined with explicit cross-modal reasoning. It offers an alternative to computationally expensive semantic generation methods while overcoming the limitations of traditional latent models that implicitly conflate modalities. The framework's ability to predict implicit latent subgoals, rather than explicit semantic artifacts, offers a path toward more fluid and integrated planning-control systems.

5. **Foundation for Future Research and Generalization:** While CLaD shows particular strength in knowledge-independent planning based on dynamics modeling, its core principle of learning cross-modal dynamics has broader applicability. The authors suggest that this approach could generalize to other embodied tasks involving multiple sensory modalities, such as mobile manipulation integrating vision, force, and tactile feedback. Future work could also explore integrating CLaD's dynamics learning with large-scale heterogeneous robot datasets to amortize training costs and further enhance representation quality across diverse task distributions. Addressing current limitations, such as incorporating object-centric or spatially-structured foresights, could further improve performance on tasks requiring high perceptual precision.
