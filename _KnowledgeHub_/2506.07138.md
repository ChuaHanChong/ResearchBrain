# Learning Compact Vision Tokens for Efficient Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Central South University developed a two-stage token fusion strategy, Spatial Token Fusion (STF) and Multi-Block Token Fusion (MBTF), to enhance the efficiency of large multimodal models. This method significantly reduces vision token sequence length by 75% and improves average performance by 0.8% over the LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline across eight vision-language benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) suffer from high computational costs, particularly due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention mechanisms when processing long sequences of vision tokens.
- Vision encoders processing high-resolution images generate hundreds to thousands of redundant vision tokens, limiting LMM deployment and scalability.
- Existing vision token reduction methods often introduce complex mechanisms, risk compromising fine-grained visual details, or rely on text tokens in the pruning process.

## Method

- A ==Multi-Block Token Fusion (MBTF)== module extracts and fuses multi-granularity features from various intermediate blocks of the vision encoder to enrich visual context.
- A ==Spatial Token Fusion (STF)== module applies a convolutional operation with a stride to effectively merge spatially adjacent vision tokens, reducing their count by 75%.
- The method integrates MBTF and STF as a ==learnable visual projector== between the frozen vision encoder and the Large Language Model (LLM), requiring no retraining of the vision encoder.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an average performance of **66.3%** across **8** vision-language benchmarks, outperforming the original LLaVA-1.5-7B by **0.8%** while using **7**5%** fewer** vision tokens.
- Ablation studies showed MBTF alone improved LLaVA-1.5 performance by **1.1%**, demonstrating the benefit of multi-granularity features, and STF alone maintained baseline performance with reduced tokens.
- Qualitative case studies illustrated improved detail recognition, counting accuracy, and detailed image descriptions compared to the LLaVA-1.5 baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs exhibit substantial inherent spatial redundancy in their vision tokens, which can be exploited for significant compression without major performance degradation.
> - Integrating multi-granularity features from different layers of a vision encoder significantly enriches the visual representation, leading to improved performance in multimodal tasks.
> - A learnable and adaptive spatial token fusion mechanism outperforms simpler, static strategies like average pooling or concatenation for reducing vision token sequence length.
