# Learning Compact Vision Tokens for Efficient Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Central South University developed a two-stage token fusion strategy, Spatial Token Fusion (STF) and Multi-Block Token Fusion (MBTF), to enhance the efficiency of large multimodal models. This method significantly reduces vision token sequence length by 75% and improves average performance by 0.8% over the LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline across eight vision-language benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) suffer from high computational costs, particularly due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention mechanisms when processing long sequences of vision tokens.
- Vision encoders processing high-resolution images generate hundreds to thousands of redundant vision tokens, limiting LMM deployment and scalability.
- Existing vision token reduction methods often introduce complex mechanisms, risk compromising fine-grained visual details, or rely on text tokens in the pruning process.

## Method

- A ==Multi-Block Token Fusion (MBTF)== module extracts and fuses multi-granularity features from various intermediate blocks of the vision encoder to enrich visual context.
- A ==Spatial Token Fusion (STF)== module applies a convolutional operation with a stride to effectively merge spatially adjacent vision tokens, reducing their count by 75%.
- The method integrates MBTF and STF as a ==learnable visual projector== between the frozen vision encoder and the Large Language Model (LLM), requiring no retraining of the vision encoder.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an average performance of **66.3%** across **8** vision-language benchmarks, outperforming the original LLaVA-1.5-7B by **0.8%** while using **7**5%** fewer** vision tokens.
- Ablation studies showed MBTF alone improved LLaVA-1.5 performance by **1.1%**, demonstrating the benefit of multi-granularity features, and STF alone maintained baseline performance with reduced tokens.
- Qualitative case studies illustrated improved detail recognition, counting accuracy, and detailed image descriptions compared to the LLaVA-1.5 baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs exhibit substantial inherent spatial redundancy in their vision tokens, which can be exploited for significant compression without major performance degradation.
> - Integrating multi-granularity features from different layers of a vision encoder significantly enriches the visual representation, leading to improved performance in multimodal tasks.
> - A learnable and adaptive spatial token fusion mechanism outperforms simpler, static strategies like average pooling or concatenation for reducing vision token sequence length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and highly active area of research within artificial intelligence: improving the efficiency and scalability of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). LMMs, by integrating powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) with vision encoders, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in multimodal reasoning, visual question answering, and image captioning. However, their computational demands are substantial, limiting their deployment and further scaling.

The broader research landscape for LMMs faces two primary computational bottlenecks:
1. **High cost of LLMs:** These models often contain billions of parameters, leading to immense computational overhead during inference.
2. **Quadratic complexity of vision token sequences:** Vision encoders, especially when processing high-resolution images, generate hundreds or even thousands of vision tokens. Since LLMs process these tokens (along with text tokens) in a transformer architecture, the self-attention mechanism incurs a computational cost that scales quadratically with the sequence length.

Prior efforts to accelerate LLMs generally fall into two categories: parameter sparsification (e.g., weight pruning, attention head reduction) and sequence compression (e.g., hierarchical transformers, adaptive token pooling). Similarly, for LMMs, acceleration strategies have included lightweight vision encoders, sparsely activated Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, and parameter-efficient language models.

This paper specifically focuses on the second bottleneck: **reducing the number of vision tokens**. It identifies a key insight that has been implicitly recognized but not fully exploited: the significant *spatial redundancy* present in vision tokens. Many existing methods for vision token reduction, such as LLaVA-PruMerge, FastV, LLaVolta, TokenPacker, TinyChart-3B, YOPO, and LLaVA-Mini, often involve complex mechanisms like adaptive attention, progressive compression, or reliance on text tokens for pruning. While effective, some of these methods risk compromising model performance due to potential loss of fine-grained visual details, or introduce additional complexity in the token pruning process.

This work distinguishes itself by proposing a novel, two-stage token fusion strategy that directly targets spatial redundancy *before* vision tokens enter the LLM, without involving text tokens in the fusion process. By focusing on learning *compact vision tokens* and incorporating *multi-granularity features* from the vision encoder, the authors aim to achieve substantial token reduction while minimizing information loss and preserving multimodal reasoning capabilities. This approach is positioned as a more "lossless" way to shorten the visual sequence compared to simple pruning, thereby contributing to the development of more efficient and robust LMMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the substantial computational burden associated with Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), particularly during inference. As LMMs grow in complexity and capability, their practical application is increasingly constrained by the high computational cost of the Large Language Model (LLM) backbone and the quadratic scaling of processing long sequences of vision tokens. The authors highlight that while the vision encoder itself is typically smaller than the LLM, the sheer number of vision tokens (hundreds to thousands) often far exceeds text tokens, making their reduction a critical path to efficiency.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Exploit Spatial Redundancy:** The paper explicitly sets out to explore and leverage the inherent spatial redundancy among vision tokens. This is motivated by an empirical observation: simply reducing vision tokens by 75% using average pooling (LLaVA-1.5-7B (AvgPool)) results in surprisingly minor performance drops on several benchmarks, and even outperforms the original LLaVA-1.5-7B on some, suggesting that current LMMs process redundant visual information.
2. **Develop a Spatial Token Fusion (STF) Method:** To systematically address this redundancy, a core objective is to design a learnable STF module that can effectively fuse spatially adjacent vision tokens into more compact representations. This aims to significantly shorten the vision token sequence fed into the LLM, thereby accelerating inference.
3. **Preserve and Enhance Information Content:** A crucial challenge in token reduction is avoiding information loss. The authors aim to go beyond simple averaging by proposing a learnable fusion mechanism that can adaptively integrate features. Furthermore, to compensate for the fixed nature of typical LMM vision encoders (which are often frozen during training), another objective is to introduce a Multi-Block Token Fusion (MBTF) module. This module is designed to supplement the reduced token sequence with multi-granularity features extracted from different layers of the vision encoder, ensuring that fine-grained visual details and diverse semantic information are retained or even enhanced for downstream vision-language tasks.
4. **Balance Efficiency and Performance:** The overarching goal is to achieve a significant reduction in vision tokens (e.g., 75%) and thus boost inference efficiency, without sacrificing the multimodal reasoning capabilities of the LMM. Ideally, the proposed method should maintain comparable or even superior performance compared to baselines using a full sequence of vision tokens.
5. **Maintain Compatibility with Existing LMMs:** The method is designed as a plug-and-play component between the vision encoder and the LLM, requiring no retraining of the vision encoder. This objective aims to facilitate easy integration and widespread adoption within existing LLaVA-style architectures.

By achieving these objectives, the authors seek to offer a practical and effective solution for developing more efficient and scalable LMMs, making them more amenable to real-world applications and resource-constrained environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces a novel dual-stage token fusion strategy, implemented through two modules: Multi-Block Token Fusion (MBTF) and Spatial Token Fusion (STF). These modules are inserted between the frozen vision encoder and the large language model (LLM) in a LLaVA-style architecture, effectively acting as a sophisticated visual projector.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The method adheres to the LLaVA architecture, comprising a vision encoder (e.g., CLIP-ViT-L/14) and an LLM (e.g., Vicuna-1.5-7B). Instead of a simple linear or MLP projector, the proposed framework replaces it with the MBTF and STF modules. The vision encoder remains frozen, while the MBTF, STF, and LLM parameters are updated during training.

#### 3.2. Multi-Block Token Fusion (MBTF)

The MBTF module is designed to capture **multi-granularity representations** from the vision encoder, addressing the limitation that a fixed vision encoder might not provide optimally adapted features for all downstream vision-language tasks.

- **Feature Extraction:** Vision tokens are extracted from `M` evenly sampled intermediate blocks of the vision encoder. For instance, in a 24-block ViT-L/14, blocks {3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24} are selected. This strategy ensures that features from different abstraction levels (low-level to high-level semantics) are considered.
- **Concatenation and Fusion:** The selected vision tokens, initially represented as sequences, are reshaped into feature maps. These feature maps are then concatenated along the channel dimension. This concatenation pools information from different layers.
- **Learnable Projection:** Two sequential 1x1 convolution modules, each followed by a GeLU activation function, are applied. These convolutions progressively reduce the channel dimension (e.g., from 4096 to 1024 for LLaVA-1.5-7B), learning to fuse the concatenated multi-block features into a more compact, multi-granularity representation. This process aims to enrich the visual context with diverse information, improving the compactness and representational power of the tokens before spatial reduction.

#### 3.3. Spatial Token Fusion (STF)

The STF module is responsible for **reducing spatial redundancy** by fusing adjacent vision tokens.

- **Convolutional Fusion:** The output of the MBTF module (multi-granularity vision tokens) is fed into the STF module. A convolution operation with a kernel size of `k x k` and a stride size of `k x k` is applied. This operation effectively processes a `k x k` neighborhood of tokens, fusing `k^2` spatially adjacent tokens into a single compact token. For example, with `k=2`, four adjacent tokens are merged into one, reducing the sequence length by a factor of four (75% reduction).
- **Information Preservation:** The design considers the dimensionality of tokens. If `C1` is the channel size of MBTF output and `C3` is the LLM's text embedding dimension, the convolution can be set to output `C2 = k^2 * C1`. This ensures that, initially, the fused token concatenates all information from the `k^2` input tokens, minimizing direct information loss.
- **Flexible Token Count (E):** The method introduces flexibility by allowing the `k^2` fused tokens to be reshaped into `E` tokens, where `E < k^2`. This allows for fine-tuning the trade-off between reduction ratio and information density. In the typical setup for 75% reduction, `k=2` and `E=1` are used, meaning 4 tokens become 1.
- **LLM Embedding Alignment:** Finally, to align the spatially fused tokens with the LLM's text embedding space, two additional 1x1 convolution modules with GeLU activation are applied. The output is the compact vision token sequence, ready to be fed into the LLM alongside text tokens.

#### 3.4. Optimization

The optimization process follows the established two-stage training scheme of LLaVA models:

- **Feature Alignment Pretraining:** In this stage, the vision encoder is kept frozen. Only the parameters of the newly introduced MBTF and STF modules (`θ`) are updated. The LLM parameters (`ϕ`) are also frozen. This stage focuses on aligning the fused vision tokens with the LLM's embedding space. The model is pretrained on a filtered subset of CC-595K for 1 epoch.
- **End-to-End Finetuning:** In the second stage, the full parameters of the LLM (`ϕ`), along with MBTF and STF modules, are fine-tuned. The vision encoder remains frozen. This stage leverages the LLaVA-Instruct-158K dataset for 1 epoch, allowing the entire system to adapt for multimodal reasoning tasks.
- **Training Details:** Standard practices are employed, including Adam optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule with warmup, DeepSpeed for memory efficiency, gradient checkpointing, bfloat16, and TensorFloat32. The loss function aims to maximize the probability of generating the correct response, mirroring the generative objective of LLMs.

This dual-stage fusion approach allows the model to first enrich visual features with multi-granularity information and then efficiently compress them spatially, ensuring that critical visual details are preserved even with a drastically reduced token count.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the proposed Spatial Token Fusion (STF) and Multi-Block Token Fusion (MBTF) method demonstrates its effectiveness in accelerating Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) while maintaining or improving performance.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Efficiency

- **Superior Efficiency and Performance:** The core finding (Table 2) is that the proposed method (STC, i.e., MBTF + STF) achieves the best average performance (66.3%) across 8 popular vision-language benchmarks (GQA, SQA, TextVQA, POPE, MMBench, MMBench-CN, VQAv2, VisWiz) among efficient LLaVA models with comparable computational costs (approx. 1.9 TFLOPs).
- **Outperforms Baseline with Fewer Tokens:** Remarkably, STC with only 25% of the vision tokens (achieved by `k=2, E=1`) not only matches but *surpasses* the performance of the original LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline, which uses 100% of the vision tokens. It outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (lora) by 0.3% and LLaVA-1.5 (full tuning) by 0.8% on average.
- **Specific Gains:** The performance gains are particularly noticeable on SQA, MMBench, VQAv2, and VisWiz, indicating improved reasoning and perception capabilities.

#### 4.2. Evidence of Spatial Redundancy

- **AvgPool Baseline:** The paper provides compelling evidence for the inherent spatial redundancy in LMMs. Simply applying average pooling to reduce vision tokens by 75% (LLaVA-1.5 (AvgPool)) still achieves a respectable average score of 64.7%. This performance, surprisingly, is better than several other sophisticated efficient LLaVA models (e.g., PruMerge+, LLaVA-Mini), strongly supporting the hypothesis that a significant portion of vision tokens are redundant.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies clearly validate the contribution of each proposed module and hyperparameter choices.

- **Effect of Fusion Modules (Table 3):**
 - **MBTF Alone:** LLaVA-1.5 with only the MBTF module (but still using 100% vision tokens, just enriched) significantly outperforms the baseline LLaVA-1.5 (full tuning) by 1.1% (66.6% vs 65.5%). This confirms that integrating multi-granularity features from different vision encoder layers is highly beneficial for downstream tasks.
 - **STF Alone:** LLaVA-1.5 with only the STF module (reducing to 25% tokens) achieves performance comparable to the original LLaVA-1.5 baseline (65.6% vs 65.5%). This directly validates the effectiveness of spatial token fusion in reducing redundancy without significant performance degradation.
 - **MBTF + STF (Ours):** Combining both MBTF and STF yields an average score of 66.3%. While slightly lower than MBTF alone (which uses 100% tokens), it represents a significant 0.7% improvement over STF alone, all while operating at 25% token count and 1.9 TFLOPs. This demonstrates a successful balance between token reduction and information preservation.

- **Effect of Fusion Parameters (k, E) (Table 4):**
 - The optimal configuration for STF is found to be a kernel size `k=2` with `E=1` fused token, achieving 66.3% average score with 25% vision tokens.
 - Increasing the kernel size (e.g., `k=4`, `k=8`) or the number of fused tokens `E` (e.g., `E=2` for `k=2`, or `E=4, 8, 16, 32` for `k=4, 8`) generally leads to performance drops, even with higher computational costs (e.g., 3.8 TFLOPs for `E=2` or `E=8`). This suggests that while more information could theoretically be retained, the current training data might be insufficient for the increased number of learnable parameters in larger fusion kernels, leading to overfitting.

- **Effect of Fusion Strategies (Table 5):**
 - The learnable STF method (66.3%) significantly outperforms simpler fusion strategies. TokenConcat (concatenating adjacent tokens) achieves 65.4%, and AvgPool (averaging adjacent tokens) achieves 64.7%. This highlights the advantage of an adaptively learned fusion mechanism over static, predefined strategies.

#### 4.4. Case Studies

- **Improved Detail Recognition:** Case studies (Figure 3) illustrate the qualitative improvements. The proposed method correctly recognizes "arrival" on a blurred cake text where LLaVA-1.5 misinterprets it as "tri-vial" (Figure 3a). It also accurately identifies "yogurt" between plates instead of a generic "bowl of food" (Figure 3b), and precisely extracts numerical data from a chart (Figure 3c, 3d), while LLaVA-1.5 struggles.
- **Better Counting and Description:** The method also shows superior performance in counting objects (e.g., cows in Figure 3e) and providing more accurate, detailed image descriptions.

In summary, the results robustly demonstrate that the proposed MBTF and STF modules effectively reduce computational overhead by compressing vision tokens without compromising, and in many cases improving, the multimodal reasoning capabilities of LLaVA-style models. The work provides strong empirical evidence for significant spatial redundancy in LMMs and offers an efficient, learnable solution to exploit it.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Learning Compact Vision Tokens for Efficient Large Multimodal Models" holds significant implications for the future development and deployment of LMMs. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Accelerated LMM Inference:** The most direct and immediate impact is the substantial acceleration of LMM inference. By effectively reducing the vision token sequence length by 75% while maintaining or even improving performance, the proposed method significantly lowers the computational cost associated with using LMMs. This makes LMMs more viable for real-time applications, edge devices, and environments with limited computational resources, broadening their practical applicability beyond high-end data centers.

2. **Enhanced Information Preservation:** Unlike simpler token reduction methods that might discard information, the dual-stage fusion strategy (MBTF for multi-granularity features and STF for adaptive spatial fusion) demonstrates an ability to preserve and even enhance critical visual information. This is crucial for tasks requiring fine-grained visual details, complex reasoning, and accurate visual perception, as evidenced by the improved performance on benchmarks like SQA and TextVQA, and the qualitative case studies. This approach advances the understanding of how to compress visual input without detrimental effects on model understanding.

3. **Increased Accessibility and Adoption:** By making LMMs more efficient, the work contributes to democratizing access to these powerful models. Researchers, developers, and organizations with fewer resources can now explore and deploy sophisticated multimodal AI solutions that were previously out of reach due to computational constraints. This could foster innovation across various domains, including robotics, accessibility tools, and intelligent assistants.

4. **Foundation for Future Efficient LMM Designs:** The findings robustly confirm the widespread spatial redundancy in current LMMs. This insight, coupled with the effective proposed solution, provides a strong foundation for future research into even more sophisticated and dynamic token compression techniques. Researchers can build upon this work to explore adaptive fusion mechanisms, more advanced multi-granularity feature integration, or combined textual and visual token compression strategies.

5. **Practical Plug-and-Play Solution:** The method's design, which keeps the vision encoder frozen and inserts the fusion modules between the encoder and LLM, makes it a highly practical and easily implementable solution. It minimizes the need for extensive retraining of large foundational models, reducing development costs and time. The release of source code and trained weights further encourages immediate adoption and experimentation within the research community.

6. **Addressing Key Bottlenecks in Vision-Language Integration:** The paper directly tackles one of the fundamental architectural bottlenecks in current LMMs: the inefficient processing of visual inputs by a language-centric transformer. By offering a refined approach to visual feature projection, it helps bridge the modality gap more efficiently, leading to more cohesive and high-performing multimodal models.

Despite the positive impacts, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as inferior performance on certain benchmarks. This suggests avenues for future work, focusing on minimizing potential information loss during fusion and exploring more advanced strategies for token compactness. Overall, this research represents a significant step forward in making large multimodal models more efficient, accessible, and practical for a broader range of applications.
