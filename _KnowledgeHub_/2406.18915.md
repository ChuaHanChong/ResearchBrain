# Manipulate-Anything: Automating Real-World Robots using Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MANIPULATE-ANYTHING is a Vision-Language Model-driven framework that automates real-world robot manipulation by generating high-quality demonstration data and enabling zero-shot task execution. It achieved up to 22% higher average zero-shot success rates in simulation and yielded behavior cloning policies that performed comparably to human-trained policies, with only a 0.27% average performance difference.

## Problem

- Robotics faces a significant "dearth of data" for training, with traditional human teleoperation being costly and unscalable.
- Prior VLM-based approaches for robotic manipulation often rely on privileged state information or hand-designed skills, limiting real-world applicability and object generalization.
- Developing robust and generalizable robotic policies for diverse, unstructured environments remains a challenge.

## Method

- A modular framework leveraging VLMs and LLMs for autonomous task plan generation, multi-viewpoint action generation, and sub-task verification.
- Integrates multi-viewpoint processing to enhance VLM reasoning, allowing selection of optimal perspectives for object interaction and task verification.
- Implements a VLM-based sub-task verification module with an error recovery mechanism to detect failures and trigger re-planning for robustness.

## Results

- Achieved an average task-averaged margin of up to **22%** higher zero-shot success rates over strong baselines in simulation, covering all 14 RLBench tasks without privileged state information.
- Behavior cloning policies trained on MANIPULATE-ANYTHING data performed comparably to those trained on human-scripted demonstrations (**0.27%** average difference) and significantly outperformed policies from other automated data generation methods.
- Demonstrated an average zero-shot success rate of **38.57%** across 7 real-world tasks, outperforming a baseline by **38%**, and showing improved robustness for trained policies in physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automated generation of robotic demonstration data using VLMs can produce trajectories comparable to or even superior to human-collected data for behavior cloning.
> - Vision-Language Models can enable robust zero-shot manipulation directly on physical robots without privileged state information or a library of hand-designed skills.
> - Incorporating error recovery through sub-task verification and multi-viewpoint reasoning is crucial for enhancing robustness and success rates in real-world robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly with learning-based approaches, is characterized by a significant reliance on high-quality, quantity, and diversity of training data. Unlike advancements in vision and language processing, which have benefited from large internet-scale datasets, robotics has faced a "dearth of data." Traditional methods for collecting robot demonstration data, such as human teleoperation, are often expensive, time-consuming, and do not scale adequately to achieve the necessary quantity or diversity for robust, generalizable policies.

Recent efforts have explored automated data collection, often leveraging the capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) to guide robotic behavior. These VLM-guided systems have shown promise in task decomposition, language planning, and code generation for executing predefined skills. However, prior VLM-based approaches frequently exhibit limitations when transitioning to real-world environments. Specific constraints identified in existing work include:
- **Dependence on privileged state information:** Many methods designed for simulation environments rely on information (e.g., precise object poses, physics engine states) that is not readily available or easily obtainable in real-world settings.
- **Requirement for hand-designed skills:** Some systems necessitate a predefined set of robotic skills, limiting their adaptability to novel tasks or objects.
- **Limited object instances:** The ability to manipulate only a fixed set of object instances with known geometric shapes restricts generalization to diverse, unstructured environments.
- **Underperformance in real-world scenarios:** Despite successes in simulation, these methods often encounter challenges with robustness and accuracy when deployed on physical robots.

This work, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING, addresses these limitations by proposing a scalable, automated method for real-world robotic manipulation data generation and zero-shot task completion. It positions itself as a system that operates in real-world environments without privileged state information, does not require hand-designed skills, and can manipulate a diverse range of static objects. By doing so, it contributes to overcoming the data scarcity bottleneck in robotics and enhancing the practical applicability of VLM-guided robotic systems. The paper aims to improve both the zero-shot task success rate and the quality of generated data for subsequent behavior cloning policy training, potentially leading to more robust and generalizable robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of MANIPULATE-ANYTHING is to develop a scalable and automated method for generating high-quality, diverse demonstration data for real-world robotic manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific sub-objectives:

1. **Address the Data Scarcity in Robotics:** The fundamental motivation stems from the observation that the lack of sufficiently large, high-quality, and diverse datasets hinders progress in robotics, similar to how data availability propelled advancements in vision and language. Current human demonstration collection methods are expensive and non-scalable, while existing automated methods often lack diversity or real-world applicability. MANIPULATE-ANYTHING aims to fill this gap by providing an autonomous data generation solution.

2. **Enable Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation in Real-World Environments:** A key objective is to allow robots to perform novel, unseen manipulation tasks in real-world settings without prior demonstrations or specific programming for those tasks. This involves leveraging the common-sense knowledge and reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to interpret high-level language instructions and translate them into a sequence of executable robot actions.

3. **Overcome Limitations of Prior VLM-Based Robotic Systems:**
 - **Eliminate reliance on privileged state information:** Many previous VLM-driven systems are effective only in simulation because they rely on information (e.g., exact object coordinates, ground truth segmentation) that is not directly observable in the real world. This work aims to operate purely from visual observations.
 - **Remove the need for hand-designed skills:** The system seeks to generate actions and skills on the fly, rather than depending on a library of pre-programmed, hand-crafted robotic primitives, thus increasing flexibility and generality.
 - **Support manipulation of any static object:** Previous methods were often restricted to a fixed set of objects or those with known geometric models. MANIPULATE-ANYTHING aims to generalize to arbitrary static objects present in an unstructured environment.

4. **Generate Robust Training Data for Behavior Cloning Policies:** Beyond zero-shot execution, a central objective is to produce high-quality demonstration trajectories that can effectively train behavior cloning (BC) policies. The hypothesis is that autonomously generated, diverse, and robust data, especially with error recovery behaviors integrated, can lead to BC policies that are more robust and performant than those trained on human-collected data or data from other automated methods.

5. **Incorporate Error Recovery and Multi-Viewpoint Reasoning:** To enhance robustness and success rates, the framework aims to include a verification mechanism that can detect failures in sub-task execution and trigger re-planning. Additionally, it seeks to improve VLM reasoning by integrating multi-viewpoint scene understanding, addressing limitations of single-viewpoint processing in complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MANIPULATE-ANYTHING is structured as a modular framework designed for autonomous robot demonstration generation and zero-shot task completion in real-world environments. Its methodology integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) with object detection, pose estimation, and error recovery mechanisms.

The core components and their interactions are as follows:

1. **Task Plan Generation:**
 - **Input:** A natural language task instruction (e.g., "open the top drawer") and an image of the current scene.
 - **Process:** A VLM first identifies task-relevant objects within the scene. Subsequently, an LLM, provided with this object information, decomposes the main task into a sequence of discrete sub-tasks ($T_i$). Each sub-task is associated with a specific verification condition ($v_i$) that defines its successful completion. For instance, "open the top drawer" might decompose into "grasp the drawer handle" ($v_1$: "did the robot grasp the handle?") and "pull open the drawer" ($v_2$: "is the drawer opened?").
 - **Output:** A sequence of sub-task-condition pairs: $\{(T_1, v_1), (T_2, v_2), \dots, (T_n, v_n)\}$.

2. **Multi-Viewpoint VLM Selection:**
 - **Motivation:** Acknowledging that single viewpoints can suffer from occlusions and ambiguity, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING enhances VLM reasoning by incorporating multi-viewpoint information.
 - **Process:** Multiple viewpoints are collected from either physical cameras or re-rendered from a single RGB-D input (e.g., point clouds). These images are concatenated into a single frame, with numbers indicating specific viewpoints. The VLM is then queried to select the "ideal" viewpoint for the current sub-task, minimizing occlusion and maximizing relevance for either action generation or sub-task verification.

3. **Action Generation Module:**
 - **Input:** The current sub-task ($T_i$) and the selected multi-view images.
 - **Output:** A sequence of low-level actions, typically a 6 DoF end-effector pose. Actions are categorized into two types:
 - **Agent-centric actions:** These modify the robot's own state (e.g., "rotate 90 degrees"). The LLM planner classifies the sub-task as agent-centric. The VLM, leveraging in-context learning techniques and the multi-view observations, generates code snippets to synthesize the desired motion. This grounds the code generation in the current visual state of the scene.
 - **Object-centric actions:** These involve interacting with objects, often by generating a task-specific grasp pose or a pre-action pose for non-prehensile tasks.
 - First, an object-agnostic grasp prediction model (e.g.,) generates an initial set of all possible 6-DOF grasping poses in the scene.
 - Next, the VLM identifies the bounding box of the target object parts relevant to the task (e.g., the handle of a knife for a "grasp a knife" task) from available viewpoints.
 - The multi-viewpoint selection process is re-applied to identify the most optimal, occlusion-free viewpoint for this object interaction.
 - Finally, using the VLM's bounding box detection from the optimal viewpoint, the system filters the initial grasp pose candidates and selects the highest confidence pose specific to the task.
 - **Execution:** A simple motion planner then moves the robot to the desired end-effector pose.

4. **Sub-task Verification Module:**
 - **Input:** The current scene state after an action sequence, and the verification condition ($v_i$) for the completed sub-task.
 - **Process:** A VLM-based verifier, again utilizing multi-viewpoint selection to avoid occlusion, assesses whether the end state satisfies the condition $v_i$.
 - **Error Recovery:** If the verifier determines that the sub-task failed, the system re-attempts the action generation step for the current sub-task from the current state. This iterative error recovery mechanism allows the robot to adapt to unexpected situations and injects recovery behaviors into the collected demonstrations. If successful, the system proceeds to the next sub-task.

5. **Demonstration Filtering:** Once all sub-tasks for a given instruction are successfully completed, the entire trajectory, including language instructions, RGB-D frames, waypoints (6 DoF gripper poses), and states, is filtered and stored as a successful demonstration for downstream policy training.

The modular design allows for continuous improvement as underlying VLMs evolve. The use of prompt engineering and few-shot demonstrations for VLMs, along with manually curated primitive action code snippets, further guides the system's reasoning and generation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MANIPULATE-ANYTHING focused on two primary aspects: its zero-shot task completion capability and the quality of the data it generates for training behavior cloning policies.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Performance in Simulation

- **Task Coverage:** MANIPULATE-ANYTHING successfully generated trajectories for all 14 diverse simulation tasks from RLBench, whereas baselines (Scaling-up, VoxPoser, Code-as-Policies (CAP)) covered only 10, 9, and 7 tasks, respectively.
- **Performance Comparison:** MANIPULATE-ANYTHING outperformed the baselines in 10 out of the 14 tasks.
 - It achieved a success rate of 96.00% for "Put\_block" compared to VoxPoser's 70.70%, CAP's 84.00%, and Scaling-up's 77.33%.
 - For tasks like "Play\_jenga," "Open\_jar," and "Close\_box," where VoxPoser and CAP showed 0.00% success, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING achieved 77.33%, 80.00%, and 33.33%, respectively.
 - The method showed an average task-averaged margin of up to 22% over VoxPoser, which was identified as the strongest baseline in tasks where it did not fail completely.
- **Limitations of Baselines:** The baselines were provided with privileged state information (ground truth simulation state, object models, segmented point clouds), a significant advantage not afforded to MANIPULATE-ANYTHING. Despite this, they struggled with tasks requiring more than 4-DoF manipulation (VoxPoser) or failed to generate any successful demonstrations for several tasks. MANIPULATE-ANYTHING's ability to operate without such information and still perform well was a notable finding.

#### 4.2. Behavior Cloning with Demonstrations from MANIPULATE-ANYTHING

- **Data Quantity:** 10 successful demonstrations per task were generated by MANIPULATE-ANYTHING and other automated methods for training. Missing data from baselines (due to task failures) was patched with RLBench-generated demonstrations.
- **Model Performance (PerAct):**
 - Policies trained on MANIPULATE-ANYTHING data performed comparably to policies trained on human hand-scripted demonstrations (p = 0.973), with a mere 0.27% average performance difference across all tasks.
 - MANIPULATE-ANYTHING data led to significantly better performance than data generated by other autonomous methods (p ≤ 0.01 for VoxPoser, Scaling-up, and CAP). For example, on "Put\_block," MA-trained PerAct achieved 85.33% vs. VoxPoser's 2.67%.
- **Model Performance (RVT-2):**
 - With the higher-performing RVT-2 model, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING data continued to show strong performance, often on par with or even slightly outperforming RLBench human-generated data on certain tasks (e.g., "Open\_box" at 24.00% for MA vs. 4.00% for RLBench; "Pick\_&\_lift" at 82.67% vs. 64.00%).
 - Overall, MA-trained RVT-2 policies demonstrated high success rates (e.g., 97.33% for "Pickup\_cup," 94.67% for "Take\_umbrella").
- **Action Distribution Analysis:** Comparison of action distributions showed a high similarity between MANIPULATE-ANYTHING generated data and human-generated data, yielding the lowest Chamfer Distance (CD=0.056) among the compared methods. This suggests that the generated trajectories possess characteristics similar to expert human demonstrations, potentially contributing to the robustness of trained policies.
- **Policy Robustness:** Policies trained on MANIPULATE-ANYTHING data exhibited a lower standard deviation (3.39) across tasks compared to its own zero-shot performance (8.48), indicating improved robustness through training.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Task Success:** MANIPULATE-ANYTHING successfully generated demonstrations for all 7 tested real-world tasks (e.g., "open\_jar," "sort\_objects," "open\_drawer"). Even for the lowest-performing task, success rates exceeded 25%.
- **Zero-Shot Performance:** In zero-shot execution, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING achieved an average success rate of 38.57% across 7 tasks, significantly outperforming CAP (38% higher average success rate).
- **Behavior Cloning in Real-World:** Policies trained on MANIPULATE-ANYTHING's real-world generated data demonstrated improved robustness compared to its zero-shot performance (outperforming zero-shot in 4 out of 5 comparable tasks). The performance of PerAct trained on MA data was comparable to PerAct trained on human expert data on several tasks (e.g., 50.00% for "Open\_drawer" with MA data vs. 53.33% with human data).

#### 4.4. Ablations

- **Scaling Effect:** The experiments showed a positive correlation between the quantity of MANIPULATE-ANYTHING-generated training data and the performance of the trained PerAct policy. The performance improvement rate (slope of 0.503 for a linear fit) was higher than that of RLBench-generated data (0.197), suggesting that MA data is particularly effective for scaling up robotic transformers.
- **Generalization:** MANIPULATE-ANYTHING data proved more generalizable and robust to changes in language instructions and target objects than VoxPoser, indicating its broader applicability.

#### 4.5. Error Breakdown

Analysis of errors in simulation (play\_jenga task) identified two main categories:
- **Perception error:** Mistakes by the VLM in detecting target objects or selecting optimal viewpoints for grasp pose generation.
- **Reasoning error:** Failures in the Sub-task Verification Module where the VLM incorrectly assessed sub-task completion. Systematically replacing VLMs with human decision-making in each component allowed for quantification of these error sources.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MANIPULATE-ANYTHING presents a significant contribution to robotic manipulation by addressing the long-standing challenge of data scarcity and the practical limitations of deploying learning-based systems in unstructured real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Scalable Data Generation:** The primary impact is the provision of a scalable, automated method for generating high-quality and diverse robot demonstration data. This directly tackles the "dearth of data" problem that has hindered progress in robotics compared to other AI fields. By automating data collection, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING can significantly reduce the cost and effort traditionally associated with creating large-scale robot datasets, enabling the development of more robust and generalizable robotic policies.

2. **Enhanced Behavior Cloning Performance:** The finding that policies trained on MANIPULATE-ANYTHING generated data can outperform or perform on par with those trained on human demonstrations is a key outcome. This suggests that autonomously generated data, especially with integrated error recovery behaviors, can offer a qualitatively superior or at least competitive alternative to expensive human supervision for training behavior cloning models. This has the potential to accelerate the adoption of learning-based control policies in various robotic applications.

3. **Real-World Zero-Shot Manipulation:** MANIPULATE-ANYTHING demonstrates robust zero-shot task completion capabilities directly on physical robots, without requiring privileged state information, hand-designed skills, or a predefined set of objects. This expands the scope of tasks that robots can perform in novel, unstructured environments, moving beyond constrained laboratory settings. The ability to manipulate any static object significantly broadens the practical utility of robotic systems.

4. **Robustness through Error Recovery and Multi-Viewpoint Reasoning:** The explicit inclusion of a VLM-based sub-task verification module with re-planning capabilities provides a mechanism for error recovery. This makes the system more resilient to unexpected failures during task execution and allows the generated demonstrations to inherently capture recovery behaviors, further enhancing the robustness of learned policies. The multi-viewpoint VLM selection also improves perception and reasoning, leading to more reliable task execution in complex scenes.

5. **Foundation for Future Robotic Systems:** By leveraging the growing capabilities of Vision-Language Models for high-level planning, scene understanding, and action generation, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING establishes a framework that can continuously improve as foundational models evolve. Its modularity means that advancements in underlying VLMs can directly translate into enhanced robotic capabilities.

6. **Addressing Key Limitations of Prior Work:** The work directly tackles several critical shortcomings of previous VLM-guided robotic systems, such as their reliance on simulation-specific privileged information, the need for predefined skill sets, and limited object generalization. By overcoming these, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING pushes the boundary towards more autonomous, adaptable, and general-purpose robotic manipulation.

In conclusion, MANIPULATE-ANYTHING represents a step towards making real-world robot deployment more feasible and scalable. Its impact lies in enabling both the efficient creation of large, high-quality robotic datasets and the direct execution of novel tasks in complex, unstructured environments, thereby contributing to the broader goal of widely deployed, capable robotic agents.
