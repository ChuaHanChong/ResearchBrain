# LLM-Planner: Few-Shot Grounded Planning for Embodied Agents with Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LLM-Planner enables embodied agents to perform complex tasks with minimal training data by leveraging large language models for few-shot, grounded planning. It dynamically updates high-level plans based on real-time environmental observations, achieving competitive performance on the ALFRED benchmark while significantly reducing data requirements compared to traditional methods.

## Problem

- Developing versatile embodied agents is hindered by the high data cost and poor sample efficiency of existing learning paradigms, requiring extensive paired language-trajectory datasets.
- Prior LLM-based embodied planning often relied on static plans, lacked dynamic adaptation to environmental changes, or assumed complete knowledge of admissible skills.
- There was a challenge in effectively grounding the linguistic outputs of LLMs in the physical world of embodied agents, leading to ungrounded or unfeasible plans.

## Method

- A hierarchical planning model utilizes an LLM (GPT-3) as its high-level planner to generate sequences of subgoals from natural language instructions in a few-shot, in-context learning manner.
- A kNN retriever selects relevant in-context examples for the LLM's prompt, and logit biases constrain the LLM's output to permitted actions and currently visible objects.
- A dynamic grounded re-planning algorithm enables the LLM to update its plan mid-execution based on real-time environmental observations and execution failures, creating a closed-loop feedback system.

## Results

- LLM-Planner achieved competitive success rates on the ALFRED benchmark, using less than **0.5%** of the full training data, and drastically outperformed other state-of-the-art models retrained in a few-shot setting.
- Dynamic grounded re-planning improved the success rate by **1.83%** for unseen tasks, confirming its role in adapting to unperceivable objects or execution failures.
- The approach demonstrated higher efficiency, requiring an average of 7 LLM calls per task compared to 22 for an adapted SayCan baseline, and ablation studies confirmed the criticality of kNN retrieval and logit biases for high-level planning accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can effectively serve as few-shot high-level planners for embodied agents, substantially reducing the need for large training datasets.
> - Dynamically grounding LLM-generated plans with real-time environmental feedback is crucial for robust adaptation in complex, partially-observable settings.
> - Strategic components like kNN retrieval for in-context examples and logit biases based on perceived objects are vital for guiding LLMs to generate accurate and executable plans.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the crucial intersection of **Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI)**, **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN)**, and the rapidly advancing field of **Large Language Models (LLMs)**. The overarching goal in this domain is to create versatile agents, such as robots, that can understand and execute complex natural language instructions in dynamic, real-world environments.

Traditionally, building such agents has faced significant hurdles, primarily due to the **high data cost and poor sample efficiency**. Existing language-driven agents typically require extensive datasets of paired language instructions and corresponding gold trajectories (demonstrations) to learn each new task. This data scarcity severely limits the scalability and versatility of embodied AI, making it difficult for agents to adapt quickly to new tasks or environments.

The emergence of **Large Language Models (LLMs)** like GPT-3 has introduced a paradigm shift, particularly through their remarkable **few-shot learning capabilities**. Recent seminal work (e.g., SayCan, Language Models as Zero-Shot Planners) has begun to demonstrate the potential of LLMs as high-level planners for embodied agents, enabling them to learn new tasks with far fewer examples than before. This work builds upon this promising trend, seeking to push the boundaries of LLM utility in embodied planning.

However, the authors identify significant limitations in existing LLM-based embodied planning approaches:
- **Limited Evaluation Settings:** Many pioneering works are evaluated in constrained environments with a small number of object types (e.g., SayCan with 15 object types). This limits their real-world applicability.
- **Assumption of Admissible Skills:** Some methods (e.g., SayCan) assume that the agent can enumerate all admissible skills (action-object pairs) upfront. This assumption breaks down in partially-observable or highly complex environments where knowing all possible actions is infeasible or computationally expensive due to the combinatorial growth of skill lists.
- **Static Planning:** Most prior work uses LLMs to generate a single, static plan at the beginning of a task, which is then executed. This approach lacks the ability to dynamically adapt to unexpected environmental changes or execution failures, leading to ungrounded or unattainable plans.
- **Lack of Physical Grounding:** LLMs, by nature, operate on linguistic data. Integrating them with physical environments requires robust mechanisms to ground their linguistic knowledge in visual perception and real-world constraints.

LLM-Planner directly addresses these critical limitations. It differentiates itself by:
- **Direct Plan Generation:** Unlike SayCan, which ranks pre-defined skills, LLM-Planner directly generates high-level plans (sequences of subgoals), bypassing the need for an exhaustive list of admissible actions.
- **Dynamic Grounded Re-planning:** This is a core innovation, allowing the LLM to dynamically adjust its plan based on real-time environmental observations, thus incorporating physical grounding feedback into the planning loop.
- **Evaluation on ALFRED:** The choice of ALFRED, a large-scale dataset with diverse, partially-observable environments and long-horizon tasks, underscores the paper's commitment to tackling real-world complexities, moving beyond limited "proof-of-concept" settings.

By doing so, LLM-Planner positions itself as a significant advancement in creating truly versatile, sample-efficient, and robust embodied agents that can effectively bridge the gap between high-level language instructions and low-level physical interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind LLM-Planner stems from the current bottlenecks in developing versatile embodied agents: the **high cost of data annotation and the poor sample efficiency** of existing learning paradigms. Building robots that can follow diverse natural language commands and rapidly learn new tasks necessitates a departure from data-hungry methods.

Based on this, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **To leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) for few-shot planning in embodied agents:** The core objective is to harness the inherent knowledge and few-shot learning capabilities of LLMs to generate high-level plans for complex tasks, thereby significantly reducing the need for large, annotated datasets.
2. **To empower LLMs with physical grounding for dynamic and adaptive planning:** Recognizing that LLM-generated plans can be abstract, a critical objective is to integrate environmental perception into the planning process. This involves enabling the LLM to dynamically generate and update plans that are "grounded" in the agent's current surroundings, allowing it to adapt to unforeseen circumstances or unperceivable objects.
3. **To develop a hierarchical planning framework that is robust across diverse and partially-observable environments:** The aim is to create a planner that can operate effectively in complex settings, unlike prior work that often relied on simplified environments or a priori knowledge of all possible actions/objects.
4. **To demonstrate competitive performance with significantly less training data:** A crucial objective is to achieve performance comparable to, or even surpassing, state-of-the-art baselines that are trained on vast amounts of data, using only a tiny fraction of it. This directly addresses the sample efficiency problem.

In essence, the paper seeks to create a **"closed-loop" system** where LLMs dynamically adapt their high-level plans based on feedback from the environment, moving beyond static, ungrounded planning to enable more intelligent and resilient embodied behavior with minimal human supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LLM-Planner adopts a **hierarchical planning model**, a well-established paradigm in embodied AI, but crucially integrates **Large Language Models (LLMs)** as its high-level planner within a **few-shot, in-context learning** framework.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Planning Structure

- **High-level Planner (LLM-Planner):** This is the core contribution. It receives a natural language instruction (e.g., "Cook a potato and put it into the recycle bin") and generates a High-Level Plan (HLP). An HLP is a sequence of subgoals, each defined as an (high-level action, object) pair (e.g., `[Navigation potato, Pickup potato, Navigation microwave...]`). The high-level action space is derived from ALFRED and includes navigation and interaction actions like `PickupObject`, `PutObject`, `OpenObject`, `CloseObject`, `ToggleOnObject`, `ToggleOffObject`, `SliceObject`.
- **Low-level Planner:** This component takes a single subgoal from the HLP and translates it into a sequence of primitive actions executable by the embodied agent in the environment. Crucially, the authors note that once the HLP is defined, low-level planning becomes conditionally independent of the natural language instruction. For experiments, LLM-Planner integrates with the perception module and low-level planner from a strong existing baseline, **HLSM**, which are pre-trained using synthetic data and do not require paired language-trajectory data.

#### 3.2. Few-Shot In-Context Learning with LLMs

The method leverages the in-context learning capabilities of LLMs (specifically GPT-3, TEXT-DAVINCI-003) without requiring any parameter updates.
- **Small Training Set:** The authors use a very small set of 100 paired (instruction, HLP) examples (less than 0.5% of the ALFRED training data), sampled strategically across all 7 task types.
- **Prompt Design:** A carefully engineered prompt is essential for guiding the LLM. The optimal prompt structure, determined through systematic experimentation (LOOCV), includes:
 - An intuitive task explanation and a list of allowed high-level actions.
 - A few "in-context examples" (demonstrations of instruction-HLP pairs).
 - The current "Task description" (natural language instruction).
 - For re-planning: "Completed plan" (subgoals achieved so far) and "Visible objects" (from the environment).
 - The query "Next plan:" to elicit the LLM's output.
- **In-context Example Retrieval:** To maximize the utility of the few-shot examples, a **k-nearest-neighbor (kNN) retriever** is used. It employs a frozen BERT-base model to compute the similarity (Euclidean distance of BERT embeddings) between the current test instruction and the 100 training examples, retrieving the K most similar examples to include in the LLM's prompt.
- **Logit Biases:** To further constrain the LLM's output and align it with the environment, logit biases are applied. These biases favor output tokens corresponding to allowed high-level actions and, critically, objects perceived in the current environment, guiding the LLM towards physically relevant plans.

#### 3.3. Grounded Re-planning Algorithm

This is a distinguishing feature that provides physical grounding and dynamic adaptability.
- **Trigger Conditions:** Re-planning is dynamically triggered during task execution if:
 1. The agent fails to execute a primitive action (e.g., bumping into a wall, failed interaction).
 2. The agent takes too many steps or attempts without completing the current subgoal (indicating it might be stuck or the plan is unfeasible).
- **Environmental Feedback:** When re-planning is triggered, the **list of objects currently perceived by the agent's object detector** (from HLSM's perception module) is injected into the LLM's prompt. This simple yet effective mechanism provides the LLM with crucial real-time environmental context.
- **Dynamic Plan Update:** The LLM then generates a new continuation of the HLP, considering the observed objects and the already completed subgoals. This allows the LLM to adapt its plan (e.g., if a target object isn't found, it might suggest looking in a common storage location like a fridge, if a fridge is observed). This creates a "closed-loop" feedback system between the environment and the LLM.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Setup

- **Dataset:** ALFRED, a challenging benchmark with diverse tasks, objects, and partially-observable household environments.
- **Metrics:** Standard ALFRED metrics are used: Success Rate (SR) and Goal-condition Success Rate (GC). Additionally, High-level Planning Accuracy (HLP ACC) is introduced to directly evaluate the quality of the LLM's plan generation.
- **True Few-Shot Setting:** Strict adherence to this principle means prompt design and hyperparameter tuning (like K for kNN) are performed via cross-validation *on the small training set itself*, avoiding bias from large separate validation sets.
- **Baselines:** Comparison is made against strong full-data baselines (HLSM, FILM, HiTUT, E.T., M-TRACK) and re-trained/adapted few-shot versions of HLSM, FILM, and SayCan. Notably, SayCan is given an "unfair competitive advantage" by providing it with oracle knowledge of all objects and affordances in the environment to make it runnable on ALFRED, highlighting the difficulty of adapting such models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments on the ALFRED dataset demonstrate LLM-Planner's significant capabilities and advantages:

- **Competitive Few-Shot Performance:** LLM-Planner, utilizing less than 0.5% (100 examples) of ALFRED's paired training data, achieves success rates (SR) and goal-condition success rates (GC) that are competitive with, and in some cases surpass, recent baselines (e.g., HiTUT, E.T., M-TRACK) that are trained using the *full* ALFRED training set. For instance, in the "step-by-step instructions" setting, LLM-Planner + HLSM achieves 16.42% SR (test unseen) compared to HLSM's full-data 20.27% (goal instruction only) or FILM's 27.80% (step-by-step). While not fully matching the best full-data models, the data efficiency is orders of magnitude better.

- **Drastic Outperformance of Few-Shot Baselines:** When existing state-of-the-art hierarchical models like HLSM and FILM are re-trained under the same few-shot setting (100 examples), they show extremely poor performance (e.g., HLSM achieves 0.61% SR and FILM 0.20% SR), barely completing any tasks. This strongly validates LLM-Planner's superior data efficiency.

- **SayCan Comparison:** Even when given an "unfair competitive advantage" of oracle knowledge about the environment's objects and affordances, the adapted SayCan model significantly underperforms LLM-Planner in the few-shot setting (e.g., SayCan's 9.88% SR vs. LLM-Planner's 15.36% SR on valid unseen with step-by-step instructions). This highlights the generative nature of LLM-Planner as more suitable for complex, partially-observable environments than ranking-based approaches.

- **Efficiency:** LLM-Planner is considerably more efficient in terms of LLM calls. It calls GPT-3 an average of 7 times per task, whereas SayCan, even with oracle knowledge, requires 22 calls, due to its ranking mechanism evaluating multiple skills at each step.

- **Effectiveness of Grounded Re-planning:** The dynamic grounded re-planning mechanism provides a notable performance boost. For the "goal instruction only" setting on the unseen test split, grounded re-planning improves the success rate by 1.83% over static planning (13.41% vs. 11.58% SR). This confirms that integrating environmental feedback is crucial for robust performance.

- **Ablation Studies:** Both the kNN retriever and logit biases are shown to be critical components. Removing the kNN retriever leads to a dramatic drop in HLP accuracy (from 40.59% to 17.48%), and removing logit biases also results in a significant decrease (from 40.59% to 38.10%), underscoring their importance in guiding the LLM effectively.

- **Fine-grained Analyses:**
 - **Effect of Number of Examples:** HLP accuracy generally improves with more training examples, though diminishing returns are observed around 250 examples.
 - **Robustness Across Task Types:** LLM-Planner's high-level planning accuracy does not vary drastically with task complexity (e.g., "Stack & Place," often considered very difficult, yields similar HLP accuracy to "Place two"), suggesting good generalization.
 - **Case Studies:** Concrete examples illustrate LLM-Planner's ability to perform object localization (e.g., finding a potato in a fridge based on observing a fridge) and disambiguation (e.g., mapping "lamp" to "desklamp" based on observed objects), thanks to its grounded re-planning capabilities and LLM's commonsense knowledge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LLM-Planner work represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI, with profound implications and potential impact:

- **Dramatically Reduced Data Requirements:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is the ability to achieve competitive performance with less than 0.5% of the training data typically required by traditional methods. This **dramatically reduces the need for costly and time-consuming human annotations** (pairs of instructions and gold trajectories), which is a major bottleneck in scaling embodied agents to real-world complexity and diversity.

- **Enabling Versatile and Sample-Efficient Agents:** By leveraging the few-shot learning capabilities of LLMs and incorporating physical grounding, LLM-Planner paves the way for truly versatile agents that can quickly learn and adapt to a wide array of new tasks and environments with minimal new data. This is crucial for practical robotic deployment where training from scratch for every new scenario is infeasible.

- **Bridging Language and Physical Grounding:** The novel grounded re-planning algorithm effectively bridges the semantic understanding of LLMs with real-world physical constraints and observations. This "closed-loop" interaction allows agents to overcome the limitations of static planning and adapt to the dynamic, partially-observable nature of real environments, leading to more robust and resilient task execution.

- **Advancing the Role of LLMs in Embodied AI:** This work reinforces the growing paradigm of using LLMs as central planning components, moving beyond auxiliary roles. By demonstrating direct plan generation and dynamic adaptation, it pushes the boundaries of how LLMs can be integrated into complex decision-making systems for robotics.

- **Setting a New Baseline for Few-Shot Embodied Learning:** The competitive results achieved in a rigorous few-shot setting on a challenging benchmark like ALFRED establish a new benchmark and inspire further research into highly data-efficient learning for embodied agents.

**Potential Future Directions:**
The paper itself highlights several promising avenues for future research:
- **Exploring Other LLMs:** Investigating the performance with other powerful LLMs like Codex, which may have different strengths, particularly in code or procedural generation, could yield further improvements.
- **Advanced Prompt Design:** Continuing to refine prompt engineering techniques, possibly through automated methods or more sophisticated instruction-tuning strategies, could further unlock LLM capabilities.
- **More Sophisticated Grounding and Re-planning:** Developing more advanced mechanisms for incorporating environmental feedback beyond simple object lists (e.g., spatial relationships, affordances, deeper semantic scene understanding) and more intelligent re-planning heuristics could enhance robustness.
- **End-to-End Performance Improvement:** While the high-level planner is strong, improving the low-level planners and perception modules (e.g., more robust object detectors) is essential to translate high HLP accuracy into higher end-to-end task success rates.

In conclusion, LLM-Planner offers a compelling solution to the data bottleneck in embodied AI, demonstrating the transformative potential of LLMs when coupled with effective physical grounding. Its implications extend to making AI-powered robots more adaptable, autonomous, and readily deployable in real-world scenarios.
