# LLM-Planner: Few-Shot Grounded Planning for Embodied Agents with Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LLM-Planner enables embodied agents to perform complex tasks with minimal training data by leveraging large language models for few-shot, grounded planning. It dynamically updates high-level plans based on real-time environmental observations, achieving competitive performance on the ALFRED benchmark while significantly reducing data requirements compared to traditional methods.

## Problem

- Developing versatile embodied agents is hindered by the high data cost and poor sample efficiency of existing learning paradigms, requiring extensive paired language-trajectory datasets.
- Prior LLM-based embodied planning often relied on static plans, lacked dynamic adaptation to environmental changes, or assumed complete knowledge of admissible skills.
- There was a challenge in effectively grounding the linguistic outputs of LLMs in the physical world of embodied agents, leading to ungrounded or unfeasible plans.

## Method

- A hierarchical planning model utilizes an LLM (GPT-3) as its high-level planner to generate sequences of subgoals from natural language instructions in a few-shot, in-context learning manner.
- A kNN retriever selects relevant in-context examples for the LLM's prompt, and logit biases constrain the LLM's output to permitted actions and currently visible objects.
- A dynamic grounded re-planning algorithm enables the LLM to update its plan mid-execution based on real-time environmental observations and execution failures, creating a closed-loop feedback system.

## Results

- LLM-Planner achieved competitive success rates on the ALFRED benchmark, using less than **0.5%** of the full training data, and drastically outperformed other state-of-the-art models retrained in a few-shot setting.
- Dynamic grounded re-planning improved the success rate by **1.83%** for unseen tasks, confirming its role in adapting to unperceivable objects or execution failures.
- The approach demonstrated higher efficiency, requiring an average of 7 LLM calls per task compared to 22 for an adapted SayCan baseline, and ablation studies confirmed the criticality of kNN retrieval and logit biases for high-level planning accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can effectively serve as few-shot high-level planners for embodied agents, substantially reducing the need for large training datasets.
> - Dynamically grounding LLM-generated plans with real-time environmental feedback is crucial for robust adaptation in complex, partially-observable settings.
> - Strategic components like kNN retrieval for in-context examples and logit biases based on perceived objects are vital for guiding LLMs to generate accurate and executable plans.
