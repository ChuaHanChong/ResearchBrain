# Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning for Contact-Rich Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework integrates a Transformer-based Diffusion Model with energy-consistent impedance control to enable robust stiffness and damping adaptation for contact-rich manipulation. It achieved 100% success rates across multiple complex peg-in-hole insertion tasks, including previously unseen geometries and tight clearances, by learning to reconstruct contact-consistent equilibrium poses.

## Problem

- Effectively bridging the 'information domain' (desired behaviors) and 'energy domain' (physical interactions) for stable and safe robotic contact.
- The manual, time-consuming, and task-specific nature of tuning stiffness and damping parameters for impedance control in contact-rich scenarios.
- Purely learning-based approaches often lack explicit impedance control and struggle with stability and fine-tuned physical interaction in contact-rich or safety-critical tasks.

## Method

- A ==Transformer==-based ==Diffusion Model== reconstructs a 'simulated Zero-Force Trajectory' (sZFT), representing a contact-consistent equilibrium pose, by iteratively denoising observed poses conditioned on external wrenches.
- An energy-based method calculates stiffness reduction terms by equating stored elastic energy (based on deviation from sZFT) with work done by external forces, dynamically adapting stiffness and damping.
- Directional impedance adaptation selectively modulates stiffness along each axis based on its 'directional relevance' to the sZFT, allowing compliance in non-critical directions while maintaining rigidity in task-relevant ones.

## Results

- Successfully traversed a parkour-style obstacle course without jamming, while fixed-impedance controllers failed due to energy accumulation and safety triggers.
- Achieved **100%** success rates in inserting cylindrical, square, and star-shaped pegs with tight clearances into corresponding holes, generalizing to unseen geometries without task-specific training.
- Outperformed both uniform fixed impedance controllers and a manually tuned non-uniform impedance controller, which yielded **0%** and **70%** success respectively on the complex star-shaped peg insertion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating impedance adaptation as an 'equilibrium reconstruction problem' enables generative models to bridge the gap between abstract task goals and concrete physical interaction dynamics.
> - Directional stiffness adaptation, guided by an inferred contact-consistent equilibrium, is critical for successful contact-rich tasks, preventing jamming and facilitating traversal where uniform stiffness reduction fails.
> - Transformer-based Diffusion Models can effectively learn complex physical interaction patterns and infer appropriate compliance parameters from relatively small datasets of human teleoperated demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic control fundamentally involves two distinct domains: the "information domain," where desired behaviors like trajectories are represented and processed, and the "energy domain," where physical interactions with the environment are governed by physical laws such as energy conservation. A significant challenge in robotics, particularly for contact-rich and unstructured environments, is effectively bridging these two domains to achieve stable, safe, and adaptable physical interaction.

Impedance control is a widely adopted framework for regulating the dynamic relationship between a robot's motion and interaction forces, providing stability and safety during contact. However, its effectiveness relies on the precise tuning of stiffness and damping parameters, a process that is often manual, time-consuming, and task-specific. Existing physics-based simulations are generally insufficient for fully offline parameter tuning, necessitating on-line adjustments.

Current approaches to contact-rich manipulation can be broadly categorized into three strands:

- **Model-based approaches:** These methods explicitly incorporate contact dynamics, such as friction cones and complementarity constraints. While producing physically consistent behavior, they suffer from high computational cost, sensitivity to parameters, and limited robustness in unstructured settings, often requiring task-specific tuning that does not generalize well.
- **Statistical-impedance heuristics:** Common in learning-from-demonstration, these approaches model demonstrated motions probabilistically (e.g., Gaussian Mixture Models) and modulate impedance parameters heuristically based on statistical variability. They have shown efficacy in structured tasks but are limited when environmental constraints change, as such changes may not be reflected in demonstration variability.
- **Learning-based approaches:** Methods such as reinforcement learning and generative models (e.g., diffusion policies, flow-matching policies, Transformer-based policies) primarily operate in the information domain, excelling at motion generation. However, they typically lack an explicit representation of impedance, often relying on PD controllers with fixed gains. This results in compliance emerging indirectly, offering limited control over physical interaction in safety-critical or contact-rich scenarios. Recent efforts like the Adaptive Compliance Policy (ACP) integrate generative models with compliance adaptation but often remain within admittance-control frameworks, which can be sensitive to transitions into and out of contact.

This paper positions itself to address the limitations of existing approaches by formulating impedance adaptation as an "equilibrium reconstruction problem" rather than solely a trajectory learning problem. It aims to integrate generative modeling, specifically Transformer-based Diffusion Models, with energy-consistent impedance control. This integration seeks to provide a data-driven method for adapting impedance online, enabling robots to perform contact-rich tasks robustly in unstructured environments while respecting physical consistency and stability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a framework, termed Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning, that unifies data-driven generative modeling with energy-consistent impedance control for robust contact-rich manipulation. The core motivation stems from several critical challenges in current robotic systems:

- **Bridging Information and Energy Domains:** Robots operating in environments involving physical interaction must effectively translate desired motions (information domain) into physical actions that respect environmental constraints and energy exchange (energy domain). Ignoring these physical laws can lead to instability and safety issues. Impedance control provides a mechanism to bridge these domains, but its effectiveness is highly dependent on parameter tuning.
- **Challenges of Impedance Parameter Tuning:** The manual, iterative, and task-specific nature of tuning stiffness and damping parameters for impedance control is a major bottleneck. Incorrect parameters can lead to task failure (e.g., jamming) or inefficient execution. Current simulation tools are not sufficiently accurate to enable reliable offline tuning, necessitating adaptive methods.
- **Limitations of Purely Learning-Based Methods:** While learning-based approaches, particularly recent generative models, have demonstrated success in motion generation, they often lack an explicit representation of impedance. They tend to converge towards admittance-like behaviors, prioritizing force or position deviation penalties over explicit regulation of interaction dynamics. This limits their applicability in scenarios requiring deliberate control over physical interaction and compliance.
- **Need for Contact-Consistent Equilibrium Estimation:** The paper highlights that previous learning methods for compliant control, such as ACP, are often formulated within admittance control, which can be unstable during contact transitions. To overcome this, the authors propose an "equilibrium reconstruction problem." Instead of learning a trajectory, the Diffusion Model learns to reconstruct a *simulated Zero-Force Trajectory (sZFT)*, which represents a contact-consistent equilibrium. This sZFT serves as a reference for online impedance modulation.
- **Generalization to Unseen Tasks and Environments:** The authors aim for a system that can adapt to unknown contact dynamics and generalize across diverse tasks and embodiments without extensive retraining or manual parameter adjustments. This is motivated by the need for robots to operate effectively in unstructured environments where explicit geometric or dynamic models may be unavailable or unreliable.

In summary, the motivation is to create an adaptive, learning-based impedance control system that leverages the generative capabilities of Diffusion Models to infer contact-consistent equilibrium behaviors, thereby automating the impedance tuning process and enabling robust, stable, and safe physical interaction in complex robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning framework integrates a Transformer-based Diffusion Model with an energy-based impedance controller. This combination enables the robot to adapt its stiffness and damping online based on measured external wrenches and contact-perturbed motion.

#### 3.1. A. Diffusion Model for Reconstruction of Simulated Zero-Force Trajectories (sZFT)

The core idea is to use a Diffusion Model to reconstruct a simulated Zero-Force Trajectory (sZFT), which represents a contact-consistent equilibrium pose, from noisy observations and external wrenches.

- **Input:** The Diffusion Model takes as input the observed robot end-effector pose (position `p` and unit-quaternion `Q`) and the measured external wrench `F_ext`.
- **Concept:** The displacement between the observed pose (`p`, `Q`) and the ground-truth equilibrium pose (`p_0`, `Q_0`) is treated as "noise." The Diffusion Model's role is to iteratively denoise this input, conditioned on `F_ext`, to reconstruct the equilibrium pose.
- **sZFT vs. ZFT:** The framework distinguishes between the *nominal Zero-Force Trajectory (ZFT)*, which is a pre-programmed or learned virtual trajectory (e.g., generated by waypoints, imitation learning, or teleoperation for free-space motion), and the *simulated Zero-Force Trajectory (sZFT)*. The sZFT (`ˆp_0`, `ˆQ_0`) is the output of the Diffusion Model, representing an *inferred equilibrium* that is consistent with the current physical interaction.
- **Translational Noise:** For translations, noise is defined as `p_noise = p - p_0`. During training, a fraction of this noise, plus Gaussian noise, is added to `p_0`. At inference, the predicted noise `ˆp_noise` is subtracted from `p` to reconstruct `ˆp_0`.
- **Rotational Noise (SLERP-based):** For rotations, standard Gaussian noise would violate the unit-norm constraint of quaternions. The paper introduces a Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP)-based noise scheduler. This method perturbs quaternions directly on the unit sphere, preserving geometric consistency. The rotational noise is represented by the relative quaternion `Q_noise = Q Q_0^-1`. During inference, the equilibrium quaternion `ˆQ_0` is reconstructed as `Q ˆQ_noise^-1`.
- **Conditional Diffusion:** The denoising process is conditioned on `F_ext` using a Transformer-based architecture. Pose tokens (translations and unit-quaternions) are embedded and enriched with positional and timestep embeddings. Wrench signals are projected to context tokens and integrated via multi-head cross-attention. This allows the model to learn the relationship between contact forces and the deviation from the ideal equilibrium.
- **Transformer Architecture:** The use of a Transformer-based network is motivated by its ability to capture temporal dependencies and context-dependent effects, crucial for time-series data like robot trajectories and interaction events. The MLP head predicts a 7D noise vector (3D translation, 4D quaternion).

#### 3.2. B. Data Collection

Training data for the Diffusion Model were collected via teleoperation using an Apple Vision Pro (AVP), which provided ground-truth sZFTs (operator's wrist pose) alongside measured interaction wrenches and robot end-effector poses.

- **Teleoperation Setup:** The AVP tracked the operator's wrist pose in 6 DoF, serving as the commanded ZFT during teleoperation and later as the ground-truth sZFT for training. This markerless tracking allowed spatially unrestricted demonstrations, capturing natural human interaction behavior.
- **Datasets:**
 - **Parkour-style scenario:** 17,457 samples covering free-space motion, contact transitions, and sustained contact with various obstacles.
 - **Robot-assisted upper-limb rehabilitation tasks:** 55,838 samples from eight healthy adults performing passive lifting, out-of-shoulder movements, and an activity-of-daily-living task under passive, cooperative, and mixed interaction conditions. This dataset was designed to capture diverse human-robot interaction behaviors.

#### 3.3. C. Model Selection

The model architecture was selected based on two criteria: sufficient accuracy in reconstructing translational and rotational components, and low computational capacity suitable for real-time deployment.

- **Metrics:** Positional error (mm), angular magnitude error (θ_error in degrees), and unit-axis deviation (α_error in degrees). Target errors were 2 mm and 2.8 degrees respectively.
- **Configuration:** A Transformer with 512 hidden dimensions, 4 attention heads, and 6 layers showed the best balance, achieving 0.994 mm positional error, 0.249° θ error, and 0.003° α error on the parkour dataset. Weighting the θ component loss further improved rotational accuracy.
- **Generalization and Data Efficiency:** Retraining on a combined parkour and therapy dataset (72,000 samples / 1.6 hours of demonstration) *improved* accuracy, demonstrating robustness across different tools and inertial properties without explicit compensation. The model exhibited strong performance with a relatively small dataset compared to typical image-based diffusion models.

#### 3.4. D. Energy-based Stiffness Estimation

The reconstructed sZFT is central to adapting impedance by relating the change in elastic energy to the work done by external wrenches.

- **Baseline Stiffness:** The robot operates with user-specified high baseline translational (`K_t,i,max = 800 N/m`) and rotational (`K_r,i,max = 150 Nm/rad`) stiffness values. The Diffusion Model then reduces these stiffnesses as needed.
- **Stiffness Reduction Calculation:** For each axis `i` (x, y, z), stiffness reduction terms (`k*t,i`, `k*r,i`) are computed by equating the elastic energy stored in a virtual spring (derived from the displacement `p - ˆp_0` or `Q Q_0^-1`) with the work done by the external force/moment (`f_ext`, `m_ext`) along this displacement.
 - `k*t = (2 * <f_ext, ˆe_t>) / ||ˆe_t||^2`
 - `k*r = (2 * <m_ext, ˆe_r>) / ||ˆe_r||^2`
 - Error terms (`ˆe_t`, `ˆe_r`) are augmented with damping components proportional to velocity to prevent oscillations.
- **Algorithm 1:** The process computes stiffness reduction over a sliding window (16 samples at 200 Hz / 80 ms), ignoring interactions below specific force/moment thresholds (1 N, 1 Nm) to suppress low-interaction phases.

#### 3.5. E. Directional Adaptation

To prevent uniform stiffness reduction from halting task execution, stiffness is modulated based on the "directional relevance" of each axis with respect to the *reconstructed sZFT*.

- **Alignment Factors:** For translations, `ψ_t,i = |ˆe_t,i| / ||ˆe_t||` quantifies the contribution of each axis `i` to the intended displacement in the sZFT. Similar factors (`ψ_r,i`) are defined for rotations.
- **Relative Factors:** `ρ_t,i = 1 - ψ_t,i` and `ρ_r,i = 1 - ψ_r,i` ensure that stiffness is reduced more strongly in directions that contribute little to the desired sZFT motion, while maintaining rigidity along task-relevant axes.
- **Final Stiffness:** `k_t,i = K_t,i,max - ρ_t,i k*t,i` and `k_r,i = K_r,i,max - ρ_r,i k*r,i`. This mechanism allows for smooth traversal and successful insertion by selectively adapting compliance.

The overall architecture involves feeding external wrenches and end-effector poses into the conditional Transformer-based Diffusion Model. The model reconstructs the sZFT, which is then used by the energy-based estimator and directional adaptation mechanism to compute and apply modulated stiffness and damping parameters to the robot's impedance controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning framework was evaluated on a KUKA LBR iiwa robot in two contact-rich scenarios: a parkour-style obstacle traversal and a multi-geometry peg-in-hole insertion task. The controller utilized KUKA's Fast Robot Interface (FRI) for real-time torque control.

#### 4.1. A. Parkour Task

- **Setup:** The robot navigated three obstacles while maintaining continuous contact with a table, following a nominal ZFT that passed through the obstacles. Stop conditions (velocity > 0.24 m/s, force > 20 N) were implemented for safety.
- **Fixed Impedance Performance:** A controller with constant high stiffness failed at the first obstacle, jamming and triggering the stop conditions due to energy accumulation.
- **Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning Performance:** The proposed method successfully traversed all obstacles smoothly, without triggering stop conditions. Stiffness was adapted dynamically in response to external wrenches. Analysis of Figure 10 indicated that stiffness reduction was not solely proportional to force magnitude but was determined by alignment with the sZFT predicted by the Diffusion Model. For instance, `K_p,y` was reduced more significantly than `K_p,z` despite similar force magnitudes, because the `y`-axis motion was less relevant to the sZFT. Similar directional adaptation was observed for rotational stiffness.

#### 4.2. B. Peg-in-Hole Insertion

- **Setup:** The task involved inserting cylindrical, square, and star-shaped pegs into corresponding holes. Clearances were 0.14 mm (square) and 0.20 mm (circular, star). The nominal ZFT was generated from three kinesthetically taught poses (start, initial contact, final insertion) and did not include geometric alignment with the complex peg shapes.
- **Fixed Impedance Performance:**
 - **Cylindrical Peg:** 30/30 (100%) successful insertions.
 - **Square Peg:** 4/30 (13%) successful insertions.
 - **Star Peg:** 0/30 (0%) successful insertions.
 This highlights the difficulty of complex peg insertion with fixed impedance, often requiring extensive manual tuning.
- **Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning Performance:** The proposed method achieved 30/30 (100%) successful insertions for *all three peg types*. This was achieved despite the Diffusion Model being trained only on parkour and upper-limb rehabilitation data, with no prior exposure to peg-in-hole tasks. The controller also received no explicit information about peg or hole geometry, relying solely on measured interaction wrenches.
- **Stiffness Adaptation (Star Peg, Figure 11):** The controller exhibited precise directional stiffness modulation. For instance, rotational stiffness `K_r,z` was significantly reduced (even to 0 Nm/rad) during the insertion phase, as rotation about the z-axis (along the peg's axis) was not critical for alignment. Conversely, stiffness along other axes crucial for alignment or resisting off-axis contact remained high or adapted dynamically based on the sZFT's demands. This demonstrated the model's ability to infer and maintain task-relevant stiffness while reducing it in non-critical directions.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Study

An ablation study was performed to assess the contributions of the reconstructed sZFT and directional impedance adaptation.

- **Uniform Stiffness Adaptation (with sZFT, without directional adaptation):** Failed in both parkour (blocked) and all peg-in-hole tasks (0% success). This indicates that simply reducing stiffness uniformly based on sZFT is insufficient.
- **Directional Adaptation (without sZFT, using nominal ZFT):** Failed in parkour (exceeded safety limits) and failed for square and star pegs (0% success), succeeding only for the cylindrical peg (100%). This confirms that directional factors must be computed relative to the *contact-consistent sZFT* (from the Diffusion Model), not just the nominal ZFT.
- **Comparison with Fixed Impedance Variants:**
 - Uniform high, medium, and low impedance settings yielded progressively worse results for complex pegs (e.g., uniform low failed for all, uniform high 0% for star).
 - A *manually tuned non-uniform impedance* (identified by an expert after 15 combinations) achieved 100% for cylindrical, 87% for square, and 70% for star. While better than uniform fixed impedance, it still fell short of the Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning's 100% success rate across all pegs and required extensive manual effort.

These results collectively confirm that both the Diffusion Model's ability to reconstruct a contact-consistent sZFT and the energy-based directional adaptation mechanism are crucial for robust performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning, a framework that represents a step toward unifying state-of-the-art generative AI algorithms with model-based control principles. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Bridging Information and Energy Domains:** The primary contribution is providing a robust method to bridge the traditionally separated information and energy domains in robotics. By using a Diffusion Model to reconstruct contact-consistent equilibrium trajectories (sZFTs), the framework enables data-driven learning to directly inform and adapt an energy-consistent impedance controller. This addresses a fundamental challenge in achieving stable and safe physical human-robot interaction and contact-rich manipulation.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The framework demonstrates significant robustness and generalization capabilities. Its 100% success rate in multi-geometry peg-in-hole insertions, achieved without task-specific training data for this particular task, highlights its ability to generalize beyond the training distribution and adapt to unseen geometries and contact dynamics. This is a critical advancement for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamically changing environments where explicit models are not available.
- **Efficiency in Data and Deployment:** The model achieved strong performance with a relatively small amount of training data (1.6 hours of teleoperated demonstrations, 72,000 samples). This contrasts with many image-based generative models that require orders of magnitude more data. Furthermore, the selected model architecture is computationally efficient enough for real-time torque control, enabling practical deployment on robotic hardware.
- **Automated Impedance Adaptation:** The framework automates the traditionally manual and time-consuming process of tuning impedance parameters. By dynamically adjusting stiffness and damping based on inferred contact-consistent equilibria, it alleviates a major bottleneck in robot programming and control for contact tasks.
- **Enhanced Physical Interaction:** The directional stiffness adaptation, driven by the reconstructed sZFT, allows the robot to remain compliant in non-task-critical directions while maintaining rigidity in task-relevant axes. This prevents jamming and enables smooth, adaptable interaction, which is crucial for tasks like assembly, physical Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), and rehabilitation.
- **Foundational for Future Robotics:** Diffusion-Based Impedance Learning establishes a general interface between learning and model-based control, expanding the utility of generative models from visuomotor trajectory planning to explicit physical interaction. This could form a basis for more sophisticated hierarchical control architectures, where high-level vision-language policies integrate with low-level impedance regulation.
- **Potential Applications:** The demonstrated capabilities suggest broad applicability in various domains, including:
 - **Industrial Assembly:** For complex, tight-tolerance assembly tasks where visual feedback may be limited or unreliable, and precise force/compliance control is paramount.
 - **Robot-Assisted Rehabilitation:** As explored in the data collection, the concept of directional stiffness adaptation could be directly applicable to tailoring robotic assistance based on patient participation and movement intent.
 - **General Human-Robot Interaction:** Enabling robots to interact physically with humans in a safe and intuitive manner by adapting their compliance to unexpected contact.

While the current implementation focuses on diagonal stiffness matrices and has considerations regarding passivity guarantees when stiffness is *increased*, these are recognized limitations and areas for future research. The work, nonetheless, provides a promising design principle for developing more adaptable, stable, and safe robots capable of complex physical interaction.
