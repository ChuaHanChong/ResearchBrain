# LAST: LeArning to Think in Space and Time for Generalist Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The LAST framework enhances generalist Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with advanced 3D spatial understanding and long video comprehension by integrating "visual thinking trajectories" through external visual tools. This extension of Chain-of-Thought reasoning yields substantial performance increases on relevant benchmarks for both powerful proprietary models and fine-tuned open-source VLMs.

## Problem

- Generalist Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate limitations in tasks requiring robust 3D spatial reasoning and comprehension of long video sequences.
- Existing solutions for 3D and long video understanding are often specialized, relying on additional 3D inputs or task-specific architectures, hindering the development of unified general-purpose VLMs.
- Traditional, language-centric Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is insufficient for visually complex tasks, as it "lacks visual engagement" and can lead to incorrect reasoning when deep visual understanding is required.

## Method

- Introduces =="visual thinking trajectories"== to extend Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, enabling VLMs to dynamically interact with visual information for spatial and temporal analysis.
- Leverages a curated set of ==external visual tools==, including frame selection, object tracking, temporal grounding, image grounding, and depth estimation, to generate intermediate visual observations and augment VLM reasoning.
- The approach is implemented via zero-shot prompting for proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o) and through a ==two-stage data curation and fine-tuning pipeline== for open-weight models (e.g., LAST-7B based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B).

## Results

- GPT-4o with LAST achieved up to a **15.8%** accuracy gain on EgoSchema (from **69.6%** to **85.4%**) and an **11.4** points CIDEr improvement on ScanQA (from **65.4%** to **76.8%**), outperforming some specialized 3D models.
- The fine-tuned LAST-7B model, built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, showed an **8.3%** accuracy increase on VSI-Bench (from **33.0%** to **41.3%**) and a **14-point** gain on EgoSchema (from **55.0%** to **69.0%**) with 16 frames.
- LAST consistently outperformed text-only CoT, which often resulted in performance degradation (e.g., EgoSchema baseline **69.6%** vs. **62.7%** with text CoT, while LAST achieved **85.4%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating external visual tool use directly into a VLM's reasoning process allows it to effectively acquire advanced spatial and temporal understanding from sequential 2D image observations without specialized architectures.
> - Purely textual Chain-of-Thought reasoning is inadequate and often degrades performance on visually complex spatial and temporal tasks, underscoring the critical need for visually-engaged reasoning.
> - A dedicated two-stage data curation process, generating visual thinking trajectories, can successfully fine-tune open-source VLMs to achieve significant performance gains on advanced visual reasoning benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has been a significant breakthrough in artificial intelligence, enabling machines to understand and generate content across visual and textual modalities. Building upon the success of Large Language Models (LLMs), VLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in a wide array of typical vision-language tasks. However, as VLMs become more powerful, their limitations in specific, yet crucial, areas become apparent.

This paper directly addresses a critical challenge in the current VLM landscape: their struggle with 3D spatial understanding and long video comprehension, despite their general prowess. Previous research has largely tackled these problems separately. For 3D spatial understanding, approaches typically involve using explicit 3D data inputs (e.g., point clouds, depth images) or designing specialized architectural components (e.g., 3D-specific encoders). Similarly, for long video understanding, solutions have often focused on specific model designs or token compression techniques to manage the high computational and memory demands of extended temporal sequences. While these specialized methods show progress, they impede the development of truly *unified* and *general-purpose* VLMs capable of handling diverse visual reasoning tasks holistically.

"LAST" positions itself as a crucial step towards overcoming this fragmentation. It deviates from specialized, architecture-specific solutions by aiming to enhance general-purpose VLMs to reason spatially and temporally *without* relying on additional 3D inputs or task-specific architectures. This aligns with the broader trend in AI research of developing more generalist foundation models that can adapt to a wide range of tasks.

Furthermore, the work builds upon the concept of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which has been highly effective in enhancing the logical and multi-step reasoning abilities of LLMs and VLMs. However, the authors correctly identify a limitation: traditional CoT is language-centric. For tasks requiring deep understanding of the visual world, such as complex geometry, visual search, or spatial reasoning, a text-only CoT "lacks visual engagement." This paper extends the CoT paradigm into the visual domain, proposing "visual thinking trajectories" or "thinking in space and time." This aligns with emerging research directions that explore "thinking with images" or agentic models that leverage external tools to augment their reasoning capabilities, effectively bridging the gap between symbolic reasoning and rich visual perception. By focusing on general VLMs and only 2D image sequences as input, LAST aims for a more universally applicable solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "LAST" (LeArning to Think in Space and Time) project is to significantly improve both 3D spatial understanding and long video comprehension in generalist Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This is to be achieved *without* resorting to specialized architectural designs or requiring additional explicit 3D data inputs, instead relying solely on sequential 2D image observations.

The core motivation behind this objective stems from several identified limitations of current state-of-the-art VLMs:
- **Performance Gap in Specialized Visual Tasks:** Despite their impressive performance on typical vision-language tasks, existing VLMs consistently struggle with tasks that demand robust 3D spatial reasoning or the ability to process and understand information across extended video sequences. This deficiency hinders their applicability in real-world scenarios like robotics, autonomous navigation, and detailed video analysis.
- **Lack of Unification in Existing Solutions:** Current efforts to address these limitations are often task-specific. Researchers typically develop distinct models or introduce specialized inputs (e.g., explicit 3D features, high-resolution video streams) for either 3D tasks or long video tasks. This fragmented approach prevents the development of a truly general-purpose VLM capable of seamlessly handling both spatial and temporal reasoning from a unified input modality (2D images/frames). The authors explicitly ask: "Can general-purpose VLMs be enhanced to reason spatially and temporally without relying on additional inputs or task-specific architectures?"
- **Limitations of Text-Only Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** The paper highlights that while CoT has been instrumental in boosting the reasoning capabilities of VLMs through sequential textual thought processes, this paradigm falters when tasks require deep visual engagement. Text-only CoT, by its nature, "ignores the rich and continuous visual world" and cannot effectively capture the nuances of spatial relationships, object correspondence across views, or temporal dynamics in a visually meaningful way. This leads to incorrect reasoning traces and hallucinations, especially in complex visual scenarios.
- **Desire for Visual Engagement in Reasoning:** The authors are motivated to inject rich visual information directly into the reasoning process. By making VLMs "think in space and time," they aim to enable the models to build "visual thinking trajectories" that mirror how humans perceive and understand 3D space and long videos from sequential visual observations. This visual engagement is deemed critical for solving complex geometry problems, visual search, and advanced spatial reasoning.

In essence, LAST seeks to equip general VLMs with an inherent capability to reason about the physical world's spatial and temporal dimensions, moving beyond purely language-centric reasoning to a more comprehensive, visually-grounded intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LAST (LeArning to Think in Space and Time) introduces a novel approach to enhance general Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by incorporating "visual thinking trajectories" into their reasoning process. This methodology allows VLMs to dynamically interact with and analyze visual information, akin to human perception, rather than relying solely on textual prompts or static visual inputs.

The core idea is to extend the concept of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to include visual steps. While traditional CoT generates intermediate text tokens to "think," LAST aims for `A = M(Q, I_0, V)`, where `V` represents a sequence of visual thoughts. Since most current VLMs generate only text, LAST leverages external, specialized visual tools to generate these intermediate visual tokens and observations.

**Key Tools Employed:**
The authors carefully select a minimal yet sufficient set of external tools to facilitate spatial and temporal thinking:
1. **Frame Selection:** This tool addresses the challenge of long video contexts by selecting the most relevant and diverse frames. It combines two strategies:
 - **Maximizing Text-Image Relevance:** Uses SigLIP-2 embeddings to select frames most similar to the text query, ensuring content relevance.
 - **Minimizing Visual Redundancy:** Employs a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) to select a diverse subset of frames, preventing too many highly similar images from being processed. This is critical for efficient processing of long videos.
2. **Object Tracking:** Utilizes SAM2 (SAM2.1-Hiera-Large) to identify and follow the movement of target objects across a sequence of visual observations. This is crucial for understanding object correspondence in 3D scenes and tracking motion in videos, providing a visual "marker" for the VLM.
3. **Temporal Grounding:** Leverages TFVTG (Training-Free Video Temporal Grounding) to localize specific video segments relevant to a given textual query. This helps VLMs focus on pertinent temporal information, reducing irrelevant visual context.
4. **Image Grounding:** Employs Grounding-DINO-L to localize specific regions within a single image based on a textual description. This is a foundational tool for detailed object interaction in 2D.
5. **Depth Estimation:** Uses Depth-Anything(-Large) to provide relative depth information, which is highly beneficial for 3D spatial understanding tasks.
6. **Zoom-in-out:** Implemented using OpenCV, this tool allows the VLM to focus on specific areas of an image, simulating a "zoom" action for fine-grained analysis.

**Enhancing VLMs with LAST (Two Scenarios):**

LAST is explored in two distinct ways to demonstrate its versatility and applicability:

1. **Zero-Shot Prompting for Proprietary Models:**
 - Powerful, closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o) are prompted to simulate "thinking in space and time."
 - The prompt guides the model to reason step-by-step, including calls to the external visual tools and incorporating their visual outputs (e.g., marked images, depth maps) into its reasoning chain before providing a final answer.

2. **Fine-tuning Open-Weight Models (LAST-7B):**
 - To make LAST applicable to open-source and lightweight models, a novel **two-stage data curation pipeline** is developed:
 - **Stage 1: Text CoT Filtering:** Public datasets (images, videos, 3D scenes) are initially processed. VLMs are prompted with text CoT, and only questions successfully answered are retained, forming a dataset of text-based reasoning traces.
 - **Stage 2: Visual Thinking Trajectory Construction:** For questions *not* solved by text CoT in Stage 1, the external visual tools (described above) are invoked. The process generates detailed visual thinking trajectories, where tool outputs (e.g., selected frames, tracked objects, depth maps) are integrated into the reasoning chain alongside textual descriptions of the tools' actions and observations. Only trajectories leading to correct answers are retained.
 - **Dataset Size:** This pipeline yields 48K samples with text-based CoT and 124K samples with visual thinking trajectories, averaging 1.72 tool calls per sample.
 - **Training:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B is chosen as the base model and fine-tuned using SWIFT with standard next token prediction loss on this curated dataset to create LAST-7B.

This comprehensive methodology, combining strategic tool integration with both zero-shot prompting and dedicated fine-tuning, establishes LAST as a flexible and effective framework for endowing general VLMs with advanced spatial and temporal reasoning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate that LAST brings substantial and consistent performance gains across a wide range of benchmarks, significantly improving both 3D spatial and long video understanding for general Vision-Language Models.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Results (with GPT-4o)

LAST consistently enhances the performance of a powerful proprietary model like GPT-4o across all tested benchmarks.
- **Spatial Understanding Tasks:**
 - **SQA3D:** Achieved a notable 9.6% accuracy gain on SQA3D (EM-R1 improved from 49.6% to 61.2%) without using any 3D-specific inputs.
 - **ScanQA:** Demonstrated an 11.4 points improvement on CIDEr (from 65.4% to 76.8%).
 - **VSI-Bench:** Showed significant improvements, with GPT-4o + LAST achieving 46.6% average accuracy compared to 40.9% for baseline GPT-4o. These results place GPT-4o with LAST comparably with or even outperforming some models specifically designed for 3D scenes.
- **Video Understanding Tasks:**
 - **EgoSchema:** Registered a remarkable 15.8% accuracy improvement (from 69.6% to 85.4%) using only eight input frames. This performance is particularly striking as it outperforms many specialized long video understanding models that use significantly more frames (e.g., VideoAgent2 at 80.6% or TCoT at 75.2% with 1 fps, implying many more frames).
 - **LVBench:** Showed a 7.7 points gain (from 32.9% to 40.6%).
 - **NExT-QA:** Improved from 78.9% to 84.8% on the validation set and from 72.5% to 76.6% on the challenging ATP-hard subset.
 - **MotionBench:** Improved from 53.5% to 56.2%.

#### 4.2. Results on Fine-tuned Model (LAST-7B)

The fine-tuned LAST-7B model, built upon Qwen2.5-VL-7B, also exhibits significant and consistent performance enhancements, validating the generalizability of the LAST approach to open-weight models.
- **Spatial Understanding Tasks:**
 - **VSI-Bench:** LAST-7B achieved 41.3% accuracy, outperforming the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 8.3%.
 - **SQA3D:** Showed improvements in both EM-1 (46.5% to 56.1%) and EM-R1 (49.8% to 59.6%).
 - **ScanQA:** Improved significantly across all reported metrics (e.g., CIDEr from 53.9% to 66.4%).
- **Video Understanding Tasks:**
 - **EgoSchema:** Reached 69.0% accuracy with 16 frames, demonstrating a 14-point gain over the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (55.0%).
 - **NExT-QA:** Achieved 86.2% on the validation set and 78.0% on the ATP-hard subset, surpassing all other open-weight baselines.
 - **LVBench:** Improved from 39.2% to 43.8%.
 - **MotionBench:** Improved from 58.6% to 62.0%.

#### 4.3. Comparative Analysis with Text-Only CoT

A crucial finding is the direct comparison with text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT). The experiments reveal that text-only CoT often *degrades* performance on benchmarks like EgoSchema (69.6% baseline vs. 62.7% with CoT) and NExT-QA (78.9% baseline vs. 77.5% with CoT). In stark contrast, LAST consistently yields significant improvements (e.g., 85.4% on EgoSchema), underscoring the inadequacy of purely textual reasoning for visually complex spatial and temporal tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Tool Usage Analysis

- **Tool Importance:** Analysis of tool usage highlights the importance of specific tools for different tasks. For long video tasks, object tracking, temporal grounding, and frame selection are paramount. For 3D spatial understanding, object tracking and depth estimation prove critical.
- **Data Quality:** Ablation studies on the fine-tuning data confirm that training with "visual thinking trajectories" (data with `V` and `T+V`) is crucial for the observed performance gains, significantly outperforming models trained only with text CoT data (`T`).
- **Image Benchmarks:** LAST also showed gains on 2D image benchmarks (HR-Bench, V*, BLINK), indicating its broader applicability beyond 3D and video.
- **Multi-turn Thinking:** Allowing multiple tool calls (multi-turn mode) showed clear advantages over single-turn thinking, enabling more complex reasoning.

In summary, the results definitively establish LAST as an effective and versatile method for imbuing general VLMs with enhanced spatial and temporal understanding capabilities, whether through zero-shot prompting or fine-tuning, and significantly outperforming traditional text-based reasoning in these contexts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "LAST: LeArning to Think in Space and Time" framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models, with broad implications for both fundamental research and practical applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Towards Truly Generalist Visual Intelligence:** The most profound impact of LAST is its contribution to the development of truly general-purpose VLMs. By demonstrating that spatial and temporal reasoning can be significantly enhanced *without* specialized architectures or additional 3D data inputs, LAST paves the way for models that can perceive and understand the world across diverse visual tasks in a unified manner. This moves beyond task-specific solutions towards a more holistic, human-like visual intelligence.
- **Reframing Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** LAST fundamentally extends the successful Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm by introducing "visual thinking trajectories." This innovation highlights the limitations of text-only reasoning for visually-grounded tasks and provides a concrete, effective mechanism to integrate visual engagement directly into the reasoning process. This opens new avenues for research into multi-modal reasoning, where thought processes dynamically adapt between different modalities.
- **Validation of External Tool-Use Paradigm:** The work strongly validates the growing paradigm of augmenting LLM/VLM capabilities through external tools. By strategically integrating a suite of off-the-shelf visual tools (frame selection, object tracking, depth estimation, etc.), LAST showcases how foundation models can overcome inherent limitations by intelligently leveraging specialized modules. This promotes further exploration into agentic AI systems that dynamically call tools for complex problem-solving.
- **Enhanced Accessibility to Advanced Capabilities:** By successfully fine-tuning an open-weight model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) to create LAST-7B, the research makes sophisticated spatial and temporal reasoning more accessible. This enables broader adoption and further development of these capabilities in resource-constrained environments or for researchers working with open-source models, democratizing advanced VLM research.
- **Valuable Data Asset:** The creation of a large-scale dataset featuring "visual thinking trajectories" is a significant contribution in itself. This dataset provides a rich resource for training future VLMs to learn complex multi-modal reasoning patterns, effectively serving as an instruction-tuning dataset for visual reasoning.
- **Broadening Real-World Applications:** The improvements in 3D spatial and long video understanding have direct and substantial implications for numerous real-world applications:
 - **Robotics:** Robots require robust 3D spatial awareness and understanding of dynamic environments to navigate, manipulate objects, and interact with the world. LAST's advancements could lead to more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Accurate perception of 3D space, object movement, and long-term scene understanding from sequential camera feeds is critical for self-driving cars.
 - **Video Content Analysis:** From surveillance to sports analysis and entertainment, better long video understanding can enable more precise event detection, summarization, and retrieval.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR)/Virtual Reality (VR):** Enhancing how models perceive and interact with 3D environments from 2D inputs could improve the realism and interactivity of AR/VR experiences.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** LAST's success will likely spur further research into:
 - Developing VLMs that can intrinsically generate visual thought steps without relying on external tools.
 - Exploring more sophisticated tool integration strategies and dynamic tool selection mechanisms.
 - Investigating the cognitive parallels between human visual reasoning and machine "thinking in space and time."

In essence, LAST not only solves a pressing problem in current VLM capabilities but also provides a robust framework and valuable insights that will influence the trajectory of visual AI research for years to come, pushing towards more intelligent, adaptable, and generalist AI systems.
