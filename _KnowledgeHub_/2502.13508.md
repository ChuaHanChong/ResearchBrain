# VLAS: Vision-Language-Action Model With Speech Instructions For Customized Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Westlake University, Zhejiang University, and Xi’an Jiaotong University developed VLAS, a Vision-Language-Action model that directly processes raw speech instructions for robot manipulation, outperforming ASR-based approaches. VLAS also enables personalized robot tasks by extracting and leveraging speaker-specific knowledge from voiceprints, achieving over 86% success on a novel customized task benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on text-based instructions, limiting natural human-robot interaction.
- Integrating speech via external Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems for VLAs introduces system complexity, computational overhead, and loss of non-semantic information (e.g., voiceprint) crucial for nuanced instructions.
- Current VLA research largely lacks the ability to perform customized, user-specific manipulation tasks based on individual preferences or identity.

## Method

- Developed **VLAS**, an end-to-end VLA model that natively integrates a Whisper speech encoder to directly process raw speech instructions, bypassing traditional ASR-to-text pipelines.
- Introduced a novel ==Voice Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)== module to extract voiceprints from raw speech, query an external database for individual-specific knowledge, and integrate this context into the LLM for personalized tasks.
- Created two new datasets, Speech Question Answering (SQA) and CALVIN with Speech Instructions (CSI), to facilitate training speech-enabled VLMs and VLAs.

## Results

- VLAS with speech instructions achieved a **54.6%** success rate on the CALVIN benchmark, outperforming ASR-integrated VLA baselines (**40.2%** for VLA+ASR, **48.3%** for Roboflamingo+ASR).
- On a new benchmark for customized tasks, VLAS with Voice RAG achieved an average success rate exceeding **86%** for personalized commands, a dramatic improvement over VLA baselines without RAG (below **20%**).
- VLAS-Base, the foundation model, achieved a Word Error Rate (WER) of **2.79%** on LibriSpeech, comparable to Whisper large-v2, demonstrating strong speech understanding without compromising multimodal capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct integration of raw speech into VLA models significantly outperforms cascading ASR-to-text approaches by avoiding error propagation and retaining non-semantic information critical for robust command execution.
> - Leveraging speaker-specific voiceprints through a Voice RAG mechanism allows robots to effectively understand and execute personalized instructions, such as distinguishing 'my cup' from others based on speaker identity.
> - An autoregressive action tokenization scheme enables the LLM to directly generate robot action sequences as a textual string, which can then be detokenized for continuous control.
