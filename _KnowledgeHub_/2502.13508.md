# VLAS: Vision-Language-Action Model With Speech Instructions For Customized Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Westlake University, Zhejiang University, and Xi’an Jiaotong University developed VLAS, a Vision-Language-Action model that directly processes raw speech instructions for robot manipulation, outperforming ASR-based approaches. VLAS also enables personalized robot tasks by extracting and leveraging speaker-specific knowledge from voiceprints, achieving over 86% success on a novel customized task benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on text-based instructions, limiting natural human-robot interaction.
- Integrating speech via external Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems for VLAs introduces system complexity, computational overhead, and loss of non-semantic information (e.g., voiceprint) crucial for nuanced instructions.
- Current VLA research largely lacks the ability to perform customized, user-specific manipulation tasks based on individual preferences or identity.

## Method

- Developed **VLAS**, an end-to-end VLA model that natively integrates a Whisper speech encoder to directly process raw speech instructions, bypassing traditional ASR-to-text pipelines.
- Introduced a novel ==Voice Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)== module to extract voiceprints from raw speech, query an external database for individual-specific knowledge, and integrate this context into the LLM for personalized tasks.
- Created two new datasets, Speech Question Answering (SQA) and CALVIN with Speech Instructions (CSI), to facilitate training speech-enabled VLMs and VLAs.

## Results

- VLAS with speech instructions achieved a **54.6%** success rate on the CALVIN benchmark, outperforming ASR-integrated VLA baselines (**40.2%** for VLA+ASR, **48.3%** for Roboflamingo+ASR).
- On a new benchmark for customized tasks, VLAS with Voice RAG achieved an average success rate exceeding **86%** for personalized commands, a dramatic improvement over VLA baselines without RAG (below **20%**).
- VLAS-Base, the foundation model, achieved a Word Error Rate (WER) of **2.79%** on LibriSpeech, comparable to Whisper large-v2, demonstrating strong speech understanding without compromising multimodal capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct integration of raw speech into VLA models significantly outperforms cascading ASR-to-text approaches by avoiding error propagation and retaining non-semantic information critical for robust command execution.
> - Leveraging speaker-specific voiceprints through a Voice RAG mechanism allows robots to effectively understand and execute personalized instructions, such as distinguishing 'my cup' from others based on speaker identity.
> - An autoregressive action tokenization scheme enables the LLM to directly generate robot action sequences as a textual string, which can then be detokenized for continuous control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. These models represent a significant advancement in robotics, moving towards more intelligent and generalizable control policies by integrating insights from large vision-language models (VLMs).

Historically, the field has progressed from large language models (LLMs) demonstrating impressive capabilities in natural language processing (e.g., FLAN-PaLM, LLaMA) to vision-language models (VLMs) like LLaVA, BLIP-2, and OpenFlamingo, which combine visual and textual understanding. VLMs have then been adapted for robotics, leading to VLAs (e.g., RT-2, Roboflamingo, OpenVLA). These VLAs aim for "end-to-end design," "remarkable performance," and "enhanced generalization" in robot manipulation tasks by taking textual instructions and visual observations to generate robot actions.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: existing VLAs predominantly rely on text-based instructions, overlooking the more natural and intuitive speech modality for human-robot interaction. While traditional approaches might integrate speech via an external Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) system (e.g., Whisper), this introduces two major limitations:
1. **System Complexity and Computational Overhead:** A cascading ASR-to-text-to-VLA pipeline increases the system's size, computational demands, and memory consumption.
2. **Loss of Non-Semantic Information:** Transcription discards crucial auxiliary information embedded in raw speech, such as voiceprint, emotion, and intonation. This non-semantic data is vital for understanding "unstructured" human instructions and for tasks requiring personalization.

Recent developments in general multimodal LLMs, such as GPT-4o, Gemini, and VITA, have started to explore direct integration of audio information. VLAS takes a significant step further by applying this direct integration principle specifically to the *robotics* domain, creating an end-to-end VLA that processes raw speech. Furthermore, VLAS introduces a novel Voice Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigm to leverage speaker-specific information, addressing the personalization aspect largely unexplored in previous VLA research. This contribution distinguishes VLAS by not only enabling speech interaction but also enhancing the robot's ability to understand and execute customized, user-specific commands, thus pushing the boundaries of accessible and intelligent robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for VLAS stems from the practical need for more natural, accessible, and customizable human-robot interaction (HRI), particularly in scenarios like home care where individuals have varied physical abilities and subjective preferences. The authors argue that speech is an "ideal modality" for achieving this goal, yet it remains largely unaddressed in current VLA models.

The primary objectives of this research are:
- **Overcome Limitations of Text-Only VLAs:** To move beyond the current reliance on text-based instructions in VLAs, which is less natural for human users and limits the robot's ability to infer nuanced intent.
- **Address Challenges of ASR Integration:** To develop a robot policy model that directly processes raw speech instructions, thereby eliminating the complexities and error propagation inherent in cascading ASR systems. A crucial aspect here is preventing the loss of non-semantic information (e.g., voiceprint, emotion) that is vital for context-rich interactions.
- **Enable Personalized Robot Manipulation:** To empower robots to perform customized tasks by leveraging auxiliary information from raw speech, specifically focusing on voiceprint for individual-specific knowledge. An example provided is distinguishing "my cup" from other cups based on the speaker's voice.
- **Develop an End-to-End Speech-Enabled VLA:** To design and implement VLAS, an innovative model that seamlessly integrates speech recognition directly into the robot policy, allowing for multimodal interaction across text, image, speech, and robot actions.
- **Introduce Novel Data and Methods:** To create new datasets (SQA and CSI) to facilitate the training of speech-enabled VLAs and propose a Voice RAG paradigm for handling tasks requiring individual-specific knowledge.
- **Contribute a Foundation Model:** To develop VLAS-Base, an extension of the widely used LLaVA model that accepts speech instructions, making it valuable for other multimodal tasks beyond robotics.

In essence, the paper aims to bridge the gap between intuitive human communication and robot capabilities, making robots more intelligent, responsive, and tailored to individual user needs through direct speech understanding and personalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLAS employs an end-to-end approach to integrate speech instructions directly into a VLA model. Its methodology can be broken down into three key aspects: architecture, data collection, and a multi-stage training paradigm.

#### 3.1. Architecture of VLAS

The VLAS model is designed to take human speech instructions (`s`) and visual observations (`O`) as input to directly generate robot actions (`a`). It builds upon the established LLaVA framework and extends it with speech processing capabilities:
- **Network Backbone:** The core is a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically LLaVA, utilizing a CLIP (Vision Transformer) as the visual encoder and Vicuna (a fine-tuned LLaMA variant) as the Large Language Model (LLM) backbone.
- **Speech Encoder:** To handle speech inputs, VLAS incorporates a Whisper encoder. Speech signals are first converted into 80-bin mel-spectrograms, then processed by the Whisper encoder. A reduction factor of 5 is applied along the time dimension to reduce computational burden, followed by an MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) to project these speech tokens into the LLM's semantic space.
- **Input Integration:** Visual, speech, and (optionally) retrieved knowledge embeddings are concatenated and fed into the LLM backbone.
 `Emb(s, O) = concat(MLPs(Embs(s)), Tokl(RAG(s)), MLPv(Embv(O)))`
 The LLM then generates predicted actions in an autoregressive manner.
- **Voice Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** This novel module addresses personalized tasks. It extracts a voiceprint from the raw speech command using a pre-trained speaker identification module. This voiceprint acts as a key to query an external database for individual-specific knowledge. The retrieved textual knowledge is then tokenized and integrated with visual and speech embeddings, providing crucial context for the LLM.
- **Action Tokenization:** Continuous robot actions (7 dimensions: x, y, z, ϕ, θ, ψ, g) are discretized into 256 uniformly spaced bins. These bins are mapped to the 256 least frequently used tokens in the LLM's vocabulary, allowing the LLM to generate action sequences directly as a textual string, which is then detokenized into continuous values for robot control.

#### 3.2. Data Collection for VLAS

Since existing VLM/VLA datasets lack speech instructions, the authors created two new datasets:
- **Speech Question Answering (SQA) Dataset:** Generated from LLaVA's visual instruction tuning dataset (specifically the multi-turn conversation subset). Textual questions are converted into speech using the ESPnet/VITS text-to-speech (TTS) model trained on LibriTTS, employing over 1,152 distinct voices. This results in 185K image-audio-text QA pairs.
- **CALVIN with Speech Instructions (CSI) Dataset:** Created by converting the 389 textual instructions of the CALVIN robot manipulation dataset into speech using the same TTS model, generating 194K audio samples with 500 different voices. For training, half of the original robot manipulation samples are replaced with these synthesized speech instructions.

#### 3.3. Training Paradigm of VLAS

The training process is divided into three distinct stages:
1. **Stage I: Speech Alignment:** Focuses on coarse-grained alignment between speech and text. Only the MLP layer connecting the speech encoder to the LLM backbone is fine-tuned using the LibriSpeech-360 speech recognition dataset. Speaker identification can also be co-trained here or use a pre-trained module.
2. **Stage II: Speech Question Answering Fine-tuning:** Aims to enhance multimodal understanding. The model is fine-tuned on the curated SQA dataset, LLaVA's original VQA datasets, and LibriSpeech-100. All network components (except pre-trained image and speech encoders) are updated. This stage yields **VLAS-Base**, a foundation model capable of responding to both text-image and speech-image instructions.
3. **Stage III: Robot Manipulation Fine-tuning:** The VLAS-Base model is further fine-tuned on the CSI dataset using behavior cloning. Each sample includes visual observations from two views, speech/text instructions, and a sequence of robot actions. Actions from five time steps are combined into a single training label to increase operating frequency.

The training leverages 8x A100 GPUs, Adam optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule, Flash Attention 2, BF16, and TF32 for efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate VLAS's superior performance in both general robot manipulation and specialized customized tasks, alongside validating its multimodal foundation model, VLAS-Base.

#### 4.1. Robot Manipulation with Speech Instructions (CALVIN Benchmark)

- **General Performance:** VLAS, whether using textual or speech instructions, significantly outperforms previous state-of-the-art models like MCIL, HULC, and RT-1 on the CALVIN benchmark.
- **Text vs. Speech Equivalence:** VLAS with textual instructions achieves performance comparable to a traditional VLA baseline (e.g., LH-5 success rate: VLAS$^+$ 56.6% vs VLA$^+$ 58.2%), indicating that integrating speech capability does not degrade existing text-based performance.
- **Superiority of End-to-End Speech:** VLAS with speech instructions (VLAS$^*$ 54.6%) substantially outperforms VLA models that rely on external ASR systems (VLA$^*$+ASR 40.2%, Roboflamingo$^*$+ASR 48.3%). This confirms the authors' hypothesis that direct speech integration avoids error propagation and is more effective for robot control commands.
- **Real-World Speech:** VLAS maintains strong performance even with real recorded speech instructions (VLAS$^*$ (Real) 51.3%), showing robust generalization beyond synthetic voices.
- **Orthogonality:** The authors note that VLAS's method is orthogonal to other VLA models, implying potential for further performance enhancements through combination.

#### 4.2. Robot Manipulation for Customized Tasks (Novel Benchmark)

- **Validation of Voice RAG:** On a newly created benchmark for personalized tasks (Object Ownership, User Preference, Compound Tasks), the VLA baseline (text-only) performs very poorly (average success rate < 20%) due to its inability to access personal knowledge.
- **Dramatic Improvement with VLAS:** VLAS with speech instructions and the Voice RAG module achieves an average success rate exceeding 86%, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling personalized commands by leveraging individual-specific knowledge. Even with real speech, performance remains high (78.6%).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing the Voice RAG module from VLAS (VLAS$^*$-RAG) causes a significant performance drop (average 16.0%), confirming RAG's critical role.
 - Integrating the Voice RAG module with the VLA baseline (VLA$^+$+RAG) dramatically improves its performance (average 82.0%), further solidifying the efficacy of the Voice RAG paradigm.

#### 4.3. Experiments with a Real-World UR5 Robot Arm

- VLAS was successfully fine-tuned using the Berkeley UR5 demonstration dataset and a custom cup-picking dataset.
- Demonstrations show the VLAS model effectively responding to speaker-specific instructions (e.g., "pick up *my* cup," distinguishing between different colored cups based on speaker identity), proving its real-world applicability for personalized tasks.

#### 4.4. Analysis for the VLAS-Base Foundation Model

- **General Multimodal Understanding:** VLAS-Base (Vicuna-7B) achieves comparable performance to LLaVA v1.5 (Vicuna-7B) on standard multimodal benchmarks (VQA v2, VizWiz, SQA I, VQA T, POPE, GQA), and significantly outperforms other VLMs like BLIP-2 and InstructBLIP. This indicates that the integration of speech modality does not compromise the model's existing visual and language understanding capabilities.
- **Speech Understanding:**
 - **Speech Recognition (LibriSpeech):** VLAS-Base achieves a Word Error Rate (WER) of 2.79%, comparable to the state-of-the-art Whisper large-v2 model (2.7%).
 - **Speech Question Answering (SGQA):** On the novel SGQA benchmark (GQA questions converted to speech), VLAS-Base (50.8% accuracy) outperforms BLIP-2 with ground-truth textual instructions (41.0%), though it lags behind LLaVA with ground-truth text (62.0%). This demonstrates strong speech understanding capabilities.

#### 4.5. Inference Efficiency Analysis (Appendix)

- The paper investigates the impact of multi-step prediction (`r`) on inference efficiency.
- `r=5` (predicting and executing 5 actions per inference step) achieves an optimal balance between speedup and robot manipulation performance on the CALVIN benchmark, demonstrating significant efficiency gains without compromising task completion rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLAS represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and human-robot interaction, addressing crucial limitations of existing VLA models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering End-to-End Speech-Enabled VLAs:** VLAS is the first vision-language-action model to natively integrate raw speech instructions for robot manipulation, circumventing the inherent drawbacks (complexity, error propagation, information loss) of traditional ASR-to-text pipelines. This simplifies the robotic system and allows for more direct, natural communication.
- **Enabling Personalized Robot Capabilities:** By introducing the Voice RAG paradigm, VLAS empowers robots to understand and act upon individual-specific knowledge derived from non-semantic speech cues like voiceprint. This capability is revolutionary for customizing robot behavior based on user identity or preferences, moving beyond generic command execution to truly personalized assistance. This is particularly impactful for applications in home care, assistive robotics, and industrial settings where individual user profiles or preferences are critical.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability for robots to directly understand spoken commands, including nuances and implicit information, fosters more natural, intuitive, and accessible human-robot interfaces. This can dramatically improve user experience, reduce cognitive load for users, and make robots more usable by a wider demographic, including those with varying physical abilities.
- **Contribution of a Versatile Foundation Model:** VLAS-Base, which extends the popular LLaVA model to accept speech inputs while maintaining strong multimodal performance, serves as a valuable open-source resource for the broader research community. It can accelerate further research in multimodal large language models and other downstream applications requiring speech-image understanding.
- **New Datasets for Research:** The introduction of the SQA and CSI datasets provides essential resources for training and evaluating speech-enabled VLMs and VLAs. These datasets will be crucial for fostering continued research and benchmarking in this emerging area.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment on a real UR5 robot arm demonstrates the practical viability of VLAS. This validation is critical for transitioning research innovations from simulation to real-world applications.
- **Future Research Avenues:** The work opens up exciting avenues for exploring other auxiliary information embedded in human speech (e.g., emotion, urgency, stress) or environmental sounds, potentially leading to robots that are even more contextually aware and adept at understanding complex, nuanced human instructions. This could lead to a new generation of robots that are truly adaptive and empathetic in their interactions.

In conclusion, VLAS marks a pivotal step towards creating more intelligent, user-friendly, and highly customizable robots. By directly integrating speech and leveraging speaker-specific knowledge, it addresses a fundamental challenge in human-robot interaction, paving the way for robots that can seamlessly and effectively serve diverse human needs in complex environments.
