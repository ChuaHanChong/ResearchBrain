# Goal Force: Teaching Video Models To Accomplish Physics-Conditioned Goals

> [!summary] Summary
> Goal Force introduces a framework that teaches video generation models to accomplish physics-conditioned goals by planning antecedent causal chains from explicit force vector specifications. The model acts as an implicit neural physics simulator, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization to complex, real-world interactions.

## Problem

- Text prompts are often too abstract to convey the precise physical interactions needed for complex, multi-step tasks.
- Specifying dynamic goals via target images can be infeasible or overly burdensome, as it requires rendering precise pixel-perfect end states for causal chains.
- Prior force-conditioned methods focus on direct, immediate interventions ('what if' scenarios) rather than reasoning about and planning to achieve a desired future force.

## Method

- A novel framework trains video generative models to act as implicit neural physics planners, generating a physically plausible antecedent causal chain to achieve a user-specified 'goal force' on a target object.
- A multi-channel physics control signal is introduced, encoding direct force, goal force, and mass, which conditions a **ControlNet-integrated** ==diffusion model==.
- The model is trained on synthetic causal primitives (dominos, rolling balls) using a strategy of randomly masking causal information, forcing it to learn both goal-to-plan and action-to-outcome reasoning paths.

## Results

- Goal Force significantly outperformed text-only baselines in human evaluations for 'Goal Force Adherence,' achieving up to 73.4% preference in two-object collision tasks.
- The model generates accurate and diverse visual plans, correctly identifying valid initiators in complex scenes (e.g., 97.96% accuracy for a pool scene) and demonstrating multi-modal planning with a diversity score of 0.6577 for domino chains.
- Visual plans effectively leverage privileged physics information, with projectile speeds adapting correctly to varying masses of projectile and target objects, even in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video models can be trained to function as implicit neural physics simulators, planning causal interactions without requiring an external physics engine during inference.
> - Defining goals through explicit force vectors and intermediate dynamics offers a more intuitive and precise control mechanism for generative world models compared to abstract text or static images.
> - Training on simple, synthetic causal primitives enables strong zero-shot generalization, allowing the model to reason about complex, out-of-domain scenarios like tool manipulation and human-object interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in video generation models, exemplified by systems like Sora, have demonstrated an increasing capability to produce photorealistic and dynamically plausible video sequences. This progress has led to an interest in leveraging these models as "world models" for applications in robotics and planning. A central challenge in utilizing these generative models for goal-oriented tasks is the precise specification of desired outcomes.

Current approaches predominantly rely on two main modalities for goal specification: text prompts and target images. Text-based instructions, such as "shoot at the goal," are often too abstract to convey the nuanced physical interactions required for complex tasks. For instance, a precise soccer kick involves specific force and precision rather than just a general instruction. Conversely, specifying goals via target images can be overly burdensome or infeasible for dynamic scenarios, as it may require rendering a precise pixel-perfect end state, which is difficult to achieve or even define for multi-step physical processes.

This work positions itself within the broader context of controllable video generation and physics-aware AI. While many methods focus on controlling camera perspectives or specifying dense motion trajectories, a more recent direction involves direct physical control through force vectors. Prior work like Force Prompting introduced the concept of directly applying a force to an object. However, these methods typically address immediate, direct interventions ("what if" scenarios) rather than reasoning about and planning a causal chain of events to achieve a *desired* future force.

Furthermore, the field has explored physics simulators and hybrid approaches, where explicit physics engines are integrated with generative models. While accurate, these methods often require access to 3D geometry and rely on an external simulator at inference time. Other research has aimed to learn physical properties or representations from video without explicit simulators. Goal Force departs from these by attempting to train the generative model itself to function as an implicit "neural simulator" that can plan causal interactions, removing the need for an external physics engine during inference. This situates Goal Force as a step towards more physically-aware and interactive world models capable of complex, multi-step planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable video generation models to accomplish physics-conditioned goals by allowing users to define these goals through explicit force vectors and intermediate dynamics. The motivation for this approach stems from the limitations of existing goal specification methods and an observation of how humans conceptualize physical tasks.

**Limitations of Current Goal Specification:**
- **Abstract Text Instructions:** Current text prompts are often too general to capture the precise physical nuances required for tasks involving multi-step dynamics. For example, instructing a robotic arm to "move the block" does not convey the specific force, direction, or timing needed for a particular interaction. This abstraction limits the model's ability to generate physically accurate and intentional actions.
- **Infeasible Target Images:** Specifying a goal state using a static target image is frequently impractical or impossible for dynamic tasks. Capturing the exact lighting, angle, and instantaneous state of objects in motion, particularly when the end state is the culmination of a causal chain, presents a significant burden for users.

**Human Intuition for Physical Tasks:**
The authors observe that humans often approach physical tasks by decomposing them into concrete sub-goals possessing distinct physical properties, such as spatial location, velocity, and force. For instance, a person taking a penalty kick in soccer does not merely focus on the ball being in the net (a static end state) or the abstract concept of "scoring." Instead, they aim to impart a specific trajectory and velocity – a "goal force" – onto the ball. This human-centric approach, where intent is expressed through desired physical interactions rather than abstract labels or static images, motivates the framework introduced in this paper.

**Proposed Solution and Core Objective:**
To address these challenges, the research proposes "Goal Force," a novel framework that allows users to define goals through desired force vectors and intermediate dynamics. The key objectives are:
1. **Introduce a New Task and Model:** To train video models to plan a causal chain of physical interactions to achieve a specified goal force, thereby moving beyond prior direct-force methods. This fundamentally changes how goals can be specified in world models, shifting from "what if" a force is applied directly to "how to" achieve a desired force through a sequence of actions.
2. **Develop a Training Paradigm for Implicit Physics Simulation:** To establish a training strategy using a multi-channel control signal (for goal forces, direct forces, and mass) that teaches the video model to act as an implicit neural physics simulator. This eliminates the dependency on external physics engines during inference, integrating physical reasoning directly into the generative model.
3. **Demonstrate Out-of-Domain Generalization:** To show that training on simple, synthetic causal primitives enables the model to generalize effectively to complex, real-world scenarios, including tool manipulation and multi-object causal chains, without explicit training on such diverse data. This aims to validate the hypothesis that grounding video generation in fundamental physical interactions can bootstrap complex reasoning capabilities.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to enable precise, physics-aware planning within generative video models, thereby advancing the capabilities of interactive world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Goal Force method redefines force-conditioned video generation by allowing users to specify a desired "goal force" on a target object, rather than a direct, immediate force. Given an initial frame and an optional text prompt, the model generates a video that synthesizes a physically plausible antecedent causal chain to achieve the declared goal force. This is achieved by training a video generative model to function as an implicit neural physics planner.

#### 3.1. Multi-Channel Physics Control Signal

A core component of the approach is a 3-channel physics control tensor, $\tilde{\pi} \in \mathbb{R}^{f \times 3 \times h \times w}$, where $f$ represents the number of frames, and $h$ and $w$ are the spatial dimensions. Each channel encodes a specific physical property:

- **Channel 0: Direct Force:** This channel encodes an immediate, direct force (the "cause"). It is represented as a "moving Gaussian blob" video, where the blob's trajectory, duration, and magnitude are proportional to the specified force vector (location, angle, and magnitude).
- **Channel 1: Goal Force:** This channel encodes the desired outcome or "effect" on a target object. It utilizes the same moving Gaussian blob representation to specify the desired force and the resulting motion on the target object. This distinguishes it from direct force by requiring the model to infer the preceding action.
- **Channel 2: Mass:** This channel provides privileged physical information, such as the relative mass of objects. It is represented as a static Gaussian blob centered on the object, with a radius proportional to its mass. This signal is optional; when not provided, the model is trained to infer mass properties from visual appearance based on its learned physics prior.

Force and mass values are normalized to a relative scale within each synthetic dataset, allowing the model to learn concepts like "small poke" versus "large poke" without requiring an absolute physical calibration.

#### 3.2. Goal Reaching via Implicit Planning

The model is trained on a curated dataset of synthetic causal primitives, designed to teach the model to propagate forces through time and space and to reason about cause-and-effect relationships. The dataset comprises three main scenarios:

- **Dominos (3,000 videos):** Blender-generated videos of domino lines. A direct force on one domino initiates a chain reaction, which is then mapped to a "goal force" on a subsequent domino in the chain. Parameters such as domino count, scene geometry, causality direction, and visuals are randomized for diversity.
- **Rolling Balls (6,000 videos):** Blender scenes featuring multiple balls. This set is divided into:
 - **Collision Set (4,500 videos):** A "projectile" ball is acted upon by an unseen force, aiming to collide with a "target" ball among "distractor" balls. Randomization includes ball count, colors, masses (1.0-4.0 kg), positions, camera, ground textures, and force calculation to ensure collision. Collisions are split between straight-on and indirect to teach diverse post-collision behaviors.
 - **Non-Collision Set (1,500 videos):** The projectile ball is aimed to miss the target ball, used to provide negative examples and refine causal understanding.
- **PhysDreamer Carnation (3,000 videos):** Videos of a flower swaying after being poked, generated using PhysDreamer (a method integrating 3D Gaussians and a physics simulator). This component introduces complex, non-rigid body dynamics from a direct force.

A key training strategy involves **randomly masking causal information**. For each training video, the model is provided with either the direct force (in Channel 0) or the goal force (in Channel 1), with the other channel zeroed out. This forces the model to learn two complementary reasoning paths:
- **Goal → Plan:** Given a goal force, the model must infer and generate the antecedent direct-force event (the cause).
- **Action → Outcome:** Given a direct force, the model must simulate the resulting collision and secondary force (the effect).
The mass channel (Channel 2) is also randomly masked, encouraging the model to use explicit mass information when available or rely on its internal learned physics prior when it is not. The text prompt provides semantic context but does not specify low-level causal plans, encouraging the model to leverage its internal prior for planning.

#### 3.3. Architecture and Training Details

The model is built upon Wan2.2, a Mixture-of-Experts diffusion model. A ControlNet module is integrated to condition the model on the physics control signal $\tilde{\pi}$. This ControlNet module is specifically fine-tuned for the high-noise expert within Wan2.2, as this expert is primarily responsible for global structure and low-frequency dynamics relevant to physics planning. The ControlNet clones and fine-tunes the first 10 DiT layers from the pretrained Wan2.2, feeding their outputs to the frozen base model via zero-convolutions. The goal force prompt is encoded using the frozen Wan2.2 encoder and processed through a randomly initialized patch embedding layer before entering the ControlNet. The model is fine-tuned for 3,000 steps with an effective batch size of 4, using videos of 81 frames at 16 frames per second.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical results demonstrating the Goal Force framework's capability in physics-aware video generation and planning.

#### 4.1. Comparison to Text-Only Baselines

To evaluate the effectiveness of the explicit physics control signal, Goal Force was compared against two text-only baselines: a zero-shot Wan2.2 model and a fine-tuned ControlNet architecture with a zeroed-out physics control signal (relying only on text suffixes). A new benchmark of 75 challenging scenes, categorized into two-object collisions, multi-object collisions, human-object interaction, and tool-object interaction, was used. A 2AFC (2-Alternative Forced Choice) human study with 40 participants evaluated videos on Goal Force Adherence, Realistic Motion, and Visual Quality.

- **Goal Force Adherence:** Goal Force significantly outperformed both text-only baselines across all four benchmark categories. For instance, in two-object collisions, it achieved 73.4% preference over zero-shot text and 66.9% over fine-tuned text. This indicates that text-only conditioning is insufficient for precise physical goal specification and that the explicit physics control signal is crucial.
- **Motion Realism and Visual Quality:** The superior goal force adherence was achieved with minimal degradation in motion realism and visual quality, suggesting that the integration of the physics control signal does not compromise the generative model's foundational capabilities.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Despite being trained solely on synthetic data involving simple balls, dominos, and a single flower, the model demonstrated generalization to complex, out-of-domain interactions such as tool usage (e.g., using a golf club to hit a ball) and human-object planning (e.g., picking up a rose by its stem).

#### 4.2. Comparison to Prior Methods

The Goal Force prompting task is novel, and existing force-conditioned methods (e.g., PhysGen, PhysDreamer, Force Prompting) are designed for simulating a direct force (the effect) rather than planning an antecedent action (the cause). Qualitative comparisons showed that these prior methods misinterpreted a goal force prompt as a direct, non-causal poke on the target object. Similarly, motion-conditioned models (e.g., ToRA) could follow trajectories but failed to adhere to causality. In contrast, Goal Force actively plans the preceding actions. The supplementary material also includes a quantitative comparison (Table 4) demonstrating that Goal Force's direct force prompting capability achieves superior visual quality and force adherence compared to these prior methods, without relying on physics simulators or 3D assets at inference.

#### 4.3. Goal Force Enables Visual Planning

The research demonstrated that Goal Force enables a form of visual planning, evaluated across accuracy, diversity, and the leverage of privileged physics information.

- **Visual Plans are Accurate:**
 - **Benchmark:** 22 scenes featuring "natural blockers" where distractor objects are physically constrained from initiating the goal force.
 - **Methodology:** 50 videos generated per scene, filtered for visual degradation. Accuracy defined as the percentage of valid trials where the goal force was initiated by the correct, unconstrained object.
 - **Results:** The model achieved high accuracy across most scenes (e.g., 97.96% for the pool scene, 100% for kitchen lemon). This indicates strong physical reasoning, as the model correctly identified and selected valid initiators while avoiding blocked paths. Failure cases primarily involved spontaneous target object movement rather than incorrect initiator selection.

- **Visual Plans are Diverse:**
 - **Task:** A 6-domino chain where the goal was to topple the rightmost domino. This task offered five possible preceding dominos to initiate the chain reaction.
 - **Metric:** A diversity metric $\delta(p)$ based on Jensen-Shannon Divergence was proposed, normalized to score from 0.39 (deterministic mode collapse) to 1.0 (perfect uniform diversity).
 - **Results:** Goal Force achieved a diversity score of 0.6577 across 26 random seeds, significantly higher than the deterministic baseline (0.3900). This demonstrates the model's ability to explore a multi-modal distribution of valid plans rather than collapsing to a single solution.

- **Visual Plans Leverage Privileged Physics (Mass):**
 - **Task:** A ball collision scenario with a fixed goal force magnitude, while varying the masses of the projectile and target balls.
 - **Expected Principles:** A physically grounded plan should account for mass, meaning a heavier target requires a faster projectile (for constant projectile mass), and a heavier projectile can move slower (for constant target mass) to achieve the same goal force.
 - **Measurement:** Faster R-CNN was used to detect ball positions, determine collision times, and compute projectile speeds.
 - **Results:** In an in-distribution scenario, the projectile's speeds satisfied all four expected speed-mass relationships. In an out-of-distribution scenario (different background, viewpoint, lighting, ball size), three out of four relationships were satisfied, with the fourth being close. This indicates that the model effectively leverages explicit mass information provided in the control signal to guide its visual planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Goal Force framework represents an advancement in controllable video generation and machine learning for physical reasoning. By shifting the paradigm from specifying a direct force (the cause) to declaring a desired goal force (the effect), the research introduces a more intuitive and powerful method for interacting with generative world models.

A key significance of this work is its demonstration that a video model, trained on relatively simple synthetic causal primitives, can learn to function as an implicit neural physics planner. This capability enables the model to reason backward from a user-defined goal and synthesize a physically plausible, antecedent causal chain to achieve that goal, without relying on an external physics engine during inference. This constitutes a step towards more autonomous and intelligent generative systems that can not only simulate but also plan.

The observed strong zero-shot generalization to complex, out-of-domain scenarios, including tool manipulation, human-object interactions, and intricate multi-object collisions, suggests that the model is not merely memorizing patterns but has acquired an understanding of fundamental physical interactions. This indicates that grounding video generation in basic physical principles can foster sophisticated reasoning capabilities.

The potential impact of Goal Force is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced World Models:** It contributes to the development of more capable and interactive "world models" that can engage in precise, physics-aware planning. This can move beyond simple "what if" simulations to "how to" problem-solving.
- **Robotics and Automation:** The ability to specify goals via desired physical outcomes could simplify task specification for robotic systems, allowing users to define tasks at a higher, more intuitive level of abstraction. Robots could then use these models to generate visual plans for complex manipulation tasks involving causal chains.
- **Content Creation and Simulation:** For content creators, this framework could offer fine-grained control over physical dynamics in generated videos, enabling the creation of intricate and physically consistent scenes without manual animation or explicit physics simulations.
- **Fundamental AI Research:** The success of training an implicit neural physics simulator from synthetic data supports the ongoing research into how neural networks can learn to represent and reason about the physical world, contributing to the development of more intelligent and general-purpose AI.

In summary, Goal Force introduces a novel and effective method for controlling generative video models, moving towards systems that can understand and plan physical interactions in a manner more aligned with human intuition, thereby expanding the potential applications of generative AI in complex physical environments.
