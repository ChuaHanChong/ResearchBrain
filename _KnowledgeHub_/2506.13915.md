# Sequence Modeling for Time-Optimal Quadrotor Trajectory Optimization with Sampling-based Robustness Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> This research develops a learning-based method for time-optimal quadrotor trajectory optimization, enabling real-time generation of dynamically feasible trajectories. The approach achieves significant computational speedups while maintaining high fidelity to full quadrotor dynamics, demonstrated through both simulation and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Generating time-optimal quadrotor trajectories is computationally intensive, often taking seconds to hours, making it unsuitable for real-time applications.
- Optimization problems for time-optimal flight are non-convex due to obstacle avoidance and non-linear quadrotor dynamics.
- Existing methods either simplify dynamics for speed (compromising performance) or use full dynamics (incurring high computational costs).

## Method

- An ==imitation learning== framework (sequence-to-sequence neural network) is trained to mimic a model-based time-optimal planner (**TOPPQuad**) using collision-free geometric paths as input.
- The model predicts a minimal set of output variables: the squared-speed profile and the cosine of the yaw angle, from which the full quadrotor trajectory is reconstructed.
- A quantitative robustness analysis framework using ==Backward Reachable Tubes (BRT)== and a data augmentation scheme with noise injection are employed to enhance model reliability and dynamic feasibility.

## Results

- The **LSTM Encoder-Decoder** achieved a computational speedup of two orders of magnitude (**0.096s** vs. **10.656s** for TOPPQuad) with minimal position deviation (**0.074m**) and a low failure rate (**4.0%**).
- Noise-augmented models (e.g., LSTM-0.1) demonstrated significantly higher in-BRT probabilities (up to **92.9%** at ϵ=0.1 perturbation) compared to models trained on clean data, indicating enhanced robustness.
- Hardware validation on a CrazyFlie 2.0 showed comparable position deviations (**0.355m** vs. **0.347m** for TOPPQuad) and generalization to unseen path lengths and geometries in real-time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a minimal set of output variables (squared-speed and yaw cosine) is effective for reconstructing complex, dynamically-feasible trajectories, avoiding direct learning of highly coupled control or state variables.
> - Data augmentation via noise injection during training significantly improves the model's robustness against input path perturbations, making it more resilient to real-world uncertainties.
> - The LSTM Encoder-Decoder architecture proved most effective for this sequence-to-sequence imitation task, balancing tracking accuracy, dynamic feasibility, and computational efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of optimal trajectory generation is a critical component for enabling agile micro aerial vehicles (MAVs) to operate efficiently and safely in various applications, including search and rescue, disaster response, and package delivery. A key challenge in this domain is the computational cost associated with generating time-optimal trajectories, which require driving quadrotors to their dynamic limits. These optimization problems are inherently non-convex due to obstacle presence and the nonlinear nature of robot dynamics.

Previous approaches to quadrotor trajectory planning generally fall into several categories:
- **Simplified Dynamics or Proxy Constraints:** Many methods optimize trajectories using simplified dynamics models or faithful dynamics with proxy actuation constraints to reduce computational complexity. While faster, these may not fully exploit the quadrotor's flight envelope or guarantee strict dynamic feasibility.
- **Polynomial Structures:** Approaches popularized by Mellinger and Kumar use polynomial structures, often leveraging the quadrotor’s differential flatness property. These can offer faster computation for smooth trajectories but may not achieve the "bang-bang" behavior characteristic of aggressive, time-optimal flight. Learning-based methods have been applied to aspects of polynomial planning, such as time allocation for waypoints.
- **Full Dynamics Optimization:** A smaller subset of approaches utilizes faithful dynamics models and suitable actuation constraints. These methods generate trajectories with superior mission execution times but incur significantly higher computational costs, ranging from seconds to hours.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** Some works employ reinforcement learning to generate near-time-optimal trajectories, particularly for drone racing. However, these often prioritize progress and may deviate from nominal paths, potentially compromising safety in cluttered environments.

This paper addresses the computational challenge of time-optimal trajectory generation by introducing a learning-based approach. Specifically, it focuses on time-optimal path parameterization for quadrotors that explicitly incorporates faithful dynamics and actuation constraints, a problem that has historically been computationally intensive when solved with model-based optimization. The authors state this is the first work to develop a learning-based algorithm for computationally efficient time-optimal path parameterization under these specific conditions. The work aims to bridge the gap between the computational efficiency of learning-based methods and the dynamic fidelity of optimization-based planners.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the necessity for quadrotors to perform tasks rapidly and safely in real-world applications. Minimizing task completion time is crucial not only due to the inherent battery life limitations of MAVs but also to fully leverage their agility and operational capabilities. The existing algorithmic challenge lies in the non-convex nature of the optimization problems required to synthesize time-optimal trajectories. These non-convexities arise from obstacle avoidance constraints and the nonlinear dynamics of the quadrotor. While previous model-based approaches, such as TOPPQuad, can generate dynamically feasible, time-optimal trajectories, they are computationally intensive, often requiring iterative nonlinear optimization that makes them unsuitable for real-time applications.

Given this context, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Accelerate Time-Optimal Trajectory Generation:** To develop a learning-based model capable of imitating a model-based time-optimal trajectory planner (specifically TOPPQuad) to significantly reduce computation time, thereby enabling real-time applications. This involves learning the complex patterns underlying time-optimal trajectories from a dataset.
2. **Develop a Minimal and Effective Input-Output Feature Design:** To formulate an imitation learning framework that maps collision-free geometric paths to a minimal set of output variables necessary to reconstruct the full time-optimal trajectory (squared-speed profile and cosine of yaw angle). This design aims to mitigate overfitting and promote robustness by avoiding direct learning of tightly coupled, high-dimensional, and non-linear variables like motor thrusts or quaternions.
3. **Establish a Robustness Analysis Framework:** To introduce a quantitative framework for analyzing the local analytic properties of the learned models. This framework connects the predicted trajectories to the Backward Reachable Tube (BRT) of a geometric tracking controller via a sampling-based approach, providing a formal measure of dynamic feasibility under tracking errors and input perturbations.
4. **Enhance Model Robustness through Data Augmentation:** To propose and evaluate a data augmentation scheme that applies random perturbations to input paths during training. This strategy aims to improve the learned model's ability to generalize and maintain robustness when faced with minor deviations in input paths.
5. **Conduct Comprehensive Architectural Evaluation:** To perform an ablation study across various neural network architectures (e.g., LSTM Encoder-Decoder, Transformer variants, MLP) to identify the most effective model for this specific sequence-to-sequence trajectory imitation task.
6. **Validate Real-time Feasibility and Generalization on Hardware:** To demonstrate the practical applicability of the learning-based planner on a physical quadrotor platform, verifying its real-time performance, its ability to generalize to previously unseen path geometries and lengths, and its adherence to dynamic constraints.

These objectives collectively aim to develop a computationally efficient, robust, and dynamically faithful approach to time-optimal quadrotor trajectory planning, addressing limitations of both purely optimization-based and prior learning-based methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on an imitation learning framework designed to accelerate time-optimal quadrotor trajectory generation by mimicking a model-based planner, TOPPQuad. The approach comprises a specialized problem formulation, a rigorous robustness analysis, and a data augmentation strategy.

#### 3.1. Imitation Learning Problem Formulation

The core of the method is a "sequence-to-sequence" imitation learning model.
- **Input:** The model receives a discretized collision-free geometric path μ(·), augmented with its first (μ′(·)) and second (μ′′(·)) derivatives. These derivatives provide explicit geometric information, such as curvature, which is critical for defining the dynamics.
- **Output:** The model predicts a minimal set of variables: the squared-speed profile h(·) and the cosine of the yaw angle cos θ_z(·).
 - **Rationale for Outputs:** This choice of output variables is based on domain-specific insights. The variables h(·) and cos θ_z(·) are identified as the minimal necessary to reconstruct the full time-optimal trajectory while avoiding the complexities of directly learning tightly coupled non-linear variables like motor thrusts (u(·)) or quaternions (q(·)). Using cos θ_z(·) instead of θ_z(·) addresses the many-to-one mapping and wraparound issues associated with yaw angles.
- **Trajectory Recovery:** After prediction, the full quadrotor state trajectory is recovered from h(·) and cos θ_z(·).
 - **Translational Components:** Position p(t) is directly the geometric path γ(·). Velocity v(t) and acceleration a(t) are derived using h(·), its derivative h′(·) (obtained via finite differences), and the path derivatives γ′(·) and γ′′(·).
 - **Rotational Components:** The quaternion q is constructed by first computing the body z-axis vector b_3. This is done by adding gravity to the derived acceleration and normalizing, ensuring b_3 aligns with the net thrust vector. Then, q is derived by composing rotations to align the drone's body-z axis with b_3 and setting the yaw to the desired θ_z. The angular velocity ω and its derivative ω′ are subsequently calculated from the change in rotation between steps.
 - **Control Inputs:** Finally, a low-level geometric controller computes the per-motor thrusts u(·) from these derived states. Unlike TOPPQuad, where u(·) is part of the optimization, here it is recovered, and its adherence to actuation constraints is verified.

#### 3.2. Robustness Analysis

To ensure the practical applicability and reliability of the learned models, a quantitative robustness analysis framework is introduced.
- **Backward Reachable Tube (BRT):** The concept of finite-time Backward Reachable Tube (BRT) is used to formalize robustness. A BRT for a target state x and time ∆t is the set of initial states from which x can be reached within ∆t using a given controller U.
 - **Dynamic Feasibility (Proposition 4.1):** The planner is considered robust if, for each step, the actual simulated state (ˆr(s_i)) is within the BRT of the planned next state (r(s_{i+1})) under the low-level controller U. This ensures recoverability of planned states despite tracking errors.
 - **Geometric Path Tracking (Proposition 4.2):** An extension states that if, in addition to dynamic feasibility, the robot's simulated position exactly matches the geometric path at each step, the planner is robust for tracking the geometric path while respecting dynamic feasibility.
- **Sampling-based BRT Approximation:** Due to the complexity of deriving an analytical BRT for a non-linear, underactuated quadrotor, a sampling-based approach is employed. This involves simulating the low-level controller from sampled initial states (ˆr(s_i)) to verify if they can reach target states (r(s_{i+1})) within the allocated time (t_i). The in-BRT probability is estimated from these simulations.
- **Robustness against Perturbations (Proposition 4.3):** To assess robustness against input variations, the concept of ϵ-robustness is introduced. This measures the planner's robustness when the input geometric path μ(·) is perturbed by at most ϵ at each discrete step. The analysis involves sampling perturbed paths and simulating their tracking performance.
- **Robustness Enhancement via Noise Injection:** Inspired by the perturbation analysis, a data augmentation scheme is proposed. During training, the dataset is augmented with randomly perturbed paths (ˆμ ∈ Ã_ϵ(μ)). The model is trained to predict the same ground-truth [h(·), cos θ_z(·)] for these perturbed inputs as for the original path μ(·). This strategy relies on Assumption 4.4, which posits that the optimal control sequence for μ(·) remains ϵ-robust for ˆμ. The perturbation scale ϵ is treated as a tunable hyperparameter.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results are presented through simulation and hardware validation, assessing architectural performance, robustness, baseline comparisons, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

A dataset of 10,000 trajectories was generated using TOPPQuad with a 5m/s speed limit, discretized into 100 points. All simulations used RotorPy with CrazyFlie 2.0 parameters. Evaluation metrics included maximum position deviation, average thrust violation, Time Difference (TD) ratio (relative to TOPPQuad), failure rate (crash or >1m deviation), and compute time.

#### 4.2. Architecture Ablation

Four neural architectures were evaluated: LSTM Encoder-Decoder, Transformer Encoder-Decoder, Encoder-Only Transformer (ETransformer), and Per-Step MLP.
- **LSTM Encoder-Decoder:** Demonstrated the best overall performance, achieving a maximum position deviation of 0.074m (compared to TOPPQuad's 0.053m), negligible thrust violation (0.009N), a near-zero failure rate (4.0%), and a substantial speedup (0.096s compute time versus 10.656s for TOPPQuad). It also showed a TD ratio of -0.40%, indicating slightly faster (or near-optimal) travel times.
- **Other Architectures:** The Transformer exhibited worse tracking accuracy and higher failure rates, attributed to difficulties with teacher forcing and limited data. ETransformer provided competitive results but still lagged behind the LSTM. The Per-Step MLP performed poorly in tracking and incurred high thrust violations, struggling with out-of-distribution issues due to its autoregressive nature.

#### 4.3. Robustness Analysis

The LSTM model was further analyzed for robustness, comparing a version trained on clean data with two variants (LSTM-0.01, LSTM-0.1) trained with noise injection (perturbation scales ϵ = 0.01 and ϵ = 0.1, respectively).
- **Clean Data Model:** The LSTM model trained solely on clean data was highly sensitive to input perturbations, showing increased maximum position deviations (e.g., from 0.143m to 0.234m under ϵ=0.001 perturbation) and reduced in-BRT probabilities, indicating a higher likelihood of dynamically infeasible predictions.
- **Noise-Augmented Models (LSTM-0.01, LSTM-0.1):** These variants exhibited improved output stability and significantly higher in-BRT probabilities, particularly LSTM-0.1, which maintained robust performance across all tested perturbation levels (up to ϵ=0.1). For example, LSTM-0.1 showed a maximum deviation of 0.127m and an in-BRT probability of 92.9% even at ϵ=0.1, compared to the clean model's 0.739m deviation and 70.0% in-BRT probability. A trade-off was observed, where increased robustness from noise augmentation could lead to a slight reduction in time-optimality and tracking accuracy in low-perturbation regimes.

#### 4.4. Baseline Comparison

The proposed method was compared against two other learning-based baselines: Time Allocation Network (AllocNet) and Multi-Fidelity Black-Box Optimization Trajectory Planner (MFBOTrajectory).
- **LSTM:** Maintained good tracking performance (0.124m max deviation), low thrust violation (0.002N), competitive travel time (5.653s), and very fast compute time (0.069s).
- **AllocNet:** Suffered from a high failure rate (28%), often finding shorter geometric paths but with a lower average speed than the LSTM. Compute time was 0.277s.
- **MFBOTrajectory:** Achieved zero failures but produced the slowest average speed, leading to longer travel times despite shorter polynomial paths. Critically, it required extensive online retraining for new waypoint arrangements, resulting in a significantly higher compute time (13609.913s).
- **Key Distinction:** The LSTM, by imitating TOPPQuad, adheres to full quadrotor dynamics and leverages its complete flight profile, unlike the baselines which primarily use polynomial structures and may not fully utilize the quadrotor's dynamic capabilities.

#### 4.5. Hardware Experiments

The approach was validated on a CrazyFlie 2.0 quadrotor within a Vicon motion capture space. To ensure safety, maximum speed, acceleration, and angular velocity limits were reduced, and a new dataset of 9,000 trajectories was generated.
- **Performance:** The learning-based method achieved position deviations comparable to TOPPQuad (0.355m vs. 0.347m).
- **Travel Time:** A slight increase in travel time difference ratio was observed (8.355s vs. 7.981s for TOPPQuad), attributed to the sim-to-real gap and challenges in precise quadrotor settling.
- **Generalization:** The method demonstrated generalization to path lengths exceeding those in the training data by predicting trajectory segments and conditioning on the robot's state at each segment's start. This modular approach enabled substantial compute speedups for longer sequences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of agile quadrotor autonomy by addressing the long-standing computational bottleneck of generating time-optimal trajectories. The key significance and potential impacts include:

- **Real-time Trajectory Generation:** The demonstrated ability to generate near-time-optimal trajectories with substantial speedups (orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based methods like TOPPQuad) is crucial for real-time applications. This enables quadrotors to react more swiftly and execute aggressive maneuvers in dynamic or time-sensitive environments, such as search and rescue, surveillance, or package delivery.
- **Preservation of Dynamic Fidelity:** Unlike many prior learning-based methods that rely on simplified dynamics or polynomial path structures, this approach successfully imitates a model-based planner that considers full quadrotor dynamics and actuation constraints. This ensures that the generated trajectories are not only fast but also dynamically feasible, which is critical for safe and robust operation near the vehicle's physical limits.
- **Enhanced Robustness through Formal Analysis and Data Augmentation:** The introduction of a quantitative robustness analysis framework, based on Backward Reachable Tubes, provides a formal method to evaluate the dynamic feasibility and tracking performance of learned models under errors and perturbations. The proposed data augmentation strategy, using noise injection during training, significantly enhances the model's resilience to input variations, making it more reliable in real-world scenarios where sensor noise or environmental uncertainties are prevalent.
- **Hardware Validation and Generalization:** The successful validation on a physical quadrotor platform demonstrates the practical viability of the approach beyond simulation. The ability of the learned models to generalize to unseen path geometries and lengths is a critical feature, indicating that they can operate effectively in diverse environments without requiring exhaustive retraining for every new task.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for integrating learning-based methods with sophisticated model-based planners, potentially leading to hybrid approaches that combine the guarantees of formal methods with the computational efficiency of machine learning.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges several limitations, including the necessity to retrain the model if the quadrotor's configuration or dynamic constraints change. Additionally, the approximate nature of learning-based methods means that dynamically infeasible path parameterizations might still occasionally be produced. Finally, the determination of optimal perturbation bounds for data augmentation remains an open research question.

Despite these limitations, this research contributes a practical and effective method for enabling agile, time-optimal quadrotor flight, offering a pathway toward more autonomous and high-performance MAV systems.
