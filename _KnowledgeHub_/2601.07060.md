# PALM: Progress-Aware Policy Learning via Affordance Reasoning for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The PALM framework enables robust long-horizon robotic manipulation by integrating structured affordance reasoning with continuous progress estimation. It achieved a 17.7% absolute improvement in task success rate on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark for five consecutive subtasks and demonstrated effective generalization across real-world scenarios with dynamic changes.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with long-horizon, multi-step robotic tasks in dynamic environments, often failing mid-task.
- VLA policies lack internal reasoning mechanisms to identify task-relevant interaction cues such as target objects, interaction regions, or appropriate motion.
- Existing methods lack explicit, continuous state and progress tracking, leading to temporal ambiguity and inefficient or incorrect task completion.

## Method

- **PALM** introduces an end-to-end VLA framework built on a ==multi-modal transformer== and a ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== for scalable policy learning.
- It employs a fine-grained ==affordance predictor== that anticipates future interaction cues by estimating an affordance-centric latent capturing object relevance, contact geometry, and motion dynamics.
- The system incorporates a ==progress-aware policy== that jointly decodes robot actions alongside a continuous scalar value quantifying within-subtask completion, stabilizing long-horizon control.

## Results

- On the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, PALM achieved an **82.0%** success rate for five consecutive subtasks, an absolute improvement of **17.7%** over the best prior baseline, and the longest average task trajectory of **4.48**.
- PALM demonstrated an average success rate of **94.5%** across LIBERO suites, including **91.8%** on the challenging LIBERO-LONG, outperforming the CoT-VLA baseline by **22.8%**.
- In real-world experiments, PALM showed enhanced robustness to random localization, visual distractions, and unseen lighting, achieving significantly higher average task lengths (e.g., 3.05 vs. 0.95 for OpenVLA in random localization) with a UFACTORY xArm6 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured affordance reasoning, encompassing global, local, spatial, and dynamic predictions, provides effective intermediate representations for guiding precise actions in complex manipulation tasks.
> - Integrating a continuous progress signal into policy learning mitigates phase ambiguity, enables smoother subtask transitions, and enhances temporal consistency in long-horizon operations.
> - A two-stage training strategy, combining large-scale pre-training on diverse datasets with fine-tuning on human-annotated, affordance-labeled data, is crucial for developing robust and generalizable robotic policies.
