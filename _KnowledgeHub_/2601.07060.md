# PALM: Progress-Aware Policy Learning via Affordance Reasoning for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The PALM framework enables robust long-horizon robotic manipulation by integrating structured affordance reasoning with continuous progress estimation. It achieved a 17.7% absolute improvement in task success rate on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark for five consecutive subtasks and demonstrated effective generalization across real-world scenarios with dynamic changes.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with long-horizon, multi-step robotic tasks in dynamic environments, often failing mid-task.
- VLA policies lack internal reasoning mechanisms to identify task-relevant interaction cues such as target objects, interaction regions, or appropriate motion.
- Existing methods lack explicit, continuous state and progress tracking, leading to temporal ambiguity and inefficient or incorrect task completion.

## Method

- **PALM** introduces an end-to-end VLA framework built on a ==multi-modal transformer== and a ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== for scalable policy learning.
- It employs a fine-grained ==affordance predictor== that anticipates future interaction cues by estimating an affordance-centric latent capturing object relevance, contact geometry, and motion dynamics.
- The system incorporates a ==progress-aware policy== that jointly decodes robot actions alongside a continuous scalar value quantifying within-subtask completion, stabilizing long-horizon control.

## Results

- On the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, PALM achieved an **82.0%** success rate for five consecutive subtasks, an absolute improvement of **17.7%** over the best prior baseline, and the longest average task trajectory of **4.48**.
- PALM demonstrated an average success rate of **94.5%** across LIBERO suites, including **91.8%** on the challenging LIBERO-LONG, outperforming the CoT-VLA baseline by **22.8%**.
- In real-world experiments, PALM showed enhanced robustness to random localization, visual distractions, and unseen lighting, achieving significantly higher average task lengths (e.g., 3.05 vs. 0.95 for OpenVLA in random localization) with a UFACTORY xArm6 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured affordance reasoning, encompassing global, local, spatial, and dynamic predictions, provides effective intermediate representations for guiding precise actions in complex manipulation tasks.
> - Integrating a continuous progress signal into policy learning mitigates phase ambiguity, enables smoother subtask transitions, and enhances temporal consistency in long-horizon operations.
> - A two-stage training strategy, combining large-scale pre-training on diverse datasets with fine-tuning on human-annotated, affordance-labeled data, is crucial for developing robust and generalizable robotic policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements driven by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which utilize pre-trained vision-language backbones to map visual observations and language instructions to robot actions. While these models have demonstrated capabilities in various tasks, they generally exhibit limitations when applied to long-horizon, multi-step manipulation in dynamic environments. Existing VLA methods frequently encounter execution errors such as repeated actions, missed steps, or premature termination.

A foundational challenge in current VLA approaches is the absence of internal reasoning mechanisms that can effectively identify task-relevant interaction cues or track progress within a subtask. Models often lack explicit representations to disambiguate which object to target, which specific region is relevant for interaction, where items should be placed, or what motion is appropriate for an upcoming step. This deficiency can lead to ambiguity in visually similar states, obscuring the robot's understanding of the current task stage and destabilizing long-horizon control. Additionally, without a continuous estimate of progress, policies struggle to reliably determine whether to proceed, transition between subtasks, or terminate execution.

PALM addresses these identified limitations by introducing a VLA framework that integrates structured affordance reasoning and continuous subtask progress cues into policy learning. It aims to provide the internal representations and temporal grounding that current methods lack, thereby enabling more stable and coherent behavior in complex, multi-step manipulation sequences. This work contributes to the development of more generalizable and robust robotic policies for tasks requiring extended planning and execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop PALM, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework designed to overcome the challenges associated with long-horizon, multi-step robotic manipulation tasks. The framework aims to enable scalable and stable execution by integrating advanced reasoning mechanisms.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Introduce Internal Reasoning Mechanisms:** A core motivation stems from the observation that existing VLA models often function as direct mapping systems from inputs to actions, lacking explicit internal representations for scene understanding relevant to robotic interaction. PALM aims to integrate mechanisms that identify task-relevant interaction cues, such as which objects are salient, which parts of an object are graspable, or what regions are suitable for placement. This is motivated by the need to reduce ambiguity in complex scenes and guide the robot's decision-making process more effectively.
2. **Enable Continuous Subtask Progress Tracking:** Current VLA policies frequently fail to maintain a coherent understanding of their advancement within a multi-step task. This absence of continuous progress estimation means they cannot reliably determine if a subtask is complete, if they should transition to the next stage, or if they should terminate. This often leads to characteristic long-horizon failure modes, including repeating already completed actions, skipping necessary steps, or terminating prematurely. PALM is motivated to develop a mechanism that provides a continuous, fine-grained progress signal, allowing for better temporal grounding and seamless transitions between subtasks.
3. **Mitigate Common Execution Errors:** The lack of internal reasoning and progress awareness in current systems leads to critical execution errors in long-horizon tasks. Examples include performing redundant actions, failing to execute all required steps, or incorrectly concluding a task. By providing structured affordance representations and a progress-aware policy, PALM seeks to inherently address these issues, promoting more efficient and accurate task completion.
4. **Structure Policy Learning Around Interaction-Centric Affordance Reasoning:** The research is motivated by the hypothesis that explicit modeling of affordances—the potential actions an agent can perform on an object or environment—can serve as a powerful intermediate representation for visuomotor control. PALM aims to distill complementary affordance representations that capture object relevance (global), contact geometry (local), spatial placements (spatial), and motion dynamics (dynamic). These representations are intended to act as task-relevant anchors, stabilizing perception and enabling more precise control.
5. **Develop a Progress-Aware Inverse-Dynamics Policy:** To further stabilize long-horizon execution, the framework is designed to predict continuous within-subtask progress. This is integrated into an inverse-dynamics formulation, where the policy jointly decodes both the robot’s action and a continuous progress value. This coupling is motivated by the goal of making actions temporally consistent and aligned with the evolving task state, thereby enabling robust subtask transitions without relying on external planners or hierarchical controllers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PALM's methodology integrates perception, action, and progress within a closed loop, addressing long-horizon manipulation through two primary, tightly coupled components: fine-grained affordance prediction and a progress-aware policy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The robot operates across a distribution of tasks, each defining an observation-action distribution. The challenge arises from cumulative control errors and varying environmental conditions (background, lighting, objects), which alter the observation space and destabilize transitions between subtasks if policies are trained in isolation. Traditional behavior cloning, which directly maps observations to actions, collapses these distinct task phases. PALM introduces an affordance-centric latent (F̂_t+n) at future offsets t+n to stabilize perception, and a progress-aware policy that jointly decodes action (a_t) and a continuous progress scalar (p_t ∈) to mitigate phase ambiguity and enable smooth transitions.

#### 3.2. PALM Architecture

The architecture of PALM comprises multi-modal encoders, a transformer backbone, specialized learnable queries, and decoders for both affordances and actions.

- **Multi-Modal Encoders:**
 - **Language Instruction (l):** Processed by a CLIP text encoder, producing a fixed-length semantic embedding.
 - **Image Observation (o_t):** Encoded using a MAE-pretrained ViT-B backbone, generating patch embeddings. A Perceiver Resampler then distills these into a compact, fixed-size set of task-relevant visual embeddings.
 - **Robot State (s_t):** Composed of the 6-DoF Cartesian pose of the end-effector and a one-hot encoded gripper status. These are projected through separate MLPs and concatenated to form a unified state token.
 - All resulting tokens are concatenated with a time index to form a multi-modal sequence.

- **Backbone:** A GPT-2-style transformer integrates the multi-modal token sequence using causal and cross-modal attention, fusing diverse inputs into a coherent latent representation of the world state.

- **Learnable Queries:** Two sets of learnable query tokens are introduced:
 - **Fine-grained affordance queries:** Composed of four sub-queries (<Global>, <Local>, <Spatial>, <Dynamic>) that attend over language, vision, and state tokens. They extract task-relevant representations at complementary scales, producing a disentangled, affordance-centric latent (F̂_t+n).
 - **Action–progress queries:** These queries gather control-relevant context and explicitly condition on F̂_t+n. They facilitate a progress-aware inverse-dynamics formulation, enabling the inference of action sequences that align with the forecasted interaction state.

- **Decoders:**
 - **Affordance Decoders:** After a shared encoding process (with 2D sine-cosine positional encodings), modality-specific heads decode the four affordance latents. <Global> and <Spatial> use lightweight 2-layer MLP heads for object mask and candidate placement points. <Local> and <Dynamic> employ 2-layer Transformer blocks followed by linear projections for contact heatmaps and dynamic regions. These are supervised at future offset t+n.
 - **Action–Progress Decoder:** A Denoising Diffusion Transformer (DiT) serves as the action head. It conditions on the latent embedding from the action–progress queries (which already integrates affordance information) to jointly generate a horizon-n trajectory, comprising 7-DoF actions and scalar progress values (â_t:t+n-1, p̂_t:t+n-1).

#### 3.3. Fine-Grained Affordance Prediction

PALM predicts four types of future affordances at time t+n, forming a structured, interaction-centric representation:

- **Global Prediction:** Identifies the instruction-relevant object and its approximate region. The referent object is localized via Grounding DINO and segmented by SAM to create a binary mask. An object representation is extracted by masked pooling. The training objective (L_global) uses pixel-wise focal loss (L_FL) and soft Dice loss (L_Dice) to supervise the predicted future mask.
- **Local Prediction:** Captures fine-grained geometric cues for precise contact. Conditioned on the global prior, it predicts a dense contact-likelihood distribution over the object. Ground truth consists of Gaussian heatmaps derived from annotated contact points. The training objective (L_local) combines focal loss (L_FL) and KL divergence (L_KL) for the normalized heatmap.
- **Spatial Prediction:** Converts abstract spatial language into executable placement proposals. It predicts a set of candidate 2D placement points. Ground-truth targets are obtained by converting instructions to spatial semantics using SpatialVLM and sampling coordinates with RoboPoint. The training objective (L_spatial) is a set-matching loss, minimizing the L2 distance between predicted and target points.
- **Dynamic Prediction:** Identifies pixels corresponding to the robot gripper and movable objects, predicting their evolution. Supervision is constructed by placing a grid of query points on an initial frame, tracking them with CoTracker, and rasterizing cumulative displacements into a dynamic region mask at t+n. The training objective (L_dynamic) is a masked reconstruction loss using a latent-variable model, encouraging calibrated per-pixel probabilities.

#### 3.4. Progress-Aware Policy via Inverse Dynamics

To track within-subtask advancement, PALM incorporates a continuous progress estimation. The model infers the active subtask stage from the affordance latent and derives a stage embedding. A scalar p_t ∈ is predicted, quantifying within-stage completion and appended to the action output, so the policy jointly predicts (a_t, p_t). This explicit progress signal reduces ambiguity, stabilizes learning, and smooths transitions.

The action-progress generation is formulated as a conditional denoising task using a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). The DiT conditions on the current observation (o_t), instruction (l), robot state (s_t), and the predicted affordance latent (F̂_t+n) to generate an n-step action–progress sequence (â_t:t+n-1, p̂_t:t+n-1). Training employs the standard diffusion objective (L_DiT) to denoise the target action–progress vector.

#### 3.5. Training Details

- **Pre-training:** Uses a mixed dataset:
 - Robot data: DROID and BridgeData V2 for foundational understanding of diverse real-world tasks.
 - Long-horizon video data: EPIC-KITCHENS and RoboCerebra for semantic progress estimation in complex, multi-step scenarios, where the model predicts entire frames.
- **Fine-tuning:** Involves 942 human-annotated robot trajectories with affordance data and continuous progress labels (semi-automated). The model is fine-tuned to learn conditional affordance foresight for inverse dynamics, outputting action-progress pairs.
- **Progress Labels:** Functional keyframes (e.g., Grasp-Contact, Release) are manually annotated as semantic progress anchors, with intermediate frames linearly interpolated. This creates a unified scale across different datasets for consistent progress tracking.
- **Hyperparameters:** Training is performed on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Pre-training uses a batch size of 80/GPU and a learning rate of 1e-4 for 30 epochs. Fine-tuning uses a batch size of 64/GPU and a learning rate of 1e-3 for 40 epochs. AdamW optimizer with cosine decay and weight decay of 1e-4 are used. The pre-trained visual and text encoders are frozen. The model has 68 million trainable parameters.

#### 3.6. Policy Roll-out Details

For inference, the DiT employs 10 diffusion steps, processes 7 observation steps, and predicts 3 future steps. This configuration yields a sampling time of approximately 40 ms. The use of fine-grained affordances for conditional visual foresight bypasses explicit image decoding. This enables a closed-loop frequency of 10-15 Hz, with each decision cycle taking less than 80 ms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PALM included extensive simulation and real-world experiments, along with ablation studies to assess the contribution of its components.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

PALM was evaluated on two long-horizon simulation benchmarks: CALVIN ABC→D and LIBERO.

- **CALVIN ABC→D Benchmark:**
 - PALM achieved a 96.9% success rate on the first subtask and maintained 82.0% success for sequences of five consecutive subtasks. This represents a 17.7% absolute improvement over the previous state-of-the-art baseline, Seer, at the 5-task horizon.
 - PALM yielded an average task trajectory length of 4.48, which exceeded Seer (3.98) and π0 (3.92), indicating more stable execution over extended sequences.
 - An ablation study showed that removing the progress prediction component ("PALM ✗ progress") reduced the average length from 4.48 to 4.02, indicating the importance of the progress-aware policy for long-horizon generalization.

- **LIBERO Benchmark:**
 - PALM achieved an average success rate of 94.5% across the four LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long).
 - The most significant improvement was observed in LIBERO-LONG tasks, where PALM achieved 91.8% success, outperforming the strongest baseline, CoT-VLA (69.0%), by 22.8%.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to quantify the contribution of PALM's individual components and training data.

- **Affordance Components:** Cumulatively adding affordance types to a vanilla VLA model showed improvements:
 - Adding Global (G) affordance yielded consistent improvements on both CALVIN and LIBERO-LONG.
 - Local (L) affordance provided additional gains on CALVIN but a slight decrease on LIBERO-LONG, potentially due to viewpoint-induced geometric bias.
 - Spatial (S) affordance restored and further improved performance, providing robust placement priors.
 - The full set, including Dynamic (D) affordance (PALM), consistently achieved the best performance, indicating that combining motion cues with structured spatial reasoning is effective for long-horizon behavior.
- **PALM Modules:**
 - Removing affordance foresight resulted in the largest performance drop during fine-tuning (average length decreased from 4.48 to 3.58), indicating its importance for precise long-horizon planning.
 - Removing progress prediction led to the largest degradation during pre-training (average length decreased from 4.48 to 3.73), highlighting the value of large-scale long-horizon datasets for learning a robust progress prior.
 - Eliminating inverse dynamics prediction also reduced performance, demonstrating the contribution of predicting multi-step action trajectories conditioned on affordance latents.
- **Training Data Composition:** Excluding any of the four data types (In-the-Wild, Long-Horizon Video, Human-Annotated, Simulation Pre-training) invariably degraded model performance, with the most pronounced decreases observed when In-the-Wild and Human-Annotated datasets were removed.
- **Progress Threshold (ϕ):** An ablation on the threshold for subtask completion (ϕ) on CALVIN ABC→D showed that a 90% threshold yielded the highest average sequence length (4.48). Lower thresholds (e.g., 70%) led to premature transitions and degraded performance, while a 100% threshold caused a slight decline, indicating that strict saturation requirements can hinder task completion.
- **Prediction vs. Reconstruction:** Comparing different foresight targets, affordance foresight achieved the highest average length (4.48) with moderate inference latency (∼70 ms). Image/video foresight yielded lower performance (4.17) at higher latency (∼90 ms), while auxiliary reconstruction was fastest (∼55 ms) but led to the largest performance degradation (3.58). This suggests that structured, action-centric affordance prediction offers a balanced trade-off.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

Real-world experiments were conducted using a UFACTORY xArm6 robot on a 6-subtask sequential pick-and-place task, evaluating generalization under various conditions.

- **Generalization Evaluation:** PALM demonstrated superior generalization compared to baselines (OpenVLA and Octo) across three challenging settings:
 - **Random Localization:** PALM achieved an average length of 3.05, significantly outperforming OpenVLA (0.95) and Octo (0.65).
 - **Visual Distraction:** PALM maintained an average length of 3.80, while OpenVLA and Octo achieved 1.60 and 0.95, respectively.
 - **Unseen Lighting:** PALM achieved an average length of 3.55, compared to OpenVLA (1.25) and Octo (1.05).
 - These results indicate PALM's robustness in long-horizon real-world scenarios.
- **Robustness of Progress Estimates:** The learned self-progress indicator (p_t) demonstrated robustness to dynamic perturbations:
 - **Random Relocation:** After a target object was relocated mid-execution, the predicted progress showed transient deviation followed by rapid recovery to its upward trend.
 - **Unseen Lighting:** Under abrupt changes in illumination, the progress estimate exhibited mild local deviations before returning to its prior trend.
 - **Multi-Object Visual Distractions:** The progress estimate remained stable when distractor objects were introduced, with localized deviations that promptly recovered.
 - This indicates that p_t tracks the underlying task state rather than spurious visual correlations.
- **Analysis of Real-Robot Failure Modes:** PALM achieved longer execution lengths and substantially reduced errors such as repeated actions (14% vs. 28-34%), skipped subtasks (8% vs. 16-26%), and premature stops (10% vs. 18-22%) compared to baselines.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results and Visualization

Visualizations of PALM's internal reasoning during a task like "Slide the pick block into the drawer" showed:
- **Global Affordance:** Accurate segmentation of the target object (block) and goal region (drawer).
- **Local Affordance:** Heatmaps precisely focused on interaction points, shifting from the block's graspable surface to the drawer handle.
- **Spatial Affordance:** Predicted keypoints indicating valid placement candidates for moving the block.
- **Dynamic Affordance:** Arrows visualizing predicted motion trajectories for the end-effector and object.
These visualizations confirm that PALM develops a comprehensive, structured representation of the task, enabling precise and robust control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PALM contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a VLA model that addresses limitations in long-horizon tasks through enhanced intermediate reasoning. By coupling structured future affordance prediction with continuous subtask progress estimation in a closed loop, the model provides an internal representation that supports more coherent and effective control across complex, multi-stage activities.

The achieved performance, including state-of-the-art results on CALVIN ABC→D (12.5% improvement) and LIBERO-LONG (91.8% success rate), along with demonstrated robustness in real-world generalization settings, indicates the technical progress made by PALM. This framework's ability to anticipate task-relevant object identities, interaction points, spatial goal regions, and motion patterns, combined with its temporal grounding through progress signals, aims to mitigate common failures observed in traditional VLA models, such as repeated actions, skipped steps, and premature termination.

The potential impact of PALM includes facilitating the transition of long-horizon manipulation capabilities from controlled laboratory environments to more unstructured, open-world settings like homes, warehouses, and hospitals. By exposing explicit affordance and progress signals, the model may also enable more natural integration with safety monitoring systems, constraint checks, and human-in-the-loop control strategies. This transparency could simplify visualization and debugging processes compared to more opaque, black-box policies.

However, the methodology acknowledges limitations. The online recovery capability of PALM remains a challenge when faced with execution drift caused by partial observability, contact uncertainty, occlusions, or geometric deformations. Furthermore, the processes of affordance-oriented segmentation, state grounding, and VLM-based semantic interpretation still incur considerable overhead in terms of annotation, perception, and inference. Future work intends to address these limitations by improving deviation detection and state-consistency checks, integrating reinforcement learning and failure analysis, and extending the framework to encompass 3D world perception. Such advancements could further enhance the stability, robustness, and scalability of robotic manipulation for broader applications.
