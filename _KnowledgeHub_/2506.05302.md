# Perceive Anything: Recognize, Explain, Caption, and Segment Anything in Images and Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> The Perceive Anything Model (PAM) extends the Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM 2) framework to achieve comprehensive region-level visual understanding, performing recognition, explanation, captioning, and segmentation in both images and videos. PAM operates 1.2-2.4 times faster and consumes less GPU memory than prior models, while achieving state-of-the-art performance across various image and video benchmarks, including novel streaming video region captioning tasks.

## Problem

- Modern segmentation models like SAM 2 excel at localizing objects but lack deep semantic understanding and cannot explain 'what' an object is or 'how' it functions.
- Existing region-level Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often provide limited semantic output granularity (basic categories, short captions) and lack a unified, efficient framework for both images and videos.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, fine-grained datasets, particularly for region-level understanding in streaming video, hindering the development of continuous, detailed visual analysis.

## Method

- The ==Perceive Anything Model (PAM)== extends the pre-existing ==SAM 2 framework== with a lightweight ==Semantic Perceiver== and a Qwen2.5 Large Language Model (LLM) as a semantic decoder, keeping SAM 2's parameters frozen.
- PAM features a ==parallel design== for its mask and semantic decoders, allowing for simultaneous generation of region masks and diverse semantic content, improving computational efficiency.
- A ==three-stage curriculum learning approach== (image pretraining, video-enhanced pretraining, multimodal fine-tuning) progressively trains the model, coupled with a novel data refinement pipeline that creates a high-quality, multi-granularity, and bilingual dataset.

## Results

- PAM runs **1.2 to 2.4 times faster** and consumes less GPU memory than prior models, demonstrating superior efficiency for both image and video processing.
- PAM-3B achieves optimal performance on the PACO benchmark and outperforms DAM-8B on LVIS for regional recognition and explanation, while also showing strong scene text recognition capabilities.
- PAM-3B sets new state-of-the-art performance on Elysium, BensMOT, and HC-STVG for video region captioning, and achieves optimal performance on the novel GPT-4o-evaluated Spatio-Temporal Description Continuity Score for streaming video region captioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficiently extending the frozen SAM 2 framework with a Semantic Perceiver allows direct bridging of its rich visual features to an LLM for comprehensive semantic understanding.
> - A parallel decoding design for mask and semantic generation significantly enhances computational efficiency, enabling simultaneous segmentation and detailed content output.
> - The development of a high-quality, multi-granularity dataset, including pioneering region-level streaming video captions, is critical for training a model capable of diverse and complex visual understanding tasks.
> - A staged curriculum learning approach, progressing from image to video understanding, optimizes the training process for robust spatio-temporal comprehension.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical gap in the capabilities of modern vision foundation models, specifically extending the Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM 2) from primarily an object segmentation tool to a comprehensive region-level visual understanding system. It operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields:

- **Foundation Models (Vision):** SAM and SAM 2 revolutionized interactive object segmentation by offering remarkable precision in localizing arbitrary objects. However, their core limitation is a lack of deep semantic understanding—they can segment "anything" but cannot explain "what it is" or "how it functions." PAM directly tackles this by integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with SAM 2's visual features. This aligns with the trend of building upon powerful, general-purpose foundation models to unlock more complex AI capabilities.

- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal AI:** The paper explicitly positions itself within the context of recent advancements in VLMs, which aim to bridge the gap between visual and linguistic understanding. Existing region-level VLMs, as highlighted by the authors, often suffer from several limitations:
 - **Limited Semantic Outputs:** Many only generate basic categories or short captions, failing to provide rich, multi-faceted semantic information (e.g., definitions, functionalities, detailed descriptions).
 - **Modality Specificity:** Most prior works focus either on images or videos, not offering a unified framework for both.
 - **Serial Processing:** Relying on external segmentation models for masks introduces computational overhead and makes overall performance dependent on mask quality.
 PAM distinguishes itself by offering simultaneous segmentation and diverse semantic outputs within an end-to-end, parallel processing framework for *both* images and videos.

- **Efficient AI and Deployment:** The paper emphasizes "lightweightness and efficiency," demonstrating that PAM runs 1.2-2.4× faster and consumes less GPU memory than prior approaches. This focus on practical deployability is crucial for real-world applications where computational resources are often constrained. It reflects a growing demand for performant yet accessible AI models.

- **Data-Centric AI:** A significant contribution lies in the development of a novel, high-quality dataset. Recognizing that existing datasets are often coarse or insufficient for fine-grained understanding, the authors implemented a sophisticated data refinement and augmentation pipeline leveraging advanced VLMs (like GPT-4o) and human validation. Crucially, they pioneer the construction of "region-level streaming video caption data," which is a largely unaddressed challenge in the field. This data-centric approach is vital for training models capable of complex, multi-granularity tasks.

- **Streaming Video Understanding:** The ability to perform "region-level streaming video captioning" is a cutting-edge capability. While some existing works focus on general event comprehension in streaming video, PAM extends this to specific regions, continuously tracking and describing them as the video progresses. This opens up new possibilities for real-time video analysis applications.

In essence, PAM represents a significant step towards truly "perceiving anything" by endowing a powerful segmentation model with a comprehensive, efficient, and multi-modal semantic understanding capability, pushing the boundaries of fine-grained visual-language comprehension in both static and dynamic contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "Perceive Anything Model (PAM)" is to create a **conceptually straightforward and efficient framework for comprehensive region-level visual understanding in images and videos**. This central goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging the Segmentation-Semantics Gap:** Modern segmentation models like SAM and SAM 2 excel at localizing objects but lack the ability to semantically interpret them. The primary motivation is to bridge this gap, enabling the model to not only identify "where an object is" but also "what it is," "how it functions," and "what it's doing."

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Region-Level VLMs:** The authors identify several shortcomings in current Vision-Language Models (VLMs that perform region-level understanding):
 - **Limited Output Granularity:** Many models provide only basic category labels or short captions, which are insufficient for a nuanced understanding of objects in context. PAM aims to generate diverse and detailed semantic outputs, including categories, definitions, contextual functionalities, and comprehensive captions.
 - **Modality Specificity:** Prior approaches often focus on either images or videos, leading to a fragmented understanding across visual modalities. PAM seeks to offer a unified, end-to-end solution for both images and videos.
 - **Inefficiency and Dependence on External Models:** Existing methods that rely on external segmentation models for masks introduce computational overhead and make the VLM's performance sensitive to the quality of these external masks. PAM's design integrates segmentation and semantic understanding in a parallel fashion to overcome these inefficiencies.

- **Enabling Fine-Grained, Contextual Understanding:** Beyond simple object recognition, there's a need for models to explain *why* an object is significant, *how* it's defined, and *what its role* is within a given scene. This fine-grained, contextual understanding is crucial for more sophisticated AI applications.

- **Achieving Practical Efficiency for Real-World Applications:** The motivation for "lightweightness and efficiency" is paramount. For AI systems to be deployed in real-world scenarios, they must be fast and resource-efficient. PAM aims to deliver strong performance without excessive computational cost, making it a practical solution.

- **Pioneering Region-Level Streaming Video Captioning:** A specific and challenging motivation is to address the unmet need for "continuous tracking and description of specific regions within a video stream." This capability is vital for applications requiring real-time, dynamic analysis of visual content.

- **Developing High-Quality, Multi-Granularity Data:** Recognizing that the success of powerful models often hinges on high-quality training data, a key objective was to curate a comprehensive dataset. This involves not only augmenting existing data but also creating novel annotation types (like streaming video captions) and ensuring bilingual support for broader applicability.

In summary, PAM is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of visual understanding towards a more holistic, detailed, efficient, and practical solution that can "perceive anything" in both static and dynamic visual content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Perceive Anything Model (PAM) adopts a modular and efficient approach, primarily by extending the powerful SAM 2 framework with Large Language Models (LLMs). The methodology can be broken down into architectural design, training strategies, and data construction.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

PAM consists of two main parts: the pre-existing SAM 2 framework and a newly introduced semantic decoder augmented by a Semantic Perceiver.

- **SAM 2 Framework (Frozen):** PAM leverages the robust spatio-temporal visual feature extraction and segmentation capabilities of the pre-trained SAM 2. This includes its image encoder, prompt encoder, memory modules, and mask decoder. Crucially, the parameters of SAM 2 are kept frozen during PAM's training to maintain its strong foundational segmentation abilities and computational efficiency.
- **Semantic Perceiver (The Bridge):** This is a key innovation. It acts as a bridge, efficiently transforming SAM 2's rich intermediate visual features (which inherently carry general vision, localization, and semantic priors) into multi-modal tokens suitable for LLM comprehension. Its architecture mirrors SAM 2's Feature Fusing Module (S2-FFM), using a lightweight two-layer transformer. It processes enhanced mask tokens and updated image embeddings from SAM 2, and concatenates learnable semantic tokens, ultimately outputting enriched visual and semantic tokens.
- **Projector:** Placed before the LLM, the projector consists of a pixel shuffle operation and an MLP projector. For images, a 2x2 pixel shuffle downsamples visual tokens. For videos, the prompted frame uses 2x2, while other frames use a more aggressive 4x4 pixel shuffle to further reduce visual tokens for efficiency. Distinct MLPs project visual and semantic tokens separately.
- **Semantic Decoder:** A pre-trained Qwen2.5 LLM (1.5B/3B parameters) is used. This LLM interprets the processed visual and semantic tokens, along with task instructions, to generate diverse semantic outputs.
- **Parallel Design:** A core efficiency feature is the parallel design of PAM's mask and semantic decoders. This allows for simultaneous generation of region masks and their corresponding semantic content, significantly improving computational efficiency compared to serial approaches.
- **Streaming Video Encode and Decode:** Building on SAM 2's memory modules for progressive historical information, PAM introduces a simple strategy for region-level streaming video captioning. An additional 2x2 pixel shuffle is applied to the last frame of each video clip to retain higher visual token density, which then serves as the initial frame for the subsequent clip. This ensures continuity. Furthermore, previous textual descriptions are incorporated into the prompt to provide additional contextual history to the LLM.

#### 3.2. Training Strategies

PAM employs a three-stage curriculum learning approach, progressively building its understanding capabilities from images to videos:

- **Stage 1: Image Pretraining and Alignment:** The initial stage focuses on aligning visual and semantic tokens with the language model's embedding space for region-level image understanding. It uses a large dataset of image classification and captioning. Only the Semantic Perceiver and Projector are trained in this stage.
- **Stage 1.5: Video-Enhanced Pretraining and Alignment:** This stage extends the training to include region-level video captions, enabling comprehension of dynamic scenes and spatio-temporal information. The trainable modules remain the Semantic Perceiver and Projector.
- **Stage 2: Multimodal Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The final stage fine-tunes the entire model (Semantic Perceiver, Projector, and Semantic Decoder/LLM) using the high-quality, refined, and augmented dataset. This phase enables PAM to perform diverse tasks and generate desired responses. Throughout all stages, SAM 2's parameters remain frozen.

#### 3.3. Data Refinement and Augmentation Pipeline

A crucial part of PAM's success is its dedicated data pipeline, yielding a high-quality, multi-granularity dataset (1.5M image and 0.6M video region-semantic annotations).

- **Image Dataset Construction:** For regional recognition, existing detection/segmentation/OCR datasets are used. For deeper semantic understanding (conceptual explanations, contextual functional roles, detailed descriptions), the authors leverage Set of Mask (SoM) prompting to identify regions, then use powerful VLMs like GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL-72B for generating refined and expanded textual responses. Manual quality assurance and a two-stage cleaning process (rule-based and manual review) ensure high quality.
- **Video Dataset Construction (Region-level Video Caption):** Existing video datasets are refined using a "storyboard-driven caption expansion" method. Keyframes (6 per video) are sampled and combined into a storyboard image. Object-centric highlighting is applied to the target object in each frame. GPT-4o is then prompted to generate refined, detailed, and temporally-aware descriptions, leveraging the multi-frame context for better comprehension.
- **Regional Streaming Video Caption Data (Novel):** This is a pioneering contribution. The refined video data is further augmented by segmenting videos into distinct events using TRACE-Uni. For each clip, the 'storyboard-driven' method is applied, but the GPT-4o prompt iteratively incorporates the description from the *preceding* video clip to ensure continuous, event-based descriptions.
- **Bilingual Annotations:** All data annotations are generated in both English and Chinese, enabling multilingual response capabilities for PAM.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a sophisticated architecture, phased training, and a meticulously curated dataset, allows PAM to achieve its ambitious goal of diverse, efficient, and comprehensive region-level visual understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PAM demonstrates its strong performance across a wide array of region-level understanding tasks for both images and videos, while notably achieving superior efficiency.

#### 4.1. Efficiency

- PAM exhibits significant computational advantages, running **1.2 to 2.4 times faster** and consuming **less GPU memory** compared to prior models for both image and video processing (as shown in Figure 8). This highlights its practical suitability for real-world applications.

#### 4.2. Image Benchmarks

- **Regional Recognition and Explanation:**
 - PAM-3B achieves **optimal performance on the PACO benchmark**, surpassing the previous best model (DAM-8B) by over 3.2% in Semantic Similarity and Semantic IoU (Table 1).
 - It also **outperforms DAM-8B on the LVIS benchmark** in terms of Semantic IoU, indicating its robust object-level recognition capabilities.
 - For scene text recognition, PAM-3B **outperforms VP-SPHINX-13B by over 3.5% on Total-Text** and achieves comparable results on COCO-Text, showcasing its promising OCR capabilities.
 - Qualitative examples (Figure 6) visually confirm PAM's ability to generate insightful explanations encompassing both general definitions and contextual functions, along with bilingual outputs.
- **Regional Captioning:**
 - PAM-3B **surpasses existing methods on Visual Genome (VG), RefCOCOg, and Ferret benchmarks** for region-level image captioning (Table 2).
 - It achieves **comparable performance to the current SOTA (VP-SPHINX-13B) on MDVP-Bench**.
 - On the challenging Ref-L4 dataset, PAM-3B demonstrates **outstanding performance**, outperforming all models except the top-performing DAM-8B, despite using fewer parameters and computational resources.

#### 4.3. Video Benchmarks

- **Video Region Captioning:**
 - Both PAM-1.5B and PAM-3B achieve **SOTA performance on the Elysium and BensMOT benchmarks** (Table 3).
 - PAM-3B surpasses the previous SOTA (DAM-8B) by 2.3% in METEOR on the **HC-STVG benchmark**.
 - While marginally lower on VideoRefer-Bench compared to some competitors, its overall strong performance across diverse benchmarks indicates robust video region captioning capabilities.
- **Streaming Video Region Captioning (Novel Task):**
 - Existing region-level video caption models show limited capabilities for streaming tasks.
 - PAM-3B significantly outperforms general streaming caption approaches like Streaming Vid2Seq on the METEOR metric on the ActivityNet dataset (Table 4).
 - Crucially, PAM-3B achieves **optimal performance on the newly proposed GPT-4o-evaluated Spatio-Temporal Description Continuity Score (G-STDC)**. This indicates its exceptional ability to maintain spatio-temporal continuity and consistent subject descriptions across sequential events in a streaming context. Qualitative examples (Figure 7) illustrate this continuity.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Number of Semantic Tokens:** 16 learnable semantic tokens were found to offer the most favorable performance-efficiency trade-off (Table 5).
- **Training Strategy:** The curriculum learning approach, specifically initialization from the image-video model checkpoint (Stage 1.5) followed by fine-tuning (Stage 2), consistently leads to enhanced performance compared to direct training or image-only pretraining (Table 6).
- **Intermediate Embeddings:** Utilizing visual embeddings *after* processing by SAM 2's S2-FFM module proved critical for improving the model's performance, underscoring the importance of feature selection (Table 7).

#### 4.5. Failure Cases (Appendix D.2)

While highly performant, PAM does exhibit some limitations:
- Inaccurate counting or identification of objects.
- OCR errors in complex scene text.
- Occasional hallucination of non-existent elements in descriptions.
- In videos, if a prompted object occupies a minor portion or temporarily disappears, PAM may default to describing a more salient object due to potential training data biases.
- In streaming captioning, historical descriptions can sometimes lead to overly similar outputs for consecutive clips.

Despite these limitations, the results firmly establish PAM as a highly capable and efficient model for comprehensive region-level visual understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Perceive Anything Model (PAM) represents a significant advancement in the field of computer vision and natural language processing, with several key areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Holistic Visual Understanding:** PAM moves beyond mere object detection and segmentation by integrating deep semantic understanding. Its ability to simultaneously segment, recognize, explain, and caption objects and regions in both images and videos fills a crucial gap, offering a more complete and human-like perception of visual content. This enables AI systems to not just "see" but truly "understand" what they are perceiving.

- **Advancing Foundation Models:** By effectively extending SAM 2's capabilities, PAM demonstrates a powerful paradigm for building upon general-purpose foundation models. It shows how specialized modules and thoughtful integration can unlock new, complex functionalities from existing strong backbones, rather than requiring entirely new architectures from scratch. This approach is resource-efficient in terms of development and leverages the robust pre-training of SAM 2.

- **Pioneering Streaming Region-Level Video Captioning:** This is a novel and highly impactful contribution. The ability to continuously track and describe specific regions in a streaming video offers unprecedented possibilities for real-time applications. This capability is critical for dynamic environments where continuous, fine-grained analysis is needed.

- **Enhanced Efficiency and Practicality:** The emphasis on lightweightness and faster inference (1.2-2.4× faster than prior approaches with less GPU memory) makes PAM highly practical for real-world deployment. This efficiency allows for its use in resource-constrained environments or applications requiring high throughput, moving advanced AI out of specialized labs and into broader commercial and public use.

- **High-Quality Data Contribution:** The meticulously curated, multi-granularity, and bilingual dataset is a substantial contribution to the research community. Such high-quality, comprehensive datasets are often bottlenecks in developing sophisticated AI models. The novel region-level streaming video caption data, in particular, will serve as a crucial resource for future research in this emerging area.

- **Multilingual Accessibility:** The inclusion of bilingual (English and Chinese) annotations broadens the model's applicability and accessibility to a wider global user base and research community, fostering cross-cultural AI development.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**
- **Accessibility Tools:** PAM could power advanced tools for visually impaired individuals, providing rich, real-time descriptions of their surroundings, including objects, their context, and their function.
- **Content Creation and Editing:** It could significantly aid in automated content generation, video summarization, and intelligent video editing by providing detailed, region-specific annotations.
- **Security and Surveillance:** Enhanced real-time monitoring capabilities, allowing for the precise description of events and objects within specific areas, could improve anomaly detection and situational awareness.
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Robots could gain a much deeper understanding of their environment, leading to more intelligent navigation, object manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
- **E-commerce and Product Understanding:** Detailed, automated descriptions of products and their features could revolutionize online shopping experiences and inventory management.
- **Educational Tools:** Interactive learning platforms could leverage PAM to provide comprehensive explanations of visual concepts in images and educational videos.

In conclusion, PAM delivers a robust, efficient, and versatile framework for fine-grained visual understanding. Its innovative integration of segmentation and semantics, coupled with novel data contributions, positions it as a strong baseline that will undoubtedly accelerate future research and development in multimodal AI, paving the way for more intelligent and practical vision-language applications.
