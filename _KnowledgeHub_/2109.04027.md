# Taxim

> [!summary] Summary
> **Taxim** is a realistic, high-speed simulation model for vision-based ==GelSight== tactile sensors. It simulates the optical response to elastomer deformation with an ==example-based polynomial look-up table== and reproduces marker-field motion via linear elastic deformation theory, calibrated from fewer than 100 real data points and runnable online on CPU.

## Problem

- Simulating tactile sensors realistically and efficiently has been a long-standing challenge, limiting large-scale data collection and sim-to-real transfer for touch-based manipulation.
- Prior GelSight simulators do not jointly model the optical response and the surface-marker motion field that arises from elastomer stretch.

## Method

- Proposes an ==example-based== optical simulation: a polynomial look-up table maps deformed contact geometry to per-pixel intensity captured by the embedded camera.
- Simulates surface-marker motion using ==linear elastic deformation theory== and the superposition principle, the first framework to combine marker-field and optical simulation.
- Calibrates the model from **fewer than 100 data points** off a real sensor, so it migrates easily to other GelSight variants.

## Results

- Achieves the **lowest pixel-wise intensity error** versus prior optical-simulation work.
- Runs **online on CPU**, making it practical for large-scale tactile data generation and sim-to-real pipelines.

## Takeaways

- A cheap-to-calibrate, CPU-fast tactile simulator that bridges the visuo-tactile sim-to-real gap; a common baseline for differentiable / learned tactile simulation.
